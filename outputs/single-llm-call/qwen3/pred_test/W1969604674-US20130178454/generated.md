# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- introduce invention overview

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic methods and pharmaceutical compositions for the selective enhancement of anabolic effects in skeletal muscle while avoiding adverse stimulation of the prostate gland. Specifically, the invention discloses a method of administering testosterone or a testosterone analogue in combination with an ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor to achieve a dissociation between the beneficial anabolic effects on muscle tissue and the undesirable proliferative effects on the prostate. This approach enables the restoration of physiological testosterone activity in individuals with low testosterone levels without triggering the growth of benign prostatic tissue or exacerbating conditions such as benign prostatic hyperplasia or prostate cancer. The invention is grounded in the discovery that ornithine decarboxylase serves as a critical molecular switch that mediates testosterone’s proliferative actions in the prostate but is dispensable for its anabolic actions in skeletal muscle. By inhibiting this enzyme, the therapeutic window for testosterone replacement is significantly widened, allowing for safer, more effective treatment of age-related muscle wasting, functional decline, and other low-testosterone-associated disorders.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce testosterone function

Testosterone is the primary androgenic steroid hormone in males, playing a central role in the development and maintenance of male reproductive tissues, secondary sexual characteristics, and overall somatic integrity. Beyond its reproductive functions, testosterone exerts profound anabolic effects on skeletal muscle, promoting protein synthesis, increasing lean body mass, enhancing muscle strength, and improving physical performance. It also contributes to bone mineral density, erythropoiesis, and metabolic regulation. These systemic actions make testosterone a critical mediator of physiological resilience, particularly in aging populations where its natural production declines.

- describe testosterone deficiency

Testosterone deficiency, or hypogonadism, is a common endocrine disorder characterized by insufficient circulating levels of testosterone below the physiological threshold required to maintain normal tissue function. This condition may arise from primary testicular failure, hypothalamic-pituitary dysfunction, or age-related decline in Leydig cell activity. The prevalence of testosterone deficiency increases substantially with advancing age, affecting a significant proportion of men over the age of 50, and is often underdiagnosed due to its nonspecific symptomatology.

- list symptoms of hypogonadism

Clinical manifestations of testosterone deficiency include reduced muscle mass and strength, increased fat accumulation, diminished physical endurance, fatigue, decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, mood disturbances such as depression and irritability, impaired cognitive function, and reduced bone density. These symptoms collectively contribute to a decline in quality of life and functional independence, particularly in older adults.

- discuss current testosterone replacement therapies

Current standard-of-care treatments for testosterone deficiency involve exogenous administration of testosterone via multiple routes, including intramuscular injections, transdermal gels and patches, buccal tablets, and subcutaneous pellets. These formulations aim to restore serum testosterone levels to the normal physiological range and alleviate the symptoms associated with deficiency. Despite their widespread use, these therapies are limited by significant safety concerns, particularly regarding their effects on the prostate gland.

- describe side effects of current therapies

A major limitation of conventional testosterone replacement therapy is its propensity to stimulate proliferation of prostate epithelial cells, leading to increased prostate volume, elevated prostate-specific antigen levels, and potential exacerbation of benign prostatic hyperplasia or promotion of latent prostate cancer. These risks have led to cautious prescribing practices, especially in older men, and have discouraged broader clinical adoption despite compelling evidence of its benefits for muscle and metabolic health.

- discuss testosterone levels in aging men

With advancing age, serum testosterone levels decline at a rate of approximately 1% per year after the age of 30, a phenomenon often referred to as andropause. This decline is not uniform across individuals but is associated with a progressive loss of lean mass, increased frailty, and higher susceptibility to metabolic and cardiovascular diseases. The reduction in bioavailable testosterone is particularly pronounced in men with obesity, chronic illness, or sedentary lifestyles.

- correlate low testosterone with diseases

Low testosterone levels have been epidemiologically linked to a constellation of age-related conditions, including sarcopenia, osteoporosis, type 2 diabetes, insulin resistance, cardiovascular disease, and cognitive decline. These associations suggest that testosterone deficiency is not merely a hormonal imbalance but a systemic state of physiological deterioration that contributes to morbidity and mortality.

- describe testosterone's role in women's health

Although testosterone is present in much lower concentrations in females, it plays a vital role in maintaining muscle mass, bone density, libido, and overall energy metabolism. Postmenopausal women, in particular, experience a decline in testosterone production due to ovarian atrophy, which contributes to muscle wasting and reduced physical function. Therapeutic testosterone administration in women has shown promise in improving strength and well-being, but is limited by virilizing side effects.

- discuss virilization in women

In women, exogenous testosterone administration can lead to undesirable androgenic effects such as hirsutism, voice deepening, clitoromegaly, and acne. These side effects are dose-dependent and often irreversible, restricting the utility of testosterone therapy in female populations and necessitating the development of selective delivery strategies.

- highlight need for alternative therapies

There remains a critical unmet medical need for therapeutic strategies that preserve or enhance the anabolic benefits of testosterone while eliminating its proliferative effects on the prostate and other androgen-sensitive tissues. The ideal approach would dissociate the signaling pathways responsible for muscle growth from those driving prostate proliferation, thereby enabling safe, long-term administration of testosterone in both men and women without compromising tissue-specific safety.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce testosterone administration objectives

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide a method of testosterone administration that selectively enhances anabolic activity in skeletal muscle while preventing unwanted stimulation of the prostate gland. This is achieved by co-administering testosterone or a testosterone analogue with an ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor, thereby blocking a key downstream mediator of testosterone’s proliferative effects without interfering with its muscle-building actions.

- define beneficial effects of testosterone

Testosterone administration promotes the synthesis of contractile proteins, increases satellite cell activation, enhances muscle fiber hypertrophy, and improves neuromuscular coordination. These effects translate into measurable gains in lean body mass, maximal voluntary strength, walking speed, stair-climbing power, and overall physical function, particularly in individuals with sarcopenia or age-related functional decline.

- describe side effects of testosterone therapy

Despite these benefits, testosterone therapy is associated with significant adverse effects, including prostatic enlargement, increased prostate-specific antigen levels, potential stimulation of undiagnosed prostate cancer, erythrocytosis, and fluid retention. These side effects are mediated through androgen receptor-dependent transcriptional programs that are active in the prostate but not in skeletal muscle.

- motivate alternative testosterone administration methods

The need for alternative methods of testosterone delivery arises from the inability of current formulations to distinguish between beneficial and harmful androgenic effects. Conventional approaches that rely on systemic testosterone elevation inherently activate all androgen-responsive tissues, making it impossible to achieve tissue-selective outcomes.

- introduce ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitor

Ornithine decarboxylase is the rate-limiting enzyme in the biosynthesis of polyamines, including putrescine, spermidine, and spermine, which are essential for cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. In the prostate, ornithine decarboxylase expression is tightly regulated by androgens and is indispensable for testosterone-induced epithelial growth.

- describe ODC's role in testosterone's growth promoting effect

The present invention is based on the discovery that ornithine decarboxylase is a critical mediator of testosterone’s proliferative effects in the prostate gland. In contrast, skeletal muscle growth induced by testosterone proceeds independently of ornithine decarboxylase activity, as demonstrated by the preservation of anabolic responses even when the enzyme is pharmacologically inhibited.

- summarize prior art on ODC and testosterone

Prior studies have established that ornithine decarboxylase is upregulated in the prostate in response to androgen stimulation and that its inhibition reduces prostate size in animal models. However, these studies did not investigate whether inhibition of ornithine decarboxylase could selectively block prostate growth while preserving muscle anabolism in the context of testosterone replacement.

- contrast prior art with inventors' discovery

Unlike previous approaches that focused on androgen receptor modulation or prostate-specific targeting, the present inventors identified that the divergence between testosterone’s effects on muscle and prostate lies not in receptor signaling but in downstream metabolic pathways. The discovery that ornithine decarboxylase is required for prostate growth but not for muscle growth represents a fundamental paradigm shift in androgen therapy.

- describe inventors' discovery on ODC and testosterone

Through transcriptomic and functional analyses, the inventors demonstrated that testosterone induces ornithine decarboxylase expression in the prostate but not in skeletal muscle. Furthermore, they showed that administration of a specific ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor, such as α-difluoromethylornithine, completely abrogates testosterone-induced prostate enlargement while fully preserving its ability to increase muscle mass and strength.

- introduce follistatin and its effects on muscle mass

The inventors further discovered that follistatin, a natural mediator of testosterone’s anabolic effects in muscle, increases skeletal muscle mass without stimulating prostate growth. This observation reinforced the hypothesis that distinct molecular pathways govern testosterone’s actions in different tissues.

- describe follistatin's lack of effect on prostate

Administration of recombinant follistatin resulted in significant increases in lean body mass and muscle weight but had no measurable effect on prostate size or epithelial proliferation markers, indicating that the follistatin pathway bypasses the ornithine decarboxylase-dependent proliferative program in the prostate.

- summarize signaling pathways activated by testosterone and follistatin

Testosterone activates the androgen receptor–β-catenin–follistatin axis in skeletal muscle to promote myogenesis, whereas in the prostate, it activates the androgen receptor–ornithine decarboxylase–polyamine axis to drive epithelial proliferation. Follistatin mimics the former pathway but does not engage the latter, providing a natural blueprint for selective anabolic therapy.

- describe blocking testosterone's effects on prostate with ODC inhibitor

By pharmacologically inhibiting ornithine decarboxylase, the inventors successfully uncoupled testosterone’s proliferative effects on the prostate from its anabolic effects on muscle. This selective blockade was achieved without altering serum testosterone levels or androgen receptor activation, confirming that the effect is downstream of receptor binding.

- introduce specific ODC inhibitors

The invention encompasses the use of irreversible ornithine decarboxylase inhibitors such as α-difluoromethylornithine, as well as reversible inhibitors including cyclohexylamine derivatives, guanidino compounds, and novel small molecules identified through high-throughput screening.

- describe method for selectively targeting testosterone's anabolic effects

The method involves the co-administration of testosterone or a testosterone analogue with an ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor in a ratio and dosage sufficient to maintain anabolic muscle signaling while suppressing polyamine biosynthesis in the prostate.

- introduce new formulation for testosterone replacement therapy

The invention further provides novel pharmaceutical formulations that combine testosterone or its derivatives with an ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor in a single dosage form, enabling synchronized delivery and optimized pharmacokinetic profiles.

- describe conditions for using combination of testosterone and ODC inhibitor

This combination is particularly indicated for men with low testosterone levels who are at risk for benign prostatic hyperplasia, men with a history of prostate cancer in remission, elderly individuals with sarcopenia, and women requiring testosterone therapy without virilizing side effects.

- summarize selective beneficial effect of testosterone with ODC inhibitor

The combination therapy produces a selective anabolic effect: increased lean mass, improved physical function, enhanced recovery from muscle injury, and greater endurance, all without increasing prostate volume, prostate-specific antigen, or epithelial proliferation markers.

- introduce low-testosterone associated disorder treatment

The invention provides a method for treating disorders associated with low testosterone levels, including sarcopenia, frailty, osteoporosis, anemia, and functional decline, by restoring physiological testosterone activity while avoiding prostate-related complications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce invention concept

The invention introduces a novel therapeutic paradigm in which the anabolic benefits of testosterone are preserved while its proliferative effects on the prostate are selectively inhibited through the pharmacological blockade of ornithine decarboxylase. This strategy overcomes the longstanding limitation of testosterone replacement therapy by decoupling tissue-specific signaling pathways downstream of the androgen receptor.

- motivate testosterone effects

Testosterone is a potent anabolic agent capable of reversing the physiological decline associated with aging and chronic disease. However, its clinical utility has been constrained by its unavoidable stimulation of the prostate, a tissue that shares the same receptor but diverges in downstream effector mechanisms.

- describe ODC inhibitor effects

Ornithine decarboxylase inhibitors prevent the synthesis of polyamines, which are essential for cell cycle progression and epithelial proliferation in the prostate. Their administration does not interfere with androgen receptor binding or transcriptional activation in muscle, allowing anabolic signaling to proceed unimpeded.

- define pharmaceutical composition

The pharmaceutical composition comprises testosterone or a testosterone analogue in combination with an ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor, formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier to ensure stability, bioavailability, and controlled release.

- outline co-administrable combination

The combination is administered simultaneously or sequentially in a manner that ensures both agents reach target tissues at concentrations sufficient to exert their respective pharmacological effects without systemic toxicity.

- describe method for increasing testosterone

The method involves the administration of testosterone or a testosterone analogue to a subject with serum testosterone levels below 350 ng/dL, in combination with an ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor, to elevate serum testosterone to the mid-normal range while preventing prostate growth.

- summarize treatment applications

Treatment applications include the management of age-related hypogonadism, sarcopenia, frailty, cachexia, postoperative recovery, and functional decline in both men and women.

- outline androgen replacement therapy

Androgen replacement therapy is redefined by this invention as a tissue-selective intervention rather than a systemic hormone replacement, enabling safer, more targeted use in vulnerable populations.

- describe methods for treating disorders

Methods for treating disorders include oral, transdermal, injectable, or implantable administration of the combination, with dosing adjusted based on serum testosterone, prostate-specific antigen, and functional outcome measures.

- outline kit composition

The diagnostic and therapeutic kit includes pre-measured doses of testosterone and ornithine decarboxylase inhibitor, along with instructions for administration, monitoring parameters, and reference ranges for serum biomarkers.

- summarize kit instructions

Kit instructions specify the initiation of therapy in subjects with confirmed low testosterone and no active prostate cancer, with monthly monitoring of prostate-specific antigen and quarterly assessment of muscle mass and physical function.

The chemical structure consists of a cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene ring system with a hydroxyl group at the 17β position and a double bond between carbons 4 and 5.

Testosterone is isolated from animal testes or synthesized from cholesterol via multi-step organic synthesis, followed by crystallization and chromatographic purification.
