## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cyclin D1 is a critical regulatory protein that governs the transition from the G1 phase to the S phase of the cell cycle, serving as a key mediator of mitogenic signaling in both normal and transformed cells. Its expression is tightly controlled at the transcriptional level by growth factor pathways, particularly the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK cascade, which induces cyclin D1 synthesis in response to extracellular stimuli. Once synthesized, cyclin D1 forms complexes with cyclin-dependent kinases CDK4 and CDK6, facilitating phosphorylation of the retinoblastoma protein and thereby promoting cell cycle progression. However, the persistence of cyclin D1 beyond the G1 phase can lead to uncontrolled proliferation, genomic instability, and tumorigenesis, making its timely degradation an essential checkpoint in cell cycle control. Dysregulation of cyclin D1 turnover is a hallmark of numerous human malignancies, including breast, colon, and glioblastoma cancers, where its overexpression correlates with poor prognosis and therapeutic resistance. While transcriptional upregulation of cyclin D1 has been extensively documented, the molecular mechanisms governing its post-translational destabilization remain incompletely understood. Cyclin D1 is known to be targeted for proteasomal degradation following phosphorylation at threonine 286, a modification that triggers its nuclear export and subsequent recognition by ubiquitin ligase complexes. Historically, glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK3β) was proposed as the primary kinase responsible for this phosphorylation event; however, recent evidence has challenged this model, suggesting alternative kinases may play dominant roles in specific cellular contexts. The ubiquitin-proteasome system mediates the degradation of cyclin D1 through a highly conserved enzymatic cascade involving E1 ubiquitin-activating enzymes, E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, and E3 ubiquitin ligases that confer substrate specificity. Among the most prominent E3 ligase families are the SCF (SKP1-CUL1-F-box protein) complexes, which recognize phosphorylated substrates via their F-box protein components. Despite extensive investigation, the precise E3 ligase responsible for cyclin D1 ubiquitination has remained elusive, with prior candidates such as SKP2 showing inconsistent or context-dependent effects across cell types. The SCF-like complex involving Cullin 7 and the F-box protein FBXW8 has been implicated in the regulation of other substrates, but its role in cyclin D1 turnover had not been established. The lack of a definitive molecular link between the MAPK signaling pathway, cyclin D1 phosphorylation, and its ubiquitin-mediated degradation has hindered the development of targeted therapeutic strategies aimed at restoring normal cyclin D1 turnover in cancer cells. Current understanding of cyclin D1 regulation is fragmented, with insufficient mechanistic clarity regarding the identity of the kinase that phosphorylates Thr286 under physiological conditions in cancer cells, the E3 ligase that recognizes this phosphorylated form, and the spatial and temporal coordination of these events during cell cycle progression. There is a critical need for compounds and methods capable of modulating the stability of cyclin D1 by specifically targeting its phosphorylation or ubiquitination pathways, particularly in cancers characterized by aberrant cyclin D1 accumulation. The present invention addresses this unmet need by identifying a novel regulatory axis in which the MAPK pathway directly phosphorylates cyclin D1 at Thr286 through a defined docking motif, and in which the F-box protein FBXW8 acts as the specific E3 ubiquitin ligase that recognizes this phosphorylated form, thereby orchestrating its polyubiquitination and degradation. This discovery provides a precise molecular target for therapeutic intervention and enables the development of diagnostic and prognostic tools based on the functional status of this pathway.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for modulating cellular proliferation by targeting the regulated degradation of cyclin D1 through its phosphorylation-dependent ubiquitination by the FBXW8-containing E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. The invention encompasses methods for detecting agents that modulate the phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at threonine 286, as well as agents that modulate its ubiquitination by the FBXW8 complex, thereby controlling cell proliferation in a manner that is therapeutically relevant to cancer. The invention further provides methods for controlling cell proliferation by modulating the activity of FBXW8, either through direct inhibition or enhancement of its function, or by altering the expression levels of the FBXW8-encoding nucleic acid through transcriptional or translational regulation. The invention also includes methods for modulating the activation of the E3 ubiquitin ligase complex by influencing the assembly of FBXW8 with its core components, including SKP1, CUL1, CUL7, and RBX1, or by modulating the stability or degradation of FBXW8 itself. Additionally, the invention provides methods for modulating the physical interaction between FBXW8 and phosphorylated cyclin D1, thereby preventing or promoting the ubiquitination of cyclin D1. The invention further introduces methods for screening test agents capable of modulating the interaction between FBXW8 and phosphorylated cyclin D1, including assays designed to detect changes in the binding affinity, complex formation, or ubiquitination efficiency of this interaction. The invention enables detection of the effect of test agents on the total levels of phosphorylated cyclin D1, total cyclin D1, and ubiquitinated cyclin D1, providing a comprehensive framework for evaluating compound efficacy. The invention further includes isolated polypeptide complexes comprising FBXW8, SKP1, CUL7, and ubiquitinated cyclin D1, as well as reaction mixtures containing purified components of the ubiquitination pathway, including E1, E2, FBXW8-containing E3 ligase, and phosphorylated cyclin D1, for use in in vitro screening and mechanistic studies. The invention encompasses the use of these complexes and mixtures to identify and characterize small molecules, peptides, nucleic acids, or biologics that inhibit or enhance the ubiquitination of cyclin D1, thereby providing a foundation for the development of novel anti-proliferative therapies. The invention further provides methods for detecting the effect of test agents on the subcellular localization of cyclin D1, particularly its redistribution from the nucleus to the cytoplasm, and on the ratio of cytoplasmic to nuclear cyclin D1, as these dynamics are integral to its degradation. The invention enables the identification of agents that modulate the activity of MAPK, thereby indirectly controlling cyclin D1 phosphorylation, and agents that directly interfere with the FBXW8-cyclin D1 interaction, offering multiple distinct therapeutic entry points. The invention further provides compositions and methods for delivering nucleic acid-based modulators, including siRNA and antisense oligonucleotides, directed against FBXW8, cyclin D1, or MAPK, to achieve targeted suppression of this pathway in vivo. The invention thus provides a comprehensive, mechanistically grounded platform for the discovery and development of therapeutics that restore normal cyclin D1 turnover in proliferative disorders, particularly cancer.

### FBXW8 Protein (F-BOX WD-40 Domain Protein 8)

FBXW8 is a member of the F-box protein family characterized by the presence of an N-terminal F-box domain and multiple C-terminal WD40 repeats, which mediate substrate recognition. The F-box domain enables FBXW8 to bind SKP1, while the WD40 repeats form a β-propeller structure that specifically interacts with phosphorylated cyclin D1 at threonine 286. FBXW8 functions as the substrate recognition subunit of an SCF-like E3 ubiquitin ligase complex composed of SKP1, CUL7, and RBX1, and is distinct from the classical SCF complex that utilizes CUL1. The protein is expressed in a wide range of human tissues and is highly conserved across mammalian species. FBXW8 is synthesized as a full-length polypeptide of approximately 800 amino acids and undergoes post-translational modifications that may regulate its stability or activity. The gene encoding FBXW8 is located on human chromosome 19q13.43 and contains multiple alternatively spliced isoforms, although the full-length variant is the primary functional form in the context of cyclin D1 regulation. Sequence variations in FBXW8, including single nucleotide polymorphisms and somatic mutations, have been identified in various cancers and may correlate with altered cyclin D1 turnover and disease progression. Regions of FBXW8 suitable for modification include the F-box domain, the WD40 repeats, and the linker regions between these domains, which may be targeted for mutagenesis, fusion, or chemical modification to generate inhibitors or stabilizers. GenBank accession numbers for human FBXW8 include NM_015168 for the mRNA sequence and NP_055983 for the predicted protein sequence. Additional isoforms and orthologs from mouse, rat, and non-human primates are available under accession numbers NM_001111053, NM_001109557, and XM_005553054, respectively. The invention includes recombinant forms of FBXW8, including tagged versions for purification and detection, as well as dominant-negative mutants lacking the F-box domain that compete with endogenous FBXW8 and stabilize cyclin D1.

### MAPK (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase)

MAPK, specifically ERK1 and ERK2, is a serine/threonine kinase that phosphorylates cyclin D1 at threonine 286 in a manner dependent on a D-domain motif located between amino acids 179 and 193 of cyclin D1. This interaction is essential for efficient phosphorylation and distinguishes MAPK from other kinases such as GSK3β, which do not require such docking motifs. MAPK is activated by upstream kinases MEK1 and MEK2 through dual phosphorylation at threonine 202 and tyrosine 204. The gene encoding ERK1 is MAPK3 (chromosome 16p11.2), and the gene encoding ERK2 is MAPK1 (chromosome 22q11.23). Both isoforms are highly conserved and share over 85% sequence identity. MAPK activity is elevated in many cancers due to oncogenic mutations in upstream components of the Ras/Raf/MEK pathway. The invention includes recombinant forms of ERK1 and ERK2, including constitutively active mutants and kinase-dead variants, for use in in vitro assays. Sequence variations in MAPK, including somatic mutations and polymorphisms, have been associated with altered kinase activity and drug resistance. GenBank accession numbers for human ERK1 and ERK2 include NM_002745 and NM_002746, respectively. The invention further includes antibodies specific for phosphorylated ERK, as well as small molecule inhibitors of MEK and ERK, which are useful for modulating cyclin D1 phosphorylation in experimental and therapeutic contexts.

### CULLIN 1 (CUL1)

CUL1 is a scaffold protein that forms the core of the canonical SCF ubiquitin ligase complex by binding SKP1 at its N-terminus and RBX1 at its C-terminus. While CUL1 is not the primary cullin partner for FBXW8, it may form alternative complexes with other F-box proteins that indirectly influence cyclin D1 turnover. The gene encoding CUL1 is located on chromosome 11q23.3 and encodes a protein of approximately 776 amino acids. CUL1 undergoes neddylation, a post-translational modification essential for its E3 ligase activity. The invention includes recombinant CUL1, dominant-negative mutants lacking the C-terminal domain, and antibodies specific for neddylated versus unneddylated forms. GenBank accession numbers for human CUL1 include NM_004935 and NP_004926. Sequence variations in CUL1 have been identified in certain cancers and may affect substrate specificity or complex assembly.

### Cullin 7 (CUL7)

CUL7 is a cullin family member that, in association with SKP1 and FBXW8, forms an SCF-like E3 ubiquitin ligase complex distinct from the classical SCF complex. CUL7 is essential for the ubiquitination of phosphorylated cyclin D1 and is required for its degradation in cancer cells. The gene encoding CUL7 is located on chromosome 6p21.3 and encodes a large protein of approximately 2,300 amino acids with multiple domains, including a cullin homology domain, a C-terminal domain, and a PHD-BBox domain. CUL7 binds SKP1 through an adaptor protein and is regulated by neddylation and proteasomal degradation. The invention includes recombinant CUL7, deletion mutants, and antibodies specific for its functional domains. GenBank accession numbers for human CUL7 include NM_014957 and NP_055772. Mutations in CUL7 are associated with 3-M syndrome, but somatic alterations in cancer may enhance or impair its function in cyclin D1 regulation.

### SKP1 (S-Phase Kinase-associated Protein 1)

SKP1 is an adaptor protein that mediates the interaction between F-box proteins and cullin scaffolds, enabling the assembly of functional E3 ubiquitin ligase complexes. SKP1 binds the F-box domain of FBXW8 and the N-terminal domain of CUL1 or CUL7, thereby positioning the substrate recognition module for ubiquitin transfer. The gene encoding SKP1 is located on chromosome 19p13.3 and encodes a conserved protein of 177 amino acids. SKP1 is essential for the activity of both SCF and SCF-like complexes and is ubiquitously expressed. The invention includes recombinant SKP1, mutant forms defective in cullin or F-box binding, and antibodies for detection. GenBank accession numbers for human SKP1 include NM_003008 and NP_002999. The invention further includes siRNAs and antisense oligonucleotides targeting SKP1 for use in experimental and therapeutic modulation of cyclin D1 degradation.

### Cyclin D1

Cyclin D1 is a regulatory protein encoded by the CCND1 gene that controls G1 to S phase progression by forming complexes with CDK4 and CDK6. Its expression is induced by mitogenic signals and its degradation is essential for cell cycle progression. Cyclin D1 contains a D-domain motif at amino acids 179–193 that mediates direct interaction with MAPK, enabling phosphorylation at threonine 286. This phosphorylation event triggers nuclear export and recognition by FBXW8. The invention includes wild-type cyclin D1, phosphorylation-deficient mutants such as T286A, D-domain deletion mutants, and fusion proteins tagged with epitopes for detection. GenBank accession numbers for human cyclin D1 include NM_001759 and NP_001750. The invention further includes phospho-specific antibodies against Thr286, as well as methods for detecting phosphorylated cyclin D1 in clinical samples as a biomarker of MAPK pathway activation.

## EXAMPLES

### Cyclin D1 Protein Is Destabilized Specifically In S Phase In Cancer Cells

Cyclin D1 expression was analyzed in normal and cancer cell lines synchronized at G0/G1 and released into the cell cycle. Flow cytometry confirmed cell cycle progression, and immunoblotting revealed that while cyclin D1 levels remained stable during S phase in normal fibroblasts, they were dramatically reduced in cancer cell lines including HCT 116, SW480, and T98G. Pulse-chase analysis using ³⁵S-methionine labeling demonstrated that the half-life of cyclin D1 in HCT 116 cells decreased from 27.5 minutes in G1 phase to 11.8 minutes in S phase, whereas no significant change was observed in NIH 3T3 cells. These findings indicate that cyclin D1 degradation is specifically accelerated during S phase in cancer cells, suggesting a cancer-specific regulatory mechanism.

### Cyclin D1 Protein Is Degraded During the S Phase Through the Ubiquitin-proteasome Pathway In Cancer Cells

Treatment of HCT 116 cells with the proteasome inhibitor MG132 resulted in significant accumulation of cyclin D1 specifically during S phase, but not in G1 phase, confirming proteasomal degradation. Transfection of HCT 116 cells with HA-tagged ubiquitin followed by immunoprecipitation of cyclin D1 and immunoblotting with anti-HA antibody revealed higher molecular weight smears indicative of polyubiquitinated cyclin D1, which were enhanced by MG132 treatment. Subcellular fractionation demonstrated that polyubiquitinated cyclin D1 was predominantly localized in the cytoplasm, and inhibition of nuclear export with leptomycin B did not increase ubiquitination in the nucleus. These results confirm that cyclin D1 is degraded in the cytoplasm during S phase via the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway in cancer cells.

### Expression of Cyclin D1 Protein in the Nucleus Decreases Through the G1-S Transition in Tumor Cells

Immunofluorescence staining of synchronized HCT 116 cells showed that cyclin D1 was predominantly nuclear during G1 phase and translocated to the cytoplasm upon entry into S phase. This redistribution coincided with a decrease in nuclear cyclin D1 levels and an increase in cytoplasmic ubiquitination. Phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at Thr286, detected using a phospho-specific antibody, was minimal in the nucleus during G1 and increased in the cytoplasm during S phase. These observations indicate that phosphorylation-dependent nuclear export is a prerequisite for cyclin D1 degradation in cancer cells.

### Phosphorylation of Cyclin D1 Protein is Mediated by the Same Mechanism as Cyclin D1 Gene Expression, Via the MAPK Signaling Pathway

Inhibition of MEK with U0126 reduced phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at Thr286 without affecting total cyclin D1 levels, and increased its half-life from 22.5 to 54.6 minutes. Mutation of Thr286 to alanine rendered cyclin D1 resistant to degradation. In vitro kinase assays using purified ERK2 demonstrated direct phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at Thr286, which was abolished by deletion of the D-domain (amino acids 179–193). Immunoprecipitation confirmed that ERK2 binds cyclin D1 via the D-domain. These results establish that MAPK directly phosphorylates cyclin D1 at Thr286 in a D-domain-dependent manner, linking transcriptional activation to post-translational degradation.

### Example 5

Ectopic expression of wild-type cyclin D1 in HCT 116 cells was stabilized by inhibition of MEK, but not by inhibition of GSK3β or CDK4. Mutation of Thr286 to alanine or deletion of the D-domain prevented ubiquitination in vitro. In vitro ubiquitination assays using fraction II HeLa cell extracts demonstrated that ERK2 enhanced cyclin D1 polyubiquitination, and this enhancement was abolished in the T286A and ΔD mutants. Overexpression of FBXW8 reduced endogenous cyclin D1 levels, while dominant-negative FBXW8 or siRNA-mediated knockdown of FBXW8, CUL7, or CUL1 caused cyclin D1 accumulation. Pulse-chase analysis confirmed stabilization of cyclin D1 upon depletion of these components. Knockdown of FBXW8 or CUL7 led to cytoplasmic accumulation of cyclin D1 and sequestration of CDK4, resulting in reduced CDK4 kinase activity and inhibition of cell proliferation. Re-expression of the nuclear-localized T286A mutant cyclin D1 rescued proliferation in FBXW8-depleted cells. These results demonstrate that FBXW8-mediated degradation of phosphorylated cyclin D1 in the cytoplasm is essential for cancer cell proliferation.
