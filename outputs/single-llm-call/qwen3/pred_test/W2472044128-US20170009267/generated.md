## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- The field of genetic engineering has advanced significantly in recent decades, particularly in the design of microbial cell factories capable of synthesizing complex biomolecules from simple carbon sources. Among these, N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) has emerged as a critically important compound due to its role as a precursor for bioactive polymers such as chitin, hyaluronic acid, and bacterial cell wall components, as well as its applications in pharmaceuticals, nutraceuticals, and cosmetic formulations. GlcNAc is traditionally produced through chemical synthesis from chitin or chitosan, processes that involve harsh reagents, high energy inputs, and significant environmental waste, rendering them economically and ecologically unsustainable. Alternatively, microbial fermentation using recombinant strains of Escherichia coli or Saccharomyces cerevisiae has been explored, yet these systems often require expensive nitrogen sources, complex media supplements, or inducible expression systems that increase production costs and complicate scale-up. Current methods suffer from low volumetric productivity, poor yield on glucose, and metabolic instability under industrially relevant conditions, particularly in minimal media where nutrient availability is tightly controlled. These limitations have impeded the widespread adoption of microbial GlcNAc production as a viable alternative to chemical extraction. There remains a critical and unmet need for a genetically stable, high-yielding, and cost-effective microbial platform capable of producing GlcNAc directly from glucose in minimal medium without reliance on costly inducers or supplemental nutrients, thereby enabling economically scalable biomanufacturing with minimal downstream processing requirements.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- The goal of the present invention is to provide a genetically engineered strain of Bacillus subtilis capable of high-yield, high-productivity synthesis of N-acetylglucosamine directly from glucose under minimal medium conditions, while simultaneously overcoming the metabolic inefficiencies that have historically limited microbial GlcNAc production. This is achieved through the targeted disruption of an unrecognized ATP-dissipating futile cycle that arises between intracellular GlcNAc and its phosphorylated derivative, GlcNAc-6-phosphate, a phenomenon previously unreported in Bacillus subtilis. The invention comprises the construction of a recombinant B. subtilis strain designated BSGN6-PxylA-glmS-P43-GNA1, which has been further modified by the precise deletion of the glcK gene encoding glucokinase. This genetic modification prevents the re-phosphorylation of extracellularly accumulated GlcNAc back into GlcNAc-6-phosphate, thereby eliminating the futile cycle that consumes ATP without net product formation. The strain is engineered to overexpress glucosamine-6-phosphate synthase (GlmS) under the control of the xylose-inducible PxylA promoter and GlcN-6-phosphate N-acetyltransferase (Gna1) under the constitutive P43 promoter, while simultaneously deleting all known genes involved in GlcNAc catabolism, including nagP, gamP, nagA, nagB, and gamA, to ensure metabolic trapping of the product. The resulting strain, designated herein as BSGNK, exhibits a more than two-fold increase in specific growth rate and a 2.3-fold improvement in GlcNAc yield per gram of glucose compared to its parental strain, without requiring any exogenous inducers or nitrogen supplements. The method for producing GlcNAc involves cultivating the recombinant BSGNK strain in a synthetic minimal medium containing glucose as the sole carbon source, ammonium chloride as the nitrogen source, and essential inorganic salts, under controlled aerobic conditions at 37°C with agitation. The synthetic medium is formulated to support robust cell growth while minimizing metabolic byproduct accumulation and maintaining optimal redox and energy balance. The benefits of the invention include a dramatic reduction in ATP waste, improved cellular energy charge, enhanced volumetric productivity, and the elimination of the need for complex media components or inducer molecules, resulting in a streamlined, cost-efficient, and scalable bioprocess suitable for industrial deployment. The strain exhibits genetic stability over multiple generations in the absence of selective pressure, making it ideal for large-scale fermentation.

### EXAMPLES

- The materials and methods employed in the examples include the use of standard molecular biology techniques for gene deletion, plasmid construction, and strain verification, as well as high-precision analytical methods for metabolite quantification. The seed medium consisted of Luria-Bertani broth supplemented with kanamycin at 20 μg/mL and spectinomycin at 100 μg/mL, used to propagate the parental BSGN strain prior to genetic modification. The fermentation medium was a defined M9 mineral salts medium containing 2 g/L glucose, 1 g/L NH4Cl, 0.5 g/L NaCl, 8.5 g/L Na2HPO4·H2O, 0.5 g/L KH2PO4, 1 mL/L of 1 M MgSO4, 1 mL/L of 0.1 M CaCl2, 1 mL/L of 0.05 M FeCl3, and a trace element solution containing CoCl2·6H2O, CuCl2·2H2O, MnCl2·4H2O, Na2MoO4·2H2O, and ZnCl2 at specified concentrations. Cultivation was conducted in shake flasks at 37°C with orbital agitation at 300 rpm, with cultures monitored for optical density at 600 nm until mid-exponential phase. The knockout of the glcK gene was achieved by homologous recombination using a PCR-amplified disruption cassette derived from a ΔglcK strain of B. subtilis 168, followed by selection on antibiotic-containing agar and verification by PCR and sequencing. Fermentation of GlcNAc was performed in triplicate using the BSGNK strain and the parental BSGN strain under identical conditions, with samples collected at 2-hour intervals for quantification of glucose consumption, cell dry weight, and extracellular GlcNAc concentration via high-performance liquid chromatography. The results of fermentation demonstrated that the BSGNK strain achieved a GlcNAc titer of 147.5 mg per gram of glucose consumed, compared to 65.0 mg per gram for the parental strain, while simultaneously achieving a specific growth rate of 0.42 h⁻¹ versus 0.18 h⁻¹. The energy charge of the BSGNK cells increased from 0.68 to 0.81, confirming restoration of cellular energy homeostasis. Intracellular GlcNAc-6-phosphate levels were reduced from 33.71 mM to 0.06 mM, confirming the elimination of the futile cycle. Volumetric productivity increased from 3.9 mg/L/h to 9.2 mg/L/h, demonstrating that the genetic modification directly translates into industrially relevant performance gains.
