# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

- define novel vaccine consortium

The present invention relates to a novel bivalent vaccine consortium comprising isolated outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) derived from *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A, formulated in a 1:1 molar ratio for the prevention of enteric fever caused by either or both pathogens. This consortium represents a first-of-its-kind acellular vaccine platform that simultaneously presents a broad spectrum of conserved and strain-specific antigens—including lipopolysaccharide, outer membrane proteins, and the Vi polysaccharide capsule—without the need for exogenous adjuvants. The OMVs are naturally secreted during bacterial exponential growth and retain their native structural integrity, preserving the spatial organization of pathogen-associated molecular patterns critical for robust innate and adaptive immune activation. The consortium is designed for oral administration, enabling the induction of mucosal immunity at the primary site of infection while concurrently stimulating systemic humoral and T-cell-mediated responses. This formulation uniquely integrates the immunogenicity of whole-cell components with the safety profile of purified subunit vaccines, offering a scalable, stable, and cost-effective solution for global deployment in endemic regions.

## BACKGROUND AND PRIOR ART OF THE INVENTION

- motivate enteric fever problem
- summarize current vaccine limitations
- highlight importance of OMVs
- emphasize need for cost-effective solution

Enteric fever, a life-threatening systemic infection caused by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A, remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in low- and middle-income countries, with an estimated 21.7 million cases and over 200,000 deaths annually. The epidemiological landscape is shifting, with rising incidence of paratyphoid fever, particularly in South Asia, challenging the adequacy of existing vaccines that target only *S.* Typhi. Current licensed vaccines include the live-attenuated Ty21a oral vaccine and the parenteral Vi polysaccharide vaccine, both of which exhibit significant limitations. Ty21a provides only partial cross-protection against *S.* Paratyphi A and requires multiple doses with strict cold-chain maintenance, while the Vi polysaccharide vaccine fails to elicit T-cell-dependent immunity, resulting in short-lived protection and poor efficacy in children under two years of age. The recently developed Vi-tetanus toxoid conjugate vaccine improves immunogenicity but remains expensive, requires parenteral delivery, and does not address the growing threat posed by *S.* Paratyphi A. Moreover, none of the existing vaccines induce robust mucosal immunity, which is essential for blocking intestinal invasion—a critical step in disease pathogenesis. Outer membrane vesicles, naturally released by Gram-negative bacteria during growth, offer a compelling alternative as they carry a comprehensive array of surface and secreted antigens in their native conformation, including immunogenic lipopolysaccharides, porins, and virulence factors, all inherently adjuvanted by endogenous pathogen-associated molecular patterns. These vesicles have demonstrated safety and efficacy in licensed meningococcal vaccines but have never been harnessed in a bivalent format for typhoidal salmonellae. There exists an urgent, unmet need for a low-cost, orally administered, broadly protective vaccine that can be manufactured at scale using simple fermentation and purification processes, without reliance on complex conjugation chemistry or refrigerated distribution, to meet the demands of global public health programs.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

- outline primary objectives
- list additional objectives

The primary objective of this invention is to provide a bivalent vaccine consortium composed of isolated outer membrane vesicles from *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A that elicits durable, broad-spectrum protective immunity against both pathogens following a single oral immunization regimen. A secondary objective is to induce simultaneous mucosal and systemic immune responses, including secretory IgA, IgG, and IgM antibodies, as well as Th1 and Th17-polarized T-cell activation, to block intestinal colonization, inhibit systemic dissemination, and promote bacterial clearance. An additional objective is to achieve this protection without the use of exogenous adjuvants, thereby enhancing safety and reducing manufacturing complexity. Further objectives include the preservation of native antigenic structures, including Vi polysaccharide and lipopolysaccharide, within the vesicle membrane to ensure immunological recognition by pre-existing and de novo immune repertoires; to demonstrate cross-protection against heterologous strains through conserved outer membrane components; to enable stable storage at ambient temperatures for at least six months; and to facilitate scalable production using standard microbiological techniques compatible with low-resource settings. The invention further aims to reduce the burden of enteric fever by providing a single-dose, needle-free, cost-effective solution that can be integrated into existing childhood immunization programs.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- describe composition of formulation
- discuss immunogenicity and protective efficacy
- compare with mutated strains
- highlight advantages over conventional methods
- describe preparation of OMVs
- discuss significance of Vi-polysaccharide in vaccine constituents

The invention discloses a novel vaccine consortium comprising purified, non-replicating outer membrane vesicles derived from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi and serovar Paratyphi A, combined in a 1:1 ratio by protein mass. Each vesicle is approximately 130 to 300 nanometers in diameter and retains the native lipid bilayer structure of the bacterial outer membrane, encapsulating periplasmic proteins, lipopolysaccharide, outer membrane proteins, and the Vi polysaccharide capsule. The formulation contains no live bacteria, no chemical adjuvants, and no synthetic carriers, relying instead on the intrinsic immunostimulatory properties of vesicle-associated pathogen-associated molecular patterns. The composition includes a diverse array of antigens, including but not limited to OmpC, OmpF, OmpS1, OmpS2, flagellin variants, and the Vi capsular polysaccharide, all presented in their native conformation and spatial orientation as naturally secreted by the bacteria. This presentation enables recognition by both B-cell receptors and pattern recognition receptors such as TLR4 and TLR2, triggering robust dendritic cell maturation and subsequent T-helper cell polarization toward Th1 and Th17 phenotypes. Immunization with this consortium in murine models results in high-titer serum IgG, IgA, and IgM antibodies specific to both homologous and heterologous strains, with sustained titers detectable for at least 90 days post-immunization. The vaccine confers 80–90% survival following lethal intraperitoneal challenge with virulent strains of either serovar, significantly outperforming heat-killed whole-cell preparations and monovalent OMV formulations. Unlike attenuated strains, which may revert or fail to express key antigens, this formulation preserves the full antigenic profile of wild-type strains without genetic manipulation. The preparation process involves culturing both serovars in tryptic soy broth under controlled exponential growth conditions, followed by sequential filtration and ultracentrifugation to isolate vesicles free of intact bacteria and debris. The resulting vesicles are resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline and quantified by protein concentration, with each dose containing 25 μg of total OMV protein. The presence of Vi polysaccharide within the vesicle membrane is critical, as it provides a T-independent antigen that, when co-presented with immunogenic outer membrane proteins, becomes T-cell-dependent due to protein-mediated antigen presentation, thereby enabling long-term memory and class-switched antibody responses. This dual mechanism distinguishes the invention from conventional Vi vaccines and underpins its superior durability and breadth of protection.

### Bacterial Strains and Culture Conditions

- describe bacterial strains and culture conditions

The outer membrane vesicles are derived from clinical isolates of *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi strain C-6953 and serovar Paratyphi A strain C-6915, both obtained from the National Institute of Cholera and Enteric Diseases culture bank. Strains are maintained in brain heart infusion broth supplemented with 20% glycerol at −80°C and are revived by subculture in tryptic soy broth at 37°C under continuous shaking at 100 rpm until mid-exponential phase, as confirmed by optical density measurements at 600 nm. Growth conditions are standardized to ensure consistent vesicle yield and antigenic composition, with cultures harvested at an optical density of 0.8–1.0 to maximize vesiculation while minimizing cell lysis. All manipulations are performed under aseptic conditions using sterile media and equipment to prevent contamination. Strain identity is confirmed by PCR amplification of genus-specific genes such as *prtA*, serovar-specific flagellin genes *fliC-j* and *fliC-a*, and serological agglutination using O- and H-antisera according to the White-Kauffmann-Le Minor scheme. Antibiotic susceptibility profiles are verified to ensure the absence of resistance determinants that could compromise vaccine safety or regulatory approval.

### Preparation of OMVs

- describe preparation of OMVs
- discuss characterization of isolated OMVs

Outer membrane vesicles are prepared by centrifuging bacterial cultures at 8,000 rpm for 40 minutes at 4°C to remove intact cells, followed by filtration through 0.22 μm membrane filters to eliminate residual bacteria and large debris. The filtrate is then subjected to ultracentrifugation at 140,000 × g for four hours at 4°C using a Sorvall T-865 rotor to pellet the vesicles. The resulting pellet is washed twice in phosphate-buffered saline and resuspended in the same buffer to a final protein concentration of 400–440 μg/mL, as determined by the modified Lowry method. The concentration of O-antigen associated with the vesicles is quantified using the Dubois method, yielding approximately 250 μg/mL of polysaccharide content. Vesicle size and morphology are confirmed by transmission electron microscopy, revealing spherical structures ranging from 130 to 300 nm in diameter. Protein profiles are analyzed by SDS-PAGE, demonstrating a distinct banding pattern between 20 and 90 kDa, with prominent bands at 40–62 kDa for Typhi and 33–73 kDa for Paratyphi A, consistent with outer membrane proteins. The absence of cytoplasmic contaminants is confirmed by immunoblotting with anti-heat shock protein antibodies. The vesicles are stable for at least six months when stored at 4°C and retain immunogenicity after lyophilization.

### Negative Staining of OMVs and OMV-Secreting Bacteria

- describe negative staining of OMVs and OMV-secreting bacteria

To visualize the structural integrity of secreted vesicles and their mode of release, log-phase bacterial cultures and purified OMV suspensions are applied to carbon-coated copper grids and allowed to adsorb for one minute. Excess fluid is removed by blotting, and samples are stained with 2% aqueous uranyl acetate for 30 seconds. Grids are air-dried and examined under a Tecnai 12 BioTwin transmission electron microscope operating at 80 kV. Images reveal spherical vesicles budding directly from the bacterial outer membrane, with no evidence of cell lysis or membrane rupture. Vesicles are uniformly distributed and exhibit smooth, intact surfaces, confirming their natural biogenesis. The absence of intracellular debris in purified preparations confirms the efficacy of the isolation protocol in removing non-vesicular contaminants.

### Animals

- describe animal model used for immunization

Seven-week-old female BALB/c mice are obtained from the animal resource division of the National Institute of Cholera and Enteric Diseases and housed under specific pathogen-free conditions at 25°C and 75% humidity, with access to autoclaved food and water. All procedures are conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA), and the experimental protocol is approved under project number PRO/117/June, 2015–June, 2018. Mice are fasted for three to four hours prior to oral immunization to enhance intestinal uptake. Each immunization consists of 200 μL of PBS containing 25 μg of total OMV protein, administered via gavage on days 0, 14, and 28. Control groups receive PBS alone. Mice are monitored daily for signs of distress, and humane endpoints are strictly enforced.

### Collection of Serum and Stool

- describe collection of serum and stool
- discuss processing of collected samples

Blood is collected from the lateral tail vein at predetermined time points—days 0, 14, 21, 28, 35, 78, and 90—into Microtainer tubes and centrifuged at 1,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4°C to separate serum, which is aliquoted and stored at −80°C. Stool samples are collected aseptically from individual mice, homogenized in PBS containing protease inhibitors, and centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 10 minutes to remove particulate matter. The supernatant is filtered through 0.22 μm membranes and stored at −20°C for subsequent analysis of secretory IgA and IgM. All samples are processed in parallel to minimize inter-assay variability, and aliquots are used exclusively for ELISA, immunoblot, and flow cytometry analyses.

### SDS-PAGE and Immunoblot

- describe SDS-PAGE and immunoblot analysis

Protein samples from OMVs, outer membrane proteins, whole-cell lysates, and lipopolysaccharide preparations are mixed with SDS-PAGE loading buffer and heated at 60°C for two hours to denature proteins and dissociate lipopolysaccharide. Samples are loaded onto 12% polyacrylamide gels and electrophoresed at 100 V until bromophenol blue migrates to the gel’s edge. Gels are stained with Coomassie Brilliant Blue or silver nitrate to visualize protein bands. For immunoblotting, proteins are transferred to nitrocellulose membranes using a semi-dry blotting apparatus. Membranes are blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin in Tris-buffered saline with Tween-20, incubated overnight with anti-OMV mouse sera, and probed with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated anti-mouse IgG. Detection is performed using chemiluminescent substrates, and bands are visualized and quantified using digital imaging systems. This confirms the presence of multiple immunogenic proteins between 20 and 90 kDa in the OMV preparation.

### Dot Blot Assay.

- describe dot blot analysis

Purified lipopolysaccharide from *S.* Typhi and *S.* Paratyphi A is serially diluted and spotted directly onto nitrocellulose membranes. After blocking, membranes are incubated with anti-OMV sera from immunized mice, followed by horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies. Chemiluminescent detection reveals strong reactivity to both native and OMV-associated LPS, confirming that the immune response recognizes conserved epitopes across structural variants. This assay demonstrates that the vaccine induces antibodies capable of binding to both vesicle-associated and free LPS, supporting its broad antigenic coverage.

### ELISA

- describe ELISA method
- discuss measurement of different immunoglobulins
- describe coating of micro-titer wells
- discuss incubation and washing steps
- describe measurement of OD492

Microtiter plates are coated with 5 μg/mL of purified OMVs from either serovar and incubated overnight at 4°C. Wells are washed with PBS-Tween 20 and blocked with 1% bovine serum albumin for one hour at 37°C. Serial dilutions of serum or stool supernatants are added in duplicate and incubated for two hours at 37°C. After washing, HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG, IgA, IgM, IgG1, IgG2a, or IgG3 is added and incubated for one hour. Plates are washed again, and the substrate o-phenylenediamine dihydrochloride is added for 10 minutes before the reaction is stopped with 2 N sulfuric acid. Optical density is measured at 492 nm using a microplate reader. Titers are calculated as the reciprocal of the highest dilution yielding an OD value twice that of the negative control. All assays are performed in triplicate across three independent experiments.

Bone marrow-derived dendritic cells are generated from naïve BALB/c mice by culturing progenitors in RPMI-1640 supplemented with granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor for seven days. Mature dendritic cells are stimulated with 10 μg/mL of bivalent OMVs for 24 hours, after which supernatants are analyzed for IL-12p70, IL-6, and IL-23 by ELISA. Splenocytes from immunized mice are re-stimulated ex vivo with the same OMV preparation, and levels of IFN-γ, IL-17, and IL-6 are quantified. Co-cultures of dendritic cells and CD4+ T cells demonstrate enhanced Th1 and Th17 polarization. These results confirm that the bivalent OMV formulation activates both innate and adaptive immune pathways. In vivo, immunized mice exhibit no significant weight loss following challenge with heterologous strains, whereas unimmunized controls lose more than 20% of body weight and succumb within five days. Survival rates reach 80–90% in immunized groups, with bacterial loads in spleen and liver reduced by three to four logs compared to controls. Colonization of the small intestine is markedly diminished in immunized animals. Statistical significance is determined using non-parametric tests, with p-values below 0.01 considered significant, confirming the reproducibility and robustness of the protective effect.

## The Non-Limiting Advantages are Given Below:

- list advantages of consortium
- demonstrate immunogenicity
- show protection against salmonellae

The bivalent OMV consortium offers multiple non-limiting advantages over existing typhoidal vaccines. It induces simultaneous mucosal and systemic immunity, enabling blockade of intestinal invasion and systemic clearance. It elicits long-lasting antibody responses, including IgG subclasses and secretory IgA, persisting for at least 90 days. It provides cross-protection against heterologous strains due to conserved outer membrane antigens. It requires no exogenous adjuvants, reducing cost and improving safety. It is stable at ambient temperature for six months, eliminating cold-chain dependency. It can be manufactured using scalable, low-cost fermentation and purification techniques compatible with global health infrastructure. It avoids genetic modification, preserving the natural antigenic repertoire of wild-type strains. It elicits Th1 and Th17 responses critical for intracellular pathogen clearance. It enhances opsonization and inhibits bacterial motility and mucin penetration, directly interfering with virulence mechanisms. It demonstrates superior immunogenicity compared to heat-killed whole-cell preparations. It is effective in adult and juvenile murine models, suggesting potential for use across age groups. It represents the first orally administered, broadly protective vaccine against both *S.* Typhi and *S.* Paratyphi A, fulfilling a critical unmet global health need.
