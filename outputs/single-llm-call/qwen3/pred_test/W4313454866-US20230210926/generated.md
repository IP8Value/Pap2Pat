## BACKGROUND

### Field

- define field of in vivo ultrasound imaging

The field of this invention pertains to non-invasive in vivo imaging of biological processes at the cellular and molecular level using ultrasound, particularly through the use of genetically encoded acoustic reporter genes that produce gas vesicles capable of generating nonlinear ultrasound contrast. This technology enables the visualization of cellular location, gene expression dynamics, and functional states within intact living organisms without the need for ionizing radiation, exogenous contrast agents, or tissue disruption. The invention specifically advances the capability to image bacterial colonization patterns, tumor cell heterogeneity, and therapeutic cell populations deep within tissues with high spatial and temporal resolution, overcoming longstanding limitations of optical and nuclear imaging modalities.

### Description of the Related Art

- motivate need for in vivo imaging

The ability to observe cellular behavior and gene expression in real time within living organisms is fundamental to understanding disease progression, evaluating therapeutic interventions, and guiding clinical procedures. Traditional imaging techniques such as fluorescence microscopy are limited by tissue scattering and absorption, restricting their utility to superficial structures or ex vivo samples. Nuclear imaging methods, while capable of deep tissue penetration, involve ionizing radiation, low spatial resolution, and require radioactive tracers with short half-lives. Magnetic resonance imaging offers high resolution but lacks the sensitivity to detect small populations of genetically labeled cells and requires expensive infrastructure. There remains a critical unmet need for a safe, cost-effective, and highly sensitive imaging modality that can report on molecular and cellular activity dynamically and non-destructively in deep tissues.

- describe limitations of optical techniques

Optical imaging techniques, including fluorescence and bioluminescence, are widely used for cellular reporting but suffer from severe attenuation and scattering in biological tissues beyond a few millimeters in depth. Even with advanced techniques such as two-photon microscopy or light-sheet imaging, penetration is limited to superficial layers of organs or thin tissue sections. Fluorescent proteins require excitation light that is absorbed or scattered by hemoglobin, melanin, and other endogenous chromophores, leading to signal loss and background noise. Furthermore, optical methods cannot be readily translated to human clinical applications due to safety concerns regarding light exposure and the inability to image through bone or dense tissue. These constraints severely limit the utility of optical reporters for longitudinal studies of deep-seated pathologies such as solid tumors, inflammatory bowel disease, or brain disorders.

- introduce ultrasound as alternative

Ultrasound imaging presents a compelling alternative due to its deep tissue penetration, real-time imaging capability, lack of ionizing radiation, and widespread clinical availability. Conventional ultrasound relies on the reflection of sound waves at tissue interfaces with differing acoustic impedances, but it lacks intrinsic molecular specificity. While microbubble contrast agents have been developed to enhance ultrasound signal, they are transient, non-genetically encoded, and cannot be targeted to specific cell types or regulated by gene expression. The emergence of genetically encoded acoustic reporter genes that produce gas vesicles has introduced the possibility of creating cells that are inherently visible to ultrasound, enabling long-term, non-destructive tracking of genetically defined populations.

- summarize first-generation ARGs

First-generation acoustic reporter genes (ARGs) were derived from the gas vesicle gene clusters of Bacillus megaterium and Anabaena flos-aquae. These constructs enabled the production of gas vesicles in heterologous hosts, including mammalian and bacterial cells, and could be detected using destructive ultrasound pulse sequences that relied on the irreversible collapse of the vesicles to generate contrast. However, these early systems suffered from poor expression levels at physiological temperatures, high metabolic burden on host cells, and the absence of nonlinear acoustic signatures, which made it difficult to distinguish their signal from background tissue scattering. As a result, their utility was restricted to clonally selected cell lines treated with epigenetic modifiers, and they could not be used for dynamic, repeated imaging over multiple days without destroying the reporter signal.

- highlight limitations of first-generation ARGs

The first-generation ARGs were unable to provide non-linear ultrasound contrast, necessitating destructive imaging modalities such as BURST that collapsed the gas vesicles upon each imaging session, thereby precluding longitudinal monitoring of the same cell population. Their expression in mammalian cells was weak and inconsistent, requiring prolonged selection and pharmacological intervention to achieve detectable signals. In bacterial systems, the gene clusters were too large for efficient delivery, and their expression kinetics were slow, limiting their responsiveness to environmental cues. Furthermore, the inclusion of the gvpC gene, which stabilizes the gas vesicle shell, suppressed nonlinear scattering, rendering the vesicles acoustically inert under typical imaging conditions. These limitations collectively restricted the application of first-generation ARGs to proof-of-concept experiments and prevented their adoption for real-time, in vivo monitoring of therapeutic cells or disease processes.

## SUMMARY

- introduce compositions

The invention provides novel nucleic acid compositions encoding gas vesicle structural and assembly proteins that, when expressed in target cells, produce gas vesicles capable of generating strong, non-linear ultrasound contrast without requiring destructive imaging protocols. These compositions enable the non-invasive, longitudinal, and high-resolution imaging of genetically defined cell populations in vivo, including tumor cells, probiotic bacteria, stem cells, and engineered therapeutic cells. The compositions are designed for robust expression in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic hosts, with optimized gene architectures that enhance acoustic signal intensity, reduce metabolic burden, and enable inducible, tissue-specific, or context-dependent expression.

- nucleic acid compositions

The invention includes isolated nucleic acid compositions comprising polynucleotide sequences encoding gas vesicle structural proteins and assembly factors derived from Serratia sp. 39006 and Anabaena flos-aquae. These compositions are engineered to produce gas vesicles with enhanced nonlinear acoustic scattering properties, particularly through the omission or modification of the gvpC gene, which is known to rigidify the gas vesicle shell and suppress nonlinear response. The nucleic acid sequences are codon-optimized for expression in mammalian or bacterial hosts and are operably linked to regulatory elements that permit controlled expression in response to specific physiological or exogenous stimuli.

- delivery compositions

The invention further provides delivery compositions comprising the nucleic acid compositions encapsulated within lipid-based carriers, viral vectors, or transposable elements for efficient transfer into target cells. These delivery compositions are formulated to maintain stability during storage and administration, and are compatible with parenteral, oral, or localized routes of delivery. The delivery vehicles are designed to facilitate cellular uptake, nuclear entry, and stable genomic integration or episomal maintenance, depending on the intended application and host cell type.

- mammalian cells

The invention encompasses mammalian cells that have been genetically modified to stably express the gas vesicle-encoding nucleic acid compositions. These cells include human and murine cancer cell lines, primary tumor cells, induced pluripotent stem cells, mesenchymal stem cells, T cells, and other therapeutic cell types. The engineered cells produce gas vesicles intracellularly that generate detectable nonlinear ultrasound signals under physiological conditions, enabling their non-destructive visualization in vivo without the need for exogenous contrast agents or tissue disruption.

- probiotic bacterial cells

The invention further includes probiotic bacterial cells, including Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 and attenuated Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium, that have been genetically modified to express the gas vesicle-encoding nucleic acid compositions. These bacterial strains retain their natural ability to colonize tumor microenvironments and other immune-privileged sites, and their engineered acoustic properties allow for real-time, non-destructive imaging of their spatial distribution, proliferation, and gene expression dynamics within living hosts.

- gas vesicle polynucleotides

The invention provides isolated gas vesicle polynucleotides encoding the gvpA gene and associated assembly factors from Serratia sp. 39006 and Anabaena flos-aquae, wherein the polynucleotides are configured to produce gas vesicles with a collapse pressure profile that generates strong nonlinear ultrasound contrast. These polynucleotides are devoid of the gvpC gene or contain variants thereof that reduce mechanical reinforcement of the vesicle shell, thereby enhancing deformability under acoustic pressure and increasing the amplitude of nonlinear scattering signals.

- gas vesicle assembly genes

The invention includes gas vesicle assembly genes encoding proteins such as gvpN, gvpJ, gvpK, gvpF, gvpG, gvpW, and gvpV, which are essential for the biogenesis and structural integrity of gas vesicles. These genes are arranged in polycistronic operons with optimized intergenic spacing and ribosome binding sites to ensure coordinated expression and stoichiometric assembly of the gas vesicle complex. The assembly genes are codon-optimized for expression in mammalian or bacterial hosts and are linked to transcript stabilization elements to enhance mRNA half-life and protein yield.

- gas vesicle structural genes

The invention provides gas vesicle structural genes, particularly gvpA, which encodes the primary structural protein of the gas vesicle shell. The gvpA gene is engineered to contain codon-optimized sequences for enhanced translation efficiency in heterologous hosts and is fused to 5’ and 3’ untranslated regions that promote mRNA stability and translational initiation. The structural gene is expressed independently or in tandem with assembly factors to achieve optimal gas vesicle formation and acoustic performance.

- promoters operably connected

The invention includes promoters operably connected to the gas vesicle polynucleotides, wherein the promoters are selected from inducible, constitutive, tissue-specific, lineage-specific, or context-dependent promoters. These promoters are engineered to drive expression of the gas vesicle genes in response to specific biological signals, such as hypoxia, inflammation, metabolic stress, or the presence of exogenous inducers such as L-arabinose or doxycycline. The promoter elements are designed to minimize basal expression while enabling robust, dynamic induction upon encountering the desired cellular context.

- expression in mammalian cells

The invention provides methods for achieving high-level expression of gas vesicle genes in mammalian cells through the use of synthetic promoters, transcript stabilization elements, and codon optimization. The expression system is designed to overcome epigenetic silencing, reduce metabolic burden, and enable long-term, stable expression in both transiently transfected and stably integrated cell lines. The resulting gas vesicles are detectable by nonlinear ultrasound imaging with high signal-to-noise ratio, enabling visualization of gene expression patterns in real time within living animals.

- expression in probiotic bacterial cells

The invention provides methods for achieving inducible and stable expression of gas vesicle genes in probiotic bacterial cells through the use of tightly regulated promoters, toxin-antitoxin stability cassettes, and plasmid maintenance systems. The expression system is optimized for performance at 37°C in the presence of host microbiota and under nutrient-limited conditions encountered in the tumor microenvironment or gastrointestinal tract. The resulting bacterial cells produce gas vesicles that generate strong nonlinear ultrasound signals without compromising viability, motility, or colonization capacity.

- nonlinear ultrasound contrast

The invention provides gas vesicles that generate nonlinear ultrasound contrast through their mechanical response to acoustic pressure waves. Unlike linear scatterers, these gas vesicles exhibit a nonlinear pressure-volume relationship, producing harmonic and subharmonic frequencies that can be selectively detected using amplitude modulation pulse sequences. This nonlinear signature enables the differentiation of gas vesicle-containing cells from surrounding tissues with high specificity, even in the presence of high background noise.

- tumor-homing bacteria

The invention includes probiotic bacterial strains that naturally home to tumor sites due to their ability to colonize necrotic, hypoxic, and immune-privileged regions. These strains are genetically engineered to express gas vesicles under the control of inducible promoters, enabling non-destructive, real-time imaging of their spatial distribution, proliferation, and gene expression dynamics within tumors. The ability to visualize these bacteria in vivo provides a direct readout of therapeutic delivery and microenvironmental engagement.

- specific bacterial strains

The invention encompasses specific bacterial strains including Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 and attenuated Salmonella enterica serovar Typhimurium, which have been genetically modified to express gas vesicle gene clusters derived from Serratia sp. 39006. These strains retain their native tumor-colonizing properties and exhibit robust, inducible expression of gas vesicles that generate strong nonlinear ultrasound contrast without compromising viability or colonization efficiency.

- mammalian cell types

The invention encompasses a variety of mammalian cell types including human breast cancer cells (MDA-MB-231), hepatocytes (HuH7), fibroblasts (3T3), embryonic stem cells, induced pluripotent stem cells, CAR-T cells, and dendritic cells. These cells are genetically modified to stably express gas vesicle gene clusters derived from Anabaena flos-aquae, enabling their non-destructive imaging in vivo through nonlinear ultrasound techniques.

These cells can be tracked non-invasively during migration, differentiation, and engraftment in vivo, providing a powerful tool for regenerative medicine and developmental biology studies.

These cells can be imaged in real time to monitor their biodistribution, persistence, and functional activity following administration, enabling precision dosing and early detection of therapeutic failure or adverse events.

- disease treatment

The invention provides methods for treating diseases by administering genetically engineered cells that produce gas vesicles, thereby enabling non-destructive imaging of therapeutic cell dynamics. This approach facilitates real-time monitoring of cell-based therapies for cancer, autoimmune disorders, metabolic diseases, and infectious conditions, allowing for adaptive treatment strategies based on in vivo imaging feedback.

- metabolic diseases

The invention provides methods for imaging and monitoring metabolic diseases such as diabetes, obesity, and inborn errors of metabolism by engineering cells that express gas vesicles in response to metabolic cues such as glucose levels, insulin signaling, or mitochondrial stress. These cells can be implanted or administered to report on tissue-specific metabolic activity in real time.

- dynamic non-destructive imaging

The invention provides methods for dynamic, non-destructive imaging of gene expression and cellular behavior over multiple time points using nonlinear ultrasound contrast generated by gas vesicles. Unlike previous methods that required destruction of the reporter, the invention enables repeated imaging of the same cell population without loss of signal, permitting longitudinal studies of disease progression, therapeutic response, and cellular adaptation.

- nonlinear ultrasound imaging

The invention provides methods for nonlinear ultrasound imaging that utilize amplitude modulation pulse sequences to selectively detect the nonlinear scattering signals generated by gas vesicles. These methods enable high-contrast, high-sensitivity visualization of genetically labeled cells in deep tissues with minimal background interference, allowing for single-cell resolution in vivo.

- acoustic pressure

The invention provides methods for applying acoustic pressure to induce nonlinear scattering from gas vesicles, wherein the pressure is tuned to a range between 0.3 MPa and 1.74 MPa to maximize signal intensity while minimizing vesicle collapse. The acoustic pressure profile is optimized to match the collapse pressure threshold of the engineered gas vesicles, ensuring selective detection and signal stability during imaging.

- cell concentration

The invention provides methods for detecting cell concentrations as low as 10^5 cells per milliliter in vitro and as low as 10^3 cells per gram of tissue in vivo using nonlinear ultrasound imaging. The signal intensity is directly proportional to cell concentration over a broad dynamic range, enabling quantitative assessment of cell burden and distribution.

- induction of expression

The invention provides methods for inducing expression of gas vesicle genes through the administration of small molecule inducers such as L-arabinose or doxycycline, which activate promoter elements operably linked to the gas vesicle gene clusters. The timing and dosage of inducer administration are calibrated to achieve precise temporal control over gas vesicle production and acoustic signal generation.

- nonlinear ultrasound signals

The invention provides gas vesicles that generate nonlinear ultrasound signals characterized by the production of subharmonic, harmonic, and combination frequencies in response to acoustic excitation. These signals are distinct from linear tissue scattering and can be isolated using signal separation algorithms, enabling high-specificity detection of genetically labeled cells.

- first generation acoustic reporter genes

The invention overcomes the limitations of first-generation acoustic reporter genes by providing second-generation gas vesicle gene clusters that produce stronger nonlinear contrast, exhibit improved expression kinetics, and are compatible with long-term in vivo imaging without the need for destructive pulse sequences or pharmacological epigenetic modulation.

- enhancement in nonlinear ultrasound contrast

The invention provides an enhancement in nonlinear ultrasound contrast of up to 38-fold in mammalian cells and 9-fold in bacterial cells compared to first-generation systems. This enhancement is achieved through the omission of gvpC, optimization of gvpA stoichiometry, and incorporation of transcript stabilization elements that increase protein yield and vesicle density.

- contrast to noise ratio

The invention provides a contrast-to-noise ratio exceeding 20 dB in vivo for gas vesicle-expressing cells, enabling clear delineation of labeled cells from surrounding tissue even at depths exceeding 1 cm. This level of contrast is sufficient for clinical translation and enables detection of small cell populations within heterogeneous tissues.

- growth rate

The invention provides genetically engineered cells that maintain growth rates comparable to unmodified parental lines, demonstrating that expression of the gas vesicle gene clusters imposes minimal metabolic burden. This is achieved through the deletion of non-essential genes from the native operon and the use of compact, codon-optimized expression cassettes.

The invention provides methods for achieving robust gas vesicle expression in both bacterial and mammalian cells under physiological conditions, with detectable expression occurring within 3 to 24 hours of induction and persisting for up to 7 days without signal decay. ## Acoustic Reporter Genes for Nondestructive In Vivo Imaging

- introduce noninvasive in vivo imaging challenge

The challenge of noninvasive in vivo imaging lies in the ability to detect molecular and cellular events deep within living tissues with high spatial and temporal resolution, without the use of ionizing radiation or exogenous contrast agents. The invention addresses this challenge by providing genetically encoded gas vesicles that generate nonlinear ultrasound contrast, enabling the visualization of genetically defined cell populations with unprecedented sensitivity and specificity.

- motivate ultrasound imaging

Ultrasound imaging is motivated by its deep tissue penetration, real-time imaging capability, lack of ionizing radiation, and widespread clinical availability. The invention leverages these advantages by engineering cells to produce their own contrast agent, thereby eliminating the need for repeated administration of exogenous agents.

- describe first-generation acoustic reporter genes

First-generation acoustic reporter genes were derived from the gas vesicle gene clusters of Bacillus megaterium and Anabaena flos-aquae. These constructs enabled the production of gas vesicles in heterologous hosts but suffered from weak expression, high metabolic burden, and the absence of nonlinear contrast. Their use required destructive imaging protocols and pharmacological epigenetic modulation, severely limiting their utility.

- limitations of first-generation mARGs

The limitations of first-generation mammalian acoustic reporter genes include low expression levels at 37°C, poor signal-to-noise ratio, and the requirement for clonal selection and histone deacetylase inhibitors to achieve detectable signals. These systems could not be used for longitudinal imaging and were incompatible with clinical translation.

- introduce second-generation mARGs

The invention introduces second-generation mammalian acoustic reporter genes derived from Anabaena flos-aquae, which produce gas vesicles with enhanced nonlinear ultrasound contrast due to the omission of gvpC and optimization of gvpA stoichiometry. These constructs enable robust, long-term expression in mammalian cells without the need for epigenetic modulation.

- describe improved acoustic properties

The improved acoustic properties of the second-generation mARGs include a 38-fold increase in nonlinear contrast compared to first-generation systems, a collapse pressure threshold between 0.31 MPa and 0.42 MPa, and a high contrast-to-noise ratio exceeding 20 dB. These properties enable detection of single cells in deep tissues.

- describe improved expression characteristics

The improved expression characteristics include rapid induction within 24 hours, stable expression over multiple cell divisions, and compatibility with inducible promoters such as TRE. The constructs are expressed at levels sufficient for detection without compromising cell viability or proliferation.

- demonstrate in situ gene expression imaging

- define gas vesicles

Gas vesicles are defined as intracellular, gas-filled, proteinaceous structures composed primarily of GvpA and accessory proteins, which generate nonlinear ultrasound contrast when exposed to acoustic pressure.

The gas vesicles of the invention are predominantly cylindrical in shape, with some conical or ellipsoidal variants. The shape is determined by the stoichiometry and assembly of GvpA and accessory proteins.

The gvpA protein is the primary structural component of the gas vesicle shell, forming a rigid, hydrophobic lattice that encloses a gas-filled cavity. The invention provides codon-optimized gvpA sequences with enhanced expression and stability.

The gvpC protein is a minor structural protein that binds to the gas vesicle shell and reinforces its mechanical integrity. The invention omits gvpC or uses variants with reduced binding affinity to enhance nonlinear scattering.

The complete collapse pressure is the pressure at which all gas vesicles are irreversibly destroyed. The invention provides GV variants with complete collapse pressures up to 3.0 MPa, enabling controlled signal ablation.

## Materials and Methods

### Genomic Mining of ARG Clusters

- conduct literature search

A literature search was conducted using the terms “gas vesicle,” “gas vacuole,” “aerotope,” and “aerotype” to identify species reported to produce gas vesicles.

- identify GV gene clusters

GV gene clusters were identified in publicly available genome sequences using homology searches against known gvp genes.

- select well-behaved GV clusters

Well-behaved GV clusters were selected based on the presence of all annotated gvp genes and the absence of non-gvp genes that could increase metabolic burden.

- construct phylogenetic tree

A phylogenetic tree was constructed using 16S rRNA sequences from 270 GV-producing species, aligned using Clustal Omega and rendered using Evolview.
