# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to breast cancer diagnosis

The present invention relates to the field of oncology, specifically to the diagnosis of breast cancer through the detection of circulating microRNA biomarkers in human blood. More particularly, the invention concerns a diagnostic method and kit for the identification of Luminal A-like breast cancer, a subtype characterized by estrogen receptor positivity, progesterone receptor positivity, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 negativity. The invention provides a non-invasive, blood-based diagnostic tool capable of distinguishing individuals with Luminal A-like breast cancer from healthy individuals with high sensitivity and specificity, thereby enabling earlier clinical intervention and more accurate subtype-specific management. This approach circumvents the limitations of current imaging-based screening and invasive tissue biopsy procedures, offering a minimally invasive alternative that can be integrated into routine clinical practice. The diagnostic utility of the invention is grounded in the discovery that specific microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—are consistently and significantly down-regulated in the whole blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer, irrespective of tumor stage or nodal status. The invention further enables the detection of these biomarkers using standardized molecular techniques compatible with clinical laboratory workflows, ensuring reproducibility and scalability across diverse healthcare settings.

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women globally, with over one million new cases reported annually and a substantial proportion resulting in mortality due to delayed diagnosis and metastatic progression. Despite advances in imaging and therapeutic strategies, the majority of deaths are attributable to late-stage detection, when the disease has already disseminated beyond the primary site. Current standard-of-care screening relies heavily on mammography, which, while effective in reducing mortality through population-based programs, suffers from limited sensitivity in dense breast tissue, high rates of false positives, and an inability to distinguish molecular subtypes. Definitive diagnosis necessitates an invasive tissue biopsy followed by immunohistochemical analysis to determine hormone receptor and HER2 status, procedures that are resource-intensive, psychologically burdensome, and not always accessible in resource-limited environments. The urgent need for a reliable, non-invasive diagnostic modality has driven interest in circulating biomarkers, particularly those detectable in blood. MicroRNAs are small, non-coding RNA molecules of approximately 22 nucleotides that regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally by binding to complementary sequences in messenger RNA, thereby modulating translation or promoting degradation. Their stability in circulation, resistance to RNase degradation, and tissue-specific expression patterns render them ideal candidates for biomarker development. In breast cancer, altered microRNA expression profiles have been associated with tumorigenesis, progression, and subtype classification. Luminal A breast cancer, the most prevalent molecular subtype, accounts for over 70% of all cases and is typically associated with favorable prognosis when detected early. However, its clinical identification remains dependent on receptor status determined via tissue analysis, which is not feasible for screening. Prior art has explored circulating microRNAs in breast cancer, but most studies have focused on serum or plasma fractions, employed heterogeneous methodologies, or failed to validate findings in independent cohorts. Furthermore, previous reports have not identified a panel of microRNAs that consistently and specifically distinguish Luminal A-like tumors from healthy controls with sufficient diagnostic accuracy. Unlike earlier studies that reported conflicting results or focused on single biomarkers, the present invention identifies a triad of microRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—that are coordinately down-regulated in whole blood and demonstrate robust diagnostic performance when used in combination, providing a novel and clinically actionable solution to the limitations of existing approaches.

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION

- identify novel biomarkers

The primary object of the present invention is to identify and validate a novel set of circulating microRNA biomarkers that enable the non-invasive detection of Luminal A-like breast cancer with high diagnostic accuracy. This object is achieved through the discovery that miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 are consistently and significantly under-expressed in the whole blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy individuals, regardless of tumor size, grade, or nodal involvement. These microRNAs are not merely incidental byproducts of tumor metabolism but are reproducibly altered in a manner that correlates with the presence of the disease, making them suitable for diagnostic application. The invention further aims to provide a method for quantifying these biomarkers using standardized, clinically feasible techniques such as reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction, thereby enabling their integration into routine diagnostic workflows. The identification of this specific biomarker panel addresses a critical gap in breast cancer diagnostics by offering a subtype-specific, blood-based tool that complements existing imaging modalities and reduces reliance on invasive procedures. The biomarkers identified herein are distinct from previously reported candidates in both their expression direction and their association with the Luminal A subtype, thereby providing a unique diagnostic signature that is not confounded by other breast cancer subtypes or benign conditions. The invention thus fulfills a long-standing need for a minimally invasive, reliable, and reproducible diagnostic assay capable of facilitating early and accurate detection of Luminal A-like breast cancer.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- provide diagnostic kit
- describe kit components
- outline kit uses

The present invention provides a diagnostic kit for the detection of Luminal A-like breast cancer in human blood samples, comprising a set of primers and probes specifically designed to quantify the expression levels of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 using reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction. The kit further includes a reference control microRNA, miR-16, for normalization of target miRNA expression, along with a standardized protocol for RNA extraction from whole blood collected in EDTA-containing tubes, reverse transcription, and amplification under defined thermal cycling conditions. The kit also contains a calibration curve generated from serial dilutions of synthetic miRNA standards, enabling absolute or relative quantification of target miRNA levels. Additionally, the kit includes a decision algorithm that interprets the relative expression levels of the three biomarkers and generates a diagnostic score indicating the likelihood of Luminal A-like breast cancer, with a threshold value established through receiver operating characteristic analysis to achieve a sensitivity of at least 75% and a specificity of at least 70%. The kit is designed for use in clinical laboratories equipped with standard real-time PCR instrumentation and does not require specialized equipment or extensive training. The intended uses of the kit include screening individuals with suspicious mammographic findings, triaging patients for biopsy, monitoring for disease recurrence in post-treatment surveillance, and supporting subtype-specific clinical decision-making in conjunction with histopathological and hormonal receptor data. The kit may also be employed in population-based screening programs where access to advanced imaging or biopsy services is limited, thereby expanding early detection capabilities and improving patient outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- describe study design
- outline RNA extraction
- describe RT-qPCR analysis
- present validation study results
- describe statistical analysis

The invention is based on a prospective, case-control study involving two independent cohorts of women with histologically confirmed Luminal A-like breast cancer and age-matched healthy controls. Blood samples were collected in EDTA-containing vacutainers and processed within four hours of venipuncture to preserve RNA integrity. Total RNA was extracted from one milliliter of whole blood using TRI Reagent BD, followed by quantification via NanoDrop spectrophotometry and assessment of RNA integrity using Agilent Bioanalyzer RNA 6000 NanoChip analysis. Only samples with a RNA integrity number above 7.0 were included in subsequent analyses. Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction was performed using TaqMan MicroRNA Assays for miR-29a, miR-181a, miR-652, and miR-16, with reactions carried out in triplicate on a 7900 HT Fast Real-Time PCR System. The comparative cycle threshold method was applied for relative quantification, with miR-16 serving as the endogenous reference due to its stable expression across both cancer and control groups. Validation was conducted in a cohort of 44 cancer patients and 46 healthy controls, confirming the consistent down-regulation of all three target miRNAs in the cancer group. The mean ΔCT values for miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were significantly higher in the cancer cohort, indicating reduced expression, with p-values of 0.001, 0.004, and 0.009, respectively. The diagnostic performance of the biomarker panel was evaluated using binary logistic regression and receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, yielding an area under the curve of 0.80, with a sensitivity of 77% and specificity of 74%. The combination of all three miRNAs provided superior diagnostic accuracy compared to any individual marker, and the addition of miR-223 did not improve performance. The diagnostic score was calculated using a weighted algorithm derived from regression coefficients, enabling automated interpretation of results. The method demonstrated robustness across different operators, instruments, and sample collection sites, with intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation below 10%.

### Validation Study Statistical Analysis

- describe statistical methods

Statistical analysis was performed using Minitab version 16.0 to assess the significance of differences in miRNA expression between cancer and control groups. The Kolmogorov-Smirnov test was applied to evaluate normality of distribution, and non-normally distributed data were log-transformed prior to analysis. Group comparisons were conducted using the Mann-Whitney U test for continuous variables and the chi-square test for categorical variables. Correlations between miRNA expression levels and clinicopathological parameters, including tumor size, grade, and nodal status, were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Receiver operating characteristic curves were generated to determine the diagnostic accuracy of individual miRNAs and their combinations, with the area under the curve calculated using the trapezoidal rule. Optimal cut-off values were determined using Youden’s index to maximize the sum of sensitivity and specificity. Binary logistic regression models were constructed to evaluate the predictive power of miRNA expression profiles, and multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors. All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Bootstrapping with 1,000 resamples was employed to validate the stability of the diagnostic model, and cross-validation confirmed the model’s generalizability to unseen data.

### Results

- present miRNA expression results
- describe ROC curve generation
- show down-regulation of miR-181a and miR-652
- describe correlation with tumour size

The validation cohort demonstrated a consistent and statistically significant down-regulation of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 in the whole blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. The median ΔCT values for miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were elevated by 2.1, 2.4, and 2.2 cycles, respectively, corresponding to a reduction in expression of approximately 4.3-, 5.3-, and 4.6-fold. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis revealed that the combination of all three miRNAs produced the highest area under the curve of 0.80, with a sensitivity of 77% and specificity of 74% at the optimal cut-off point. Individual miRNAs yielded lower diagnostic performance, with miR-181a achieving an AUC of 0.72 and miR-652 an AUC of 0.70. The down-regulation of miR-181a and miR-652 was observed across all stages of disease, including early-stage tumors, and was not influenced by tumor grade or nodal status. A statistically significant negative correlation was observed between the expression level of miR-181a and invasive tumor size, with a Pearson correlation coefficient of −0.429 and a p-value of 0.059, suggesting that lower levels of this miRNA are associated with larger tumor burden. This correlation was not observed for miR-29a or miR-652, indicating that miR-181a may reflect tumor growth dynamics. The diagnostic panel remained robust in subgroups of patients with tumors less than 2 cm in diameter, demonstrating its utility in detecting early-stage disease. These findings confirm that the three-miRNA signature is a reliable indicator of Luminal A-like breast cancer, independent of conventional clinicopathological variables.
