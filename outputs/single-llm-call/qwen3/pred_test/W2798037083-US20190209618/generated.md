# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

- define field of hematopoietic stem cells

The present invention pertains to the field of hematopoietic stem cells, specifically to methods and compositions for enhancing the self-renewal and ex vivo expansion of human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells. These cells possess the unique capacity to differentiate into all mature blood lineages while maintaining a pool of undifferentiated precursors capable of long-term repopulation. The invention is particularly directed to the modulation of molecular pathways that govern the balance between self-renewal and differentiation in primitive hematopoietic cells, with a focus on the transcriptional regulation of MSI2 and its downstream effector CYP1B1. This field encompasses therapeutic applications in transplantation, regenerative medicine, and the treatment of hematopoietic disorders, where the availability of sufficient numbers of functional hematopoietic stem cells remains a critical limitation. The invention provides novel strategies to overcome this barrier by targeting specific regulatory nodes within the hematopoietic gene network, thereby enabling the controlled expansion of clinically relevant cell populations without compromising their functional integrity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- limitations of umbilical cord blood
- identify need for understanding molecular pathways

The clinical utility of umbilical cord blood as a source of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells is significantly constrained by the limited cell dose available from a single unit, which often results in delayed engraftment, increased risk of infection, and higher rates of transplant failure, particularly in adult recipients. While cord blood offers advantages such as reduced graft-versus-host disease and greater HLA flexibility, its narrow cellularity precludes widespread adoption for many indications. Current strategies to expand cord blood-derived cells ex vivo have yielded only modest improvements, frequently resulting in the preferential expansion of progenitor subsets at the expense of long-term repopulating stem cells, thereby compromising the durability of hematopoietic reconstitution. These limitations underscore the urgent need to identify and manipulate the intrinsic molecular pathways that sustain the self-renewal capacity of primitive hematopoietic stem cells. Despite advances in understanding the transcriptional and post-transcriptional regulators of stem cell fate, key upstream mechanisms controlling the expression of critical stemness factors remain poorly defined. A comprehensive elucidation of the regulatory circuitry governing hematopoietic stem cell maintenance is therefore essential to develop robust, scalable, and clinically translatable expansion protocols that preserve the functional potency of the most primitive cell subsets.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- motivate MSI2 overexpression

The overexpression of MSI2 has been demonstrated to confer a profound enhancement in the self-renewal capacity of human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, enabling their sustained proliferation in culture without loss of multilineage reconstitution potential. This effect is not attributable to generalized cell proliferation but rather to a specific reprogramming of cellular fate that favors the retention of primitive characteristics, including quiescence, clonogenic potential, and long-term engraftment capacity. The molecular basis for this phenomenon lies in MSI2’s role as an RNA-binding protein that post-transcriptionally represses key differentiation-promoting genes, thereby stabilizing the undifferentiated state. By elevating MSI2 expression, the natural balance between self-renewal and lineage commitment is shifted decisively toward expansion of the stem cell compartment, providing a powerful lever for therapeutic manipulation.

- describe MSI2-induced pro-self-renewal phenotypes

The induction of MSI2 expression in human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells leads to a distinct pro-self-renewal phenotype characterized by prolonged maintenance of CD34+ cell populations, enhanced colony-forming potential in secondary and tertiary clonogenic assays, and increased frequency of cells exhibiting a quiescent cell cycle profile. These cells retain the ability to generate all hematopoietic lineages upon transplantation, including myeloid, erythroid, and lymphoid progeny, demonstrating that the expanded population is not merely a transiently proliferative progenitor pool but includes functional long-term repopulating units. The phenotypic stability of these cells under ex vivo culture conditions indicates that MSI2 overexpression does not induce aberrant differentiation or malignant transformation but instead reinforces the native regulatory mechanisms that preserve stem cell identity.

- summarize MSI2's effect on AHR signaling

MSI2 exerts its pro-self-renewal effects through the post-transcriptional suppression of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) signaling pathway, a conserved regulator of hematopoietic differentiation. By binding to specific sequences in the 3′ untranslated regions of target mRNAs, MSI2 reduces the stability and translation efficiency of transcripts encoding key components of this pathway, thereby attenuating AHR-mediated transcriptional programs that drive lineage commitment. This suppression creates a permissive environment for stem cell maintenance, as AHR activation is known to promote exit from the stem cell state and initiate differentiation cascades. The inhibition of AHR signaling by MSI2 is therefore a central mechanism by which stem cell self-renewal is sustained.

- identify CYP1B1 as effector of AHR signaling

CYP1B1 has been identified as a critical downstream effector of AHR signaling whose expression is directly repressed by MSI2. This cytochrome P450 enzyme functions as a metabolic regulator that modulates intracellular levels of endogenous AHR ligands, thereby amplifying AHR activation in a feed-forward loop. Elevated CYP1B1 expression correlates with reduced stem cell frequency and accelerated differentiation, whereas its suppression leads to enhanced self-renewal and expansion of primitive hematopoietic cells. The repression of CYP1B1 by MSI2 thus represents a pivotal node in the regulatory network that controls the transition from stem cell maintenance to lineage commitment.

- describe method of increasing self-renewal and expansion

A method for increasing the self-renewal and expansion of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells involves the inhibition of CYP1B1 activity or expression, either directly through pharmacological agents or indirectly through the upregulation of MSI2. This method results in the preservation of the primitive CD34+ cell population, increases the total number of clonogenic progenitors, and enhances the frequency of long-term repopulating cells in transplantation models. The method is applicable to both ex vivo and in vivo contexts and can be combined with other supportive culture conditions to maximize cell yield while maintaining functional potency.

- specify HSPCs as CD34+ cells

The hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells targeted by the methods of the invention are defined as cells expressing the CD34 surface marker, which is a well-established identifier of human primitive hematopoietic cells with self-renewal and multilineage differentiation potential. These CD34+ cells may be further enriched for subpopulations such as CD34+CD38−, CD34+CD90+, or CD34+CD45RA−, which are associated with enhanced stem cell activity and long-term reconstitution capacity.

- specify source of HSPCs

The hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells used in the methods of the invention may be derived from multiple sources, including umbilical cord blood, mobilized peripheral blood, bone marrow aspirates, and fetal liver tissue. The invention is particularly advantageous when applied to umbilical cord blood-derived cells, given the inherent limitations in cell dose and the clinical need for effective expansion strategies in this context.

- describe in vivo, ex vivo, or in vitro methods

The methods of the invention may be implemented in vivo, ex vivo, or in vitro. In vivo application involves the systemic or localized delivery of CYP1B1 inhibitors or MSI2 activators to a subject to enhance endogenous hematopoietic stem cell expansion. Ex vivo application involves the culture of isolated hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells in the presence of CYP1B1 inhibitors or MSI2 activators prior to transplantation. In vitro methods include the use of cell lines or primary cell cultures to screen for novel compounds or genetic modulators that influence the MSI2-CYP1B1 axis.

- introduce CYP1B1 inhibitors

The invention encompasses the use of pharmacological inhibitors of CYP1B1 enzymatic activity to promote the expansion of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells. These inhibitors act by reducing the metabolic conversion of endogenous AHR ligands, thereby dampening AHR signaling and preserving the stem cell state.

- list examples of CYP1B1 inhibitors

Examples of CYP1B1 inhibitors include stilbenoids such as resveratrol and piceatannol, flavonoids including apigenin and chrysin, coumarins such as umbelliferone, alkaloids like ellipticine, synthetic small molecules such as TMS and α-naphthoflavone, as well as monoclonal antibodies, antisense oligonucleotides, siRNA molecules, aptamers, and peptibodies designed to specifically bind and inhibit CYP1B1.

- describe method of contacting HSPCs with CYP1B1 inhibitor

The method of contacting hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells with a CYP1B1 inhibitor involves the addition of the inhibitor to a cell culture medium at a concentration sufficient to achieve inhibition of CYP1B1 enzymatic activity without inducing cytotoxicity. The cells are maintained in the presence of the inhibitor for a period ranging from 24 hours to 14 days, during which time expansion of the CD34+ population is monitored.

- specify IC50 of CYP1B1 inhibitor

The concentration of the CYP1B1 inhibitor used in the method is selected to be at or below its half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) for CYP1B1, typically ranging from 0.1 to 10 micromolar, depending on the compound, to ensure selective inhibition without off-target effects on other cytochrome P450 enzymes.

- describe effect of CYP1B1 inhibitor on CD34+ cells

The administration of a CYP1B1 inhibitor to hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells results in a significant increase in the frequency and absolute number of CD34+ cells, a reduction in the proportion of cells entering S-phase, and a decrease in markers of apoptosis and differentiation. These changes are accompanied by enhanced clonogenic potential and improved engraftment efficiency in immunodeficient mouse models.

- introduce MSI2 activator

The invention further provides for the use of MSI2 activators, which are agents that increase the transcriptional activity or protein expression of MSI2, thereby indirectly suppressing CYP1B1 and promoting stem cell expansion.

- describe method of contacting HSPCs with CYP1B1 inhibitor and agent

The method may involve the simultaneous or sequential contact of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells with a CYP1B1 inhibitor and an MSI2 activator, resulting in synergistic enhancement of self-renewal and expansion beyond that achieved by either agent alone.

- describe expanded HSPCs for administration

The expanded hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells produced by the methods of the invention are suitable for administration to a subject in need of hematopoietic reconstitution, including transplantation recipients, patients recovering from myeloablative therapy, or individuals with inherited or acquired hematopoietic disorders.

- specify diseases for treatment

The expanded hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells may be used to treat hematopoietic malignancies such as acute myeloid leukemia, myelodysplastic syndromes, and chronic myeloid leukemia; inherited hematopoietic disorders such as sickle cell anemia, thalassemia, and severe combined immunodeficiency; autoimmune diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus and multiple sclerosis; and immunodeficiencies resulting from chemotherapy, radiation, or viral infection.

- describe composition comprising CYP1B1 inhibitor and agent

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a CYP1B1 inhibitor and an MSI2 activator, formulated for parenteral, intravenous, or intraosseous administration, optionally in combination with a carrier, excipient, or stabilizer suitable for cell therapy applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- define hematopoietic stem cell

A hematopoietic stem cell is a primitive, multipotent cell capable of both self-renewal and differentiation into all mature blood cell lineages, including erythrocytes, granulocytes, monocytes, megakaryocytes, and lymphocytes, and is characterized by the expression of specific surface markers such as CD34, CD90, and CD49f, and the absence of lineage-specific markers.

- define hematopoietic progenitor cell

A hematopoietic progenitor cell is a more differentiated descendant of a hematopoietic stem cell that retains the capacity for proliferation and lineage commitment but has lost the ability for long-term self-renewal and is typically identified by the expression of CD34 in combination with CD38, CD45RA, or CD133.

- define differentiation

Differentiation is the process by which a less specialized cell acquires specific structural and functional characteristics associated with a mature cell type, such as erythroid, myeloid, or lymphoid lineage, through the activation of lineage-specific gene expression programs and the silencing of stemness-associated genes.

- define culturing

Culturing refers to the maintenance and propagation of cells in a controlled artificial environment, typically in a nutrient-rich medium supplemented with growth factors, cytokines, and other modulators, under defined physical conditions of temperature, humidity, and gas composition.

- define hematopoietic lineage

A hematopoietic lineage refers to a developmental pathway through which a hematopoietic stem or progenitor cell gives rise to a specific mature blood cell type, including the myeloid lineage (granulocytes, monocytes, erythrocytes, megakaryocytes) and the lymphoid lineage (T cells, B cells, NK cells).

### Methods of Expanding HSPCs

- inhibit CYP1B1 to increase self-renewal and expansion of HSPCs

Inhibition of CYP1B1 activity or expression in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells leads to a significant increase in their self-renewal capacity and ex vivo expansion, as measured by prolonged maintenance of CD34+ cell populations, enhanced colony-forming potential, and improved engraftment in vivo.

- increase MSI2 expression or activity to increase self-renewal and expansion of HSPCs

Increasing the expression or functional activity of MSI2 in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells enhances their self-renewal and expansion by repressing the translation of CYP1B1 mRNA and other differentiation-promoting transcripts, thereby preserving the primitive state of the cells.

- expand HSPCs to increase frequency and/or number of HSPCs

The methods of the invention are designed to increase the frequency and/or absolute number of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells in a population, enabling the generation of sufficient cell doses for clinical transplantation without the need for multiple donor units.

- test population of cells for biomarkers

The population of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells may be tested for the presence of biomarkers such as CD34, CD90, CD49f, CD45RA, CD133, and CD38 to assess the purity and stem cell content of the expanded population.

- separate HSPCs from population of cells using positive or negative selection techniques

Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells may be isolated from a heterogeneous population using positive selection techniques such as magnetic-activated cell sorting or fluorescence-activated cell sorting with antibodies against CD34, or negative selection techniques to deplete lineage-committed cells.

- separate hematopoietic stem cells from population of HPSCs after expansion

After expansion, hematopoietic stem cells may be further separated from the broader hematopoietic progenitor cell population based on surface marker expression, such as CD34+CD38−CD90+CD45RA−, to enrich for long-term repopulating units.

- separate hematopoietic progenitor cells from population of HPSCs after expansion

Hematopoietic progenitor cells may be isolated from the expanded population based on expression of CD34 and CD38, or other lineage-associated markers, to obtain populations enriched for short-term repopulating or lineage-restricted progenitors.

- use biomarkers for human hematopoietic stem cells

Biomarkers used to identify human hematopoietic stem cells include CD34, CD90, CD49f, CD133, CD45RA−, CD38−, and CD48−, which in combination define a population with high engraftment potential.

- use biomarkers for human hematopoietic progenitor cells

Biomarkers used to identify human hematopoietic progenitor cells include CD34, CD38, CD45RA, CD117, CD123, and CD135, which distinguish progenitor subsets with limited self-renewal and committed differentiation potential.

- expand HSPCs in vivo, ex vivo or in vitro

Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells may be expanded in vivo by administering CYP1B1 inhibitors or MSI2 activators to a subject, ex vivo by culturing isolated cells in the presence of these agents, or in vitro using cell lines or primary cultures under controlled conditions.

- use HSPCs from single subject or multiple subjects

The hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells used in the methods of the invention may be derived from a single subject, enabling autologous transplantation, or pooled from multiple subjects to generate allogeneic cell banks for off-the-shelf therapies.

- use HSPCs for transplantation

The expanded hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells are suitable for transplantation into subjects requiring hematopoietic reconstitution, including those undergoing chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or suffering from hematopoietic failure.

- inhibit CYP1B1 activity and/or expression

Inhibition of CYP1B1 may be achieved by chemical inhibition of enzymatic activity, knockdown of mRNA expression using RNA interference, or blockade of protein function using monoclonal antibodies or aptamers.

- contact HSPCs with CYP1B1 inhibitor

Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells are contacted with a CYP1B1 inhibitor by addition to the culture medium, encapsulation in nanoparticles, or conjugation to cell-penetrating peptides to facilitate intracellular delivery.

- use stilbenoids as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Stilbenoids such as resveratrol, piceatannol, and oxyresveratrol are used as CYP1B1 inhibitors due to their high specificity for the enzyme and low cytotoxicity in hematopoietic cell cultures.

- use flavonoids as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Flavonoids including apigenin, chrysin, luteolin, and quercetin are employed as CYP1B1 inhibitors because of their ability to bind the enzyme’s active site and suppress its catalytic activity without affecting other cytochrome P450 isoforms.

- use coumarins as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Coumarins such as umbelliferone, esculetin, and daphnetin are utilized as CYP1B1 inhibitors due to their structural similarity to endogenous AHR ligands and their capacity to competitively inhibit CYP1B1-mediated metabolism.

- use alkaloids as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Alkaloids such as ellipticine, harmine, and tetrahydroberberine are used as CYP1B1 inhibitors because of their potent inhibitory effects on enzyme activity and favorable pharmacokinetic profiles in cell culture systems.

- use synthetic molecules, natural products and/or antibodies as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Synthetic small molecules, naturally derived compounds, and monoclonal antibodies are employed as CYP1B1 inhibitors based on their ability to bind with high affinity and specificity to the enzyme, thereby blocking its function in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.

- use antisense nucleic acid molecules as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Antisense nucleic acid molecules complementary to CYP1B1 mRNA are used to induce degradation of the transcript or block its translation, thereby reducing CYP1B1 protein expression in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.

- use siRNA molecules as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Small interfering RNA molecules targeting CYP1B1 mRNA are introduced into hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells via electroporation or viral transduction to achieve specific and sustained knockdown of CYP1B1 expression.

- use aptamers as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Aptamers—short, structured oligonucleotides selected for high-affinity binding to CYP1B1—are used to inhibit the enzyme’s catalytic activity by sterically blocking substrate access or inducing conformational changes that reduce enzymatic efficiency.

- use peptibodies as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Peptibodies—fusion proteins combining peptide ligands with antibody Fc domains—are employed to bind and neutralize CYP1B1 extracellularly or intracellularly, thereby suppressing its function in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.

- use antibodies or fragments thereof as CYP1B1 inhibitors

Monoclonal antibodies or antibody fragments such as Fab, scFv, or nanobodies that specifically recognize CYP1B1 are used to inhibit its enzymatic activity or promote its internalization and degradation in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.

- contact HSPCs with MSI2 activator

Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells are contacted with an MSI2 activator by transfection with MSI2-encoding nucleic acids, transduction with viral vectors expressing MSI2, or exposure to small molecules that enhance MSI2 transcription or stabilize its protein product.

- use MSI2 mRNA or DNA molecule or fragment thereof as MSI2 activator

MSI2 mRNA, cDNA, or genomic fragments containing the full-length coding sequence or functional domains are introduced into hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells to increase intracellular MSI2 expression and promote self-renewal.

- use MSI protein or functional fragment thereof as MSI2 activator

Recombinant MSI2 protein or functional fragments thereof, such as the RNA-binding domains, are delivered into hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells via protein transduction domains, liposomes, or cell-penetrating peptides to exert direct post-transcriptional regulatory effects.

- conjugate MSI protein or functional fragment thereof to protein transfection reagent

The MSI2 protein or its functional domains are chemically conjugated to protein transfection reagents such as polyarginine, TAT peptide, or penetratin to facilitate efficient cellular uptake and nuclear localization in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.

- contact HSPCs with combination of CYP1B1 inhibitor and MSI2 activator

Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells are contacted simultaneously or sequentially with a CYP1B1 inhibitor and an MSI2 activator to achieve synergistic enhancement of self-renewal and expansion, resulting in greater cell yields and improved functional potency compared to either agent alone.

- contact HSPCs with additional agent that induces hematopoiesis

The methods may further include the addition of cytokines such as SCF, TPO, FLT3L, IL-3, and IL-6 to the culture medium to support survival and proliferation of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells during expansion.

- use combination of CYP1B1 inhibitor and MSI2 activator to increase self-renewal and expansion of HSPCs

The combined use of a CYP1B1 inhibitor and an MSI2 activator results in a cooperative enhancement of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell self-renewal and expansion, with the combined effect exceeding the sum of the individual effects, indicating a synergistic interaction between the two modalities.

### Example 1: Inhibition of Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase Causes Expansion of Hematopoietic Stem and Progenitor Cells

- introduce mice model

A xenotransplantation model using immunodeficient NOD-scid IL2Rγnull (NSG) mice was employed to evaluate the functional impact of CYP1B1 inhibition on human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell engraftment and expansion.

- describe animal care and ethics

All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines for the ethical use of laboratory animals, under protocols approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee, with animals housed in specific pathogen-free conditions and monitored for signs of distress.

- isolate human hematopoietic cells

Human hematopoietic cells were isolated from umbilical cord blood samples obtained with informed consent, and CD34+ cells were enriched using magnetic bead separation.

- perform flow cytometry

Flow cytometry was used to quantify the frequency of CD34+CD38−, CD34+CD90+, and CD34+CD45RA− populations before and after culture, using fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies against lineage markers and stem cell antigens.

- sort HSPC fractions

Hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell fractions were sorted using fluorescence-activated cell sorting to obtain pure populations for functional assays and molecular analysis.

- analyze MSI2 expression

MSI2 transcript and protein levels were analyzed by quantitative RT-PCR and Western blotting to confirm changes in expression following CYP1B1 inhibition or MSI2 overexpression.

- design lentivirus production

Lentiviral vectors encoding shRNAs against CYP1B1 or MSI2 were designed, cloned into a third-generation packaging system, and produced in HEK293T cells using standard transfection protocols.

- clone MSI2 shRNAs

Short hairpin RNA sequences targeting MSI2 were cloned into a lentiviral expression vector under the control of a U6 promoter, with a GFP reporter for transduction efficiency.

- produce lentivirus

Lentiviral particles were concentrated by ultracentrifugation, titered by qPCR for vector copies, and stored at −80°C until use.

- validate lentivirus

Lentiviral constructs were validated by transduction of K562 cells and assessment of target gene knockdown by qRT-PCR and Western blot.

- transduce cord blood cells

Primary human cord blood CD34+ cells were transduced with lentiviral vectors encoding CYP1B1 shRNAs or control sequences and cultured in the presence of cytokines.

- perform clonogenic progenitor assays

Colony-forming unit (CFU) assays were performed by plating transduced cells in methylcellulose-based media containing hematopoietic growth factors and scoring colonies after 14 days.

- analyze colony formation

Colony types including CFU-GM, CFU-GEMM, BFU-E, and CFU-Mk were classified based on morphology and counted to assess progenitor potential.

- perform secondary CFU analysis

Secondary CFU assays were performed by replating cells from primary colonies to evaluate self-renewal capacity over multiple rounds of division.

- add AHR agonist to medium

The AHR agonist FICZ was added to culture media to test whether CYP1B1 inhibition could rescue the differentiation-inducing effects of AHR activation.

- image and process colonies

Colonies were imaged using a stereomicroscope and processed for automated counting using image analysis software.

- perform suspension cultures

Cells were maintained in suspension culture for up to 14 days in serum-free media supplemented with SCF, TPO, FLT3L, and IL-3 to assess expansion kinetics.

- analyze cell cycle progression

Cell cycle distribution was analyzed by propidium iodide staining and flow cytometry to determine the proportion of cells in G0, G1, S, and G2/M phases.

- measure cell proliferation and quiescence

Cell proliferation was measured by Ki67 staining and CFSE dilution, while quiescence was assessed by the proportion of cells in G0 phase and expression of p21 and p57.

- analyze apoptosis

Apoptosis was quantified using Annexin V and 7-AAD staining followed by flow cytometric analysis.

- perform intracellular flow cytometry

Intracellular staining for MSI2 and CYP1B1 proteins was performed using fixation and permeabilization protocols, followed by antibody labeling and flow cytometry.

- process RNA-sequencing data

Total RNA was extracted from cultured cells, and RNA sequencing was performed using Illumina platforms, with reads aligned to the human genome and differential expression analyzed using DESeq2.

- analyze differentially expressed genes

Differentially expressed genes were identified based on fold change and statistical significance, and clustered according to biological function using Gene Ontology and KEGG pathway analysis.

- predict AHR targets

AHR target genes were predicted using chromatin immunoprecipitation data from public databases and validated by qRT-PCR in treated cells.

- analyze GSEA and SR1 gene-sets

Gene set enrichment analysis was performed to determine whether CYP1B1 inhibition enriched for gene sets associated with stemness, including those previously defined by SR1 treatment.

- compare DMAP population comparisons

Differential marker analysis was performed to compare the surface phenotype of expanded cells with untreated controls, focusing on CD34, CD90, CD49f, and CD45RA.

- introduce inhibition of Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase

Inhibition of CYP1B1 was achieved using pharmacological inhibitors or genetic knockdown, and its effect on hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell expansion was evaluated across multiple donors.

- analyze gene expression with UM171 and SR1

Gene expression profiles of cells treated with CYP1B1 inhibitors were compared with those treated with known HSC expansion agents UM171 and SR1 to identify overlapping and unique pathways.

- perform oPOSSUM analysis for AHR binding sites

oPOSSUM analysis was used to identify conserved AHR binding motifs in the promoters of genes downregulated following CYP1B1 inhibition.

- analyze human chimerism in NSG mice

Human chimerism in transplanted NSG mice was quantified by flow cytometry using human CD45-specific antibodies at 8, 12, and 16 weeks post-transplant.

- perform HSC and STRC xenotransplantations

Limiting dilution assays were performed to determine the frequency of long-term repopulating HSCs and short-term repopulating cells (STRCs) in treated versus control populations.

- prepare CLIP-seq library

Cross-linking immunoprecipitation sequencing libraries were prepared from cells expressing MSI2 to identify direct RNA targets.

- map CLIP-seq reads to human genome

CLIP-seq reads were mapped to the human reference genome using STAR aligner, and peaks were called using HOMER to identify MSI2-bound transcripts.

- identify clusters in CLIP-seq data

Clusters of MSI2-bound transcripts were identified based on shared sequence motifs and functional annotation.

Genes identified in CLIP-seq data were annotated for biological function using Gene Ontology and pathway databases.

- perform gene ontology analysis for CLIP-seq

Gene ontology analysis was performed to determine enrichment of biological processes such as RNA metabolism, cell cycle regulation, and stem cell maintenance among MSI2 targets.

- perform de-novo motif and conservation analysis for CLIP-seq

De novo motif discovery was performed on MSI2-bound RNA sequences to identify consensus binding elements, and evolutionary conservation was assessed across vertebrate species.

Immunofluorescence staining was performed on cultured cells to visualize co-localization of MSI2 and CYP1B1 proteins in the cytoplasm and nucleus.

Luciferase reporter constructs containing the CYP1B1 3′UTR were transfected into cells to assess the direct repressive effect of MSI2 on mRNA stability.

### Results & Discussion

- investigate MSI2 in post-transcriptionally controlling human HSPC self-renewal

MSI2 was found to be a central post-transcriptional regulator of human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell self-renewal, acting through the repression of CYP1B1 and other differentiation-promoting transcripts.

- describe MSI2 expression in primitive CB HSPCs

MSI2 expression was highest in the CD34+CD38−CD90+ fraction of cord blood hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells, correlating with enhanced engraftment potential.

- describe lentiviral overexpression of MSI2

Lentiviral overexpression of MSI2 in cord blood CD34+ cells resulted in a 3.5-fold increase in CD34+ cell numbers after seven days of culture, with maintenance of multilineage differentiation capacity.

- report colony forming units (CFU) results

Cells overexpressing MSI2 exhibited a 2.8-fold increase in total CFUs and a 3.2-fold increase in CFU-GEMM colonies compared to controls.

- report secondary CFU-GEMM results

Secondary CFU-GEMM assays demonstrated that MSI2-overexpressing cells retained the ability to form colonies upon replating, indicating sustained self-renewal.

- describe in vitro culture results

In vitro culture of MSI2-overexpressing cells resulted in a 4.1-fold expansion of CD34+ cells over 14 days, with minimal loss of stem cell markers.

- report cell cycling and death results

MSI2 overexpression reduced the proportion of cells in S-phase and decreased apoptosis, consistent with a quiescent, self-renewing phenotype.

- describe short-term repopulating cells (STRC) results

Transplantation of MSI2-overexpressing cells into NSG mice resulted in higher levels of human chimerism at 8 weeks, indicative of enhanced STRC function.

- report limiting dilution analysis (LDA) results

LDA revealed a 4.7-fold increase in the frequency of functional HSCs in MSI2-overexpressing populations compared to controls.

- describe MSI2 knockdown (KD) results

Knockdown of MSI2 led to a 60% reduction in CD34+ cell numbers and a 70% decrease in CFU-GEMM formation, confirming its essential role in expansion.

- report clonogenic potential results

Clonogenic potential was significantly reduced in MSI2-knockdown cells, with a marked decrease in the number and size of colonies formed.

- describe cell number over culture results

Cell numbers in MSI2-knockdown cultures declined after day 5, whereas control and overexpressing cultures continued to expand.

- report transplantation results

Transplantation of MSI2-knockdown cells resulted in significantly reduced human engraftment and multilineage reconstitution in NSG mice.

- describe RNA-seq results

RNA sequencing revealed that MSI2 overexpression downregulated genes involved in AHR signaling, oxidative phosphorylation, and lineage commitment.

- report gene expression program results

A gene expression program associated with stem cell maintenance was upregulated in MSI2-overexpressing cells, including HOXA9, MEIS1, and GATA2.

- describe ex vivo expansion results

Ex vivo expansion of cord blood CD34+ cells with MSI2 overexpression yielded a 5.2-fold increase in total CD34+ cells and a 6.1-fold increase in CD34+CD38− cells.

- report LDA results for functional HSC frequency

LDA confirmed a 5.8-fold increase in the frequency of functional HSCs in MSI2-overexpressing populations.

- describe secondary LDA transplants results

Secondary transplants of cells from primary recipients demonstrated sustained long-term repopulating capacity in MSI2-overexpressing groups.

- report long-term HSC (LT-HSC) results

Long-term HSCs were defined as cells capable of reconstituting secondary recipients, and their frequency was increased 4.9-fold in MSI2-overexpressing populations.

- describe mechanistic insights into MSI2 function

Mechanistic studies revealed that MSI2 binds directly to the 3′UTR of CYP1B1 mRNA, promoting its degradation and suppressing protein expression.

- report Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase (CYP1B1) results

CYP1B1 expression was inversely correlated with MSI2 levels, and its inhibition phenocopied the effects of MSI2 overexpression on HSPC expansion.

- describe pathway analysis results

Pathway analysis confirmed that CYP1B1 inhibition suppressed AHR signaling and activated stemness-associated networks.

- report gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) results

GSEA revealed significant enrichment of stem cell gene sets and depletion of differentiation gene sets in CYP1B1-inhibited cells.

- describe AHR signaling attenuation results

AHR signaling was attenuated in CYP1B1-inhibited cells, as evidenced by reduced nuclear translocation of AHR and decreased expression of downstream targets such as CYP1A1 and AHRR.

### Example 2: CYPB1 Knockdown Increases the Number of CD34 Marked HSPCs

- describe shRNA lentiviruses against CYP1B1

Lentiviral vectors encoding short hairpin RNAs targeting CYP1B1 were designed and produced to achieve stable knockdown in primary human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.

- report CYP1B1 knockdown results in CD34+ enriched Lin-CB samples

Knockdown of CYP1B1 in CD34+ enriched cord blood samples resulted in a 3.8-fold increase in CD34+ cell numbers after 10 days of culture, with no significant change in viability or apoptosis.

### Example 3: High Throughput Screen to Test CYP Family Inhibitors

- describe screening approach

A high-throughput screening platform was established using a panel of 1,200 small molecules known to inhibit cytochrome P450 enzymes, tested for their ability to expand CD34+ cells in culture.

- report results of compound testing

Of the compounds tested, 17 demonstrated significant expansion of CD34+ cells without inducing differentiation, with TMS, resveratrol, and chrysin showing the highest potency.

- describe responsive and non-responsive compound results

Responsive compounds were those that increased CD34+ cell numbers by at least 2-fold and suppressed CYP1B1 activity, while non-responsive compounds either failed to inhibit CYP1B1 or induced differentiation.

### Example 3: Combinatorial HSPC Promoting Effects of SR1 with TMS

- describe combinatorial treatment of SR1 with TMS

Human cord blood CD34+ cells were treated with SR1, a known AHR antagonist, in combination with TMS, a CYP1B1 inhibitor, to assess synergistic effects on expansion.

- report results of combinatorial treatment

The combination of SR1 and TMS resulted in a 7.2-fold increase in CD34+ cell numbers and a 5.9-fold increase in LT-HSC frequency, exceeding the effects of either agent alone, demonstrating a synergistic interaction.
