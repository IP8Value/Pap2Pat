# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- relate to electrochemical exfoliation

The present invention relates to systems and methods for the electrochemical exfoliation of layered carbonaceous materials, particularly those derived from petroleum coke, to produce high-purity graphene-like nanosheets. The invention encompasses a novel reactor configuration designed to maintain consistent electrical contact and mechanical compression during the exfoliation process, enabling efficient and scalable production of carbon nanomaterials from industrial by-products. This technology is particularly suited for the transformation of low-value petroleum coke streams into high-value nanoscale carbon products suitable for use in energy storage, electronics, and advanced composites. The method leverages electrochemical intercalation under controlled pressure and electrolytic conditions to achieve exfoliation without reliance on mechanical milling or harsh chemical oxidation, thereby preserving the structural integrity and electronic properties of the resulting nanosheets. The invention further enables the selective isolation of exfoliated material through sequential centrifugation protocols, allowing for the purification of graphene-like products from unreacted feedstock and amorphous by-products. This approach represents a significant advancement in the field of carbon nanomaterial synthesis by enabling the utilization of non-graphitic, petroleum-derived feedstocks that were previously considered unsuitable for high-quality graphene production.

## BACKGROUND

- motivate electrochemical exfoliation

The global demand for high-performance carbon nanomaterials has surged in recent years, driven by applications in lithium-ion batteries, supercapacitors, transparent conductive films, and flexible electronics. Graphene, in particular, has emerged as a material of exceptional promise due to its extraordinary electrical conductivity, mechanical strength, and thermal stability. However, conventional methods of graphene production—such as mechanical exfoliation, chemical vapor deposition, and chemical oxidation followed by reduction—are either prohibitively expensive, low-yielding, or result in materials with significant structural defects and residual oxygen functionalities. Electrochemical exfoliation has been proposed as a scalable alternative, particularly for graphite-based precursors, but its application to petroleum coke has remained largely unexplored and underdeveloped. Petroleum coke, a by-product of oil refining, is typically combusted as a low-grade fuel or used in the production of carbon anodes for aluminum smelting, both of which contribute substantially to global carbon emissions. The economic and environmental imperative to repurpose this abundant waste stream into higher-value materials has motivated the search for novel conversion pathways. Existing attempts to exfoliate coke via electrochemical means have yielded inconsistent results, often producing small quantum dots or amorphous carbon aggregates rather than well-defined, few-layer nanosheets. These failures stem from inadequate control over the physical state of the feedstock during exfoliation, poor electrolyte penetration due to particle agglomeration, and the absence of a mechanism to maintain electrical continuity as layers separate. The present invention addresses these longstanding limitations by introducing a compressed reactor system that ensures intimate contact between the electrode and the particulate feedstock throughout the exfoliation process, thereby enabling the efficient and reproducible production of high-quality graphene-like material from petroleum coke.

## SUMMARY

- disclose reactor for electrochemical exfoliation
- disclose method of electrochemical exfoliation

The invention discloses a novel reactor system configured for the electrochemical exfoliation of layered carbonaceous materials, comprising a sealed container housing a porous chamber filled with particulate parent material, a working electrode inserted into the material, at least one counter electrode positioned externally, and a pressure source capable of applying uniform compressive force to the chamber. The reactor further includes an electrolyte solution that permeates the particulate bed and facilitates ion intercalation between carbon layers, and an electrical power source that applies a controlled potential difference between the working and counter electrodes to drive exfoliation. The porous chamber is constructed from a chemically inert, electrically insulating material with pore sizes selected to retain the parent material while permitting electrolyte ingress and exfoliated product egress. The invention further discloses a method of electrochemical exfoliation comprising the steps of preparing a particulate parent material with a layered planar structure, pretreating the material to enhance hydrophilicity and remove impurities, compacting the material within the porous chamber under controlled pressure, submerging the chamber in an electrolyte solution, inserting a working electrode into the compacted bed, applying a voltage sufficient to induce intercalation and layer separation, and subsequently separating the exfoliated nanosheets from unreacted material and non-graphitic by-products through a multi-stage centrifugation process. The method further includes optional post-treatment steps such as washing, drying, dispersion, and thermal annealing to enhance the electrical conductivity and structural order of the resulting nanosheets. The reactor and method together enable the high-yield, scalable production of graphene-like material from petroleum coke with minimal chemical modification and without the use of aggressive oxidants.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce electrochemical exfoliation

Electrochemical exfoliation is a process by which layered carbonaceous materials are subjected to an applied electrical potential in the presence of an electrolyte, resulting in the intercalation of ions between the atomic planes of the material. As these ions accumulate, they generate repulsive forces that overcome the van der Waals interactions holding adjacent layers together, leading to their separation into individual or few-layer nanosheets. Unlike mechanical or chemical methods, electrochemical exfoliation does not require the fragmentation of bulk material into smaller fragments prior to exfoliation, nor does it rely on the introduction of oxygen-containing functional groups that degrade the intrinsic properties of the resulting carbon sheets. Instead, it preserves the sp² carbon network, enabling the production of materials with high electrical conductivity and low defect density. The process is particularly effective when applied to materials with extended, ordered crystalline domains and a high degree of planarity, as these structural features facilitate uniform ion penetration and layer separation. The present invention demonstrates that certain grades of petroleum coke, particularly needle coke, possess sufficient crystalline order and layered morphology to serve as viable precursors for this process, despite their historical classification as non-graphitic feedstocks.

- relate to processes and systems for electrochemical exfoliation

The processes and systems described herein are specifically engineered to overcome the limitations of prior electrochemical exfoliation methods, which typically employ graphite monoliths or loose powders that lose electrical contact during exfoliation. In conventional setups, as layers separate, the conductive network is disrupted, halting further exfoliation and limiting yield. The present invention introduces a compressed reactor system that maintains continuous electrical contact between the working electrode and the particulate feedstock throughout the entire exfoliation cycle. This is achieved by confining the parent material within a porous, non-conductive chamber that is mechanically compressed by an external pressure source. The compression ensures that as individual layers are exfoliated, the remaining material remains in intimate contact with the electrode, allowing the reaction to proceed until the majority of exfoliable material has been liberated. The system further incorporates a counter electrode positioned externally to the chamber, enabling uniform current distribution and minimizing localized overpotential that could lead to electrolyte decomposition or electrode corrosion.

- describe limitations of current techniques

Current techniques for electrochemical exfoliation suffer from several critical limitations that preclude their effective application to petroleum coke. First, most systems rely on graphite blocks or dense pellets as feedstock, which are incompatible with the irregular, particulate nature of coke. Second, the use of loose powders in suspension leads to poor electrical contact, inconsistent current flow, and rapid sedimentation that shields particles from the applied potential. Third, existing methods lack a mechanism for separating exfoliated nanosheets from unreacted feedstock and non-graphitic debris during or immediately after the reaction, resulting in contaminated products that require extensive downstream purification. Fourth, the absence of controlled compression allows for the loss of structural integrity in the feedstock bed, leading to heterogeneous exfoliation and reduced yield. Finally, prior approaches do not account for the variable crystallinity and morphology of petroleum coke, treating all grades as equivalent feedstocks, despite significant differences in their microstructure and exfoliation potential.

- motivate electrochemical exfoliation

The motivation for advancing electrochemical exfoliation lies in its potential to transform low-value, high-emission industrial by-products into high-value, low-carbon nanomaterials. Petroleum coke, particularly needle coke, is produced in quantities exceeding one million tons annually and is currently either combusted for energy or used in the production of carbon anodes, both of which contribute significantly to greenhouse gas emissions. By repurposing this material into graphene-like nanosheets, the invention provides a pathway to decarbonize the petrochemical industry while simultaneously meeting the growing demand for advanced carbon materials in energy storage and electronics. The economic incentive is compelling: the market value of graphene powder is orders of magnitude greater than that of raw coke, and the scalability of the electrochemical process enables cost-effective production at industrial scales. Furthermore, unlike natural graphite, which is finite and often contaminated with silicates and metals, needle coke can be produced with high purity and consistent composition, making it a more reliable and sustainable feedstock for high-performance applications.

- describe advantages of electrochemical exfoliation

The advantages of the electrochemical exfoliation method disclosed herein include high material yield, minimal chemical modification, operational simplicity, and compatibility with industrial-scale processing. The process operates at ambient pressure and moderate temperatures, avoiding the energy-intensive conditions required for graphitization or chemical reduction. It does not require strong acids or oxidizing agents, thereby eliminating the generation of hazardous waste streams and preserving the intrinsic electronic properties of the carbon lattice. The resulting nanosheets exhibit low oxygen content, high crystallinity, and a well-defined 2D Raman signature, confirming their similarity to high-quality graphene. The method also enables the recycling of unexfoliated material back into the reactor, further improving overall yield and reducing feedstock waste. Additionally, the use of a compressed reactor ensures reproducibility across batches, a critical factor for commercial adoption. The ability to tailor the exfoliation outcome by adjusting voltage, pressure, and pretreatment parameters provides unprecedented control over the size, thickness, and conductivity of the final product.

- introduce compression reactor

The compression reactor is a novel apparatus designed to facilitate the electrochemical exfoliation of particulate carbonaceous materials under controlled mechanical and electrical conditions. It consists of a sealed, non-conductive container that houses a porous chamber filled with the parent material. The chamber is compressed by an external pressure source that applies uniform force across its surface, ensuring that the particulate bed remains densely packed while allowing electrolyte to permeate through its pores. The working electrode is inserted directly into the center of the compressed bed, maintaining continuous electrical contact throughout the exfoliation process. The counter electrode is positioned outside the chamber, surrounding the container to ensure homogeneous current distribution. The reactor is designed to withstand the electrochemical conditions of the process, including exposure to aqueous electrolytes and applied potentials up to 15 volts, without degradation or leakage.

- describe parent material

The parent material utilized in the invention is a particulate form of petroleum coke, specifically needle coke, which exhibits a layered planar structure characterized by extended aromatic domains and high crystallinity. Unlike fuel coke or anode coke, which possess amorphous, spherical, or honeycomb-like morphologies, needle coke is composed of elongated, anisotropic particles with well-defined internal layering. These structural features enable the intercalation of ions between carbon planes during electrochemical exfoliation, facilitating the separation of individual graphene-like sheets. The parent material is selected based on its particle size, surface chemistry, and degree of graphitization, with needle coke fines proving optimal for high-yield exfoliation. The material may be derived from the coking of high-aromaticity feedstocks such as Fluid Catalytic Cracker decant oil and may be further purified through thermal or chemical treatments to enhance its exfoliation potential.

- define layered planar structure

A layered planar structure refers to a carbonaceous arrangement in which carbon atoms are organized in two-dimensional hexagonal lattices stacked in parallel planes, held together by weak van der Waals forces. This structure is characteristic of graphitic materials and is essential for successful electrochemical exfoliation, as it permits the insertion of ions between layers without disrupting the covalent bonding within each plane. In the context of this invention, the parent material must possess sufficient long-range order and alignment of these planes to allow for uniform intercalation and subsequent separation into nanosheets. The presence of such a structure is confirmed through electron diffraction, Raman spectroscopy, and transmission electron microscopy, which reveal the characteristic hexagonal lattice and 2D peak indicative of graphitic ordering.

- illustrate layered parent material

The layered parent material, as illustrated in the invention, consists of elongated, needle-like particles with visible internal striations when examined under high-resolution microscopy. These striations correspond to the stacking of graphene-like planes along the long axis of the particle. The material exhibits a high degree of crystallinity, as evidenced by sharp diffraction rings in electron diffraction patterns and the presence of a distinct G-band in Raman spectra. The surface of the particles is relatively smooth, with minimal amorphous carbon coating, allowing for efficient electrolyte penetration. The particles are typically in the range of 10 to 50 micrometers in length and 1 to 5 micrometers in diameter, with a narrow size distribution that enhances packing density within the reactor chamber.

- describe exfoliation of parent material

The exfoliation of the parent material occurs upon the application of a controlled electrical potential between the working and counter electrodes, with the material immersed in an electrolyte solution. As the potential is applied, cations from the electrolyte migrate into the interlayer spaces of the carbon structure, inducing electrostatic repulsion that overcomes the interlayer binding energy. This repulsion causes the layers to separate, generating individual or few-layer nanosheets that are liberated from the parent particle. The exfoliation process is sustained by the compression applied to the reactor chamber, which ensures that as layers are removed, the remaining material remains in electrical contact with the electrode. The process continues until the majority of exfoliable material has been separated, at which point the applied voltage is terminated and the reaction mixture is collected for downstream separation.

- define nanosheets

Nanosheets, as defined herein, are two-dimensional carbon structures derived from the exfoliation of layered carbonaceous materials, characterized by a lateral dimension significantly greater than their thickness. These sheets exhibit a thickness of less than 10 nanometers and a lateral dimension ranging from 1 to 10 micrometers, resulting in an aspect ratio greater than 100:1. The nanosheets possess a sp²-hybridized carbon lattice with minimal structural defects and low oxygen content, as confirmed by Raman spectroscopy, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy, and transmission electron microscopy. They are distinct from graphene oxide or reduced graphene oxide in that they are produced without the introduction of covalent oxygen functionalities, preserving their intrinsic electronic and mechanical properties.

- describe suitable parent materials

Suitable parent materials for the invention include needle coke, which is the most effective due to its high crystallinity and layered morphology. Other potential feedstocks include graphitized petroleum coke, high-temperature cokes derived from aromatic hydrocarbons, and biomass-derived cokes with similar structural characteristics. Fuel coke and anode coke are unsuitable due to their amorphous, isotropic, or sponge-like structures, which prevent uniform ion intercalation and layer separation. The parent material must be in particulate form, with particle sizes optimized for packing density and electrolyte penetration, typically in the range of 1 to 50 micrometers. The material must also be free of excessive metal impurities, sulfur, and nitrogen compounds that could interfere with the electrochemical process or contaminate the final product.

- classify petroleum cokes

Petroleum cokes are classified into three primary categories based on their production method, morphology, and intended application: fuel coke, anode coke, and needle coke. Fuel coke is the lowest-grade coke, produced from low-quality vacuum residues and characterized by a spherical, agglomerated morphology with high impurity content. It is primarily used as a fuel in cement kilns and power plants. Anode coke is of intermediate quality, produced from medium-grade residues and exhibiting a honeycomb-like structure with moderate crystallinity; it is used in the production of aluminum smelting anodes. Needle coke is the highest-grade coke, derived from high-aromaticity feedstocks such as FCC decant oil, and is distinguished by its elongated, needle-like particles and high degree of crystallinity. It is used in the manufacture of high-performance graphite electrodes for steelmaking and lithium-ion batteries.

- describe fuel coke

Fuel coke is a low-value by-product of petroleum refining, formed from the coking of low-quality vacuum residues under relatively mild conditions. It is composed of spherical, agglomerated particles with a highly amorphous structure and contains elevated levels of sulfur, nitrogen, and heavy metals. Its low crystallinity and irregular morphology render it unsuitable for electrochemical exfoliation, as it lacks the layered planar structure necessary for ion intercalation and layer separation. Attempts to exfoliate fuel coke result in the production of small, disordered carbon fragments with no discernible graphene signature.

- describe anode coke

Anode coke is a medium-grade petroleum coke produced from moderately refined vacuum residues and characterized by a sponge-like or honeycomb morphology. While it possesses a higher degree of crystallinity than fuel coke, its structure is isotropic and lacks the long-range alignment of carbon layers required for efficient exfoliation. Anode coke is primarily used in the aluminum industry for the production of carbon anodes, where its moderate electrical conductivity and mechanical strength are sufficient. However, its structural heterogeneity prevents the formation of uniform nanosheets during electrochemical exfoliation, resulting in low yields and poor product quality.

- describe needle coke

Needle coke is the highest-grade petroleum coke, produced from high-aromaticity feedstocks such as Fluid Catalytic Cracker decant oil under controlled coking conditions. It is distinguished by its elongated, needle-like morphology and highly ordered crystalline structure, with carbon layers aligned parallel to the long axis of the particles. This alignment facilitates the intercalation of ions during electrochemical exfoliation, enabling the efficient separation of graphene-like nanosheets. Needle coke exhibits low impurity content, high thermal stability, and excellent electrical conductivity, making it the only petroleum coke grade suitable for use as a precursor in the invention. Its high graphitic content and structural uniformity allow for the production of nanosheets with high aspect ratios and low defect densities.

- describe particulate form of parent material

The parent material is used in particulate form to ensure uniform packing within the reactor chamber and efficient electrolyte penetration. The particles are typically in the size range of 1 to 50 micrometers, with a narrow size distribution that minimizes void spaces and maximizes contact between particles and the working electrode. The particulate form allows for the creation of a cohesive, conductive bed that maintains structural integrity under compression while permitting ion diffusion. The particles may be obtained by grinding larger coke pieces or by direct collection from the coker unit, followed by sieving and washing to remove fines and impurities.

- describe particle sizes

Particle sizes of the parent material are optimized to balance packing density and electrolyte accessibility. Particles smaller than 1 micrometer tend to form dense, impermeable beds that restrict ion transport, while particles larger than 50 micrometers are too irregular to pack uniformly and may contain internal defects that hinder exfoliation. The optimal size range is between 5 and 20 micrometers, where the material exhibits sufficient surface area for electrolyte interaction while maintaining mechanical stability under compression. Particle size distribution is determined by laser diffraction or sieve analysis, and materials with a coefficient of variation less than 20% are preferred for consistent exfoliation performance.

- relate crystalline grain size to exfoliation

The crystalline grain size of the parent material directly influences the efficiency and quality of exfoliation. Larger crystalline domains, typically greater than 50 nanometers in lateral dimension, provide extended regions of sp² carbon bonding that facilitate uniform ion intercalation and layer separation. Materials with small or disordered crystalline grains exhibit poor exfoliation yields due to the presence of grain boundaries and defects that impede ion migration and promote localized structural collapse. Needle coke, with its long-range crystalline order and minimal grain boundaries, is uniquely suited for this process, as its large, aligned domains enable the production of large, high-quality nanosheets. The crystalline grain size is determined by high-resolution transmission electron microscopy and confirmed by the sharpness of the (002) peak in X-ray diffraction patterns.

- describe exfoliated material

The exfoliated material produced by the invention consists of a mixture of graphene-like nanosheets, unreacted parent material, and non-graphitic carbon debris. The nanosheets are thin, flexible, and transparent under optical microscopy, with lateral dimensions ranging from 1 to 5 micrometers and thicknesses of 1 to 5 nanometers. They exhibit a hexagonal lattice structure under high-resolution imaging and possess a characteristic 2D Raman peak, confirming their graphitic nature. The exfoliated material is initially suspended in the electrolyte solution and requires separation from the unreacted feedstock and impurities through centrifugation and washing.

- separate exfoliated material into distinct products

The separation of exfoliated material into distinct products is achieved through a two-step centrifugation protocol. In the first step, the reaction mixture is centrifuged at low speed (2000 rpm) to sediment the unreacted parent material, which is larger and denser than the exfoliated nanosheets. The supernatant, containing the nanosheets and smaller non-graphitic particles, is collected. In the second step, the supernatant is centrifuged at high speed (5000 rpm), which pellets the non-graphitic debris while leaving the nanosheets in suspension. The final product is recovered by decanting the supernatant and drying the suspended nanosheets. This method enables the isolation of high-purity graphene-like material with minimal contamination.

- describe exfoliated nanosheets

The exfoliated nanosheets are two-dimensional carbon structures with a thickness of less than 10 nanometers and a lateral dimension exceeding 1 micrometer, resulting in an aspect ratio greater than 100:1. These sheets are composed of sp²-hybridized carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice, with minimal structural defects and low oxygen content. They exhibit high electrical conductivity, thermal stability, and mechanical flexibility, making them suitable for use in energy storage devices, conductive inks, and composite materials. The nanosheets are free of covalent oxygen functionalities, distinguishing them from graphene oxide and enabling their direct integration into electronic and electrochemical systems without further reduction.

- define largest lateral dimension

The largest lateral dimension of the exfoliated nanosheets refers to the maximum distance between two points on the plane of the sheet, measured in the direction of its greatest extent. This dimension is typically determined by analyzing high-resolution scanning electron microscopy or atomic force microscopy images of individual sheets. In the invention, the largest lateral dimension ranges from 1 to 10 micrometers, with a median value of approximately 3 micrometers. This size range is optimal for applications requiring high surface area and efficient charge transport, such as battery electrodes and supercapacitors.

- describe thickness of exfoliated nanosheets

The thickness of the exfoliated nanosheets is measured using atomic force microscopy and transmission electron microscopy, and is found to range from 1 to 5 nanometers, corresponding to one to five layers of carbon atoms. The majority of sheets exhibit a thickness of 2 to 3 nanometers, consistent with few-layer graphene. The thickness distribution is narrow, with a standard deviation of less than 0.5 nanometers, indicating a high degree of uniformity in the exfoliation process. This low thickness is critical for maximizing the surface-to-volume ratio and enhancing the electrochemical performance of the material in energy storage applications.

- relate aspect ratio to electrochemical exfoliation

The aspect ratio of the exfoliated nanosheets—the ratio of their largest lateral dimension to their thickness—is a direct indicator of the efficiency of the electrochemical exfoliation process. High aspect ratios, exceeding 100:1, are achieved only when the exfoliation is uniform, selective, and complete, with minimal fragmentation or defect formation. The compression reactor disclosed herein enables the maintenance of high aspect ratios by preventing mechanical shear forces and ensuring that exfoliation occurs through intercalation rather than fracture. The aspect ratio is correlated with the electrical conductivity of the final product, as higher aspect ratios facilitate better percolation networks in composite films and reduce inter-sheet contact resistance.

- describe compositions with high exfoliated material content

Compositions with high exfoliated material content are those in which the proportion of graphene-like nanosheets exceeds 80% by weight, as determined by thermogravimetric analysis and Raman mapping. These compositions are obtained through the optimized two-step centrifugation protocol and exhibit superior electrical conductivity, mechanical strength, and thermal stability compared to mixtures containing significant amounts of unreacted feedstock or amorphous carbon. Such high-purity compositions are suitable for use in high-performance applications, including lithium-ion battery anodes, transparent conductive films, and electromagnetic shielding materials.

- describe electrochemical exfoliation methods

The electrochemical exfoliation methods disclosed herein involve the application of a direct current potential between a working electrode inserted into a compressed bed of particulate parent material and a counter electrode positioned externally. The material is immersed in an aqueous electrolyte solution, typically 0.1 M ammonium sulfate, and the voltage is maintained between 8 and 15 volts for a duration of 1 to 4 hours. The process is conducted at ambient temperature and pressure, with the reactor chamber maintained under constant mechanical compression. The exfoliation rate is proportional to the applied voltage, with higher voltages accelerating ion intercalation but risking membrane damage or electrolyte decomposition. The method is scalable and repeatable, with consistent yields achieved across multiple batches.

- relate voltage to exfoliation rate

The rate of exfoliation is directly proportional to the magnitude of the applied voltage, as higher potentials increase the driving force for ion intercalation and layer separation. However, beyond a threshold of approximately 12 volts, the rate of exfoliation plateaus and the risk of membrane degradation or electrolyte breakdown increases. The optimal voltage range for needle coke is between 10 and 12 volts, balancing exfoliation efficiency with system integrity. Below 8 volts, the process is too slow to be industrially viable, with insufficient ion flux to overcome interlayer binding energies. The relationship between voltage and exfoliation rate is linear within the optimal range, allowing for precise control over product yield and quality.

- describe pre-treatment of parent material

The pre-treatment of the parent material involves washing with dichloromethane and deionized water to remove surface impurities, followed by acid treatment with 6 M nitric acid at 120°C for four hours. This step enhances the hydrophilicity of the carbon surface, improves electrolyte wetting, and removes residual hydrocarbons and metal contaminants. The acid treatment also introduces mild surface oxidation that facilitates ion adsorption without compromising the graphitic structure. After treatment, the material is thoroughly rinsed with deionized water and dried at 100°C to remove residual moisture.

- describe solvent washing

Solvent washing is performed to remove organic impurities, residual hydrocarbons, and processing aids from the parent material prior to exfoliation. The material is washed with dichloromethane to dissolve non-polar contaminants, followed by deionized water to remove polar residues and salts. The washing is conducted under vacuum filtration to ensure complete removal of solvents and to prevent particle agglomeration. The washed material is then dried under vacuum at 100°C for 12 hours to eliminate residual moisture that could interfere with electrolyte penetration.

- describe thermal treatment

Thermal treatment is employed to enhance the crystallinity and remove volatile components from the parent material. The material is heated in an inert atmosphere at temperatures up to 500°C for a duration of 2 to 4 hours. This step removes residual hydrocarbons and stabilizes the carbon structure, reducing the likelihood of side reactions during electrochemical exfoliation. Thermal treatment also improves the mechanical integrity of the particles, enabling them to withstand the compressive forces applied in the reactor without fracturing.

- describe parent material properties

The parent material must possess a high degree of crystallinity, low impurity content, and a layered planar morphology to be suitable for electrochemical exfoliation. Key properties include a high carbon content (>95%), low sulfur and nitrogen content (<1% each), a narrow particle size distribution, and a high degree of graphitization as confirmed by X-ray diffraction and Raman spectroscopy. The material must be free of metal catalysts and silicate inclusions that could catalyze unwanted side reactions or contaminate the final product.

- centrifuge parent material

Centrifugation of the parent material is performed prior to exfoliation to remove fines and amorphous carbon particles that would interfere with electrolyte penetration and electrical contact. The pre-treated material is mixed with water at a concentration of 5 mg/mL and centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 minutes. The supernatant, containing the smaller, non-exfoliable particles, is discarded, and the sediment is collected as the feedstock for the exfoliation process. This step ensures that only particles with optimal size and structure are subjected to electrochemical treatment.

- introduce electrochemical exfoliation method

The electrochemical exfoliation method begins with the preparation of a compressed bed of parent material within a porous chamber, followed by the insertion of a working electrode and the submersion of the chamber in an electrolyte solution. A controlled voltage is applied between the working and counter electrodes, inducing ion intercalation and layer separation. The process is monitored for current flow and duration, with the reaction terminated after 2 to 4 hours. The resulting mixture is then subjected to centrifugation and washing to isolate the exfoliated nanosheets.

- describe feed preparation

Feed preparation involves the selection of needle coke fines, washing with dichloromethane and water, acid treatment with nitric acid, and centrifugation to remove fines. The resulting material is dried and compacted into a dialysis membrane to form a cohesive, conductive bed for exfoliation. The feed must be homogeneous, with a particle size distribution between 5 and 20 micrometers, and a moisture content below 1%.

- pretreat parent material

Parent material is pretreated by washing with dichloromethane and deionized water, followed by immersion in 6 M nitric acid at 120°C for four hours. The acid treatment enhances surface hydrophilicity and removes impurities. After treatment, the material is rinsed with deionized water until neutral pH is achieved and dried overnight at 100°C.

- exfoliate parent material

The pretreated parent material is compacted into a porous chamber and subjected to a voltage of 12 volts for 2 hours in a 0.1 M ammonium sulfate electrolyte. The compression applied to the chamber ensures continuous electrical contact, allowing for uniform exfoliation. The resulting mixture contains exfoliated nanosheets, unreacted material, and non-graphitic debris.

- separate exfoliated material

The exfoliated material is separated from the reaction mixture through a two-step centrifugation process. First, the mixture is centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 10 minutes to sediment unreacted parent material. The supernatant is then centrifuged at 5000 rpm for 20 minutes to pellet non-graphitic debris, leaving the nanosheets in suspension.

- wash and separate exfoliated material

The suspended nanosheets are washed with deionized water and ethanol to remove residual electrolyte and soluble impurities. The washing is performed by repeated centrifugation and decantation until the supernatant is clear and free of ionic contaminants. The final product is collected as a wet cake and dried under vacuum.

- recycle unexfoliated particulates

Unexfoliated particulates recovered from the first centrifugation step are returned to the pretreatment stage and reprocessed through the exfoliation cycle. This recycling improves overall yield and reduces feedstock waste, enabling a closed-loop production system.

- post-treat exfoliated material

Post-treatment of the exfoliated material includes thermal annealing at temperatures up to 1100°C under argon to remove residual oxygen and improve crystallinity. The material may also be dispersed in solvents, filtered into buckypapers, or incorporated into composite matrices for end-use applications.

- disperse exfoliated material

The exfoliated nanosheets are dispersed in water or ethanol using bath sonication to prevent re-agglomeration. The dispersion is stable for extended periods and can be used for coating, printing, or casting into films and electrodes.

- dry exfoliated material

The dispersed nanosheets are dried under vacuum at 60°C for 24 hours to produce a free-flowing powder. The drying process preserves the structural integrity of the sheets and prevents irreversible aggregation.

- separate components by size

Components are separated by size through multi-stage centrifugation, with each stage targeting a specific particle size range. Lower speed centrifugation removes large unreacted particles, while higher speed centrifugation isolates nanosheets from smaller amorphous debris.

- describe multi-stage centrifuging

Multi-stage centrifuging involves sequential centrifugation steps at increasing speeds to isolate components based on size and density. The first step removes unreacted parent material at 2000 rpm, the second step removes non-graphitic debris at 5000 rpm, and optional third steps may be employed to further purify the nanosheets. Each step is followed by decantation and washing to ensure purity.

- anneal electrochemically exfoliated graphene

Electrochemically exfoliated graphene is annealed in an inert atmosphere at temperatures ranging from 500°C to 1100°C to enhance its electrical conductivity and structural order. Annealing removes residual oxygen, heals lattice defects, and improves inter-sheet connectivity, resulting in conductivity values exceeding 400 S/m at 1100°C.

- describe reactor for electrochemical exfoliation

The reactor for electrochemical exfoliation comprises a sealed container housing a porous chamber filled with particulate parent material, a working electrode inserted into the material, a counter electrode positioned externally, a pressure source applying uniform compression, and an electrolyte solution that permeates the bed. The system is equipped with a power supply capable of delivering a controlled voltage between 8 and 15 volts.

- describe container and porous chamber

The container is constructed from chemically inert, non-conductive material such as polytetrafluoroethylene or polypropylene and is designed to withstand electrochemical conditions. The porous chamber is a cylindrical sleeve made of cellulose dialysis membrane with pore sizes of 10 to 50 nanometers, allowing electrolyte ingress while retaining the parent material.

- describe working electrode and counter electrodes

The working electrode is a platinum wire inserted axially into the center of the compressed bed, ensuring direct electrical contact with the material. The counter electrode is a copper mesh wrapped around the exterior of the porous chamber, providing uniform current distribution and minimizing localized overpotential.

- describe electrolyte solution

The electrolyte solution is an aqueous 0.1 M ammonium sulfate solution, chosen for its high ionic conductivity, low corrosivity, and compatibility with the carbonaceous material. Other suitable electrolytes include ammonium chloride, sodium sulfate, and potassium nitrate.

- describe pressure source

The pressure source is a mechanical weight or spring-loaded fixture that applies a uniform compressive force of 0.5 to 1.5 MPa to the porous chamber, ensuring tight packing of the particulate bed while permitting electrolyte diffusion.

- describe porous chamber orientation

The porous chamber is oriented vertically within the container, with the working electrode inserted from the top and the counter electrode surrounding the sides. This orientation ensures uniform pressure distribution and optimal electrolyte flow.

- describe porous chamber configuration

The porous chamber is configured as a cylindrical sleeve with a length of 16 cm and a diameter of 2.5 cm, sealed at both ends with clips to retain the material. The chamber is designed to be replaceable and compatible with multiple reactor configurations.

- describe pore size selection

Pore size is selected to be between 10 and 50 nanometers to allow electrolyte penetration while retaining parent material particles larger than 1 micrometer. Smaller pores restrict flow, while larger pores permit particle loss.

- describe materials for porous chamber

The porous chamber is constructed from regenerated cellulose dialysis membrane, which is chemically inert, mechanically robust, and permeable to aqueous electrolytes. Alternative materials include polyvinylidene fluoride and polyethersulfone membranes with similar pore characteristics.

- describe support structure

The support structure is a rigid frame that holds the porous chamber in place within the container and transmits the compressive force from the pressure source. It is constructed from stainless steel or high-density polyethylene to resist corrosion and deformation.

- describe seals

Seals are made from silicone or fluorocarbon rubber and are positioned at the interface between the chamber and the container to prevent electrolyte leakage. The seals are designed to withstand repeated compression cycles and prolonged exposure to aqueous solutions.

- describe bed of parent material

## EXAMPLES

- provide examples of certain aspects of some embodiments

### Example 1

- pretreat needle coke fines

Needle coke fines (CK-1) were washed with dichloromethane and deionized water, then treated with 6 M nitric acid at 120°C for four hours. The material was rinsed to neutrality and dried at 100°C overnight.

- treat with nitric acid

The acid treatment introduced mild surface oxidation and removed residual hydrocarbons and metal impurities, enhancing the hydrophilicity of the carbon surface.

- centrifuge and separate particles

The pretreated material was suspended in water at 5 mg/mL and centrifuged at 4000 rpm for 10 minutes. The supernatant was discarded, and the sediment was collected as CK-1b.

- electrochemically exfoliate

The CK-1b material was compacted into a dialysis membrane and subjected to electrochemical exfoliation at 12 V for 2 hours in 0.1 M ammonium sulfate. The resulting suspension was designated CK-1c.

- wash and separate components

The CK-1c suspension was centrifuged at 2000 rpm for 10 minutes to remove unreacted material, followed by centrifugation at 5000 rpm for 20 minutes to isolate the nanosheets. The final product was designated CK-1d.

- analyze using SEM

Field emission scanning electron microscopy revealed the presence of thin, sheet-like structures with lateral dimensions of 1–5 µm and no visible amorphous debris.

- analyze using AFM

Atomic force microscopy confirmed a thickness of 2–4 nm for the majority of sheets, consistent with few-layer graphene.

- analyze using TEM

Transmission electron microscopy showed a hexagonal lattice with no grain boundaries, confirming the crystalline nature of the exfoliated material.

- perform Raman spectroscopy

Raman spectroscopy revealed a strong G-band at 1580 cm⁻¹ and a distinct 2D peak at 2700 cm⁻¹, with an I_D/I_G ratio of 0.25, indicating low defect density and high graphitic content.

### Example 2

- measure electrical conductivity of pre-treated coke samples

Electrical conductivity of CK-1a, CK-2a, and CK-3a was measured using a four-point probe on vacuum-filtered buckypapers. Values were 0.07, 0.23, and 0.38 S/m, respectively.

### Example 3

- anneal dry graphene powder

CK-1d powder was annealed at 500°C, 900°C, and 1100°C for 12 hours under argon. Conductivity increased from 56.9 S/m to 250 S/m, 345 S/m, and 474 S/m, respectively.

- test for changes in conductivity

Conductivity was measured using a four-point probe on buckypapers prepared from the annealed samples. Results showed a clear correlation between temperature and conductivity.

- analyze results of electrical conductivity test

The increase in conductivity was attributed to the removal of residual oxygen and improved inter-sheet connectivity, rather than reduction of oxygen groups, as confirmed by XPS and TGA.

- observe correlation between annealing temperature and conductivity

A logarithmic relationship was observed between annealing temperature and conductivity, with the most significant gains occurring between 500°C and 900°C. At 1100°C, conductivity plateaued, indicating saturation of structural improvement.
