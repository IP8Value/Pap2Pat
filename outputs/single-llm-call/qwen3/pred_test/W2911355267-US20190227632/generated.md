# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- define technical field

The present invention relates to wearable haptic devices designed to deliver controlled compressive and shear forces to the fingertip for the purpose of modulating and studying human perception of relative digit position. Specifically, the invention encompasses a compact, wearable fingertip interface capable of independently applying normal (compressive) and tangential (shear) forces with high precision and repeatability, enabling the investigation of sensorimotor integration in the absence of voluntary muscle activation. The device is particularly suited for use in neuroscience research, clinical assessment of somatosensory function, and the development of advanced human-machine interfaces that require accurate replication of natural tactile and proprioceptive feedback during object manipulation. The system operates without requiring the subject to exert active force, thereby isolating the contribution of cutaneous afferent signals to spatial perception, and is adaptable for integration into virtual reality, prosthetic control, and rehabilitation systems where fidelity of haptic feedback is critical.

## BACKGROUND

- limitations of current devices

Existing devices for delivering haptic feedback to the fingertips are primarily designed for force display in virtual environments or for tactile stimulation in neurophysiological studies, but they suffer from critical limitations in their ability to decouple compressive and shear force components with sufficient fidelity and independence. Many systems rely on rigid mechanical linkages or pneumatic actuators that introduce unintended coupling between normal and tangential force directions, resulting in non-physiological skin deformation patterns that confound perceptual interpretation. Others employ surface vibration or pressure arrays that lack the capacity to generate sustained, directional shear forces necessary to mimic the mechanical deformation of the skin during precision grip tasks. Furthermore, current platforms are often bulky, require external support structures, or necessitate subject force production to generate feedback, thereby introducing confounding proprioceptive and efferent signals that obscure the contribution of purely cutaneous input. These limitations prevent accurate isolation of tactile cues in perceptual experiments and hinder the development of reliable biomimetic interfaces for prosthetics or teleoperation. No existing device provides a wearable, self-contained, motor-driven mechanism capable of independently modulating compressive and shear forces on the fingertip pad with real-time force feedback, angular position monitoring, and minimal mechanical interference with natural hand posture.

## SUMMARY

- introduce device for cutaneous sensations

The invention introduces a wearable fingertip device engineered to deliver precise, independently controllable compressive and shear forces to the fingertip skin surface, enabling the systematic investigation of how tactile inputs influence the perception of relative digit position without requiring voluntary motor effort. The device is designed to replicate the mechanical deformation of the fingertip pad that occurs during natural grasping, thereby providing a physiologically relevant stimulus for studying somatosensory integration.

- describe device components

The device comprises two primary assemblies: an upper assembly and a lower assembly, each housing distinct mechanical and electronic subsystems. The upper assembly includes a platform that applies compressive force to the fingertip, a shear plate that generates tangential skin deformation, and a central slot that allows for relative motion between the two force components. The lower assembly provides structural support, encloses the actuation motors, and integrates sensors for real-time monitoring of force and angular displacement. Both assemblies are housed within a lightweight, ergonomic casing designed for secure attachment to the fingertip via a flexible thimble-like interface.

- describe compressive force application

Compressive force is applied through a motor-driven pinion and rack mechanism that translates rotational motion into linear displacement perpendicular to the fingertip surface. A compressive motor, coupled to a precision pinion gear, engages with dual compressive racks mounted on either side of the central slot, ensuring symmetrical and stable vertical displacement of the upper platform. This mechanism allows for controlled indentation of the fingertip pad with forces ranging from 0.5 N to 5.0 N, adjustable in increments of 0.1 N, with a positional resolution of less than 10 micrometers.

- describe shear force application

Shear force is generated by a separate motor-driven system that translates the shear plate horizontally across the fingertip surface while maintaining constant normal contact. The shear plate is constrained to move only in the tangential plane, preventing any vertical displacement during shear application. A second motor, coupled to a pinion and rack configuration, drives the shear plate with a range of motion of up to 15 mm and a force capability of up to 3.5 N in either direction, replicating the skin stretch experienced during object manipulation.

- describe wearable fingertip device

The device is designed as a lightweight, low-profile wearable unit that conforms to the natural curvature of the distal phalanx. It is secured to the fingertip using a flexible, breathable thimble interface that distributes contact pressure evenly and minimizes slippage. The entire assembly weighs less than 80 grams and is powered by a rechargeable microbattery, enabling untethered operation for extended experimental sessions. The device is compatible with standard motion capture systems and can be used on either hand without modification.

- describe advantages of device

The primary advantage of the device is its ability to independently and simultaneously control compressive and shear forces without introducing cross-coupling artifacts, thereby enabling the isolation of tactile inputs from proprioceptive and efferent signals. This capability allows for the first time the direct quantification of how skin deformation alone contributes to the perception of fingertip distance. Additionally, the device provides real-time feedback of applied force and angular position, enabling closed-loop control and calibration for each subject. Its compact form factor and minimal invasiveness make it suitable for prolonged use in clinical and research settings.

- introduce first aspect of device

The first aspect of the invention is a wearable fingertip device configured to apply compressive force to the fingertip via a motorized linear actuation system.

- describe first aspect components

This aspect includes an upper platform that contacts the fingertip, a compressive rack system mounted on either side of a central slot, a compressive pinion gear driven by a stepper motor, and an angular sensor that measures the rotational position of the motor shaft. The upper platform is connected to the compressive racks via rigid linkages that ensure vertical motion without lateral deviation.

- describe first aspect operation

During operation, the compressive motor rotates the pinion gear, which engages with the compressive racks to move the upper platform vertically toward or away from the fingertip. The angular sensor provides continuous feedback to a control unit, which adjusts motor current to maintain a target compressive force as measured by a force sensor embedded in the upper platform. This system enables precise, repeatable indentation of the fingertip skin without requiring subject effort.

- introduce second aspect of device

The second aspect of the invention is a wearable fingertip device configured to apply shear force to the fingertip via a motorized horizontal displacement mechanism.

- describe second aspect components

This aspect includes a shear plate positioned beneath the fingertip, front and rear retaining members that constrain the plate to horizontal motion only, a shear motor coupled to a pinion gear, and a shear rack connected to the shear plate. An angular sensor is integrated into the shear motor to monitor the angular displacement of the drive shaft.

- describe second aspect operation

The shear motor rotates the pinion gear, which drives the shear rack to translate the shear plate horizontally across the fingertip surface. The front and rear retaining members prevent vertical or rotational movement of the shear plate, ensuring that skin deformation occurs exclusively in the tangential plane. The angular sensor provides feedback to a control system that regulates shear force magnitude and direction with millisecond precision.

- introduce third aspect of device

The third aspect of the invention is a wearable fingertip device that integrates both compressive and shear force delivery systems into a single unified structure.

- describe third aspect components

This aspect combines the components of the first and second aspects into a single housing, including a central slot that allows independent vertical and horizontal motion of the upper platform and shear plate, respectively. The device further includes an electronics housing that contains the control unit, battery, and communication module, and a force sensor embedded in the upper platform to measure normal force.

- describe third aspect operation

In operation, the compressive and shear systems function independently but concurrently. The upper platform applies compressive force while the shear plate simultaneously applies tangential force, with both systems monitored and controlled via a shared microcontroller. The device can be programmed to deliver force profiles that mimic natural grasping dynamics, enabling the study of combined tactile inputs on perceptual bias and spatial estimation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce embodiments

The invention may be embodied in multiple configurations, each optimized for specific applications in research, clinical diagnostics, or human-machine interaction. The core design principles remain consistent across embodiments: independent actuation of compressive and shear forces, real-time feedback control, and minimal interference with natural hand posture.

- define terms

For the purposes of this disclosure, “compressive force” refers to a normal force applied perpendicular to the surface of the fingertip, resulting in indentation of the skin and deformation of mechanoreceptors. “Shear force” refers to a tangential force applied parallel to the fingertip surface, resulting in skin stretch and displacement of superficial layers. “Fingertip support” refers to the interface that secures the device to the distal phalanx without restricting joint motion. “Angular sensor” refers to a rotary encoder that measures the angular position of a motor shaft with sub-degree resolution.

- describe relative terms

Relative terms such as “upper,” “lower,” “front,” “rear,” “vertical,” and “horizontal” are used for descriptive convenience and refer to the orientation of the device when worn on the right hand in a neutral posture, with the fingertip oriented upward and the palm facing downward.

- explain terminology

The term “independently controllable” means that the magnitude and direction of compressive and shear forces can be adjusted without affecting each other, even when applied simultaneously. The term “wearable” indicates that the device is designed to be donned and removed by the user without requiring external mounting or fixed support structures.

- provide device overview

The device is a self-contained, motorized interface that delivers precise tactile stimuli to the fingertip through two orthogonal force channels. It is composed of an upper assembly responsible for compressive force delivery, a lower assembly housing the actuation and sensing systems, and an electronics module that coordinates force profiles and records real-time data.

- describe wearable fingertip device

The wearable fingertip device is constructed from lightweight polymers and aluminum alloys to minimize weight while maintaining structural rigidity. It is designed to fit the distal phalanx of any adult human finger, with interchangeable thimble interfaces available for different finger sizes. The device is worn during experimental sessions without requiring the subject to exert voluntary force, thereby isolating cutaneous feedback as the sole source of sensory information.

- outline device features

Key features include dual independent motor systems, real-time force and angular feedback, a central slot enabling uncoupled motion, a low-friction shear plate interface, and a compact electronics housing that supports wireless communication and battery operation for up to four hours.

- discuss device applications

Applications include neuroscientific investigations of somatosensory integration, clinical assessment of tactile acuity in neurological disorders, development of haptic feedback systems for prosthetic limbs, training interfaces for robotic teleoperation, and virtual reality environments requiring realistic fingertip sensation.

Figures 1 through 4 illustrate the structural components and operational principles of the device, including exploded views of the upper and lower assemblies, cross-sectional schematics of the force delivery mechanisms, and diagrams of the control architecture.

- describe wearable fingertip device 10

Wearable fingertip device 10 comprises an upper assembly 12 and a lower assembly 14, joined by a central slot 22 that permits independent vertical and horizontal motion. The device is secured to the fingertip via a flexible fingertip support 34 that conforms to the natural curvature of the distal phalanx.

- outline upper assembly 12

Upper assembly 12 includes an upper platform 16 that contacts the fingertip, compressive racks connected to the platform, and a central slot 22 that aligns with the lower assembly. The upper platform is designed with a contoured surface to match the fingertip geometry and is embedded with a force sensor to measure normal contact pressure.

- describe upper platform 16

Upper platform 16 is fabricated from a biocompatible polymer with a textured surface to enhance grip and prevent slippage. It is connected to the compressive racks via rigid linkages that ensure pure vertical translation without lateral or rotational deviation during force application.

- explain compressive force provision

Compressive force is provided by a compressive motor 28 that drives a pinion gear engaging with dual compressive racks. As the motor rotates, the racks translate linearly, moving the upper platform toward or away from the fingertip. The force magnitude is regulated by closed-loop control based on feedback from the embedded force sensor.

- introduce shear plate 20

Shear plate 20 is a flat, low-friction surface positioned beneath the fingertip within the central slot 22. It is designed to slide horizontally across the fingertip pad, generating skin stretch without altering the normal force.

- describe shear force provision

Shear force is provided by a shear motor and pinion system that drives the shear plate along a linear track constrained by front and rear retaining members. The shear plate moves in a single plane parallel to the fingertip surface, ensuring that skin deformation is purely tangential.

- outline central slot 22

Central slot 22 is a longitudinal aperture in the lower assembly that accommodates the shear plate and allows the upper platform to move vertically without interference. The slot is precisely machined to minimize play and ensure smooth, unconstrained motion of both components.

- describe compressive rack apertures

Compressive rack apertures are precision-machined slots in the lower assembly that guide the compressive racks, ensuring linear motion and preventing lateral deviation. These apertures are lined with low-friction bearings to reduce mechanical resistance.

- introduce compressive pinion

Compressive pinion is a small, high-precision gear attached to the output shaft of the compressive motor. It engages with the compressive racks to convert rotational motion into linear displacement of the upper platform.

- describe compressive motor 28

Compressive motor 28 is a brushless DC stepper motor with integrated encoder feedback. It is capable of generating torque sufficient to apply up to 5.0 N of compressive force at a resolution of 0.1 N and a response time of less than 20 milliseconds.

- outline angular sensor 30

Angular sensor 30 is a high-resolution rotary encoder mounted directly on the shaft of the compressive motor. It provides real-time feedback on motor rotation, enabling precise control of platform position and force application.

- describe finger support 32

Finger support 32 is a rigid, adjustable frame that stabilizes the proximal phalanx and wrist, minimizing movement of the hand during testing. It is detachable and compatible with multiple hand sizes.

- introduce fingertip support 34

Fingertip support 34 is a flexible, elastic thimble made from medical-grade silicone that conforms to the shape of the distal phalanx. It is securely fastened to the upper platform and ensures consistent contact without restricting blood flow or joint mobility.

- outline electronics housing 36

Electronics housing 36 is a sealed compartment located on the dorsal side of the device, containing the microcontroller, battery, wireless transceiver, and signal conditioning circuits. It is designed for easy access and replacement.

- describe lower assembly 14

Lower assembly 14 provides the structural foundation for the device, housing the shear motor, shear rack, compressive motor, and sensor systems. It is constructed from a rigid polymer composite to maintain alignment under load.

- outline lower base 18

Lower base 18 is the primary structural member of the lower assembly, providing mounting surfaces for the motors, sensors, and retaining members. It is designed with a low center of gravity to enhance stability during use.

- describe central slot 52

Central slot 52 is the longitudinal channel in the lower base that allows the shear plate to move horizontally and the upper platform to move vertically. Its dimensions are optimized to minimize friction and mechanical play.

- introduce front and rear sidewalls 54a, 54b

Front and rear sidewalls 54a and 54b are vertical barriers that extend from the lower base to constrain the shear plate within the horizontal plane. They are machined with smooth, polished surfaces to reduce friction during sliding motion.

- describe front and rear retaining members 56a, 56b

Front and rear retaining members 56a and 56b are adjustable clamps that secure the shear plate against vertical and rotational displacement. They are equipped with low-friction bearings to allow smooth horizontal translation.

- outline shear plate 20 components

Shear plate 20 is composed of a flat, low-friction polymer surface with a textured bottom for engagement with the shear rack. It is mounted on linear bearings that slide along rails within the central slot.

- describe shear plate position

Shear plate 20 is positioned directly beneath the fingertip when the device is worn, such that its surface is in direct contact with the skin of the distal phalanx. Its position is calibrated to align with the center of the fingertip pad.

- describe shear plate constraints

Shear plate 20 is constrained to move only in the horizontal plane by the front and rear retaining members and the sidewalls. These constraints eliminate any vertical or rotational degrees of freedom, ensuring that shear force is applied purely tangentially.

- introduce shear motor and pinion

Shear motor and pinion form the actuation system for the shear plate. The shear motor is a high-torque, low-inertia stepper motor that drives the pinion, which in turn engages with the shear rack to produce horizontal displacement.

- describe shear motor function

Shear motor function is to generate controlled tangential force on the fingertip by translating the shear plate. It operates independently of the compressive system and is capable of delivering forces up to 3.5 N in either direction with millisecond response.

- introduce angular sensor

An angular sensor is integrated into the shear motor shaft to monitor rotational displacement and provide closed-loop feedback for force control.

- describe angular sensor function

The angular sensor function is to measure the precise angular position of the shear motor shaft, enabling the control system to calculate the exact displacement of the shear plate and regulate shear force magnitude and direction with high fidelity.

- introduce compressive racks

Compressive racks are linear gear segments mounted on either side of the central slot, connected to the upper platform. They translate the rotational motion of the compressive pinion into vertical displacement.

- describe compressive rack function

The compressive rack function is to convert the rotational output of the compressive motor into linear motion of the upper platform, ensuring symmetrical and stable indentation of the fingertip without tilting or lateral drift.

- introduce contact panel and force sensor

The contact panel is the surface of the upper platform that interfaces with the fingertip, and the force sensor is a thin-film piezoresistive element embedded within the panel.

- describe contact panel and force sensor function

The contact panel and force sensor function together to measure the normal force applied to the fingertip in real time. The sensor output is used by the control system to maintain a target force level, compensating for variations in skin compliance and device alignment.

- describe upper platform connection

The upper platform is rigidly connected to the compressive racks via stainless steel linkages that ensure pure vertical motion. These linkages are designed to eliminate torsional compliance and maintain alignment under load.

- describe shear plate connection

The shear plate is connected to the shear rack via a low-friction coupling that allows for smooth horizontal translation while preventing any vertical or rotational movement.

- describe compressive motor function

The compressive motor function is to generate precise vertical displacement of the upper platform by converting electrical input into rotational torque, which is then translated into linear motion via the pinion and rack system.

- describe shear motor function

The shear motor function is to generate controlled horizontal displacement of the shear plate by converting electrical input into rotational motion, which is transmitted via the pinion and rack to produce tangential skin deformation.

- describe normal force application

Normal force is applied by the upper platform pressing vertically against the fingertip, resulting in indentation of the skin and activation of mechanoreceptors such as Merkel cells and Ruffini endings.

- describe shear force application

Shear force is applied by the shear plate sliding horizontally across the fingertip skin, resulting in stretching of the epidermis and activation of Pacinian corpuscles and Meissner’s corpuscles.

- describe concurrent force application

Concurrent force application involves the simultaneous delivery of compressive and shear forces, enabling the replication of natural tactile stimuli experienced during precision grip tasks, such as holding a cup or turning a doorknob.

Haptic feedback is applied to modulate the subject’s perception of fingertip distance by delivering force combinations that mimic the tactile cues of opposing digit forces, enabling the quantification of perceptual bias in the absence of vision or proprioceptive feedback.
