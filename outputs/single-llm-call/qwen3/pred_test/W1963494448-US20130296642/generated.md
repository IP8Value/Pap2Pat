## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- The field of invention relates to methods and compositions for assessing the risk of incisional hernia formation and recurrence in patients undergoing abdominal surgical procedures. This invention specifically addresses the need for predictive biomarkers that can identify individuals with an inherent predisposition to defective wound healing, thereby enabling personalized surgical planning and intervention strategies. Current clinical practice relies heavily on demographic and procedural risk factors such as obesity, smoking, wound infection, and prior abdominal surgery, none of which provide sufficient sensitivity or specificity to reliably predict which patients will develop incisional hernias following laparotomy or laparoscopic intervention. Despite advances in prosthetic mesh technology and surgical technique, recurrence rates remain unacceptably high, with up to 50 percent of patients experiencing recurrent herniation, resulting in significant morbidity, prolonged hospitalization, and increased healthcare expenditures. The underlying biological mechanisms responsible for this failure of tissue integrity remain poorly understood, and no molecular diagnostic tools exist to evaluate a patient’s intrinsic capacity for connective tissue repair prior to surgery.

- The related art in this field has long focused on structural and mechanical factors influencing hernia formation, including the use of surgical mesh, suture techniques, and incision closure methods. While these approaches have improved outcomes, they do not address the fundamental biological variability among patients in their ability to regenerate and remodel extracellular matrix components following tissue injury. Previous studies have suggested that alterations in collagen composition, particularly a reduced ratio of type I to type III collagen, may be associated with hernia development. However, these findings have been inconsistent across tissue types and have not been validated as predictive biomarkers in prospective cohorts. Moreover, prior investigations have typically analyzed tissue harvested from the hernia defect itself, which introduces confounding variables such as chronic inflammation, fibrosis, and mechanical stress that obscure the identification of pre-existing molecular signatures. No prior work has systematically evaluated genome-wide gene expression profiles in histologically normal skin and fascia obtained from patients prior to surgical incision, nor has any study identified a consistent set of molecular markers capable of distinguishing patients destined to develop recurrent incisional hernias from those who heal without complication.

- The limitations of current incisional hernia repair strategies stem from their reactive nature. Surgeons are unable to identify high-risk patients before the initial procedure, and thus cannot tailor repair techniques, mesh selection, or postoperative care to mitigate inherent biological vulnerabilities. As a result, many patients undergo multiple surgeries with escalating risks of complications, chronic pain, and functional impairment. The absence of a reliable preoperative diagnostic tool means that all patients are treated with a one-size-fits-all approach, regardless of their underlying molecular propensity for tissue failure. This inefficiency not only compromises patient outcomes but also imposes a substantial economic burden on the healthcare system due to repeated interventions, prolonged recovery times, and management of chronic complications.

- Key risk factors for incisional hernia formation include advanced age, male sex, obesity, diabetes, smoking, wound infection, and prior abdominal surgery. However, these factors are neither necessary nor sufficient to predict hernia development, as many patients with multiple risk factors heal without complication, while others with minimal risk factors develop recurrent hernias. This clinical variability strongly suggests the presence of an underlying genetic or epigenetic predisposition that modulates the wound healing response. There is a critical and unmet need for a method that can detect this predisposition prior to surgery, allowing clinicians to stratify patients into risk categories and implement preventive measures such as reinforced mesh placement, minimally invasive techniques, or pharmacological modulation of tissue remodeling pathways.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The invention introduces a novel method for determining an individual’s risk level for developing an incisional hernia following abdominal surgery by measuring the expression levels of specific biomarker genes in surgically obtained tissue samples prior to incision. The method involves obtaining a tissue sample from the skin and/or fascia of a patient, quantifying the expression levels of a defined panel of genes including GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10, comparing these expression levels to a predetermined control value derived from a population of patients who healed without hernia formation, and determining the patient’s risk level based on the deviation of the measured expression from the control. This approach enables preoperative identification of patients with a molecular signature indicative of impaired extracellular matrix remodeling and defective wound healing, thereby facilitating personalized surgical planning.

- The method further includes the step of treating the patient based on the determined risk level, wherein high-risk patients are managed with enhanced surgical interventions such as placement of a reinforced synthetic or biologic mesh, use of laparoscopic techniques with precise fascial closure, or administration of adjunctive therapies designed to modulate collagen synthesis or inflammatory response. The specific genes used in the method include GREM1, which is downregulated in high-risk patients, and COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10, which are upregulated in high-risk patients relative to controls. The tissue samples used are obtained from non-herniated skin and fascia, collected prior to the initiation of the surgical procedure, ensuring that the measured gene expression reflects the patient’s intrinsic biological state rather than secondary pathological changes.

- The measurement of gene expression is performed using quantitative nucleic acid amplification techniques, including real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), reverse transcription PCR, or microarray-based hybridization assays. The control sample is defined as the median or mean expression level of the biomarker genes in a reference population of patients who underwent abdominal surgery and did not develop an incisional hernia within a minimum of two years postoperatively. The determination of risk level is based on a statistically validated threshold derived from differential expression analysis, where a combination of GREM1 downregulation and elevated COL1A2/COL3A1 or COL3A1/IL10 ratios is indicative of high risk.

- The invention further introduces a diagnostic method for identifying patients at risk for incisional hernia formation by measuring the expression levels of the same biomarker genes in a tissue sample, comparing the expression profile to a reference database of known risk profiles, and generating a risk classification output. The biomarkers used in this diagnostic method are GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10, either individually or in combination as a signature panel. The tissue samples are derived from skin and fascia obtained during preoperative preparation for abdominal surgery, and the measurement is performed using standardized molecular assays including microarray hybridization, qPCR, or PCR array platforms.

- The invention also introduces a diagnostic product comprising a biochip array containing immobilized nucleic acid probes complementary to the mRNA sequences of GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10, configured to detect and quantify expression levels in a single assay. The biochip is integrated into a kit that includes reagents for RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, amplification, and hybridization, along with instructions for correlating the resulting expression profile with a risk classification. The kit further includes a substrate with adsorbent molecules capable of binding the biomarker proteins, enabling detection via immunohistochemistry or immunoassay. The product may also include pre-fractionation spin columns for purifying RNA from small tissue biopsies, purified biomarker proteins bound to a microarray for calibration, and software for automated analysis of gene expression data and risk stratification.

### Methods

- The invention provides methods for determining the risk level of incisional hernia formation in a subject by measuring the expression levels of a defined set of biomarker genes in a tissue sample obtained prior to surgical incision. Gene expression profiles are generated by extracting RNA from skin and fascia specimens, converting the RNA to complementary DNA, and quantifying transcript abundance using high-throughput platforms such as microarrays or PCR arrays. Microarray experiments are conducted using whole-genome expression BeadChips, and differentially expressed genes are identified through statistical analysis of normalized intensity values, applying empirical Bayes methods to account for small sample sizes and adjusting for multiple comparisons using the false discovery rate. GREM1 expression is found to be significantly lower in patients who subsequently develop recurrent incisional hernias compared to controls, while COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10 are consistently upregulated. The method involves comparing the expression level of each biomarker in the patient sample to a control value derived from a reference population of patients who healed without hernia formation. A risk level is assigned based on the magnitude and direction of deviation from the control, with a combination of low GREM1 and high COL3A1 expression being predictive of high risk. Quantitative assessment includes calculation of fold change, while qualitative assessment involves detection of presence or absence of expression above a defined threshold. Nucleic acid detection assays include reverse transcription followed by real-time PCR, digital PCR, or hybridization-based methods. Array or chip hybridization-based methods involve labeling cDNA with fluorescent or biotin tags and hybridizing to complementary probes immobilized on a solid support. A “control” is defined as a reference expression value established from a cohort of patients who did not develop incisional hernia following abdominal surgery. The control value is determined by averaging expression levels across multiple individuals and is used as a baseline for comparison. Tissue samples are obtained from non-herniated regions of skin and fascia, preferably from the site of trocar insertion or planned incision, and are stabilized immediately in RNA-preserving reagents. GREM1 expression below a threshold of 0.5-fold change relative to control is associated with increased risk, while elevated expression of COL1A2, COL3A1, or IL10 above 1.5-fold change is similarly indicative of high risk. Differential expression refers to statistically significant differences in transcript abundance between patient groups, with upregulation indicating increased expression and downregulation indicating decreased expression. Treatment options include the use of a reinforced surgical mesh, fixation of the mesh using laparoscopic suturing or tacks, and application of biological adhesives or growth factor therapies to enhance tissue integration. Laparoscopic surgery is preferred for high-risk patients to minimize tissue trauma and incision size, and the mesh is affixed to the abdominal wall using a combination of mechanical fixation and biocompatible sealants to ensure long-term stability.

### Kits

- The invention provides kits for the prognosis of incisional hernia risk, comprising PCR primers specific for GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10, along with detailed instructions for use that correlate measured expression levels with risk classification. The kit further includes a DNA microarray containing immobilized complementary sequences for the biomarker genes, enabling multiplexed detection of expression levels in a single assay. Reagents and enzymes necessary for amplifying or isolating nucleic acids, including reverse transcriptase, DNA polymerase, dNTPs, and buffer systems, are provided in pre-measured aliquots. Additional kits are provided for qualifying risk level using biomarker detection, incorporating a substrate with adsorbent molecules engineered to bind the biomarker proteins, along with instructions for detecting bound markers via colorimetric or fluorescent readouts. Kits containing antibodies specific for GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, or IL10, paired with detection reagents such as horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies, are provided for immunohistochemical or ELISA-based analysis. Pre-fractionation spin columns are included for purifying RNA from small tissue biopsies, ensuring high-quality input material. A product comprising purified biomarker proteins bound to a microarray is provided for calibration and standardization of detection assays. A kit for diagnosing risk level using a microarray includes all components necessary for sample preparation, hybridization, washing, and signal detection, along with software for automated data analysis and risk scoring. A biochip array of isolated biomarkers is provided as a ready-to-use diagnostic platform, pre-coated with capture probes and optimized for rapid, point-of-care testing.

## EXAMPLES

### Patient Samples and Tissue Acquisition

- Patient samples were collected under institutional review board approval following informed consent. Skin and fascia tissues were harvested from the site of trocar insertion in patients undergoing laparoscopic incisional hernia repair or laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Approximately one square centimeter of tissue was excised prior to any incision or manipulation of the abdominal cavity, ensuring that the sample reflected pre-existing molecular states rather than surgical trauma. Tissue specimens were immediately divided, with one portion placed in RNALater™ for RNA preservation and the other fixed in 10% buffered formalin for histological analysis.

### RNA Isolation and RNA Amplification

- Total RNA was extracted from skin and fascia tissues using the RNeasy® Lipid Tissue Mini Kit, with on-column DNase digestion to eliminate genomic DNA contamination. RNA was amplified using the WT-Ovation™ Pico RNA Amplification System, which enables robust cDNA synthesis from nanogram quantities of starting material. RNA quality was assessed using the Bioanalyzer 2100, with RNA integrity numbers ranging from 5 to 8, confirming suitability for downstream molecular analysis.

### Immunohistochemistry

- Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections were stained using automated immunohistochemistry with antibodies specific for collagen I, collagen III, and GREM1. Staining intensity was quantified using digital image analysis, revealing reduced GREM1 protein expression and elevated collagen III deposition in tissue from patients who developed recurrent hernias.

### Statistical Analysis of Microarray Data

- Microarray data were analyzed using R software, with genes exhibiting detection p-values greater than 0.01 in more than 50% of samples excluded. Differential expression was assessed using moderated t-statistics with empirical Bayes shrinkage, and significance was defined as a fold change of at least 1.5 and a false discovery rate-adjusted q-value below 0.3. Gene ontology analysis was performed to identify enriched biological processes, revealing significant overrepresentation of terms related to extracellular matrix organization, immune response, and wound healing.

### Demographics

- The study cohort included 33 patients, with 18 diagnosed with recurrent incisional hernia and 15 serving as controls. Demographic data showed no significant differences in age, sex, or body mass index between groups, except for higher rates of diabetes and prior surgery in the hernia group. The subset of 17 patients analyzed by microarray was similarly matched, ensuring that observed gene expression differences were not confounded by demographic variables.

### Gene Ontology Analysis of Differentially Expressed Genes

- Gene ontology analysis of differentially expressed genes in skin tissue identified 53 enriched biological processes, including response to wounding, regulation of immune response, and cell adhesion. Molecular functions were dominated by enzyme inhibitor activity and receptor binding, while cellular components were enriched for extracellular region and plasma membrane. These findings support the hypothesis that altered wound healing pathways underlie hernia formation.

### COL1/COL3 Ratio by Microarray, PCR Array, and Immunohistochemistry

- The COL1A2/COL3A1 ratio was significantly lower in patients with recurrent hernias compared to controls, as confirmed by microarray, PCR array, and immunohistochemistry. The PCR array validated the microarray findings with high concordance (Pearson r = 0.74), and immunohistochemistry revealed increased collagen III staining in the dermis and fascia of high-risk patients. Validation by qPCR confirmed downregulation of GREM1 and upregulation of COL3A1 in the same samples.
