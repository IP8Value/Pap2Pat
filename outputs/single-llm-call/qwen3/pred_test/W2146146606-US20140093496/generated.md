## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The invention relates to engineered polypeptides comprising modified Fc regions derived from immunoglobulin G, particularly human IgG1 and IgG4, that exhibit selectively enhanced binding affinity for the inhibitory Fc gamma receptor IIB (FcγRIIb) while maintaining or reducing binding to the activating Fc gamma receptors, including FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa, and FcγRIa. The invention further relates to methods for producing such polypeptides, methods for their therapeutic use, and pharmaceutical compositions containing them. These polypeptides are designed to modulate immune cell signaling through selective engagement of FcγRIIb, thereby enabling targeted suppression of aberrant immune activation without triggering pro-inflammatory or thrombotic pathways associated with activating Fcγ receptors. The invention encompasses both full-length monoclonal antibodies and Fc fusion proteins wherein the Fc domain has been structurally altered to achieve this unique binding profile, making it applicable to a broad spectrum of immunological, oncological, and autoimmune therapeutic contexts.

- The polypeptides of the invention are characterized by specific amino acid substitutions within the CH2 domain and lower hinge region of the Fc segment, which collectively alter the conformational dynamics of the Fc-FcγRIIb interface to favor high-affinity interaction with FcγRIIb while disfavoring comparable interactions with FcγRIIa and other activating receptors. These modifications are not merely additive mutations but are synergistically arranged to exploit structural disparities between FcγRIIb and its highly homologous counterpart, FcγRIIaR131, thereby achieving unprecedented binding selectivity. The invention further encompasses polynucleotides encoding such modified Fc regions, recombinant expression systems for their production, and methods for purifying and formulating them into stable, therapeutically viable agents. The technical scope includes not only the polypeptide structures themselves but also their functional consequences in biological systems, including suppression of B-cell activation, inhibition of mast cell degranulation, modulation of dendritic cell function, and enhancement of agonistic activity of therapeutic antibodies targeting tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily members.

- The methods of the invention include the determination of binding kinetics between the engineered Fc polypeptides and human Fcγ receptors using surface plasmon resonance, analytical ultracentrifugation, or competitive binding assays, as well as methods for assessing the functional outcomes of FcγRIIb engagement in vitro and in vivo. The invention further encompasses methods for evaluating the pharmacokinetic profile, thermal stability, and aggregation propensity of these polypeptides under pharmaceutical storage conditions, ensuring their suitability for clinical development. The technical field thus spans the molecular design, biochemical characterization, functional validation, and pharmaceutical formulation of a novel class of Fc-engineered molecules that achieve a previously unattainable balance of enhanced inhibitory receptor binding and reduced activating receptor engagement.

## BACKGROUND ART

- Antibodies have become foundational therapeutic agents across a wide array of diseases, including cancer, autoimmune disorders, and infectious conditions, owing to their high specificity, long serum half-life, and capacity to engage immune effector functions through their Fc domains. The Fc region of IgG antibodies mediates interactions with Fc gamma receptors expressed on immune cells, thereby dictating downstream biological outcomes such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, phagocytosis, and immune modulation. While early efforts focused on enhancing binding to activating Fcγ receptors like FcγRIIIa to amplify cytotoxic activity, recent advances have recognized the therapeutic potential of selectively engaging the inhibitory FcγRIIb receptor to dampen pathological immune responses without inducing inflammation or thrombosis.

- Despite the clinical success of conventional monoclonal antibodies, their therapeutic utility is often limited by off-target immune activation, systemic inflammation, and adverse events such as thromboembolism, which are frequently linked to unintended engagement of activating Fcγ receptors, particularly FcγRIIa. The FcγRIIa receptor, especially its R131 allotype, is expressed on platelets and myeloid cells, and its ligation by immune complexes can trigger intracellular signaling cascades leading to platelet aggregation, degranulation, and vascular complications. These risks are exacerbated in patients homozygous for the FcγRIIaR131 allele, which is prevalent in multiple ethnic populations. Consequently, antibodies with enhanced binding to FcγRIIa, even if beneficial for immune activation, carry an inherent risk of inducing life-threatening thrombotic events, thereby limiting their clinical applicability.

- The IgG1 subclass is the most commonly used isotype for therapeutic antibodies due to its robust effector functions, which are mediated primarily through interactions with FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa, and FcγRIIb. Among these, FcγRIIb is the sole inhibitory receptor in the FcγR family and contains an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM) in its cytoplasmic tail. Upon ligand binding, FcγRIIb becomes phosphorylated, recruiting the SH2-domain-containing inositol phosphatase SHIP, which suppresses downstream signaling cascades initiated by activating receptors. This mechanism enables FcγRIIb to act as a critical checkpoint for immune homeostasis, particularly in B cells, dendritic cells, and macrophages, where it constrains proliferation, antigen presentation, and cytokine production.

- The FcγRIIb receptor is structurally homologous to FcγRIIa, particularly in the extracellular domain, with over 90% sequence identity, making selective discrimination between the two receptors an extraordinary challenge in protein engineering. The hinge region and CH2 domain of the Fc fragment are critical for receptor binding, with the conserved N-linked glycan at Asn297 playing a pivotal role in maintaining structural integrity and modulating receptor accessibility. Alterations to this glycan or to surrounding amino acids can profoundly affect binding kinetics, but most known mutations, such as S267E/L328F, enhance binding to both FcγRIIb and FcγRIIaR131, thereby eliminating the desired selectivity. Furthermore, the structural plasticity of the Fc region, particularly the dynamic behavior of the lower hinge and CH2 loops, has been shown to influence receptor specificity, yet prior attempts to exploit this plasticity have failed to yield variants that distinguish FcγRIIb from FcγRIIaR131 with high fidelity.

- The inhibitory function of FcγRIIb extends beyond B-cell regulation to include suppression of dendritic cell maturation, inhibition of mast cell degranulation, and modulation of T-cell activation via co-engagement with agonistic antibodies targeting TNFR superfamily members such as CD40, DR5, and CD137. However, the therapeutic exploitation of FcγRIIb has been hindered by the lack of Fc variants that selectively enhance its binding without concomitantly increasing affinity for FcγRIIa. Existing engineered Fc domains either fail to achieve sufficient selectivity or compromise other critical properties such as serum half-life, thermal stability, or resistance to aggregation. The absence of a clinically viable Fc variant with pure FcγRIIb selectivity has thus impeded the development of safer, more effective immunomodulatory therapies.

- Prior art has demonstrated that FcγRIIb engagement can suppress autoantibody production, inhibit allergic responses mediated by IgE, and promote peripheral tolerance in autoimmune models. Nevertheless, the clinical translation of these findings has been constrained by the risk of FcγRIIa-mediated platelet activation and accelerated clearance of antibodies with enhanced FcγRIIa binding. Moreover, the structural basis for FcγRIIb selectivity has remained elusive, with no crystal structures previously available to reveal the molecular determinants that enable discrimination between FcγRIIb and FcγRIIaR131. This gap in mechanistic understanding has rendered rational design of selective Fc variants largely empirical and inefficient.

- The need for a novel Fc variant with enhanced binding to FcγRIIb and minimal or no increase in binding to FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa, or FcγRIa remains unmet in the art. Existing variants, while capable of increasing FcγRIIb affinity, invariably elevate binding to FcγRIIaR131, thereby introducing unacceptable safety risks. There is therefore a compelling and persistent need for engineered polypeptides that achieve selective FcγRIIb engagement, thereby unlocking the full therapeutic potential of this inhibitory receptor without compromising patient safety or pharmacokinetic performance.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

- A primary problem addressed by the invention is the inability of prior Fc-engineered antibodies to selectively enhance binding to the inhibitory FcγRIIb receptor without concomitantly increasing affinity for the activating FcγRIIa receptor, particularly the R131 allotype. This lack of selectivity has led to unintended platelet activation, thromboembolic events, and accelerated clearance of therapeutic antibodies in patients expressing FcγRIIaR131, thereby limiting the clinical utility of FcγRIIb-targeted therapies. Current approaches that enhance FcγRIIb binding, such as the S267E/L328F substitution, simultaneously increase binding to FcγRIIaR131 to an equal or greater extent, rendering them unsuitable for safe and broad application.

- Another problem is the absence of Fc variants that maintain or reduce binding to other activating Fcγ receptors, including FcγRIIIa and FcγRIa, while enhancing FcγRIIb affinity. Many existing modifications that improve FcγRIIb binding inadvertently diminish binding to FcγRIIIa, thereby compromising antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity in contexts where dual modulation is desired. Additionally, prior variants often exhibit reduced thermal stability, increased aggregation propensity, or compromised FcRn binding, leading to shortened serum half-life and poor manufacturability, which are critical barriers to pharmaceutical development.

- A further problem is the lack of structural insight into the molecular basis of FcγRIIb selectivity, which has hindered rational design of improved variants. Without a clear understanding of how the Fc region distinguishes between FcγRIIb and FcγRIIaR131, efforts to optimize binding have relied on random mutagenesis and screening, which are inefficient and unlikely to yield high-selectivity variants. The invention solves these problems by identifying novel amino acid substitutions that induce unique conformational changes in the Fc domain, enabling precise discrimination between FcγRIIb and FcγRIIaR131 through structural mechanisms not previously described.

### Means for Solving the Problems

- The invention provides engineered polypeptides comprising an Fc region derived from human IgG1 or IgG4, wherein the Fc region contains one or more amino acid substitutions at positions corresponding to Pro238, Glu233, Gly237, His268, Pro271, and Ala330 according to the EU numbering system, such that the polypeptide exhibits enhanced binding affinity for FcγRIIb while maintaining or reducing binding to FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa, and FcγRIa. These substitutions are selected to induce structural rearrangements in the CH2 domain that create novel interactions with FcγRIIb, including a hydrogen bond between Gly237 and Tyr160 of FcγRIIb and a salt bridge between Asp270 and Arg131 of FcγRIIb, which are not possible with FcγRIIa due to sequence differences at these positions.

- The polypeptide of the invention is characterized by a dissociation constant ratio for FcγRIIb to FcγRIIaR131 of at least 10:1, preferably at least 50:1, and more preferably at least 200:1, as determined by surface plasmon resonance, while maintaining a dissociation constant for FcγRIIaR131 that is less than or equal to that of wild-type IgG1. The polypeptide further exhibits a dissociation constant for FcγRIIIa and FcγRIa that is reduced by at least 50% compared to wild-type IgG1, thereby minimizing off-target activation. The Fc region may be incorporated into a full-length IgG antibody or an Fc fusion protein, wherein the variable regions are selected to target antigens associated with autoimmune disease, cancer, or viral infection.

- The invention further provides a method for enhancing FcγRIIb-binding activity by introducing the substitution P238D into the Fc region, optionally in combination with one or more additional substitutions selected from E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, and A330R. The method for maintaining or decreasing FcγRIIa-binding activity involves the exclusion of substitutions known to enhance binding to FcγRIIaR131, such as S267E and L328F, and instead employs substitutions that induce conformational destabilization of the FcγRIIa-binding interface. The invention also provides a method for suppressing antibody production against therapeutic proteins by administering the polypeptide in fusion with a biologically essential protein, thereby promoting immune tolerance through FcγRIIb-mediated suppression of B-cell activation.

- The invention further encompasses a pharmaceutical composition comprising the polypeptide in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the composition is formulated for parenteral administration, including intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular injection. The polypeptide is used as a therapeutic agent for treating immunological inflammatory diseases, including systemic lupus erythematosus, rheumatoid arthritis, and IgE-mediated allergies, by suppressing autoantibody production, mast cell degranulation, and dendritic cell activation. The polypeptide is also used as an agonistic enhancer for antibodies targeting TNFR superfamily members, including CD137, CD40, and DR5, by promoting their cross-linking on target cells via FcγRIIb.

### Effects of the Invention

- The polypeptides of the invention achieve a previously unattainable level of binding selectivity for FcγRIIb over FcγRIIaR131, enabling therapeutic modulation of inhibitory immune pathways without triggering thrombotic or pro-inflammatory side effects. This selectivity translates into a significantly improved safety profile compared to prior Fc variants, as demonstrated by the absence of platelet activation in donor samples homozygous for FcγRIIaR131 or FcγRIIaH131. The invention further provides polypeptides with maintained or improved pharmacokinetic properties, including comparable serum half-life to wild-type IgG1, due to preserved FcRn binding, and enhanced stability under pharmaceutical storage conditions, with no increase in high-molecular-weight species formation.

- The invention enables the development of safer and more effective therapies for autoimmune diseases by suppressing B-cell activation and autoantibody production without inducing systemic immune suppression. It further enhances the efficacy of agonistic antibodies in cancer immunotherapy by promoting FcγRIIb-mediated clustering of target receptors on tumor cells, leading to amplified intracellular signaling and apoptosis. The invention also provides a platform for treating diseases requiring protein replacement, such as enzyme deficiencies, by suppressing immune responses against the administered therapeutic protein, thereby extending its functional half-life and reducing immunogenicity.

- The structural insights derived from the invention, including the identification of novel interactions between Asp238, Asp270, and FcγRIIb, provide a blueprint for future engineering of Fc domains with tailored receptor specificity. The polypeptides of the invention represent a new class of immunomodulatory agents that can be broadly applied across therapeutic modalities, from monoclonal antibodies to Fc fusion proteins, without requiring redesign of the antigen-binding domain. This versatility, combined with the absence of thrombotic risk and preserved pharmacokinetics, establishes the invention as a transformative advancement in the field of antibody therapeutics.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

- The invention provides polypeptides comprising an Fc region derived from human immunoglobulin G, wherein the Fc region contains specific amino acid substitutions that selectively enhance binding to the inhibitory Fc gamma receptor IIB (FcγRIIb) while maintaining or reducing binding to activating Fc gamma receptors, including FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa, and FcγRIa. These polypeptides are designed to modulate immune cell signaling through selective engagement of FcγRIIb, thereby enabling targeted suppression of aberrant immune activation without triggering pro-inflammatory or thrombotic pathways associated with activating Fcγ receptors. The invention encompasses both full-length monoclonal antibodies and Fc fusion proteins wherein the Fc domain has been structurally altered to achieve this unique binding profile, making it applicable to a broad spectrum of immunological, oncological, and autoimmune therapeutic contexts.

- Fcγ receptors are transmembrane proteins expressed on immune cells that bind the Fc portion of IgG antibodies and mediate downstream cellular responses. The FcγRIIb receptor is the sole inhibitory member of the FcγR family and contains an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM) in its cytoplasmic tail. Upon ligand binding, FcγRIIb becomes phosphorylated, recruiting the SH2-domain-containing inositol phosphatase SHIP, which suppresses downstream signaling cascades initiated by activating receptors. This mechanism enables FcγRIIb to act as a critical checkpoint for immune homeostasis, particularly in B cells, dendritic cells, and macrophages, where it constrains proliferation, antigen presentation, and cytokine production.

- The polypeptides of the invention are defined as proteins comprising at least the Fc region of an immunoglobulin G heavy chain, wherein the Fc region contains one or more amino acid substitutions at positions corresponding to Pro238, Glu233, Gly237, His268, Pro271, and Ala330 according to the EU numbering system. The parent polypeptide may be derived from naturally occurring IgG1 or IgG4, wherein the constant region is modified to enhance FcγRIIb binding while preserving FcRn binding and structural stability. The Fc region may be obtained by re-elution from a protein A column following expression in mammalian cells, and may be further purified by ion exchange or size exclusion chromatography.

- The binding activity of the polypeptides to human Fcγ receptors is determined using surface plasmon resonance, wherein the polypeptide is immobilized on a sensor chip and Fcγ receptors are flowed over the surface to measure association and dissociation rates. The dissociation constant (KD) is calculated using a 1:1 Langmuir binding model, and selectivity is defined as the ratio of KD for FcγRIIa to KD for FcγRIIb. A polypeptide with enhanced FcγRIIb-binding activity exhibits a KD for FcγRIIb that is at least tenfold lower than that of the parent polypeptide, while the KD for FcγRIIaR131 is maintained at or below that of the parent. A polypeptide with maintained or decreased FcγRIIa-binding activity exhibits a KD for FcγRIIaR131 that is unchanged or increased relative to the parent.

- The Fc region of the polypeptide may contain multiple substitutions, including the combination of P238D with E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, and A330R, resulting in a variant designated V12, which exhibits a 217-fold increase in binding affinity for FcγRIIb relative to wild-type IgG1, while binding to FcγRIIaR131 remains comparable to wild-type levels. Additional alterations may be introduced to improve FcRn-binding activity, such as M252Y/S254T/T256E, or to enhance antibody stability, such as substitutions at positions 239, 269, or 356. The polypeptide may be incorporated into an IgG1 or IgG4 antibody, wherein the variable regions are selected to target antigens associated with autoimmune disease, cancer, or viral infection.

- The invention further provides methods for producing the polypeptides by recombinant expression in mammalian cells, including CHO, HEK293, or NS0 cells, using expression vectors containing nucleic acid sequences encoding the modified Fc region under the control of a strong promoter such as CMV or EF1α. The polypeptide may be secreted into the culture medium and purified using protein A affinity chromatography, followed by ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography. The polypeptide may be formulated into a pharmaceutical composition comprising a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as saline, histidine buffer, or polysorbate 80, for parenteral administration.

- The polypeptide may be used as a therapeutic agent for treating immunological inflammatory diseases by suppressing B-cell activation, mast cell degranulation, and dendritic cell maturation. It may be administered as a fusion protein with a biologically essential protein to suppress immune responses against the protein, thereby enabling long-term replacement therapy. The polypeptide may also be used as an enhancer of agonistic antibodies targeting TNFR superfamily members, including CD137, CD40, and DR5, by promoting their clustering on target cells via FcγRIIb, thereby amplifying intracellular signaling and apoptosis.

## EXAMPLES

- The invention is further illustrated by the following examples, which demonstrate the generation, characterization, and functional evaluation of novel Fc variants with selectively enhanced binding to FcγRIIb. These examples include the comprehensive mutagenesis of the lower hinge and CH2 region of human IgG1, the structural determination of Fc-FcγRIIb complexes, and the assessment of binding selectivity, pharmacokinetics, and biological activity in vitro and in vivo. The results confirm that the polypeptides of the invention achieve unprecedented selectivity for FcγRIIb over FcγRIIaR131, maintain favorable pharmacokinetic properties, and enhance the agonistic activity of therapeutic antibodies without inducing platelet activation.

### Example 1

- A library of over 500 single amino acid substitutions was generated in the lower hinge and CH2 region of human IgG1, replacing each of approximately 30 residues with every naturally occurring amino acid except cysteine. Each variant was expressed in FreeStyle™ 293F cells and purified by protein A affinity chromatography. Binding to human FcγRIIaH131, FcγRIIaR131, and FcγRIIb was evaluated by surface plasmon resonance using Biacore instruments. The binding of each variant was normalized to wild-type IgG1, and substitutions that enhanced FcγRIIb binding while decreasing binding to both FcγRIIa allotypes were identified. Only two substitutions, P238D and L328E, met these criteria, with P238D demonstrating the most pronounced enhancement of FcγRIIb binding and reduction in FcγRIIa binding. The P238D variant exhibited a 15-fold increase in affinity for FcγRIIb and a 3-fold decrease in affinity for FcγRIIaR131, while binding to FcγRIIIa and FcγRIa was reduced by more than 50%.

### Example 2

- The P238D variant was subjected to kinetic analysis using surface plasmon resonance to determine dissociation constants for all human Fcγ receptors. The KD values for wild-type IgG1, IgG1-P238D, and additional variants were calculated using a 1:1 binding model. The P238D variant exhibited a KD for FcγRIIb of 0.12 μM, compared to 1.8 μM for wild-type IgG1, representing a 15-fold improvement. The KD for FcγRIIaR131 was 1.9 μM for P238D, compared to 2.1 μM for wild-type IgG1, indicating no significant increase. The selectivity ratio of FcγRIIb to FcγRIIaR131 was calculated as 15.8, demonstrating a marked improvement over wild-type IgG1. The variant also exhibited reduced binding to FcγRIIIaV158 and FcγRIa, with KD values increased by 2.5-fold and 3.1-fold, respectively.

### Example 3

- The P238D variant was used as a template for a second round of mutagenesis, wherein an additional 400 single substitutions were introduced across the Fc region. Variants were screened for enhanced FcγRIIb binding while maintaining low FcγRIIa binding. Six substitutions were identified that further improved FcγRIIb affinity: E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, Y296D, and A330R. These substitutions were combined to generate a variant designated V12, containing all six changes. The V12 variant exhibited a 217-fold increase in affinity for FcγRIIb compared to wild-type IgG1, with a KD of 0.0083 μM. The KD for FcγRIIaR131 remained unchanged at 2.0 μM, yielding a selectivity ratio of 240:1. Binding to FcγRIIIa and FcγRIa was reduced by more than 90%, confirming the high selectivity of the variant.

### Example 4

- The crystal structure of the Fc(P238D)/FcγRIIb complex was determined at 2.6 Å resolution using X-ray crystallography. The structure revealed that the substitution of Pro238 to Asp induced a conformational shift in the CH2 domain, exposing Asp238 to the solvent and allowing the formation of a novel hydrogen bond between Gly237 and Tyr160 of FcγRIIb. A salt bridge was formed between Asp270 and Arg131 of FcγRIIb, which is not possible with FcγRIIa due to the presence of His131 in the latter. These interactions were absent in the Fc(IgG1)/FcγRIIaR131 complex, explaining the selectivity of P238D for FcγRIIb. The structure also revealed a rearrangement of the CH2-A and CH2-B domains, which altered the overall geometry of the Fc-FcγRIIb interface.

### Example 5

- The crystal structure of the Fc(V12)/FcγRIIb complex was determined at 2.86 Å resolution. The structure confirmed the conformational changes observed in Fc(P238D) and revealed additional interactions introduced by the six substitutions. Asp233 formed a weak electrostatic interaction with Lys113 of FcγRIIb, Asp237 formed van der Waals contacts with Trp87 and Tyr160, and Arg330 was positioned near Glu86 of FcγRIIb. The substitution P271G allowed greater flexibility in the loop containing Asp270, enabling optimal positioning for the salt bridge with Arg131. The structure demonstrated that the cumulative effect of the six substitutions stabilized the FcγRIIb interface without inducing destabilization of the Fc core, thereby preserving thermal stability and aggregation resistance.

### Example 6

- The V12 variant was evaluated for its ability to enhance the agonistic activity of an anti-CD137 antibody in a co-culture system of CTLL-2 and Raji cells. The V12 variant increased IFN-γ production by more than fivefold compared to wild-type IgG1, comparable to the S267E/L328F variant. However, unlike S267E/L328F, the V12 variant did not induce platelet aggregation in human blood samples from donors homozygous for FcγRIIaR131 or FcγRIIaH131. The variant also exhibited a serum half-life in human FcRn transgenic mice comparable to wild-type IgG1, confirming preserved FcRn binding. These results demonstrate that the V12 variant enhances therapeutic efficacy without increasing safety risks.

### Example 7

- The V12 variant was formulated into a pharmaceutical composition comprising 100 mg/mL in 20 mM histidine buffer, 150 mM NaCl, pH 6.0, and stored at 25°C for four weeks. High-molecular-weight species were analyzed by size exclusion chromatography, and the percentage of aggregates remained below 0.5%, comparable to wild-type IgG1. Thermal shift assays showed a melting temperature of 62°C for V12, compared to 70°C for wild-type IgG1, indicating moderate reduction in CH2 stability that did not compromise formulation integrity. The variant was administered intravenously to human FcRn transgenic mice, and serum concentrations were measured over 21 days. The elimination half-life was 2.6 days, indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1, confirming that the modifications did not impair FcRn-mediated recycling.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

- The polypeptides of the invention, with selectively enhanced binding to FcγRIIb and maintained or reduced binding to activating Fcγ receptors, have broad industrial applicability in the development of next-generation therapeutic antibodies for autoimmune diseases, cancer immunotherapy, and protein replacement therapies. These polypeptides can be incorporated into monoclonal antibodies, Fc fusion proteins, or bispecific constructs to enhance their safety profile and efficacy. The invention enables the production of therapeutic agents that suppress pathological immune responses without inducing thrombosis, making them suitable for chronic administration in diverse patient populations.
