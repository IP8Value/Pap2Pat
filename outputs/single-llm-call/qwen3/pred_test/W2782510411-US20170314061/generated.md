# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention relates to the field of bioluminescence imaging and molecular reporting systems, particularly to engineered luciferase enzymes and novel luciferin substrates that produce near-infrared emission for enhanced in vivo imaging applications. More specifically, the invention encompasses genetically modified click beetle red luciferase variants with altered spectral properties, substrate specificity, and thermal stability, as well as corresponding naphthyl-based luciferin analogs that, when paired with these variants, enable high-sensitivity, deep-tissue bioluminescence detection in living organisms. The invention further includes polynucleotides encoding these variants, expression vectors, recombinant cells, transgenic animals, diagnostic kits, and methods for detecting molecular interactions, gene expression, and enzymatic activity using near-infrared bioluminescence signals.

## BACKGROUND

- motivate bioluminescence

Bioluminescence imaging has emerged as a powerful, non-invasive tool for monitoring biological processes in live animals, enabling real-time visualization of gene expression, tumor progression, cell migration, and therapeutic response without the need for ionizing radiation or exogenous contrast agents. The widespread adoption of firefly luciferase and its natural substrate D-luciferin has facilitated countless preclinical studies due to the system’s high signal-to-noise ratio, genetic encodability, and compatibility with standard imaging instrumentation. However, the emission maximum of this system, approximately 560 to 610 nanometers, is subject to significant attenuation by biological tissues, particularly hemoglobin, melanin, and lipids, which strongly absorb photons in the visible spectrum. This limitation restricts the depth and resolution of detectable signals, especially in organs such as the brain, lungs, and bone marrow, where tissue density and pigmentation impede photon escape.

- describe limitations of current systems

Existing bioluminescent systems, including optimized variants of firefly luciferase and alternative beetle luciferases, continue to suffer from insufficient photon penetration in deep tissues. While some modifications have extended emission wavelengths toward the red end of the spectrum, these shifts are often marginal and accompanied by substantial reductions in quantum yield or enzyme stability. Furthermore, the pharmacokinetic profile of D-luciferin, including its rapid clearance, limited blood-brain barrier permeability, and suboptimal biodistribution in certain tissues, further constrains the sensitivity and reliability of imaging in physiologically relevant contexts. Attempts to overcome these limitations through the use of synthetic luciferin analogs have yielded mixed results, with many derivatives exhibiting poor solubility, chemical instability, or low catalytic efficiency when paired with available luciferases.

- discuss importance of near-infrared bioluminescence

The near-infrared region of the electromagnetic spectrum, spanning approximately 650 to 900 nanometers, offers a critical advantage for in vivo imaging due to its reduced absorption and scattering by biological tissues. Photons in this range penetrate deeper into tissue with less distortion, enabling higher spatial resolution and more accurate quantification of signal origin. The development of a bioluminescent system that emits efficiently within this window, while maintaining high photon output, enzyme stability, and substrate bioavailability, would represent a transformative advancement for longitudinal studies of disease progression, cell trafficking, and drug efficacy in preclinical models.

- summarize previous approaches

Previous efforts to achieve near-infrared bioluminescence have included mutagenesis of luciferase enzymes to alter the electronic environment of the oxyluciferin emitter, chemical modification of luciferin to extend π-conjugation and lower the energy gap of the excited state, and the use of bioluminescence resonance energy transfer (BRET) systems that couple luciferase activity with fluorescent protein acceptors. While some of these strategies have produced modest red-shifts, they often require complex multi-component systems, suffer from low brightness, or are incompatible with standard imaging platforms. Notably, no single enzyme-substrate pair has previously demonstrated both a pronounced shift into the near-infrared (>700 nm) and sufficient signal intensity to rival or surpass the performance of the conventional firefly luciferase/D-luciferin system in deep tissue contexts.

- highlight need for improvement

There remains a critical unmet need for a bioluminescence system that combines a genetically encodable luciferase with high catalytic efficiency, thermal stability in mammalian cells, and the ability to produce bright, stable near-infrared emission upon reaction with a cell-permeable, non-toxic substrate. Such a system would enable high-fidelity, quantitative imaging of biological processes in deep tissues without the need for external excitation, fluorescent labels, or complex instrumentation, thereby expanding the scope and precision of in vivo molecular imaging.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce click beetle luciferase variant polypeptides

The invention provides novel polypeptides derived from click beetle red luciferase that exhibit enhanced luminescence, altered emission spectra, and modified substrate specificity compared to the wild-type enzyme. These variants are engineered through targeted amino acid substitutions that optimize the active site for interaction with novel naphthyl-based luciferin analogs while preserving or enhancing catalytic efficiency with native substrates.

- describe enhanced luminescence

The engineered luciferase variants demonstrate significantly increased bioluminescence intensity in mammalian cells compared to wild-type click beetle red luciferase and, in certain contexts, surpass the signal output of firefly luciferase. This enhancement is attributable to improved enzyme stability at physiological temperatures, resulting in higher intracellular accumulation and prolonged functional half-life, rather than a direct increase in turnover rate.

- describe altered light emission wavelength

When reacted with specific naphthyl-luciferin derivatives, the engineered luciferase variants emit light with a peak wavelength exceeding 700 nanometers, representing a substantial red-shift relative to the wild-type enzyme. This shift enables efficient detection in deep tissues where visible light is strongly attenuated.

- describe altered substrate specificity

The variants exhibit altered relative substrate specificity, with markedly increased affinity for novel naphthyl-luciferin analogs over D-luciferin, while retaining sufficient activity with D-luciferin to support dual-mode imaging applications. This selectivity allows for orthogonal detection in multiplexed systems.

- list specific amino acid substitutions

The engineered luciferase variants comprise at least one substitution at position 334 and at least one substitution at position 351 of the wild-type click beetle red luciferase sequence, wherein position 334 is substituted with serine and position 351 is substituted with arginine. Additional variants include combinations of substitutions at these positions and neighboring residues that further modulate spectral properties or catalytic efficiency.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce click beetle red luciferase (CBR) variants

The present invention is directed to engineered variants of click beetle red luciferase (CBR) that exhibit improved bioluminescent properties for in vivo applications. Wild-type CBR, derived from the beetle Pyrophorus plagiophthalamus, naturally emits light with a peak wavelength near 614 nanometers when reacted with D-luciferin. While this emission is red-shifted relative to firefly luciferase, it remains insufficient for deep-tissue imaging. Through rational design and directed evolution, the inventors identified specific amino acid substitutions that dramatically alter the spectral output and catalytic efficiency of CBR, particularly in response to novel luciferin analogs.

- describe near-IR bioluminescence systems

The invention introduces a near-infrared bioluminescence system based on the pairing of a genetically encoded CBR variant with a naphthyl-based luciferin derivative. This system produces a bioluminescent signal with a peak emission between 720 and 750 nanometers, a range that minimizes tissue absorption and scattering while maintaining sufficient photon flux for sensitive detection. The system is fully genetically encodable, requires no external excitation, and is compatible with standard bioluminescence imaging platforms, making it uniquely suited for longitudinal, quantitative studies in live animals.

The invention provides a family of click beetle red luciferase variants engineered to exhibit enhanced luminescence, altered emission spectra, and improved substrate specificity. - describe techniques for evolving CBR

The variants were generated using site-directed mutagenesis, random mutagenesis, and saturation mutagenesis at key residues surrounding the luciferin binding pocket. Residues R334 and G351 were identified as primary targets due to their proximity to the 6′-hydroxyl group of the luciferin substrate and their involvement in the hydrogen-bonding network that stabilizes the transition state of the reaction.

### 3. NEAR-INFRARED BIOLUMINESCENCE SYSTEMS

- introduce near-IR bioluminescence systems

The invention provides a near-infrared bioluminescence system comprising the CBR2 variant polypeptide and a naphthyl-based luciferin substrate, wherein the system emits photons with a peak wavelength between 720 and 750 nanometers, enabling high-resolution imaging in deep tissues.

- describe CBR variants and luciferin derivatives

The CBR2 variant is specifically optimized for use with NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2, two novel luciferin derivatives featuring a naphtho[2,1]thiazole core that extends π-conjugation beyond the traditional benzothiazole ring.

- motivate near-IR luminescence for in-depth tissue imaging

Near-infrared luminescence minimizes absorption by hemoglobin and melanin, reduces tissue autofluorescence, and allows for deeper penetration of photons, enabling accurate localization of biological signals in organs such as the brain, liver, and bone marrow.

- describe advantages of near-IR bioluminescence systems

Advantages include improved signal-to-noise ratio, enhanced spatial resolution, compatibility with multispectral imaging, and the ability to perform quantitative tomographic reconstruction without the need for external excitation.

- introduce novel luciferin derivatives

The novel luciferin derivatives NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2 are synthesized by coupling a naphthalene moiety to the thiazole ring of luciferin, resulting in extended conjugation and a lower energy gap for photon emission.

- define compound of Formula (Ia), (Ib), (Ic), (II), (III), (IV), (V), (VI)

The invention includes compounds of Formula (Ia), (Ib), and (Ic), wherein the core structure comprises a naphtho[2,1]thiazole ring substituted at the 2-position with a carboxylic acid group and at the 6-position with an amino or hydroxy group.

- describe structure of Formula (Ia), (Ib), (Ic)

Formula (Ia) is 2-(6-amino-1,3-benzothiazol-2-yl)-1,3-naphtho[2,1]thiazole-4-carboxylic acid; Formula (Ib) is 2-(6-hydroxy-1,3-benzothiazol-2-yl)-1,3-naphtho[2,1]thiazole-4-carboxylic acid; Formula (Ic) is the monopotassium salt of Formula (Ia).

- describe structure of Formula (II)

Formula (II) is a derivative of Formula (Ia) with an additional phenyl ring fused to the naphthalene moiety.

- describe structure of Formula (III)

Formula (III) is a derivative of Formula (Ia) with a methyl ester substitution at the carboxylic acid group.

- describe structure of Formula (IV)

Formula (IV) is a derivative of Formula (Ia) with a polyethylene glycol chain conjugated to the amino group.

- describe structure of Formula (V)

Formula (V) is a derivative of Formula (Ia) with a biotin tag attached via a spacer.

- describe structure of Formula (VI)

Formula (VI) is a derivative of Formula (Ia) with a fluorescent dye conjugated to the amino group for BRET applications.

- describe structure of Formula (VII)

Formula (VII) is a pro-substrate form of NH2-NpLH2, masked by a glucuronide group for enzymatic activation.

- describe emission maximum of compounds

The emission maximum of the CBR2/NH2-NpLH2 system is 730 nanometers, while the CBR2/OH-NpLH2 system emits at 743 nanometers.

- introduce pro-substrates for luciferase enzymes

Pro-substrates are inactive precursors of luciferin that are converted to active substrates by endogenous enzymes such as β-glucuronidase, proteases, or reductases.

- describe reductase substrates

Reductase substrates include nitroaromatic compounds that are reduced to amines by intracellular reductases, thereby activating the luciferin.

- describe glycosidase substrates

Glycosidase substrates include glucuronide-, galactoside-, or sialic acid-conjugated luciferin derivatives that are cleaved by lysosomal or extracellular glycosidases.

- describe protease and protease-dependent protein modifying substrates

Protease substrates include luciferin derivatives linked by a peptide sequence cleavable by caspases, cathepsins, or matrix metalloproteinases.

- describe oxidase substrates

Oxidase substrates include luciferin analogs containing phenolic groups that are oxidized by peroxidases or laccases to generate active luciferin.

- describe carboxyl-based pro-substrates and glutathione transferase substrates

Carboxyl-based pro-substrates are esterified forms of luciferin that are hydrolyzed by esterases; glutathione transferase substrates are conjugated to glutathione and cleaved by GST enzymes.

- describe beta-lactamase substrates and other pro-substrates

Beta-lactamase substrates include cephalosporin-linked luciferin derivatives that release active luciferin upon cleavage by beta-lactamase.
