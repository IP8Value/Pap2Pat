# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD AND INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic applications targeting the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src signaling complex for the modulation of cellular proliferation, survival, and hypertrophic responses in pathological conditions including cancer, cardiac hypertrophy, and fibrotic disorders. The invention provides a class of peptide-based and small-molecule agents that selectively disrupt or stabilize the interaction between the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit and Src family kinases, thereby enabling precise control over downstream signaling cascades such as ERK1/2 activation without perturbing the ion-pumping function of the ATPase. This represents a paradigm shift from conventional kinase inhibitors that target catalytic domains and cause broad off-target effects. By exploiting the endogenous regulatory interface between a membrane transporter and a cytoplasmic kinase, the invention enables tissue-specific, pathway-selective intervention in diseases driven by aberrant Src activation. The therapeutic utility extends to oncology, where Src hyperactivity promotes tumor invasion and metastasis, and cardiovascular medicine, where ouabain-like endogenous steroids trigger pathological remodeling. The agents disclosed herein are amenable to formulation for systemic or localized delivery, including intravenous, intraperitoneal, or topical administration, and are compatible with nanoparticle-based or cell-penetrating peptide delivery systems to enhance bioavailability and target engagement. The invention further enables the development of diagnostic tools for identifying patients whose tumors or cardiac tissues exhibit elevated Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src complex formation, thereby facilitating personalized therapeutic strategies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cardiotonic steroids, including ouabain and marinobufagenin, are endogenous steroid hormones that bind to the extracellular domain of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and modulate intracellular signaling pathways independent of their classical role in ion transport. These molecules have long been recognized for their inotropic effects on cardiac muscle, but recent evidence reveals their capacity to activate Src family kinases and initiate cascades involving Ras, ERK1/2, and reactive oxygen species production. Src family kinases are non-receptor tyrosine kinases that serve as critical nodes in signal transduction networks regulating cell adhesion, migration, proliferation, and apoptosis. Their dysregulation is implicated in a wide spectrum of malignancies, inflammatory diseases, and cardiovascular pathologies. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, traditionally viewed as a passive ion pump, has emerged as a scaffold protein capable of assembling multiprotein signaling complexes upon ligand binding. The interaction between the α1 subunit of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and Src occurs via specific cytoplasmic domains and results in the suppression of Src kinase activity under basal conditions. Upon binding of cardiotonic steroids, this inhibitory constraint is relieved, leading to Src autophosphorylation at tyrosine 418 and subsequent propagation of downstream signals. Despite the therapeutic potential of modulating this interaction, existing Src inhibitors such as PP2 and dasatinib lack specificity, inhibiting multiple kinases and disrupting essential physiological functions. These compounds also fail to distinguish between Src molecules engaged in signaling versus those participating in structural roles at focal adhesions. Furthermore, cardiotonic steroids themselves are limited by narrow therapeutic windows, systemic toxicity, and pleiotropic effects arising from their interaction with multiple isoforms of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. There remains a critical unmet need for molecular tools that can selectively target the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src interface without affecting ion transport or other kinase pathways. Such agents would provide unprecedented precision in modulating Src-dependent signaling, enabling the development of novel agonists and antagonists that mimic or block the physiological effects of endogenous cardiotonic steroids. Additionally, there is an urgent need for high-throughput screening assays capable of identifying small molecules that either stabilize or disrupt this protein–protein interaction, which would accelerate the discovery of next-generation therapeutics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method for regulating Src kinase activity by modulating its interaction with the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit through the use of synthetic peptides derived from the N-terminal domain of the α1 subunit’s cytoplasmic region. The invention further provides a receptor for ouabain-provoked signal transduction, identified as the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src complex, which functions as a bona fide membrane-bound signaling platform. A specific target for interacting sites is defined by the ND1 region of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit, spanning amino acids 379 to 435, which directly binds to the Src kinase domain and suppresses its catalytic activity. The invention encompasses a pharmaceutical composition comprising a peptide derived from the ND1 domain, or a functional variant thereof, capable of inhibiting Src kinase activity when delivered intracellularly. The invention further provides a Src inhibitor or activator comprising a peptide sequence derived from the ND1 domain or the Src kinase domain, wherein the peptide is engineered for enhanced stability, cell permeability, or binding affinity. A therapeutic composition is disclosed that includes a peptide or small molecule capable of disrupting the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src interaction, formulated for administration to a subject suffering from a Src-dependent disorder. The invention provides a method for developing small molecules by screening compound libraries for agents that bind to the ND1 domain or the Src kinase domain interface, using binding assays based on fluorescence resonance energy transfer or surface plasmon resonance. The invention further provides a signal transducer comprising the ND1 peptide fused to a cell-penetrating sequence, enabling intracellular delivery and targeted inhibition of Src. A composition containing a functional domain derived from the ND1 peptide is disclosed, wherein the domain retains the ability to bind Src and suppress its autophosphorylation. A composition containing the ND1 peptide is provided, optionally conjugated to a fluorescent tag or a therapeutic payload. A peptide derived from the ND1 domain, designated as NaKtide, is disclosed, with the sequence corresponding to amino acids 379–435 of the human Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit. A peptide derived from the Src kinase domain is provided, capable of competing with the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase for binding and thereby activating Src. A peptide for activating or inhibiting the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase signaling pathway is disclosed, wherein the peptide is selected from the group consisting of ND1-derived inhibitors and Src kinase domain-derived activators. The invention provides Src inhibitors and/or activators that are isoform-specific, targeting the α1 isoform of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase without affecting other isoforms. A rapid screen assay is provided, comprising immobilized ND1 peptide or Src kinase domain coupled to a detection system for identifying modulators of their interaction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

Src and Src family kinases are central regulators of intracellular signaling pathways that control cell growth, motility, and survival. Their aberrant activation is a hallmark of numerous cancers, including those of the prostate, breast, lung, and colon, as well as cardiovascular diseases such as cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis. The development of Src inhibitors has been a major focus of pharmaceutical research, yet existing compounds such as PP2 and dasatinib inhibit multiple tyrosine kinases, leading to dose-limiting toxicities and loss of therapeutic specificity. The present invention overcomes these limitations by identifying a novel class of peptide Src inhibitors derived from the endogenous regulatory interface between the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit and Src. These peptides, particularly the ND1 fragment, bind with high affinity to the Src kinase domain and suppress its autophosphorylation at tyrosine 418 without interfering with ATP binding or catalytic site integrity. In contrast to conventional inhibitors, these peptides act as allosteric modulators that mimic the natural inhibitory function of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Peptide Src activators are also disclosed, comprising fragments of the Src kinase domain that compete with the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase for binding, thereby releasing Src from its inhibitory constraint. The interaction between Src and the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase is not merely structural but functional, forming a dynamic signaling complex that responds to cardiotonic steroids such as ouabain. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase functions as a Src inhibitor under basal conditions, maintaining Src in a quiescent state through direct binding to its kinase domain. Mapping of the functional domains reveals that the ND1 region of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit, corresponding to amino acids 379–435, is both necessary and sufficient for Src binding and inhibition. The Src-inhibitory peptide, designated NaKtide, is synthesized as a 20-amino acid sequence derived from this region and demonstrates potent inhibition of Src with an IC₅₀ of approximately 70 nM in vitro. When delivered intracellularly via fusion to the HIV-Tat cell-penetrating peptide, NaKtide effectively suppresses Src-dependent signaling in live cells and blocks ouabain-induced ERK1/2 activation. The Src-activating peptide, derived from the Src kinase domain, disrupts the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src interaction and induces Src autophosphorylation, thereby serving as a molecular tool to probe signaling dynamics. The advantages of these peptide inhibitors and activators include their high specificity for the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src interface, minimal off-target effects on other kinases, and compatibility with targeted delivery systems. Future development of small molecules that mimic the structural features of NaKtide will enable oral bioavailability and long-term therapeutic use. High-throughput screening assays based on fluorescence resonance energy transfer or bioluminescence resonance energy transfer are disclosed for identifying novel modulators of this interaction, accelerating the discovery of non-peptidic therapeutics.

### Binding of Src to Na+/K+-ATPase Forms a Functional Signaling Complex

The interaction between Src and the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit forms a functional signaling complex that serves as a receptor for cardiotonic steroids such as ouabain. This complex is assembled at the plasma membrane and is distinct from the ion-pumping function of the ATPase. In vitro binding assays using purified GST-Src and His-tagged Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase demonstrate a direct, concentration-dependent interaction that persists in the absence of detergents, indicating a stable protein–protein interface. FRET and BRET analyses in live cells confirm that Src and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase are in close proximity at the plasma membrane, with energy transfer efficiencies consistent with molecular distances of less than 10 nanometers. Colocalization studies using confocal microscopy further validate the spatial association of these proteins in lipid raft domains. Domain mapping reveals that the ND1 region of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit binds specifically to the Src kinase domain and SH2 domain, with the kinase domain being the primary site of interaction. The binding of ouabain to the extracellular domain of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase induces a conformational change that dissociates Src from the inhibitory interface, resulting in Src autophosphorylation at tyrosine 418 and activation of downstream effectors such as ERK1/2. This activation is abolished in cells lacking Src or in the presence of Src inhibitors such as PP2, confirming the essential role of Src in mediating ouabain-induced signaling. The interaction is not dependent on ion transport, as pumping-null mutants of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase retain the ability to bind and regulate Src. These findings establish the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src complex as a bona fide signal transduction unit that translates extracellular steroid signals into intracellular kinase activation.

### Functional Characterization of Src-Interacting Na+/K+-ATPase Using RNA Interference Assay

RNA interference-based knockdown of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit in mammalian cell lines results in a significant increase in basal Src activity, demonstrating that the ATPase serves as a physiological suppressor of Src. Stable knockdown cell lines were generated using siRNA targeting the α1 mRNA, and Src autophosphorylation was measured by Western blot using phospho-specific antibodies. Re-expression of a rat α1 subunit bearing silent mutations that render it resistant to the siRNA rescued the basal Src activity to control levels, confirming the specificity of the knockdown. Importantly, a pumping-null mutant of the rat α1 subunit (D371E), which lacks ion transport capability, fully restored Src inhibition, indicating that the regulatory function of the ATPase is independent of its ion-pumping activity. FRET analysis in these rescued cells confirmed the reformation of the Src–Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase complex, and co-immunoprecipitation assays demonstrated direct physical interaction. The knockdown of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase also abolished ouabain-induced activation of Src and ERK1/2, confirming that the ATPase is required for signal transduction initiated by cardiotonic steroids. Furthermore, the knockdown cells exhibited increased tyrosine phosphorylation of focal adhesion kinase, a known Src substrate, which was reversed by treatment with the Src inhibitor PP2. These results establish that the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit is not merely a passive scaffold but an active regulator of Src kinase activity, and that disruption of this interaction has profound consequences for downstream signaling. The RNA interference protocol described herein provides a robust platform for functional validation of Src–Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase interactions in diverse cellular contexts and enables the identification of disease-relevant modulators of this pathway.

### Example III

The ND1 peptide, derived from amino acids 379 to 435 of the human Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit, binds directly to the Src kinase domain as demonstrated by in vitro pull-down assays and surface plasmon resonance. Fluorescence polarization and isothermal titration calorimetry confirmed a binding affinity in the low nanomolar range. Co-expression of ND1 fused to yellow fluorescent protein in live cells resulted in co-localization with endogenous Src at the plasma membrane and reduced Src autophosphorylation. Immunoprecipitation of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase complex from cell lysates consistently pulled down the ND1 peptide, confirming its integration into the native signaling complex. Mutation of key hydrophobic residues within ND1 abolished binding to Src, establishing the structural basis for the interaction.

### Example IV

The KD1 peptide, corresponding to the kinase domain of Src (amino acids 275–350), binds directly to the ND1 region of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit. Binding was confirmed by GST pull-down assays using purified His-tagged KD1 and immobilized GST-ND1. Surface plasmon resonance analysis revealed a dissociation constant of 85 nM. When added exogenously to cells, KD1 disrupted the endogenous Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase–Src interaction, leading to increased Src autophosphorylation and downstream ERK1/2 activation. This confirms that KD1 functions as a competitive activator of Src by displacing the inhibitory ND1 domain.

### Example V

The involvement of ND1 and KD1 in Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase-mediated regulation of Src was confirmed through reciprocal rescue experiments. Expression of ND1 in cells with KD1 overexpression restored Src inhibition, while expression of KD1 in cells overexpressing ND1 reversed inhibition. These findings establish a direct, bidirectional regulatory mechanism wherein the ND1 domain of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and the KD1 domain of Src form a mutually exclusive binding interface that controls kinase activity.

### Example VI

Treatment of prostate cancer cell lines with the ND1-derived peptide NaKtide significantly inhibited cell proliferation and colony formation in soft agar assays. In xenograft models, systemic administration of cell-penetrating NaKtide reduced tumor volume by over 60% compared to controls, with no observable toxicity. Immunohistochemical analysis of tumor tissues revealed decreased Src phosphorylation and reduced expression of cyclin D1 and survivin, confirming suppression of oncogenic signaling. These results demonstrate the therapeutic potential of ND1-derived peptides in Src-driven cancers.

### Example VII

The peptide designated P3, corresponding to residues 385–404 of the ND1 domain, was identified as a potent Src inhibitor with an IC₅₀ of 15 nM in kinase assays. P3 exhibited no inhibition of Lyn, PKC, or other tyrosine kinases, confirming its specificity. When labeled with fluorescein, P3 rapidly entered the cytoplasm and plasma membrane of live cells within 15 minutes, demonstrating high membrane permeability. In vivo administration of P3 suppressed tumor growth in a murine model of prostate cancer, with no alteration in serum electrolytes or cardiac function, underscoring its safety and specificity as a therapeutic agent.
