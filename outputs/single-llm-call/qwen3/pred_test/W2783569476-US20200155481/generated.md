# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of infectious disease prophylaxis, specifically to the prevention of malaria caused by Plasmodium species, including Plasmodium falciparum and Plasmodium vivax. The invention encompasses novel pharmaceutical formulations and administration methods designed to provide sustained, long-acting chemoprophylaxis against malaria through intramuscular delivery of atovaquone in a controlled-release nanoformulation. This approach addresses the critical limitations of current oral prophylactic regimens by eliminating the need for daily or weekly dosing, thereby improving adherence and extending the duration of protective drug exposure in individuals at risk of exposure to malaria-endemic regions. The invention further extends to the use of such formulations in both non-immune travelers and in targeted public health interventions where consistent, long-term protection is required without reliance on patient compliance.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Malaria remains one of the most significant global infectious disease burdens, with hundreds of millions of clinical cases reported annually and hundreds of thousands of deaths, predominantly among children in sub-Saharan Africa. Despite decades of progress in vector control and treatment, the incidence of malaria among travelers to endemic regions continues to rise, particularly in those visiting West Africa, where the risk of infection can exceed 3.4% per month of exposure. Current chemoprophylactic strategies rely almost exclusively on oral medications such as doxycycline, mefloquine, or atovaquone-proguanil, which require strict daily or weekly adherence to maintain therapeutic plasma concentrations. Noncompliance with these regimens is widespread and frequently leads to breakthrough infections, rendering prophylaxis ineffective even when the drug itself is potent and well-tolerated. Moreover, the pharmacokinetic profile of orally administered atovaquone, while favorable in terms of half-life and bioavailability, is subject to significant variability due to food interactions, gastrointestinal absorption inconsistencies, and individual metabolic differences. These factors collectively undermine the reliability of oral prophylaxis, particularly in settings where consistent dosing cannot be assured. There is therefore an urgent and unmet need for a long-acting, single-dose formulation capable of maintaining protective drug concentrations for an extended period—ideally four weeks or more—thereby aligning prophylactic coverage with the typical duration of travel to endemic areas and eliminating the burden of daily medication.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses the discovery that a long-acting intramuscular nanoformulation of atovaquone, when administered as a single dose, provides complete and sustained chemoprophylaxis against malaria for up to four weeks in a preclinical model of Plasmodium berghei infection. This prophylactic effect is attributable to causal activity against the liver stage of the parasite lifecycle, preventing the establishment of erythrocytic infection before it can begin. The invention further encompasses a pharmaceutical composition comprising atovaquone formulated as solid drug nanoparticles stabilized by a combination of a polymer and a surfactant, wherein the atovaquone content is at least 60% by weight of the total solid mass, and wherein the formulation is suspended in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier suitable for parenteral administration. The invention further includes a method of preventing malaria in a human subject by administering a single intramuscular injection of this formulation, wherein plasma concentrations of atovaquone remain above 200 ng/mL for a duration of at least 28 days following administration. The invention also encompasses specific embodiments wherein the formulation is prepared using emulsion-templated freeze drying, resulting in a dry, porous monolith that is reconstituted immediately prior to injection. The prophylactic effect is maintained without the need for follow-up doses, and the formulation is effective at plasma concentrations substantially lower than those required for treatment of established infection. Additionally, the invention includes applications for prophylaxis in non-immune travelers, military personnel, and populations in high-transmission zones, and further encompasses the use of the formulation in combination with other antimalarial agents for enhanced coverage or resistance mitigation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The scope of the present invention is directed to a novel method and composition for the long-acting prophylaxis of malaria through intramuscular administration of a nanoformulated atovaquone preparation. The invention is based on the unexpected finding that a solid drug nanoparticle formulation of atovaquone, when delivered intramuscularly, provides sustained release of the active compound into systemic circulation over a period exceeding four weeks, sufficient to prevent the establishment of liver-stage infection following sporozoite challenge. The term “patient,” “individual,” or “subject” as used herein refers to any mammalian organism, including but not limited to humans, who is at risk of exposure to malaria parasites and requires chemoprophylactic intervention. The term “preventing” or “prevention” refers to the complete inhibition of the development of detectable parasitemia following exposure to infectious sporozoites, as confirmed by the absence of parasites in peripheral blood over a minimum observation period of 42 days. The term “treatment” or “treating” refers to the reduction or elimination of established parasitemia, which is not the primary objective of the claimed invention but may be addressed in combination therapies. The term “effective” denotes the ability of the formulation to maintain plasma concentrations of atovaquone at or above 200 ng/mL during the critical window of liver-stage development following sporozoite inoculation. The atovaquone used in the invention is a hydroxynaphthoquinone compound characterized by a log P value of approximately 4.7, which confers high lipophilicity and low systemic clearance, making it uniquely suited for depot formulation. The microparticle formulation comprises atovaquone as the primary active ingredient, combined with a polymer selected from the group consisting of polyvinyl alcohol, polyethylene glycol-block-polypropylene glycol-block-polyethylene glycol copolymers, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, polyvinylpyrrolidone, and polyethylene oxide, and a surfactant selected from the group consisting of Tween 20, Tween 80, TPGS, sodium deoxycholate, and AOT. The formulation may contain atovaquone in a concentration of at least 60% by weight, and preferably between 70% and 80% by weight, with the remainder comprising the stabilizing excipients. The formulation is prepared using emulsion-templated freeze drying, resulting in a dry, porous monolith that is reconstituted with sterile water or a pharmaceutically acceptable aqueous vehicle immediately prior to injection. The formulation does not contain nanoparticles smaller than 100 nm in Z-average diameter, and is not encapsulated within liposomes or polymeric micelles. The prophylaxis of malaria is achieved by a single intramuscular injection, with no requirement for additional doses during the protection period. The invention further encompasses the use of the formulation for the prevention of Plasmodium vivax malaria, although it is not expected to prevent the establishment of hypnozoites. Combination therapy with other antimalarial agents such as proguanil, doxycycline, or mefloquine may be employed for enhanced coverage, but is not required for efficacy. The pharmaceutical composition is formulated in unit-dose or multi-dose sealed containers suitable for parenteral administration, and may include preservatives, tonicity agents, or buffering agents as necessary for stability and compatibility. The preferred carrier is sterile water for injection, but other parenteral vehicles such as saline, dextrose solutions, or aqueous suspensions containing surfactants may be used. The amount of atovaquone administered per dose ranges from 10 mg to 500 mg, with a preferred dose of 200 mg for an adult human subject. Follow-up injections are not required for the duration of protection, which is expected to extend beyond four weeks in humans based on pharmacokinetic extrapolation from preclinical models. The invention includes specific embodiments wherein the formulation is administered as a single injection into the deltoid or gluteal muscle, and wherein plasma concentrations of atovaquone remain above 200 ng/mL for a period of 28 to 42 days. The invention further includes methods for preparing the pharmaceutical product by dissolving atovaquone in chloroform, combining it with aqueous solutions of polymer and surfactant, forming an oil-in-water emulsion, cryogenically freezing the emulsion, and lyophilizing to obtain a dry, porous solid. The resulting monolith is reconstituted with sterile water to form a suspension suitable for injection. The formulation is stable for storage at room temperature and may be reconstituted immediately prior to use without loss of efficacy. The invention further includes the use of the formulation in travelers, military personnel, pregnant women, and children, and in public health programs aimed at reducing malaria transmission in endemic regions.

### Examples

The invention is further illustrated by the following examples, which demonstrate the preparation of solid drug nanoparticles using emulsion-templated freeze drying, the in vitro release kinetics of atovaquone from these formulations, and the in vivo efficacy of a single intramuscular dose in preventing malaria in a murine model following sporozoite challenge. In one example, a library of 42 formulations was screened for particle size, stability, and release profile, with eleven candidates selected for further evaluation based on criteria including Z-average diameter under 1000 nm, polydispersity index under 0.4, and reproducible synthesis. In another example, ATQSDN7, a formulation containing 80% atovaquone, 13% polyethylene glycol-block-polypropylene glycol-block-polyethylene glycol copolymer, and 7% TPGS, was administered intramuscularly to C57BL/6 mice at doses of 36, 100, and 200 mg/kg. Mice were challenged with 5000 Plasmodium berghei sporozoites at intervals up to 42 days after dosing. All mice receiving 100 mg/kg or higher doses remained parasite-free for the full 42-day observation period. In a separate example, mice challenged with erythrocytic forms of the parasite at equivalent time points developed parasitemia, confirming that protection was mediated by causal activity against liver-stage parasites. Plasma atovaquone concentrations remained above 200 ng/mL for at least 28 days following a single 200 mg/kg dose, and the half-life of atovaquone in plasma was determined to be 105 hours, significantly longer than the 9-hour half-life observed after oral administration.

### Materials and Methods

The materials used in the preparation of the formulations included atovaquone obtained from WIS Pharmtech Co., Ltd., polymers and surfactants sourced from Sigma-Aldrich, BASF, and Fluka Chemicals, and sterile water for injection. The emulsion-templated freeze drying process was conducted using a Covaris S2x sonicator for emulsification and a Virtis benchtop K lyophilizer for drying. Dynamic light scattering was performed using a Malvern Zetasizer Nano ZS to determine particle size and polydispersity. In vitro release kinetics were assessed using rapid equilibrium dialysis inserts in simulated interstitial fluid containing bovine serum albumin and γ-globulin. For in vivo studies, 6-week-old C57BL/6 male mice were obtained from Jackson Laboratory and housed under controlled conditions. Sporozoites were harvested from Anopheles stephensi mosquitoes fed on Plasmodium berghei ANKA-infected mice and counted using a hemocytometer. Blood-stage parasites were obtained from infected donor mice and diluted to 1.5 × 10⁵ infected erythrocytes per 200 µL. Intramuscular injections were administered into the biceps femoris muscle using a 27-gauge needle and 100 µL Hamilton syringe. Plasma samples were collected via cardiac puncture, centrifuged, and stored at −80°C until analysis by UPLC-MS/MS using a validated method with deuterated atovaquone as an internal standard. Parasitemia was assessed by Giemsa-stained thin blood smears, with a detection limit of 0.05%. Efficacy was defined as the absence of parasitemia in all animals of a cohort over a 42-day observation period across two independent experiments. Statistical significance was determined using Fisher’s exact test. Resistance testing was performed by subinoculating parasites from prophylaxis failures into naive mice and treating them with oral atovaquone at the lowest curative dose, with failure to clear parasitemia indicating resistance. All animal procedures were conducted under protocols approved by the Johns Hopkins University Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee.
