# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the combination of decanoic acid and perampanel, or other AMPA receptor inhibitors, for the treatment of neurological and neurodegenerative disorders, particularly epilepsy, ischemia, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, cancer, and Alzheimer’s disease. The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, kits, and methods of administration wherein decanoic acid and perampanel are used in tandem to achieve synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors, resulting in enhanced therapeutic efficacy and reduced adverse effects compared to either agent administered alone. The combination is specifically designed to modulate excitatory neurotransmission through noncompetitive, non-overlapping binding sites on the AMPA receptor, thereby providing a novel pharmacological strategy for conditions characterized by pathological glutamatergic hyperactivity.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Epilepsy is a chronic neurological disorder defined by recurrent, unprovoked seizures resulting from abnormal, excessive, or synchronous neuronal activity in the brain. These seizures manifest in a variety of forms, including partial-onset and generalized tonic-clonic seizures, and often persist despite treatment with conventional antiepileptic drugs. The pathophysiology of epilepsy involves dysregulation of excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmission, with particular emphasis on the overactivation of ionotropic glutamate receptors, especially the AMPA subtype, which mediates the majority of fast excitatory synaptic transmission in the central nervous system.

Current pharmacological approaches to epilepsy include sodium channel blockers, GABA enhancers, and more recently, AMPA receptor antagonists such as perampanel. Perampanel is a noncompetitive antagonist of the AMPA receptor, approved for adjunctive therapy in partial-onset and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures. However, its clinical utility is limited by dose-dependent neuropsychiatric side effects, including dizziness, somnolence, aggression, and suicidal ideation, which often necessitate dose reduction or discontinuation.

Nonpharmacological interventions, such as the ketogenic diet, have demonstrated efficacy in drug-resistant epilepsy. Among these, the medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) ketogenic diet has gained attention due to its ability to elevate circulating levels of medium-chain fatty acids, particularly decanoic acid, without requiring strict carbohydrate restriction. While ketone bodies were initially thought to mediate the antiseizure effects of the diet, recent evidence indicates that decanoic acid itself exerts direct anticonvulsant properties through inhibition of AMPA receptor function.

Despite these advances, the MCT ketogenic diet presents significant challenges, including poor palatability, gastrointestinal intolerance, and variability in metabolic response across individuals. Moreover, dietary adherence is often suboptimal, limiting its long-term sustainability. Decanoic acid, as a single bioactive component, offers a more controlled and reproducible therapeutic alternative, but its efficacy as a monotherapy is constrained by the high concentrations required for meaningful AMPA receptor inhibition.

AMPA receptors are tetrameric ligand-gated ion channels composed of GluA1–4 subunits, and their overactivation contributes to excitotoxicity in multiple neurological conditions beyond epilepsy, including ischemic stroke, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer’s. The identification of distinct molecular binding sites for different AMPA receptor antagonists has opened the possibility of combinatorial inhibition, where agents acting at non-overlapping sites may produce synergistic effects without increasing individual drug burdens.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention discloses a synergistic interaction between decanoic acid and perampanel, wherein the combined administration of these two agents results in a supra-additive inhibition of AMPA receptor-mediated currents, leading to significantly enhanced suppression of epileptiform activity and neuronal hyperexcitability. This synergy arises from the noncompetitive and spatially distinct binding of decanoic acid and perampanel to separate regions of the AMPA receptor, allowing simultaneous modulation without steric interference. The invention further demonstrates that this synergistic effect is not limited to epilepsy but extends to other pathological conditions involving glutamate excitotoxicity, including ischemia, ALS, cancer, and Alzheimer’s disease. The combination enables a reduction in the required dosage of perampanel, thereby mitigating its adverse effect profile while maintaining or improving therapeutic outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Combination

The term “combination” as used herein refers to the simultaneous, sequential, or separate administration of decanoic acid and perampanel, or an AMPA receptor inhibitor, such that their combined pharmacological effects are greater than the sum of their individual effects. Administration may occur via the same or different routes, and may be delivered in a single dosage form or in multiple formulations. Simultaneous administration includes co-formulation in a single pharmaceutical preparation, such as a tablet, capsule, or liquid suspension, wherein both active ingredients are present in fixed or variable ratios. Sequential administration may involve the prior, concurrent, or subsequent delivery of one agent following the other, with time intervals ranging from minutes to hours, depending on pharmacokinetic profiles. Separate formulations may be prescribed as distinct products, such as an oral solution of decanoic acid and a tablet of perampanel, to be taken together by the patient. A kit may comprise two or more containers, each containing one of the active agents, along with instructions for co-administration. The product may be a combined preparation in which decanoic acid and perampanel are chemically or physically integrated into a single unit, such as a lipid-based nanoparticle, emulsion, or solid dispersion. Alternative embodiments include extended-release formulations, transdermal patches, or intranasal delivery systems designed to maintain sustained plasma concentrations of both agents.

### Decanoic Acid and Compositions Comprising the Same

Decanoic acid, also known as capric acid, is a saturated medium-chain fatty acid with the chemical formula CH₃(CH₂)₈COOH. It may be administered in its free acid form, as a pharmaceutically acceptable salt such as sodium, potassium, or calcium decanoate, or as an ester derivative including methyl, ethyl, or glycerol esters. Decanoic acid may be delivered as a homotriglyceride, wherein all three fatty acid chains are decanoic acid, or as a heterotriglyceride, wherein decanoic acid constitutes one or more of the fatty acid moieties alongside other medium- or long-chain fatty acids. Compositions may be formulated to be free from or substantially free from long-chain triglycerides, polyunsaturated fatty acids, or other components that may interfere with metabolic stability or bioavailability. Natural sources of decanoic acid include coconut oil, palm kernel oil, and dairy fats, which may be purified or fractionated to enrich decanoic acid content. Synthetic methods for producing decanoic acid include chemical esterification, enzymatic hydrolysis, or microbial fermentation. Compositions may further include long-chain triglycerides to modulate absorption kinetics or to serve as a carrier for lipid-soluble agents. Additional components may include minerals such as calcium, magnesium, and zinc; vitamins such as B-complex, D, and E; antioxidants such as tocopherols; and emulsifying agents such as lecithin or polysorbates. The composition may be formulated as an oil-in-water emulsion suitable for enteral or parenteral administration, with a total fat content ranging from 10% to 80% by weight and an energy density of 7–9 kcal/g. The composition may be administered as part of a ketogenic diet, wherein the ratio of total fat to combined protein and carbohydrate intake is maintained between 2:1 and 4:1. Administration may occur orally, enterally, intravenously, intranasally, or transdermally. The composition may be incorporated into medical foods, nutritional supplements, or specialized dietary products designed for patients with neurological disorders.

### Perampanel

Perampanel is a noncompetitive antagonist of the AMPA receptor, with the chemical name 2-phenyl-N-(pyridin-3-yl)pyridine-3-carboxamide. It binds to a site located at the interface of the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linkers of the AMPA receptor, stabilizing the closed state of the ion channel and preventing glutamate-induced ion flux. Perampanel may be administered in its free base form or as a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, including hydrochloride, sulfate, or phosphate salts, to enhance solubility and stability. The typical daily dosage ranges from 2 mg to 12 mg, titrated according to clinical response and tolerability. Other AMPA receptor inhibitors that bind to the same site as perampanel may be substituted, provided they exhibit noncompetitive inhibition and do not share overlapping binding determinants with decanoic acid. Techniques for identifying and characterizing such inhibitors include electrophysiological assays using Xenopus oocytes expressing recombinant AMPA receptor subunits, patch-clamp recordings from primary neuronal cultures, and fluorescence-based calcium imaging. The IC₅₀ value for inhibition of AMPA receptor currents is determined using concentration-response curves fitted by the Hill equation. Small molecule inhibitors may include derivatives of perampanel, thiophene-based compounds, or quinoline analogs, while polypeptide inhibitors may comprise engineered peptides or monoclonal antibodies targeting the AMPA receptor extracellular domain.

### Treatment

Treatment as used herein encompasses both the prevention of seizure onset and the reduction of seizure frequency, severity, or duration. The subject to be treated is a human patient diagnosed with epilepsy, ischemia, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, cancer, or Alzheimer’s disease, or at risk of developing any of these conditions. Epilepsy is characterized by recurrent, spontaneous seizures arising from cortical or subcortical hyperexcitability, often refractory to monotherapy. AMPA receptor overactivation contributes to seizure generation and propagation, particularly in the hippocampus and neocortex. The combination of decanoic acid and perampanel provides superior control of epileptiform activity compared to either agent alone, as demonstrated by reduced IC₅₀ values and suppressed local field potential discharges. Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder involving glutamate-mediated motor neuron excitotoxicity. Elevated extracellular glutamate levels and AMPA receptor dysfunction are well-documented in ALS models and patient tissue. The combination may slow disease progression by reducing chronic excitotoxic stress. Ischemia, whether cerebral or spinal, results in energy failure and glutamate release, triggering excitotoxic cell death. AMPA receptor blockade during reperfusion has been shown to reduce infarct volume, and the combination enhances neuroprotection beyond single-agent efficacy. In cancer, particularly glioblastoma, AMPA receptor signaling promotes tumor cell proliferation and migration. In Alzheimer’s disease, synaptic loss and hyperexcitability are early features, and AMPA receptor modulation may preserve cognitive function. The combination is therefore applicable across these diverse indications, all of which share a common pathophysiological mechanism of glutamatergic dysregulation.

### Administration

Administration may occur via oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, intranasal, rectal, transdermal, or intrathecal routes, depending on the formulation and clinical context. Oral administration is preferred for chronic conditions such as epilepsy and Alzheimer’s disease, utilizing capsules, tablets, or liquid suspensions. Intravenous administration may be employed in acute settings such as status epilepticus or ischemic stroke. Intranasal delivery offers rapid CNS penetration and may be advantageous for seizure rescue. Transdermal patches may provide sustained release over 24–72 hours. Formulations may be tailored to pediatric, geriatric, or cachectic populations, with adjustments for body weight, renal function, or metabolic status. Co-administration with food may enhance absorption of decanoic acid, particularly when delivered in triglyceride form. Dosing schedules may be once daily, twice daily, or as needed, with titration guided by clinical response and plasma concentration monitoring.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Methods

Standard electrophysiological techniques were employed to record AMPA receptor-mediated currents in Xenopus laevis oocytes injected with cRNA encoding GluA1/2 or GluA2/3 subunits. Perampanel and decanoic acid were dissolved in dimethyl sulfoxide or ethanol and diluted in recording buffer. Currents were elicited by brief applications of glutamate (100 μmol/L), and drug effects were measured as percent inhibition of peak current amplitude. Seizure-like activity in rat entorhinal cortex-hippocampal slices was induced by perfusion with pentylenetetrazol (100 μmol/L). Local field potentials were recorded using extracellular electrodes, and discharge frequency was quantified over 5-minute intervals. Data were normalized to baseline and analyzed using one-way ANOVA with Dunnett’s post hoc test.

### Example 2—Direct Inhibition of AMPA Receptors by Perampanel and Decanoic Acid

Perampanel inhibited GluA2/3 and GluA1/2 receptor currents with IC₅₀ values of 8.11 μmol/L and 6.27 μmol/L, respectively, in a noncompetitive manner. A GluA3 mutant resistant to GYKI retained full sensitivity to decanoic acid but showed markedly reduced inhibition by perampanel, confirming distinct binding sites. In the presence of 1 μmol/L or 4 μmol/L perampanel, the IC₅₀ of decanoic acid decreased from 0.52 mmol/L to 0.15 mmol/L and 0.12 mmol/L for GluA2/3, and from 1.16 mmol/L to 0.21 mmol/L and 0.10 mmol/L for GluA1/2. Conversely, the IC₅₀ of perampanel decreased from 8.1 μmol/L to 1.7 μmol/L and 1.6 μmol/L in the presence of 50 and 100 μmol/L decanoic acid, respectively. Isobolographic analysis confirmed synergistic interaction, with combination points falling below the additive line for both receptor subtypes.

### Example 3—Effects of Perampanel and Decanoic Acid on Seizure Models

In rat hippocampal slices, decanoic acid alone reduced seizure-like activity with an IC₅₀ of 352 μmol/L. When combined with 100 nmol/L perampanel, the IC₅₀ decreased to 196 μmol/L, and with 500 nmol/L perampanel, it decreased further to 122 μmol/L. In human neocortical slices, decanoic acid (100 μmol/L) and perampanel (0.5 μmol/L) individually failed to significantly reduce epileptiform activity, but their combination reduced the area power of local field potentials by over 90%, achieving statistical significance (p < 0.0001) and virtually abolishing pathological network activity.

## SUMMARY

The data demonstrate that the combination of decanoic acid and perampanel produces a synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors, resulting in significantly enhanced suppression of neuronal hyperexcitability in both animal and human tissue models. This synergy arises from non-overlapping binding sites and is not attributable to pharmacokinetic interactions. The combination enables lower dosing of perampanel, reducing its adverse effect burden while maintaining or improving seizure control. The therapeutic potential extends beyond epilepsy to ischemia, ALS, cancer, and Alzheimer’s disease, all of which involve pathological AMPA receptor activation. The invention provides a novel, mechanistically grounded strategy for treating neurological disorders through rational polypharmacology, offering improved efficacy, safety, and patient compliance.
