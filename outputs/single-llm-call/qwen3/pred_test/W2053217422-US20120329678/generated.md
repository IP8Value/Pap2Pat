# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

High-throughput sequencing technologies have revolutionized the field of genomics by enabling rapid, cost-effective generation of vast quantities of DNA sequence data. These platforms, including those developed by Illumina, Roche 454, and Life Technologies, rely on short-read sequencing to reconstruct entire genomes from fragmented DNA. Despite their throughput and scalability, these technologies face inherent limitations in de novo genome assembly due to the short length of individual sequencing reads, typically ranging from 50 to 300 base pairs. This constraint severely hampers the ability to resolve repetitive genomic regions, resolve large-scale structural variations, or accurately scaffold contiguous sequences into complete chromosomes. To overcome these challenges, mate-pair sequencing strategies have been developed to provide long-range contextual information by capturing the relative orientation and approximate distance between two distant segments of genomic DNA. However, conventional methods for constructing mate-pair libraries suffer from significant technical drawbacks. Existing protocols often involve multiple enzymatic steps, including random shearing, end repair, adapter ligation, size selection, circularization, and secondary fragmentation, each introducing potential biases, loss of complexity, and low yields. Many approaches rely on ligation-based circularization, which is inefficient and prone to generating chimeric products due to intermolecular ligation events. Furthermore, the absence of a defined junction sequence between the paired ends in some systems prevents reliable identification of true mate-pair reads, leading to misassembly and elevated rates of false-positive structural variant calls. Commercially available kits, while standardized, are often restricted to insert sizes below five kilobases and require proprietary reagents that limit flexibility and scalability. Additionally, methods employing in vivo cloning systems, such as fosmid or BAC vectors, yield libraries with limited complexity due to host cell transformation biases and clonal expansion artifacts. These shortcomings collectively impede the generation of high-quality, large-insert mate-pair libraries that are essential for accurate de novo assembly, genome finishing, and comprehensive structural variant detection.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel, in vitro method for constructing high-quality, large-insert mate-pair libraries using a combination of Cre-LoxP site-specific recombination and inverse PCR amplification. The method enables the efficient circularization of randomly sheared genomic DNA fragments through the action of Cre recombinase acting on flanking LoxP sequences, thereby bringing distant ends of the original DNA molecule into close proximity. Following circularization, the DNA is subjected to controlled fragmentation, after which linear fragments are self-ligated to form closed loops containing the original paired ends and a defined junction sequence. These re-circularized molecules are then selectively amplified using primers complementary to the adapter sequences flanking the LoxP sites, resulting in a highly enriched population of mate-pair fragments suitable for high-throughput sequencing. The method eliminates the need for multiple ligation steps, reduces chimeric artifact formation, and introduces a precisely defined junction site that allows unambiguous identification of true mate-pair reads during downstream data analysis. The invention further enables the generation of mate-pair libraries with insert sizes exceeding twenty kilobases, far surpassing the capabilities of conventional ligation-based approaches. The use of inverse PCR ensures that only molecules containing both ends of the original genomic fragment, correctly oriented and joined via recombination, are amplified, thereby maximizing library specificity and complexity. This approach is fully compatible with standard Illumina sequencing platforms and does not require proprietary reagents or specialized instrumentation beyond those commonly available in molecular biology laboratories.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Definitions

A mate-pair library refers to a collection of DNA fragments in which each fragment contains two sequences derived from distant regions of the original genomic DNA, separated by a known insert size and oriented in a reverse-complementary configuration relative to one another. Forward and reverse primers are oligonucleotide sequences designed to bind to complementary strands of a target DNA molecule in opposing orientations, enabling amplification of the intervening sequence. A distal site denotes a genomic location that is physically separated from another site by a defined distance, typically greater than one kilobase, and is captured as a paired end within a single sequencing fragment. Circularized DNA refers to a covalently closed DNA molecule formed by the ligation of two ends of a linear DNA fragment, either through enzymatic recombination or chemical ligation. Primer sites in opposing directions describe the spatial arrangement of two primer binding sequences located at opposite ends of a DNA molecule such that amplification proceeds outward from each site toward the center. Recombinase is an enzyme that catalyzes site-specific recombination between two defined DNA sequences, such as Cre recombinase acting on loxP sites. Recombination sites are short, specific DNA sequences recognized and acted upon by a recombinase enzyme, resulting in precise cleavage and rejoining of DNA strands.

### I. Introduction

The present invention introduces a novel methodology for generating improved mate-pair libraries that overcomes the limitations of prior art by integrating site-specific recombination with inverse PCR amplification. This approach leverages the high efficiency and specificity of the Cre-LoxP recombination system to circularize large genomic fragments, thereby ensuring that only intramolecular ligation events are favored, minimizing the formation of chimeric products. The use of recombinase-mediated circularization eliminates the need for inefficient intermolecular ligation steps that plague conventional methods, resulting in libraries with significantly higher complexity and lower background noise. Furthermore, the inverse PCR strategy employed in this invention ensures that amplification is restricted exclusively to molecules containing the original paired ends connected via the recombination junction. This selective amplification dramatically enriches for true mate-pair fragments while suppressing non-specific amplification products. The introduction of a defined junction sequence, formed at the site of recombination, enables precise trimming of sequencing reads to avoid contamination from overlapping sequences, thereby permitting the use of longer read lengths without compromising data integrity. This innovation facilitates more accurate alignment, improved scaffold construction, and enhanced detection of structural variants across complex genomes.

### II. Generation of Mate-Pair Libraries

The method begins with the fragmentation of source genomic DNA using mechanical shearing to produce fragments of a desired size range, typically between five and twenty-two kilobases. The sheared DNA fragments are then subjected to end-repair using a combination of T4 DNA polymerase, Klenow fragment, and T4 polynucleotide kinase to generate blunt-ended molecules suitable for adapter ligation. Following end-repair, the fragments are purified using size-selective magnetic beads to remove small fragments and debris. Next, double-stranded adapters containing LoxP recombination sites and Illumina P1 or P2 primer binding sequences are ligated to the ends of the DNA fragments using a high-efficiency DNA ligase. The ligated fragments are then treated with Bst DNA polymerase to fill in any overhangs, ensuring uniform blunt termini. Circularization is achieved by incubating the adapter-ligated DNA with Cre recombinase under optimized conditions, which catalyzes recombination between the two LoxP sites on the same molecule, forming a circular DNA structure. Non-circularized linear DNA is subsequently degraded using an ATP-dependent DNase to eliminate background material. The circularized DNA is then subjected to secondary fragmentation using a restriction enzyme that recognizes a four-base sequence absent from the adapter-LoxP region, generating linear fragments with cohesive ends. These fragments are allowed to self-ligate under conditions favoring intramolecular circularization, reforming closed loops that now contain the original paired ends flanking the recombination junction. The resulting circular molecules are amplified by inverse PCR using primers complementary to the P1 and P2 sequences, which bind in outward-facing orientations to amplify only the region spanning the original genomic insert. The amplified products are purified by gel electrophoresis to isolate fragments of approximately 300 to 600 base pairs, which are then ready for high-throughput sequencing. Alternative recombinase systems, such as Flp-FRT or PhiC31-attB/attP, may be substituted for Cre-loxP to provide additional flexibility. Different restriction enzymes, ligases, polymerases, and adaptor designs may be employed to optimize performance for specific genome types or sequencing platforms.

### III. Software and Data Analysis

The sequencing reads generated from the mate-pair libraries are processed using a combination of bioinformatic tools designed to maximize alignment accuracy and minimize false positives. Raw reads are first trimmed to remove adapter sequences and low-quality bases, with special attention paid to the junction region defined by the restriction enzyme recognition site, which serves as a molecular marker distinguishing the two ends of the original fragment. Sequence alignment is performed using Burrows-Wheeler-based algorithms such as BWA or Bowtie2 to map reads against a reference genome with high sensitivity and specificity. For de novo assembly, assemblies are constructed using velvet or SPAdes with parameters adjusted to account for the known insert size and orientation of the mate-pair reads. DNA assembly algorithms are configured to prioritize joins supported by multiple mate-pair connections, thereby improving scaffold continuity. The presence of the defined junction site enables the exclusion of chimeric reads that might otherwise be misinterpreted as genuine long-range linkages. Read trimming is performed to eliminate sequences downstream of the restriction site, ensuring that each read contains only one end of the original fragment. This strategy significantly reduces ambiguity in read pairing and enhances the reliability of structural variant calling.

### IV. Kits

The invention further encompasses kits for the practical implementation of the described method. Each kit includes pre-optimized reagents sufficient for the preparation of multiple mate-pair libraries. The kit components include purified Cre recombinase enzyme, synthetic LoxP-containing adapters with integrated P1 and P2 primer sequences, T4 DNA polymerase, Klenow fragment, T4 polynucleotide kinase, Quick Ligase, Bst DNA polymerase, ATP-dependent DNase, and a selection of restriction enzymes such as NlaIII and HpyCH4IV. The kit also contains size-selection beads, purification columns, PCR master mix containing Phusion DNA polymerase, and detailed protocols for library preparation across a range of insert sizes. Optional components include pre-validated primer sets for alternative sequencing platforms, control DNA templates, and software tools for post-sequencing analysis. All reagents are supplied in lyophilized or stabilized liquid form to ensure consistency and long-term storage stability.

## EXAMPLES

### CLIP-PE Methodology

The CLIP-PE method was developed to address the limitations of existing mate-pair library preparation techniques. The procedure involves the sequential application of mechanical shearing, end repair, LoxP-adapter ligation, Cre-mediated circularization, restriction digestion, self-ligation, and inverse PCR amplification. This workflow was applied to genomic DNA from Haloterrigena turkmenica and Saccharomyces cerevisiae to generate libraries with insert sizes of 5 kb, 12 kb, and 22 kb. The resulting libraries exhibited a markedly higher proportion of correctly paired reads compared to those generated using the Illumina jumping library protocol. The inclusion of a defined junction site enabled the use of 76-bp sequencing reads without the risk of chimeric contamination, a feat unattainable with conventional ligation-based methods. The method consistently produced libraries with low chimeric rates, high coverage depth, and minimal gaps in genome assembly.

### Advantages of CLIP-PE Over Existing Methods

Compared to the Illumina jumping method, CLIP-PE libraries demonstrated a 2.4-fold increase in non-redundant mate-pair reads with the expected insert size and a four-fold reduction in chimeric reads. Unlike the 454 Jump Recombi system, CLIP-PE does not require proprietary reagents or specialized platforms and achieves higher throughput using standard Illumina sequencers. The elimination of multiple ligation steps reduces background noise and increases library complexity. The use of inverse PCR ensures that only molecules with the correct topology are amplified, resulting in superior enrichment efficiency. The defined junction site allows for precise read trimming, enabling longer read lengths and improved downstream analysis.

### CLIP-PE Library Construction

The construction of CLIP-PE libraries followed a standardized protocol involving six major steps: DNA shearing, end repair, adapter ligation, circularization, fragmentation, and inverse PCR amplification. The use of NlaIII for secondary fragmentation yielded the highest efficiency, producing libraries with 11.1% non-redundant pairs compared to 2.5% for blunt-end shearing. The method was successfully applied to genomes with GC content ranging from 28% to 74%, with no detectable coverage bias. The resulting libraries were sequenced on Illumina GAIIx platforms, producing high-quality data suitable for de novo assembly.

### Comparison with Illumina Jumping Method

When compared side-by-side, CLIP-PE libraries generated from H. turkmenica at 5 kb insert size yielded 20.6% non-redundant pairs, compared to only 8.7% for the Illumina jumping library. The average clone coverage was 4,746× for CLIP-PE versus 18× for Illumina. The number of uncovered gaps in the reference genome was reduced from 767 to just seven. Chimeric read rates were reduced from 9.2% to 2.3%. These results demonstrate a clear superiority of CLIP-PE in both yield and fidelity.

### Quality Assessment of CLIP-PE Libraries

Library quality was assessed using three criteria: the percentage of non-redundant mate-pair reads with the expected insert size, the percentage of chimeric reads, and the number of uncovered gaps in the reference genome. CLIP-PE libraries consistently outperformed competing methods across all metrics. The 12 kb and 22 kb libraries from S. cerevisiae demonstrated high reproducibility, with average non-redundant pair rates of 59% and 11.2%, respectively, and chimeric rates below 1.7%.

### Effects of Ligation Efficiency

The efficiency of self-ligation following restriction digestion was found to be a critical determinant of library quality. Enzymes producing 4 bp overhangs, such as NlaIII, yielded significantly higher non-redundant pair rates than those producing 2 bp overhangs or blunt ends. The use of cohesive ends increased ligation efficiency by more than four-fold, directly translating into improved library complexity and reduced background noise.

### Genome Assembly Improvements

Combining CLIP-PE libraries with standard short-insert Illumina data resulted in a seven-fold increase in scaffold N50 values compared to short-read assemblies alone. The number of scaffolds was reduced by more than 30%, and misassembly rates remained low. Simulated mate-pair datasets produced comparable results, confirming that the quality of CLIP-PE libraries is sufficient to support high-fidelity genome assembly.

### Discussion

The CLIP-PE method represents a significant advancement in mate-pair library construction by combining the specificity of site-specific recombination with the selectivity of inverse PCR. Unlike in vivo cloning methods, CLIP-PE generates libraries with high complexity and no host bias. Compared to ligation-based methods, it eliminates multiple enzymatic steps and reduces chimeric artifacts. The defined junction site enables longer read lengths and more accurate data analysis. While restriction enzyme bias is theoretically possible, the initial random shearing step ensures comprehensive genome coverage. The method is scalable, cost-effective, and compatible with existing sequencing infrastructure. Potential applications include de novo assembly of complex eukaryotic genomes, detection of large structural variants, and genome finishing in clinical and agricultural genomics. The versatility of the system allows adaptation to other recombinase systems and sequencing platforms, making it a broadly applicable tool for next-generation genomics.
