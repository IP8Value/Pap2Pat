## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of pharmaceutical compositions and therapeutic methods directed to the modulation of growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor signaling for the treatment of pulmonary fibrosis, lung inflammation, and neoplastic diseases. Specifically, the invention relates to synthetic peptide antagonists of the growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor that exhibit potent inhibitory activity against pathological cellular proliferation, fibrotic remodeling, and inflammatory cascades in the lung and other tissues. The invention further encompasses methods of identifying subjects suffering from or at risk of developing pulmonary fibrosis, lung cancer, or chronic inflammatory lung conditions through the detection of GHRH receptor expression or activity, and the administration of GHRH receptor antagonists to ameliorate disease progression. The compositions and methods disclosed herein are particularly useful in conditions characterized by aberrant fibroblast activation, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and dysregulated T-cell signaling, including idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, sarcoidosis, and non-small cell lung carcinoma. The invention represents a novel therapeutic paradigm that targets the GHRH receptor pathway independently of the classical pituitary-growth hormone-insulin-like growth factor-1 axis, thereby offering a mechanism-based intervention with reduced systemic endocrine side effects.

## BACKGROUND

Growth hormone-releasing hormone is a 44-amino acid hypothalamic peptide that stimulates the synthesis and secretion of growth hormone from anterior pituitary somatotrophs. Beyond its endocrine role, GHRH and its receptor are expressed in a wide array of extra-pituitary tissues, including the lung, where they mediate paracrine and autocrine signaling events that influence cellular proliferation, survival, and tissue repair. The presence of a truncated, ligand-independent splice variant of the GHRH receptor, designated SV1, has been identified in human lung epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and tumor tissues, suggesting a physiological role for GHRH signaling in local tissue homeostasis and pathology. In the context of lung disease, GHRH has been implicated in promoting fibroblast activation, extracellular matrix deposition, and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition through activation of the PI3K/Akt, ERK, and STAT3 signaling pathways. Elevated levels of GHRH have been detected in the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid of patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis and in tumor tissues of patients with lung adenocarcinoma and carcinoid tumors. Furthermore, GHRH has been shown to enhance the production of reactive oxygen species and to suppress mitochondrial respiration in lung fibroblasts, contributing to a pro-fibrotic and pro-inflammatory microenvironment. In cancer, GHRH acts as a mitogen for tumor cells expressing the GHRH receptor, stimulating proliferation and inhibiting apoptosis through downregulation of p53 and p21 and upregulation of cyclin-dependent kinases. The discovery that synthetic GHRH receptor antagonists can reverse these pathological processes without significantly altering systemic growth hormone levels has opened a new therapeutic avenue for the treatment of chronic lung diseases and malignancies. Prior attempts to target the GHRH pathway have been limited by the lack of stable, high-affinity peptide antagonists capable of penetrating tissue and resisting enzymatic degradation. The present invention overcomes these limitations through the development of structurally optimized GHRH receptor antagonists with enhanced pharmacokinetic properties and potent in vivo efficacy.

## SUMMARY

The invention discloses a novel class of synthetic peptides that function as high-affinity antagonists of the growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor, wherein these peptides are capable of inhibiting pulmonary fibrosis, reducing lung inflammation, and suppressing tumor growth in vivo and in vitro. The peptides are characterized by specific amino acid substitutions that confer resistance to proteolytic cleavage, increased receptor binding affinity, and enhanced tissue penetration. The invention further encompasses methods of treating pulmonary fibrosis by administering to a subject in need an effective amount of a GHRH receptor antagonist, thereby reducing collagen deposition, suppressing fibroblast proliferation, and ameliorating histopathological signs of fibrosis. The invention also provides methods for reducing lung inflammation by inhibiting the infiltration of T cells and macrophages, downregulating the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and suppressing the activation of the NF-κB and STAT3 signaling pathways. Additionally, the invention describes methods for inhibiting tumor growth in lung cancer and other malignancies by inducing apoptosis, disrupting mitochondrial function, and downregulating oncogenic signaling cascades such as PI3K/Akt and ERK. The disclosed GHRH receptor antagonists are effective in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings and may be administered alone or in combination with standard-of-care therapies including chemotherapy, radiation, immunotherapy, and surgical resection. The invention further contemplates the use of these antagonists to reduce the expression of T cell receptor complex genes, inhibit epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, and enhance mitochondrial respiration in lung fibroblasts, thereby restoring cellular homeostasis and promoting tissue repair.

The invention provides novel compositions comprising synthetic peptides that act as potent and selective antagonists of the growth hormone-releasing hormone receptor. These peptides are designed to bind with high affinity to the GHRH receptor and block its activation by endogenous GHRH, thereby inhibiting downstream signaling pathways that drive fibrosis, inflammation, and tumor growth. A preferred embodiment of the invention is the GHRH receptor antagonist MIA-602, which exhibits nanomolar binding affinity and potent in vivo efficacy in murine models of pulmonary fibrosis and lung cancer. Additional embodiments include the peptides AVR-235, AVR-333, AVR-352, AVR-353, AVR-354, AVR-104, AVR-107, and AVR-116, each of which contains conserved structural motifs including D-Arg2, Har9, Abu15, Nle27, Orn12, Orn21, His11, His20, and pentafluoro-Phe6, which confer metabolic stability and receptor specificity. ## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Synthesis of GHRH Antagonists

The synthetic GHRH receptor antagonists described herein were prepared using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques employing Fmoc-chemistry on Rink amide resin. The C-terminus was modified to yield either a methylamide or ethylamide, depending on the desired pharmacokinetic profile. Side chains of amino acids such as aspartic acid, glutamic acid, lysine, arginine, and tyrosine were protected with tert-butyl, trityl, Boc, and tert-butyloxycarbonyl groups, respectively, to prevent unwanted reactions during coupling. Coupling reactions were performed using HBTU and HOAt as activating agents in DMF, with double coupling cycles for sterically hindered residues. After completion of chain assembly, the peptide-resin was treated with a cleavage cocktail consisting of TFA, water, triisopropylsilane, and ethanedithiol to remove protecting groups and release the peptide from the resin. The crude peptide was precipitated in cold diethyl ether, centrifuged, and washed repeatedly to remove residual solvents and byproducts. The resulting peptide was purified by preparative reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) using a C18 column and a gradient of water and acetonitrile, both containing 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid. Fractions containing the target peptide were collected, lyophilized, and analyzed by analytical HPLC to confirm purity exceeding 95%. Mass spectrometry analysis using electrospray ionization time-of-flight (ESI-TOF) confirmed the molecular weight of each peptide within ±0.1 Da of the theoretical value. The synthesis of AVR-235, AVR-354, and other analogs followed identical protocols with specific substitutions at positions 6, 10, 12, 20, and 21. The synthesis of C-terminal amide compounds was achieved by using Rink amide resin, while ethylamide derivatives were prepared using Sieber amide resin. The synthesis of AVR-333, AVR-352, and AVR-353 involved sequential incorporation of ornithine at positions 12 and 21, pentafluorophenylalanine at position 6, and hydroxyarginine at the C-terminus. All synthesized peptides were characterized for identity, purity, and stability under physiological conditions prior to biological testing.

### Example 2: Evaluation of Inhibitory Effects of GHRH Antagonists In Vitro

Human lung cancer cell lines including A549, H460, and H1299, as well as primary human lung fibroblasts isolated from idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis patients, were cultured in RPMI-1640 or DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 5,000 cells per well and allowed to adhere overnight. The GHRH receptor antagonists MIA-602, AVR-235, AVR-354, and other analogs were dissolved in DMSO and diluted in culture medium to final concentrations ranging from 0.1 nM to 10 μM. Cells were treated for 24, 48, and 72 hours, after which cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. Absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader, and viability was calculated relative to untreated controls. Dose-response curves were generated, and IC50 values were determined using nonlinear regression analysis. MIA-602 demonstrated an IC50 of 1.2 μM in A549 cells and 0.8 μM in fibroblasts, while AVR-354 exhibited an IC50 of 0.4 μM in both cell types, indicating superior potency. Inhibition of proliferation was confirmed by BrdU incorporation assays and flow cytometric analysis of cell cycle distribution, which revealed G1/S arrest. Inhibition of GHRH receptor signaling was further confirmed by Western blot analysis showing dose-dependent downregulation of phosphorylated Akt, ERK, and STAT3, and upregulation of p53 and p21. These results demonstrate that the disclosed GHRH receptor antagonists effectively inhibit proliferation and induce cell cycle arrest in both cancerous and fibrotic lung cells.

### Example 3: Evaluation of Tumor Inhibitory Activity of GHRH Antagonists In Vivo

Nude mice (Crl:NU-Foxn1nu) were subcutaneously injected with 5 × 10^6 A549 human lung adenocarcinoma cells suspended in Matrigel. Once tumors reached a mean volume of 100 mm³, mice were randomized into treatment groups (n=8 per group) and administered daily subcutaneous injections of either vehicle (saline with 5% DMSO) or the GHRH receptor antagonist MIA-602 at doses of 1, 3, or 5 μg/kg/day. Treatment continued for 21 days, during which tumor volume was measured every 3 days using calipers and calculated according to the formula V = (L × W²)/2, where L is length and W is width. At the end of the study, mice were euthanized and tumors were excised, weighed, and processed for histological and molecular analysis. Mice treated with 5 μg/kg/day MIA-602 exhibited a 72% reduction in mean tumor volume compared to vehicle controls (p < 0.001). Tumor weights were reduced by 68% in the high-dose group. Immunohistochemical staining of tumor sections revealed decreased Ki-67 staining, increased TUNEL positivity, and reduced CD31-positive microvessel density in treated animals. Western blot analysis of tumor lysates confirmed downregulation of phospho-Akt, phospho-ERK, and cyclin D1, and upregulation of cleaved caspase-3 and Bax. No significant changes in body weight or serum growth hormone levels were observed, indicating a lack of systemic endocrine toxicity. These results demonstrate that the GHRH receptor antagonist MIA-602 potently inhibits tumor growth in vivo without inducing systemic hormonal disruption.

### Example 4: Binding Affinities of AVR GHRH Antagonists

Competitive binding assays were performed using membranes prepared from HEK293 cells stably transfected with human GHRH receptor cDNA. Cells were lysed in ice-cold buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4, 10 mM MgCl2, and protease inhibitors. Membranes were incubated with 0.1 nM [125I]-labeled GHRH(1-29)NH2 and increasing concentrations of unlabeled GHRH receptor antagonists (AVR-235, AVR-333, AVR-352, AVR-353, AVR-354, MIA-602) for 90 minutes at 4°C. Non-specific binding was determined in the presence of 1 μM unlabeled GHRH. Bound radioactivity was separated by filtration through glass fiber filters and quantified using a gamma counter. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism software to determine IC50 values and Ki values using the Cheng-Prusoff equation. MIA-602 exhibited a Ki of 0.8 nM, while AVR-354 demonstrated a Ki of 0.3 nM, indicating a 2.7-fold increase in binding affinity compared to MIA-602. AVR-333 and AVR-352 exhibited Ki values of 0.6 nM and 0.5 nM, respectively. All analogs showed greater affinity than native GHRH(1-29)NH2 (Ki = 4.2 nM). These results confirm that the structural modifications incorporated into the AVR series significantly enhance receptor binding affinity, providing a molecular basis for their superior biological activity.

### Example 5: Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone Receptor Antagonist Modulates Lung Inflammation and Fibrosis Due to Bleomycin

C57BL/6 mice were administered a single intratracheal dose of bleomycin (2.5 U/kg) to induce pulmonary fibrosis. Twenty-four hours later, mice were randomized into four groups (n=10 per group): vehicle control, bleomycin alone, bleomycin + MIA-602 (5 μg/kg/day), and bleomycin + pirfenidone (200 mg/kg/day). Treatments were administered subcutaneously daily for 21 days. On day 21, mice underwent micro-CT imaging to assess lung density and fibrotic burden. Lungs were then harvested for hydroxyproline assay, histopathological analysis, RNA sequencing, and fibroblast isolation. Micro-CT scans revealed a 64% reduction in lung density in MIA-602-treated mice compared to bleomycin-only controls. Hydroxyproline content was reduced by 58% in the MIA-602 group, comparable to the reduction observed with pirfenidone. Histopathological scoring using Masson’s trichrome and H&E staining showed significant reduction in collagen deposition, alveolar wall thickening, and inflammatory cell infiltration in the MIA-602-treated group. RNA sequencing of lung tissue revealed downregulation of genes involved in extracellular matrix organization, Wnt signaling, and T-cell activation, including Col1a1, Col3a1, Fn1, Wnt4, CD3E, CD4, and CD8A. Upregulated genes included those involved in mitochondrial respiration, antioxidant defense, and apoptosis, such as Sod2, Prdx3, Bax, and Casp3. In vitro studies using primary lung fibroblasts isolated from bleomycin-treated mice demonstrated that MIA-602 restored basal and maximal oxygen consumption rates, increased spare respiratory capacity, and reduced mitochondrial ROS production. MIA-602 also suppressed phosphorylation of PAK1 and STAT3, and disrupted PI3K/Akt signaling. These findings demonstrate that MIA-602 exerts multi-modal anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory effects through modulation of mitochondrial function, signaling pathways, and immune cell activity, establishing its potential as a novel therapeutic agent for pulmonary fibrosis.
