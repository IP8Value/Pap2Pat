# DESCRIPTION

## 1. FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncological diagnostics, specifically to the detection, diagnosis, and monitoring of renal cell carcinoma (RCC) through the measurement of humoral biomarkers in biological fluids. Renal cell carcinoma is a highly heterogeneous malignancy arising from the epithelial cells of the renal tubules and represents the most common type of kidney cancer in adults. It often presents asymptomatically in its early stages, leading to delayed diagnosis and poor prognosis in advanced cases. Current diagnostic approaches rely heavily on imaging modalities such as computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging, which are costly, expose patients to ionizing radiation, and lack the sensitivity to detect microscopic or preclinical disease. There remains a critical unmet need for non-invasive, reliable, and reproducible biomarkers that can facilitate early detection, risk stratification, and longitudinal monitoring of RCC. The present invention addresses this need by identifying matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP-7) as a novel humoral biomarker detectable in serum and other bodily fluids. A humoral biomarker, as defined herein, refers to a molecular entity secreted or released into the circulatory system by tumor cells or in response to tumor presence, which can be quantitatively measured in blood or other extracellular fluids to provide diagnostic, prognostic, or therapeutic monitoring information. Unlike tissue-based markers, humoral biomarkers offer the advantage of being accessible through minimally invasive sampling, enabling repeated assessments over time without the need for surgical intervention.

## 2. BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Renal cell carcinoma frequently manifests with nonspecific symptoms such as hematuria, flank pain, and palpable abdominal masses, which typically emerge only after the tumor has progressed to an advanced stage. Early-stage RCC is often asymptomatic, and as a result, many patients are diagnosed incidentally during imaging for unrelated conditions. Current diagnostic methods rely predominantly on radiological techniques, including contrast-enhanced computed tomography, ultrasound, and magnetic resonance imaging. While these modalities provide anatomical information, they lack the ability to distinguish indolent from aggressive tumors, predict recurrence, or monitor treatment response in real time. Efforts to identify molecular markers for RCC have been ongoing for decades, with various candidates investigated, including interleukin-6, fibroblast growth factor, and plasminogen activator inhibitor-1, each demonstrating limited sensitivity or specificity in clinical validation studies. E-cadherin expression has been explored as a potential indicator of epithelial-mesenchymal transition in RCC, yet its utility is constrained by the necessity of tissue biopsy and intra-tumoral heterogeneity. International patent application WO 02/082076 describes the use of serum markers in renal cancer, but focuses exclusively on cytokines and growth factors without addressing the role of proteolytic enzymes such as MMP-7. Despite extensive research, no single serum biomarker has achieved regulatory approval or widespread clinical adoption for RCC diagnosis or monitoring. The absence of a reliable, non-invasive marker has hindered efforts to implement population-based screening, particularly in high-risk cohorts such as patients with von Hippel-Lindau syndrome, end-stage renal disease on dialysis, or post-transplant immunosuppression. The limitations of existing approaches underscore the urgent need for a novel, robust, and specific humoral biomarker capable of detecting RCC at an early, curable stage and providing actionable information throughout the clinical trajectory of the disease.

## 3. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP-7) as a novel humoral biomarker for the diagnosis and management of renal cell carcinoma. MMP-7, also known as matrilysin, is a member of the zinc-dependent endopeptidase family involved in extracellular matrix remodeling, epithelial cell migration, and modulation of cell surface signaling molecules. In healthy individuals, MMP-7 is expressed at low levels primarily in epithelial tissues of the gastrointestinal tract, uterus, and mammary glands, and its presence in serum is typically undetectable or present at negligible concentrations. The invention demonstrates, for the first time, that serum levels of MMP-7, particularly in its pro-form, are significantly elevated in patients with renal cell carcinoma compared to healthy individuals and patients with benign renal conditions or other malignancies. The term “level,” as used in the context of this invention, refers to the quantitative concentration of MMP-7 or its pro-form in a biological sample, expressed in mass per volume units such as micrograms per liter or nanograms per milliliter, and determined by immunoassay or other validated analytical method. The structure of MMP-7 includes a signal peptide, a pro-domain containing a conserved cysteine switch motif, a catalytic domain with a zinc-binding site, and a hemopexin-like C-terminal domain. Activation occurs through proteolytic cleavage of the pro-domain, yielding the mature, enzymatically active form. However, the present invention reveals that in the serum of RCC patients, the predominant circulating form is the inactive pro-MMP-7, which retains immunogenic properties and is detectable at significantly elevated concentrations. Prior art has described MMP-7 overexpression in various epithelial cancers, including colorectal, breast, and pancreatic carcinomas, but none have identified serum MMP-7 as a diagnostic marker for RCC. The novelty of this invention lies in the discovery that serum MMP-7 levels correlate specifically and robustly with the presence of renal cell carcinoma, independent of tumor stage or histological subtype. This biomarker enables a wide range of clinical applications, including early detection in high-risk populations, differential diagnosis of renal masses, monitoring of therapeutic response, detection of minimal residual disease, and identification of tumor recurrence following nephrectomy. The invention further encompasses methods for measuring MMP-7 using highly specific monoclonal antibodies, standardized immunoassays, and diagnostic kits, all of which provide a non-invasive, reproducible, and scalable platform for clinical implementation.

## MMP-7

Matrix metalloproteinase-7 is a 28-kDa precursor protein composed of a 19-amino acid signal peptide, a 100-amino acid pro-domain, a 200-amino acid catalytic domain, and a 70-amino acid hemopexin-like domain. The pro-domain contains a conserved cysteine residue that coordinates with the catalytic zinc ion, maintaining the enzyme in an inactive state until proteolytic cleavage occurs. Activation is mediated by other proteases such as plasmin, furin, or other MMPs, resulting in the release of the pro-peptide and formation of the mature 19-kDa enzyme. MMP-7 exhibits broad substrate specificity, cleaving components of the extracellular matrix including fibronectin, laminin, collagen IV, and proteoglycans, as well as non-matrix substrates such as E-cadherin, Fas ligand, and pro-tumor necrosis factor-alpha. Its expression is predominantly restricted to epithelial cells under physiological conditions, but aberrant overexpression has been documented in multiple epithelial malignancies. In renal cell carcinoma, MMP-7 is overexpressed at the mRNA and protein levels within tumor tissue, yet prior studies have failed to establish its presence as a circulating biomarker. The inventors have now demonstrated, through comprehensive proteomic and serological analyses, that pro-MMP-7 is secreted by RCC tumor cells into the bloodstream and is detectable in patient serum at significantly elevated concentrations. Traditional methods of mRNA quantification are insufficient for clinical application due to tissue heterogeneity, sampling bias, and the inability to reflect systemic tumor burden. The present invention overcomes these limitations by focusing on the humoral form of MMP-7, which integrates signals from all tumor sites and reflects dynamic changes in tumor activity. The discovery that pro-MMP-7, rather than the mature form, is the predominant circulating species in RCC patients represents a critical insight, as it suggests that the tumor microenvironment may actively suppress activation or that the pro-form is stabilized by serum proteins. The invention further identifies that monoclonal antibodies raised against recombinant MMP-7 recognize both the pro-form and mature form, enabling the development of sandwich immunoassays with high sensitivity and specificity. Detection of MMP-7 in serum is performed using time-resolved fluorescent immunoassays, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, or other quantitative platforms, with results correlating strongly with tumor presence and burden. Autoantibodies against MMP-7 or pro-MMP-7 are also detected in a subset of RCC patients, further supporting the immunogenic nature of this protein and offering potential for dual-marker detection strategies. The invention provides a method for aiding in the diagnosis of RCC by measuring serum MMP-7 levels above a predefined threshold, which has been empirically determined to yield a sensitivity of 93% and a specificity of 75% in validation cohorts. Monitoring of treatment efficacy is achieved by serial measurement of MMP-7 levels, where a decline correlates with tumor regression and a rise suggests progression or recurrence. The invention further encompasses the use of MMP-7 in combination with other biomarkers to improve diagnostic accuracy, and provides a diagnostic kit comprising immobilized anti-MMP-7 antibodies, detection reagents, calibrators, and instructions for use in clinical laboratories.

## 4. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION AND EXAMPLES

Serum samples were collected from 30 patients diagnosed with renal cell carcinoma, 40 patients with benign renal conditions or non-renal malignancies, and 30 healthy individuals as controls. All samples were obtained prior to any surgical or pharmacological intervention and processed within 30 minutes of venipuncture by centrifugation at 2000g for 15 minutes at 4°C. Supernatants were aliquoted and stored at −20°C until analysis. The CAL54 human renal cell carcinoma cell line was cultured in serum-free medium for 48 hours, and secreted proteins were concentrated by ethanol precipitation and subjected to two-dimensional electrophoresis. Immunoblotting with pooled sera from RCC patients revealed a distinct immunoreactive spot at 29 kDa and pI 7.7, identified by MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry as pro-MMP-7. Monoclonal antibodies against human MMP-7 were generated by immunizing BALB/c mice with recombinant protein, followed by hybridoma selection and purification via protein A affinity chromatography. Two clones, 01S and 29S, were selected for their ability to recognize both pro-MMP-7 and mature MMP-7 in Western blot analysis of CAL54 supernatant. A homogeneous sandwich immunoassay based on time-resolved amplified cryptate emission (TRACE) technology was developed using cryptate-labeled clone 01S and AF647-labeled clone 29S. Calibration curves were generated using recombinant MMP-7 spiked into newborn calf serum, yielding a linear range from 0.5 to 40 µg/L with a correlation coefficient greater than 0.99. Intra-assay coefficient of variation was 4.2% at 3.1 µg/L, and recovery rates ranged from 89% to 109%. Serum MMP-7 levels in RCC patients had a median concentration of 7.56 µg/L (range 3.12–30.5 µg/L), significantly higher than in healthy controls (median 2.13 µg/L, range 0.17–3.5 µg/L; P < 0.0001). The Mann-Whitney U test confirmed statistical significance, and receiver operating characteristic curve analysis demonstrated an area under the curve of 0.91, with a clinical sensitivity of 93% and specificity of 75% at the optimal cutoff. Similar results were obtained using an enzyme-linked immunoassay format, with a standard curve showing comparable linearity and reproducibility. No significant elevation of MMP-7 was observed in patients with benign renal tumors or other cancers, supporting the specificity of the marker for RCC. These findings establish MMP-7 as a clinically viable humoral biomarker for the detection and monitoring of renal cell carcinoma.
