# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

- Infectious diseases represent a persistent and evolving threat to global public health, arising from pathogenic microorganisms such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites that propagate through respiratory droplets, bodily fluids, contaminated surfaces, and vector-borne transmission. These diseases have historically caused widespread morbidity and mortality, with pandemics such as the 1918 influenza pandemic, the HIV/AIDS crisis, and the Ebola outbreaks demonstrating the capacity of pathogens to disrupt healthcare systems, economies, and social structures on a global scale. The emergence of novel pathogens, often through zoonotic spillover events, continues to challenge diagnostic infrastructure and public health preparedness, particularly in resource-limited settings where access to advanced laboratory equipment is restricted. The rapid global spread of severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2), the causative agent of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), underscored the critical need for diagnostic tools that are not only highly sensitive and specific but also deployable outside centralized laboratories, enabling timely isolation, contact tracing, and containment measures. Traditional diagnostic approaches, including antigen-based rapid tests and serological assays, suffer from limitations in sensitivity and the inability to distinguish active infection from prior exposure, respectively. Nucleic acid detection remains the gold standard for confirming active infection due to its high specificity and quantitative capability, yet conventional polymerase chain reaction (PCR) methodologies require thermocycling equipment, skilled personnel, and extended processing times that hinder their utility in point-of-care or field-based applications.

- The development of nucleic acid detection systems has undergone significant transformation with the advent of CRISPR-based technologies, which leverage the programmable target recognition and collateral enzymatic activities of CRISPR-associated proteins to enable rapid, instrument-free diagnostics. These systems offer a compelling alternative to PCR by eliminating the need for thermal cycling while maintaining high analytical sensitivity and specificity. Point-of-care devices incorporating CRISPR-Cas systems have been designed for use in low-resource environments, often relying on simple visual readouts or portable fluorometers, thereby expanding diagnostic access to remote and underserved populations. Despite these advances, current CRISPR-based diagnostics are largely constrained by their reliance on single-effector systems, such as Cas12 or Cas13, each of which is limited to detecting either DNA or RNA targets and requires pre-amplification steps to achieve clinically relevant sensitivity. The necessity for amplification introduces additional complexity, increases the risk of contamination, and extends the time-to-result, undermining the advantages of CRISPR-based approaches in rapid diagnostic settings.

- CRISPR-Cas effector molecules, particularly those derived from Type III systems, possess unique biochemical properties that distinguish them from the more commonly utilized Cas12 and Cas13 enzymes. Unlike Cas12 and Cas13, which require direct binding and cleavage of their target nucleic acid to activate collateral nuclease activity, Type III CRISPR-Cas systems are activated by transcription of their target sequence and generate secondary signaling molecules that amplify the detection signal. This dual-layered activation mechanism—target recognition followed by the synthesis of cyclic oligoadenylate second messengers—enables a cascade of enzymatic responses that can be harnessed for highly sensitive detection. The Type III-A CRISPR-Cas system, exemplified by the Lactococcus lactis Csm complex, integrates four distinct enzymatic activities within a single ribonucleoprotein complex: target RNA cleavage by the Csm3 subunit, DNA degradation by the HD domain of Csm1, synthesis of cyclic oligoadenylate (cOA) by the GGDD motif of Csm1, and activation of a separate ancillary RNase, Csm6, by the cOA product. This integrated architecture permits the simultaneous detection of both the presence of a target RNA and its transcriptional activity, offering a versatile platform for detecting both RNA and DNA viruses without requiring separate assay formats.

- Cas13-based methods have demonstrated utility in detecting viral RNA through RNA-stimulated cleavage of reporter RNA molecules, while Cas12-based systems detect DNA targets via DNA-stimulated cleavage of DNA reporters. However, both systems are limited by their dependence on a single enzymatic activity and their requirement for high target concentrations to overcome weak binding affinities relative to the low viral loads present in clinical samples. These limitations necessitate the use of isothermal amplification techniques such as recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA) or loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) to pre-concentrate the target, thereby increasing assay complexity and reducing robustness. Furthermore, the collateral activity of Cas13 and Cas12 is strictly dependent on the initial cleavage of the target nucleic acid, meaning that mutations in the target sequence that prevent binding can render the system ineffective. In contrast, the Type III-A system does not require cleavage of the target for signal generation, allowing for detection even when the target is partially degraded or mutated, thereby enhancing diagnostic resilience against viral evolution.

- The limitations of current CRISPR-based detection platforms are further compounded by their inability to generate multiplexed signals from a single reaction. Most systems rely on a single fluorescent reporter, limiting their capacity to distinguish between different viral strains or to confirm the presence of a target through orthogonal readouts. The lack of signal amplification beyond the initial binding event also restricts sensitivity, particularly in the absence of pre-amplification. Additionally, many existing systems are incompatible with direct detection of unamplified clinical specimens due to the low abundance of viral nucleic acids in patient samples such as nasopharyngeal swabs, which often fall below the attomolar detection threshold of current Cas12 and Cas13 assays. These constraints have hindered the widespread adoption of CRISPR diagnostics in clinical settings, despite their theoretical advantages in speed, cost, and simplicity.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

- The MORIARTY system is a novel, multipronged, one-pot, target RNA-induced, augmentable, rapid test system that leverages the intrinsic biochemical properties of the Type III-A CRISPR-Cas effector complex to enable highly sensitive, amplification-free, and transcription-coupled detection of viral nucleic acids. This system integrates the dual collateral activities of the Lactococcus lactis Csm complex—DNA degradation by the Csm1 HD domain and cyclic oligoadenylate-mediated RNA degradation by the ancillary Csm6 subunit—into a single reaction format that generates two independent, cumulative fluorescent signals upon recognition of a target RNA or its transcriptional product. By simultaneously monitoring cleavage of DNA and RNA fluorescent reporters, MORIARTY provides orthogonal confirmation of target presence, significantly enhancing diagnostic specificity and reducing the likelihood of false positives. The system operates without the need for thermocyclers or external amplification steps, making it uniquely suited for deployment in point-of-care and low-resource environments.

- Virus detection using the MORIARTY system is achieved through the programmed recognition of a viral RNA sequence by a CRISPR RNA (crRNA)-guided Csm effector complex, which triggers the synthesis of cyclic oligoadenylate (cOA6) upon binding. The cOA6 molecule then activates the Csm6 ancillary RNase, leading to the cleavage of a fluorescent RNA reporter, while the Csm1 subunit concurrently degrades a fluorescent DNA reporter in a metal ion-dependent manner. The combined fluorescence increase from both reporters provides a robust, additive signal that can be detected in real time using standard fluorometers or even portable devices. The system is adaptable to both direct detection of viral RNA and indirect detection via transcription of a DNA template, enabling a unified platform for detecting RNA and DNA viruses alike. The MORIARTY system’s ability to function under multiple buffer conditions—such as magnesium-only or magnesium-manganese combinations—further enhances its versatility, allowing optimization for different clinical sample types and target concentrations.

- The advantages of the MORIARTY system include its amplification-free sensitivity down to attomolar concentrations of target RNA, its compatibility with transcription-coupled detection using T7 RNA polymerase, its capacity for multiplexed targeting through the use of multiple crRNAs, and its resilience to target mutations due to the separation of target recognition from signal generation. Unlike Cas12 and Cas13 systems, which require precise target binding and cleavage to initiate detection, MORIARTY generates signal through secondary messenger amplification, enabling detection even when the target is present at sub-threshold concentrations or contains single-nucleotide polymorphisms. The system’s one-pot format eliminates the need for multiple handling steps, minimizes contamination risk, and reduces time-to-result to under one hour. Furthermore, the modular nature of the Csm and Csm6 components allows for facile reprogramming to detect new pathogens by simply altering the crRNA sequence, making MORIARTY a rapidly adaptable platform for emerging infectious threats.

- Embodiments of the MORIARTY method include an amplification-free configuration for direct detection of viral RNA in clinical samples, a transcription-coupled configuration (T7-MORIARTY) for detection of DNA targets or amplified products, and a combined configuration that utilizes both DNA and RNA fluorescent reporters to enhance signal contrast and diagnostic confidence. The system can be optimized for use with various metal ions, ATP concentrations, and probe designs to maximize sensitivity and minimize background signal. In one embodiment, the system employs a multiplexed approach using three distinct crRNAs targeting non-overlapping regions of the SARS-CoV-2 Spike gene, enabling detection at concentrations as low as 2,000 copies per microliter without amplification. In another embodiment, coupling MORIARTY with reverse transcription-recombinase polymerase amplification (RT-RPA) enables attomolar sensitivity, with detection limits comparable to or exceeding those of quantitative PCR, while maintaining the simplicity and speed of a single-tube reaction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- The MORIARTY system is a CRISPR-Cas-based diagnostic platform built upon the Type III-A Csm effector complex derived from Lactococcus lactis, which, upon recognition of a complementary viral RNA target, initiates a cascade of enzymatic activities that generate detectable fluorescent signals. The system comprises a reconstituted ribonucleoprotein complex containing the Csm1 through Csm6 subunits and a programmable crRNA, along with a separate ancillary protein, Csm6, that functions as a cyclic oligoadenylate-activated RNase. The Csm1 subunit possesses two distinct enzymatic functions: an HD domain that cleaves single-stranded DNA in the presence of manganese ions and a GGDD motif that synthesizes cyclic hexa-adenylate (cOA6) from ATP upon target RNA binding. The cOA6 molecule diffuses and activates the Csm6 subunit, which then cleaves RNA substrates with high efficiency. These dual activities are harnessed in MORIARTY by incorporating two fluorescent reporters: a DNA oligonucleotide labeled with a fluorophore-quencher pair that is cleaved by Csm1, and an RNA oligonucleotide labeled with a distinct fluorophore-quencher pair that is cleaved by Csm6. The simultaneous increase in fluorescence from both reporters provides a cumulative, highly specific signal that confirms the presence of the target nucleic acid.

- The motivation for developing a virus detection system based on the Type III-A CRISPR-Cas system stems from the limitations of existing Cas12 and Cas13 platforms, which require high target concentrations, are restricted to either DNA or RNA detection, and are vulnerable to target mutations that disrupt binding. The Type III-A system overcomes these limitations by decoupling target recognition from signal generation, allowing for signal amplification through the production of a diffusible second messenger. This design enables detection of viral RNA at concentrations far below those detectable by conventional CRISPR systems, even in the absence of pre-amplification. The system’s ability to respond to transcription of a DNA template further expands its utility to DNA viruses and to samples where viral RNA is degraded or present in low abundance. The MORIARTY system is therefore uniquely positioned to serve as a universal diagnostic platform capable of detecting a broad spectrum of pathogens with high sensitivity and minimal instrumentation.

- The sensitivity of the MORIARTY system is achieved through the synergistic amplification of two independent enzymatic pathways: the Csm1-mediated DNase activity and the cOA6-mediated RNase activity of Csm6. Under optimal conditions, the system can detect as few as five femtomolar of in vitro transcribed SARS-CoV-2 Spike gene mRNA without amplification, and attomolar concentrations when coupled with RT-RPA. This sensitivity is further enhanced by the use of multiplexed crRNAs targeting multiple regions of the viral genome, which collectively increase the probability of target engagement and signal generation. The system’s performance is highly reproducible across multiple replicates, with statistically significant signal increases observed above background in all target-positive samples. The fluorescence readout is linear over a broad dynamic range, enabling semi-quantitative estimation of viral load without the need for calibration curves or reference standards.

- The advantages of the MORIARTY system include its amplification-free detection capability, its compatibility with transcription-coupled detection, its multiplexed signal generation, its resilience to target mutations, and its one-pot reaction format that eliminates sample transfer and reduces contamination risk. Unlike other CRISPR-based systems, MORIARTY does not require cleavage of the target RNA to initiate detection, making it effective even when the target is partially degraded or contains single-nucleotide variants that would otherwise evade detection. The system’s modular design allows for rapid reprogramming to detect new pathogens by synthesizing a new crRNA, without requiring changes to the core enzymatic components. Furthermore, the system operates at a constant temperature of 37°C, eliminating the need for expensive thermal cycling equipment and enabling integration with low-cost heating devices such as hand warmers or portable incubators.

- The Type III-A CRISPR-Cas system, as implemented in MORIARTY, is composed of a multi-subunit effector complex that includes Csm1, Csm2, Csm3, Csm4, Csm5, and Csm6, along with a crRNA that guides target recognition. The Csm3 subunit cleaves the target RNA at specific sites, while the Csm1 subunit simultaneously degrades single-stranded DNA and synthesizes cOA6 from ATP. The cOA6 molecule acts as a diffusible second messenger that binds to and activates the Csm6 subunit, which then cleaves any RNA molecule in its vicinity, including the fluorescent reporter. This dual-activity architecture ensures that detection is contingent upon both target binding and the subsequent generation of a secondary signal, providing a built-in mechanism for reducing false positives. The system’s reliance on ATP and divalent metal ions allows for fine-tuning of reaction conditions to optimize sensitivity for different target types and sample matrices.

- The MORIARTY system enables amplification-free detection by leveraging the intrinsic signal amplification provided by the cOA6-mediated activation of Csm6. In the absence of pre-amplification, the system can detect viral RNA at concentrations as low as five femtomolar, corresponding to approximately 300 copies per microliter, by optimizing the concentrations of ATP, Csm6, and divalent metal ions. The use of magnesium ions promotes cOA6 synthesis without activating the DNase activity, allowing for a clean RNA-based readout. The addition of manganese ions enables simultaneous DNase and RNase activity, producing a cumulative signal that enhances diagnostic confidence. The system’s ability to function under these distinct conditions provides flexibility in assay design and allows for adaptation to different clinical scenarios, such as high-viral-load or low-viral-load samples.

- The one-pot detection with DNA amplification is achieved through the integration of reverse transcription-recombinase polymerase amplification (RT-RPA) with the T7-MORIARTY configuration. In this variant, a DNA template containing a T7 promoter sequence is first reverse transcribed and amplified using RPA enzymes, generating a double-stranded DNA product that serves as a transcription template for T7 RNA polymerase. The resulting RNA transcript then activates the Csm complex, triggering the same dual-fluorescence cascade as in the amplification-free system. This configuration enables detection of viral RNA at attomolar concentrations, with sensitivity comparable to quantitative PCR, while maintaining the simplicity of a single-tube reaction. The T7 promoter sequence must be included in the RPA primers to ensure transcriptional activation, and the system is rendered inactive in the absence of this sequence, ensuring high specificity.

- The MORIARTY-based assay results demonstrate consistent and statistically significant fluorescence increases in response to target RNA, with signal-to-noise ratios exceeding 10-fold over negative controls in all tested conditions. The system reliably distinguishes between target and non-target RNA sequences, including those with single or double mismatches, and shows no cross-reactivity with human RNA or other common respiratory pathogens. In clinical validation studies, the system correctly identified all positive patient samples with high viral loads and showed strong correlation with qPCR results, even in samples with low Ct values. The system’s performance was reproducible across multiple operators and laboratory settings, confirming its robustness and suitability for widespread deployment.

- The L1Csm effector complex, derived from Lactococcus lactis, is the core catalytic component of the MORIARTY system. It is produced as a single polycistronic expression construct encoding all Csm subunits and the crRNA, allowing for high-yield purification in two chromatographic steps. The purified complex retains full enzymatic activity and stability for extended periods when stored at −80°C. The L1Csm complex is reprogrammed by replacing the native crRNA with a synthetic guide sequence complementary to the target viral RNA, enabling rapid adaptation to new pathogens. The complex is functionally stable across a wide range of buffer conditions, including varying concentrations of magnesium, manganese, and ATP, which allows for optimization of detection sensitivity without requiring re-engineering of the protein components.

- System embodiments of the MORIARTY platform include an amplification-free configuration for direct detection of viral RNA, a transcription-coupled configuration for detection of DNA templates or RT-RPA products, and a multiplexed configuration that employs multiple crRNAs targeting distinct regions of the viral genome. Each embodiment can be configured to use either a single fluorescent reporter or dual reporters for cumulative signal detection. The system can be adapted for use with handheld fluorometers, smartphone-based readers, or even lateral flow strips by incorporating colorimetric or electrochemical readouts. The modular nature of the system allows for integration with existing point-of-care platforms, enabling seamless incorporation into global diagnostic networks.

- The versatility of the MORIARTY system lies in its ability to detect both RNA and DNA viruses, its compatibility with amplification-free and amplification-coupled formats, its resilience to target mutations, and its capacity for multiplexed detection. The system can be rapidly reprogrammed to detect emerging pathogens by synthesizing a new crRNA, making it a scalable and future-proof diagnostic platform. Its one-pot, isothermal design eliminates the need for complex instrumentation, making it ideal for use in low-resource settings, field hospitals, and border screening facilities. The system’s high sensitivity, specificity, and adaptability position it as a transformative tool for global infectious disease surveillance and response.

### EXAMPLES

- The MORIARTY system was constructed by cloning the Lactococcus lactis Csm operon into a single expression plasmid under the control of a T7 promoter, followed by transformation into Escherichia coli NiCo21(DE3) cells for protein expression. The complex was purified using Ni-NTA affinity chromatography and size-exclusion chromatography, yielding a homogeneous ribonucleoprotein complex with high functional activity. The L1Csm6 subunit was expressed separately from a dedicated plasmid and purified using identical methods to ensure purity and activity.

- The L1Csm/L1Csm6 CRISPR-Cas system was reprogrammed to detect the SARS-CoV-2 Spike gene by designing a 29-nucleotide crRNA complementary to nucleotides 22280–22308 of the viral genome, ensuring a non-complementary 3′ protospacer flanking sequence to enable activation. The crRNA was incorporated into the expression plasmid via BbsI cloning, and the resulting L1Csm_S0 complex was purified and characterized for target specificity and activity.

- DNA and RNA fluorescent probes were designed as oligonucleotides flanked by fluorophore-quencher pairs: a DNA probe labeled with Alexa Fluor 594 and Iowa Black RQ, and an RNA probe labeled with 6-FAM and Iowa Black FQ. These probes were synthesized with high purity and validated for background fluorescence and cleavage kinetics in the absence of target RNA.

- The L1Csm1-stimulated DNase activity was confirmed by observing a time-dependent increase in fluorescence at 570/630 nm excitation/emission wavelengths upon addition of manganese ions and target RNA, with no signal observed in the absence of either component. The activity was abolished in the H13A/D14N mutant of Csm1, confirming the role of the HD domain.

- The cOA6-stimulated L1Csm6-mediated RNase activity was demonstrated by the increase in fluorescence at 480/530 nm upon addition of ATP and target RNA, with no signal observed in the absence of ATP or in the R355A mutant of Csm6. The activity was shown to be dependent on the synthesis of cOA6, as confirmed by mass spectrometry analysis of reaction products.

- One-pot reaction components for amplification detection included 250 nM L1Csm complex, 1.0 nM L1Csm6, 0.5 μM DNA-Alexa, 0.5 μM RNA-FAM, 10 mM MgCl2 or 10 mM MnCl2, 0.5 mM ATP, and 500 nM target RNA in a 25 μL reaction volume, all incubated at 37°C. Fluorescence was monitored in real time using a multi-mode microplate reader.

- The simultaneous rise in DNA-Alexa and RNA-FAM fluorescence was observed under Mn2+ and low ATP conditions, confirming the concurrent activation of DNase and RNase activities. The total fluorescence signal was augmented when DNA-Alexa was replaced with DNA-FAM, demonstrating the additive nature of the dual-reporter system.

- The augmentability of MORIARTY by combined use of DNA-FAM and RNA-FAM probes was demonstrated by comparing single-reporter and dual-reporter reactions, with dual-reporter conditions yielding a 2.5-fold higher signal-to-noise ratio than either reporter alone.

- Optimized conditions for MORIARTY were determined to be 33 mM Tris acetate pH 7.6, 66 mM potassium acetate, 10 mM MgCl2, 1.5 mM ATP, 250 nM L1Csm, 250 nM L1Csm6, and 2.0 μM RNA-FAM for amplification-free detection, and 30 mM K-HEPES pH 7.6, 2 mM spermidine, 17 mM MgCl2, 0.5 mM MnCl2, 0.5 mM rNTPs, 60 μg/mL T7 RNA polymerase, and 2.0 μM each of DNA-FAM and RNA-FAM for T7-MORIARTY.

- The effects of metal ions, ATP, and L1Csm/Csm6 mutations on MORIARTY were systematically evaluated, revealing that Mg2+ supports cOA6 synthesis but not DNase activity, Mn2+ enables both activities, and ATP concentrations above 2 mM inhibit signal generation. Mutations in the HD domain of Csm1 or the HEPN domain of Csm6 abolished the respective fluorescence signals, confirming the specificity of the readouts.

- Fluorescence signals from RNase and DNase activities were quantified using linear regression of the initial 30-minute slope of fluorescence increase, with all target-positive samples showing statistically significant increases compared to water controls (p < 0.0001).

- The T7-MORIARTY system was developed by incorporating a T7 promoter sequence into the DNA template used to generate the target RNA, enabling transcription-coupled detection. Reaction components included T7 RNA polymerase, rNTPs, and a DNA template encoding the SARS-CoV-2 Spike gene under T7 control.

- The T7 MORIARTY one-pot reaction components for transcription-coupled detection were identical to those of the amplification-free system, with the addition of T7 RNA polymerase and rNTPs, and the substitution of DNA template for RNA target. No signal was observed in the absence of the T7 promoter, confirming transcription dependence.

- The multiprongness of T7 MORIARTY was demonstrated by its ability to detect both the DNA template and its RNA transcript, with dual-reporter fluorescence providing a cumulative signal that enhanced diagnostic confidence.

- The augmentability of T7 MORIARTY by combined use of DNA-FAM and RNA-FAM probes was confirmed by a 2.8-fold increase in signal intensity compared to single-reporter conditions, with no increase observed in negative controls.

- Optimized conditions for T7-MORIARTY were established as 30 mM K-HEPES pH 7.6, 2 mM spermidine, 0.01% Triton X-100, 17 mM MgCl2, 0.5 mM MnCl2, 0.5 mM rNTPs, 10 mM TCEP, 60 μg/mL T7 RNA polymerase, 250 nM L1Csm, 250 nM L1Csm6, and 2.0 μM each of DNA-FAM and RNA-FAM.

- The effects of metal ions and ATP on T7-MORIARTY were found to be similar to those in the amplification-free system, with Mn2+ enhancing signal contrast and ATP concentrations above 0.5 mM reducing background noise.

- Example 2 introduced amplification-free, direct detection of SARS-CoV-2 viral RNA via MORIARTY, using a reprogrammed 29mer crRNA targeting the Spike gene. The L1Csm_RNP was expressed and co-purified with the crRNA, and its specificity was validated against non-target RNA sequences.

- The L1Csm_S complex was evaluated for detection of in vitro transcribed S gene mRNA, demonstrating a detection limit of 5 fM (approximately 300 copies/μL) with a signal-to-noise ratio of 12-fold over water controls.

- Example 3 introduced attomolar detection of SARS-CoV-2 virus with amplification-coupled MORIARTY, employing an RT-RPA protocol to amplify the S gene prior to transcription and detection. The RT-RPA products were used to stimulate T7-MORIARTY, achieving detection of 32 copies/μL with statistical significance.

- The RT-RPA protocol was optimized using primers containing a T7 promoter sequence, and the sensitivity of MORIARTY to RT-RPA reaction products was evaluated using serial dilutions of Quantitative PCR control RNA extracted from heat-inactivated SARS-CoV-2 virus.

- MORIARTY was applied to human patient samples obtained from a COVID-19 testing center, with RNA extracted from nasopharyngeal swabs and tested in parallel with qPCR. The system correctly identified 14 out of 15 qPCR-positive samples and all 2 qPCR-negative samples, with no false positives.

- The results from MORIARTY were consistent with qPCR outcomes across two independent experiments, with a correlation coefficient of 0.94 between fluorescence slope and Ct value.

- In vitro transcribed S-gene mRNA at concentrations ranging from 1 aM to 1 pM was used to validate the sensitivity of RT-RPA-T7-MORIARTY, demonstrating linear detection across six orders of magnitude.

- The requirement for a T7 promoter sequence in S-gene-specific primers was confirmed by testing primers lacking the promoter, which yielded no fluorescence signal, confirming transcriptional dependence.

- Raw fluorescence data from patient samples showed a clear, time-dependent rise in fluorescence for positive samples, with no increase observed in negative controls or in samples with insufficient RNA.

- The diagnosis of patient samples by MORIARTY was summarized as highly concordant with qPCR results, with 93% sensitivity and 100% specificity, establishing its clinical utility as a rapid, instrument-free diagnostic platform.

### Material and Methods

- The L1Csm plasmid was cloned using a codon-optimized gene sequence encoding the Csm1–Csm6 subunits and the crRNA, inserted into a pACYC vector under T7 control, and transformed into Escherichia coli NiCo21(DE3) for protein expression.

- Mutations in the L1Csm complex, including H13A/D14N in Csm1 and R355A in Csm6, were introduced via Q5 site-directed mutagenesis, and all constructs were verified by Sanger sequencing.

- The crRNA protospacer region was reprogrammed to target the SARS-CoV-2 Spike gene by synthesizing a 29-nucleotide guide sequence complementary to nucleotides 22280–22308, cloned between two BbsI restriction sites for modular exchange.

- L1Csm complexes were expressed by inducing log-phase cultures with 0.3 mM IPTG at 18°C for 16 hours, followed by cell lysis and purification using Ni-NTA affinity chromatography and size-exclusion chromatography.

- The L1Csm6 subunit was expressed from a separate plasmid and purified using identical methods, yielding a homogeneous protein preparation with no contaminating nuclease activity.

- T7 RNA polymerase was produced by cloning the T7 gene into a pET vector, expressing it in BL21(DE3) cells, and purifying via ion-exchange and size-exclusion chromatography.

- In vitro transcription of the SARS-CoV-2 S gene mRNA was performed using linearized plasmid DNA as template, incubated with T7 RNA polymerase and NTPs at 37°C for three hours, followed by DNase treatment and purification using a commercial RNA cleanup kit.

- RNA transcripts were quantified by spectrophotometry and validated by gel electrophoresis and qPCR.

- RT-RPA was performed using the TwistAmp Basic kit, with primers containing a T7 promoter sequence, 100 U Protoscript reverse transcriptase, and 5 μL of RNA template, incubated at 42°C for 30 minutes.

- T7-MORIARTY detection assays were set up by combining RT-RPA products with the MORIARTY reaction cocktail, including L1Csm, L1Csm6, T7 RNA polymerase, rNTPs, and fluorescent probes, and incubating at 37°C for 60 minutes.

- Amplification-free MORIARTY methodology was optimized by varying concentrations of ATP, divalent ions, Csm6, and fluorescent probes, identifying the conditions that maximized signal-to-noise ratio.

- Amplification-free MORIARTY reactions were performed by adding target RNA directly to the reaction mixture and monitoring fluorescence in real time.

- T7-MORIARTY methodology was optimized by titrating T7 RNA polymerase, rNTPs, and Mn2+ concentrations to balance transcription efficiency with signal generation.

- T7-MORIARTY reactions were performed by adding DNA templates encoding the target sequence under T7 control to the reaction mixture and monitoring fluorescence over time.

- Target RNA sequences and crRNA sequences were provided as synthetic oligonucleotides, with sequences designed to ensure complementarity to the SARS-CoV-2 Spike gene and a non-complementary 3′ PFS.

- DNA templates for T7-MORIARTY were generated by PCR amplification of the S gene with primers containing a T7 promoter sequence, followed by purification and quantification.

- RPA primers and target DNA sequences were designed to amplify a 150-bp region of the S gene, with forward primer containing a T7 promoter sequence and reverse primer complementary to the 3′ end of the target.

- All reagents were tested for nuclease contamination, and all reactions included negative controls with water or non-target RNA to establish baseline fluorescence.
