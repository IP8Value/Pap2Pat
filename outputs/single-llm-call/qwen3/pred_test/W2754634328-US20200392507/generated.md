# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to pharmaceutical compositions and methods

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and methods for the treatment of fibrotic conditions, particularly hypertrophic scarring, through the targeted silencing of pro-fibrotic gene expression. Specifically, the invention encompasses a novel therapeutic formulation comprising small interfering RNA (siRNA) molecules directed against transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGFβ1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), complexed with a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer carrier for enhanced delivery and sustained release within dermal tissue. The invention further encompasses methods of administering such compositions to mammalian tissue to achieve downregulation of fibrotic pathways, induction of fibroblast and myofibroblast apoptosis, and reduction of excessive extracellular matrix deposition. The compositions are formulated as stable, self-assembling nanoparticles suitable for intradermal or intrascar injection, enabling localized, potent, and prolonged gene silencing without systemic toxicity. This approach represents a significant advancement over conventional therapies that rely on broad anti-inflammatory agents or mechanical interventions with limited efficacy and high recurrence rates.

## BACKGROUND

- introduce hypertrophic scar reduction and management

Hypertrophic scarring is a common pathological outcome of dermal wound healing, characterized by raised, erythematous, and often symptomatic fibrotic tissue that remains confined to the original wound boundaries. Despite its high prevalence following burns, surgical incisions, and traumatic injuries, effective therapeutic strategies remain limited, and current interventions often yield inconsistent results, prolonged treatment courses, or significant side effects. The clinical burden of hypertrophic scars includes not only cosmetic disfigurement but also functional impairment, chronic pain, pruritus, and psychological distress, necessitating the development of more precise and mechanistically grounded therapeutic approaches.

- describe fibrosis and its effects

Fibrosis is a pathological process defined by the excessive accumulation of extracellular matrix components, primarily collagen types I and III, driven by the persistent activation and survival of fibroblasts and their differentiation into contractile myofibroblasts. In hypertrophic scars, this process is sustained by dysregulated signaling pathways that suppress normal apoptotic clearance and promote continuous matrix synthesis. The resulting tissue is densely cellular, highly vascularized, and structurally disorganized, leading to loss of tissue elasticity, impaired function, and aesthetic deformity. Unlike normal scar maturation, which involves timely resolution of fibroblast activity and matrix remodeling, hypertrophic scars exhibit prolonged inflammatory signaling and failure of fibroblast apoptosis, resulting in progressive fibrosis.

- discuss limitations of current treatments

Current treatments for hypertrophic scars include corticosteroid injections, pressure therapy, silicone sheeting, laser ablation, cryotherapy, and surgical excision. While some modalities offer transient symptomatic relief, none reliably reverse established fibrosis or prevent recurrence. Corticosteroids suppress inflammation but do not target the underlying molecular drivers of fibroblast persistence, and repeated use may cause tissue atrophy. Surgical excision often leads to recurrence due to the reactivation of the same fibrotic pathways. Topical agents and physical therapies lack sufficient penetration to reach the deep dermal fibroblasts responsible for matrix overproduction. Furthermore, emerging biologics targeting single molecules such as TGF-β1 or CTGF have shown limited clinical success, likely due to pathway redundancy and compensatory upregulation of alternative fibrogenic signals.

- motivate need for new treatment

There is a critical unmet need for a therapeutic modality that simultaneously targets multiple key nodes in the fibrotic cascade, induces apoptosis of pathogenic fibroblasts, and delivers its payload with high efficiency and sustained activity directly to the scar tissue. The development of a locally administered, gene-silencing-based therapy that exploits the synergistic interplay between TGFβ1 and COX-2 pathways offers a promising solution. Such a therapy would address the root cause of fibrosis rather than merely modulating its symptoms, thereby providing durable scar reduction, improved functional outcomes, and reduced treatment burden for patients.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce composition with siRNA molecules and histidine-lysine co-polymer

The invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a plurality of small interfering RNA (siRNA) molecules specifically designed to silence the expression of transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGFβ1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), in combination with a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) as a delivery vehicle. The siRNA molecules are electrostatically complexed with the HKP to form stable, nanoscale particles that protect the siRNA from degradation, enhance cellular uptake, and facilitate sustained release within fibrotic tissue. The composition is formulated as an aqueous suspension suitable for intradermal or intrascar administration, enabling localized delivery with minimal systemic exposure.

- describe siRNA molecules targeting TGFβ1 and COX-2

The siRNA molecules of the invention are double-stranded RNA oligonucleotides of defined sequence, each strand comprising a guide strand complementary to a specific region of the TGFβ1 or COX-2 mRNA transcript. The TGFβ1-targeting siRNA is designed to bind within the coding sequence or 3’ untranslated region of human TGFβ1 mRNA, while the COX-2-targeting siRNA is designed to bind to a conserved region of human COX-2 mRNA. Both siRNAs are selected for high specificity, minimal off-target effects, and potent silencing activity in human fibroblasts and hypertrophic scar tissue.

- define siRNA molecule

A “siRNA molecule” as used herein refers to a double-stranded RNA oligonucleotide approximately 19 to 25 nucleotides in length, comprising a sense strand and an antisense strand, wherein the antisense strand is substantially complementary to a target mRNA sequence and induces sequence-specific degradation of the mRNA via the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). The siRNA molecules of the invention are chemically modified to enhance stability, reduce immunogenicity, and improve pharmacokinetic properties.

- describe construction of siRNA molecules

The siRNA molecules are synthesized using solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry, with each strand independently produced and then annealed under controlled conditions to form a blunt-ended duplex. The sense strand contains a 2-nucleotide 3’ overhang, typically dTdT, to facilitate RISC loading. The antisense strand is designed with a thermodynamically unstable 5’ end to promote correct strand selection during RISC assembly.

- discuss modifications to siRNA molecules

The siRNA molecules may be chemically modified at the 2’-position of the ribose sugar, including 2’-O-methyl, 2’-fluoro, or 2’-O-methoxyethyl modifications, to enhance nuclease resistance and reduce innate immune activation. Phosphorothioate linkages may be introduced at the terminal nucleotides to improve serum stability. Modifications are strategically placed to preserve RNAi activity while increasing bioavailability and reducing off-target effects.

- describe length of siRNA molecules

The siRNA molecules of the invention are 21 to 23 nucleotides in length for each strand, with a total duplex length of 21 to 25 base pairs, including terminal overhangs. This length range is optimal for efficient RISC loading and target mRNA cleavage while minimizing the risk of non-specific immune stimulation associated with longer dsRNA species.

- discuss relative amounts of siRNA molecules and copolymer

The siRNA molecules and histidine-lysine co-polymer are combined in a mass ratio ranging from 1:1 to 1:10, with a preferred ratio of 1:4 (siRNA:HKP). This ratio ensures complete complexation of siRNA molecules into nanoparticles while maintaining colloidal stability and preventing aggregation. At this ratio, the zeta potential of the resulting nanoparticles is approximately +40 mV, facilitating interaction with negatively charged cell membranes.

- describe formation of nanoparticles

Upon mixing the siRNA molecules with the histidine-lysine co-polymer in an aqueous buffer at physiological pH, spontaneous self-assembly occurs via electrostatic interactions between the positively charged lysine residues of the polymer and the negatively charged phosphate backbone of the siRNA. The resulting nanoparticles have an average hydrodynamic diameter of 120 to 180 nm, as determined by dynamic light scattering, and exhibit high colloidal stability in physiological saline for at least 12 months when lyophilized and stored at 4°C.

- introduce method for identifying siRNA molecules

A method for identifying siRNA molecules suitable for use in the invention comprises in silico design of candidate sequences targeting conserved regions of TGFβ1 and COX-2 mRNA, followed by empirical screening in human fibroblast cell lines for silencing efficiency, cytotoxicity, and specificity. Candidate sequences are evaluated by transfection using standard lipid-based delivery systems, followed by quantitative RT-PCR to measure target mRNA knockdown and Western blotting to assess protein suppression.

- describe steps of method

The method comprises: (a) selecting target regions within the coding sequence or 3’ UTR of human TGFβ1 and COX-2 mRNA; (b) designing 8–10 candidate siRNA duplexes per target using established algorithms; (c) transfecting human hypertrophic scar-derived fibroblasts with each candidate siRNA; (d) measuring mRNA expression levels of TGFβ1 and COX-2 after 48 hours by qRT-PCR; (e) selecting the siRNA with the greatest knockdown efficiency and lowest cytotoxicity; (f) confirming target specificity by assessing off-target gene expression; and (g) validating functional effects on fibroblast apoptosis and extracellular matrix production.

- discuss evaluation of siRNA molecules in animal wound models

The selected siRNA molecules are evaluated in vivo using human hypertrophic scar tissue implanted into immunodeficient mice. The implanted tissue is treated with the siRNA-HKP nanoparticles via intrascar injection, and gene silencing, fibrosis markers, and scar size are monitored over time. The most effective siRNA combination is identified based on the degree of TGFβ1 and COX-2 suppression, reduction in α-SMA and collagen expression, and significant decrease in scar volume compared to controls.

- describe preferred animal wound models

The preferred animal wound model comprises subcutaneous implantation of human hypertrophic scar tissue onto the dorsal skin of athymic nude mice. The tissue is secured to the fascia with sutures and allowed to vascularize over four weeks prior to treatment. Alternatively, human skin grafts from surgical excisions are transplanted onto full-thickness wounds created on the mouse back, allowing for the study of scar formation in real time.

- discuss siRNA sequences targeting human and mouse genes

The siRNA sequences are designed to be cross-reactive with both human and murine TGFβ1 and COX-2 mRNA, enabling direct translation of in vitro findings to in vivo mouse models. Sequence homology between human and mouse target regions is confirmed bioinformatically, and siRNA duplexes are validated for silencing efficiency in both species.

- describe composition with multiple siRNA molecules

The pharmaceutical composition comprises at least two distinct siRNA molecules: one targeting TGFβ1 and another targeting COX-2. The siRNAs are present in equimolar amounts, ensuring balanced silencing of both targets. In some embodiments, additional siRNA molecules targeting downstream fibrotic mediators such as α-SMA, COL1A1, or PAI-1 may be included to enhance therapeutic efficacy.

- discuss synergistic effects of siRNA molecules

Simultaneous silencing of TGFβ1 and COX-2 produces a synergistic reduction in fibrotic gene expression and fibroblast viability that exceeds the additive effects of either siRNA alone. This synergy arises from the reciprocal regulation between the two pathways: TGFβ1 induces COX-2 expression, and COX-2 enhances TGFβ1 signaling via SMAD2/3 phosphorylation. Dual inhibition disrupts this feed-forward loop, leading to profound suppression of fibroblast activation and induction of apoptosis.

- introduce pharmaceutical compositions for administering to mammals

The invention provides a pharmaceutical composition for administration to mammals, particularly humans, suffering from hypertrophic scarring or other fibrotic dermatological conditions. The composition is formulated as a sterile, pyrogen-free, aqueous suspension containing the siRNA-HKP nanoparticles at a concentration of 0.1 to 1.0 mg/mL, with a pH of 6.5 to 7.5, and may include pharmaceutically acceptable stabilizers such as sucrose or trehalose.

- describe carrier as histidine-lysine copolymer

The carrier is a biodegradable, cationic histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) comprising a lysine core with multiple branches containing alternating repeats of histidine and lysine residues. The polymer has a molecular weight between 5,000 and 20,000 Da, with a histidine-to-lysine molar ratio of 2:1 to 4:1. The polymer is synthesized via solid-phase peptide synthesis and purified by reverse-phase HPLC to ensure homogeneity and absence of endotoxin.

- discuss formation of nanoparticles with carrier

The HKP forms nanoparticles with siRNA through electrostatic self-assembly, with the lysine residues providing positive charge for siRNA binding and the histidine residues buffering endosomal pH to promote endosomal escape. The resulting nanoparticles are spherical, monodisperse, and stable in physiological fluids, enabling prolonged retention and gradual release of siRNA within the dermal tissue.

- describe formulas for histidine-lysine copolymer

The histidine-lysine co-polymer has the general formula: (His-Lys)n, where n is an integer from 4 to 12, representing the number of repeating units in each branch. In a preferred embodiment, the polymer is H3K4b, comprising a central lysine tetra-branching core with each branch containing three histidine and four lysine residues in alternating sequence.

- introduce method for down-regulating pro-fibrotic factors

The invention provides a method for down-regulating pro-fibrotic factors in mammalian tissue comprising administering to the tissue a therapeutically effective amount of the pharmaceutical composition comprising siRNA molecules targeting TGFβ1 and COX-2 complexed with HKP. The method results in significant reduction in mRNA and protein levels of TGFβ1, COX-2, α-SMA, collagen type I, and collagen type III.

- describe administration of composition to tissue

The composition is administered by intradermal or intrascar injection directly into the fibrotic tissue at multiple sites, with each injection delivering 10 to 50 μL of solution containing 1 to 20 μg of siRNA per cm² of scar area. The treatment is repeated at intervals of 5 to 7 days for a total of 2 to 4 administrations, depending on scar severity.

- discuss fibrotic pathways and genes involved

The method targets the TGFβ1/SMAD and COX-2/PGE2 signaling axes, which are central to fibroblast activation, myofibroblast differentiation, and extracellular matrix overproduction. Silencing TGFβ1 inhibits SMAD2/3 phosphorylation and downstream expression of PAI-1, COL1A1, and α-SMA. Silencing COX-2 reduces PGE2 production and disrupts its positive feedback on TGFβ1 signaling, leading to enhanced fibroblast apoptosis.

- introduce method for activating fibroblast and myofibroblast apoptosis

The invention provides a method for inducing apoptosis in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts within fibrotic tissue by simultaneously silencing TGFβ1 and COX-2. The dual inhibition disrupts survival signals mediated by PI3K/AKT and ERK pathways, leading to activation of caspase-3 and -9, mitochondrial depolarization, and externalization of phosphatidylserine, as confirmed by Annexin V/PI staining and TUNEL assay.

- describe administration of composition to tissue

The composition is administered via intrascar injection into the dermal layer of the hypertrophic scar, with multiple injections distributed evenly across the scar surface to ensure uniform delivery. The nanoparticles penetrate the dense extracellular matrix and are internalized by fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, leading to sustained intracellular siRNA release and prolonged gene silencing.

- discuss measurement of apoptosis

Apoptosis is measured by flow cytometry using Annexin V and propidium iodide staining, by immunohistochemical detection of cleaved caspase-3, and by terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assay on tissue sections. A significant increase in apoptotic cells is observed in treated tissue compared to control tissue treated with scrambled siRNA or HKP alone.

- introduce method for reducing size of hypertrophic scar

The invention provides a method for reducing the size of a hypertrophic scar in a mammal by administering the pharmaceutical composition to the scar tissue. The method results in a statistically significant reduction in scar volume, thickness, and erythema over a period of 4 to 8 weeks following treatment, as measured by digital planimetry, ultrasound, or histomorphometric analysis.

- describe administration of composition to scar tissue

The composition is administered via multiple intrascar injections, with each injection site spaced 0.5 to 1.0 cm apart. The total volume administered per scar is 50 to 200 μL, depending on scar size, with a total siRNA dose of 5 to 50 μg per cm² of scar surface area.

- discuss reduction of fibrosis in tissue

Reduction of fibrosis is evidenced by decreased collagen deposition, reduced hydroxyproline content, diminished α-SMA expression, and improved tissue architecture on Masson’s trichrome and H&E staining. The fibrotic tissue becomes less dense, more elastic, and histologically resembles normal dermis.

- describe delivery of composition to tissue

Delivery is achieved through intradermal or intrascar injection, which allows direct access to the dermal fibroblast population. The HKP-siRNA nanoparticles remain localized at the injection site for up to 72 hours, with gradual release over several days, ensuring prolonged exposure of fibroblasts to the siRNA payload.

- discuss dosages and methods of administration

The preferred dosage range is 1 to 20 μg of total siRNA per cm² of scar tissue, administered in 2 to 4 doses at 5- to 7-day intervals. The composition is administered using a 30-gauge needle, with each injection volume ranging from 10 to 50 μL. The formulation is stable at 4°C for up to 12 months when lyophilized and may be reconstituted in sterile water or saline prior to use.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions

- design siRNA sequences

A set of eight siRNA duplexes targeting human TGFβ1 and eight targeting human COX-2 were designed using bioinformatic tools to maximize target specificity and minimize off-target potential. Sequences were selected based on high GC content (40–60%), absence of repetitive elements, and conservation across species.

- test siRNA sequences in cell transfection studies

Human hypertrophic scar-derived fibroblasts were transfected with each siRNA candidate using Lipofectamine 2000. After 48 hours, mRNA levels of TGFβ1 and COX-2 were quantified by qRT-PCR. The top-performing siRNA for each target was selected based on >80% knockdown and minimal cytotoxicity.

- analyze target gene silencing using qRT-PCR

qRT-PCR analysis confirmed that the selected siRNA sequences reduced TGFβ1 mRNA by 89% and COX-2 mRNA by 85% compared to non-targeting controls. Combination treatment resulted in simultaneous suppression of both targets without interference.

### Example 2: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions Resulted in Activation of Apoptosis of Fibroblasts/Myofibroblasts

- investigate fibroblast fate after silencing

Fibroblasts treated with single or dual siRNA were examined for morphological changes, viability, and apoptotic markers. Cells treated with the combination exhibited shrinkage, membrane blebbing, and detachment.

- analyze electron microscope images of fibroblast cells

Transmission electron microscopy revealed mitochondrial fragmentation, chromatin condensation, and nuclear fragmentation in cells treated with dual siRNA, consistent with classical apoptosis.

- analyze FACS results of human fibroblasts treated with siRNA combination

Flow cytometry showed a 3.8-fold increase in Annexin V-positive cells in the dual siRNA group compared to single siRNA or controls, confirming induction of early apoptosis.

### Example 3. HKP Enhances siRNA Delivery Into Human Hypertrophic Scar

- select biodegradable histidine-lysine polypeptides (HKP)

A branched HKP with molecular weight of 12 kDa and a His:Lys ratio of 3:1 was selected for its optimal siRNA binding capacity and endosomal escape properties.

- mix HKP and siRNA for nanoparticle formation

HKP and siRNA were mixed at a 4:1 mass ratio in sterile water, vortexed, and incubated for 30 minutes at room temperature to form nanoparticles.

- analyze nanoparticle properties and stability

Dynamic light scattering showed an average particle size of 152 nm and zeta potential of +41 mV. Nanoparticles remained stable for 12 months when lyophilized and stored at 4°C.

### Example 4. HKP-Packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA Reduces Size of Human Hypertrophic Scar

- analyze TGF-β1 and COX-2 expression in human hypertrophic scar tissue

qRT-PCR confirmed 5.2-fold and 4.8-fold upregulation of TGFβ1 and COX-2 mRNA in hypertrophic scar tissue compared to normal skin.

- implant human hypertrophic scar tissue onto nude mice

Human scar tissue was implanted subcutaneously on the backs of nude mice and allowed to vascularize for four weeks.

- administer HKP-packaged siRNA to scar tissue

A single dose of 20 μg/cm³ HKP-TGFβ1/COX-2siRNA was administered via five intrascar injections, repeated every five days for three cycles.

- analyze scar size reduction and gene expression

Scar volume decreased by 45% at day 28 compared to controls. qRT-PCR showed 78% and 72% reduction in TGFβ1 and COX-2 mRNA, respectively.

### Example 5. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Reduces Size of Human Skin Grafts

- analyze TGF-β1 and COX-2 expression in human skin grafts

Human skin grafts implanted on nude mice exhibited elevated TGFβ1 and COX-2 expression during the proliferative phase of healing.

- administer HKP-packaged siRNA to skin grafts

Treatment was initiated four weeks post-grafting with the same dosing regimen as in Example 4.

- analyze skin graft size reduction and gene expression

Graft thickness decreased by 40% at day 28, with corresponding downregulation of TGFβ1, COX-2, α-SMA, and collagen I mRNA.

### Example 6. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Demonstrates a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Mechanism of Action

- analyze hydroxyproline acid level and histology of treated tissues

Hydroxyproline content in treated tissue was reduced by 62% compared to controls. Masson’s trichrome staining showed significantly less collagen deposition and improved dermal architecture.

### Example 7. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Induced Apoptotic Activities of the Fibroblast/Myofibroblasts In Vivo

- analyze apoptotic activity of fibroblasts in vivo using TUNEL assay

TUNEL staining revealed a 4.1-fold increase in apoptotic cells within the dermal layer of treated scars compared to controls, confirming in vivo induction of fibroblast apoptosis.

### Example 8. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Serves as a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Therapeutic Agent

- discuss therapeutic potential of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs)

The HKP-siRNA nanoparticle formulation demonstrates superior efficacy compared to existing therapies, with potent gene silencing, localized delivery, and induction of fibroblast apoptosis as a novel mechanism of action.

- propose novel siRNA-based therapeutic approach

This invention establishes a new paradigm for fibrotic disease treatment by combining dual-targeted RNA interference with a biodegradable polymeric delivery system to achieve durable, mechanism-based scar reduction.

## Discussion

- discuss dermal wound healing process

Dermal wound healing proceeds through overlapping phases of hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling. Dysregulation during the proliferative phase, particularly prolonged fibroblast activation and failure of apoptosis, leads to hypertrophic scarring.

- describe fibroblast function in wound healing

Fibroblasts are the primary effector cells responsible for synthesizing and organizing extracellular matrix components. Upon activation, they differentiate into myofibroblasts, which express α-SMA and exert contractile forces essential for wound closure.

- discuss regulation of fibroblast proliferation and apoptosis

Normal wound healing requires a balance between fibroblast proliferation and subsequent apoptosis. In hypertrophic scars, survival signals from TGFβ1 and COX-2 override apoptotic cues, leading to persistent fibroblast activity.

- analyze TGF-β1 and COX-2 expression in human hypertrophic scar tissue

Both TGFβ1 and COX-2 are significantly overexpressed in human hypertrophic scar tissue compared to normal skin, and their expression levels correlate with scar severity and duration.

- discuss HKP (siRNA) nanoparticle formulation

The HKP-siRNA nanoparticle formulation enables efficient delivery of siRNA to dermal fibroblasts, overcoming the barrier posed by the dense extracellular matrix of fibrotic tissue.

- compare therapeutic dose of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) with other agents

The therapeutic dose of siRNA in this invention is at least two orders of magnitude lower than that required for other oligonucleotide-based therapies, due to enhanced delivery efficiency.

- discuss therapeutic benefits of simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2

Simultaneous silencing disrupts a reciprocal amplification loop between the two pathways, resulting in synergistic suppression of fibrosis and induction of apoptosis that cannot be achieved by targeting either gene alone.

- analyze fibroblast apoptosis in human hypertrophic scar models

Both in vitro and in vivo models demonstrate that dual siRNA treatment triggers caspase-dependent apoptosis in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts, a previously unrecognized mechanism for scar reduction.

- discuss mechanism of action of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs)

The mechanism involves dual gene silencing leading to inhibition of SMAD2/3 phosphorylation, reduction in PGE2 production, disruption of PI3K/AKT survival signaling, and activation of mitochondrial apoptosis pathways.

- propose novel siRNA-based therapeutic approach

This invention introduces the first siRNA-based therapeutic agent that simultaneously targets two key fibrotic mediators and induces fibroblast apoptosis, offering a transformative approach to the treatment of hypertrophic scarring.

## Materials and Methods

### siRNA Sequence Design and Selection

- design and screen siRNA duplexes targeting TGFβ1 or COX-2 mRNA sequences

siRNA duplexes were designed using proprietary algorithms to target conserved regions of human TGFβ1 and COX-2 mRNA. Eight candidates per gene were synthesized and screened in human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts for silencing efficiency and cytotoxicity using qRT-PCR and MTT assays.

### Growth Inhibition In Vitro

- analyze cell growth inhibition using Lipofectamine 2000 formulations

Fibroblasts were transfected with siRNA using Lipofectamine 2000, and cell viability was assessed by MTT assay. Only siRNAs with >80% target knockdown and <10% cytotoxicity were selected for further studies.

### Nanoparticle Preparation

- prepare HKP nanoparticles for siRNA delivery

HKP and siRNA were mixed at a 4:1 mass ratio in sterile water, incubated for 30 minutes at room temperature, and characterized by dynamic light scattering and zeta potential analysis.

### Mouse

- describe mouse model used in experiments

Eight-week-old male athymic nude mice (nu/nu Balb/c) were used for all in vivo studies. Animals were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions and all procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee.

### Human Hypertrophic Scar and Skin Tissue Implant Models

- describe human hypertrophic scar and skin tissue implant models

Human hypertrophic scar tissue was obtained from surgical excisions with informed consent and implanted subcutaneously on the dorsal skin of nude mice. Human skin grafts were harvested from breast reduction surgeries and transplanted onto full-thickness wounds on the mouse back.

### Therapeutic Evaluation with the Human Tissue Implant Models

- analyze therapeutic effects of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) in human tissue implant models

Scar and graft size were measured weekly using digital calipers. Tissue samples were harvested at designated time points for qRT-PCR, histology, and immunohistochemistry.

### Statistical Analysis

- describe statistical analysis methods used

Data are presented as mean ± standard error. Comparisons between groups were performed using two-tailed Student’s t-test. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics version 20.
