# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and cellular reprogramming, specifically to the generation of induced dopaminergic precursor cells from somatic cells for the treatment of neurodegenerative disorders, particularly Parkinson’s disease. The invention encompasses novel compositions and methods for the direct transdifferentiation of non-neural somatic cells into regionally specified, lineage-restricted dopaminergic progenitors capable of maturing into functional dopaminergic neurons in vitro and in vivo. These induced dopaminergic precursor cells exhibit a midbrain identity, express molecular markers characteristic of endogenous dopaminergic progenitors, and demonstrate the capacity for terminal differentiation into electrophysiologically active, synaptically integrated dopaminergic neurons without requiring exogenous morphogenic cues. The invention further includes methods for the controlled expansion of these precursor cells through conditional expression of safe, non-oncogenic proliferative regulators, enabling scalable production for therapeutic cell transplantation. The disclosed compositions and methods represent a significant advancement over existing stem cell-based and indirect reprogramming approaches by providing a direct, efficient, and lineage-restricted source of dopaminergic neurons derived from patient-specific somatic cells, thereby enabling autologous cell replacement therapy with reduced risk of tumorigenesis, immune rejection, and epigenetic instability.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce neurodegenerative disorders

Neurodegenerative disorders represent a class of progressive, debilitating conditions characterized by the selective loss of specific neuronal populations in the central nervous system. Among these, Parkinson’s disease is one of the most prevalent, marked by the degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta, leading to a profound depletion of dopamine in the striatum and the emergence of cardinal motor symptoms including bradykinesia, rigidity, resting tremor, and postural instability. Current pharmacological interventions, such as levodopa and dopamine agonists, offer symptomatic relief but fail to halt disease progression and are often associated with long-term complications including dyskinesias and motor fluctuations. As the global population ages, the incidence of Parkinson’s disease continues to rise, underscoring the urgent need for therapies that address the underlying neuronal loss rather than merely compensating for neurotransmitter deficiency.

- motivate cell replacement therapy

Cell replacement therapy has emerged as a promising strategy for restoring lost neuronal function in neurodegenerative diseases by reintroducing functional dopaminergic neurons into the denervated striatum. Early clinical trials using fetal midbrain tissue demonstrated that transplanted dopaminergic neurons can survive, integrate into host circuitry, and alleviate motor deficits in some patients. However, the widespread application of this approach has been severely limited by ethical concerns, insufficient tissue availability, variability in graft composition, and inconsistent clinical outcomes. These challenges have spurred the search for alternative, scalable, and reproducible sources of dopaminergic neurons that can be standardized for clinical use.

- summarize stem cell biology breakthroughs

Significant advances in stem cell biology have led to the derivation of dopaminergic neurons from human embryonic stem cells and induced pluripotent stem cells through multi-step differentiation protocols that mimic embryonic midbrain development using sequential exposure to morphogens such as sonic hedgehog and fibroblast growth factor 8. While these methods have yielded dopaminergic neurons with functional properties, they are time-consuming, inefficient, and often result in heterogeneous cell populations containing non-dopaminergic lineages. Moreover, the pluripotent origin of these cells introduces a risk of teratoma formation if residual undifferentiated cells persist in the final product, necessitating rigorous purification steps that further complicate manufacturing and increase costs.

- describe limitations of direct reprogramming

Direct reprogramming, or transdifferentiation, offers an alternative pathway by converting somatic cells directly into neuronal lineages without passing through a pluripotent state. Although several studies have reported the generation of induced dopaminergic neurons from fibroblasts using combinations of transcription factors, these approaches have largely produced terminally differentiated neurons with limited proliferative capacity, making large-scale production impractical. Furthermore, many of these cells lack the regional specificity of midbrain dopaminergic neurons, express aberrant markers of other neuronal subtypes, or fail to integrate functionally in vivo. The inability to generate a stable, expandable precursor population that retains the potential to mature into authentic dopaminergic neurons under physiological conditions has remained a critical barrier to clinical translation.

- discuss challenges of inducing dopaminergic neurons

The specification of midbrain dopaminergic neurons during development is governed by a tightly regulated network of transcription factors, including Foxa2, Lmx1a, and Otx2, which act in concert with extracellular signals to establish regional identity and lineage commitment. Reproducing this complex regulatory landscape in vitro has proven difficult, as most reprogramming cocktails fail to recapitulate the precise spatiotemporal expression dynamics required for authentic dopaminergic fate determination. Additionally, the absence of a defined precursor stage that can be expanded and characterized prior to terminal differentiation has hindered quality control and safety assessment in preclinical and clinical settings.

- review previous studies on transcription factors

Previous efforts to reprogram fibroblasts into dopaminergic neurons have employed combinations of factors such as Ascl1, Lmx1a, and Nurr1, or Brn2 and Sox2, with varying degrees of success. However, these approaches typically yield low efficiencies, generate mixed neuronal populations, or require prolonged culture periods with exogenous morphogens to induce dopaminergic characteristics. The role of Foxa2 in this context has been largely unexplored as a direct reprogramming factor, despite its well-established function as a master regulator of midbrain floor plate development and dopaminergic progenitor specification. The potential for Foxa2 to act as a lineage-determining factor in direct conversion, independent of Lmx1a or morphogenic stimulation, has not been previously demonstrated or leveraged for therapeutic cell generation.

- highlight need for dopaminergic precursors

There remains a critical unmet need for a method to generate a homogeneous, expandable, and lineage-restricted population of dopaminergic precursor cells that can be reliably produced from patient-derived somatic cells, expanded under controlled conditions, and subsequently differentiated into functional dopaminergic neurons without tumorigenic risk or lineage drift. Such a population would enable standardized, scalable, and safe cell therapies for Parkinson’s disease and related disorders, overcoming the limitations of both pluripotent stem cell-derived neurons and terminally differentiated transdifferentiated neurons.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce induced dopaminergic precursor (iDP)

The present invention introduces a novel class of induced dopaminergic precursor cells, referred to herein as iDP cells, which are generated by the direct transdifferentiation of somatic cells through the ectopic expression of a specific combination of transcription factors. These iDP cells are not terminally differentiated neurons but rather multipotent progenitors committed exclusively to the dopaminergic lineage, possessing the capacity for limited self-renewal and the ability to mature into fully functional dopaminergic neurons upon appropriate differentiation cues.

- define iDP composition

The iDP cells are characterized by the ectopic expression of three key transcription factors: Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. These factors act synergistically to reprogram somatic cells into a neural progenitor state with a distinct midbrain dopaminergic identity, as evidenced by the upregulation of endogenous dopaminergic progenitor markers and the suppression of alternative lineage determinants.

- describe iDP characteristics

The iDP cells express a panel of molecular markers consistent with midbrain dopaminergic progenitors, including Aldh1A1, Lmx1a, Otx2, Pitx3, Nkx6.1, Corin (Lrp4), and Ngn2, while simultaneously exhibiting minimal or absent expression of forebrain markers such as FoxG1, GSX2, and Nkx2.1. These cells are negative for glial lineage markers such as GFAP, Olig2, and S100β, demonstrating a strict commitment to the neuronal lineage. The iDP cells are morphologically distinct, exhibiting a neural progenitor-like appearance with elongated processes and high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratios.

- summarize iDP differentiation

Upon removal of reprogramming factors and exposure to neurotrophic support, iDP cells undergo terminal differentiation into mature dopaminergic neurons that express tyrosine hydroxylase, aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, the dopamine transporter, and the vesicular monoamine transporter 2. These neurons exhibit functional electrophysiological properties, including the ability to generate action potentials and respond to depolarizing stimuli with voltage-gated sodium and potassium currents, and form synaptic structures positive for synaptophysin.

- introduce population of iDPs

The invention further encompasses a substantially homogeneous population of iDP cells, wherein at least 85% of the cells express a combination of neural progenitor markers and dopaminergic progenitor markers, and less than 5% express glial or non-dopaminergic neuronal markers. This population is capable of clonal expansion and can be cryopreserved, thawed, and replated without loss of identity or differentiation potential.

- describe method of transdifferentiating somatic cells

The invention provides a method for transdifferentiating somatic cells into iDP cells by introducing into the somatic cells a nucleic acid construct encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 under the control of inducible or constitutive promoters, thereby initiating a reprogramming cascade that bypasses pluripotency and directly establishes a dopaminergic progenitor fate.

- outline method of treating neurodegenerative disease

The invention further provides a method for treating neurodegenerative disease, particularly Parkinson’s disease, by transplanting a therapeutically effective number of iDP cells into the striatum of a subject in need thereof, wherein the transplanted cells survive, differentiate into dopaminergic neurons, reinnervate the host striatum, and restore motor function without forming tumors or aberrant cell types.

- introduce method of identifying regulatory agents

The invention further includes a method for identifying regulatory agents that modulate the generation, expansion, or differentiation of iDP cells by screening compounds for their ability to enhance the expression of dopaminergic progenitor markers, suppress glial lineage genes, or promote proliferation in the presence of a conditional proliferative regulator such as L-Myc.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce compositions and methods for transdifferentiation of somatic cells

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for the direct transdifferentiation of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic precursor cells through the coordinated ectopic expression of the transcription factors Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. These factors act in concert to rewire the transcriptional network of non-neural somatic cells, silencing fibroblast-specific gene programs and activating a cascade of endogenous neural and dopaminergic progenitor genes. The resulting iDP cells are not transient intermediates but stable, self-sustaining progenitors capable of propagation and terminal differentiation into functional dopaminergic neurons.

- describe ectopic expression of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 for transdifferentiation

The ectopic expression of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 is achieved through the delivery of nucleic acid constructs encoding these factors into somatic cells using viral or non-viral vectors. The combination of these three factors is essential and sufficient to induce the conversion of fibroblasts into iDP cells, as omission of any single factor results in failure to establish a stable dopaminergic progenitor state. Brn2 and Sox2 initiate neural reprogramming by activating core neural progenitor networks, while Foxa2 imposes midbrain regional identity and dopaminergic lineage commitment by upregulating key determinants such as Lmx1a, Otx2, and Pitx3, while suppressing alternative fates such as GABAergic or glial lineages.

- detail advantages of the present disclosure over currently available technologies

Unlike existing methods that rely on pluripotent intermediates or require prolonged morphogen exposure, the disclosed method generates iDP cells in a single step without passing through a pluripotent state, thereby eliminating the risk of teratoma formation. Furthermore, the iDP cells do not require exogenous SHH or FGF8 for dopaminergic specification, a significant departure from all prior protocols that depend on these morphogens to pattern neural progenitors. The resulting iDP cells exhibit a higher purity of dopaminergic lineage commitment, greater reproducibility, and enhanced scalability compared to previously described transdifferentiated neurons or stem cell-derived dopaminergic neurons.

- describe use of iDP cells for cell or tissue replacement therapy

The iDP cells are suitable for cell or tissue replacement therapy in neurodegenerative disorders characterized by dopaminergic neuron loss, particularly Parkinson’s disease. The cells can be transplanted directly into the striatum or substantia nigra of a subject, where they survive, integrate into host circuitry, extend axonal projections, and restore dopamine-dependent motor function. The lineage-restricted nature of the iDP cells ensures that they differentiate exclusively into dopaminergic neurons, minimizing the risk of off-target cell types such as astrocytes or oligodendrocytes, which are commonly observed in transplants of multipotent neural progenitors.

- discuss modification of iDP cells for limited proliferation

To overcome the limited proliferative capacity inherent to progenitor cells, the iDP cells may be engineered to express a conditional proliferative regulator, such as L-Myc, under the control of a tetracycline-responsive promoter. Upon addition of doxycycline, the cells undergo reversible expansion without loss of dopaminergic identity or acquisition of tumorigenic properties. Removal of doxycycline halts proliferation and allows the cells to resume differentiation, enabling the production of large, homogeneous batches of transplant-ready iDP cells.

- provide examples of diseases that can be treated with iDP cells

The iDP cells are particularly suited for the treatment of Parkinson’s disease, but may also be applied to other neurodegenerative conditions involving dopaminergic dysfunction, including dopaminergic neuron loss in multiple system atrophy, dopamine-responsive dystonia, and certain forms of restless legs syndrome. The method may also be adapted for the generation of iDP cells from patient-derived somatic cells to model disease mechanisms in vitro for drug screening and personalized therapeutic development.

- summarize therapeutic efficiency of iDP cells

Transplantation of iDP cells into animal models of Parkinson’s disease results in significant survival and differentiation of grafted cells into tyrosine hydroxylase-positive neurons, reinnervation of the striatum, and partial but statistically significant recovery of motor deficits in behavioral assays such as rotarod performance. The absence of tumor formation, glial contamination, or ectopic differentiation confirms the safety and specificity of the iDP cells as a therapeutic product.

### Definitions

- define transdifferentiation and reprogramming

Transdifferentiation refers to the direct conversion of one differentiated somatic cell type into another differentiated cell type without intermediate passage through a pluripotent state. Reprogramming encompasses any process by which the gene expression profile of a cell is altered to confer a new cellular identity, including but not limited to transdifferentiation, dedifferentiation, and induced pluripotency.

- define somatic cell

A somatic cell is any biological cell forming the body of a multicellular organism, excluding germ cells, gametes, and stem cells. Somatic cells are diploid and include fibroblasts, keratinocytes, adipocytes, hepatocytes, and blood-derived cells.

- define dopaminergic precursor or dopaminergic progenitor

A dopaminergic precursor or dopaminergic progenitor is a multipotent or lineage-restricted neural cell capable of self-renewal and differentiation into dopaminergic neurons, expressing molecular markers associated with midbrain dopaminergic development such as Foxa2, Lmx1a, Otx2, Aldh1A1, and Pitx3, but not expressing terminal neuronal or glial markers.

- define endogenous DP cell

An endogenous dopaminergic progenitor cell is a naturally occurring neural progenitor cell within the developing or adult midbrain that gives rise to dopaminergic neurons through physiological developmental processes.

- define induced dopaminergic precursor cell

An induced dopaminergic precursor cell is a cell generated by the artificial manipulation of gene expression in a somatic cell, resulting in the acquisition of a dopaminergic progenitor identity, including expression of dopaminergic progenitor markers and the capacity to differentiate into functional dopaminergic neurons.

- define ectopic expression

Ectopic expression refers to the expression of a gene or protein in a cell type or developmental stage in which it is not normally expressed, achieved through the introduction of exogenous nucleic acid sequences encoding the gene under the control of heterologous regulatory elements.

- define expression and expression products

Expression refers to the process by which information encoded in a gene is converted into a functional product, such as a protein or RNA molecule. Expression products include messenger RNA, non-coding RNA, and polypeptides encoded by the gene.

- define Brn2, Brn4, Sox2, Foxa2, and Lmx1a

Brn2 is a POU-domain transcription factor involved in neural development; Brn4 is a related POU-domain transcription factor; Sox2 is a SRY-box transcription factor essential for maintaining pluripotency and neural progenitor identity; Foxa2 is a forkhead box transcription factor critical for midbrain floor plate and dopaminergic progenitor specification; Lmx1a is a LIM-homeodomain transcription factor required for dopaminergic neuron development.

- define variant of a polypeptide

A variant of a polypeptide is a modified form of a reference polypeptide sequence that retains the biological function of the original, including substitutions, deletions, or insertions of amino acids that do not substantially alter the structure or activity of the protein.

- define operably linked

Operably linked refers to the functional association of two or more nucleic acid sequences such that one sequence influences the expression or function of the other, as when a promoter is positioned to drive transcription of a coding sequence.

- define viral vectors

Viral vectors are engineered viral particles modified to deliver genetic material into target cells without causing disease, including retroviral, lentiviral, adenoviral, and adeno-associated viral vectors.

- define transcription factor

A transcription factor is a protein that binds to specific DNA sequences to regulate the transcription of genetic information from DNA to messenger RNA.

### Reprogramming (Transdifferentiation)

- define transdifferentiation

Transdifferentiation is the direct conversion of one differentiated somatic cell type into another without intermediate dedifferentiation to a pluripotent state, resulting in a stable change in cellular identity.

- describe master transcription factors

Master transcription factors are regulatory proteins that dictate cell fate by activating lineage-specific gene networks and repressing alternative developmental programs. In the present invention, Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 act as master transcription factors for dopaminergic progenitor specification.

- list master TFs for iDP

The master transcription factors for the generation of iDP cells are Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2.

- describe ectopic expression methods

Ectopic expression is achieved by introducing nucleic acid constructs encoding the transcription factors into somatic cells using viral vectors such as retroviruses or lentiviruses, or non-viral methods including electroporation, lipofection, or synthetic mRNA delivery.

- describe endogenous gene expression modulation

The ectopic expression of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 triggers the activation of endogenous dopaminergic progenitor genes such as Lmx1a, Otx2, and Pitx3, while suppressing fibroblast-specific genes such as Col1a1 and Vim, leading to a complete transcriptional reprogramming.

- describe epigenetic modification

The reprogramming process involves remodeling of chromatin structure, including loss of repressive histone marks such as H3K27me3 at dopaminergic gene loci and gain of activating marks such as H3K4me3, facilitating stable gene expression changes.

- describe vector design for multiple TFs

Vectors are designed to co-express Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 from a single construct using internal ribosome entry sites, 2A peptide linkers, or bicistronic promoters to ensure coordinated expression.

- describe integration of transgene at safe harbor locus

Transgenes may be targeted to genomic safe harbor loci such as AAVS1 or ROSA26 using CRISPR/Cas9 or TALEN-mediated homologous recombination to ensure consistent expression and minimize insertional mutagenesis.

- describe synthetic modified mRNA for TF expression

Synthetic modified mRNA encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2, with nucleoside modifications to reduce immunogenicity and enhance stability, may be delivered repeatedly to somatic cells to achieve transient but sufficient expression for reprogramming.

- describe iDP cell characteristics

iDP cells exhibit a neural progenitor morphology, express Sox1, Nestin, and CD133, and co-express dopaminergic progenitor markers including Aldh1A1, Lmx1a, and Corin, while lacking expression of glial or forebrain markers.

- describe applications of transdifferentiated cells

Transdifferentiated iDP cells are applicable for cell replacement therapy, disease modeling, drug screening, and high-throughput toxicity testing of dopaminergic neurons.

- describe screening methods for agents

Screening methods involve exposing iDP cells to libraries of small molecules or biologics and measuring changes in dopaminergic marker expression, proliferation rate, or survival under stress conditions.

- describe iDP cells as model systems

iDP cells derived from patients with Parkinson’s disease serve as in vitro models to study disease mechanisms, identify pathogenic pathways, and test personalized therapeutic interventions.

- describe limitations of current cell therapies

Current cell therapies using pluripotent stem cells are plagued by low efficiency, long differentiation timelines, heterogeneity, and tumorigenic risk. Terminally differentiated transdifferentiated neurons lack expandability, limiting scalability.

- describe goal of direct reprogramming

The goal of direct reprogramming is to generate functional, lineage-restricted cells from accessible somatic sources in a single step, with minimal genetic manipulation, high reproducibility, and clinical translatability.

## DISCUSSION

- introduce stem cell-based therapy for neurodegenerative disorders

Stem cell-based therapies have long been envisioned as a curative strategy for neurodegenerative disorders such as Parkinson’s disease, offering the potential to replace lost neurons and restore neural circuitry. Despite decades of research, clinical translation has been hampered by ethical concerns, limited cell availability, and safety issues associated with pluripotent intermediates.

- discuss limitations of current approaches

Current approaches relying on embryonic or induced pluripotent stem cells require multi-stage differentiation protocols that are inefficient, time-consuming, and prone to heterogeneity. The risk of residual undifferentiated cells forming teratomas remains a significant barrier to regulatory approval.

- introduce direct conversion of cells as an alternative strategy

Direct conversion of somatic cells into neurons bypasses pluripotency and offers a faster, safer, and more direct route to generating transplantable cells. However, prior methods have failed to produce expandable, lineage-restricted dopaminergic precursors.

- describe generation of induced DA neurons and iNPCs

Previous studies have generated induced dopaminergic neurons and induced neural progenitor cells, but these cells either lack regional specificity, require morphogens, or cannot be expanded for clinical use.

- discuss importance of Foxa2 and Lmx1a in midbrain DA specification

Foxa2 and Lmx1a are master regulators of midbrain dopaminergic development, and their combined expression has been shown to enhance dopaminergic specification in neural progenitors. However, their role as direct reprogramming factors has not been established.

- introduce novel cocktail of transcription factors for reprogramming fibroblasts

The present invention introduces the novel combination of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 as a minimal and sufficient set of transcription factors capable of directly converting fibroblasts into dopaminergic precursor cells with midbrain identity.

- describe resulting iDPs and their characteristics

The resulting iDP cells exhibit a stable, homogeneous, and lineage-restricted dopaminergic progenitor phenotype, express key midbrain markers, and differentiate into functional dopaminergic neurons without morphogen stimulation.

- discuss role of Foxa2 in driving direct conversion of fibroblasts into iNPCs

Foxa2 is the critical determinant that imposes dopaminergic and midbrain identity on the neural progenitors generated by Brn2 and Sox2, enabling the direct conversion of fibroblasts into regionally specified precursors.

- describe terminal differentiation of iDPs into mature DA neurons

iDPs terminally differentiate into mature dopaminergic neurons that express dopamine-synthesizing enzymes, transporters, and synaptic proteins, and exhibit electrophysiological properties of native neurons.

- discuss in vivo grafting of iDPs and their survival and differentiation

Transplanted iDPs survive in vivo, differentiate into tyrosine hydroxylase-positive neurons, and integrate into host striatal circuitry without forming tumors or glial cells.

- describe recovery of motor function in PD mouse model

Grafted iDPs lead to partial but significant recovery of motor function in a mouse model of Parkinson’s disease, as measured by improved performance on the rotarod test.

- discuss potential of iDPs as a therapeutic cell source for PD

The iDP cells represent a scalable, safe, and patient-specific cell source for Parkinson’s disease therapy, overcoming the limitations of prior approaches and enabling clinical translation.

- introduce possibility of using patient skin fibroblasts for reprogramming

Patient-derived skin fibroblasts can be reprogrammed into iDPs, enabling autologous transplantation without immune rejection and facilitating personalized medicine.

- discuss managed expansion of engineered iDPs

Conditional expression of L-Myc allows for controlled expansion of iDPs, enabling production of sufficient cell numbers for clinical dosing while maintaining lineage fidelity.

- conclude success of the work and its implications

The successful generation of iDP cells from somatic cells represents a transformative advance in regenerative neurology, providing a clinically viable pathway for cell replacement therapy in Parkinson’s disease and related disorders.

## Methods

### Cell Preparation, Retroviral Packaging, Infection and Direct Reprogramming

- describe isolation of mouse skin fibroblasts

Mouse skin fibroblasts were isolated from adult Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice aged 5.5 to 7.0 weeks following euthanasia and skin excision. Tissue was minced and digested with collagenase type I and dispase for 1 hour at 37°C. Dissociated cells were filtered through a 70-μm cell strainer and plated in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 1× non-essential amino acids, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin. Cells were maintained at 37°C in a 5% CO2 humidified atmosphere and used between passages 2 and 5 to avoid replicative senescence.

- describe retroviral packaging and infection

Retroviral vectors encoding mouse Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 were cloned into the pMXs retroviral backbone. Plat-E packaging cells were transfected with 15 μg of each retroviral plasmid using Lipofectamine LTX. Supernatants containing retroviruses were collected at 48 and 72 hours post-transfection, filtered through 0.45-μm cellulose acetate filters, and mixed in equal volumes. Fibroblasts were infected with virus-containing supernatant supplemented with 10 μg/mL polybrene overnight. Medium was replaced with NeuroCult NSC Basal Medium supplemented with NeuroCult NSC Proliferation Supplements, 20 ng/mL bFGF, and 20 ng/mL EGF 72 hours post-infection.

- describe direct reprogramming of fibroblasts

Direct reprogramming was initiated immediately following retroviral infection. Cells were monitored for EGFP expression under fluorescence microscopy. Colonies exhibiting neural progenitor morphology emerged between days 9 and 14. Colonies were manually picked, expanded, and characterized for marker expression.

- describe culture conditions for iDPs

iDPs were maintained in NeuroCult NSC Basal Medium supplemented with NeuroCult NSC Proliferation Supplements, 20 ng/mL bFGF, and 20 ng/mL EGF. Medium was changed every 48 hours. Cells were passaged using Accutase and replated at 1:3 to 1:5 ratios. For differentiation, cells were switched to neuronal differentiation medium containing DMEM/F12, 1× N2, 1× B27, 1 mM Glutamax, 0.11 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 1 mM dibutyrylcAMP, 0.2 mM ascorbic acid, 10 ng/mL BDNF, and 10 ng/mL GDNF.

### Quantitative Real-Time RT-PCR

- describe RT-PCR analysis for neural stem cell-specific mRNAs

Total RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol Reagent followed by purification with the RNeasy Mini Kit. Reverse transcription was performed using the Transcriptor First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit. SYBR Green-based quantitative real-time PCR was conducted on a StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System using SYBR Select Master Mix. Primer sequences are listed in Table S1. Reactions were run in triplicate, and relative gene expression was calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method with Gapdh as the reference gene.

### Immunocytochemistry

- describe immunocytochemistry protocol for cultured cells

Cells were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 minutes at room temperature, washed three times with PBS, and permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 10 minutes. Non-specific binding was blocked with 2% bovine serum albumin in PBS for 1 hour. Primary antibodies were applied overnight at 4°C, followed by three washes in PBS and incubation with fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies for 2 hours at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Images were acquired using a Zeiss 710 Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope.

### Differentiation

- describe astrocyte differentiation protocol

For astrocyte differentiation, iDPs were cultured in DMEM/F12 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum for 7 days, with medium changes every other day.

- describe oligodendrocyte differentiation protocol

For oligodendrocyte differentiation, iDPs were cultured in DMEM/F12 supplemented with 1× N2, 10 ng/mL PDGF, 10 ng/mL FGF-2, and 10 μM forskolin for 4 days, followed by replacement with 30 ng/mL T3 and 200 μM ascorbic acid for an additional 7 days.

- describe neuronal differentiation protocol (SHH/FGF8 independent)

For SHH/FGF8-independent neuronal differentiation, iDPs were plated on poly-L-ornithine/laminin-coated coverslips in neuronal differentiation medium containing 1× N2, 1× B27, 1 mM Glutamax, 0.11 mM β-mercaptoethanol, 1 mM dibutyrylcAMP, 0.2 mM ascorbic acid, 10 ng/mL BDNF, and 10 ng/mL GDNF for 4 weeks, with medium changes every 3–4 days.

- describe neuronal differentiation protocol (SHH/FGF8 dependent)

For SHH/FGF8-dependent neuronal differentiation, iDPs were first cultured for 6 days in DMEM/F12 supplemented with 1× N2, 10 ng/mL bFGF, 100 ng/mL SHH, and 100 ng/mL FGF8, followed by switching to neuronal differentiation medium for an additional 4 weeks.

### Recording of Action Potentials and Total Currents

- describe whole cell patch-clamp technique

Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings were performed using an Axonpatch 200B amplifier and pClamp 10.2 software. Patch pipettes were pulled from borosilicate glass and had resistances of 3–5 MΩ. Series resistance was compensated by 80–90%. Data were sampled at 10 kHz and filtered at 5 kHz.

- describe patch-pipette solution composition

The intracellular pipette solution contained 105 mM K-aspartate, 20 mM KCl, 1 mM CaCl2, 5 mM MgATP, 10 mM HEPES, 10 mM EGTA, and 25 mM glucose, pH 7.2, 320 mOsm/L.

- describe bath solution composition

The extracellular bath solution contained 140 mM NaCl, 5.4 mM KCl, 0.5 mM MgCl2, 2.5 mM CaCl2, 5.5 mM HEPES, 11 mM glucose, and 10 mM sucrose, pH 7.4, 330 mOsm/L.

- describe action potential elicitation protocol

Action potentials were elicited by injecting depolarizing current pulses ranging from 20 pA to 100 pA in 20-pA increments for 1 second duration.

- describe total current measurement protocol

Total membrane currents were measured by stepping the membrane potential from −80 mV to +70 mV in 10-mV increments for 500 ms.

- describe data acquisition and analysis

Data were analyzed using Clampfit 10.2 software. Voltage-gated currents were normalized to cell capacitance. Action potential thresholds were determined as the voltage at which dV/dt exceeded 10 V/s.

### Motor Function and Behavior Tests

- describe rotarod test for motor function evaluation

Mice were trained on an accelerating rotarod (2–12 rpm over 5 minutes) for four consecutive days prior to MPTP intoxication. Two trials per day were performed to establish baseline performance. After transplantation, mice were tested twice weekly for 3 weeks. Latency to fall was recorded, and performance was normalized to baseline and expressed as a percentage of the PBS control group mean.

### Statistical Analysis

- describe statistical analysis methods

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance was determined using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All experiments were repeated at least twice with triplicate samples per condition.

### Cell Labeling, Transplantation and Histology

- describe lentiviral labeling of iDPs

iDPs were transduced with a lentiviral vector expressing green fluorescent protein under the EF1α promoter and selected with puromycin (2 μg/mL) for 72 hours to enrich for GFP-expressing cells.

- describe transplantation of iDPs into mouse brain

Mice were anesthetized with ketamine and xylazine and placed in a stereotaxic frame. Two 1-mm burr holes were drilled at coordinates 0.22 mm posterior and 3.25 mm lateral to bregma. A total of 0.25 million iDPs in 5 μL of PBS were injected bilaterally into the striatum at a depth of 2.9 mm using a Hamilton syringe. Injection rate was 0.5 μL/min. Mice were allowed to recover and housed under standard conditions.

- describe histological analysis of transplanted brains

At 21 days post-transplantation, mice were perfused transcardially with 4% paraformaldehyde. Brains were post-fixed overnight, cryoprotected in 30% sucrose, and sectioned coronally at 30 μm thickness using a cryostat. Sections were stored in cryoprotectant at −20°C until staining.

- describe imaging and analysis of brain sections

Sections were immunostained for tyrosine hydroxylase, GFP, and GFAP. Fluorescent images were acquired using a Zeiss 710 Confocal Laser Scanning Microscope. Quantification of TH+ terminals in the striatum was performed using ImageJ software with blinded analysis. Cell counts were normalized to the area of the striatum.
