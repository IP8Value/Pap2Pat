## DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and immunotherapy, specifically to methods and compositions for the treatment of cancer through modulation of myeloid cell function via integrin CD11b signaling. The invention encompasses therapeutic strategies that target the polarization state of tumor-associated macrophages, enhance anti-tumor immune responses, and normalize tumor vasculature by modulating the expression or activity of CD11b, a myeloid-specific integrin subunit. This invention further includes the use of CD11b agonists, miRNA regulators, and combination therapies to reprogram the tumor microenvironment and improve the efficacy of conventional cancer treatments.

### Background Information

Myeloid cells, including monocytes, macrophages, and granulocytes, are critical mediators of both innate and adaptive immune responses within the tumor microenvironment. These cells exhibit remarkable plasticity, adopting either pro-inflammatory, anti-tumor phenotypes or immune-suppressive, pro-tumorigenic phenotypes depending on local environmental cues. Tumor-associated macrophages, in particular, frequently assume an immunosuppressive M2-like state that promotes angiogenesis, tissue remodeling, and inhibition of cytotoxic T cell activity, thereby facilitating tumor progression and resistance to therapy. The signaling pathways that govern this polarization are complex and involve cytokines, growth factors, and cell adhesion receptors. Among these, the colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) has been identified as a key regulator of macrophage survival and recruitment, and its inhibition has been explored clinically to deplete tumor-promoting myeloid populations. However, CSF1R blockade often fails to induce durable anti-tumor immunity due to compensatory mechanisms and incomplete reprogramming of residual macrophages. Integrin CD11b, also known as αMβ2 or Mac-1, is a heterodimeric adhesion molecule expressed predominantly on myeloid cells and has been historically associated with leukocyte adhesion, migration, and phagocytosis during acute inflammation. Prior studies suggested that CD11b was dispensable for myeloid cell trafficking into tumors, yet its functional role in modulating macrophage transcriptional programming remained unexplored. The discovery that CD11b signaling actively suppresses immune suppressive gene expression and promotes anti-tumor immunity represents a paradigm shift in understanding myeloid cell regulation in cancer. Furthermore, the identification of microRNA Let7a as a downstream effector of CD11b-mediated repression of interleukin-6 and c-Myc provides a novel molecular axis through which tumor-associated macrophages can be reprogrammed. Despite advances in immune checkpoint blockade and targeted therapies, a significant proportion of patients remain unresponsive due to the persistence of an immunosuppressive myeloid compartment. There remains a critical need for therapeutic approaches that directly reprogram tumor-associated myeloid cells to restore immune surveillance, inhibit pathological angiogenesis, and sensitize tumors to chemotherapy and immunotherapy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention discloses that ligation or activation of integrin CD11b on myeloid cells induces a transcriptional program that suppresses immune suppressive polarization, enhances pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, and promotes the recruitment and activation of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells within the tumor microenvironment. This effect is mediated through the upregulation of microRNA Let7a, which in turn inhibits the expression of interleukin-6 and the oncogenic transcription factor c-Myc, thereby disrupting a key axis of immune suppression and vascular normalization. The invention further provides a method of treating cancer by administering an agonist of CD11b expression or activity, thereby shifting the balance of tumor-associated macrophages from an immunosuppressive to an immunostimulatory phenotype. The invention encompasses the use of small molecule CD11b agonists such as leukadherin 1, as well as genetic or pharmacological strategies to enhance CD11b signaling, including gene delivery systems and antibodies that promote CD11b conformational activation. The method is applicable to a broad spectrum of epithelial and solid tumors, including but not limited to lung carcinoma, melanoma, breast cancer, pancreatic adenocarcinoma, and colorectal cancer. The invention further provides for combination therapies in which CD11b agonists are co-administered with chemotherapeutic agents such as gemcitabine, paclitaxel, cisplatin, or 5-fluorouracil, wherein the agonist enhances tumor perfusion and drug delivery by modulating vascular maturation. The invention further describes a method of treating cancer by inhibiting the expression or function of microRNA Let7a, thereby increasing immune suppressive gene expression and vascular normalization, which in turn sensitizes tumors to chemotherapy. The invention further provides for the use of a transgenic non-human mammal expressing a constitutively active form of CD11b, which exhibits reduced tumor burden and enhanced survival compared to wild-type controls. The invention further provides a method of enhancing anti-tumor immunity by adoptive transfer of CD11b-activated macrophages into a subject with cancer, resulting in direct suppression of tumor growth and recruitment of endogenous CD8+ T cells. The invention further provides a method of rendering cancer cells susceptible to therapeutic intervention by modulating CD11b signaling in the tumor microenvironment to improve vascular perfusion and immune cell infiltration. The invention further provides a method of screening for therapeutic agents that activate CD11b signaling or increase Let7a expression, using in vitro assays that measure macrophage polarization, cytokine secretion, or adhesion to ICAM-1.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is based on the discovery that integrin CD11b, a myeloid-specific adhesion receptor, functions as a central regulator of macrophage polarization within the tumor microenvironment. Unlike its role in acute inflammation, where it facilitates leukocyte recruitment, CD11b in the context of cancer does not mediate trafficking but instead acts as a signaling hub that suppresses the transcriptional signature of immune suppressive macrophages. Ligation of CD11b by its ligands, including ICAM-1 and fibrinogen, triggers intracellular signaling events that lead to the activation of NF-κB and the subsequent induction of microRNA Let7a. This microRNA directly targets and represses the mRNA of interleukin-6 and the transcription factor c-Myc, both of which are master regulators of the immunosuppressive M2-like phenotype. The suppression of IL-6 and c-Myc results in decreased expression of arginase-1, VEGF-A, and PDGF-BB, thereby reducing the recruitment of regulatory T cells and inhibiting the formation of mature, pericyte-covered blood vessels. Paradoxically, while vascular normalization enhances tumor perfusion and improves chemotherapy delivery, it also promotes tumor growth in the absence of immune activation. The invention demonstrates that CD11b activation achieves a dual benefit: it promotes vascular normalization through suppression of PDGF-BB while simultaneously activating anti-tumor immunity through Let7a-mediated suppression of IL-6 and c-Myc, thereby preventing the pro-tumorigenic consequences of vascular maturation. The invention further provides that genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of CD11b leads to a profound increase in immune suppressive gene expression, elevated IL-6 levels, and enhanced tumor growth, whereas constitutive activation of CD11b through the I332G point mutation in the Itgam gene results in reduced tumor burden and prolonged survival. The invention further provides that the small molecule agonist leukadherin 1, which binds to the I-domain of CD11b and stabilizes its active conformation, is sufficient to repolarize tumor-associated macrophages, increase CD8+ T cell infiltration, and inhibit tumor progression in multiple murine and human xenograft models. The invention further provides that the effects of leukadherin 1 are entirely dependent on the presence of functional CD11b, as demonstrated by the absence of therapeutic efficacy in Itgam−/− mice. The invention further provides that the modulation of CD11b signaling affects both T cell-dependent and T cell-independent mechanisms of tumor suppression, including direct inhibition of macrophage-mediated angiogenesis and suppression of myeloid-derived suppressor cell trafficking. The invention further provides that inhibition of Let7a expression using anti-miRNA oligonucleotides delivered via targeted nanoparticles recapitulates the phenotype of CD11b deficiency, resulting in increased vascular maturity, reduced CD8+ T cell recruitment, and accelerated tumor growth, yet simultaneously enhances the efficacy of chemotherapy by improving drug delivery. ### EXAMPLE 1

Cell lines including C57BL/6 LLC, B16 melanoma, CL66-Luc breast, and MDA-MB-231 breast carcinoma were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection and cultured in antibiotic-free Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium or RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. Cell lines were authenticated by short tandem repeat profiling and tested negative for mycoplasma contamination using PCR-based detection. C57BL/6 wild-type and Itgam−/− mice were purchased from Jackson Laboratories and maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions. The ITGAM I332G knock-in mouse strain was generated by homologous recombination in C57BL/6 embryonic stem cells, replacing exon 9 of the Itgam gene with a modified sequence encoding an isoleucine-to-glycine substitution at position 332. Correctly targeted clones were confirmed by Southern blotting and sequencing. Heterozygous mice were crossed with ROSA26::FLPe mice to excise the neomycin cassette, followed by six generations of backcrossing to C57BL/6N. Homozygous I332G knock-in mice were phenotypically indistinguishable from wild-type littermates. Mice were housed in individually ventilated cages with controlled temperature and humidity, and all procedures were approved by institutional animal care and use committees. For tumor studies, 5×10⁵ LLC or B16 cells were injected subcutaneously into the right flank of 6–8-week-old mice. Tumor volume was calculated using the formula V = (L × W²)/2, where L is the longest diameter and W is the perpendicular diameter. Mice were randomized into treatment groups based on initial tumor volume to ensure balanced cohorts. Leukadherin 1 was dissolved in 2% DMSO and 1% Tween-20 in sterile saline and administered daily via intraperitoneal injection at 2 mg/kg. Control groups received vehicle only. Tumor burden was monitored three times weekly until endpoint, defined as tumor volume exceeding 1500 mm³. At endpoint, tumors were excised, weighed, and divided for histological analysis, RNA extraction, or single-cell suspension preparation. Tissues were fixed in 10% formalin or embedded in OCT for cryosectioning. Immunohistochemistry was performed using antibodies against CD31, Desmin, SMA, F4/80, CD8, and CD4, followed by fluorescent secondary antibodies and DAPI counterstaining. Images were acquired using a Nikon Eclipse TE2000-U microscope and analyzed with Metamorph software. Flow cytometry was conducted using a BD Canto RUO 11-color analyzer, with cells stained for CD45, CD11b, F4/80, Gr1, CD4, and CD8. Single-cell suspensions from tumors were prepared by enzymatic digestion with collagenase IV, hyaluronidase, and DNase I, followed by red blood cell lysis. Bone marrow-derived macrophages were generated by culturing bone marrow cells in RPMI supplemented with 50 ng/mL murine M-CSF for six days. Macrophages were polarized with IFNγ/LPS or IL-4 and treated with leukadherin 1, anti-CD11b antibodies, or control IgG. Cell adhesion assays were performed by plating macrophages on ICAM-1 or VCAM-1-coated plates and quantifying adherent cells after washing. Lentiviral transduction of bone marrow-derived macrophages was performed using VSV-G pseudotyped particles encoding shRNA against Itgam or Il6. Viral titer was determined by qPCR for vector copies. Gene expression was analyzed by quantitative RT-PCR using QuantiTect Primer Assays for murine and human targets, normalized to Gapdh. Immunoblotting was performed using RIPA lysates, separated on gradient gels, transferred to PVDF membranes, and probed with antibodies against p65, phospho-p65, c-Myc, phospho-c-Myc, and β-actin. ELISA assays were used to quantify IL-6, TNFα, and PDGF-BB in culture supernatants. In vivo adoptive transfer experiments were performed by co-injecting 5×10⁵ LLC tumor cells with 5×10⁵ macrophages pre-treated with leukadherin 1 or vehicle. MicroRNA and anti-miRNA delivery was performed using siPORT transfection reagent in vitro or cRGDfK-conjugated lipid nanoparticles in vivo. Nanoparticles were formulated with cholesterol, DOPE, DSPC, DSPE-PEG, and cRGDfK at a 6:6:6:1:1 molar ratio and loaded with anti-miRNA Let7a at a 4:1 lipid-to-RNA molar ratio. Animals received 5 µg of anti-miRNA or scrambled control via tail vein injection every three days. Statistical analysis was performed using one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons or unpaired Student’s t-test for two-group comparisons. Data are presented as mean ± SEM, and significance was defined as p < 0.05. All experiments were conducted in a blinded manner, with treatment assignments concealed until data analysis was complete.
