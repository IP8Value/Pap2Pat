# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to antibodies and antigen-binding fragments

The present invention relates to novel antibodies and antigen-binding fragments that specifically bind to the human prolactin receptor (PRLR), including full-length immunoglobulins, antigen-binding fragments thereof, and engineered variants thereof. These antibodies and fragments are designed to recognize and bind with high affinity and specificity to the extracellular domain of monomeric and dimeric forms of human PRLR expressed on the surface of tumor cells. The invention further encompasses conjugates of such antibodies or antigen-binding fragments with cytotoxic agents, radionuclides, or other therapeutic moieties, as well as nucleic acids encoding the variable regions of these antibodies, recombinant expression vectors, host cells for their production, and pharmaceutical compositions containing them. The antibodies and their derivatives are particularly useful for the targeted delivery of cytotoxic payloads to cancer cells expressing PRLR, including breast, prostate, endometrial, and other malignancies where PRLR is overexpressed relative to normal tissues. The invention also includes methods for producing, purifying, characterizing, and utilizing these molecules for therapeutic, diagnostic, and research purposes.

## BACKGROUND

- introduce prolactin receptor
- motivate blockade of PRLR signaling

The prolactin receptor (PRLR) is a single-pass transmembrane protein belonging to the class I cytokine receptor family, which includes receptors for growth hormone, erythropoietin, and interleukins. PRLR is activated upon binding of its ligand, prolactin, leading to dimerization and subsequent activation of intracellular signaling cascades involving JAK2, STAT5, MAPK, and PI3K pathways. While PRLR plays a physiological role in mammary gland development and lactation, its expression is markedly elevated in a substantial subset of human breast cancers, as well as in certain prostate, endometrial, and ovarian malignancies. Unlike many other receptors targeted in oncology, PRLR is expressed at low to moderate levels in most normal adult tissues, with highest expression observed in mammary epithelium, placenta, adrenal gland, and kidney, thereby providing a favorable therapeutic window. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that prolactin signaling promotes proliferation, survival, and migration of cancer cells in vitro and in vivo, suggesting that PRLR functions not merely as a bystander marker but as a functional driver of tumor progression in specific contexts. Despite this, therapeutic strategies aimed at blocking PRLR signaling using soluble receptor antagonists or neutralizing antibodies have shown limited clinical efficacy, likely due to insufficient potency, short serum half-life, or inability to induce direct tumor cell killing. This limitation underscores the need for alternative approaches that leverage the internalizing properties of PRLR to deliver potent cytotoxic agents directly into tumor cells, thereby overcoming the insufficiency of signaling inhibition alone. The present invention addresses this unmet need by providing high-affinity, fully human monoclonal antibodies capable of binding PRLR with exceptional specificity, inducing rapid internalization, and serving as effective vehicles for targeted delivery of cytotoxic payloads in the form of antibody-drug conjugates.

The term “prolactin receptor” refers to the human prolactin receptor (PRLR), a type 1 cytokine receptor encoded by the PRLR gene located on chromosome 5, which exists in multiple isoforms generated by alternative splicing. The monomeric human PRLR is the single-chain, full-length transmembrane form comprising an extracellular domain, a transmembrane helix, and an intracellular domain, while the dimeric human PRLR refers to the ligand-induced, two-subunit complex formed upon prolactin binding, which is the biologically active signaling state. The term “human” when applied to PRLR or any other protein herein refers to the naturally occurring amino acid sequence as encoded by the human genome, excluding sequences derived from non-human species or synthetic variants unless otherwise specified. “Cell surface-expressed PRLR” denotes the form of the receptor localized on the plasma membrane of viable cells, as opposed to intracellular pools or soluble isoforms. An “anti-PRLR antibody” is an immunoglobulin molecule that binds specifically to human PRLR with a dissociation constant (KD) of less than 5 nM for the monomeric form and less than 500 pM for the dimeric form. An “antibody” as used herein encompasses full-length immunoglobulins, including IgG, IgM, IgA, IgE, and IgD isotypes, as well as antigen-binding fragments thereof, including Fab, Fab′, F(ab′)2, scFv, diabodies, minibodies, nanobodies, and single-domain antibodies derived from camelid or shark variable domains. The heavy chain variable region (HCVR) and light chain variable region (LCVR) are the N-terminal domains of the heavy and light chains, respectively, responsible for antigen recognition, and each contains four framework regions interspersed with three hypervariable complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) that directly contact the antigen. An “antigen-binding portion” refers to any structural unit of an antibody capable of retaining specific binding to PRLR, whether derived from natural or engineered sources. “Antigen-binding fragments” include, but are not limited to, Fab, Fab′, F(ab′)2, Fv, scFv, sdAb, and diabody constructs, each of which may be produced recombinantly or synthetically. Engineered molecules include CDR-grafted antibodies, chimeric antibodies with human constant regions and non-human variable regions, and domain-deleted variants lacking non-essential domains. Hinge heterogeneity refers to natural or engineered variations in the inter-heavy-chain linker region that influence flexibility, stability, and susceptibility to proteolysis; single amino acid substitutions in the hinge, such as proline-to-serine or cysteine-to-alanine mutations, may be introduced to modulate half-life or conjugation efficiency. An “isolated antibody” is one that has been separated from components of its natural environment, including other cellular proteins and nucleic acids, and is substantially free of contaminants, typically achieved through affinity chromatography, ion exchange, or size exclusion. Antibodies may contain mutations, including germline mutations that reflect natural polymorphisms, or conservative substitutions that preserve structure and function while potentially enhancing stability or reducing immunogenicity. An “epitope” is the specific region of PRLR recognized by the antibody, which may be conformational, dependent on tertiary structure, or linear, defined by a contiguous sequence of amino acids. “Substantial identity” refers to nucleotide or amino acid sequences sharing at least 85%, preferably 90%, and more preferably 95% sequence identity over a defined length, as determined by BLAST or FASTA algorithms. “Conservative substitutions” involve replacement of an amino acid with another having similar physicochemical properties, such as hydrophobic, hydrophilic, acidic, basic, or aromatic residues. “pH-dependent binding” describes antibodies whose binding affinity to PRLR is altered under acidic conditions, such as those found in endosomes (pH 5.5–6.0), relative to neutral pH (7.4); such antibodies may exhibit reduced binding at low pH to facilitate payload release or enhanced binding to improve internalization efficiency. Histidine residues, due to their protonation state changes near physiological pH, are frequently introduced or substituted to modulate pH-dependent binding behavior. The KD value ratio between neutral and acidic pH is used to quantify this property, with ratios greater than 5 indicating strong pH dependence.

### Anti-PRLR Antibodies Comprising Fc Variants

- introduce Fc domain mutations
- enhance or diminish antibody binding to FcRn receptor
- increase serum half-life of antibody

The invention encompasses anti-PRLR antibodies with engineered Fc domains designed to modulate interaction with the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), thereby altering pharmacokinetic properties. Mutations in the Fc region, such as M252Y/S254T/T256E (YTE), M428L/N434S (LS), or H433K/N434F, are introduced to enhance binding to FcRn at acidic pH in endosomes, resulting in increased recycling and prolonged serum half-life, which may reduce dosing frequency in therapeutic regimens. Conversely, mutations such as D265A, L234A/L235A, or N297A are employed to diminish binding to Fcγ receptors and complement components, thereby reducing effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) or complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), which may be desirable to minimize off-target toxicity in non-tumor tissues. These modifications are introduced into the CH2 domain of the IgG1 constant region and are compatible with standard antibody production platforms. The resulting Fc variants retain full antigen-binding capacity and internalization properties while exhibiting improved pharmacokinetic profiles or reduced immunological activation, making them suitable for use in antibody-drug conjugates or as naked therapeutics in contexts where immune activation is undesirable.

The antibodies of the invention bind monomeric human PRLR with equilibrium dissociation constants (KD) of less than 4 nM, as determined by surface plasmon resonance under physiological conditions, and demonstrate dissociative half-lives exceeding five minutes, indicating stable, long-lived interactions. Binding to dimeric human PRLR is even more robust, with KD values below 250 pM and dissociative half-lives greater than 55 minutes, reflecting high avidity resulting from bivalent engagement. These antibodies effectively block prolactin-induced activation of the STAT5 signaling pathway in cells expressing human PRLR, with half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) of less than 1.3 nM in luciferase reporter assays. In contrast, certain control or non-neutralizing variants of the antibodies, which bind PRLR without disrupting ligand-receptor interaction, exhibit IC50 values greater than 10 nM, confirming that neutralization is a distinct functional property separable from mere binding. The antibodies further exhibit high specificity for human PRLR, with no detectable cross-reactivity to rodent orthologs, and are capable of internalizing rapidly into PRLR-expressing tumor cells, with over 80% of surface-bound antibody internalized within one hour at 37°C. They do not induce receptor aggregation, activation in the absence of ligand, or significant cytotoxicity when unconjugated, and they remain stable in human serum for more than seven days without degradation. Additional biological characteristics include resistance to aggregation under stress conditions, low immunogenicity in humanized mouse models, and compatibility with site-specific conjugation chemistries for the generation of homogeneous antibody-drug conjugates.

The invention provides antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) comprising any of the anti-PRLR antibodies or antigen-binding fragments described herein, covalently linked to a therapeutic moiety selected from cytotoxic agents, radionuclides, immunomodulators, or pro-apoptotic peptides. Cytotoxic agents include maytansinoids such as DM1 and DM4, auristatins such as MMAE and MMAF, calicheamicins, duocarmycins, pyrrolobenzodiazepines, and tubulin inhibitors such as vinca alkaloids. Radionuclides include 177Lu, 90Y, 225Ac, 131I, and 64Cu, suitable for radioimmunotherapy or imaging. The linker connecting the antibody to the payload may be cleavable, such as a valine-citrulline dipeptide linked to para-aminobenzyloxycarbonyl (vc-PABC), or non-cleavable, such as succinimidyl-4-(N-maleimidomethyl)cyclohexane-1-carboxylate (SMCC). The linker-drug composition is optimized to ensure stability in circulation while enabling efficient intracellular release upon lysosomal degradation. The ADCs are effective at killing tumor cells expressing as few as 10,000 PRLR molecules per cell, with IC50 values below 20 nM, demonstrating potent activity even in models with low receptor density. This efficacy is achieved through rapid internalization and efficient lysosomal trafficking, resulting in payload release and induction of apoptosis independent of receptor signaling blockade.

The invention provides ADCs specifically designed to target tumor cells expressing low levels of PRLR, defined as expression levels less than 30-fold above background as measured by flow cytometry or less than 1 million copies per cell as determined by quantitative mass spectrometry. These ADCs, comprising the anti-PRLR antibodies described herein conjugated to cytotoxic payloads such as DM1 or MMAE, demonstrate potent cytotoxic activity against such cells, with IC50 values consistently below 20 nM in vitro. In vivo, administration of these ADCs to mice bearing xenografts derived from human breast cancer cell lines expressing low levels of PRLR results in significant inhibition of tumor growth, with some models exhibiting complete tumor regression. In particular, in MCF7 and T47Dv11 xenograft models, single or repeat doses of the ADCs lead to sustained tumor suppression, with tumor volumes reduced to undetectable levels by Day 52 post-initial administration, and no tumor recurrence observed for over 100 days. This efficacy is achieved despite the absence of significant receptor overexpression, demonstrating that the internalization kinetics and payload potency of these ADCs are sufficient to overcome low target density, a feature distinguishing them from conventional ADCs requiring high antigen expression for activity.

The invention extends to the broader class of class I cytokine receptors, a family of single-pass transmembrane proteins that includes PRLR, growth hormone receptor, erythropoietin receptor, and interleukin receptors such as IL-4R, IL-6R, and IL-13R. These receptors share structural homology, including fibronectin type III domains in their extracellular regions and conserved WSXWS motifs, and many are overexpressed in malignancies while exhibiting limited expression in normal tissues. The present invention demonstrates that antibodies targeting PRLR, a prototypical homodimeric class I cytokine receptor, can be successfully converted into ADCs capable of eradicating tumors with low receptor density. This principle is applicable to other members of the class I cytokine receptor family, including heterodimeric receptors such as IL-4Rα/IL-13Rα1 or IL-6R/gp130, where antibodies specific for one subunit can mediate internalization and payload delivery. The invention provides methods for generating and evaluating antibodies against these receptors, selecting those with high internalization rates and low off-target binding, and conjugating them to cytotoxic agents to achieve tumor cell killing even when receptor expression is modest. The success of anti-PRLR ADCs validates class I cytokine receptors as a viable class of targets for next-generation ADCs, particularly in cancers where traditional targets such as HER2 or EGFR are absent or downregulated.

The anti-PRLR antibodies of the invention bind to a conformational epitope located within the extracellular domain of human PRLR, specifically encompassing residues in the D1 and D2 fibronectin type III domains, as determined by epitope mapping using hydrogen/deuterium exchange mass spectrometry, alanine scanning mutagenesis, and cross-blocking assays. Peptide blots and cleavage analysis confirm that linear sequences alone are insufficient for binding, indicating that the epitope is dependent on tertiary structure. Antibodies exhibiting overlapping binding footprints, as determined by competition assays using surface plasmon resonance or flow cytometry-based inhibition, are considered to bind the same epitope and are grouped together as a single binding cluster. These methods allow for the classification of anti-PRLR antibodies into distinct epitope bins, enabling selection of non-competing pairs for bispecific constructs or combination therapies. The invention includes antibodies that bind to this epitope regardless of their variable region origin, provided they share the same binding characteristics and do not interfere with each other’s binding.

### Preparation of Human Antibodies

- introduce methods for generating fully human antibodies
- describe VELOCIMUNE technology for generating fully human monoclonal antibodies

The anti-PRLR antibodies of the invention were generated using the VELOCIMUNE platform, a transgenic mouse system in which the endogenous murine immunoglobulin loci have been replaced with human heavy and kappa light chain variable region gene segments. These mice are immunized with recombinant human PRLR extracellular domain, and antigen-specific B cells are isolated from spleen or lymph nodes. The variable region genes are amplified by RT-PCR, cloned into expression vectors, and screened for binding, internalization, and neutralization. This process yields fully human antibodies with natural somatic hypermutation and affinity maturation, avoiding the immunogenicity risks associated with murine or chimeric antibodies. The resulting clones are human IgG1 with germline-encoded sequences that have undergone physiological selection, resulting in high-affinity, low-immunogenicity candidates suitable for clinical development.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Generation of Anti-PRLR Antibodies

- generate anti-PRLR antibodies using VELOCIMMUNE mouse
- isolate and characterize anti-PRLR antibodies

Human anti-PRLR antibodies were generated by immunizing VELOCIMUNE transgenic mice with recombinant human PRLR extracellular domain fused to human IgG1 Fc. Spleen cells were harvested and single B cells were sorted based on binding to PRLR-Fc. Variable region genes were amplified by RT-PCR and cloned into IgG1 expression vectors. Over 300 clones were screened for binding to PRLR by ELISA, internalization in T47D cells by flow cytometry, and inhibition of PRL-induced STAT5 activation in a luciferase reporter assay. Lead candidates were selected based on high affinity, low off-rate, and potent neutralization. One clone, designated REGN2878, demonstrated a KD of 1.05 nM for monomeric PRLR and blocked signaling with an IC50 of 0.4 nM. The heavy and light chain variable regions were sequenced and confirmed to be fully human.

### Example 2. Heavy and Light Chain Variable Region Amino Acid and Nucleic Acid Sequences

The heavy chain variable region (HCVR) of REGN2878 comprises the amino acid sequence of SEQ ID NO: 1, and the light chain variable region (LCVR) comprises SEQ ID NO: 13. The corresponding nucleic acid sequences encoding these regions are provided as SEQ ID NOs: 49 and 50, respectively. The Fc region is of the IgG1 isotype, chosen for its favorable pharmacokinetics, effector function potential, and compatibility with standard manufacturing processes. These controls were used in all in vitro and in vivo experiments to distinguish target-specific effects from non-specific background.

### Example 3. Surface Plasmon Resonance Derived Binding Affinities and Kinetic Constants of Human Monoclonal Anti-PRLR Antibodies

- determine binding affinities and kinetic constants using surface plasmon resonance
- describe experimental conditions and data analysis
- summarize results of binding affinity and kinetic constant measurements

Binding kinetics of REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 to monomeric and dimeric human PRLR were measured using a Biacore T200 instrument at 37°C. Antibodies were captured on anti-human Fc sensor chips, and PRLR ligands were flowed over the surface at concentrations ranging from 3.125 nM to 200 nM. Association and dissociation rates were globally fitted to a 1:1 binding model. REGN2878 bound monomeric PRLR with a KD of 1.05 nM (ka = 1.2 × 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹, kd = 1.26 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹), and dimeric PRLR with a KD of 180 pM (ka = 4.5 × 10⁶ M⁻¹s⁻¹, kd = 8.1 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹). REGN2878-DM1 exhibited nearly identical kinetics, confirming that conjugation did not impair binding.

### Example 4A. Anti-PRLR Antibodies Bind to Endogenous and Overexpressed PRLR Cell Lines

Cell lines MCF7, MCF7/PRLR, and T47D were analyzed by flow cytometry using REGN2878 and an APC-conjugated secondary antibody. MCF7 cells expressed approximately 5,000 PRLR molecules per cell, T47D cells expressed 27,000, and MCF7/PRLR expressed 185,000. Fluorescently labeled REGN2878 was incubated with T47D and MCF7/PRLR cells at 4°C to allow binding, followed by warming to 37°C to initiate internalization. After one hour, >85% of surface-bound antibody was internalized and localized to lysosomes, as confirmed by confocal microscopy. No internalization was observed in PRLR-negative cells.
