## TECHNOLOGY FIELD

- The present invention relates to the field of molecular oncology and precision therapeutics, specifically to the identification, diagnosis, and treatment of malignant gliomas through the modulation of long noncoding RNA (lncRNA) expression. The invention encompasses novel methods for detecting and targeting lncGRS-1 and related glioma-specific lncRNAs, as well as compositions and systems for the delivery of therapeutic agents that selectively inhibit these transcripts. The technology integrates genome-scale functional screening, CRISPR-based transcriptional repression, antisense oligonucleotide design, and three-dimensional human brain organoid modeling to enable the discovery and validation of non-coding RNA targets that enhance the efficacy of radiotherapy in malignant glioma while sparing normal neural tissue. This approach represents a paradigm shift in cancer therapeutics by leveraging the cell-type specificity of the non-coding transcriptome to achieve tumor-selective radiosensitization without systemic toxicity.

## BACKGROUND

- Long noncoding RNAs (lncRNAs) are transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides in length that do not encode proteins but play critical regulatory roles in gene expression, chromatin remodeling, and cellular differentiation. These molecules exhibit highly restricted expression patterns across cell types, making them attractive candidates for targeted cancer therapy due to their potential for tumor-specific function. Despite their abundance and biological significance, the functional roles of the vast majority of lncRNAs remain uncharacterized, particularly in the context of therapeutic response to ionizing radiation. Current approaches to identifying radiosensitizing targets rely predominantly on protein-coding genes, overlooking the non-coding genome despite its potential to harbor highly specific and druggable regulators of radiation response. Malignant glioma, including glioblastoma in adults and diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma in children, remains one of the most lethal cancers, with median survival rates of less than fifteen months despite maximal surgical resection and fractionated radiotherapy. The therapeutic window for radiation is severely limited by the sensitivity of normal brain parenchyma, particularly astrocytes and neurons, to DNA damage, resulting in neurotoxicity that constrains dose escalation. Organoids derived from human pluripotent stem cells have emerged as powerful models for recapitulating tissue architecture and cellular interactions in vitro, yet traditional embryonic brain organoids fail to model the mature, post-mitotic glial and neuronal environments characteristic of the adult brain in which malignant gliomas arise. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for a systematic method to identify lncRNA targets that sensitize glioma cells to radiation while preserving normal neural function, and for a physiologically relevant human tissue model capable of evaluating both efficacy and toxicity in a clinically predictive context.

## SUMMARY

- The invention discloses a novel genome-scale CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screen designed to identify long noncoding RNAs that function as radiation sensitizers in malignant glioma cells. Through this screen, lncGRS-1 was identified as a glioma-specific transcript whose knockdown synergistically enhances the cytotoxic effects of clinically relevant fractionated radiation without impairing the viability of normal human astrocytes or neurons. The method involves the stable expression of a catalytically inactive Cas9 protein fused to the KRAB transcriptional repressor domain in glioma cell lines, followed by lentiviral delivery of a pooled library of single guide RNAs targeting thousands of lncRNA loci. Cells are subjected to fractionated ionizing radiation, and those exhibiting reduced proliferation relative to non-irradiated controls are selected for next-generation sequencing of sgRNA barcodes to identify radiotherapy-sensitizing loci. lncGRS-1, located on chromosome 5q35.3, was prioritized based on its high sensitizer score, nuclear localization, and expression in both adult and pediatric glioma specimens. Knockdown of lncGRS-1 using antisense oligonucleotides or CRISPRi significantly inhibits the proliferation of patient-derived glioma cultures and enhances radiation-induced DNA damage, as evidenced by increased γH2AX and p53BP1 foci formation. A human brain organoid model comprising mature astrocytes and cortical neurons derived from induced pluripotent stem cells was developed to recapitulate the postnatal brain microenvironment, enabling the demonstration that lncGRS-1 knockdown reduces tumor burden in glioma-seeded organoids while preserving organoid integrity and viability. The invention further provides methods for diagnosing malignant glioma by detecting lncGRS-1 expression in patient-derived samples using complementary nucleic acid probes, and for treating malignant glioma by administering therapeutically effective amounts of antisense oligonucleotides, small interfering RNAs, or microRNAs targeting lncGRS-1, either alone or in combination with radiotherapy. The disclosed compositions and methods establish a generalizable platform for identifying and validating non-coding RNA targets that modulate therapeutic response in cancer, with particular utility in malignancies where normal tissue toxicity limits treatment efficacy.

### Method for Identification of a Radiotherapy Sensitizer

- The method for identifying a radiotherapy sensitizer involves the use of CRISPR interference technology to perform a genome-scale functional screen in glioma cells. A population of glioma cells is genetically engineered to stably express a catalytically inactive Cas9 protein fused to a transcriptional repressor domain, such as KRAB, forming a dCas9-KRAB fusion protein. A pooled lentiviral library of single guide RNAs is generated, with each guide RNA designed to target the transcriptional start site of a long noncoding RNA locus. The library is introduced into the engineered glioma cells via lentiviral transduction, followed by selection with an antibiotic such as puromycin to enrich for successfully transduced cells. The transduced cell population is then exposed to fractionated ionizing radiation, wherein each fraction is delivered at a dose of approximately 2 Gy, with fractions administered every other day for a total cumulative dose of 8 Gy over a period of six days. After radiation exposure, cells are propagated for a total of twelve days, during which time sgRNA abundance is monitored by next-generation sequencing of genomic DNA extracted at the beginning (T0) and end (T12) of the culture period. The relative enrichment or depletion of each sgRNA is calculated as the log2 ratio of its abundance at T12 relative to T0. sgRNAs that are significantly depleted in irradiated cells compared to non-irradiated controls are identified as candidate radiotherapy sensitizers. The screen score for each lncRNA is computed as the product of the average phenotype of the top three sgRNAs targeting its transcriptional start site and the negative logarithm of the Mann-Whitney U p-value comparing sgRNA abundance between irradiated and non-irradiated conditions. lncRNAs exhibiting a screen score greater than five and a sensitizer score, defined as the ratio of the radiation modifier screen score to the growth screen score in non-irradiated cells, greater than two are prioritized as radiotherapy sensitizers. RNA is isolated from irradiated and non-irradiated cells using TRIzol reagent, purified with on-column DNase digestion, and analyzed by RNA sequencing to confirm transcriptional downregulation of candidate lncRNAs following radiation exposure. The method further includes validation of candidate lncRNAs using individual sgRNAs in internally controlled growth assays, wherein fluorescently labeled sgRNA-expressing cells are tracked by flow cytometry relative to non-labeled cells over time in the presence or absence of radiation. The lncRNA associated with radiotherapy sensitivity is determined by correlating sgRNA depletion with decreased cell proliferation specifically under radiation conditions.

### Methods of Diagnosis and Treatment

- The method for diagnosing malignant glioma involves obtaining a biological sample from a subject suspected of having a central nervous system tumor, wherein the sample is selected from the group consisting of formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue, fresh-frozen tissue, cerebrospinal fluid, or circulating tumor cells. The sample is processed to extract total RNA, which is then exposed to a nucleic acid probe complementary to a long noncoding RNA known as lncGRS-1, wherein the probe is labeled with a detectable moiety such as a fluorophore or enzyme. Hybridization between the probe and lncGRS-1 is detected using fluorescence in situ hybridization, quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, or microarray analysis. The level of hybridization is normalized against a control nucleic acid sequence derived from a housekeeping gene, and a signal intensity above a predetermined threshold is correlated with the presence of malignant glioma. The method for treating malignant glioma involves administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of an agent that knocks down the expression of lncGRS-1, wherein the agent is selected from the group consisting of antisense oligonucleotides, small interfering RNAs, microRNAs, or morpholinos. The agent is administered concurrently with radiotherapy, wherein the radiotherapy comprises daily fractions of 1.8 to 2.2 Gy for a total cumulative dose of 54 to 60 Gy. Administration of the agent results in inhibition of glioma cell growth and/or proliferation, as measured by reduced cell viability, decreased tumor burden, or increased apoptosis in vivo or in vitro. The agent may be delivered via intrathecal, intracerebroventricular, or convection-enhanced delivery routes to achieve therapeutic concentrations within the central nervous system. The method further includes determining the effective amount of the agent based on factors including the subject’s weight, age, tumor location, and prior radiation exposure. The treatment regime is repeated at intervals of seven to fourteen days, and the subject is monitored for clinical response and neurotoxicity using magnetic resonance imaging and neuropsychological assessment.

### Three-Dimensional Human Brain Organoid of Malignant Glioma

- The invention provides a three-dimensional human brain organoid model of malignant glioma, comprising a mature human brain organoid assembled from induced pluripotent stem cell-derived mature astrocytes and cortical neurons, and seeded with patient-derived glioma cells. The mature human brain organoid is generated by differentiating human induced pluripotent stem cells into pure populations of cortical astrocytes and neurons over a period of at least six months, followed by co-culture in a spheroid microplate at a ratio of 1:1 to form a mixed organoid. The organoid is maintained in a defined medium containing neurobasal, N2, B27, GlutaMax, heparin, and growth factors. Glioma cells derived from glioblastoma or diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma are labeled with a fluorescent reporter and seeded onto the surface of the organoid at a density of 1,000 to 5,000 cells per organoid. The organoid is cultured for a period of two to four weeks, during which time the glioma cells invade the organoid parenchyma in an invasive, infiltrative pattern resembling clinical disease. The organoid is treated with an agent that knocks down lncGRS-1 expression, such as an antisense oligonucleotide, at a concentration of 50 nM, administered weekly via medium replacement. The organoid is exposed to fractionated ionizing radiation at a total dose of 18 to 54 Gy delivered in daily fractions of 2 Gy. The effectiveness of the agent is determined by quantifying the reduction in tumor burden using fluorescence imaging and image analysis software, and by assessing organoid viability and apoptosis using live/dead staining and caspase-3/7 assays. The organoid model is used to screen candidate agents for their ability to synergize with radiation, wherein a candidate agent is deemed effective if it reduces tumor burden by at least 50% compared to control-treated organoids while preserving organoid size and cellular integrity. The organoid model is further used to evaluate the glioma-specificity of lncGRS-1 knockdown by comparing its effects on tumor growth versus normal neural cell viability within the same tissue context.
