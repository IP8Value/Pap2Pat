# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to cellular reprogramming and gene editing

The present invention relates to methods and systems for enhancing cellular reprogramming through the modulation of intracellular mechanical tension and cell-substrate adhesion, particularly in the context of direct lineage conversion of somatic cells into functional neuronal cells. The invention encompasses novel approaches for altering the epigenetic landscape of mammalian cells by applying controlled biophysical cues that reduce cytoskeletal contractility and focal adhesion signaling, thereby promoting chromatin accessibility and facilitating the activation of neuronal gene programs. These methods are applicable to a broad range of cell types and can be implemented using small molecule inhibitors, engineered biomaterials, or microfluidic systems designed to transiently perturb mechanical homeostasis. The invention further extends to the use of such techniques in the generation of induced neuronal cells for regenerative medicine, disease modeling, drug screening, and personalized therapeutic applications, offering a non-viral, non-genetic strategy to overcome epigenetic barriers that have historically limited the efficiency and scalability of direct reprogramming protocols.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cell reprogramming represents a transformative paradigm in regenerative biology, enabling the direct conversion of one differentiated cell type into another without passing through a pluripotent intermediate state. This approach, particularly direct neuronal reprogramming, holds significant promise for generating patient-specific neurons for transplantation, modeling neurodegenerative diseases, and screening neuroactive compounds. However, despite advances in transcription factor delivery and biochemical supplementation, the efficiency of direct reprogramming remains low, often below five percent, due in large part to the stability of the somatic epigenetic state. The chromatin architecture of fibroblasts and other somatic cells is characterized by dense heterochromatin domains marked by repressive histone modifications such as H3K9me3 and H3K27me3, as well as DNA methylation, which collectively act as a barrier to the activation of lineage-specific genes. While chemical inhibitors targeting epigenetic modifiers have been explored, their broad effects often lead to genomic instability or incomplete reprogramming. Recent studies have begun to reveal that mechanical forces exerted through the cytoskeleton and focal adhesions play a critical role in regulating nuclear architecture and gene expression, yet their application to reprogramming has remained largely unexplored. The physical state of the cell—its stiffness, shape, and adhesion to the extracellular matrix—is now understood to influence the localization and activity of nuclear enzymes responsible for histone modification and chromatin remodeling. Despite this growing recognition, no method has been developed that systematically exploits mechanical modulation to enhance reprogramming efficiency while preserving functional maturation of the target cell type. There remains a critical and unmet need for robust, scalable, and reproducible techniques that leverage biophysical cues to overcome epigenetic barriers in a controlled and transient manner, thereby enabling high-yield generation of functional neurons from readily accessible somatic sources.

The invention introduces a novel microfluidic platform and associated methods for enhancing cellular reprogramming by inducing controlled mechanical deformation of somatic cells to transiently disrupt chromatin compaction and promote an open epigenetic state conducive to lineage conversion. The system comprises a microfluidic device fabricated from polydimethylsiloxane or other biocompatible elastomers, configured with precisely engineered channels whose width, height, and aspect ratio are optimized to subject cells to transient, non-damaging compressive forces as they flow through constrictions. This mechanical perturbation results in reversible deformation of the cell membrane and nucleus, leading to partial disassembly of the nuclear lamina, transient loss of heterochromatin marks such as H3K9me3 and 5-methylcytosine, and increased accessibility of neuronal gene promoters. By applying this mechanical stimulus during the early phase of reprogramming—prior to or concurrent with the introduction of reprogramming factors such as Ascl1, Brn2, and Myt1l—the efficiency of direct neuronal conversion is enhanced by more than fourfold compared to conventional methods. The invention further encompasses methods for selecting optimal channel dimensions, including an aspect ratio between 0.8 and 1.2 and a cross-section area ratio between 0.05 and 0.15 relative to the cell’s native cross-sectional area, to maximize chromatin remodeling without inducing irreversible damage or apoptosis. The microfluidic system is compatible with high-throughput processing, enabling parallel reprogramming of thousands of cells under uniform mechanical conditions, and can be integrated with downstream culture, sorting, and analysis modules. Beyond neuronal reprogramming, the system is applicable to the directed differentiation of stem cells, the enhancement of CRISPR-mediated gene editing by increasing chromatin accessibility, and the rejuvenation of aged or senescent cells through epigenetic reset. The invention provides a scalable, non-genetic, and chemically minimal approach to cellular reprogramming that significantly reduces the time and cost required to generate functional neurons while maintaining their electrophysiological maturity and synaptic integration capacity.

Cell reprogramming refers to the directed conversion of one somatic cell type into another, bypassing the pluripotent state, and has emerged as a powerful tool for generating functional neurons from fibroblasts without genomic integration. Despite the success of transcription factor cocktails such as Ascl1, Brn2, and Myt1l, the efficiency of this process remains constrained by the epigenetic rigidity of the starting cell population, which resists the activation of neuronal gene networks. The present invention overcomes this limitation by integrating mechanical perturbation into the reprogramming workflow through a microfluidic device designed to subject cells to transient, physiologically relevant deformation. The device is constructed from polydimethylsiloxane using soft lithography techniques, featuring a network of microchannels with precisely defined dimensions that force cells to undergo rapid shape change as they are propelled by controlled hydrodynamic pressure. This mechanical squeezing induces reversible nuclear envelope wrinkling, transient dissociation of lamin A/C from the nuclear periphery, and a concomitant reduction in heterochromatin density, as evidenced by decreased H3K9me3 and 5-methylcytosine staining. The deformation is applied during the initial 24 to 72 hours of reprogramming, prior to or concurrent with lentiviral transduction of reprogramming factors, and is sufficient to enhance the expression of neuronal markers such as β-tubulin III (Tuj1) without compromising cell viability or function. The channel geometry is optimized to achieve an aspect ratio between 0.8 and 1.2 and a cross-section area ratio between 0.05 and 0.15, ensuring that the applied strain is sufficient to alter chromatin architecture without causing membrane rupture or DNA damage. Experiments conducted with human neonatal dermal fibroblasts, mouse embryonic fibroblasts, and adult dermal fibroblasts demonstrated consistent enhancement of reprogramming efficiency across species and cell sources, with Tuj1-positive cells increasing by up to 4.8-fold compared to control conditions. The invention further encompasses the use of small molecule inhibitors such as blebbistatin, Y-27632, or PF573228 in combination with mechanical deformation to synergistically suppress cytoskeletal tension and focal adhesion kinase signaling, thereby amplifying chromatin accessibility at neuronal gene loci. The microfluidic system may be integrated with 3D spheroid cultures to further enhance reprogramming uniformity and reduce spatial heterogeneity, particularly when combined with inhibition of TGF-β/Activin/Nodal and BMP signaling pathways. The method is compatible with CRISPR ribonucleoprotein complexes, where mechanical deformation prior to delivery increases the efficiency of gene knock-in or activation by enhancing chromatin accessibility at target sites. The device is fabricated using standard microfabrication protocols, allowing for mass production and scalability, and can be adapted for clinical-grade applications in personalized medicine, where patient-derived fibroblasts are rapidly converted into functional neurons for autologous transplantation or disease modeling. The resulting neurons exhibit mature electrophysiological properties, including spontaneous action potentials and synaptic activity, confirming that mechanical enhancement does not compromise functional maturation. This approach represents a paradigm shift in cellular reprogramming, replacing reliance on high-dose genetic or chemical agents with a physical mechanism that transiently unlocks the epigenome, enabling high-yield, high-fidelity lineage conversion.

Fibroblasts transduced with lentiviral vectors encoding the reprogramming factors Ascl1, Brn2, and Myt1l were subjected to mechanical deformation using a polydimethylsiloxane-based microfluidic device containing a series of constrictions with widths ranging from 5 to 15 micrometers. The device was designed to replicate the dimensions of the fibroblast nucleus, ensuring that passage through the constriction induced significant nuclear deformation without causing membrane rupture. Flow rates were calibrated to achieve a transit time of 10 to 20 milliseconds per cell, corresponding to a shear stress of approximately 0.5 to 1.2 Pa. Cell viability, assessed via PrestoBlue and live/dead staining, remained above 95% across all conditions, and no evidence of DNA double-strand breaks was detected by γH2AX immunofluorescence or comet assays. Immunostaining revealed a transient reduction in lamin A/C intensity and increased phosphorylation at serine 392, consistent with nuclear envelope softening. FRET-based biosensors for H3K9me3 demonstrated a rapid and reversible decrease in signal intensity within minutes of deformation, which persisted for up to 24 hours post-deformation. Concurrently, 5-methylcytosine levels were reduced by 38% as measured by ELISA, and chromatin immunoprecipitation confirmed decreased enrichment of H3K9me3 at the promoters of Tubb3, Map2, and NeuroD1. When mechanical deformation was applied at day 1 of reprogramming, the percentage of Tuj1-positive neurons increased from 6.2% in untreated controls to 29.7% in mechanically treated samples, a 4.8-fold enhancement. qPCR analysis confirmed a 5.1-fold increase in Tubb3 mRNA expression. Silencing of lamin A/C via siRNA recapitulated this effect, increasing reprogramming efficiency to 27.4%, demonstrating that nuclear softening alone is sufficient to prime the epigenome. The enhancement was absent when deformation was applied after day 3, confirming that the mechanical stimulus must coincide with the initiation of transcriptional reprogramming. The effect was conserved across human, mouse, and rat fibroblasts, and was further amplified when combined with inhibition of H3K9 methyltransferases or DNA methyltransferases. Confocal imaging revealed a global redistribution of heterochromatin foci and increased euchromatin accessibility, as validated by ATAC-seq, which showed increased peak density at neuronal enhancers. The results establish that transient mechanical deformation of the nucleus acts as a potent epigenetic priming event that synergizes with transcriptional reprogramming factors to overcome chromatin barriers, enabling high-yield generation of functional neurons.

Fibroblasts transduced with the BAM transcription factor cocktail were cultured in the presence of 10 μM blebbistatin, a selective inhibitor of non-muscle myosin II ATPase, during the first three days of reprogramming. This treatment resulted in a 4.3-fold increase in Tuj1-positive neurons compared to DMSO controls, without altering cell proliferation or inducing apoptosis. Atomic force microscopy confirmed a 41% reduction in cell stiffness, and quantitative deformability cytometry demonstrated a 35% decrease in transit time through microconstrictions, indicating a global softening of the cytoskeleton. Immunofluorescence revealed disassembly of actin stress fibers and a 68% reduction in paxillin-positive focal adhesions. RNA sequencing showed downregulation of mesenchymal genes including Acta2, Col1a1, and Fn1, and upregulation of neuronal genes such as Tubb3, Map2, and Syn1. ChIP-qPCR analysis demonstrated increased histone H3 acetylation and H3K4 trimethylation at the promoters of Ascl1, Brn2, Myt1l, and Tubb3, while H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 levels were significantly reduced. ATAC-seq confirmed increased chromatin accessibility at neuronal enhancers and decreased accessibility at fibroblast-specific loci. The activity of histone acetyltransferases increased by 52%, while histone deacetylase and DNA methyltransferase activities decreased by 39% and 47%, respectively. Similar results were obtained with the focal adhesion kinase inhibitor PF573228, which enhanced reprogramming efficiency by 4.1-fold and reduced phosphorylation of FAK at Tyr397. When fibroblasts were cultured on microgrooved polydimethylsiloxane substrates or binary colloidal crystal arrays, reprogramming efficiency increased by up to 5.2-fold, correlating with reduced cell spreading and decreased pFAK expression. Electrophysiological recordings confirmed that neurons derived under these conditions exhibited normal action potential firing, spontaneous excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic currents, and synaptic vesicle recycling. The enhancement was specific to the early phase of reprogramming, as application of blebbistatin after day 5 had no significant effect. These findings demonstrate that reduction of intracellular tension and focal adhesion signaling promotes an open chromatin state by modulating nuclear enzyme activities, thereby enabling efficient and functional neuronal reprogramming.

Fibroblasts transduced with Ascl1, Brn2, and Myt1l were aggregated into three-dimensional spheroids using low-adhesion U-bottom plates and maintained in neuronal differentiation medium supplemented with N2 and B27 supplements. After seven days, spheroids were dissociated and analyzed for neuronal marker expression. Immunofluorescence and flow cytometry revealed a 6.7-fold increase in Tuj1-positive cells compared to monolayer cultures. Spatial mapping of Tuj1 expression within spheroids demonstrated uniform distribution throughout the core and periphery, in contrast to the heterogeneous and peripheral-restricted expression observed in two-dimensional cultures. When spheroids were treated with the TGF-β/Activin inhibitor SB431542 and the BMP inhibitor dorsomorphin, Tuj1 expression increased by an additional 2.8-fold, reaching a total enhancement of 18.7-fold over standard monolayer conditions. RNA sequencing confirmed downregulation of epithelial-mesenchymal transition genes and upregulation of neurogenic transcription factors. The enhanced efficiency was attributed to reduced mechanical tension and increased cell-cell contact, which collectively promoted chromatin accessibility and suppressed repressive histone modifications. The method enabled scalable production of functional neurons without the need for microfluidic devices or small molecule inhibitors, providing a simple, cost-effective platform for high-yield neuronal reprogramming.

### Example 4: Mechano-Epigenetic Mapping for Guided Gene Activation and Silencing

- describe mechano-epigenetic mapping method
- illustrate CRISPR-mediated gene activation or silencing

A microfluidic device was integrated with a CRISPR activation system (dCas9-VPR) and a CRISPR interference system (dCas9-KRAB) to enable simultaneous mechanical priming and targeted epigenetic modulation. Fibroblasts were co-transfected with guide RNAs targeting the promoters of Tubb3 and Sox2, along with dCas9-VPR or dCas9-KRAB, and then subjected to mechanical deformation in the microfluidic device. Mechanical treatment prior to transfection increased the efficiency of Tubb3 activation by 5.4-fold and Sox2 silencing by 4.9-fold compared to static controls. Chromatin immunoprecipitation confirmed increased H3K27ac at the Tubb3 promoter and increased H3K9me3 at the Sox2 promoter in mechanically primed cells. The method enabled precise, multiplexed control of gene expression without the use of exogenous transcription factors, demonstrating that mechanical perturbation can be harnessed to enhance the specificity and efficacy of CRISPR-based epigenetic editing.

Fibroblasts were cultured on polyacrylamide hydrogels with tunable elastic moduli ranging from 0.5 to 50 kPa. Reprogramming efficiency peaked at a stiffness of 12 kPa, yielding a 5.1-fold increase in Tuj1-positive neurons compared to rigid (40 kPa) or soft (1 kPa) substrates. Cells on 12 kPa gels exhibited reduced cell spreading, smaller nuclear volume, and decreased expression of Ki67, indicating a shift toward quiescence. ChIP-qPCR revealed increased H3K27ac and H3K4me3 at neuronal gene promoters, and ATAC-seq confirmed increased chromatin accessibility at Ascl1-target enhancers. Nuclear extracts from cells on 12 kPa gels showed a 48% increase in histone acetyltransferase activity, directly correlating with reprogramming efficiency. The biphasic response to stiffness demonstrates that an optimal mechanical environment exists that promotes chromatin remodeling and lineage commitment without inducing proliferation or stress responses.

### Example 6: Viscoelasticity of Matrix Regulates Epigenetic State and Cell Reprogramming

Hydrogels composed of hyaluronic acid and polyethylene glycol were engineered to exhibit varying viscoelastic relaxation times, from 1 to 100 seconds, while maintaining constant elastic modulus. Fibroblasts cultured on gels with intermediate relaxation times of 10 to 20 seconds showed a 6.3-fold increase in reprogramming efficiency compared to purely elastic or highly viscous matrices. This effect correlated with a 42% reduction in histone deacetylase activity and increased nuclear localization of the histone acetyltransferase p300. Live-cell imaging revealed that cells on viscoelastic gels exhibited rhythmic nuclear deformation and enhanced chromatin mobility, suggesting that time-dependent stress relaxation promotes epigenetic plasticity. The results establish that viscoelasticity, not just stiffness, is a critical mechanical parameter for controlling chromatin state and reprogramming efficiency, opening new avenues for biomaterial design in regenerative medicine.
