# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to magnesium zinc oxide based high voltage thin film transistors

The present invention relates to high voltage thin film transistors fabricated using magnesium-doped zinc oxide as the active semiconductor channel layer, specifically designed for integration into transparent substrates such as glass for applications in building-integrated photovoltaics, self-powered smart glass systems, and high-voltage electronic circuits. The invention encompasses novel device architectures, material compositions, and interface engineering techniques that collectively enable unprecedented levels of electrical stability, thermal resilience, and high-voltage blocking capability under demanding operational conditions. These transistors are particularly suited for use in micro-inverter systems where compactness, transparency, and reliability under prolonged thermal and electrical stress are critical. The invention further extends to methods of manufacturing such devices on commercially available glass substrates without requiring high-temperature processing or exotic materials, thereby enabling scalable, cost-effective production compatible with existing flat-panel display and photovoltaic manufacturing infrastructure.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce solar energy as sustainable energy source

Solar energy represents one of the most abundant and sustainable sources of renewable power available today, offering a viable pathway toward reducing global dependence on fossil fuels and mitigating climate change. As photovoltaic technologies continue to advance, the integration of solar energy harvesting systems directly into architectural surfaces—such as windows, facades, and skylights—has emerged as a transformative approach to urban energy generation. This concept, known as building-integrated photovoltaics, seeks to transform everyday building materials into functional power generators without compromising aesthetic or structural integrity.

- describe limitations of conventional PV systems

Conventional photovoltaic systems rely on rigid, opaque silicon-based solar cells that are typically mounted as external panels, limiting their applicability to rooftops or open land. These systems are incompatible with transparent surfaces such as glass windows, which constitute a significant portion of modern building envelopes. Consequently, the vast potential for energy generation from vertical and overhead glazing remains largely untapped.

- motivate micro-inverter technology

To maximize energy harvesting efficiency in distributed photovoltaic systems, micro-inverter technology has been developed to convert direct current generated by individual solar cells or small modules into alternating current at the point of generation. This approach improves system reliability, enables module-level maximum power point tracking, and reduces losses associated with long DC wiring. However, the integration of micro-inverters into transparent building surfaces requires electronic components that are not only compact and efficient but also optically transparent and capable of operating reliably under elevated temperatures and high voltage conditions.

- describe limitations of micro-inverters

Existing micro-inverter architectures often employ silicon-based power semiconductors that are opaque, brittle, and incompatible with glass substrates. Moreover, these components typically require bulky heat sinks and protective enclosures, making them unsuitable for seamless integration into transparent glazing systems. Alternative semiconductor materials such as silicon carbide and gallium nitride offer high-voltage performance but are incompatible with low-temperature processing on glass and suffer from high manufacturing costs and limited scalability.

- introduce building integrated photovoltaics

Building-integrated photovoltaics represents a paradigm shift in solar energy deployment, where photovoltaic functionality is embedded directly into architectural elements such as windows, curtain walls, and roofing materials. For this vision to be realized, the underlying electronic components must meet stringent requirements: optical transparency, mechanical compatibility with glass, thermal stability under ambient and solar heating, and the ability to withstand high operating voltages without degradation.

- describe need for transparent solar PV system on glass

A critical technological gap exists in the development of high-voltage thin film transistors that can be fabricated directly on transparent glass substrates while maintaining excellent electrical performance, long-term stability, and compatibility with low-temperature processing. Such devices are essential for enabling fully transparent, self-powered smart glass systems that integrate energy harvesting, power conversion, and electronic control in a single monolithic structure.

- describe limitations of SiC and GaN transistors

Silicon carbide and gallium nitride transistors, while possessing high breakdown voltages and thermal conductivity, require epitaxial growth at temperatures exceeding 1000°C, rendering them incompatible with standard glass substrates that soften or deform at such temperatures. Additionally, their opaque nature precludes their use in transparent applications, and their high cost and complex fabrication processes hinder widespread adoption in consumer-facing building technologies.

- describe limitations of amorphous Si and poly-Si HVTFTs

Amorphous silicon and polycrystalline silicon high-voltage thin film transistors exhibit poor electron mobility and significant threshold voltage instability under prolonged bias stress and elevated temperatures. Their performance degrades rapidly under high electric fields, and their fabrication often requires vacuum deposition and annealing steps that are incompatible with large-area, low-cost glass manufacturing.

- describe limitations of organic TFTs and IGZO HVTFTs

Organic thin film transistors suffer from low thermal stability, poor environmental durability, and limited charge carrier mobility, rendering them unsuitable for high-voltage or high-temperature applications. Indium gallium zinc oxide transistors, while offering reasonable transparency and mobility, demonstrate significant threshold voltage shifts under thermal stress and exhibit kink effects at high drain biases, leading to unreliable operation and premature device failure. Furthermore, indium-based materials face supply chain constraints and environmental concerns due to the scarcity and toxicity of indium.

## SUMMARY

- introduce MZO-HVTFT

The present invention introduces a magnesium-zinc oxide-based high voltage thin film transistor engineered for operation on transparent glass substrates with exceptional thermal stability, high blocking voltage, and minimal electrical degradation under prolonged bias stress. This device architecture overcomes the fundamental limitations of prior art materials by integrating a magnesium-doped zinc oxide channel layer with a modulation-doped transition layer at the dielectric interface, resulting in unprecedented performance metrics for transparent high-voltage electronics.

- describe MZO-HVTFT structure

The high voltage thin film transistor comprises a bottom gate electrode, a dielectric insulating layer deposited on the gate, a modulation-doped magnesium-zinc oxide transition layer formed adjacent to the dielectric, a magnesium-zinc oxide channel layer deposited atop the transition layer, source and drain electrodes in contact with the channel, and a passivation layer covering the channel region. The entire structure is fabricated on a transparent glass substrate, enabling optical transparency and seamless integration into architectural glazing systems.

- describe passivation layer

A photoresist-based passivation layer is deposited over the channel region to prevent moisture and ambient gas adsorption during operation, thereby preserving electrical characteristics and enhancing long-term device stability. This passivation layer is applied post-fabrication and does not interfere with the underlying semiconductor or interface engineering.

- describe Mg composition tailored for TFT stability

The magnesium content in the zinc oxide channel layer is precisely controlled at approximately 3 atomic percent, a composition that optimizes the balance between carrier mobility, oxygen vacancy suppression, and thermal stability. This specific doping level reduces bulk trap density by strengthening the Mg–O bond relative to Zn–O, thereby minimizing thermally activated charge trapping and enabling stable threshold voltage behavior across extended temperature ranges.

- describe circular-symmetric design for high blocking voltage

The transistor channel is configured in a ring-shaped geometry, eliminating sharp corners and enabling uniform electric field distribution from source to drain. This circular-symmetric design mitigates field crowding at electrode edges, which is a primary cause of premature breakdown in conventional rectangular-channel transistors, thereby enabling significantly higher blocking voltages.

- describe interface modification with transition layer

A modulation-doped magnesium-zinc oxide transition layer is inserted between the dielectric and the channel layer, with magnesium composition graded from 100% magnesium oxide at the dielectric interface to 3% magnesium at the channel interface. This gradient composition acts as a diffusion barrier, suppressing interdiffusion of zinc and silicon atoms between the channel and the silicon dioxide dielectric.

- describe optimized transition layer thickness

The modulation-doped transition layer has a thickness of approximately 10 nanometers, a dimension sufficient to block atomic interdiffusion while remaining optically transparent and electrically non-detrimental to carrier transport. Thicker layers impede channel conductivity, while thinner layers fail to provide adequate diffusion suppression.

- describe channel length and offset distance design

The channel length is variable between 10 and 25 micrometers, with corresponding gate-to-drain and gate-to-source offset distances designed to reduce fringing fields and suppress hot carrier effects. The gate-to-drain offset is proportionally scaled with channel length to maintain optimal electric field distribution and maximize blocking voltage without compromising on-current.

- describe substrate material options

The transistor is fabricated on commercial soda-lime glass substrates with thicknesses ranging from 0.3 to 0.6 millimeters, although other transparent substrates including borosilicate glass, fused silica, and transparent polymers may be employed provided they are compatible with processing temperatures below 450°C.

- describe application articles

The invention encompasses articles of manufacture including transparent solar panels, smart windows with integrated power conversion, digital radiology detectors, portable high-voltage electronic systems, and building-integrated photovoltaic glass modules that incorporate the magnesium-zinc oxide high voltage thin film transistor as a core component.

- describe method of manufacturing MZO-HVTFT

The method of manufacturing includes depositing a chromium gate electrode on a glass substrate via sputtering, followed by plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition of a silicon dioxide dielectric layer. A magnesium-zinc oxide transition layer is then deposited using metal organic chemical vapor deposition with graded precursor flow rates, followed by a magnesium-zinc oxide channel layer. Source and drain electrodes are formed by electron beam evaporation of titanium and gold, and a photoresist passivation layer is applied over the channel region. All processing steps occur at temperatures below 450°C, ensuring compatibility with standard glass substrates.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce MZO HVTFT on glass

The magnesium-zinc oxide high voltage thin film transistor is fabricated directly on commercially available transparent glass substrates, enabling its integration into architectural glazing systems without requiring additional mechanical support or opaque encapsulation. The device operates reliably under ambient conditions and maintains performance stability across temperature ranges from room temperature to 94°C, making it uniquely suited for use in building-integrated photovoltaic systems exposed to solar heating.

- describe advantages of MZO HVTFT

The magnesium-zinc oxide channel layer provides superior thermal stability compared to pure zinc oxide or indium gallium zinc oxide alternatives, due to the suppression of oxygen vacancies and the formation of stronger Mg–O bonds. This results in a significantly reduced threshold voltage shift under elevated temperatures, enhanced subthreshold swing, and higher on-current density. The device exhibits an on/off ratio exceeding 10⁷ and maintains stable operation at drain biases up to 200 volts without degradation.

- clarify scope of invention

The scope of the invention includes not only the specific device structure and materials described herein but also any variations in geometry, composition, or fabrication method that achieve the same functional outcomes: high-voltage blocking capability, thermal stability, and transparency on glass substrates. The invention is not limited to the exact compositions or dimensions disclosed but encompasses all embodiments falling within the functional and structural principles described.

- define "a" and "an"

As used herein, the terms “a” and “an” are intended to mean one or more unless otherwise indicated by context. For example, a gate electrode may refer to a single electrode or multiple electrodes arranged in parallel, provided they serve the same electrical function.

- define "about"

The term “about” as used in reference to numerical values, such as composition percentages or thicknesses, indicates a tolerance of ±10% unless otherwise specified. For instance, a magnesium content of about 3 atomic percent encompasses values ranging from 2.7 to 3.3 atomic percent.

- describe MZO-HVTFT structure

The device comprises a bottom gate electrode formed on a transparent substrate, a silicon dioxide dielectric layer deposited atop the gate, a modulation-doped magnesium-zinc oxide transition layer formed on the dielectric, a magnesium-zinc oxide channel layer deposited on the transition layer, source and drain electrodes in ohmic contact with the channel, and a photoresist passivation layer covering the channel region. The channel is configured in a circular ring geometry with gate-to-drain and gate-to-source offsets to minimize electric field crowding.

- specify dielectric insulating layer materials

The dielectric insulating layer is composed of silicon dioxide deposited via plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition, although other dielectrics such as aluminum oxide, hafnium oxide, or silicon nitride may be employed provided they are compatible with low-temperature processing and exhibit low leakage current under high electric fields.

- describe channel layer composition

The channel layer is composed of magnesium-zinc oxide with a magnesium composition of approximately 3 atomic percent, resulting in a stoichiometry of Mg₀.₀₃Zn₀.₉₇O. This composition is selected to optimize electron mobility while minimizing oxygen vacancy formation and thermal instability.

- explain benefits of MZO channel layer

The incorporation of magnesium into the zinc oxide lattice reduces the density of oxygen vacancies, which are primary sources of charge trapping and threshold voltage instability. The stronger Mg–O bond compared to Zn–O enhances lattice stability under thermal stress, resulting in a significantly smaller shift in threshold voltage over temperature cycles.

- describe transition layer composition

The transition layer is composed of a graded magnesium-zinc oxide film with magnesium composition decreasing from 100% magnesium oxide at the dielectric interface to 3% magnesium at the channel interface. This composition gradient is achieved by dynamically adjusting the flow rates of magnesium and zinc precursors during deposition.

- explain benefits of transition layer

The transition layer acts as a diffusion barrier, preventing the migration of zinc ions from the channel into the silicon dioxide dielectric and silicon ions from the dielectric into the channel. This suppression of interdiffusion eliminates fixed charges at the interface, reduces interface trap density, and improves subthreshold swing and threshold voltage stability.

- specify electrode materials

The source and drain electrodes are composed of a bilayer of titanium and gold, with titanium providing adhesion to the channel and gold enabling low-resistance ohmic contact. Alternative electrode materials such as indium tin oxide or aluminum may be employed provided they maintain low contact resistance and optical transparency.

- describe ring-shaped gate design

The gate electrode is patterned into a continuous circular ring surrounding the channel region, ensuring uniform electric field distribution from source to drain. Unlike rectangular gate designs, which concentrate electric fields at the corners, the ring geometry distributes the field evenly, reducing peak field intensity by approximately 50%.

- explain benefits of ring-shaped gate

The ring-shaped gate design eliminates field crowding at geometric discontinuities, thereby increasing the device’s breakdown voltage and enabling stable operation at drain biases exceeding 200 volts. This design also reduces the likelihood of localized dielectric breakdown and minimizes hot carrier injection into the gate dielectric.

- describe offset distances

The gate-to-drain offset distance is proportional to the channel length, with values of 5, 10, and 20 micrometers corresponding to channel lengths of 10, 15, and 25 micrometers, respectively. The gate-to-source offset is maintained at 3 micrometers to balance field control and on-current performance.

- specify substrate materials

The substrate is composed of soda-lime glass with a thickness of 0.4 millimeters, although other transparent substrates such as borosilicate glass, fused silica, or polymer films may be used provided they are thermally stable below 450°C and optically transparent in the visible spectrum.

- describe applications of MZO-HVTFT

The magnesium-zinc oxide high voltage thin film transistor is applicable in building-integrated photovoltaic systems, self-powered smart glass windows, digital radiology detectors requiring high-voltage biasing, portable electronic systems operating at elevated voltages, and transparent power conversion modules for electric vehicles or architectural facades.

- describe PV-SOG system

The photovoltaic smart opaque glass system integrates the magnesium-zinc oxide high voltage thin film transistor directly into the glass substrate to function as a micro-inverter, converting direct current generated by embedded photovoltaic elements into alternating current for grid compatibility. The device operates without external heat sinks or encapsulation, enabling full transparency and seamless architectural integration.

- describe self-powered smart glass technology

Self-powered smart glass technology employs the magnesium-zinc oxide high voltage thin film transistor as an integrated power converter, enabling glass surfaces to simultaneously harvest solar energy, regulate light transmission, and power embedded electronics such as sensors, displays, or heating elements without external wiring or batteries.

- describe high voltage applications

The device is suitable for high-voltage applications requiring reliable operation at potentials exceeding 100 volts, including electrophoretic displays, electrochromic windows, piezoelectric actuators, and medical imaging systems requiring stable biasing.

- describe digital radiology application

In digital radiology, the transistor serves as a high-voltage switching element in X-ray detector arrays, where its thermal stability and low leakage current enable precise control of detector biasing under prolonged exposure conditions.

- describe portable electronic systems application

The transistor enables compact, transparent high-voltage circuitry for portable electronic systems such as wearable medical monitors, energy-harvesting wearables, and transparent touchscreen interfaces requiring integrated power management.

- describe method of manufacturing MZO-HVTFT

The method comprises depositing a chromium gate electrode on a glass substrate via magnetron sputtering, followed by plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition of a 200-nanometer silicon dioxide dielectric layer. A modulation-doped magnesium-zinc oxide transition layer is deposited using metal organic chemical vapor deposition with a gradient in magnesium precursor flow rate, followed by a 50-nanometer magnesium-zinc oxide channel layer. Source and drain electrodes are formed by electron beam evaporation of a 100-nanometer titanium layer and a 50-nanometer gold layer, followed by lift-off patterning. A photoresist passivation layer is spin-coated over the channel region and cured at 150°C for 30 minutes.

- specify deposition methods

All semiconductor layers are deposited using metal organic chemical vapor deposition at temperatures below 400°C. Dielectric layers are deposited via plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition, and electrodes are formed via sputtering or electron beam evaporation. All processes are compatible with roll-to-roll and large-area manufacturing techniques.

- describe passivation layer formation

The passivation layer is formed by spin-coating a photosensitive polymer onto the channel region and curing it at 150°C for 30 minutes. This layer prevents environmental adsorption of moisture and oxygen, which would otherwise degrade the electrical characteristics of the device over time.

- specify passivation layer materials

The passivation layer is composed of a photoresist material such as SU-8 or AZ-5214, although other polymer-based materials with low permeability to water and oxygen may be substituted provided they maintain optical transparency and thermal stability below 200°C.

### Material Preparation and Device Fabrication Process

- describe HVTFT fabrication process

The fabrication process begins with cleaning a commercial glass substrate using sequential ultrasonic baths in acetone, isopropanol, and deionized water. A 50-nanometer chromium layer is sputtered and patterned using dry etching to form the gate electrode. A 200-nanometer silicon dioxide layer is deposited via plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition. The magnesium-zinc oxide transition layer and channel layer are sequentially deposited using metal organic chemical vapor deposition with controlled precursor flow rates. Source and drain electrodes are formed by electron beam evaporation of titanium and gold, followed by lift-off. A photoresist passivation layer is applied and cured.

- specify materials used

Materials include chromium for the gate electrode, silicon dioxide for the dielectric, magnesium-zinc oxide for the channel and transition layer, titanium and gold for the source and drain electrodes, and SU-8 photoresist for passivation. Precursors include diethyl zinc and bis(methylcyclopentadienyl)magnesium for the metal organic chemical vapor deposition process.

- describe device structure

The device structure is bottom-gate, inverted-staggered, with the gate electrode on the substrate, followed by the dielectric, transition layer, channel layer, source and drain electrodes, and passivation layer. The channel is ring-shaped with gate-to-drain and gate-to-source offsets.

### Device Testing Conditions

- describe electrical measurement setup

Electrical measurements are conducted using an HP-4156C semiconductor parameter analyzer with an HP-41501B pulse generator for low-bias characterization and a Tektronix 370 high-voltage system for breakdown testing. All measurements are performed in a light-tight, temperature-controlled probe station. High-voltage tests are conducted with the device immersed in Fluorinert FC-40 to prevent arcing.

### Material Characterizations

- describe material analysis methods

Material characterization is performed using transmission electron microscopy with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy for interfacial analysis, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy for elemental depth profiling, and atomic force microscopy for surface morphology. All analyses are conducted on samples prepared via focused ion beam cross-sectioning.

### Device Design

- describe ring structure design

The ring structure is designed as a continuous circular loop surrounding the source and drain contacts, with the gate electrode forming a concentric ring around the channel region. This geometry ensures uniform electric field distribution and eliminates high-field regions at corners.

- compare ring and rectangular structures

Simulations and experimental measurements demonstrate that the ring structure reduces peak electric field intensity by approximately 50% compared to a rectangular structure with identical channel dimensions, resulting in a doubling of the blocking voltage.

### Transfer Characteristics and Thermal Stability

- describe transfer characteristics of HVTFTs

The transfer characteristics exhibit a subthreshold swing of less than 150 mV/decade, an on/off ratio exceeding 10⁷, and a threshold voltage near zero volts. The device shows minimal hysteresis and no kink effect at low gate biases.

- compare MZO and ZnO HVTFTs

The MZO HVTFT exhibits a threshold voltage shift of −6 volts over a temperature range from 294 K to 367 K, compared to −10.5 volts for the ZnO HVTFT. The MZO device also demonstrates a steeper subthreshold slope and higher on-current.

- describe thermal stability of MZO HVTFT

The MZO HVTFT maintains stable electrical performance over 100 hours of continuous operation at 94°C, with less than 5% variation in threshold voltage and on-current.

- compare thermal stability of MZO and ZnO HVTFTs

The MZO HVTFT exhibits superior thermal stability due to reduced oxygen vacancy concentration, as confirmed by activation energy analysis and trap density measurements.

- describe activation energy of drain currents

The activation energy of drain current decreases with increasing gate bias, with a steeper slope observed for MZO HVTFTs, indicating lower trap density.

- compare activation energy of MZO and ZnO HVTFTs

The MZO HVTFT exhibits an activation energy slope of 0.177 eV/V, compared to 0.107 eV/V for ZnO HVTFT, indicating a 65% reduction in trap density.

- describe trap density in MZO and ZnO HVTFTs

The total trap density in MZO HVTFT is 2.14 × 10¹² cm⁻², compared to 3.4 × 10¹² cm⁻² in ZnO HVTFT.

- compare m-MZO and MZO HVTFTs

The m-MZO HVTFT exhibits a 40% reduction in total trap density compared to the MZO HVTFT, primarily due to reduced interface trap density.

- describe interface properties of m-MZO HVTFT

The interface between the transition layer and the dielectric is abrupt and free of interdiffusion, as confirmed by TEM and XPS analysis.

- describe TEM analysis of interface regions

TEM images show no zinc diffusion into the silicon dioxide layer in the m-MZO HVTFT, whereas significant zinc penetration is observed in the MZO HVTFT.

- describe XPS analysis of interface regions

XPS depth profiles confirm the presence of a magnesium gradient in the transition layer and show negligible overlap between silicon and zinc signals in the m-MZO HVTFT, indicating effective diffusion suppression.

### High Voltage Blocking Capability

- compare blocking voltages of MZO and m-MZO HVTFTs

The MZO HVTFT fails at 90 V, while the m-MZO HVTFT sustains operation up to 200 V with leakage current below 10⁻¹² A.

- show drain leakage current of MZO HVTFT

The drain leakage current of the MZO HVTFT increases exponentially beyond 80 V, reaching 10⁻⁶ A at 90 V.

- show drain leakage current of m-MZO HVTFT

The drain leakage current of the m-MZO HVTFT remains below 10⁻¹² A even at 200 V.

- compare transfer characteristics of m-MZO HVTFTs with different channel lengths

Longer channel lengths increase blocking voltage but reduce on-current, with a 25 μm device achieving 609 V blocking voltage and 4.57 × 10⁻⁶ A on-current.

- show trade-off between blocking capability and driving capability

The trade-off is quantified by the product of blocking voltage and on-current, which peaks at a channel length of 15 μm.

- present output characteristics of m-MZO HVTFT

The output characteristics show strong saturation behavior at low gate biases and a kink effect at high gate biases, consistent with channel length modulation.

- show kink effect at high gate bias

The kink effect is attributed to self-heating and is mitigated by optimizing the gate-to-drain offset.

- show highest operating drain bias of m-MZO HVTFTs

The highest operating drain bias is 200 V for the 25 μm device, 110 V for the 15 μm device, and 70 V for the 10 μm device.

- show on/off ratio and VOFF of m-MZO HVTFTs

All m-MZO HVTFTs exhibit on/off ratios greater than 10⁷ and VOFF values within ±0.5 V of zero across all drain biases.

- describe interface engineering using MZO-TL

The modulation-doped transition layer suppresses interdiffusion, reduces interface trap density, and stabilizes threshold voltage.

- explain improvement in blocking voltage due to MZO-TL

The reduction in interface charges and traps lowers the maximum electric field near the channel-dielectric interface, thereby increasing the breakdown voltage.

- simulate electric field distributions in MZO and m-MZO HVTFTs

Simulations show a 7% reduction in peak electric field in the m-MZO HVTFT compared to the MZO HVTFT, directly correlating with increased blocking voltage.

### Statistical Data of Electrical Performance

- present statistics of electrical performances of m-MZO HVTFTs

A statistical analysis of 50 devices shows a mean threshold voltage of −0.2 V with a standard deviation of 0.15 V, mean on/off ratio of 2.1 × 10⁸, and mean blocking voltage of 580 V.

### Simulation of Electrical Field Distribution

- simulate electrical field distribution in circular and rectangular configurations

Finite element simulations demonstrate uniform field distribution in the ring structure and severe field crowding at corners in the rectangular structure.

- compare electrical field distribution in circular and rectangular configurations

The maximum electric field in the rectangular structure is 1.8 times higher than in the ring structure.

- show electrical field crowding in rectangular configuration

Field lines converge sharply at the corners of the rectangular channel, creating localized high-field zones.

- show uniform field distribution in ring structure

Field lines are evenly distributed along the entire channel perimeter, with no localized peaks.

- simulate electrical field distribution in MZO and m-MZO HVTFTs

The MZO HVTFT exhibits higher field intensity near the interface due to trapped positive charges, while the m-MZO HVTFT shows a more uniform field profile.

- compare electrical field distribution in MZO and m-MZO HVTFTs

The peak field in the MZO HVTFT is 1,520 kV/cm, compared to 1,410 kV/cm in the m-MZO HVTFT.

- show influence of gate connection on electrical field

The connection of the gate ring to the probing pad introduces minor field distortion, which is mitigated by optimizing the pad geometry.

- simulate electrical field distribution in path 1 and path 2 regions

Path 1, along the channel, and Path 2, near the gate edge, show comparable field magnitudes in the ring structure, whereas in the rectangular structure, Path 2 exhibits significantly higher field intensity.

- compare electrical field distribution in path 1 and path 2 regions

In the ring structure, the field difference between Path 1 and Path 2 is less than 5%, whereas in the rectangular structure, the difference exceeds 40%.

- show effects of gate connection on electrical field

The gate connection slightly increases field intensity near the pad but does not compromise overall device performance.

- define electrical parameters in exemplary embodiments

Threshold voltage is defined as the gate voltage at which the drain current reaches 1 × 10⁻⁹ A. Turn-off voltage is the gate voltage at which the drain current drops below 1 × 10⁻¹² A. Subthreshold slope is the inverse of the slope of the transfer curve in the subthreshold region. On-current is the drain current at a gate bias of 10 V. On/off ratio is the ratio of on-current to off-current. Blocking voltage is the maximum drain bias at which the device maintains leakage current below 10⁻¹² A.
