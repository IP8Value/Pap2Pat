# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

- introduce expression system for transgene in plants

The present invention relates to a novel, tightly regulated, reversible expression system for the spatially and temporally controlled expression of transgenes in plants, particularly within the tapetum tissue of anthers during postmeiotic development. This system enables precise modulation of gene expression in a manner that is both high-level and stringently restricted to a specific developmental window and cellular compartment, thereby facilitating the engineering of male sterility and its subsequent restoration in hybrid seed production systems. The invention is particularly suited for use in crop species where heterosis or hybrid vigor is exploited for enhanced yield, uniformity, and agronomic performance. By integrating a transcriptional complementation system based on a mutated TATA box with a light-regulated degradation mechanism, the invention provides a platform for the conditional activation and complete suppression of transgene expression in a heritable, non-leaky, and reproducible fashion. This system overcomes longstanding limitations in plant biotechnology related to off-target expression, insufficient stringency, and irreversible genetic modifications, offering a robust and scalable solution for commercial hybrid seed production without reliance on chemical inducers or foreign restorer genes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION AND PRIOR ART

- introduce hybrid vigor

Hybrid vigor, or heterosis, refers to the phenomenon wherein the offspring of two genetically distinct parental lines exhibit superior agronomic traits compared to either parent, including increased biomass, yield, stress tolerance, and uniformity. This biological advantage has been harnessed for decades in major crop species such as maize, rice, sorghum, and Brassica, forming the foundation of modern agricultural productivity. The consistent and reliable production of hybrid seeds, however, requires the controlled prevention of self-pollination in the female parent while ensuring full fertility in the hybrid progeny. Traditional methods of achieving this involve manual emasculation, which is labor-intensive, economically prohibitive at scale, and prone to contamination. Consequently, genetic systems for male sterility and fertility restoration have become indispensable tools in hybrid seed breeding programs.

- describe benefits of hybrid crops

Hybrid crops offer significant economic and agronomic benefits, including higher yields, improved resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses, greater uniformity in maturity and plant architecture, and enhanced resource use efficiency. These advantages translate into reduced input costs, increased farmer profitability, and greater food security. The commercial viability of hybrid seed systems depends critically on the ability to produce large quantities of pure F1 seeds with minimal contamination from self-pollinated progeny. This necessitates a genetically stable, heritable, and scalable method for inducing male sterility in the female parent and restoring fertility in the F1 generation without compromising seed quality or plant fitness.

- discuss history of hybrid breeding

The systematic use of hybrid breeding dates back to the early 20th century, with the commercialization of hybrid maize in the 1920s marking a turning point in agricultural science. Since then, hybrid systems have been refined across numerous crops, relying on natural or induced male sterility mechanisms to eliminate the need for manual detasseling. Early systems exploited cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS), which is maternally inherited and requires a nuclear restorer gene to reverse sterility in the F1. While effective, CMS systems are limited by their dependence on specific cytoplasmic backgrounds and the difficulty of identifying broadly effective restorer lines, particularly in polyploid or outcrossing species.

- explain emasculation in hybrid seed production

Emasculation—the physical removal of anthers from the female parent before pollen maturation—was the original method used to prevent self-fertilization in hybrid seed production. This technique, though effective, is highly labor-intensive, requiring skilled labor and precise timing to avoid contamination. It is impractical for large-scale operations and unsuitable for species with small or densely packed flowers. As a result, the agricultural industry has sought genetic alternatives that eliminate the need for manual intervention while maintaining the purity and efficiency of hybrid seed production.

- introduce male sterility

Male sterility refers to the inability of a plant to produce functional pollen, while maintaining normal female fertility. This trait can be induced through genetic, cytoplasmic, or transgenic means and serves as the cornerstone of modern hybrid seed systems. Genetic male sterility arises from nuclear mutations that disrupt pollen development, while cytoplasmic male sterility results from mitochondrial gene mutations that interfere with anther development. Transgenic approaches have emerged as a more flexible and controllable alternative, enabling the targeted expression of cytotoxic or developmental-disrupting genes specifically in the tapetum, the somatic tissue that supports microspore maturation.

- describe types of male sterility

Male sterility can be classified into three primary types: genetic, cytoplasmic, and transgenic. Genetic male sterility is governed by nuclear genes and is typically recessive, requiring homozygous individuals to exhibit the sterile phenotype. Cytoplasmic male sterility is maternally inherited through mitochondrial DNA and is often accompanied by nuclear restorer genes that suppress the sterility phenotype in the F1 hybrid. Transgenic male sterility, the focus of the present invention, involves the targeted expression of genes that induce programmed cell death, autophagy, or metabolic disruption specifically in the tapetum, leading to the collapse of pollen development without affecting other plant tissues.

- discuss genetic male sterility

Genetic male sterility systems rely on naturally occurring or induced mutations in nuclear genes essential for pollen development. While these systems are stable and heritable, they often require complex breeding schemes to maintain the sterile line and identify suitable restorers. Their utility is further limited by the lack of tissue specificity, potential pleiotropic effects on plant vigor, and the difficulty of achieving complete sterility without compromising female fertility or seed set.

- discuss limitations of genetic male sterility

The principal limitations of genetic male sterility systems include their dependence on specific genetic backgrounds, the frequent occurrence of partial sterility or fertility leakage, and the absence of temporal control over the sterility phenotype. Additionally, the identification of effective restorer genes is often a slow and unpredictable process, particularly in species with complex genomes. These constraints make genetic male sterility systems less adaptable for rapid deployment across diverse crop species and commercial environments.

- describe cytoplasmic male sterility

Cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS) arises from mutations in mitochondrial genes that disrupt energy metabolism or protein synthesis in the tapetum, leading to premature degeneration and failure of pollen formation. CMS is inherited maternally and is widely used in commercial hybrid seed production, particularly in crops such as maize, sorghum, and Brassica. However, CMS systems are constrained by their narrow genetic base, the risk of fertility reversion due to nuclear modifier genes, and the challenge of developing restorer lines that function consistently across diverse genetic backgrounds.

- discuss cytoplasmic genetic male sterility

Cytoplasmic genetic male sterility combines mitochondrial dysfunction with nuclear restorer genes to achieve a two-component system for sterility and restoration. While this approach has been commercially successful, it suffers from several drawbacks, including limited compatibility between CMS sources and restorer alleles, the potential for environmental influence on fertility restoration, and the risk of contamination due to incomplete penetrance of the restorer gene. Furthermore, the reliance on mitochondrial genomes introduces regulatory and biosafety concerns in some jurisdictions due to their non-Mendelian inheritance and potential for horizontal gene transfer.

- introduce transgenic male sterility

Transgenic male sterility represents a modern, precise, and highly controllable approach to inducing male sterility through the targeted expression of cytotoxic or developmental-disrupting genes under the control of tapetum-specific promoters. Unlike natural systems, transgenic approaches allow for the selection of genes that induce cell death or metabolic arrest specifically during postmeiotic stages of anther development, minimizing off-target effects and maximizing the efficiency of sterility induction. This method enables the creation of sterile lines that are phenotypically indistinguishable from fertile plants in all other aspects, including growth, flowering, and seed set, making them ideal for large-scale seed production.

- describe tapetum ablation

Tapetum ablation refers to the targeted destruction or functional disruption of the tapetum, a single-layered somatic tissue that nourishes developing microspores during pollen maturation. The tapetum plays a critical role in the synthesis of sporopollenin, the deposition of the pollen exine, and the provision of nutrients and enzymes required for pollen viability. Disruption of tapetal function—through the expression of nucleases, proteases, or autophagy-inducing proteins—leads to the collapse of pollen development without affecting other floral organs or vegetative tissues. This tissue-specificity makes tapetum ablation an ideal strategy for inducing male sterility in transgenic systems.

- discuss genes used for male sterility

A variety of genes have been employed to induce male sterility through tapetum ablation, including BARNASE, a ribonuclease from Bacillus amyloliquefaciens; BAX, a pro-apoptotic protein from mammals; DIPHTHERIA TOXIN A-chain, which inhibits protein synthesis; and AtBECLIN1, an autophagy-related gene from Arabidopsis thaliana. Each of these genes induces cell death through distinct mechanisms, but all require precise spatiotemporal control to avoid pleiotropic effects. The expression of these genes under constitutive or non-specific promoters often results in reduced plant vigor, abnormal flower development, or even lethality, underscoring the necessity for highly regulated expression systems.

- describe BARNASE

BARNASE is a secreted ribonuclease that degrades RNA and induces cell death when expressed intracellularly. When driven by tapetum-specific promoters such as TA29, BARNASE effectively ablates the tapetum and results in complete male sterility. However, its use requires a corresponding restorer gene, BARSTAR, which encodes a specific inhibitor of BARNASE. The identification and maintenance of BARSTAR lines with sufficient expression levels to fully restore fertility have proven challenging in many species, limiting the scalability and reliability of this system.

- describe RNase T-I

RNase T-I is another ribonuclease derived from bacteria that has been used in transgenic male sterility systems. Like BARNASE, it induces RNA degradation and cell death when expressed in the tapetum. However, its utility is similarly constrained by the requirement for a specific inhibitor to restore fertility, and its expression often leads to incomplete sterility or variable restorer efficacy across genetic backgrounds.

- describe Mammalian uncoupling protein

Mammalian uncoupling proteins, such as UCP1, disrupt mitochondrial membrane potential and reduce ATP synthesis. When expressed in the tapetum, these proteins impair energy metabolism and lead to pollen abortion. While effective, their expression can result in pleiotropic effects on plant growth if not tightly restricted, and their mechanism of action is less predictable than direct cytotoxic agents.

- describe Diptheria toxin A-chain

The diphtheria toxin A-chain inhibits protein synthesis by ADP-ribosylating elongation factor 2. When expressed under tapetum-specific promoters, it induces rapid and complete pollen abortion. However, its use raises biosafety concerns due to its origin from a human pathogen, and regulatory agencies often impose restrictions on its deployment in food crops.

- describe BAX

BAX is a mammalian pro-apoptotic protein that triggers mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization, leading to caspase activation and programmed cell death. Its expression in the tapetum has been shown to induce male sterility in several plant species. However, BAX can induce unintended cell death in other tissues if promoter specificity is compromised, and its activity is influenced by endogenous plant anti-apoptotic factors, leading to variable sterility levels.

- describe AtBECLIN1

AtBECLIN1, the Arabidopsis homolog of the yeast autophagy gene ATG6, plays a central role in the initiation of autophagy, a conserved cellular degradation pathway. When overexpressed in the tapetum under a strong, developmentally regulated promoter, AtBECLIN1 induces excessive autophagic flux, leading to the dismantling of cellular components and the collapse of pollen development. This results in complete male sterility without affecting female fertility or vegetative growth, making it a highly effective and safe candidate for transgenic male sterility systems.

- describe melon ethylene receptor genes

Melon ethylene receptor genes, such as CmETR1, have been used to induce male sterility by interfering with ethylene signaling, which is critical for anther dehiscence and pollen maturation. While this approach offers a non-toxic mechanism, it often results in partial sterility and is highly sensitive to environmental conditions, limiting its reliability in commercial settings.

- describe apoptotic protein inhibitor

Apoptotic protein inhibitors, such as Bcl-2, have been explored as restorer genes in transgenic systems. By counteracting the action of pro-apoptotic proteins like BAX, these inhibitors can restore fertility in F1 hybrids. However, their effectiveness is often incomplete, and their expression can lead to unintended suppression of programmed cell death in other tissues, potentially compromising plant health.

- describe cysteine proteases

Cysteine proteases, such as papain or cathepsin-like enzymes, have been expressed in the tapetum to degrade cellular proteins and induce pollen abortion. While effective, their activity is often difficult to control, and their endogenous homologs in plants can partially compensate for their function, leading to inconsistent sterility levels.

- discuss restoration of fertility

Restoration of fertility in transgenic male sterility systems is a critical requirement for hybrid seed production. Conventional approaches rely on the expression of a complementary gene—such as BARSTAR for BARNASE—that directly neutralizes the sterility-inducing protein. Alternative strategies include RNA interference to degrade sterility gene transcripts, site-specific recombination to excise the sterility cassette, or temperature-sensitive expression systems. However, these methods often suffer from incomplete restoration, genetic instability, environmental sensitivity, or regulatory barriers, highlighting the need for a novel, robust, and universally applicable restoration mechanism.

### LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR ART

- limitations of prior art systems

Prior art systems for transgenic male sterility and fertility restoration are fundamentally limited by their lack of transcriptional reversibility, insufficient stringency of expression control, and dependence on exogenous restorer genes that are difficult to identify, maintain, or scale. Systems relying on BARNASE/BARSTAR, for example, require the co-introduction and stable inheritance of two separate transgenes, increasing genetic complexity and the risk of segregation in breeding lines. Temperature-sensitive or chemically inducible systems are impractical for field applications due to their dependence on external inputs. Moreover, many systems exhibit leaky expression, resulting in partial sterility or unintended effects on plant fitness. Critically, none of the existing systems provide a mechanism for complete, heritable, and environmentally stable transcriptional suppression of the sterility gene in the F1 hybrid without requiring a specific restorer protein. The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a novel, light-regulated, degradation-based system that enables complete and reversible control over transgene expression through the targeted destruction of a transcriptional activator, thereby eliminating the need for a complementary restorer gene.

### Objectives of the Present Invention

- develop tightly regulated reversible expression system

The primary objective of the present invention is to develop a tightly regulated, reversible expression system capable of achieving high-level, tapetum-specific expression of a transgene during postmeiotic anther development, followed by its complete and heritable suppression in the F1 hybrid generation. This system is designed to function without the need for external chemical inducers, temperature shifts, or exogenous restorer proteins, thereby enabling a streamlined, genetically stable, and commercially viable platform for hybrid seed production.

- develop tapetum specific expression system

A further objective is to develop a tapetum-specific expression system that restricts transgene activity exclusively to the tapetal cells during stages 2 to 4 of anther development, ensuring that the sterility phenotype is confined to the tissue responsible for pollen maturation and does not affect other floral organs or vegetative tissues. This spatial precision minimizes pleiotropic effects and preserves plant vigor, fertility, and seed set in non-target tissues.

- use TA29 promoter for tapetum specific expression

The invention employs the TA29 promoter, a well-characterized, tapetum-specific promoter from tobacco, to drive the expression of the transgene. This promoter is known to exhibit strong, developmentally regulated activity specifically in the tapetum during the postmeiotic stages of anther development, making it ideal for inducing male sterility without compromising other aspects of plant biology.

- develop regulatory component for stringent expression

The invention further introduces a novel regulatory component that enables stringent, conditional suppression of transgene expression in the F1 hybrid. This component consists of a fusion protein comprising the N-terminal 131-amino-acid fragment of HFR1, which retains its ability to interact with COP1, fused to a mutant TATA-box binding protein (TBPm3). The fusion protein is expressed under the control of the TA29 promoter and is responsible for activating transcription from a TGTA-mutated version of the same promoter. In the F1 hybrid, the expression of a nuclear-localized, mutated COP1 protein (COP1L105A) under a tapetum-specific promoter leads to the proteasomal degradation of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein, thereby abolishing transcriptional activation and completely silencing the sterility gene. This mechanism ensures that the sterility phenotype is fully reversible in the hybrid without requiring a separate restorer gene.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- provide MS DNA construct

The present invention provides a male sterility (MS) DNA construct comprising a first expression cassette and a second regulatory cassette. The first expression cassette includes a tapetum-specific promoter operably linked to a gene of interest encoding a protein that induces male sterility when expressed in the tapetum, and a transcriptional terminator. The second regulatory cassette includes a second promoter operably linked to a fusion protein comprising the N-terminal 131-amino-acid fragment of HFR1 fused to a mutant TATA-box binding protein (TBPm3), and a transcriptional terminator. The gene of interest is placed under the control of a TGTA-mutated version of the tapetum-specific promoter, which is transcriptionally inactive in the absence of TBPm3. The MS DNA construct, when introduced into a plant, results in complete male sterility due to the high-level, tapetum-specific expression of the gene of interest during postmeiotic anther development.

- provide ES DNA construct

The invention further provides an expression system (ES) DNA construct comprising a first expression cassette and a second regulatory cassette. The first expression cassette includes a tapetum-specific promoter operably linked to a gene of interest encoding a protein of commercial or agronomic value, and a transcriptional terminator. The second regulatory cassette includes a second promoter operably linked to a fusion protein comprising the N-terminal 131-amino-acid fragment of HFR1 fused to TBPm3, and a transcriptional terminator. The gene of interest is placed under the control of a TGTA-mutated version of the tapetum-specific promoter, enabling high-level, stringently regulated expression only in the presence of the TBPm3 fusion protein. The ES DNA construct allows for the conditional expression of any desired transgene in the tapetum during postmeiotic development.

- provide RS DNA construct

The invention further provides a restoration system (RS) DNA construct comprising a third expression cassette, wherein a third tapetum-specific promoter is operably linked to a nucleic acid sequence encoding a mutated COP1 protein (COP1L105A) and a transcriptional terminator. The COP1L105A protein exhibits enhanced nuclear localization while retaining its ability to interact with and mediate the proteasomal degradation of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein. The RS DNA construct, when introduced into a plant, enables the complete suppression of transgene expression in the F1 hybrid when crossed with a plant harboring the MS or ES DNA construct.

- provide reversible expression system for modulating male sterility

The invention provides a reversible expression system for modulating male sterility in plants, wherein the expression of a sterility-inducing gene in the tapetum is activated by the TBPm3-HFR1NT131 fusion protein under the control of a TGTA-mutated promoter, and is completely suppressed in the F1 hybrid through the expression of the COP1L105A protein, which targets the fusion protein for degradation. This system enables the production of male-sterile female lines and their subsequent restoration to full fertility in the F1 hybrid without the need for a separate restorer gene or external inducers.

- provide reversible expression system for modulating transgene expression

The invention further provides a reversible expression system for modulating the expression of any gene of interest in the tapetum of plants. The system enables high-level, developmentally restricted expression during postmeiotic stages and complete transcriptional silencing in the F1 hybrid through the COP1L105A-mediated degradation of the TBPm3-HFR1NT131 fusion protein. This system is applicable to the expression of agronomic traits, metabolic enzymes, or biopharmaceutical proteins in a spatially and temporally controlled manner.

The invention provides a DNA vector comprising the ES DNA construct, wherein the construct is flanked by T-DNA border sequences for Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. ## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- define invention scope

The scope of the invention encompasses the MS, ES, and RS DNA constructs, the vectors comprising them, the recombinant host cells, the transgenic plants, and the methods of their production and use for the purpose of inducing and restoring male sterility in plants. The invention further encompasses the use of these constructs for the conditional expression of any gene of interest in the tapetum, including but not limited to genes encoding enzymes, biopharmaceutical proteins, metabolic regulators, or agronomic traits.

- introduce expression subsystem (ES) construction

The expression subsystem (ES) is constructed by cloning a TGTA-mutated version of the TA29 promoter upstream of a gene of interest, followed by a transcriptional terminator. The gene of interest may encode any protein whose expression in the tapetum is desired, such as a reporter gene, an enzyme, or a biopharmaceutical protein. The ES construct further includes a second regulatory cassette comprising a constitutive promoter driving the expression of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein, which is required for transcriptional activation of the TGTA-mutated promoter.

- describe ES expression mechanism

The ES expression mechanism operates through the binding of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein to the TGTA-mutated TATA box of the TA29 promoter, which is otherwise non-functional in the presence of native TBP. The fusion protein recruits the transcriptional machinery to initiate high-level transcription of the gene of interest exclusively in the tapetum during stages 2 to 4 of anther development. Expression is absent in all other tissues due to the lack of the fusion protein and the inactivity of the TGTA promoter without it.

- introduce restoration subsystem (RS) construction

The restoration subsystem (RS) is constructed by cloning the Arabidopsis tapetum-specific promoter A9 (PA9) upstream of a nucleic acid sequence encoding the COP1L105A protein, followed by a transcriptional terminator. The COP1L105A mutation enhances nuclear localization of COP1 without compromising its E3 ubiquitin ligase activity, enabling efficient degradation of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein.

- describe RS expression mechanism

The RS expression mechanism functions through the tapetum-specific expression of COP1L105A during early anther development. The COP1L105A protein interacts with the HFR1NT131 moiety of the fusion protein, ubiquitinates it, and targets it for proteasomal degradation. This eliminates the transcriptional activator necessary for the TGTA-mutated promoter, resulting in complete transcriptional silencing of the gene of interest in the F1 hybrid.

- outline F1 plant generation

The F1 plant is generated by crossing a female plant comprising the MS or ES DNA construct with a male plant comprising the RS DNA construct. The resulting hybrid inherits both transgenes and expresses the COP1L105A protein in the tapetum during early anther development, leading to the degradation of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein and the complete suppression of the sterility or expression cassette.

- describe gusA reporter gene expression

The gusA reporter gene, encoding β-glucuronidase, was used to validate the functionality of the ES and RS constructs. In the absence of COP1L105A, gusA expression was high and restricted to the tapetum. In the F1 hybrid, gusA expression was completely abolished, as confirmed by fluorimetric assays and histochemical staining, demonstrating the efficacy of the restoration mechanism.

- define cloning vector

A cloning vector is a DNA molecule used to carry and replicate a foreign DNA fragment in a host organism. In the present invention, the cloning vector is a binary vector derived from pCAMBIA1300 or pBI101, containing T-DNA borders, a selectable marker, and multiple cloning sites for the insertion of the MS, ES, or RS DNA constructs.

- define reporter gene

A reporter gene is a gene whose expression can be easily detected and quantified. In the present invention, the gusA gene encoding β-glucuronidase is used as a reporter gene to monitor the spatial and temporal expression patterns of the ES and MS constructs.

- define promoter

A promoter is a DNA sequence that initiates transcription of a gene by recruiting RNA polymerase and associated transcription factors. In the present invention, the TA29 promoter and its TGTA-mutated variant are used to drive tapetum-specific expression, while the PA9 promoter drives expression of COP1L105A.

- define constitutive promoter

A constitutive promoter is a promoter that drives continuous gene expression in most tissues and developmental stages. In the present invention, the CaMV 35S minimal promoter is used to constitutively express the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein.

- define tapetum-specific promoter

A tapetum-specific promoter is a promoter that drives gene expression exclusively in the tapetum layer of the anther. In the present invention, the TA29 and PA9 promoters are tapetum-specific and active during postmeiotic stages of anther development.

- define expression

Expression refers to the process by which information encoded in a gene is used to synthesize a functional gene product, such as a protein. In the present invention, expression of the gene of interest occurs only when the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein is present and active.

- define expression cassette

An expression cassette is a DNA construct comprising a promoter, a coding sequence, and a terminator, designed to drive the expression of a gene in a host cell. In the present invention, the MS, ES, and RS constructs each comprise one or more expression cassettes.

- define transcription factor

A transcription factor is a protein that binds to specific DNA sequences to regulate transcription. In the present invention, the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein functions as a synthetic transcription factor that activates transcription from the TGTA-mutated promoter.

- define recombinant vector

A recombinant vector is a DNA molecule engineered to contain genetic material from different sources. In the present invention, the recombinant vectors contain the MS, ES, or RS DNA constructs inserted into a binary vector backbone.

- define expression vector

An expression vector is a recombinant vector designed to facilitate the expression of a cloned gene in a host organism. In the present invention, the binary vectors containing the MS, ES, or RS constructs are expression vectors.

- define binary vector

A binary vector is a plasmid used in Agrobacterium-mediated plant transformation, comprising a T-DNA region flanked by border sequences and a replication origin for E. coli and Agrobacterium. In the present invention, binary vectors derived from pCAMBIA1300 or pBI101 are used.

- describe suitable promoter

Suitable promoters for use in the invention include the TA29 promoter, the PA9 promoter, the CaMV 35S minimal promoter, and other tapetum-specific or constitutive promoters known in the art. The choice of promoter determines the spatial and temporal pattern of transgene expression.

- describe anther-specific promoter

An anther-specific promoter is a promoter that directs gene expression specifically in the anther. In the present invention, the TA29 and PA9 promoters are anther-specific and exhibit activity exclusively in the tapetum during postmeiotic development.

- define program cell death (PCD)/apoptosis

Programmed cell death (PCD) or apoptosis is a genetically controlled process of cell suicide that plays a critical role in development and stress responses. In the present invention, the expression of BECLIN1 induces autophagic PCD in the tapetum, leading to male sterility.

- define autophagy

Autophagy is a cellular degradation process in which cytoplasmic components are sequestered into autophagosomes and delivered to the vacuole for degradation. In the present invention, the overexpression of BECLIN1 induces excessive autophagy in the tapetum, resulting in the collapse of pollen development.

- define explants

Explants are plant tissues or cells used as starting material for in vitro culture and transformation. In the present invention, leaf discs, cotyledons, or hypocotyls of Nicotiana tabacum are used as explants for Agrobacterium-mediated transformation.

- define photo-morphogenesis

Photo-morphogenesis is the regulation of plant growth and development by light. In the present invention, the interaction between HFR1 and COP1 is derived from the light-signaling pathway, wherein COP1 mediates the degradation of HFR1 in the dark.

- outline invention objectives

The objectives of the invention are to provide a genetically stable, reversible, and scalable system for inducing male sterility and restoring fertility in plants, to eliminate the need for exogenous restorer genes or chemical inducers, and to enable the high-level, tapetum-specific expression of any gene of interest with complete transcriptional control in the F1 hybrid.

- describe ES construct components

The ES construct comprises two expression cassettes: the first contains the TGTA-mutated TA29 promoter driving the gene of interest, and the second contains the CaMV 35S minimal promoter driving the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein. Both cassettes are flanked by T-DNA borders and include transcriptional terminators.

- describe TA29(TGTA) promoter

The TA29(TGTA) promoter is a modified version of the native TA29 promoter in which the TATA box sequence TATATATG is mutated to TGTA. This mutation renders the promoter non-functional in the presence of native TBP but fully activatable by the TBPm3 fusion protein.

- describe gusA reporter gene

The gusA reporter gene encodes β-glucuronidase, an enzyme that hydrolyzes X-Gluc to produce a blue precipitate. In the present invention, gusA is used to visualize and quantify the spatial and temporal expression of the ES construct in the tapetum.

- describe nosT transcriptional terminator

The nosT transcriptional terminator is derived from the nopaline synthase gene of Agrobacterium tumefaciens and is used to ensure proper termination of transcription in plant cells.

- describe HFR1n-131-TBPm3 fusion protein

The HFR1n-131-TBPm3 fusion protein comprises the N-terminal 131 amino acids of HFR1 fused to the mutant TATA-box binding protein TBPm3. The HFR1 fragment retains its ability to interact with COP1, while the TBPm3 fragment enables binding to the TGTA-mutated promoter.

- describe MS module construct

The MS module construct is identical to the ES construct except that the gene of interest is replaced with the Arabidopsis BECLIN1 gene, which induces autophagic cell death in the tapetum, resulting in complete male sterility.

- describe BECLIN1 gene expression

The BECLIN1 gene is expressed under the control of the TGTA-mutated TA29 promoter in the MS construct, resulting in high-level, tapetum-specific expression during stages 2 to 4 of anther development. This expression leads to excessive autophagy, collapse of microspore development, and complete male sterility.

- describe RS module construct

The RS module construct comprises the PA9 promoter driving the expression of the COP1L105A protein. The COP1L105A mutation enhances nuclear localization, enabling efficient degradation of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein in the tapetum.

- describe COP1L105A gene expression

The COP1L105A gene is expressed under the control of the PA9 promoter, resulting in high levels of the mutant COP1 protein in the tapetum during early anther development. The protein interacts with the HFR1NT131 moiety of the fusion protein and targets it for proteasomal degradation, thereby abolishing transcriptional activation of the TGTA-mutated promoter.

- outline method for reversible gene expression

The method for reversible gene expression involves transforming a plant with the ES construct to enable high-level transgene expression in the tapetum, and subsequently crossing it with a plant transformed with the RS construct. In the F1 hybrid, the COP1L105A protein degrades the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein, resulting in complete transcriptional silencing of the transgene.

- describe MS construct transformation

The MS construct is introduced into plant cells via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation using leaf disc or cotyledon explants. Transformed cells are selected on antibiotics, regenerated into whole plants, and verified for the presence of the transgene by PCR and Southern blot analysis.

- describe RS construct transformation

The RS construct is introduced into plant cells via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation using the same methodology as for the MS construct. Transgenic plants are selected, regenerated, and verified for COP1L105A expression by qRT-PCR and Western blot analysis.

- outline F1 hybrid development

F1 hybrid development is achieved by crossing a female plant homozygous for the MS or ES construct with a male plant homozygous for the RS construct. The resulting F1 seeds are collected, germinated on selective media, and confirmed for the presence of both transgenes by PCR.

- describe COP1L105A protein function

The COP1L105A protein functions as an E3 ubiquitin ligase that recognizes the HFR1NT131 moiety of the fusion protein and mediates its polyubiquitination and subsequent degradation by the 26S proteasome. The L105A mutation enhances nuclear localization, ensuring that degradation occurs in the nucleus where transcriptional activation takes place.

- describe HFR1n-131aa-TBPm3 fusion protein function

The HFR1n-131aa-TBPm3 fusion protein functions as a synthetic transcription factor that binds to the TGTA-mutated TATA box and recruits the transcriptional machinery to initiate gene expression. The HFR1n-131 fragment enables interaction with COP1, allowing for conditional degradation and transcriptional silencing in the F1 hybrid.

- outline Agrobacterium-mediated transformation method

Agrobacterium-mediated transformation is performed by co-cultivating plant explants with Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain EHA105 harboring the binary vector. After co-cultivation, explants are transferred to selection media containing kanamycin and hygromycin. Regenerated shoots are rooted and transferred to soil. Transgene integration is confirmed by PCR and Southern blot analysis.

- describe plant transformation

Plant transformation refers to the stable integration of foreign DNA into the plant genome. In the present invention, transformation is achieved via Agrobacterium-mediated delivery of the MS, ES, or RS constructs into the nuclear genome of Nicotiana tabacum.

- describe transgenic plant selection

Transgenic plant selection is performed by growing regenerated plants on media containing antibiotics corresponding to the selectable marker genes in the binary vector. PCR and Southern blot analysis are used to confirm transgene integration and copy number.

- outline invention applications

The invention has applications in hybrid seed production, transgene containment, metabolic engineering, and biopharming. It enables the production of male-sterile female lines for hybrid seed multiplication, the conditional expression of valuable traits in the tapetum, and the complete suppression of transgene expression in the F1 generation without the need for restorer genes.

- describe ms transgenic plants

MS transgenic plants exhibit complete male sterility, with no viable pollen, normal female fertility, and no morphological abnormalities. These plants serve as the female parent in hybrid seed production.

- describe rs transgenic plants

RS transgenic plants exhibit normal growth, development, and fertility. They express the COP1L105A protein in the tapetum and are used as the male parent to restore fertility in F1 hybrids derived from MS or ES plants.

- describe crossing of ms and rs transgenic plants

Crossing of MS and RS transgenic plants is performed by emasculating the flowers of the MS plant and pollinating them with pollen from the RS plant. The resulting F1 seeds are collected and germinated on selective media to confirm the presence of both transgenes.

- analyze f1 plants

F1 plants are analyzed for transgene segregation, expression of the gene of interest, pollen viability, germination rates, and seed set. Fluorimetric GUS assays, qRT-PCR, and SEM are used to confirm complete suppression of the gene of interest and restoration of fertility.

- describe expression of beclin1

In F1 hybrids, BECLIN1 expression is completely abolished due to the degradation of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein by COP1L105A.

- describe hybrid crops

Hybrid crops produced using the invention exhibit heterosis, with increased yield, uniformity, and stress tolerance. The system enables the production of pure F1 seeds without manual emasculation, reducing costs and increasing efficiency.
