# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to nasobiliary drainage

The present invention relates to medical devices and methods employed in endoscopic procedures, specifically to an improved system and technique for the repositioning of nasobiliary drainage catheters following endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography. The invention provides a novel, integrated device designed to facilitate the safe, efficient, and reliable transfer of a drainage catheter from the oral cavity to the nasal passage without requiring direct manual manipulation within the patient’s oropharynx. This device is particularly suited for use in sedated patients undergoing endoscopic biliary interventions, where rapid, minimally invasive catheter repositioning is clinically desirable to reduce procedural time, minimize patient discomfort, and eliminate risks associated with manual grasping of catheter tips in the oral cavity. The system is engineered for compatibility with standard endoscopic equipment and does not require additional imaging modalities such as fluoroscopy for successful deployment, making it broadly applicable across clinical settings with varying levels of technological access.

## BACKGROUND ART

- describe existing endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography

Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) is a well-established diagnostic and therapeutic procedure used to evaluate and manage biliary and pancreatic ductal pathologies. Following ERCP, endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) is frequently employed to provide external biliary decompression in cases of obstructive cholangitis, biliary leaks, or preoperative bile duct preparation. The ENBD procedure typically involves the placement of a drainage catheter through the papilla of Vater, with the distal end of the catheter retained within the biliary tree and the proximal end exiting the oral cavity. To complete the drainage pathway, the proximal end must be repositioned from the mouth to the nasal cavity, allowing for secure external fixation and minimizing the risk of accidental dislodgement or patient discomfort during recovery.

- describe repositioning procedure of nasobiliary drainage tube

The conventional method for repositioning the ENBD catheter involves withdrawing the endoscope from the mouth, removing the oral mouthpiece, manually opening the patient’s mouth, inserting a flexible plastic guide tube through the nasal passage, and then using forceps or fingers to grasp the exposed proximal end of the ENBD catheter within the oral cavity. Once grasped, the plastic tube is pulled outward, thereby drawing the ENBD catheter through the pharynx and into the nasal cavity, where it is secured. This process requires precise manual coordination and direct physical contact with the patient’s oral tissues, often under conditions of limited visibility and patient sedation.

- describe limitations of existing repositioning method

The conventional repositioning technique is fraught with significant limitations. It is time-consuming, often requiring more than three minutes to complete, and is highly dependent on the operator’s dexterity and the patient’s cooperation. In sedated patients, the need to remove the mouthpiece and wait for partial awakening to open the mouth introduces unnecessary delays and increases procedural complexity. Furthermore, the manual manipulation of the catheter tip within the oral cavity is inherently imprecise, particularly in patients with anatomical variations such as a high Mallampati score, limited mouth opening, or a short neck. These factors frequently result in repeated attempts, prolonged exposure to endoscopic instrumentation, and increased risk of procedural failure.

- describe risks of teeth/oral mucosa damage

The direct contact between forceps or fingers and the oral mucosa during catheter grasping poses a substantial risk of trauma, including lacerations, abrasions, and even dental injury. Patients with fragile oral tissues, edentulous states, or preexisting dental prostheses are especially vulnerable. These injuries not only compromise patient safety but may also necessitate additional interventions, prolong hospital stays, and increase overall healthcare costs.

- describe risks of physician's injuries

Operators performing the conventional technique are at considerable risk of finger injury due to unexpected patient biting, particularly in partially sedated individuals who may exhibit involuntary reflexes. Such injuries, ranging from superficial lacerations to deep puncture wounds, carry the potential for infection, occupational exposure to bloodborne pathogens, and temporary or permanent impairment of manual function. The psychological burden of performing this procedure under fear of injury further contributes to procedural stress and reduced efficiency.

- describe need for solution

There is a critical and unmet clinical need for a device and method that eliminates the necessity for direct oral manipulation during ENBD catheter repositioning, reduces procedural time, enhances safety for both patient and provider, and maintains high technical success rates regardless of anatomical variability. A solution that enables seamless, contact-free transfer of the catheter from the oral cavity to the nasal passage while preserving the integrity of oral structures and minimizing operator exposure is essential for advancing the standard of care in endoscopic biliary interventions.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

### Technical Problem

- solve limitations of existing repositioning method

The present invention addresses the shortcomings of conventional ENBD repositioning techniques by eliminating the need for manual grasping of the catheter tip within the oral cavity, reducing procedural time, mitigating risks of oral and dental trauma, and preventing operator injury. The invention provides a fully integrated, pre-assembled system that enables the repositioning of the ENBD catheter through a controlled, non-contact mechanism that functions independently of patient cooperation, oral anatomy, or visual access to the oropharynx. This system resolves the inherent inefficiencies and dangers of existing methods by introducing a novel mechanical interface that guides the catheter through the pharynx with precision and consistency.

### Technical Solution

- describe ring type module

The invention comprises a ring-type module, also referred to as a J-Loop, which is a pre-formed, rigid yet flexible curved structure designed to conform to the natural curvature of the oropharynx. The ring-type module is constructed from a biocompatible, radiopaque polymer that allows for visualization under fluoroscopic guidance when necessary, while maintaining sufficient stiffness to resist collapse during manipulation. The module is attached to a bar-shaped handle that provides the operator with optimal control and leverage during insertion and withdrawal.

- describe nasal tube

A specialized nasal tube, composed of a soft, kink-resistant, medical-grade polyurethane, is integrally connected to the ring-type module. The nasal tube is marked with a series of multistep longitudinal indicators that correspond to standardized insertion depths, enabling consistent and reproducible placement from the nares to the posterior pharynx. The distal end of the nasal tube is designed to be captured securely by the curved loop of the ring-type module, ensuring that when the module is withdrawn, the tube is pulled in a controlled, linear trajectory.

- describe adjuster

An adjustable locking mechanism, referred to as an adjuster, is incorporated into the bar-shaped handle of the ring-type module. This adjuster permits the operator to fine-tune the tension and alignment between the ring and the nasal tube, accommodating variations in patient anatomy and catheter thickness. The adjuster includes a sliding groove and a fixing means, such as a threaded collar or friction-based clamp, that allows the nasal tube to be locked at a predetermined position relative to the ring, ensuring that the catheter remains securely coupled during repositioning.

- describe method of using device

The method of using the device involves first withdrawing the endoscope from the oral cavity after completion of ERCP. The ring-type module is then gently inserted into the mouth, guided along the base of the tongue toward the posterior pharynx. Simultaneously, the nasal tube is advanced through the nasal passage to a depth indicated by the multistep markings, ensuring that its distal end reaches the level of the oropharynx. Once properly positioned, the operator withdraws the ring-type module, which, by virtue of its curved design and secure coupling with the nasal tube, pulls the proximal end of the ENBD catheter from the oral cavity through the pharynx and into the nasal tube. The ENBD catheter is then connected to the distal end of the nasal tube, and the entire assembly is withdrawn through the nose, completing the repositioning.

### Advantageous Effects

- describe safety and efficiency

The invention significantly enhances procedural safety by eliminating direct contact with the oral mucosa and teeth, thereby preventing trauma, bleeding, and dental injury. Simultaneously, it removes the risk of operator finger injury from patient biting, as no manual manipulation within the mouth is required. The procedure is completed in a fraction of the time required by conventional methods, with average repositioning times reduced from over three minutes to less than one minute, thereby improving overall workflow efficiency and reducing sedation duration.

- describe adjustability

The adjuster mechanism allows for customization of the coupling tension between the ring-type module and the nasal tube, enabling adaptation to a wide range of catheter diameters and patient anatomies. The multistep markings on the nasal tube provide objective, reproducible depth guidance, reducing variability between operators and improving consistency across clinical settings.

- describe economic efficiency

By reducing procedural time, minimizing complications, and eliminating the need for additional instruments or imaging, the invention lowers overall healthcare costs. The device is single-use, sterile, and cost-effective to manufacture, making it suitable for widespread adoption in both high-resource and resource-limited environments. Its simplicity also reduces the learning curve for novice practitioners, enhancing training efficiency and expanding access to safe ENBD procedures.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

- introduce ring type module

The ring type module is a self-contained, pre-formed component composed of a curved loop and a proximal bar handle, integrally molded from a single piece of radiopaque, medical-grade polymer. The loop is shaped to approximate the natural curvature of the oropharynx, ensuring smooth passage and minimizing resistance during insertion.

- describe ring part

The ring part is a semi-rigid, J-shaped arc with a diameter of approximately 15 mm and a thickness of 1.2 mm, designed to maintain structural integrity under axial force while remaining flexible enough to navigate anatomical contours. Its inner surface is coated with a low-friction hydrophilic layer to facilitate smooth passage over mucosal surfaces.

- describe ring handle

The ring handle extends proximally from the ring part and is ergonomically contoured to fit comfortably within the operator’s grip. It is textured for non-slip handling and includes a thumb rest to enhance control during withdrawal. The handle is approximately 8 cm in length and is designed to remain outside the oral cavity during use.

- describe adjuster

The adjuster is a sliding collar mechanism mounted along the length of the ring handle, which engages with a longitudinal sliding groove on the nasal tube. The adjuster includes a locking screw that, when tightened, fixes the position of the nasal tube relative to the ring, preventing slippage during repositioning.

- describe nasal tube

The nasal tube is a transparent, flexible polyurethane catheter with an outer diameter of 4.5 Fr and a length of 60 cm. It features three distinct multistep markings at 15 cm, 30 cm, and 45 cm from the distal tip, corresponding to standardized insertion depths for different patient anatomies.

- describe multistep third mark

The multistep third mark, located at 45 cm from the distal tip, indicates the optimal insertion depth for patients with a high Mallampati score or limited mouth opening. This mark ensures that the distal end of the nasal tube reaches the posterior pharynx even in challenging anatomical configurations.

- describe insertion of ring part

The ring part is inserted into the oral cavity under direct visualization using the endoscope or by tactile guidance. It is advanced along the midline of the tongue until the curved portion is positioned just above the epiglottis, ensuring alignment with the path of the ENBD catheter.

- describe insertion of nasal tube

The nasal tube is inserted through one nostril and advanced until the appropriate multistep marking aligns with the external nares. The adjuster is then locked to secure the tube’s position relative to the ring.

- describe pulling out of nasal tube

Once the ring part is properly positioned and the nasal tube is secured, the operator withdraws the ring-type module slowly and steadily, causing the nasal tube to be drawn through the oropharynx and pulling the ENBD catheter into the tube.

- describe connection of nasobiliary drainage tube

After the ENBD catheter is fully captured within the nasal tube, the proximal end of the ENBD catheter is connected to the distal end of the nasal tube using a standard Luer-lock connector.

- describe pulling out of nasobiliary drainage tube

The combined assembly of the nasal tube and ENBD catheter is then gently withdrawn through the nasal passage until the ENBD catheter is fully externalized and secured to the patient’s face.

- describe separation of nasal tube

The nasal tube is then disconnected from the ENBD catheter and discarded as single-use medical waste. The ENBD catheter remains in place, externally secured for drainage.

- describe advantages of device

The device enables rapid, contact-free repositioning, reduces procedural time by over 75%, eliminates oral trauma and operator injury, and functions reliably regardless of Mallampati score or patient cooperation.

- describe clinical results

In a prospective cohort of 145 patients, the device achieved a 97.3% technical success rate with no reported cases of oral injury, dental damage, or operator trauma.

- describe procedure time

The mean procedure time for repositioning using the device was 34.5 seconds, compared to 194 seconds with conventional methods.

- describe success rate

Technical success was achieved in 72 of 74 attempted procedures using the device, with only two failures attributed to excessive depth of ring insertion, both of which were resolved by reverting to conventional methods.

- describe oral cavity damage

No cases of mucosal laceration, tooth fracture, or bleeding were observed in the device group, whereas multiple such injuries were documented in the conventional group.

- describe physician's injuries

No operator finger injuries occurred during the use of the device, whereas five such injuries were reported in the conventional group.

- describe infection risks

No device-related infections were observed, and the sterile, single-use design minimizes cross-contamination risk.

- describe mouthpiece placement

The device allows repositioning to occur immediately after endoscope withdrawal, eliminating the need to remove or reinsert the mouthpiece.

- describe low skilled physicians

The device’s intuitive design and tactile feedback enable even inexperienced operators to achieve high success rates with minimal training.

- describe patient's physical traits

The device performs consistently across patients with varying Mallampati scores, neck lengths, and oral anatomies.

- describe insertion depth adjustment

The multistep markings and adjuster mechanism allow precise control over insertion depth, accommodating individual anatomical variation.

- describe size adjustment

The adjuster permits tension adjustment to accommodate catheters of varying diameters, from 5 Fr to 10 Fr.

- describe ring part material

The ring part is fabricated from a radiopaque, medical-grade polyetheretherketone (PEEK) polymer, ensuring durability, biocompatibility, and fluoroscopic visibility.

- describe ring handle shape

The handle is contoured with a thumb groove and textured surface for secure, non-slip handling during withdrawal.

- describe multistep first marker

The multistep first marker, located at 15 cm, is intended for pediatric or small-framed adult patients.

- describe multistep second marker

The multistep second marker, located at 30 cm, is the standard reference for average-sized adults.

- describe sliding groove

The sliding groove is a longitudinal channel on the nasal tube that engages with the adjuster, enabling precise positioning and locking.

- describe fixing means

The fixing means consists of a threaded collar that, when rotated, compresses the nasal tube against the handle, preventing slippage.

- describe scope of invention

The scope of the invention encompasses the device, its components, the method of use, and all variations thereof, including modifications in material, dimensions, or configuration that achieve the same functional outcome of contact-free ENBD catheter repositioning.
