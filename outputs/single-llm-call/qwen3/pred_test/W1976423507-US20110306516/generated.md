## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- introduce iPSC production from fibroblasts

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and cellular reprogramming, specifically to methods for the production of induced pluripotent stem cells from adult human fibroblasts. These methods enable the conversion of terminally differentiated somatic cells into a pluripotent state capable of self-renewal and differentiation into all three embryonic germ layers. The invention is particularly directed to the isolation and purification of fully reprogrammed induced pluripotent stem cells from heterogeneous populations of reprogramming fibroblasts using a defined set of cell surface markers, including the simultaneous detection of pluripotency-associated antigens and the exclusion of fibroblast-specific markers. This approach facilitates the generation of clonal, genetically stable, and functionally validated induced pluripotent stem cell lines suitable for disease modeling, drug screening, and autologous cell therapy applications.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- define stem cells

Stem cells are undifferentiated biological cells characterized by their capacity for self-renewal and their potential to differentiate into specialized cell types. They serve as a foundational reservoir for tissue maintenance, repair, and regeneration throughout an organism’s lifespan. Stem cells can be broadly categorized based on their developmental origin and differentiation potential, with key distinctions drawn between embryonic and adult stem cells.

- describe embryonic stem cells

Embryonic stem cells are derived from the inner cell mass of the blastocyst stage of early embryonic development and possess the unique ability to give rise to all cell types of the body, including germ cells. These cells exhibit unlimited proliferative capacity in culture under defined conditions and maintain a stable pluripotent state through the coordinated expression of core transcription factors such as OCT4, SOX2, NANOG, and KLF4. Their pluripotency has made them invaluable for developmental biology studies and as a gold standard for evaluating the quality of reprogrammed cells.

- describe adult stem cells

Adult stem cells, also known as somatic or tissue-specific stem cells, reside in specific niches within differentiated tissues such as bone marrow, skin, and the intestinal epithelium. They are multipotent, meaning they can generate a limited range of cell types typically restricted to their tissue of origin. While essential for homeostasis and repair, their restricted differentiation potential and low abundance in vivo limit their utility for broad therapeutic applications.

- motivate iPSC

The discovery that somatic cells can be reprogrammed into a pluripotent state through the forced expression of defined transcription factors revolutionized the field of regenerative medicine by circumventing the ethical and immunological challenges associated with embryonic stem cells. Induced pluripotent stem cells, generated from patient-derived somatic cells, offer the potential for personalized cell therapies, disease modeling, and pharmacological screening without the need for donor embryos or immune suppression.

- limitations of current iPSC production

Despite their promise, the current methods for generating induced pluripotent stem cells suffer from significant limitations. Reprogramming efficiency remains exceedingly low, often below one percent of starting cells, and the process is time-intensive, requiring four to six weeks to yield stable colonies. Furthermore, the identification of bona fide induced pluripotent stem cells has historically relied on subjective, labor-intensive manual selection of morphologically distinct colonies under a microscope, a process that demands expert training, introduces significant operator bias, and fails to distinguish between fully reprogrammed cells and partially reprogrammed or transformed intermediates.

- describe existing techniques for iPSC identification

Existing techniques for identifying reprogrammed cells include immunocytochemical staining for pluripotency markers such as OCT4 and SSEA4, reporter gene expression driven by pluripotency promoters, and quantitative PCR for endogenous pluripotency gene activation. While useful, these methods are typically performed post-hoc on fixed or lysed cells and are incompatible with live-cell sorting or real-time purification. Other approaches have attempted to enrich for reprogrammed populations using positive selection for surface markers like SSEA4 and TRA-1-60, but these methods do not adequately exclude contaminating fibroblasts or partially reprogrammed cells that retain somatic signatures.

- need for improved iPSC production methods

There remains a critical and unmet need for a standardized, high-throughput, and objective method to isolate fully reprogrammed induced pluripotent stem cells at an early stage of reprogramming, free from contamination by parental somatic cells or aberrant intermediates. Such a method must be compatible with both integrating and non-integrating reprogramming vectors, scalable for clinical manufacturing, and capable of yielding clonally pure, genetically stable, and functionally validated cell lines suitable for therapeutic use.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce inventive method for iPSC production

The present invention provides a novel method for the rapid and efficient production of induced pluripotent stem cells from adult human fibroblasts by combining positive selection for pluripotency-associated surface markers with negative selection against a fibroblast-specific marker. This method enables the isolation of a highly purified population of fully reprogrammed cells as early as seven days post-reprogramming initiation, significantly accelerating the derivation timeline and improving the quality and consistency of resulting cell lines.

- contact adult fibroblasts with reprogramming composition

The method comprises contacting adult human fibroblasts with a reprogramming composition comprising a delivery vector encoding the transcription factors OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, and cMYC, wherein the vector is selected from the group consisting of retroviral, lentiviral, Sendai viral, episomal plasmid, or mRNA-based systems. The fibroblasts are maintained under conditions conducive to reprogramming, including the presence of small molecule inhibitors that modulate key signaling pathways to enhance efficiency and fidelity.

- culture contacted fibroblasts

The contacted fibroblasts are cultured in a defined medium supplemented with a ROCK inhibitor, a TGF-β receptor inhibitor, and a MEK inhibitor to promote survival and reprogramming progression. Culture conditions are maintained in a humidified atmosphere at 37°C with 5% CO₂, with daily medium exchanges to ensure optimal nutrient availability and waste removal.

- sort cultured cells based on surface markers

At an early time point between seven and fourteen days post-infection, the cultured cells are harvested, dissociated into a single-cell suspension, and stained with a panel of fluorescently labeled antibodies targeting the cell surface markers CD13, SSEA4, and TRA-1-60. Cells exhibiting a CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, and TRA-1-60-positive phenotype are isolated by fluorescence-activated cell sorting.

- identify iPSC colonies

The sorted cells are plated onto a feeder layer of mitotically inactivated murine embryonic fibroblasts or onto a Matrigel-coated substrate in the presence of a ROCK inhibitor. Within days, discrete, compact colonies with high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratios and well-defined borders emerge, indicative of fully reprogrammed induced pluripotent stem cells. These colonies are expanded without manual picking or additional enrichment steps.

- describe reprogramming composition

The reprogramming composition may be delivered via viral vectors such as Sendai virus or retrovirus, or via non-viral methods including mRNA transfection or episomal plasmids. The composition is optimized to achieve high transduction efficiency while minimizing genomic integration and insertional mutagenesis risk.

- describe inhibitors of pathways

The reprogramming medium contains a combination of small molecule inhibitors including SB431542 to inhibit TGF-β signaling, PD0325901 to inhibit MEK/ERK signaling, and Thiazovivin or Y-27632 to inhibit Rho-associated kinase activity. These inhibitors synergistically enhance reprogramming efficiency, reduce apoptosis, and promote the stabilization of the pluripotent state.

- identify iPSC colonies visually

Colony morphology is assessed using phase-contrast microscopy to identify colonies exhibiting characteristics consistent with human embryonic stem cells, including tight cell-cell adhesion, prominent nucleoli, and a lack of spontaneous differentiation.

- test sorted cells by flow cytometry or immunofluorescent microscopy

The identity and purity of sorted cells are confirmed by flow cytometry analysis for the continued expression of SSEA4, TRA-1-60, and the absence of CD13. Immunofluorescent microscopy is employed to validate intracellular expression of pluripotency markers such as OCT4, NANOG, and SOX2.

- test sorted cells by Southern blotting or quantitative real-time PCR

Genomic integration of reprogramming transgenes is assessed by Southern blotting using probes specific for OCT4, SOX2, and KLF4 following digestion with BglII, while silencing of exogenous transgenes is confirmed by quantitative real-time PCR using primers designed to distinguish endogenous from viral transcripts.

- test sorted cells by teratoma formation or embryoid body formation

Functional pluripotency is validated by in vitro differentiation into embryoid bodies followed by immunohistochemical detection of markers representative of endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm, and by in vivo teratoma formation in immunocompromised mice, where histological analysis confirms the presence of differentiated tissues derived from all three germ layers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce iPSC production methods

The invention provides a comprehensive and reproducible protocol for the generation of induced pluripotent stem cells from adult human fibroblasts, encompassing cell sourcing, reprogramming, sorting, expansion, and characterization. This method is designed for scalability, clinical compliance, and compatibility with automated systems, enabling the production of high-quality, clonally derived iPSC lines suitable for therapeutic and research applications.

- provide methods for reprogramming adult fibroblasts

Adult human fibroblasts are obtained from dermal biopsies or cryopreserved cell lines and expanded in culture under standard conditions using Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with fetal bovine serum. Cells are used within three passages post-isolation to ensure genetic stability. Reprogramming is initiated by transduction with viral vectors encoding the four Yamanaka factors, with infection optimized to achieve high transduction efficiency while minimizing cytotoxicity.

- define adult and induced pluripotent stem cells

Adult fibroblasts are mature, non-dividing somatic cells derived from the mesoderm that express markers such as CD13 and vimentin. Induced pluripotent stem cells are somatic cells that have been epigenetically reprogrammed to a state functionally equivalent to embryonic stem cells, characterized by the reactivation of endogenous pluripotency genes, loss of somatic markers, and acquisition of self-renewal and multilineage differentiation capacity.

- describe reprogramming composition and conditions

The reprogramming composition includes a combination of transcription factors delivered via Sendai virus or retrovirus, along with a small molecule cocktail comprising SB431542, PD0325901, and Thiazovivin. The medium is supplemented with basic fibroblast growth factor and Knockout Serum Replacement to support pluripotency. Cells are maintained at low density to reduce competition and promote clonal expansion.

- outline culturing and sorting steps

Following reprogramming, cells are harvested at days 7–14 using enzymatic dissociation with Dispase or Accutase, filtered through a 35 µm strainer, and stained with a cocktail of fluorescent antibodies against CD13, SSEA4, and TRA-1-60. Sorting is performed on a BD Aria IIu cell sorter using gentle FACS conditions to preserve viability. The CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, TRA-1-60-positive population is collected directly onto feeder layers or Matrigel-coated plates.

- identify iPSC colonies

Within three to five days post-sorting, distinct, dome-shaped colonies emerge with smooth edges and high refractility. These colonies are allowed to expand for two weeks without manual intervention, during which time contaminating cells are outcompeted or undergo apoptosis, resulting in highly pure iPSC cultures.

- provide alternative embodiment for iPSC production

An alternative embodiment employs non-viral delivery of reprogramming factors using synthetic mRNA or episomal plasmids. In this embodiment, cells are transfected using lipid nanoparticles or cell-penetrating peptides, and reprogramming is monitored using the same surface marker signature for FACS sorting.

- enrich early reprogrammed fibroblasts using FACS

The method enables the enrichment of early reprogrammed cells as early as seven days post-transduction, significantly reducing the time required to obtain clonal lines compared to traditional manual picking methods that require 14–21 days.

- describe application to fibroblasts from normal and disease samples

The method is applicable to fibroblasts derived from healthy donors as well as patients with genetic disorders including type I diabetes, Parkinson’s disease, Alzheimer’s disease, and cardiac myopathies. All fibroblast lines are validated for identity by short tandem repeat analysis and karyotype integrity prior to reprogramming.

- enable live sorting of fully reprogrammed iPSC cells

The use of live-cell sorting based on the CD13−/SSEA4+/TRA-1-60+ signature allows for the isolation of viable, functional iPSCs without fixation or lysis, preserving their pluripotent state and enabling immediate downstream applications such as cryopreservation, differentiation, or gene editing.

- discuss general reprogramming protocol

The general protocol involves fibroblast isolation, reprogramming vector delivery, culture in reprogramming medium, surface marker staining, FACS sorting, plating on supportive substrates, colony expansion, and validation through molecular, cellular, and functional assays.

- describe transfection of fibroblasts with expression vectors

Expression vectors are delivered using high-titer viral preparations or non-viral transfection agents such as lipofectamine, electroporation, or cell-penetrating peptides. Transfection efficiency is verified by reporter gene expression or qPCR for transgene mRNA.

- outline cell culture and expansion methods

Sorted cells are cultured on mitotically inactivated murine embryonic fibroblasts or Matrigel-coated plates in human embryonic stem cell medium supplemented with bFGF and ROCK inhibitor. Media is changed daily, and colonies are passaged using enzymatic dissociation when they reach 70–80% confluence.

- discuss transfection methods and agents

Transfection agents include lipid-based nanoparticles, polymer-based carriers, and cell-penetrating peptides such as TAT or Penetratin. These agents are optimized for low cytotoxicity and high delivery efficiency in primary fibroblasts.

- employ antibodies for FACS sorting

Antibodies used for sorting include anti-CD13 (clone 34411), anti-SSEA4 (clone MC-813-70), and anti-TRA-1-60 (clone TRA-1-60), all conjugated to fluorophores such as FITC, PE, or APC. Antibody concentrations are titrated to ensure specificity and minimize background staining.

- identify stem cell specific surface proteins

The surface proteins SSEA4 and TRA-1-60 are glycolipid and glycoprotein markers associated with the pluripotent state, while CD13 is a zinc-dependent metalloprotease expressed on fibroblasts and mesenchymal cells. The co-expression profile of these markers provides a unique signature for distinguishing fully reprogrammed cells.

- describe genetic analysis for iPSC confirmation

Genetic confirmation includes Southern blotting to detect integration sites of transgenes, quantitative RT-PCR to assess endogenous pluripotency gene expression, and NanoString analysis to profile global transcriptomes. Karyotype analysis is performed to ensure chromosomal stability.

- discuss detection of enzymatic activity and gene expression

Alkaline phosphatase activity is detected using a colorimetric assay, and gene expression profiling is conducted using qPCR and RNA sequencing to confirm silencing of exogenous transgenes and activation of endogenous pluripotency networks.

- contemplate compositions comprising iPSCs

The invention further encompasses compositions comprising purified induced pluripotent stem cells in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for cryopreservation, storage, and therapeutic administration.

- relate to methods of treating diseases with iPSCs

The iPSCs generated by this method may be differentiated into clinically relevant cell types such as cardiomyocytes, neurons, pancreatic beta cells, or hepatocytes for transplantation in degenerative diseases, metabolic disorders, or organ failure.

- describe administration modes for iPSCs

Administration modes include direct injection into target tissues, encapsulation in biomaterial scaffolds, or delivery via intravenous or intra-arterial routes, depending on the target organ and disease indication.

- discuss use of iPSCs for gene correction and biomedical research

The iPSCs are amenable to CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing for correction of disease-causing mutations prior to differentiation and transplantation. They are also utilized for high-throughput drug screening, toxicity testing, and disease modeling in vitro.

- provide examples of iPSC production and characterization

Examples include the generation of 228 independent iPSC lines from 76 fibroblast sources, including those derived from patients with type I diabetes, Parkinson’s disease, and familial cardiomyopathies. All lines exhibited normal karyotypes, silenced transgenes, and demonstrated trilineage differentiation potential.

- describe fibroblast cell culture and reprogramming

Fibroblasts were isolated from skin biopsies under IRB-approved protocols, expanded in DMEM with 10% FBS, and reprogrammed using Sendai virus at an MOI of 3. Cells were cultured in HUESM supplemented with small molecule inhibitors for seven to fourteen days prior to sorting.

- outline FACS sorting and iPSC colony formation

Sorted cells were plated at 5,000–10,000 cells per well on MEFs in the presence of Y-27632. Colonies emerged within five days and were expanded without manual picking. Flow cytometry confirmed >95% purity of the CD13−/SSEA4+/TRA-1-60+ population.

- characterize iPSCs using Lyoplate assay

Lyoplate assays were performed to assess pluripotency marker expression across multiple clones, demonstrating consistent upregulation of OCT4, NANOG, and SOX2 and absence of CD13 expression.

- validate cell surface markers by flow cytometry

Flow cytometry analysis confirmed the sustained expression of SSEA4 and TRA-1-60 and absence of CD13 across multiple passages, validating the stability of the reprogrammed state.

### Rapid Production of iPS Cells and Colonies

- derive fibroblast lines from skin biopsies

Fibroblast lines were derived from 3-mm skin biopsies taken from the upper arm, temple, and foreskin of healthy donors and patients with genetic diseases. Biopsies were minced and explanted in culture dishes under sterile conditions.

- culture and expand fibroblasts

Fibroblasts were expanded in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, Glutamax, and penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were passaged at 80–90% confluence and used for reprogramming within three passages.

- reprogram fibroblasts using retroviruses or Sendai viruses

Reprogramming was performed using either retroviral vectors encoding OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, and cMYC or the non-integrating CytoTune™ Sendai virus system. Infection efficiency exceeded 90% as confirmed by GFP reporter expression.

- describe infection and culture conditions

Cells were infected in fibroblast medium for 48 hours, then switched to HUESM containing SB431542, PD0325901, and Thiazovivin. Media was changed daily, and cells were monitored for morphological changes indicative of reprogramming.

- outline FACS sorting and iPSC colony formation

At day 7, cells were stained with anti-CD13, anti-SSEA4, and anti-TRA-1-60 antibodies and sorted using a BD Aria IIu sorter. Sorted cells were plated on MEFs and colonies formed within 5–7 days.

- characterize iPSCs using Lyoplate assay

Lyoplate assays confirmed expression of pluripotency markers and absence of fibroblast markers across all sorted lines.

- validate cell surface markers by flow cytometry

Flow cytometry confirmed >95% purity of the CD13−/SSEA4+/TRA-1-60+ population at day 14 and maintained purity through passage 15.

- provide examples of iPSC production and characterization

Twenty-eight independent iPSC lines were generated from three fibroblast sources using this method. All lines exhibited normal karyotypes, silenced transgenes, and formed teratomas containing tissues from all three germ layers.

- describe fibroblast cell culture and reprogramming

Fibroblasts were maintained under standard conditions and reprogrammed using Sendai virus with consistent efficiency across donor types.

- outline FACS sorting and iPSC colony formation

Sorting was performed at day 7, and colonies formed rapidly without manual intervention.

- characterize iPSCs using Lyoplate assay

Lyoplate analysis confirmed pluripotency marker expression and absence of CD13.

- validate cell surface markers by flow cytometry

Flow cytometry confirmed consistent marker expression across passages.

- provide additional examples of iPSC production and characterization

Additional lines derived from patients with Alzheimer’s disease and long QT syndrome demonstrated identical reprogramming efficiency and pluripotency profiles.

- conclude iPSC production and characterization

The method enables rapid, scalable, and high-fidelity production of clinical-grade iPSCs from diverse somatic sources.

### Immunofluorescence and Microscopy

- provide antibodies for microscopy experiments

Antibodies against OCT4, NANOG, SOX2, SSEA4, TRA-1-60, and CD13 were used for immunofluorescence staining. Secondary antibodies conjugated to Alexa Fluor dyes were applied for signal amplification.

- describe RNA isolation using RNAeasy kit

Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen) according to manufacturer instructions, including DNase treatment to remove genomic DNA contamination.

- perform cDNA synthesis using SuperScript III First-Strand system

cDNA was synthesized from 1 µg of total RNA using oligo(dT) primers and SuperScript III reverse transcriptase.

- conduct quantitative real-time PCR using LightCycler SYBR Green Master kit

qPCR was performed using SYBR Green chemistry on a LightCycler 480 system with primers specific for endogenous and exogenous pluripotency genes.

- describe PCT primers

Primer sequences were designed to span introns to distinguish genomic DNA from cDNA and to differentiate between endogenous and viral transcripts.

- dissociate cells using Dispase

Cells were dissociated using Dispase for 15 minutes at 37°C to generate single-cell suspensions or small clumps for transplantation.

- resuspend iPS cells in HuESM and Matrigel

Sorted iPSCs were resuspended in HUESM mixed 1:1 with Matrigel and injected subcutaneously into immunocompromised mice.

- inject cell mixtures into NOD-SCID Il2rg-null mice

Each mouse received 100–200 cells in 100 µL of Matrigel mixture via subcutaneous injection.

- allow teratomas to grow for 6–8 weeks

Teratomas were allowed to develop for six to eight weeks before harvest.

- recover and fix teratomas

Tumors were excised, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde overnight, and processed for paraffin embedding.

- analyze teratomas using histology and H&E staining

Sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin to identify differentiated tissues including neural rosettes, cartilage, and gut epithelium.

### Comparision of Retrovirus and Sendai Virus Vectors in Reprogramming

- test Sendai virus as a reprogramming vector

Sendai virus was tested as a non-integrating alternative to retroviral vectors, demonstrating comparable reprogramming efficiency with reduced risk of insertional mutagenesis.

- compare reprogramming efficiency of Sendai virus and retroviral construct

Reprogramming efficiency was quantified by the number of CD13−/SSEA4+/TRA-1-60+ colonies formed per input cell. Sendai virus produced similar colony numbers to retrovirus but with faster kinetics and lower CD13 contamination by day 14.
