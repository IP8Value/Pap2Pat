## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The invention relates to ion-selective membranes fabricated through the controlled assembly of polyelectrolyte thin films on nanoporous substrates, and more particularly to membranes engineered to exhibit tunable ionic selectivity and conductivity through layer-by-layer deposition techniques and chemical cross-linking strategies. These membranes are designed for applications requiring precise regulation of ion transport, including electrodialysis, water purification, energy storage systems such as flow batteries and fuel cells, and separation processes in chemical manufacturing. The invention specifically encompasses membranes in which the surface charge density, pore filling, and interfacial interactions between oppositely charged polymers are systematically modulated to achieve desired transport properties, thereby enabling performance characteristics that are unattainable with conventional ion-exchange membranes. The ion-selective nature of these membranes arises not from bulk material composition alone, but from the nanoscale architecture of self-assembled polyelectrolyte coatings that govern the preferential permeation of cations or anions through nanoporous supports.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ion-exchange membranes have long served as critical components in electrochemical separation technologies, where their ability to selectively permit the passage of specific ions while blocking others underpins processes such as desalination, acid recovery, and battery operation. These membranes typically consist of polymeric matrices functionalized with fixed ionic groups—either cationic or anionic—that create an electrostatic environment favoring the transport of counter-ions over co-ions. However, conventional ion-exchange membranes suffer from inherent limitations, including poor control over ion selectivity, low mechanical and chemical stability under prolonged operational conditions, and high manufacturing costs associated with complex synthesis or limited scalability. The transport of ions through these materials is governed by diffusion, electrostatic repulsion, and membrane swelling, but the inability to independently tune selectivity and conductivity remains a persistent challenge. In recent years, nanoporous membranes have emerged as promising platforms for ion transport control due to their high surface-to-volume ratio, which amplifies the influence of surface charge on ion behavior. Despite advances in surface modification techniques, most approaches rely on single-step grafting or coating methods that yield non-uniform, poorly adhered, or chemically unstable layers. Prior art in nanoporous membranes has demonstrated that pore geometry, such as conical or tapered structures, can induce rectifying ion transport, but such architectures are difficult to fabricate at scale and offer limited tunability. Furthermore, existing polyelectrolyte coatings on nanoporous supports have been limited in their ability to maintain structural integrity under operational conditions, often exhibiting delamination, re-dissolution, or inconsistent pore coverage. The importance of precisely controlling ion transport lies in the need for membranes that simultaneously achieve high selectivity, high conductivity, and long-term durability—properties that are rarely co-optimized in existing systems. The development of a scalable, chemically robust, and tunable membrane platform capable of decoupling these two critical parameters represents a significant technological advancement with broad industrial implications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel method for fabricating ion-selective membranes through the layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of oppositely charged polyelectrolytes on nanoporous supports, followed by selective chemical cross-linking to modulate the charge density and structural integrity of the resulting thin films. This approach enables independent control over ionic selectivity and ionic conductivity, a feat unattainable with conventional membrane technologies. By sequentially depositing layers of anionic and cationic polymers, a nanostructured coating is formed that conforms to the pore geometry of the substrate, effectively filling and modifying the internal surface of the nanopores. The thickness of the coating, the number of deposited bilayers, and the choice of cross-linking agent are each used as independent variables to tailor the membrane’s electrostatic environment. Cross-linking with glutaraldehyde selectively reduces the density of primary amine groups, thereby enhancing the net negative charge of the film and increasing cation selectivity without significantly compromising conductivity. In contrast, cross-linking with carbodiimide reagents reduces both cationic and anionic charges equally, preserving the overall charge neutrality of the film and maintaining selectivity close to that of the un-cross-linked state. The resulting membranes exhibit exceptional uniformity, chemical stability, and reproducibility, even at large scale, and are fabricated using low-cost, aqueous-based processes compatible with industrial roll-to-roll manufacturing. The invention thus provides a versatile platform for the design of next-generation ion-selective membranes with performance characteristics optimized for specific applications in energy storage, water treatment, and chemical separations.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is directed toward the fabrication of ion-selective membranes for large-scale industrial applications in which precise control over ion transport is required, such as electrodialysis for desalination, flow batteries for grid-scale energy storage, and purification of industrial effluents. The core innovation lies in the use of layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition to construct nanoscale polyelectrolyte coatings on nanoporous substrates, enabling the fine-tuning of ionic selectivity and conductivity through controlled variations in film thickness, polymer composition, and cross-linking chemistry. LbL deposition is a bottom-up, solution-based technique that involves the sequential immersion of a substrate into solutions of oppositely charged polyelectrolytes, allowing for the spontaneous assembly of thin, uniform films through electrostatic and hydrogen bonding interactions. This method is particularly advantageous because it requires no specialized equipment, operates at ambient conditions, and is compatible with a wide range of substrates, including track-etched polycarbonate, anodic alumina, mesoporous silica, and other nanoporous materials. Polyelectrolytes, as defined herein, are water-soluble polymers bearing a high density of ionizable functional groups, such as carboxylic acids, amines, sulfonates, or quaternary ammonium groups, which confer permanent or pH-dependent charge to the polymer chain. The LbL deposition process begins with the preparation of a nanoporous support membrane, which is first cleaned to remove surface contaminants and then activated to enhance polymer adhesion. Subsequent immersion in aqueous solutions of a cationic polymer, such as poly(ethyleneimine), followed by an anionic polymer, such as poly(acrylic acid), results in the formation of a single bilayer. This process is repeated multiple times to build up a multilayered coating, with each bilayer contributing incrementally to the total film thickness and surface charge density. The substrates used in this invention include polycarbonate membranes with pore diameters ranging from 20 to 200 nanometers, although other materials with similar porosity and mechanical stability may be employed. Film growth on planar surfaces typically follows a linear or exponential trend depending on deposition parameters, while growth within nanopores is governed by pore size, polymer molecular weight, and solution ionic strength, leading to the formation of dense, gel-like coatings that fully penetrate and line the internal pore walls. Cooperative interactions between the polyelectrolyte layers arise from strong electrostatic attractions between positively charged amine groups and negatively charged carboxylate groups, supplemented by hydrogen bonding between adjacent polymer chains. These interactions ensure the stability of the multilayer structure even in aqueous environments. To further enhance mechanical integrity and prevent delamination or re-dissolution, the deposited films are chemically cross-linked using agents such as glutaraldehyde or N-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)-N′-ethylcarbodiimide hydrochloride. Glutaraldehyde reacts selectively with primary amines, reducing the number of cationic sites and thereby increasing the net negative charge of the film, which enhances cation selectivity. In contrast, EDC mediates amide bond formation between amines and carboxylic acids, reducing both cationic and anionic charges proportionally and preserving the overall charge balance. The resulting membranes exhibit a remarkable decoupling of ionic selectivity and conductivity: while conductivity is initially reduced by pore occlusion, it is partially restored through cross-linking-induced densification and improved polymer packing, while selectivity is amplified by charge asymmetry introduced through selective cross-linking. The polyelectrolytes used in this invention include, but are not limited to, poly(acrylic acid), poly(ethyleneimine), poly(diallyldimethylammonium chloride), poly(styrene sulfonate), and poly(allylamine hydrochloride), with molecular weights ranging from 10,000 to 100,000 g/mol. The concentration of polymer solutions, immersion times, rinsing protocols, and cross-linking conditions are optimized to achieve reproducible and scalable membrane fabrication.

### Example: LbL Deposition of Bilayer PAA and PEI Polyelectrolytes

In a representative embodiment, a nanoporous polycarbonate membrane with 50-nanometer-diameter pores is prepared by etching in sodium hydroxide to remove a surface polyvinylpyrrolidone layer, followed by UV-ozone treatment to generate hydroxyl groups that enhance polymer adhesion. The membrane is then alternately immersed in aqueous solutions of poly(ethyleneimine) at pH 10.4 and poly(acrylic acid) at pH 3.2, with each immersion lasting one minute, followed by thorough rinsing with deionized water. After deposition of three bilayers, the membrane is either left un-cross-linked, cross-linked with glutaraldehyde, or cross-linked with EDC. Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy confirms the presence of characteristic amine and carboxyl peaks, with intensity changes indicating successful cross-linking. Glutaraldehyde cross-linking reduces the amine peak intensity relative to the carboxyl peak, confirming selective amine consumption, while EDC cross-linking reduces both peaks equally. Scanning electron microscopy reveals that un-cross-linked films exhibit patchy coverage and exposed pores, whereas cross-linked films show complete pore filling and uniform surface morphology. Cross-sectional imaging demonstrates that the polyelectrolyte layers penetrate deeply into the nanopores, forming continuous, tube-like structures that line the pore walls. Membrane conductivity, measured via electrochemical impedance spectroscopy, is lowest for un-cross-linked films due to poor adhesion and swelling, but increases significantly upon cross-linking, approaching that of the bare substrate. Ionic selectivity, determined by potentiometric measurements across a concentration gradient, shows that glutaraldehyde-cross-linked membranes exhibit a cation transference number of 0.78, indicating strong preference for cation transport, while EDC-cross-linked membranes maintain a transference number near 0.60, consistent with charge neutrality. These results demonstrate that the invention enables precise, independent control over ion transport properties through rational design of the polyelectrolyte architecture and cross-linking chemistry. The resulting membranes are scalable, chemically stable, and suitable for continuous industrial operation.
