# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The regulation of adipocyte biology remains a central challenge in the treatment of metabolic disorders such as obesity and type 2 diabetes mellitus. Adipose tissue serves as the primary site for energy storage in the form of triglycerides, and its capacity to expand in response to caloric excess is critical to maintaining systemic metabolic homeostasis. When adipose tissue expansion is limited, excess lipids are redirected to ectopic sites including the liver, skeletal muscle, and pancreas, leading to lipotoxicity, inflammation, and insulin resistance. Historically, adipose tissue expansion has been viewed as occurring through two distinct mechanisms: hypertrophy, characterized by an increase in the size of existing adipocytes, and hyperplasia, which involves the generation of new adipocytes from progenitor populations. While hypertrophy is commonly observed in obese individuals and is strongly associated with impaired insulin sensitivity, hyperplasia is linked to a metabolically favorable phenotype, with smaller, newly formed adipocytes exhibiting enhanced glucose uptake and reduced inflammatory signaling. The inability of adipose tissue to undergo hyperplasia under conditions of chronic energy surplus is now recognized as a key determinant of metabolic dysfunction. Despite this understanding, therapeutic strategies aimed at promoting adipocyte hyperplasia have remained elusive, largely due to the prevailing belief that mature adipocytes are terminally differentiated and incapable of re-entering the cell cycle. Recent evidence, however, suggests that under certain metabolic perturbations, fully differentiated adipocytes may retain the capacity for proliferation, offering a novel avenue for intervention. The molecular mechanisms governing this potential re-entry into the cell cycle remain poorly defined, but emerging data implicate metabolic fluxes involving monocarboxylates such as lactate and pyruvate as critical regulators of adipocyte plasticity. In particular, the monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1), which facilitates the bidirectional transport of lactate, pyruvate, and ketone bodies across the plasma membrane, has been shown to be dynamically expressed during adipocyte differentiation and to influence intracellular redox balance. Given its role in mediating lactate efflux and its association with proliferative states in other cell types, MCT1 represents a compelling but unexplored target for modulating adipocyte behavior. The discovery that pharmacological or genetic inhibition of MCT1 induces a transcriptional and phenotypic shift in mature adipocytes toward proliferation, without inducing de-differentiation or cell death, fundamentally alters the understanding of adipose tissue adaptability and opens a new therapeutic paradigm for the treatment of insulin resistance and related metabolic diseases.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a method for treating insulin resistance and related metabolic disorders by inhibiting the activity of monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) in adipocytes. This method is based on the unexpected finding that suppression of MCT1 function in fully differentiated adipocytes promotes their re-entry into the cell cycle, resulting in adipocyte hyperplasia, enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, and increased lipid storage capacity. The invention encompasses the administration of a therapeutically effective amount of an MCT1 inhibitor to a subject suffering from or at risk of developing insulin resistance, obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, or a related metabolic disorder. The MCT1 inhibitor acts by disrupting lactate efflux from mature adipocytes, thereby inducing intracellular accumulation of lactate and pyruvate, altering the NAD+/NADH ratio, and triggering a transcriptional program that upregulates cell cycle-promoting genes such as CDK1, PLK1, and TOP2A. This transcriptional shift leads to increased proliferation of mature adipocytes, as evidenced by elevated Ki67 expression and increased cell number, without compromising adipocyte identity or inducing de-differentiation. The resulting hyperplasia redistributes lipid burden across a greater number of insulin-sensitive adipocytes, thereby improving systemic glucose homeostasis and reducing ectopic lipid deposition. The invention further includes the use of MCT1 inhibitors for prophylactic intervention in individuals exhibiting early signs of metabolic dysregulation, including elevated serum lactate, increased adipocyte size, or impaired insulin sensitivity. The method is applicable to both pharmacological inhibition using small molecule compounds and genetic modulation of MCT1 expression, and is effective in both in vitro and in vivo contexts. The therapeutic benefit is achieved without inducing cytotoxicity, inflammation, or systemic metabolic disruption, making it a safe and targeted strategy for enhancing adipose tissue functionality.

### EXAMPLES

Obesity and type 2 diabetes mellitus are global health epidemics driven by an imbalance between energy intake and expenditure, leading to adipose tissue dysfunction and systemic insulin resistance. Epidemiological evidence demonstrates that over 85% of individuals with type 2 diabetes are overweight or obese, and that adipocyte hypertrophy, rather than hyperplasia, is the predominant mode of adipose tissue expansion in these patients. Molecular studies have revealed that enlarged adipocytes exhibit reduced insulin sensitivity, increased pro-inflammatory cytokine secretion, and impaired glucose uptake, whereas smaller adipocytes maintain metabolic health. The transition from hyperplasia to hypertrophy is associated with depletion of adipose progenitor cells and loss of adipose tissue expandability. Monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) is a proton-coupled transporter responsible for the bidirectional movement of lactate, pyruvate, and ketone bodies across the plasma membrane. In adipocytes, MCT1 expression increases during differentiation and is critical for maintaining redox homeostasis by exporting lactate generated through glycolysis. Elevated lactate levels correlate with insulin resistance in both obese and non-obese individuals, suggesting a direct role for lactate metabolism in metabolic dysfunction. The present invention demonstrates that inhibition of MCT1 in mature adipocytes, using either pharmacological inhibitors such as AZD3965 or genetic knockdown via shRNA, induces a profound shift in adipocyte biology. In the 3T3-L1 murine adipocyte model, MCT1 inhibition led to a significant increase in intracellular lactate and pyruvate, a decrease in the NAD+/NADH ratio, and a transcriptional upregulation of cell cycle genes including CDK1, PLK1, and TOP2A. These changes were accompanied by increased Ki67 expression, elevated cell number, and enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, without evidence of de-differentiation or cytotoxicity. Lipid content was transiently reduced due to increased lipolysis, but long-term triglyceride storage capacity was significantly enhanced following MCT1 inhibition, indicating that newly proliferated adipocytes exhibited superior lipid-handling capacity. RNA sequencing confirmed activation of pathways related to mitosis, cytokinesis, and cellular proliferation, while pathway analysis identified CDK1 as a central node in the observed phenotype. Functional validation using the CDK1 inhibitor RO-3306 demonstrated that the increase in glucose uptake was dependent on CDK1 activity, linking cell cycle re-entry directly to improved insulin sensitivity. In vivo studies in diet-induced obese mice confirmed that MCT1 inhibition reduced adipocyte size, increased adipocyte number, and improved glucose tolerance. The method of treatment involves administering an MCT1 inhibitor to a subject in need thereof, with dosing regimens tailored to achieve sustained inhibition of lactate efflux without systemic toxicity. The invention further includes diagnostic methods for identifying subjects likely to respond to MCT1 inhibition based on adipocyte size, serum lactate levels, or gene expression signatures of cell cycle regulators in adipose tissue biopsies. The therapeutic utility of MCT1 inhibition lies in its ability to convert metabolically dysfunctional hypertrophic adipose tissue into a healthier, hyperplastic state, thereby addressing the root cause of insulin resistance rather than merely managing its symptoms. The compounds described herein, including AZD3965, AR-C155858, and Compound A, are suitable for formulation into oral tablets, injectable solutions, or sustained-release formulations for chronic administration. The safety profile of AZD3965, previously established in oncology trials, supports its repurposing for metabolic indications. The invention provides a novel, mechanistically grounded approach to treating insulin resistance by targeting adipocyte proliferation, a strategy previously considered unfeasible due to the perceived terminal differentiation of mature adipocytes.
