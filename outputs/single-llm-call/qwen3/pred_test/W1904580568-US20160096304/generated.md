# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to electrospinning of microscale or nanoscale fibers  
The present invention pertains to the electrospinning of microscale and nanoscale fibers, particularly those exhibiting a core-sheath architecture, wherein a central core material is encased within a surrounding sheath polymer matrix. This technology enables the fabrication of complex fibrous structures with tailored physical, chemical, and mechanical properties that are unattainable through conventional single-component electrospinning. The resulting fibers find utility in biomedical applications, drug delivery systems, tissue engineering scaffolds, filtration media, and advanced textiles due to their ability to compartmentalize distinct materials within a single continuous filament.

- introduce core-sheath fibers  
Core-sheath fibers consist of two distinct polymeric or fluid phases arranged concentrically along the longitudinal axis of a single fiber, where the inner core is surrounded by an outer sheath layer. This architecture permits the encapsulation of sensitive agents such as proteins, small molecule drugs, fluorophores, or nanoparticles within the core while protecting them from degradation or premature release by the sheath material. The sheath may also serve as a diffusion barrier, a structural reinforcement, or a functional interface for biological interaction, while the core may provide mechanical compliance, responsiveness to stimuli, or act as a sacrificial template for hollow fiber formation.

- describe limitations of conventional manufacturing methods  
Traditional methods for producing core-sheath fibers rely on needle-based coaxial electrospinning, which employs concentrically aligned needles to deliver core and sheath solutions simultaneously. While effective for laboratory-scale demonstrations, this approach suffers from severely restricted volumetric flow rates, typically limited to tens of milliliters per hour, resulting in polymer mass production rates on the order of mere grams per hour. The use of multiple needles to increase throughput introduces significant operational complexity, frequent clogging, inconsistent jet formation, and prohibitive maintenance costs. Alternative methods involving emulsion-based electrospinning are constrained by the narrow range of compatible solvent systems and the inherent instability of emulsified phases under high electric fields.

- introduce electrospinning as an alternative method  
Electrospinning has emerged as a versatile and scalable technique for generating continuous fibers with diameters ranging from nanometers to micrometers through the application of a high-voltage electric field to a polymer solution. The process exploits electrostatic forces to overcome surface tension, forming a charged jet that elongates and thins into a fiber as solvent evaporates during flight toward a grounded collector. When adapted for bicomponent systems, electrospinning enables the simultaneous processing of two distinct materials into a single fibrous architecture without requiring chemical bonding or mechanical blending.

- describe coaxial electrospinning  
Coaxial electrospinning involves the co-extrusion of two immiscible or partially miscible fluids through a dual-lumen needle, where the inner fluid forms the core and the outer fluid forms the sheath. Upon application of a high voltage, both fluids are drawn into a single Taylor cone and ejected as a unified jet, which subsequently solidifies into a core-sheath fiber. This method allows for precise control over core-to-sheath ratios and enables the incorporation of materials that are otherwise non-electrospinnable by using a compatible sheath polymer as a processing aid.

- discuss applications of core-sheath fibers  
Core-sheath fibers have demonstrated utility in controlled drug delivery, where therapeutic agents are encapsulated within the core and released over time through diffusion or degradation of the sheath. They are also employed in the creation of hollow fibers for microfluidic channels, in the fabrication of bicomponent fibers with dissimilar mechanical or surface properties, and in the production of fibers from materials that lack sufficient molecular weight or viscosity to be electrospun independently. Additionally, core-sheath architectures enable the encapsulation of reactive species, living cells, or phase-change materials, thereby expanding their applicability into energy storage, sensing, and smart textiles.

- describe limitations of coaxial electrospinning  
Despite its versatility, conventional coaxial electrospinning remains fundamentally limited by its reliance on single-point nozzle systems, which constrain the total throughput and scalability of the process. The narrow internal diameters of coaxial needles are prone to clogging, particularly when processing suspensions, high-viscosity solutions, or particulate-laden fluids. Furthermore, the need for precise alignment and frequent cleaning renders the method unsuitable for continuous, high-volume manufacturing. The inability to generate multiple simultaneous jets from a single source further restricts productivity, making commercial-scale production economically unfeasible.

- introduce coaxial nozzle arrays  
To address throughput limitations, researchers have explored the use of arrays of multiple coaxial nozzles arranged in parallel. While this approach increases the number of jets and thus the total output, it simultaneously multiplies the complexity of fluid delivery, voltage distribution, and maintenance requirements. Each nozzle must be individually calibrated, and any failure in one unit can compromise the entire system. Moreover, the spatial density of nozzles often leads to electrical interference between adjacent jets, resulting in inconsistent fiber morphology and reduced reproducibility.

- describe Nanospider technology  
Nanospider technology represents a needleless approach to electrospinning that utilizes a rotating spinneret immersed in a polymer solution to generate multiple electrospinning jets from a free liquid surface. Although capable of high production rates for single-component fibers, this method is not inherently suited for core-sheath architectures due to the lack of controlled co-localization of two distinct fluid phases. Attempts to layer two immiscible solutions beneath the spinneret have yielded limited success, constrained by interfacial instability and poor control over core-sheath ratios.

- introduce Arsenal Electrospinning Technology  
The present invention introduces Arsenal Electrospinning Technology, a novel needleless platform for the high-throughput production of core-sheath fibers using a slit-surface configuration that enables the simultaneous generation of dozens of stable, well-defined core-sheath electrospinning jets. Unlike prior methods, this technology does not rely on concentric needles or rotating wires but instead employs precisely aligned parallel slits to co-localize core and sheath solutions along a planar interface, enabling scalable, stable, and reproducible fiber formation at unprecedented volumetric flow rates.

- describe apparatus for Arsenal Electrospinning Technology  
The apparatus comprises a first vessel containing a core polymer solution and a second vessel containing a sheath polymer solution, each connected via independent fluidic pathways to a slit fixture positioned between the two reservoirs. The slit fixture consists of two triangular-shaped nozzles aligned vertically within a single plane, such that the core solution exits through a lower slit and the sheath solution exits through an upper slit, creating a continuous interface between the two fluids. The entire fixture is electrically connected to a high-voltage power supply, while a grounded collector is positioned at a controlled distance below to receive the electrospun fibers.

- explain formation of Taylor cones and electrospinning jets  
Upon application of a critical electric field, multiple Taylor cones form simultaneously along the length of the slit interface, with each cone originating from the sheath fluid layer. The sheath solution, flowing at a higher velocity than the core solution, generates sufficient viscous shear stress at the core-sheath boundary to entrain the core fluid into the electrospinning jet. This entrainment mechanism, analogous to selective withdrawal in stratified fluid systems, ensures that the core material is drawn into the jet without mixing, preserving the integrity of the core-sheath architecture. The resulting jets elongate and thin as solvent evaporates, yielding continuous core-sheath fibers that deposit onto the collector.

- discuss advantages of Arsenal Electrospinning Technology  
Arsenal Electrospinning Technology enables volumetric flow rates exceeding one liter per hour, corresponding to polymer mass production rates of 50 to 250 grams per hour—over 1,000 times greater than conventional coaxial needle systems. The absence of narrow capillaries eliminates clogging, reduces maintenance, and permits the use of suspensions, high-viscosity solutions, and particulate-laden fluids. The planar slit design allows for uniform electric field distribution across multiple jets, ensuring consistent fiber morphology and enabling long-duration, continuous operation. Furthermore, the system permits electrospinning in any orientation, including downward collection, and supports sterile processing conditions suitable for biomedical applications.

- identify need for further improvements  
Despite its significant advantages, existing slit-surface systems require further optimization to precisely control core-sheath ratios, fiber diameter, and jet stability across diverse material systems. Variables such as slit geometry, flow rate ratios, solution viscosities, and interfacial shear stresses must be systematically characterized to enable predictive design of core-sheath fibers for specific applications. The present invention addresses these gaps by establishing a comprehensive framework for tuning these parameters to achieve reproducible, high-quality fiber production at industrial scales.
