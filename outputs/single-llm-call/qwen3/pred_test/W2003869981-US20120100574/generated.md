## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of virology and infectious disease diagnostics, particularly to systems and methods for the production, detection, and analysis of hepatitis C virus particles. It further pertains to novel cell-based platforms that enable the generation of infectious hepatitis C virus particles independent of viral RNA replication, thereby circumventing the limitations imposed by adaptive mutations commonly associated with traditional cell culture systems. The invention provides a robust and scalable method for producing authentic, non-adapted hepatitis C virus particles of diverse genotypes, including clinically dominant strains such as genotype 1a and 1b, which have historically proven refractory to propagation in vitro. This technology enables the study of viral entry, assembly, and dissemination in physiologically relevant contexts, including human liver tissue, and facilitates the identification of host factors and therapeutic compounds that modulate the hepatitis C virus life cycle.

## BACKGROUND

Hepatitis C virus infects an estimated 2 to 3 percent of the global population and remains a leading cause of chronic liver disease, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma. Despite advances in antiviral therapy, the absence of a preventive vaccine and the persistence of treatment-resistant strains underscore the need for improved models to study viral biology and identify novel therapeutic targets. The virus belongs to the Hepacivirus genus within the Flaviviridae family and exhibits remarkable genetic diversity, with at least seven major genotypes and numerous subtypes. Genotypes 1a and 1b account for more than two-thirds of global infections and are associated with reduced responsiveness to older interferon-based regimens. The natural target of hepatitis C virus is the hepatocyte, yet primary human hepatocytes are difficult to maintain in culture and exhibit limited permissiveness to infection. The chimpanzee, once the only reliable animal model, is no longer available for routine research due to ethical and logistical constraints. Prior efforts to replicate the full viral life cycle in vitro relied on engineered cell lines such as Huh-7.5, which, despite enabling the production of infectious particles known as HCVcc, required extensive viral adaptation through the accumulation of mutations that compromise viral fitness in vivo. These adaptations often alter envelope protein structure, lipid association, and entry mechanisms, rendering the resulting particles poor surrogates for clinical isolates. Furthermore, the viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase possesses a high error rate, leading to quasispecies diversity that complicates reproducible experimentation. The reliance on viral replication for particle production has also hindered the separation of entry mechanisms from downstream replication events, limiting the ability to study the initial steps of infection. Existing systems such as HCV pseudoparticles (HCVpp) rely on heterologous viral backbones and do not recapitulate the native structure or composition of authentic hepatitis C virions. A critical unmet need exists for a system capable of producing infectious, non-adapted hepatitis C virus particles from clinical isolates without the confounding influence of cell culture-driven mutations, and for a platform that decouples particle production from viral genome replication to enable precise dissection of entry, assembly, and release mechanisms.

## SUMMARY

A mammalian cell system is disclosed that permits the production of infectious hepatitis C virus particles without requiring the viral RNA genome to undergo replication. This system comprises a first host cell line stably transfected with a subgenomic replicon derived from a member of the Flaviviridae family, which induces cytoplasmic membrane rearrangements and alters cellular trafficking pathways to create a permissive environment for hepatitis C virus particle assembly and release. A plasmid encoding a full-length or bicistronic hepatitis C virus polynucleotide is introduced into this host cell, wherein the polynucleotide is flanked at its 5′ end by a bacteriophage T7 promoter and at its 3′ end by a hepatitis delta virus ribozyme sequence, enabling cytoplasmic transcription of uncapped viral RNA by bacteriophage T7 RNA polymerase. The resulting RNA is translated into hepatitis C virus structural proteins, including core, E1, and E2, which assemble into infectious particles that are released into the culture supernatant. These particles contain the hepatitis C virus genome and are capable of infecting hepatocyte-derived cell lines such as Huh-7.5 and HepG2-CD81, initiating viral protein expression and RNA replication upon entry. Isolated hepatitis C virus structural proteins, including core, E1, and E2, are produced in the absence of viral replication and are incorporated into particles with native glycosylation patterns and lipid associations. A system for assaying hepatitis C virus entry is further disclosed, comprising a second host cell line co-transfected with a reporter plasmid encoding a reporter gene under the control of a bacteriophage SP6 promoter and a bacteriophage T7 promoter, wherein the reporter gene is selected from the group consisting of firefly luciferase, enhanced green fluorescent protein, and Renilla luciferase. Upon infection with particles produced by the first host cell line, the viral genome is delivered into the cytoplasm and translated into bacteriophage SP6 RNA polymerase, which then drives expression of the reporter gene, allowing quantification of viral entry independent of downstream replication events. A method for identifying cellular proteins necessary for hepatitis C virus entry is disclosed, wherein the second host cell line is transfected with small interfering RNA targeting a candidate cellular protein, followed by measurement of reporter gene expression after exposure to the particles. A method for identifying hepatitis C virus glycoproteins necessary for entry is disclosed, wherein the particles are incubated with an antibody specific to a candidate HCV envelope protein prior to infection, and reporter gene expression is measured. A method for identifying test compounds effective at blocking viral entry or genome uncoating is disclosed, wherein the second host cell line is pre-incubated with a test compound prior to exposure to the particles, and reporter gene expression is quantified. A method for identifying cellular factors involved in the release of infectious particles is disclosed, wherein clones of the first host cell line that fail to produce infectious particles are isolated, their cDNA is subjected to subtractive hybridization against the parental cell line, and host-specific genes differentially expressed in the non-producing clone are identified. An alternative method for identifying cellular factors involved in viral release is disclosed, wherein the first host cell line is transduced with a lentivirus expressing a candidate cellular protein, and the production of infectious particles is quantified. A method for identifying test compounds that inhibit viral assembly is disclosed, wherein the first host cell line is incubated with a test compound, particles are collected from the supernatant, and their ability to infect the second host cell line is measured by reporter gene expression.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS

The term “mammalian cell” refers to any cell derived from a mammalian organism, including but not limited to human, monkey, hamster, rat, or mouse cells, and includes both primary cells and immortalized cell lines. The term “plasmid” refers to a circular, double-stranded DNA molecule capable of autonomous replication within a host cell and is used herein to encompass both conventional plasmid vectors and modified DNA constructs encoding heterologous genes under the control of specific promoters. The term “promoter” refers to a DNA sequence that directs the initiation of transcription by RNA polymerase, and includes both eukaryotic and bacteriophage promoters such as the cytomegalovirus immediate early promoter, the T7 promoter, and the SP6 promoter. The term “operably linked” means that two or more genetic elements are connected in a manner that allows for the proper expression of a gene product, such that a promoter is functionally associated with a coding sequence to enable transcription and translation. The term “reporter gene” refers to a gene encoding a detectable protein or enzyme whose expression can be measured, including firefly luciferase, enhanced green fluorescent protein, Renilla luciferase, and β-galactosidase. The term “transformed cell” refers to a cell that has been genetically modified by the introduction of exogenous nucleic acid, whether by transfection, transduction, electroporation, or other means, and includes both transiently and stably modified cells. The term “transformation” refers to the process by which exogenous nucleic acid is introduced into a cell and stably maintained as a replicating or integrated element. The term “replicon” refers to a nucleic acid molecule capable of autonomous replication within a host cell, and includes subgenomic flavivirus replicons that encode non-structural proteins and a reporter gene but lack structural genes. The term “sub-genomic flavivirus replicon” refers to a modified flavivirus genome lacking structural genes but retaining the non-structural proteins required for RNA replication and the cis-acting elements necessary for replication initiation and elongation. The term “test compound” refers to any chemical entity, biologic agent, or pharmaceutical composition intended for evaluation as an inhibitor or modulator of viral entry, assembly, or release, including small molecules, antibodies, peptides, nucleic acids, and natural products. The term “subtractive hybridization” refers to a molecular biology technique used to isolate differentially expressed genes between two populations of cells by hybridizing cDNA from a test population with excess cDNA from a reference population, thereby removing common sequences and enriching for unique transcripts. ## EXAMPLES

### Materials and Methods

The T7 DNA-dependent RNA polymerase was produced by in vitro transcription and translation using a plasmid encoding the T7 polymerase gene under the control of a SP6 promoter, with the SP6 polymerase gene encoded on a reciprocal plasmid under the control of a T7 promoter. The plasmid constructs pCR-T7p/SP6pol and pSL-SP6p/T7pol were generated by cloning the respective polymerase genes into pCR2.1 and pSL1180 vectors, each in-frame with the second ATG of the EMCV internal ribosome entry site. The HCV bicistronic construct was generated by replacing the neomycin resistance gene in pH-Neo-SG(L+I)-HDV with the HCV 5′-UTR to NS2 coding sequence, followed by the EMCV IRES and the Renilla luciferase gene. The wild-type HCV genomic construct p90-T7p/H77FL-HDV was generated by inserting a hepatitis delta virus antisense ribozyme at the 3′ end of a full-length H77 strain genome. The reporter plasmid pT7-SP6p2/EGFPLuc was derived from pEGFPLuc by inserting tandem T7 and SP6 promoters upstream of the EGFP-luciferase fusion gene. The pHCVp7 plasmid encodes the HCV 1a structural proteins core, E1, E2, and p7 under the CMV promoter. The pIRES1hyg plasmid encodes the West Nile virus structural proteins under the CMV promoter. Antibodies used in the study included monoclonal antibodies against HCV E1, E2, and core, polyclonal antibodies against NS5A and HVR-1, and antibodies against cellular receptors CD81, SR-BI, ASGP-R, and claudin-1. Rabbit polyclonal antibody against NS5A was generated by immunizing rabbits with a synthetic peptide corresponding to amino acids 2261–2308 of the H77 strain. Cell cultures included BHK-21, BHK-WNV, Huh-7.5, HepG2, and HEK-293 cells, maintained in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium supplemented with fetal bovine serum and antibiotics. Production of HCV particles involved transfection of BHK-WNV cells with the p2B system and HCV-encoding plasmids, followed by ultracentrifugation of culture supernatants. Transmission electron microscopy was performed on fixed cells and purified particles, and glycosylation status was assessed by endo-beta-N-acetyl-glucosaminidase H treatment. RNA analysis was performed by total RNA extraction and RT-qPCR targeting the HCV 5′-UTR. Infectivity assays were conducted using Huh-7.5 and HepG2-CD81 cells, with reporter gene expression measured by luminescence or fluorescence. Live-cell imaging was performed using biarsenical dyes FIAsH and ReAsH to label tetracysteine-tagged NS5A. Subtractive hybridization was performed using two rounds of hybridization between cDNA from non-producing and parental BHK-WNV clones. siRNA knockdowns were performed using pooled siRNAs targeting candidate receptors and cellular factors. Lentiviral transduction of HepG2 cells with human CD81 was performed using the ViraPower Lentiviral Expression System. Human liver slices were obtained from surgical specimens and maintained in culture for ex vivo infection studies.

### Example 2

Infectious hepatitis C-like particles were produced in BHK-WNV cells transfected with the bicistronic HCV construct, and their release was compared to parental BHK-21 and Huh-7.5 cells. Renilla luciferase activity in the supernatant was measured to quantify particle release. No trans-encapsidation of West Nile virus or dengue virus subgenomic replicons was observed, confirming specificity for the HCV genome.

### Example 3

BHK-WNV cells producing HCV particles were treated with glycosylation inhibitors including tunicamycin and castanospermine. Western blot analysis revealed impaired cleavage of E1 and E2 envelope proteins, indicating that N-glycosylation is required for proper structural protein processing and particle maturation.

### Example 4

Transfection of BHK-WNV cells with the HCVbp-coding plasmid resulted in the formation of large vesicles containing 50–60 nm enveloped particles, as observed by transmission electron microscopy. These particles were absent in control cells. Sucrose density gradient analysis showed HCV RNA and structural proteins co-migrating in fractions between 1.05 and 1.20 g/cm³. Infectivity assays confirmed that these particles could infect Huh-7.5 cells, inducing NS5A expression and BrU incorporation into newly synthesized HCV RNA. The tetracysteine-tagged HCVbp-4cys was visualized in infected cells using biarsenical dyes, confirming intracellular replication.

### Example 5

BHK-WNV cells transfected with the full-length H77 genome produced authentic wild-type HCV particles, as confirmed by negative-stain electron microscopy showing spherical particles of 50–60 nm in diameter. These particles were recognized by antibodies from an HCV-cured patient in immuno-gold labeling. When applied to human liver slices, the particles induced specific staining with anti-HCV antibodies and biarsenical dye in cells expressing NS5A, demonstrating infectivity in a physiologically relevant tissue model.

### Example 6

HepG2 cells transduced with human CD81 exhibited increased susceptibility to HCV particle entry, as measured by reporter gene expression, whereas parental HepG2 cells remained non-permissive, confirming the essential role of CD81 in HCV entry.

### Example 7

The release of HCV particles was significantly enhanced in BHK-WNV cells compared to parental BHK-21 cells, and this enhancement was dependent on the presence of the West Nile virus subgenomic replicon, suggesting that replicon-induced cellular remodeling creates a permissive environment for HCV assembly.

### Example 8

Treatment of BHK-WNV cells with antiviral agents including interferon-alpha and ribavirin inhibited West Nile virus replicon activity but did not reduce HCV particle production, confirming that HCV particle formation is uncoupled from flavivirus replication. Intracellular membrane rearrangements persisted despite antiviral treatment, suggesting a distinct trafficking pathway supports HCV assembly.

### Example 9

Transfer of the West Nile virus subgenomic replicon into naïve BHK-21 cells resulted in the establishment of stable clones, one of which—BHK-WNV2.13—demonstrated enhanced HCV particle production. Differential mRNA screening of this clone identified a set of host genes uniquely upregulated compared to parental cells.

### Example 10

Knockdown of SERF2 and TUBA4 in BHK-WNV1 cells using siRNA resulted in a significant reduction in HCV particle release, implicating these host factors in the late stages of viral assembly or egress.

### Example 11

Analysis of envelope protein glycosylation revealed that inhibition of N-linked glycosylation impaired the maturation of E1 and E2, leading to reduced particle infectivity, consistent with the requirement for proper glycosylation in HCV particle formation and entry.
