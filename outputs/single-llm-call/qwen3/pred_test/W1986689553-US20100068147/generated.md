# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- relate to medicine, cell biology, molecular biology and genetics

The present invention pertains to the field of medicine, with specific relevance to cell biology, molecular biology, and genetics, particularly in the context of infectious disease diagnostics and therapeutics. It encompasses the identification of host-derived molecular signatures and pathways that are dynamically modulated during dengue virus infection, and the application of these discoveries to the development of novel diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic strategies. The invention integrates advanced genomic profiling techniques with functional validation in both in vitro cellular models and clinical patient samples to define biologically relevant targets that are conserved across experimental systems and human disease states. These targets include components of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, interferon-stimulated genes, and NF-κB-mediated chemokine responses, all of which are shown to be intimately involved in the host’s response to dengue virus replication and disease progression. The invention further extends to the design and implementation of molecular assays, pharmaceutical compositions, and screening methodologies that exploit these biological insights for the detection, monitoring, and treatment of dengue fever and its more severe manifestations.

## BACKGROUND

- describe dengue infection
- discuss dengue virus replication
- highlight limitations of current diagnostics
- emphasize need for new therapy options

Dengue infection is a mosquito-borne viral illness caused by one of four closely related serotypes of dengue virus, a positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus belonging to the Flaviviridae family. It is transmitted primarily by the Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes and is endemic in tropical and subtropical regions worldwide, affecting an estimated 50 to 100 million individuals annually. The clinical spectrum ranges from asymptomatic or mild febrile illness, known as dengue fever, to a life-threatening condition characterized by plasma leakage, thrombocytopenia, and hemorrhagic manifestations, termed dengue hemorrhagic fever or dengue shock syndrome. The pathogenesis of severe dengue remains incompletely understood, though it is widely believed to involve a complex interplay between viral replication kinetics and the host’s immune response, particularly in the context of secondary heterologous infections where pre-existing antibodies may enhance viral uptake via Fc receptor-mediated mechanisms. Despite the global burden of disease, current diagnostic approaches rely predominantly on serological detection of viral antigens or antibodies, or on nucleic acid amplification techniques that are often limited by timing, sensitivity, and specificity, especially during the early febrile phase when therapeutic intervention is most critical. There are no approved antiviral drugs for dengue, and supportive care remains the standard of practice, leaving a critical unmet need for targeted therapeutic strategies that can modulate the host response to suppress viral replication or mitigate immunopathology. Furthermore, the absence of reliable early biomarkers for predicting disease severity impedes clinical decision-making and resource allocation in endemic settings. The lack of a robust small animal model that fully recapitulates human disease further complicates preclinical development, underscoring the necessity for alternative approaches grounded in human-relevant molecular signatures.

The ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, a conserved cellular system responsible for the selective degradation of misfolded or regulatory proteins, is activated during dengue infection and is required for efficient viral assembly and egress. This discovery enables the development of methods for diagnosing and prognosticating dengue infection by measuring the expression levels of key pathway components, including HERC5, UBE2L6, USP15, and PSMB proteins, in patient blood or serum. The invention further provides methods for modulating the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway using specific inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN, which have been shown to reduce dengue viral titers by more than 50% without inducing cytotoxicity at therapeutic concentrations. Administration of interferon-alpha or recombinant viperin protein is shown to inhibit dengue replication in vitro and represents a viable therapeutic strategy. The invention also identifies NF-κB-mediated chemokines IP-10 (CXCL10) and I-TAC (CXCL11) as highly specific biomarkers of acute dengue infection, whose serum concentrations are elevated during the febrile phase and decline during convalescence, enabling their use in early diagnosis and severity stratification. The invention identifies a panel of human serum proteins—specifically IP-10, I-TAC, viperin, and ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes—that serve as reliable biomarkers for the detection and prognosis of dengue infection. Two major host-response pathways are consistently activated during dengue virus replication: the interferon-mediated antiviral pathway and the NF-κB-driven inflammatory chemokine pathway, both of which are accompanied by upregulation of components of the ubiquitin-proteasome system. Gene expression profiling of infected human cell lines and patient peripheral blood mononuclear cells reveals a coordinated transcriptional signature that is distinct from other febrile illnesses such as influenza, chikungunya, or malaria, enabling differential diagnosis based on molecular profiling. The magnitude and persistence of IP-10 and I-TAC expression in serum are predictive of disease severity, with higher concentrations correlating with thrombocytopenia and prolonged fever duration. Interferon-alpha levels, while elevated during acute infection, are less specific than IP-10 and I-TAC but may be used in combination to improve diagnostic accuracy. Compounds that inhibit the expression or function of key viral dependency factors, such as proteasome inhibitors MG-132 and ALLN, are shown to reduce dengue viral titers by disrupting the ubiquitin-dependent maturation and release of viral particles. ### Dengue

Dengue is defined as an acute viral illness caused by infection with any of the four serotypes of dengue virus, transmitted by Aedes mosquitoes. Dengue fever is the milder clinical form, characterized by sudden onset of high fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain, myalgia, arthralgia, nausea, vomiting, and a transient maculopapular rash. Dengue hemorrhagic fever, a more severe manifestation, is defined by the presence of fever, thrombocytopenia, plasma leakage, and evidence of hemorrhagic tendency, which may progress to dengue shock syndrome characterized by circulatory failure and hypotension. Diagnosis is traditionally based on clinical criteria, serological testing for IgM/IgG antibodies, or detection of viral RNA by reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, although these methods often lack sensitivity during the early febrile phase or cannot distinguish between primary and secondary infections. Treatment is currently supportive, involving fluid management, analgesia, and close monitoring for warning signs of progression. Prevention strategies include vector control, public health education, and the use of partially effective vaccines such as Dengvaxia, which carries limitations in seronegative individuals. Personal prevention methods include the use of insect repellents, protective clothing, and mosquito netting, particularly during peak biting hours. The present invention provides a molecular diagnostic and therapeutic framework that complements existing approaches by enabling earlier detection, risk stratification, and targeted intervention.

The invention employs a suite of validated assays for the detection and quantification of dengue virus and its host response. The plaque assay is used to determine infectious viral titers by enumerating lytic foci on monolayers of BHK-21 cells following serial dilution of supernatants and overlay with methylcellulose. Cytotoxicity of test compounds is assessed using fluorescein diacetate, a cell-permeable substrate that is cleaved by intracellular esterases in viable cells to produce fluorescent fluorescein, measured by fluorimetry. The fluorescent focus assay utilizes antibody-based detection of viral antigens in fixed, permeabilized cells using fluorophore-conjugated monoclonal antibodies, allowing for rapid quantification of infected cells. The TaqMan assay is employed for sensitive and specific quantification of dengue viral RNA using primers and probes targeting the envelope gene, with amplification monitored in real time on a quantitative PCR platform. The Flavivirus detection and quantification assay combines pan-flavivirus primers with serotype-specific probes to enable broad screening and precise identification. Additional assays include flow cytometry for intracellular detection of viral envelope protein, ELISA for quantification of chemokines such as IP-10 and I-TAC, and microarray-based transcriptome profiling to identify differentially expressed host genes. These assays are integrated into the invention to validate the biological relevance of identified targets and to support the development of clinical diagnostic tools.

The ubiquitin-proteasome pathway is a highly conserved cellular mechanism responsible for the ATP-dependent degradation of intracellular proteins tagged with polyubiquitin chains. Ubiquitin is a small, 76-amino acid protein that is covalently attached to target substrates through a three-step enzymatic cascade involving E1 (ubiquitin-activating enzyme), E2 (ubiquitin-conjugating enzyme), and E3 (ubiquitin ligase) enzymes. The pathway is regulated by deubiquitinating enzymes, which remove ubiquitin moieties and prevent degradation, and by post-translational modifications that alter enzyme activity. The proteasome is a large multi-subunit protease complex composed of a 20S catalytic core and 19S regulatory caps, which recognizes polyubiquitinated proteins, unfolds them, and cleaves them into small peptides. In the context of dengue infection, components of this pathway—including HERC5, UBE2L6, and PSMB5—are upregulated and appear to facilitate viral particle maturation or trafficking. Inhibition of the proteasome using specific agents such as MG-132, a tripeptide aldehyde, or ALLN, a calpain inhibitor with proteasome-blocking activity, disrupts this process and significantly reduces viral yield. Other proteasome inhibitors, including vinyl sulfones and lactacystin, are equally effective and may be substituted in therapeutic formulations. Interferon-related proteins, such as viperin and IFI44, are induced in parallel and act as intrinsic antiviral effectors that suppress viral replication independently of proteasome inhibition.

A representative example describes the infection of HepG2 cells with dengue virus serotype 2 strain TSV01, with viral replication monitored over 72 hours using quantitative RT-PCR and plaque assay. Host transcriptional responses were analyzed using 19K oligonucleotide microarrays, revealing significant upregulation of genes in the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, including HERC5, UBE2L6, and USP15. Inhibition of the proteasome with MG-132 or ALLN at concentrations of 1–5 µM reduced viral titers by 50–90% without inducing cytotoxicity, as confirmed by fluorescein diacetate staining. In dengue-infected patients, elevated expression of these same genes was confirmed in peripheral blood mononuclear cells using TaqMan low-density arrays. The highest yielding human cell lines, HepG2 and A549, were selected for further analysis based on maximal plaque-forming unit output. Gene expression profiles were analyzed using Significance Analysis of Microarrays (SAM), a statistical method that employs a modified t-statistic with a fudge constant to reduce false positives, followed by permutation testing to calculate false discovery rates. Pathway enrichment was performed using the PANTHER database, and gene networks were mapped using MetaCore software, revealing strong clustering of IP-10 and I-TAC around NF-κB signaling. Serum concentrations of IP-10 and I-TAC were measured by ELISA and found to be significantly elevated in acute-phase dengue patients compared to convalescent or non-dengue controls. Overexpression of viperin in A549 cells reduced viral replication by 60%, and inhibition of NF-κB with dexamethasone did not affect viral yield, indicating that IP-10 and I-TAC are bystanders of inflammation rather than direct facilitators of replication. The data collectively demonstrate that the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway is a critical host dependency factor for dengue virus, and that its pharmacological inhibition represents a viable therapeutic strategy.
