## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention resides in the field of molecular diagnostics and oncology, specifically in the identification, detection, and clinical application of the Odontogenic Ameloblast-Associated Protein (ODAM) and anti-ODAM autoantibodies as biomarkers for the diagnosis, prognosis, monitoring, and management of epithelial cancers. The invention further encompasses methods for detecting the presence or absence of ODAM protein or anti-ODAM immunoglobulins in biological samples, including tissue sections, serum, plasma, and other bodily fluids, for the purpose of guiding clinical decision-making in oncology. This technology is particularly applicable to cancers of the breast, lung, stomach, and other epithelial-derived tissues, where conventional biomarkers exhibit limited sensitivity or specificity. The invention provides a novel, biologically grounded tool for early detection, risk stratification, and therapeutic response assessment in patients with epithelial malignancies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce tumor biomarkers

Tumor biomarkers are molecular entities—such as proteins, nucleic acids, or metabolites—that are produced by cancer cells or by other cells in the body in response to cancer. These markers serve as measurable indicators of the presence, progression, or biological behavior of malignant disease. Their utility spans multiple domains of clinical oncology, including screening, diagnostic confirmation, prognostic evaluation, and therapeutic monitoring. The identification of reliable tumor biomarkers has long been a central goal in cancer research, as they offer the potential to detect disease at earlier, more treatable stages and to tailor therapeutic interventions based on individual molecular profiles.

- describe types of tumor biomarkers

Tumor biomarkers can be broadly categorized into genetic, proteomic, and immunologic types. Genetic biomarkers include mutations, amplifications, or epigenetic modifications in specific genes. Proteomic biomarkers encompass overexpressed, underexpressed, or aberrantly modified proteins detectable in tissue or circulation. Immunologic biomarkers consist of autoantibodies generated by the host immune system in response to tumor-associated antigens, often appearing before clinical symptoms manifest. Each category offers distinct advantages: genetic markers provide insight into driver mutations, proteomic markers reflect functional protein activity, and immunologic markers may indicate an ongoing anti-tumor immune response with potential prognostic significance.

- explain use of tumor biomarkers

The clinical use of tumor biomarkers includes early detection in asymptomatic populations, confirmation of malignancy when imaging or histology is inconclusive, stratification of patients into risk groups, prediction of response to specific therapies, and longitudinal monitoring for disease recurrence or progression. Biomarkers that are highly specific to particular cancer types and detectable in minimally invasive samples, such as blood or urine, are especially valuable for improving patient outcomes and reducing the need for invasive procedures.

- define diagnosis

Diagnosis refers to the process of identifying the nature and cause of a disease or condition based on clinical signs, symptoms, laboratory findings, and pathological examination. In oncology, diagnosis typically involves the histopathological confirmation of malignant tissue, but the integration of molecular biomarkers enhances diagnostic accuracy, particularly in cases where tissue sampling is limited or ambiguous.

- define prognosis

Prognosis is the prediction of the likely course and outcome of a disease, including the likelihood of survival, recurrence, or response to treatment. Prognostic biomarkers provide information independent of therapy, enabling clinicians to anticipate disease behavior and tailor surveillance strategies accordingly.

- define monitor treatment

Monitoring treatment involves the serial assessment of disease status during and after therapeutic intervention to determine efficacy, detect resistance, or identify early signs of relapse. Biomarkers that correlate with tumor burden or biological activity allow for real-time adjustments in therapy without reliance on repeated imaging or invasive biopsies.

- define recurrence

Recurrence denotes the reappearance of cancer after a period of remission following initial treatment. Early detection of recurrence is critical for timely intervention, yet current methods often fail to identify minimal residual disease until it becomes clinically apparent. Biomarkers capable of detecting molecular signs of recurrence before structural changes occur offer a significant clinical advantage.

- define management

Management encompasses the comprehensive strategy for treating and overseeing a patient’s disease, including diagnostic confirmation, therapeutic selection, response assessment, surveillance for recurrence, and supportive care. Effective management relies on accurate, timely, and reproducible biomarkers that inform each stage of the clinical pathway.

- introduce ODAM protein

The Odontogenic Ameloblast-Associated Protein (ODAM) is a previously underappreciated molecule encoded by a gene located within the secretory calcium-binding phosphoprotein (SCPP) cluster on chromosome 4q13. Originally identified in the context of tooth development, ODAM is now recognized as a protein expressed not only in normal epithelial tissues such as the junctional epithelium and salivary glands, but also in a wide array of epithelial malignancies, including breast, lung, and gastric carcinomas. Its expression in tumors is not merely incidental; it is frequently upregulated and associated with distinct molecular pathways implicated in carcinogenesis. Moreover, the immune system of cancer patients often mounts a detectable antibody response against ODAM, suggesting its role as a tumor-associated antigen. These characteristics position ODAM as a unique dual biomarker—both as a protein expressed by tumor cells and as a target of humoral immunity—offering unprecedented opportunities for diagnostic and prognostic applications.
