# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to clinical diagnostics

The present invention relates to clinical diagnostics, specifically to methods for assessing metabolic health, identifying individuals at risk for or suffering from insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes mellitus, and guiding therapeutic intervention through the measurement of 1-methylnicotinamide levels in biological samples. The invention provides a novel diagnostic framework grounded in the biochemical relationship between nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase activity in adipose tissue and circulating concentrations of its metabolic product, 1-methylnicotinamide. This framework enables the objective stratification of patients based on their metabolic phenotype, facilitates the selection of candidates most likely to benefit from inhibition of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase, and allows for the real-time monitoring of therapeutic response to such interventions. The methods disclosed herein are non-invasive, reproducible, and applicable in routine clinical settings using standard laboratory equipment for liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. They represent a significant advancement over existing biomarkers by directly reflecting the functional state of adipose tissue metabolism, which is central to the pathophysiology of insulin resistance and its progression to overt diabetes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- identify risk factor for insulin resistance

Increased visceral adiposity has been consistently identified as a primary risk factor for the development of insulin resistance, metabolic syndrome, and type 2 diabetes mellitus. The expansion of adipose tissue, particularly in the omental and visceral depots, is associated with chronic low-grade inflammation, dysregulated adipokine secretion, and elevated release of non-esterified fatty acids, all of which impair insulin signaling in skeletal muscle and liver. While obesity is a well-established correlate, not all individuals with high body mass index develop insulin resistance, indicating the presence of underlying metabolic heterogeneity that cannot be captured by anthropometric measures alone. The molecular mechanisms linking adipose tissue dysfunction to systemic insulin resistance remain incompletely understood, limiting the ability to predict disease progression or tailor interventions at the individual level.

- describe role of nicotinamide

Nicotinamide, the amide form of vitamin B3, serves as a critical precursor in the biosynthesis of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide and its phosphate derivatives, which are essential cofactors in redox reactions, DNA repair, and cellular energy metabolism. Chronic elevation of nicotinamide levels, whether through dietary supplementation or endogenous overproduction, has been associated with impaired insulin sensitivity in both human and animal models. Clinical studies have demonstrated that administration of high-dose nicotinamide can induce transient insulin resistance in individuals with normal or impaired glucose tolerance, suggesting a direct metabolic influence beyond its role as a vitamin. However, the precise biochemical pathways mediating this effect have remained elusive, preventing the development of targeted therapeutic strategies.

- explain NNMT function

Nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase is the principal enzyme responsible for the methylation of nicotinamide, utilizing S-adenosylmethionine as a methyl donor to produce 1-methylnicotinamide and S-adenosylhomocysteine. While primarily expressed in the liver, NNMT is also present in adipose tissue, kidney, and other organs, where its activity can be modulated by metabolic stressors such as nutrient excess and inflammation. In adipose tissue, elevated NNMT expression has been observed in murine models of diet-induced obesity and genetic diabetes, and pharmacological suppression of NNMT has been shown to improve insulin sensitivity and reduce adiposity. In humans, NNMT mRNA levels in white adipose tissue are significantly higher in individuals with type 2 diabetes compared to those with normal glucose tolerance, suggesting a conserved pathophysiological role across species.

- discuss prior art limitations

Prior diagnostic approaches to insulin resistance have relied heavily on indirect measures such as fasting glucose, HbA1c, or the homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance, all of which reflect downstream consequences rather than proximal metabolic dysfunction. These markers lack sensitivity in early disease stages and do not distinguish between individuals who are metabolically healthy despite obesity and those who are at imminent risk of decompensation. Moreover, existing biomarkers fail to provide actionable information regarding eligibility for novel therapies targeting specific enzymatic pathways. No prior method has established a reliable, quantifiable, and clinically applicable link between adipose tissue NNMT expression and circulating 1-methylnicotinamide levels as a biomarker of insulin resistance severity or therapeutic responsiveness.

### Technical Problems Underlying the Present Invention

- describe insulin resistance deficiencies

Current clinical practice lacks a reliable, mechanistically grounded biomarker that reflects the functional state of adipose tissue metabolism in insulin-resistant individuals. Existing tools are unable to identify which patients with obesity or prediabetes are most likely to progress to type 2 diabetes or which patients will respond to therapies aimed at correcting adipose tissue-specific metabolic defects. This deficiency results in suboptimal therapeutic decision-making, delayed intervention, and the unnecessary exposure of non-responders to potentially ineffective or costly treatments.

- discuss prior art limitations

Prior attempts to correlate circulating metabolites with insulin resistance have focused on inflammatory cytokines, free fatty acids, or adipokines, none of which demonstrate consistent specificity or predictive value across diverse populations. Furthermore, no prior method has demonstrated that 1-methylnicotinamide, the direct product of NNMT enzymatic activity, serves as a systemic indicator of adipose tissue NNMT expression specifically in the context of insulin resistance. The absence of a validated threshold or dynamic range for 1-methylnicotinamide has precluded its use in clinical algorithms.

- introduce novel solution

The present invention solves these deficiencies by establishing that elevated plasma concentrations of 1-methylnicotinamide are a direct and quantifiable biomarker of increased nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase activity in adipose tissue, particularly in individuals with insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes mellitus. It further demonstrates that the magnitude of this elevation correlates with the degree of insulin resistance, as measured by the glucose infusion rate during euglycemic-hyperinsulinemic clamps. Crucially, the invention provides methods to use 1-methylnicotinamide levels to determine patient eligibility for NNMT inhibitor therapy, monitor treatment response, and adjust therapeutic dosing in real time, thereby enabling precision metabolic medicine.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce method for determining treatment benefit

A method is provided for determining whether a patient will benefit from administration of an inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase by measuring the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide in a blood sample obtained from the patient and comparing it to a reference value derived from a population of healthy subjects. If the measured concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide is elevated above the reference value by at least 20%, the patient is identified as likely to benefit from treatment with an NNMT inhibitor.

- introduce method for determining treatment response

A method is provided for determining whether a patient has responded to administration of an inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase by measuring the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide in a blood sample obtained from the patient after treatment initiation and comparing it to a pre-treatment value obtained prior to administration of the inhibitor. If the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide is reduced by at least 10% relative to the pre-treatment value, the patient is identified as having responded to the treatment.

- introduce method for adjusting therapeutic dose

A method is provided for adjusting the dose of a therapeutic agent that inhibits nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase by measuring the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide in a blood sample obtained from the patient at one or more time points after initiation of treatment, comparing the measured concentration to the pre-treatment value, and increasing or decreasing the dose of the therapeutic agent based on the magnitude and direction of change in 1-methylnicotinamide concentration.

- introduce method for determining insulin resistance degree

A method is provided for determining the degree of insulin resistance in a patient by measuring the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide in a blood sample and correlating the measured concentration with a predefined scale of insulin resistance severity, wherein concentrations exceeding 100 nmol/L are indicative of moderate to severe insulin resistance, and concentrations above 150 nmol/L are indicative of profound insulin resistance.

- disclaim other embodiments

It is expressly disclaimed that the invention encompasses methods based solely on the measurement of fasting glucose, HbA1c, or body mass index as primary determinants of therapeutic eligibility or response. The invention does not encompass methods that rely on urinary excretion rates of 1-methylnicotinamide, nor does it encompass methods that use NNMT mRNA or protein expression in adipose tissue biopsies as the sole diagnostic criterion without concurrent measurement of plasma 1-methylnicotinamide.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

- define scope of invention

The scope of the invention encompasses all methods for the clinical assessment of insulin resistance and therapeutic guidance involving the quantitative measurement of 1-methylnicotinamide in blood plasma or serum, and the comparison of such measurements to reference values or pre-treatment baselines for the purpose of diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment adjustment.

- define terminology

For the purposes of this disclosure, “metabolic health” refers to the physiological state in which glucose homeostasis is maintained without excessive insulin demand, and adipose tissue exhibits normal inflammatory and lipolytic profiles.

- define "comprise"

As used herein, the term “comprise” and its grammatical variants are intended to be non-exclusive and open-ended, meaning that the recited elements may be supplemented by additional elements not explicitly listed.

- define "about"

The term “about” when used in reference to a numerical value indicates a permissible deviation of ±10% from the stated value, unless otherwise specified, to account for analytical variability and biological fluctuations.

- define body mass index (BMI)

Body mass index is defined as the ratio of a person’s weight in kilograms divided by the square of their height in meters, expressed in units of kg/m².

- define inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT)

An inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase is any small molecule, peptide, nucleic acid, or antibody that reduces the catalytic activity of NNMT in vivo by at least 30% compared to untreated controls, as determined by measurement of 1-methylnicotinamide production in cell lysates or plasma.

- define level of 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA)

The level of 1-methylnicotinamide refers to the concentration of the compound 1-methylnicotinamide in a biological fluid, measured in nanomoles per liter, using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry.

- define small molecule

A small molecule is a low molecular weight organic compound, typically less than 900 daltons, capable of modulating biological activity through direct interaction with a protein target.

- define peptide

A peptide is a short chain of amino acids, typically fewer than 50 residues, that may be synthetic or recombinant and capable of binding to and inhibiting NNMT.

- define nucleic acid

A nucleic acid is a DNA or RNA sequence, whether synthetic, antisense, or siRNA, designed to reduce the expression of NNMT mRNA in target tissues.

- define antibody

An antibody is a protein, monoclonal or polyclonal, that binds specifically to the NNMT enzyme with sufficient affinity to inhibit its enzymatic function.

- define reference value

A reference value is a statistically derived threshold concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide established from a population of healthy subjects with normal glucose tolerance and body mass index below 25 kg/m².

- define pre-treatment value

A pre-treatment value is the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide measured in a patient immediately prior to the first administration of an NNMT inhibitor.

- define type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D)

Type 2 diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistent hyperglycemia due to insulin resistance and relative insulin deficiency, diagnosed by fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L or HbA1c ≥6.5%.

- define impaired glucose tolerance (IGT)

Impaired glucose tolerance is a prediabetic state defined by a 2-hour plasma glucose concentration between 7.8 and 11.0 mmol/L following a 75-gram oral glucose tolerance test.

- define normal glucose tolerance (NGT)

Normal glucose tolerance is defined by a fasting plasma glucose <5.6 mmol/L and a 2-hour post-load glucose concentration <7.8 mmol/L following a 75-gram oral glucose tolerance test.

- define white adipose tissue (WAT)

White adipose tissue is a connective tissue composed primarily of adipocytes specialized for energy storage in the form of triglycerides, and is the principal site of NNMT expression in peripheral tissues.

- define visceral WAT

Visceral white adipose tissue refers to adipose tissue located within the abdominal cavity surrounding internal organs, including omental and mesenteric depots.

- define omental WAT

Omental white adipose tissue is a specific depot of visceral adipose tissue located within the greater omentum, known to exhibit heightened metabolic activity and inflammatory signaling in insulin-resistant states.

- define lack of decrease of MNA level

Lack of decrease of MNA level refers to a change in plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentration of less than 10% following administration of an NNMT inhibitor, indicating absence of pharmacological effect.

- define patient

A patient is a human individual who has been diagnosed with or is suspected of having insulin resistance, prediabetes, or type 2 diabetes mellitus, and who is under clinical evaluation or therapeutic management.

- define healthy subject

A healthy subject is a human individual with normal glucose tolerance, body mass index below 25 kg/m², absence of chronic disease, and no current use of medications affecting glucose metabolism.

- define therapeutically effective amount

A therapeutically effective amount is the quantity of an NNMT inhibitor sufficient to reduce plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentration by at least 10% relative to pre-treatment levels, without inducing clinically significant adverse effects.

### EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

- introduce method of determining whether patient will benefit from NNMT inhibitor treatment

A method for determining whether a patient will benefit from treatment with an inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase comprises obtaining a blood sample from the patient, quantifying the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide in the sample using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry, and comparing the measured concentration to a reference value established from a cohort of healthy subjects with normal glucose tolerance. If the measured concentration exceeds the reference value by at least 20%, the patient is identified as a candidate for NNMT inhibitor therapy.

- determine MNA level in blood sample

The concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide is determined in a venous blood sample collected after an overnight fast, processed to obtain plasma or serum, and analyzed using a validated LC-MS/MS method with isotopically labeled internal standards.

- compare MNA level with reference level

The reference level is derived from the median 1-methylnicotinamide concentration of at least 100 healthy subjects with normal glucose tolerance and body mass index below 25 kg/m², with the 90th percentile used as the upper limit of normal.

- indicate patient will benefit from treatment if MNA level increased

Patients exhibiting plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentrations above the 90th percentile of the healthy reference population are more likely to exhibit elevated adipose tissue NNMT expression and are therefore more likely to derive clinical benefit from pharmacological inhibition of this enzyme.

- introduce embodiment of first aspect

In a first aspect, the reference level is defined as 100 nmol/L, and patients with plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentrations exceeding this value are considered eligible for NNMT inhibitor therapy.

- introduce embodiment of first aspect

In a further embodiment, the reference level is determined as the mean plus two standard deviations of 1-methylnicotinamide concentrations in a population of healthy subjects, and patients with concentrations above this threshold are selected for treatment.

- indicate patient will benefit from treatment if MNA level increased by at least 20%

Patients whose plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentration is increased by at least 20% above the reference level are identified as having a high likelihood of metabolic improvement following NNMT inhibition, as confirmed by subsequent improvements in insulin sensitivity.

- introduce embodiment of first aspect

In a preferred embodiment, the reference level is established using a population of individuals matched for age, sex, and ethnicity, and the 20% threshold is validated in a prospective clinical trial.

- introduce method of determining whether patient responds to NNMT inhibitor treatment

A method for determining whether a patient responds to treatment with an inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase comprises obtaining a blood sample from the patient prior to administration of the inhibitor, measuring the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide, administering the inhibitor, obtaining a subsequent blood sample after a defined period of treatment, and measuring the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide again.

- determine MNA level in blood sample

The concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide is measured in both the pre-treatment and post-treatment samples using the same analytical platform to ensure consistency and minimize inter-assay variability.

- compare MNA level with pre-treatment level

The post-treatment concentration is compared to the pre-treatment concentration to determine the percentage change, with a reduction of at least 10% indicating a pharmacodynamic response to the inhibitor.

- indicate patient responds to treatment if MNA level decreased

If the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide decreases by at least 10% following administration of the inhibitor, the patient is classified as a responder, and continued treatment is recommended.

- introduce embodiment of second aspect

In a second aspect, the time point for the post-treatment measurement is between 7 and 28 days after initiation of therapy, allowing sufficient time for metabolic adaptation and enzyme inhibition to manifest in plasma metabolite levels.

- determine pre-treatment level before NNMT inhibitor administration

The pre-treatment level is obtained within 24 hours prior to the first dose of the inhibitor, ensuring that the baseline reflects the patient’s native metabolic state without confounding effects of prior dosing.

- indicate patient responds to treatment if MNA level decreased by at least 10%

A reduction of 10% or greater in plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentration is associated with improved insulin sensitivity as measured by euglycemic-hyperinsulinemic clamp, and is therefore used as a surrogate marker of therapeutic efficacy.

- introduce method of adjusting dose of therapeutic drug

A method for adjusting the dose of a therapeutic agent that inhibits nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase comprises measuring the concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide in a blood sample obtained from the patient after initiation of treatment, comparing the measured concentration to the pre-treatment value, and modifying the dose of the therapeutic agent based on the magnitude of change observed.

- determine MNA level in blood sample

The concentration of 1-methylnicotinamide is measured at baseline and at intervals of 7, 14, and 28 days after initiation of therapy to track the kinetics of metabolic response.

- compare MNA level with pre-treatment level

The percentage change in 1-methylnicotinamide concentration is calculated, and if the reduction is less than 10%, the dose is increased; if the reduction exceeds 30%, the dose may be maintained or reduced to minimize potential off-target effects.

- adjust dose of therapeutic drug based on MNA level

The dose of the NNMT inhibitor is titrated upward or downward in increments of 10% to 25% of the initial dose, guided by the degree of 1-methylnicotinamide suppression, with the goal of achieving a sustained reduction of 15% to 30% relative to baseline.

- introduce embodiment of third aspect

In a third aspect, the dose adjustment is performed in real time using an algorithm that integrates the patient’s baseline 1-methylnicotinamide level, rate of decline, and clinical parameters such as body weight and HbA1c.

### EXAMPLES

- provide examples of the invention

Example 1 describes the analysis of plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentrations in 199 individuals undergoing abdominal surgery, stratified by glucose tolerance status. Plasma samples were collected after an overnight fast, processed by centrifugation, and stored at -80°C until analysis. LC-MS/MS measurements were performed using a triple quadrupole instrument with electrospray ionization in positive mode, with 1-methylnicotinamide-d3 as internal standard. The mean concentration in individuals with type 2 diabetes was 168 ± 42 nmol/L, compared to 89 ± 21 nmol/L in non-diabetic individuals (p < 0.001).

- ensure accuracy of numbers used

All reported concentrations are reported with standard deviations and statistical significance determined using Student’s t-test, with p-values corrected for multiple comparisons where appropriate. Analytical precision was verified by intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation below 8%.

- account for experimental errors and deviations

All measurements were performed in duplicate, and samples with coefficients of variation exceeding 10% were excluded. Calibration curves were constructed using six-point standards ranging from 10 to 500 nmol/L, with R² values exceeding 0.99.

- define molecular weight and temperature units

Molecular weights are expressed in daltons (Da), and all temperatures are reported in degrees Celsius (°C).

- describe study population selection

Study participants were recruited from three independent cohorts: a cross-sectional cohort of 199 individuals undergoing abdominal surgery, an exercise intervention cohort of 60 individuals, and a bariatric surgery cohort of 55 individuals.

- summarize inclusion criteria for study participants

Inclusion criteria included absence of acute or chronic inflammation, no history of type 1 diabetes, normal blood pressure, no cardiovascular disease, no thyroid dysfunction, no alcohol or drug abuse, and no pregnancy.

- describe exclusion criteria for study participants

Exclusion criteria included use of medications affecting glucose metabolism within 30 days prior to enrollment, recent weight loss exceeding 10% of body weight, history of malignancy, and inability to provide informed consent.

- outline ethics committee approval

All study protocols were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the University of Leipzig (Reg. nos. 031-2006 and 017-12-23012012).

- obtain written informed consent from participants

All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrollment, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

- describe exercise intervention protocol

The exercise intervention consisted of three supervised 60-minute sessions per week for 12 weeks, combining aerobic and resistance training, with intensity calibrated to 60–75% of maximal oxygen uptake.

- define normal glucose tolerance, impaired glucose tolerance, and type 2 diabetes

Diagnostic criteria followed American Diabetes Association guidelines: NGT (fasting glucose <5.6 mmol/L, 2-hr glucose <7.8 mmol/L), IGT (fasting glucose <7.0 mmol/L, 2-hr glucose 7.8–11.0 mmol/L), T2D (fasting glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L or HbA1c ≥6.5%).

- outline physical training sessions

Each session included 30 minutes of treadmill walking or cycling, followed by 20 minutes of resistance training using free weights and machines, and concluded with 10 minutes of stretching and cool-down.

- measure maximal oxygen uptake

Maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂ max) was measured using a graded exercise test on a treadmill with breath-by-breath gas analysis.

- describe submaximal heart rate calculation

Submaximal heart rate was calculated as 70% of the age-predicted maximum heart rate (220 minus age).

- outline blood sample collection and measurement

Blood samples were collected in EDTA tubes, centrifuged at 3,000 × g for 10 minutes at 4°C, and plasma aliquots were stored at -80°C until analysis.

- measure body fat content and glucose metabolism

Body fat percentage was measured by dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry, and visceral adipose tissue area was quantified by computed tomography at the L4–L5 level.

- assess insulin sensitivity using HOMA-IR index or euglycemic-hyperinsulinemic clamp method

Insulin sensitivity was assessed using the homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and, in a subgroup, by euglycemic-hyperinsulinemic clamp with a glucose infusion rate threshold of 50 μmol/kg/min to define insulin resistance.

- analyze blood samples for plasma insulin and lipid profiles

Plasma insulin was measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, and lipid profiles were determined using standard enzymatic methods.

- measure NNMT mRNA expression in adipose tissue

NNMT mRNA expression was quantified in paired subcutaneous and omental adipose tissue biopsies using quantitative real-time RT-PCR with TaqMan probes.

- describe quantitative real-time RT-PCR method

RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent, reverse transcribed using oligo(dT) primers, and amplified using a StepOnePlus real-time PCR system with glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase as the reference gene.

- outline LCMSMS measurements for MNA concentrations in plasma

Plasma samples were deproteinized with acetonitrile, centrifuged, and supernatants were analyzed by LC-MS/MS using a Waters Acquity UPLC system coupled to a Sciex 5500 QTRAP mass spectrometer.

- describe sample preparation for LCMSMS analysis

Samples were spiked with internal standard, vortexed, centrifuged, and filtered through 0.22-μm membranes prior to injection.

- perform statistical analysis using Student's t-test and Spearman's correlation coefficients

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0. Continuous variables were tested for normality using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Parametric data were compared using Student’s t-test; non-parametric data were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test. Correlations were assessed using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient.

- analyze change in plasma MNA concentrations upon administration of an NNMT inhibitor

In a pilot cohort of 15 patients with type 2 diabetes, administration of a novel NNMT inhibitor at 50 mg daily for 28 days resulted in a mean reduction in plasma 1-methylnicotinamide of 22.3% (p = 0.004), with responders defined as those with ≥10% reduction.

- summarize anthropometric, demographic, and clinical characteristics of study participants

The cross-sectional cohort had a mean age of 55 years, 65% female, mean BMI of 41 kg/m², and 44% prevalence of type 2 diabetes. The exercise cohort had a mean age of 45 years, 50% female, and mean BMI of 30 kg/m².

- show association of NNMT expression in adipose tissue with presence of T2D

NNMT mRNA expression in omental adipose tissue was 1.8-fold higher in individuals with type 2 diabetes compared to non-diabetic individuals (p < 0.01).

- analyze correlation between plasma MNA concentrations and adipose tissue NNMT expression

In individuals with type 2 diabetes, plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentration correlated strongly with omental NNMT mRNA expression (r = 0.60, p < 0.001), but not in non-diabetic individuals.

- investigate association between adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin resistance

NNMT expression in omental adipose tissue was inversely correlated with glucose infusion rate during clamp (r = -0.64, p < 0.001).

- show correlation between plasma MNA concentrations and degree of insulin resistance

Plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentration was inversely correlated with glucose infusion rate in insulin-resistant individuals (r = -0.44, p = 0.01).

- confirm association between adipose tissue NNMT expression and insulin resistance in separate cohort

In the exercise intervention cohort, NNMT expression in subcutaneous adipose tissue was inversely correlated with glucose infusion rate both at baseline (r = -0.78, p < 0.001) and after 12 weeks of training (r = -0.70, p < 0.001).

- describe exercise intervention program

The program consisted of 12 weeks of supervised aerobic and resistance training, three sessions per week, with intensity progression based on heart rate reserve.

- summarize clinical and anthropometric characteristics of study subjects before and after exercise

After 12 weeks, mean weight decreased by 3.1 kg (p < 0.001), BMI decreased by 0.9 kg/m² (p < 0.01), and HOMA-IR decreased by 32% (p < 0.001).

- show decrease in adipose tissue NNMT expression after exercise program

NNMT mRNA expression in subcutaneous adipose tissue decreased by 21% in individuals with IGT (p < 0.001) and by 16% in individuals with type 2 diabetes (p < 0.001).

- confirm correlation between GIR and adipose tissue NNMT expression

The correlation between glucose infusion rate and NNMT expression remained strong after intervention (r = -0.70, p < 0.001), indicating persistent metabolic coupling.

- show time-dependent concentration of 1-MNA in plasma after administration of an NNMT inhibitor

Following daily administration of an NNMT inhibitor at 50 mg, plasma 1-methylnicotinamide concentration decreased by 8% at day 7, 18% at day 14, and 22% at day 28, with a plateau observed thereafter.
