# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The immune system serves as a highly coordinated defense network that protects the body from pathogens while maintaining tolerance to self-antigens. Dysregulation of this balance can lead to chronic inflammation, autoimmunity, and tissue destruction. Neuroactive molecules, once thought to function exclusively within the central nervous system, have now been recognized as critical modulators of immune cell behavior. Among these, gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) stands out as a principal inhibitory neurotransmitter that also exerts profound influence on peripheral immune cells. GABA is synthesized and secreted not only by neurons but also by pancreatic islet β-cells, certain antigen-presenting cells, and subsets of lymphocytes, positioning it as a bridge between neural and immune signaling. GABA exerts its effects primarily through two classes of receptors: GABAA and GABAB. The GABAA receptor is a ligand-gated chloride ion channel composed of multiple subunits that, upon activation, hyperpolarizes the cell membrane and dampens cellular excitability. This receptor exists in numerous isoforms, each with distinct pharmacological profiles and tissue distributions, allowing for nuanced regulation across diverse physiological contexts. In the central nervous system, GABAA receptor activation is well established as a mechanism for reducing neuronal firing, which underlies its therapeutic use in epilepsy, anxiety, and insomnia. However, emerging evidence demonstrates that GABAA receptors are also expressed on T cells, dendritic cells, and macrophages, where their activation modulates cytokine production, cell proliferation, and differentiation. GABA signaling in the immune system has been shown to suppress pro-inflammatory Th1 and Th17 responses while promoting the expansion and function of regulatory T cells. This dual capacity to dampen destructive immunity and enhance regulatory mechanisms makes GABA a compelling target for immune modulation. Autoimmune diseases such as type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis, and systemic lupus erythematosus are characterized by the loss of immune tolerance and persistent inflammation that leads to organ-specific damage. Similarly, graft rejection following organ transplantation arises from the recognition of foreign antigens by recipient T cells, triggering cytotoxic and inflammatory cascades. Current immunosuppressive regimens, while effective in the short term, often carry significant risks of infection, malignancy, and metabolic toxicity due to their broad, non-specific suppression of immune function. There remains a critical and unmet need for targeted, safe, and reversible strategies that can selectively modulate pathological immune responses without compromising overall host defense. The discovery that GABA and its receptor modulators can influence both immune and metabolic pathways offers a novel therapeutic paradigm that addresses this need by simultaneously restoring immune homeostasis and preserving tissue integrity.

## SUMMARY

GABA has been demonstrated to exert potent inhibitory effects on inflammatory immune responses, particularly through the activation of GABAA receptors expressed on T lymphocytes and antigen-presenting cells. This inhibition manifests as reduced proliferation of autoreactive T cells, diminished secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, and an increase in the frequency and functional capacity of regulatory T cells. These effects occur at concentrations of GABA that are physiologically relevant and do not require systemic immunosuppression. Building upon this foundation, the present invention introduces the use of positive allosteric modulators of the GABAA receptor as a means to enhance the endogenous actions of GABA in immune tissues. Unlike direct agonists, positive allosteric modulators do not activate the receptor independently but instead amplify the receptor’s response when GABA is bound, thereby enabling more precise and physiologically grounded modulation. This approach leverages the natural spatiotemporal release of GABA by immune and islet cells, avoiding the off-target effects and pharmacokinetic challenges associated with high-dose exogenous GABA administration. The combination of a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator with GABA or a GABA receptor activating ligand results in a synergistic suppression of inflammatory pathways and a concomitant enhancement of regulatory immune mechanisms. This synergy allows for the use of significantly lower doses of each agent than would be required individually, reducing the potential for adverse effects while maintaining or even improving therapeutic efficacy. The invention therefore provides a novel strategy for the treatment of immune-mediated diseases by harnessing the intrinsic regulatory capacity of the GABAergic system in a controlled, targeted, and dose-efficient manner.

## Embodiment 1

A method for reducing an inflammatory immune response in a subject comprises the administration of a positive allosteric modulator of a GABAA receptor in conjunction with a GABA receptor activating ligand. The inflammatory immune response may be characterized by the activation and proliferation of autoreactive T cells, the infiltration of inflammatory cells into target tissues, and the elevated production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. The positive allosteric modulator enhances the binding affinity or channel-opening efficacy of the GABAA receptor when occupied by the GABA receptor activating ligand, thereby increasing chloride ion influx into immune cells and suppressing their activation state. This method is particularly effective in contexts where endogenous GABA levels are insufficient to fully engage GABAA receptors, such as in the islets of individuals with evolving type 1 diabetes or in lymphoid tissues during chronic inflammation. The administration may be systemic or localized, depending on the target tissue, and may be delivered via oral, parenteral, or transdermal routes. The reduction in inflammatory immune response is measurable by decreased T cell proliferation, diminished cytokine secretion, and reduced histological evidence of tissue infiltration. This method provides a mechanism-based intervention that restores immune balance without inducing broad immunosuppression.

## Embodiment 2

The positive allosteric modulator of the GABAA receptor enhances the inhibitory effect of the GABA receptor activating ligand on inflammatory immune cells by increasing the duration and magnitude of chloride ion channel opening following GABA binding. This potentiation results in a more pronounced hyperpolarization of the cell membrane, which raises the threshold for T cell receptor signaling and downstream activation cascades. Consequently, the modulator amplifies the suppressive effects of even low concentrations of GABA receptor activating ligands, enabling therapeutic efficacy at doses that would otherwise be subthreshold. This effect is particularly advantageous in tissues where GABA availability is limited, such as in the pancreatic microenvironment of pre-diabetic or diabetic individuals, where β-cell loss has diminished endogenous GABA secretion. The modulator does not induce receptor activation in the absence of GABA, thereby preserving the physiological fidelity of signaling and minimizing the risk of off-target effects. This selective potentiation allows for sustained immune modulation without the development of tolerance or receptor desensitization commonly observed with direct agonists.

## Embodiment 3

The inflammatory immune response comprises the proliferation of antigen-specific T cells, including CD4+ T helper subsets such as Th1 and Th17 cells, as well as cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, in response to self-antigens or alloantigens. These T cell populations are central mediators of tissue destruction in autoimmune diseases and graft rejection. The administration of a positive allosteric modulator of the GABAA receptor in combination with a GABA receptor activating ligand significantly reduces the proliferation of these effector T cell populations in vitro and in vivo. This reduction is accompanied by a decrease in the expression of activation markers such as CD25 and CD69 and a suppression of intracellular signaling pathways including NF-κB and STAT3. The effect is dose-dependent and synergistic, such that the combination achieves a level of suppression not attainable by either agent alone. This mechanism directly targets the cellular drivers of inflammation, offering a precise intervention that preserves non-pathogenic immune functions.

## Embodiment 4

The positive allosteric modulator of the GABAA receptor promotes a regulatory immune response by enhancing the differentiation, expansion, and functional activity of regulatory T cells. These cells, characterized by the expression of FoxP3, CD25, and CTLA-4, play a critical role in maintaining immune tolerance and suppressing aberrant immune activation. The modulator, in the presence of a GABA receptor activating ligand, increases the frequency of FoxP3+ regulatory T cells in lymphoid organs and inflamed tissues. This effect is mediated through GABAA receptor signaling that alters intracellular calcium flux and modulates the activity of transcription factors involved in T cell fate determination. The resulting regulatory T cells exhibit enhanced suppressive capacity, as evidenced by their ability to inhibit the proliferation of effector T cells in co-culture assays. This shift from an inflammatory to a regulatory immune phenotype represents a fundamental restoration of immune homeostasis.

## Embodiment 5

The regulatory immune response is characterized by the increased presence and activity of CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells, the secretion of anti-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β, and the downregulation of co-stimulatory molecules on antigen-presenting cells. This response is associated with the suppression of effector T cell proliferation, the inhibition of dendritic cell maturation, and the promotion of immune tolerance. The administration of a positive allosteric modulator of the GABAA receptor in conjunction with a GABA receptor activating ligand induces this regulatory phenotype in both naive and activated T cell populations. The effect is sustained over time and is observed in multiple lymphoid compartments, including the spleen, lymph nodes, and pancreatic draining lymph nodes. The regulatory immune response induced by this combination therapy is distinct from nonspecific immunosuppression, as it preserves protective immunity against pathogens and does not impair the generation of memory responses.

## Embodiment 6

The reduction in inflammatory immune response is quantified by a decrease in the number of infiltrating leukocytes in target tissues, a reduction in the expression of activation markers on T cells, and a decline in the production of pro-inflammatory mediators. This reduction is observed in models of autoimmune diabetes, experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, and allogeneic transplantation. The effect is dose-dependent and synergistic, requiring lower concentrations of both the positive allosteric modulator and the GABA receptor activating ligand than either agent alone. Histological analysis reveals diminished insulitis, reduced islet destruction, and improved graft survival. Functional assays demonstrate reduced delayed-type hypersensitivity and decreased antigen-specific T cell recall responses. The reduction in inflammation is not accompanied by generalized immunosuppression, as evidenced by intact responses to novel antigens and preserved resistance to infectious challenges.

## Embodiment 7

The downregulation of cytokines or chemokines occurs through GABAA receptor-mediated inhibition of intracellular signaling pathways that drive transcriptional activation of pro-inflammatory genes. This includes suppression of NF-κB, JAK-STAT, and MAPK cascades in T cells and antigen-presenting cells. The result is a broad reduction in the secretion of multiple inflammatory mediators, including interleukins, interferons, and chemokines that recruit and activate immune cells. The downregulation is specific to inflammatory cytokines, with minimal effect on homeostatic or anti-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-10 and IL-35. This selective modulation preserves immune surveillance while dampening pathological inflammation.

## Embodiment 8

The downregulation of IL-1 is achieved through GABAA receptor signaling that suppresses the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome in macrophages and dendritic cells. This inhibition reduces the cleavage of pro-IL-1β into its mature, bioactive form and decreases its secretion. The reduction in IL-1 levels correlates with decreased recruitment of neutrophils and monocytes to inflamed tissues and diminished tissue damage. This effect is particularly relevant in type 1 diabetes, where IL-1 contributes to β-cell apoptosis and islet inflammation.

## Embodiment 9

The downregulation of IL-12 occurs as a consequence of GABAA receptor activation on dendritic cells, which suppresses their ability to produce this key cytokine that drives Th1 differentiation. The reduction in IL-12 leads to a diminished frequency of IFN-γ-producing T cells and a shift away from cell-mediated immunity toward a more regulatory phenotype. This effect is observed both in vitro and in vivo in models of autoimmune disease and transplantation.

## Embodiment 10

The downregulation of IL-6 is mediated through GABAA receptor signaling that inhibits the phosphorylation of STAT3 in T cells and macrophages. IL-6 is a central mediator of Th17 differentiation and acute-phase inflammation. Its reduction leads to decreased Th17 cell expansion, diminished production of IL-17, and reduced neutrophil recruitment. This effect contributes to the attenuation of tissue inflammation in autoimmune and transplant settings.

## Embodiment 11

The downregulation of TNF is achieved through GABAA receptor-mediated suppression of NF-κB activation in macrophages and T cells. TNF is a primary driver of inflammation, endothelial activation, and apoptosis. Its reduction leads to decreased vascular permeability, reduced leukocyte adhesion, and improved tissue survival. This effect is particularly beneficial in islet transplantation, where TNF contributes to early graft loss.

## Embodiment 12

The downregulation of IFN-γ occurs as a direct result of GABAA receptor signaling in CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, which inhibits T-bet expression and subsequent IFN-γ transcription. IFN-γ is a key mediator of macrophage activation and antigen presentation in autoimmune diseases. Its suppression reduces the amplification of inflammatory loops and diminishes the cytotoxic activity of CD8+ T cells against β-cells.

## Embodiment 13

The downregulation of GM-CSF and IL-23 is mediated through GABAA receptor activation on dendritic cells and macrophages, which suppresses their ability to produce these cytokines. GM-CSF promotes the survival and differentiation of inflammatory myeloid cells, while IL-23 sustains Th17 cell populations. ## Definitions

A GABA receptor activating ligand is a compound that binds directly to the GABA binding site on GABAA or GABAB receptors and induces receptor activation, leading to downstream signaling events such as chloride ion influx or G-protein modulation. A positive allosteric modulator of a GABAA receptor is a compound that binds to a site distinct from the GABA binding site and enhances the receptor’s response to GABA without activating it independently. A subtherapeutic dosage is a concentration of a compound that, when administered alone, produces no clinically detectable pharmacological effect on the target system, but which, when combined with another agent, produces a synergistic therapeutic outcome. “In conjunction with” or “in combination with” refers to the simultaneous or sequential administration of two or more agents in a manner that results in a combined effect greater than the sum of their individual effects. A regulatory immune response is a state of immune homeostasis characterized by the dominance of regulatory T cells, suppression of effector T cell proliferation, and reduced production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, leading to tolerance and tissue preservation.

Unlike direct agonists, these modulators preserve the spatial and temporal dynamics of GABA signaling, allowing for physiologically appropriate regulation of immune cell activity. The combination of a positive allosteric modulator with a GABA receptor activating ligand results in a synergistic suppression of inflammatory immune responses and a concomitant enhancement of regulatory immune mechanisms. This synergy enables the use of substantially lower doses of each agent, reducing the risk of adverse effects while maintaining or improving therapeutic efficacy. The method of reducing an inflammatory immune response and/or promoting a regulatory immune response involves the administration of these agents to a subject in need, either systemically or locally, via oral, parenteral, intranasal, or transdermal routes. The use of this combination for the preparation of a medicament is disclosed, with particular application in the treatment of autoimmune diseases, graft rejection, and allergic disorders. The benefits of combination treatment include reduced dosing, minimized side effects, enhanced specificity, and sustained immune modulation without tolerance. Pharmaceutical formulations comprising these agents may include excipients, stabilizers, and carriers suitable for the intended route of administration. ## GABAA Receptor Positive Allosteric Modulators (PAMs)

Positive allosteric modulators of the GABAA receptor are a diverse class of compounds that bind to sites distinct from the GABA binding pocket and enhance receptor function in the presence of GABA. These modulators include barbiturates, benzodiazepines, neurosteroids, and synthetic non-benzodiazepine molecules. Barbiturates such as phenobarbital and pentobarbital prolong channel opening, while benzodiazepines such as alprazolam and midazolam increase the frequency of channel opening. Neurosteroids like allopregnanolone bind to transmembrane domains and enhance chloride conductance. Synthetic PAMs such as AP325 and Algiax offer improved selectivity and reduced CNS penetration. Certain compounds, including general anesthetics and volatile gases, are excluded due to their non-specific actions and safety concerns.

## GABA Receptor Activating Ligands

GABA receptor activating ligands are compounds that directly bind to and activate GABAA or GABAB receptors. These include GABA itself, gabapentin, pregabalin, baclofen, muscimol, and 4,5,6,7-tetrahydroisoxazolo[5,4-c]pyridin-3-ol. These ligands are selected for their ability to activate GABA receptors on immune cells and their compatibility with subtherapeutic doses of positive allosteric modulators. Alcohol, kavalactones, skullcap, valerian, and volatile gases are excluded due to their non-specific pharmacology, toxicity, or lack of standardization.

## EXAMPLES

The use of a positive allosteric modulator in combination with a GABA receptor activating ligand was tested in vitro and in vivo to assess immune modulation and β-cell protection. In a T cell proliferation assay, GABA alone at 0.1 mM significantly inhibited HEL-specific T cell proliferation, while alprazolam alone at 1 × 10⁻⁶ M had no effect. However, when GABA at 0.01 mM was combined with alprazolam at 1 × 10⁻⁶ M, T cell proliferation was suppressed to the same extent as GABA at 0.1 mM alone, demonstrating a tenfold enhancement in potency. In a human islet xenograft model, alprazolam at 0.25 mg/kg/day reduced β-cell apoptosis and increased β-cell replication, and the combination with oral GABA further enhanced these effects. These findings demonstrate that the combination therapy achieves superior outcomes at lower doses than either agent alone. The results were generalized to other GABAA receptor PAMs, including midazolam and clonazepam, confirming the broad applicability of the invention. The combination therapy was shown to reduce inflammatory cytokines, increase regulatory T cells, and preserve islet mass, supporting its potential for clinical translation in autoimmune and transplant settings.
