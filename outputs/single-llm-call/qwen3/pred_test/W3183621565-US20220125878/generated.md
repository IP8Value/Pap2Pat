## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of virology and infectious disease therapeutics, specifically to novel compositions and methods for the treatment of viral infections caused by SARS-CoV-2 and related coronaviruses. The invention further pertains to the modulation of endothelial cell activation and coagulopathy associated with severe viral infection, particularly in the context of COVID-19. The disclosed methods and compositions target the interaction between the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and the liver/lymph node–specific C-type lectin receptor L-SIGN, thereby providing a mechanism-based therapeutic strategy distinct from ACE2-directed interventions.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Coronaviruses are a family of enveloped, positive-sense RNA viruses known to cause respiratory and systemic illnesses in humans and animals. Among these, SARS-CoV-2 is the etiological agent of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), a global pandemic associated with high morbidity and mortality. While many infected individuals experience mild or asymptomatic disease, a significant subset develops severe complications including acute respiratory distress syndrome, multiorgan failure, and life-threatening coagulopathy. The pathogenesis of severe COVID-19 involves not only direct viral cytopathic effects but also dysregulated host immune responses and endothelial dysfunction leading to widespread microvascular thrombosis.

The primary receptor for SARS-CoV-2 entry into host cells was previously identified as angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2), which is expressed on epithelial cells of the lung, kidney, and gastrointestinal tract. However, ACE2 expression is notably absent or minimal on vascular endothelial cells, raising questions regarding the origin of endothelial injury and coagulopathy observed in critically ill patients. Clinical studies have consistently demonstrated elevated levels of von Willebrand factor (vWF) and coagulation factor VIII in the plasma of patients with severe COVID-19, markers indicative of endothelial cell activation. Despite these findings, the molecular mechanism linking SARS-CoV-2 infection to endothelial dysfunction remained unexplained.

Coagulopathy in severe COVID-19 is characterized by hyperfibrinolysis, increased D-dimer, and microthrombi formation without the typical features of disseminated intravascular coagulation, suggesting a unique pathophysiological pathway. The liver, a major site of coagulation factor synthesis, has been shown to harbor viral RNA and structural proteins in autopsy specimens, yet the cell types responsible for viral uptake and subsequent procoagulant activation were not definitively identified. Prior studies had suggested that other C-type lectin receptors, such as DC-SIGN, could bind coronaviruses, but their relevance to SARS-CoV-2 and human tissue specificity remained unclear. A critical gap in understanding existed regarding whether SARS-CoV-2 could directly infect endothelial cells and trigger procoagulant responses independent of ACE2, particularly in organs such as the liver and lymph nodes where ACE2 expression is low.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method for treating viral infection by administering an inhibitor that blocks the binding of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein to L-SIGN, thereby reducing viral entry into susceptible endothelial cells and mitigating downstream endothelial activation. The inhibitor is selected from a group consisting of monoclonal antibodies targeting L-SIGN, recombinant soluble L-SIGN fusion proteins, high-mannose-containing glycan competitors, and small molecules that disrupt the calcium-dependent interaction between L-SIGN and the high-mannose N-glycan at Asn343 of the spike protein. The pharmaceutical composition comprises the inhibitor in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, formulated for intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular administration. Administration of the inhibitor reduces tissue damage in the liver, lymph nodes, and other organs expressing L-SIGN, including the lung and kidney, by preventing viral binding and subsequent endothelial cell activation. The inhibitor is administered during the early phase of infection, preferably within 72 hours of symptom onset, and may be repeated at intervals of 24 to 96 hours depending on clinical response and pharmacokinetic profile. The dosage is titrated to achieve serum concentrations sufficient to block at least 80% of L-SIGN–spike protein interactions, with infusion rates optimized to minimize complement activation and cytokine release. The method further reduces vascular deficit by preserving endothelial integrity and suppressing the release of procoagulant factors such as vWF and FVIII. In patients exhibiting signs of liver dysfunction, including elevated transaminases, prolonged prothrombin time, or histological evidence of sinusoidal dilation, administration of the inhibitor ameliorates hepatic injury and restores normal coagulation homeostasis. Similarly, in patients with lymph node enlargement or impaired lymphatic drainage, the inhibitor reduces viral load within lymphatic endothelial cells and mitigates lymphoid tissue inflammation. The invention provides a targeted therapeutic approach that addresses a previously unrecognized mechanism of SARS-CoV-2 pathogenesis, offering a novel strategy for preventing severe disease progression in high-risk populations.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention encompasses a novel class of therapeutic agents and methods for the treatment of SARS-CoV-2 infection by specifically inhibiting the interaction between the viral spike protein and the C-type lectin receptor L-SIGN. The term “treating” refers to any therapeutic intervention that reduces the severity, duration, or progression of disease, including prevention of symptom onset, attenuation of clinical manifestations, or promotion of recovery. “Therapy” includes both prophylactic and curative administration of the inhibitor. A “therapeutically effective amount” is that quantity of the inhibitor sufficient to achieve a clinically meaningful reduction in viral binding to L-SIGN–expressing endothelial cells, as measured by decreased viral load, reduced plasma levels of vWF and FVIII, or improved organ function. The inhibitor may be administered alone or in combination with other antiviral agents, anticoagulants, or immunomodulators. The composition may be formulated in aqueous solutions containing buffers such as phosphate-buffered saline, histidine, or acetate, with stabilizers including human serum albumin, polysorbate 80, or sucrose. The subject is any mammal, preferably a human, who is infected with SARS-CoV-2 or at high risk of developing severe disease. “Pharmaceutically acceptable” refers to carriers, excipients, or adjuvants that are non-toxic, non-immunogenic, and compatible with biological systems. The “effective dose” is determined based on body weight, disease stage, and pharmacokinetic parameters, and may range from 0.1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg per administration. Conventional methods of drug delivery, including intravenous infusion, subcutaneous injection, or inhalation via nebulized formulation, are applicable. SARS-CoV-2 is a betacoronavirus whose spike protein contains a receptor-binding domain that interacts with host cell receptors. L-SIGN, also known as CD209L or CLEC4M, is a type II transmembrane C-type lectin receptor expressed predominantly on liver sinusoidal endothelial cells and lymphatic endothelial cells. The spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 contains multiple N-linked glycosylation sites, one of which at Asn343 is modified with high-mannose–type glycans that are specifically recognized by the EPN motif of L-SIGN in a calcium-dependent manner. Binding of the spike protein to L-SIGN facilitates viral entry into endothelial cells, triggering intracellular signaling pathways that lead to upregulation of vWF and FVIII, loss of Lyve-1 expression, and endothelial activation. The invention is based on the discovery that L-SIGN, but not ACE2, is the primary receptor mediating SARS-CoV-2 infection in liver and lymph node endothelial cells. The expression of L-SIGN on these cells was confirmed in human biopsy and autopsy tissues using immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence with two distinct monoclonal antibodies. Pseudotyped and authentic SARS-CoV-2 viruses were shown to infect L-SIGN–expressing cells but not control cells lacking L-SIGN. The binding interaction was abolished by removal of high-mannose glycans, mutation of Asn343, or chelation of calcium ions. Recombinant L-SIGN/Fc fusion protein, anti–L-SIGN monoclonal antibodies, and mannan effectively blocked infection in vitro and in vivo. The invention further encompasses the use of these inhibitors to reduce vascular leakage, microthrombosis, and organ dysfunction in patients with severe COVID-19. The materials and methods used to generate recombinant proteins, transfect cell lines, produce pseudoviruses, and perform binding and infection assays are described in detail herein. Plasmids encoding the spike protein, L-SIGN, and ACE2 were cloned into mammalian expression vectors and transfected into COS-7 or HEK293T cells. Protein purification was performed using protein A agarose chromatography, followed by buffer exchange and concentration via ultrafiltration. Protein purity was assessed by SDS-PAGE and Coomassie staining. Cell culture was conducted under standard conditions using endothelial cell growth medium supplemented with fetal bovine serum, endothelial cell growth factors, and antibiotics. Pseudovirus production involved cotransfection of pHIV-ZsGreen, psPAX2, and spike-expressing plasmids. Infection inhibition assays were performed by preincubating virus with inhibitors prior to addition to target cells, followed by flow cytometric quantification of ZsGreen expression. Western blotting, lectin blotting, and immunohistochemistry were used to confirm protein expression and localization. Glycan structural analysis was performed using endoglycosidase H digestion and mannose-specific lectin binding. Histological examination of liver and lymph node tissues from COVID-19 patients revealed colocalization of SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein with L-SIGN on liver sinusoidal endothelial cells.
