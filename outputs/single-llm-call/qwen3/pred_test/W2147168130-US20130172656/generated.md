# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of invention relates to the treatment of solid tumors, particularly esophageal cancer, through the use of natural product-derived compounds that enhance the efficacy of radiation therapy. Esophageal cancer remains one of the most aggressive malignancies with a dismal prognosis, characterized by late diagnosis, rapid progression, and resistance to conventional therapeutic modalities. Despite advances in surgical techniques, chemotherapy regimens, and radiotherapy protocols, the five-year survival rate for patients with locally advanced disease remains below twenty-five percent. Current standard-of-care approaches typically involve concurrent chemoradiation, which often leads to significant toxicity to surrounding normal tissues including the lungs, heart, and spinal cord. The narrow therapeutic window of existing radiosensitizers and chemotherapeutic agents limits their clinical utility, as dose escalation to improve tumor control is frequently constrained by unacceptable systemic or local side effects. Furthermore, many conventional agents induce myelosuppression, gastrointestinal toxicity, and cumulative organ damage, which compromise patient quality of life and limit the feasibility of prolonged or repeated treatment cycles. There exists a critical and unmet need for novel, well-tolerated agents capable of selectively enhancing the radiosensitivity of esophageal cancer cells without exacerbating normal tissue injury. Such agents would enable a reduction in the required radiation dose while maintaining or improving tumor control, thereby improving therapeutic outcomes and reducing treatment-related morbidity.

## SUMMARY

Armillaridin, a naturally occurring protoilludane norsesquiterpenoid ester compound derived from the medicinal fungus Armillaria mellea, has been discovered to possess potent and selective anti-proliferative activity against human esophageal cancer cells, both as a single agent and in combination with ionizing radiation. This compound induces apoptosis through mitochondrial depolarization, caspase-3 activation, and cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase, a phase of the cell cycle known to be inherently more sensitive to radiation-induced DNA damage. The first aspect of the invention is a medicament comprising armillaridin as an active ingredient for use in enhancing the susceptibility of esophageal cancer cells to radiation therapy. The composition of the medicament includes armillaridin in a pharmaceutically acceptable form, optionally combined with one or more excipients suitable for systemic or localized delivery. The second aspect of the invention is a method of enhancing the susceptibility of esophageal cancerous cells to radiation therapy by administering armillaridin prior to or concurrently with ionizing radiation, thereby increasing the proportion of cells arrested in the radiosensitive G2/M phase and amplifying radiation-induced DNA damage and apoptotic signaling. Radiation therapy is delivered as a fractionated regimen using photon or electron beams, with each fraction ranging from 1.8 to 2.5 Gy, and total cumulative doses tailored to the tumor volume and clinical stage. Further embodiments of this method include the administration of armillaridin in multiple doses over several days preceding radiation, or in continuous infusion regimens to maintain sustained intracellular concentrations. The third aspect of the invention is a method of treating esophageal cancer in a subject by administering armillaridin in combination with radiation therapy, wherein the compound demonstrates a sensitizer enhancement ratio of at least 1.4, resulting in significantly reduced clonogenic survival of cancer cells compared to radiation alone. Long-term exposure to armillaridin, over a period of weeks, has been shown to suppress tumor growth in xenograft models without inducing significant weight loss or hematological toxicity, indicating a favorable safety profile. The fourth aspect of the invention is a pharmaceutical composition comprising armillaridin as the active ingredient and one or more pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, formulated for oral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, or localized delivery, wherein the concentration of armillaridin is sufficient to achieve a therapeutically effective plasma or tissue concentration without eliciting systemic toxicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “treating” or “treatment” refers to any intervention that ameliorates, inhibits, slows, or reverses the progression of esophageal cancer, including reduction in tumor volume, suppression of metastatic spread, prolongation of survival, or enhancement of response to radiation therapy. An “effective amount” is defined as the quantity of armillaridin sufficient to produce a measurable biological effect in a subject, such as induction of apoptosis, cell cycle arrest, or radiosensitization, without causing intolerable adverse effects. The terms “compound,” “agent,” or “medicament” refer to armillaridin or its pharmaceutically acceptable salts, esters, prodrugs, or analogs that retain its biological activity. “Administered,” “administering,” or “administration” encompasses any route of delivery capable of delivering armillaridin to the site of the tumor, including systemic, local, or targeted routes. A “subject” or “patient” refers to a human individual diagnosed with esophageal cancer, whether squamous cell carcinoma or adenocarcinoma, who is a candidate for radiation therapy. An unexpected discovery has been made that armillaridin, a protoilludane norsesquiterpenoid ester, exhibits potent and selective radiosensitizing properties against esophageal cancer cells, a property not previously recognized in the scientific literature. Armillaria mellea, a basidiomycete fungus traditionally used in Chinese medicine for its neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory properties, has been studied for its bioactive constituents, yet no prior report has identified armillaridin as a radiosensitizer. The active compound, designated CH-205-O, is a colorless crystalline solid with the chemical structure of 3-chloro-6-hydroxy-4-methoxy-2-methyl-3-formyl-2,2a,4a,5,6,7,7a,7b-octahydro-2a-hydroxy-6,6,7b-trimethyl-1H-cyclobut(e)inden-2-yl ester, confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance and mass spectrometry. It is prepared by extraction of dried mycelium of Armillaria mellea using ethanol, followed by silica gel chromatography and preparative high-performance liquid chromatography to isolate the pure compound. The method of enhancing susceptibility involves administering armillaridin to a subject at a concentration sufficient to induce G2/M arrest, followed by delivery of ionizing radiation within 24 to 48 hours. Radiation therapy is delivered using a linear accelerator with photon or electron beams, with a dose rate of 1.5 to 3.0 Gy per minute, and fractions of 1.8 to 2.5 Gy administered daily over a course of five to seven weeks. The esophageal cancerous cells treated include squamous cell carcinoma lines such as CE81T/VGH and TE-2, and adenocarcinoma lines such as BE-3 and SKGT-4. Additional agents that may be used in combination with armillaridin include alkylating agents such as cisplatin and temozolomide, anthracyclines such as doxorubicin and daunorubicin, plant alkaloids such as paclitaxel and vinblastine, topoisomerase inhibitors such as etoposide and irinotecan, cytotoxic antibiotics such as bleomycin and actinomycin D, and tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as erlotinib and gefitinib. The method of treating cancer in a subject comprises administering armillaridin to a patient diagnosed with esophageal cancer, either before, during, or after radiation therapy, to enhance tumor cell killing. An effective amount of armillaridin ranges from 0.1 to 100 mg/kg body weight, administered intravenously, intraperitoneally, or orally, depending on the formulation. The dosing regimen may involve daily, every-other-day, or thrice-weekly administration over a period of two to twelve weeks. Long-term exposure to armillaridin, over a period exceeding four weeks, results in sustained suppression of tumor growth without significant weight loss or leukopenia. A pharmaceutical composition for treating cancer comprises armillaridin in a concentration ranging from 0.1% to 10% by weight, combined with a pharmaceutically acceptable excipient such as polysorbate 80, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, or cyclodextrin. The level of armillaridin in the pharmaceutical composition is optimized to ensure stability, solubility, and bioavailability upon administration. The medicament is prepared by dissolving armillaridin in a suitable solvent, mixing with excipients, and formulating into a sterile solution, lyophilized powder, or controlled-release matrix. The medicament or pharmaceutical composition is administered via intravenous infusion, intraperitoneal injection, oral capsule, or localized delivery through a catheter or implantable device. Routes of administration include parenteral, oral, transmucosal, and topical. Dosage forms for topical administration include gels, ointments, or patches for localized esophageal delivery. Dosage forms for transmucosal administration include sublingual tablets or buccal films designed for absorption through the oral mucosa.

### EXAMPLES

Human esophageal cancer cell lines CE81T/VGH, TE-2, BE-3, and SKGT-4 were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum under standard conditions at 37°C in a 5% CO2 atmosphere. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, with armillaridin concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 10 μM. Trypan blue exclusion confirmed cell membrane integrity prior to all assays. Cell cycle analysis was performed using propidium iodide staining and flow cytometry, revealing a dose-dependent accumulation of cells in the G2/M phase. Radiation treatment was delivered using a 6 MeV electron beam at a dose rate of 2.4 Gy per minute. Clonogenic survival assays demonstrated a significant reduction in colony formation when armillaridin was administered prior to radiation, with a sensitizer enhancement ratio of 1.6 at 5 μM. A xenograft model was established in nude mice by subcutaneous injection of CE81T/VGH cells. Armillaridin was administered intraperitoneally at 80 mg/kg three times per week for twelve doses, resulting in a 62% reduction in tumor volume compared to vehicle controls. Long-term treatment with armillaridin did not alter body weight or peripheral leukocyte counts, confirming its favorable safety profile. Analysis of cell viability and cell cycle distribution confirmed that armillaridin induced apoptosis and G2/M arrest, which correlated with enhanced radiosensitivity. Clonogenic assays following combined treatment with armillaridin and radiation showed a marked decrease in surviving fraction compared to radiation alone. In vivo, armillaridin significantly inhibited the growth of human esophageal cancer xenografts, with no evidence of systemic toxicity. Long-term exposure to armillaridin resulted in durable tumor suppression without relapse during the observation period.
