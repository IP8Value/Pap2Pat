# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- Energy harvesting represents a critical enabler for the deployment of autonomous, wireless sensor networks in structural health monitoring, industrial machinery, and embedded microsystems where conventional power sources such as batteries are impractical due to maintenance constraints, environmental limitations, or operational longevity requirements. Among the various forms of ambient energy available in engineered environments, mechanical vibrations constitute a pervasive and often underutilized source of kinetic energy. These vibrations arise from rotating machinery, fluid flow, wind-induced oscillations, and human activity, and are present across a broad spectrum of frequencies, typically ranging from a few hertz to several kilohertz. The conversion of these mechanical oscillations into usable electrical energy through piezoelectric transduction offers a compelling pathway toward self-sustaining power systems. Unlike photovoltaic or thermal harvesting methods, which depend on environmental conditions such as light intensity or temperature differentials, vibration-based energy harvesting is uniquely suited for indoor, underground, or enclosed systems where other ambient sources are inconsistent or absent. The development of low-power microelectromechanical systems has further intensified the demand for compact, reliable, and adaptive energy harvesters capable of operating efficiently under variable and unpredictable vibrational conditions without requiring external power for regulation or tuning.

## BACKGROUND

- Piezoelectric vibration energy harvesting devices have emerged as one of the most promising technologies for converting ambient mechanical motion into electrical energy due to their direct energy conversion mechanism, solid-state reliability, and scalability to microdimensions. These devices typically consist of a cantilevered beam with a piezoelectric material bonded to its surface, which generates an alternating voltage in response to mechanical strain induced by ambient vibrations. However, a fundamental limitation of conventional piezoelectric harvesters lies in their narrow operational bandwidth, which is constrained by their fixed resonant frequency determined by the mass, stiffness, and geometry of the structure. As a result, such devices exhibit peak power output only when the ambient vibration frequency closely matches their natural resonant frequency, rendering them ineffective in real-world environments where vibration frequencies fluctuate due to load variations, temperature changes, or operational modes. While passive methods such as mass loading, notching, or multi-beam configurations have been proposed to broaden bandwidth, these approaches sacrifice peak efficiency, increase device size, or offer only discrete tuning steps. Active tuning methods, including electromagnetic actuators or shape memory alloys, have been explored but are often hindered by high power consumption, slow response times, or complex integration requirements. The need for a low-power, continuous, and dynamically responsive tuning mechanism that can adapt the harvester’s resonant frequency in real time to match ambient conditions remains unmet by existing technologies. This limitation has impeded the widespread adoption of piezoelectric harvesters in applications requiring long-term, unattended operation under variable vibrational environments.

## SUMMARY

- A tunable piezoelectric vibration energy harvesting device is disclosed that integrates a macro fiber composite piezoelectric transducer with one or more ionic polymer metal composites to enable dynamic, low-power adjustment of the system’s resonant frequency. The device comprises a flexible cantilever beam upon which a macro fiber composite is bonded in a unimorph configuration, allowing for efficient conversion of mechanical strain into electrical energy. An ionic polymer metal composite, composed of a hydrated ionic polymer such as Nafion coated with conductive metal layers, is positioned adjacent to the cantilever and configured to apply a controlled mechanical load to the beam upon actuation. When a low-voltage electric field is applied across the ionic polymer metal composite, cation migration induces osmotic pressure gradients that cause the strip to bend, thereby altering the effective stiffness of the cantilever and shifting its resonant frequency. Multiple ionic polymer metal composite elements may be arranged in a stacked configuration to amplify the actuation force and provide finer control over frequency tuning. The device further includes a sensor circuit that monitors the output voltage of the piezoelectric transducer to detect ambient vibration frequency, and a control circuit that adjusts the voltage applied to the ionic polymer metal composite to maintain optimal power generation. Energy harvested by the piezoelectric transducer is rectified using a full-wave bridge circuit and stored in an energy storage element such as a capacitor or rechargeable battery. The actuation force generated by the ionic polymer metal composite is proportional to the applied voltage and the hydration state of the polymer, enabling precise, continuous, and energy-efficient tuning of the resonant frequency across a wide range of ambient vibration frequencies. The entire system operates autonomously, with the power required for tuning drawn from the harvested energy, thereby enabling a self-sustaining, closed-loop energy harvesting architecture.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Introduction

- Tunable piezoelectric vibration energy harvesters represent a class of electromechanical systems designed to overcome the inherent frequency-matching limitations of conventional piezoelectric harvesters by dynamically adjusting their mechanical resonance to align with the frequency of ambient vibrations. The present invention introduces a novel architecture that leverages the unique actuation characteristics of ionic polymer metal composites to achieve low-power, continuous, and reversible tuning of resonant frequency without requiring external power sources or bulky mechanical components. Macro fiber composites are employed as the primary piezoelectric transducer due to their high flexibility, compatibility with curved surfaces, and superior strain-to-voltage conversion efficiency under low-frequency excitation. These composites consist of piezoelectric ceramic fibers embedded in a polymer matrix and patterned with interdigitated electrodes to facilitate in-plane poling and actuation. Ionic polymer metal composites, by contrast, function as electroactive actuators that deform in response to low-voltage stimuli, typically between one and four volts, due to the migration of hydrated cations within their polymer network. This actuation mechanism generates a bending moment that can be precisely applied to the cantilever beam, thereby modifying its effective stiffness and shifting its resonant frequency. The low current draw and minimal power consumption of ionic polymer metal composites—on the order of microwatts—make them uniquely suited for integration into energy harvesting systems where the available power budget is constrained. The combination of macro fiber composite transduction and ionic polymer metal composite actuation enables a compact, lightweight, and fully autonomous system capable of maintaining peak power output across a continuously varying vibrational environment.

### Example 1

- A tunable piezoelectric vibration energy harvesting device is disclosed comprising a cantilever beam fabricated from a lightweight metallic substrate, such as aluminum, with a first section of greater thickness and a second, thinner extension section bonded to form a stepped geometry. A macro fiber composite piezoelectric transducer is bonded to the upper surface of the thicker section in a unimorph configuration, extending along a substantial portion of the beam’s length to maximize strain generation. An ionic polymer metal composite actuator is mounted on a fixed support adjacent to the free end of the cantilever, oriented such that its actuation direction is perpendicular to the plane of beam deflection. Upon application of a low-voltage signal to the ionic polymer metal composite, the strip bends toward the cantilever, applying a localized compressive load at a predetermined contact point. This load increases the effective stiffness of the cantilever, resulting in a measurable upward shift in the system’s resonant frequency. The magnitude of the frequency shift is directly proportional to the applied voltage, allowing for fine-grained control over tuning without the need for mechanical reconfiguration. The actuator is positioned such that its deflection does not interfere with the primary vibrational mode of the beam, ensuring that energy conversion efficiency remains high while enabling dynamic resonance adjustment. The device is configured to operate in environments with ambient vibrations ranging from 5 to 10 hertz, with the ability to tune the resonant frequency in increments of 0.1 to 0.5 hertz through incremental changes in actuation voltage.

### Example 2

- A second embodiment of the tunable piezoelectric vibration energy harvesting device incorporates a dual-actuator configuration in which two ionic polymer metal composite strips are arranged symmetrically on either side of the cantilever beam, each bonded to independent support structures. These strips are actuated simultaneously using a common voltage source, producing a balanced, symmetric load on the beam that minimizes lateral deflection and torsional effects while maximizing axial stiffness modulation. The strips are positioned such that their tips contact the cantilever at points located at different distances from the fixed end, enabling a broader range of stiffness modulation and a wider tuning bandwidth. The actuation of both strips is controlled by a single microcontroller that adjusts the voltage based on feedback from the piezoelectric output, allowing the device to track ambient frequency variations with high precision. The use of two actuator elements enhances the repeatability and stability of the tuning process, reducing hysteresis and improving long-term reliability. This configuration is particularly suited for applications requiring robust performance under multi-directional or non-uniform vibrational inputs, such as in rotating machinery or aerospace structures.

### Example 3

- An energy harvesting system is disclosed comprising a tunable piezoelectric vibration energy harvesting device, a control circuit, an energy storage unit, and a full-wave bridge rectifier. The harvesting device includes a cantilever beam with a macro fiber composite transducer and two stacked ionic polymer metal composite strips arranged in a diamond-shaped configuration to amplify actuation force. The output of the piezoelectric transducer is connected to a full-wave bridge rectifier composed of four diodes arranged in a Wheatstone bridge configuration, followed by a smoothing capacitor to convert the alternating current output into a stable direct current. The rectified output is directed to an energy storage element, such as a lithium-polymer capacitor or a thin-film battery, which powers the control circuit and the ionic polymer metal composite actuators. The control circuit includes a microprocessor that samples the voltage waveform from the piezoelectric transducer, identifies the dominant frequency component using a fast Fourier transform algorithm, and compares it to a pre-stored calibration table correlating actuation voltage with resonant frequency. Based on this comparison, the control circuit adjusts the voltage applied to the ionic polymer metal composite strips to shift the resonant frequency toward the ambient vibration frequency. The system operates in a closed-loop manner, with the power consumed by the actuators constituting less than five percent of the total harvested energy, ensuring net positive energy gain. The ionic polymer metal composite strips are configured as tunable load members, with their actuation force modulated by varying the applied voltage, hydration level, or stacking configuration to achieve desired tuning ranges.

### Example 4

- The dimensions of the tunable piezoelectric vibration energy harvesting device are optimized for low-frequency operation, with the cantilever beam measuring 25.5 centimeters in total length, composed of a 15-centimeter root section with a thickness of 0.64 millimeters and a 10.5-centimeter extension section with a thickness of 0.37 millimeters, joined with a 3-centimeter overlap to ensure structural continuity. The macro fiber composite transducer has an active length of 85 millimeters, an active width of 14 millimeters, and a thickness of 0.15 millimeters, bonded to the upper surface of the root section. Each ionic polymer metal composite actuator has dimensions of 30 millimeters in length, 5 millimeters in width, and 0.2 millimeters in thickness, fabricated from a Nafion membrane coated with platinum electrodes. The distance between the fixed end of the cantilever and the point of actuator contact is maintained at 14 centimeters to ensure optimal stiffness modulation without inducing nonlinear effects. The entire device is encapsulated in a moisture-resistant polymer housing to preserve the hydration state of the ionic polymer metal composite, which is critical for consistent actuation performance. The housing includes a vent to allow for controlled humidity exchange, ensuring long-term operational stability in varying environmental conditions.

### Example 5

- The resonant frequency tuning capability of the device is demonstrated through a series of controlled experiments in which the ambient vibration frequency is swept across a range of values while the actuation voltage applied to the ionic polymer metal composite is varied in discrete steps. At zero actuation voltage, the device exhibits a resonant frequency of 5.9 hertz with a power output of 29 microwatts. Upon application of a 1.5-volt actuation signal, the resonant frequency shifts to 7.57 hertz, with the power output increasing to 52.03 microwatts while the power consumed by the actuators remains below one microwatt. Further increases in actuation voltage to 1.9 volts and 2.1 volts result in resonant frequency shifts to 7.7 hertz and 8.0 hertz, respectively, with corresponding power outputs of 49.98 microwatts and 64.19 microwatts. The net power gain—the difference between harvested energy and actuation energy—increases with each tuning step, demonstrating that the system not only adapts to ambient conditions but enhances overall energy capture efficiency. A linear relationship is observed between actuation voltage and resonant frequency shift, enabling predictive control and eliminating the need for complex feedback algorithms in many applications. The tuning range extends from 5.9 hertz to 8.0 hertz with a single actuator, and can be extended further through the use of multiple stacked actuators or alternative polymer formulations.

### Example 6

- A method of energy harvesting is disclosed comprising the steps of exposing a tunable piezoelectric vibration energy harvesting device to ambient mechanical vibrations, detecting the dominant frequency of the vibrations through analysis of the alternating voltage output generated by a macro fiber composite piezoelectric transducer bonded to a cantilever beam, and applying a low-voltage signal to one or more ionic polymer metal composite actuators positioned adjacent to the beam to induce a mechanical load that alters the effective stiffness of the cantilever. The applied voltage is selected from a range between 0.5 volts and 4.0 volts to achieve a desired resonant frequency shift, with the magnitude of the shift being proportional to the voltage and the hydration state of the ionic polymer metal composite. The output of the piezoelectric transducer is rectified using a full-wave bridge circuit and stored in an energy storage element, a portion of which is then used to power the ionic polymer metal composite actuators, thereby forming a self-sustaining closed-loop system. The method further includes the step of calibrating the relationship between actuation voltage and resonant frequency shift prior to deployment, enabling the system to autonomously adjust its tuning parameters in response to changing environmental conditions without requiring external communication or intervention.
