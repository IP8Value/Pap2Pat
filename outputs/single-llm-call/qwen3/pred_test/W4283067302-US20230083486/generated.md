# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

- introduce reinforcement learning

Reinforcement learning is a computational framework through which an agent learns to make sequential decisions by interacting with an environment to maximize cumulative reward. Unlike supervised learning, where correct outputs are provided for every input, reinforcement learning relies on delayed, sparse, or noisy feedback signals that indicate the desirability of actions taken over time. The agent must therefore infer which actions lead to favorable outcomes through trial and error, balancing the need to exploit known rewarding behaviors with the necessity of exploring unfamiliar states that may yield higher long-term returns. This framework is particularly suited to complex, dynamic, and partially observable systems where explicit programming of behavior is infeasible. Applications span robotics, autonomous navigation, game playing, resource allocation, and adaptive control systems. The core challenge lies in the credit assignment problem—determining which actions among many contributed to a delayed reward—especially when the environment exhibits high dimensionality, stochasticity, or long temporal dependencies. Traditional approaches often struggle in such settings due to inefficient exploration strategies that rely on random or heuristic-driven actions, leading to prohibitively slow convergence or complete failure to discover meaningful policies. Recent advances have sought to address this by incorporating internal motivational signals derived from the agent’s own predictive models of the environment, enabling more directed and efficient exploration without reliance on external reward shaping or human demonstrations.

## SUMMARY

- describe reinforcement learning system

A reinforcement learning system comprises an agent that perceives its environment through observations and selects actions to influence future states, with the goal of maximizing a cumulative reward signal. The system operates iteratively over discrete time steps, wherein each action triggers a transition in the environment, resulting in a new observation and a scalar reward. The agent’s behavior is governed by a policy that maps historical observations and actions to a probability distribution over possible next actions. The environment may be deterministic or stochastic, fully or partially observable, and may vary in complexity from simulated digital worlds to physical systems such as robotic arms or autonomous vehicles. The agent’s learning process is driven by an optimization objective that seeks to adjust the policy parameters to increase expected future returns, typically estimated through value functions or direct policy gradients. To handle high-dimensional sensory inputs such as images or sensor streams, the system employs neural networks to extract compact, meaningful representations of the environment state, enabling generalization across similar perceptual inputs and efficient decision-making under partial observability.

- motivate neural network system

Neural networks are essential to modern reinforcement learning systems because they enable the representation of complex, nonlinear relationships between high-dimensional observations and optimal actions without requiring hand-engineered features. In environments where observations consist of raw pixel data, multi-sensor inputs, or sequential textual instructions, traditional tabular or linear methods are intractable due to the combinatorial explosion of possible states. Neural networks, particularly those with hierarchical architectures, can automatically learn invariant features that capture the underlying structure of the environment, such as object shapes, spatial relationships, or temporal patterns in dynamics. This capacity for representation learning allows the agent to generalize across previously unseen situations and to compress vast state spaces into manageable latent embeddings. Furthermore, neural networks support end-to-end training, where the entire system—from perception to action selection—is optimized jointly, eliminating the need for modular pipelines that introduce error propagation and suboptimal decoupling. Their ability to model uncertainty, incorporate memory through recurrent units, and adapt dynamically to changing conditions makes them uniquely suited for real-world reinforcement learning applications where robustness and scalability are paramount.

- describe environment representation neural network

The environment representation neural network is a deep learning module responsible for transforming raw observations into a compact, predictive latent representation that encodes the essential dynamics and structure of the environment. This network consists of an encoder that maps each observation, such as an image or sensor reading, into a fixed-dimensional embedding, followed by a recurrent component that integrates historical context to form a state representation of the agent’s partial history. The encoder is typically implemented as a convolutional neural network for visual inputs or a feedforward network for structured data, while the recurrent component, often an LSTM or GRU, maintains a hidden state that evolves over time in response to new observations and prior actions. The output of this network is a latent vector that serves as the internal state of the agent, capturing not only the current perceptual input but also inferred properties of the environment such as object positions, causal relationships, and temporal dependencies. This representation is designed to be predictive, meaning it enables the agent to forecast future observations based on plausible sequences of actions, thereby forming the foundation for intrinsic motivation and curiosity-driven exploration.

- describe action selection policy neural network

The action selection policy neural network determines the probability distribution over possible actions given the current internal representation of the environment. This network takes as input the latent state produced by the environment representation network and outputs a parametric distribution over the agent’s action space, which may include discrete choices, continuous control signals, or hybrid combinations thereof. The policy is typically implemented as a multilayer perceptron with a final layer that applies a softmax function for discrete actions or a Gaussian parameterization for continuous actions. During training, the policy is optimized to maximize the expected cumulative reward, using methods such as policy gradients or actor-critic frameworks. The policy network operates in tandem with the environment representation network, ensuring that actions are selected based on a rich, internally consistent model of the world rather than raw sensory inputs. This separation allows the policy to remain robust to perceptual noise and irrelevant variations in observation, focusing instead on the semantically meaningful features encoded in the latent space.

- train neural network system with auxiliary neural networks

The neural network system is trained through a combination of reinforcement learning objectives and auxiliary self-supervised prediction tasks that jointly refine the environment representation and enhance exploration efficiency. Auxiliary neural networks are introduced to predict future latent states, reverse trajectories, or decompose representations into predictive subcomponents, each serving as a surrogate objective that encourages the development of a more informative and causally grounded internal model. These auxiliary networks are trained concurrently with the main policy and environment representation networks, using the same stream of experience collected during interaction with the environment. Their losses are combined with the reinforcement learning signal to form a multi-task objective, ensuring that the latent representation not only supports high reward accumulation but also exhibits strong predictive consistency across time and action sequences. This joint training regime prevents the representation from collapsing into trivial solutions and promotes the emergence of structured, disentangled features that reflect the true dynamics of the environment.

- describe auxiliary neural networks

Auxiliary neural networks are specialized components that operate in parallel with the primary reinforcement learning architecture to provide additional learning signals derived from self-supervised prediction tasks. These networks include a forward prediction network that forecasts future latent states given current representations and actions, a reverse prediction network that reconstructs past states from future ones, and a partial representation network that isolates and predicts specific components of the latent embedding. Each auxiliary network is trained using a loss function that measures the discrepancy between predicted and actual latent representations, with targets generated by a slowly updated exponential moving average of the main encoder. These networks do not directly influence action selection but instead constrain the learning of the environment representation network to favor representations that are stable, predictable, and causally coherent. Their presence ensures that the latent space encodes meaningful temporal structure rather than superficial correlations, thereby improving sample efficiency and generalization across diverse tasks.

- train environment representation neural network

The environment representation neural network is trained by minimizing a predictive loss between its own output and the latent representations generated by a target network, which is an exponentially weighted moving average of the encoder’s parameters. At each training step, the network receives a sequence of observations and corresponding actions, processes them through the encoder and recurrent cell to produce a current latent representation, and then predicts future latent states over a multi-step horizon. The target network, which lags behind in parameter updates, provides stable targets for these predictions, preventing the learning process from becoming unstable due to rapidly changing representations. The loss function is computed as the cosine distance between the predicted future latents and the target latents, encouraging the network to learn representations that are not only predictive but also invariant to irrelevant variations in the observation space. This training mechanism, inspired by bootstrap methods in self-supervised learning, ensures that the representation evolves toward a space where future states can be reliably inferred from past context, forming the basis for intrinsic curiosity.

- generate predictive internal representation

The system generates a predictive internal representation by processing a sequence of observations and actions through the environment representation network, which recursively updates its hidden state to accumulate information about the agent’s history. This internal representation, or latent state, encapsulates not only the current perceptual input but also inferred dynamics, object relationships, and anticipated future states. It is designed to be a compact, abstract encoding that enables the agent to simulate possible outcomes of actions without direct environmental interaction. The representation is updated at each time step using a recurrent architecture that integrates new sensory data with prior context, allowing the agent to maintain a coherent model of the world even under partial observability. This internal state serves as the foundation for both action selection and intrinsic reward computation, ensuring that decisions are grounded in a predictive understanding of the environment rather than reactive responses to immediate stimuli.

- describe method of training environment representation neural network

The method of training the environment representation neural network involves minimizing a predictive loss over multi-step latent trajectories using a bootstrapped target mechanism. At each training iteration, a batch of experience sequences is sampled from a replay buffer, and for each sequence, the network computes a sequence of latent representations based on the observed observations and actions. A separate target network, whose parameters are updated via exponential moving average from the main encoder, generates corresponding target latent representations for future time steps. The main network then predicts these future targets using its current parameters, and the loss is computed as the average cosine distance between predicted and target representations across all valid horizons. The stop-gradient operator is applied to the target representations to prevent backpropagation through them, ensuring that learning is driven solely by the predictive capability of the online network. This process is repeated over multiple epochs using stochastic gradient descent, with regularization and normalization techniques applied to stabilize training and prevent representation collapse.

- receive current and future observation inputs

The system receives as input a sequence of current and future observations drawn from the agent’s interaction with the environment, each corresponding to a discrete time step in a trajectory. These observations may include visual frames, sensor readings, textual instructions, or other modalities that describe the state of the environment at a given moment. The current observation is processed by the encoder to produce an initial latent embedding, while future observations are used to construct target representations for predictive training. The temporal alignment between current and future inputs is maintained through synchronized sampling from the same trajectory, ensuring that the network learns to predict how the environment evolves in response to the agent’s actions. This paired input structure enables the model to learn causal relationships between actions and outcomes, forming the basis for accurate internal modeling and effective exploration.

- generate future latent representation

The future latent representation is generated by applying the target network to future observations in the trajectory, producing a sequence of latent embeddings that serve as ground truth for the predictive task. These representations are computed independently of the online network and are updated slowly via exponential moving average to ensure stability during training. The future latent representation captures the semantic and dynamic content of the environment at a future point in time, including object positions, state transitions, and contextual cues that are not immediately apparent from the current observation. By aligning these targets with the online network’s predictions, the system encourages the development of a latent space that accurately reflects the underlying structure of the environment, enabling the agent to anticipate consequences of its actions and prioritize exploration toward states of high prediction error.

- process current observation input

The current observation input is processed through a hierarchical neural architecture that first extracts low-level features using convolutional or feedforward layers, then integrates these features over time using a recurrent neural network to form a temporally coherent internal state. The encoder transforms raw sensory data into a dense, low-dimensional embedding that discards irrelevant noise while preserving predictive information. This embedding is then combined with the previous action and other auxiliary signals—such as reward history, grip force, or task instructions—to form a unified input to the recurrent cell. The recurrent component updates its hidden state based on this combined input, producing a current latent representation that encodes the agent’s belief about the environment’s state. This representation is used both for action selection and for predicting future states, ensuring that the agent’s decisions are informed by a comprehensive and evolving model of its surroundings.

- generate predicted future latent representation

The predicted future latent representation is generated by the online environment representation network through a forward pass that takes the current latent state and a sequence of planned actions as input. This prediction is computed using an open-loop recurrent mechanism that unrolls the dynamics of the environment over multiple time steps without feedback from actual observations. The network applies learned transition functions to project the current state forward in time, producing a sequence of predicted latent embeddings that correspond to hypothetical future states. These predictions are compared against the target representations derived from actual future observations, and the discrepancy between them forms the basis for the self-supervised learning signal. The accuracy of these predictions reflects the quality of the agent’s internal model, with higher error indicating regions of the environment that are poorly understood and thus prioritized for exploration.

- evaluate objective function

The objective function is evaluated as the aggregate loss across multiple predictive and reinforcement learning components, including the cosine distance between predicted and target latent representations, the expected return under the current policy, and auxiliary prediction errors from forward and reverse trajectory models. Each component is weighted according to its contribution to overall learning efficiency and stability, with the total loss computed as a convex combination of these terms. The objective function is minimized using stochastic gradient descent, with gradients propagated through the entire network architecture to jointly optimize the environment representation, policy, and auxiliary networks. This multi-objective formulation ensures that the agent develops a representation that is simultaneously predictive, reward-maximizing, and information-rich, enabling efficient exploration and robust generalization across diverse tasks.

- determine update to environment representation parameters

The update to the environment representation parameters is determined by computing the gradient of the predictive loss with respect to the encoder and recurrent network weights, using backpropagation through time. This gradient reflects how changes in the representation affect the accuracy of future state predictions, guiding the network toward embeddings that better capture the causal structure of the environment. The update is applied using an adaptive optimizer such as Adam, with learning rates and momentum parameters tuned to ensure stable convergence. The target network’s parameters are updated separately via exponential moving average, providing a slowly evolving reference that prevents the learning signal from becoming volatile. This dual-update mechanism ensures that the representation evolves gradually and consistently, avoiding catastrophic forgetting and promoting the emergence of stable, predictive features.

- generate future internal representation

The future internal representation is generated by simulating the evolution of the agent’s latent state over a multi-step horizon, using the learned dynamics of the environment representation network. This simulation does not rely on actual observations but instead uses the current latent state and a sequence of hypothetical actions to project forward in time. The resulting representation encodes the anticipated state of the environment if the agent were to execute those actions, capturing changes in object positions, environmental conditions, and task-relevant variables. This predicted internal representation is used both to compute intrinsic rewards and to guide the training of the auxiliary prediction networks, ensuring that the agent’s internal model remains aligned with the true dynamics of the environment.

- generate predicted future internal representation

The predicted future internal representation is produced by the online environment representation network as a forward simulation of the agent’s latent state under a sequence of actions. It is computed by recursively applying the learned transition function to the current latent state, incorporating the effects of planned actions without feedback from actual environmental outcomes. This prediction is inherently uncertain and may diverge from the true future state due to incomplete modeling or stochastic dynamics. The difference between this predicted representation and the actual future latent representation, generated by the target network, forms the core of the intrinsic curiosity signal. This discrepancy is used to identify states that are poorly understood, thereby guiding exploration toward regions of high prediction error and promoting the continuous refinement of the agent’s internal model.

- determine update to latent embedding network parameters

The update to the latent embedding network parameters is determined by minimizing the combined loss from predictive, auxiliary, and reinforcement learning objectives. Gradients are computed using automatic differentiation across the entire network, including the encoder, recurrent cell, and auxiliary prediction heads. The loss function is designed to penalize deviations between predicted and target latent representations, while simultaneously encouraging the latent space to support high-reward policies. The resulting gradients are used to adjust the weights of the embedding network via stochastic optimization, ensuring that the latent representation becomes increasingly predictive, disentangled, and aligned with the agent’s goals. This process is repeated over multiple training iterations, with each update refining the agent’s ability to model the environment and exploit its structure for efficient exploration.

- control agent using fixed action selection policy

The agent is controlled using a fixed action selection policy that maps the current internal representation to a probability distribution over possible actions, without further adaptation during deployment. This policy is trained offline using reinforcement learning and remains static during inference, ensuring consistent and deterministic behavior in response to known states. The policy does not undergo online learning or adjustment during interaction, preventing instability and ensuring that exploration is driven solely by the intrinsic reward signal derived from prediction error. This separation between policy and representation learning allows the agent to maintain a stable behavioral strategy while continuously improving its understanding of the environment through self-supervised prediction.

- process current internal representation

The current internal representation is processed by the action selection policy network to determine the next action the agent should take. This network receives the latent state generated by the environment representation module and applies a series of fully connected layers to map it to a probability distribution over the action space. For discrete actions, this distribution is modeled using a softmax function, while for continuous actions, it is represented as a Gaussian with mean and variance parameters derived from the latent state. The output of this network is sampled to produce a concrete action, which is then executed in the environment. The processing of the internal representation ensures that actions are selected based on a rich, predictive model of the world rather than raw sensory inputs, enabling robust decision-making under uncertainty and partial observability.

- determine reinforcement learning loss

The reinforcement learning loss is determined by comparing the expected cumulative reward under the current policy to the actual returns observed during interaction with the environment. This loss is computed using a value function estimator that predicts the future return from the current state, and a policy gradient estimator that adjusts the policy parameters in the direction of higher expected reward. The loss function incorporates temporal difference errors, entropy regularization, and advantage estimation to ensure stable and efficient learning. It is computed over a batch of trajectories sampled from the agent’s experience buffer and is minimized using gradient descent, with gradients propagated through the policy network to update its parameters. The reinforcement learning loss serves as the primary objective for reward maximization, while auxiliary losses guide the development of a predictive and informative internal representation.

- determine update to policy parameters

The update to the policy parameters is determined by computing the gradient of the reinforcement learning loss with respect to the policy network weights, using methods such as actor-critic or policy gradient algorithms. This gradient indicates the direction in which changes to the policy will increase the expected cumulative reward, and is used to adjust the parameters via an adaptive optimizer such as Adam. The update is applied after each training batch, ensuring that the policy evolves toward actions that maximize long-term returns. The policy parameters are updated independently of the environment representation parameters, allowing for modular training and preventing interference between exploration and exploitation objectives. This separation ensures that the agent can refine its understanding of the environment while maintaining a stable and effective behavioral strategy.

- backpropagate reinforcement learning loss

The reinforcement learning loss is backpropagated through the policy network to compute gradients with respect to its parameters, enabling the optimization of action selection toward higher reward outcomes. This process involves computing the derivative of the loss function with respect to each weight in the network, using the chain rule to propagate error signals from the output layer to the input layer. The gradients are then used to update the policy parameters in the direction that maximizes expected future returns. Backpropagation is performed in conjunction with the auxiliary prediction losses, ensuring that the policy is trained on a representation that is both predictive and informative. This joint optimization ensures that the agent’s behavior is guided by a deep understanding of the environment, rather than superficial correlations between observations and rewards.
