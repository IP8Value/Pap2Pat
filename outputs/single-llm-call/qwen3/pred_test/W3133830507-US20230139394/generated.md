# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- define technical field

The present invention relates to systems and methods for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of automatic speech recognition in individuals with speech disorders, including aphasia, apraxia, and dysarthria, through the integration of non-invasive electroencephalographic (EEG) signals with conventional acoustic audio signals. Specifically, the invention encompasses a multimodal speech recognition architecture that leverages neural activity patterns captured via scalp-recorded EEG to generate acoustic representations that complement and augment traditional audio-based features, thereby improving the decoding performance of speech-to-text systems for users with impaired articulation. The invention further includes specialized hardware configurations and signal processing pipelines designed for real-time deployment in clinical, rehabilitative, and assistive technology environments, enabling more accurate and responsive communication aids for individuals whose speech is distorted, fragmented, or otherwise unintelligible to conventional speech recognition systems.

## BACKGROUND

- motivate speech therapy

Speech therapy plays a critical role in the recovery and long-term communication capabilities of individuals who have suffered neurological injury, such as stroke or traumatic brain trauma, resulting in aphasia, apraxia, or dysarthria. These conditions impair the neural mechanisms responsible for speech planning, motor execution, or linguistic formulation, leading to speech that is inconsistent, slow, incomplete, or phonetically distorted. Traditional speech therapy relies heavily on auditory feedback and repetitive articulation exercises, but its efficacy is often limited by the inability of standard speech recognition tools to accurately interpret the altered speech patterns of these patients. Without reliable feedback, patients may struggle to self-correct, leading to diminished motivation, prolonged recovery times, and reduced quality of life. The development of assistive technologies capable of interpreting and responding to impaired speech with high fidelity is therefore not merely a technical challenge, but a vital clinical imperative.

- limitations of audio-only speech recognition

Conventional automatic speech recognition systems are trained on datasets composed primarily of clear, unimpaired speech from neurotypical individuals. As a result, they exhibit severe performance degradation when confronted with the atypical acoustic signatures of aphasic, apraxic, or dysarthric speech. These distortions—such as phoneme substitutions, prolonged pauses, reduced articulatory precision, and irregular prosody—cause word error rates to exceed 90% in some cases, rendering existing voice assistants and speech-to-text interfaces unusable for affected populations. Even large-scale datasets like AphasiaBank, while valuable, fail to fully capture the dynamic and variable nature of speech impairments across individuals and over time. Furthermore, audio-only models lack access to the underlying neural intent that precedes and drives speech production, meaning they attempt to reconstruct meaning from corrupted acoustic outputs rather than inferring the intended linguistic content from its neural precursors. This fundamental limitation restricts their utility in rehabilitation contexts, where the goal is not merely to transcribe what is spoken, but to support the restoration of communicative intent.

## SUMMARY

- summarize EEG-based speech prosthetics

The present invention introduces a novel class of EEG-based speech prosthetics that bridge the gap between neural intention and audible output by integrating electroencephalographic signals with conventional audio inputs to significantly enhance the accuracy of speech recognition for individuals with speech disorders. Unlike prior approaches that rely solely on acoustic features, this invention leverages the high temporal resolution of EEG to extract neural correlates of speech production, transforming them into predictive acoustic representations that are fused with traditional audio features to guide speech recognition models toward more accurate transcriptions. This approach enables the system to infer intended speech even when the actual acoustic output is severely degraded, effectively compensating for the motor and articulatory deficits characteristic of aphasia, apraxia, and dysarthria.

- introduce first embodiment

In a first embodiment, the invention comprises a system that receives a contemporaneous audio signal generated by a speaker and simultaneously records electroencephalographic signals from multiple scalp electrodes. These EEG signals are processed to extract neural features indicative of speech production, which are then mapped to corresponding acoustic representations using a trained deep learning regression model. The resulting acoustic representations are concatenated with Mel frequency cepstral coefficients derived from the audio signal to form a multimodal feature vector. This combined feature set is then provided as input to an automatic speech recognition model, which outputs a text-based transcription of the spoken utterance. The system is capable of operating in real time and is specifically optimized for use with speech patterns exhibiting pauses, distortions, or incomplete articulation.

- obtain audio signal

An audio signal is acquired in real time using a high-fidelity microphone positioned in close proximity to the speaker’s mouth, ensuring capture of the full acoustic spectrum of the utterance, including low-energy phonemes and subtle articulatory transitions. The signal is digitized at a sampling rate of 16 kHz and preprocessed to remove ambient noise and normalize amplitude levels.

- obtain EEG signals

Electroencephalographic signals are recorded simultaneously using a multi-channel EEG cap compliant with the 10–20 international electrode placement system, with at least 29 sensors distributed across the frontal, temporal, and parietal lobes. The signals are amplified, filtered to retain neural activity between 0.1 Hz and 70 Hz, and digitally sampled at 1 kHz to preserve the temporal dynamics of speech-related cortical activity.

- obtain acoustic representations

A regression model, trained on paired EEG and audio data, processes the EEG feature vectors to predict a set of acoustic representations, specifically Mel frequency cepstral coefficients of dimensionality 13, that correspond to the intended speech output. These representations are not direct recordings of sound but rather learned neural proxies of the acoustic structure of speech.

- concatenate features

The predicted acoustic representations derived from EEG are concatenated with the Mel frequency cepstral coefficients extracted from the contemporaneous audio signal to form a unified feature vector that encodes both the external acoustic output and the internal neural intention.

- provide features to ASR model

This concatenated feature vector is then fed as input to a deep learning-based automatic speech recognition model, which has been trained to associate multimodal inputs with corresponding text outputs. The model is capable of processing both isolated utterances and continuous speech streams.

- obtain text-based output

The ASR model generates a sequence of text tokens corresponding to the spoken utterance, which is displayed or vocalized to the user as immediate feedback, enabling real-time correction and reinforcement during speech therapy or daily communication.

- introduce second embodiment

In a second embodiment, the system further incorporates electromyographic signals recorded from facial and neck musculature to identify and remove motion-induced artifacts from the EEG recordings prior to feature extraction. The cleaned EEG signals are then processed as described above, yielding enhanced acoustic representations and improved recognition accuracy, particularly in environments with high muscular activity.

- illustrate architecture 300

Architecture 300 comprises two sequential stages: a deep learning regression stage that maps EEG features to acoustic representations, and a speech recognition stage that fuses these representations with audio features to generate text output.

- describe deep learning regression stage 301

Deep learning regression stage 301 employs a single-layer gated recurrent unit with 128 hidden units followed by a time-distributed dense layer with 13 linear outputs, trained to minimize mean squared error between predicted and actual MFCCs.

- describe speech recognition stage 351

Speech recognition stage 351 integrates the predicted acoustic representations with true audio MFCCs into a unified feature vector, which is then processed by a GRU-based ASR model to produce text output.

- obtain EEG signals

EEG signals are acquired from 29 scalp electrodes, filtered to remove power-line noise and low-frequency drift, and resampled at 100 Hz to match the temporal resolution of acoustic feature extraction.

- obtain audio signals

Audio signals are captured at 16 kHz, downsampled to 100 Hz, and converted into 13-dimensional MFCCs using a Hamming window and 26 mel-filter banks.

- obtain EMG signals

EMG signals are recorded from two facial muscles at 1 kHz, filtered between 20 Hz and 500 Hz, and used to compute regression coefficients for artifact removal from concurrent EEG recordings.

- remove EMG artifacts from EEG signals

EMG artifacts are removed by applying a linear regression model that estimates the contribution of EMG activity to each EEG channel and subtracts the estimated artifact component from the raw signal.

- extract EEG features

Five features are extracted per EEG channel: root mean square, zero-crossing rate, moving window average, kurtosis, and power spectral entropy, forming a 145-dimensional feature vector per time step.

- provide EEG features to deep learning model 303

The 145-dimensional EEG feature vector is normalized and passed to deep learning model 303, which applies kernel principal component analysis to reduce dimensionality to 10, preserving non-linear structure.

- train deep learning model 303

Deep learning model 303 is trained using mean squared error loss over 70 epochs with the Adam optimizer, minimizing the difference between predicted and ground-truth MFCCs across thousands of speech-EEG pairs.

- obtain acoustic representations from EEG signals

The trained model generates 13-dimensional acoustic representations from the compressed EEG features, capturing the spectral and temporal structure of intended speech even when the actual audio is degraded.

- train ASR model 360

ASR model 360 is trained using categorical cross-entropy loss on isolated utterances and connectionist temporal classification loss on continuous speech, with the concatenated EEG-audio feature vector as input.

- describe audio input processing

Audio input is processed using a standard MFCC extraction pipeline with 13 coefficients, 26 mel filters, and a 25 ms window with 10 ms overlap, aligned temporally with EEG features.

- obtain Mel frequency cepstral coefficients

Mel frequency cepstral coefficients are computed using a discrete cosine transform applied to the log-mel spectrogram, capturing perceptually relevant spectral envelope information.

- concatenate audio input with acoustic representations

The 13-dimensional audio MFCCs are concatenated with the 13-dimensional EEG-derived acoustic representations to form a 26-dimensional multimodal feature vector per time step.

- train ASR model

The ASR model is trained on a corpus of 8,854 utterances from nine patients with aphasia, apraxia, and dysarthria, using a batch size of 50 and early stopping to prevent overfitting.

- describe ASR model architecture

The ASR model consists of a single GRU layer with 512 hidden units, followed by a dropout layer with a rate of 0.2, a dense layer with 57 output units corresponding to the vocabulary size, and a softmax activation function to produce label probabilities.

- describe isolated speech recognition

Isolated speech recognition is performed on discrete utterances, each corresponding to a complete sentence from a predefined vocabulary of 57 common phrases, with output generated at the final time step of the GRU.

- illustrate ASR model 400 architecture

ASR model 400 architecture depicts a sequential pipeline: input feature vector → GRU layer → dropout layer → dense layer → softmax layer → text output, with all layers fully connected and trained end-to-end.

- describe GRU layer

The GRU layer captures temporal dependencies in the feature sequence using update and reset gates, enabling the model to weigh the relevance of past features in predicting the current utterance.

- describe dropout regularization function

Dropout regularization function randomly deactivates 20% of hidden units during training to prevent co-adaptation and improve generalization to unseen speech patterns.

- describe dense layer

The dense layer projects the GRU output into a 57-dimensional space, where each dimension corresponds to the likelihood of a specific sentence label in the vocabulary.

- describe softmax activation function

Softmax activation function converts the dense layer outputs into a probability distribution over the vocabulary, with the highest-probability label selected as the recognized utterance.

- describe training process

Training is performed over 10 epochs using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, with validation loss monitored to trigger early stopping and prevent overfitting.

- describe continuous speech recognition

Continuous speech recognition processes uninterrupted speech streams, generating character-level outputs at each time step, with a 4-gram language model guiding beam search during inference.

- illustrate ASR model 500 architecture

ASR model 500 architecture comprises an encoder-decoder structure: a GRU encoder processes the input feature sequence, and a decoder with a dense layer and softmax activation generates character probabilities, optimized using CTC loss.

- describe GRU layer as encoder

The GRU layer functions as an encoder, compressing the input feature sequence into a latent representation that captures the temporal structure of speech, regardless of duration or pauses.

- describe dense layer with 4-gram language model

The dense layer outputs character-level logits, which are combined with a 4-gram language model during beam search to favor linguistically plausible sequences over phonetically plausible but syntactically invalid ones.

- describe softmax activation function

Softmax activation function is applied at each time step to produce a probability distribution over the character set, enabling the model to predict one character per time step.

- describe CTC loss function

Connectionist temporal classification loss function aligns variable-length input sequences with fixed-length output sequences, allowing the model to recognize speech with variable timing and pauses without requiring explicit segmentation.

- describe training process

Training for continuous recognition spans 100 epochs with batch size 50, using Adam optimization and CTC loss, with validation on held-out utterances to monitor convergence.

- describe speaker identification

Speaker identification is integrated into the ASR pipeline by appending a speaker embedding layer to the dense layer, enabling the system to personalize recognition based on individual speech patterns.

- illustrate ASR model 600 architecture

ASR model 600 architecture extends model 400 by adding a speaker embedding layer before the final dense layer, allowing the model to condition predictions on the identity of the speaker.

- describe GRU layer

The GRU layer remains unchanged, preserving its ability to model temporal dynamics while incorporating speaker-specific context.

- introduce dropout regularization

Dropout regularization is retained to ensure robustness against variability in neural and acoustic signals across sessions and fatigue states.

- describe dense layers

Dense layers are expanded to include speaker embeddings as additional inputs, increasing the dimensionality of the final layer to accommodate both vocabulary and speaker identity.

- introduce softmax activation function

Softmax activation function is applied to the combined output of vocabulary and speaker dimensions, producing a joint probability distribution over possible utterances and speakers.

- illustrate ASR architecture

The overall ASR architecture unifies isolated and continuous recognition modes within a single framework, allowing dynamic switching based on user intent and context.

- describe real-time speech recognition decoding pipeline

The real-time decoding pipeline begins with synchronized acquisition of EEG, EMG, and audio signals, followed by artifact removal, feature extraction, acoustic representation generation, multimodal fusion, and final text output—all within a latency of less than 500 milliseconds.

- introduce sensor signals

Sensor signals include EEG, EMG, and audio, each sampled at their native rates and temporally aligned using a common clock reference.

- describe dry EEG sensor signals

Dry EEG sensor signals are acquired without conductive gel, using capacitive electrodes that maintain signal quality while enhancing user comfort and ease of application.

- describe ear EEG sensor signals

Ear EEG sensor signals are captured via miniaturized sensors embedded in the device’s earpiece, providing a secondary, less noisy source of temporal lobe activity.

- describe dry EMG sensor signals

Dry EMG sensor signals are recorded using textile-based electrodes placed on the jaw and neck, minimizing skin preparation and enabling long-term wear.

- describe audio signals

Audio signals are captured with adaptive gain control to accommodate variations in speaking volume, from whispers to normal speech.

- introduce stream generation modules

Stream generation modules independently process EEG, EMG, and audio data into synchronized, time-aligned feature streams, each sampled at 100 Hz for compatibility.

- describe EEG and EMG data streams

EEG and EMG data streams are preprocessed to remove line noise and motion artifacts, then transformed into five-dimensional feature vectors per channel.

- describe audio data streams

Audio data streams are converted into 13-dimensional MFCCs, aligned to the same temporal grid as neural features.

- remove EMG artifacts

EMG artifacts are removed by computing a linear regression model between EMG and EEG signals, then subtracting the predicted artifact component from each EEG channel.

- obtain EEG features

EEG features are extracted using root mean square, zero-crossing rate, moving window average, kurtosis, and power spectral entropy, forming a 145-dimensional vector per time step.

- obtain audio features

Audio features are extracted as 13-dimensional MFCCs, aligned temporally with EEG features using interpolation and resampling.

- reduce EEG feature dimensionality

EEG feature dimensionality is reduced from 145 to 10 using kernel principal component analysis with a polynomial kernel of degree three, preserving non-linear structure while reducing computational load.

- combine EEG features

The reduced EEG features are concatenated with the audio features to form a 23-dimensional input vector for the ASR model.

- introduce deep learning model

The deep learning model is a GRU-based architecture trained to map the combined feature vector to text output, with parameters optimized for performance on impaired speech.

- concatenate EEG and audio features

Concatenation occurs at the feature level, ensuring that both neural and acoustic information contribute equally to the model’s decision-making process.

- introduce ASR models

ASR models are implemented in two variants: one for isolated utterances using categorical cross-entropy loss, and another for continuous speech using CTC loss, both sharing the same feature input pipeline.

- describe EEG-based speech recognition method

The EEG-based speech recognition method comprises the steps of acquiring synchronized EEG and audio signals, removing EMG artifacts, extracting neural and acoustic features, generating acoustic representations from EEG, fusing these with audio features, and decoding the combined representation into text using a deep learning model trained on impaired speech data.

- receive audio signal

An audio signal is received from a microphone positioned adjacent to the speaker’s mouth, capturing the full acoustic output of the utterance.

- obtain EEG signals

EEG signals are simultaneously recorded from 29 scalp electrodes, filtered, and resampled to match the temporal resolution of the audio stream.

- obtain acoustic representations

Acoustic representations are generated by feeding the processed EEG features into a pre-trained regression model that predicts MFCCs corresponding to the intended speech.

- concatenate acoustic and audio features

The predicted acoustic representations are concatenated with the actual audio-derived MFCCs to form a single, enriched feature vector.

- provide text-based output

The concatenated feature vector is processed by a trained ASR model, which outputs a text transcription of the spoken utterance, displayed or spoken aloud to the user in real time.
