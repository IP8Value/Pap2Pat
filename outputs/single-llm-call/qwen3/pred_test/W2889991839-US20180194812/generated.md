## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- The invention relates to the field of immunology and infectious disease prevention, specifically to multivalent conjugate vaccines designed to elicit protective immune responses against Gram-negative bacterial pathogens responsible for nosocomial infections. The invention encompasses compositions and methods for the prevention and treatment of infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa through the use of chemically conjugated antigens derived from surface polysaccharides and flagellar proteins. The invention further includes pharmaceutical formulations, methods of production, and therapeutic regimens for active and passive immunization against these pathogens, particularly in high-risk clinical settings such as intensive care units, surgical wards, and burn units.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa are Gram-negative bacterial pathogens that are among the leading causes of healthcare-associated infections worldwide. These organisms are frequently associated with pneumonia, urinary tract infections, bloodstream infections, and wound infections, particularly in immunocompromised or post-surgical patients. Both pathogens have demonstrated alarming rates of multidrug resistance, including resistance to carbapenems and colistin, rendering conventional antibiotic therapies increasingly ineffective and contributing to elevated mortality rates. The emergence of extensively drug-resistant and pan-drug-resistant strains has intensified the urgency for alternative prophylactic strategies that are not susceptible to the mechanisms of antimicrobial resistance.

- Klebsiella pneumoniae expresses two major surface antigens: a capsular polysaccharide (K antigen) and a lipopolysaccharide-associated O polysaccharide (OPS). While over eighty distinct K serotypes have been identified, their distribution is highly heterogeneous across geographic regions and clinical settings, making a capsule-based vaccine impractical due to the need for excessive valency. In contrast, only nine OPS structures have been characterized, with four—O1, O2, O3, and O5—accounting for more than 80% of clinically relevant isolates globally. These OPS antigens remain accessible to antibodies despite the presence of a capsule and have been shown to mediate opsonophagocytic killing and protection in animal models of systemic and pulmonary infection.

- Pseudomonas aeruginosa expresses a single polar flagellum composed of flagellin subunits, of which only two major serotypes exist: FlaA and FlaB. Flagella are critical virulence factors that facilitate motility, biofilm formation, epithelial colonization, and activation of innate immune responses via Toll-like receptor 5. Antibodies targeting flagellin can inhibit bacterial motility, block adherence to host tissues, and promote clearance through opsonization. However, immunity to flagellin is serotype-specific, necessitating inclusion of both FlaA and FlaB in any broadly protective vaccine.

- Traditional vaccine approaches for these pathogens have been limited by the poor immunogenicity of polysaccharides alone, which fail to induce T-cell-dependent immune responses, immunological memory, or class switching. While glycoconjugate vaccines have revolutionized protection against other bacterial pathogens such as Haemophilus influenzae type b and Streptococcus pneumoniae, their application to Klebsiella and Pseudomonas has been hindered by challenges in antigen production, conjugation chemistry, and the need for multivalent formulations. Purified lipopolysaccharide or whole-cell killed vaccines are unsuitable due to the endotoxicity of lipid A and the risk of adverse immune reactions. Furthermore, the use of conventional carrier proteins such as tetanus toxoid or diphtheria toxoid is limited by pre-existing immunity in the human population, which may suppress responses to the conjugated polysaccharide.

- Previous attempts to develop multivalent vaccines for Klebsiella and Pseudomonas have relied on co-administration of separate formulations, resulting in inconsistent immune responses and logistical complexity. The development of a single, unified conjugate vaccine that simultaneously targets the four predominant Klebsiella OPS types and the two major Pseudomonas flagellin serotypes represents a significant advancement in vaccine design. Such a formulation would simplify immunization schedules, reduce manufacturing and regulatory burdens, and enhance coverage against the most prevalent and virulent strains circulating in hospital environments. The use of flagellin as a carrier protein is particularly advantageous, as it is pathogen-relevant, non-cross-reactive with commonly used carrier proteins, and retains intrinsic immunostimulatory properties when appropriately modified.

- Despite promising preclinical data with individual components, no licensed vaccine currently exists for either Klebsiella pneumoniae or Pseudomonas aeruginosa. Clinical trials of polysaccharide-based vaccines for Klebsiella have yielded inconsistent results, while Pseudomonas flagellin vaccines tested in humans have demonstrated safety and immunogenicity but limited efficacy when administered without conjugation. The integration of OPS antigens with flagellin proteins into a single multivalent conjugate platform overcomes these limitations by enhancing immunogenicity, enabling T-cell help, and inducing functional, opsonophagocytic antibodies against both pathogens. This approach represents a novel and synergistic strategy to combat the growing threat of multidrug-resistant Gram-negative infections.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The invention provides a non-limiting example embodiment of a single conjugate vaccine comprising at least one Klebsiella pneumoniae O polysaccharide antigen covalently linked to at least one Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein. In a preferred embodiment, the vaccine includes four distinct Klebsiella pneumoniae O polysaccharide antigens—O1, O2, O3, and O5—each chemically conjugated to either Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin type A or flagellin type B, resulting in a quadrivalent conjugate formulation. In another embodiment, the vaccine comprises six individual antigens: four Klebsiella OPS antigens and two Pseudomonas flagellin proteins, wherein at least two of the OPS antigens are conjugated to flagellin proteins and at least two are administered unconjugated to enhance immunogenicity. In a further embodiment, the vaccine is formulated as a single quadrivalent conjugate containing two conjugates of O1 and O3 linked to FlaB, and two conjugates of O2 and O5 linked to FlaA. The invention further encompasses a method for preparing a conjugate vaccine by covalently linking an O polysaccharide antigen from Klebsiella pneumoniae to a flagellin protein from Pseudomonas aeruginosa using a crosslinking agent that facilitates thioether bond formation between aminooxy-modified polysaccharide and maleimide-derivatized flagellin. The invention further includes a method for preparing a conjugate vaccine using recombinant expression vectors encoding codon-optimized flagellin genes in genetically modified Gram-negative bacteria, wherein the flagellin is secreted into the culture supernatant and purified without cell lysis. The invention further provides a method for passive immunization by administering purified immunoglobulins derived from sera of animals immunized with the conjugate vaccine. The invention further describes a method for eliciting a passive immune response by transferring immune sera or purified IgG fractions from vaccinated animals to susceptible hosts. The invention further provides a method for constructing a conjugate vaccine by chemically modifying the reducing end of the O polysaccharide with an aminooxy-thiol linker and derivatizing lysine residues on the flagellin with a maleimide-containing crosslinker. The invention further provides a method for producing a conjugate vaccine using bacterial expression systems, wherein attenuated strains of Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa are engineered to overproduce OPS and flagellin, respectively, and the antigens are purified and conjugated in vitro. The invention further provides a method for eliciting an active immune response by administering the conjugate vaccine parenterally, nasally, or orally to a subject in need thereof, thereby inducing IgG antibodies specific to both Klebsiella OPS and Pseudomonas flagellin antigens.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- The invention provides a conjugate vaccine for the prevention and treatment of bacterial infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The vaccine comprises at least one O polysaccharide antigen derived from Klebsiella pneumoniae covalently linked to at least one flagellin protein derived from Pseudomonas aeruginosa. This conjugate structure enables the induction of a robust, T-cell-dependent immune response against both pathogens, overcoming the limitations of polysaccharide-only vaccines and the serotype-specific nature of flagellin immunity. The invention is particularly suited for use in high-risk populations including hospitalized patients, surgical candidates, burn victims, and individuals with prolonged ICU stays, where infections caused by multidrug-resistant strains are prevalent and often fatal.

- The Klebsiella pneumoniae surface polysaccharide antigen used in the invention is an O polysaccharide (OPS), which is a component of the lipopolysaccharide molecule and is distinct from the capsule polysaccharide (K antigen). The OPS is derived from Klebsiella pneumoniae serovars expressing the O1, O2, O3, or O5 antigenic structures, which collectively account for over 80% of clinical isolates worldwide. These OPS antigens are isolated from genetically attenuated strains of Klebsiella pneumoniae, wherein the capsule biosynthesis genes (wza, wzb, wzc) and guanine biosynthesis genes (guaA, guaB) have been deleted to eliminate capsule expression and render the strain auxotrophic for guanine, thereby ensuring safety and facilitating purification. The OPS is chemically liberated from the lipopolysaccharide core via mild acid hydrolysis or nitrous acid deamination and purified by a series of chromatographic and tangential flow filtration steps to yield a highly pure, endotoxin-free preparation.

- The Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin used in the invention is a recombinantly expressed protein derived from either the FlaA or FlaB serotype. These flagellin proteins are encoded by the fliC gene and are expressed in heterologous Gram-negative bacterial hosts such as Escherichia coli or Salmonella enterica, using codon-optimized synthetic genes to maximize yield and solubility. The flagellin is secreted into the culture supernatant via native or engineered secretion signals and purified using affinity chromatography, ion exchange, and size exclusion techniques to obtain a monomeric, endotoxin-free preparation. The flagellin retains its native conformational epitopes and is capable of binding to Toll-like receptor 5, although conjugation to OPS ablates this activity, thereby reducing potential inflammatory side effects while preserving immunogenicity.

- The conjugate composition comprises a defined ratio of OPS to flagellin, typically ranging from 1:1 to 3:1 by weight, ensuring optimal antigen presentation and immune recognition. The linkage between the OPS and flagellin is covalent and achieved through a two-step chemical conjugation process. First, the reducing end of the OPS is modified with an aminooxy-thiol linker, which introduces a free thiol group. Second, lysine residues on the flagellin are derivatized with a maleimide-containing crosslinker, such as N-γ-maleimidobutyryl-oxysuccinimide ester, which reacts selectively with the thiol group to form a stable thioether bond. This chemistry preserves the structural integrity of the OPS chain and allows for the formation of “sun-type” conjugates in which multiple OPS chains radiate from the flagellin protein surface. The resulting conjugate is purified by size-exclusion chromatography to remove unconjugated components and is formulated in a sterile, isotonic buffer suitable for parenteral administration.

- The invention further encompasses vaccine compositions that include both conjugated and unconjugated antigens. In one embodiment, the composition contains the quadrivalent conjugate mixture described above, along with additional unconjugated flagellin proteins to enhance the magnitude of the anti-flagellin response. In another embodiment, the composition includes unconjugated OPS antigens to provide a broader antigenic stimulus. The inclusion of unconjugated flagellin enhances the overall immunogenicity of the formulation, particularly for the induction of high-titer IgG antibodies against flagellin, which are critical for motility inhibition and opsonophagocytic clearance.

- The invention further provides a multivalent conjugate vaccine comprising multiple distinct conjugates, wherein each conjugate contains a different Klebsiella OPS antigen linked to a Pseudomonas flagellin protein. In a preferred embodiment, the vaccine contains four conjugates: O1:FlaB, O2:FlaA, O3:FlaB, and O5:FlaA. Each conjugate is manufactured under identical conditions and combined in defined proportions to ensure balanced immune responses against all four OPS types and both flagellin serotypes. The vaccine may also include adjuvants such as aluminum salts, monophosphoryl lipid A, or saponins to further enhance immunogenicity, particularly in elderly or immunocompromised populations.

- The vaccine composition may be formulated for various routes of administration, including parenteral, intranasal, oral, transdermal, mucosal, or pulmonary delivery. For parenteral administration, the vaccine is formulated as an isotonic aqueous solution containing buffering agents such as phosphate or histidine, stabilizers such as sucrose or trehalose, and preservatives such as benzyl alcohol or phenol. For mucosal or pulmonary delivery, the vaccine may be formulated as a nebulized suspension, powder inhaler, or metered-dose inhaler, optionally incorporating mucoadhesive polymers such as chitosan or hyaluronic acid to prolong residence time at the mucosal surface. The vaccine may also be prepared as a lyophilized powder for long-term storage and reconstituted prior to administration.

- The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising the conjugate vaccine and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the carrier may include liposomes, emulsions, nanoparticles, or microparticles designed to enhance antigen uptake by antigen-presenting cells. The composition may also include thickening agents, pH adjusters, and surfactants to optimize stability and delivery. The vaccine may be packaged in single-dose or multi-dose vials, accompanied by instructions for administration, storage conditions, and recommended immunization schedules.

- The invention further provides a method for inducing an immune response in a subject by administering an immunologically effective amount of the conjugate vaccine. The subject may be a human patient at risk for Klebsiella or Pseudomonas infection, including those undergoing surgery, suffering from burns, or receiving immunosuppressive therapy. The vaccine may be administered in a prime-boost regimen, with doses given at intervals of 14 to 28 days, and may be followed by a heterologous boost using live attenuated Salmonella strains expressing the same antigens. The immunologically effective amount ranges from 0.5 to 20 micrograms of each OPS component and 1 to 10 micrograms of each flagellin component per dose, depending on the age and immune status of the recipient.

- The invention further provides a method for preparing the conjugate vaccine by covalently linking the Klebsiella OPS antigen to the Pseudomonas flagellin protein using a crosslinking agent. The OPS is isolated from genetically engineered Klebsiella strains, modified with an aminooxy-thiol linker, and then reacted with maleimide-derivatized flagellin under controlled pH and temperature conditions. The reaction is monitored by size-exclusion chromatography and SDS-PAGE to confirm conjugation efficiency. The final product is purified by ion-exchange and size-exclusion chromatography, sterile-filtered, and tested for endotoxin, protein content, and polysaccharide purity.

- The invention further provides genetically engineered strains of Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa that are attenuated and optimized for high-yield production of OPS and flagellin. These strains include deletions in the guaBA locus to induce guanine auxotrophy and deletions in the wza-wzc locus to eliminate capsule expression. In Pseudomonas aeruginosa, the fliC gene is codon-optimized and expressed under a strong promoter, and mutations in the clpP or clpX genes are introduced to enhance flagellin secretion and stability. These strains are used as reagent organisms for scalable, cost-effective production of antigens under GMP conditions.

### Conjugate

- The conjugate of the invention comprises a Klebsiella pneumoniae O polysaccharide antigen covalently bound to a Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein. The O polysaccharide antigen is derived from Klebsiella pneumoniae serovars expressing the O1, O2, O3, or O5 antigenic structure and is isolated from genetically attenuated strains lacking capsule biosynthesis and guanine biosynthesis genes. The flagellin protein is derived from Pseudomonas aeruginosa strains expressing either the FlaA or FlaB serotype and is produced recombinantly in heterologous hosts such as Escherichia coli. The conjugate is formed by linking the reducing end of the OPS to lysine residues on the flagellin via a thioether bond formed between an aminooxy-thiol group on the OPS and a maleimide group on the flagellin. The conjugate may contain multiple OPS chains per flagellin molecule, forming a branched, sun-type architecture. The conjugate may also comprise fragments or derivatives of flagellin that retain key epitopes for antibody recognition, including conserved regions of the D0 and D1 domains. Derivative amino acid sequences of flagellin may include substitutions, deletions, or insertions that preserve structural integrity while reducing endotoxicity or enhancing solubility.

### Compositions

- The vaccine composition of the invention comprises one or more of the conjugates described above, optionally in combination with unconjugated flagellin proteins or unconjugated OPS antigens. The composition may contain a quadrivalent mixture of four distinct conjugates: O1:FlaB, O2:FlaA, O3:FlaB, and O5:FlaA. The composition may further include unconjugated FlaA and FlaB proteins to enhance the anti-flagellin immune response. The composition may be formulated with adjuvants such as aluminum hydroxide, MPLA, or QS-21 to enhance immunogenicity. The composition may be formulated for parenteral administration as a sterile, isotonic solution, or for mucosal delivery as a nebulized suspension or nasal spray. The composition may be prepared as a lyophilized powder, liposomal formulation, or particulate delivery system. The composition may include buffering agents such as histidine or phosphate, stabilizers such as sucrose or mannitol, and preservatives such as benzyl alcohol or phenol. The composition may be packaged in single-dose vials or multi-dose containers with accompanying instructions for reconstitution and administration.

### Methods of Treatment

- The invention provides a method for inducing an immune response in a subject by administering an immunologically effective amount of the conjugate vaccine. The subject may be a human patient at risk for Klebsiella pneumoniae or Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection, including those undergoing surgery, suffering from burns, or receiving immunosuppressive therapy. The vaccine may be administered intramuscularly, subcutaneously, intravenously, intranasally, orally, or by pulmonary inhalation. The vaccine may be administered in a prime-boost regimen, with primary doses given at intervals of 14 to 28 days and a booster dose administered 6 to 12 months later. The immunologically effective amount ranges from 0.5 to 20 micrograms of each OPS component and 1 to 10 micrograms of each flagellin component per dose. The vaccine may be administered prior to, during, or after surgical procedures to prevent postoperative infections. The immune response induced by the vaccine includes the production of IgG antibodies specific to Klebsiella OPS and Pseudomonas flagellin, which mediate opsonophagocytic killing and inhibition of bacterial motility. The protective immune response is characterized by a significant reduction in bacterial burden in blood, liver, spleen, and wound tissues following challenge with virulent strains.

### Methods of Making the Conjugate

- The invention provides a method for making the conjugate by isolating Klebsiella pneumoniae O polysaccharide from genetically attenuated strains and Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin from recombinant expression systems. The OPS is purified by nitrous acid deamination, ion exchange chromatography, and tangential flow filtration. The flagellin is purified by affinity chromatography, anion exchange, and size exclusion. The OPS is modified with an aminooxy-thiol linker and the flagellin is derivatized with a maleimide-containing crosslinker. The modified OPS and flagellin are mixed under controlled pH and temperature conditions to allow conjugation via thioether bond formation. The reaction is quenched with dithiothreitol and the conjugate is purified by size-exclusion chromatography. The final product is sterile-filtered, tested for purity, and formulated for storage.

### Genetically Engineered Strains

- The invention provides genetically engineered strains of Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa that are attenuated and optimized for high-yield antigen production. The Klebsiella strains include deletions in the guaA and guaB genes to induce guanine auxotrophy and deletions in the wza, wzb, and wzc genes to eliminate capsule expression. These strains are non-encapsulated, non-pathogenic, and suitable for large-scale fermentation. The Pseudomonas strains include codon-optimized fliC genes inserted into expression vectors under strong promoters, and mutations in the clpP or clpX genes to enhance flagellin secretion. These strains are grown in chemically defined media and flagellin is harvested from the culture supernatant without cell lysis. The strains are used as reagent organisms for the scalable, cost-effective production of antigens under GMP conditions.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- The invention demonstrates the construction of a quadrivalent conjugate vaccine comprising Klebsiella pneumoniae O1, O2, O3, and O5 polysaccharides chemically conjugated to Pseudomonas aeruginosa FlaA and FlaB flagellins. Recombinant flagellins were expressed in Escherichia coli and purified to homogeneity. Klebsiella OPS was isolated from genetically attenuated strains using nitrous acid deamination and purified by chromatography. The OPS was modified with an aminooxy-thiol linker and the flagellin was derivatized with maleimide. Conjugation was performed under controlled conditions, and the resulting conjugates were purified by size-exclusion chromatography. The conjugates were characterized by SDS-PAGE, HPLC-SEC, and BCA/resorcinol assays to confirm polysaccharide-to-protein ratios. The conjugates were tested for TLR5 activation and found to be non-stimulatory. Mice immunized with the quadrivalent conjugate developed high-titer IgG antibodies against all four OPS antigens and both flagellin serotypes. Passive transfer of immune sera protected mice against lethal challenge with Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa in intravenous and burn wound infection models. The vaccine induced opsonophagocytic activity and inhibited bacterial motility, demonstrating functional efficacy. The invention further demonstrates the scalability of the manufacturing process, with successful production at 20-liter fermentation scale. The conjugate vaccine was stable for over 12 months at 4°C and retained immunogenicity after lyophilization. The invention provides a novel, broadly protective vaccine against the two most critical multidrug-resistant Gram-negative pathogens in healthcare settings.
