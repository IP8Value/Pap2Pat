# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define invention scope

The present invention relates to a novel therapeutic composition and method for the treatment of inflammatory bowel diseases, particularly ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease, through the targeted sequestration of high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) protein within the gastrointestinal lumen. The invention encompasses non-absorbable, biocompatible beads coated with specific nucleic acid sequences that bind HMGB1 with high affinity and selectivity, thereby reducing its pro-inflammatory activity in the intestinal microenvironment. The composition is designed for oral administration and functions as a luminal scavenger, removing HMGB1 from fecal material, intestinal tissue fluid, and luminal contents without systemic absorption or immunogenic stimulation. This approach represents a paradigm shift in the management of inflammatory bowel disease by directly neutralizing a key alarmin mediator at its site of pathological release, rather than relying on systemic immunomodulation or broad anti-inflammatory agents.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce inflammatory bowel diseases

Inflammatory bowel diseases, including ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease, are chronic, relapsing conditions characterized by dysregulated immune responses within the gastrointestinal tract, leading to mucosal inflammation, epithelial barrier disruption, and significant morbidity. These diseases affect millions of individuals worldwide and impose substantial economic burdens due to prolonged medical care, hospitalizations, and surgical interventions. Despite advances in biologic therapies targeting cytokines such as TNF-alpha and IL-12/23, a substantial proportion of patients exhibit inadequate response, loss of response over time, or intolerance to existing treatments. There remains a critical unmet need for therapies that address the root drivers of inflammation without inducing systemic immunosuppression or compromising host defense mechanisms.

- discuss current treatment limitations

Current therapeutic strategies for inflammatory bowel disease primarily involve corticosteroids, immunomodulators, and biologics that target specific components of the immune cascade. While these agents can induce remission in some patients, they often fail to achieve durable mucosal healing, are associated with significant side effects including increased infection risk and malignancy, and are ineffective in a large subset of the patient population. Furthermore, systemic administration of these agents does not directly address the localized accumulation of danger-associated molecular patterns within the intestinal lumen, which perpetuate inflammation independently of systemic immune activation. The inability of existing therapies to neutralize luminal mediators that directly damage epithelial integrity and activate resident immune cells limits their long-term efficacy and safety profile.

- highlight HMGB1 role in inflammation

High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) is a nuclear protein that, when released extracellularly during cellular stress, necrosis, or activation, functions as a potent damage-associated molecular pattern and pro-inflammatory alarmin. In the context of inflammatory bowel disease, HMGB1 is secreted by inflamed intestinal epithelial cells and activated macrophages, where it accumulates at high concentrations in fecal material and mucosal tissues. Extracellular HMGB1 promotes inflammation by binding to receptors such as RAGE and TLR4, inducing the production of TNF-alpha, IL-6, and other cytokines, increasing epithelial permeability, and impairing mucosal repair. Its presence correlates strongly with disease severity, and its levels in stool and serum are elevated in both murine models and human patients with active disease.

- motivate HMGB1 inhibition

Given its central role in sustaining mucosal inflammation and epithelial dysfunction, HMGB1 represents a compelling therapeutic target. Neutralization of HMGB1 using monoclonal antibodies or pharmacological inhibitors has demonstrated efficacy in preclinical models of colitis, reducing weight loss, histological damage, and cytokine production. However, systemic administration of anti-HMGB1 agents carries risks of unintended immune suppression and is not specific to the gastrointestinal tract. A localized, luminal approach to HMGB1 neutralization offers the potential to achieve therapeutic benefit while minimizing systemic exposure and off-target effects.

- limitations of direct DNA injection

While free DNA molecules have been shown to bind HMGB1 and inhibit its inflammatory activity in vitro, their direct administration as soluble agents is impractical for clinical use. Free DNA is rapidly degraded by nucleases in the gastrointestinal tract, fails to achieve sufficient local concentration at the site of inflammation, and may trigger unintended immune responses through Toll-like receptor activation. Moreover, soluble DNA lacks the structural stability and retention capacity required for sustained interaction with HMGB1 in the dynamic environment of the intestinal lumen. Therefore, a delivery system that immobilizes HMGB1-binding DNA on a non-absorbable, stable carrier is necessary to enable effective, safe, and targeted sequestration within the gut.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce therapeutic beads

The invention introduces a novel class of therapeutic beads composed of a non-absorbable solid matrix coated with specific nucleic acid sequences that bind HMGB1 with high affinity and selectivity. These beads are designed for oral administration and function as luminal scavengers that capture and remove HMGB1 from the gastrointestinal tract without systemic absorption or immune stimulation. The beads retain their structural integrity and binding capacity under acidic, enzymatic, and physiological conditions encountered in the stomach and intestines, enabling sustained interaction with HMGB1 throughout the gastrointestinal lumen.

- summarize treatment methods

The method of treatment involves the oral administration of these HMGB1-binding beads to patients suffering from inflammatory bowel disease, including ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease. Upon ingestion, the beads traverse the gastrointestinal tract, binding HMGB1 present in fecal material, intestinal mucus, and luminal fluid. The sequestered HMGB1 is subsequently excreted in the feces, thereby reducing the local concentration of this pro-inflammatory mediator and interrupting its ability to activate immune cells and disrupt epithelial barrier function. This approach leads to a reduction in clinical symptoms, including weight loss and abdominal pain, as well as improvements in histological markers of mucosal injury and inflammatory cytokine expression, without systemic immunosuppression or toxicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce method of treating disease

The method of treating inflammatory bowel disease comprises administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of non-absorbable beads coated with nucleic acid sequences capable of binding HMGB1. The beads are administered orally, allowing them to interact directly with HMGB1 in the intestinal lumen. The treatment is administered repeatedly over a course of days to weeks, depending on disease severity and clinical response. The method does not require concomitant systemic immunosuppressive therapy and is suitable for use as monotherapy or as an adjunct to standard treatments.

- describe method of reducing HMGB1 levels

The reduction of HMGB1 levels is achieved through direct physical sequestration of the protein by immobilized nucleic acid sequences on the surface of the beads. The nucleic acids are selected for their high-affinity binding to HMGB1, regardless of its redox or acetylation state, enabling capture of multiple pathogenic isoforms present in the inflamed gut. Once bound, HMGB1 is retained on the bead surface and is eliminated from the body via fecal excretion, thereby depleting its concentration in the intestinal microenvironment and interrupting its pro-inflammatory signaling.

- provide therapeutic beads composition

The therapeutic beads comprise a solid, non-absorbable matrix composed of cross-linked polysaccharide or polymer material, with nucleic acid sequences covalently conjugated to the surface. The matrix is inert, non-toxic, and resistant to degradation in the gastrointestinal tract. The nucleic acid sequences are designed to bind HMGB1 with high specificity and are selected from a group of oligonucleotides known to exhibit strong affinity for HMGB1, including linear, kinked, and four-way junction structures.

- specify bead coating agents

The coating agents consist of single-stranded or structured DNA molecules, covalently linked to the bead surface via amino or thiol linkers. The DNA sequences are synthesized with phosphorothioate or phosphodiester backbone modifications to enhance nuclease resistance. The conjugation is achieved through carbodiimide-mediated coupling or NHS-ester chemistry, ensuring stable attachment under physiological conditions.

- describe bead administration methods

The beads are administered orally in the form of a suspension, capsule, or enteric-coated tablet. The suspension is formulated in a biocompatible vehicle such as saline or methylcellulose and is taken once or multiple times daily. The formulation is designed to remain stable in gastric acid and to release the beads intact in the small intestine and colon, where HMGB1 concentrations are highest.

- specify bead materials

The beads are composed of sepharose, agarose, polystyrene, or polyacrylamide matrices, each selected for their mechanical stability, low protein non-specific binding, and compatibility with aqueous biological environments. The bead diameter ranges from 45 to 165 micrometers, with an average size of approximately 90 micrometers, optimized for luminal retention and efficient mixing with intestinal contents.

- describe enteric coating

In certain embodiments, the beads are encapsulated within an enteric coating that resists dissolution in gastric acid (pH < 4) but dissolves at pH 5.5 or higher, ensuring targeted release in the duodenum or distal small intestine. The coating is composed of cellulose acetate phthalate, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate, or methacrylic acid copolymers, and is applied in a uniform layer to protect the beads from premature degradation.

- state bead non-absorbability

The beads are non-absorbable and do not permeate the intestinal epithelium. They remain confined to the gastrointestinal lumen throughout transit and are excreted intact in the feces. This property ensures that the therapeutic agent does not enter systemic circulation, eliminating the risk of off-target effects or systemic toxicity.

- describe HMGB1 level reduction

The reduction of HMGB1 levels is quantifiable in stool, serum, and colonic tissue following bead administration. In animal models, treatment results in a significant decrease in fecal HMGB1 concentration, a reduction in serum HMGB1 levels, and diminished expression of downstream inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and IL-1β in colonic tissue. The reduction is dose-dependent and sustained with repeated administration.

- specify bead diameter

The diameter of the beads is maintained between 45 and 165 micrometers, with a preferred average diameter of 90 micrometers. This range ensures optimal dispersion within intestinal contents, prevents rapid transit through the colon, and maximizes surface area for HMGB1 binding while remaining large enough to avoid absorption or passage through epithelial tight junctions.

- describe HMGB1 binding agents

The HMGB1 binding agents are nucleic acid sequences selected for their high-affinity interaction with HMGB1, including linear sequences S1 and S2, a four-way junction sequence S3, and a kinked duplex sequence S4. These sequences are derived from previously characterized HMGB1-binding motifs and are synthesized with modifications to enhance stability and binding kinetics.

- provide nucleic acid sequence options

The nucleic acid sequences include, but are not limited to, the following: S1 (5′-GCGCGCGCGCGC-3′), S2 (5′-CGCGCGCGCGCG-3′), S3 (5′-GCGCGCGCGCGCGCGCGCGCGC-3′ forming a four-way junction), and S4 (5′-GCGCGCGCGCGCGCGCGCGCGCGCGC-3′ forming a kinked duplex). These sequences are designed to maximize contact with HMGB1’s DNA-binding domains.

- describe nucleic acid backbone options

The nucleic acid backbones are either phosphorothioate or phosphodiester. Phosphorothioate linkages are incorporated at terminal positions or throughout the sequence to confer resistance to nuclease degradation, particularly in the fecal environment where DNase activity is elevated. Phosphodiester backbones are used in sequences where enhanced binding kinetics are prioritized over stability.

- state agent attachment methods

The nucleic acid sequences are attached to the bead surface via covalent bonding using amino-linker chemistry. A C6 amino linker is conjugated to the 5′ end of the oligonucleotide, enabling coupling to activated carboxyl groups on the bead matrix through EDC/NHS chemistry. Alternative methods include thiol-maleimide coupling or streptavidin-biotin conjugation for modular assembly.

### EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

- introduce DNA conjugated to beads

DNA oligonucleotides were covalently conjugated to CNBr-activated sepharose beads to generate stable, non-leaching HMGB1-binding agents. The conjugation efficiency was confirmed by spectrophotometric measurement of DNA remaining in solution prior to and after coupling, with immobilization rates exceeding 90% for all tested sequences.

- describe binding of HMGB1 to DNA beads

Binding of recombinant HMGB1 to DNA-conjugated beads was demonstrated through western blot analysis, ELISA, and surface plasmon resonance. Beads coated with S1, S2, and S3 sequences bound HMGB1 with high affinity, capturing over 90% of added protein at physiological concentrations, while control beads without DNA showed negligible binding.

- list materials used

Materials included CNBr-activated sepharose 4 fast flow resin, synthetic oligonucleotides from Genemed Synthesis, Inc., recombinant HMGB1 protein, lipopolysaccharide-free reagents, and analytical tools including BIAcore T200, ELISA kits, and western blotting systems.

- describe cell culture methods

RAW 264.7 murine macrophage cells and Caco-2 human colorectal adenocarcinoma cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with fetal bovine serum and antibiotics. Cells were stimulated with LPS to induce HMGB1 release, and supernatants were used to test bead efficacy in vitro.

- detail DNA-bead generation

DNA oligonucleotides were dissolved in coupling buffer and incubated with washed sepharose beads for 18 hours at room temperature with rotation. Unbound DNA was removed by extensive washing, and bead concentration was standardized by optical density at 260 nm.

- describe preparation of DNA-beads

Prepared beads were suspended in PBS to form a 50% slurry, sterilized by filtration, and stored at 4°C until use. Each batch was tested for HMGB1 binding capacity and absence of endotoxin contamination.

- calculate binding ratio of HMGB1 to DNA beads

The molar binding ratio of HMGB1 to DNA was determined by quantifying the amount of HMGB1 bound per microliter of bead slurry and comparing it to the known concentration of immobilized DNA. Ratios ranged from 6:1 to 12:1 depending on the nucleic acid structure.

- describe FAM-labeled B2 DNA

FAM-labeled S2 oligonucleotides were synthesized with a terminal fluorophore and conjugated to beads. Fluorescence intensity was measured before and after incubation with fecal extracts to confirm retention of DNA on beads and absence of degradation.

- detail ethidium bromide staining of DNA beads

Ethidium bromide staining confirmed the presence of immobilized DNA on bead surfaces under fluorescence microscopy, with no detectable release of DNA into solution under physiological conditions.

- describe concentration-dependent binding of HMGB1 and DNA beads

Increasing concentrations of HMGB1 were incubated with fixed volumes of DNA beads, and bound protein was quantified by western blot. Saturation of binding capacity was observed at 2 µg HMGB1 per 5 µl bead slurry.

- detail HMGB1 protein preparation

Recombinant HMGB1 was expressed in E. coli and purified using affinity chromatography. Endotoxin was removed using Triton X-114 phase separation, and residual LPS was verified to be below 10 pg/mg by limulus amebocyte lysate assay.

- describe removal of LPS

LPS removal was confirmed through multiple rounds of Triton X-114 extraction and dialysis, followed by quantitative endotoxin testing to ensure that observed biological effects were attributable to HMGB1 and not contaminating endotoxin.

- detail cytokine measurements

TNF-alpha and IL-6 levels in cell culture supernatants were measured using commercial ELISA kits according to manufacturer instructions, with detection limits of 5 pg/ml.

- describe HMGB1 measurement

HMGB1 levels in serum, feces, and tissue homogenates were quantified using a commercial ELISA kit and confirmed by western blot with specific anti-HMGB1 antibodies.

- detail surface plasmon resonance analysis

Surface plasmon resonance was performed using a BIAcore T200 instrument, with HMGB1 immobilized on a CM5 sensor chip and DNA oligonucleotides flowed as analytes. Binding kinetics were analyzed using BIAeval software to determine association and dissociation constants.

- describe binding of DNA-beads with HMGB1 in vitro

In vitro binding assays were conducted by incubating DNA beads with recombinant HMGB1 in PBS for 1 hour at room temperature, followed by centrifugation and analysis of supernatant and bead fractions.

- detail depletion and saturation studies

Depletion studies involved incubating fixed amounts of beads with increasing HMGB1 concentrations, while saturation studies used fixed HMGB1 concentrations with increasing bead volumes to determine maximum binding capacity.

- describe time course of DNA-beads and HMGB1 binding

Time course experiments were performed by incubating beads with HMGB1 for intervals ranging from 0 to 16 hours, with samples taken at each time point and analyzed for bound protein.

- detail stability study

Stability was assessed by incubating beads in acidic buffer (pH 1–2), fecal extract, or with DNase I, followed by measurement of HMGB1 binding capacity to confirm structural and functional integrity.

- describe cytotoxicity of DNA beads

Cytotoxicity was evaluated using LDH release assays in Caco-2 and Hela cell lines exposed to increasing concentrations of beads for up to 72 hours. No significant increase in LDH was observed compared to untreated controls.

- detail removal of HMGB1 by DNA-beads in vitro

DNA beads were incubated with LPS-stimulated RAW 264.7 cell supernatants, and HMGB1 levels in supernatant and bead fractions were quantified by western blot and ELISA.

- describe animal experiments

Female BALB/c and IL-10 knockout mice were used to model acute and chronic colitis. Beads were administered orally daily for up to six weeks, and disease parameters including body weight, colon length, histology, and cytokine levels were monitored.

- detail ex vivo removal of HMGB1 from septic mice

Serum from cecal ligation and puncture-induced septic mice was incubated with DNA beads, and HMGB1 levels were measured before and after incubation to assess ex vivo binding capacity.

- describe removal of HMGB1 from intestinal tissue and stool

Colonic tissue explants and fecal suspensions from colitic mice were incubated with DNA beads, and HMGB1 bound to beads was detected by western blot and ELISA.

- detail treatment with anti-HMGB1 antibodies in DSS-induced colitis

Mice with DSS-induced colitis were treated with neutralizing anti-HMGB1 antibodies via intraperitoneal injection as a positive control to compare efficacy with oral bead therapy.

- describe treatment with DNA beads in colitis mice

Mice with DSS-induced or IL-10 knockout colitis received daily oral doses of DNA beads or control beads, and clinical and histological outcomes were assessed at study endpoint.

- detail monitoring of body weight

Body weight was recorded daily for all mice, and percent weight change was calculated relative to baseline. Significant weight stabilization was observed in bead-treated groups.

- describe harvesting of blood, feces, and colon tissues

At euthanasia, blood was collected by cardiac puncture, feces were collected from the colon lumen, and full-length colons were excised, weighed, and processed for histology and molecular analysis.

- detail analysis of HMGB1 levels

HMGB1 concentrations in serum, feces, and colon homogenates were quantified by ELISA and normalized to total protein content.

- conclude therapeutic potential of DNA-conjugated beads

The data demonstrate that DNA-conjugated beads effectively reduce HMGB1 levels in the gastrointestinal tract and ameliorate disease severity in multiple models of colitis, establishing their therapeutic potential.

- describe histology protocol

Colon tissues were fixed in 10% formalin, embedded in paraffin, sectioned at 5 µm, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Slides were scored in a blinded manner using a validated histological scoring system.

- detail colitis scoring system

The scoring system evaluated inflammation severity (0–3), inflammation extent (0–3), crypt damage (0–4), and percentage of involvement (0–4), with a maximum score of 14.

- explain quantitative PCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted from colon tissue, reverse transcribed, and amplified using SYBR Green-based qPCR with primers specific for IL-6 and IL-1β. Expression was normalized to HPRT.

- outline statistical analysis

Data were expressed as mean ± SEM. Differences between groups were analyzed using Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA with post-hoc testing. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

- introduce DNA conjugated beads

DNA-conjugated beads were generated using four distinct oligonucleotide sequences with known HMGB1 affinity, each covalently linked to sepharose beads to form B1, B2, B3, and B4 variants.

- describe DNA constructs

The DNA constructs included linear sequences S1 and S2, a four-way junction S3, and a kinked duplex S4, each selected for high binding affinity and minimal immunogenicity.

- detail DNA-bead conjugation

Conjugation was performed using carbodiimide chemistry with a C6 amino linker attached to the 5′ end of each oligonucleotide, enabling stable attachment to activated sepharose.

- test DNA-bead binding to HMGB1

Binding was confirmed by western blot, ELISA, and fluorescence assays, demonstrating specific and saturable interaction with HMGB1 but not with histone H3 or TNF-alpha.

- determine binding saturation

Saturation was achieved at 2 µg HMGB1 per 5 µl bead slurry, indicating a defined binding capacity suitable for therapeutic dosing.

- calculate binding ratio

The binding ratio of HMGB1 to DNA was calculated as 6:1 for B1 and B2 beads and 12:1 for B3 beads, based on molar concentrations of immobilized DNA and bound protein.

- examine time kinetics of binding

Maximal binding occurred within 30 minutes for B1 and B2 beads and within 15 minutes for B3 beads, supporting rapid action in the intestinal lumen.

- determine binding affinity using Biacore

Surface plasmon resonance analysis revealed dissociation constants (Kd) of 5 nM for S1 and S2, and 1 nM for S3, confirming high-affinity binding.

- test suppressive effects on HMGB1-induced TNF release

DNA beads significantly suppressed HMGB1-induced TNF-alpha release from macrophages in vitro, without affecting LPS-induced responses.

- evaluate cytotoxicity of DNA beads

No cytotoxicity was observed in Caco-2 or Hela cells exposed to beads for up to 72 hours, as measured by LDH release.

- examine binding to different forms of HMGB1

Beads bound all redox forms of HMGB1, including fully reduced, disulfide, and sulfonyl isoforms, as confirmed by mass spectrometry.

- discuss results of DNA-bead binding

The results demonstrate that DNA beads bind HMGB1 with high affinity, specificity, and capacity across multiple isoforms and biological matrices.

- discuss implications of DNA-bead binding

The ability to sequester HMGB1 locally without systemic absorption offers a novel, non-immunosuppressive strategy for treating inflammatory bowel disease.

- conclude DNA-bead binding studies

Collectively, these studies validate the molecular and functional basis for using DNA-conjugated beads as HMGB1-sequestering agents.

- evaluate binding specificity of DNA beads to HMGB1

Specificity was confirmed by lack of binding to histone H3, TNF-alpha, or other unrelated proteins, and by competitive inhibition with free DNA.

- examine binding of DNA beads to HMGB1 in presence of heparin

Binding was unaffected by the presence of heparin, indicating that DNA-HMGB1 interaction is not mediated by glycosaminoglycan competition.

- examine binding of DNA beads to HMGB1 in presence of plasma

Binding remained efficient in the presence of human plasma, demonstrating resilience to protein interference.

- examine binding of DNA beads to HMGB1 in presence of acid

Binding was preserved at pH 1 and pH 2, confirming stability in gastric conditions.

- remove HMGB1 from RAW 264.7 cell supernatant stimulated with LPS

DNA beads effectively removed HMGB1 from LPS-stimulated macrophage supernatants, reducing detectable levels by over 90%.

- remove HMGB1 from mouse sepsis serum

DNA beads removed HMGB1 from serum of septic mice, confirming efficacy in a systemic inflammatory context.

- remove HMGB1 from intestinal tissue and from stool of DSS induced colitis in mice

DNA beads bound and removed HMGB1 from both colonic tissue homogenates and fecal extracts of colitic mice.

- estimate severity of colitis

Disease severity was estimated using body weight loss, colon weight, histological scoring, and cytokine levels, all of which improved significantly with bead treatment.

- bind HMGB1 from intestinal tissue and from stool of DSS induced colitis in mice

HMGB1 captured from these samples was confirmed by western blot and mass spectrometry to include disulfide and fully reduced isoforms.

- examine stability of DNA-coated sepharose beads in biological fluid and temperature

Beads retained binding capacity after 24 hours in fecal extract at 37°C and after exposure to pH 1–2, confirming suitability for oral delivery.

- administer neutralizing anti-HMGB1 antibodies to ameliorate DSS-induced colon injury and inflammation in mice

Anti-HMGB1 antibodies reduced disease severity, validating HMGB1 as a therapeutic target and providing a benchmark for bead efficacy.

- administer B2 beads to ameliorate colitis-induced inflammation in both IL-10 KO and DSS-induced colitis in mice

Oral administration of B2 beads significantly improved clinical and histological outcomes in both models, surpassing control bead groups.

- examine effects of DNA beads in modulating disease severity in DSS-induced colitis

Bead-treated mice exhibited reduced weight loss, improved colon histology, lower cytokine expression, and decreased serum HMGB1 levels.

- examine effects of DNA beads in modulating disease severity in IL-10 KO mice

In IL-10 knockout mice, chronic bead administration prevented progressive weight loss, reduced colonic inflammation, and lowered IL-6 and IL-1β mRNA expression.

- discuss advantages of DNA-based HMGB1 sequester therapy in IBD

This approach offers localized action, minimal systemic exposure, no immunogenicity, resistance to degradation, and compatibility with oral delivery.

- discuss stability of DNA beads in acidic conditions and fecal microenvironment

DNA beads maintained structural integrity and binding function in acidic pH and in the presence of fecal nucleases, due to phosphorothioate modifications.

- discuss minimal toxicity of DNA beads

No cytotoxicity was observed in epithelial cell lines or in vivo, and no adverse effects were noted in treated animals.

- discuss administration of DNA beads directly to gastrointestinal tract by oral route

Oral delivery enables direct luminal interaction with HMGB1, bypassing systemic circulation and maximizing local therapeutic effect.

- discuss non-toxicity of DNA-based beads to animals or cultured epithelial cells

Repeated dosing over six weeks in mice caused no weight loss, organ damage, or behavioral changes, confirming safety.

- discuss feasibility, effectiveness, specificity, and safety of DNA-based HMGB1 sequester therapy

The therapy is feasible due to simple oral administration, effective due to high HMGB1 sequestration, specific due to lack of binding to unrelated proteins, and safe due to non-absorbability and non-immunogenicity.

- describe custom-made oligonucleotides from Genemed Synthesis, Inc.

All oligonucleotides were synthesized by Genemed Synthesis, Inc., with sequence fidelity confirmed by mass spectrometry and HPLC purification.

- describe conjugation of amino group linker to 5′ of oligos

A C6 amino linker (NH2(CH2)6O-P(O)2) was chemically attached to the 5′ phosphate end of each oligonucleotide to enable covalent coupling to activated sepharose.

- describe synthesis of oligos with phosphorothioate or phosphodiester backbone

Oligonucleotides S1 and S2 were synthesized with full phosphorothioate backbones for nuclease resistance; S3 and S4 were synthesized with phosphodiester backbones for optimal binding kinetics.

- describe structure of C6 amino linker

The C6 amino linker consists of a six-carbon alkyl chain terminating in a primary amine, enabling stable amide bond formation with carboxyl-activated surfaces.

- describe structure of DNA in between base

The DNA sequences contain alternating purine-pyrimidine repeats that facilitate structural flexibility and optimal interaction with HMGB1’s DNA-binding domains.

- conclude DNA-based HMGB1 sequester therapy as a novel approach in IBD

This invention establishes a novel, orally administered, non-systemic, and non-immunosuppressive therapy for inflammatory bowel disease by directly targeting and removing HMGB1 from the intestinal lumen, offering a significant advancement in the treatment of chronic gastrointestinal inflammation.
