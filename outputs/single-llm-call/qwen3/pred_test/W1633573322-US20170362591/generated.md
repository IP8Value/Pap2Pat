# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to non-aggregated immunostimulatory oligonucleotide

The present invention relates to novel immunostimulatory oligonucleotides that possess potent Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9)-mediated biological activity without forming high-order aggregates in aqueous physiological solutions. Specifically, the invention encompasses synthetic oligodeoxynucleotides containing a CpG motif essential for TLR9 activation, linked to a phosphorothioate-modified polydeoxynucleotide tail that prevents self-association while preserving or enhancing interferon-alpha induction capacity. These non-aggregating oligonucleotides represent a breakthrough in the field of nucleic acid-based immunomodulators, overcoming longstanding limitations associated with the physical instability, batch-to-batch variability, and formulation challenges of traditional A/D-type CpG oligonucleotides. The invention enables the development of clinically viable, reproducible, and scalable therapeutics and vaccine adjuvants with predictable pharmacokinetics and improved safety profiles.

## BACKGROUND ART

- introduce immunostimulatory oligonucleotides

Immunostimulatory oligonucleotides are synthetic DNA sequences designed to activate the innate immune system through engagement of pattern recognition receptors, particularly Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9), which is expressed predominantly in plasmacytoid dendritic cells and B lymphocytes. These molecules mimic microbial DNA by containing unmethylated cytosine-guanine dinucleotide motifs, triggering downstream signaling cascades that result in the production of type I interferons, pro-inflammatory cytokines, and co-stimulatory molecules. Their ability to bridge innate and adaptive immunity has made them attractive candidates for use as vaccine adjuvants, cancer immunotherapeutics, and antiviral agents.

- categorize immunostimulatory oligonucleotides

Immunostimulatory oligonucleotides are broadly classified into four distinct structural and functional classes: A/D, B/K, C, and P types. Each class exhibits unique sequence architectures, backbone chemistries, and cytokine induction profiles. These classifications reflect not only differences in molecular structure but also divergent mechanisms of cellular uptake, intracellular trafficking, and TLR9 activation kinetics.

- describe A/D type ODNs

A/D-type oligonucleotides are characterized by a phosphodiester backbone and a 3′-terminal polyguanosine tract, typically a hexamer, which facilitates the formation of G-quadruplex structures. These structures promote self-assembly into higher-order nanoparticles in physiological salt conditions, leading to aggregation and precipitation. Despite their physical instability, A/D-type ODNs are the most potent inducers of interferon-alpha from plasmacytoid dendritic cells, making them highly desirable for applications requiring robust type I interferon responses.

- describe B/K type ODNs

B/K-type oligonucleotides consist entirely of phosphorothioate-modified backbones and lack extended guanine-rich sequences. They are efficient activators of B cells and induce high levels of interleukin-6 and other pro-inflammatory cytokines, but they elicit minimal interferon-alpha production. Their enhanced nuclease resistance and improved cellular uptake due to protein binding render them more stable in biological fluids, although their immunostimulatory profile is less suited for applications demanding strong antiviral or Th1-polarizing immunity.

- compare A/D and B/K type ODNs

The functional dichotomy between A/D and B/K type ODNs is profound. A/D types drive a type I interferon-dominant response critical for antiviral defense and dendritic cell maturation, whereas B/K types favor humoral immunity and macrophage activation via interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha. This divergence in cytokine signatures translates into distinct therapeutic applications: A/D types are preferred for cancer immunotherapy and viral vaccine adjuvancy, while B/K types excel in antibacterial vaccine contexts. However, the inherent instability of A/D types has historically limited their clinical translation, despite their superior immunological potency.

- describe P type ODNs

P-type oligonucleotides represent a recently identified subclass characterized by phosphorothioate backbones and two palindromic sequences that facilitate dimerization or higher-order structural conformations. These molecules induce interferon-alpha levels comparable to or exceeding those of A/D types, yet they retain the stability advantages of phosphorothioate modification. Their cytokine profile overlaps with C-type ODNs but with enhanced potency, suggesting a hybrid mechanism of action that combines structural stability with robust TLR9 activation.

- discuss INF-α induction profile

The interferon-alpha induction profile is the most distinguishing feature among immunostimulatory oligonucleotide classes. A/D types elicit sustained, high-magnitude interferon-alpha secretion from plasmacytoid dendritic cells, a property directly linked to their ability to form multimeric complexes. In contrast, B/K types induce negligible interferon-alpha, while C and P types produce intermediate levels with dose-dependent saturation or suppression at higher concentrations. The magnitude and kinetics of interferon-alpha production are critical determinants of therapeutic efficacy, particularly in oncology and infectious disease contexts.

- discuss differences in vitro profiles

In vitro studies reveal that A/D-type ODNs induce a transcriptional signature enriched for interferon-stimulated genes, while B/K types upregulate genes associated with bacterial defense and neutrophil recruitment. C-type ODNs exhibit a mixed profile, activating both interferon and interleukin pathways. These differences are not merely quantitative but reflect fundamentally distinct signaling dynamics, including the timing of TLR9 endosomal trafficking and the recruitment of adaptor proteins such as MyD88 and IRF7.

- discuss differences in vivo profiles

In vivo, these in vitro distinctions manifest as divergent immune outcomes. A/D-type ODNs enhance cytotoxic T-cell responses and protective immunity in viral and intracellular pathogen models, whereas B/K types augment antibody titers and are superior in extracellular bacterial vaccine settings. The clinical utility of A/D types has been hampered by their propensity to aggregate in saline, leading to inconsistent dosing, injection site reactions, and poor manufacturability under Good Manufacturing Practice standards.

- discuss aggregate formation

Aggregate formation in A/D-type ODNs arises from the self-assembly of guanine-rich sequences into G-quadruplexes, which further associate into micron-sized particles in physiological buffers. These aggregates are heterogeneous in size and morphology, leading to unpredictable biodistribution, rapid clearance, and potential immunogenicity. The formation of such structures has been erroneously regarded as a necessary condition for interferon-alpha induction, despite emerging evidence suggesting that cellular uptake mechanisms may compensate for structural absence.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Solution to Problem

- introduce problem of aggregate formation

The clinical development of A/D-type immunostimulatory oligonucleotides has been impeded by their tendency to form insoluble, heterogeneous aggregates in aqueous solutions, particularly in saline or phosphate-buffered media. These aggregates result in poor solubility, inconsistent dosing, formulation instability, and unpredictable pharmacokinetics, rendering them unsuitable for scalable manufacturing and reliable clinical administration. The prevailing assumption that aggregation is indispensable for interferon-alpha induction has discouraged alternative structural designs, despite the clear need for stable, reproducible, and injectable formulations.

- describe modification of D35

The invention addresses this problem through a novel structural modification of the prototypical A/D-type oligonucleotide D35, wherein a 40-mer phosphorothioate polydeoxyadenylic acid tail is appended to its 3′ terminus. This modification eliminates the formation of visible aggregates and reduces hydrodynamic particle size to below 20 nanometers in physiological buffers, while preserving the core CpG motif responsible for TLR9 recognition and interferon-alpha induction.

- examine physical and biological properties

The modified oligonucleotides exhibit exceptional physical stability in saline, remaining fully soluble upon lyophilization and reconstitution without gelation or precipitation. Biological evaluation demonstrates that these constructs retain or exceed the interferon-alpha-inducing capacity of the original D35, while simultaneously reducing the variability in cytokine responses across human donor samples. The physical and biological properties are decoupled from guanine-mediated aggregation, establishing a new paradigm for A/D-type ODN design.

- describe addition of phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide

The addition of a phosphorothioate-modified polydeoxynucleotide tail, preferably composed of deoxyadenylic acid residues, confers resistance to nuclease degradation and enhances cellular uptake through protein-mediated endocytosis pathways. This tail does not participate in TLR9 binding but serves as a delivery enhancer, facilitating efficient internalization into endosomal compartments where TLR9 signaling is initiated.

- describe prevention of aggregate formation

The phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail effectively prevents the formation of G-quadruplexes and subsequent higher-order aggregates by sterically and electrostatically inhibiting the self-association of guanine-rich sequences. Even when the native G-hexamer is retained, the presence of the tail suppresses particle formation, resulting in monodisperse, homogeneous solutions suitable for parenteral administration.

- describe maintenance of IFN-α induction

Despite the absence of aggregated structures, interferon-alpha induction remains robust and dose-proportional, indicating that aggregation is not a prerequisite for TLR9 activation. The core CpG sequence, in conjunction with the phosphorothioate tail, sustains signaling through the MyD88-IRF7 pathway, resulting in interferon-alpha production equivalent to or greater than that of unmodified D35.

- describe agent for delivery of immunostimulatory oligonucleotide

The invention provides an agent for the delivery of immunostimulatory oligonucleotides comprising a CpG-containing core sequence covalently linked to a phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail. This agent functions as a self-contained, non-aggregating immunostimulatory molecule capable of systemic administration without the need for external carriers or lipid formulations.

- describe nucleic acid with phosphorothioated nucleotide

The nucleic acid of the invention contains at least one phosphorothioate linkage within the polydeoxynucleotide tail, with a preferred embodiment comprising full phosphorothioation of the tail backbone. The phosphorothioate modification is essential for maintaining biological activity, as phosphodiester analogues of the tail fail to induce cytokine responses.

- describe prevention of high order aggregate formation

The invention prevents the formation of high-order aggregates by substituting the structural role of guanine quadruplexes with a phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail that promotes solubility and cellular internalization without inducing self-association. This structural substitution eliminates the polymorphic heterogeneity characteristic of traditional A/D-type ODNs.

- describe phosphorothioated nucleotide content

The phosphorothioated nucleotide content of the tail is maintained at a minimum of 50% modification, with optimal activity observed at 100% phosphorothioation. Reduction below this threshold results in a sharp decline in interferon-alpha and interleukin-6 induction, indicating that backbone charge and nuclease resistance are critical for function.

- describe length of nucleic acid

The phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail has a length of at least 20 nucleotides, with preferred embodiments ranging from 30 to 80 nucleotides. A tail of 40 nucleotides provides the optimal balance between solubility enhancement, cellular uptake efficiency, and synthetic feasibility.

- describe agent for delivery bound to moiety

The agent for delivery may be further conjugated to a moiety with biological activity, such as a targeting ligand, peptide, or antibody fragment, to direct the oligonucleotide to specific cell types or tissues. The moiety is covalently linked to the 5′ or 3′ terminus of the oligonucleotide without disrupting the core CpG motif or phosphorothioate tail.

- describe moiety with biological activity

The moiety with biological activity may be selected from the group consisting of peptides that bind scavenger receptors, antibodies that target dendritic cell surface markers, or ligands for endocytic receptors such as DEC-205. Such conjugates enhance tissue-specific delivery and reduce off-target effects.

- describe agent for immunostimulation

The agent for immunostimulation comprises the non-aggregating oligonucleotide as a single molecular entity, capable of inducing interferon-alpha and interleukin-6 in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells without requiring external delivery vehicles.

- describe core moiety with biological activity

The core moiety with biological activity consists of a 12-base palindromic sequence containing an unmethylated CpG dinucleotide, derived from the D35 oligonucleotide. This core is sufficient for TLR9 activation when paired with the phosphorothioate tail, even in the absence of flanking sequences or G-hexamers.

- describe biological activity

The biological activity of the invention includes the induction of interferon-alpha, interleukin-6, and other type I interferon-stimulated genes in human immune cells, leading to dendritic cell maturation, B-cell proliferation, and enhanced antigen-specific antibody responses in vivo.

- describe core moiety

The core moiety is defined as the minimal sequence required for TLR9 activation, comprising the CpG motif and its immediate flanking nucleotides, which together form a structural unit recognized by the receptor’s binding pocket.

- describe vehicle for cell introduction

The vehicle for cell introduction is the oligonucleotide itself, which, due to its phosphorothioate tail, enters cells via receptor-mediated endocytosis without requiring encapsulation in liposomes, nanoparticles, or other delivery systems.

- describe oligonucleotide with phosphorothioated nucleotide

The oligonucleotide of the invention contains a phosphorothioated nucleotide in the tail region, with the phosphorothioate linkages distributed along the entire length of the tail to maximize stability and cellular uptake efficiency.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

- describe advantageous effects of invention

The invention provides a class of immunostimulatory oligonucleotides that overcome the fundamental limitations of prior art A/D-type molecules by eliminating aggregate formation while preserving or enhancing interferon-alpha induction. These molecules exhibit superior solubility in saline, enabling direct reconstitution from lyophilized powder without gelation or precipitation. They demonstrate enhanced batch-to-batch consistency, simplified manufacturing, and improved pharmacokinetic profiles. In vivo, they function as superior vaccine adjuvants compared to both original D35 and K3-type ODNs, eliciting more consistent and robust antibody responses in non-human primates. The invention enables the clinical translation of A/D-type immunostimulators for use in cancer immunotherapy, antiviral vaccines, and autoimmune modulation, with a safety profile suitable for repeated dosing.

- explain terminology usage

The term “phosphorothioate” refers to a modified phosphate linkage in which one non-bridging oxygen atom is replaced by sulfur, conferring resistance to nucleases and enhancing protein binding. The term “CpG motif” denotes an unmethylated cytosine-guanine dinucleotide sequence flanked by specific nucleotides that confer TLR9 agonist activity. The term “non-aggregating” describes oligonucleotides that remain monodisperse in physiological saline at concentrations up to 1 mg/mL without forming detectable particles larger than 100 nm.

- introduce immunostimulatory oligonucleotide

An immunostimulatory oligonucleotide of the invention comprises a core sequence containing a CpG motif and a 3′-terminal phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail of at least 20 nucleotides in length. The core sequence is derived from the D35 oligonucleotide and is sufficient to activate TLR9 when presented in the context of the phosphorothioate tail.

- define CpG oligonucleotide

A CpG oligonucleotide is a synthetic DNA molecule containing at least one unmethylated CpG dinucleotide in a sequence context that permits recognition by Toll-like receptor 9. The invention encompasses CpG oligonucleotides that do not require G-quadruplex formation for biological activity.

- explain CpG motif

The CpG motif is defined as a dinucleotide sequence of cytosine followed by guanine, wherein the cytosine is not methylated, and the flanking nucleotides are selected to optimize TLR9 binding affinity. In the invention, the CpG motif is embedded within a 12-nucleotide palindromic sequence that retains full activity even when truncated from its native flanking regions.

- describe polydeoxyadenylic acid

Polydeoxyadenylic acid refers to a homopolymer chain of deoxyadenosine residues, which may be modified with phosphorothioate linkages throughout its backbone. In the invention, a 40-mer polydeoxyadenylic acid tail is preferred for optimal solubility and immunostimulatory activity.

- summarize CpG oligonucleotide properties

CpG oligonucleotides of the invention are characterized by their ability to induce interferon-alpha and interleukin-6 without forming aggregates, their stability in saline, their capacity for lyophilization and direct reconstitution, and their enhanced adjuvanticity in vivo compared to conventional A/D-type ODNs.

- describe Toll-like receptor 9 activation

Activation of Toll-like receptor 9 occurs upon internalization of the oligonucleotide into endosomal compartments, where the CpG motif binds to the receptor’s leucine-rich repeat domain. The phosphorothioate tail facilitates endocytosis and endosomal trafficking, ensuring efficient receptor engagement.

- explain adjuvant properties

The adjuvant properties of the invention arise from its ability to activate dendritic cells, enhance antigen presentation, and promote T helper 1 polarization and germinal center formation, leading to robust and sustained antigen-specific antibody responses.

- introduce four types of CpG ODNs

The four types of CpG ODNs—A/D, B/K, C, and P—are distinguished by backbone chemistry, sequence architecture, and cytokine profile. The invention introduces a novel subclass of A/D-type ODNs that function independently of G-quadruplex formation.

- describe A/D type CpG ODNs

A/D-type CpG ODNs of the invention contain a CpG motif and a phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail, replacing the traditional G-hexamer. These molecules induce high interferon-alpha without aggregation.

- explain B/K type CpG ODNs

B/K-type CpG ODNs are phosphorothioate-modified molecules that induce interleukin-6 but minimal interferon-alpha. The invention does not modify B/K-type ODNs but provides a structural alternative that overcomes the limitations of A/D types.

- describe C type CpG ODNs

C-type CpG ODNs induce both interferon-alpha and interleukin-6 but with lower interferon-alpha magnitude than A/D types. The invention provides a superior alternative with higher interferon-alpha induction and improved physical stability.

- explain P type CpG ODNs

P-type CpG ODNs contain palindromic sequences that form dimers and induce interferon-alpha, but their activity is dose-suppressive. The invention provides a non-suppressive, dose-proportional alternative.

- introduce modified ODNs

Modified ODNs of the invention are characterized by the absence of G-quadruplex-forming sequences and the presence of a phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail. These modifications confer solubility, stability, and consistent biological activity.

- describe physical properties of modified ODNs

- explain immunostimulatory activity of modified ODNs

The immunostimulatory activity of the modified ODNs is equivalent to or greater than that of unmodified D35, as measured by interferon-alpha and interleukin-6 production in human PBMCs. Activity is dependent on the CpG motif and the phosphorothioate tail, but not on G-hexamer sequences.

Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from healthy donors and stimulated with various oligonucleotides to assess cytokine induction and physical stability.

- outline CpG ODN stimulation with or without DOTAP

ODNs were added to PBMCs at concentrations ranging from 0.01 to 10 μM, with or without DOTAP, and incubated for 24 hours.

- detail measurement of cytokines by ELISA

Supernatants were collected and analyzed for interferon-alpha and interleukin-6 using commercial ELISA kits according to manufacturer protocols.

- describe dynamic light scattering (DLS)

Samples were analyzed by DLS using a Wyatt DynaPro PlateReader II to determine hydrodynamic radius and polydispersity index.

- outline transmission electron microscopy (TEM)

Samples were deposited on carbon-coated grids, stained with uranyl acetate, and imaged at ×10,000 magnification.

- describe complexation of CpG ODN and schizophyllan (SPG)

ODNs were mixed with alkaline-denatured SPG at a 0.27 molar ratio and incubated overnight at 4°C.

- detail cynomolgus monkey immunization

Cynomolgus monkeys were immunized subcutaneously with influenza vaccine and adjuvant, and serum antibody titers were measured by ELISA at weeks 4 and 8.

- outline statistical analysis
