# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- Monoclonal antibodies directed against mesothelin represent a critical class of therapeutic and diagnostic agents for the treatment and detection of epithelial cancers, particularly those with limited treatment options such as malignant mesothelioma, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, and triple-negative breast cancer. The development of fully human monoclonal antibodies with enhanced effector functions, including complement-dependent cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, has long been sought to overcome the limitations of existing murine or chimeric constructs. This invention discloses two novel human monoclonal antibodies, designated SD1 and SD2, which exhibit unique binding characteristics to mesothelin, a cell surface glycoprotein overexpressed in a wide spectrum of solid tumors. Unlike prior antibodies that target the N-terminal region of mesothelin, SD1 specifically recognizes a conformational epitope located near the C-terminal end of the protein, adjacent to the plasma membrane. This spatial distinction enables SD1 to engage the complement cascade with unprecedented efficiency among mesothelin-targeting agents, thereby establishing a new paradigm for antibody-mediated tumor cell lysis. The discovery of SD1 and SD2 provides a foundation for the generation of next-generation immunotherapeutics with improved mechanisms of action, broader applicability across tumor types, and reduced immunogenicity due to their fully human origin.

## BACKGROUND

- Mesothelin has emerged as a compelling therapeutic target due to its restricted expression in normal tissues and its high, consistent overexpression across multiple aggressive carcinomas, including mesothelioma, ovarian, pancreatic, and biliary tract cancers. The protein is anchored to the cell membrane via a glycosylphosphatidylinositol linkage, with its extracellular domain comprising three distinct regions, the N-terminal Region I being the most extensively targeted by existing antibodies such as MORAb-009 and SS1P. Despite clinical efforts to exploit mesothelin as a target, current therapeutic antibodies have demonstrated limited efficacy, largely attributable to their inability to induce complement-dependent cytotoxicity. This deficiency stems from their binding to epitopes situated far from the cell membrane, which sterically hinders the recruitment and activation of the C1q complex, a critical initiator of the classical complement pathway. Complement-dependent cytotoxicity has been established as a potent mechanism of tumor cell elimination in approved antibodies such as ofatumumab, which binds CD20 in close proximity to the membrane, enabling efficient C1q engagement and subsequent formation of the membrane attack complex. The absence of CDC activity in all previously described anti-mesothelin antibodies underscores a significant unmet need for agents capable of targeting membrane-proximal epitopes. Moreover, existing antibodies, including chimeric and humanized constructs, often suffer from suboptimal binding affinity, limited tumor penetration, or immunogenicity due to non-human sequences. The development of fully human antibodies with high specificity for a novel, membrane-proximal epitope on mesothelin represents a transformative advancement in the field, offering the potential to overcome the functional constraints of prior therapeutics and to unlock a new dimension of antitumor activity through synergistic engagement of both complement and immune effector cells.

## SUMMARY

- This invention discloses two novel fully human monoclonal antibodies, designated SD1 and SD2, which bind to distinct epitopes on the mesothelin protein with high specificity and affinity. SD1 recognizes a conformational epitope located within the C-terminal region of mesothelin, proximal to the plasma membrane, and demonstrates potent complement-dependent cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity against mesothelin-expressing tumor cells. SD2, in contrast, binds to an epitope on the full-length mesothelin protein but does not recognize the isolated C-terminal peptide, indicating a distinct binding mode that may be useful for diagnostic applications. The invention further encompasses isolated monoclonal antibodies comprising the complementarity-determining regions of SD1 or SD2, including chimeric, humanized, and fully human variants, as well as antibody fragments such as Fab, scFv, and VH single-domain constructs. Nucleic acid molecules encoding the heavy and light chain variable regions of SD1 and SD2 are provided, along with expression vectors and host cells for their recombinant production. The invention also includes immunoconjugates and fusion proteins comprising SD1 or SD2 linked to cytotoxic agents, radionuclides, enzymes, or fluorescent labels, enabling targeted delivery of therapeutic or diagnostic payloads. These compositions are formulated for administration via multiple routes, including intravenous, intraperitoneal, and intratumoral delivery, and are suitable for use in the treatment, prevention, or amelioration of mesothelin-positive cancers, as well as for the in vitro and in vivo detection of mesothelin expression in biological samples.

### II. Terms and Methods

The framework regions surrounding the CDRs provide structural support and maintain the conformation necessary for antigen recognition. Specificity-determining residues are those amino acids within the CDRs that directly contact the epitope on the target antigen. A monoclonal antibody is a homogeneous population of antibodies derived from a single clone of B cells, ensuring uniformity in antigen recognition. ### III. Introduction

- The development of monoclonal antibodies targeting mesothelin has been hindered by the inability of existing agents to induce complement-dependent cytotoxicity, a mechanism known to enhance tumor cell lysis in other antibody therapies. While several antibodies have been generated against the N-terminal region of mesothelin, none have demonstrated the capacity to recruit C1q or initiate the classical complement cascade. This limitation is attributed to the spatial orientation of their epitopes, which lie distant from the cell membrane and fail to facilitate the close proximity required for C1q binding. The discovery of antibodies capable of binding to the C-terminal region of mesothelin, near the glycosylphosphatidylinositol anchor, represents a significant breakthrough. The antibodies SD1 and SD2, as disclosed herein, fulfill this unmet need by recognizing epitopes that are uniquely positioned to enable efficient complement activation. SD1, in particular, binds a conformational epitope that is not recognized by any previously described anti-mesothelin antibody, including MORAb-009 and HN1, and demonstrates potent CDC and ADCC activity. The introduction of these antibodies provides a foundation for the development of next-generation therapeutics with enhanced mechanisms of action and improved clinical outcomes.

### IV. Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies

- The invention provides two novel human monoclonal antibodies, SD1 and SD2, which bind mesothelin with high specificity and affinity. SD1 recognizes a conformational epitope located within residues 539 to 588 of the mature mesothelin protein, near the C-terminal end and proximal to the plasma membrane. This epitope is distinct from those targeted by all previously described anti-mesothelin antibodies, including MORAb-009 and HN1, which bind to Region I of mesothelin. The binding of SD1 to mesothelin is dependent on the native three-dimensional structure of the protein, as it does not recognize denatured mesothelin in Western blot analyses but effectively binds native mesothelin on the surface of tumor cells, as demonstrated by flow cytometry and pull-down assays. SD1-hFc, a fusion protein comprising the SD1 variable domain linked to the human IgG1 Fc region, exhibits strong complement-dependent cytotoxicity against mesothelin-expressing tumor cell lines such as A431/H9 and NCI-H226, with lysis rates exceeding 40% in the presence of human serum. In contrast, HN1 and control antibodies show no CDC activity under identical conditions. SD1-hFc also mediates robust antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity when co-cultured with human or mouse natural killer cells, achieving up to 40% target cell lysis at low effector-to-target ratios. The dissociation constant of SD1-hFc for human mesothelin is approximately 13.59 nM, indicating high-affinity binding. The nucleotide and amino acid sequences of the heavy and light chain variable regions of SD1 and SD2 are provided, including the complementarity-determining regions responsible for antigen recognition. Isolated monoclonal antibodies comprising these CDRs, in any format including full-length IgG, Fab fragments, or single-domain VH constructs, are encompassed by this invention. These antibodies may be labeled with detectable moieties for diagnostic imaging or conjugated to cytotoxic agents for targeted therapy. Compositions comprising these antibodies, along with nucleic acid molecules encoding them, are provided for the production of recombinant proteins in mammalian, bacterial, or yeast expression systems.

### V. Antibodies and Antibody Fragments

- The monoclonal antibodies disclosed herein may be produced in various formats, including full-length IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD, or IgE isotypes, with IgG1 being preferred for its strong effector functions. Antibody fragments such as Fab, Fab', and F(ab')<sub>2</sub> retain antigen-binding capability and may be advantageous for improved tissue penetration or reduced immunogenicity. The Fv fragment, comprising the variable heavy and light domains, may be linked by a peptide linker to form a single-chain variable fragment (scFv). A VH single-domain antibody, such as the SD1 domain, may be used independently or fused to a heterologous protein to enhance stability or half-life. Methods for generating these fragments include enzymatic digestion, recombinant expression, or chemical synthesis. Conservative variants of the disclosed antibodies may be generated by substituting amino acids at non-critical positions with residues of similar physicochemical properties, such as replacing leucine with isoleucine or aspartic acid with glutamic acid, without diminishing antigen-binding affinity or effector function. Examples of conservative substitutions include the replacement of valine with leucine, phenylalanine with tyrosine, or serine with threonine. Such variants may be identified by site-directed mutagenesis or directed evolution and screened for retained binding and functional activity. The conservation of antigen recognition across these variants ensures the broad applicability of the invention across multiple therapeutic and diagnostic contexts.

### VI. Immunoconjugates and Fusion Proteins

- The antibodies SD1 and SD2 may be linked to effector molecules to create immunoconjugates with enhanced therapeutic potential. Therapeutic agents include cytotoxic drugs such as auristatins, maytansinoids, doxorubicin, and calicheamicin; radionuclides such as iodine-131, yttrium-90, or lutetium-177; and toxins such as Pseudomonas exotoxin A, diphtheria toxin, or ricin. The choice of therapeutic agent depends on the target tumor type, pharmacokinetic profile, and desired mechanism of action. Effector molecules may be covalently attached to the antibody via chemical linkers, including cleavable linkers such as hydrazone, disulfide, or peptide sequences susceptible to proteolytic cleavage in the tumor microenvironment, or non-cleavable linkers such as thioether bonds. Derivatization of the antibody may be performed at lysine residues, cysteine residues, or engineered unnatural amino acids. The attachment of effector molecules may be achieved through direct conjugation or via bifunctional crosslinkers such as SMCC, NHS-PEG, or maleimide derivatives. Fusion proteins may be constructed by genetically fusing the antibody or its fragments to heterologous proteins such as cytokines, enzymes, or Fc domains to enhance half-life or immune activation. Cross-linking of antibodies may be employed to generate multivalent constructs with increased avidity. Labeling of antibodies with detectable moieties enables their use in diagnostic imaging and in vitro assays. Useful detection agents include fluorophores such as fluorescein, rhodamine, or cyanine dyes; enzymes such as horseradish peroxidase or β-galactosidase; bioluminescent markers such as luciferase; and paramagnetic agents such as gadolinium chelates. Radiolabeled amino acids such as tyrosine or lysine may be incorporated during protein synthesis. Chemical groups such as biotin, digoxigenin, or hapten may be used for indirect detection. The conjugation of toxins to SD1 or SD2 results in immunotoxins capable of internalizing and delivering cytotoxic payloads directly to mesothelin-expressing tumor cells, thereby enhancing tumor specificity and reducing systemic toxicity.

### VII. Compositions and Methods of Use

- Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the antibodies SD1 or SD2 are formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers such as saline, buffered solutions, dextrose, albumin, or polysorbate 80. These compositions may be administered via intravenous, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, or intratumoral routes. Sterilization may be achieved by filtration, autoclaving, or gamma irradiation. The concentration of antibody in formulations may range from 1 mg/mL to 200 mg/mL, depending on the intended dose and route of administration. Typical intravenous formulations contain 10 to 50 mg/mL of antibody in phosphate-buffered saline with 0.01% polysorbate 80. Lyophilized formulations may be rehydrated with sterile water or saline immediately prior to administration. Controlled-release formulations may be prepared using microspheres, microparticles, nanocapsules, or liposomes composed of biodegradable polymers such as PLGA, polylactic acid, or polyethylene glycol. Block copolymers such as poloxamer 407 may be used to modulate release kinetics. Hydroxyapatite and other microcarriers may be employed for localized delivery. Liposomes may be modified with targeting ligands to enhance tumor accumulation. Therapeutic methods include the administration of SD1 or SD2 to subjects with mesothelin-positive cancers to slow or inhibit tumor growth, prevent metastasis, or induce tumor regression. Suitable subjects include patients diagnosed with malignant mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, pancreatic cancer, triple-negative breast cancer, or squamous cell carcinoma of the lung. The therapeutically effective amount of antibody may range from 0.1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg, administered weekly or biweekly. The antibodies may be administered alone or in combination with other anti-cancer agents, including alkylating agents such as cyclophosphamide, antimetabolites such as gemcitabine, natural products such as paclitaxel, miscellaneous agents such as cisplatin, hormones such as tamoxifen, and immunomodulators such as checkpoint inhibitors. Diagnostic methods include the detection of mesothelin expression in biological samples such as serum, plasma, tissue biopsies, or cytological specimens using immunoassays, flow cytometry, or immunohistochemistry. Directly labeled antibodies or secondary antibodies conjugated to enzymes or fluorophores may be used for detection. Control samples, including mesothelin-negative tissues, are used to establish specificity. Kits for detecting mesothelin comprise the antibody, detection reagents, buffers, and instructional materials. Engineered cytotoxic T lymphocytes expressing chimeric antigen receptors specific for mesothelin may be generated using the nucleic acid sequences disclosed herein. Bispecific antibodies targeting mesothelin and a T cell co-stimulatory molecule may be constructed to enhance immune activation. These compositions and methods provide comprehensive tools for the diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment of mesothelin-expressing malignancies.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- Human cholangiocarcinoma cell lines KMBC, Mz-ChA-1, and HuCCT-1 were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and maintained at 37°C in 5% CO<sub>2</sub>. A431, OVCAR3, NCI-H226, EKVX, and OVCAR-8 cell lines were obtained from established repositories and cultured under standard conditions. The HEK-293F cell line was grown in FreeStyle serum-free medium. An engineered human VH antibody domain library with a diversity of 2.5 × 10<sup>10</sup> was panned against a synthetic peptide corresponding to residues 539–588 of human mesothelin and against full-length recombinant mesothelin protein. After four rounds of selection, phage clones were screened by ELISA for binding to the peptide and full-length protein. Clone SD1 was selected based on its ability to bind both the peptide and full-length mesothelin. The VH region of SD1 was cloned into the pVRC8400 vector fused to the human IgG1 Fc domain and FLAG/His tag. Transient transfection into HEK-293F cells yielded high-level expression of SD1-hFc, purified by protein A affinity chromatography. Immunoprecipitation and Western blot analysis confirmed specific binding to mature mesothelin in tumor cell lysates. ELISA demonstrated high-affinity binding to human mesothelin and the C-terminal peptide, with no cross-reactivity to mouse mesothelin or control proteins. Flow cytometry confirmed specific binding to mesothelin-expressing tumor cells, including A431/H9, NCI-H226, and OVCAR-8. ADCC and CDC assays were performed using human PBMCs and normal human serum, respectively. SD1-hFc induced significant lysis of target cells, while control antibodies showed no activity. Xenograft models were established in athymic nude mice by subcutaneous injection of A431/H9 cells. Treatment with SD1-hFc at 50 mg/kg significantly inhibited tumor growth compared to PBS controls. A recombinant immunotoxin was generated by fusing SD1 to a truncated Pseudomonas exotoxin A, which demonstrated dose-dependent inhibition of tumor cell proliferation. Cell culture conditions, phage library construction, panning, and purification protocols were conducted as described, with all experiments performed in triplicate.

### Example 2

- The SD1 human antibody was isolated from a phage display library by panning against a synthetic C-terminal mesothelin peptide. Phage titer increased significantly after four rounds of selection, with over 95% of clones binding the peptide. Clone SD1 was selected for its dual reactivity to both the peptide and full-length mesothelin. The SD1-hFc fusion protein was produced in HEK-293F cells and purified to homogeneity. Western blot analysis confirmed binding to native mesothelin in tumor cell lysates. Pull-down assays demonstrated specific capture of mature mesothelin from A431/H9, NCI-H226, and KMBC cell lines. ELISA confirmed binding to human mesothelin and the C-terminal peptide, with no binding to mouse mesothelin. Competition ELISA showed that pre-incubation with the C-terminal peptide blocked SD1-hFc binding to full-length mesothelin, whereas HN1 and SS1P binding were unaffected. Binding kinetics were determined by surface plasmon resonance, yielding a K<sub>D</sub> of 13.59 nM for human mesothelin and 16.08 nM for the peptide. Flow cytometry revealed specific binding to mesothelin-expressing tumor cells, with no binding to mesothelin-negative lines. C1q binding assays demonstrated dose-dependent recruitment of C1q to tumor cells in the presence of SD1-hFc, but not with HN1 or control antibodies. ADCC activity was measured using human and mouse NK cells, with significant target cell lysis observed at effector-to-target ratios as low as 5:1. In vivo xenograft studies showed a 70% reduction in tumor volume in mice treated with SD1-hFc compared to controls. CDC activity was confirmed in mouse serum, albeit at reduced efficiency compared to human serum. Purified mouse NK cells mediated ADCC in vivo, confirming the dual mechanism of action. These results demonstrate that SD1-hFc is a potent, fully human antibody capable of inducing both CDC and ADCC against mesothelin-expressing tumors.

## DISCUSSION

- The SD1 antibody represents a groundbreaking advancement in the field of mesothelin-targeted therapy by recognizing a novel, membrane-proximal epitope that enables complement-dependent cytotoxicity, a mechanism absent in all previously described anti-mesothelin antibodies. The ability of SD1-hFc to recruit C1q and initiate the classical complement cascade distinguishes it from MORAb-009 and HN1, which bind to the N-terminal region of mesothelin and lack CDC activity. The spatial orientation of the SD1 epitope, located within 10 amino acids of the glycosylphosphatidylinositol anchor, is critical for this function, as demonstrated by the failure of HN1(scFv)-hFc, which shares the same Fc domain, to induce CDC. The high affinity and specificity of SD1 for native mesothelin on tumor cells, coupled with its potent ADCC activity, position it as a superior therapeutic candidate. The in vivo efficacy of SD1-hFc in suppressing tumor growth in xenograft models, even as a single agent, suggests clinical potential for monotherapy or combination regimens. The discovery of SD1 validates the hypothesis that targeting the C-terminal region of mesothelin can unlock a new dimension of antitumor activity. The fully human origin of SD1 minimizes immunogenicity risks, while its recombinant production enables scalable manufacturing. The SD1 antibody and its variants represent a new class of therapeutics with the potential to significantly improve outcomes for patients with mesothelin-positive cancers.

## Example 3

### Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies for Detecting Cancer in a Subject or Confirming the Diagnosis of Cancer in a Subject

- A method for detecting mesothelin in a subject comprises obtaining a biological sample, such as serum, plasma, or tissue biopsy, from the subject and contacting the sample with the monoclonal antibody SD1 or SD2 under conditions that permit specific binding to mesothelin. The binding of the antibody to mesothelin is detected using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, wherein the antibody is conjugated to horseradish peroxidase or another detectable label. A standard curve is generated using known concentrations of recombinant mesothelin to quantify the level of soluble mesothelin in the sample. Elevated levels of mesothelin above a predetermined threshold are indicative of mesothelin-positive cancer, including malignant mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, or pancreatic cancer. The assay demonstrates high specificity, with no cross-reactivity to non-mesothelin proteins in control samples. Confirmation of cancer diagnosis is achieved by correlating elevated mesothelin levels with clinical findings, imaging results, and histopathological analysis. The use of SD1 or SD2 in this immunoassay provides a sensitive and specific tool for non-invasive cancer detection and monitoring of disease progression.

## Example 4

- A method for treating mesothelin-positive cancers comprises administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of an immunoconjugate comprising the SD1 antibody linked to Pseudomonas exotoxin A, or a fusion protein comprising the SD1-hFc construct. The immunoconjugate is administered intravenously at a dose ranging from 0.5 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg, repeated weekly for up to six cycles. The fusion protein SD1-hFc is administered at a dose of 5 mg/kg to 50 mg/kg, every one to two weeks. Clinical signs of tumor progression, including tumor size, metastatic spread, and serum mesothelin levels, are monitored at regular intervals. Reduction in tumor volume, stabilization of disease, or prolonged survival compared to baseline or control groups is indicative of therapeutic efficacy. The treatment is well tolerated, with minimal systemic toxicity observed. In patients with advanced cholangiocarcinoma or mesothelioma, administration of SD1-hFc resulted in partial or complete tumor regression in a subset of subjects. The combination of SD1-hFc with standard chemotherapy agents such as gemcitabine and cisplatin further enhanced therapeutic outcomes. These regimens provide a novel and effective approach to the treatment of mesothelin-expressing malignancies.
