## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- A metal hydride battery is disclosed that operates using a non-aqueous electrolyte composition comprising an ionic liquid and a protic acid, enabling operation beyond the electrochemical stability window of water while maintaining high proton conductivity through a Grotthuss-type proton hopping mechanism. This battery configuration overcomes the inherent voltage limitations of conventional aqueous nickel/metal hydride systems by eliminating hydroxide ion transport and replacing it with proton conduction in a non-aqueous medium, thereby permitting the use of electrode materials with higher redox potentials and significantly enhancing energy density. The electrolyte composition is formulated to maintain ionic conductivity comparable to that of conventional alkaline electrolytes while providing superior thermal stability, non-flammability, and negligible vapor pressure, making it suitable for extended operational lifetimes under diverse environmental conditions. The battery embodiments include a metal hydride negative electrode, a nickel hydroxide-based positive electrode, a porous separator, and a sealed cell housing, all configured to function efficiently in the presence of the novel electrolyte, which enables reversible hydrogen storage and release at the metal hydride interface through proton transfer rather than hydroxide ion diffusion.

## DETAILED DISCLOSURE

- A metal hydride battery is defined by its use of a non-aqueous electrolyte system that facilitates proton transport via a hydrogen-bonded network, wherein the electrolyte comprises an ionic liquid and a protic acid, and wherein the metal hydride alloy anode undergoes reversible hydrogen absorption and desorption through proton reduction and oxidation reactions at its surface. Unlike conventional alkaline nickel/metal hydride batteries that rely on hydroxide ion mobility in aqueous potassium hydroxide solutions, this invention operates through the Grotthuss mechanism, wherein protons migrate through a dynamic network of hydrogen bonds formed between the ionic liquid cations and anions and the protic acid molecules, enabling rapid proton hopping without the need for bulk solvent movement. The absence of water eliminates the thermodynamic constraint imposed by water’s decomposition potential, allowing the battery to operate at open-circuit voltages exceeding 1.8 volts, thereby enabling the utilization of high-voltage cathode materials and achieving energy densities unattainable with aqueous electrolytes. The metal hydride anode is composed of an ABx-type alloy capable of reversible hydrogen storage, and the cathode is formed from a nickel hydroxide matrix modified to enhance stability in weakly acidic environments, ensuring long-term electrochemical performance without significant corrosion or passivation.

- The electrolyte composition consists of a mixture of an ionic liquid and a protic acid selected from the group consisting of acetic acid, phosphoric acid, methanesulfonic acid, trifluoromethanesulfonic acid, and combinations thereof, wherein the protic acid serves as a proton donor and the ionic liquid provides structural framework for proton transport. The ionic liquid is selected from protic ionic liquids or aprotic ionic liquids modified by the addition of protic acid to induce proton conductivity. The weight ratio of ionic liquid to protic acid ranges from 1:1 to 10:1, with optimal performance observed at ratios between 3:1 and 6:1, where ionic conductivity exceeds 10 mS/cm at ambient temperature. The electrolyte exhibits negligible volatility, non-flammability, and thermal stability up to 120 °C, making it suitable for applications requiring extended operational lifetimes under elevated temperatures or in safety-critical environments.

## EXAMPLES

- Electrolyte compositions are prepared by mixing anhydrous ionic liquids with anhydrous protic acids under argon atmosphere in a glovebox, wherein the mixture is stirred for 24 hours at 25 °C until homogeneous, and wherein the water content is verified to be less than 100 ppm by Karl Fischer titration.

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Acetic Acid

- Electrolyte compositions are prepared by mixing [EMIM][Ac] with glacial acetic acid in weight ratios of 1:1, 2:1, 3:1, 4:1, and 5:1, wherein the mixtures are stirred for 24 hours at 25 °C, and wherein the ionic conductivity is measured using a conductivity meter, wherein the highest conductivity of 14.3 mS/cm is observed at a 3:1 ratio of [EMIM][Ac] to acetic acid.

### Electrolyte Composition Comprising Ionic Liquid and Solvent

- An electrolyte composition is prepared by mixing [EMIM][Ac] with ethylene carbonate in a 9:1 weight ratio, wherein the mixture is stirred for 24 hours at 25 °C, and wherein the ionic conductivity is measured at 8.7 mS/cm, wherein the addition of solvent reduces viscosity but does not significantly enhance proton conductivity.

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Protic Acid

- Electrolyte compositions are prepared using [EMIM][TFSI] and acetic acid, [BMIM][Ac] and methanesulfonic acid, and [DEMA][TfO] and phosphoric acid, wherein the conductivity of each is measured, and wherein the [EMIM][TFSI]/acetic acid system exhibits the highest proton mobility and lowest polarization in electrochemical testing.

### Electrolyte Composition Containing 2 M Acetic Acid in EMIM Ac

- A ternary electrolyte composition is prepared by dissolving 2 M acetic acid in [EMIM][Ac], wherein the mixture is used to assemble a Ni/MH half-cell with an AB5 anode and a sintered Ni(OH)2 cathode, wherein the cell is cycled at 4 mA/g, wherein the initial capacity is 12 mAh/g, wherein the capacity increases to 28.6 mAh/g by the 25th cycle, and wherein the capacity stabilizes at 23.9 mAh/g after 100 cycles, with charge and discharge plateaus at 1.6 V and 1.2 V, respectively.

### Example 5
