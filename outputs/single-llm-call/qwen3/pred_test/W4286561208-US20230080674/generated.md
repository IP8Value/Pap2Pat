# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

- introduce internet-based skills assessment  
Internet-based skills assessment has revolutionized the way educational and professional competencies are measured, enabling scalable, on-demand evaluation of complex cognitive abilities across diverse populations. Unlike traditional paper-based testing, digital assessments allow for dynamic item delivery, real-time scoring, and adaptive administration tailored to individual performance levels. These systems support a broader range of response formats—including text selection, drag-and-drop interactions, and timed responses—that more accurately reflect authentic cognitive behaviors observed in academic and workplace contexts. The shift to digital platforms has also facilitated the integration of machine learning and natural language processing tools into the assessment lifecycle, allowing for the automated creation, delivery, and scoring of items that previously required extensive human curation. As a result, institutions can now administer high-stakes evaluations with greater efficiency, reduced logistical burden, and enhanced accessibility for global test-taker populations.

- describe advantages of computerized assessments  
Computerized assessments offer numerous advantages over conventional methods, including immediate feedback to learners, reduced scoring latency, and the ability to deliver personalized testing experiences through adaptive algorithms. These systems eliminate manual grading errors and enable consistent application of scoring rubrics across thousands of test-takers, ensuring fairness and reliability at scale. Furthermore, digital platforms support multimedia-rich item formats, such as embedded audio, video, or interactive diagrams, which can better capture nuanced skills like listening comprehension, critical synthesis, or contextual reasoning. The data-rich nature of digital assessments also allows for continuous monitoring of item performance, enabling rapid identification of biased or poorly functioning items. This real-time analytics capability supports iterative improvements to test content and enhances the validity of inferences drawn from test scores.

- discuss challenge of test item development  
Despite these advantages, the development of high-quality test items remains a significant bottleneck in large-scale assessment systems. Creating valid, reliable, and fair items—particularly for complex constructs such as reading comprehension, critical thinking, or inferential reasoning—demands substantial expertise, time, and financial investment. Each item must be carefully aligned with learning objectives, linguistically precise, culturally neutral, and psychometrically sound. For reading comprehension tasks, this challenge is compounded by the need to generate coherent, authentic passages alongside contextually appropriate questions and plausible distractors. Manual item development is not only labor-intensive but also limits the diversity and volume of content available for testing, especially in low-resource domains or underrepresented languages. As demand for assessment grows, traditional approaches become economically and operationally unsustainable.

- introduce automatic item generation  
Automatic item generation (AIG) represents a promising solution to this challenge by leveraging computational methods to produce test items at scale. Rather than relying on human authors to craft each item individually, AIG systems use structured templates, rule-based transformations, or statistical models to generate new items from a set of predefined parameters. Early implementations focused on domains with clear structural patterns, such as mathematics or grammar, where items could be instantiated from fixed schemas. However, these approaches have struggled to generalize to open-ended, context-dependent tasks like reading comprehension, where meaning emerges from the interplay of lexical, syntactic, and discourse-level features. The limitations of template-based systems have spurred interest in more flexible, data-driven approaches capable of generating naturalistic, context-sensitive content without requiring exhaustive manual encoding.

- describe limitations of item model approach  
The item model approach, while effective in constrained domains, is inherently limited in its applicability to complex, open-ended tasks. It requires domain experts to manually define the structural rules and parameter spaces that govern item variation, a process that is both time-consuming and difficult to scale. Moreover, item models often fail to capture the subtleties of language use, such as rhetorical flow, pragmatic implication, or stylistic coherence, which are essential for authentic reading comprehension. Because each question must be tightly coupled to its passage, the model cannot easily generate novel combinations of text and item without re-engineering the underlying schema. This rigidity severely restricts the diversity of content that can be produced and makes it difficult to adapt to new domains or evolving educational standards. As a result, the item model approach has been largely confined to low-complexity tasks, leaving a critical gap in the automated assessment of higher-order verbal reasoning.

## SUMMARY

- define invention and scope  
The invention disclosed herein encompasses systems and methods for the fully automated generation of reading comprehension assessments, including original passages, multiple-choice questions, correct responses, and plausible distractors, using transformer-based language models conditioned on minimal human-provided instructions. The invention enables the end-to-end creation of psychometrically valid, contextually coherent, and linguistically natural test items without reliance on manual item templates or rule-based transformations. The scope of the invention includes the generation of source texts, the derivation of comprehension questions from those texts, the selection of correct answers based on textual alignment, the generation of distractors from semantically similar but factually distinct alternative passages, and the filtering and ranking of all components to ensure quality, fairness, and discrimination. The invention further includes the integration of these processes into scalable, cloud-based platforms accessible to educational institutions, testing organizations, and content developers.

- introduce systems and methods for automated generation  
The invention introduces a novel system and method for automated generation of reading comprehension assessments through the sequential application of transformer-based language models, guided by structured prompts and evaluation criteria. The system begins by generating source passages based on high-level instructions such as topic, register, and narrative style. These passages are then evaluated for coherence, length, and linguistic quality before being used to condition the generation of comprehension items. The system simultaneously generates multiple candidate correct answers and distractors, evaluates them using natural language processing metrics, and selects the highest-quality options to construct complete test items. All components are filtered for bias, redundancy, and psychometric suitability, ensuring that the final output meets the standards required for high-stakes assessment.

- describe use of Transformer-based Language Model  
The invention employs a transformer-based language model as the core engine for content generation, leveraging its capacity to understand and produce long-range contextual dependencies in natural language. Unlike prior systems that rely on shallow syntactic rules or fixed templates, this model generates text and items by learning patterns from vast corpora of human-written material, enabling it to produce passages that are stylistically consistent, semantically rich, and linguistically plausible. The model is conditioned through carefully designed prompts that specify desired attributes such as domain, tone, and structural format, allowing for fine-grained control over the output without requiring retraining. This approach enables the generation of diverse, high-quality content across multiple subject areas with minimal human intervention.

- generate content-controlled passages  
The system generates content-controlled passages by providing the transformer model with a set of instructions and exemplars that define the desired characteristics of the output. These instructions may include topic descriptors, stylistic cues, or structural constraints such as paragraph length or sentence count. The model then produces a passage that conforms to these specifications, ensuring that the resulting text is both authentic and suitable for assessment purposes. The generated passages are designed to reflect the complexity and variability of real-world reading materials encountered in academic and professional settings.

- generate test items for each passage  
For each generated passage, the system produces a suite of test items designed to measure various dimensions of reading comprehension, including main idea identification, detail retrieval, inference, vocabulary in context, and text completion. Each item is constructed to be answerable solely based on information contained within the passage, ensuring alignment with the construct of reading comprehension. The system generates multiple potential items for each passage, evaluates them for clarity and difficulty, and selects those that best meet psychometric criteria.

- describe test items for probing comprehension  
The test items generated by the system are designed to probe multiple levels of comprehension, ranging from literal recall to inferential synthesis. Items include multiple-choice questions with one correct answer and three distractors, as well as text-highlighting tasks in which test-takers select the portion of the passage that best answers the question. The correct answers are derived from the passage itself, while distractors are generated from alternative passages that share similar style and topic but differ in factual content. This ensures that distractors are plausible yet incorrect, enhancing the discrimination power of the item.

- generate and select test items  
The system generates a pool of candidate test items for each passage and applies a series of automated filters to select the highest-quality items. These filters evaluate items based on linguistic fluency, answerability, distractor plausibility, and psychometric indicators such as item difficulty and discrimination. Items that fail to meet predefined thresholds are discarded, while those that pass are compiled into a final set ready for human review or direct deployment.

- generate source passages using language model  
Source passages are generated by inputting a set of conditioning attributes—such as topic, genre, and intended reading level—into a transformer-based language model. The model produces a passage that adheres to these constraints while maintaining natural language structure and coherence. Multiple passages are generated per set of instructions to ensure diversity and reduce the risk of repetition or bias.

- filter source passages  
Generated passages are filtered using automated criteria to ensure they meet quality standards. Passages are evaluated for length (100–175 words), sentence count (5–20), lexical diversity, and coherence. Passages containing repetitive n-grams, offensive language, or excessive syntactic complexity are removed. Additionally, passages are scored based on the negative log likelihood of their sentences to ensure that each sentence is statistically probable given its context, thereby eliminating incoherent or unnatural text.

- generate alternative passages  
To support the generation of plausible distractors, the system generates alternative passages using the same conditioning instructions and exemplars as the source passage. These alternative passages differ in specific factual content but maintain identical stylistic and structural features, making them ideal for producing incorrect answer options that are contextually convincing yet factually inaccurate.

- generate correct responses to multiple-choice questions  
Correct responses are generated by prompting the language model to produce answers to comprehension questions based on the source passage. The model generates multiple candidate answers, which are then evaluated for their semantic similarity to the passage, their alignment with specific sentence-level content, and their likelihood under the language model. The highest-scoring candidates are selected as correct answers, ensuring that they are both accurate and well-supported by the text.

- generate incorrect responses to multiple-choice questions  
Incorrect responses are generated by applying the same question-generation prompts to the alternative passages, thereby producing answers that are plausible within the context of those passages but incorrect relative to the source. These distractors are evaluated for their similarity to the correct answer, their plausibility, and their ability to attract test-takers who lack full comprehension. The most effective distractors are selected for inclusion in the final item.

- filter test items  
All generated test items undergo a final filtering process to ensure they meet psychometric and editorial standards. Items with ambiguous wording, overly long or short answers, or distractors that are too similar to the correct answer are removed. Items are also checked for cultural bias, technical jargon, and sensitivity to diverse audiences. Only items that pass all filters are retained for use in assessments.

- construct test items  
Test items are constructed by combining a source passage with a selected comprehension question, a single correct answer, and three distractors. Each item is formatted consistently to ensure uniform presentation to test-takers. Metadata including item type, difficulty estimate, and discrimination index are attached to each item for use in adaptive testing and psychometric analysis.

- describe system for automated generation  
The system for automated generation comprises a series of interconnected modules that execute the processes of passage generation, item generation, filtering, and selection. These modules are implemented as software components running on a computing system and are coordinated through a central control engine that manages the flow of data between stages. The system is designed to operate autonomously, requiring minimal human input beyond initial instruction sets.

- describe non-transitory computer-readable medium  
The invention includes a non-transitory computer-readable medium encoded with instructions that, when executed by one or more processors, cause the system to perform the steps of generating source passages, generating comprehension items, evaluating and filtering candidates, and constructing final test items. The medium may be embodied in a hard drive, solid-state drive, optical disc, or other persistent storage device capable of retaining data in the absence of power.

- provide services through SaaS or multi-tenant platform  
The system is accessible via a software-as-a-service (SaaS) platform that supports multiple tenants, allowing educational institutions, testing organizations, and content developers to generate and manage their own assessment content within isolated, secure environments. Each tenant has dedicated storage, user accounts, and configuration settings, ensuring data privacy and operational independence while benefiting from shared computational resources.

- describe access to multiple entities  
Multiple entities, including universities, certification bodies, and private testing companies, may access the platform simultaneously through authenticated user accounts. Each entity may generate, review, and deploy its own set of assessment items without interfering with the content or configurations of other entities. The platform supports role-based access control, audit trails, and usage analytics to ensure accountability and compliance.

- describe each account and associated data storage  
Each user account is associated with a dedicated data storage environment that contains all generated passages, items, review notes, and psychometric metadata. Data is stored in encrypted, partitioned databases that are accessible only to authorized users within the account. Storage is scalable and automatically backed up to ensure data integrity and availability.

- describe services instantiated in each account  
Within each account, services are instantiated to support passage generation, item generation, filtering, review, and export. These services include natural language processing modules, machine learning scoring models, bias detection tools, and human-in-the-loop review interfaces. All services are configurable to meet the specific needs of the account holder, including language, domain, and assessment format.

- describe advantages of systems and methods  
The systems and methods disclosed herein provide a significant advancement over prior art by enabling the fully automated, high-quality generation of reading comprehension assessments at scale. Unlike previous approaches, the invention does not rely on manual item templates or rule-based transformations, allowing for greater flexibility, diversity, and authenticity in generated content. The integration of transformer-based language models ensures that passages and items are linguistically coherent and psychometrically sound. The platform’s scalability, security, and multi-tenant architecture make it suitable for global deployment, reducing the cost and time required to develop high-stakes assessments.
