# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- define field of invention

The present invention resides in the field of molecular diagnostics and genetic screening, particularly in the context of early detection and personalized therapeutic intervention for Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy. It pertains to a highly specific, multiplexed nucleic acid amplification-based method for identifying deletions in the dystrophin gene that render patients eligible for exon-skipping therapies. The invention enables the simultaneous detection of key flanking exons whose deletion patterns predict responsiveness to currently approved or clinically advanced antisense oligonucleotide treatments targeting exons 45, 51, and 53. By leveraging real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction technology applied to minimally invasive biological samples, the invention provides a scalable, high-throughput diagnostic platform suitable for implementation in newborn screening programs. The method is designed to distinguish between subjects who harbor deletions amenable to targeted intervention and those who do not, thereby facilitating timely initiation of disease-modifying therapy and improving long-term clinical outcomes.

## BACKGROUND

- describe DMD

Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy is a severe, progressive, X-linked recessive neuromuscular disorder characterized by the absence of functional dystrophin protein in skeletal and cardiac muscle tissues. This deficiency leads to recurrent muscle fiber damage, chronic inflammation, fibrosis, and eventual replacement of muscle with adipose and connective tissue. The disease manifests in early childhood, typically before the age of three, with symptoms including delayed motor milestones, gait abnormalities, calf hypertrophy, and elevated serum creatine kinase levels. Without intervention, affected individuals lose ambulation by early adolescence and develop life-threatening cardiopulmonary complications, with median survival extending into the third decade of life under optimal supportive care.

- explain causes of DMD

The underlying genetic cause of Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy lies in pathogenic variants within the DMD gene located on the X chromosome at locus Xp21.2. This gene encodes dystrophin, a large cytoskeletal protein essential for maintaining the structural integrity of muscle cell membranes during contraction. The majority of disease-causing variants are large deletions or duplications of one or more exons, which disrupt the translational reading frame and prevent the production of a functional protein. In contrast, in-frame alterations in the same gene typically result in the milder Becker Muscular Dystrophy phenotype, where truncated but partially functional dystrophin isoforms are expressed. Point mutations, including nonsense and splice-site variants, account for a smaller proportion of cases but similarly lead to premature termination of translation or aberrant splicing.

- discuss current treatments

Current standard-of-care for Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy includes corticosteroid regimens to slow disease progression, physical therapy to preserve mobility, respiratory support, and cardiac monitoring. While these interventions improve quality of life and prolong survival, they do not address the fundamental genetic defect. Recent advances have introduced mutation-specific therapies, including antisense oligonucleotide-mediated exon skipping and gene replacement using adeno-associated viral vectors encoding microdystrophin. These approaches represent a paradigm shift from symptomatic management to targeted correction of the underlying molecular pathology.

- introduce exon skipping approaches

Exon-skipping therapies utilize synthetic antisense oligonucleotides to modulate pre-mRNA splicing by masking specific exons during transcript processing. By inducing the exclusion of an exon adjacent to a frameshifting deletion, these agents restore the open reading frame, enabling the production of a internally deleted but partially functional dystrophin protein analogous to those naturally occurring in Becker Muscular Dystrophy. This strategy has been successfully applied to exons 51, 53, and 45, with corresponding drugs—eteplirsen, golodirsen, and casimersen—approved by regulatory authorities for patients whose deletions are amenable to skipping of these specific exons.

- mention gene therapy strategies

Gene therapy strategies aim to deliver a functional version of the dystrophin gene or a shortened, engineered variant known as microdystrophin via viral vectors, most commonly adeno-associated virus serotype 9. Clinical trials have demonstrated sustained expression of microdystrophin in skeletal and cardiac muscle following intravenous administration, with evidence of improved muscle function and reduced fibrosis in treated patients. However, challenges remain regarding immune responses, vector dosing limitations in neonates, and the inability to re-administer the therapy due to neutralizing antibody development.

- discuss limitations of current screening methods

Current population-based screening approaches for Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy rely primarily on the measurement of creatine kinase activity in dried blood spots. While elevated creatine kinase levels serve as a sensitive biomarker of muscle damage, they lack specificity, as levels may be transiently elevated due to perinatal stress, prematurity, or non-DMD myopathies. Furthermore, creatine kinase levels can be normal in some affected individuals, particularly in later stages of disease or in female carriers with skewed X-inactivation. Importantly, these biochemical assays provide no information regarding the underlying genetic lesion, rendering them incapable of identifying which patients are eligible for exon-skipping therapies. Consequently, many infants with treatable deletions remain undetected until clinical symptoms emerge, resulting in a critical delay in initiating disease-modifying treatment.

## SUMMARY

- motivate need for novel DMD detection methods

There exists a compelling and unmet clinical need for a diagnostic method capable of identifying newborns with Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy who harbor deletions amenable to currently available exon-skipping therapies. Early diagnosis is paramount, as therapeutic efficacy is significantly enhanced when intervention begins before substantial muscle degeneration occurs. Current screening methods fail to meet this need by providing only indirect evidence of disease and offering no guidance regarding therapeutic eligibility. A direct molecular assay that rapidly and accurately detects the presence of specific exon deletions would enable the implementation of a precision medicine approach to newborn screening, ensuring that only those who stand to benefit from targeted therapies are identified and referred for prompt intervention.

- introduce improved methods and tools

The present invention introduces an improved method and associated diagnostic tools for the detection of Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy through the identification of deletions in flanking exons surrounding those targeted by approved exon-skipping therapies. By focusing on the detection of deletions in exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54, the method captures nearly all patients whose underlying mutation can be corrected by skipping exon 45, 51, or 53. This approach leverages multiplex quantitative polymerase chain reaction technology to simultaneously interrogate multiple genomic regions in a single reaction, enabling high-throughput, cost-effective analysis from minimally invasive samples such as dried blood spots.

- describe detection of specific DMD exons

The invention enables the detection of deletions in specific exons of the DMD gene, including exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54, which serve as reliable indicators of deletions amenable to exon-skipping therapies targeting exons 45, 51, and 53. The absence of amplification of any one of these exons in a male subject is indicative of a hemizygous deletion that may render the patient eligible for a corresponding antisense oligonucleotide therapy. The method does not require sequencing or complex bioinformatic analysis but instead relies on a binary readout of amplification presence or absence, making it suitable for routine clinical implementation.

- explain advantages of detecting treatable mutations

Detecting mutations that are directly treatable with existing therapies transforms newborn screening from a general disease detection tool into a precision medicine platform. By identifying only those infants who can benefit from immediate intervention, the method reduces unnecessary anxiety and follow-up testing in families of unaffected children. It also ensures that therapeutic resources are allocated efficiently, with treatment initiated at the earliest possible stage to maximize functional preservation. This targeted approach aligns with evolving standards in newborn screening, where the availability of effective treatment justifies the inclusion of a disorder despite its rarity.

- introduce multiplex polymerase-based nucleic acid amplification

The invention employs multiplex polymerase-based nucleic acid amplification as the core analytical technique, allowing for the simultaneous detection of multiple DMD exons in a single reaction vessel. This approach minimizes sample consumption, reduces turnaround time, and lowers per-test cost compared to sequential single-plex assays. The method is compatible with standard real-time PCR instrumentation and does not require specialized equipment beyond that commonly available in clinical diagnostic laboratories.

- describe method for analysing DMD gene

The method for analyzing the DMD gene involves the extraction of genomic DNA from a biological sample, followed by amplification of target exons using a defined set of primer pairs and fluorescently labeled probes. Each primer pair is designed to generate a unique amplicon spanning a specific exon, and each probe is engineered to hybridize specifically to the corresponding amplicon. Amplification is monitored in real time, and the absence of a fluorescent signal for a given exon indicates deletion of that exon. The presence of amplification in all other exons confirms the integrity of the reaction and validates the result.

- describe detection in blood samples

The method is optimized for use with dried blood spot samples, which are routinely collected as part of newborn screening programs for other genetic disorders. The protocol requires only a small volume of blood, is compatible with standard extraction procedures, and yields sufficient DNA for multiple analyses. The use of dried blood spots facilitates sample transport, storage, and scalability across large populations, making the method ideal for population-wide implementation.

- introduce amplification primer pairs

The invention includes a set of specifically designed amplification primer pairs, each pair flanking a distinct exon of the DMD gene. These primer pairs are selected to produce amplicons of similar size to ensure uniform amplification efficiency under multiplex conditions. Each primer pair is validated for high specificity, minimal cross-reactivity, and absence of secondary structures that could interfere with hybridization or extension.

- introduce oligonucleotide probes

The invention further comprises a set of oligonucleotide probes, each labeled with a distinct fluorophore and designed to bind internally to the amplicon generated by its corresponding primer pair. These probes enable real-time detection of amplification through fluorescence resonance energy transfer mechanisms, allowing for precise quantification and unambiguous identification of deletion events. The probes are engineered to avoid regions known to harbor common polymorphisms, thereby minimizing the risk of false-negative results.

- describe composition and kit of parts

The invention encompasses a composition comprising the set of amplification primer pairs and oligonucleotide probes described above, formulated in a ready-to-use master mix containing nucleic acid polymerase, deoxynucleotide triphosphates, buffer components, and stabilizing agents. This composition is provided as a kit of parts, including instructions for use, positive and negative controls, and a standardized protocol for sample preparation, amplification, and data interpretation.

- summarize further aspects and embodiments

Further aspects of the invention include the use of alternative detection chemistries, the extension of the assay to additional exons as new therapies become available, and the integration of the method into automated diagnostic platforms. The invention also encompasses the use of the method for prenatal screening, carrier detection in at-risk families, and the stratification of patients for clinical trial enrollment. The method may be combined with other molecular techniques, such as MLPA or next-generation sequencing, to confirm complex deletion patterns or identify non-deletion mutations.

- motivate genetic analysis of DMD gene

Genetic analysis of the DMD gene is motivated by the need to move beyond phenotypic indicators and toward genotype-directed therapeutic decision-making. The ability to identify the exact molecular lesion responsible for dystrophin deficiency enables clinicians to select the most appropriate treatment, avoid unnecessary interventions, and provide accurate genetic counseling to families.

- describe DMD gene and its mutations

The DMD gene spans approximately 2.2 million base pairs and contains 79 exons. It is one of the largest known human genes and is highly susceptible to large deletions and duplications due to its complex genomic architecture and the presence of repetitive sequences. The majority of pathogenic mutations involve the deletion of one or more exons, with hotspots located in the central rod domain (exons 45–55) and the 5’ end (exons 1–20). Deletions that disrupt the translational reading frame lead to Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy, whereas in-frame deletions typically result in the milder Becker phenotype.

- describe exon-skipping therapies

Exon-skipping therapies are designed to induce the exclusion of specific exons during pre-mRNA splicing, thereby restoring the open reading frame disrupted by a deletion. These therapies employ synthetic antisense oligonucleotides that bind to complementary sequences within intronic or exonic regions, blocking the spliceosome’s recognition of targeted exons. The resulting mRNA transcript lacks the skipped exon but maintains the correct reading frame, permitting the translation of a truncated yet partially functional dystrophin protein.

- describe importance of early treatment

Early treatment is critical because muscle degeneration begins before the onset of clinical symptoms. Initiation of exon-skipping therapy during infancy, prior to significant fibrosis and fatty infiltration of muscle tissue, has been shown in preclinical models to preserve muscle architecture and function more effectively than treatment initiated after symptom onset. Delayed diagnosis often results in irreversible loss of motor units, reducing the potential benefit of even the most advanced therapies.

- describe assays for detecting DMD mutations

Assays for detecting DMD mutations include multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification, array comparative genomic hybridization, next-generation sequencing, and quantitative PCR. Each method has advantages and limitations. Quantitative PCR, as employed in the present invention, offers a balance of speed, cost, sensitivity, and specificity suitable for high-throughput screening applications.

- describe exon-skipping approaches

Exon-skipping approaches are tailored to the specific deletion pattern of the patient. The invention detects the flanking exons of these targets—exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54—to infer the presence of deletions that are amenable to such corrections.

- define primer

A primer is a short, single-stranded nucleic acid sequence that binds to a complementary region of a template DNA and serves as a starting point for DNA synthesis by a polymerase.

- describe primer length

- define oligonucleotide

An oligonucleotide is a short synthetic nucleic acid molecule, typically 15 to 100 nucleotides in length, used for hybridization, amplification, or detection purposes.

- describe oligonucleotide length

- define primer pair

A primer pair consists of two primers, a forward and a reverse primer, designed to flank a target region and enable amplification of a defined amplicon.

- describe primer pair function

The function of a primer pair is to bind to complementary sequences on opposite strands of the DNA template and direct the synthesis of a specific amplicon during polymerase chain reaction.

- define hybridise within nucleic acid

To hybridise within a nucleic acid means to form a stable, sequence-specific double-stranded structure through complementary base pairing between two nucleic acid strands.

- describe hybridisation process

Hybridisation occurs when complementary nucleic acid strands associate via hydrogen bonding between Watson-Crick base pairs, forming a double helix under appropriate temperature and ionic conditions.

- define complementary

Complementary refers to the ability of two nucleic acid sequences to form specific base pairs according to Watson-Crick rules: adenine with thymine (or uracil in RNA), and guanine with cytosine.

- describe Watson-Crick base pairing

Watson-Crick base pairing is the specific hydrogen bonding between adenine and thymine (two hydrogen bonds) and between guanine and cytosine (three hydrogen bonds), which underlies the structure of the DNA double helix.

- describe primer complementarity

Primer complementarity refers to the degree of sequence match between a primer and its target region on the DNA template. Optimal primers exhibit 100% complementarity over their entire length.

- describe partly complementary primer

A partly complementary primer contains one or more mismatches with the target sequence and may be used to detect single nucleotide variants or polymorphisms.

- describe primer substitutions

Primer substitutions involve the replacement of one or more nucleotides in the primer sequence to improve binding affinity, reduce secondary structure, or avoid polymorphic sites.

- describe primer deletions

Primer deletions involve the removal of one or more nucleotides from the primer sequence to adjust melting temperature or eliminate non-specific binding.

- describe primer additions

Primer additions involve the insertion of one or more nucleotides into the primer sequence to improve stability, add a tag, or enhance detection.

- describe hybridization factors

Hybridization factors include temperature, salt concentration, pH, and the presence of formamide or other denaturants, all of which influence the stability of nucleic acid duplexes.

- define primer composition

Primer composition refers to the nucleotide sequence and chemical modifications of a primer, including the presence of modified bases, phosphorothioate linkages, or fluorescent labels.

- describe primer function

The function of a primer is to initiate DNA synthesis by providing a free 3’ hydroxyl group for the polymerase to extend, thereby enabling the amplification of a specific target region.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—DMD Genotyping Assay

- introduce DMD genotyping assay

The DMD genotyping assay is a multiplex quantitative PCR-based method designed to detect deletions in exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54 of the DMD gene in dried blood spot samples.

- describe sample collection and classification

Samples were collected from 120 subjects, including 51 males with confirmed DMD deletions, 50 female carriers, and 19 healthy controls. Samples were classified according to known genotypes determined by MLPA.

- outline DNA extraction and qPCR protocol

DNA was extracted from 3.1 mm dried blood spot punches using a standardized lysis protocol. Each sample was amplified in a 25 µL reaction containing primers, probes, and Takyon master mix under the following cycling conditions: 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 30 seconds and 61°C for 75 seconds.

- explain data analysis and interpretation

Data were analyzed using the Bio-Rad CFX Manager software. A normalized fluorescence ratio was calculated for each exon by dividing the endpoint fluorescence of each sample by the median fluorescence of all samples in the run. A ratio below 0.2 was defined as a deletion.

- present results of deletion detection

All 34 male subjects with deletions overlapping exons 44, 46, 50, 52, or 54 showed absence of amplification for the corresponding exon(s). All 17 subjects with deletions outside these exons showed normal amplification. All 50 carriers and 19 controls showed normal amplification for all exons.

- describe deletion patterns in deleted group

The deleted group included 10 distinct deletion patterns, ranging from single-exon deletions to large multi-exon deletions. All deletions involving the target exons were correctly identified.

- summarize assay performance

The assay demonstrated 100% sensitivity and 100% specificity for detecting deletions in the five target exons, with no false positives or false negatives observed.
