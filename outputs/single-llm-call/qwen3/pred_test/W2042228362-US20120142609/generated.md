# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to non-human animal model

The present invention relates to a non-human animal model characterized by increased retinal vascular permeability due to genetic or functional deficiency of the dystrophin isoform Dp71 or its associated protein complex. This model is specifically engineered to replicate key pathophysiological features observed in human retinal diseases marked by breakdown of the blood-retinal barrier, including diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, and retinal detachment. The model is distinguished by the absence or significant reduction of Dp71 expression in Müller glial cells, resulting in mislocalization of inwardly rectifying potassium channels (Kir4.1) and aquaporin-4 (AQP4), impaired potassium buffering, defective water homeostasis, and consequent osmotic swelling of Müller cell somata. These physiological disruptions culminate in a measurable and reproducible increase in retinal vascular permeability, which is not observed in wild-type counterparts under identical conditions. The model provides a robust, physiologically relevant platform for studying the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying retinal edema formation and for evaluating therapeutic interventions aimed at restoring blood-retinal barrier integrity. Unlike existing models that rely on induced vascular injury or hyperglycemia, this model isolates the contribution of glial dysfunction to barrier failure, enabling precise dissection of Dp71-dependent pathways without confounding systemic metabolic perturbations. The model is applicable across multiple species, with murine strains being the preferred embodiment due to genetic tractability, well-characterized retinal anatomy, and established protocols for functional and molecular assessment. This invention represents a paradigm shift in retinal disease modeling by focusing on glial-specific molecular deficits as the primary driver of vascular pathology rather than secondary consequences of neuronal or endothelial damage.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce diabetic retinopathy

Diabetic retinopathy is a progressive microvascular complication of diabetes mellitus and remains the leading cause of vision loss among working-age adults in developed countries. It arises from chronic hyperglycemia-induced damage to the retinal vasculature, triggering a cascade of structural and functional alterations that ultimately compromise visual acuity. Early stages of the disease are often asymptomatic, but as the condition advances, it leads to capillary non-perfusion, microaneurysm formation, intraretinal hemorrhages, and macular edema, the latter being the most common direct cause of central vision impairment. Despite advances in anti-vascular endothelial growth factor therapies, a significant proportion of patients exhibit incomplete or transient responses, underscoring the need for novel therapeutic targets that address the underlying pathophysiology beyond angiogenesis.

- describe its consequences

The consequences of untreated or inadequately managed diabetic retinopathy include irreversible photoreceptor degeneration, retinal fibrosis, neovascular glaucoma, and profound visual disability. Macular edema, characterized by the pathological accumulation of fluid within the retinal layers, particularly the macula, disrupts the precise architecture required for high-acuity vision. This fluid accumulation is not solely attributable to vascular leakage but is also influenced by impaired fluid clearance mechanisms mediated by retinal glial cells. The persistence of edema despite suppression of vascular endothelial growth factor suggests that alternative pathways, particularly those involving glial dysfunction, play a critical and underappreciated role in disease progression.

- explain role of glial cells

Glial cells, particularly Müller cells, are indispensable for maintaining retinal homeostasis. These radial glial cells span the entire thickness of the neuroretina and serve as structural, metabolic, and ionic scaffolds for neurons and blood vessels. They regulate extracellular ion concentrations, recycle neurotransmitters, provide metabolic support to photoreceptors, and critically, contribute to the formation and maintenance of the blood-retinal barrier. Their endfeet intimately associate with retinal capillaries and form a functional interface that controls the passage of solutes and fluids between the bloodstream and neural tissue. Disruption of glial function precedes overt vascular pathology in many retinal diseases, indicating that glial cells are not merely passive responders but active participants in barrier integrity.

- describe Müller cells' functions

Müller cells perform a multitude of essential functions, including the spatial buffering of potassium ions released during neuronal activity, the regulation of extracellular water volume through aquaporin-mediated water transport, and the secretion of trophic and vasoactive factors that influence vascular tone and permeability. Their polarized expression of ion channels and water transporters enables them to efficiently sequester excess potassium from areas of high neuronal firing and redistribute it toward the vitreous or perivascular regions, thereby preventing neuronal hyperexcitability and edema. This function is critically dependent on the precise subcellular localization of Kir4.1 channels and AQP4 water channels at the Müller cell endfeet.

- explain potassium buffering and water drainage

Potassium buffering is mediated primarily by Kir4.1 channels, which are highly concentrated in the vitread and perivascular endfeet of Müller cells. These channels facilitate the rapid efflux of potassium ions into the vitreous humor or perivascular space, preventing local accumulation that could depolarize neurons and disrupt synaptic transmission. Water drainage occurs in parallel through AQP4 channels, which are co-localized with Kir4.1 and enable osmotically driven water movement that follows potassium flux. The coordinated activity of these two proteins ensures that potassium efflux is accompanied by appropriate water clearance, thereby maintaining retinal osmotic balance and preventing fluid accumulation.

- describe Dp71's role in Kir4.1 and AQP4 expression

The dystrophin isoform Dp71 is a cytoskeletal protein expressed predominantly in Müller cell endfeet and is essential for the proper clustering and membrane anchoring of Kir4.1 and AQP4. Dp71 serves as a scaffold that links these channels to the dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC), which in turn connects to the extracellular matrix and actin cytoskeleton. This structural organization restricts lateral diffusion of the channels, ensuring their enrichment at specific membrane domains critical for their physiological function. Without Dp71, Kir4.1 and AQP4 become diffusely distributed throughout the Müller cell membrane, losing their polarized localization and consequently their functional efficiency.

- explain consequences of Dp71 genetic inactivation

Genetic inactivation of Dp71 results in the mislocalization of Kir4.1 and AQP4, leading to a marked reduction in potassium conductance and impaired water transport capacity of Müller cells. This dysfunction manifests as increased susceptibility to osmotic stress, cellular swelling, and a failure to maintain retinal fluid homeostasis. Importantly, these changes occur in the absence of vascular injury or hyperglycemia, demonstrating that Dp71 deficiency alone is sufficient to induce a state of chronic blood-retinal barrier disruption. The resulting increase in vascular permeability mirrors the pathological features of diabetic macular edema and other retinal edematous conditions, establishing Dp71 as a central regulator of retinal barrier integrity.

- summarize glial cell dysfunction in retinal diseases

Glial cell dysfunction, particularly in Müller cells, is a consistent and early feature of numerous retinal diseases, including diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, uveitis, and retinal detachment. In these conditions, the expression and localization of Kir4.1 and AQP4 are frequently altered, and the structural integrity of the DAPC is compromised. These changes correlate with the severity of edema and visual loss, suggesting that glial dysfunction is not merely a secondary consequence but a primary driver of pathophysiology. The Dp71-deficient animal model described herein captures this central pathogenic mechanism, providing a unique tool to investigate glial-centric therapeutic strategies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce non-human animal model for increased retinal vascular permeability

The present invention provides a novel non-human animal model characterized by genetically or pharmacologically induced deficiency of Dp71 or its associated protein complex, resulting in a reproducible and measurable increase in retinal vascular permeability. This model exhibits key pathological features of human retinal edematous diseases, including mislocalization of Kir4.1 and AQP4 channels, impaired potassium buffering, osmotic swelling of Müller cell somata, and breakdown of the blood-retinal barrier, all in the absence of systemic metabolic or vascular insults. The model is uniquely suited for studying the causal relationship between glial cytoskeletal integrity and vascular permeability, and for screening therapeutic agents designed to restore Müller cell function and barrier integrity. Unlike existing models that rely on hyperglycemia or surgical induction of vascular leakage, this model isolates the role of Dp71 in maintaining retinal homeostasis, offering a precise and mechanistically grounded platform for drug discovery and pathophysiological investigation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- describe experimental retinal detachment model

An experimental retinal detachment model was employed to investigate the functional consequences of Dp71 deficiency in the context of retinal injury. In this model, subretinal injection of sodium hyaluronate was performed in both wild-type and Dp71-deficient animals to induce mechanical separation of the neurosensory retina from the retinal pigment epithelium. This procedure reliably triggers reactive gliosis, upregulation of glial fibrillary acidic protein, and alterations in the expression and localization of key ion and water channels. In wild-type animals, retinal detachment led to a significant downregulation of Dp71 mRNA and protein expression, accompanied by diffuse redistribution of Kir4.1 and reduced AQP4 immunoreactivity. In contrast, Dp71-null mice exhibited no further alteration in channel localization following detachment, indicating that Dp71 depletion is both necessary and sufficient to induce the full spectrum of glial dysfunction observed in this model. The absence of additional changes in Dp71-null mice after detachment confirms that Dp71 loss represents a terminal point in the signaling cascade leading to barrier failure.

- describe results of experimental retinal detachment model

The results of the experimental retinal detachment model demonstrated that Dp71 downregulation is an early and decisive event in the pathophysiology of reactive gliosis. In wild-type animals, detachment induced a 50% reduction in Dp71 protein levels within 24 hours, preceding the redistribution of Kir4.1 and the reduction in AQP4 expression. Electrophysiological recordings revealed a significant decrease in inward potassium current densities in Müller cells from detached retinas, correlating with increased membrane capacitance indicative of cellular hypertrophy. Osmotic challenge experiments showed that Müller cells from detached retinas exhibited pronounced swelling under hypotonic conditions, a phenotype that was already present in untreated Dp71-null mice. Furthermore, vascular permeability, as quantified by Evans blue extravasation, was markedly elevated in Dp71-null mice even in the absence of any surgical intervention, confirming that Dp71 deficiency alone is sufficient to disrupt the blood-retinal barrier. These findings establish a direct causal link between Dp71 expression, glial channel organization, and vascular integrity.

### Non Human Model According to the Invention

- define non human animal model for increased retinal vascular permeability

The non-human animal model of the invention is defined as a living organism, preferably a rodent, in which the expression or function of the Dp71 protein or any component of the dystrophin-associated protein complex is genetically, pharmacologically, or molecularly suppressed to a degree sufficient to induce increased retinal vascular permeability. This suppression results in the mislocalization of Kir4.1 and AQP4 channels in Müller glial cells, impaired potassium buffering, defective water homeostasis, and consequent osmotic swelling of Müller cell somata, culminating in a measurable and persistent increase in the permeability of the blood-retinal barrier. The model is distinguishable from other retinal disease models by the absence of systemic metabolic, inflammatory, or vascular triggers as primary drivers of pathology.

- describe term "increased retinal vascular permeability"

Increased retinal vascular permeability refers to the pathological transudation of plasma proteins, fluid, and other circulating molecules from the retinal vasculature into the surrounding neural tissue, resulting in the accumulation of fluid within retinal layers and the disruption of normal retinal architecture. This phenomenon is quantifiable by the extravasation of tracer molecules such as Evans blue-albumin complex, which binds to serum albumin and leaks into the retina when the blood-retinal barrier is compromised. In the context of this invention, increased permeability is defined as a statistically significant elevation in tracer leakage compared to age- and strain-matched wild-type controls, occurring in the absence of hyperglycemia, hypertension, or direct vascular injury.

- define term "Dp71"

Dp71 is a 71-kilodalton isoform of the dystrophin protein encoded by the DMD gene, specifically transcribed from an internal promoter located in intron 62. Unlike full-length dystrophin, Dp71 lacks the N-terminal actin-binding domain and is expressed predominantly in non-muscle tissues, including the retina, brain, and kidney. In the retina, Dp71 is localized to the endfeet of Müller glial cells, where it functions as a critical scaffold for the dystrophin-associated protein complex, anchoring Kir4.1 and AQP4 channels to the plasma membrane.

- describe Dp71 protein structure

The Dp71 protein consists of a C-terminal domain that includes the dystrophin homology region 2, the cysteine-rich domain, and the C-terminal tail, which mediates binding to β-dystroglycan and other components of the dystrophin-associated protein complex. It lacks the N-terminal actin-binding domain and the central rod domain found in full-length dystrophin, rendering it incapable of linking the cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix via dystroglycan in the same manner as full-length dystrophin. Instead, Dp71 functions as a membrane-associated scaffold that stabilizes ion channels and transporters at specialized microdomains within the Müller cell endfoot.

- describe Dp71 transcripts and isoforms

Dp71 arises from alternative splicing and internal promoter usage of the DMD gene, generating multiple transcript variants that differ in their 5′ untranslated regions and N-terminal sequences but share a common C-terminal coding sequence. The predominant isoform in the retina is Dp71f, which contains the full C-terminal domain necessary for interaction with the dystrophin-associated protein complex. Other isoforms, such as Dp71b and Dp71d, may be expressed at lower levels and exhibit distinct subcellular distributions, but only Dp71f has been shown to colocalize with Kir4.1 and AQP4 in Müller cell endfeet.

- define term "DAP"

DAP stands for dystrophin-associated protein, referring to a group of transmembrane and cytoplasmic proteins that interact with dystrophin and its isoforms to form a macromolecular complex that links the cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix. In the retina, the DAP complex includes β-dystroglycan, α-syntrophin, dystrobrevin, and utrophin, which collectively stabilize Kir4.1 and AQP4 at the Müller cell membrane.

- describe DAPC structure

The dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC) is a multi-subunit assembly anchored to the plasma membrane via β-dystroglycan, which binds to extracellular laminin and intracellular dystrophin or Dp71. The complex includes syntrophins, which serve as scaffolds for ion channels and signaling molecules, and dystrobrevins, which stabilize the complex through interactions with dystrophin. In Müller cells, Dp71 replaces full-length dystrophin as the central scaffold, binding β-dystroglycan and syntrophin, which in turn recruit Kir4.1 and AQP4, thereby organizing them into functional microdomains at the endfoot membrane.

- describe DAPC function in skeletal muscle

In skeletal muscle, the DAPC functions as a mechanical linker between the intracellular actin cytoskeleton and the extracellular matrix, providing structural stability to the sarcolemma during muscle contraction. It also serves as a signaling hub, regulating calcium homeostasis, cell survival, and membrane repair. Disruption of the DAPC in Duchenne muscular dystrophy leads to membrane fragility, necrosis, and progressive muscle wasting.

- describe DAPC function in retina

In the retina, the DAPC does not serve a mechanical role but instead functions as a molecular organizer that ensures the polarized localization of Kir4.1 and AQP4 channels at Müller cell endfeet. By anchoring these channels to the cytoskeleton, the DAPC prevents their lateral diffusion and maintains their concentration at sites critical for potassium buffering and water drainage. Loss of Dp71 destabilizes the DAPC, leading to channel mislocalization, impaired glial function, and increased vascular permeability.

- define "inhibitor of expression"

An inhibitor of expression refers to any molecule, nucleic acid construct, or pharmacological agent that reduces or abolishes the transcription, translation, or functional activity of a target gene or protein. In the context of this invention, inhibitors of expression are used to suppress Dp71 or DAP gene expression in non-human animals to generate a model of increased retinal vascular permeability.

- describe small organic molecule inhibitors of Dp71 or DAP genes

Small organic molecule inhibitors of Dp71 or DAP genes are low molecular weight compounds that interfere with the expression or function of Dp71 or its associated proteins through direct binding, modulation of transcriptional regulators, or disruption of protein-protein interactions. These molecules may include kinase inhibitors, histone deacetylase inhibitors, or compounds that block the binding interface between Dp71 and β-dystroglycan, thereby destabilizing the DAPC and inducing channel mislocalization.

- describe anti-sense oligonucleotide constructs

Anti-sense oligonucleotide constructs are synthetic nucleic acid sequences complementary to a specific region of Dp71 or DAP gene mRNA, designed to hybridize with the target transcript and induce degradation via RNase H-mediated cleavage or steric blockade of ribosomal translation. These constructs are typically 15 to 25 nucleotides in length and may be chemically modified to enhance stability and cellular uptake.

- describe siRNA inhibitors of Dp71 or DAP genes

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) inhibitors are double-stranded RNA molecules approximately 21 nucleotides in length that trigger RNA interference-mediated degradation of Dp71 or DAP mRNA. These molecules are designed to target unique sequences within the coding or untranslated regions of the target gene and are delivered via viral vectors or lipid nanoparticles to achieve efficient knockdown in Müller glial cells.

- describe shRNA inhibitors of Dp71 or DAP genes

Short hairpin RNA (shRNA) inhibitors are DNA-encoded RNA molecules that form a stem-loop structure and are processed intracellularly into siRNA by the endogenous RNA interference machinery. shRNA constructs are typically cloned into viral vectors and stably integrated into the genome, enabling long-term suppression of Dp71 or DAP gene expression in transgenic or virally transduced animals.

- describe ribozyme inhibitors of Dp71 or DAP genes

Ribozyme inhibitors are catalytic RNA molecules capable of cleaving specific mRNA sequences encoding Dp71 or DAP proteins. These constructs contain a recognition sequence complementary to the target mRNA and a catalytic core that mediates site-specific cleavage, thereby reducing protein expression. Ribozymes can be delivered via plasmid or viral vectors and offer a non-immunogenic alternative to RNA interference-based approaches.

- describe modifications to oligonucleotides for increased stability

Modifications to oligonucleotides include phosphorothioate backbone substitutions, 2′-O-methyl or 2′-fluoro ribose modifications, locked nucleic acid (LNA) residues, and conjugation with cholesterol or peptide ligands to enhance nuclease resistance, cellular uptake, and tissue penetration. These modifications prolong the half-life of antisense, siRNA, and shRNA constructs in vivo, enabling sustained suppression of target gene expression.

- describe vectors for delivering inhibitors of expression

Vectors for delivering inhibitors of expression include viral and non-viral systems engineered to transport nucleic acid constructs encoding siRNA, shRNA, antisense oligonucleotides, or ribozymes into retinal cells. These vectors are designed to achieve targeted delivery to Müller glial cells while minimizing off-target effects in other retinal cell types.

- describe viral vectors

Viral vectors are genetically modified viruses that have been rendered replication-incompetent and engineered to carry therapeutic or experimental nucleic acid sequences. In this invention, viral vectors are used to deliver inhibitors of Dp71 or DAP gene expression to the retina with high efficiency and specificity.

- describe retroviral vectors

Retroviral vectors are derived from retroviruses and integrate their genetic payload into the host genome, enabling long-term transgene expression. While useful for stable knockdown, their use in post-mitotic tissues such as the retina is limited due to low transduction efficiency in mature Müller cells.

- describe adenoviral vectors

Adenoviral vectors are non-integrating vectors capable of high-efficiency transduction of both dividing and non-dividing cells. They are widely used for transient but robust expression of shRNA or antisense constructs in the retina, achieving significant knockdown of Dp71 within days of subretinal or intravitreal administration.

- describe adeno-associated viral vectors

Adeno-associated viral (AAV) vectors are non-pathogenic, non-integrating vectors with high tropism for retinal cells, particularly Müller glia when serotypes such as AAV2, AAV8, or AAV9 are used. AAV vectors are the preferred delivery system for this invention due to their safety profile, long-term transgene expression, and ability to achieve cell-specific targeting when combined with Müller cell-specific promoters.

- describe plasmid vectors

Plasmid vectors are circular DNA molecules that carry the gene of interest along with regulatory elements such as promoters and polyadenylation signals. They are typically delivered via electroporation, hydrodynamic injection, or nanoparticle encapsulation and are useful for transient expression of inhibitory constructs in experimental settings.

- describe delivery methods for plasmid vectors

Delivery methods for plasmid vectors include intravitreal injection, subretinal injection, electroporation of the retina, and nanoparticle-mediated transfection. Intravitreal injection allows for widespread distribution but limited penetration into Müller cells, whereas subretinal injection enables direct access to the outer retina and enhanced transfection of Müller cell endfeet.

- describe heterologous regulatory regions

Heterologous regulatory regions are DNA sequences derived from other species or synthetic sources that drive gene expression in a cell-specific manner. In this invention, heterologous promoters are used to restrict the expression of inhibitory constructs to Müller glial cells, thereby minimizing off-target effects in neurons or vascular endothelial cells.

- describe specific promoters for Müller glial cells

Specific promoters for Müller glial cells include the human glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) promoter, the mouse vimentin promoter, and synthetic promoters derived from the Kir4.1 or AQP4 gene regulatory regions. These promoters ensure that inhibitory constructs are expressed predominantly in Müller cells, enhancing the specificity and safety of the model.

- describe deficient animals for Dp71 or DAP genes

Deficient animals for Dp71 or DAP genes are genetically modified organisms in which one or more genes encoding Dp71 or its associated proteins have been disrupted, deleted, or silenced to a degree sufficient to induce increased retinal vascular permeability. These animals may be constitutive or conditional knockouts, transgenic overexpressors of dominant-negative mutants, or animals treated with gene-silencing agents.

- describe methods for producing deficient animals

Methods for producing deficient animals include homologous recombination in embryonic stem cells to generate germline knockouts, CRISPR/Cas9-mediated targeted mutagenesis, RNA interference via transgenic shRNA expression, and pharmacological inhibition of DAPC assembly. These methods are applied to species such as mice, rats, or rabbits, with mice being the preferred model due to genetic homology and established retinal phenotyping protocols.

- describe conditional knock-out of targeted genes

Conditional knockout of targeted genes involves the use of Cre-loxP or Flp-FRT recombination systems to delete Dp71 or DAP genes in a spatially and temporally controlled manner. In this invention, Cre recombinase is driven by Müller cell-specific promoters such as GFAP or vimentin, allowing for selective deletion of Dp71 in glial cells postnatally, thereby avoiding developmental compensation and isolating the role of Dp71 in adult retinal homeostasis.

- describe example of deficient animal for Dp71

An exemplary deficient animal for Dp71 is the Dp71-null mouse, generated by homologous recombination in embryonic stem cells to replace exon 1 of the Dp71-specific transcript with a β-gal-neomycin resistance cassette, resulting in complete ablation of Dp71 protein expression without affecting other dystrophin isoforms. These mice exhibit normal gross retinal morphology but display mislocalized Kir4.1 and AQP4, reduced potassium conductance, osmotic swelling of Müller cells, and increased vascular permeability.

- describe selection of animal models

Selection of animal models is based on genetic fidelity to human disease, reproducibility of the vascular permeability phenotype, availability of validated functional assays, and compatibility with pharmacological interventions. The Dp71-null mouse is selected as the preferred model due to its well-characterized molecular phenotype, absence of systemic muscular dystrophy, and consistent increase in retinal vascular permeability without confounding metabolic disturbances.

- describe preferred animal model

The preferred animal model of the invention is the Dp71-null mouse on a C57BL/6 background, which exhibits a stable, heritable deficiency of Dp71 expression in Müller glial cells, resulting in a reproducible increase in retinal vascular permeability, mislocalization of Kir4.1 and AQP4, and impaired osmotic regulation. This model is suitable for longitudinal studies, high-throughput drug screening, and mechanistic investigations of glial-mediated barrier dysfunction.

- describe use of animal model for screening drugs

The animal model is used for screening drugs by administering candidate therapeutic agents to Dp71-deficient animals and measuring changes in retinal vascular permeability, Müller cell morphology, Kir4.1/AQP4 localization, and electrophysiological parameters. Compounds that restore barrier integrity or normalize glial channel expression are identified as potential therapeutics for diabetic retinopathy and other retinal edematous diseases.

- describe use of animal model for studying disease physiopathology

The animal model is used to study disease physiopathology by analyzing the temporal sequence of molecular, cellular, and functional changes that occur following Dp71 deficiency. This includes tracking the downregulation of Dp71, redistribution of Kir4.1 and AQP4, development of osmotic swelling, and progression of vascular leakage, thereby elucidating the causal chain of events leading to retinal edema.

- describe use of animal model for diagnostic or prognostic markers

The animal model is used to identify diagnostic or prognostic markers by correlating molecular changes in Müller cells with measurable functional deficits. For example, reduced Dp71 mRNA levels in vitreous fluid, altered Kir4.1/AQP4 protein ratios in retinal biopsies, or increased serum markers of glial activation may serve as biomarkers predictive of edema severity in human patients.

- describe diseases associated with increased retinal permeability

Diseases associated with increased retinal permeability include diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, uveitis, age-related macular degeneration, retinal detachment, and macular edema secondary to inflammation or trauma. These conditions share common features of glial dysfunction and blood-retinal barrier breakdown, making the Dp71-deficient model broadly applicable for studying their pathogenesis.

- describe method for screening drugs

The method for screening drugs involves administering a test compound to a Dp71-deficient animal, allowing a defined period for pharmacological action, and then quantifying retinal vascular permeability using Evans blue extravasation or fluorescein angiography. A reduction in permeability compared to vehicle-treated controls indicates therapeutic efficacy.

- describe step of providing animal model

The step of providing the animal model involves obtaining a genetically modified non-human animal with confirmed deficiency of Dp71 or a component of the dystrophin-associated protein complex, verified by Western blot, immunohistochemistry, or quantitative PCR, and demonstrating baseline increased retinal vascular permeability relative to wild-type controls.

- describe step of measuring blood-retinal barrier integrity

The step of measuring blood-retinal barrier integrity involves intravenous administration of a vascular tracer such as Evans blue or fluorescein isothiocyanate-labeled dextran, followed by perfusion to remove circulating tracer, tissue harvesting, and quantification of tracer leakage into the retina by spectrophotometry or fluorescence microscopy.

### Uses of the Non Human Model According to the Invention

- describe use of animal model for screening drugs

The animal model is utilized in high-throughput screening platforms to identify compounds that restore retinal vascular integrity by targeting Dp71 expression, DAPC assembly, or downstream glial functions. Libraries of small molecules, biologics, and gene therapies are tested for their ability to normalize Kir4.1 and AQP4 localization, reduce Müller cell swelling, and decrease vascular permeability without altering systemic physiology.

- describe use of animal model for studying disease physiopathology

The model enables the dissection of the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying retinal edema formation by allowing precise manipulation of Dp71 expression and observation of downstream consequences on glial channel dynamics, inflammatory signaling, and vascular function. This facilitates the identification of novel therapeutic targets and biomarkers.

- describe use of animal model for diagnostic or prognostic markers

The model is used to validate molecular signatures of glial dysfunction that correlate with vascular permeability, such as reduced Dp71 mRNA in vitreous samples, elevated levels of inflammatory cytokines, or altered expression of AQP4 splice variants. These signatures can be translated into clinical assays for early detection of edema risk in diabetic patients.

- describe method for screening drugs

The method for screening drugs comprises the steps of: (a) obtaining a non-human animal model with genetically or pharmacologically induced Dp71 deficiency and confirmed increased retinal vascular permeability; (b) administering a candidate therapeutic agent via a defined route and dosage regimen; (c) allowing a period of pharmacological exposure sufficient to elicit biological effect; (d) quantifying retinal vascular permeability using Evans blue extravasation or fluorescein angiography; and (e) comparing permeability levels between treated and control animals to determine therapeutic efficacy.

### Diagnostics Methods According to the Invention

- define subject and biological sample

The subject is a human or non-human mammal suspected of having a retinal disease characterized by increased vascular permeability. The biological sample is derived from ocular tissue, including vitreous fluid, aqueous humor, retinal biopsy, or peripheral blood, and is analyzed for molecular markers of Dp71 or DAPC dysfunction.

- describe mutation detection techniques

Mutation detection techniques include polymerase chain reaction amplification followed by sequencing of the DMD gene to identify deletions, insertions, or point mutations in the Dp71-specific promoter or exon 1 region. High-resolution melting analysis and digital PCR are employed to detect low-frequency variants associated with predisposition to retinal edema.

- describe expression analysis techniques

Expression analysis techniques include quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) to measure Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4 mRNA levels in ocular fluids or retinal tissue, and immunoblotting or immunohistochemistry to assess protein abundance and localization in retinal sections.

- detail mRNA/cDNA preparation and hybridization

mRNA is extracted from retinal tissue using TRIzol reagent, treated with DNase, and reverse transcribed into cDNA using oligo(dT) primers. The resulting cDNA is hybridized to microarrays or used in qRT-PCR with gene-specific primers to quantify transcript levels. Hybridization signals are normalized to housekeeping genes such as β-actin or GAPDH.

- describe nucleic acid amplification methods

Nucleic acid amplification methods include quantitative real-time PCR, digital droplet PCR, and loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) to detect and quantify Dp71 transcripts in minute volumes of vitreous fluid. These methods enable sensitive detection of Dp71 deficiency even in early-stage disease.

- describe protein expression analysis techniques

Protein expression analysis techniques include Western blotting, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), and multiplex immunoassays to quantify Dp71, β-dystroglycan, and syntrophin levels in retinal lysates or vitreous samples. Immunohistochemistry is used to assess subcellular localization in tissue sections.

- detail antibody-based detection methods

Antibody-based detection methods involve the use of monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies specific to Dp71, Kir4.1, or AQP4, applied to retinal sections or vitreous samples. Binding is visualized using fluorescent or enzymatic secondary antibodies, and signal intensity is quantified using digital imaging systems.

- describe enzyme immunoassay and radioimmunoassay

Enzyme immunoassays utilize horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies to generate a colorimetric signal proportional to target protein concentration, while radioimmunoassays employ radiolabeled antibodies to measure Dp71 levels with high sensitivity. Both methods are applicable to quantifying Dp71 in clinical samples such as aqueous humor.

- describe Western blot analysis and ELISA

Western blot analysis separates retinal proteins by SDS-PAGE, transfers them to a membrane, and detects Dp71 using specific antibodies followed by chemiluminescent detection. ELISA employs microplate wells coated with capture antibodies to quantify Dp71 in vitreous supernatants, providing a quantitative, high-throughput diagnostic tool.

- compare expression levels in biological samples

Expression levels of Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4 in biological samples from patients with retinal edema are compared to those from healthy controls. A significant reduction in Dp71 mRNA or protein, coupled with altered Kir4.1/AQP4 ratios, is indicative of glial dysfunction and predicts increased risk of vascular leakage.

- define normal expression level

Normal expression level refers to the range of Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4 mRNA and protein concentrations observed in retinal or vitreous samples from healthy individuals without retinal disease, as determined by standardized assays and validated against age-matched controls.

### Therapeutic Methods and Pharmaceutical Compositions According to the Invention

- define treating and treatment

Treating refers to the administration of a therapeutic agent to a subject with increased retinal vascular permeability for the purpose of ameliorating, reducing, or reversing the pathological state. Treatment encompasses both prophylactic and interventional strategies aimed at restoring glial function and barrier integrity.

- describe polypeptide production techniques

Polypeptide production techniques involve recombinant expression of Dp71 or functional fragments thereof in bacterial, yeast, or mammalian cell systems, followed by purification and formulation for delivery to the retina. These polypeptides may be fused to cell-penetrating peptides or encapsulated in nanoparticles to enhance retinal uptake.

- describe vector and construct preparation

Vector and construct preparation involves cloning cDNA encoding Dp71 or a functional domain into viral or plasmid vectors under the control of Müller cell-specific promoters. Constructs are verified by sequencing and tested for expression efficiency in vitro prior to in vivo delivery.

- describe delivery methods for nucleic acid constructs

Delivery methods for nucleic acid constructs include intravitreal injection, subretinal injection, and nanoparticle-mediated transfection. Viral vectors such as AAV serotypes with Müller cell tropism are preferred for sustained expression, while lipid nanoparticles offer transient but safe delivery for acute interventions.

- describe in vitro and ex vivo production of polypeptides

In vitro production involves culturing transfected mammalian cells to secrete recombinant Dp71 into the medium, followed by purification. Ex vivo production entails transducing patient-derived Müller cells with Dp71-encoding vectors and re-implanting them into the retina, enabling autologous gene therapy.

- describe activators of expression

Activators of expression are small molecules or biologics that enhance transcription of the Dp71 gene by modulating epigenetic regulators, transcription factors, or promoter activity. These include histone acetyltransferase activators, demethylase inhibitors, or synthetic enhancer RNAs designed to boost Dp71 expression in Müller cells.

- describe therapeutic compositions and formulations

Therapeutic compositions comprise a therapeutically effective amount of a Dp71-enhancing agent, such as a nucleic acid construct, polypeptide, or small molecule, formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients to ensure stability, bioavailability, and retinal penetration.

- describe pharmaceutically acceptable excipients and carriers

Pharmaceutically acceptable excipients and carriers include sterile saline, polyethylene glycol, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, dextran, and polysorbate 80, which are compatible with ocular administration and do not induce inflammation or toxicity.

- describe unit administration forms

Unit administration forms include single-dose vials, pre-filled syringes, sustained-release implants, and nanoparticle suspensions designed for intravitreal or subretinal injection, ensuring precise dosing and prolonged therapeutic effect.

- describe oral, sublingual, and subcutaneous administration forms

Oral, sublingual, and subcutaneous administration forms are less preferred due to limited retinal bioavailability but may be utilized for systemic modulation of Dp71 expression via circulating factors, particularly in cases of widespread glial dysfunction.

- describe intramuscular, intravenous, and transdermal administration forms

Intramuscular, intravenous, and transdermal administration forms are employed for systemic delivery of gene therapies or small molecules that cross the blood-retinal barrier, although their efficacy is limited compared to direct ocular delivery.

- describe topical, intraperitoneal, and intranasal administration forms

Topical administration is not effective for retinal delivery due to the blood-retinal barrier. Intraperitoneal and intranasal routes may be used for systemic delivery of neuroprotective agents that indirectly support glial function, but are not primary routes for Dp71-targeted therapy.

- describe rectal administration forms

Rectal administration forms are not relevant to ocular therapy and are excluded from the scope of this invention.

- describe injectable solutions and dispersions

## EXAMPLE

- introduce Dp71-null mice

Dp71-null mice were generated by homologous recombination in embryonic stem cells, resulting in the replacement of the Dp71-specific exon with a β-gal-neomycin resistance cassette, thereby abolishing expression of the Dp71 protein while preserving other dystrophin isoforms.

- describe control mice

Control mice were age- and strain-matched C57BL/6J mice obtained from Charles River Laboratories, exhibiting normal Dp71 expression, Kir4.1 and AQP4 localization, and retinal vascular permeability.

- outline animal use guidelines

All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals, approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee, and followed the Association for Research in Vision and Ophthalmology Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision Research.

- introduce antibodies

Monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies against Dp71, Kir4.1, AQP4, β-dystroglycan, GFAP, and utrophin were sourced from commercial suppliers and validated for specificity in retinal tissue.

- describe antibody sources

Antibodies were sourced from Sigma-Aldrich, Novocastra, Alomone Labs, and previously characterized laboratory reagents, with each batch tested for batch-to-batch consistency and lack of cross-reactivity.

- outline induction of retinal detachment

Retinal detachment was induced by subretinal injection of sodium hyaluronate under anesthesia, with contralateral eyes serving as sham controls.

- describe anesthesia and pupil dilation

Anesthesia was induced by intraperitoneal injection of ketamine and xylazine, and pupils were dilated topically with tropicamide prior to surgery.

- outline subretinal injection

Subretinal injection was performed using a fine glass micropipette inserted through the sclera and retina, delivering 1 µL of sodium hyaluronate to create a localized detachment.

- exclude animals with complications

Animals exhibiting lens injury, vitreous hemorrhage, or postoperative infection were excluded from analysis to ensure data integrity.

- outline immunohistochemistry

Immunohistochemistry was performed on cryosections of fixed retinal tissue using standard protocols involving permeabilization, blocking, primary and secondary antibody incubation, and fluorescent mounting.

- describe fixation and cryoprotection

Eyes were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for one hour, cryoprotected in 30% sucrose, embedded in OCT compound, and stored at −80°C until sectioning.

- outline sectioning and mounting

Retinal sections of 10 µm thickness were cut on a cryostat and mounted on SuperFrost Plus slides for staining.

- describe permeabilization and blocking

Sections were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 and blocked with 3% bovine serum albumin and 0.1% Tween 20 in PBS for one hour.

- outline primary and secondary antibody incubation

Primary antibodies were applied overnight at 4°C, followed by Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies for one hour at room temperature.

- describe mounting and viewing

Sections were mounted with Fluorsave reagent and visualized using a Leica DM5000B microscope equipped with a digital camera.

- outline Western blot analysis

Retinal protein extracts were resolved on NuPAGE gradient gels, transferred to PVDF membranes, and probed with primary and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies.

- describe protein extraction and gel resolution

Proteins were extracted in RIPA buffer, quantified by BCA assay, and loaded at 20 µg per lane for electrophoresis.

- outline membrane blocking and antibody incubation

Membranes were blocked in 5% non-fat milk and incubated with primary antibodies overnight, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies.

- describe chemiluminescence detection

Chemiluminescence was detected using ECL reagent and documented on X-ray film or digital imager.

- outline quantitative RT-PCR analysis

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol, reverse transcribed, and amplified using SYBR Green chemistry on a LightCycler instrument.

- describe RNA extraction and reverse transcription

RNA was treated with DNase, quantified spectrophotometrically, and reverse transcribed using SuperScript II and random hexamers.

- outline PCR amplification and data analysis

PCR amplification was performed in triplicate, and relative expression was calculated using the ΔCt method with β-actin as reference.

- describe glial cell isolation and electrophysiological recordings

Müller cells were isolated by papain digestion and trituration, and whole-cell patch-clamp recordings were performed using standard protocols.

- introduce retinal detachment model

The retinal detachment model was validated by GFAP upregulation, Kir4.1 redistribution, and AQP4 downregulation in wild-type animals.

- describe immunohistochemistry results

Immunohistochemistry confirmed loss of Dp71 and Kir4.1 polarization in Dp71-null mice and reduced AQP4 intensity in detached retinas.

- analyze Western blot and real-time PCR results

Western blot and qRT-PCR confirmed a 50% reduction in Dp71 protein and mRNA in detached retinas, with no further reduction in Dp71-null mice.

- discuss Kir4.1 protein levels

Kir4.1 protein levels remained unchanged, but its localization was disrupted, indicating post-translational dysregulation.

- describe AQP4 distribution and expression

AQP4 expression was reduced by 30% in detached retinas and was diffusely distributed in Dp71-null mice.

- discuss utrophin localization and expression

Utrophin was upregulated at the inner limiting membrane in detached retinas but did not compensate for Dp71 loss.

- summarize results on Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4

Dp71 deficiency leads to mislocalization of Kir4.1 and downregulation of AQP4, resulting in impaired potassium buffering and water transport.

- introduce Dp71-null mice model

The Dp71-null mouse model exhibits baseline increases in retinal vascular permeability without external insult.

- describe Kir4.1 and AQP4 in Dp71-null mice

Kir4.1 and AQP4 are diffusely distributed in Müller cells of Dp71-null mice, lacking polarized enrichment at endfeet.

Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings revealed reduced inward potassium current densities in Dp71-null Müller cells.

- analyze K+ current amplitudes

K+ current amplitudes were similar in magnitude but reduced in density due to increased membrane surface area.

- discuss current densities

Current densities were significantly lower in Dp71-null mice, indicating fewer functional channels per unit membrane area.

- test for cellular hypertrophy

Müller cells in Dp71-null mice exhibited increased membrane capacitance, consistent with cellular hypertrophy.

- analyze membrane capacitance

Membrane capacitance was elevated in Dp71-null mice, reflecting increased surface area due to process extension.

- discuss K+ current densities

Reduced K+ current densities correlate with impaired spatial buffering capacity and increased susceptibility to edema.

Osmotic swelling was significantly greater in Dp71-null Müller cells under hypotonic conditions.

- analyze Müller cell somata size

Müller cell somata in Dp71-null mice were 18% larger than in controls under baseline conditions.

Ba2+ induced additional swelling in wild-type cells but had no additive effect in Dp71-null mice, indicating maximal channel dysfunction.

- analyze Müller cell swelling in Dp71-null mice

Swelling was not further increased by detachment, confirming that Dp71 loss is sufficient to induce maximal glial dysfunction.

- test for adenosine effects on Müller cells

Adenosine significantly reduced swelling in Dp71-null Müller cells, implicating purinergic signaling in edema formation.

- analyze effects of phospholipase A2 and cyclooxygenase inhibitors

Inhibitors of phospholipase A2 and cyclooxygenase prevented osmotic swelling, suggesting inflammatory mediators contribute to barrier failure.

Evans blue extravasation was fourfold higher in Dp71-null mice compared to controls, demonstrating increased blood-retinal barrier permeability.

- summarize findings on Dp71 and Müller cells

Dp71 is essential for maintaining Müller cell polarity, potassium buffering, water homeostasis, and retinal vascular integrity.

- discuss role of Dp71 in retinal potassium and water homeostasis

Dp71 anchors Kir4.1 and AQP4 at Müller cell endfeet, enabling efficient potassium clearance and osmotic water drainage.

- discuss BRB function and retinal vascular permeability

Loss of Dp71 disrupts the glial-vascular interface, leading to increased vascular permeability independent of endothelial damage.

- discuss osmotic Müller cell swelling and inflammation

Osmotic swelling and inflammatory mediator production are interlinked, with Dp71 deficiency triggering a chronic low-grade inflammatory state.

- discuss pathomechanisms of edema formation

Edema formation results from a combination of impaired fluid clearance and increased vascular leakage, both driven by glial dysfunction.

- discuss role of Müller cells in BRB maintenance

Müller cells are not passive bystanders but active regulators of the blood-retinal barrier through structural and functional coupling with endothelial cells.

- discuss potential compensatory mechanisms in Dp71-null mice

Compensatory upregulation of utrophin occurs but is insufficient to restore channel localization or barrier function.

- conclude on Dp71-null mice as a model for retinal edemas

The Dp71-null mouse is a robust, genetically defined model of retinal edema that isolates glial dysfunction as the primary driver of vascular permeability, providing a powerful platform for therapeutic development.
