# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase S (EndoS)

The present invention relates to novel mutant enzymes derived from endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase S (EndoS) originating from Streptococcus pyogenes, and more specifically to engineered variants with enhanced transglycosylation activity and substantially reduced hydrolysis activity toward N-linked glycan structures on immunoglobulin G (IgG) and other Fc-containing molecules. These mutant enzymes are designed for use in the precise and efficient remodeling of glycoforms on therapeutic antibodies and other glycoproteins, enabling the site-specific attachment of homogeneous oligosaccharide moieties without the need for prior chemical synthesis of activated glycan donors. The invention further encompasses recombinant nucleic acid constructs encoding such mutant enzymes, host cells expressing them, and methods for their large-scale production and application in chemoenzymatic glycan remodeling processes.

## BACKGROUND ART

- introduce glycoproteins

Glycoproteins are proteins that contain covalently attached carbohydrate moieties, which play critical roles in protein folding, stability, solubility, and biological function. Among the most clinically significant glycoproteins are monoclonal antibodies, which constitute a major class of biopharmaceuticals used in the treatment of cancer, autoimmune disorders, and infectious diseases. The biological activity of these antibodies is profoundly influenced by the structure of the conserved N-linked glycan attached to the asparagine residue at position 297 (N297) within the Fc region of the heavy chain.

- describe importance of sugar chains

The composition and branching pattern of these sugar chains, including the presence or absence of core fucose, terminal sialic acids, galactose residues, and bisecting N-acetylglucosamine, directly modulate effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), and serum half-life. Homogeneous glycoforms are therefore highly desirable for ensuring consistent therapeutic efficacy and safety profiles.

- explain posttranslational modification

Posttranslational glycosylation occurs in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus of eukaryotic cells and is subject to variability depending on cell line, culture conditions, and bioprocessing parameters. This heterogeneity poses challenges in the manufacturing of biotherapeutics and limits the ability to establish structure–function relationships for specific glycoforms.

- introduce antibodies

Immunoglobulin G (IgG) is the predominant antibody isotype in human serum and serves as the backbone for most therapeutic monoclonal antibodies. Its Fc domain contains a single conserved N-glycosylation site at Asn297, which is essential for maintaining the structural integrity required for Fcγ receptor binding and complement activation.

- describe effects of sugar chains on antibodies

The glycan at N297 is typically a biantennary complex-type structure with a core of two N-acetylglucosamine and three mannose residues, often extended with galactose and terminated with sialic acid or fucose. Removal of core fucose enhances ADCC, while increased sialylation reduces inflammatory activity. Precise control over these modifications is therefore a major goal in antibody engineering.

- introduce endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidases

Endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidases (ENGases) are glycoside hydrolases that cleave the β-1,4-glycosidic bond between the two N-acetylglucosamine residues of the chitobiose core of N-linked glycans. These enzymes have been exploited for the deglycosylation and subsequent remodeling of glycoproteins through transglycosylation reactions.

- describe EndoA, EndoD, EndoM, EndoH, EndoF2, EndoE, and EndoS

EndoA, EndoD, EndoM, EndoH, EndoF2, EndoE, and EndoS represent distinct members of the ENGase family, each exhibiting unique substrate specificities. EndoH acts on high-mannose and hybrid glycans, EndoF2 targets biantennary complex-type glycans lacking core fucose, EndoM exhibits broad specificity but hydrolyzes core-fucosylated glycans, and EndoS is uniquely specific for the Fc N-glycan of IgG regardless of fucosylation status.

- introduce EndoS D233Q

The D233Q mutant of EndoS, first reported by Huang et al., retains transglycosylation capability while exhibiting significantly reduced hydrolytic activity toward the reaction product. This variant enables the transfer of glycan oxazolines to deglycosylated IgG, facilitating the generation of homogeneous glycoforms.

- describe properties of EndoS D233Q

EndoS D233Q demonstrates a marked reduction in hydrolysis of the transglycosylated product compared to wild-type EndoS, yet its transglycosylation efficiency remains suboptimal, requiring high molar excesses of glycan oxazoline donor (typically >20 equivalents) and prolonged reaction times to achieve high yields. Furthermore, residual hydrolysis activity limits the maximum attainable conversion and complicates process scalability.

- introduce EndoS2

EndoS2, a homolog from Streptococcus pyogenes serotype M49, has been engineered to exhibit improved transglycosylation efficiency, particularly through the D184M mutation. However, its substrate specificity differs slightly from EndoS, and its expression yield and stability in bacterial systems remain less favorable for industrial applications.

- describe limitations of known EndoS enzymes

Existing EndoS variants, including D233Q and its derivatives, suffer from slow reaction kinetics, low catalytic efficiency under physiological conditions, and insufficient suppression of hydrolytic side reactions. These limitations necessitate the use of large quantities of expensive and unstable glycan oxazoline donors, making large-scale manufacturing economically unfeasible.

- describe need for technical improvement

There exists a critical need for EndoS variants with substantially enhanced transglycosylation activity, minimal residual hydrolysis, and compatibility with low donor-to-acceptor ratios, thereby enabling cost-effective, scalable, and industrially viable glycoengineering of therapeutic antibodies without reliance on chemical synthesis of activated donors.

- summarize current situation

Current methodologies for glycan remodeling rely either on inefficient enzymatic transglycosylation using suboptimal EndoS mutants or on labor-intensive chemical synthesis of glycan oxazolines. Neither approach provides a robust, high-yield, and broadly applicable solution for the production of next-generation antibody therapeutics with precisely defined glycoforms.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

- describe technical problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inability of existing endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase S variants to efficiently catalyze transglycosylation reactions with low molar equivalents of glycan donor while simultaneously suppressing hydrolysis of the newly formed glycoprotein product. This limitation impedes the development of scalable, cost-effective, and reproducible methods for the production of homogeneous glycoforms on therapeutic antibodies.

- summarize technical problem

Despite advances in enzyme engineering, no EndoS variant has been identified that combines high transglycosylation efficiency (>90%) with negligible hydrolysis activity under conditions using donor-to-acceptor ratios of five or less, thereby enabling practical, large-scale glycan remodeling without the need for stoichiometric excesses of unstable oxazoline donors.

### Solution to Problem

- introduce novel mutant enzymes

The present invention solves this problem through the identification and characterization of novel mutant forms of EndoS that possess dramatically improved transglycosylation activity and minimal hydrolytic side reactions. These mutants are generated by introducing specific amino acid substitutions into the D233Q background, resulting in enzymes with enhanced catalytic efficiency and product stability.

- describe mutations

The mutations are strategically selected based on structural analysis of the EndoS active site and its interactions with the glycan substrate, focusing on residues involved in donor binding, acceptor recognition, and transition state stabilization.

- introduce EndoS mutant enzyme

The novel mutant enzyme of the present invention is a variant of EndoS comprising the D233Q mutation and at least one additional mutation selected from the group consisting of Q303L, E350Q, D405A, Y402F, D279S, Y348F, R406K, H122R, P184A, and combinations thereof.

- describe additional mutations

These additional mutations are not merely additive but synergistic, altering the enzyme’s binding pocket geometry, electrostatic environment, and hydrophobic interactions to favor transglycosylation over hydrolysis.

- describe specific mutations

In particular, the combination of D233Q with Q303L results in a mutant enzyme that achieves transglycosylation efficiencies exceeding 96% within 24 hours at a donor-to-acceptor ratio of five, while maintaining hydrolysis below 5% over the same period.

- describe specific mutations

Another preferred embodiment comprises the D233Q/D405A double mutant, which demonstrates rapid transglycosylation kinetics, achieving 78% conversion within two hours and negligible hydrolysis throughout the reaction course.

- describe specific mutations

The D233Q/E350Q mutant exhibits comparable performance, with transglycosylation efficiency reaching 97% and hydrolysis undetectable beyond 24 hours.

- describe specific mutations

The triple mutant D233Q/Q303L/E350Q further enhances product stability, achieving near-complete conversion without detectable hydrolysis even after 48 hours of incubation.

- describe specific mutations

The D233Q/Y402F/D405A triple mutant shows no detectable hydrolysis over 48 hours, although its maximum transglycosylation efficiency is limited to approximately 75%, indicating that certain combinations optimize stability at the expense of catalytic rate.

- describe specific mutations

Mutations at positions H122, P184, D279, Y282, Q303, Y348, E350, Y402, D405, and R406 are identified as critical determinants of transglycosylation efficiency and hydrolysis suppression.

- describe specific mutations

Substitutions at these positions alter the enzyme’s affinity for the glycan donor, its ability to retain the glycosyl-enzyme intermediate, and its steric accessibility to water molecules that would otherwise hydrolyze the product.

- describe specific mutations

The most preferred embodiments include the double mutants D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, and D233Q/D405A, as well as the triple mutant D233Q/Q303L/E350Q.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

- describe advantages of invention

The mutant enzymes of the present invention enable highly efficient transglycosylation of IgG and other Fc-containing molecules at donor-to-acceptor ratios as low as five, significantly reducing the cost and complexity of glycan donor synthesis. They exhibit negligible hydrolysis of the transglycosylated product, allowing for complete conversion without the need for reaction quenching or product purification during synthesis. These enzymes are compatible with one-pot enzymatic cascades using auxiliary ENGases such as Endo-M N175Q or Endo-CC N180H, eliminating the need for pre-synthesis of glycan oxazolines entirely. Their high expression yield in bacterial systems, ease of purification, and stability under standard reaction conditions make them ideal for industrial-scale manufacturing of next-generation biotherapeutics with precisely defined glycoforms.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

- introduce notation of amino acids

In the context of this invention, amino acid residues are designated using the standard single-letter code as defined by the IUPAC-IUB Joint Commission on Biochemical Nomenclature. Mutations are described relative to the wild-type EndoS sequence from Streptococcus pyogenes (GenBank accession number AY174969.1).

- define N297-linked sugar chains

The N297-linked sugar chain refers to the conserved N-glycan attached to the asparagine residue at position 297 of the Fc region of immunoglobulin G, which consists of a chitobiose core (GlcNAcβ1-4GlcNAc) linked to a trimannosyl core and extended with variable antennae terminating in galactose, sialic acid, or fucose.

- describe structure of N297-linked sugar chains

The canonical structure comprises a biantennary complex-type glycan with a core of two N-acetylglucosamine and three mannose residues, often bearing a core fucose linked α1,6 to the innermost GlcNAc, and terminal galactose and sialic acid residues on the antennae.

- introduce sugar donors

Sugar donors in the context of this invention refer to activated glycan structures capable of serving as substrates for transglycosylation, including glycan oxazolines, sialoglycopeptides (SGPs), and glycopeptide intermediates generated in situ by auxiliary ENGases.

- describe sugar donors

Preferred sugar donors include sialoglycopeptides derived from egg yolk or other biological sources, as well as chemically or enzymatically generated SG-Asn and SG-oxazoline structures, wherein the glycan moiety is linked to a peptide backbone or directly to an oxazoline ring.

- introduce acceptor molecules

Acceptor molecules are glycoproteins or Fc-containing molecules that have been deglycosylated at the N297 site, thereby exposing the GlcNAc residue for transglycosylation.

- describe acceptor molecules

Preferred acceptor molecules include trastuzumab, rituximab, infliximab, and other therapeutic monoclonal antibodies, as well as Fc-fusion proteins and engineered antibody fragments containing the conserved N297 glycosylation site.

- introduce EndoS

EndoS is an endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase secreted by Streptococcus pyogenes that specifically cleaves the N-glycan at Asn297 of human IgG, leaving a single N-acetylglucosamine residue attached to the protein backbone.

- describe structure of EndoS

EndoS comprises an N-terminal catalytic domain and a C-terminal binding domain, with the catalytic site located in a deep pocket that accommodates the chitobiose core of the N-glycan. The catalytic residues include D233, E235, and Q303, which participate in substrate binding and transition state stabilization.

- describe hydrolysis activity of EndoS

Wild-type EndoS catalyzes the hydrolytic cleavage of the β-1,4-glycosidic bond between the two GlcNAc residues of the chitobiose core, releasing the glycan and leaving a GlcNAc residue on the protein.

- describe transglycosylation activity of EndoS

Under controlled conditions, EndoS can also catalyze transglycosylation by transferring an oligosaccharide from a donor substrate to the GlcNAc acceptor on the deglycosylated IgG, forming a new glycosidic linkage.

- introduce EndoS D233Q

EndoS D233Q is a previously described mutant in which aspartic acid at position 233 is replaced with glutamine, resulting in reduced hydrolysis activity while retaining transglycosylation capability.

- describe EndoS D233Q

This mutant forms a stable glycosyl-enzyme intermediate but exhibits slow kinetics and incomplete suppression of hydrolysis, limiting its utility in industrial applications.

- introduce EndoS-related enzymes

EndoS-related enzymes include homologs such as EndoS2, Endo-M, Endo-CC, and other ENGases from bacterial and fungal sources that share structural and mechanistic similarities with EndoS.

- describe EndoS-related enzymes

These enzymes differ in substrate specificity, pH optimum, temperature stability, and catalytic efficiency, and are employed in combination with the mutant EndoS enzymes of the present invention to enable one-pot transglycosylation strategies.

- describe mutant enzymes of the present invention

The mutant enzymes of the present invention are characterized by a combination of mutations that collectively enhance transglycosylation efficiency, suppress hydrolysis, and improve catalytic turnover under low donor concentrations.

- describe characteristics of mutant enzymes of the present invention

These enzymes exhibit transglycosylation efficiencies exceeding 90% at donor-to-acceptor ratios of five or less, hydrolysis activities below 5% over 48 hours, and retention of substrate specificity for IgG Fc glycans.

### <Mutant Enzyme of the Present Invention>

- introduce mutant enzymes of the present invention

The mutant enzymes of the present invention are defined by the presence of the D233Q substitution in combination with one or more additional mutations at positions H122, P184, D279, Y282, Q303, Y348, E350, Y402, D405, and R406.

- define additional mutations

Additional mutations refer to amino acid substitutions introduced at positions other than D233Q that enhance the transglycosylation-to-hydrolysis ratio.

- describe group of positions of essential additional mutations

The essential positions for additional mutations are H122, P184, D279, Q303, E350, Y402, D405, and R406, each of which contributes to donor binding, transition state stabilization, or exclusion of water from the active site.

- describe characteristics of mutant enzymes of the present invention

These enzymes display a marked increase in transglycosylation rate and yield, with minimal product degradation, enabling single-step, high-yield glycan remodeling under industrially feasible conditions.

- describe hydrolysis activity of mutant enzymes

Hydrolysis activity is reduced to undetectable levels in the most preferred mutants, with less than 2% hydrolysis observed after 48 hours of incubation under standard reaction conditions.

- describe transglycosylation activity of mutant enzymes

Transglycosylation activity is enhanced to levels exceeding 95% conversion within 24 hours when using a five-fold molar excess of glycan donor relative to IgG acceptor.

- describe regions important for transglycosylation activity

The regions surrounding the catalytic pocket, particularly the loops near residues Q303, E350, and D405, are critical for stabilizing the glycosyl-enzyme intermediate and preventing nucleophilic attack by water.

- introduce related enzymes

Related enzymes include Endo-M N175Q, Endo-CC N180H, and other engineered ENGases capable of generating glycan intermediates from complex glycopeptides.

- describe related enzymes

These enzymes act synergistically with the mutant EndoS enzymes to enable one-pot transglycosylation without the need for pre-synthesized oxazoline donors.

- describe amino acid alterations

Amino acid alterations include substitutions such as Q303L, E350Q, D405A, Y402F, H122R, P184A, and R406K, each selected to optimize donor binding and reduce hydrolysis.

- describe positions for additional mutations

The positions for additional mutations are selected from the group consisting of H122, P184, D279, Y282, Q303, Y348, E350, Y402, D405, and R406.

- describe number of positions for additional mutations

The mutant enzymes may contain one, two, three, or four additional mutations, with combinations of two or three mutations yielding the most favorable balance of activity and stability.

- describe mutant amino acid at H122

The mutant amino acid at position H122 is preferably arginine, lysine, or glutamine, which alters the electrostatic environment near the α1–3 arm of the glycan.

- describe mutant amino acid at P184

The mutant amino acid at position P184 is preferably alanine or glycine, increasing loop flexibility and enhancing donor access to the catalytic site.

- describe mutant amino acid at D279

The mutant amino acid at position D279 is preferably serine or alanine, reducing steric hindrance and improving transition state stabilization.

- describe mutant amino acid at Y282

The mutant amino acid at position Y282 is preferably phenylalanine or alanine, modulating hydrophobic interactions with the glycan core.

- describe mutant amino acid at Q303

The mutant amino acid at position Q303 is preferably leucine, isoleucine, or valine, enhancing hydrophobic packing and reducing water accessibility.

- describe mutant amino acid at Y348

The mutant amino acid at position Y348 is preferably phenylalanine or tryptophan, stabilizing the glycan through π-stacking interactions.

- describe mutant amino acid at E350

The mutant amino acid at position E350 is preferably glutamine, asparagine, or alanine, neutralizing negative charge and reducing repulsion with the glycan oxazoline.

- describe mutant amino acid at Y402

The mutant amino acid at position Y402 is preferably phenylalanine or alanine, reducing steric bulk near the branched mannose residues.

- describe mutant amino acid at D405

The mutant amino acid at position D405 is preferably alanine, serine, or asparagine, eliminating a negative charge that may destabilize the transition state.

- describe mutant amino acid at R406

The mutant amino acid at position R406 is preferably lysine or glutamine, modulating charge interactions with the glycan’s terminal sialic acid.

- describe combination of mutations

Combinations of mutations are selected to synergistically enhance transglycosylation while suppressing hydrolysis, with the most effective combinations including D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, D233Q/D405A, and D233Q/Q303L/E350Q.

- provide examples of combinations of additional mutations

Examples of preferred combinations include: D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, D233Q/D405A, D233Q/Q303L/E350Q, D233Q/Y402F/D405A, and D233Q/Q303L/D405A.

- describe preferred mutant enzymes of the present invention

The most preferred mutant enzymes of the present invention are EndoS D233Q/Q303L, EndoS D233Q/E350Q, and EndoS D233Q/Q303L/E350Q, each exhibiting transglycosylation efficiencies greater than 95% and hydrolysis below 2% after 48 hours.

### <Gene, Host Cell, Method of Producing Enzyme>

- provide recombinant gene encoding mutant enzyme

The present invention provides a recombinant nucleic acid molecule encoding the mutant EndoS enzyme, wherein the nucleotide sequence has been modified to encode the amino acid substitutions described herein.

- describe gene construct and host cell

The recombinant gene is operably linked to a promoter and terminator sequence suitable for expression in prokaryotic hosts, and is typically cloned into an expression vector such as pGEX-4T-3 for fusion with a glutathione-S-transferase tag to facilitate purification.

- outline method of producing mutant enzyme

The method comprises transforming a suitable host cell, such as Escherichia coli BL21(DE3), with the recombinant plasmid, inducing expression with isopropyl-β-d-1-thiogalactopyranoside, harvesting cells, lysing them, and purifying the fusion protein using affinity chromatography followed by tag cleavage and size-exclusion chromatography.

- detail nucleotide sequence modifications

Nucleotide sequence modifications are introduced via site-directed mutagenesis using primers encoding the desired amino acid substitutions, with verification by DNA sequencing.

- explain collection and purification of mutant enzyme

The mutant enzyme is collected from the soluble fraction of the cell lysate, purified using glutathione affinity resin, cleaved with thrombin or PreScission protease, and further purified by hydroxyapatite or ion-exchange chromatography to yield homogenous enzyme preparations with purity exceeding 95%.

### <Sugar Remodeling>

- introduce sugar remodeling method

The present invention further provides a method for the enzymatic remodeling of glycoforms on IgG and other Fc-containing molecules through a two-step process involving deglycosylation followed by transglycosylation.

- define sugar remodeling

Sugar remodeling refers to the enzymatic replacement of a native N-linked glycan with a defined oligosaccharide structure using an endoglycosidase-mediated transglycosylation reaction.

- describe IgG or molecule comprising Fc region

The molecule to be remodeled is an IgG antibody or a fusion protein comprising the Fc region of IgG, wherein the N297-linked glycan has been removed to expose the GlcNAc residue.

- outline method of producing acceptor molecule

The acceptor molecule is produced by incubating the native antibody with wild-type EndoS or another ENGase under controlled conditions to remove the native glycan, followed by purification using protein A affinity chromatography and hydroxyapatite chromatography.

- detail ENGase treatment

ENGase treatment is performed in phosphate or Tris buffer at pH 6.0–7.5, at 30–37°C for 2–6 hours, with enzyme-to-antibody ratios ranging from 1:10 to 1:50.

- describe sugar donor molecules

Sugar donor molecules include glycan oxazolines, sialoglycopeptides (SGPs), SG-Asn, and glycopeptides generated in situ by auxiliary ENGases such as Endo-M N175Q or Endo-CC N180H.

- specify preferred sugar donor structure

The preferred sugar donor structure is a sialoglycopeptide derived from egg yolk, containing a sialylated biantennary N-glycan attached to a peptide backbone.

- outline hydrolysis reaction conditions

Hydrolysis reaction conditions involve incubation of the antibody with wild-type EndoS at 37°C in 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer, pH 7.4, for 4 hours, followed by immediate purification to prevent re-glycosylation.

- describe reaction temperature and time

Transglycosylation reactions are conducted at temperatures between 28°C and 37°C for periods ranging from 2 to 48 hours, depending on the mutant enzyme and donor concentration.

- detail monitoring of hydrolysis reaction

Hydrolysis reaction progress is monitored by SDS-PAGE or capillary electrophoresis using the Experion Pro260 system, with quantification of deglycosylated heavy chain relative to total protein.

- outline transglycosylation reaction conditions

Transglycosylation reactions are carried out in 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer, pH 7.4, at 30°C, with 1–5 molar equivalents of sugar donor relative to acceptor and 0.1–1 μg/mL mutant EndoS enzyme.

- describe monitoring of transglycosylation reaction

Transglycosylation progress is monitored by measuring the appearance of the glycosylated heavy chain band using electrophoretic analysis, with quantification based on peak area ratios.

## EXAMPLES

- introduce examples of embodiments of the present invention

The following examples illustrate the preparation, characterization, and application of the mutant EndoS enzymes and associated methods of glycan remodeling as described herein.

- describe preparation examples of EndoS mutant enzymes, sugar donor, and acceptor molecule

EndoS mutant enzymes were expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) using pGEX-4T-3 vectors, purified via glutathione affinity chromatography, and cleaved to remove the GST tag. Sugar donors were prepared from egg yolk sialoglycopeptides or chemically synthesized SG-oxazoline. Acceptor molecules were generated by deglycosylation of trastuzumab using wild-type EndoS.

- provide measurement examples of hydrolysis and transglycosylation activities

Hydrolysis and transglycosylation activities were quantified using the Experion Pro260 Analysis System, with reaction progress monitored at multiple time points and percent conversion calculated based on relative peak areas of deglycosylated and glycosylated heavy chain species.

### Example 1

- design EndoS mutant enzymes

A panel of 120 EndoS mutants was designed by introducing single, double, and triple mutations into the D233Q background, targeting residues H122, P184, D279, Y282, Q303, Y348, E350, Y402, D405, and R406.

- express EndoS mutant enzymes

Each mutant gene was cloned into pGEX-4T-3 and expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) cultures grown at 16°C for 20 hours following induction with 0.1 mM IPTG.

- purify EndoS mutant enzymes

Cells were lysed by sonication, and the soluble fraction was purified using Glutathione Sepharose 4B, followed by thrombin cleavage and size-exclusion chromatography.

- describe preparation of reaction solution of EndoS mutant enzymes

Reaction solutions were prepared by diluting purified enzyme to 10–20 μg/mL in 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer, pH 7.4, supplemented with 1 mM CaCl₂.

- detail expression of EndoS mutant enzymes

Expression levels were comparable to wild-type EndoS, with yields ranging from 2 to 7 mg per liter of culture.

- detail purification of EndoS mutant enzymes

Purified enzyme preparations exhibited >95% purity as assessed by SDS-PAGE and Coomassie staining.

- describe yields of EndoS mutant enzymes

Yields of purified mutant enzymes ranged from 1.5 to 6.8 mg per liter of culture, with no significant difference in solubility or stability compared to wild-type.

- prepare EndoS mutant enzyme solutions

Enzyme solutions were stored at 4°C in PBS with 10% glycerol and used within two weeks without loss of activity.

### Example 2

- prepare reaction solution of SG-Oxa

Sialoglycan oxazoline (SG-Oxa) was synthesized from sialoglycopeptide using trifluoromethanesulfonic acid under anhydrous conditions.

- produce SG-Oxa

The oxazoline was purified by reverse-phase HPLC and characterized by mass spectrometry.

- confirm structure of SG-Oxa

The molecular weight of SG-Oxa was confirmed as 1,420 Da by ESI-MS, with a purity of 98% as determined by HPLC.

### Example 3

- prepare (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-Trastuzumab

Trastuzumab was incubated with wild-type EndoS at 37°C for 4 hours to remove the native glycan, yielding (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-trastuzumab.

- incubate Trastuzumab with wild type EndoS

The reaction was carried out in 50 mM phosphate buffer, pH 6.0, with a 1:20 enzyme-to-antibody molar ratio.

- purify (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-Trastuzumab

The product was purified using rProteinA affinity chromatography followed by hydroxyapatite chromatography.

- detail purification with affinity column

The antibody was bound to HiTrap rProteinA HP, washed with PBS, and eluted with 0.1 M glycine-HCl, pH 2.7.

- detail purification with hydroxyapatite column

Eluate was neutralized and applied to a CHT Type I column, washed with 20 mM phosphate buffer, and eluted with a linear gradient of 20–200 mM phosphate.

- confirm structure of (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-Trastuzumab

LC-ESI-MS analysis confirmed the mass of the deglycosylated heavy chain as 49,505.5 Da, consistent with the expected mass of a single GlcNAc residue at N297.

### Example 4

- measure hydrolysis activity of EndoS mutant enzymes

Hydrolysis activity was measured by incubating 10 μg of each mutant enzyme with 500 μg of trastuzumab in 50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4, at 30°C.

- prepare substrate solution for hydrolysis reaction

Substrate solution contained 1 mg/mL trastuzumab in reaction buffer.

- incubate substrate with EndoS mutant enzymes

Reactions were incubated for up to 48 hours, with samples taken at 1, 2, 4, 8, 24, and 48 hours.

- measure degree of progress of hydrolysis reaction

Progress was measured by Experion Pro260 analysis, with deglycosylated heavy chain quantified as a percentage of total heavy chain.

- calculate percent hydrolysis

Percent hydrolysis was calculated as the ratio of deglycosylated peak area to total heavy chain peak area multiplied by 100.

- measure transglycosylation activity of EndoS mutant enzymes

Transglycosylation activity was measured by incubating 16 μg of mutant enzyme with 800 μg of (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-trastuzumab and 53.5 μg of SG-Oxa (5 eq) in 50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4.

- prepare acceptor molecule for transglycosylation reaction

The acceptor was purified (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-trastuzumab from Example 3.

- incubate acceptor molecule with EndoS mutant enzymes and sugar donor

Reactions were incubated at 30°C for up to 48 hours, with sampling at the same time points.

- measure degree of progress of transglycosylation reaction

Progress was measured by Experion Pro260 analysis, with glycosylated heavy chain quantified as a percentage of total heavy chain.

- calculate percent transglycosylation

Percent transglycosylation was calculated as the ratio of glycosylated peak area to total heavy chain peak area multiplied by 100.

- purify and confirm structure of transglycosylation product

Products were purified using rProteinA and CHT columns, and structure was confirmed by LC-ESI-MS, which revealed a mass increase of 2,000 Da consistent with the attachment of a sialylated biantennary glycan.
