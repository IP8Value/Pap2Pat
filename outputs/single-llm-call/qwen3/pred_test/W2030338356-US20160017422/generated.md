# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- introduce novel method for identifying biomarker genes

The present invention relates to a novel method for identifying biomarker genes in cumulus cells that enable the objective assessment of oocyte competence and the prediction of pregnancy outcomes following assisted reproductive technologies. This method leverages the molecular signature of cumulus cells, which are somatic cells intimately associated with the oocyte during follicular development and maturation, to determine the developmental potential of individual oocytes prior to fertilization and embryo transfer. Unlike conventional morphological grading systems that rely on subjective visual evaluation of embryo appearance, this invention utilizes quantitative gene expression profiling to identify a panel of biomarker genes whose expression levels correlate strongly with the likelihood of successful implantation and live birth. The biomarker genes identified herein are not merely associated with general cellular activity but are functionally linked to key biological processes including steroidogenesis, oxidative stress response, and cell signaling pathways critical for oocyte maturation and embryo viability. The invention provides a reproducible, standardized, and biologically grounded approach to selecting the single most viable oocyte for transfer in single embryo transfer protocols, thereby improving clinical outcomes while minimizing the risks associated with multiple gestations. The method is applicable across diverse patient populations and independent of variations in ovarian stimulation protocols or embryo culture conditions, making it suitable for integration into routine clinical practice in fertility clinics worldwide.

Single embryo transfer has become the standard of care in assisted reproductive technology to mitigate the significant maternal and neonatal risks associated with multiple pregnancies. However, the success of this approach hinges critically on the accurate selection of the single embryo with the highest implantation potential. Current selection methods primarily rely on morphological assessment of embryos at various stages of development, a technique that is inherently subjective, lacks biological specificity, and demonstrates limited predictive power for live birth outcomes. Alternative approaches have explored the use of gene expression profiles in cumulus cells, which are known to communicate bidirectionally with the oocyte and reflect its metabolic and developmental status. Despite promising early findings, existing studies have suffered from inconsistent results due to heterogeneous patient populations, variable stimulation protocols, differing culture conditions, and a lack of standardized endpoints. Many prior studies have focused on embryo development as a surrogate endpoint, which does not reliably predict successful pregnancy or live birth. Furthermore, inter-patient comparisons have dominated the literature, obscuring the potential for intra-patient discrimination of oocyte quality within the same retrieval cycle. The present invention addresses these limitations by employing a rigorous, multi-step analytical framework that first validates previously identified predictive genes in an independent cohort and then builds novel multiparametric models using both gene expression data and clinically relevant patient and cycle characteristics. The experimental design uniquely incorporates intra-patient comparisons between cumulus cells from oocytes that resulted in pregnancy following frozen embryo transfer and those from sibling oocytes that failed to result in pregnancy during the fresh transfer cycle. This design eliminates confounding variables such as patient age, ovarian response, and stimulation regimen, allowing for the identification of gene expression patterns that are truly indicative of oocyte competence. The invention further validates the predictive power of prior models and systematically constructs enhanced models that integrate multiple biomarkers, achieving significantly improved accuracy, positive and negative predictive values, and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve compared to existing methods.

The invention introduces a method for detecting biomarker gene expression in cumulus cells to evaluate oocyte competence with high precision and clinical relevance. The selection endpoint for this method is live birth, which is the most clinically meaningful outcome in assisted reproduction, rather than intermediate endpoints such as embryo morphology or fertilization rate, which fail to reliably predict successful pregnancy. The method employs exon-level analysis of gene expression to capture isoform-specific transcriptional activity that may be functionally critical for oocyte maturation and developmental competence. Intra-patient comparison is a defining feature of this invention, enabling the identification of gene expression signatures that distinguish between sibling oocytes from the same retrieval cycle with divergent pregnancy outcomes, thereby eliminating inter-patient variability and enhancing diagnostic specificity. Expression levels are considered statistically significant when p-values are less than 0.05, with appropriate correction for multiple comparisons applied where necessary. Alternative embodiments of the invention encompass the use of different combinations of biomarker genes, including but not limited to EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA3, GSTA4, GPX3, GSR, PGR, SASH1, and HSPH1, each of which may serve as a primary or secondary marker depending on the clinical context. Compared to existing methods, this invention demonstrates superior predictive accuracy, higher area under the curve values in receiver operating characteristic analyses, and greater reproducibility across diverse patient populations. The biomarker gene expression model is constructed using stepwise multiparametric regression analysis, which identifies the most predictive combination of genes and adjusts for confounding variables such as age, days of stimulation, and estradiol levels. The overall p-value of the model is determined to ensure statistical robustness. The invention further introduces an in vitro method for evaluating oocyte competence by quantifying the expression level of one or more biomarker genes in cumulus cells collected immediately after oocyte denudation. Expression levels are normalized using a combination of housekeeping genes, including beta-2-microglobulin and ubiquitin C, to account for variations in RNA extraction and amplification efficiency. Oocyte competence is evaluated by comparing the normalized expression levels of the biomarker genes against predefined thresholds derived from validated models. The biomarker gene combination model assesses the correlation between multiple gene expression levels to generate a composite score predictive of pregnancy probability. An alternative method involves the use of protein-level detection of biomarkers via immunohistochemistry or mass spectrometry. The method is applicable to cumulus cells collected from patients undergoing ovarian stimulation for in vitro fertilization, with samples stored in liquid nitrogen prior to analysis. A control or reference sample is defined as cumulus cells from oocytes that did not result in pregnancy, against which expression levels are compared. Preferred combinations of biomarkers include EFNB2 and CAMK1D, EFNB2 and GSTA4, and combinations involving PGR and glutathione-related genes. The in vitro method comprises the steps of collecting cumulus cells after oocyte denudation, extracting total RNA, synthesizing complementary DNA, performing quantitative real-time PCR, normalizing expression levels, and calculating a predictive score based on a validated mathematical model. This method enables the identification of viable oocytes prior to fertilization, allowing for the selection of the single most competent oocyte for transfer. The invention further encompasses the establishment of an oocyte bank wherein cumulus cell samples are archived alongside cryopreserved oocytes or embryos, enabling retrospective analysis and longitudinal tracking of oocyte quality. Oocyte competence is defined as the intrinsic ability of an oocyte to undergo successful fertilization, support embryonic development to the blastocyst stage, and result in a viable pregnancy leading to live birth. The oocytes used in this method are obtained from patients undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation for in vitro fertilization, typically following the administration of recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone in combination with a gonadotropin-releasing hormone antagonist. Oocyte collection is performed 36 hours after human chorionic gonadotropin administration, at which point cumulus cells remain tightly adherent to the oocyte and retain molecular signatures reflective of oocyte quality. Granulosa cells are the broader population of somatic cells lining the ovarian follicle, while cumulus cells are the specialized subset directly surrounding the oocyte and are the exclusive source of biological material used in this invention. Marker gene expression refers to the quantifiable levels of messenger RNA transcripts derived from genes functionally implicated in oocyte maturation, including but not limited to EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA3, GSTA4, GPX3, GSR, PGR, SASH1, and HSPH1. Detection methods include quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction, digital PCR, microarray analysis, RNA sequencing, and protein-based assays such as Western blotting and immunofluorescence. Marker gene expression levels are expressed as normalized relative quantities, calculated using the ΔΔCt method with reference to the geometric mean of two validated housekeeping genes, beta-2-microglobulin and ubiquitin C. Splice variants of HSPH1 include isoforms differing in exon 5 and exon 9 usage; splice variants of CAMK1D include those with alternative splicing in exons 7 and 11; splice variants of NCOA7 involve exon 15 skipping; splice variants of SASH1 include variants with insertions in intron 10; splice variants of MROH9 involve alternative 3’ untranslated regions; splice variants of SPTBN5 include truncations in the spectrin repeat domain; splice variants of GALNTL6 involve differential exon 3 inclusion; and splice variants of DNAH3 include alternative splicing in the dynein heavy chain region. Combinations of splice variants are specified to include those that result in functional protein isoforms with altered binding affinity or enzymatic activity relevant to oocyte signaling. Further combinations involve co-expression of specific splice variants of EFNB2 and CAMK1D, which together enhance predictive accuracy beyond either gene alone. Additional combinations include the simultaneous detection of EFNB2, GSTA4, and PGR, which reflect coordinated activity in cell signaling and steroidogenesis. More combinations involve the integration of glutathione pathway genes such as GPX3 and GSR with EFNB2 and CAMK1D to assess oxidative stress resilience. Even more combinations include the inclusion of SASH1 and HSPH1 to capture stress response and chaperone activity. In vitro models are established using cumulus cells collected under standardized conditions and analyzed in parallel with known pregnancy outcomes. A positive control is defined as cumulus cells from oocytes that resulted in live birth in a prior validated cohort. Marker gene combinations are selected based on stepwise regression analysis that maximizes the overall p-value of the model while minimizing redundancy. Predictive models are constructed using linear or logistic regression equations that assign weighted coefficients to each gene based on its contribution to pregnancy probability. One such equation for the probability of pregnancy is: P = 1 / (1 + e^-(a + b1×EFNB2 + b2×CAMK1D + b3×GSTA4 + b4×PGR)), where a is the intercept and b1–b4 are regression coefficients derived from the training cohort. Gene expression levels are measured as normalized relative quantities. An alternative gene set includes SASH1, HSPH1, and EFNB2, with the equation: P = 1 / (1 + e^-(c + d1×SASH1 + d2×HSPH1 + d3×EFNB2)). Another alternative gene set incorporates GSTA3, GPX3, and CAMK1D, with the equation: P = 1 / (1 + e^-(e + f1×GSTA3 + f2×GPX3 + f3×CAMK1D)). Yet another alternative gene set combines EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA4, GPX3, and GSR, yielding the equation: P = 1 / (1 + e^-(g + h1×EFNB2 + h2×CAMK1D + h3×GSTA4 + h4×GPX3 + h5×GSR)). Normalization of gene expression levels is essential to account for technical variability and is achieved using housekeeping genes with stable expression across all samples. Oocytes are ranked by quality based on their predicted probability of pregnancy, with the highest-ranked oocyte selected for fertilization and transfer. Clinical diagnostic kits are introduced, comprising lyophilized primers and probes for target genes, reverse transcriptase, PCR master mix, reference RNA controls, and detailed instructions for sample handling, RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and data interpretation. Reagents for mRNA detection include TaqMan probes and SYBR Green chemistry; reagents for protein detection include monoclonal antibodies against EFNB2, CAMK1D, and GSTA4. The kits are designed for use in clinical laboratories with standard molecular biology infrastructure. Possible applications of the method include identifying women with abnormal oocyte production due to diminished ovarian reserve or mitochondrial dysfunction, detecting underlying health conditions such as polycystic ovary syndrome or endometriosis through aberrant gene expression patterns, assessing the efficacy of fertility treatments such as androgen priming or growth hormone co-treatment, enhancing fertility treatments by guiding personalized stimulation protocols, administering gene products such as recombinant EFNB2 or HSPH1 to improve oocyte quality via local intrafollicular delivery, and ranking oocytes for cryopreservation in oocyte banking. The method is also applicable in non-human animals for improving breeding efficiency in livestock and in animal models for deriving genetic models of infertility. The invention facilitates the identification of female subjects with subclinical abnormalities in oocyte maturation and enables the objective assessment of treatment efficacy in clinical trials. The method ultimately supports the selection of the single most viable oocyte for fertilization and implantation, thereby optimizing the success of single embryo transfer and reducing the emotional, physical, and financial burden of repeated assisted reproductive cycles.

A retrospective cohort of 47 patients undergoing intracytoplasmic sperm injection with single embryo transfer was enrolled, all of whom received ovarian stimulation with recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone and a gonadotropin-releasing hormone antagonist. Patient selection criteria included absence of severe male factor infertility, normal uterine anatomy, and availability of both fresh and frozen embryo transfer cycles. Cumulus cells were collected immediately after oocyte denudation using Cumulase enzyme and snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Embryos were cultured in sequential SAGE media and transferred on day 3 or day 5. Gene selection followed a cascade strategy, beginning with genes previously associated with pregnancy outcomes and expanding to include candidates from unpublished microarray data. Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Micro Kit, followed by DNase treatment and reverse transcription with the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit. Real-time PCR was performed using SYBR Green chemistry on a 10 µL reaction volume, with amplification efficiency validated for all primers. Inter-patient analysis employed a two-tailed t-test with log-transformed data and Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons, identifying EFNB2 as the only gene significantly upregulated in the pregnant group. Stepwise multiple regression analysis was performed to construct predictive models, with EFNB2, GSTA4, and PGR forming the initial three-gene model with an AUC of 0.82. Addition of GPX3 and GSTA3 improved the model to an AUC of 0.93. Incorporation of patient age, days of stimulation, and relative estradiol levels yielded a final model with 93% accuracy and an AUC of 0.95. Intra-patient analysis compared cumulus cells from sibling oocytes within the same retrieval cycle that resulted in either pregnancy or no pregnancy, revealing consistent upregulation of EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSR, and PGR in the pregnant group. Fold changes ranged from 1.1 to 2.5, and the multiparametric model correctly ranked 6 out of 7 patients’ oocytes by pregnancy potential. The model failed only in one case, likely due to endometrial factors unrelated to oocyte quality. The most recurrent genes across all models were EFNB2 and GSTA4. The AUC results demonstrated superior performance over morphological grading. Individual gene predictions showed moderate accuracy, but the multiparametric model significantly outperformed single-gene analysis. The cascade strategy successfully filtered out the most robust predictors across three independent studies. EFNB2 and CAMK1D were confirmed as central to steroidogenesis and cell signaling, with strong correlations to PGR and GSTA3. Glutathione pathway genes indicated a protective role against oxidative stress. The method’s potential applications extend to clinical decision-making, personalized stimulation, and oocyte banking.

Exon-level analysis was performed using microarray technology to detect isoform-specific expression patterns in cumulus cells. Material was collected from 60 patients undergoing IVF, with RNA extracted using the same protocol as in Example 1. Array analysis was conducted in three sequential experiments. Array experiment 1 compared cumulus cells from pregnant versus non-pregnant patients, identifying 15 candidate genes with differential exon usage. Array experiment 2 focused on intra-patient comparisons, revealing that specific splice variants of CAMK1D and EFNB2 were consistently upregulated in oocytes leading to live birth. Array experiment 3 integrated both inter- and intra-patient data to refine gene selection. Exon-level analysis employed a filtering criterion requiring a minimum fold change of 1.5, a p-value below 0.01, and conservation of open reading frame. Results indicated that alternative splicing in exon 7 of CAMK1D and exon 5 of EFNB2 correlated strongly with pregnancy outcomes. Stepwise multiple regression analysis identified an 11-gene list including EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA3, GSTA4, GPX3, GSR, PGR, SASH1, HSPH1, NCOA7, and SPTBN5. Alternative models were tested, including those excluding splice variants, which showed reduced predictive power. The 11-gene model achieved an AUC of 0.96. Confirmation experiments validated these findings in an independent cohort using qPCR with isoform-specific primers. Retrospective analysis confirmed consistency across multiple clinics, and prospective validation is currently underway. The significance of these results lies in the demonstration that exon-level analysis provides superior discrimination over gene-level analysis alone, enabling the identification of functionally relevant isoforms that drive oocyte competence.

A retrospective study was conducted on 82 patients who underwent ovarian stimulation with either recombinant FSH or human menopausal gonadotropin in combination with a GnRH antagonist. Cumulus cells were collected under identical conditions and analyzed by quantitative PCR. Statistical analysis included paired t-tests for intra-patient comparisons and independent t-tests for inter-patient comparisons. One-parametric analysis revealed that EFNB2 and CAMK1D were the most consistently upregulated genes in oocytes leading to pregnancy, with p-values below 0.03. Multiparametric analysis incorporating age, BMI, and stimulation duration improved predictive accuracy by 12% compared to single-gene models. Inter-patient analysis confirmed that EFNB2, GSTA4, and PGR were significantly elevated in the pregnant group across both stimulation protocols. In patients stimulated with rFSH, the 5-gene model achieved an AUC of 0.94; in those stimulated with HP-hMG, the AUC was 0.91, demonstrating robustness across protocols. The study concluded that the biomarker panel is effective regardless of the stimulation regimen, supporting its broad clinical applicability.

A prospective, multicenter study was initiated to validate the biomarker panel in a real-world clinical setting. The study enrolled 150 patients undergoing single embryo transfer, with cumulus cells collected from each oocyte and analyzed for expression of EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA3, GSTA4, and GPX3. Procedure steps included standardized oocyte denudation, RNA extraction, qPCR, and calculation of a Cumulus Cell Score using the equation derived from Example 1. The CC score was used to rank oocytes prior to transfer, with the highest-scoring oocyte selected. Pregnancy rates increased from 41% in the control group (morphology-based selection) to 68% in the biomarker-guided group. The beneficial effect of CC evaluation was most pronounced in patients with poor ovarian response and advanced maternal age. Conclusions confirmed that the method significantly improves live birth rates without increasing multiple pregnancy risk. Ongoing work includes automation of the assay and integration into clinical workflow. The general conclusion is that biomarker gene expression in cumulus cells represents a transformative advance in oocyte selection. Validation of the gene list across three independent cohorts confirms its reliability. The applicability of these oocyte quality markers extends to all patients undergoing IVF, regardless of etiology of infertility, stimulation protocol, or embryo culture conditions.
