# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Anomaly detection is a critical task in modern machine learning systems, enabling the identification of rare, atypical, or malicious patterns in data across diverse domains such as manufacturing quality control, cybersecurity, financial transaction monitoring, and medical diagnostics. The ability to distinguish between normal and anomalous instances without exhaustive labeling is essential for scalable deployment in real-world environments where labeled data is scarce, expensive to obtain, or subject to temporal evolution. In the fully supervised setting, anomaly detection models are trained using labeled examples of both normal and anomalous instances, allowing for precise discrimination through binary classification techniques such as weighted logistic regression, support vector machines, or deep neural networks with class-balanced loss functions. These methods achieve high accuracy when sufficient labeled data is available, but their practical utility is severely constrained by the labor-intensive and often infeasible requirement of annotating large volumes of data with ground-truth anomaly labels. At the opposite extreme, fully unsupervised approaches operate without any labeled examples, relying instead on statistical modeling, density estimation, or reconstruction error to identify outliers. Techniques such as Isolation Forest, One-Class Support Vector Machines, and autoencoder-based methods fall into this category. While these methods eliminate labeling costs entirely, they often suffer from poor discriminative power due to the absence of explicit supervision, leading to high false positive rates and limited generalization to novel anomaly types not represented in the training distribution. Between these two extremes lies the semi-supervised setting, which seeks to leverage a small number of labeled examples alongside a large pool of unlabeled data to improve detection performance while minimizing annotation burden. In this setting, methods typically assume that the labeled and unlabeled data are drawn from identical underlying distributions, enabling the propagation of label information from the labeled subset to the unlabeled set through techniques such as consistency regularization, pseudo-labeling, or self-training. However, this assumption is frequently violated in practical applications, where labeling is biased by human expertise, temporal changes, or sampling constraints, resulting in distributional discrepancies between the labeled and unlabeled data. These mismatches cause standard semi-supervised methods to overfit to the limited and potentially unrepresentative labeled set, leading to degraded performance on the broader, more diverse unlabeled population. Furthermore, in many real-world systems, such as fraud detection or industrial defect inspection, anomalies are not static but evolve dynamically over time as adversaries adapt or processes change, rendering previously labeled data obsolete. The challenge is compounded when only partial or one-class labels are available—for instance, when only anomalous examples are labeled (positive-unlabeled setting) or when only normal examples are labeled (negative-unlabeled setting). In such cases, conventional approaches either ignore valuable information from unlabeled data or risk contaminating the training process by mislabeling anomalous instances as normal. These limitations collectively impede the deployment of robust, adaptive anomaly detection systems in dynamic, data-constrained environments where labeling resources are limited and data distributions are inherently non-stationary.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

A novel semi-supervised anomaly detection framework is disclosed that enables high-performance anomaly identification in the presence of distribution mismatch between labeled and unlabeled data. The framework employs an ensemble of one-class classifiers to generate reliable pseudo-labels for unlabeled data without relying on a binary classifier that may be biased by the limited and potentially skewed labeled set. These pseudo-labels are assigned only when there is unanimous agreement among all one-class classifiers, ensuring high confidence in the predicted labels and reducing the risk of propagating erroneous annotations. A machine learning predictor is then trained using both the original labeled data and the high-confidence pseudo-labeled unlabeled data, enabling the model to learn from a broader and more representative sample space while mitigating the influence of distributional bias. The framework further incorporates a partial distribution matching mechanism that automatically determines decision thresholds for pseudo-labeling without requiring a separate validation set, thereby preserving the limited labeled data for training and eliminating the need for manual hyperparameter tuning. This is achieved by aligning the marginal distribution of anomaly scores from the labeled data with that of the unlabeled data using a Wasserstein distance minimization criterion, which identifies the optimal thresholds for classifying unlabeled samples as pseudo-anomalous or pseudo-normal. The system is trained end-to-end using a composite loss function that combines supervised binary cross-entropy on labeled data, supervised binary cross-entropy on pseudo-labeled data, and a self-supervised auxiliary loss derived from representation learning techniques such as contrastive learning or reconstruction objectives. The framework is designed to be agnostic to the underlying data modality, supporting both tabular and image data through appropriate encoder architectures. The resulting system achieves superior anomaly detection performance under conditions of distributional shift, evolving anomalies, and partial labeling, outperforming conventional semi-supervised, unsupervised, and supervised baselines across multiple benchmark datasets and real-world fraud detection scenarios. The method is particularly effective in scenarios where labeled data is sparse, biased toward easy-to-label samples, or restricted to a single class, and where unlabeled data contains novel or previously unseen anomaly types not present in the labeled set.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The disclosed framework, referred to as SPADE—Semi-supervised Pseudo-labeler Anomaly Detection with Ensembling—is a novel architecture designed to overcome the limitations of existing semi-supervised anomaly detection methods under conditions of distribution mismatch. The core innovation lies in the decoupling of the pseudo-labeling mechanism from the final prediction model, allowing the system to generate high-confidence labels for unlabeled data without being influenced by the biases inherent in small, potentially non-representative labeled sets. The framework comprises four key components: an encoder, a predictor, a pseudo-labeler, and a projection head. The encoder maps raw input data into a latent feature space, enabling the extraction of discriminative representations suitable for anomaly detection. The predictor, a binary classifier operating on these latent representations, is responsible for generating the final anomaly scores and is trained exclusively on labeled data and high-confidence pseudo-labeled data. The pseudo-labeler, composed of an ensemble of one-class classifiers, evaluates unlabeled data samples and assigns pseudo-labels only when all classifiers in the ensemble agree on the prediction—either as anomalous or normal—thereby ensuring robustness and reducing the risk of erroneous label propagation. Samples that fail to achieve unanimous agreement are marked as unknown and excluded from training, preserving the integrity of the learning process. The projection head facilitates representation learning through self-supervised objectives such as contrastive learning or reconstruction, enhancing the quality of the latent space and improving generalization. The pseudo-labeling thresholds are determined automatically via partial distribution matching, a technique that minimizes the Wasserstein distance between the anomaly score distributions of labeled and unlabeled data to identify optimal decision boundaries without requiring additional validation data. This eliminates the need to withhold labeled samples for hyperparameter tuning, maximizing the utility of limited annotation resources. The training objective integrates three components: a supervised loss on labeled data, a supervised loss on pseudo-labeled data weighted by confidence indicators, and a self-supervised loss applied to the entire dataset. The optimization is performed jointly across the encoder, predictor, and projection head, with convergence determined by stabilization of the composite training loss. The framework is evaluated across multiple domains, including tabular datasets such as Covertype, Thyroid, and Drug, as well as image datasets including MVTec and Magnetic Tile, demonstrating consistent performance gains over state-of-the-art baselines. In scenarios involving evolving anomalies, where new anomaly types appear exclusively in the unlabeled data, SPADE achieves up to a 10.6% improvement in AUC over competing methods. In positive-unlabeled settings, where only anomalous examples are labeled, SPADE maintains robust performance where other methods fail due to distributional imbalance. The system is further validated on real-world fraud detection datasets, including Kaggle Credit Card Fraud and Xente, where temporal shifts in anomaly patterns are prevalent, confirming its ability to adapt to dynamic environments without re-labeling. The architecture is implemented on standard computing systems with multiple processors and memory units, supporting distributed training and inference across networked devices. The framework is stored as executable instructions on non-transitory computer-readable media and may be deployed on server or user computing devices for real-time anomaly detection in production environments. The disclosed system enables scalable, adaptive, and label-efficient anomaly detection in complex, real-world settings where traditional methods are fundamentally limited by their assumptions of data homogeneity and static anomaly distributions.
