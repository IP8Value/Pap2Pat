# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology, specifically to diagnostic and therapeutic methods for neurodegenerative disorders, with particular emphasis on Alzheimer’s disease. The invention encompasses the identification of TRPC6 as a molecular regulator of amyloid-beta production, the development of biomarkers based on TRPC6 expression levels, and the design of therapeutic agents that modulate TRPC6 activity or mimic its functional domains to selectively inhibit amyloid-beta generation without disrupting other γ-secretase substrates. The invention further includes diagnostic kits, pharmaceutical compositions, and screening methods for compounds that influence TRPC6 expression or function, all of which are grounded in the novel discovery that TRPC6 modulates amyloid-beta pathology through a channel activity-independent mechanism involving direct interaction with the amyloid precursor protein.

## BACKGROUND

- Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the accumulation of extracellular amyloid-beta plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein, leading to synaptic dysfunction, neuronal loss, and cognitive decline. Clinically, the disease manifests as progressive memory impairment, disorientation, language deficits, and behavioral disturbances, which ultimately result in complete dependency and premature mortality. The Amyloid Hypothesis, the most widely accepted model of Alzheimer’s pathogenesis, posits that the aberrant cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein by β- and γ-secretases generates neurotoxic amyloid-beta peptides, particularly Aβ42, which aggregate into oligomers and plaques that initiate a cascade of inflammatory, oxidative, and apoptotic events. Amyloid-beta is generated through sequential proteolytic processing: first by β-secretase (BACE1), which cleaves APP to produce a C-terminal fragment known as C99, followed by γ-secretase, which cleaves C99 to release amyloid-beta peptides of varying lengths and the APP intracellular domain. Despite decades of research and substantial investment, current diagnostic methods remain inadequate for early detection, relying largely on invasive cerebrospinal fluid analysis or expensive neuroimaging techniques such as PET scans that detect plaques only after significant accumulation has occurred. Furthermore, numerous clinical trials targeting γ-secretase have failed due to mechanism-based toxicities arising from the inhibition of essential substrates such as Notch, which plays critical roles in cell differentiation and immune function. These failures underscore the urgent need for a therapeutic strategy that selectively reduces amyloid-beta production without perturbing the physiological functions of γ-secretase, as well as for a non-invasive, reliable, and early biomarker capable of identifying at-risk individuals prior to irreversible neuronal damage.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention provides a novel biomarker for the prediction and diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease based on the measurement of TRPC6 mRNA expression levels in peripheral blood cells, wherein reduced expression correlates with increased disease risk and progression. The invention further provides a therapeutic agent for the treatment or prevention of Alzheimer’s disease comprising a polypeptide derived from the transmembrane domain of TRPC6, specifically the region spanning amino acids 487 to 507, which selectively inhibits γ-secretase cleavage of the C99 fragment of amyloid precursor protein without affecting cleavage of Notch or other substrates. The invention includes a diagnostic kit for detecting TRPC6 mRNA levels in peripheral blood samples, comprising reagents for total RNA extraction, primers for reverse transcription, reverse transcriptase, DNA polymerase for quantitative PCR, and instructions for interpreting expression levels relative to established normal controls. The invention further provides methods for detecting TRPC6 mRNA expression using real-time fluorogenic quantitative PCR following RNA extraction from peripheral blood mononuclear cells, wherein a statistically significant decrease in TRPC6 mRNA levels compared to age-matched healthy controls indicates an elevated risk for Alzheimer’s disease. The invention also encompasses the use of TRPC6 protein, its functional fragments, or agonists thereof as therapeutic agents, and provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a TRPC6-derived polypeptide or a small molecule that upregulates TRPC6 expression, formulated with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for administration via intravenous, intraperitoneal, or intranasal routes. The invention further provides a method for screening compounds that modulate TRPC6 expression or activity, wherein candidate compounds are tested for their ability to increase TRPC6 mRNA or protein levels and reduce amyloid-beta production in cell-based assays, followed by validation in transgenic mouse models of Alzheimer’s disease. The invention enables earlier diagnosis than conventional methods, provides a mechanism-specific therapeutic approach that avoids the toxicities associated with broad γ-secretase inhibition, and identifies a core functional domain of TRPC6 that serves as a template for the design of peptide-based therapeutics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention is based on the discovery that TRPC6 mRNA expression levels in peripheral blood cells are significantly lower in individuals with Alzheimer’s disease compared to cognitively normal controls, and that this reduction correlates with disease severity and progression. TRPC6 is a member of the transient receptor potential canonical family of non-selective cation channels, but its role in Alzheimer’s disease is independent of its ion channel activity. Instead, TRPC6 directly interacts with the C-terminal fragment of amyloid precursor protein, known as C99, thereby preventing its association with the γ-secretase complex and reducing the production of amyloid-beta peptides. The TRPC6 protein contains six transmembrane domains, with a cytoplasmic N-terminus and C-terminus, and conserved sequences shared across the TRPC family, including a coiled-coil domain and ankyrin repeats. The functional domain responsible for inhibiting γ-secretase cleavage of C99 resides within the second transmembrane segment and adjacent extracellular loop, specifically amino acids 437 to 507, with the core inhibitory region localized to residues 487 to 507. This region, designated TM2, is sufficient to disrupt the interaction between C99 and presenilin-1, the catalytic subunit of γ-secretase, without interfering with γ-secretase processing of Notch, E-cadherin, or other substrates. TRPC6 fragments, derivatives, and analogs encompassing this domain, including fusion peptides conjugated to cell-penetrating sequences such as TAT, are effective in reducing amyloid-beta levels in vitro and in vivo. The invention further recognizes the genetic association between the APOE ε3 allele and elevated TRPC6 mRNA expression, suggesting a potential mechanism by which APOE genotype influences Alzheimer’s risk through regulation of TRPC6. The pharmaceutical composition of the invention may be formulated as a peptide, a nucleic acid encoding the functional TRPC6 domain, or a small molecule agonist that enhances TRPC6 transcription or stabilizes its interaction with C99. The diagnostic method involves the extraction of total RNA from peripheral blood mononuclear cells, reverse transcription into cDNA, and quantification of TRPC6 mRNA using real-time PCR with specific primers and probes, with normalization to housekeeping genes such as GAPDH or ACTB. Expression levels are compared against a reference range established from healthy populations, and a value below the 25th percentile is indicative of increased Alzheimer’s disease risk. The invention further provides a nucleic acid chip containing immobilized oligonucleotides complementary to TRPC6 mRNA for high-throughput screening of patient samples. The utility of the invention is demonstrated through the use of transgenic mouse models, including APP/PS1 mice and TRPC6-overexpressing mice, in which TRPC6 upregulation leads to reduced amyloid plaque burden, improved synaptic density, and enhanced performance in spatial memory tasks. The invention further discloses methods for protein extraction from brain tissues and peripheral blood cells, Western blot analysis for TRPC6 and C99 protein levels, immunohistochemistry for amyloid plaque quantification, and luciferase reporter assays to assess transcriptional regulation of TRPC6. The invention enables the identification of novel therapeutic candidates through screening assays that measure TRPC6 expression and amyloid-beta reduction in HEK293APP stable cells and primary cortical neurons, with validation in vivo. The advantages of the invention include the provision of a non-invasive, early diagnostic biomarker, the identification of a highly specific therapeutic target that avoids the pitfalls of previous γ-secretase inhibitors, and the development of a peptide-based therapeutic with demonstrated efficacy in reducing amyloid pathology without Notch-related toxicity.

### EXAMPLE 1

- Blood samples were collected from patients diagnosed with Alzheimer’s disease according to NIA-AA criteria and from age- and sex-matched cognitively normal controls. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated using density gradient centrifugation, and total RNA was extracted using a commercial kit containing guanidinium thiocyanate and silica-membrane purification columns. RNA integrity was confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis and spectrophotometric analysis. Reverse transcription was performed using oligo(dT) primers and M-MLV reverse transcriptase under standardized conditions. Real-time fluorogenic quantitative PCR was carried out using SYBR Green chemistry with primers specific for human TRPC6 and reference genes. The relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. TRPC6 mRNA levels were significantly lower in the Alzheimer’s disease group compared to controls, with a mean fold change of 0.42 ± 0.08 (p < 0.001). Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis demonstrated an area under the curve of 0.91, indicating high diagnostic accuracy. TRPC5 mRNA levels were unchanged between groups, confirming specificity. TRPC6 expression correlated positively with Mini-Mental State Examination scores (r = 0.73, p < 0.001). In early-stage Alzheimer’s patients, TRPC6 levels were already significantly reduced compared to controls, establishing its utility as an early biomarker.

### EXAMPLE 2

- In HEK293APP cells, TRPC6 overexpression resulted in a 60% reduction in secreted Aβ40 and Aβ42, while TRPC6 knockdown increased Aβ levels by 2.5-fold. A strong inverse correlation was observed between TRPC6 mRNA levels and Aβ concentration in cell culture supernatants (r = −0.89, p < 0.001). In APP/PS1 transgenic mice, endogenous TRPC6 expression in the hippocampus was inversely correlated with amyloid plaque load as quantified by immunohistochemistry and ELISA (r = −0.84, p < 0.001). These findings establish TRPC6 mRNA levels as a reliable predictor of amyloid burden both in vitro and in vivo, confirming its suitability as a biomarker for early diagnosis.

### EXAMPLE 3

- Overexpression of TRPC6 in HEK293APP stable cells reduced Aβ40 and Aβ42 levels by 65% and 70%, respectively, compared to empty vector controls. Conversely, siRNA-mediated knockdown of TRPC6 in primary rat cortical neurons increased Aβ40 and Aβ42 by 2.8-fold and 3.1-fold, respectively. In APP/PS1 mice injected with an AAV vector expressing TRPC6 in the forebrain, Aβ levels in the hippocampus were reduced by 58% compared to vehicle-injected controls. These results demonstrate that TRPC6 expression levels directly modulate amyloid-beta production in multiple biological systems.

### EXAMPLE 4

- In vitro γ-secretase activity assays using fluorogenic substrates showed no change in enzyme activity upon TRPC6 overexpression. However, TRPC6 expression in HEK293C99 cells reduced AICD generation by 70% without altering C99 levels. TRPC6 did not affect the cleavage of NotchΔE or the CTF2/CTF1 ratio of E-cadherin or N-cadherin. Co-immunoprecipitation revealed that TRPC6 disrupted the interaction between C99 and PS1, but not between PS1 and nicastrin. These data confirm that TRPC6 specifically inhibits γ-secretase cleavage of APP without broadly suppressing γ-secretase function.

### EXAMPLE 5

- A series of TRPC6 truncation mutants were expressed in HEK293APP cells. Constructs containing amino acids 437–508 reduced Aβ levels by more than 50%, whereas those lacking this region had no effect. Point mutations within the TM2 domain (C6-mut1–C6-mut4) abolished Aβ inhibition. Immunoprecipitation confirmed that only wild-type TRPC6 and constructs containing the 437–508 region co-precipitated with APP. These findings identify the 437–508 region as essential for TRPC6’s inhibitory function, with the 487–507 segment being the minimal core domain.

### EXAMPLE 6

- A cell-penetrating fusion peptide, TAT-TM2, comprising the TAT sequence fused to TRPC6 residues 487–507, was synthesized. TAT-TM2 reduced Aβ levels in HEK293APP cells by 62% and in primary cortical neurons by 59%, while a mutated version (TAT-TM2-mut1) had no effect. In APP/PS1 mice, intraperitoneal injection of TAT-TM2 reduced brain Aβ40 and Aβ42 by 55% and 58%, respectively, without altering Notch cleavage. TAT-TM2-biotin bound directly to APP in co-immunoprecipitation experiments, confirming target engagement.

### EXAMPLE 7

- A library of 5,000 small molecules was screened in HEK293APP cells for their ability to increase TRPC6 mRNA expression. Compound C, identified through qPCR validation, upregulated TRPC6 expression by 3.2-fold and reduced Aβ42 by 48%. Compound C was non-toxic and showed no effect on TRPC5 or other TRP channels, confirming specificity.

### EXAMPLE 8

- Peripheral blood samples from individuals genotyped for APOE were analyzed for TRPC6 mRNA levels. Carriers of the APOE ε3/ε3 genotype exhibited significantly higher TRPC6 expression than APOE ε4 carriers (p < 0.01). In Jurkat cells transfected with APOE3 or APOE4, APOE3 increased TRPC6 mRNA levels by 2.1-fold, while APOE4 had no effect. Similar results were observed in primary rat cortical neurons, establishing a functional link between APOE genotype and TRPC6 regulation.

### EXAMPLE 9

- TAT-TM2 was tested in vitro for its effect on γ-secretase cleavage of C99 and NotchΔE. TAT-TM2 reduced AICD generation by 67% but had no effect on Notch intracellular domain production. In HEK293C99 cells, TAT-TM2 disrupted the C99–PS1 interaction without affecting PS1–nicastrin binding. These results confirm that TAT-TM2 specifically inhibits γ-secretase cleavage of APP, mirroring the activity of full-length TRPC6 and validating its therapeutic potential.
