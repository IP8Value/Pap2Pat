# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

- relate to cell-based therapeutic treatments for diabetes

The present invention relates to novel methods for the treatment of insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus through the use of autologous bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells that have been manipulated to express key regenerative factors, particularly vascular endothelial growth factor and/or pancreatic and duodenal homeobox 1. These methods are designed to restore normoglycemia in diabetic subjects by promoting the regeneration of endogenous pancreatic β-cells, enhancing intra-islet vascularization, and reactivating critical signaling pathways involved in β-cell survival, proliferation, and functional maturation. The invention specifically encompasses the ex vivo manipulation, differentiation, and transplantation of human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells to achieve sustained metabolic recovery without reliance on exogenous insulin administration. The therapeutic approach is grounded in the discovery that transient expression of vascular endothelial growth factor in these cells induces a permissive microenvironment within the diabetic pancreas, enabling endogenous β-cell recovery through activation of the insulin/IGF receptor/PI-3K/AKT signaling cascade. This represents a paradigm shift from direct cell replacement strategies to microenvironment-mediated regeneration, offering a clinically translatable, autologous, and potentially curative intervention for type 1 and advanced type 2 diabetes.

## BACKGROUND

- motivate understanding of β-cell homeostasis and regenerative repair

The maintenance of glucose homeostasis in mammals is critically dependent on the precise regulation of insulin secretion by pancreatic β-cells, whose functional integrity is essential for metabolic stability. Disruption of β-cell mass or function, whether through autoimmune destruction, chronic metabolic stress, or environmental toxins, leads to persistent hyperglycemia and the clinical manifestation of diabetes mellitus. Despite decades of research, the capacity of the adult human pancreas to regenerate β-cells following injury remains limited, and current therapeutic strategies such as exogenous insulin delivery or whole-pancreas transplantation fail to fully restore physiological glucose control. While islet transplantation has demonstrated the feasibility of replacing lost β-cells, its widespread application is hindered by donor scarcity, the need for lifelong immunosuppression, and the gradual loss of graft function over time. Consequently, there is an urgent and unmet clinical need for therapies that can either regenerate endogenous β-cells or generate functional insulin-producing cells from accessible, autologous sources without triggering immune rejection.

- discuss limitations of islet cell transplantation

Islet cell transplantation, though promising, is constrained by multiple biological and logistical barriers. The availability of donor pancreata is severely limited, and even when islets are successfully isolated and transplanted, they often undergo significant attrition due to ischemia, inflammation, and immune-mediated destruction. The requirement for chronic immunosuppression exposes recipients to increased risks of infection, malignancy, and nephrotoxicity, further diminishing the risk-benefit profile of this intervention. Moreover, transplanted islets frequently fail to revascularize adequately within the host liver or other ectopic sites, resulting in hypoxia, impaired nutrient sensing, and reduced glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. These limitations underscore the necessity for alternative approaches that circumvent donor dependency and immune complications while actively promoting endogenous tissue repair.

- describe β-cell mass maintenance in adulthood

In adult mammals, β-cell mass is maintained through a delicate balance between replication of existing β-cells, neogenesis from progenitor populations, and apoptosis. Under normal physiological conditions, β-cell turnover is exceedingly slow, with replication rates declining sharply after early postnatal life. This quiescent state is regulated by a network of cell cycle inhibitors, including p27Kip1, which suppresses proliferation and preserves functional maturity. However, under conditions of metabolic demand or injury, such as partial pancreatectomy or insulin resistance, β-cells can re-enter the cell cycle and expand their mass through proliferation. The molecular mechanisms governing this adaptive response involve insulin/IGF signaling, nutrient-sensing pathways, and transcriptional regulators such as PDX1 and FoxO1. In diabetes, this regenerative capacity is suppressed, and β-cells become increasingly susceptible to apoptosis, leading to progressive loss of functional tissue.

- discuss pancreatic progenitor cells

Evidence suggests the existence of progenitor or stem-like cell populations within the adult pancreas, particularly in the ductal epithelium and islet periphery, which may serve as a reservoir for β-cell neogenesis. These cells express markers associated with developmental plasticity, including nestin, Sox9, and Ngn3, and under specific inductive conditions, can differentiate into insulin-producing cells. However, their endogenous activation in human diabetes is minimal, likely due to the absence of appropriate signaling cues and a hostile microenvironment characterized by inflammation, fibrosis, and vascular insufficiency. The failure to recruit or stimulate these progenitors represents a critical barrier to natural β-cell regeneration.

- introduce nestin as a marker of neuroepithelial stem cells

Nestin, an intermediate filament protein originally identified in neural stem and progenitor cells, has been detected in a subset of cells within the developing and adult pancreas, particularly in association with ductal structures and islet margins. Its expression correlates with multipotency and a less differentiated state, suggesting that nestin-positive cells may represent a common precursor population capable of giving rise to both neuronal and endocrine lineages. The presence of nestin in bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells further supports the concept of a shared developmental origin between neural and pancreatic tissues, offering a mechanistic basis for the plasticity observed in these cells.

- describe nestin expression in pancreas development

During embryonic development, nestin is transiently expressed in the pancreatic bud and later in the mesenchymal stroma surrounding nascent islets. Its expression coincides with the emergence of endocrine progenitors and the initial phases of islet morphogenesis. As differentiation progresses, nestin is downregulated in mature β-cells but persists in a subset of adult pancreatic cells, potentially marking a quiescent progenitor pool. The dynamic regulation of nestin during development underscores its role as a marker of cellular plasticity and highlights its relevance as a target for regenerative strategies aimed at reactivating developmental programs in adult tissues.

- discuss nestin-positive cells in islets and pancreatic ducts

In the adult pancreas, nestin-positive cells are found in low numbers within the ductal epithelium and in close proximity to islets, often co-localizing with markers of endothelial and neural lineages. These cells exhibit morphological features consistent with multipotent progenitors and can be induced in vitro to differentiate into insulin-producing cells under specific culture conditions. Their spatial localization suggests a potential role in islet maintenance and repair, although their contribution to β-cell regeneration in vivo remains incompletely understood. The presence of these cells in human pancreata, coupled with their responsiveness to growth factors and epigenetic modulators, positions them as a viable target for therapeutic intervention.

- describe suppression of nestin expression in embryonic stem cells

In pluripotent embryonic stem cells, nestin expression is absent during the undifferentiated state and is only induced upon commitment to neural or mesodermal lineages. This temporal regulation indicates that nestin serves as a lineage-specific marker rather than a universal stem cell identifier. Its absence in embryonic stem cells distinguishes them from adult progenitor populations and reinforces the notion that adult stem cells, such as those derived from bone marrow, possess a more restricted but still substantial differentiation potential that can be harnessed for tissue-specific regeneration.

- discuss common markers of precursor stem cells for neuronal and islet cell types

Beyond nestin, other shared markers such as Sox2, Pax6, and Ngn3 have been identified in both neural and pancreatic progenitor populations, suggesting a common developmental blueprint for these lineages. The overlapping transcriptional networks governing neurogenesis and endocrinogenesis provide a molecular foundation for the observed plasticity of bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells, which can be directed toward insulin-producing phenotypes through the manipulation of these conserved pathways.

- describe bone marrow-derived stem cells differentiating into neural phenotypes

Human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells have been shown to differentiate into neurons, astrocytes, and oligodendrocytes under defined inductive conditions, demonstrating their capacity to adopt ectodermal fates despite their mesodermal origin. This plasticity is mediated by epigenetic remodeling, activation of neural transcription factors, and exposure to neurotrophic signals, indicating that these cells retain a latent developmental potential that can be reawakened in vitro and in vivo.

- discuss bone marrow-derived stem cells reversing hyperglycemic phenotype

Multiple preclinical studies have demonstrated that transplantation of bone marrow-derived cells into diabetic animal models can lead to partial or complete reversal of hyperglycemia. However, the mechanisms underlying this effect have remained controversial, with conflicting reports attributing improvement to direct differentiation into β-cells, paracrine signaling, or immune modulation. The present invention resolves this ambiguity by demonstrating that sustained metabolic recovery is primarily mediated not by transdifferentiation of transplanted cells into insulin-producing cells, but by the creation of a regenerative microenvironment that reactivates endogenous β-cell proliferation and survival pathways.

- motivate need for cell therapy for diabetes

Given the limitations of pharmacological and surgical interventions, cell-based therapies represent the most promising avenue for achieving durable, physiologic glucose control in diabetes. The ability to generate functional insulin-producing cells from a patient’s own bone marrow offers a scalable, autologous, and immunologically compatible solution that avoids the ethical and logistical challenges associated with embryonic or allogeneic sources. The invention disclosed herein provides a robust, reproducible protocol for generating therapeutic cells that not only deliver regenerative factors but also orchestrate the restoration of the pancreatic niche, thereby addressing the root causes of β-cell failure.

- discuss limitations of current methods of exogenous insulin replacement

Current standard-of-care treatments for diabetes, including multiple daily insulin injections and insulin pump therapy, require constant patient vigilance, carry a high risk of hypoglycemia, and fail to replicate the dynamic, glucose-responsive insulin secretion of healthy β-cells. Long-term complications such as retinopathy, nephropathy, and neuropathy persist despite glycemic control, underscoring the inadequacy of insulin replacement as a curative strategy. The invention described herein moves beyond symptomatic management by targeting the underlying pathophysiology of β-cell loss and dysfunction, offering the potential for true disease modification and long-term remission.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce method for differentiating BMSCs into pancreatic precursor cells

The present invention provides a novel, multi-stage method for differentiating human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells into functional pancreatic precursor cells and insulin-producing cells through sequential exposure to defined culture media that modulate chromatin structure, activate key developmental transcription factors, and recapitulate the temporal sequence of pancreatic embryogenesis. This method enables the generation of cells capable of glucose-responsive insulin secretion and demonstrates the capacity to reverse hyperglycemia in diabetic animal models through both direct transdifferentiation and, more significantly, through the induction of endogenous β-cell regeneration via paracrine signaling.

- describe culturing BMSCs in first culture medium for chromatin remodeling

The method begins with the culture of human bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells in a first medium containing a DNA methyltransferase inhibitor and a histone deacetylase inhibitor, which together induce global chromatin remodeling by reducing DNA methylation and increasing histone acetylation. This epigenetic modulation loosens the condensed chromatin structure of the cells, thereby increasing accessibility of regulatory regions of genes critical for pancreatic development, including PDX1, NGN3, and PAX6. The first medium is maintained for a period sufficient to establish a permissive epigenetic state, enabling subsequent transcriptional activation without the need for genetic modification.

- describe culturing BMSCs in second culture medium for cell induction

Following epigenetic priming, the cells are transferred to a second culture medium containing retinoic acid, activin A, and fibroblast growth factor 10, which act synergistically to initiate the specification of endodermal and pancreatic progenitor fates. Retinoic acid plays a pivotal role in activating PDX1 expression, a master regulator of pancreatic development, while activin A promotes definitive endoderm formation. Fibroblast growth factor 10 supports the expansion of pancreatic progenitor cells and suppresses alternative lineages. During this phase, the cells begin to express early pancreatic markers such as SOX9 and PDX1, establishing a committed progenitor population.

- describe culturing BMSCs in third culture medium for differentiation

The third culture medium is designed to promote the differentiation of pancreatic progenitors into endocrine precursors and is composed of a serum-free formulation supplemented with epidermal growth factor, basic fibroblast growth factor, and nicotinamide. These components support the survival and proliferation of endocrine-committed cells while inhibiting exocrine differentiation. Under these conditions, the cells upregulate expression of the endocrine transcription factors NGN3, NEUROD1, and PAX6, and begin to express proinsulin and other islet hormones. The medium is changed every 48 hours to maintain optimal concentrations of signaling molecules and prevent metabolic waste accumulation.

- describe culturing BMSCs in fourth culture medium for maturation

In the final stage, the differentiating cells are transferred to a maturation medium containing exendin-4, insulin-like growth factor 1, and high glucose concentrations, which mimic the postnatal islet microenvironment and drive functional maturation. Exposure to elevated glucose levels stimulates insulin biosynthesis and processing, while exendin-4 enhances β-cell survival and glucose-stimulated insulin secretion. After seven to ten days in this medium, the cells form three-dimensional clusters resembling islet-like structures and demonstrate robust, glucose-responsive insulin release, confirming their functional maturity.

- introduce method for transfecting BMSCs with virus vectors encoding PDX1 and/or VEGF

In an alternative embodiment, the method includes the genetic modification of bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells using adenoviral vectors encoding human PDX1 and/or vascular endothelial growth factor. These vectors are introduced into the cells prior to culture initiation, allowing transient but high-level expression of the transgenes during the differentiation process. The expression of PDX1 enhances the efficiency of pancreatic lineage commitment, while VEGF promotes vascularization and survival of both transplanted and endogenous β-cells, thereby amplifying the regenerative effect.

- describe obtaining BMSCs from bone marrow aspirate

The bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells are obtained from a subject via bone marrow aspiration from the iliac crest or sternum under sterile conditions. The aspirate is processed to isolate mononuclear cells, which are then plated in culture flasks and allowed to adhere. Non-adherent cells are removed after 24 to 48 hours, and adherent cells are expanded through serial passaging until sufficient numbers are obtained for differentiation or transplantation.

- describe administering BMSCs expressing VEGF and/or PDX1 to treat diabetes

The differentiated cells, whether genetically modified or not, are administered to a subject suffering from insulin-dependent diabetes via intravenous or intra-pancreatic delivery. The cells home to the pancreatic tissue, where they secrete paracrine factors that activate endogenous β-cell regeneration, reduce apoptosis, and promote revascularization. In cases where the cells express VEGF, they facilitate the restoration of intra-islet capillary networks, which is essential for β-cell function and survival.

- describe producing pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells from BMSCs

The invention provides a method for producing pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells from bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells through the sequential application of epigenetic, biochemical, and growth factor cues that mirror the natural developmental sequence of pancreatic organogenesis. The resulting cells express key β-cell markers including insulin, C-peptide, PDX1, NKX6.1, and GLUT2, and demonstrate glucose-responsive insulin secretion in vitro and in vivo.

- describe administering pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells to treat diabetes

The pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells generated by the method are administered to a diabetic subject to restore endogenous insulin production. The cells are delivered in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, either as a single bolus or in repeated doses, and are capable of engrafting in the pancreatic parenchyma or associated vasculature, where they contribute to the restoration of normoglycemia.

- describe obtaining bone marrow from subject and producing pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells

The method includes the autologous procurement of bone marrow from a diabetic subject, followed by ex vivo expansion, epigenetic and biochemical manipulation, and differentiation into pancreatic precursor or insulin-producing cells. These cells are then reintroduced into the same subject, eliminating the need for immunosuppression and reducing the risk of rejection.

- describe producing pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells from BMSCs using multiple culture media

The invention encompasses the use of four distinct culture media applied in a defined temporal sequence to guide the stepwise differentiation of bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells into functional insulin-producing cells. Each medium is formulated to induce specific molecular and phenotypic changes, ensuring fidelity to the natural developmental trajectory of pancreatic cells and maximizing the functional maturity of the final product.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- incorporate references

The methods and compositions described herein are supported by extensive preclinical data demonstrating the efficacy of bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells in reversing hyperglycemia in diabetic animal models. These findings are consistent with published studies on the plasticity of mesenchymal stem cells, the role of VEGF in islet vascularization, and the critical function of PDX1 in β-cell development and maintenance.

- define technical terms

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cell” refers to a multipotent stromal cell isolated from the bone marrow that exhibits adherence to plastic, expresses CD73, CD90, and CD105, and lacks expression of hematopoietic markers such as CD34 and CD45. The term “pancreatic precursor cell” refers to a cell that expresses markers of pancreatic lineage such as PDX1 and SOX9 but has not yet acquired the full functional characteristics of a mature β-cell. The term “insulin-producing cell” refers to a cell that synthesizes, processes, and secretes insulin in a glucose-responsive manner.

- provide general guide to terms used

Throughout this disclosure, the terms “treating,” “treatment,” and “therapy” refer to any intervention aimed at ameliorating, reducing, or eliminating the symptoms or underlying pathology of diabetes mellitus. The term “subject” refers to any mammal, including humans, suffering from or at risk of developing insulin-dependent diabetes.

- define "mammal"

The term “mammal” includes, but is not limited to, humans, non-human primates, dogs, cats, rabbits, rats, mice, and other species commonly used in biomedical research or veterinary medicine.

- define "treatment" and "treating"

The terms “treatment” and “treating” encompass any intervention that prevents, delays, reduces, or reverses the progression of diabetes mellitus, including but not limited to the restoration of normoglycemia, the increase in endogenous insulin production, the reduction of β-cell apoptosis, and the enhancement of islet vascularization.

- describe novel culture system

The novel culture system comprises four sequential media, each tailored to induce specific stages of pancreatic differentiation: epigenetic remodeling, endodermal specification, endocrine commitment, and functional maturation. This system is distinguished by its ability to generate insulin-producing cells without the need for genetic modification, although genetic enhancement with VEGF or PDX1 further improves outcomes.

- inhibit DNA methylation

The first medium contains 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine, a nucleoside analog that incorporates into DNA during replication and inhibits DNA methyltransferases, thereby reducing CpG island methylation and promoting gene expression.

- enhance chromatin remodeling

The first medium further includes trichostatin A, a histone deacetylase inhibitor that increases histone acetylation, leading to a more open chromatin configuration that facilitates transcriptional activation of developmental genes.

- regulate gene expressions

The sequential application of media components regulates the temporal expression of key transcription factors, including PDX1, NGN3, and PAX6, in a manner that mirrors the natural progression of pancreatic development.

- loosen chromatin structure

The combined action of DNA demethylation and histone acetylation results in a significant loosening of chromatin structure, permitting access of transcriptional machinery to previously silenced loci associated with pancreatic differentiation.

- permit differentiation

This epigenetic priming permits the cells to respond to subsequent inductive signals, enabling them to differentiate along the pancreatic lineage rather than defaulting to mesenchymal or fibroblastic fates.

- modify timing of pancreatic cell fate

The controlled timing of medium changes ensures that each developmental stage is completed before progression to the next, preventing premature or aberrant differentiation.

- express transcription factors

By the end of the differentiation protocol, the cells express high levels of PDX1, NGN3, PAX6, NKX6.1, and MAFA, which are essential for β-cell identity and function.

- follow normal mammalian pancreatic development

The protocol recapitulates the developmental sequence observed in mammalian embryogenesis, beginning with endoderm induction, followed by pancreatic specification, endocrine commitment, and finally functional maturation.

- express PDX1, NgN3, and Pax6

The cells express PDX1 during the second stage, NGN3 during the third stage, and PAX6 during the final maturation phase, in accordance with their known roles in pancreatic ontogeny.

- induce ductal and endocrine pancreatic cells

The culture conditions promote the formation of both ductal-like structures and endocrine clusters, with the latter exhibiting insulin and C-peptide expression.

- use RA to induce differentiation

Retinoic acid is used in the second medium to activate PDX1 expression and initiate pancreatic specification.

- control multiple steps in motor neuron differentiation

Although the method is directed toward pancreatic differentiation, the use of retinoic acid and fibroblast growth factors also reflects their known roles in controlling multiple steps of motor neuron differentiation, highlighting the conserved nature of these signaling pathways.

- require RA for pancreas development

Retinoic acid is required for the proper patterning of the foregut endoderm into pancreatic tissue, as demonstrated in both in vitro and in vivo models.

- promote generation of PDX1-positive pancreatic progenitor cells

The combination of retinoic acid, activin A, and FGF10 in the second medium promotes the efficient generation of PDX1-positive pancreatic progenitor cells from bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells.

- induce pancreatic differentiation

The sequential application of media components induces a complete pancreatic differentiation program, culminating in the generation of functional insulin-producing cells.

- activate Pdx1 in the second step

PDX1 expression is activated specifically during the second stage of differentiation, consistent with its role as an early determinant of pancreatic fate.

- activate both endocrine and non-endocrine pathways

The culture system activates both endocrine (insulin, glucagon) and non-endocrine (ductal, acinar) pathways, allowing for the generation of a heterogeneous but functional islet-like structure.

- believe in potential multipotent precursor stem cell

The invention is based on the belief that bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells contain a subpopulation with multipotent potential capable of giving rise to both neural and pancreatic lineages, and that this potential can be unlocked through epigenetic and biochemical manipulation.

- demonstrate epigenetic manipulation

The method demonstrates that epigenetic manipulation is a critical first step in enabling the differentiation of mesenchymal stem cells into pancreatic lineages, overcoming the epigenetic barriers that restrict their plasticity.

- induce expression of transcription factors

The culture conditions induce the sequential expression of transcription factors that are known to be essential for pancreatic development, including PDX1, NGN3, and PAX6.

- differentiate into a more mature phenotype

The final maturation stage drives the cells to acquire a more mature β-cell phenotype, including glucose-responsive insulin secretion and the expression of mature β-cell markers.

- address unmet needs for cell therapy

This invention addresses the unmet need for a safe, autologous, and scalable cell therapy for diabetes that does not require immunosuppression or donor tissue.

- induce reversion of diabetes

Administration of the differentiated cells induces sustained reversion of hyperglycemia in diabetic animal models, with restoration of normoglycemia lasting for the duration of the observation period.

- test hypothesis of de novo β-cell differentiation

The invention tests and confirms the hypothesis that bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells can be directed to differentiate into functional β-cells, but further reveals that the primary mechanism of metabolic recovery is the regeneration of endogenous β-cells via paracrine signaling.

Sustained recovery from diabetes is achieved only when VEGF is expressed, indicating that vascular support is essential for long-term β-cell regeneration.

The transplanted cells do not significantly contribute to the insulin-producing cell pool but instead support the regeneration and functional recovery of endogenous β-cells through the activation of the insulin/IGF receptor/PI-3K/AKT pathway.

- provide new insight into mechanism of β-cell recovery

The invention provides new insight into the mechanism of β-cell recovery by demonstrating that vascular reconstitution and reactivation of insulin signaling are the primary drivers of endogenous regeneration.

## EXAMPLES

- provide examples of claimed invention

### Cell Isolation and Culture

- isolate adult Fisher rats

Adult Fisher rats were anesthetized and euthanized in accordance with institutional guidelines. The femurs and tibias were aseptically removed, and the bone marrow was flushed using a syringe filled with Alfa-MEM.

- harvest whole bone marrow

Whole bone marrow was collected into a sterile tube and passed through a 70 μm cell strainer to remove debris and bone fragments.

- culture nestin-positive spheres

The cell suspension was plated in low-adherence dishes in serum-free medium supplemented with EGF and bFGF to promote the formation of nestin-positive spheres.

- maintain cell populations

Cells were maintained in culture for up to 14 days, with medium changes every 48 hours, and passaged when confluence exceeded 80%.

### Cell Culture and Differentiation

- establish complex differentiation protocol

A four-stage differentiation protocol was established, beginning with epigenetic modulation using 5-aza-2'-deoxycytidine and trichostatin A, followed by endoderm induction, endocrine specification, and functional maturation.

- conduct control experiments

Control experiments were conducted using cells cultured in standard growth medium without differentiation cues, and no insulin-producing cells were observed.

- describe four basic steps of differentiation

The four basic steps of differentiation are: (1) epigenetic remodeling, (2) endoderm specification, (3) endocrine commitment, and (4) functional maturation.

### Quantitative Real-Time PCR

- analyze gene expression using qRT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from differentiated cells using a commercial kit, converted to cDNA, and analyzed by quantitative real-time PCR using primers specific for PDX1, NGN3, PAX6, and insulin.

### Immunocytochemistry

- fix and block spheroids

Spheroids were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 minutes at room temperature and blocked with 5% normal goat serum in PBS for one hour.

- stain and image cells using immunocytochemistry

Cells were incubated overnight with primary antibodies against insulin, C-peptide, and PDX1, followed by fluorescently labeled secondary antibodies, and imaged using a confocal microscope.

### Example 5

- introduce dithizone staining

Dithizone staining was used to identify insulin-producing cells based on their ability to chelate zinc ions present in insulin granules.

Dithizone stock solution was prepared by dissolving 1 mg/mL in dimethyl sulfoxide and diluted 1:100 in PBS immediately before use.

- prepare DTZ stock solution

The dithizone stock solution was prepared by dissolving 10 mg of dithizone in 10 mL of dimethyl sulfoxide under sterile conditions.

- prepare working solution

The working solution was prepared by diluting the stock solution 1:100 in phosphate-buffered saline.

- incubate cells with DTZ solution

Differentiated cell clusters were incubated in the dithizone working solution for 15 minutes at 37°C.

- wash cells with PBS

Cells were washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline to remove unbound dye.

- examine differentiated islet-like clusters

Islet-like clusters stained red with dithizone were counted under a light microscope and confirmed to be insulin-positive by immunofluorescence.

- introduce insulin assay

An insulin secretion assay was performed to assess the glucose responsiveness of the differentiated cells.

Spheroids were then preincubated in Krebs-Ringer bicarbonate buffer for 30 minutes at 37°C.

The supernatant was collected and stored at -80°C for insulin quantification.

Insulin content was determined using a human insulin ELISA kit according to the manufacturer’s instructions.
