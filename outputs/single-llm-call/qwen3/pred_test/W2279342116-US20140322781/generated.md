# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define invention field

The present invention relates to the catalytic conversion of bio-derived alcohols, particularly ethanol, into hydrocarbon blendstocks suitable for use as transportation fuels or fuel additives. More specifically, the invention encompasses a novel catalytic process utilizing a heterobimetallic zeolite catalyst to convert aqueous fermentation streams directly into a high-yield mixture of liquid hydrocarbons without requiring prior purification or complete dewatering of the feedstock. The process enables the efficient transformation of renewable ethanol sources into a hydrocarbon product rich in branched alkanes, olefins, and aromatic compounds, with enhanced selectivity toward C3+ hydrocarbons, improved catalyst durability, and reduced benzene content in the final product stream. The invention further includes the synthesis, characterization, and application of metal-loaded zeolite catalysts, particularly those incorporating indium and vanadium within a ZSM-5 framework, for the direct upgrading of low-purity ethanol streams derived from biomass fermentation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- describe current conversion limitations
- highlight water concentration obstacle
- mention benzene content issue

Current methods for converting bio-ethanol into hydrocarbon fuels require extensive purification of fermentation streams to remove water and trace impurities such as acetaldehyde, methanol, acetic acid, and esters prior to catalytic upgrading. This requirement stems from the well-documented sensitivity of conventional zeolite catalysts, such as H-ZSM-5, to the presence of water, which competes for active acid sites and promotes catalyst deactivation through hydrolysis of framework aluminum or inhibition of dehydration steps. Additionally, many existing processes yield hydrocarbon products with elevated benzene content, which is undesirable due to regulatory restrictions on aromatic content in transportation fuels and associated health and environmental concerns. Conventional catalytic systems also suffer from rapid coking, necessitating frequent regeneration cycles that reduce process efficiency and increase operational costs. Furthermore, monometallic catalysts such as V-ZSM-5 or In-ZSM-5, while effective in certain reaction pathways, exhibit suboptimal selectivity profiles—either producing excessive ethylene at the expense of higher-value C3+ hydrocarbons or failing to sustain catalytic activity over extended operating periods. There remains a critical need for a catalytic system capable of operating efficiently on partially concentrated or even crude fermentation streams, delivering a hydrocarbon product with high octane number, low benzene content, and minimal oxygenate contamination, while maintaining prolonged operational stability without frequent regeneration.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce catalytic conversion method
- describe conversion without purification
- outline metal-loaded zeolite catalyst use
- explain benzene alkylation catalyst use
- mention reactor embodiments

The invention introduces a catalytic method for the direct conversion of aqueous ethanol streams into hydrocarbon blendstocks without the need for complete dewatering or purification of the feed. This method employs a heterobimetallic zeolite catalyst comprising indium and vanadium ions incorporated into the framework of a ZSM-5 zeolite, which enables high selectivity toward C3+ hydrocarbons while suppressing ethylene formation. The catalyst facilitates the conversion of ethanol and associated fermentation impurities into a complex hydrocarbon mixture rich in iso-paraffins, olefins, and aromatics, with a calculated research octane number exceeding 105 and a motor octane number above 90. The process operates effectively on ethanol streams containing up to 60% water by weight and trace impurities totaling less than 1%, eliminating the economic burden of energy-intensive distillation steps. In certain embodiments, the process further incorporates a benzene alkylation catalyst to selectively convert residual benzene into higher-value alkylated aromatics such as toluene, xylene, and ethylbenzene, thereby reducing overall benzene content in the final hydrocarbon product. The method may be implemented in fixed-bed, fluidized-bed, or continuous stirred-tank reactor configurations, either in single-stage or two-stage formats, and is compatible with integration into existing biomass-to-ethanol production facilities.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- define "about"

The term “about” as used herein refers to a value that may vary by ±10% from the stated numerical value, unless otherwise indicated, and encompasses all values within the range of experimental variability inherent to the measurement techniques employed, including but not limited to elemental analysis, chromatographic quantification, and spectroscopic characterization.

- define "alcohol"

The term “alcohol” as used herein refers to any organic compound containing one or more hydroxyl groups (-OH) attached to a saturated carbon atom, including but not limited to methanol, ethanol, propanol, butanol, and mixtures thereof, whether derived from fermentation, synthetic processes, or other biological or chemical sources.

- describe bio-alcohols

Bio-alcohols are alcohols produced through the fermentation of sugars derived from lignocellulosic biomass, starch crops, or sugarcane, and include ethanol as the predominant component, typically accompanied by minor quantities of methanol, propanol, acetaldehyde, acetic acid, ethyl acetate, and diacetyl, which are naturally formed during microbial metabolism and remain in the fermentation broth even after initial distillation.

- describe other alcohols

Other alcohols include synthetically produced alcohols such as those derived from syngas fermentation, electrochemical reduction of carbon dioxide, or catalytic hydrogenation of carbonyl compounds, which may be blended with bio-alcohols to form feedstocks for the catalytic conversion process described herein.

- describe fermentation streams

Fermentation streams are aqueous mixtures resulting from the microbial conversion of sugars into ethanol and byproducts, typically containing between 5% and 20% ethanol by weight, with the remainder comprising water and trace organic impurities, and may be obtained at any stage of processing, including after primary distillation, condensation, or partial dehydration.

- describe concentration of fermentation streams

The concentration of fermentation streams may range from 5% to 60% ethanol by weight, and the process of the invention is operable across this entire range without requiring additional concentration steps beyond those typically employed in standard industrial ethanol production, such as flash evaporation or membrane separation.

- describe dewatering of fermentation streams

Dewatering of fermentation streams refers to any process that reduces the water content of the feed, including distillation, molecular sieve adsorption, pervaporation, or membrane filtration; however, the invention does not require complete dewatering, and the process functions effectively even when water content remains above 40% by weight.

- define "hydrocarbon"

The term “hydrocarbon” as used herein refers to any organic compound consisting exclusively of carbon and hydrogen atoms, including alkanes, alkenes, cycloalkanes, aromatics, and their substituted derivatives, whether gaseous, liquid, or solid under ambient conditions.

- describe saturated hydrocarbons

Saturated hydrocarbons are acyclic or cyclic hydrocarbons containing only single bonds between carbon atoms, including linear, branched, and cyclic alkanes, which contribute to the stability and combustion efficiency of the final fuel blendstock.

- describe alkanes

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with the general formula CnH2n+2, including ethane, propane, butane, pentane, hexane, and higher homologs, which may be present in the product stream as components of the gaseous and liquid fractions.

- describe branched alkanes

Branched alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons in which the carbon backbone contains one or more alkyl substituents, such as isobutane, isopentane, and 2-methylhexane, which exhibit higher octane numbers than their linear counterparts and are preferred components in gasoline blendstocks.

- describe other hydrocarbon products

Other hydrocarbon products include olefins such as propylene and butylene, naphthenes such as cyclohexane and methylcyclopentane, and aromatic hydrocarbons such as benzene, toluene, xylenes, ethylbenzene, and cumene, which collectively contribute to the energy density and combustion characteristics of the final product.

- describe hydrocarbon product as fuel or blendstock

The hydrocarbon product produced by the process of the invention is suitable for use as a standalone transportation fuel or as a blendstock for gasoline, diesel, or jet fuel, with physical and chemical properties that meet or exceed industry standards for volatility, octane rating, and aromatic content.

- describe composition of hydrocarbon product

The hydrocarbon product comprises, by volume, between 3% and 5% paraffins, 20% and 30% iso-paraffins, 5% and 8% olefins, 4% and 7% naphthenes, 55% and 65% aromatics, and less than 1% oxygenates, with an average molecular weight between 90 and 110 and a Reid vapor pressure between 4.5 and 6.0 psi.

- describe applications of hydrocarbon product

The hydrocarbon product finds application in the production of renewable gasoline, aviation fuel, and diesel substitutes, as well as in the formulation of high-octane racing fuels, chemical feedstocks, and specialty solvents, offering a drop-in replacement for petroleum-derived hydrocarbons without requiring engine modification.

- describe versatility of process

The process is versatile in its feedstock tolerance, operating effectively on ethanol derived from corn, sugarcane, cellulosic biomass, algae, or waste streams, and is compatible with both batch and continuous operation modes, enabling integration into existing biorefineries with minimal retrofitting.

- introduce metal-loaded zeolite catalyst

The invention employs a metal-loaded zeolite catalyst comprising a ZSM-5 framework structure into which indium and vanadium cations have been incorporated via ion exchange, resulting in a heterobimetallic catalyst with synergistic catalytic properties that enhance C3+ selectivity and extend catalyst lifetime.

- introduce benzene alkylation catalyst

A benzene alkylation catalyst, selected from the group consisting of zeolite-based catalysts, Friedel-Crafts catalysts, and supported acid catalysts, may be employed downstream or in tandem with the metal-loaded zeolite catalyst to convert residual benzene into alkylated aromatic compounds, thereby reducing benzene content in the final hydrocarbon fraction.

- describe two-stage process

In a two-stage process, the first stage involves the conversion of ethanol and impurities into a hydrocarbon mixture over the metal-loaded zeolite catalyst, followed by a second stage in which the effluent is passed over a benzene alkylation catalyst to selectively alkylate benzene, reducing its concentration in the product stream.

- describe first stage of two-stage process

The first stage operates at temperatures between 250°C and 450°C, under pressures ranging from atmospheric to 10 bar, with a weight hourly space velocity (WHSV) between 1.0 and 10.0 h⁻¹, and results in the formation of a hydrocarbon product containing ethylene, propylene, butenes, paraffins, and aromatics, with minimal oxygenate content.

- describe second stage of two-stage process

The second stage operates at temperatures between 200°C and 350°C, with a WHSV between 2.0 and 8.0 h⁻¹, and employs a catalyst such as H-ZSM-5, Y-zeolite, or a Friedel-Crafts catalyst to facilitate the alkylation of benzene with C2–C4 olefins, producing toluene, xylene, and ethylbenzene while consuming benzene.

- describe reaction temperatures

Reaction temperatures for the primary conversion stage are maintained between 250°C and 450°C, with optimal performance observed between 300°C and 400°C, while alkylation temperatures are maintained between 200°C and 350°C to balance reaction kinetics and catalyst stability.

- describe pressure conditions

Pressure conditions for the conversion process range from atmospheric to 10 bar absolute, with preferred operation at 1 to 5 bar to facilitate product removal and minimize side reactions such as cracking or excessive coking.

- describe reactor design

The reactor may be configured as a fixed-bed, fluidized-bed, or moving-bed system, constructed from materials resistant to thermal and chemical degradation, such as stainless steel, Inconel, or high-silica ceramics, and may incorporate internal heat exchangers or catalyst zoning to optimize temperature profiles.

- describe continuous or batch mode

The process may be operated in either continuous or batch mode, with continuous operation being preferred for industrial scale-up, enabling steady-state production and seamless integration with upstream fermentation and downstream separation units.

- describe additional reaction zones or processes

Additional reaction zones may include pre-treatment units for impurity removal, in-line quenching systems, condensation traps, or fractionation columns to separate water, light gases, and heavy residues from the liquid hydrocarbon product.

- introduce single-stage process

In a single-stage process, a physical mixture of the metal-loaded zeolite catalyst and a benzene alkylation catalyst is loaded into a single reactor, enabling simultaneous ethanol conversion and benzene alkylation in a single pass, thereby simplifying process design and reducing capital costs.

- describe mixture of metal-loaded zeolite and benzene alkylation catalysts

The mixture comprises a weight ratio of metal-loaded zeolite to benzene alkylation catalyst ranging from 1:1 to 10:1, with a preferred ratio of 5:1, ensuring sufficient activity for both ethanol upgrading and benzene consumption without compromising catalyst stability.

- describe alkylated benzene product

The alkylated benzene product includes toluene, xylenes, ethylbenzene, and propylbenzenes, which possess higher octane numbers than benzene and are desirable components in gasoline blendstocks, with total benzene content in the final product reduced to less than 0.5% by volume.

- describe other alkylated aromatic compounds

Other alkylated aromatic compounds include cumene, diethylbenzenes, and trimethylbenzenes, which may form in trace amounts under prolonged reaction conditions and contribute to the overall aromatic content and fuel performance.

- define "reduced in benzene content"

The term “reduced in benzene content” refers to a hydrocarbon product in which the concentration of benzene is less than 0.5% by volume, as determined by gas chromatography, and is at least 50% lower than the benzene concentration in the product stream produced by the metal-loaded zeolite catalyst alone.

- describe benzene content in hydrocarbon fraction

The benzene content in the hydrocarbon fraction, prior to alkylation, ranges from 1.5% to 4.0% by volume, depending on feedstock composition and operating conditions, and is reduced to below 0.5% by volume following alkylation over the benzene alkylation catalyst.

- integrate fermentation process with conversion process

The conversion process is integrated with the fermentation process by directing the partially concentrated fermentation stream directly from the distillation unit to the catalytic reactor, eliminating intermediate storage, purification, or drying steps and reducing overall energy consumption.

- describe fermentation process

The fermentation process involves the anaerobic conversion of sugars derived from biomass feedstocks by microorganisms such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae or engineered bacterial strains, producing an aqueous ethanol solution containing 5% to 20% ethanol and trace impurities, which is then subjected to minimal separation prior to catalytic upgrading.

- describe biomass reactor facility

The biomass reactor facility includes a pretreatment unit for lignocellulosic material, a hydrolysis reactor for sugar release, a fermentation bioreactor, and a primary distillation column, all of which are coupled directly to the catalytic conversion unit via a transfer line designed to maintain stream integrity and prevent contamination.

- describe biomass-to-ethanol conversion

Biomass-to-ethanol conversion encompasses the enzymatic or acid-catalyzed depolymerization of cellulose and hemicellulose into fermentable sugars, followed by microbial fermentation to ethanol, with yields ranging from 0.4 to 0.5 grams of ethanol per gram of sugar consumed.

- describe alternative sugar sources

Alternative sugar sources include molasses, whey permeate, glycerol from biodiesel production, and syrups derived from food processing waste, which may be fermented to ethanol and fed directly into the catalytic conversion process without requiring additional purification.

- integrate conversion process with ethanol production methods

The conversion process is designed to be modular and scalable, allowing integration with both first-generation ethanol plants using corn or sugarcane and second-generation biorefineries utilizing lignocellulosic feedstocks, enabling retrofitting without major infrastructure changes.

- describe zeolite portion of catalyst

The zeolite portion of the catalyst is a crystalline aluminosilicate with a MFI topology, characterized by a three-dimensional pore system with 10-membered ring openings, providing shape selectivity that favors the formation of branched and aromatic hydrocarbons over linear or bulky molecules.

- describe metal loaded into zeolite

The metals indium and vanadium are loaded into the zeolite framework via ion exchange, replacing protons or other cations at exchange sites within the zeolite channels, resulting in the formation of heterobimetallic active centers that promote dehydrogenation, oligomerization, and aromatization reactions.

- describe zeolite structure

The zeolite structure comprises a framework of SiO₄ and AlO₄ tetrahedra linked by shared oxygen atoms, forming channels and cages that accommodate the metal species, with the aluminum content determining the density of cationic exchange sites available for metal incorporation.

- describe zeolite properties

The zeolite exhibits high thermal stability, mechanical strength, and resistance to hydrothermal degradation, with a surface area exceeding 400 m²/g and a pore volume greater than 0.2 cm³/g, enabling sustained catalytic activity under prolonged reaction conditions.

- describe microporous zeolite

The zeolite is microporous, with pore diameters less than 2 nm, which restricts the diffusion of larger molecules and enhances selectivity toward desired hydrocarbon products by limiting the formation of coke precursors.

- describe mesoporous zeolite

In alternative embodiments, the zeolite may contain mesopores with diameters between 2 and 50 nm, introduced via desilication or templating methods, to improve mass transfer of bulkier intermediates and reduce diffusion limitations during prolonged operation.

- describe zeolite with both micropores and mesopores

The zeolite may possess a hierarchical pore structure combining micropores for shape selectivity and mesopores for enhanced accessibility, resulting in improved catalyst utilization and extended operational lifetime.

- list examples of zeolites

Examples of suitable zeolites include ZSM-5, ZSM-11, MCM-22, Beta, and Y-zeolite, with ZSM-5 being preferred due to its optimal balance of acidity, pore geometry, and thermal stability.

- describe silica-to-alumina ratio of zeolite

The silica-to-alumina ratio of the zeolite ranges from 15:1 to 50:1, with a preferred range of 20:1 to 35:1, balancing the number of acid sites required for catalysis with the structural stability necessary for long-term operation.

- describe ZSM-5 zeolite

ZSM-5 is a medium-pore, high-silica zeolite with the MFI topology, characterized by intersecting 10-membered ring channels, which provides excellent shape selectivity for the formation of aromatic hydrocarbons and suppresses the formation of large coke molecules.

- describe cationic species in zeolite

Cationic species in the zeolite include protons, alkali metal ions, alkaline earth metal ions, transition metal ions, and rare earth metal ions, which may be exchanged or partially replaced by indium and vanadium to tailor catalytic activity.

- list examples of cationic species

Examples of cationic species include H⁺, Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Cu²⁺, Fe³⁺, Zn²⁺, and La³⁺, each of which may influence the acidity, redox properties, or hydrothermal stability of the zeolite.

- describe amount of cationic species

The total concentration of exchangeable cationic species in the zeolite prior to metal loading is typically between 0.5 and 2.0 mmol/g, corresponding to an aluminum content of 1% to 4% by weight.

- describe catalytically active metal loaded into zeolite

The catalytically active metals loaded into the zeolite are indium and vanadium, which function synergistically to suppress ethylene formation, promote C–C coupling, and enhance aromatization, resulting in a hydrocarbon product with high C3+ selectivity.

- describe type of metal loaded into zeolite

The metals are loaded in their ionic forms, specifically In³⁺ and V⁵⁺, which are incorporated into the zeolite framework via ion exchange and stabilized by coordination to framework oxygen atoms.

- describe amount of metal loaded into zeolite

The amount of indium loaded into the zeolite ranges from 1.0 to 2.0 weight percent, while the amount of vanadium ranges from 0.5 to 1.0 weight percent, with a preferred composition of 1.6 wt% In and 0.6 wt% V.

- describe trivalent metal ion in zeolite

The trivalent metal ion in the zeolite is indium, which contributes to the stabilization of reaction intermediates and enhances the catalyst’s resistance to coking by modulating the strength and distribution of acid sites.

- describe amount of trivalent metal ion

The amount of trivalent metal ion, specifically indium, is maintained between 1.0 and 2.0 weight percent, with optimal performance observed at 1.6 weight percent, corresponding to approximately one indium ion per 500 silicon atoms.

- describe examples of trivalent metal ions

Examples of trivalent metal ions include In³⁺, Fe³⁺, Ga³⁺, Al³⁺, Cr³⁺, and lanthanide ions such as La³⁺, Ce³⁺, and Yb³⁺, each of which may be substituted for indium in alternative embodiments.

- describe zeolite catalysts

Zeolite catalysts employed in the invention are characterized by their ability to facilitate multiple reaction steps—including dehydration, oligomerization, cyclization, and aromatization—within a single catalyst particle, thereby minimizing the need for multi-reactor systems.

- define trivalent metal ions

Trivalent metal ions are metal species with a +3 oxidation state that can occupy cationic exchange sites within the zeolite framework and participate in redox or Lewis acid-catalyzed reactions.

- list main group metal ions

Main group metal ions include Al³⁺, Ga³⁺, and In³⁺, which are characterized by their ability to form stable tetrahedral or octahedral coordination environments within the zeolite lattice.

- list lanthanide metal ions

Lanthanide metal ions include La³⁺, Ce³⁺, Pr³⁺, Nd³⁺, Sm³⁺, Eu³⁺, Gd³⁺, Tb³⁺, Dy³⁺, Ho³⁺, Er³⁺, Tm³⁺, Yb³⁺, and Lu³⁺, which may be used singly or in combination to modify the acidity and redox properties of the catalyst.

- describe combinations of trivalent metal ions

Combinations of trivalent metal ions, such as In³⁺ and Fe³⁺, In³⁺ and Ga³⁺, or In³⁺ and La³⁺, may be employed to fine-tune the catalyst’s selectivity, activity, and stability, with In³⁺ being essential for suppressing ethylene formation.

- describe zeolite catalyst compositions

Zeolite catalyst compositions include the base zeolite, the exchanged metal ions, and any residual counterions, with the total metal content not exceeding 5 weight percent to preserve structural integrity and avoid pore blockage.

- describe metal-loaded zeolite synthesis

Metal-loaded zeolite synthesis involves the ion exchange of a protonated or ammonium-form zeolite with aqueous solutions of metal salts, followed by washing, drying, and calcination to stabilize the metal species within the zeolite framework.

- describe impregnating zeolite with metals

Impregnation is performed by contacting the zeolite with an aqueous solution of metal nitrate or chloride salts under reflux conditions at temperatures between 70°C and 90°C for a duration of 12 to 24 hours to ensure complete ion exchange.

- describe thermal treatment step

The thermal treatment step involves heating the exchanged zeolite in an oxidative atmosphere at a ramp rate of 1°C/min to a final temperature of 500°C, followed by holding for 3 to 5 hours to decompose residual anions and stabilize the metal in its active oxidation state.

- describe metal-impregnating solution

The metal-impregnating solution is an aqueous solution containing indium(III) nitrate and vanadium(III) chloride or vanadium(V) oxide, with concentrations sufficient to achieve the desired metal loading, typically 0.015 M for indium and 0.05 M for vanadium.

- describe forming slurry and extruding

The catalyst may be formed into pellets or extrudates by mixing the powdered zeolite with a binder such as alumina or silica, forming a slurry, extruding into cylindrical shapes, drying, and calcining to produce mechanically robust catalyst particles.

- describe drying and firing steps

Drying is conducted at 100°C to 120°C for 12 hours to remove physisorbed water, followed by firing at 450°C to 550°C for 4 to 6 hours in air to complete the removal of organic residues and stabilize the metal species.

- describe heat treatment conditions

Heat treatment conditions include a controlled atmosphere of dry air or nitrogen, with ramp rates not exceeding 2°C/min and hold times sufficient to ensure complete decomposition of precursor salts and formation of stable metal-oxo species.

- describe preparing Cu-ZSM-5 catalyst

Cu-ZSM-5 catalyst is prepared by ion exchange of H-ZSM-5 with copper(II) nitrate solution, followed by calcination at 500°C, serving as a comparative catalyst in experimental validation.

- describe preparing Cu-Fe-ZSM-5 catalyst

Cu-Fe-ZSM-5 catalyst is prepared by sequential ion exchange with copper and iron salts, followed by calcination, and serves as a reference bimetallic system for comparison with InV-ZSM-5.

- describe particle sizes of zeolite catalyst

The particle size of the zeolite catalyst ranges from 100 to 500 micrometers, optimized for fluid dynamics in fixed-bed reactors, with smaller particles used in fluidized systems and larger particles employed in trickle-bed configurations.

- describe film or coating forms of zeolite catalyst

The zeolite catalyst may be deposited as a thin film or coating on structured supports such as honeycombs, monoliths, or corrugated metal sheets to enhance heat transfer and reduce pressure drop in reactor systems.

- describe bulk solid form of zeolite catalyst

The bulk solid form of the catalyst comprises extruded pellets, spheres, or granules with densities between 0.6 and 1.0 g/cm³, suitable for use in fixed-bed reactors with minimal attrition.

- describe fibrous or mesh forms of zeolite catalyst

Fibrous or mesh forms of the catalyst are produced by embedding zeolite crystals within ceramic or polymer matrices to form flexible, high-surface-area structures suitable for use in rotating or fluidized reactors.

- describe benzene alkylation catalysts

Benzene alkylation catalysts include zeolites such as H-ZSM-5, H-Beta, and Y-zeolite, as well as Friedel-Crafts catalysts such as AlCl₃, FeCl₃, or supported phosphoric acid, which facilitate the reaction between benzene and olefins to form alkylated aromatics.

- describe zeolite-based alkylation catalysts

Zeolite-based alkylation catalysts are preferred due to their regenerability, thermal stability, and ability to operate under vapor-phase conditions without generating hazardous waste streams.

- describe Friedel-Crafts type alkylation catalysts

Friedel-Crafts type alkylation catalysts are homogeneous or heterogeneous Lewis acid catalysts that promote electrophilic substitution reactions between benzene and alkenes, with supported variants being preferred for ease of separation and reuse.

- describe process conditions for alkylation

Process conditions for alkylation include temperatures between 200°C and 350°C, pressures between 1 and 5 bar, and WHSV values between 2 and 8 h⁻¹, with the feed stream containing benzene and C2–C4 olefins generated from the primary conversion stage.

- describe tuning fractionation of hydrocarbon product

Fractionation of the hydrocarbon product may be tuned by adjusting the ratio of conversion catalyst to alkylation catalyst, the reactor temperature profile, or the residence time, thereby controlling the relative abundance of paraffins, olefins, and aromatics in the final product.

- describe weight ratio of conversion and alkylation catalysts

The weight ratio of the metal-loaded zeolite conversion catalyst to the benzene alkylation catalyst is maintained between 1:1 and 10:1, with a preferred ratio of 5:1 to ensure complete consumption of benzene without over-alkylation or excessive cracking.

- describe support materials for catalysts

Support materials for the catalysts include refractory oxides such as alumina, silica, titania, and zirconia, as well as metallic substrates such as stainless steel, Inconel, or nickel alloys, selected for thermal expansion compatibility and mechanical durability.

- describe honeycomb structure support

The honeycomb structure support is a monolithic ceramic or metallic substrate with parallel channels, providing high geometric surface area and low pressure drop, ideal for coating with thin layers of zeolite catalyst for use in compact reactor designs.

- describe refractory composition of support material

The refractory composition of the support material includes aluminum silicate, silicon carbide, or cordierite, chosen for their ability to withstand repeated thermal cycling and exposure to reactive hydrocarbon streams without degradation.

- describe corrugated metal sheet support

The corrugated metal sheet support is fabricated from high-temperature alloys and coated with a porous layer of zeolite catalyst, enabling high heat transfer rates and efficient contact between reactant gases and catalytic surfaces.

- describe layered metal sheet structure

The layered metal sheet structure comprises alternating layers of catalytic material and inert spacers, forming a stacked reactor configuration that enhances radial mixing and minimizes hot spot formation during exothermic reactions.

- describe examples of the invention

In a first example, a catalyst comprising 1.6 wt% In and 0.6 wt% V in ZSM-5 was tested with a 40% aqueous ethanol stream containing 0.1% impurities, yielding a hydrocarbon product with 35% C3+ selectivity and 19% ethylene, with benzene content at 2.8%. When coupled with a H-ZSM-5 alkylation catalyst, benzene was reduced to 0.3%. In a second example, the catalyst was operated continuously for 120 hours at 350°C and WHSV 4.0 h⁻¹, with regeneration at 500°C restoring 98% of initial activity.

The scope of the invention encompasses the catalyst composition, the process conditions, the reactor configurations, the integration with fermentation systems, and the resulting hydrocarbon product, all of which are novel and non-obvious over the prior art, and are protected by the claims herein.

- describe zeolite catalyst preparation methods

Zeolite catalyst preparation methods include ion exchange, impregnation, co-precipitation, and solid-state reaction, with ion exchange being preferred for uniform metal distribution and minimal framework damage.

- describe metal exchange processes

Metal exchange processes involve contacting the zeolite with a solution of metal salt under reflux, followed by washing to remove unexchanged ions, and drying and calcination to stabilize the metal in its catalytically active form.

- describe calcination step

The calcination step is performed in air or oxygen at temperatures between 450°C and 550°C for 3 to 6 hours to decompose organic residues, oxidize metal precursors, and activate the catalyst for hydrocarbon conversion.

- describe drying step

The drying step is conducted at 100°C to 120°C for 12 to 24 hours to remove water and volatile components prior to calcination, ensuring consistent catalyst performance and reproducibility.

- describe thermal treatment conditions

Thermal treatment conditions are controlled to avoid sintering of metal species or collapse of the zeolite framework, with ramp rates not exceeding 2°C/min and maximum temperatures not exceeding 550°C.

- describe catalyst composition variations

Catalyst composition variations include altering the silica-to-alumina ratio, the metal loading levels, the sequence of metal incorporation, and the presence of secondary metals, each of which affects selectivity, activity, and durability.

- describe catalyst application variations

Catalyst application variations include use in fixed-bed, fluidized-bed, or membrane reactors; integration with membrane-based water removal; use in hybrid systems with electrochemical upgrading; and deployment in modular, mobile units for decentralized biofuel production.
