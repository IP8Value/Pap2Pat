# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to rheumatoid arthritis diagnosis

The present invention relates to the field of diagnostic medicine, specifically to methods and reagents for the detection, diagnosis, and monitoring of rheumatoid arthritis through the measurement of specific microRNA expression levels in biological samples derived from human blood. The invention provides a novel, non-invasive, and highly specific approach to identifying rheumatoid arthritis in its early stages, even in patients who are seronegative for conventional biomarkers such as anti-citrullinated protein antibodies and rheumatoid factor. The diagnostic methodology is grounded in the discovery that certain microRNAs, particularly miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, exhibit consistent and statistically significant alterations in plasma concentration among individuals with rheumatoid arthritis compared to healthy controls and patients with other inflammatory or autoimmune conditions. These microRNAs serve as molecular signatures that reflect the underlying pathophysiology of rheumatoid arthritis independently of systemic inflammation or joint degeneration, thereby enabling precise differentiation from osteoarthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, and other arthropathies. The invention further encompasses the use of composite values derived from the combined expression levels of these microRNAs to enhance diagnostic accuracy, predict disease activity, and guide therapeutic decision-making.

## BACKGROUND ART

- introduce rheumatoid arthritis
- introduce microRNA
- introduce prior art in rheumatoid arthritis diagnosis

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic, systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by persistent inflammation of the synovial joints, leading to progressive cartilage destruction, bone erosion, and functional impairment. It affects approximately one percent of the global population and is associated with significant morbidity, reduced quality of life, and increased mortality if left untreated. Early diagnosis is critical, as timely initiation of disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs and biologic therapies can halt or significantly slow disease progression. Current diagnostic criteria, including those established by the American College of Rheumatology and the European League Against Rheumatism, rely on a combination of clinical symptoms, radiographic findings, and serological markers such as rheumatoid factor and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies. However, these markers lack sensitivity in early disease stages and exhibit poor specificity, particularly in seronegative patients, who may remain undiagnosed or misdiagnosed for extended periods. 

MicroRNAs are small, non-coding RNA molecules of approximately 22 nucleotides in length that regulate gene expression at the post-transcriptional level by binding to complementary sequences in messenger RNAs, thereby inducing mRNA degradation or translational repression. They are stable in biological fluids such as plasma, protected from degradation by encapsulation within extracellular vesicles or association with RNA-binding proteins such as Argonaute 2. Their expression profiles are tissue-specific and dynamically altered in response to pathological processes, making them promising candidates for biomarker development. Prior studies have explored the utility of circulating microRNAs in rheumatoid arthritis, identifying candidates such as miR-132, miR-16, and miR-223. However, these markers have demonstrated limited specificity, failing to distinguish rheumatoid arthritis from other inflammatory conditions such as osteoarthritis or systemic lupus erythematosus. Furthermore, prior methods have relied on normalization using endogenous microRNAs whose expression varies with disease state, introducing significant analytical variability. There remains a critical unmet need for a reliable, reproducible, and highly specific diagnostic tool that can identify rheumatoid arthritis with high sensitivity in seronegative individuals and differentiate it from other arthritic conditions with minimal false positives.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

- describe limitations of current diagnosis methods
- describe need for new diagnosis marker

Current diagnostic approaches for rheumatoid arthritis are hindered by substantial limitations in sensitivity and specificity, particularly during the early phases of disease when intervention is most effective. The reliance on serological markers such as rheumatoid factor and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies excludes a significant subset of patients—up to 30 percent—who are seronegative yet exhibit clear clinical and radiological evidence of disease. Additionally, these markers do not correlate reliably with disease activity or progression, limiting their utility in monitoring therapeutic response. Existing biomarkers derived from inflammatory cytokines, such as C-reactive protein and erythrocyte sedimentation rate, reflect general systemic inflammation rather than disease-specific pathology, resulting in frequent false positives in patients with infections, trauma, or other autoimmune conditions. There is therefore a pressing need for a novel diagnostic marker that is not only specific to rheumatoid arthritis but also detectable in the pre-clinical or early clinical stages, independent of serological status, and capable of distinguishing rheumatoid arthritis from other forms of arthritis with high accuracy. Such a marker must be quantifiable in easily accessible biological fluids, stable under routine clinical handling, and amenable to standardized, high-throughput measurement techniques.

The invention addresses these challenges by demonstrating that the plasma concentrations of microRNA-24, microRNA-125a-5p, and microRNA-30a-5p are significantly elevated in individuals with rheumatoid arthritis compared to healthy controls and patients with osteoarthritis or systemic lupus erythematosus. These microRNAs are not merely indicators of general inflammation but are specifically associated with the pathogenic processes underlying rheumatoid arthritis, as evidenced by their lack of correlation with conventional inflammatory markers such as C-reactive protein and erythrocyte sedimentation rate in the case of miR-125a-5p. The sensitivity and specificity of individual microRNAs are substantial, with miR-125a-5p achieving a specificity of 89.5 percent and miR-24 achieving 89.5 percent, but the combination of these three microRNAs into a composite diagnostic score, termed ePRAM, increases diagnostic accuracy to an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.89, with sensitivity of 78.4 percent and specificity of 92.3 percent. This composite value is calculated using a multivariate logistic regression model that incorporates the relative expression levels of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, with the latter serving as a stabilizing internal reference to mitigate technical variability. The method of testing involves the extraction of total RNA from plasma, followed by quantitative measurement of the target microRNAs using reverse transcription followed by real-time polymerase chain reaction, hybridization-based arrays, or Northern blotting, with normalization achieved through the use of exogenous synthetic cel-miR-39 as a spike-in control. The expression levels are then correlated with the presence of rheumatoid arthritis pathology by comparison against predefined cutoff values derived from statistical analysis of large, independent cohorts. Variations of the method include the addition of anti-citrullinated protein antibody levels to the composite score to further refine diagnostic precision, the use of different nucleic acid probes or primers designed to detect single-nucleotide variants of the target microRNAs, and the application of the method to dried blood spots or other stabilized plasma derivatives. The diagnostic reagent comprises a set of nucleic acid probes or primers specific for miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, immobilized on solid supports such as microarrays or integrated into multiplexed real-time PCR kits, along with necessary reagents for RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and amplification. The reagent may further include calibration standards, internal controls, and software algorithms for automated calculation of the ePRAM score, enabling its implementation in clinical laboratories without specialized expertise in molecular biology.

The present invention provides a transformative advancement in the diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis by offering a highly specific, sensitive, and reproducible method that overcomes the critical limitations of existing serological and clinical criteria. It enables the detection of rheumatoid arthritis in seronegative patients with a diagnostic accuracy that surpasses conventional markers, thereby facilitating earlier initiation of disease-modifying therapy and improving long-term outcomes. The composite ePRAM score is not confounded by general inflammation, allowing for reliable differentiation from osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus, conditions that frequently mimic rheumatoid arthritis in clinical presentation. The method is minimally invasive, requiring only a small volume of plasma, and is compatible with standard laboratory instrumentation, making it readily scalable for routine clinical use. Furthermore, the ePRAM score correlates with disease activity indices such as DAS28, providing not only diagnostic but also prognostic utility. The invention thus represents a paradigm shift in rheumatoid arthritis diagnostics, transitioning from reliance on indirect, non-specific markers to a direct, molecularly defined signature that reflects the true pathobiology of the disease.

The test method for determining the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis involves the collection of a blood sample from a mammalian test subject, preferably a human, followed by the separation of plasma by centrifugation under controlled conditions to prevent cellular contamination. The plasma sample is then subjected to total RNA extraction using a phenol-chloroform-based protocol, with the addition of a synthetic spike-in control, cel-miR-39, to normalize for variations in RNA recovery and amplification efficiency. MicroRNA expression levels are quantified using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, wherein specific primers are designed to bind to the mature sequences of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, with amplification monitored in real time using fluorescent dyes or sequence-specific probes. Alternatively, the expression levels may be determined via hybridization-based microarray platforms or Northern blotting, utilizing labeled oligonucleotide probes complementary to the target microRNAs. The expression levels of each microRNA are normalized relative to the spike-in control and then compared to predefined cutoff values derived from receiver operating characteristic analysis of large, independent cohorts of rheumatoid arthritis patients and healthy controls. The cutoff values are established to maximize the sum of sensitivity and specificity, with miR-24 having a cutoff of 1.46 pM, miR-125a-5p at 8.17×10⁻² pM, and miR-30a-5p serving as a stabilizing reference. A composite diagnostic score, ePRAM, is calculated using a multivariate logistic regression model incorporating the normalized expression levels of these three microRNAs, with a threshold of 0.50 used to classify samples as positive or negative for rheumatoid arthritis. This score demonstrates a sensitivity of 78.4 percent and a specificity of 92.3 percent, significantly outperforming individual markers. The method further includes the optional integration of anti-citrullinated protein antibody levels into the composite score to enhance diagnostic precision, particularly in borderline cases. The test is validated by demonstrating that the expression levels of these microRNAs are significantly elevated in rheumatoid arthritis compared to osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus, with p-values below 0.01 for all three markers in these comparisons. The method is applicable to both treatment-naïve and treated patients, as the microRNA levels are unaffected by disease-modifying therapies. The nucleic acid probes and primers used in the method may be chemically modified to enhance binding affinity or resistance to nuclease degradation, and may be synthesized using solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry. The method enables the diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis even in anti-CCP antibody-negative patients, with 73 percent of such individuals testing positive by ePRAM. The correlation between ePRAM and clinical indices such as DAS28 confirms its utility not only for diagnosis but also for monitoring disease activity. The method is applicable to any mammalian subject, with human application being the primary embodiment, and is suitable for use in point-of-care settings with appropriate miniaturization and automation.

The diagnostic reagent for determining the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis comprises a kit containing a set of nucleic acid probes or primers specifically designed to detect the mature sequences of microRNA-24, microRNA-125a-5p, and microRNA-30a-5p. Each probe or primer is chemically synthesized with high purity and may be labeled with fluorescent dyes, biotin, or other detectable moieties to facilitate quantification. The reagent further includes a synthetic spike-in control, cel-miR-39, for normalization, along with reagents for RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and real-time polymerase chain reaction, including reverse transcriptase, DNA polymerase, deoxynucleotide triphosphates, reaction buffers, and amplification controls. The probes may be immobilized on a solid support such as a glass slide, nitrocellulose membrane, or microbead array for use in hybridization-based detection platforms. For real-time PCR applications, the reagent includes TaqMan probes or SYBR Green-compatible primers with optimized melting temperatures and amplicon lengths to ensure specificity. The reagent may also include calibration standards with known concentrations of the target microRNAs, negative controls consisting of plasma from healthy individuals, and software for automated calculation of the ePRAM score based on input Ct values. For use in microarray or Northern blotting formats, the reagent includes hybridization buffers, wash solutions, and detection reagents such as streptavidin-conjugated enzymes or fluorescently labeled antibodies. The reagent may be further configured to include components for the simultaneous measurement of anti-citrullinated protein antibody levels, enabling the integration of serological and molecular data into a unified diagnostic output. The reagent is packaged in a sterile, temperature-stable format suitable for storage at –20°C and is designed for compatibility with standard laboratory equipment, including real-time PCR thermocyclers, microarray scanners, and automated liquid handling systems. The composition of the reagent ensures reproducibility across multiple testing sites and operators, fulfilling the requirements for clinical validation and regulatory approval.

## EXAMPLES

- describe method of plasma sample preparation

### Method

- prepare plasma sample
- prepare total RNA
- isolate miRNA

- perform reverse transcription and real-time PCR

Blood samples were collected from human subjects into EDTA-containing tubes and centrifuged at 1400 × g for seven minutes at 4°C to separate plasma, followed by a second centrifugation at 3000 × g for five minutes to remove residual cellular debris. Plasma was aliquoted and stored at –80°C until analysis. Total RNA was extracted from 150 µl of plasma using TriPure Isolation Reagent and purified using RNeasy Mini Spin Columns. Synthetic cel-miR-39 was added at 25 fmol per sample prior to extraction to serve as an exogenous normalization control. For microarray analysis, 200 ng of total RNA was reverse transcribed using the TaqMan MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit and amplified using TaqMan Human miRNA Array Cards A v3.0 and B v2.0 on an Applied Biosystems 7900HT system. For quantitative real-time PCR, reverse transcription was performed using the NCode VILO miRNA cDNA Synthesis Kit, followed by amplification with EXPRESS SYBR GreenER qPCR SuperMix on a 7500 Real-Time PCR System. Clinical indices including DAS28, CRP, ESR, RF, and anti-CCP were determined using standard clinical assays. Statistical analysis was performed using JMP 8 software, with Student’s t-test, Pearson correlation, and receiver operating characteristic analysis applied as appropriate. Internal standard candidates were evaluated using geNorm and NormFinder algorithms, with cel-miR-39 selected as the optimal normalization control due to its minimal variability across samples.

Microarray analysis of plasma from rheumatoid arthritis patients and healthy controls revealed 17 microRNAs with differential expression, of which 11 were confirmed by quantitative PCR. Among these, miR-24, miR-26a, and miR-125a-5p demonstrated the highest area under the ROC curve, with values of 0.84, 0.81, and 0.91, respectively. The combination of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p into the ePRAM score yielded an AUC of 0.89, with a sensitivity of 78.4 percent and specificity of 92.3 percent at a cutoff of 0.50. The ePRAM score showed significant correlation with DAS28(ESR) and DAS28(CRP) (r = 0.41 and r = 0.39, respectively), but not with CRP or ESR alone. In patients with osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus, ePRAM levels were significantly lower than in rheumatoid arthritis, confirming disease specificity. The ePRAM score correctly identified 73 percent of anti-CCP antibody-negative rheumatoid arthritis patients, demonstrating its utility in seronegative populations.

## COMPARATIVE EXAMPLE

- examine expression levels of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p in osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus
- analyze results and conclude specificity to rheumatoid arthritis

Plasma samples from 24 patients with osteoarthritis and 11 patients with systemic lupus erythematosus were analyzed for miR-24 and miR-125a-5p expression levels using the same quantitative PCR protocol. The mean expression levels of both microRNAs in osteoarthritis patients were significantly lower than in rheumatoid arthritis patients (p = 4.5×10⁻²⁴ and p = 3.2×10⁻¹⁵, respectively). Similarly, in systemic lupus erythematosus patients, miR-24 levels were significantly reduced (p = 0.01), while miR-125a-5p levels showed a trend toward reduction (p = 0.1). The ePRAM score was significantly lower in both groups compared to rheumatoid arthritis (p = 5.4×10⁻⁹ for osteoarthritis and p = 0.01 for systemic lupus erythematosus). These findings demonstrate that the elevated expression of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p is specific to rheumatoid arthritis and not a general response to joint damage or systemic autoimmunity, thereby validating the diagnostic specificity of the method.
