## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to brain-machine interfaces, and more specifically to systems and methods for decoding neural signals into continuous motor commands for control of external devices such as computer cursors, robotic limbs, or prosthetic actuators. The invention provides a novel computational framework for translating patterns of neural activity recorded from the cerebral cortex into accurate, stable, and robust kinematic outputs, even in the presence of substantial temporal variability in neural signal characteristics. This technology is particularly suited for applications in neuroprosthetics, assistive robotics, and neurorehabilitation, where long-term reliability and minimal need for recalibration are critical for clinical utility and user independence.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Brain-machine interfaces have long been envisioned as a means to restore motor function to individuals with paralysis or limb loss by translating neural activity directly into control signals for external devices. However, a persistent and clinically significant challenge has been the instability of neural recordings over time. Even in controlled laboratory settings with chronically implanted electrode arrays, the amplitude, firing rate, and spatial distribution of neural signals can drift due to factors such as tissue encapsulation, micro-movements of the implant, changes in electrode impedance, and fluctuations in the subject’s physiological or behavioral state. Conventional decoding algorithms, such as linear Kalman filters, are highly sensitive to such nonstationarities and typically require frequent recalibration—often daily—to maintain acceptable performance. This need for repeated user engagement in calibration sessions imposes a substantial burden on users, limits the practicality of long-term deployment, and undermines the autonomy that such systems are intended to provide. Existing approaches that attempt to mitigate this issue through adaptive filtering or online parameter updates introduce delays, require periods of user inactivity, and often fail to recover performance when the nature of the neural signal change exceeds the model’s capacity to adapt. There remains a critical unmet need for a decoder architecture that can maintain high-fidelity control over extended periods without requiring recalibration, even when confronted with abrupt or gradual changes in the underlying neural signal.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel brain-machine interface decoder based on a Big-Data Multiplicative Recurrent Neural Network (BD-MRNN) that achieves unprecedented robustness to temporal variability in neural recordings while maintaining high decoding accuracy. Unlike conventional decoders that rely on linear mappings or adaptive parameter updates, the BD-MRNN employs a nonlinear, multiplicative recurrent architecture that learns a rich, high-dimensional representation of the relationship between neural population activity and intended movement kinematics. The decoder is trained on large, heterogeneous datasets spanning hundreds of days of neural recordings, enabling it to internalize a broad spectrum of possible signal configurations. Crucially, during training, the input neural spike trains are systematically perturbed to simulate a wide range of potential recording condition changes, including global firing rate modulations and selective loss of electrode inputs. This training strategy, combined with the multiplicative dynamics of the recurrent network, allows the decoder to generalize effectively to novel, unseen recording states without requiring online adaptation. The resulting system delivers consistent, high-performance cursor control under ideal conditions and retains functionality even after the loss of multiple informative electrodes or when trained on data that is months or years old. These capabilities eliminate the need for daily recalibration and represent a transformative advance in the clinical viability of brain-machine interfaces.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Big-Data Multiplicative Recurrent Neural Network (BD-MRNN)

The invention comprises a computational system for decoding neural signals into continuous kinematic outputs using a multiplicative recurrent neural network architecture trained on extensive, temporally diverse datasets. The core of the system is a recurrent neural network whose dynamics are governed by a multiplicative interaction between the incoming neural spike counts and the internal hidden state of the network. This multiplicative structure enables the network to dynamically reconfigure its internal transformation rules based on the statistical properties of the input signal, effectively allowing the network to select an appropriate decoding strategy for the current neural context without requiring explicit parameter updates during operation. The network is trained offline using a large corpus of neural and kinematic data collected across many days, ensuring that the learned mapping encompasses a broad distribution of possible signal configurations. The resulting decoder operates as a fixed, non-adaptive system during closed-loop use, yet exhibits remarkable resilience to changes in neural signal characteristics that would render conventional decoders unusable.

### MRNN Definition

The recurrent network model is defined by a continuous-time dynamical system in which the state of the network evolves according to a set of differential equations that incorporate multiplicative interactions between the input and the recurrent weights. The activation state of the network is represented by an N-dimensional vector x(t), whose time derivative is governed by a combination of recurrent connections, input modulation, and a bias term. The recurrent connectivity matrix is not fixed but is instead parameterized by the input signal, allowing the input to modulate the strength and structure of the recurrent dynamics. This is achieved through a tensor-based formulation in which the input vector u(t) influences the recurrent weights via a low-rank factorization, reducing computational complexity while preserving the expressive power of multiplicative interactions. The hidden state evolves continuously over time, enabling the network to integrate information across temporal sequences and maintain context-dependent representations of neural activity.

### Multiplicative Interaction

The multiplicative interaction distinguishes the network from conventional additive recurrent architectures by allowing the input to directly modulate the recurrent weight matrix rather than merely adding a bias term. This is accomplished by expressing the recurrent weight tensor as a product of three low-dimensional matrices: one that maps the input to a set of latent factors, another that maps those factors to the recurrent dynamics, and a third that projects the hidden state back into the factor space. This factorization enables the network to represent a vast number of possible input-dependent dynamical regimes using a manageable number of parameters. Each unique combination of input statistics induces a distinct effective recurrent connectivity pattern, allowing the network to adapt its internal transformation rules implicitly during training. This mechanism enables the decoder to respond to changes in neural signal amplitude, noise structure, or electrode sensitivity without requiring explicit retraining or parameter adjustment during closed-loop operation.

### Equation Governing Dynamics

The dynamics of the network are governed by the following continuous-time differential equation: τ dx(t)/dt = -x(t) + J_u(t) r(t) + b_x, where τ is a time constant, r(t) = tanh(x(t)) is the firing rate vector, b_x is a bias term, and J_u(t) is a tensor that depends on the input u(t). The tensor J_u(t) is factorized as J_u(t) = J_{xf} diag(J_{fu} u(t)) J_{fx}, where J_{xf}, J_{fu}, and J_{fx} are learnable weight matrices of dimensions N×F, F×E, and F×N respectively, with E being the number of input electrodes and F the number of latent factors. This factorization reduces the number of parameters from O(N²E) to O(F(N+E)), making the model computationally tractable while preserving the multiplicative nature of the interaction. The time constant τ is set to a physiologically plausible value on the order of hundreds of milliseconds to ensure smooth, biologically realistic output trajectories.

### MRNN Output Definition

The output of the network is defined as a linear readout of the hidden firing rates, given by z(t) = W_o r(t) + b_z, where W_o is an M×N output weight matrix and b_z is an M-dimensional bias vector, with M being the dimensionality of the output kinematic variables (e.g., position and velocity in two dimensions). The output is computed continuously at each time step and is used to drive the position and velocity of a cursor or prosthetic device. The network is trained to minimize the mean squared error between the predicted output z(t) and the true kinematic trajectory recorded during behavioral tasks, using a supervised learning framework based on backpropagation through time.

### Network Construction for Cursor BMI Decoder

The network is constructed to simultaneously decode both hand position and hand velocity from neural spike counts, using two independent but parallel MRNNs. One network is trained to predict position, while the other is trained to predict velocity, with each network receiving the same neural input but optimized for a different kinematic variable. The outputs of these two networks are then linearly combined in real time to produce a final position estimate, with a high weighting given to velocity and a small stabilizing contribution from position to prevent drift. This dual-output architecture allows the decoder to leverage the temporal derivative information inherent in velocity signals while maintaining spatial stability through position feedback, resulting in smoother and more accurate cursor control than either signal alone.

### MRNN Initialization

The parameters of the network are initialized using a principled, randomized procedure designed to ensure stable dynamics at the onset of training. The weight matrices J_{xf}, J_{fu}, and J_{fx} are initialized with elements drawn independently from zero-mean Gaussian distributions with variances scaled by the number of latent factors and input dimensions to maintain a balanced signal-to-noise ratio across layers. The output matrix W_o is initialized to zero, and the bias vectors b_x and b_z are set to zero to ensure that the network begins in a neutral state with no prior bias toward any particular kinematic output. The number of hidden units N and latent factors F are chosen based on the number of input electrodes and the complexity of the task, with N set to 100 for monkeys with two electrode arrays and 50 for those with one, and F set equal to N in all cases to maximize representational capacity.

### Concatenating Neural Trials for Seeding the MRNN During Training

To provide the network with sufficient temporal context for learning long-range dependencies, training trials are constructed by concatenating five consecutive behavioral trials into a single continuous input sequence. The first two trials within each concatenated sequence are used solely to initialize the hidden state of the network and are not used for gradient computation. The subsequent three trials are used for learning, with the network’s parameters updated to minimize the error between predicted and actual kinematics over this segment. This approach ensures that the network learns to decode from a contextually rich, temporally extended neural stream rather than from isolated, short-duration bursts of activity, enhancing its ability to generalize across variable behavioral conditions.

### Perturbing the Neural Input During Training

During training, the input neural spike counts are intentionally perturbed to simulate a wide range of potential recording condition changes that may occur during closed-loop use. These perturbations are implemented as multiplicative scaling factors applied independently to each electrode and each trial. For each electrode and each training trial, a global scaling factor η_j and an electrode-specific scaling factor η_c are sampled from Gaussian distributions with mean one and standard deviations σ_trial and σ_electrode, respectively. These factors are then applied multiplicatively to the observed spike counts, resulting in a modified input that may include increased or decreased firing rates on individual electrodes or across the entire array. This process is repeated on every iteration of the optimization algorithm, ensuring that the network is exposed to a diverse and continuously varying set of signal configurations. The perturbations are designed to mimic real-world phenomena such as electrode drift, changes in arousal, or partial signal loss, thereby training the network to be robust to these conditions without requiring explicit knowledge of their source.

### Using Many Days Training Data

The decoder is trained using neural and kinematic data collected over hundreds of days, spanning many months of behavioral recording. This extensive dataset includes trials from diverse recording conditions, including different behavioral tasks, varying levels of neural signal quality, and distinct physiological states. By sampling trials randomly from across this entire dataset during each minibatch, the network is forced to learn a generalized mapping that is not overfit to any single day’s recording. The nonlinear, multiplicative architecture of the MRNN is uniquely suited to this task, as it can capture complex, nonlinear relationships between neural patterns and kinematics that vary across time. The use of such a large and heterogeneous dataset enables the decoder to internalize a broad library of possible neural-to-kinematic transformations, allowing it to accurately reconstruct movement even when confronted with conditions that differ substantially from any single training day.

### Network Output

Two separate MRNN networks are trained in parallel: one to output a two-dimensional estimate of hand position and another to output a two-dimensional estimate of hand velocity. The outputs of these networks are combined in real time using a weighted sum, with the velocity component weighted at 99% and the position component at 1% to minimize drift while retaining spatial stability. This hybrid output strategy allows the decoder to exploit the temporal precision of velocity signals for rapid cursor movement while using the position signal to correct for slow, cumulative errors that arise from integration noise or signal drift. The combined output is then mapped to the virtual workspace coordinates to drive the cursor position on a display, enabling smooth and accurate closed-loop control.

### Training and Running the Networks

The networks are trained offline using the Hessian-Free optimization method, which computes second-order gradients through backpropagation over time to efficiently navigate the high-dimensional parameter space. Training is performed over 200 optimization steps, with snapshots of the network saved every ten steps and the best-performing model selected based on cross-validation error on held-out data. Once trained, the network parameters are compiled into an embedded real-time operating system and executed on a dedicated processor with millisecond-level timing precision. During closed-loop operation, the system receives binned spike counts from the electrode array at 25-millisecond intervals, processes them through the MRNN, and outputs a continuous stream of position and velocity estimates. The cursor position is updated on a display in real time, providing visual feedback to the user. The hidden state of the network is initialized to zero at the start of each session, and the velocity and position outputs are similarly initialized to zero before the first input is processed. The model parameters remain fixed during closed-loop use, ensuring that the decoder does not adapt or learn during operation, but instead relies on its pre-trained robustness to handle changes in neural signal characteristics.

### Remark on Using Multiple Days of Training Data

The use of multiple days of training data is essential to the success of the invention. The network does not merely accumulate more data; it learns to recognize and generalize across the statistical structure of neural variability that emerges over time. This includes day-to-day fluctuations in firing rates, changes in the spatial distribution of active neurons, and variations in the relationship between neural activity and movement direction. The multiplicative architecture enables the network to encode these variations as distinct dynamical regimes within its recurrent weights, allowing it to respond appropriately to novel combinations of signal properties without requiring explicit recalibration. The training data distribution is assumed to be representative of the full range of possible recording conditions that the user may encounter during long-term use, and the network’s performance is validated across a wide spectrum of these conditions during offline and closed-loop testing.

### Description of Analogous Example of Handwritten Letter Classification

An analogous scenario arises in the field of handwriting recognition, where a system trained on a single writer’s handwriting may fail when presented with the writing of a different individual, even when the letters are identical in form. A robust system, however, can be trained on thousands of samples from hundreds of different writers, learning the underlying structure of letter shapes while accommodating variations in stroke speed, pressure, and orientation. Similarly, the BD-MRNN learns the underlying structure of the neural-to-kinematic mapping across hundreds of recording days, allowing it to generalize to new neural patterns in the same way that a handwriting recognizer generalizes to new writers. The perturbations applied during training serve as a form of synthetic variation, much like the natural variation introduced by different writers, enabling the system to become invariant to irrelevant signal changes while preserving sensitivity to meaningful kinematic signals.
