# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The construction of enantioenriched quaternary stereocenters remains a persistent challenge in organic synthesis due to the steric congestion and conformational rigidity inherent in such motifs. These structural elements are ubiquitous in biologically active molecules, including pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals, and natural products, where their three-dimensional architecture directly influences binding affinity, metabolic stability, and pharmacokinetic behavior. Despite decades of advancement in asymmetric catalysis, the efficient and selective formation of quaternary centers adjacent to carbonyl functionalities—particularly within saturated heterocyclic frameworks—has seen limited progress. The development of catalytic methods capable of installing these stereocenters with high enantiocontrol, using readily available starting materials and operationally simple conditions, continues to represent a critical unmet need in synthetic chemistry.

Transition metal-catalyzed allylic alkylation has long stood as a cornerstone strategy for the stereoselective formation of carbon–carbon bonds at allylic positions. Pioneered by Tsuji and later expanded by Trost and others, this transformation leverages the ability of late transition metals to generate π-allyl intermediates from allylic electrophiles, which are then intercepted by soft carbon nucleophiles. When applied to prochiral enolates derived from carbonyl compounds, this approach enables the direct construction of quaternary stereocenters. However, the extension of this methodology to α-substituted lactones and lactams has been notably underdeveloped. These heterocyclic scaffolds, which combine the structural rigidity of cyclic esters and amides with the reactivity of α-carbonyl systems, offer unique opportunities for the synthesis of complex, functionally dense architectures. Yet, prior to this invention, no general, catalytic, enantioselective method existed for their direct intermolecular allylic alkylation using unactivated allylic alcohols as electrophiles.

Previous efforts to access such products have relied on stoichiometric chiral auxiliaries, multistep sequences involving prefunctionalized substrates, or palladium-catalyzed decarboxylative processes that require the preparation of enol carbonates. These approaches suffer from significant limitations, including poor atom economy, restricted substrate scope, and the necessity for tedious synthetic preactivation. In particular, the use of α-acyl lactones and lactams as nucleophiles in enantioselective allylic alkylations has remained largely unexplored, despite their potential to serve as versatile precursors to acyclic quaternary centers via ring-opening reactions and to functionalized piperidines upon reduction—structures that dominate the landscape of CNS-active drugs and protease inhibitors. The absence of a broadly applicable catalytic system for this transformation has hindered the efficient exploration of chemical space around these privileged scaffolds.

Recent advances in nickel catalysis have demonstrated unique reactivity profiles in allylic substitution reactions, particularly in the context of β-keto esters and other activated carbonyls. Nickel’s lower oxophilicity compared to palladium, combined with its ability to facilitate single-electron transfer pathways and tolerate a wider array of functional groups, makes it an attractive candidate for addressing the challenges associated with less activated nucleophiles. However, no prior report has successfully applied nickel catalysis to the enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones or lactams with free allylic alcohols under intermolecular conditions. The development of such a method would represent a significant conceptual and practical advance, enabling direct, convergent, and scalable access to enantioenriched quaternary centers embedded within medicinally relevant heterocyclic cores.

The need for new reaction protocols that overcome the limitations of existing methodologies is therefore acute. A catalytic system capable of operating under mild conditions, employing inexpensive and commercially available catalysts and ligands, and accommodating a broad range of nucleophiles and electrophiles without requiring preactivation would substantially expand the synthetic toolbox available to medicinal chemists and process developers. Such a method would not only streamline the synthesis of complex targets but also enable the rapid generation of structurally diverse compound libraries for biological screening. The present invention fulfills this need through the discovery of a novel nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation protocol specifically tailored for α-acyl lactones and lactams.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel nickel-catalyzed allylic alkylation method for the enantioselective formation of quaternary stereocenters in α-acyl lactones and lactams. This method enables the direct coupling of unactivated allylic alcohols with α-substituted lactone and lactam nucleophiles under mild catalytic conditions to afford enantioenriched products bearing a chiral quaternary carbon center. The invention further encompasses compounds of Formula (IX), wherein the quaternary stereocenter is formed at the α-position of a five- or six-membered lactone or lactam ring, and wherein the substituents R₁ and R₂ are independently selected from hydrogen, alkyl, aryl, heteroaryl, alkenyl, alkynyl, or combinations thereof, with the proviso that at least one of R₁ or R₂ is not hydrogen. The invention further provides compounds of Formula (XI), wherein the nitrogen atom of the lactam ring is substituted with a protecting group selected from acyl, sulfonyl, or carbamate groups, and wherein the quaternary stereocenter is similarly formed at the α-position.

The nickel catalyst employed in the method is selected from Ni(0) sources, including but not limited to bis(1,5-cyclooctadiene)nickel(0), nickelocene, or nickel carbonyl, optionally in combination with a chiral ligand L. The chiral ligand L is a bidentate phosphine ligand, preferably a bisphosphine ligand, more preferably an enantioenriched bisphosphine ligand selected from the group consisting of (R)-P-phos, (S)-BINAP, (R,R)-DuPhos, (S,S)-Me-DuPhos, (R)-SegPhos, (S)-SynPhos, or derivatives thereof. The ligand is present in an amount ranging from 1.0 to 2.0 equivalents relative to the nickel source, and the catalyst loading is typically between 0.01 mol% and 10 mol%. The reaction is conducted in an organic solvent selected from diethyl ether, toluene, tetrahydrofuran, or mixtures thereof, at temperatures ranging from –20 °C to 30 °C.

The invention further provides methods for preparing compounds of Formula (VI), (VII), and (VIII), wherein Formula (VI) corresponds to a diol bearing a quaternary stereocenter, Formula (VII) corresponds to an enone bearing a quaternary stereocenter, and Formula (VIII) corresponds to a spirocyclic compound bearing a quaternary stereocenter, each derived from the compound of Formula (IX) or (XI) through selective reduction, nucleophilic addition, or ring-closing metathesis, respectively. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical agents synthesized from compounds of Formula (IX), (XI), (VI), (VII), or (VIII), wherein the pharmaceutical agent is selected from a piperidine derivative, a tetrahydrofuran derivative, or a fused bicyclic heterocycle. The invention further includes methods for treating a subject in need thereof by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a pharmaceutical composition comprising a compound of Formula (IX), (XI), (VI), (VII), or (VIII).

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention provides a novel and highly efficient method for the enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones and lactams using nickel catalysis and unactivated allylic alcohols as electrophiles. This transformation enables the direct, intermolecular construction of enantioenriched quaternary stereocenters at the α-position of five- and six-membered lactone and lactam rings, a structural motif previously inaccessible through catalytic asymmetric methods. The method is distinguished by its operational simplicity, broad substrate scope, high enantioselectivity, and compatibility with a wide variety of functional groups, including aryl, heteroaryl, alkenyl, and aliphatic substituents. The resulting products serve as versatile building blocks for the synthesis of complex, biologically relevant molecules, including piperidine derivatives, spirocycles, and acyclic polyfunctional scaffolds.

### Exemplary Methods

A compound of Formula (IX) is prepared by treating a compound of Formula (I) with a compound of Formula (IIa) or (IIb) in the presence of a nickel(0) source and a chiral ligand L in an organic solvent. The compound of Formula (I) is an α-acyl lactone or lactam, wherein the acyl group is selected from aryl, alkyl, or heteroaryl, and wherein the ester or amide group is selected from alkyl or aryl. The compound of Formula (IIa) or (IIb) is an allylic alcohol, wherein the substituent on the allylic system is selected from hydrogen, alkyl, aryl, heteroaryl, alkenyl, or alkynyl. The nickel(0) source is selected from Ni(COD)₂, Ni(acac)₂, or Ni₂(dba)₃. The ligand L is a chiral bisphosphine ligand, preferably (R)-P-phos or (S)-BINAP. The solvent is selected from diethyl ether, toluene, or tetrahydrofuran. The reaction is conducted at a temperature between –20 °C and 30 °C for a period of 12 to 72 hours. The compound of Formula (XI) is prepared by treating a compound of Formula (XII), wherein the nitrogen atom is protected with an acyl, sulfonyl, or carbamate group, with a nickel(0) source and ligand L under identical conditions. The compound of Formula (IX) or (XI) may be further transformed into a pharmaceutical agent by reduction, nucleophilic addition, or ring-closing metathesis. A compound of Formula (VI) is prepared by treating a compound of Formula (III) with a reducing agent selected from lithium aluminium hydride, sodium borohydride, or diisobutylaluminum hydride.

### Transition Metal Catalysts

The transition metal catalyst employed in the method is a nickel-based catalyst, preferably in the zero oxidation state. The nickel source is selected from bis(1,5-cyclooctadiene)nickel(0), nickelocene, or bis(benzonitrile)nickel dichloride, reduced in situ. The catalyst is generated in situ by combining the nickel source with a chiral ligand in a molar ratio ranging from 1:1 to 1:2. The catalyst loading is typically between 0.01 mol% and 10 mol%, with preferred loadings of 0.5 mol%, 1 mol%, or 2 mol%. In certain embodiments, the catalyst loading is 1 mol%. The catalyst system is stable under inert atmosphere and may be prepared in advance or generated directly in the reaction vessel. The use of nickel catalysts provides superior selectivity and functional group tolerance compared to palladium or copper analogues.

### Chiral Ligands

The enantioselectivity of the method is conferred by a chiral ligand that complexes with the nickel center to create an asymmetric environment around the metal. The ligand is preferably a bidentate phosphine ligand, more preferably a bisphosphine ligand, and most preferably an enantioenriched bisphosphine ligand. Preferred ligands include (R)-P-phos, (S)-BINAP, (R,R)-DuPhos, and (S,S)-Me-DuPhos. The ligand is used in an amount ranging from 1.0 to 2.0 equivalents relative to the nickel source. The ligand loading is typically between 1.0 mol% and 20 mol%, with preferred loadings of 1.2 mol%, 2.0 mol%, or 2.4 mol%. The enantioenrichment of the product is measured by chiral HPLC or GC and is typically greater than 80% ee, with preferred enantiomeric excess values of 85% ee, 90% ee, or greater than 95% ee. The compound of Formula (IX), (VI), (VII), (VIII), or (XI) is obtained in enantioenriched form, with the quaternary stereocenter being configured in the R or S configuration as determined by X-ray crystallography or optical rotation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Nickel-Catalyzed Enantioselective Allylic Alkylation of Lactones and Lactams with Unactivated Allylic Alcohols

A mixture of ethyl 1-benzoyl-2-oxopiperidine-3-carboxylate (1a, 0.2 mmol), allyl alcohol (2a, 0.6 mmol), Ni(COD)₂ (0.002 mmol), and (R)-P-phos (0.0024 mmol) in diethyl ether (2 mL) was stirred at 0 °C under argon for 48 hours. The reaction mixture was concentrated under reduced pressure and purified by flash chromatography to afford ethyl (R)-3-allyl-2-oxopiperidine-3-carboxylate (3aa) in 82% yield and 82% ee. The product was characterized by ¹H NMR, ¹³C NMR, and high-resolution mass spectrometry. Similar reactions were conducted with a variety of lactones and lactams, including methyl, tert-butyl, and tosyl-protected variants, as well as with substituted allylic alcohols bearing aryl, heteroaryl, alkenyl, and aliphatic substituents. Enantioselectivities ranged from 78% to 91% ee. Control experiments in the absence of chiral ligand yielded racemic product. Product transformations included reduction to diol 6 (88% yield), vinyl Grignard addition to enone 7 (67% yield), and lactam reduction to piperidine 9 (85% yield). X-ray crystallography confirmed the absolute configuration of 3af as R. All products were characterized by spectroscopic and analytical data.

### Example 2

A compound of Formula (XI) was prepared by treating a benzoyl-protected lactam with allyl alcohol under the conditions of Example 1, yielding the corresponding allylated product in 85% yield and 87% ee. The product was analyzed by chiral HPLC and found to be enantioenriched.
