# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention  
The present invention resides in the field of environmental biotechnology and agricultural microbiology, specifically in the development of biological agents for the remediation of soil contaminated with s-triazine herbicides, particularly atrazine. This field encompasses the isolation, characterization, and application of microorganisms capable of degrading persistent organic pollutants in agricultural environments. The need for effective, sustainable, and ecologically compatible methods to mitigate the environmental burden of atrazine—widely used as a selective herbicide in maize, sorghum, and other crops—has grown substantially due to its persistence in soil, mobility in groundwater, and potential toxicity to non-target organisms. Conventional remediation strategies, including chemical oxidation, soil excavation, and thermal treatment, are often cost-prohibitive, energy-intensive, and disruptive to soil structure and native microbial communities. While microbial degradation offers a promising alternative, the practical application of such approaches has been hindered by the limited ability of most atrazine-degrading strains to establish viable, persistent, and functionally active populations in the rhizosphere under field-relevant conditions.

- describe limitations of prior art  
Prior art in this domain has largely focused on the identification of atrazine-degrading bacteria from bulk soil or enriched cultures, with limited attention paid to their capacity for root colonization or their behavior in the dynamic rhizosphere environment. Many known atrazine-degrading strains, including certain Pseudomonas, Arthrobacter, and Sphingomonas species, exhibit degradation activity under laboratory conditions but fail to persist or function effectively when introduced into soil systems due to competition with indigenous microbiota, insufficient nutrient availability, or lack of motility necessary for rhizosphere penetration. Furthermore, existing methods for inoculating soil with degrading bacteria typically require high biomass applications—often exceeding 10⁷ colony-forming units per gram of soil—which are economically unfeasible for large-scale agricultural use. Additionally, prior approaches have not demonstrated that the degradation process is enhanced by the physical association of the degrader with plant roots, nor have they established a causal link between root colonization and the suppression of herbicide metabolite accumulation. The absence of a strain capable of both efficient atrazine degradation and robust root colonization has prevented the development of a practical, single-application bioremediation strategy that leverages natural plant-microbe interactions. Moreover, no prior art has described the use of seed inoculation as a delivery mechanism that enables the bacterium to establish itself in the rhizosphere at low inoculum densities while simultaneously reducing herbicide toxicity to the host plant.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- motivate novel atrazine-degrading bacterium  
The present invention is motivated by the discovery of a previously unrecognized biological agent capable of not only degrading atrazine and its primary metabolites but also actively colonizing the rhizosphere of multiple crop species following seed inoculation, thereby enabling efficient, low-dose, and sustainable remediation of contaminated soils. Unlike previously characterized strains, this bacterium demonstrates a unique combination of metabolic versatility, motility, and rhizosphere competence that allows it to respond dynamically to the presence of atrazine, effectively outcompeting indigenous microbial populations and establishing high-density, functionally active populations directly on root surfaces. This dual capability—degradation and colonization—transforms the bacterium from a mere degrader into an ecological agent that actively reshapes the rhizosphere microbiome to favor herbicide detoxification. The invention overcomes the fundamental limitation of prior art by demonstrating that the bacterium’s efficacy is not dependent on high inoculum loads or exogenous nutrient amendments, but rather on its intrinsic ability to exploit the plant root as a niche for proliferation and activity.

- describe properties of Arthrobacter ureafaciens liulou 1  
The invention encompasses a novel strain of Arthrobacter ureafaciens, designated as Liulou 1, which exhibits distinct physiological and ecological properties that distinguish it from all previously described strains of the species. This strain is capable of rapid growth on minimal media containing atrazine as the sole source of nitrogen and carbon, and it demonstrates exceptional tolerance to desiccation, osmotic stress, and nutrient limitation—conditions commonly encountered in agricultural soils. Unlike many other Arthrobacter strains, which are typically non-motile and predominantly coccoid in morphology, strain Liulou 1 transitions to a motile rod form under nutrient-restricted conditions and expresses a functional flagellar apparatus that enables active swimming motility. It is capable of chemotaxis toward amino acids such as asparagine, which are exuded by plant roots, and it actively migrates along fungal hyphae, suggesting a sophisticated behavioral adaptation for navigating complex soil matrices. The strain is non-pathogenic, does not produce detectable toxins or volatile organic compounds harmful to plants, and does not interfere with the growth of beneficial mycorrhizal fungi or other rhizosphere symbionts.

- deposit bacterium in culture collection center  
The strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 has been deposited with the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC) under accession number CGMCC No. 9667 on the date of filing this application. The deposit was made in accordance with the Budapest Treaty on the International Recognition of the Deposit of Microorganisms for the Purposes of Patent Procedure, and the deposit will be maintained for a period of at least thirty years from the date of deposit, or for the enforceable life of any patent issuing from this application, whichever is longer. Access to the deposit will be available to the public upon issuance of the patent, and the deposit serves as a definitive reference for the identity and biological characteristics of the strain claimed herein.

- identify bacterium by 16S rRNA gene analysis  
The identity of strain Liulou 1 has been confirmed through molecular analysis of its 16S ribosomal RNA gene. The full-length 16S rRNA sequence was amplified by polymerase chain reaction using universal bacterial primers, sequenced, and subjected to comparative analysis against reference sequences in public databases. The resulting sequence exhibits a 99.8% nucleotide identity with the type strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens CGMCC 1.1897T, confirming its classification within the species. However, phylogenetic analysis reveals a distinct clade separate from other known atrazine-degrading Arthrobacter isolates, indicating that strain Liulou 1 represents a novel genotypic variant with unique functional traits not found in previously characterized strains.

- describe physical properties of bacterium  
Strain Liulou 1 is a Gram-positive, aerobic, non-spore-forming bacterium that exhibits pleomorphic morphology, transitioning from coccoid to rod-shaped forms depending on growth phase and environmental conditions. In nutrient-rich media, cells appear predominantly coccoid, whereas in atrazine-containing minimal media, the majority of cells adopt a motile rod morphology approximately 1.2 to 1.8 micrometers in length and 0.5 to 0.7 micrometers in diameter. Colonies on SM agar are circular, convex, smooth, and cream-colored, with a diameter of 1.5 to 2.0 mm after 48 hours of incubation at 28°C. The strain grows optimally at pH 7.0–7.5 and temperatures between 25°C and 30°C, and it tolerates salinity up to 2% NaCl. It does not reduce nitrate, does not produce indole, and is catalase-positive and oxidase-negative. The strain is resistant to multiple antibiotics, including ampicillin and chloramphenicol, but remains susceptible to streptomycin and tetracycline.

- describe degradation of atrazine by bacterium  
Strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 completely degrades atrazine within 48 hours in liquid culture at concentrations up to 2 mg/L, converting it primarily into hydroxyatrazine (HA), which is subsequently metabolized at a slower rate. The strain also efficiently degrades atrazine-desethyl (DEA) and atrazine-desisopropyl (DIA), reducing their concentrations by over 99% within four days. However, it does not metabolize atrazine-desethyl-desisopropyl (DAA), indicating a specific enzymatic pathway limited to the initial dealkylation steps. Degradation occurs under both aerobic and low-oxygen conditions, and the process is not inhibited by the presence of common soil organic matter or humic acids. The degradation kinetics are significantly accelerated in the presence of plant root exudates, particularly amino acids, suggesting that root-derived compounds stimulate the expression of atrazine catabolic genes.

- isolate bacterium from rhizosphere of maize  
Strain Liulou 1 was isolated from the rhizosphere of maize plants grown in soil previously exposed to repeated applications of atrazine. The rhizosphere soil was collected from fields in Shandong Province, China, where atrazine had been applied for over a decade. The bacterium was not detected in bulk soil samples collected from the same location, nor was it recovered from non-atrazine-exposed soils, indicating that its presence is directly linked to the selective pressure exerted by the herbicide and its association with plant roots. The strain was consistently recovered from the rhizosphere of maize across multiple growing seasons, suggesting long-term ecological adaptation and persistence.

- describe method of isolating bacterium  
The isolation procedure involved direct plating of rhizosphere soil suspensions onto selective mineral medium SM agar supplemented with 2 mg/L atrazine as the sole nitrogen source. Soil samples were gently shaken in sterile saline to dislodge root-adherent microorganisms, and serial dilutions were plated in triplicate. After incubation at 28°C for 72 hours, colonies exhibiting a clearing halo around the perimeter—indicative of atrazine degradation—were selected for purification. Each colony was re-streaked three times on fresh SM agar to ensure purity, and isolates were stored in glycerol stocks at −80°C. Only those strains capable of growth on atrazine and producing a clear halo were retained for further characterization.

- describe maintenance of pure culture of bacterium  
Pure cultures of Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 are maintained on SM agar slants at 4°C and subcultured every 14 days. For long-term storage, cells are harvested during mid-log phase, washed twice in sterile phosphate-buffered saline, and resuspended in 15% glycerol. Aliquots are frozen at −80°C and remain viable for over ten years without loss of degradation activity or motility. Freeze-dried preparations of the strain, formulated with a protective matrix of skim milk and trehalose, retain viability for at least 18 months at ambient temperature, enabling practical storage and distribution for agricultural use.

- describe biological agent for remediation  
The invention provides a biological agent for the remediation of atrazine-contaminated soil, comprising viable cells of Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The agent is formulated as a dry powder, liquid suspension, or seed coating, and is designed for direct application to seeds prior to planting. The formulation contains a minimum of 10⁴ colony-forming units per seed, sufficient to establish a persistent rhizosphere population following germination. The agent is non-toxic to plants, compatible with common seed treatments, and stable under standard agricultural storage conditions. When applied, the agent enables the bacterium to colonize emerging roots, degrade atrazine in situ, and prevent the accumulation of toxic metabolites, thereby reducing herbicide injury and promoting plant health.

- describe method of applying bacterium for remediation  
The method of applying the biological agent for remediation involves inoculating seeds of a target crop with a suspension or coating containing viable cells of Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 prior to sowing. The seeds are immersed in a suspension containing at least 10⁴ CFU/mL for a period of 30 to 60 minutes, air-dried at ambient temperature for 2 to 4 hours, and then planted directly into atrazine-contaminated soil. No additional amendments, such as carbon sources or nutrients, are required. Upon germination, the bacterium colonizes the root surface, proliferates in response to root exudates, and degrades atrazine in the immediate rhizosphere. The method is applicable to a broad range of crops, including maize, wheat, alfalfa, and sorghum, and is effective at atrazine concentrations up to 1750 μg/kg soil. A single application at seeding is sufficient to achieve near-complete degradation of atrazine and its metabolites within four weeks, eliminating the need for repeated treatments.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

- introduce experiments on atrazine-degrading bacterium and applications  
The invention is further illustrated through a series of controlled experiments demonstrating the isolation, identification, metabolic activity, motility, root colonization, and remediation efficacy of Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1. These experiments confirm the strain’s ability to degrade atrazine under field-relevant conditions, establish persistent populations on plant roots following low-dose seed inoculation, and reduce herbicide injury in multiple crop species. The results collectively validate the strain as a novel, self-sustaining biological agent capable of transforming contaminated soil into a non-toxic environment through natural plant-microbe interactions.

### Isolation of Strain Liulou 1

- isolate strain liulou 1 on SM agar  
Strain Liulou 1 was isolated by direct plating of rhizosphere soil suspensions onto mineral medium SM agar containing 2 mg/L atrazine as the sole nitrogen source. After incubation at 28°C for 72 hours, colonies surrounded by a clear zone of atrazine depletion were selected for purification. These halos, visible to the naked eye, indicated enzymatic degradation of the herbicide by the underlying bacterial colony. Each candidate isolate was subcultured three times on fresh SM agar to eliminate contaminants and ensure monoculture purity. The resulting colonies were uniform in morphology, color, and size, and all exhibited identical degradation activity in subsequent assays.

- describe soil sampling and processing  
Soil samples were collected from the rhizosphere of mature maize plants growing in a field historically treated with atrazine in Shandong Province, China. At each sampling site, 200 grams of soil adhering to the fine roots were carefully removed using sterile forceps and placed in sterile plastic bags. Samples were transported on ice to the laboratory within four hours and processed immediately. The soil was gently shaken in 10 mL of sterile 0.85% NaCl solution for 10 minutes to dislodge root-adherent microorganisms. The suspension was allowed to settle for five minutes, and the supernatant was serially diluted from 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁶. One hundred microliters of each dilution were plated in triplicate onto SM agar plates supplemented with atrazine.

- detail colony selection and purification  
Colonies exhibiting a clear halo greater than 2 mm in diameter were selected as primary isolates. Each colony was transferred to a fresh SM agar plate using a sterile inoculating loop and incubated for 48 hours. This process was repeated three times to ensure genetic homogeneity. Isolates were then preserved in 15% glycerol at −80°C. A total of 14 isolates were obtained, all of which were screened for atrazine degradation in liquid culture. Only one isolate, designated Liulou 1, consistently degraded atrazine within 48 hours and exhibited motility and root colonization capacity in subsequent assays.

- characterize strain liulou 1  
Strain Liulou 1 was characterized for its growth kinetics, substrate utilization, and morphological plasticity. It grew optimally at 28°C and pH 7.2, with a doubling time of 2.1 hours in SM medium containing atrazine. It utilized atrazine, DEA, and DIA as sole nitrogen sources but could not utilize DAA. It did not grow on glucose, sucrose, or lactose as sole carbon sources, confirming its obligate dependence on atrazine-derived nitrogen. Microscopic examination revealed a transition from coccoid to motile rod morphology under nutrient limitation, and flagella were visualized by transmission electron microscopy. The strain was confirmed as Gram-positive via crystal violet staining.

### Phylogenetic Identification of Strain Liulou 1

- describe PCR method for 16S rRNA gene amplification  
Genomic DNA was extracted from a 48-hour culture of strain Liulou 1 using a commercial bacterial DNA extraction kit. The 16S rRNA gene was amplified using universal primers 27F and 1492R in a 50 μL reaction containing 1× PCR buffer, 2.5 mM MgCl₂, 0.2 mM dNTPs, 0.5 μM of each primer, 1 U Taq DNA polymerase, and 50 ng of template DNA. The amplification protocol consisted of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 30 cycles of 95°C for 30 seconds, 55°C for 30 seconds, and 72°C for 90 seconds, with a final extension at 72°C for 10 minutes.

- detail sequencing and analysis of PCR products  
The amplified product was purified using a gel extraction kit and sequenced using Sanger sequencing with the same primers. The resulting 1,462-base-pair sequence was assembled and edited using BioEdit software. The sequence was submitted to the NCBI GenBank database under accession number MN987654. Comparative analysis was performed using the BLASTn algorithm against the non-redundant nucleotide database.

- perform BLASTn similarity search  
BLASTn analysis revealed 99.8% nucleotide sequence identity with Arthrobacter ureafaciens CGMCC 1.1897T, the type strain of the species. The next closest match was Arthrobacter citreus, with 97.2% identity. No other species exhibited greater than 97% similarity, confirming classification within Arthrobacter ureafaciens. The sequence contained no insertions, deletions, or ambiguous bases, indicating high-quality amplification and sequencing.

- conduct phylogenetic analysis using MEGA5 software  
Phylogenetic relationships were inferred using the neighbor-joining method in MEGA5 software, with 1,000 bootstrap replicates. The resulting tree placed strain Liulou 1 in a distinct clade within the Arthrobacter ureafaciens lineage, separate from other atrazine-degrading isolates. The branch length was significantly longer than those connecting other strains, indicating genetic divergence. The topology was supported by a bootstrap value of 98%, confirming the uniqueness of the strain.

- present results of phylogenetic identification  
The phylogenetic analysis confirmed that strain Liulou 1 is a novel genotypic variant of Arthrobacter ureafaciens, with sufficient genetic divergence to warrant recognition as a distinct ecological strain. While taxonomically classified as A. ureafaciens, its functional traits—including motility, root colonization, and atrazine degradation kinetics—are not shared by the type strain or other isolates in the species, establishing its novelty for patent purposes.

### Taxonomic and Individual Identification of Strain Liulou 1 by Repetitive Elements Sequence-Based PCR (Rep-PCR)

- describe Rep-PCR method using BOXA1R and ERIC2 primers  
To differentiate strain Liulou 1 from closely related strains at the strain level, repetitive extragenic palindromic PCR (Rep-PCR) was performed using two primer sets: BOXA1R and ERIC2. Genomic DNA was amplified in a 25 μL reaction containing 1× PCR buffer, 2.5 mM MgCl₂, 0.2 mM dNTPs, 0.5 μM primer, and 1 U Taq polymerase. The cycling conditions for BOXA1R were: 95°C for 5 min, followed by 35 cycles of 95°C for 1 min, 50°C for 1 min, and 65°C for 8 min, with a final extension at 65°C for 16 min. For ERIC2, the conditions were: 94°C for 5 min, 30 cycles of 94°C for 1 min, 52°C for 1 min, and 65°C for 8 min, with a final extension at 65°C for 16 min.

- detail PCR conditions and product analysis  
Amplification products were separated on a 1.5% agarose gel in 1× TAE buffer at 100 V for 2 hours and visualized under UV light after ethidium bromide staining. Banding patterns were captured using a digital imaging system. The Rep-PCR profiles of strain Liulou 1 were compared to those of the type strain A. ureafaciens CGMCC 1.1897T and five other atrazine-degrading isolates.

- present results of BOX-PCR genotyping  
BOX-PCR generated a complex banding pattern with 12 distinct bands ranging from 200 to 3,000 bp. The pattern was unique to strain Liulou 1 and differed from all other tested strains, including the type strain. The absence of shared bands confirmed genetic distinctiveness at the strain level.

- present results of ERIC-PCR genotyping  
ERIC-PCR produced a pattern of 8 bands between 500 and 2,500 bp. The profile of strain Liulou 1 was entirely distinct from that of the type strain and other isolates, with no band identity exceeding 60%. These results confirmed that strain Liulou 1 is a genetically unique strain within the species Arthrobacter ureafaciens.

### Identification of the Genes for Atrazine Degradation in Strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1

- describe multiplex PCR method for gene identification  
Multiplex PCR was employed to detect the presence of known atrazine degradation genes, including trzN, atzB, and atzC. Primer pairs specific to each gene were designed based on published sequences. The reaction mixture contained 1× PCR buffer, 2.5 mM MgCl₂, 0.2 mM dNTPs, 0.3 μM of each primer, and 1 U Taq polymerase. The cycling conditions were: 95°C for 5 min, followed by 35 cycles of 95°C for 30 s, 58°C for 30 s, and 72°C for 1 min, with a final extension at 72°C for 10 min.

- detail PCR conditions and product analysis  
Amplification products were resolved on a 2% agarose gel. A 500-bp band corresponding to trzN, a 700-bp band for atzB, and a 900-bp band for atzC were observed in strain Liulou 1. No bands were detected in negative controls or in the type strain A. ureafaciens CGMCC 1.1897T, which lacks atrazine degradation capability.

- present results of multiplex PCR  
The presence of trzN, atzB, and atzC confirmed that strain Liulou 1 possesses the complete atrazine degradation pathway. The absence of these genes in the type strain further distinguishes Liulou 1 as a unique variant with acquired catabolic capability.

- interpret results and discuss implications  
The acquisition of the trzN-atzBC gene cluster likely occurred via horizontal gene transfer, enabling strain Liulou 1 to exploit atrazine as a nitrogen source. This genetic trait, combined with its motility and root colonization ability, makes it uniquely suited for rhizosphere-based remediation. The presence of these genes in a naturally occurring rhizosphere isolate, rather than a laboratory-engineered strain, enhances its regulatory acceptability and environmental safety profile.

### Example 5

- introduce degradation of atrazine by Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1  
The degradation capacity of strain Liulou 1 was evaluated in liquid SM medium supplemented with 2 mg/L atrazine. Culture samples were taken at 0, 24, 48, 72, and 168 hours. The medium was extracted with methanol, and atrazine and its metabolites were quantified using HPLC-MS/MS.

- describe method of degradation experiment  
Cultures were inoculated at 1:1000 from a 48-hour seed culture. Flasks were incubated at 28°C without shaking. At each time point, 1 mL of culture was mixed with 1 mL methanol, centrifuged at 8,000×g for 2 minutes, and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22-μm membrane. Samples were stored at −20°C until analysis.

- detail inoculation and incubation procedures  
Each flask contained 30 mL of SM medium and was inoculated with 30 μL of cell suspension. The initial cell density was 1.2 × 10⁶ CFU/mL. Incubation was conducted in a temperature-controlled room at 28°C under ambient light. No agitation was applied to simulate static soil conditions.

- explain analysis by HPLC-MS/MS  
Analysis was performed on a Thermo Fisher UltiMate 3000 HPLC system coupled to a TSQ Vantage triple-quadrupole mass spectrometer. Atrazine and metabolites were separated on an Acclaim C18 column using a methanol/formic acid gradient. Multiple reaction monitoring (MRM) transitions were used for quantification, with internal standards for calibration.

- present results of degradation experiment  
Atrazine was undetectable after 48 hours. Hydroxyatrazine (HA) peaked at 72 hours at 1.8 μg/mL and declined to 0.5 μg/mL by day 7. DEA and DIA were degraded within 96 hours. DAA was not degraded. Degradation followed first-order kinetics with a half-life of 22 hours.

- introduce examination of motility in Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1  
Motility was assessed in semisolid (0.2%) SM and SA agar and in liquid SA medium.

- describe method of motility experiment  
A 2 μL drop of a 10⁸ CFU/mL suspension was inoculated at the center of semisolid agar plates. Plates were incubated at 28°C for 14 days. Liquid motility was assessed by light microscopy after 24 hours of incubation in SA medium.

- detail preparation of bacterial suspension  
Cells were harvested from a 48-hour SMY culture, washed twice in sterile saline, and resuspended to 10⁸ CFU/mL. The suspension was vortexed for 10 seconds to ensure homogeneity.

- explain incubation and measurement procedures  
For semisolid agar, radial expansion was measured daily. For liquid motility, 100 cells were observed under 400× magnification, and directional movement was recorded as positive if ≥50% exhibited sustained linear motion.

- present results of motility experiment  
Strain Liulou 1 exhibited extensive radial migration on SA agar, reaching the plate edge (40 mm) in 14 days. No migration occurred on SM agar. In liquid SA, 82% of cells exhibited rapid, directional swimming. Flagella were confirmed by electron microscopy.

- introduce colonization of plant roots by Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1  
Root colonization was assessed in soil-sand tubes and pot assays using maize, wheat, and alfalfa.

- describe method of colonization experiment  
Seeds were soaked in a 10⁴ CFU/mL suspension for 1 hour, air-dried, and planted in tubes or pots containing atrazine-amended soil. Roots were harvested 21 days post-planting, vortexed in saline, and plated on SM agar.

- detail preparation of soil and seeds  
Soil-sand tubes contained 33 g sand and 2 g S1 soil, amended with 175 μg atrazine. Pots contained 300 g S1 soil amended with 1750 μg atrazine. Seeds were surface-sterilized with 70% ethanol for 30 seconds and rinsed with sterile water.

- explain inoculation and incubation procedures  
Inoculated seeds were planted in triplicate tubes or pots. Tubes were incubated at 26°C under 12-h light/dark cycle. Pots were placed outdoors in Jinan, China, under natural conditions. No water was added to tubes.

- present results of colonization experiment  
Root populations reached 6.2 log CFU/g fresh root in wheat and 5.8 log CFU/g in maize. Alfalfa supported 5.4 log CFU/g. No colonization occurred in non-inoculated controls. BOX-PCR confirmed all isolates were identical to Liulou 1.

- introduce survival of Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 on dry seed  
The ability of strain Liulou 1 to survive on dry seeds was tested for storage and field application.

- describe method of survival experiment  
Wheat seeds were inoculated with a 10⁶ CFU/mL suspension, air-dried for 4 hours, and stored at 25°C for 30 days. Seed viability was assessed weekly by plating.

- detail inoculation and drying procedures  
Seeds were immersed in suspension for 1 hour, removed, and placed on sterile filter paper in a laminar flow hood. Drying occurred at ambient humidity and temperature.

- explain evaluation of CFU density  
Seeds were homogenized in 1 mL saline, serially diluted, and plated on SM agar. Colonies were counted after 4 days.

- present results of survival experiment  
Viability remained at 9.2 log CFU per seed after 30 days, with no significant decline. Survival was unaffected by humidity or temperature fluctuations.

- introduce colonization of wheat roots by Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 after drying and storage  
Seeds stored for 30 days were planted in atrazine-amended soil to assess field-relevant efficacy.

- describe method of colonization experiment after drying and storage  
Stored seeds were planted in pots as above. Roots were harvested after 28 days and processed as described.

- describe atrazine content determination  
Soil was extracted with methanol-water (4:1), and atrazine and metabolites were analyzed by HPLC-MS/MS.

- extract atrazine from soil-sand sample  
One gram of freeze-dried soil was vortexed with 1 mL extraction solvent for 1 hour, centrifuged, and the supernatant collected. Four additional extractions were performed.

- analyze atrazine by HPLC-MS/MS  
Samples were analyzed as described above. Limits of detection were 0.045 ng/g for atrazine.

- present results of atrazine injury symptoms  
Non-inoculated wheat seedlings exhibited severe chlorosis and stunting. Inoculated plants showed no visible injury.

- show atrazine degradation by Arthrobacter ureafaciens liulou 1  
Residual atrazine in inoculated pots was 125.1 ng/g, compared to 3,404.7 ng/g in controls.

- describe method of promoting root growth  
Alfalfa seeds were inoculated with Liulou 1 and planted in atrazine-free soil. Root length was measured after 14 days.

- inoculate alfalfa seeds with Arthrobacter ureafaciens liulou 1  
Seeds were soaked in 10⁴ CFU/mL suspension for 1 hour, air-dried, and planted in sterile sand.

- measure root length  
Roots were scanned, and length was measured using ImageJ software. Ten plants per treatment were analyzed.

- present results of root growth promotion  
Inoculated alfalfa exhibited 38% longer roots than controls, indicating growth promotion independent of atrazine degradation.

- describe phosphate solubilization test  
Strain Liulou 1 was tested for phosphate solubilization on Pikovskaya’s agar.

- conduct phosphate solubilization test  
Strain was spotted on agar containing tricalcium phosphate. Clear zones around colonies indicated solubilization.

- present results of phosphate solubilization  
A 12-mm clear zone formed after 7 days, indicating phosphate solubilization activity, enhancing nutrient availability for plant growth.

- provide sequence listing of 16S rRNA gene  
The 16S rRNA gene sequence of strain Liulou 1 is provided in SEQ ID NO: 1, comprising 1,462 nucleotides.

- describe degradation of atrazine and its derivatives  
Strain Liulou 1 was incubated in SM medium with DEA, DIA, and DAA at 1 mg/L each.

- inoculate Arthrobacter ureafaciens liulou 1 into liquid medium  
Cultures were prepared as above and sampled at 0, 24, 48, and 72 hours.

- analyze degradation of atrazine and its derivatives  
HPLC-MS/MS analysis showed complete degradation of DEA and DIA within 96 hours. DAA remained unchanged.

- present results of degradation  
Degradation rates were 99.9% for DEA and 99.8% for DIA. No metabolites of DAA were detected.

- describe remediation of atrazine-contaminated soil  
Soil pots were amended with 1750 μg atrazine per kg and planted with inoculated or non-inoculated wheat seeds.

- inoculate wheat seeds with Arthrobacter ureafaciens liulou 1  
Seeds were inoculated as described and planted in triplicate pots.

- extract atrazine and its metabolites from soil  
Soil was extracted and analyzed as described above.

- analyze atrazine and its metabolites by HPLC-MS/MS  
Total atrazine and metabolites were quantified and expressed as molar concentrations.

- present results of remediation  
Total atrazine and metabolites were reduced from 24.72 μmol/kg in controls to 1.83 μmol/kg in inoculated pots—a 92.6% reduction.

- conclude remediation of atrazine-contaminated soil  
The results demonstrate that seed inoculation with Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1 enables complete remediation of atrazine-contaminated soil without chemical or mechanical intervention, establishing a sustainable, low-input, and ecologically sound method for herbicide detoxification.
