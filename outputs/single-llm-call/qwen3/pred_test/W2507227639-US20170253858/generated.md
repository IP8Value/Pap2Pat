# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine and cardiovascular therapeutics, specifically to methods and compositions for the maturation of cardiomyocytes derived from pluripotent stem cells using three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffolds. The invention further encompasses the use of such matured cardiomyocytes for therapeutic transplantation, drug screening, disease modeling, and prognostic assessment in patients with cardiovascular pathology. The compositions and methods described herein are designed to recapitulate the native biomechanical and biochemical microenvironment of the adult human heart, thereby enabling the generation of functionally mature cardiomyocytes that exhibit structural, electrophysiological, and contractile properties indistinguishable from those of adult myocardial tissue. This advancement addresses a critical unmet need in cardiac regenerative therapy, where the clinical efficacy of stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes has been historically limited by their fetal-like immaturity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

- motivate cardiovascular disease

Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with myocardial infarction accounting for a substantial proportion of deaths due to the irreversible loss of functional cardiomyocytes. The adult human heart possesses an extremely limited capacity for endogenous regeneration, and following ischemic injury, the damaged myocardium is replaced by fibrotic scar tissue that lacks contractile function and disrupts electrical conduction. This pathological remodeling predisposes patients to arrhythmias, progressive heart failure, and sudden cardiac death. Despite advances in pharmacological and device-based interventions, no current therapy restores lost myocardial tissue or reverses the underlying cellular deficit, leaving millions of patients with no viable option for true cardiac repair.

- describe limitations of current treatments

Current standard-of-care treatments, including beta-blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, and implantable cardioverter-defibrillators, are palliative in nature and do not address the fundamental loss of cardiomyocytes. While revascularization strategies such as percutaneous coronary intervention and coronary artery bypass grafting can mitigate ischemic insult, they cannot regenerate lost muscle. Furthermore, heart transplantation, though potentially curative, is severely constrained by donor organ scarcity, lifelong immunosuppression, and high rates of graft rejection. These limitations underscore the urgent need for novel therapeutic modalities that can replace or regenerate functional myocardium.

- describe limitations of immature cardiomyocytes

Induced pluripotent stem cells offer a promising autologous source for cardiomyocyte generation, yet the cardiomyocytes derived from these cells through conventional differentiation protocols remain phenotypically and functionally immature, closely resembling fetal rather than adult cardiomyocytes. These immature cells exhibit disorganized sarcomeres, spontaneous automaticity, weak contractile force, aberrant calcium handling, and altered electrophysiological properties, including delayed repolarization and reduced expression of key ion channels and calcium-handling proteins. Such immaturity renders them unsuitable for therapeutic transplantation, as they are prone to arrhythmogenesis and fail to integrate electrically and mechanically with host tissue. Moreover, their pharmacological responses are inconsistent with those of adult myocardium, limiting their utility in predictive drug screening and cardiotoxicity assays.

- identify need for mature cardiomyocytes

There is a compelling and unmet need for methods that reliably induce the maturation of pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes to a state that mirrors the structural, functional, and molecular characteristics of adult human myocardium. Mature cardiomyocytes are essential not only for safe and effective cell-based therapies but also for accurate modeling of cardiac disease, reliable prediction of drug efficacy and toxicity, and the development of personalized treatment strategies. The ability to generate such cells in scalable, reproducible, and physiologically relevant systems would transform the landscape of cardiovascular medicine.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

- introduce method of inducing cardiomyocyte maturation

The present invention provides a novel method for inducing the maturation of cardiomyocytes derived from pluripotent stem cells by culturing them within a three-dimensional scaffold composed of decellularized cardiac extracellular matrix derived from adult human or mammalian cardiac tissue. This method leverages the native biochemical composition and biomechanical properties of the adult cardiac microenvironment to drive structural organization, electrophysiological refinement, and functional maturation of the cardiomyocytes beyond what is achievable in conventional two-dimensional culture systems.

- describe 3D cardiac ECM scaffold

The three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold is prepared through a process of decellularization, lyophilization, enzymatic digestion, and hydrogel reconstitution that preserves the structural integrity of key matrix components including collagen, fibronectin, laminin, elastin, and periostin, while eliminating cellular and immunogenic material. The resulting hydrogel exhibits mechanical stiffness comparable to that of adult myocardial tissue, with a storage modulus ranging from 50 to 100 pascals, and contains a highly organized fibrillar architecture that mimics the native myocardial extracellular matrix.

- describe in vitro maturation

In vitro maturation is achieved by seeding pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes into the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogel under controlled culture conditions that maintain physiological temperature, pH, and oxygen tension. Over a period of seven to twenty-one days, the cells undergo profound morphological and functional changes, including sarcomere alignment, increased cell size, reduced spontaneous beating, enhanced calcium transient kinetics, and upregulation of adult-specific cardiac genes and proteins.

- describe in vivo maturation

The method further encompasses the in vivo application of the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold seeded with pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes for transplantation into damaged myocardium. Upon implantation, the scaffold provides structural support, promotes vascular ingrowth, and facilitates integration with host tissue, thereby enhancing survival, electromechanical coupling, and functional recovery of the transplanted cardiomyocytes.

- describe role of endothelial cells

Endothelial cells play a critical role in promoting cardiomyocyte maturation through paracrine signaling, including the secretion of endothelin-1 and activation of Notch signaling pathways. Co-culture of cardiomyocytes with endothelial cells or exposure to endothelial cell-conditioned medium significantly enhances the expression of maturation markers such as KCNJ2, MYL2, and Cx43, and improves calcium handling properties.

- describe role of stromal cells

Stromal cells, including cardiac fibroblasts and pericytes, contribute to the maturation process by secreting matrix-modifying enzymes and growth factors that remodel the extracellular environment and provide mechanical cues. Their presence within the scaffold enhances the structural organization of the cardiomyocyte network and promotes the development of intercellular junctions necessary for synchronous contraction.

- describe maturation markers

Maturation is quantitatively assessed by the upregulation of specific molecular markers including cardiac troponin T, myosin light chain 2, connexin 43, inward rectifier potassium channel 2.1, junctin, triadin, sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase 2a, and ryanodine receptor 2. Functional maturation is confirmed by increased calcium transient amplitude, faster upstroke and decay kinetics, reduced automaticity, and appropriate response to pharmacological agents such as isoproterenol and caffeine.

- describe harvesting mature cardiomyocytes

Mature cardiomyocytes may be harvested from the three-dimensional scaffold through enzymatic digestion with collagenase and dispase, followed by mechanical dissociation and purification via flow cytometry or magnetic-activated cell sorting using cardiac-specific surface markers. The purified cells retain their mature phenotype and can be cryopreserved or immediately transplanted.

- describe preparation of 3D cardiac ECM scaffold

The preparation of the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold involves the decellularization of cardiac tissue using detergent-based protocols, followed by lyophilization, mechanical milling into a fine powder, and enzymatic digestion with pepsin under acidic conditions. The resulting solution is neutralized and polymerized into a hydrogel at physiological temperature, forming a porous, biocompatible matrix that supports cell infiltration and nutrient diffusion.

- describe applications of mature cardiomyocytes

The mature cardiomyocytes generated by this method are applicable in a wide range of clinical and research contexts, including cell-based therapies for myocardial infarction, in vitro drug screening platforms for cardiotoxicity and efficacy testing, disease modeling of inherited arrhythmias and cardiomyopathies, and the development of bioengineered cardiac patches for surgical implantation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- incorporate references

The methods and compositions described herein are supported by extensive experimental validation, including quantitative gene expression analysis, proteomic profiling, calcium imaging, rheological characterization, and functional electrophysiological assessments. All procedures were conducted under standardized conditions to ensure reproducibility and clinical translatability.

- define technical terms

For the purposes of this disclosure, “cardiomyocyte” refers to a contractile cell of the heart muscle, derived from pluripotent stem cells and exhibiting structural and functional characteristics of myocardial tissue. “Maturation” refers to the process by which immature, fetal-like cardiomyocytes acquire the molecular, structural, and physiological properties of adult cardiomyocytes. “Three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold” refers to a hydrogel composed of decellularized, native cardiac extracellular matrix components that retain the biochemical and biomechanical properties of the adult heart. “Seeding” refers to the introduction of cells into the scaffold, and “culture” refers to the maintenance of cells under controlled environmental conditions to promote growth and maturation.

- introduce immature cell definition

Immature cardiomyocytes are defined as cells derived from pluripotent stem cells that exhibit spontaneous beating, disorganized sarcomeric structure, low expression of adult cardiac isoforms, and calcium handling characteristics typical of fetal myocardium, including elevated expression of NCX1 and reduced expression of SERCA2a and KCNJ2.

- introduce cardiac microenvironment definition

The cardiac microenvironment encompasses the complex interplay of extracellular matrix proteins, mechanical forces, soluble signaling molecules, and neighboring cell types—including endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and pericytes—that collectively regulate cardiomyocyte development, function, and homeostasis.

- introduce seed and seeding definition

“Seeding” refers to the process of introducing pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes into the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold at a density sufficient to promote cell-cell contact and matrix remodeling. “Seed” refers to the population of cells introduced into the scaffold for subsequent maturation.

- describe method of making mature cardiomyocytes

The method comprises the steps of differentiating pluripotent stem cells into cardiomyocytes, preparing a three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold from adult cardiac tissue, seeding the differentiated cardiomyocytes into the scaffold, and culturing the seeded scaffold under conditions that promote maturation for a period of at least seven days.

- introduce parameters of cardiac microenvironment

Key parameters of the cardiac microenvironment that influence maturation include matrix stiffness, fiber alignment, composition of matrix proteins, presence of endothelial and stromal cells, and exposure to mechanical strain or shear stress. The scaffold of the invention is engineered to optimize these parameters to mirror the adult cardiac niche.

- describe method of inducing cardiomyocyte maturation

Cardiomyocyte maturation is induced by culturing the cells within the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold under normoxic conditions at 37°C in a medium supplemented with insulin, transferrin, selenium, and fatty acids. The scaffold provides both biochemical cues through retained matrix proteins and biomechanical cues through its stiffness and topography, which together activate integrin-mediated signaling pathways that drive transcriptional reprogramming toward the adult phenotype.

- describe 3D cardiac ECM scaffold preparation

The scaffold is prepared by decellularizing bovine or porcine adult cardiac tissue using sequential exposure to sodium dodecyl sulfate and Triton X-100, followed by extensive washing, lyophilization, and milling into a fine powder. The powder is digested in 0.01 M HCl containing 2 mg/mL pepsin for 48 to 72 hours, neutralized to pH 7.4, and polymerized into a hydrogel at 37°C. The resulting gel exhibits a storage modulus of 60–80 Pa and contains a fibrillar collagen network with elastin fibers.

- describe cardiomyocyte maturation demonstration

Maturation was demonstrated through increased expression of MYL2, Cx43, and KCNJ2 at both mRNA and protein levels, enhanced calcium transient amplitude and kinetics, reduced spontaneous beating frequency, and appropriate pharmacological responses to β-adrenergic agonists and ryanodine receptor modulators.

- describe maturation marker proteins

Maturation marker proteins include cardiac troponin T, myosin light chain 2, connexin 43, inward rectifier potassium channel 2.1, junctin, triadin, sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase 2a, ryanodine receptor 2, and sodium-calcium exchanger 1. Their expression levels are quantified by Western blot, immunofluorescence, and quantitative RT-PCR.

- describe 3D cardiac ECM scaffold composition

The scaffold comprises a complex mixture of extracellular matrix proteins including collagen types I and III, fibronectin, laminin, elastin, periostin, fibulin, and versican, with a protein profile that closely resembles that of adult human myocardium. It is substantially free of cellular debris, nucleic acids, and immunogenic components.

- describe method of treating disease in a mammal

The method of treating cardiovascular disease in a mammal comprises administering a therapeutically effective amount of the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold seeded with mature cardiomyocytes directly into the damaged myocardium via injection or surgical patch implantation, thereby restoring contractile function and reducing fibrotic remodeling.

- describe generation of mature cardiomyocytes

Mature cardiomyocytes are generated by differentiating human induced pluripotent stem cells using a defined cardiac induction protocol, followed by culture within the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold for a minimum of seven days under conditions that promote structural and functional maturation.

- describe 3D cardiac ECM scaffold preparation

The preparation of the scaffold involves decellularization of cardiac tissue using detergent-based protocols, lyophilization, enzymatic digestion with pepsin, and hydrogel formation under physiological conditions to preserve the native architecture and mechanical properties of the extracellular matrix.

- describe cardiomyocyte maturation demonstration

Maturation was demonstrated by increased expression of adult-specific genes, improved calcium handling kinetics, reduced automaticity, and enhanced contractile force production in response to pharmacological stimuli.

- describe maturation marker proteins

Maturation marker proteins include cardiac troponin T, myosin light chain 2, connexin 43, inward rectifier potassium channel 2.1, junctin, triadin, sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium ATPase 2a, ryanodine receptor 2, and sodium-calcium exchanger 1, whose expression levels are indicative of the transition from fetal to adult cardiomyocyte phenotype.

- describe composition comprising 3D ECM scaffold and cardiomyocytes

The composition comprises a three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold seeded with mature cardiomyocytes, wherein the scaffold is biocompatible, biodegradable, and capable of supporting cell survival, integration, and functional maturation upon transplantation.

- describe method of inducing cardiomyocyte maturation

The method of inducing cardiomyocyte maturation involves exposing pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes to a three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold under conditions that mimic the biomechanical and biochemical environment of the adult heart, resulting in the upregulation of adult-specific genes and proteins and the acquisition of adult-like electrophysiological properties.

- describe method of using mature cardiomyocytes as drug screening platform

The mature cardiomyocytes are plated in multi-well formats within the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold and exposed to pharmacological agents to assess contractile response, calcium handling, and electrophysiological changes, thereby enabling high-fidelity prediction of drug efficacy and cardiotoxicity.

- describe method of generating cardiomyocytes in vitro

Cardiomyocytes are generated in vitro by differentiating human induced pluripotent stem cells using a stepwise protocol involving Wnt activation followed by Wnt inhibition, yielding a population of spontaneously contracting cells that are subsequently seeded into the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold for maturation.

- describe three dimensional scaffold composition

The three-dimensional scaffold is composed of a hydrogel formed from decellularized adult cardiac extracellular matrix, containing a network of collagen fibers, elastin filaments, and glycoproteins that provide structural support and biochemical signaling cues essential for cardiomyocyte maturation.

- describe cardiomyocyte maturation promotion

Cardiomyocyte maturation is promoted through the synergistic action of matrix stiffness, topographical cues, and paracrine signals from endothelial and stromal cells, which together activate integrin-mediated signaling pathways and transcriptional programs that drive the transition to an adult phenotype.

- describe method of treating cardiovascular disease

The method of treating cardiovascular disease comprises the intramyocardial delivery of a composition comprising a three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold seeded with mature cardiomyocytes into the ischemic or infarcted region of the heart, resulting in improved ejection fraction, reduced scar size, and enhanced electrical stability.

The method of prognosing cardiovascular disease comprises assessing the functional maturity of patient-derived cardiomyocytes cultured in the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold, wherein the degree of maturation correlates with disease severity and response to therapy.

- describe method of diagnosing cardiovascular disease

The method of diagnosing cardiovascular disease comprises generating cardiomyocytes from patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cells, maturing them in the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold, and evaluating their electrophysiological and contractile properties to identify disease-specific phenotypes such as arrhythmogenicity or contractile dysfunction.

- describe method of screening for treatment of disease

The method of screening for treatment of disease comprises exposing mature cardiomyocytes cultured in the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold to candidate therapeutic compounds and quantifying changes in calcium transients, contractile force, or gene expression to identify agents that reverse disease-associated phenotypes.

- describe method of inducing cell maturation

The method of inducing cell maturation comprises culturing pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes in a three-dimensional scaffold composed of decellularized adult cardiac extracellular matrix under physiological conditions for a period sufficient to induce expression of adult-specific markers and functional maturation.

- describe composition comprising 3D ECM scaffold and cardiomyocytes

The composition comprises a three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold seeded with mature cardiomyocytes, wherein the scaffold is biocompatible, biodegradable, and capable of supporting cell survival, integration, and functional maturation upon transplantation.

- describe pharmaceutical composition

The pharmaceutical composition comprises a therapeutically effective amount of the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold seeded with mature cardiomyocytes suspended in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, wherein the scaffold is formulated for intramyocardial delivery.

- describe pharmaceutically acceptable excipient

The pharmaceutically acceptable excipient includes saline, phosphate-buffered saline, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, or hyaluronic acid, which provide viscosity, stability, and biocompatibility for delivery and retention within the myocardium.

- describe route of administration

The route of administration is intramyocardial injection, transendocardial delivery via catheter, or surgical implantation of a cardiac patch, each method ensuring direct delivery of the composition to the site of myocardial injury.

- describe pharmaceutical compositions formulation

The pharmaceutical composition is formulated as a sterile, pyrogen-free hydrogel suitable for injection or patch formation, with a shelf life of at least six months when stored at -80°C in the presence of cryoprotectants.

- describe therapeutically effective amount

A therapeutically effective amount is defined as the quantity of mature cardiomyocytes delivered within the three-dimensional scaffold sufficient to restore contractile function, reduce fibrosis, and improve cardiac output in a subject with myocardial infarction, typically ranging from 1×10⁶ to 1×10⁸ cells per cubic centimeter of tissue.

- describe typical dosages

Typical dosages range from 1×10⁷ to 5×10⁷ mature cardiomyocytes per cardiac injection, delivered in volumes of 0.5 to 2.0 mL, with repeat administrations possible at intervals of four to twelve weeks depending on clinical response.

- describe examples of the present disclosure

Examples of the present disclosure include the generation of mature cardiomyocytes from human induced pluripotent stem cells using a three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold, the demonstration of enhanced calcium handling and drug responsiveness in these cells, and the successful transplantation of the scaffold-cell construct into infarcted murine hearts, resulting in significant improvement in left ventricular ejection fraction.

### EXAMPLES

- introduce iPSC maintenance and cardiac differentiation

Human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) of the WTC-11 GCaMP line were maintained on Matrigel-coated surfaces in mTeSR1 medium and differentiated into cardiomyocytes using a chemically defined protocol involving sequential modulation of Wnt signaling.

- describe iPSC line derivation

The WTC-11 iPSC line was derived from a healthy male donor with no known cardiac history and genetically modified to express the calcium-sensitive fluorescent protein GCaMP6f for real-time monitoring of intracellular calcium fluxes.

- outline iPSC maintenance protocol

iPSCs were passaged every four to five days using enzymatic dissociation and replated at a density of 5,000 to 10,000 cells per cm² in the presence of Y-27632 to enhance survival.

- detail cardiac induction protocol

Cardiac induction was initiated at 75–90% confluence by treatment with CHIR99021 for 24 hours, followed by IWP2 on day 3, and maintenance in RPMI/B27 medium with insulin from day 7 onward.

- describe iPSC seeding and culture

Differentiated cardiomyocytes were harvested on day 12 and seeded at densities of 1–5×10⁵ cells per mL into 3D cardiac ECM hydrogels or 2D control substrates.

- introduce cardiac tissue decellularization

Bovine adult and fetal hearts were decellularized using a detergent-based protocol involving SDS and Triton X-100 to remove cellular components while preserving extracellular matrix structure.

- describe tissue processing and storage

Tissues were cut into 3 mm³ fragments, frozen at -80°C for 16 hours, and processed under sterile conditions to prevent microbial contamination.

- outline decellularization protocol

Tissues were sequentially incubated in distilled water, PBS, 1% SDS (adult) or 0.5% SDS (fetal), and 1% Triton X-100, followed by extensive washing and antibiotic treatment.

- describe extracellular matrix powder creation

Decellularized tissues were lyophilized and milled into a fine powder using a mechanical grinder to facilitate enzymatic digestion.

- detail pepsin digestion protocol

ECM powder was digested in 0.01 M HCl containing 2 mg/mL pepsin for 48–72 hours at room temperature with constant stirring until complete solubilization.

- introduce multiphoton imaging and fiber analysis

Second-harmonic generation and autofluorescence microscopy were employed to visualize collagen and elastin fiber architecture within the decellularized matrix.

- describe imaging setup and parameters

A Ti:Sapphire laser at 740 nm and 860 nm was used for SHG and autofluorescence excitation, respectively, with a 40× water-immersion objective and bandpass filters for signal collection.

- outline SHG signal collection

SHG signals were collected between 320 and 390 nm to detect fibrillar collagen, with images captured every 5 µm through the depth of the scaffold.

- describe autofluorescence signal collection

Elastin fibers were visualized using long-pass filtering at 500 nm to capture intrinsic autofluorescence.

- detail image analysis protocol

Images were analyzed using ctFIRE software to quantify fiber width, density, and orientation across multiple z-stacks per sample.

- introduce statistical analysis

Quantitative data were analyzed using split-unit ANOVA and Student’s t-test, with significance defined as p < 0.05, p < 0.01, or p < 0.001.

- describe ANOVA analysis

ANOVA was used to compare gene expression across multiple groups, including 2D versus 3D and fetal versus adult matrix conditions.

- outline Student's T-test analysis

Student’s t-test was applied to compare calcium transient parameters between two groups, with data presented as mean ± SEM.

- introduce iPSC-derived cardiomyocyte maturation

iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes were cultured for up to 21 days in 2D or 3D environments to assess maturation kinetics.

- describe 2D culture setup

Cells were plated on Matrigel-coated plates in standard culture medium without scaffold components.

- outline 3D culture setup

Cells were suspended in 3D cardiac ECM hydrogels at a concentration of 1×10⁶ cells/mL and allowed to polymerize at 37°C for 30 minutes prior to culture.

- detail cell seeding and culture

Cells were seeded into hydrogels and maintained in RPMI/B27 medium with insulin, with medium changes every 72 hours.

- introduce quantitative RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from cells using TRIzol, and cDNA was synthesized using reverse transcriptase for quantitative PCR analysis.

- describe RNA harvesting and treatment

Cells were lysed in TRIzol, RNA was purified using column-based extraction, and integrity was confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis.

- outline cDNA synthesis protocol

cDNA was synthesized using oligo(dT) primers and Superscript III reverse transcriptase according to manufacturer instructions.

- detail qPCR protocol

qPCR was performed using SYBR Green chemistry with primers specific for cardiac maturation genes, normalized to GAPDH and ACTB housekeeping genes.

- introduce calcium imaging and analysis

Calcium transients were recorded using the GCaMP6f reporter in live cells under a fluorescence microscope.

- describe imaging setup and parameters

Images were acquired at 10 Hz using a 20× objective and a high-speed camera, with excitation at 488 nm and emission collected at 510–550 nm.

- outline image analysis protocol

Calcium transients were analyzed using custom MATLAB algorithms to determine amplitude, time to peak, time to 50% decay, and beat rate.

- detail algorithm for calcium wave analysis

The algorithm identified individual calcium events, calculated peak amplitude, and computed kinetic parameters using first-derivative analysis of fluorescence traces.

- introduce pharmacological treatment of cardiomyocytes

Cells were exposed to isoproterenol (1 µM) and propranolol (10 µM) to assess β-adrenergic responsiveness, and caffeine (10 mM) to assess ryanodine receptor function.

- describe drug treatment protocol

Drugs were added to culture medium for 10–15 minutes, with video recordings made before, during, and after exposure.

- outline video recording protocol

Videos were captured at 10 frames per second using a fluorescence microscope equipped with a motorized stage and automated focus.

- introduce nanoLC-MS/MS analysis

Proteomic profiling of decellularized ECM was performed using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry.

- describe protein solubilization and quantitation

ECM samples were digested with trypsin, peptides were quantified using BCA assay, and 1 µg of peptide was loaded per run.

- outline peptide desalting and analysis

Peptides were desalted using C18 StageTips and analyzed using a nanoAcquity UPLC system coupled to a Q-Exactive HF mass spectrometer.

- detail mass spectrometry protocol

MS/MS spectra were acquired in data-dependent mode with a resolution of 60,000 and dynamic exclusion of 30 seconds.

- introduce mechanical analysis of 3D cardiac extracellular matrix hydrogels

Rheological properties of hydrogels were measured using a parallel-plate rheometer.

- describe rheology setup and parameters

A 20-mm diameter plate was used with a 1-mm gap, and oscillatory strain sweeps were performed at 1 Hz from 0.1% to 100% strain.

- outline storage and loss moduli measurement

Storage modulus (G′) and loss modulus (G″) were measured at 1% strain and 37°C to determine gel stiffness.

- introduce flow cytometry and immunocytochemistry

Cells were fixed, permeabilized, and stained with antibodies against cardiac troponin T, connexin 43, and MYL2 for flow cytometry and confocal microscopy.

- describe cell staining and analysis protocol

Primary antibodies were incubated overnight at 4°C, followed by Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondary antibodies, with nuclei counterstained with DAPI.
