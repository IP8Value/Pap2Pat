## FIELD

The present invention relates to quantum sensing technologies, particularly to high-sensitivity, solid-state magnetometers based on nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond. More specifically, the invention pertains to a novel quantum logic-enhanced sensing protocol that leverages the long-lived nuclear spin states of neighboring nitrogen isotopes as quantum memory elements to amplify the signal-to-noise ratio and sensitivity of ensemble NV center measurements. This approach enables unprecedented performance in the detection of weak, time-varying magnetic fields at room temperature, with applications spanning biomedical imaging, materials characterization, geophysical surveying, and fundamental physics experiments requiring sub-nanotesla resolution.

## BACKGROUND

Nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond have emerged as a leading platform for quantum sensing due to their atomic-scale dimensions, ambient stability, and optical readout capability. However, the sensitivity of conventional NV-based magnetometers is fundamentally limited by the short electronic spin coherence time and the statistical noise inherent in single-shot fluorescence measurements. While dynamical decoupling sequences have extended coherence times and improved spectral resolution, the readout process remains constrained by photon shot noise and the finite fluorescence yield per excitation cycle. Prior approaches have sought to mitigate these limitations through ensemble averaging, cryogenic operation, or advanced pulse sequences, but none have successfully decoupled the measurement bandwidth from the readout fidelity. The persistent challenge lies in the inability to extract multiple independent measurements from a single quantum state preparation without degrading the signal integrity or introducing excessive overhead. The present invention overcomes this fundamental limitation by introducing a quantum logic architecture that transfers the sensor spin state to a long-lived nuclear memory, enabling repeated, non-destructive readouts that dramatically enhance both signal-to-noise ratio and sensitivity without compromising temporal resolution.

## SUMMARY

The invention introduces a quantum sensing method that employs a hybrid two-qubit system composed of an electronic spin of a nitrogen-vacancy center and an associated nitrogen-15 nuclear spin to achieve quantum logic-enhanced signal acquisition. The method begins with the initialization of the electronic spin state using a polarizing optical pulse, followed by the application of a dynamical decoupling sequence to sensitize the sensor to a target magnetic field. Information regarding the target signal is encoded into the electronic spin population through phase accumulation during the sensing interval. A SWAP operation is then applied to map this information onto the nuclear spin memory, thereby preserving the acquired signal while decoupling it from the rapidly relaxing electronic spin. A subsequent optical pulse resets the electronic spin to its ground state, enabling immediate re-use of the sensor for additional cycles. The information stored in the nuclear spin is then repeatedly mapped back to the electronic spin via a series of controlled-not gates, each followed by an optical readout pulse, thereby generating multiple independent measurements within the nuclear spin lifetime. A data acquisition readout pulse is applied after each mapping to detect the resulting fluorescence, and the collected signals are weighted according to their signal-to-noise characteristics to maximize the overall measurement fidelity. The invention further introduces an ensemble of nitrogen-vacancy centers embedded in a diamond lattice with a density of at least 1.5 parts per million, each coupled to a nearby nitrogen-15 nuclear spin. A magnetic bias field of magnitude between 2000 gauss and 4000 gauss is applied to suppress flip-flop relaxation channels and extend the nuclear spin lifetime to greater than 3 milliseconds. The quantum sensor comprises a diamond chip with a nitrogen-doped layer of thickness 10 to 20 micrometers, an antenna for delivering microwave and radiofrequency pulses, a laser source emitting at 532 nanometers, and a fluorescence detection system. The method enables a signal-to-noise ratio enhancement exceeding 30-fold and a sensitivity enhancement exceeding 11-fold compared to conventional readout techniques under identical experimental conditions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The quantum sensor system comprises a high-purity diamond crystal with a nitrogen-doped layer of approximately 13 micrometers thickness, grown via chemical vapor deposition, containing a nitrogen-vacancy center density of approximately 2.3 parts per million and a total nitrogen concentration of 14 parts per million. The diamond is oriented such that the [100] crystal axis is perpendicular to the top surface, and the [111] axis of the NV centers is aligned parallel to the applied magnetic field. The ensemble contains at least 10^12 nitrogen-vacancy centers within the illuminated volume, each forming a hybrid two-qubit system with a nearby nitrogen-15 nuclear spin. An external magnetic bias field is generated by a feedback-stabilized electromagnet and maintained at a magnitude of 3700 gauss to suppress dominant T1 relaxation mechanisms arising from flip-flop transitions between electronic and nuclear spins. A single-loop antenna, with a diameter of one millimeter, delivers microwave pulses in the range of 2.87 gigahertz and radiofrequency pulses between 1 and 3 megahertz to simultaneously control the electronic and nuclear spin states. The microwave and radiofrequency pulses are generated by an arbitrary waveform generator with 83-picosecond temporal resolution and amplified to sufficient power to drive Rabi oscillations with high fidelity. A solid-state laser emitting at 532 nanometers provides optical pulses of 3-microsecond duration, with a power of 130 milliwatts, to initialize and read out the NV spin state via fluorescence. The emitted fluorescence is collected by a liquid light guide and detected by a photomultiplier tube with quantum efficiency greater than 60%. A test coil, comprising four loops with a diameter of 10 millimeters, generates a controlled AC magnetic field for calibration and signal testing. The external DC magnetic field is maintained within a range of 2000 to 4000 gauss to optimize nuclear spin lifetime, which is measured to be 3.44 milliseconds under these conditions. Microwave and radiofrequency pulses are delivered through the antenna in a synchronized sequence, with variations in pulse amplitude, duration, and phase tailored to implement SWAP, CNOT, and dynamical decoupling operations with fidelities exceeding 90%. Optical pulses are applied following each SWAP operation to reset the electronic spin and again after each nuclear-to-electronic mapping to enable fluorescence readout. A controller, comprising a digital signal processor and programmable pulse sequencer, orchestrates the entire sensing protocol, including preparation, sensing, swap, and repetitive readout stages. The preparation stage involves optical polarization of the NV ensemble, followed by the sensing stage, during which a dynamical decoupling sequence such as XY8-6 or DROID-60 accumulates phase proportional to the target magnetic field. The swap stage transfers the encoded information to the nuclear spin via a SWAP gate with an estimated fidelity of 93%. The readout stage consists of N repetitions of a CNOT operation followed by fluorescence detection, where N is determined by the nuclear spin lifetime and the duration of each readout cycle. The repetitive readouts are weighted by the inverse square of the noise variance at each cycle to maximize the signal-to-noise ratio. Compared to prior art, which relies on single-shot optical readouts, the present invention achieves a signal-to-noise ratio enhancement of 33.3-fold and a sensitivity enhancement of up to 11.3-fold, as demonstrated in correlation spectroscopy experiments with a 1-megahertz test signal. Experimental results confirm that sensitivity gains scale with the ratio of sensing duration to swap and readout overhead, with optimal performance achieved when the sensing interval exceeds 100 microseconds. Alternatives include the use of lower nitrogen doping to extend electronic coherence times, integration of multiple nuclear spins per NV center, and adaptation to sensing of electric fields, temperature, or strain. The disclosure concludes with the recognition that this quantum logic architecture is broadly applicable to any solid-state spin system exhibiting long-lived nuclear memory and controllable spin-spin coupling.
