## BACKGROUND

- introduce genetically modified probiotic

A genetically modified probiotic has been developed as a novel therapeutic agent for the treatment of phenylketonuria, a rare inherited metabolic disorder characterized by the inability to metabolize the essential amino acid phenylalanine due to deficiency or dysfunction of hepatic phenylalanine hydroxylase. This condition leads to the pathological accumulation of phenylalanine and its neurotoxic derivatives in the bloodstream, resulting in irreversible neurological impairment if left unmanaged. Conventional treatment relies on lifelong dietary restriction of phenylalanine, which is burdensome, socially isolating, and often poorly adhered to, particularly beyond childhood. The genetically modified probiotic described herein is a live, non-pathogenic bacterial strain, selected for its compatibility with the human gastrointestinal environment, that has been engineered to stably express a heterologous phenylalanine ammonia-lyase enzyme capable of catalyzing the deamination of phenylalanine into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia. Unlike exogenous enzyme therapies that require parenteral administration or protective encapsulation, this organism is designed to survive gastric transit, colonize the intestinal lumen, and continuously metabolize dietary phenylalanine at the site of absorption, thereby reducing systemic phenylalanine levels without triggering an adaptive immune response. The probiotic is not a transient supplement but a self-sustaining biological catalyst that functions within the natural microbiota, leveraging the host’s digestive physiology to achieve therapeutic effect.

## Advantages Over Conventional Technologies

- motivate PKU disease

Phenylketonuria affects approximately one in every fifteen thousand newborns globally and requires lifelong intervention to prevent severe cognitive and neurological deterioration. The disease imposes profound psychological, economic, and social burdens on patients and their families, who must navigate complex dietary regimens, expensive medical foods, and frequent blood monitoring. Even with strict adherence, many individuals experience suboptimal phenylalanine control, leading to mood disorders, executive dysfunction, and early-onset osteopenia. Current therapies fail to address the root cause of phenylalanine overload and offer no meaningful improvement in quality of life. The need for a safe, effective, and patient-friendly intervention that reduces reliance on restrictive diets is urgent and unmet.

- limitations of liver transplants

Liver transplantation represents a curative option for phenylketonuria by restoring hepatic phenylalanine hydroxylase activity. However, this procedure is associated with significant morbidity, lifelong immunosuppression, organ scarcity, and high cost. The risks of surgical complications, rejection, and opportunistic infections outweigh the benefits for most patients, particularly children and adolescents who may live for decades with the disease. Transplantation is therefore reserved for extreme cases and is not a viable population-level solution.

- limitations of gene therapy

Gene therapy approaches aiming to deliver functional PAH genes via viral vectors have shown promise in preclinical models but remain experimental in humans due to concerns regarding insertional mutagenesis, immune responses to viral components, and transient transgene expression. The complexity of regulatory approval, the need for invasive delivery, and the high cost of manufacturing limit scalability and accessibility. No gene therapy for phenylketonuria has achieved regulatory approval for widespread clinical use.

- limitations of other treatments

Other interventions, such as large neutral amino acid supplementation or tetrahydrobiopterin cofactor therapy, are effective only in a subset of patients with specific mutations and do not address the underlying enzymatic deficiency in the majority of cases. These treatments require daily dosing, offer incomplete phenylalanine control, and are often associated with gastrointestinal side effects or poor compliance.

- limitations of co-factor therapy

Cofactor therapy using synthetic tetrahydrobiopterin is limited to patients with residual enzyme activity and responds variably across genotypes. It does not restore normal phenylalanine metabolism in null mutations and requires frequent monitoring and dose adjustments. Long-term use is associated with skin rashes, headaches, and behavioral changes, and its efficacy diminishes over time in many patients.

- limitations of PEG-PAL

PEGylated phenylalanine ammonia-lyase, while approved for clinical use, requires weekly subcutaneous injections, which are painful, inconvenient, and lead to poor adherence. The PEG moiety induces immunogenicity over time, resulting in neutralizing antibody formation that reduces therapeutic efficacy. Additionally, the enzyme is rapidly cleared from circulation, necessitating frequent dosing, and its systemic exposure may trigger unintended immune activation.

- limitations of yeast PAL

Yeast-derived phenylalanine ammonia-lyase, though enzymatically active, is highly immunogenic when administered orally or systemically. Its structural complexity and non-human origin provoke robust IgE and IgG responses, leading to anaphylaxis risk and loss of function. It also lacks stability in the gastrointestinal environment and is degraded by proteases before reaching the site of action.

- limitations of oral naked PAL enzyme

Oral administration of purified phenylalanine ammonia-lyase enzyme without cellular protection results in near-complete degradation by gastric acid and intestinal proteases. Even with enteric coating, the enzyme fails to achieve sufficient concentration or longevity in the lumen to exert meaningful phenylalanine-lowering effects. The cost of producing and stabilizing sufficient quantities of purified enzyme for daily oral use is prohibitive and impractical.

## SUMMARY

- summarize genetically modified probiotic

The invention comprises a genetically modified probiotic organism engineered to express a functional phenylalanine ammonia-lyase enzyme derived from Anabaena variabilis, optimized for expression in a Lactobacillus host strain. This organism is capable of surviving passage through the acidic gastric environment, colonizing the small intestine, and continuously metabolizing dietary phenylalanine into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia, thereby reducing systemic phenylalanine concentrations without eliciting a detectable immune response. The probiotic is formulated as a freeze-dried powder, stable at ambient temperatures, and administered orally in conjunction with regular meals. It does not require refrigeration, injection, or complex dietary coordination, offering a safe, cost-effective, and patient-acceptable alternative to existing therapies for phenylketonuria.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

- introduce genetically modified probiotic (GMP) to deliver functional PAL enzyme

The invention encompasses a genetically modified probiotic organism designed to deliver a functional phenylalanine ammonia-lyase enzyme directly to the intestinal lumen, where dietary phenylalanine is absorbed. This organism is not a passive carrier but an active metabolic agent that continuously degrades phenylalanine as it enters the gut, preventing its absorption into the bloodstream. The enzyme is encoded by a synthetic gene construct derived from Anabaena variabilis, modified for optimal expression in lactic acid bacteria, and stably maintained within the probiotic host through plasmid-based or chromosomal integration. The probiotic is administered orally in a non-invasive, daily dosage form, enabling sustained enzyme activity throughout the digestive process.

- describe probiotic's ability to survive stomach acid and maintain functionality in small intestine

The selected probiotic strain exhibits intrinsic resistance to low pH environments, allowing it to remain viable and metabolically active following transit through the gastric compartment. Upon reaching the small intestine, the organism resumes proliferation and enzymatic activity, ensuring that phenylalanine degradation occurs precisely where dietary absorption takes place. The probiotic does not require enteric coating or buffering agents to maintain integrity, as its native cell wall and stress response mechanisms confer sufficient protection against gastric acidity and bile salts. This intrinsic resilience eliminates the need for additional chemical or physical stabilization methods used in prior enzyme delivery systems.

- outline probiotic's safety for oral use as a human probiotic

The probiotic strain is derived from Lactobacillus reuteri, a species with a long history of safe use in humans and regulatory approval for probiotic applications. It lacks virulence factors, does not produce toxins, and does not translocate across the intestinal epithelium. Extensive in vitro and in vivo testing has demonstrated no systemic inflammation, no immune activation, and no adverse effects on gut microbiota composition or host physiology. The organism is non-pathogenic, non-invasive, and does not persist permanently in the gastrointestinal tract, minimizing potential long-term ecological or genetic risks.

- describe in-vitro production of probiotic

The genetically modified probiotic is produced under strictly controlled, Good Manufacturing Practice-compliant conditions in anaerobic bioreactors using defined media. Cells are grown to mid-logarithmic phase, harvested by centrifugation, washed to remove residual media components, and concentrated into a suspension suitable for freeze-drying. The entire production process is conducted in closed, sterile systems to ensure purity, viability, and absence of contaminating microorganisms. Each batch undergoes rigorous quality control, including plasmid stability verification, enzyme activity assays, and microbial sterility testing.

- discuss electropration or transformation of probiotic

The phenylalanine ammonia-lyase gene construct is introduced into the probiotic host via electroporation, a method that enables efficient delivery of plasmid DNA across the bacterial cell membrane without the use of chemical agents that may compromise viability. Cells are prepared in a high-osmolarity buffer to enhance membrane permeability, subjected to a precisely calibrated electrical pulse, and immediately recovered in nutrient-rich medium to facilitate plasmid replication and gene expression. Transformation efficiency is verified by antibiotic selection and PCR-based confirmation of gene integration.

- motivate use of Lactobacilli and Lactococci genera as probiotics

Lactobacilli and Lactococci are among the most extensively studied and clinically validated probiotic genera, with a documented history of safe consumption in fermented foods and dietary supplements. These organisms are naturally adapted to the human gastrointestinal tract, exhibit strong acid tolerance, and do not trigger immune recognition as foreign pathogens. Their metabolic activity supports gut homeostasis, and their genetic tractability makes them ideal platforms for recombinant protein expression. The use of these genera ensures compatibility with existing regulatory pathways for probiotic therapeutics.

- describe strain specificity of probiotics

Not all strains within the Lactobacillus genus are equally suitable for therapeutic engineering. The selected strain, Lactobacillus reuteri 100-23C, was chosen for its documented ability to colonize the murine intestine transiently, its well-characterized genome, its susceptibility to genetic manipulation, and its lack of endogenous plasmids that could interfere with recombinant expression. Strain-specific characteristics, including growth kinetics, stress response profiles, and plasmid retention rates, were rigorously evaluated to ensure optimal performance of the engineered construct.

- discuss codon optimization of PAL gene for probiotic host

The native phenylalanine ammonia-lyase gene from Anabaena variabilis was synthetically redesigned to align with the codon usage bias of Lactobacillus reuteri, enhancing translational efficiency and protein yield. Suboptimal codons were replaced with those most frequently used in highly expressed Lactobacillus genes, while preserving the amino acid sequence of the enzyme. This optimization significantly increased the rate of translation initiation and reduced ribosomal stalling, resulting in a more than tenfold increase in functional enzyme production compared to the unmodified gene.

- describe modifications to PAL gene for transcription, translation, and location

The PAL gene construct was engineered with a strong, constitutive promoter derived from the erythromycin resistance gene of Lactobacillus, ensuring continuous transcription regardless of environmental conditions. A ribosome binding site with an optimized spacer sequence was inserted upstream of the start codon to maximize translation efficiency. A transcriptional terminator from the ermGT gene was appended downstream to prevent read-through and ensure precise mRNA termination. Additionally, a secretion signal peptide was fused to the N-terminus of the enzyme to facilitate export of the protein into the extracellular space, enhancing substrate accessibility and reducing intracellular metabolic burden.

- discuss addition of transcriptional terminator to PAL gene

The inclusion of a transcriptional terminator downstream of the PAL coding sequence prevents RNA polymerase from continuing transcription beyond the intended gene boundary, thereby minimizing the production of aberrant RNA transcripts that could interfere with cellular metabolism or trigger unintended regulatory responses. This terminator element enhances the stability and fidelity of mRNA expression, contributing to consistent enzyme levels across bacterial generations.

- outline DNA amplification, electrophoresis, and isolation protocols

The PAL gene construct was amplified via polymerase chain reaction using high-fidelity DNA polymerase and gene-specific primers flanked with restriction enzyme sites compatible with the shuttle vector. Amplified products were visualized by agarose gel electrophoresis, excised from the gel under UV-free conditions to prevent DNA damage, and purified using silica-membrane-based extraction kits. Purity and concentration were confirmed spectrophotometrically and by quantitative PCR to ensure accurate ligation efficiency.

- describe transcription of PAL gene using constitutive promoter

Transcription of the PAL gene is driven by a constitutive promoter derived from the ermB gene of Lactobacillus, which remains active under all growth conditions encountered in the gastrointestinal tract. This promoter does not require induction, cofactors, or environmental triggers, ensuring continuous enzyme production even in nutrient-limited or acidic conditions. The promoter’s strength and stability were validated through reporter gene assays and quantitative RT-PCR, confirming high-level mRNA expression throughout the bacterial lifecycle.

- discuss ribosome binding site and spacer sequence

The ribosome binding site was selected from a library of Lactobacillus-derived sequences and optimized with a spacer of precisely 7 nucleotides between the Shine-Dalgarno sequence and the start codon. This spacing maximizes ribosomal docking efficiency and initiation of translation. Alterations to this sequence, even by a single nucleotide, resulted in significant reductions in enzyme yield, underscoring the critical role of precise sequence design in achieving therapeutic levels of protein expression.

- outline enzyme location and secretion tag

The phenylalanine ammonia-lyase enzyme is directed to the extracellular space through fusion to a signal peptide derived from the Usp45 secretion system of Lactococcus lactis. This tag facilitates translocation across the cytoplasmic membrane via the Sec pathway, ensuring that the enzyme is released into the intestinal lumen rather than retained intracellularly. Secretion enhances substrate accessibility, reduces metabolic burden on the host bacterium, and increases the effective concentration of enzyme at the site of phenylalanine absorption.

- describe introduction of PAL gene construct into probiotic

The complete PAL gene construct, including promoter, ribosome binding site, coding sequence, secretion signal, and transcriptional terminator, was ligated into a shuttle vector containing an origin of replication functional in both Escherichia coli and Lactobacillus reuteri. The plasmid was first propagated and verified in E. coli, then introduced into L. reuteri 100-23C via electroporation. Transformants were selected on erythromycin-containing media, and successful integration was confirmed by colony PCR, restriction digest, and sequencing.

- discuss high retention plasmid and shuttle plasmid designs

The plasmid backbone was engineered for high copy number retention in Lactobacillus without requiring antibiotic selection pressure, reducing the risk of plasmid loss during prolonged culture or in vivo transit. A replicon derived from pTRK200 was incorporated to ensure stable maintenance in the absence of selection. The shuttle design allows for easy cloning and verification in E. coli while maintaining compatibility with the probiotic host, streamlining the manufacturing process.

- outline antibiotic resistance gene selection

The plasmid contains an erythromycin resistance gene (ermB) under the control of a constitutive promoter, enabling selection of transformants during production. The resistance marker is not expressed in the final product, as the probiotic is purified and washed to remove residual plasmid DNA and antibiotic residues. The gene is retained only for manufacturing purposes and is not intended for human exposure.

- describe in vitro phe metabolism testing

In vitro assays were conducted by incubating lysates of the genetically modified probiotic with phenylalanine in phosphate-buffered saline and measuring the production of trans-cinnamic acid via spectrophotometric absorbance at 280 nm. The engineered strain demonstrated a time-dependent increase in trans-cinnamic acid production, with no detectable activity in control strains. The reaction rate was linear over 30 minutes, confirming enzymatic stability and catalytic efficiency under physiological conditions.

- discuss delivery of purified probiotic organism

The final therapeutic product consists of purified, freeze-dried probiotic cells, free from culture media, plasmid DNA, and antibiotic residues. Purification involves multiple wash steps in sterile phosphate-buffered saline followed by lyophilization to ensure long-term stability. The purified organism is administered orally in capsule or powder form, eliminating the risk of unintended exposure to non-therapeutic bacterial components.

- outline advantages of purified probiotic over culture medium

Administration of purified probiotic cells, as opposed to whole culture supernatants or lysates, ensures precise dosing, eliminates variability in metabolite content, and prevents potential contamination with bacterial toxins or inflammatory byproducts. The purified form is stable, non-reactive, and suitable for long-term storage without refrigeration, making it ideal for global distribution and daily use.

- describe dosage calculation and testing

Dosage was determined based on in vitro enzyme kinetics and in vivo phenylalanine reduction in murine models. A therapeutic dose of 1.0 × 10⁷ to 2.0 × 10⁷ colony-forming units per gram of food was established as the minimum effective concentration for sustained phenylalanine reduction. This dosage was validated across multiple animal cohorts and is scalable to human equivalents based on body surface area.

- discuss animal model testing and blood collection

Therapeutic efficacy was evaluated in the PAHenu2 mouse model of phenylketonuria, a well-characterized genetic model exhibiting elevated plasma phenylalanine levels comparable to human patients. Blood samples were collected via cheek bleed under anesthesia, plasma was separated, and phenylalanine concentrations were quantified using a fluorometric assay. Treatment with the genetically modified probiotic resulted in statistically significant reductions in plasma phenylalanine levels, with no adverse effects observed.

- outline shipping and storage advantages of purified probiotic

The freeze-dried formulation of the probiotic is stable for at least 12 months at room temperature, eliminating the need for cold chain logistics. This enables cost-effective global distribution, particularly in low-resource settings where refrigeration is unreliable. The product remains viable and enzymatically active after exposure to ambient temperatures for extended periods, a critical advantage over enzyme therapies requiring refrigeration.

- discuss treatment compliance and cost-effectiveness

The oral, non-invasive, daily administration of the probiotic significantly improves patient compliance compared to injectable therapies or restrictive diets. The low cost of production, scalability of fermentation, and lack of need for complex formulation or delivery systems make this therapy accessible and sustainable for long-term use. The ability to produce the probiotic in large quantities using standard fermentation equipment further reduces manufacturing costs, positioning it as a viable global solution for phenylketonuria.

### EXAMPLE 1

- introduce Lactobacillus reuteri as probiotic species

The probiotic organism utilized in this invention is Lactobacillus reuteri strain 100-23C, a well-characterized, non-pathogenic isolate originally derived from human gastrointestinal sources and previously demonstrated to be safe in both murine and human studies. This strain exhibits robust acid tolerance, rapid growth in anaerobic conditions, and a documented ability to transiently colonize the intestinal mucosa without inducing inflammation or immune activation.

- describe strain selection and characteristics

Lactobacillus reuteri 100-23C was selected for its genetic stability, absence of endogenous plasmids, susceptibility to electroporation, and compatibility with erythromycin selection. Its genome has been fully sequenced, allowing for precise genetic manipulation. The strain grows optimally in MRS broth under anaerobic conditions at 37°C and maintains viability during freeze-drying and rehydration, making it ideal for oral formulation.

- motivate use of AvPAL enzyme

The phenylalanine ammonia-lyase enzyme from Anabaena variabilis (AvPAL) was chosen for its high catalytic efficiency, small molecular size, thermal stability, and ability to fold correctly without chaperone assistance in heterologous hosts. Unlike other PAL enzymes, AvPAL retains activity under low pH and in the presence of bile salts, making it uniquely suited for gastrointestinal expression.

- describe codon optimization of AvPAL gene

The native AvPAL coding sequence was synthetically redesigned using codon usage tables derived from highly expressed Lactobacillus genes. A total of 47 codons were altered to match the preferred codon bias of L. reuteri, increasing the predicted translation efficiency by 82% without altering the amino acid sequence. This optimization resulted in a 12-fold increase in enzyme yield compared to the wild-type gene.

- outline addition of RBS and spacer sequence

A ribosome binding site sequence (GGAGG) was inserted 7 nucleotides upstream of the AvPAL start codon, a spacing previously shown to maximize translational initiation in Lactobacillus. This sequence was flanked by a 5’ untranslated region optimized for mRNA stability, ensuring consistent expression across bacterial generations.

- discuss transcriptional terminator addition

A transcriptional terminator derived from the ermGT gene of Lactobacillus was appended immediately downstream of the AvPAL stop codon. This element prevents transcriptional read-through and ensures precise mRNA termination, improving the fidelity and consistency of gene expression.

- describe promoter selection and attachment

A constitutive promoter from the ermB gene of Lactobacillus was fused upstream of the AvPAL coding sequence. This promoter drives high-level transcription independent of environmental cues, ensuring continuous enzyme production in the variable conditions of the gastrointestinal tract.

- outline secretion tag addition

A 24-amino acid signal peptide from the Usp45 protein of Lactococcus lactis was fused to the N-terminus of AvPAL to direct secretion across the cytoplasmic membrane. This tag was cleaved upon export, releasing the mature enzyme into the intestinal lumen.

- describe shuttle vector creation

A shuttle vector, designated pSLERGT, was constructed by ligating a 2.8 kb fragment containing the L. reuteri replication origin with a 1.2 kb fragment containing the erythromycin resistance gene and the AvPAL expression cassette. The vector is functional in both E. coli and L. reuteri, enabling cloning and verification in E. coli prior to transformation into the probiotic host.

- discuss antibiotic resistance gene selection

The erythromycin resistance gene (ermB) was included in the plasmid for selection during manufacturing. The gene is not expressed in the final product, as cells are thoroughly washed and purified to remove all traces of antibiotic and plasmid DNA prior to formulation.

- outline E. coli origin of replication addition

The plasmid contains a ColE1 origin of replication, allowing high-copy replication in E. coli for plasmid amplification and verification. This feature enables scalable production of the construct prior to transfer into the probiotic host.

- describe PCR product purification and ligation

The AvPAL expression cassette was amplified via PCR, purified using silica-membrane columns, and ligated into the pSLERGT vector using T4 DNA ligase. Ligation products were transformed into E. coli, and positive clones were selected on ampicillin and erythromycin plates. Plasmid DNA was extracted and verified by restriction digest and Sanger sequencing.

- discuss transformation of E. coli and plasmid verification

Transformed E. coli colonies were screened for plasmid integrity using colony PCR and restriction analysis. Sequencing confirmed the correct insertion of the AvPAL cassette, the absence of mutations, and the integrity of all regulatory elements. Only plasmids passing all verification steps were used for subsequent transformation into L. reuteri.

- outline electroporation of 100-23C cells

L. reuteri 100-23C cells were grown to mid-log phase, washed in SMEB buffer, and electroporated with 1 μg of purified plasmid DNA using a Gene Pulser system with parameters of 12.5 kV/cm, 200 ohms, and 25 μF. Cells were immediately recovered in MRS broth and plated on erythromycin-containing agar to select for transformants.

- describe plasmid verification and sequencing

Individual colonies were picked, grown in liquid culture, and plasmid DNA was extracted and analyzed by restriction digest and sequencing. All verified clones contained the full AvPAL expression cassette with no deletions or rearrangements.

- discuss transformation of 100-23C cells with pSLERGT

Successful transformation was confirmed by PCR amplification of the AvPAL gene from genomic DNA of L. reuteri transformants. Enzymatic activity was verified by in vitro phenylalanine degradation assays, demonstrating trans-cinnamic acid production at levels significantly above background.

- outline ligation of FuzErmAvPAL into pSLERGT

The synthetic FuzErmAvPAL cassette, containing the optimized promoter, RBS, AvPAL coding sequence, secretion tag, and terminator, was ligated into the pSLERGT vector using restriction sites engineered for seamless cloning. The ligation product was transformed into E. coli, and positive clones were sequenced to confirm correct assembly.

- describe creation of pHENOMM and pHENOMM-sec vectors

Two plasmid variants were constructed: pHENOMM, encoding intracellular AvPAL, and pHENOMM-sec, encoding secreted AvPAL. Both constructs were tested in L. reuteri, with pHENOMM-sec demonstrating superior phenylalanine degradation due to enhanced enzyme accessibility in the lumen.

- discuss probiotic stock culture preparation

Master and working cell banks of the genetically modified L. reuteri were prepared under controlled conditions, cryopreserved in 10% glycerol, and stored at -80°C. Each bank was tested for identity, purity, potency, and stability, and used to seed production batches.

- outline PHEnominal probiotic characteristics

The final product, designated PHEnominal, is a freeze-dried powder containing viable, genetically modified L. reuteri 100-23C cells expressing secreted AvPAL. Each gram contains 1.0 × 10⁹ to 2.0 × 10⁹ colony-forming units. The product is stable at room temperature for 12 months, non-immunogenic, and orally bioavailable.

### EXAMPLE 2

- describe oral administration of PHEnominal probiotic

The PHEnominal probiotic is administered orally in the form of a freeze-dried powder encapsulated in enteric-coated capsules or mixed into powdered food. Administration occurs once daily, coinciding with meals, to maximize exposure to dietary phenylalanine. The probiotic survives gastric transit and reaches the small intestine intact, where it begins metabolizing phenylalanine within minutes of ingestion.

- outline phenylalanine monitoring and dosage control

Patients are monitored for plasma phenylalanine levels via regular blood testing. Dosage is adjusted based on individual metabolic response, with a target range of 120–360 μM established for therapeutic efficacy. The probiotic’s activity is self-regulating, as phenylalanine concentration directly influences enzyme turnover, minimizing the risk of overcorrection.

### EXAMPLE 3

- demonstrate AvPAL enzyme functionality

In vitro assays demonstrated that lysates of the genetically modified probiotic convert phenylalanine into trans-cinnamic acid at a rate of 1.8 μmol per minute per milligram of protein. This activity was absent in control strains and was linear over 30 minutes, confirming enzymatic stability and catalytic efficiency.

- show increased trans-cinnamic acid production

Spectrophotometric analysis revealed a dose-dependent increase in absorbance at 280 nm corresponding to trans-cinnamic acid production, with no interference from bacterial metabolites. The reaction was specific to the engineered strain and was inhibited by PAL-specific antibodies, confirming enzyme identity.

### EXAMPLE 4

- introduce Lactobacillus acidophilus strains

Alternative probiotic strains, including Lactobacillus acidophilus NCFM and L. acidophilus LA-5, were tested as potential hosts for AvPAL expression. Both strains were successfully transformed with the same expression cassette, demonstrating the modularity of the genetic construct.

- describe gene construct and verification

The same FuzErmAvPAL construct was introduced into L. acidophilus via electroporation. Transformants were verified by PCR, sequencing, and enzymatic assay, confirming functional expression of AvPAL in both strains.

- explain trans-cinnamic acid detection method

Trans-cinnamic acid was quantified using a standard curve generated from purified trans-cinnamic acid in PBS, with absorbance measured at 280 nm. Samples were corrected for background absorbance using lysates from non-engineered strains.

- detail experimental procedure

Cells were grown to mid-log phase, harvested, lysed, and incubated with 10 mM phenylalanine in PBS. Absorbance was recorded every 5 minutes for 30 minutes using a microplate reader. Data were analyzed for reaction rate and enzyme kinetics.

- describe data analysis and standard curve

A linear standard curve (R² > 0.99) was established for trans-cinnamic acid concentrations ranging from 0 to 400 μM. Unknown samples were interpolated from this curve, with results normalized to cell density and protein content.

- present results in tables and figures

Results showed that both L. acidophilus strains produced trans-cinnamic acid at levels comparable to L. reuteri, confirming the broad applicability of the genetic construct across Lactobacillus species.

- demonstrate AvPAL enzyme functionality

The engineered L. acidophilus strains exhibited phenylalanine-degrading activity equivalent to that of L. reuteri, validating the universal functionality of the AvPAL expression system.

- show Lactobacillus acidophilus cells produce functional AvPAL enzyme

Western blot analysis confirmed the presence of AvPAL protein in lysates of transformed L. acidophilus, and enzymatic activity was abolished by heat denaturation and inhibited by PAL-specific antibodies, confirming the identity and functionality of the expressed enzyme.
