# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biomedicine and artificial intelligence, specifically to a deep learning-based system for high-throughput screening of nanoformulated drugs. The integration of advanced computational methodologies with biomedical imaging technologies enables the automated, objective, and highly sensitive evaluation of drug efficacy at the single-cell level. This invention bridges the gap between traditional pharmacological assays and modern machine learning paradigms by leveraging image-based data acquisition and deep neural network architectures to overcome inherent limitations of conventional screening techniques. The system is particularly suited for applications involving nanomaterial-based drug delivery systems, where physicochemical interference, morphological variability, and temporal dynamics complicate traditional analytical approaches. By combining high-content imaging with convolutional neural networks, this invention provides a scalable, reproducible, and interpretable platform for drug discovery and development in the era of precision medicine.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The development of novel therapeutic agents, particularly those formulated with nanoscale carriers, has become a central focus in modern drug discovery. However, the evaluation of these agents remains constrained by the limitations of classical in vitro assays such as MTT and lactate dehydrogenase release, which rely on bulk measurements and are susceptible to interference from the optical, adsorptive, or fluorescent properties of nanoparticles. These methods require prolonged incubation periods, typically 24 hours or more, to detect statistically significant changes in cell viability, rendering them unsuitable for rapid screening or early-stage efficacy assessment. High-throughput screening technologies have been developed to accelerate compound evaluation, yet they remain dependent on predefined biochemical markers and are unable to capture subtle, spatially localized cellular responses that precede overt phenotypic changes. 

Computer-assisted methods, including virtual screening and structure-based modeling, have emerged as alternatives, but their utility is fundamentally limited by the availability and quality of curated molecular databases and the necessity for manual feature engineering. These approaches fail to account for the complex, emergent behaviors of cells exposed to nanomaterials, where morphology, membrane dynamics, and intracellular redistribution play critical roles in biological response. Machine learning has begun to address some of these shortcomings by enabling pattern recognition in high-dimensional data, yet most existing models are trained on tabular or molecular descriptor-based inputs, which discard the rich spatial information contained in cellular images. 

Deep learning, particularly convolutional neural networks, has demonstrated exceptional capability in extracting hierarchical features from visual data, mimicking the human visual system’s ability to discern complex patterns from raw pixel inputs. Despite this potential, no prior system has been developed to fully exploit single-cell image data from image flow cytometry for the purpose of real-time, interference-resistant drug screening. Chinese Patent Application No. 2017101273955 describes a machine learning method for drug activity prediction based on molecular fingerprints, but it does not incorporate cellular imaging, lacks adaptability to nanomaterial interference, and cannot resolve subcellular morphological changes induced by short-term drug exposure. There remains an urgent and unmet need for a screening system that is independent of biochemical labeling, immune to physicochemical interference, capable of rapid analysis, and able to detect early-stage cellular responses with high precision.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a deep learning-based drug screening system that utilizes single-cell images acquired via image flow cytometry to evaluate the efficacy of nanoformulated drugs with unprecedented speed, accuracy, and robustness. The system comprises a series of integrated modules designed to preprocess, standardize, and classify cellular images through a deep convolutional neural network architecture. The picture preprocessing module extracts and isolates individual cell regions from raw image streams, ensuring consistent input dimensions and eliminating background noise. The channel merging module combines multiple fluorescent and bright-field channels into a unified multi-channel representation, preserving spatial relationships critical for feature discrimination. The picture standardization module normalizes pixel intensity distributions across samples to mitigate batch-to-batch variability and enhance model generalizability. 

The neural network module is a deep convolutional architecture inspired by the network-in-network paradigm, comprising alternating convolutional, pooling, and regularization layers that progressively extract hierarchical features from the input images. Unlike conventional networks that terminate in fully connected dense layers, this architecture employs convolutional layers in the final stages to maintain spatial awareness and reduce parameter redundancy. The network structure is optimized to prevent overfitting through weight decay, dropout, and batch normalization, while training is accelerated using the Adam optimizer with adaptive learning rates. The training method employs balanced mini-batch sampling to ensure equal representation of all efficacy classes, thereby improving classification stability and reducing bias. 

The method of deep learning-based drug screening involves acquiring single-cell images from drug-treated and untreated cells, preprocessing and merging the image channels, standardizing pixel intensities, and feeding the resulting tensors into the trained neural network for classification into categories of drug efficacy: ineffective, low, medium, or high. The picture preprocessing module ensures uniform cell cropping and alignment, while the neural network module autonomously identifies morphological and textural cues indicative of drug-induced cellular changes, including membrane asymmetry, cytoplasmic condensation, and organelle redistribution. 

The advantages of this invention include the elimination of manual interpretation, reduction of experimental time from days to hours, immunity to nanoparticle-induced optical interference, and the ability to detect subtle cellular responses within two hours of drug exposure. The system is adaptable to diverse nanocarrier types, including inorganic and lipid-based systems, and can be enhanced by incorporating additional fluorescent markers without architectural modification. This invention represents a paradigm shift in drug screening by replacing biochemical assays with image-based, AI-driven classification, enabling faster, more accurate, and more interpretable evaluation of next-generation therapeutics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Example 1 A Deep Learning-Based Quick and Precise High-Throughput Drug Screening System

The construction process of the drug screening system model begins with the acquisition of single-cell images from cultures of A549 and HEpG2 cells exposed to nanoformulated drugs, including LDH-VP16 and SLN-Cur, as well as their respective blank carriers. Cells are incubated for two or six hours, stained with Annexin V-APC and, optionally, anti-EGFR-FITC, and imaged using an image flow cytometer capable of capturing both bright-field and fluorescence channels. Each image sample consists of two to three individual channels, which are concatenated channelwise to form a multi-spectral input tensor. 

The picture preprocessing module applies automated cell segmentation to isolate individual cell regions, discarding overlapping or fragmented cells, and resizes all images to a uniform dimension of 70 by 70 pixels using bicubic interpolation. The channel merging module aligns and combines the bright-field, Annexin V-APC, and EGFR-FITC channels into a single multi-channel image, preserving spatial co-localization of morphological and molecular features. The picture standardization module normalizes pixel intensities across all images using z-score normalization to eliminate variations arising from illumination, staining efficiency, or instrument drift. 

The neural network module is implemented as a 46-layer deep convolutional architecture derived from the network-in-network design, with no fully connected layers. Each convolutional block consists of sequential convolutional operations, batch normalization, and ReLU activation, followed by max pooling or average pooling to reduce spatial resolution. Dropout layers are inserted after every third convolutional block to inhibit overfitting. The final layers consist of a global average pooling operation followed by a 1×1 convolutional layer that maps extracted features to the four efficacy classes. 

The network structure is trained using a dataset comprising 103,568 and 115,968 labeled single-cell images from A549 and HEpG2 cells, respectively, categorized as ineffective, low, medium, or high efficacy based on 24-hour MTT assay results. Training is performed over 150 epochs using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001, beta1 of 0.9, beta2 of 0.999, and epsilon of 1×10⁻⁸, on two NVIDIA GTX 1080Ti GPUs. Balanced sampling ensures equal representation of each efficacy class in every mini-batch. Regularization is further enhanced through weight decay and batch normalization. 

The classification model achieves testing accuracies of 0.851, 0.864, and 0.908 on mixed-cell, HEpG2, and A549 datasets, respectively, when trained on bright-field and Annexin V-APC channels. When an additional EGFR-FITC channel is included, accuracy increases to 0.966, demonstrating the system’s flexibility. Removal of the Annexin V-APC channel reduces accuracy to 0.705, confirming the contribution of molecular markers while maintaining functional viability. Model testing is performed on independent datasets derived from two- and six-hour exposures, with no significant performance degradation, validating rapid detection capability. 

Class activation mapping reveals that the model focuses on cell membrane boundaries and intracellular regions associated with phosphatidylserine externalization and receptor redistribution, confirming its ability to detect early apoptotic and signaling events. The system demonstrates consistent performance across diverse nanocarriers, including LDH and SLN, despite differences in morphology, composition, and fluorescence properties. The DeepScreen model requires no manual parameter tuning, eliminates subjective interpretation, and reduces assay duration from days to hours, offering a transformative advance in high-throughput drug screening.
