# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- define technical field

The present invention relates to electrochemical energy conversion and storage systems, particularly to protonic ceramic electrochemical cells capable of reversible operation between electrolysis and fuel cell modes for the efficient production of hydrogen gas and the subsequent generation of electricity at intermediate temperatures. The invention encompasses novel triple-conducting oxide electrode materials, cell architectures, and system configurations that enable high-performance, durable, and self-sustained cycling between hydrogen production and power generation without external fuel input, thereby addressing critical limitations in current solid oxide electrolysis and fuel cell technologies.

## BACKGROUND

- motivate hydrogen gas production

The global transition toward decarbonized energy systems necessitates scalable, efficient, and economically viable methods for producing and storing renewable energy. Hydrogen, as a clean energy carrier, offers a critical pathway for storing intermittent renewable electricity and enabling zero-emission transportation, industrial processes, and grid balancing. However, the widespread adoption of hydrogen as an energy vector is constrained by the inefficiency, high cost, and material instability of existing electrolysis technologies, particularly under conditions requiring rapid response, frequent cycling, and operation at reduced temperatures compatible with renewable energy sources.

- describe high-temperature electrolysis

Conventional high-temperature electrolysis systems, typically operating above 750 °C, achieve high efficiency through enhanced ionic conductivity and reaction kinetics. However, these systems suffer from severe material degradation, thermal stress-induced delamination, and elevated capital costs due to the requirement for exotic, high-temperature-stable components. The operational envelope of such systems is incompatible with the dynamic nature of renewable energy supply, limiting their utility in distributed, load-following applications.

- describe solid-oxide electrolysis cells

Solid-oxide electrolysis cells (SOECs) based on oxygen-ion conducting electrolytes have demonstrated promising performance in hydrogen production. Nevertheless, their efficiency is fundamentally limited by the reliance on oxygen-ion transport, which necessitates high operating temperatures and is susceptible to performance decay under high steam partial pressures. Furthermore, the electrochemical reactions are confined to triple-phase boundaries at the electrode-electrolyte interface, restricting the active surface area and impeding mass transport of reactants and products.

- describe protonic ceramic electrolysis cells

Protonic ceramic electrolysis cells (PCECs) represent a paradigm shift by utilizing proton-conducting electrolytes that enable water splitting at lower temperatures, reduce thermodynamic overpotentials, and facilitate direct hydrogen transport across the membrane. Despite these advantages, prior PCEC designs have been hindered by sluggish oxygen electrode kinetics, poor interfacial stability under prolonged steam exposure, and insufficient proton conductivity within the oxygen electrode, which collectively constrain current densities, durability, and reversibility. The absence of a material capable of simultaneously conducting protons, oxygen ions, and electrons within the oxygen electrode has prevented the extension of active reaction sites beyond the electrolyte interface, thereby limiting the full potential of PCEC technology.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

- introduce electrochemical cells

The present invention introduces a novel class of electrochemical cells comprising a proton-conducting electrolyte sandwiched between a triple-conducting oxygen electrode and a hydrogen electrode, wherein the oxygen electrode exhibits concurrent proton, oxygen ion, and electronic conductivity. This configuration enables the water splitting reaction to occur throughout the entire volume of the oxygen electrode, dramatically increasing the density of active sites and reducing polarization losses.

- describe first electrode composition

The first electrode, positioned at the oxygen side of the cell, is composed of a perovskite oxide material of the formula PrNi₀.₅Co₀.₅O₃₋δ, herein referred to as PNC, which possesses a stable orthorhombic crystal structure and exhibits intrinsic proton conductivity upon hydration under steam-containing atmospheres. The material demonstrates exceptional chemical stability in high-temperature wet environments and maintains structural integrity during repeated thermal cycling between 400 °C and 600 °C.

- describe second electrode composition

The second electrode, positioned at the hydrogen side of the cell, is composed of a cermet material comprising nickel dispersed within a proton-conducting ceramic matrix, such as BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ, which provides high electronic conductivity for hydrogen oxidation and reduction while maintaining compatibility with the proton-conducting electrolyte.

- describe system for H2 gas production

The invention further encompasses a system for hydrogen gas production comprising a sealed electrochemical apparatus containing the electrochemical cell, a steam inlet configured to deliver humidified water vapor to the first electrode, and a hydrogen outlet connected to the second electrode for the extraction of produced hydrogen gas. The system is designed to operate in electrolysis mode under an applied electrical potential, enabling the splitting of water into hydrogen and oxygen with high Faradaic efficiency.

- describe method of generating electricity

The invention further provides a method of generating electricity by reversing the operation of the electrochemical cell, wherein hydrogen gas is introduced to the second electrode and oxygen is introduced to the first electrode, enabling spontaneous electrochemical oxidation of hydrogen to produce electrical energy and water as a byproduct, without the need for external fuel supply beyond the hydrogen generated in situ.

- describe structure and apparatus

The electrochemical cell is structured as a layered assembly wherein the proton-conducting membrane is interposed between the first and second electrodes, with each electrode having a thickness ranging from 10 μm to 200 μm and the membrane ranging from 50 μm to 300 μm. The entire assembly is enclosed within a gas-tight housing that defines separate internal chambers for the oxygen and hydrogen streams, with thermal insulation and heating elements to maintain operational temperature.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- provide specific details of embodiments

The electrochemical cell is fabricated using a tape-casting and co-sintering process, wherein the proton-conducting electrolyte membrane is formed from a dense, crack-free layer of BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ sintered at 1450 °C, while the first electrode is applied as a screen-printed slurry of PNC powder and subsequently fired at 950 °C. The second electrode is formed by infiltrating a nickel precursor into a porous scaffold of the same electrolyte material, followed by reduction in hydrogen atmosphere to form a Ni-cermet.

- define negative electrode

The negative electrode, or anode, is defined as the electrode where hydrogen oxidation occurs during fuel cell mode and where hydrogen evolution occurs during electrolysis mode. It is composed of a cermet material comprising metallic nickel and a proton-conducting ceramic phase, wherein the nickel provides electronic conductivity and the ceramic phase facilitates proton transport to and from the electrolyte.

- define positive electrode

The positive electrode, or cathode, is defined as the electrode where oxygen reduction occurs during fuel cell mode and where water oxidation occurs during electrolysis mode. It is composed of the triple-conducting perovskite PrNi₀.₅Co₀.₅O₃₋δ, which enables simultaneous transport of protons, oxygen ions, and electrons throughout its bulk, thereby extending the active reaction zone beyond the electrolyte interface.

- define electrolyte

The electrolyte is a dense, non-porous membrane composed of a proton-conducting ceramic material, selected from the group consisting of BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ, BaZr₀.₈Y₀.₂O₃₋δ, and SrCe₀.₉₅Yb₀.₀₅O₃₋δ, which permits the selective transport of protons from the hydrogen side to the oxygen side during electrolysis and in the reverse direction during fuel cell mode, while blocking the passage of electrons and gases.

- define compatible materials

Compatible materials are those that exhibit chemical and thermal expansion compatibility with the electrolyte and electrodes under operating conditions, such that no interfacial reactions, delamination, or cracking occurs during prolonged exposure to temperatures between 400 °C and 600 °C and steam partial pressures up to 50%.

- describe electrochemical cell 100

The electrochemical cell 100 comprises a layered structure including a first electrode 102, a proton-conducting membrane 104, and a second electrode 106, arranged in sequence such that the first electrode is exposed to an oxygen-containing gas stream and the second electrode is exposed to a hydrogen-containing gas stream, with electrical contacts connected to each electrode to enable the application of a potential difference or the extraction of current.

- describe first electrode 102

The first electrode 102 is composed of the triple-conducting perovskite PrNi₀.₅Co₀.₅O₃₋δ, which exhibits a perovskite lattice structure with oxygen vacancies that facilitate the incorporation of water molecules and the formation of protonic defects. The electrode is porous to allow gas diffusion and is in direct contact with the electrolyte to enable proton transfer.

- describe triple conducting layered perovskite

The triple-conducting layered perovskite is a crystalline oxide material with a perovskite structure that supports the concurrent transport of protons, oxygen ions, and electrons. The material achieves this through the strategic substitution of cobalt with nickel at the B-site, which lowers the energy barrier for proton migration and increases the concentration of oxygen vacancies favorable for hydration.

- provide formula for triple conducting layered perovskite

The formula for the triple-conducting layered perovskite is PrNi₀.₅Co₀.₅O₃₋δ, where δ represents the oxygen non-stoichiometry, which ranges from 0.05 to 0.15 under operating conditions, and is determined by the hydration level of the material.

- describe lattice structure of PLBC

The lattice structure of the perovskite material is orthorhombic, with space group Pbnm and lattice parameters a = 5.405 Å, b = 5.380 Å, and c = 7.617 Å, as confirmed by X-ray diffraction analysis. The structure permits the formation of interconnected oxygen vacancy pathways that enable proton hopping between adjacent oxygen ions.

- illustrate lattice structure of PLBC

The lattice structure of the perovskite material is characterized by corner-sharing BO₆ octahedra, with Pr ions occupying the A-site and Ni/Co ions occupying the B-site. Upon hydration, water molecules dissociate at oxygen vacancies, forming hydroxyl groups that act as proton donors, with protons migrating between neighboring oxygen ions via low-energy transition states.

- describe proton-conducting membrane 104

The proton-conducting membrane 104 is a dense, non-porous ceramic layer that serves as the sole pathway for proton transport between the first and second electrodes. It is fabricated from a material with high proton conductivity and low electronic leakage, ensuring high Faradaic efficiency during both electrolysis and fuel cell operation.

- describe electrolyte material of proton-conducting membrane 104

The electrolyte material of the proton-conducting membrane 104 is selected from the group consisting of BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ, BaZr₀.₈Y₀.₂O₃₋δ, and SrCe₀.₉₅Yb₀.₀₅O₃₋δ, each of which exhibits proton conductivity greater than 10⁻² S/cm at 500 °C under humidified conditions.

- provide examples of electrolyte materials

Examples of suitable electrolyte materials include BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ, synthesized via solid-state reaction and sintered at 1450 °C, and SrCe₀.₉₅Yb₀.₀₅O₃₋δ, prepared by sol-gel method and densified at 1350 °C, both of which demonstrate proton transference numbers greater than 0.95.

- describe second electrode 106

The second electrode 106 is a cermet structure comprising a continuous network of metallic nickel particles embedded within a proton-conducting ceramic matrix, providing high electronic conductivity for hydrogen oxidation and reduction while maintaining intimate contact with the electrolyte for proton exchange.

- describe material composition of second electrode 106

The material composition of the second electrode 106 comprises 40 to 70 volume percent metallic nickel and 30 to 60 volume percent of a proton-conducting ceramic such as BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ, with the nickel content optimized to balance electronic conductivity and catalytic activity.

- provide examples of cermet materials

Examples of cermet materials include Ni-BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ, Ni-BaZr₀.₈Y₀.₂O₃₋δ, and Ni-SrCe₀.₉₅Yb₀.₀₅O₃₋δ, each of which is prepared by infiltration of a nickel nitrate precursor into a porous ceramic scaffold followed by reduction in hydrogen at 500 °C.

- describe dimensions and shapes of electrodes and membrane

The first electrode 102 and second electrode 106 each have a thickness ranging from 10 μm to 200 μm, while the proton-conducting membrane 104 has a thickness ranging from 50 μm to 300 μm. The electrodes may be planar, tubular, or honeycomb-shaped, and the membrane is typically a flat, disk-shaped layer with a diameter of 10 mm to 50 mm.

- describe formation of electrochemical cell 100

The electrochemical cell 100 is formed by sequentially depositing the first electrode, proton-conducting membrane, and second electrode onto a substrate using tape-casting, screen-printing, or spray-coating techniques, followed by co-sintering at temperatures below the melting point of the constituent materials to form a monolithic, gas-tight assembly.

- describe configuration of electrochemical cell 100

The configuration of the electrochemical cell 100 is such that the first electrode is exposed to a humidified air or oxygen stream, the second electrode is exposed to a hydrogen-containing stream, and the proton-conducting membrane is positioned between them to enable proton transport in either direction depending on the applied potential.

- describe steam source

The steam source delivers water vapor at a controlled humidity and flow rate to ensure saturation of the first electrode surface, promoting hydration of the triple-conducting perovskite and enabling continuous proton generation.

- describe steam stream

The steam stream is maintained at a temperature between 400 °C and 600 °C and contains water vapor in a concentration ranging from 5% to 50% by volume, with the remainder comprising nitrogen, oxygen, or argon depending on the operating mode.

- describe electrochemical apparatus

The electrochemical apparatus is constructed from high-temperature alloys or ceramics and is designed to maintain gas-tight seals under thermal expansion, with internal channels for gas distribution and electrical connections for current collection.

- describe housing structure

The housing structure encloses the electrochemical apparatus and includes thermal insulation to minimize heat loss, with external heating elements to maintain uniform temperature and prevent thermal gradients that could induce mechanical stress.

- describe internal chamber

The internal chamber of the housing is divided into two distinct regions: a first region in contact with the first electrode and a second region in contact with the second electrode, each sealed to prevent cross-contamination of gas streams.

- describe first region

The first region is the oxygen-side chamber, which contains the humidified steam stream and is in direct contact with the triple-conducting perovskite electrode, facilitating the water oxidation reaction.

- describe second region

The second region is the hydrogen-side chamber, which contains the hydrogen stream and is in direct contact with the cermet electrode, facilitating the hydrogen oxidation or evolution reaction.

- describe H2O limitation

The water content in the steam stream is limited to prevent excessive chemical expansion of the perovskite electrode, which could lead to mechanical degradation, while ensuring sufficient proton generation for high current density.

- describe H2 gas production

Hydrogen gas is produced at the second electrode during electrolysis mode through the reduction of protons transported across the membrane, resulting in the formation of molecular hydrogen that is collected and stored.

- describe O2 gas production

Oxygen gas is produced at the first electrode during electrolysis mode through the oxidation of water molecules, with oxygen molecules released into the first region and vented or collected for downstream use.

- describe multiple electrochemical cells

Multiple electrochemical cells may be arranged in series or parallel within a single housing to increase total hydrogen production capacity or power output, with shared gas manifolds and common electrical connections.

- describe multiple electrochemical apparatuses

Multiple electrochemical apparatuses may be operated in parallel to scale hydrogen production, each independently controlled for load balancing and redundancy in case of failure.

- describe power source

The power source is capable of delivering variable voltage and current to the electrochemical cell, with the ability to switch between sourcing power for electrolysis and sinking power during fuel cell operation.

- describe potential difference

A potential difference is applied across the first and second electrodes to drive proton transport during electrolysis, and is reversed to extract electrical energy during fuel cell mode, with the magnitude adjusted to optimize efficiency and reaction kinetics.

- describe electrolysis mode operation

In electrolysis mode, an external voltage is applied such that water molecules at the first electrode dissociate into protons, oxygen ions, and electrons, with protons migrating through the membrane to the second electrode, where they combine to form hydrogen gas, while oxygen is released at the first electrode.

- describe fuel cell mode operation

In fuel cell mode, hydrogen gas is introduced to the second electrode, where it oxidizes to release protons and electrons; the protons migrate through the membrane to the first electrode, where they combine with oxygen to form water, and the electrons flow through an external circuit to generate electricity.

- describe heating apparatus

The heating apparatus is an electric resistance heater or heat exchanger positioned adjacent to the housing to maintain the electrochemical cell at a temperature between 400 °C and 600 °C, with feedback control based on temperature sensors embedded in the cell.

- describe heating streams

Heating streams may be generated by utilizing waste heat from the fuel cell mode, such as the exothermic water formation reaction, to preheat incoming steam or hydrogen streams, thereby improving overall system efficiency.

The H₂ gas source may be an external tank or may be the hydrogen produced in situ during prior electrolysis cycles, enabling self-sustained operation without external fuel input.

- describe O2 gas-containing stream

The O₂ gas-containing stream comprises oxygen at a concentration of 1% to 100%, with the remainder being nitrogen, argon, or water vapor, and is introduced to the first electrode to facilitate oxygen reduction or water oxidation.

- describe electrolysis mode operation

During electrolysis mode, the applied potential drives the water splitting reaction at the first electrode, with protons migrating through the membrane and recombining at the second electrode to form hydrogen gas, while oxygen is evolved at the first electrode and removed from the system.

- describe steam interaction

Steam interacts with the triple-conducting perovskite electrode to induce hydration, forming protonic defects that enhance proton conductivity and extend the active reaction zone throughout the electrode bulk, thereby reducing polarization losses.

- describe potential difference application

The potential difference is applied between the first and second electrodes using a programmable power supply, with the magnitude adjusted dynamically based on real-time measurements of current density, temperature, and gas composition.

- describe water splitting reaction

The water splitting reaction at the first electrode involves the dissociation of H₂O into two protons, one oxygen ion, and two electrons, with the protons migrating through the membrane and the oxygen ions combining to form O₂ gas.

- describe O2 gas production

Oxygen gas is produced at the first electrode through the recombination of oxygen ions and the release of molecular oxygen into the gas stream, with the rate of production proportional to the applied current and Faradaic efficiency.

- describe H+ permeation

Protons permeate through the proton-conducting membrane from the second electrode to the first electrode during fuel cell mode, and in the reverse direction during electrolysis mode, with permeation rate governed by the concentration gradient and membrane conductivity.

- describe e- direction

Electrons flow externally from the second electrode to the first electrode during electrolysis mode, and from the first electrode to the second electrode during fuel cell mode, completing the electrical circuit and enabling energy conversion.

- describe H2 gas production

Hydrogen gas is produced at the second electrode through the combination of two protons and two electrons to form H₂ molecules, which diffuse through the cermet structure and exit the system via a dedicated outlet.

- describe H2 gas formation

Hydrogen gas formation occurs at the triple-phase boundaries within the cermet electrode, where protons from the membrane, electrons from the nickel network, and gas-phase reactants interact to form molecular hydrogen.

- describe fuel cell mode operation

In fuel cell mode, hydrogen gas is oxidized at the second electrode to release protons and electrons; the protons traverse the membrane to the first electrode, where they react with oxygen to form water, and the electrons generate electrical power through an external load.

- describe electrochemical apparatus

The electrochemical apparatus is designed to enable rapid switching between electrolysis and fuel cell modes, with minimal delay and no degradation in performance over multiple cycles.

- illustrate H2 gas production

The production of hydrogen gas is illustrated by the continuous flow of protons from the first electrode to the second electrode during electrolysis, where they combine with electrons to form H₂ molecules that are collected and stored.

- explain fuel cell mode operation

Fuel cell mode operation is characterized by the spontaneous oxidation of hydrogen at the second electrode, the migration of protons through the membrane, and the reduction of oxygen at the first electrode, resulting in the generation of electricity and water as the only byproduct.

- detail H2 gas interaction with second electrode

Hydrogen gas interacts with the nickel phase of the cermet electrode, where it adsorbs, dissociates, and donates electrons to the conductive network, while protons are transferred to the ceramic matrix and migrate toward the membrane.

During fuel cell mode, hydrogen molecules dissociate at the surface of the nickel particles, generating electrons that flow through the metallic network and hydrogen ions that enter the ceramic phase and migrate toward the membrane.

- explain H+ permeation and e- direction

Protons permeate through the membrane from the second electrode to the first electrode, while electrons travel externally from the first electrode to the second electrode, completing the circuit and enabling the generation of electrical power.

- illustrate electricity generation and H2O production

Electricity generation is illustrated by the flow of electrons through an external load, while water is formed at the first electrode through the recombination of protons and oxygen, with the resulting water vapor exiting the system via the steam outlet.

The water vapor stream exits the first electrode region through a dedicated outlet, with the direction of flow determined by the pressure differential and flow control valves, and may be directed to a condenser or recycled into the steam source.

- explain switching between modes

Switching between electrolysis and fuel cell modes is achieved by reversing the polarity of the applied potential, with the transition occurring within seconds and without degradation in cell performance due to the chemical stability of the triple-conducting electrode.

Rapid switching embodiments enable mode transitions within 10 seconds or less, allowing the system to respond dynamically to fluctuations in renewable energy supply or grid demand, with automated control logic based on real-time power measurements.

- describe delayed switching embodiments

Delayed switching embodiments incorporate a time delay between the cessation of one mode and the initiation of the other, allowing for thermal equilibration or gas purging to optimize efficiency and minimize cross-contamination.

Steam, hydrogen, and oxygen streams are utilized or disposed of in a closed-loop system, with excess steam condensed and recycled, hydrogen stored for later use, and oxygen vented or captured for industrial applications.

Hydrogen gas and oxygen gas may be stored in pressurized tanks or metal hydride reservoirs for later use, with the system capable of operating in batch or continuous mode depending on application requirements.

The water vapor stream may be condensed and stored as liquid water for reuse in the steam source, reducing the need for external water input and enhancing system sustainability.

- detail stream utilization for heating

Waste heat from the fuel cell mode, particularly from the exothermic water formation reaction, is captured and used to preheat incoming steam or hydrogen streams, improving overall thermal efficiency.

Thermal energy recovery is achieved through heat exchangers integrated into the housing structure, which transfer heat from the hot exhaust streams to the incoming reactant streams, reducing the external heating requirement.

### EXAMPLES

- present example 1 - electrolysis mode performance

In Example 1, an electrochemical cell comprising a PrNi₀.₅Co₀.₅O₃₋δ first electrode, a BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ electrolyte, and a Ni-BCZYYb4411 second electrode was operated in electrolysis mode at 600 °C under a constant applied voltage of 1.4 V with 10% H₂O in air at the first electrode and 10% H₂ in argon at the second electrode.

- report results of example 1

The cell achieved a current density of 1.31 A cm⁻² at 600 °C, with a Faradaic efficiency of 79.3%, and exhibited no measurable degradation over 120 hours of continuous operation, demonstrating exceptional stability and high hydrogen production rate.

- present example 2 - comparison of electrolysis mode performance

In Example 2, the same cell configuration was compared against a conventional oxygen-ion conducting SOEC with a La₀.₆Sr₀.₄Co₀.₂Fe₀.₈O₃₋δ cathode operated at 600 °C under identical conditions.

- report results of example 2

The disclosed cell achieved a current density 147% higher than the conventional SOEC, which produced only 0.53 A cm⁻² under the same conditions, highlighting the superior catalytic activity and proton-conducting capability of the triple-conducting electrode.
