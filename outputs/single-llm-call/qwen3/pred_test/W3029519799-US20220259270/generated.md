## TECHNICAL DOMAIN

The present invention pertains to a novel strain of the filamentous fungus Trichoderma, specifically engineered for the high-yield production of cellulolytic and hemicellulolytic enzymes. This strain is derived from the well-characterized Trichoderma reesei RutC30, a historically significant industrial workhorse in the field of lignocellulosic biomass degradation. The invention encompasses a genetically modified fungal organism that exhibits substantially enhanced secretion of extracellular enzymes, particularly cellulases and hemicellulases, under controlled fermentation conditions using low-cost, non-purified carbon sources such as sugarcane molasses. The strain is designed to operate efficiently in industrial bioreactor systems without reliance on expensive inducing sugars like lactose or Avicel, thereby significantly reducing production costs while maintaining high catalytic output. The organism is capable of sustained, high-titer enzyme secretion over extended fermentation periods, making it uniquely suited for scalable, economically viable biofuel and biorefinery applications. The technical domain of this invention lies at the intersection of industrial biotechnology, microbial metabolic engineering, and sustainable enzyme manufacturing, addressing critical bottlenecks in the commercial deployment of enzymatic biomass conversion technologies.

## PRIOR ART

Cellulases and hemicellulases are complex mixtures of carbohydrate-active enzymes responsible for the depolymerization of plant cell wall polysaccharides into fermentable monosaccharides. These enzymes have long been recognized as indispensable catalysts in the production of second-generation biofuels, biochemicals, and bioproducts derived from non-food biomass. The industrial production of these enzymes has historically relied on the ascomycete fungus Trichoderma reesei, which naturally secretes a highly efficient, albeit limited, suite of cellulolytic proteins. Early efforts to enhance enzyme yields focused on random mutagenesis and selection, leading to the development of hyperproducing strains such as RutC30, which emerged from irradiation of the original QM6a isolate. Subsequent research identified key regulatory mechanisms governing enzyme expression, including carbon catabolite repression mediated by the Cre1 transcription factor and the central activator XYR1, whose function is essential for the transcriptional induction of cellulase and hemicellulase genes. Techniques to increase enzyme production have included gene deletions to eliminate extracellular proteases such as SLP1 and PEP1, which degrade secreted enzymes, and the overexpression of β-glucosidase-encoding genes to compensate for the inherent deficiency in this activity within T. reesei’s native secretome. Additionally, genetic modifications to enable utilization of alternative carbon sources, such as sucrose-rich molasses, have been pursued through the heterologous expression of invertase genes from Aspergillus niger. Despite these advances, prior art strains have remained constrained by their dependence on expensive inducing substrates, suboptimal nitrogen sources, and incomplete relief of transcriptional repression. Most published strains, even those with multiple modifications, fail to achieve protein titers exceeding 40 g L−1 under industrially relevant conditions, and none have demonstrated the ability to simultaneously achieve high enzyme productivity, broad carbon source utilization, and protease deficiency in a single, publicly accessible strain derived from RutC30.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention defines a novel, genetically engineered strain of Trichoderma reesei, designated herein as CTBE_R2 and CTBE_R4, which exhibits unprecedented extracellular protein titers and enzymatic activities when cultivated under industrially scalable conditions using sugarcane molasses as the primary carbon source. This strain is the product of a precisely orchestrated series of six targeted genetic modifications introduced into the publicly available RutC30 background using CRISPR/Cas9-mediated genome editing. These modifications include the deletion of two major protease-encoding genes, slp1 and pep1, the constitutive expression of a glucose-insensitive variant of the transcriptional activator xyr1 (xyr1-V821F), the heterologous expression of a β-glucosidase gene (cel3a) from Talaromyces emersonii under a strong hemicellulase promoter, and the introduction of an invertase gene (suc1) from Aspergillus niger to enable sucrose utilization. The resulting strain is capable of achieving extracellular protein concentrations exceeding 80 g L−1 in fed-batch bioreactor fermentations without the need for inducing sugars, and it demonstrates superior saccharification efficiency compared to both its parental strain and commercially available enzyme cocktails. This strain represents a transformative advancement in microbial enzyme production, combining multiple previously reported genetic enhancements into a single, stable, and reproducible platform that is accessible to the scientific and industrial communities.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

The genetic modifications employed in the development of the novel Trichoderma reesei strain were implemented using a markerless, CRISPR/Cas9-based system that enabled precise, sequential integration of donor cassettes into specific genomic loci without the retention of selectable markers. Gene deletion was achieved by replacing target coding sequences with homologous flanking regions, thereby excising the gene while preserving the integrity of surrounding regulatory elements. Gene disruption was accomplished through the insertion of stop codons or frameshift mutations within essential coding regions to abolish protein function without altering promoter architecture. Gene overexpression was effected by placing heterologous coding sequences under the control of strong, constitutive promoters such as pdc1 and xyn1, ensuring sustained transcription regardless of carbon source availability. The rationale for these modifications was to simultaneously enhance transcriptional activation of cellulase genes, reduce proteolytic degradation of secreted enzymes, augment β-glucosidase activity, and expand substrate utilization to include sucrose-rich molasses. The RutC30 strain, which already carries a truncated cre1 allele and an 85-kb chromosomal deletion, served as the foundational host due to its proven industrial compatibility and well-documented genetic background. The xyr1 gene encodes the master transcriptional activator of cellulase and hemicellulase genes, and its V821F mutation confers resistance to glucose-mediated repression, enabling continuous enzyme production even in the presence of high glucose concentrations. The ace1 gene encodes a transcriptional repressor that inhibits cellulase expression under repressive conditions, and its deletion removes a critical bottleneck in enzyme induction. The slp1 gene encodes a subtilisin-like protease that accounts for a significant portion of proteolytic activity in the secretome, and its removal enhances the stability of secreted enzymes. The cel3a gene encodes a highly active β-glucosidase that efficiently converts cellobiose to glucose, thereby preventing product inhibition of cellulases. The suc1 gene encodes an invertase that hydrolyzes sucrose into glucose and fructose, enabling the strain to utilize the predominant sugar in molasses. Additional gene modifications include the deletion of pep1, encoding an aspartic protease, and the use of ribozyme-flanked guide RNAs to ensure precise Cas9 cleavage. Cultivation of the engineered strains was performed under fed-batch conditions in bioreactors using sugarcane molasses as the sole carbon source and either yeast extract or whole molasses-grown yeast cells as the organic nitrogen source, achieving sustained enzyme production over 14 days.

### Examples

The strain designated CTBE_R2 was generated by introducing three genetic modifications into the RutC30 background: deletion of the slp1 gene, constitutive expression of the xyr1-V821F allele under the pdc1 promoter, and deletion of the ace1 gene. This strain exhibited a threefold increase in extracellular protein titer compared to RutC30 when cultivated in lactose medium and demonstrated the ability to produce significant enzyme levels in glucose-containing media, confirming relief of carbon catabolite repression. The strain designated CTBE_R4 was derived from CTBE_R2 through the addition of two further modifications: insertion of the cel3a gene under the xyn1 promoter and replacement of the pep1 gene with the suc1 gene from Aspergillus niger. CTBE_R4 was capable of utilizing sucrose as a carbon source, producing extracellular protein titers of 80.6 g L−1 in 336 hours when cultivated in a medium containing sugarcane molasses and molasses-grown yeast cells. In direct comparison, CTBE_R2, when cultivated under identical conditions but with acid-inverted molasses, reached a maximum titer of 59.2 g L−1, demonstrating that the suc1 insertion was critical for optimal performance with native, non-inverted molasses. The enzymatic profile of CTBE_R4 showed a 72-fold increase in β-glucosidase activity and a 42-fold increase in xylanase activity compared to RutC30, while maintaining high levels of cellobiohydrolase and endoglucanase activities. Saccharification of pretreated sugarcane straw with CTBE_R4’s enzyme cocktail resulted in glucose yields equivalent to those of the commercial CTec2 enzyme preparation, with no detectable accumulation of cellobiose, indicating complete hydrolysis of oligosaccharides. The whole fermentation broth of CTBE_R4, when used without purification, retained full saccharification efficiency, demonstrating its potential for on-site enzyme production in biorefineries.
