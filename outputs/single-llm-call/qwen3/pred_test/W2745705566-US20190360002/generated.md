## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to genetic engineering tools, specifically to novel CRISPR-associated proteins derived from thermophilic microorganisms and their use in sequence-specific manipulation of nucleic acids under elevated temperature conditions. The invention encompasses isolated Cas proteins, expression vectors encoding such proteins, guide RNA molecules, ribonucleoprotein complexes formed therefrom, and methods for targeted binding, cleavage, marking, or modification of DNA or RNA sequences in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic host cells. These tools are particularly suited for applications requiring enzymatic activity across a broad thermal range, including industrial bioprocessing, metabolic engineering of thermophiles, and high-temperature molecular diagnostics. The invention further includes kits and systems for genome editing, transcriptional silencing, and nucleic acid detection that leverage the thermostable properties of the disclosed Cas proteins, enabling precise genetic modifications in organisms previously inaccessible to conventional CRISPR-Cas9 systems due to thermal instability of existing nucleases.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

The CRISPR-Cas system is an adaptive immune mechanism naturally employed by bacteria and archaea to defend against invading genetic elements such as bacteriophages and plasmids. This system is broadly classified into two major classes and six distinct types, with Class 2 systems—characterized by single-effector proteins—being the most widely adapted for genome engineering applications. Among these, the type-II CRISPR-Cas9 system has emerged as the most extensively utilized tool for targeted DNA cleavage in diverse organisms, owing to its simplicity and programmability through a guide RNA. The Cas9 endonuclease functions by forming a ribonucleoprotein complex with a chimeric single-guide RNA (sgRNA), which directs the enzyme to a complementary DNA sequence adjacent to a short protospacer adjacent motif (PAM). The PAM sequence is essential for target recognition and varies among Cas9 orthologues, with Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 (SpCas9) requiring a 5′-NGG-3′ motif. While SpCas9 has enabled transformative advances in genetic research, its utility is fundamentally constrained by its mesophilic nature; it exhibits maximal activity between 25°C and 44°C and rapidly loses structural integrity and function above 45°C, rendering it ineffective in thermophilic organisms or high-temperature bioprocesses. Although some efforts have been made to engineer SpCas9 variants with improved thermostability through directed evolution or rational design, these approaches remain labor-intensive and often compromise catalytic efficiency or specificity. Furthermore, natural Cas9 orthologues from thermophilic bacteria have remained uncharacterized for genome editing purposes, despite the evolutionary advantage conferred by thermostability in such organisms. The absence of a functional, naturally evolved Cas9 system capable of operating at temperatures above 50°C has severely limited the application of CRISPR technologies to obligate thermophiles, which are of significant industrial interest for their enhanced metabolic rates, reduced contamination risks, and suitability for consolidated bioprocessing. Additionally, the reliance on mesophilic Cas9 variants has restricted the development of robust in vitro assays, diagnostic platforms, and synthetic biology circuits that require sustained enzymatic activity under elevated thermal conditions. The need for a Cas9 nuclease with intrinsic thermostability, broad temperature tolerance, and reliable PAM recognition in thermophilic environments has thus remained unmet.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses ThermoCas9, a novel CRISPR-associated endonuclease derived from the thermophilic bacterium Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, which exhibits robust DNA cleavage activity across a temperature range extending from 20°C to 70°C. ThermoCas9 is a type-IIC Cas9 protein comprising 1082 amino acids and demonstrates functional activity both in vitro and in vivo at temperatures exceeding 55°C, a range in which conventional Cas9 orthologues are completely inactive. The invention demonstrates that ThermoCas9 requires a specific PAM sequence for target recognition, identified as 5′-NNNNCNR-3′, with optimal cleavage efficiency conferred by an adenine at the eighth position of the PAM, resulting in preferred motifs such as 5′-CNAA-3′, 5′-CMCA-3′, and 5′-CVAA-3′. The activity of ThermoCas9 is critically dependent on the structural integrity of the guide RNA, particularly the presence of three stem-loop structures within the tracrRNA-derived portion of the sgRNA; truncation of these hairpins, especially the distal stem-loop, significantly reduces cleavage efficiency at elevated temperatures. The invention further establishes that ThermoCas9 forms a stable ribonucleoprotein complex with its cognate sgRNA, which confers exceptional thermostability, allowing the complex to retain activity even after pre-incubation at 70°C for 10 minutes. ThermoCas9 is dependent on divalent cations for catalytic function, with magnesium and manganese supporting optimal double-stranded DNA cleavage, while calcium supports only nicking activity. The invention provides methods for modifying genetic material in eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells by integrating a polynucleotide encoding ThermoCas9 into the host genome or by transforming the cell with an expression vector comprising ThermoCas9 under the control of a constitutive or inducible promoter, in combination with a guide RNA and a repair oligonucleotide to facilitate homologous recombination. The repair oligonucleotide may be single-stranded or double-stranded and may be designed to achieve targeted deletions, insertions, or point mutations. The invention further encompasses catalytically inactive variants of ThermoCas9, generated by mutation of conserved residues in the HNH and RuvC nuclease domains, which retain sequence-specific DNA binding capability and are employed for transcriptional repression, gene silencing, and visualization of genomic loci when fused to functional moieties such as fluorescent proteins or transcriptional repressors. The invention includes expression vectors comprising ThermoCas9 under the control of promoters such as the xylL promoter, the pta promoter, or the Pm promoter, and further includes kits comprising purified ThermoCas9 protein, synthetic sgRNAs, repair templates, and buffers optimized for activity across the full thermal range. The invention further provides isolated polypeptides of ThermoCas9, including fragments retaining nuclease or DNA-binding activity, with at least 85% sequence identity to SEQ ID NO: 1, and encompasses nucleic acids encoding such polypeptides, including those comprising conservative amino acid substitutions that preserve thermostability and function. The invention further defines the PAM sequences required for binding, cleavage, marking, or modification of target nucleic acids by ThermoCas9 at temperatures ranging from 30°C to 70°C, and specifies the structural requirements of the guide RNA for optimal activity under these conditions. The invention enables genome editing in thermophilic bacteria such as Bacillus smithii, Geobacillus thermoglucosidans, and Pseudomonas putida, and further provides methods for silencing gene expression in these organisms using catalytically inactive ThermoCas9 variants.

## RNA Guides and Target Sequences

The invention provides guide RNA molecules specifically configured for use with ThermoCas9, wherein the guide RNA comprises a spacer sequence complementary to a target nucleic acid and a scaffold sequence derived from the tracrRNA of Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, which includes at least two stem-loop structures and a tetranucleotide loop at the 3′ end. The spacer sequence is between 18 and 25 nucleotides in length, with optimal cleavage efficiency observed at 20–23 nucleotides. The scaffold sequence contains a 5′-GAAA-3′ linker connecting the spacer to the tracrRNA-derived portion, and the tracrRNA portion includes three predicted stem-loop structures whose integrity is essential for maintaining ThermoCas9 stability at temperatures above 55°C. The invention further defines the target nucleic acid as double-stranded DNA containing a protospacer sequence complementary to the spacer region of the guide RNA, immediately adjacent to a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) of the sequence 5′-NNNNCNR-3′, wherein the eighth nucleotide of the PAM is preferably adenine. The PAM sequence may be selected from the group consisting of 5′-CNAA-3′, 5′-CMCA-3′, 5′-CVAA-3′, and 5′-CCCA-3′, depending on the operational temperature, with 5′-CNAA-3′ and 5′-CMCA-3′ being optimal at 30–55°C and 5′-CVAA-3′ and 5′-CCCA-3′ being preferred at temperatures below 30°C. The invention further specifies that the target nucleic acid strand complementary to the spacer sequence is referred to as the target strand, while the non-complementary strand is termed the non-target strand, and that ThermoCas9 binds and cleaves the target strand at a position three nucleotides upstream of the PAM. The guide RNA may be delivered as a chimeric single-guide RNA or as separate crRNA and tracrRNA molecules, and the ribonucleoprotein complex formed between ThermoCas9 and the guide RNA exhibits a dissociation constant (Kd) for target DNA of less than 10 nM at 55°C. The invention further provides methods for designing guide RNAs based on genomic sequence information, wherein the spacer sequence is selected to minimize off-target binding by avoiding homology with non-target sequences possessing similar PAM motifs. The invention further describes the use of ThermoCas9 to detect single-nucleotide polymorphisms in target DNA by exploiting its reduced tolerance for mismatches at positions 4, 5, and 10 of the protospacer, thereby enabling high-fidelity discrimination of closely related alleles under elevated temperature conditions.

### Binding, Cleavage, Marking and Modifying Temperatures

The invention provides methods for binding, cleaving, marking, or modifying target nucleic acids using ThermoCas9 across a temperature range extending from 20°C to 70°C, with optimal activity observed between 37°C and 65°C. At temperatures below 30°C, ThermoCas9 exhibits stringent PAM specificity, cleaving only substrates containing the preferred PAM sequences 5′-CVAA-3′ and 5′-CCCA-3′, thereby reducing off-target effects in low-temperature applications. At temperatures between 37°C and 60°C, ThermoCas9 cleaves a broader spectrum of PAM variants including 5′-CNAA-3′ and 5′-CMCA-3′, enabling greater targeting flexibility for genome editing. The invention further demonstrates that ThermoCas9 retains full cleavage activity after pre-incubation at 70°C for 10 minutes when complexed with its cognate sgRNA, whereas the apo-protein denatures at temperatures above 65°C. The tracrRNA scaffold must retain all three stem-loop structures for maximal cleavage efficiency at elevated temperatures; deletion of the distal stem-loop reduces activity by more than 70% at 65°C, and removal of both the middle and distal stem-loops abolishes detectable cleavage. The invention further specifies that the sgRNA may be modified to include one, two, or three stem-loop structures, with the full-length scaffold containing three stem-loops being preferred for applications requiring maximum thermostability. The invention further provides that the spacer length may be varied between 18 and 25 nucleotides, with 20–23 nucleotides yielding the highest cleavage efficiency. The invention further describes that the use of a synthetic linker between the crRNA and tracrRNA portions of the sgRNA, such as the 5′-GAAA-3′ sequence, enhances complex stability and cleavage kinetics. The invention further specifies that the cleavage activity of ThermoCas9 is not detectable on single-stranded DNA substrates, confirming its specificity for double-stranded DNA. The invention further establishes that the binding, cleavage, marking, and modifying activities of ThermoCas9 are maintained in the presence of physiological concentrations of divalent cations, with magnesium and manganese supporting full nuclease activity, while calcium supports only nicking activity. The invention further provides that the temperature range for binding is 20–70°C, for cleavage is 25–70°C, for marking is 20–70°C, and for modifying is 25–70°C, with all activities being significantly enhanced at temperatures above 45°C compared to mesophilic Cas9 orthologues.

### Functional Moieties

The invention provides ThermoCas9 polypeptides fused or linked to functional moieties that confer additional biochemical properties without compromising target specificity or thermostability. Such functional moieties include fluorescent proteins such as GFP, mCherry, or mNeonGreen, which enable visualization of genomic loci in live cells; transcriptional repressor domains such as KRAB, SID, or Mxi1, which mediate gene silencing when fused to catalytically inactive ThermoCas9; DNA methyltransferases, which introduce epigenetic modifications at target sites; and base editors or deaminases, which enable direct nucleotide conversion without inducing double-strand breaks. The invention further provides that the functional moiety may be fused to the N-terminus, C-terminus, or internal loop region of ThermoCas9 via a flexible peptide linker of 5–20 amino acids in length, and that such fusions retain full DNA-binding activity and thermostability up to 70°C. The invention further encompasses the use of ThermoCas9 fused to a marker protein such as β-galactosidase or luciferase for reporter-based detection of successful targeting events. The invention further provides that the fusion of ThermoCas9 to a FokI nuclease domain enables dimerization-dependent cleavage, thereby increasing targeting specificity and reducing off-target effects. The invention further describes the use of ThermoCas9 fused to a helicase domain to facilitate strand displacement during genome editing in tightly packed chromatin or nucleoid structures. The invention further provides that the fusion of ThermoCas9 to a recombinase or integrase enables targeted insertion of large DNA fragments without reliance on homologous recombination.

### Nuclease Activity

The invention provides a ribonucleoprotein complex comprising ThermoCas9 and a guide RNA, wherein the complex exhibits sequence-specific nuclease activity directed toward double-stranded DNA targets bearing a compatible PAM sequence. The nuclease activity is mediated by the HNH and RuvC catalytic domains of ThermoCas9, which cleave the target and non-target DNA strands, respectively, generating a blunt-ended double-strand break located three nucleotides upstream of the PAM. The invention further provides that the nuclease activity of ThermoCas9 is temperature-dependent, with maximal cleavage rates observed between 55°C and 65°C, and that the complex remains catalytically active following prolonged incubation at 70°C. The invention further describes methods for inactivating the nuclease activity of ThermoCas9 by introducing point mutations in conserved catalytic residues, specifically D8A and H582A, which result in a catalytically dead variant (dThermoCas9) that retains full DNA-binding capacity. The invention further provides that the dThermoCas9 variant may be used for transcriptional repression, chromatin remodeling, or fluorescent labeling of genomic loci when fused to functional moieties. The invention further describes the generation of a nickase variant of ThermoCas9 by inactivating either the HNH or RuvC domain, enabling single-strand breaks that can be exploited for base editing or to reduce off-target mutagenesis. The invention further provides that the nuclease activity of ThermoCas9 is not inhibited by the presence of RNA or single-stranded DNA, and that the complex does not cleave RNA substrates. The invention further specifies that the nuclease activity is dependent on the presence of magnesium or manganese ions, and that chelation of divalent cations with EDTA abolishes cleavage. The invention further provides that the nuclease activity of ThermoCas9 may be attenuated by altering the guide RNA structure, reducing the concentration of the complex, or by modulating the temperature of the reaction environment. The invention further describes the use of ThermoCas9 in diagnostic assays for the detection of nucleic acid sequences, wherein cleavage of a labeled substrate is monitored by fluorescence or gel electrophoresis. The invention further provides that the catalytically inactive ThermoCas9 may be used in conjunction with a fluorescent reporter to visualize target loci in real time within living cells.

## Expression Vectors

The invention provides expression vectors comprising a polynucleotide encoding ThermoCas9 operably linked to a promoter capable of driving expression in prokaryotic or eukaryotic host cells. The promoter may be constitutive, such as the pta promoter from Bacillus coagulans, or inducible, such as the xylL promoter from Bacillus smithii or the Pm promoter from Pseudomonas putida, which is activated by 3-methylbenzoate. The expression vector further comprises a regulatory element for the expression of a guide RNA, which may be transcribed from a separate promoter or co-transcribed with ThermoCas9 as a single polycistronic transcript. The vector may further comprise a homologous recombination template flanked by sequences homologous to the target locus, enabling targeted gene deletion, insertion, or replacement upon Cas9-mediated cleavage. The invention further provides that the expression vector may be a plasmid, a viral vector, or a synthetic chromosome, and that it may be designed for replication in Escherichia coli for cloning purposes and for transfer into thermophilic or mesophilic hosts. The invention further specifies that the vector may be delivered as a DNA construct, as an mRNA molecule encoding ThermoCas9, or as a pre-assembled ribonucleoprotein complex. The invention further provides that the expression vector may include a selectable marker such as an antibiotic resistance gene, a fluorescent protein, or a metabolic complementation gene to facilitate selection of transformed cells. The invention further describes the use of adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors for delivery of ThermoCas9 and guide RNA into eukaryotic cells, including fungal, plant, and animal cells. The invention further provides that the expression vector may be engineered to express multiple guide RNAs targeting distinct genomic loci simultaneously, enabling multiplexed genome editing. The invention further describes the use of self-cleaving ribozymes or tRNA sequences to process polycistronic transcripts into individual guide RNAs. The invention further provides that the expression vector may include a temperature-sensitive origin of replication, enabling control of vector copy number through temperature shifts. The invention further specifies that the vector may be designed for integration into the host genome via homologous recombination or transposon-mediated insertion, ensuring stable inheritance of the editing machinery.

## Host Cells

The invention provides transformed host cells comprising a polynucleotide encoding ThermoCas9 or a catalytically inactive variant thereof, operably linked to a guide RNA expression cassette, wherein the host cell is selected from the group consisting of thermophilic bacteria, mesophilic bacteria, fungi, yeast, plant cells, protists, and animal cells, excluding human cells. The invention further specifies that the host cell may be a thermophilic bacterium belonging to the genera Geobacillus, Bacillus, Clostridium, or Thermus, including Geobacillus thermodenitrificans, Bacillus smithii, Geobacillus thermoglucosidans, and Thermus thermophilus. The invention further provides that the host cell may be a mesophilic bacterium such as Escherichia coli, Pseudomonas putida, or Pseudomonas aeruginosa, wherein ThermoCas9 enables efficient genome editing at temperatures up to 37°C with reduced off-target effects compared to SpCas9. The invention further encompasses fungal cells including Aspergillus species, Candida species, Yarrowia lipolytica, and Penicillium species, wherein ThermoCas9 facilitates targeted gene disruption or insertion under elevated temperature culture conditions. The invention further provides that the host cell may be a thermophilic yeast such as Kluyveromyces marxianus or Hansenula polymorpha, wherein ThermoCas9 enables genome editing at temperatures above 45°C. The invention further specifies that the host cell may be a plant cell, wherein ThermoCas9 is expressed under the control of a heat-inducible promoter to enable temperature-controlled genome editing. The invention further provides that the host cell may be a protist or animal cell, wherein ThermoCas9 is delivered as a ribonucleoprotein complex to minimize off-target effects and immune recognition. The invention further describes the use of the transformed host cell for industrial bioproduction of enzymes, biofuels, or specialty chemicals under elevated temperature conditions, wherein the genome has been modified to enhance metabolic flux, substrate utilization, or stress tolerance. The invention further provides that the host cell may be cultured at temperatures between 30°C and 70°C, with optimal growth and editing efficiency occurring between 50°C and 60°C.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1: Isolation of Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

Geobacillus thermodenitrificans strain T12 was isolated from a compost sample collected in the Netherlands and maintained in liquid culture at 65°C in LB2 medium supplemented with chloramphenicol. The strain was identified by 16S rRNA sequencing and confirmed to be a moderately thermophilic, Gram-positive bacterium with an optimal growth temperature of 65°C and a maximum growth temperature of 70°C. Genomic DNA was extracted using a modified phenol-chloroform protocol and subjected to whole-genome sequencing using Illumina HiSeq technology. The assembled genome was annotated using Prokka and BLAST-based homology searches, revealing the presence of a type-IIC CRISPR-Cas system encoding a Cas9 protein of 1082 amino acids, designated ThermoCas9.

### Example 2: Defining the Essential Consensus Sequences for Cas9 in Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

A database search of the NCBI non-redundant protein database using the ThermoCas9 sequence as query identified 17 highly homologous Cas9 orthologues from other Geobacillus and Bacillus species, with amino acid identities ranging from 87% to 99%. Multiple sequence alignment using Clustal Omega revealed conserved motifs in the REC, HNH, and RuvC domains, with the HNH domain containing the conserved His-Asn-His triad and the RuvC domain containing the conserved DEDx motif. Phylogenetic analysis using MEGA7 confirmed that these Cas9 orthologues form a distinct clade separate from mesophilic Cas9 variants, suggesting evolutionary adaptation to high-temperature environments.

### Example 3: Identifying Core Amino Acid Motifs which are Essential for the Function of CAS9 and Those which Confer Thermostability in Thermophilic Cas9 Nucleases

Protein sequence alignment of ThermoCas9 with SpCas9 and other thermophilic Cas9 orthologues identified 12 residues uniquely conserved in the thermophilic clade but absent in mesophilic variants. These residues were located primarily in the REC lobe and the linker region between the REC and NUC domains. Homology modeling using Phyre2 revealed that these residues form a dense hydrophobic core and additional salt bridges that stabilize the tertiary structure at elevated temperatures. Site-directed mutagenesis of these residues in ThermoCas9 resulted in loss of activity at 60°C, confirming their role in thermostability.

### Example 4: Determination of the PAM Sequence of G. thermodenitrificans gtCas9

CRISPR array analysis of the G. thermodenitrificans T12 genome identified 10 spacers flanked by conserved repeat sequences. A tracrRNA was predicted using CRISPRdirect, and a chimeric sgRNA was designed by fusing the first spacer with the predicted tracrRNA via a 5′-GAAA-3′ linker. A plasmid library containing randomized 7-nucleotide PAM sequences was constructed and used in an in vitro cleavage assay with purified ThermoCas9 and the sgRNA. Deep sequencing of cleaved fragments revealed a consensus PAM of 5′-NNNNCNR-3′, with a strong preference for cytosine at positions 1, 3, 4, and 6, and adenine at position 8.

### Example 5: Target Generation with Randomized PAM

A 122-bp DNA fragment containing the protospacer sequence was amplified by PCR and ligated into the pNW33n vector upstream of a 7-nucleotide degenerate region. The resulting plasmid library was transformed into E. coli DH10B, and plasmids were isolated and linearized with SapI for use in cleavage assays. The library contained over 10⁶ unique PAM variants, enabling comprehensive PAM profiling.

### Example 6: In Vitro Determination of PAM Sequences for gtCas9

Purified ThermoCas9 was incubated with the PAM library and sgRNA at 55°C for one hour. Cleaved fragments were gel-purified, A-tailed with Taq polymerase, and sequenced using Illumina HiSeq. Analysis of over 500,000 reads revealed a clear enrichment of sequences containing 5′-CNNNCNR-3′ motifs, with adenine at position 8 being the most frequent. The most abundant PAM sequences were 5′-CCCA-3′, 5′-CNAA-3′, and 5′-CMCA-3′.

### Example 7: In Silico PAM Prediction for gtCas9

The 10 spacers from the G. thermodenitrificans T12 CRISPR array were used to search for protospacers in viral and plasmid databases using CRISPRtarget. Two hits were identified in phage genomes, both of which were flanked by PAM sequences containing cytosine at position 5 and adenine at position 8, corroborating the in vitro findings.

### Example 8: Determination of 8 Nucleotide Long PAM Sequences for gtCas9

A set of 16 DNA substrates containing 5′-CCCCCNNA-3′ PAM variants was synthesized and tested in cleavage assays at temperatures ranging from 20°C to 65°C. Cleavage efficiency was highest at 55°C for substrates with 5′-CNAA-3′ and 5′-CMCA-3′ PAMs. At 30°C, only 5′-CVAA-3′ and 5′-CCCA-3′ were cleaved, demonstrating temperature-dependent PAM stringency.

### Example 9: In Vivo Genome Editing of Bacillus smithii ET138 with gtCas9 and 8 Nucleotide Length PAM Sequences

A single-plasmid editing construct was assembled containing the thermocas9 gene under the xylL promoter, a sgRNA targeting the pyrF gene, and homologous recombination flanks of 1 kb upstream and downstream of pyrF. The construct was transformed into B. smithii ET138 at 55°C, and transformants were screened by colony PCR. Ten colonies were analyzed, and one exhibited a clean deletion of pyrF, confirming in vivo activity of ThermoCas9 at 55°C.

### Example 10: ThermoCas9-Based Gene Deletion in Pseudomonas putida

A plasmid containing ThermoCas9 under the Pm promoter, a sgRNA targeting pyrF, and homologous recombination flanks was transformed into P. putida KT2440. Expression was induced with 3-methylbenzoate, and colonies were screened for pyrF deletion. Fifty percent of induced colonies exhibited clean deletions, demonstrating efficient editing in a mesophilic host at 37°C.

### Example 11: ThermoCas9-Based Gene Silencing

A catalytically inactive ThermoCas9 variant (D8A, H582A) was expressed under the xylL promoter with a sgRNA targeting the ldhL gene in B. smithii. RT-qPCR revealed a 70% reduction in ldhL transcript levels, and HPLC analysis showed a 40% reduction in lactate production, confirming effective transcriptional repression.

### Example 12: ThermoCas9 Expression and Purification

ThermoCas9 was cloned into pML-1B with an N-terminal hexahistidine tag and expressed in E. coli Rosetta (DE3). Cells were lysed, and the protein was purified by Ni-NTA affinity chromatography, followed by ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography. SDS-PAGE confirmed a single band at 126 kDa, consistent with the predicted molecular weight.

### Example 13: In Vitro Synthesis of sgRNA

The sgRNA scaffold was synthesized as a DNA template under the T7 promoter and transcribed in vitro using the HiScribe T7 High Yield RNA Synthesis Kit. The RNA was purified by urea-PAGE and ethanol precipitation, yielding high-purity sgRNA suitable for biochemical assays.

### Example 14: In Vitro Cleavage Assay

ThermoCas9, sgRNA, and target DNA were pre-incubated separately at 55°C for 10 minutes, then combined in cleavage buffer containing 25 mM MgCl₂. After one hour, reactions were analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis, showing complete cleavage of the target DNA.

### Example 15: Library Construction for In Vitro PAM Screen

A 122-bp fragment containing the protospacer and a 7-nucleotide degenerate PAM was ligated into pNW33n and transformed into E. coli DH10B. Plasmids were isolated, linearized with SapI, and used as substrates in cleavage assays.

### Example 16: PAM Screening Assay

Cleaved fragments from the PAM library were gel-purified, ligated to Illumina adaptors, and sequenced. Reads with perfect matches to the reference were analyzed using WebLogo, revealing the 5′-NNNNCNR-3′ consensus.

### Example 17: Library Construction for In Vitro Mismatch Tolerance Screen

Twenty pairs of complementary oligonucleotides containing single or double mismatches in the protospacer were annealed, ligated into pNW33n, and transformed into E. coli DH5α. Plasmids were isolated and used in cleavage assays at 37°C and 55°C.

### Example 18: B. smithii and P. putida Editing and Silencing Constructs

Plasmids were assembled using NEBuilder HiFi DNA Assembly. The pThermoCas9_bsΔpyrF1 construct contained the thermocas9 gene under the xylL promoter, a sgRNA targeting pyrF, and 1 kb homology arms. The pThermoCas9_ppΔpyrF construct contained ThermoCas9 under the Pm promoter and 0.5 kb homology arms.

### Example 19: Editing Protocol for P. putida

Electrocompetent P. putida KT2440 was transformed with pThermoCas9_ppΔpyrF, and integrants were selected on kanamycin. Induction with 3-methylbenzoate led to 50% deletion efficiency, confirmed by colony PCR.

### Example 20: RNA Isolation

RNA was extracted from B. smithii cultures using phenol-chloroform extraction and zirconia bead disruption. DNase treatment was performed, and RNA integrity was confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis.

### Example 21: Quantification of mRNA by RT-qPCR

cDNA was synthesized from RNA using SuperScript III, and qPCR was performed using primers specific for ldhL and rpoD. Relative expression was calculated using the ΔΔCt method, confirming significant downregulation in silenced strains.

### Example 22: High-Pressure Liquid Chromatography

Lactate concentrations in culture supernatants were quantified using an Aminex HPX-87H column and refractive index detection. Silenced cultures showed a 40% reduction in lactate production compared to controls.

Plasmids expressing ThermoCas9 and ThermodCas9 with corresponding sgRNAs have been deposited with Addgene under accession numbers #100981 and #100982.
