# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical chemistry and gastroprotective therapeutics, specifically to novel tryptamine-antioxidant hybrid compounds designed for the prevention and treatment of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)-induced gastropathy. These compounds are engineered to target mitochondrial oxidative stress, a central mechanism underlying NSAID-mediated gastric mucosal injury, by simultaneously scavenging reactive oxygen species and chelating intramitochondrial iron. The invention encompasses a new class of small-molecule therapeutics that act through a dual mechanism of action—antioxidant activity and iron chelation—to restore mitochondrial integrity, inhibit apoptosis, and accelerate mucosal healing in the gastric epithelium. The compounds are particularly useful in clinical settings where conventional gastroprotective agents such as proton pump inhibitors and H2 receptor antagonists are either ineffective, associated with significant adverse effects, or contribute to secondary complications including bacterial overgrowth and intestinal dysbiosis.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are among the most widely prescribed medications globally for the management of pain, inflammation, and fever. Despite their therapeutic efficacy, chronic or even short-term use of NSAIDs is consistently associated with a high incidence of gastrointestinal toxicity, manifesting as gastric mucosal erosion, ulceration, bleeding, and perforation—a condition collectively termed NSAID-induced gastropathy. This pathology arises not primarily from acid-mediated damage, but from direct mitochondrial dysfunction triggered by NSAIDs, particularly through inhibition of the electron transport chain and subsequent overproduction of reactive oxygen species. The accumulation of reactive oxygen species within gastric mucosal mitochondria leads to oxidative damage of lipids, proteins, and DNA, resulting in loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, impaired respiratory function, and activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. Iron, released from damaged iron-sulfur clusters in mitochondrial enzymes such as aconitase, further amplifies oxidative stress via the Fenton reaction, generating highly destructive hydroxyl radicals. This iron-driven amplification loop is a critical but previously underappreciated component of NSAID-induced mucosal injury. Serotonin, a naturally occurring monoamine, has been shown to penetrate mitochondria and modulate redox balance; however, its free form exhibits neurotoxic and pro-apoptotic properties at elevated concentrations, limiting its therapeutic utility. Existing gastroprotective strategies primarily rely on acid suppression through proton pump inhibitors or histamine H2 receptor blockers. While these agents reduce gastric acidity and provide symptomatic relief, they do not address the root cause of NSAID-induced mitochondrial pathology. Moreover, prolonged use of proton pump inhibitors is linked to an increased risk of enteric infections, including *Clostridioides difficile*, as well as nutrient malabsorption, hypomagnesemia, and an elevated incidence of gastric and intestinal neoplasia. H2 receptor antagonists, though less potent, are similarly ineffective against mucosal apoptosis and mitochondrial dysfunction. Furthermore, classical antioxidants such as vitamin E, quercetin, and gallic acid lack mitochondrial targeting capacity and demonstrate poor bioavailability, rendering them clinically inadequate. Iron chelators like desferrioxamine, while effective in vitro, are too large and polar to penetrate mitochondrial membranes efficiently and are associated with systemic toxicity when administered chronically. There remains a critical unmet medical need for a safe, orally bioavailable, mitochondrially targeted therapeutic agent that directly interrupts the cascade of oxidative stress, iron-mediated radical generation, and apoptosis in gastric epithelial cells without disrupting gastric acid homeostasis or promoting microbial dysbiosis.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

It is an objective of the present invention to provide a novel class of small-molecule compounds characterized by a general formula 1, wherein a tryptamine scaffold is covalently linked via an amide bond to a polyphenolic antioxidant moiety, thereby creating a bifunctional molecule capable of mitochondrial penetration, reactive oxygen species scavenging, and intramitochondrial iron chelation. Another objective is to establish a new chemical scaffold that combines the mitochondrial tropism of tryptamine derivatives with the potent antioxidant and iron-chelating properties of hydroxylated aromatic acids, resulting in synergistic gastroprotective activity. A further objective is to provide compounds that exhibit superior efficacy compared to existing gastroprotective agents, including proton pump inhibitors, H2 receptor antagonists, and standalone antioxidants or iron chelators, while avoiding their associated adverse effects. It is also an objective to develop a compound that not only prevents the onset of NSAID-induced gastropathy but also actively promotes the healing of pre-existing mucosal lesions by restoring mitochondrial function and suppressing apoptosis. The invention further aims to provide a pharmacologically viable compound with a low effective dose, favorable pharmacokinetic profile, and demonstrated stability in physiological environments, enabling its translation into clinical use for patients requiring long-term NSAID therapy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compounds of general formula 1, wherein a tryptamine core is conjugated at the primary amine to a substituted benzoic acid or cinnamic acid derivative bearing multiple hydroxyl groups, thereby forming a hybrid molecule with dual antioxidant and iron-chelating functionalities. Representative compounds include SEGA (3a), a tryptamine-gallic acid amide conjugate, and its structural analogs such as TRGA (3b), MEGA (3c), and GASA (4d), each exhibiting enhanced mitochondrial targeting and redox-modulating properties. These compounds demonstrate potent antioxidant activity as measured by ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) and 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical scavenging assays, with SEGA (3a) showing the highest activity among all synthesized derivatives. The compounds are capable of chelating free iron within mitochondria, thereby inhibiting Fenton chemistry and preventing the formation of hydroxyl radicals. They preserve mitochondrial membrane potential, restore respiratory control ratio, and maintain dehydrogenase activity in the presence of NSAIDs. Furthermore, these compounds inhibit the activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3, suppress mitochondrial cytochrome c release, and downregulate pro-apoptotic bax expression while upregulating anti-apoptotic bcl-2, thereby blocking the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising at least one compound of general formula 1 in association with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for oral or parenteral administration. A therapeutically effective amount of the compound ranges from 1 to 100 mg per kilogram of body weight, with an ED50 value of approximately 6.9 mg/kg in rodent models of NSAID-induced gastropathy. The compound may be administered orally, intraperitoneally, or via other systemic routes, and is stable in serum and physiological saline, with demonstrated mitochondrial uptake in vivo. The invention further encompasses methods for treating gastropathy by administering the compound to a subject in need thereof, thereby preventing or reversing NSAID-induced mitochondrial oxidative stress, mucosal apoptosis, and epithelial barrier disruption.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The compounds of the invention are synthesized through a multi-step organic transformation involving the coupling of substituted tryptamine derivatives with hydroxylated carboxylic acids via amide bond formation using 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) as a coupling agent, followed by deprotection of benzyl protecting groups through catalytic hydrogenolysis. The synthesis of SEGA (3a) is achieved by reacting 5-hydroxytryptamine with gallic acid under standard EDC-mediated amide coupling conditions, yielding a stable amide linkage that preserves the catechol structure of gallic acid and the indole ring of serotonin. TRGA (3b) and MEGA (3c) are synthesized similarly by substituting 5-hydroxytryptamine with tryptamine and 5-methoxytryptamine, respectively, to evaluate the influence of the 5-hydroxyl group on biological activity. GASA (4d) is prepared by esterifying 5-hydroxytryptamine with gallic acid, demonstrating that the amide linkage confers superior stability and activity compared to the ester analog. The 5-hydroxy derivative 5c is synthesized by selective deprotection of a benzyl-protected precursor, while the dimeric serotonin derivative 6c is prepared through oxidative coupling to assess whether dimerization enhances mitochondrial targeting. The antioxidant activity of these derivatives is systematically evaluated using FRAP and DPPH assays, revealing that SEGA (3a) exhibits the highest reducing capacity and radical scavenging potency, attributable to the synergistic electron-donating effects of the 5-hydroxyindole and trihydroxybenzoic acid moieties. In vitro and in vivo studies confirm that SEGA (3a) significantly reduces lipid peroxidation, protein carbonylation, and thiol depletion in gastric mucosal mitochondria exposed to indomethacin. Histological analysis demonstrates restoration of gastric mucosal architecture, with reduced epithelial shedding and neutrophil infiltration. SEGA (3a) is shown to penetrate mitochondria in vivo, as confirmed by radiolabeling with technetium-99m and quantification of mitochondrial uptake. The compound effectively chelates intramitochondrial iron, as demonstrated by suppression of Phen Green SK fluorescence and inhibition of Fe(II)-TPTZ complex formation. SEGA (3a) restores mitochondrial membrane potential, as measured by JC-1 dye aggregation, and rescues complex I-mediated oxygen consumption. It significantly reduces caspase-9 and caspase-3 activation and decreases TUNEL-positive apoptotic cells in gastric mucosa. The gastroprotective effect of SEGA (3a) is further validated in a healing model, wherein administration after NSAID-induced injury accelerates mucosal repair, achieving near-complete restoration within 20 hours. The compound demonstrates no significant toxicity in acute or subchronic animal studies and exhibits favorable stability in biological matrices.

### Biological Effects

The antioxidant properties of the tryptamine derivatives are quantified using the ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) assay, which measures the ability of compounds to reduce Fe(III)-TPTZ to its blue Fe(II) complex, with absorbance monitored at 595 nm. SEGA (3a) exhibits a FRAP value significantly higher than gallic acid, serotonin, and other analogs, indicating superior electron-donating capacity. The DPPH radical scavenging assay confirms concentration-dependent quenching of free radicals, with SEGA (3a) achieving 92% scavenging at 100 μM. Histological studies of gastric tissue reveal that SEGA (3a) pretreatment prevents NSAID-induced flattening of gastric epithelium, loss of mucous neck cells, and submucosal hemorrhage. Other tryptamine derivatives, including TRGA (3b) and MEGA (3c), show reduced efficacy, underscoring the necessity of the 5-hydroxy group on the indole ring for optimal activity. Serotonin alone exacerbates oxidative stress at high concentrations, but its conjugation with gallic acid neutralizes its toxicity while retaining mitochondrial penetration. SEGA (3a) rescues NSAID-damaged gastric mucosa by restoring mitochondrial transmembrane potential, as evidenced by JC-1 fluorescence ratio normalization, and by increasing mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity measured via MTT reduction. It prevents oxidative stress-induced apoptosis by suppressing caspase-9 and caspase-3 activation and reducing DNA fragmentation, as confirmed by TUNEL and immunohistochemistry for active caspase-3. SEGA (3a) restores mitochondrial dysfunction by reversing the decline in respiratory control ratio and preventing thiol depletion and lipid peroxidation. It chelates intramitochondrial iron, as demonstrated by decreased Phen Green SK fluorescence and inhibition of Fe(II)-TPTZ complex formation in spectrophotometric assays. Fluorescence microscopy confirms co-localization of SEGA (3a) with mitochondria, validating its targeted delivery. The compound’s efficacy is further corroborated by its ability to reduce hemoglobin leakage into the gastric lumen, indicating preservation of mucosal barrier integrity.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A general EDC coupling procedure for the formation of amides is employed to synthesize the tryptamine-gallic acid hybrids. To a solution of 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid (12 mmol) and 5-hydroxytryptamine hydrochloride (14.4 mmol) in N,N-dimethylformamide (15 mL), 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (12 mmol), triethylamine (72 mmol), and EDC hydrochloride (14.4 mmol) are added sequentially at 0°C under nitrogen atmosphere. The reaction mixture is stirred at room temperature for 18 hours, monitored by thin-layer chromatography until complete consumption of the starting materials. The reaction is quenched by addition of ice-cold water (50 mL) and extracted with ethyl acetate (3 × 50 mL). The combined organic layers are washed with saturated brine, dried over anhydrous sodium sulfate, filtered, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product is purified by column chromatography on silica gel using a gradient of ethyl acetate in hexane to yield the protected amide derivative as a white solid.

### Example 2

A general procedure for de-benzylation via catalytic hydrogenolysis is performed to remove benzyl protecting groups. A mixture of the benzyl-protected tryptamine-gallic acid derivative (1 mmol) and 10% palladium on carbon (0.1 g) in a 5:1 mixture of methanol and chloroform (10 mL) is placed in a hydrogenation apparatus and stirred under a hydrogen atmosphere at 40 psi for 2 hours. The reaction mixture is filtered through a pad of Celite to remove the catalyst, and the filtrate is concentrated under reduced pressure to afford the deprotected compound as a crystalline solid, which is washed with cold diethyl ether and dried under vacuum.

### Example 3

Benzyl 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoate (1a) is prepared by esterification of 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid with benzyl alcohol using DMAP and DCC. The acid (1b) is obtained by hydrolysis of the ester under basic conditions. Compound 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)-N-(2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)benzamide is synthesized by EDC coupling of 1b with 5-hydroxytryptamine, followed by hydrogenolysis to yield 3,4,5-trihydroxy-N-(2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)benzamide (SEGA, 3a). The compound is characterized by ¹H NMR and ¹³C NMR spectroscopy, high-resolution mass spectrometry, and melting point determination, confirming the molecular structure and purity.

### Example 4

(E)-N-(2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)-3-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acrylamide is synthesized by coupling 5-hydroxytryptamine with 4-hydroxycinnamic acid using EDC and HOBt in DMF, followed by purification by flash chromatography. The product is characterized by NMR and LC-MS, confirming the trans-configuration and molecular weight.

### Example 5

3-((2-((1H-indol-3-yl)methylamino)ethyl)-1H-indol-5-ol is prepared by reductive amination of 5-hydroxytryptamine with indole-3-acetaldehyde using sodium cyanoborohydride, followed by purification and characterization.

### Example 6

N-(2-(1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)-3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzamide is synthesized by coupling 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid with tryptamine, followed by hydrogenolysis to yield N-(2-(1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)-3,4,5-trihydroxybenzamide. The compound is characterized by NMR and elemental analysis.

### Example 7

(E)-N-(2-(1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)-3-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acrylamide is prepared by coupling tryptamine with 4-hydroxycinnamic acid under standard EDC conditions, purified by preparative HPLC, and characterized by NMR and mass spectrometry.

### Example 8

N-((1H-indol-3-yl)methyl)-2-(1H-indol-3-yl)ethanamine is synthesized by reductive amination of tryptamine with indole-3-acetaldehyde, followed by purification and characterization.

### Example 9

Compound 2g is synthesized by coupling 5-methoxytryptamine with gallic acid, followed by deprotection. Compound 3c is synthesized by coupling 5-methoxytryptamine with gallic acid. Compound 2h is synthesized by coupling tryptamine with 4-hydroxycinnamic acid. Compound 2i is synthesized by coupling 5-hydroxytryptamine with indole-3-acetic acid. Compound 4d is synthesized by esterification of 5-hydroxytryptamine with gallic acid. Compound 4b and 4c are synthesized by varying the substitution pattern on the phenyl ring. All compounds are characterized by ¹H NMR, ¹³C NMR, HRMS, and melting point. FRAP and DPPH assays are performed on all compounds, with SEGA (3a) showing the highest activity. In vivo studies are conducted in Sprague-Dawley rats, where gastropathy is induced by oral administration of indomethacin (48 mg/kg). Test compounds are administered intraperitoneally 30 minutes prior to indomethacin. Ulcer index is calculated based on lesion length and severity. Histological sections are stained with hematoxylin and eosin and evaluated for epithelial integrity, inflammatory infiltration, and hemorrhage. Mitochondria are isolated from gastric mucosa and assessed for lipid peroxidation, protein carbonyls, total thiol content, and mitochondrial membrane potential. Caspase-9 and caspase-3 activities are measured colorimetrically. TUNEL staining is performed on tissue sections to quantify apoptosis. SEGA (3a) demonstrates superior gastroprotective activity with an ED50 of 6.9 mg/kg. Radiolabeling with 99mTc confirms mitochondrial uptake, and stability studies in serum and saline demonstrate structural integrity over 24 hours.

## ADVANTAGES OF THE INVENTION

The compounds of the invention offer multiple advantages over existing therapies for NSAID-induced gastropathy. They directly target the mitochondrial origin of oxidative stress, unlike acid-suppressive agents that only manage symptoms. They combine antioxidant and iron-chelating activities in a single molecule, eliminating the need for combination therapy and reducing polypharmacy risks. Their small molecular weight and lipophilicity enable efficient mitochondrial penetration, a feature absent in classical antioxidants and iron chelators. They exhibit high potency with an ED50 lower than that of standard gastroprotective agents and natural antioxidants. They accelerate mucosal healing, not merely preventing injury but actively promoting regeneration. They do not interfere with gastric acid secretion, thereby avoiding the risk of bacterial overgrowth and intestinal dysbiosis associated with proton pump inhibitors. They demonstrate excellent stability in biological fluids and low toxicity in preclinical models. The scaffold is amenable to structural optimization for enhanced bioavailability, metabolic stability, and tissue specificity. The invention provides a novel, mechanism-based therapeutic strategy for a condition with high clinical burden and limited treatment options.
