## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to ophthalmic surgical devices and methods, and more specifically to a laser-based system and technique for the thermal remodeling of redundant conjunctival tissue in the treatment of conjunctivochalasis. The invention encompasses a handheld laser probe, a controlled laser delivery system, and associated methods for precisely delivering near-infrared laser energy to achieve controlled tissue shrinkage without thermal damage to adjacent structures, thereby restoring normal tear dynamics and alleviating ocular surface symptoms.

## BACKGROUND

Conjunctivochalasis is a degenerative condition characterized by the formation of redundant, loose folds of conjunctival tissue along the inferior bulbar surface of the eye. These folds frequently prolapse onto the lower eyelid margin during blinking, disrupting the continuity of the tear meniscus and impairing the uniform distribution of tears across the ocular surface. As a result, patients commonly experience chronic ocular irritation, foreign body sensation, blurred vision, and symptoms consistent with dry eye disease. Conventional treatments such as artificial tears, anti-inflammatory drops, and punctal plugs provide only transient symptomatic relief and fail to address the underlying anatomical abnormality. Surgical intervention is often required to restore normal tear dynamics by removing or reducing the excess tissue.

Current surgical approaches include thermocautery, electrocautery, and the paste-pinch-cut technique. Thermocautery and electrocautery generate temperatures exceeding the boiling point of water, resulting in full-thickness thermal injury to the conjunctival epithelium and underlying stroma. These methods frequently cause postoperative pain, prolonged healing times of up to four weeks, hemorrhage, scarring, and the potential development of pyogenic granulomas requiring additional interventions. The paste-pinch-cut method, while less invasive, still necessitates an operating room setting, carries a risk of incomplete hemostasis, and requires several weeks for surface epithelial recovery. Furthermore, all these techniques lack precise control over the depth and extent of thermal energy delivery, increasing the risk of inadvertent damage to the cornea or limbal stem cells if the eye moves during the procedure.

Laser-based techniques offer a promising alternative due to their potential for spatial and temporal control of energy deposition. However, existing laser systems, such as argon lasers operating at 532 nm, are poorly suited for conjunctival remodeling because their energy is strongly absorbed by hemoglobin, leading to vascular rupture, hemorrhage, and inflammatory sequelae. Given that the conjunctiva is composed of approximately 70% water, a more effective and safer approach involves targeting the absorption characteristics of water within the near-infrared spectrum. The development of a system capable of delivering precisely controlled, water-absorbing laser energy in a minimally invasive, outpatient-compatible format represents a significant advancement in the management of conjunctivochalasis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF DISCLOSED EMBODIMENTS

The present patent application discloses a novel system and method for laser thermal conjunctivoplasty (LTC) designed specifically for the treatment of conjunctivochalasis. The invention comprises a handheld laser probe, a programmable laser source, a control system with foot pedal activation, and a set of optimized laser parameters that enable controlled tissue shrinkage through selective water absorption without thermal necrosis. The core of the invention is a handheld laser probe configured to deliver a line-focused laser beam to a region of redundant conjunctival tissue held in place by an angled forceps. The probe incorporates a cylindrical lens that transforms the circular output of a multimode optical fiber into a linear focal spot measuring approximately 10 mm in length and 1 mm in width. This geometry ensures uniform energy distribution across the targeted tissue fold, minimizing hot spots and enabling consistent shrinkage. The line-shaped beam is oriented perpendicular to the axis of the forceps, ensuring that the laser energy is applied directly to the grasped tissue with minimal scatter or unintended exposure to surrounding structures.

The laser source is a diode laser operating at a wavelength of 1460 nm, selected for its strong absorption by water and minimal absorption by hemoglobin. This wavelength lies within a primary water absorption peak, allowing efficient energy transfer to the conjunctival stroma while avoiding vascular damage. Alternative wavelengths within the near-infrared range, such as 1940 nm, may be suitable but are less desirable due to lower electro-optical efficiency and higher system cost. The 1460 nm diode laser is capable of delivering pulsed output with programmable parameters including peak power, pulse duration, duty cycle, repetition rate, and total working duration. The system is driven by custom-built control circuits interfaced with a LabVIEW-based software platform, enabling precise, repeatable delivery of laser energy tailored to tissue response.

The handheld probe is designed for ergonomic use by a single surgeon, with an integrated aiming beam at 650 nm to facilitate accurate targeting. Upon activation via a foot pedal, the aiming beam is automatically deactivated and the 1460 nm laser is delivered according to preprogrammed parameters. The foot pedal serves as a safety mechanism: release of the pedal immediately terminates laser output, preventing unintended exposure. The system includes an optical shutter or switch to ensure that laser energy is only transmitted when the foot pedal is depressed and the probe is properly positioned.

The line-focused laser beam is delivered to conjunctival tissue grasped by angled forceps, which serve to stabilize the tissue, create a defined working distance, and orient the tissue perpendicular to the incident laser beam. The forceps are mechanically connected to the probe via a precision adapter that maintains consistent angular alignment and focal plane positioning. Ray-tracing simulations confirm that the focal plane of the laser beam coincides precisely with the surface of the tissue held by the forceps, ensuring optimal energy coupling.

The laser operates in a pulsed mode with a repetition rate of 1 Hz, a pulse duration of 100 to 300 milliseconds, and a duty cycle ranging from 10% to 30%. These parameters are selected to exploit the thermal relaxation time of conjunctival tissue, estimated at approximately 0.5 seconds based on tissue thickness and thermal diffusivity. The cumulative effect of multiple pulses allows the tissue temperature to rise gradually to an optimal range of 65°C to 90°C, sufficient to induce collagen denaturation and shrinkage without reaching the boiling point of water or causing epithelial carbonization. Temperature monitoring via thermal imaging and structural monitoring via optical coherence tomography confirm that this parameter set achieves reproducible shrinkage of up to 45% without surface damage.

The system includes a control circuit that modulates the laser diode’s driving current to achieve precise output power levels, with a maximum continuous power of 12 W and a minimum pulse width of 100 microseconds. Spectral measurements confirm a peak emission wavelength of approximately 1456 nm, with a spectral bandwidth of less than 4 nm, which remains well within the broad water absorption profile and does not significantly affect energy coupling efficiency. Temporal measurements demonstrate stable pulse shapes and precise control over pulse number and duration, enabling customizable treatment protocols.

The invention further includes a method for performing laser thermal conjunctivoplasty, comprising the steps of: identifying redundant conjunctival folds; stabilizing the fold with angled forceps; aligning the handheld laser probe such that the line-focused beam is directed perpendicularly onto the grasped tissue; activating the laser via foot pedal to deliver a series of pulses with specified duration, power, and repetition rate; and monitoring tissue response in real time using optical coherence tomography or visual inspection. The method may be performed in an outpatient setting without general anesthesia and without the need for sutures or amniotic membrane grafts.

## EXAMPLES

An ex vivo experiment was conducted using porcine eyes to optimize laser parameters for conjunctival shrinkage. Porcine eyes were thawed in phosphate-buffered saline and marked with parallel blue lines spaced 1 mm apart to define a region of interest. A thermal camera recorded temperature changes during laser application, while a swept-source optical coherence tomography system operating at 1310 nm captured real-time structural changes in the tissue. Particle image velocimetry analysis of OCT B-scans revealed directional tissue movement toward the heated region, confirming active shrinkage rather than passive compression.

Laser parameters were systematically varied, including peak power (ranging from 1.51 W to 5.77 W) and duty cycle (10%, 20%, and 30%). At 3 W peak power and 20% duty cycle, the maximum tissue temperature reached 88°C, and tissue shrinkage averaged 36%. Increasing the duty cycle to 30% resulted in temperatures exceeding 100°C and visible surface damage, despite similar shrinkage levels. Shrinkage increased with peak power up to 3 W, after which saturation occurred, indicating that higher power did not enhance efficacy but increased risk of thermal injury.

The contribution of mechanical force from the forceps alone was quantified and found to produce only 10% apparent shrinkage, whereas combined laser and forceps application yielded up to 45% shrinkage, confirming that thermal effects were the primary driver of tissue reduction. In vivo animal studies were subsequently initiated to evaluate wound healing, conjunctival hyperemia, hemorrhage, and long-term tissue stability. Histological analysis confirmed epithelial regeneration, absence of fibrosis, and preservation of underlying stromal architecture. The prototype system demonstrated reliable performance across multiple trials, with consistent output power, spectral stability, and temporal precision. The entire system, including the handheld probe and control unit, was compact, portable, and operable in a clinical setting without specialized infrastructure.
