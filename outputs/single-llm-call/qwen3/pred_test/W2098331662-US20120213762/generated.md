# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Lysosomal enzymes are a class of hydrolytic proteins essential for the degradation of macromolecules within the lysosome, maintaining cellular homeostasis through the breakdown of lipids, glycoproteins, glycosaminoglycans, and glycogen. These enzymes are synthesized in the endoplasmic reticulum, processed through the Golgi apparatus, and directed to lysosomes via specific molecular signals that facilitate their recognition by receptor proteins on the lysosomal membrane. The most well-characterized targeting mechanism involves the addition of mannose 6-phosphate (M6P) residues to N-linked oligosaccharides during post-translational modification, enabling high-affinity binding to the cation-dependent and cation-independent M6P receptors. Defects in the synthesis, trafficking, or function of lysosomal enzymes result in a group of inherited metabolic disorders known as lysosomal storage diseases, characterized by the progressive accumulation of undigested substrates within lysosomes, leading to cellular dysfunction, tissue damage, and multi-organ pathology. Enzyme replacement therapy has emerged as a clinically validated approach for several of these conditions, involving the intravenous administration of recombinant forms of the deficient enzyme to restore lysosomal catabolic capacity. However, the efficacy of this strategy is often limited by inefficient delivery of the enzyme to target tissues, particularly skeletal and cardiac muscle, due to low receptor affinity, variable glycosylation patterns, and rapid clearance from circulation. To overcome these limitations, alternative targeting strategies have been developed, including Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting (GILT), which utilizes peptide ligands capable of binding directly to the cation-independent M6P receptor without requiring post-translational phosphorylation. This approach circumvents the dependency on glycosylation machinery, offering a more consistent and scalable platform for therapeutic enzyme production across diverse expression systems.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

Improved compositions and methods for lysosomal targeting have been developed through the engineering of a modified insulin-like growth factor II (IGF-II) polypeptide that functions as a highly effective lysosomal targeting moiety. This invention introduces a furin-resistant IGF-II mutein that retains high binding affinity for the cation-independent M6P receptor while exhibiting significantly reduced interaction with the insulin receptor and the IGF-I receptor, thereby minimizing off-target signaling and potential mitogenic effects. An unexpected discovery was made that specific amino acid substitutions within the IGF-II sequence not only abolish cleavage by the furin protease but also concurrently diminish binding to the insulin receptor, a property not previously recognized as being structurally linked to furin resistance. The resulting IGF-II mutein is engineered to resist proteolytic cleavage at the furin recognition motif located between residues Arg-154 and Arg-155, preserving the integrity of the targeting domain during intracellular trafficking and extracellular circulation. This mutein is fused to a lysosomal enzyme, such as acid alpha-glucosidase, to form a therapeutic fusion protein that is efficiently internalized via the cation-independent M6P receptor and delivered to lysosomes without reliance on M6P glycosylation. The IGF-II mutein contains specific amino acid substitutions, including the replacement of arginine residues at positions 154 and 155 with lysine or alanine, and may further include deletions or substitutions in the C-terminal region to reduce affinity for the IGF-I receptor. The minimal functional region of the IGF-II mutein required for receptor binding encompasses residues 8 through 67 of the mature IGF-II sequence, with additional stabilizing mutations introduced to enhance solubility and stability. The fusion protein may include a flexible spacer sequence between the IGF-II mutein and the lysosomal enzyme to optimize folding, secretion, and receptor engagement. Nucleic acids encoding the IGF-II mutein or the fusion protein are provided in expression vectors suitable for mammalian, insect, or microbial cell systems, enabling scalable production of the therapeutic agent. Pharmaceutical compositions containing the fusion protein in a therapeutically effective amount, formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, are described for the treatment of lysosomal storage diseases, including Pompe disease, Fabry disease, and Gaucher disease.

## DEFINITIONS

Amelioration refers to the reduction, suppression, or reversal of disease symptoms, pathological features, or biochemical abnormalities associated with a lysosomal storage disorder. A furin-resistant IGF-II mutein is a modified form of the insulin-like growth factor II polypeptide that lacks susceptibility to proteolytic cleavage by the furin protease due to specific amino acid substitutions within or adjacent to the furin cleavage site. The furin protease cleavage site is a conserved amino acid sequence recognized by the furin enzyme, typically comprising the motif Arg-X-(Arg/Lys)-Arg, located at positions 154–157 of mature human IGF-II. Furin is a calcium-dependent serine endoprotease expressed in the trans-Golgi network and at the cell surface, responsible for processing precursor proteins by cleaving at polybasic motifs. Furin-deficient cells are mammalian cell lines genetically engineered or naturally lacking functional furin activity, used to assess the resistance of fusion proteins to proteolytic degradation. Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting (GILT) is a strategy for directing lysosomal enzymes to lysosomes by fusing them to a peptide ligand that binds directly to the cation-independent M6P receptor, bypassing the need for mannose 6-phosphate modification. Human acid alpha-glucosidase (GAA) is the lysosomal enzyme deficient in patients with Pompe disease, encoded by the GAA gene, responsible for hydrolyzing glycogen to glucose within lysosomes. Improve, increase, or reduce refers to a statistically significant change in a measurable biological parameter, such as enzyme uptake, substrate clearance, or receptor binding affinity, compared to a control condition. Individual, subject, or patient refers to a human or non-human mammal requiring therapeutic intervention for a lysosomal storage disease. Lysosomal storage diseases are a group of inherited metabolic disorders caused by deficiencies in lysosomal enzymes or transporters, resulting in the accumulation of undigested substrates within lysosomes. Lysosomal enzyme denotes any hydrolytic protein that functions within the lysosome to degrade macromolecules, including acid alpha-glucosidase, alpha-galactosidase A, glucocerebrosidase, and others. Spacer refers to a polypeptide sequence inserted between two functional domains to provide structural flexibility, prevent steric interference, and enhance protein folding or secretion. Therapeutically effective amount is the quantity of a composition sufficient to produce a clinically meaningful benefit in the treatment of a lysosomal storage disease, as determined by reduction in substrate accumulation, improvement in organ function, or prolongation of survival. Treatment encompasses the administration of a composition to a subject for the purpose of preventing, delaying, reducing, or reversing the progression of a lysosomal storage disease. The terms about and approximately are used to indicate a range of ±10% around a stated value, accounting for normal variability in biological systems. Specific terms related to Pompe disease include infantile-onset, juvenile-onset, and adult-onset forms, defined by age of symptom onset and residual enzyme activity levels. Specific terms related to Fabry disease include globotriaosylceramide accumulation, renal and cardiac involvement, and X-linked inheritance. Specific terms related to Gaucher disease include glucocerebroside accumulation, hepatosplenomegaly, bone lesions, and types I, II, and III classifications. 

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting (GILT) technology provides a robust platform for the delivery of lysosomal enzymes to target cells by exploiting the natural high-affinity interaction between insulin-like growth factor II and the cation-independent M6P receptor. Unlike traditional M6P-dependent targeting, which relies on variable and often inefficient post-translational glycosylation, GILT enables consistent and reproducible lysosomal delivery regardless of the expression system used. The IGF-II-derived targeting moiety binds specifically to domain 10–13 of the cation-independent M6P receptor, facilitating receptor-mediated endocytosis and subsequent trafficking to lysosomes. This mechanism has been demonstrated to significantly enhance the uptake of therapeutic enzymes into skeletal and cardiac muscle, tissues that are notoriously difficult to target using conventional enzyme replacement therapies. The fusion of IGF-II to lysosomal enzymes such as acid alpha-glucosidase results in chimeric proteins that retain enzymatic activity while gaining superior cellular internalization properties. The GILT tag is designed to be cleaved and degraded within the lysosome, ensuring that the mature enzyme functions identically to its native counterpart. This approach eliminates the need for complex glycoengineering processes and allows for production in non-mammalian systems, thereby reducing manufacturing costs and increasing scalability.

### Lysosomal Enzymes

Lysosomal enzymes encompass a broad class of hydrolases that catalyze the degradation of complex biomolecules within the acidic environment of the lysosome. Suitable lysosomal enzymes for use in the present invention include acid alpha-glucosidase, alpha-galactosidase A, glucocerebrosidase, iduronate-2-sulfatase, and arylsulfatase A, among others. These enzymes must retain catalytic activity when fused to the IGF-II mutein and must be capable of being secreted from the producing cell and internalized by target tissues. Sequence identity between the enzyme and its naturally occurring human counterpart should be at least 95%, as determined by alignment using the Needleman-Wunsch algorithm with a gap opening penalty of 10 and a gap extension penalty of 0.5. For example, in the context of Pompe disease, the acid alpha-glucosidase used in the fusion protein must share at least 98% sequence identity with the wild-type human GAA polypeptide (UniProt P17253), with conservative substitutions permitted in non-critical regions to enhance stability or expression. The fusion protein must be capable of hydrolyzing glycogen at rates comparable to native enzyme, as demonstrated by Michaelis-Menten kinetics using synthetic substrates such as p-nitrophenyl-alpha-D-glucopyranoside or 4-methylumbelliferyl-alpha-D-glucopyranoside.

### Pompe Disease

Pompe disease is a rare, autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disorder caused by mutations in the GAA gene, leading to deficiency of acid alpha-glucosidase and progressive accumulation of glycogen within lysosomes of skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle cells. The disease manifests in three clinical forms: infantile-onset, characterized by severe cardiomyopathy and hypotonia within the first few months of life; juvenile-onset, with progressive skeletal muscle weakness beginning in childhood; and adult-onset, marked by slowly progressive limb-girdle weakness and respiratory insufficiency. Without treatment, infantile-onset Pompe disease is uniformly fatal by age two, while adult-onset forms lead to premature death due to respiratory failure. Residual enzyme activity correlates inversely with disease severity, with less than 1% activity typically observed in infantile cases. Current enzyme replacement therapies have shown limited efficacy in skeletal muscle, particularly in the diaphragm and heart, due to poor uptake and rapid clearance. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a fusion protein with enhanced receptor-mediated delivery to these critical tissues.

### Enzyme Replacement Therapy

Enzyme replacement therapy involves the intravenous administration of recombinant lysosomal enzymes to supplement endogenous deficiency. The success of this approach depends on the ability of the enzyme to bind to cell surface receptors, be internalized via endocytosis, and reach the lysosomal compartment where its substrate accumulates. Conventional therapies rely on M6P-mediated uptake, which is inefficient in muscle tissues due to low receptor expression and competition with endogenous glycoproteins. The invention overcomes these barriers by utilizing the IGF-II mutein to engage the cation-independent M6P receptor with higher affinity and specificity, resulting in greater internalization and lysosomal delivery. This enables lower dosing regimens and improved clinical outcomes, particularly in tissues previously refractory to treatment.

### Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting

Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting (GILT) is a targeting strategy that replaces the natural M6P signal with a peptide ligand capable of binding directly to the cation-independent M6P receptor. The IGF-II-derived GILT tag binds to domain 10–13 of the receptor with a dissociation constant ranging from 10 to 25 nM, as determined by surface plasmon resonance, which is sufficient to mediate efficient endocytosis. Unlike M6P, which requires complex glycosylation machinery for biosynthesis, the GILT tag is encoded directly in the gene sequence, ensuring consistent production across diverse expression systems. The fusion protein is secreted in a homogeneous form, free from glycosylation heterogeneity, and exhibits superior pharmacokinetic properties, including prolonged circulation time and enhanced tissue penetration.

### Furin-Resistant GILT Tag

The native IGF-II sequence contains a furin cleavage site at Arg-Arg-Arg-Arg at positions 154–157, rendering it susceptible to proteolytic degradation during secretion and circulation. To prevent this, the arginine residues at positions 154 and 155 are substituted with lysine or alanine, abolishing furin recognition while preserving receptor binding. Additional mutations, such as deletion of residues 158–160 or substitution of Glu-156 with Gln, further stabilize the structure. The resulting mutein retains binding to the cation-independent M6P receptor with a Kd of less than 25 nM and exhibits no detectable cleavage in furin-expressing cell lines such as HEK293 or CHO. The minimal functional region for receptor binding is residues 8–67 of mature IGF-II, with the furin-resistant mutations introduced within the C-terminal extension beyond this region.

### Binding Affinity for the Insulin Receptor

The IGF-II mutein exhibits a greater than 100-fold reduction in binding affinity for the insulin receptor compared to wild-type IGF-II, as measured by competitive binding assays using radiolabeled insulin. This reduction is achieved through substitutions at residues 154–157 and deletion of residues 161–165, which disrupt the structural interface required for insulin receptor engagement. This modification eliminates the potential for mitogenic signaling, a critical safety consideration for chronic therapeutic use.

### Mutagenesis

Site-directed mutagenesis is employed to introduce specific nucleotide changes into the IGF-II coding sequence, resulting in amino acid substitutions, insertions, or deletions. Conservative mutations involve substitution with residues of similar size, charge, or hydrophobicity, while non-conservative mutations alter key functional residues. Scanning mutagenesis across the IGF-II sequence identifies regions critical for receptor binding and furin resistance. Mutations are introduced using PCR-based methods, and clones are verified by DNA sequencing.

### Spacer

A flexible spacer sequence, such as (Gly-Gly-Gly-Gly-Ser)n where n is 1–5, is inserted between the IGF-II mutein and the lysosomal enzyme to prevent steric hindrance and facilitate proper folding. The spacer is designed to be protease-resistant and non-immunogenic, ensuring stability during secretion and circulation.

### Cells

The fusion protein is expressed in mammalian cell lines including HEK293, CHO, BHK, and NS/0, which support proper folding, secretion, and post-translational modification. Stable cell lines are generated using retroviral or episomal expression systems for large-scale production. Non-mammalian systems such as insect cells, yeast, or prokaryotes may be used if the fusion protein remains functional and soluble.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

ZC-701 protein, comprising the IGF-II signal peptide fused to residues 8–67 of mature IGF-II, a three-amino acid spacer, and human GAA, was expressed in HEK293 cells. N-terminal sequencing of purified ZC-701 revealed cleavage at the furin site between Arg-154 and Arg-155, generating heterogeneous species. SDS-PAGE analysis showed multiple bands corresponding to cleaved and uncleaved forms, with approximately 40% of the protein degraded. This heterogeneity compromised batch consistency and therapeutic efficacy, demonstrating the need for furin-resistant variants.

### Example 2

Mutant constructs of ZC-701 were generated with substitutions R154K, R155A, R154K/R155A, and deletion of residues 154–157. Constructs 1459, 1460, 1461, 1463, 1479, and 1487 were expressed in CHO cells. Western blot analysis confirmed complete resistance to furin cleavage, with a single band at the expected molecular weight. Constructs 1726, 1749, 1746, and 1747 contained additional C-terminal deletions, further reducing insulin receptor binding. Deletion mutants 1758, 1750, 1748, 1751, 1752, 1753, 1754, 1755, 1756, and 1763 retained GILT activity and exhibited enhanced homogeneity.

### Example 3

Proteins were purified from culture supernatants using phenyl-Sepharose and Q Sepharose chromatography. Enzyme activity was measured using 4-methylumbelliferyl-alpha-D-glucoside substrate. All furin-resistant constructs retained specific activity comparable to wild-type ZC-701, with values ranging from 135,000 to 152,000 units/mg, confirming that mutations did not impair enzymatic function.

### Example 4

Competitive binding assays demonstrated that constructs 1487 and 1749 exhibited less than 1% binding to the insulin receptor compared to wild-type IGF-II, while maintaining high affinity for the cation-independent M6P receptor. IC50 values for IGF2R binding were 18 nM and 22 nM, respectively, confirming functional integrity of the targeting moiety.

### Example 5

Uptake assays in L6 myoblasts showed that furin-resistant GILT-tagged GAA was internalized 25-fold more efficiently than untagged GAA. Uptake was inhibited by excess soluble IGF-II but not by M6P, confirming GILT-mediated internalization. Glycogen clearance in Pompe mice treated with the fusion protein was significantly greater than with standard enzyme replacement therapy.
