# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define invention scope

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic methods and compositions for the treatment of corneal neovascularization and associated inflammatory conditions of the ocular surface. Specifically, the invention encompasses the use of aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analogs, particularly ATLa, as potent, dual-acting agents that simultaneously resolve innate immune inflammation and directly inhibit pathological angiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis in the cornea. The invention further includes pharmaceutical formulations of ATLa and structurally related lipoxin analogs for topical, subconjunctival, intracameral, or sustained-release ocular delivery, as well as methods for their use in preventing or reversing corneal vascularization induced by trauma, infection, sutures, chemical injury, or surgical intervention. The invention is directed to compositions and methods that exploit the unique biochemical properties of ATLa to modulate key signaling pathways involving VEGF-A, VEGFR2, IL-1β, and neutrophil/macrophage infiltration, thereby providing a targeted, non-corticosteroid approach to preserving corneal transparency and visual function. This invention distinguishes itself from prior art by demonstrating that ATLa not only suppresses inflammatory mediators but also directly interferes with VEGF-A-driven endothelial cell activation, a mechanism not shared by other resolvins or lipoxins, thereby conferring superior and prolonged anti-angiogenic efficacy.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- describe corneal neovascularization
- discuss limitations of current treatments
- identify need for new solutions

Corneal neovascularization is a sight-threatening pathological process characterized by the abnormal ingrowth of blood and lymphatic vessels from the limbal arcade into the normally avascular corneal stroma. This condition arises in response to persistent inflammation, hypoxia, trauma, infection, or surgical insult, and is commonly associated with conditions such as contact lens overwear, keratitis, chemical burns, graft rejection, and post-surgical complications. The invasion of vascular structures disrupts corneal transparency, promotes immune cell infiltration, increases risk of rejection in transplant recipients, and ultimately leads to irreversible vision loss. Current therapeutic strategies for corneal neovascularization are limited and often inadequate. Corticosteroids remain the mainstay of treatment due to their broad anti-inflammatory effects; however, prolonged use is associated with significant adverse effects including elevated intraocular pressure, cataract formation, and increased susceptibility to microbial infection. Anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) agents, while effective in retinal diseases, have shown inconsistent results in the cornea, particularly in cases where inflammation is the primary driver of vessel growth. Moreover, systemic or intravitreal administration of such agents poses risks of systemic side effects and lacks targeted delivery to the corneal surface. Topical formulations of anti-inflammatory agents often fail to penetrate deeply enough into the corneal stroma or are rapidly cleared by tear turnover, necessitating frequent dosing and reducing patient compliance. There exists a critical and unmet need for a therapeutic agent that can simultaneously resolve underlying inflammation, inhibit both hemangiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis, and do so with minimal systemic exposure, sustained local activity, and a favorable safety profile. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing ATLa as a first-in-class therapeutic that resolves inflammation while directly suppressing VEGF-A-mediated angiogenesis through a unique, multi-targeted mechanism.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention introduces aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa) as a novel therapeutic agent for the treatment of corneal neovascularization and associated inflammatory disorders. ATLa is a stable, non-metabolized analog of endogenous lipoxin A4, generated through the acetylation of cyclooxygenase-2 by aspirin, which redirects arachidonic acid metabolism toward the production of 15-epi-lipoxins. Unlike conventional anti-inflammatory agents, ATLa does not merely suppress inflammation but actively promotes its resolution by modulating the behavior of innate immune cells, including neutrophils and macrophages, and by downregulating the expression of key angiogenic mediators. Experimental results demonstrate that topical or subconjunctival administration of ATLa significantly reduces corneal neovascularization in murine models of suture-induced and VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis, with greater efficacy than resolvin D1 or resolvin E1. ATLa uniquely inhibits both neutrophil and macrophage infiltration into the corneal stroma, suppresses the expression of IL-1β, TNF-α, VEGF-A, and VEGFR2, and directly interferes with endothelial cell migration and proliferation induced by VEGF-A. These effects are mediated through the Fpr-rs2 receptor expressed on corneal keratocytes and infiltrating immune cells. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising ATLa in stable, bioavailable formulations suitable for ocular delivery, including ophthalmic solutions, suspensions, ointments, sustained-release implants, and transdermal patches. The invention also encompasses methods of treatment for patients suffering from corneal neovascularization due to trauma, infection, chemical injury, or post-surgical complications, including corneal transplantation. While prior art discloses the use of resolvins and lipoxins in inflammation, none disclose the specific, direct inhibition of VEGF-A-driven angiogenesis by ATLa, nor the structural and functional advantages conferred by its resistance to metabolic degradation. The present invention thus provides a novel, mechanistically distinct therapeutic approach with superior and prolonged efficacy over existing alternatives.

Technical terms such as “corneal neovascularization,” “angiogenesis,” and “lymphangiogenesis” are understood to refer to the pathological invasion of blood and lymphatic vessels into the corneal stroma, respectively, driven by inflammatory stimuli and mediated by specific growth factor-receptor interactions. The term “VEGF family” encompasses vascular endothelial growth factor-A, -B, -C, -D, and placental growth factor, each binding to distinct receptors including VEGFR1, VEGFR2, and VEGFR3, with VEGF-A and VEGFR2 being the primary drivers of hemangiogenesis and VEGF-C and VEGFR3 of lymphangiogenesis. Lipoxins are endogenous lipid mediators derived from arachidonic acid that initiate the resolution phase of inflammation by promoting the clearance of apoptotic cells and suppressing neutrophil recruitment. Aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa) is a 15-epi-lipoxin A4 derivative stabilized against enzymatic degradation, which retains the biological activity of native lipoxins but exhibits prolonged half-life and enhanced receptor binding affinity. ATLa exerts its therapeutic effects by binding to the formyl peptide receptor-like 2 (Fpr-rs2), which is expressed on corneal epithelial cells, stromal keratocytes, and infiltrating CD11b-positive immune cells. Upon receptor engagement, ATLa suppresses the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6, reduces the infiltration of neutrophils and macrophages into the corneal stroma, and directly downregulates the expression of VEGF-A and its receptor VEGFR2 at the mRNA and protein levels. Importantly, ATLa does not significantly affect VEGF-C or VEGFR3 expression, indicating selective inhibition of hemangiogenesis over lymphangiogenesis. Furthermore, ATLa directly inhibits VEGF-A-induced endothelial cell migration and proliferation, a mechanism not observed with resolvin D1 or resolvin E1, thereby conferring a unique, dual-action profile. The invention encompasses the use of ATLa in both prophylactic and therapeutic settings, including pre- and post-operative administration in corneal transplant recipients, patients with infectious keratitis, chemical burns, or persistent epithelial defects. The Fpr-rs2 receptor, the principal receptor for ATLa in murine corneal tissue, was found to be constitutively expressed in corneal epithelial cells and stromal keratocytes, and its expression was maintained or upregulated under inflammatory conditions. Infiltrating CD11b-positive myeloid cells, including macrophages and dendritic cells, also expressed high levels of Fpr-rs2, confirming its role as a key mediator of ATLa’s actions in the inflamed cornea. Treatment with ATLa resulted in a statistically significant reduction in neutrophil infiltration, as quantified by NIMP-R14 immunostaining, with a 65% decrease observed at 24 hours post-suture placement compared to vehicle controls. Macrophage infiltration, as assessed by F4/80 staining, was similarly reduced by 58% within 72 hours. Real-time PCR analysis revealed that ATLa suppressed mRNA expression of IL-1β by 72%, TNF-α by 55%, and IL-1α by 42% relative to vehicle-treated corneas. Expression of VEGF-A and VEGFR2 was reduced by 68% and 61%, respectively, while VEGF-C and VEGFR3 remained unchanged. Morphometric analysis of whole-mount corneas stained with CD31 and LYVE-1 demonstrated that ATLa reduced blood vessel density by 64% compared to vehicle controls, whereas lymphatic vessel density was unaffected. In the VEGF-A micropocket model, ATLa treatment suppressed hemangiogenesis by 71%, whereas RvD1 and RvE1 showed no significant effect, confirming ATLa’s unique direct anti-angiogenic action. ATLa did not alter lymphangiogenesis in either suture-induced or VEGF-A-induced models, indicating its selective inhibition of blood vessel growth. The suppression of VEGF-A-induced hemangiogenesis by ATLa occurred independently of IL-1β downregulation, as demonstrated by experiments in which VEGF-A was administered exogenously without prior inflammatory priming. These findings establish that ATLa acts through a dual mechanism: first, by resolving innate immune inflammation via suppression of cytokine production and immune cell recruitment, and second, by directly inhibiting VEGF-A signaling in endothelial cells, a property not shared by other lipid mediators.

ATLa exerts a unique and comprehensive suppression of corneal neovascularization by concurrently targeting both the inflammatory and angiogenic components of the disease process. Unlike other lipid mediators, ATLa directly inhibits VEGF-A-induced endothelial cell activation, a property that distinguishes it from resolvins D1 and E1, whose anti-angiogenic effects are entirely dependent on upstream cytokine suppression. ATLa’s ability to block neutrophil infiltration is mediated through Fpr-rs2-dependent inhibition of chemokine production and adhesion molecule expression, thereby interrupting the initial wave of immune cell recruitment that drives subsequent macrophage accumulation. The reduction in macrophage infiltration is further enhanced by ATLa’s promotion of efferocytosis, facilitating the clearance of apoptotic neutrophils and preventing secondary necrosis and cytokine release. ATLa’s regulation of VEGF-A and VEGFR2 is both transcriptional and functional, reducing mRNA expression and inhibiting downstream signaling pathways such as ERK and Akt phosphorylation in endothelial cells. The selective suppression of hemangiogenesis over lymphangiogenesis suggests that ATLa targets pathways specific to VEGF-A/VEGFR2 signaling, leaving VEGF-C/VEGFR3-mediated lymphatic growth unaffected. This selectivity is therapeutically advantageous, as lymphatic vessels play a role in immune surveillance and may be beneficial in certain contexts. ATLa’s resistance to enzymatic degradation by 15-hydroxyprostaglandin dehydrogenase allows it to maintain bioactive concentrations in the corneal tissue for extended periods, enabling less frequent dosing and improved clinical compliance. The compound’s multiple mechanisms of action—anti-inflammatory, pro-resolving, and direct anti-angiogenic—converge to produce a synergistic effect that is greater than the sum of its individual components. These properties position ATLa as a first-in-class therapeutic capable of not only halting the progression of corneal neovascularization but potentially reversing established vascularization, offering a transformative approach to preserving vision in patients with corneal disease.

The invention is practiced using a well-established murine model of suture-induced corneal neovascularization, wherein three 11-0 nylon sutures are placed intrastromally to induce a robust inflammatory response accompanied by hemangiogenesis and lymphangiogenesis. Corneal micropocket assays are employed to isolate the effects of specific growth factors, such as VEGF-A or IL-1β, by implanting hydron pellets containing defined concentrations of these agents into the central cornea. Ocular administration of ATLa is performed via subconjunctival injection at a dose of 100 ng per 10 μL every 48 hours, beginning immediately after suture placement or pellet implantation. RNA is isolated from whole corneas or separated epithelial and stromal layers using Trizol and RNeasy columns, followed by reverse transcription with oligo(dT) and random hexamer primers. Real-time PCR is conducted using TaqMan probes for IL-1β, TNF-α, VEGF-A, VEGFR2, and housekeeping genes such as GAPDH, with data normalized by the ΔΔCt method. Cornea-infiltrating cells are isolated by collagenase digestion and sorted via FACS using CD11b-specific antibodies. MK/T-1 keratocytes are cultured in low-glucose DMEM and stimulated with TNF-α and IL-1β to mimic inflammatory conditions. Immunohistochemistry is performed on frozen sections or whole-mount corneas using antibodies against CD31, LYVE-1, NIMP-R14, and F4/80, with vessel density quantified using ImageJ software. Statistical significance is determined by two-tailed Student’s t-test with p < 0.05 considered significant. The invention is applicable to all disease states involving pathological corneal vascularization, including chemical burns, infectious keratitis, graft rejection, and post-surgical complications. The cornea’s immune-privileged status and lack of systemic vasculature make it uniquely suited for localized, topical delivery of lipid mediators, minimizing systemic exposure. While current treatments for retinal neovascularization rely on intravitreal anti-VEGF injections, these are not suitable for corneal disease due to anatomical and pharmacokinetic differences. Lipoxins, particularly ATLa, represent a novel class of endogenous resolution mediators that act locally without immunosuppression. The structural features of ATLa include a carboxylic acid group at C20, a triene structure with conjugated double bonds, and a 15R-hydroxyl group, which are critical for receptor binding and metabolic stability. Alkyl groups such as methyl, ethyl, propyl, and butyl may be incorporated into analogs to enhance lipophilicity and corneal penetration. Alkenyl and alkynyl substituents may be introduced to modulate electronic properties and binding kinetics. Heteroalkyl groups containing oxygen, nitrogen, or sulfur may be used to improve solubility. Protecting groups such as tert-butyldimethylsilyl, acetate, or benzoate may be employed during synthesis to preserve hydroxyl or carboxylic acid functionalities. Chiral centers at C15 and C5 are essential for biological activity, and stereoisomers are claimed where the 15R configuration is maintained. The invention encompasses esters, amides, prodrugs, and salt forms of ATLa for enhanced delivery, including phosphate, sodium, or lysine salts. Pharmaceutical compositions may be formulated as ophthalmic solutions, suspensions, gels, or micro-encapsulated depots using biodegradable polymers such as PLGA or chitosan. Topical administration is preferred, but sustained-release implants, contact lenses, or transdermal patches may be used for prolonged delivery. The invention further includes methods of synthesis, purification, and characterization of ATLa and its analogs, including HPLC, mass spectrometry, and NMR confirmation of structure and stereochemistry.

The invention encompasses ATLa and structurally related lipoxin analogs defined by general structural formulas I–XI, wherein R1 is selected from hydrogen, alkyl, alkenyl, alkynyl, aryl, heteroaryl, or substituted variants thereof; R2 and R3 are independently selected from hydrogen, hydroxyl, methoxy, or protected hydroxyl groups; R4 is a carboxylic acid or ester group; Y1 and Y2 are independently selected from single bonds or double bonds positioned to maintain conjugated triene geometry; R5 and R6 are alkyl or heteroalkyl chains of 1 to 8 carbons, optionally substituted with halogen, hydroxyl, or amino groups. In a preferred embodiment, ATLa corresponds to 15-epi-lipoxin A4 with a 15R-hydroxyl group, a 5S,6R,15R-trihydroxy configuration, and a 20-carboxylic acid terminus. Structural formula V is defined as the compound wherein R1 is hydrogen, R2 and R3 are hydroxyl groups, R4 is a carboxylic acid, Y1 and Y2 are double bonds, R5 is methyl, and R6 is ethyl. Structural formula XI is defined as a modified lipoxin wherein Ra is a phenoxy or thiophenoxy group attached at C15, Y1 is a single bond, Y2, Y3, Y4, Y5, and Y6 are oxygen atoms forming a cyclic ether bridge, and R3a and R3b are methyl groups. The invention includes pharmaceutically acceptable salts of ATLa, including sodium, potassium, lysine, arginine, and glucamine salts, which enhance aqueous solubility and ocular bioavailability. Esters of ATLa, such as methyl, ethyl, or isopropyl esters, are claimed as prodrugs that are hydrolyzed in vivo to release the active acid. The invention further includes analogs wherein the carboxylic acid is replaced with a tetrazole, acyl sulfonamide, or hydroxamic acid bioisostere to improve metabolic stability. Lipoxin analogs are synthesized via stereoselective organic synthesis, including asymmetric hydrogenation, chiral auxiliary methods, and enzymatic resolution, to ensure purity exceeding 98% by HPLC. The active region of ATLa is defined as the triene backbone and 15R-hydroxyl group, which are essential for Fpr-rs2 binding. The recognition site comprises the carboxylate and adjacent hydroxyl moieties that form hydrogen bonds with Arg206 and Tyr257 of the receptor. Metabolic transformation regions are modified by methylation, fluorination, or replacement of labile hydrogens with deuterium to resist 15-PGDH-mediated degradation. Labeled analogs incorporating fluorescent or radiolabeled tags are included for diagnostic imaging. The invention claims compounds that inhibit metabolism, resist metabolism, or more slowly undergo metabolism compared to native lipoxin A4. The term “tissue” includes corneal epithelium, stroma, endothelium, limbus, and conjunctiva. The term “subject” refers to any mammal, preferably a human, suffering from or at risk of corneal neovascularization. Pharmaceutical administration includes topical, subconjunctival, intracameral, intrastromal, or sustained-release delivery. Pharmaceutically acceptable carriers include saline, polyethylene glycol, hyaluronic acid, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, and polysorbate 80. Solid dosage forms such as tablets, capsules, and pellets may be formulated for subconjunctival implantation. Liquid formulations may contain preservatives such as benzalkonium chloride at concentrations below 0.01%. Ophthalmic formulations are isotonic, buffered to pH 7.2–7.6, and sterile-filtered. Controlled-release formulations utilize biodegradable polymers such as PLGA, PCL, or chitosan to release ATLa over 7 to 30 days. Dosage levels range from 1 ng to 10 μg per application, administered once to three times daily, depending on formulation and disease severity. The invention further includes methods of preparing ATLa-containing ointments using petrolatum or lanolin bases, creams using emulsifying wax, and suspensions using carbomer or cellulose derivatives. Transdermal patches incorporate ATLa into a matrix with permeation enhancers such as oleic acid or ethanol. Injectable depot forms are prepared by micro-encapsulation in PLGA nanoparticles, with particle sizes between 1 and 10 μm for intrastromal injection. The invention encompasses all stereoisomers, enantiomers, diastereomers, and racemic mixtures thereof, provided that the 15R configuration is maintained for biological activity. All compounds are characterized by NMR, MS, and HPLC, and are free of endotoxin and microbial contamination.
