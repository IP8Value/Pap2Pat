## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Technical Field

The present invention relates to magnetoresistive memory devices and, more specifically, to spin-orbit torque magnetoresistance effect elements and magnetic memory systems that utilize spin current-driven magnetization switching for high-speed, low-power, and high-endurance data storage applications. The invention is particularly directed to the structural and material design of spin-orbit torque wiring, inserted layers, and ferromagnetic constituent layers that enable reliable magnetization reversal with minimal Joule heating and structural degradation, thereby achieving write endurance exceeding 10¹² cycles without compromising the integrity of the tunnel barrier or adjacent functional layers.

### Description of Related Art

Magnetoresistive elements based on the spin-transfer torque (STT) mechanism have been widely adopted in non-volatile memory architectures due to their scalability and non-volatility. However, STT-based magnetic random access memory (MRAM) suffers from inherent limitations in write speed and endurance, primarily due to current-induced stress on the thin tunnel barrier layer of the magnetic tunnel junction. As write pulses are shortened to improve speed, the required current density increases, leading to accelerated degradation of the insulating barrier and limiting the number of reliable write cycles. To circumvent these issues, spin-orbit torque (SOT)-based memory architectures have been proposed, wherein an in-plane current flowing through a heavy metal layer generates a spin current via the spin Hall effect, which then exerts a torque on the magnetization of an adjacent ferromagnetic layer. This three-terminal configuration decouples the write current path from the tunnel junction, theoretically eliminating barrier degradation. 

In conventional SOT-MRAM designs, the spin-orbit torque wiring is typically composed of non-magnetic heavy metals such as tungsten, tantalum, or platinum, chosen for their large spin Hall angles. These materials efficiently convert charge current into spin current, enabling magnetization switching of a ferromagnetic free layer without direct current injection through the tunnel barrier. However, despite the theoretical advantages, practical implementations have encountered significant reliability challenges. High resistivity in the spin-orbit torque wiring, often necessary to enhance spin Hall efficiency, leads to substantial Joule heating during repeated write operations. This heating induces electromigration, thermal expansion mismatch, and structural failure in the wiring itself, manifesting as electrical-open or electrical-short failures that compromise device integrity. Furthermore, the asymmetry in switching probability between opposite current polarities, often attributed to interfacial magnetic stray fields or imperfect spin injection efficiency, results in write errors that limit operational robustness. 

Recent efforts to improve SOT performance have explored the use of topological insulators and engineered interfaces to enhance spin current generation, yet these approaches have not resolved the fundamental issue of wiring degradation under high-current-density operation. Additionally, prior art has not demonstrated a systematic method to mitigate the mechanical and thermal stress on the spin-orbit torque wiring while maintaining high spin-to-charge conversion efficiency. The absence of an inserted layer between the spin-orbit torque wiring and the ferromagnetic layer, or the failure to control the polarity and magnitude of spin current generation within such a layer, has left critical reliability gaps unaddressed. Consequently, existing SOT-based memory elements have not achieved the combination of ultra-high endurance (>10¹² cycles), low reversal current density, and structural stability required for deployment in next-generation embedded memory systems.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- motivate reducing reversal current density

The present invention is motivated by the need to significantly reduce the reversal current density required for reliable magnetization switching in spin-orbit torque magnetoresistance effect elements, thereby minimizing Joule heating and associated structural degradation in the spin-orbit torque wiring. Conventional approaches rely on increasing the thickness or resistivity of heavy metal layers to enhance spin Hall efficiency, but this inadvertently elevates power dissipation and accelerates electromigration. By introducing a precisely engineered inserted layer between the spin-orbit torque wiring and the ferromagnetic constituent layer, the invention achieves a synergistic enhancement in spin current injection efficiency without requiring higher current densities. This reduction in reversal current density directly translates to lower power consumption, reduced thermal stress, and extended operational lifetime, enabling write endurance exceeding 10¹² cycles while preserving the integrity of all functional layers, including the tunnel barrier and the wiring itself.

- describe SOT type magnetoresistance effect element

The invention encompasses a spin-orbit torque magnetoresistance effect element comprising a spin-orbit torque wiring, a ferromagnetic constituent layer, and an inserted layer positioned between them. The spin-orbit torque wiring is configured to carry an in-plane charge current that generates a pure spin current via the spin Hall effect or Rashba-Edelstein effect. The inserted layer, which may be composed of a magnetic or non-magnetic material with tailored spin Hall angle polarity, modulates the spin current before it is injected into the ferromagnetic constituent layer. This configuration enables efficient magnetization reversal with a reversal current density substantially lower than that observed in conventional SOT elements, while simultaneously suppressing the generation of localized heat and electromigration within the spin-orbit torque wiring.

- describe problem of SOT type magnetoresistance effect element

A critical limitation of prior SOT magnetoresistance effect elements is the unavoidable trade-off between spin current generation efficiency and wiring durability. High resistivity materials such as tungsten or tantalum, while effective at generating spin currents, are prone to structural failure under repeated high-current-density operation due to Joule heating. This results in electrical-open failures caused by wiring rupture or electrical-short failures due to metal migration across adjacent structures. Furthermore, the asymmetry in switching probability between positive and negative current directions, often caused by interfacial magnetic stray fields or unbalanced spin injection, leads to unreliable write operations and limits the practical endurance of the device.

- describe solution using inserted layer

The invention solves these problems by introducing an inserted layer with controlled spin Hall angle polarity and optimized thickness, positioned directly between the spin-orbit torque wiring and the ferromagnetic constituent layer. This inserted layer acts as a spin current amplifier or polarity modulator, enhancing the effective spin torque on the ferromagnetic layer without increasing the charge current density in the wiring. By selecting materials with complementary spin Hall angles or by engineering interfacial spin-orbit coupling, the inserted layer enables a net spin current that is both stronger and more symmetric than that achievable in conventional configurations. This allows magnetization reversal to occur at significantly reduced current densities, thereby minimizing Joule heating and eliminating the primary mechanisms of wiring degradation.

- describe spin current magnetization rotational element

The invention further defines a spin current magnetization rotational element that enables controlled rotation of the magnetization vector within the ferromagnetic constituent layer, rather than simple bistable switching. This is achieved by configuring the spin-orbit torque wiring and inserted layer to generate a spin current with a specific angular dependence relative to the magnetization axis. The element permits continuous, non-volatile magnetization reorientation, enabling multi-level storage and analog memory applications. The rotational behavior is stabilized by interfacial perpendicular magnetic anisotropy and controlled by the relative orientation of the spin current direction and the magnetization easy axis.

- describe first aspect of spin current magnetization rotational element

The first aspect of the spin current magnetization rotational element comprises a spin-orbit torque wiring composed of a non-magnetic heavy metal, an inserted layer formed of a magnetic metal with a spin Hall angle of opposite polarity to that of the wiring, and a ferromagnetic constituent layer exhibiting perpendicular magnetic anisotropy. The inserted layer is thinner than 2 nanometers and is positioned such that the spin current generated in the wiring is partially reversed in polarity before injection into the ferromagnetic layer, resulting in a net spin torque that is amplified and more uniform than in conventional configurations.

- describe second aspect of spin current magnetization rotational element

The second aspect incorporates a topological insulator as the inserted layer, wherein the surface states generate a spin current with intrinsic spin-momentum locking. This configuration eliminates the need for a conventional heavy metal wiring, as the topological insulator both generates and modulates the spin current. The ferromagnetic constituent layer is directly coupled to the topological insulator’s surface, enabling ultra-low power magnetization switching with negligible Joule heating and exceptional thermal stability.

- describe third aspect of spin current magnetization rotational element

The third aspect introduces a bilayer inserted structure, wherein a first sub-layer of non-magnetic heavy metal is coupled to the spin-orbit torque wiring, and a second sub-layer of magnetic metal is interfaced with the ferromagnetic constituent layer. The spin Hall angles of the two sub-layers are engineered to partially cancel opposing spin currents, resulting in a net spin current with enhanced directionality and reduced lateral spread, thereby improving switching uniformity across large-area arrays.

- describe fourth aspect of spin current magnetization rotational element

The fourth aspect defines a spin current magnetization rotational element wherein the spin-orbit torque wiring and inserted layer are fabricated with identical polarity of spin Hall angle, such that the spin current generated in the wiring is reinforced rather than reversed upon entering the inserted layer. This configuration is particularly effective for in-plane magnetized ferromagnetic layers, where the combined spin current induces a coherent rotation of the magnetization vector along a predetermined azimuthal angle, enabling analog memory states and continuous tuning of resistance.

- describe spin-orbit torque magnetoresistance effect element

The spin-orbit torque magnetoresistance effect element of the invention integrates the spin current magnetization rotational element with a tunnel barrier and a reference magnetic layer to form a functional magnetic tunnel junction. The tunnel barrier is electrically isolated from the spin-orbit torque wiring, ensuring that write currents do not traverse the barrier and thus preserving its integrity over extended write cycles. The element exhibits a tunnel magnetoresistance ratio exceeding 80% after 10¹² write cycles, demonstrating unprecedented endurance.

- describe magnetic memory

The invention further provides a magnetic memory comprising an array of the spin-orbit torque magnetoresistance effect elements, each connected to a dedicated word line, source line, and read line. The memory is configured for simultaneous write and read operations, with the spin-orbit torque wiring serving exclusively as a write path and the tunnel junction serving exclusively as a read path. This three-terminal architecture enables independent optimization of write speed, endurance, and read sensitivity, making the memory suitable for embedded AI applications requiring high-density, low-power, and non-volatile storage.
