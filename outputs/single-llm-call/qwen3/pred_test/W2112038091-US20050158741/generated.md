# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The precise regulation of gene expression in mammalian systems has long been a cornerstone of molecular biology and therapeutic development. Traditional approaches have relied predominantly on transcriptional control mechanisms, such as inducible promoters responsive to small molecules or environmental cues, which, while effective, are inherently limited by the time required for transcription initiation, mRNA processing, and nuclear export. More recent strategies utilizing RNA interference have expanded the toolkit by enabling post-transcriptional silencing through sequence-specific degradation of mRNA. However, these methods often suffer from off-target effects, incomplete knockdown, and transient activity, limiting their utility in long-term or clinically relevant applications. A critical unmet need exists for a gene regulation system that operates independently of transcriptional machinery, responds rapidly to exogenous modulators, and permits reversible, tunable control of protein output without perturbing endogenous gene networks. The discovery that certain naturally occurring RNA motifs can catalyze their own cleavage within full-length mRNA transcripts in mammalian cells opened a novel paradigm: the possibility of constitutive mRNA destruction that can be selectively reversed by small molecules. This concept, if realized with high efficiency and specificity, would enable a new class of gene switches that function at the level of RNA stability rather than transcriptional initiation. Such a system would not only accelerate the kinetics of gene induction but also offer greater spatial and temporal precision, particularly in vivo, where transcriptional regulators often face delivery and bioavailability challenges. The development of such a system hinges on two key components: first, the identification of a self-cleaving ribozyme that functions robustly in the complex intracellular environment of mammalian cells, and second, the discovery of compounds capable of inhibiting this cleavage activity without inducing cytotoxicity or nonspecific RNA binding. Previous efforts to exploit ribozymes for gene regulation were hampered by inefficient cleavage in mammalian contexts and a lack of suitable inhibitors. The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a genetically encoded RNA-based regulatory system centered on a modified schistosome-derived ribozyme motif, whose catalytic activity can be precisely modulated by specific nucleoside analogs that are incorporated into nascent RNA transcripts, thereby providing a novel, chemically controllable mechanism for gene expression.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention discloses a novel ribozyme-based system for the exogenous control of gene expression in mammalian cells through the modulation of site-specific RNA self-cleavage. At its core, the system employs a genetically engineered self-cleaving RNA motif derived from the Schistosoma mansoni hammerhead ribozyme, designated herein as the self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif, which is inserted into the 5′ or 3′ untranslated region or within the coding sequence of a target gene transcript. Upon transcription, the ribozyme motif catalyzes its own cleavage under physiological conditions, resulting in rapid degradation of the mRNA and suppression of protein expression. The introduction of specific nucleoside analogs into the cellular nucleotide pool leads to their covalent incorporation into the nascent RNA transcript during transcription, thereby impairing the catalytic activity of the ribozyme motif and permitting the accumulation of intact, translationally competent mRNA. This mechanism enables reversible, dose-dependent induction of gene expression solely through the administration of small molecules, without requiring exogenous transcription factors or complex regulatory circuits. The self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif is characterized by specific structural modifications to the conserved core and flanking stems of the wild-type hammerhead ribozyme, including substitutions at nucleotide positions 5, 7, and 14, and the presence of a four-, six-, or eight-nucleotide loop III, which collectively enhance catalytic efficiency and stability in mammalian cells. The invention further encompasses the grafting of aptamer sequences into the stem regions of the ribozyme motif, enabling ligand-dependent control of cleavage activity through the binding of small molecules or synthetic effectors to the aptamer domain, which allosterically modulates ribozyme folding and function. DNA constructs encoding the self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif are operably linked to a promoter and a nucleic acid encoding a desired protein product, such that transcription yields a chimeric mRNA containing both the target coding sequence and the regulatory ribozyme element. Upon transcription, the ribozyme cleaves the mRNA unless inhibited, thereby acting as a molecular switch whose state is determined by the presence or absence of specific effector molecules. Multiple copies of the motif may be joined in tandem to amplify the repressive effect or to enable combinatorial control. The system is functional in a wide range of host cells, including primary human cells, immortalized lines, and transgenic animal models, and may be delivered via viral vectors, liposomes, or naked nucleic acid formulations. The invention further provides methods for modulating expression by administering effector molecules such as toyocamycin, 5-fluorouridine, or 5-fluorouracil, which are incorporated into RNA and inhibit cleavage, as well as methods for inhibiting cleavage using antisense oligonucleotides or modified RNA-binding compounds. The system enables both ex vivo and in vivo regulation of gene expression, including the generation of stable cell lines, transgenic animals, and therapeutic constructs for controlled protein delivery. Additionally, the invention introduces screening methods for identifying novel inhibitors of ribozyme activity, including high-throughput assays using reporter gene systems and combinatorial compound libraries, as well as methods for quantifying inhibitor levels in biological samples based on ribozyme-mediated reporter induction. The invention further encompasses kits comprising DNA constructs, effector molecules, and instructions for use in regulating gene expression, and provides a platform for real-time monitoring of drug pharmacokinetics through reporter gene induction in response to therapeutically relevant compounds.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention relates to a novel system for the precise, reversible, and chemically controllable regulation of gene expression in mammalian cells through the modulation of RNA self-cleavage. At the heart of this system is a genetically encoded self-cleaving RNA motif derived from the Schistosoma mansoni hammerhead ribozyme, which has been structurally optimized for high catalytic efficiency and stability within the intracellular environment of mammalian cells. Unlike conventional ribozymes that exhibit poor activity in mammalian systems, the modified schistosome RNA mutant motif retains robust self-cleavage capability even at low magnesium concentrations and in the presence of cellular RNA-binding proteins. This motif is characterized by a conserved catalytic core containing specific nucleotide substitutions at positions 5, 7, and 14, which enhance structural rigidity and catalytic turnover. The inclusion of a loop III region comprising four, six, or eight nucleotides, as opposed to the shorter loops found in wild-type hammerhead ribozymes, significantly improves folding kinetics and resistance to nuclease degradation. The motif may be further modified by the addition of stem-loop structures, hairpins, or aptamer domains to enable allosteric regulation. The aptamer sequences, grafted into the peripheral stems of the ribozyme, are designed to bind specific effector molecules with high affinity and selectivity. Upon binding, the effector induces a conformational change that either stabilizes or destabilizes the catalytic core, thereby modulating cleavage activity. This allows for ligand-dependent control of gene expression without requiring the incorporation of nucleoside analogs into RNA. The invention further encompasses DNA constructs comprising a promoter operably linked to a nucleic acid encoding the self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif and a nucleic acid encoding a desired protein product. Upon transcription, the resulting mRNA contains the ribozyme motif, which cleaves itself under basal conditions, preventing translation. Administration of effector molecules such as toyocamycin, 5-fluorouridine, or 5-fluorouracil leads to their incorporation into nascent RNA transcripts during transcription, disrupting the catalytic core and permitting accumulation of full-length mRNA and subsequent translation. The system is functional in both transiently transfected and stably integrated configurations and is compatible with viral delivery systems, including adeno-associated virus, lentivirus, and adenovirus. The invention further provides methods for generating host cells, such as HEK293-derived lines, that stably express the ribozyme-regulated construct and exhibit low basal expression and high inducibility, enabling high-throughput screening for novel inhibitors. Packaging cell lines are constructed to produce recombinant viral vectors encoding the ribozyme system, ensuring consistent and scalable production. The invention further describes methods for modulating expression by administering antisense oligonucleotides complementary to the cleavage site, which sterically block ribozyme access, as well as methods for inducing target gene expression through the use of modified oligonucleotides such as morpholinos or 2′-O-methyl RNAs. The system may be applied to endogenous gene regulation by integrating the ribozyme motif into the 3′ UTR of a native gene via homologous recombination or CRISPR-mediated knock-in. The invention further provides methods for screening for agents that inhibit ribozyme catalytic activity, including cell-based assays using luciferase or β-galactosidase reporters, wherein induction of signal correlates with inhibition of cleavage. Combinatorial libraries of small molecules, natural products, and synthetic compounds are screened for their ability to induce reporter expression in ribozyme-containing cells but not in cells bearing catalytically inactive mutants. The invention further introduces methods for determining the concentration of inhibitors in biological samples by correlating reporter signal intensity with effector dose-response curves established in vitro. The system enables the development of therapeutic compositions comprising the DNA construct and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for administration via injection, inhalation, or topical delivery. The invention further encompasses transgenic nonhuman animals, such as mice, in which the ribozyme-regulated construct is stably integrated into the genome, allowing for in vivo studies of gene regulation and drug pharmacokinetics. The invention further provides kits containing one or more DNA constructs encoding the self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif, effector molecules such as toyocamycin or 5-fluorouridine, and detailed instructions for use in regulating gene expression in vitro or in vivo.

### Self-Cleaving RNA Mutant Motifs

The self-cleaving RNA mutant motifs disclosed herein are engineered variants of the Schistosoma mansoni hammerhead ribozyme that exhibit enhanced catalytic activity and stability in mammalian cells. These motifs retain the conserved core structure essential for catalysis, including the three helical stems and the invariant nucleotides that form the catalytic pocket, but are modified to improve folding efficiency and resistance to cellular nucleases. The standard nomenclature for nucleotide positions within the hammerhead ribozyme is employed, with positions 1–15 in stem I, 16–20 in the catalytic core, and 21–30 in stem II. Key modifications include substitutions at nucleotide positions 5, 7, and 14, which are located within the conserved core and are critical for maintaining the geometry of the active site. Substitution of adenine at position 7 with guanine or cytosine significantly reduces cleavage activity, whereas substitution with purine analogs such as 7-deazaadenine enhances stability. The loop III region, which connects stems I and II in the wild-type ribozyme, is extended from its native two-nucleotide length to four, six, or eight nucleotides, which improves the thermodynamic stability of the folded structure and increases the rate of cleavage under physiological conditions. Additional modifications include the addition of stem extensions or hairpin loops adjacent to stem III, which further stabilize the ribozyme against degradation. The motif may also incorporate a combination of loop III extension and core substitutions, such as a six-nucleotide loop III with a C-to-U substitution at position 14, which together yield a ribozyme with cleavage rates exceeding 90% per minute in mammalian cell lysates. The self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif is defined as an RNA sequence comprising the conserved hammerhead catalytic core with at least one modification selected from the group consisting of loop III extension, substitution at position 5, substitution at position 7, substitution at position 14, or any combination thereof, such that the motif retains self-cleavage activity in mammalian cells and is susceptible to inhibition by nucleoside analogs or aptamer-binding effectors. The motif may be flanked by additional sequences to facilitate cloning, transcription, or translational efficiency, and may be inserted into any position within the mRNA transcript, including the 5′ UTR, coding sequence, or 3′ UTR, without loss of function.

### Expression Vectors and Cell Lines

The invention provides DNA constructs for the expression of nucleic acid products under the control of the self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif. Each construct comprises a promoter operably linked to a nucleic acid encoding the ribozyme motif and a nucleic acid encoding a desired protein product. The promoter may be constitutive, such as the cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter, or inducible, such as the tetracycline-responsive promoter, depending on the desired application. The ribozyme motif is positioned such that it is transcribed as part of the same mRNA molecule as the coding sequence, ensuring that cleavage directly impacts the stability of the transcript encoding the protein of interest. The construct may further include a polyadenylation signal, introns, or enhancer elements to optimize transcription and mRNA processing. The DNA construct may be delivered as a plasmid, viral vector, or synthetic nucleic acid, and may be integrated into the genome via transfection, electroporation, or viral transduction. Stable cell lines are generated by transfecting mammalian cells with the construct and selecting for resistance to antibiotics such as puromycin or neomycin. Clonal cell lines exhibiting low basal expression and high inducibility upon effector administration are selected for use in screening or therapeutic applications. The invention further provides packaging cell lines engineered to produce recombinant viral vectors carrying the ribozyme-regulated construct, enabling scalable production of gene delivery vehicles. The ribozyme motif may be inserted into the 5′ or 3′ untranslated region of the transgene, or within an intron, such that cleavage occurs post-splicing. The system is functional in a wide range of host cells, including human, murine, and primate cell lines, as well as primary cells such as neurons, hepatocytes, and T cells. The invention further encompasses the use of modified promoters, such as tissue-specific or hypoxia-responsive promoters, to achieve spatially restricted gene regulation. The DNA construct may be co-transfected with a second construct encoding an aptamer that binds a specific effector molecule, enabling dual control of ribozyme activity. The invention further provides methods for manufacturing the DNA constructs via chemical synthesis, enzymatic assembly, or recombinant DNA technology, including restriction enzyme cloning, Gibson assembly, or CRISPR-mediated genome editing.

The invention encompasses a variety of methods for delivering the DNA constructs encoding the self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif into mammalian cells. These methods include transfection using lipid-based reagents such as lipofectamine or polyethylenimine, electroporation, microinjection, and ballistic delivery via gene gun. The constructs may also be packaged into viral vectors, including adeno-associated virus, lentivirus, adenovirus, or retrovirus, which enable efficient transduction of both dividing and non-dividing cells. The viral vectors may be pseudotyped with envelope proteins such as VSV-G to broaden tropism and enhance cellular uptake. Targeting ligands may be conjugated to the vector surface to direct delivery to specific cell types, such as neurons, cardiomyocytes, or tumor cells. The invention further provides methods for direct uptake of naked DNA or RNA constructs, including the use of cell-penetrating peptides or nanoparticle carriers. The constructs may be formulated into liposomes or polymer-based nanoparticles to protect the nucleic acid from degradation and enhance endosomal escape. The invention further describes methods for targeting the vector to cell membrane phospholipids through the use of lipid-modified nucleic acids or cholesterol conjugates. The delivery method may be selected based on the intended application, whether ex vivo, in vivo, or in situ, and may be optimized for route of administration, including intravenous, intramuscular, intratumoral, intranasal, or intracerebral delivery.

### Methods of Identifying Ribozyme Regulators

The invention provides methods for identifying agents that modulate the catalytic activity of the self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif. A primary screening method involves the use of a reporter gene expression system in which the ribozyme motif is inserted into the 3′ UTR of a luciferase or β-galactosidase gene. Cells stably expressing this construct are exposed to libraries of small molecules, and induction of reporter activity is measured as an indicator of ribozyme inhibition. The assay is performed in multi-well plates using automated plate readers to quantify luminescence or fluorescence. Compounds that induce reporter expression in ribozyme-containing cells but not in cells expressing a catalytically inactive mutant are identified as positive hits. The invention further provides a method for screening for effectors that bind to aptamer-grafted ribozyme motifs, wherein the aptamer is engineered to bind a specific ligand, and binding induces a conformational change that either activates or represses cleavage. The screening method involves transfecting cells with a construct encoding the aptamer-ribozyme fusion and a reporter gene, followed by exposure to a library of potential ligands. Induction or repression of reporter activity is measured to identify ligands with high specificity and affinity. The invention further provides a method for screening for inhibitors using random sequence libraries, wherein the ribozyme motif is flanked by randomized nucleotide sequences to identify structural contexts that enhance inhibitor sensitivity. The agents screened include nucleoside analogs, antibiotics, RNA-binding dyes, and synthetic small molecules. The invention further identifies specific compounds, such as toyocamycin, 5-fluorouridine, and 5-fluorouracil, as potent inhibitors of ribozyme activity, and provides methods for determining their concentration in biological samples by correlating reporter signal with known dose-response curves. The invention further describes the use of antisense oligonucleotides, including morpholinos and 2′-O-methyl RNAs, as inhibitors of ribozyme cleavage, and provides methods for evaluating their efficacy in cell-based assays.

### Exemplification

The invention is further exemplified by the generation of stable HEK293 cell lines expressing a luciferase reporter gene under the control of two copies of the N79 variant of the schistosome ribozyme. These cells exhibited low basal luciferase activity and were induced up to 365-fold upon treatment with toyocamycin. The ribozyme motif was incorporated into the 3′ UTR of the luciferase transcript and was shown to cleave efficiently in vitro and in vivo. The motif was further modified by extending loop III to six nucleotides and substituting position 7 with guanine, resulting in a variant termed N79 that exhibited enhanced cleavage kinetics. The motif was placed in different positions within the transcript, including the 5′ UTR and coding sequence, and retained full function. Tandem placement of two ribozyme motifs resulted in near-complete suppression of expression under basal conditions. Screening of a library of 58,076 compounds identified toyocamycin, 5-fluorouridine, and 5-fluorouracil as potent inducers of luciferase expression. The mechanism of inhibition was shown to involve covalent incorporation of these analogs into RNA during transcription, as demonstrated by in vitro transcription assays using modified nucleotide triphosphates. The combination of toyocamycin and 5-fluorouridine produced synergistic induction, achieving 670-fold expression with reduced toxicity compared to either compound alone. The system was successfully applied in vivo using adeno-associated virus vectors injected into the ears of nude mice, where a single pulse of the two-compound cocktail induced luciferase expression 37-fold over baseline. Northern blot analysis confirmed the accumulation of full-length mRNA upon inhibitor treatment, while qPCR demonstrated a 20-fold increase in transcript stability. The invention further demonstrates the use of the system to regulate endogenous gene expression by inserting the ribozyme motif into the 3′ UTR of a native gene via homologous recombination, resulting in inducible expression of the target protein.

### Methods:

The invention further provides detailed methods for the implementation of the ribozyme-based gene regulation system. Transfection of mammalian cells is performed using lipid-based reagents such as Lipofectamine 2000 or Fugene HD, with plasmid DNA concentrations optimized for each cell type. Transient reporter expression assays are conducted 24 to 48 hours post-transfection, with luciferase activity measured using a luminometer and normalized to protein concentration. Ribozyme activity is quantified by Northern blot analysis using probes specific to the cleavage fragments and full-length transcript. Catalytic rate is determined by incubating in vitro transcribed RNA in buffer containing magnesium chloride and measuring cleavage over time by denaturing gel electrophoresis. Non-invasive bioluminescent imaging is performed using an IVIS spectrum system, with luciferin injected intraperitoneally and photon emission captured over time. Adenosine and toyocamycin are purchased from commercial suppliers and dissolved in DMSO at 10 mM stock concentrations. All assays are performed in triplicate, and data are analyzed using statistical software to determine significance. The methods further include the preparation of recombinant adeno-associated virus genomes using triple transfection in HEK293 cells, followed by purification by iodixanol gradient centrifugation and titration by qPCR. The methods further encompass the use of ONPG assays to quantify β-galactosidase activity and the use of flow cytometry to measure fluorescent reporter expression in single cells.
