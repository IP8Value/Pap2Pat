## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention relates to novel compositions and methods for the treatment of cystinosis, a rare inherited metabolic disorder characterized by the pathological accumulation of cystine within lysosomes. Specifically, the invention encompasses the use of N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (diNACA) as therapeutic agents capable of reducing intracellular cystine levels and mitigating oxidative stress in affected tissues. The invention further includes pharmaceutical formulations and delivery systems tailored for systemic and ocular administration, particularly for the treatment of corneal and retinal manifestations of cystinosis, which are not adequately addressed by existing therapies. The compounds disclosed herein represent a new class of cystine-depleting agents with enhanced bioavailability, reduced toxicity, and superior tissue penetration compared to conventional aminothiols such as cysteamine, thereby offering a significant advancement in the clinical management of this debilitating disease.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- Cystinosis is a rare, autosomal recessive lysosomal storage disorder caused by mutations in the CTNS gene, which encodes the lysosomal cystine transporter cystinosin. The absence or dysfunction of this protein results in the progressive accumulation of cystine crystals within lysosomes of virtually all cell types, leading to widespread cellular dysfunction and multi-organ damage. The disease manifests in three distinct clinical forms: infantile nephropathic, intermediate, and non-nephropathic (ocular) cystinosis. The most severe and common form, infantile nephropathic cystinosis, typically presents within the first year of life with renal Fanconi syndrome, characterized by polyuria, polydipsia, dehydration, metabolic acidosis, hypophosphatemic rickets, and failure to thrive. Without intervention, this form leads to end-stage renal disease by early adolescence. Even with renal transplantation, patients continue to suffer from extra-renal complications, including myopathy, hypothyroidism, diabetes mellitus, and progressive ocular pathology. The non-nephropathic form, while less severe systemically, is marked by the accumulation of cystine crystals in the cornea, resulting in photophobia, ocular discomfort, and, over time, corneal opacity and visual impairment. Diagnosis is confirmed through the detection of elevated leukocyte cystine levels, genetic testing, or slit-lamp examination revealing characteristic corneal crystals. Current standard-of-care therapy relies exclusively on cysteamine, an aminothiol that reacts with intralysosomal cystine to form a mixed disulfide that can exit the lysosome via the lysine transporter. Oral formulations such as CYSTAGON® and PROCYSBI® are approved for systemic treatment, while ocular formulations like CYSTARAN® and CYSTADROPS® are used to reduce corneal crystal burden. However, these therapies are burdened by significant limitations: the unpalatable odor and gastrointestinal side effects of cysteamine lead to poor patient adherence, particularly in pediatric populations. Moreover, topical cysteamine eye drops require administration every waking hour, causing ocular irritation, blurred vision, and reduced compliance. Even with optimal dosing, cysteamine fails to penetrate deeply into avascular ocular tissues such as the corneal stroma and retina, leaving these structures vulnerable to progressive damage. Long-term complications include retinal degeneration, pigmentary disturbances of the peripheral fundus, and electroretinogram abnormalities indicative of photoreceptor dysfunction. Despite decades of clinical use, no therapy has been developed that effectively halts or reverses the neurodegenerative and ocular sequelae of cystinosis. There remains a critical and unmet medical need for novel, well-tolerated, and tissue-penetrating agents capable of depleting intracellular cystine and reducing oxidative stress in both systemic and ocular compartments.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention provides a novel method for the treatment of cystinosis through the administration of N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA) or (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (diNACA), either alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents. These compounds demonstrate superior cystine-depleting activity and enhanced cellular viability compared to cysteamine in human cystinotic fibroblasts, without inducing cytotoxicity at therapeutic concentrations. NACA is a lipophilic amide derivative of N-acetylcysteine (NAC), designed to overcome the poor membrane permeability and rapid systemic clearance that limit the efficacy of NAC. DiNACA, a dimeric prodrug of NACA, further extends the duration of action by serving as a sustained-release reservoir for both NACA and NAC upon hydrolysis within cells. Unlike cysteamine, which relies on lysosomal uptake and disulfide exchange, NACA and diNACA enter cells via passive diffusion and are metabolized intracellularly to replenish glutathione, thereby restoring redox balance and reducing oxidative stress—a key contributor to cellular damage in cystinosis. The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions for oral, intravenous, and topical ophthalmic administration, including novel ocular inserts that provide controlled, prolonged release of NACA or diNACA directly to the cornea and anterior chamber. These formulations eliminate the need for frequent dosing, reduce local irritation, and achieve therapeutic concentrations in ocular tissues unreachable by conventional eye drops. The invention further includes methods for determining the efficacy of treatment through measurement of intracellular cystine levels, glutathione concentrations, and markers of oxidative stress in patient samples. The disclosed compounds and delivery systems represent a paradigm shift in the management of cystinosis, offering a safe, effective, and patient-friendly alternative to existing therapies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- N-acetylcysteine (NAC) is a well-characterized antioxidant and precursor to glutathione, with established use in the treatment of acetaminophen overdose and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Its chemical structure consists of an acetylated cysteine molecule with a free thiol group, which confers its reducing capacity. However, NAC suffers from rapid systemic clearance, poor cellular uptake, and instability in biological fluids, limiting its utility in chronic conditions such as cystinosis. N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA), also known as NPI-001, is a lipophilic prodrug of NAC in which the carboxylic acid group of NAC is converted to an amide moiety. This structural modification enhances membrane permeability, prolongs half-life, and increases intracellular delivery of the active thiol. The chemical structure of NACA is characterized by the molecular formula C5H10N2O3S, with a central carbon backbone bearing an acetyl group, an amide linkage, and a terminal thiol. DiNACA, or NPI-002, is a symmetric dimeric compound formed by the oxidative coupling of two NACA molecules via a disulfide bridge between their cysteine residues, yielding the structure (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide). This dimer serves as a prodrug that, upon intracellular reduction, releases two molecules of NACA, which are subsequently hydrolyzed to NAC. Glutathione (GSH), the predominant intracellular antioxidant, is synthesized in two ATP-dependent steps: first by glutamate cysteine ligase (GCL), which catalyzes the condensation of glutamate and cysteine, and second by glutathione synthase (GS), which adds glycine. The rate-limiting step in GSH synthesis is the activity of GCL, which is subject to feedback inhibition by GSH itself. Therefore, the availability of cysteine is the primary determinant of GSH production. In cystinosis, lysosomal cystine accumulation depletes cytosolic cysteine, impairing GSH synthesis and leading to oxidative stress. NACA and diNACA bypass this limitation by delivering cysteine equivalents directly into the cytosol, thereby reactivating GSH synthesis and restoring redox homeostasis. Unlike cysteamine, which acts extralysosomally and requires specific transport mechanisms, NACA and diNACA diffuse freely across membranes, allowing them to replenish GSH in all cellular compartments, including mitochondria and nuclei. This broad intracellular distribution is critical for protecting neurons, retinal cells, and renal tubular epithelia from oxidative damage. The compounds are stable in aqueous formulation and compatible with a variety of pharmaceutical carriers, including polymeric matrices for sustained-release ocular inserts. The invention includes formulations for topical ocular delivery, wherein NACA or diNACA is incorporated into biodegradable or non-biodegradable inserts designed for placement in the conjunctival sac. These inserts provide continuous release over days or weeks, maintaining therapeutic concentrations in the cornea, aqueous humor, and vitreous without the need for repeated instillation. Oral formulations of NACA are also disclosed, including tablets and capsules with enteric coatings to protect against gastric degradation. The invention further encompasses methods for co-administration with other agents, such as cysteamine, antioxidants, or nephroprotective compounds, to achieve synergistic therapeutic effects. The compounds may be administered in a variety of dosage forms, including solutions, suspensions, emulsions, gels, and solid dosage forms, each formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients to ensure stability, bioavailability, and patient tolerability.

### Cytotoxicity of NACA and diNACA in Cystinotic Fibroblast Cell Culture

- Human cystinotic fibroblasts were cultured under standardized conditions and exposed to concentrations of NACA and diNACA ranging from 25 to 75 micromolar for periods of 48 and 72 hours. Parallel cultures were treated with cysteamine hydrochloride at equivalent concentrations as a positive control. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay, which measures the reduction of tetrazolium salt to formazan by metabolically active cells. The results demonstrated that neither NACA nor diNACA induced cytotoxicity at any concentration tested. In fact, treatment with NACA at 50 and 75 micromolar resulted in statistically significant increases in cell viability compared to untreated controls, with enhancements of up to 22% at 72 hours. DiNACA exhibited a similar pattern, with viability increases of 18% and 20% at the highest concentrations. In contrast, cysteamine at 25 micromolar failed to produce a statistically significant improvement in viability, while only the 50 and 75 micromolar concentrations achieved significance. These findings indicate that NACA and diNACA not only lack the cytotoxic potential associated with high-dose cysteamine but actively promote cellular health in cystinotic cells, likely through their ability to restore intracellular redox balance and reduce oxidative damage to mitochondria and other organelles.

### Screening of Cystine-Depleting Activity of NACA and diNACA

- Human cystinotic fibroblasts were seeded in culture flasks and incubated with 50 micromolar concentrations of NACA, diNACA, or cysteamine for 6, 24, 48, and 72 hours. Cells were harvested, lysed, and processed to quantify intracellular cysteine levels using a sensitive enzymatic assay based on papain activation by free thiols. Cysteine concentration was normalized to total protein content and expressed as a percentage of untreated control values. NACA demonstrated the most rapid and potent cystine-depleting activity, achieving statistically significant reductions at all time points, with a 48% decrease observed at 6 hours and a 72% reduction at 72 hours. DiNACA also produced significant reductions, with peak depletion of 65% at 48 hours and a 58% reduction at 72 hours. Cysteamine, in contrast, showed minimal activity at early time points, with only a 12% reduction at 6 hours and a 41% reduction at 24 hours, reaching statistical significance only at 72 hours. The rank order of potency was NACA > diNACA ≥ cysteamine throughout the time course, with NACA consistently outperforming cysteamine at earlier intervals. These results indicate that NACA and diNACA act more rapidly and sustainably than cysteamine to deplete intracellular cystine, likely due to their superior cellular uptake and intracellular conversion to bioactive thiols.

### Ophthalmic Tolerability of 1% NPI-001, 1% diNACA, 1% NAC or Vehicle

- Prototype ophthalmic formulations containing 1% w/v NACA (NPI-001), diNACA (NPI-002), NAC, or vehicle control were prepared in sterile aqueous buffer and evaluated for ocular tolerability in New Zealand White rabbits. Each formulation was administered as a single 50-microliter drop into the conjunctival sac of each eye, with observations recorded at 1, 4, 24, and 48 hours post-instillation. Clinical assessments included slit-lamp examination for corneal clarity, conjunctival redness, and lacrimation, as well as tonometry to measure intraocular pressure. No signs of corneal erosion, stromal haze, or iris inflammation were observed in any animal treated with NACA or diNACA. Mild, transient conjunctival redness was noted in one animal treated with NAC, resolving within four hours. No changes in intraocular pressure were detected in any group. In contrast, vehicle-treated animals showed no adverse effects, confirming the biocompatibility of the formulation base. The results demonstrate that NACA and diNACA are well-tolerated in ocular tissues at concentrations sufficient to exert therapeutic effects, supporting their suitability for sustained delivery via ocular inserts or topical formulations.

### NACA (NPI-001), diNACA (NPI-002), NAC or cysteamine ophthalmic insert.

- Non-biodegradable ophthalmic inserts were fabricated from ethylene-vinyl acetate copolymer matrices containing 10% w/w NACA or diNACA, molded into thin, flexible discs measuring 5 mm in diameter and 0.2 mm in thickness. Bioerodible inserts were prepared using polylactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) with molecular weights of 10,000 to 15,000 daltons, loaded with 15% w/w active agent and coated with a hydroxypropyl methylcellulose layer to modulate release kinetics. Inserts were sterilized by gamma irradiation and stored under desiccated conditions. In vitro release profiles demonstrated continuous release over 14 days for non-biodegradable inserts and 7–10 days for bioerodible inserts, with zero-order kinetics maintained throughout. In vivo application in rabbits involved surgical placement of the insert into the inferior conjunctival sac under topical anesthesia. Post-operative monitoring revealed no signs of infection, foreign body reaction, or corneal abrasion. Histopathological examination of ocular tissues at 14 days showed no inflammatory infiltrates or tissue necrosis. These inserts provide a novel, long-acting delivery system capable of maintaining therapeutic concentrations of NACA or diNACA in the cornea and anterior chamber for durations exceeding current topical therapies, thereby offering a transformative approach to the management of ocular cystinosis.

### Example 5

- In vitro release studies of NACA, diNACA, NAC, and cysteamine from ocular inserts were conducted in phosphate-buffered saline at 37°C with continuous agitation. Samples were collected at predetermined intervals and analyzed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) using validated methods for quantification. Release rates were adjusted by modifying polymer composition, crosslinking density, and coating thickness. Acceptance criteria for drug product were established based on release profiles matching those observed in vivo. Stability testing was performed under accelerated conditions (40°C/75% RH) and long-term storage (25°C/60% RH) over six months. No significant degradation of active agents was detected. In vivo studies in rabbits confirmed sustained delivery of NACA and diNACA to the cornea and aqueous humor, with peak concentrations achieved within 24 hours and maintained above therapeutic thresholds for up to 14 days. Nonclinical toxicology studies in rabbits and rats demonstrated no systemic toxicity, ocular irritation, or histopathological abnormalities following repeated administration. A phase 2/3 clinical trial is planned to evaluate the safety and efficacy of a biodegradable NACA insert in patients with corneal cystinosis over a three-month period, with endpoints including corneal crystal density, photophobia scores, and quality-of-life metrics.
