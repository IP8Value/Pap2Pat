## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention resides in the field of immunology and molecular medicine, specifically in the development of therapeutic and diagnostic methods for autoimmune and inflammatory diseases affecting skeletal muscle, including idiopathic inflammatory myositis and related immune-mediated myopathies. The invention further pertains to the use of insulin-like 6 (Insl6) protein and its functional derivatives as biological agents to modulate immune responses, particularly through the regulation of T-regulatory cell activity and suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokine production. This invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, gene therapy vectors, and fusion proteins containing Insl6 or its bioactive fragments for systemic or localized administration. The field includes methods for diagnosing disease susceptibility, monitoring disease progression, and evaluating therapeutic efficacy based on the quantification of Insl6 expression levels in biological samples derived from muscle tissue or peripheral blood. The invention bridges the gap between molecular signaling in immune-muscle crosstalk and clinical intervention strategies for chronic autoimmune conditions with limited treatment options.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- describe myositis

Idiopathic inflammatory myositis represents a group of rare, chronic autoimmune disorders characterized by persistent inflammation of skeletal muscle, leading to progressive weakness, structural damage, and functional impairment. The principal subtypes—polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and inclusion body myositis—share common clinical features such as elevated serum creatine kinase levels and histopathological evidence of lymphocytic infiltration, yet differ in their immunopathogenic mechanisms and tissue distribution of inflammation. Polymyositis is distinguished by endomysial infiltration of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells that directly surround and invade non-necrotic muscle fibers, whereas dermatomyositis exhibits perimysial and perivascular inflammation dominated by CD4+ T helper cells and complement-mediated vascular injury. In both conditions, macrophages, dendritic cells, and B lymphocytes contribute to the inflammatory milieu, which is further amplified by the local production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interferon-gamma, and interleukin-6. Despite advances in understanding the immunological underpinnings of these diseases, current therapies remain largely empirical, relying on corticosteroids and non-specific immunosuppressants that often fail to halt disease progression and are associated with significant long-term toxicity. There is a critical unmet need for targeted therapies that restore immune homeostasis without inducing broad immunosuppression. Furthermore, diagnostic biomarkers capable of distinguishing disease subtypes, predicting response to therapy, or identifying patients at risk for severe progression are lacking. Existing clinical tools rely on subjective assessments of muscle strength, non-specific serological markers, and invasive muscle biopsy with limited reproducibility. No molecular agent has been identified that directly modulates the balance between effector T cells and regulatory T cells within the muscle microenvironment in a manner that correlates with clinical improvement. The absence of a physiologically relevant, endogenous regulator capable of simultaneously suppressing pathogenic T cell activation and promoting muscle repair has hindered the development of precision therapies for these debilitating conditions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- summarize invention

The invention discloses a novel therapeutic and diagnostic paradigm centered on the insulin-like 6 (Insl6) protein as a key endogenous modulator of immune tolerance and muscle regeneration in autoimmune myopathies. It is demonstrated that Insl6 expression is dynamically regulated in response to muscle injury and inflammation, and that its deficiency exacerbates disease severity, while its restoration—either through genetic overexpression, recombinant protein administration, or gene delivery—markedly attenuates inflammation, reduces T cell infiltration, improves muscle function, and suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine production. The invention establishes Insl6 as a critical checkpoint molecule in the immune-muscle axis, capable of enhancing the suppressive function of regulatory T cells and inhibiting the activation and proliferation of pathogenic CD4+ and CD8+ T lymphocytes. This discovery enables the development of Insl6-based biologics, gene therapies, and diagnostic assays for the prevention, diagnosis, and treatment of autoimmune diseases characterized by T cell-mediated muscle inflammation, including polymyositis and dermatomyositis. The invention further provides methods for identifying patients with low Insl6 expression as candidates for targeted therapy and for monitoring therapeutic response through quantification of Insl6 levels in muscle biopsies or serum. The therapeutic utility of Insl6 is enhanced through its fusion with immunoglobulin Fc domains to prolong serum half-life and facilitate purification, and through the use of modified nucleic acid constructs for sustained in vivo expression. The invention thus offers a first-in-class approach to treating autoimmune myositis by harnessing a naturally occurring, muscle-derived immunoregulatory signal.

- describe Insl6 protein

The insulin-like 6 (Insl6) protein is a secreted polypeptide belonging to the insulin/relaxin superfamily, encoded by the INSL6 gene in humans and homologous genes in other mammals. It is synthesized as a preproprotein containing an N-terminal signal peptide, a B-domain, a C-domain, and an A-domain, which are processed post-translationally to yield a mature, biologically active form composed of two chains linked by disulfide bonds. The mature Insl6 protein is expressed predominantly in skeletal muscle under basal conditions and is significantly upregulated following acute muscle injury or inflammatory stimuli. Unlike other insulin-like peptides, Insl6 does not bind to classical insulin or IGF receptors but instead signals through a distinct, as yet uncharacterized G-protein-coupled receptor pathway that modulates intracellular cAMP levels and downstream transcriptional regulators involved in immune cell quiescence. Its expression is tightly regulated in a tissue-specific and context-dependent manner, with minimal expression observed in non-muscle tissues under physiological conditions. The protein exhibits no mitogenic or anabolic effects on healthy myofibers but exerts potent anti-inflammatory and regenerative properties in damaged or inflamed muscle environments. Insl6 is stable in circulation when fused to carrier proteins such as IgG1 Fc, and its biological activity is retained in recombinant, synthetic, or chemically modified forms, including retro-inverso analogs and D-amino acid-containing variants that confer enhanced proteolytic resistance.

- describe fusion protein

The invention encompasses fusion proteins comprising the Insl6 polypeptide covalently linked to a heterologous carrier protein, most notably the Fc region of human immunoglobulin G1 (IgG1), to enhance pharmacokinetic properties and bioavailability. The Insl6-Fc fusion protein is engineered to retain the native conformation and biological activity of Insl6 while benefiting from the extended serum half-life conferred by neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn)-mediated recycling. The fusion is constructed in-frame with a secretion signal peptide to ensure efficient extracellular release, and the junction between Insl6 and Fc is optimized to prevent steric hindrance of receptor binding. Alternative fusion partners include serum albumin, transferrin, or other long-circulating plasma proteins, as well as affinity tags such as His-tag or FLAG-tag for purification purposes. The fusion protein may be produced recombinantly in mammalian cell systems to ensure proper folding, disulfide bond formation, and glycosylation. The resulting fusion protein is capable of systemic administration and demonstrates superior tissue penetration and sustained activity compared to unmodified Insl6. The fusion protein may further be conjugated to targeting ligands such as agrin or M-cadherin domains to direct delivery specifically to skeletal muscle, thereby minimizing off-target effects and reducing the required therapeutic dose.

- describe method of treatment

The invention provides a method of treating autoimmune diseases characterized by muscle inflammation by administering to a subject in need a therapeutically effective amount of an Insl6 protein, a functional fragment thereof, or a fusion protein comprising Insl6. The method involves the systemic or localized delivery of the Insl6 agent to suppress the activation and proliferation of autoreactive CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, reduce the infiltration of inflammatory cells into skeletal muscle, lower the concentration of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α and IFN-γ, and enhance the frequency and suppressive capacity of CD4+CD25+Foxp3+ regulatory T cells within the muscle microenvironment. Treatment results in measurable improvements in muscle strength, endurance, and histological integrity, as assessed by rotarod performance, treadmill running distance, and reduction in inflammatory infiltrates on muscle biopsy. The method is applicable to both acute exacerbations and chronic management of autoimmune myopathies and may be used prophylactically in individuals with genetic or biomarker-defined susceptibility. The treatment is administered independently or in combination with conventional immunosuppressive agents, and its efficacy is monitored by serial measurement of Insl6 expression levels in biological samples.

- describe autoimmune diseases

The autoimmune diseases treatable by the methods of the invention include, but are not limited to, polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis, and other T cell-mediated inflammatory myopathies. The invention further extends to autoimmune conditions involving immune dysregulation and chronic inflammation in other tissues, including rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, multiple sclerosis, type 1 diabetes, inflammatory bowel disease, and autoimmune thyroiditis, where dysregulation of T-regulatory cell function contributes to disease pathogenesis. The therapeutic effect of Insl6 is particularly pronounced in diseases characterized by a Th1-dominant immune response, where interferon-gamma and tumor necrosis factor-alpha drive tissue destruction. The invention is also applicable to immune-mediated conditions associated with muscle wasting, such as cancer cachexia or sarcopenia of chronic disease, where inflammation contributes to muscle atrophy. The method is suitable for patients who are refractory to conventional therapies or who experience intolerable side effects from long-term corticosteroid use.

- describe Insl6 regulation of T-regulatory cells

The invention demonstrates that Insl6 directly enhances the functional capacity of CD4+CD25+Foxp3+ regulatory T cells by promoting their survival, stability, and suppressive activity in inflamed muscle tissue. In the presence of Insl6, regulatory T cells exhibit increased expression of cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-associated protein 4 (CTLA-4), programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), and interleukin-10, and demonstrate enhanced ability to suppress the proliferation of effector T cells in co-culture assays. In vivo, Insl6 overexpression leads to a significant increase in the proportion of regulatory T cells infiltrating inflamed muscle, while Insl6 deficiency results in their functional impairment and numerical decline. The mechanism involves modulation of intracellular cAMP signaling pathways that stabilize Foxp3 expression and inhibit the conversion of regulatory T cells into pro-inflammatory Th17-like phenotypes. Insl6 does not induce regulatory T cell differentiation from naïve precursors but rather potentiates the function of existing populations, making it a unique agent for restoring immune tolerance without inducing global immunosuppression.

- describe method of using Insl6 gene product

The invention provides a method of using the Insl6 gene product by introducing a nucleic acid encoding Insl6 into a subject’s cells to achieve sustained endogenous expression of the protein. The nucleic acid may be delivered via viral vectors such as adeno-associated virus, lentivirus, or adenovirus, or non-viral methods such as hydrodynamic injection, lipofection, or electroporation. The nucleic acid construct includes a promoter that drives expression in muscle tissue, such as the creatine kinase M promoter or a muscle-specific enhancer, ensuring localized production of Insl6 within the target organ. The method results in prolonged secretion of Insl6 into the circulation and local muscle microenvironment, leading to suppression of inflammation and improvement in muscle function over weeks to months. The gene product may be encoded by a modified RNA molecule, including chemically stabilized mRNA or RNA activator (RNAa) constructs that enhance endogenous gene expression, thereby avoiding genomic integration and reducing immunogenicity risks.

- describe method to treat autoimmune diseases

The method to treat autoimmune diseases comprises administering to a subject diagnosed with an autoimmune condition characterized by T cell-mediated inflammation a therapeutically effective amount of an Insl6 protein, a functional fragment of Insl6, or a fusion protein comprising Insl6. The administration is performed via parenteral, intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular routes, and may be repeated according to a regimen designed to maintain serum concentrations above a threshold level required for biological activity. The method is particularly effective in diseases where regulatory T cell dysfunction is a hallmark, and where pro-inflammatory cytokines such as interferon-gamma and tumor necrosis factor-alpha are elevated. Treatment leads to a statistically significant reduction in inflammatory cell infiltration, a decline in serum and tissue levels of pro-inflammatory mediators, and an increase in the frequency and suppressive capacity of regulatory T cells. Clinical outcomes include improved muscle strength, reduced fatigue, and decreased need for adjunctive immunosuppressive therapy. The method may be initiated at the time of diagnosis or during disease flare and is suitable for long-term maintenance therapy.

- describe method to treat muscle inflammation

The method to treat muscle inflammation involves the administration of an Insl6 agent to a subject exhibiting signs of inflammatory myopathy, including muscle weakness, elevated creatine kinase, and histological evidence of lymphocytic infiltration. The agent is delivered systemically or directly into affected muscle tissue, where it acts to inhibit the recruitment and activation of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, reduce macrophage accumulation, and suppress the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines. The method results in a reduction in muscle fiber atrophy, preservation of myofiber cross-sectional area, and restoration of normal muscle architecture. Functional recovery is demonstrated by improved performance on rotarod and treadmill tests, and histological analysis confirms decreased inflammatory scores and increased presence of regenerating myofibers. The method is effective in both acute and chronic models of muscle inflammation and is applicable to conditions such as polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and post-infectious myositis.

- describe method to diagnose myositis

The method to diagnose myositis comprises obtaining a biological sample from a subject suspected of having an inflammatory myopathy, quantifying the expression level of Insl6 in the sample, and comparing the measured level to a reference value derived from healthy individuals. The biological sample may be a skeletal muscle biopsy, peripheral blood mononuclear cells, or serum. Insl6 expression is measured by quantitative real-time PCR for mRNA, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay for protein, or immunohistochemistry for tissue localization. A significantly reduced level of Insl6 relative to the reference value is indicative of myositis, particularly polymyositis or dermatomyositis. The method may be used to distinguish myositis from other causes of muscle weakness, to assess disease severity, or to predict response to therapy. The diagnostic method may be integrated into automated platforms using high-throughput protein detection systems, mass spectrometry, or digital pathology analysis of immunohistochemically stained tissue sections.

- describe Insl6 expression in subjects

Insl6 expression is naturally present in skeletal muscle of healthy individuals at low to moderate levels and increases transiently following muscle injury or exercise. In subjects with autoimmune myositis, Insl6 expression is significantly and consistently downregulated in affected muscle tissue, regardless of disease subtype or duration. This reduction is not observed in subjects with non-inflammatory myopathies or in healthy controls. Serum levels of Insl6 are also lower in myositis patients compared to healthy individuals, suggesting systemic dysregulation of the protein. The degree of Insl6 suppression correlates with clinical severity, as measured by muscle strength, histological inflammation scores, and levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines. Subjects with very low Insl6 expression are more likely to exhibit rapid disease progression and poor response to conventional immunosuppressive therapy. In contrast, subjects with preserved or elevated Insl6 expression, either due to genetic factors or prior therapeutic intervention, demonstrate milder disease courses and better functional outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce Insl6 protein and its variants

The Insl6 protein is a member of the insulin-like/relaxin family of peptides, encoded by the INSL6 gene, and is expressed predominantly in skeletal muscle. The mature protein is derived from a preprohormone that undergoes proteolytic cleavage to remove the signal peptide and C-domain, resulting in a heterodimeric structure composed of A- and B-chains linked by disulfide bonds. Variants of Insl6 include naturally occurring polymorphisms, alternatively spliced isoforms, and recombinant derivatives with amino acid substitutions, deletions, or insertions that preserve or enhance biological activity. These variants may be generated by site-directed mutagenesis, directed evolution, or chemical synthesis and may include D-amino acid residues, retro-inverso configurations, or non-natural amino acids to improve stability, bioavailability, or receptor specificity. Variants may also be engineered to have altered binding kinetics, increased half-life, or reduced immunogenicity while retaining the core functional domains responsible for immune modulation.

- describe conjugation to fusion partner

The Insl6 polypeptide may be conjugated to a fusion partner via covalent or non-covalent linkages to enhance its pharmacological properties. Covalent conjugation may be achieved through chemical cross-linking agents such as NHS esters, maleimides, or hydrazones that react with lysine, cysteine, or carboxyl groups on the protein. Non-covalent conjugation may involve affinity tags such as biotin-streptavidin or His-tag-nickel interactions. The fusion partner may be selected from IgG1 Fc, serum albumin, transferrin, or polyethylene glycol. The conjugation may be site-specific, using engineered cysteine residues or unnatural amino acids to ensure homogeneity. The resulting conjugate exhibits improved serum persistence, reduced renal clearance, and enhanced tissue penetration.

- summarize discovery of Insl6's protective effects

The discovery that Insl6 exerts protective effects in models of autoimmune myositis represents a paradigm shift in the understanding of muscle-immune crosstalk. Previously unrecognized as an immunomodulatory factor, Insl6 is now established as an endogenous regulator that suppresses pathogenic T cell responses and enhances regulatory T cell function in inflamed muscle. This protective effect is observed across multiple experimental models, including genetic knockout, transgenic overexpression, and acute gene delivery, and is corroborated by clinical data from human myositis biopsies showing consistent downregulation of Insl6 in diseased tissue. The mechanism involves direct inhibition of T cell proliferation and cytokine production, as well as indirect modulation of the muscle microenvironment to favor immune tolerance. The discovery enables the development of Insl6-based therapies that target the root cause of immune dysregulation rather than broadly suppressing the immune system.

- describe regulation of T-regulatory cells

Insl6 enhances the suppressive function of CD4+CD25+Foxp3+ regulatory T cells by stabilizing Foxp3 expression through cAMP-dependent signaling pathways. In the presence of Insl6, regulatory T cells exhibit increased expression of CTLA-4, PD-1, and IL-10, and demonstrate enhanced capacity to inhibit the proliferation of effector T cells in vitro and in vivo. Insl6 does not induce the differentiation of naïve T cells into regulatory T cells but rather potentiates the activity of existing populations, thereby restoring immune homeostasis without inducing global immunosuppression. This selective enhancement of regulatory T cell function distinguishes Insl6 from other immunomodulatory agents and provides a novel mechanism for treating autoimmune diseases.

- outline method of treating autoimmune diseases

The method of treating autoimmune diseases comprises identifying a subject with an autoimmune condition characterized by T cell-mediated inflammation, determining the level of Insl6 expression in a biological sample, and administering a therapeutically effective amount of an Insl6 agent if the expression level is below a reference threshold. The Insl6 agent may be administered as a recombinant protein, fusion protein, gene therapy vector, or modified nucleic acid. The administration is repeated as necessary to maintain therapeutic levels, and the subject is monitored for clinical improvement, reduction in inflammatory markers, and normalization of regulatory T cell frequency. The method may be used for both treatment and prophylaxis in high-risk individuals.

- specify autoimmune diseases to be treated

The autoimmune diseases treatable by the method include polymyositis, dermatomyositis, inclusion body myositis, rheumatoid arthritis, systemic lupus erythematosus, multiple sclerosis, type 1 diabetes, inflammatory bowel disease, autoimmune thyroiditis, and Sjögren’s syndrome. The method is particularly effective in diseases where Th1-dominant immune responses and regulatory T cell dysfunction are central to pathogenesis.

- describe use of Insl6 expression to identify disease risk

The use of Insl6 expression as a biomarker allows for the identification of individuals at increased risk of developing autoimmune myositis or other T cell-mediated diseases. Subjects with genetically determined low Insl6 expression, or those exhibiting progressive decline in Insl6 levels over time, may be flagged for early intervention. Screening may be performed on peripheral blood mononuclear cells or muscle biopsy specimens using quantitative PCR, ELISA, or mass spectrometry. Early detection enables prophylactic administration of Insl6 agents to prevent disease onset or delay progression.

- outline pharmaceutical compositions and administration methods

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention are formulated for parenteral, oral, transdermal, or inhalation administration, depending on the mode of delivery. Parenteral formulations include sterile solutions, suspensions, or lyophilized powders containing Insl6 protein, fusion proteins, or nucleic acid constructs. Oral formulations may utilize enteric-coated capsules or nanoparticle carriers to protect the agent from gastric degradation. Transdermal patches may incorporate Insl6 into lipid-based matrices for slow release. Administration methods include single or repeated injections, implantable pumps, gene therapy vectors delivered via viral or non-viral means, and inhalable aerosols for systemic distribution. Dosing regimens are tailored to the route of administration, patient weight, disease severity, and pharmacokinetic profile of the formulation.
