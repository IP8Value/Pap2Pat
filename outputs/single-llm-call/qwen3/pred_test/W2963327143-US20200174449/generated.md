# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Metamaterials are artificial structures engineered at subwavelength scales to exhibit electromagnetic properties not found in naturally occurring materials. These materials are composed of periodic or aperiodic arrays of unit cells whose dimensions are significantly smaller than the operating wavelength, enabling precise control over the propagation, phase, amplitude, and polarization of electromagnetic waves. Unlike conventional materials whose responses are dictated by atomic or molecular composition, metamaterials derive their macroscopic electromagnetic behavior from geometric design, allowing for the realization of negative refractive indices, anomalous refraction, and extreme anisotropy. The ability to tailor the effective permittivity and permeability through structural arrangement has enabled breakthroughs in wave manipulation, including superlensing, cloaking, and ultra-compact beam steering. In recent years, two-dimensional variants known as metasurfaces have gained prominence due to their reduced thickness, ease of integration, and compatibility with planar fabrication techniques. These surfaces can impart abrupt phase shifts across an interface, effectively replacing bulky optical components with thin, lightweight alternatives. However, the performance of many existing metamaterials remains constrained by material losses, narrow operational bandwidths, and limited degrees of freedom in design, particularly when relying on resonant elements that are sensitive to fabrication tolerances and frequency variations.

## SUMMARY

The invention discloses a method for creating metadevices capable of precisely controlling the propagation of electromagnetic radiation through inverse electromagnetic design and additive manufacturing. This method begins by defining a desired optical functionality in terms of input and output electromagnetic field distributions, which are then used as boundary conditions in an optimization framework that solves Maxwell’s equations without predefining a geometric archetype. The system for generating metadevices integrates a computational algorithm that iteratively adjusts a spatial distribution of dielectric and air regions to maximize conformity with the target performance criteria, resulting in non-intuitive, high-efficiency structures that surpass conventional designs in bandwidth and functionality. Principal features of the invention include the use of low-loss dielectric materials such as high impact polystyrene to enable broadband operation, the application of a two-dimensional inverse design approach that avoids resonant constraints, and the seamless coupling of computational design with fused deposition modeling for direct fabrication of complex three-dimensional metadevices. The method enables the creation of polarization splitters, beam benders, and metalenses that operate over relative bandwidths exceeding 25%, with efficiencies greater than 60%, while maintaining subwavelength thicknesses and eliminating the need for metallic components or lithographic patterning.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Dielectric metadevices are electromagnetic structures composed entirely of non-conductive materials, designed to manipulate incident waves through spatial modulation of the refractive index rather than through plasmonic resonances or metallic inclusions. These devices exploit the phase delay induced by varying the effective optical path length across the structure, enabling control over wavefront shaping, polarization conversion, and angular deflection. Unlike conventional optical elements such as lenses, prisms, and polarizers, which rely on gradual phase accumulation through bulk material thickness, dielectric metadevices achieve similar or superior functionality within a fraction of a wavelength, offering dramatic reductions in size, weight, and complexity. The absence of conductive elements eliminates ohmic losses, thereby enhancing transmission efficiency and thermal stability, particularly in high-power or millimeter-wave applications where metallic losses become prohibitive.

Conventional optical elements suffer from inherent limitations including bulkiness, chromatic aberration, limited angular tolerance, and the inability to simultaneously control multiple degrees of freedom such as polarization, phase, and amplitude. These constraints arise from the reliance on geometric shapes whose optical response is governed by simple refraction or diffraction principles, which are fundamentally constrained by Snell’s law and the diffraction limit. As a result, achieving multifunctional behavior—such as simultaneous polarization splitting and beam bending—requires cascading multiple components, increasing system volume, alignment complexity, and insertion loss. The use of metamaterials and metasurfaces offers a pathway to overcome these limitations by enabling subwavelength engineering of the local electromagnetic environment, but existing designs are often restricted to predefined unit cell geometries such as rectangles, ellipses, or V-shaped antennas, which severely limit the available design space and lead to narrowband, low-efficiency performance.

Existing metasurface designs are further burdened by their dependence on resonant modes, which are inherently sensitive to fabrication imperfections and operating frequency. These resonances, while effective for phase control at a single wavelength, result in steep efficiency roll-offs outside narrow spectral bands and are incompatible with broadband applications. Moreover, the reliance on lithographic techniques for fabrication imposes constraints on material selection, layer thickness, and structural complexity, making it difficult to realize three-dimensional architectures that could enhance performance. The present invention overcomes these drawbacks by introducing an inverse electromagnetic design method that dispenses with preconceived geometric templates and instead optimizes the entire spatial distribution of material properties to satisfy a specified electromagnetic objective.

The objective-first inverse design algorithm treats the electromagnetic wave equation as a constrained optimization problem, where the electric field and spatial permittivity distribution are solved iteratively under boundary conditions that define the desired input and output field configurations. This approach does not assume any symmetry or periodicity a priori, allowing the algorithm to discover non-intuitive, heterogeneous structures that maximize performance across a broad frequency range. The optimization is performed using a non-convex solver that alternately fixes the electric field and permittivity distribution, solving each subproblem with linear methods until convergence is achieved. The resulting design is a binary distribution of dielectric and air regions, optimized to produce the target wave transformation with minimal reflection and maximal transmission.

Additive manufacturing plays a critical role in the practical realization of these designs, as it enables the direct fabrication of complex, three-dimensional dielectric structures with subwavelength feature resolution using low-loss polymers such as high impact polystyrene. HIPS is selected for its low dielectric loss tangent (tan δ < 0.003) and stable relative permittivity (ε′ ≈ 2.3) across the millimeter-wave band, making it ideal for broadband metadevices. The minimum device thickness required to achieve a full 2π phase shift is derived from the relation Δφ = 2π(n − 1)t/λ, yielding a thickness of approximately 2λ for HIPS at typical operating frequencies. This thickness constraint ensures sufficient phase modulation while maintaining structural integrity and manufacturability.

The electromagnetic properties of the fabricated metadevices are tested using a vector network analyzer and horn antennas configured to generate plane waves incident perpendicular to the device surface. Far-field angular transmission is measured over a range of angles and frequencies, with data compared against full-wave electromagnetic simulations performed using finite-difference time-domain methods. Simulated field distributions, including the Hz and Ez components for parallel and perpendicular polarizations, reveal the internal wave propagation mechanisms and confirm the absence of resonant behavior, instead demonstrating continuous phase accumulation through dielectric path modulation. The broadband operation of the devices is demonstrated through far-field intensity maps spanning frequencies from 26 GHz to 44 GHz, showing consistent deflection angles and high transmission efficiency across the band.

The invention further illustrates the design and fabrication of a 15° polarization splitter, a polarization-independent beam-bending metadevice, and flat metalenses with focal lengths of 2λ and 15λ, all fabricated from HIPS using fused deposition modeling. Each device is optimized for a specific objective, with boundary conditions tailored to the desired output field—whether a cylindrical wave for focusing or a diffracted plane wave for beam steering. The performance of these inverse-designed devices is shown to surpass that of conventional blazed gratings, with significantly reduced power leakage into higher diffraction orders and improved rejection ratios. Electron microscope images confirm the high fidelity of the printed structures, with feature sizes matching computational predictions to within fabrication tolerances.

The method for creating metadevices involves determining the desired functionality, specifying boundary conditions, inputting them into an inverse-design algorithm, receiving a structural design, converting it into a 3D-printer-compatible file format, and transmitting it to an additive manufacturing system for fabrication. The computing system responsible for this process includes a processor executing a specialized operating system, memory for storing electromagnetic simulation data, storage for design files, input/output interfaces for user interaction, and a network interface for communication with remote fabrication units. The additive manufacturing device may be directly connected to the computing system or integrated as a modular component, enabling automated design-to-fabrication workflows. The invention encompasses all modifications and variations that achieve equivalent electromagnetic functionality through inverse design and dielectric additive manufacturing, including operation at frequencies ranging from microwave to optical bands, provided that suitable low-loss materials are available for fabrication. The purpose of the invention is to provide a scalable, versatile, and high-performance platform for the rapid prototyping of compact electromagnetic components, enabling applications in communications, sensing, imaging, and radar systems. The advantages include broadband operation, high efficiency, low loss, mechanical robustness, and compatibility with low-cost manufacturing. The scope of the invention includes all embodiments, variations, and equivalents thereof that fall within the claims, which define the novel combination of inverse electromagnetic design and dielectric additive manufacturing for the creation of metadevices with unprecedented performance characteristics.
