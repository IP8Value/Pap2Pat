# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention  
The present invention resides in the field of oncology and targeted therapeutics, specifically in the development of fully human monoclonal antibodies and antibody-based conjugates directed against the receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 2 (ROR2), a cell surface antigen selectively overexpressed in a variety of human neoplastic tissues. The invention encompasses novel antibody molecules, antibody-drug conjugates, antibody-effector conjugates, chimeric antigen receptors, and diagnostic methods that exploit the tumor-restricted expression of ROR2 to enable precise targeting of malignant cells while sparing healthy tissues. The disclosed compositions and methods are designed for the treatment, detection, and monitoring of cancers characterized by aberrant ROR2 expression, including but not limited to breast, renal, osteosarcoma, melanoma, colorectal, oral, pancreatic, and stromal tumors. The invention further extends to the use of these agents in adoptive cell therapies, immunoconjugate formulations, and companion diagnostic assays, thereby establishing a comprehensive platform for ROR2-targeted precision medicine.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce cancer  
Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, characterized by uncontrolled cellular proliferation, evasion of immune surveillance, and metastatic dissemination. Despite advances in surgical, radiological, and chemotherapeutic interventions, many malignancies persistently recur or develop resistance to conventional therapies, underscoring the urgent need for more selective and potent treatment modalities. The heterogeneity of tumor biology and the frequent overlap of molecular markers between malignant and normal tissues have historically limited the therapeutic window of systemic interventions, resulting in significant off-target toxicity and suboptimal clinical outcomes.

- describe genetic alterations  
The molecular pathogenesis of cancer is driven by a complex interplay of somatic mutations, epigenetic dysregulation, and aberrant signaling pathway activation. Among the most compelling targets for therapeutic intervention are cell surface receptors that are either overexpressed, mutated, or constitutively activated in tumor cells but exhibit minimal or absent expression in healthy adult tissues. These tumor-associated antigens serve as molecular beacons for targeted delivery of cytotoxic agents, immune effectors, or imaging probes, enabling a paradigm shift from broad cytotoxicity to precision oncology.

- discuss ROR1 and ROR2  
ROR1 and ROR2 are structurally homologous type I transmembrane proteins belonging to the receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor family. Both proteins share conserved extracellular domains comprising an immunoglobulin-like domain, a cysteine-rich frizzled domain, and a kringle domain, followed by a transmembrane segment and an intracellular tyrosine kinase-like region. While ROR1 has been extensively studied as a target in hematologic and solid malignancies, ROR2 has emerged as a distinct and equally compelling target due to its restricted expression profile and functional role in tumor progression.

- describe ROR2 expression in cancer  
ROR2 is minimally expressed in most adult human tissues but is markedly upregulated in a broad spectrum of carcinomas and sarcomas, including triple-negative breast cancer, renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, colorectal cancer, oral squamous cell carcinoma, and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. Its overexpression correlates with aggressive disease phenotypes, enhanced cell migration, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and poor patient prognosis. In contrast to ROR1, ROR2 is rarely co-expressed with its paralog in tumor specimens, suggesting non-redundant biological functions and a unique therapeutic niche.

- discuss ROR protein structure  
The extracellular region of ROR2 contains three distinct domains: an N-terminal immunoglobulin-like fold, a central frizzled domain enriched in cysteine residues that mediates ligand binding, and a membrane-proximal kringle domain of unknown structural function. The intracellular domain, while lacking canonical kinase activity, retains tyrosine residues capable of phosphorylation and recruitment of downstream signaling adaptors. ROR2 interacts with Wnt5a and Wnt3a to activate non-canonical and canonical Wnt signaling pathways, respectively, promoting cytoskeletal reorganization, cell polarity, and invasive behavior in tumor cells.

- highlight challenges in developing ROR2 antibodies  
The development of therapeutic antibodies against ROR2 has been hindered by the scarcity of high-affinity, fully human clones capable of inducing robust internalization and cytotoxic payload delivery. Many previously described antibodies exhibit suboptimal binding kinetics, limited cross-reactivity with preclinical species, or poor internalization efficiency, rendering them unsuitable for antibody-drug conjugate applications. Furthermore, murine or chimeric antibodies often elicit human anti-mouse immune responses, compromising long-term therapeutic efficacy and safety.

- describe normal physiology of hROR2  
During embryonic development, ROR2 plays a critical role in skeletal morphogenesis, neural crest cell migration, and cardiac outflow tract formation. Its expression is tightly regulated and largely silenced in postnatal tissues, with residual activity confined to specific stem cell populations and mesenchymal progenitors. This developmental restriction underpins the tumor-selective nature of ROR2 expression and provides a compelling rationale for its exploitation as a therapeutic target.

- identify need for high-quality anti-ROR2 antibodies  
There exists a critical unmet need for fully human monoclonal antibodies with high affinity, exceptional specificity, and potent internalization capacity directed against ROR2. Such antibodies must be capable of mediating efficient internalization upon antigen binding, enabling effective delivery of cytotoxic payloads to tumor cells while maintaining low immunogenicity and favorable pharmacokinetics. The absence of such agents has impeded the clinical translation of ROR2-targeted therapies despite strong preclinical validation of the target.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce fully human antibodies binding to ROR2  
The present invention provides a novel class of fully human monoclonal antibodies that bind with high specificity and affinity to human ROR2, along with antibody derivatives, conjugates, and engineered cell therapies derived therefrom. These antibodies are isolated from immune libraries generated in transgenic mice expressing human immunoglobulin variable regions and are functionally selected for their ability to induce internalization and mediate tumor cell killing in the context of antibody-drug conjugate formats. The disclosed antibodies exhibit nanomolar binding affinities, cross-reactivity with cynomolgus monkey and murine ROR2 orthologs, and superior performance in both in vitro and in vivo models of ROR2-expressing cancers. The invention further encompasses methods for their production, formulation, and use in therapeutic and diagnostic applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce invention  
The invention relates to isolated, fully human monoclonal antibodies and antibody-based binding proteins that specifically recognize and bind to human ROR2 with high affinity and selectivity. These antibodies are derived from immune B-cell repertoires of transgenic mice expressing human immunoglobulin variable gene segments and are selected through a functional screening platform that concurrently evaluates antigen binding and internalization competence. The antibodies are suitable for use as naked therapeutics, antibody-drug conjugates, antibody-effector conjugates, chimeric antigen receptors, and diagnostic reagents. The invention further includes polynucleotides encoding the antibody sequences, expression vectors for their production, host cells for their recombinant expression, and pharmaceutical compositions for clinical administration.

- describe non-limiting embodiments  
In one embodiment, the invention provides a monoclonal antibody comprising a heavy chain variable region and a light chain variable region, wherein the heavy chain variable region comprises complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) with the amino acid sequences set forth in Table 3, and the light chain variable region comprises CDRs with the amino acid sequences also set forth in Table 3. In another embodiment, the antibody is an IgG1 isotype with a human kappa light chain constant region. In yet another embodiment, the antibody is conjugated to a cytotoxic agent via a cleavable linker. In further embodiments, the antibody is fused to a transmembrane domain and intracellular signaling domain to form a chimeric antigen receptor expressed on T cells. The invention also includes bispecific antibodies targeting ROR2 and a second immune effector antigen, such as CD3 or CD16.

- provide antibody selection process  
The antibodies of the invention were identified through a functional screening process involving immunization of transgenic mice expressing human immunoglobulin variable regions with recombinant human ROR2 extracellular domain fused to a Twin-Strep tag. Splenic B cells from immunized mice were enriched for ROR2-binding cells via magnetic-activated cell sorting, and RNA was extracted to generate cDNA libraries encoding human heavy and kappa light chain variable regions. These libraries were cloned into transposable expression vectors and stably transfected into murine pro-B cells to generate a cellular display library. Single-cell clones expressing surface-bound antibodies were sorted by flow cytometry based on dual staining for human IgG and ROR2 binding. Supernatants from sorted clones were screened in a secondary cell-killing assay using a cytotoxic agent conjugated to an anti-human Fc antibody, enabling identification of clones whose antibodies induced efficient internalization and payload delivery. Only clones demonstrating both high ROR2 binding and potent cytotoxicity in this assay were selected for sequence recovery and recombinant expression.

- describe antibody drug conjugates  
The invention encompasses antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) comprising a fully human anti-ROR2 antibody covalently linked to a cytotoxic agent via a cleavable linker. The linker is selected from a group consisting of protease-cleavable dipeptides, hydrazine linkers, thiourea linkers, self-immolative spacers, maleimide-thioether linkers, and disulfide-containing spacers. The cytotoxic agent is selected from the group consisting of auristatins, maytansinoids, calicheamicins, duocarmycins, anthracyclines, and pyrrolobenzodiazepines. In a preferred embodiment, the cytotoxic agent is PNU-159682, a derivative of the anthracycline daunorubicin, conjugated via a valine-citrulline-p-aminobenzyloxycarbonyl (vc-PABC) linker to the antibody’s heavy or light chain C-terminus. The drug-to-antibody ratio (DAR) of the conjugate ranges from 2 to 4, optimized for stability and potency.

- describe antibody effector conjugates  
The invention further provides antibody-effector conjugates (AECs) in which the anti-ROR2 antibody is conjugated to a non-cytotoxic effector molecule, such as a radionuclide, a fluorescent dye, a magnetic nanoparticle, or a cytokine. These conjugates are employed for diagnostic imaging, tumor localization, or immune modulation. In one embodiment, the antibody is labeled with a near-infrared fluorophore for intraoperative tumor margin delineation. In another embodiment, the antibody is conjugated to interleukin-2 or interferon-alpha to enhance local immune activation within the tumor microenvironment.

- describe chimeric antigen receptors  
The invention includes chimeric antigen receptors (CARs) comprising an extracellular antigen-binding domain derived from the anti-ROR2 antibody, a transmembrane domain, and an intracellular signaling domain comprising one or more costimulatory domains and a CD3ζ activation domain. The CAR is expressed on the surface of T cells, natural killer cells, or other immune effector cells, enabling them to recognize and lyse ROR2-expressing tumor cells. In a preferred embodiment, the CAR comprises a single-chain variable fragment (scFv) derived from the anti-ROR2 antibody, fused to a CD28 or 4-1BB costimulatory domain and the CD3ζ signaling domain. The CAR-expressing cells are expanded ex vivo and administered to patients for adoptive cell therapy.

- describe bi- or multispecific antibodies  
The invention provides bispecific or multispecific antibodies capable of simultaneously binding ROR2 and a second target antigen, such as CD3, CD16, PD-1, or CTLA-4. These molecules are engineered to redirect immune effector cells to ROR2-positive tumor cells or to block immune checkpoint pathways in the tumor microenvironment. In one embodiment, the bispecific antibody is a tandem scFv construct with one arm specific for ROR2 and the other for CD3, enabling T-cell engagement and tumor cell killing independent of MHC restriction.

- describe isolated polynucleotides and expression vectors  
The invention includes isolated polynucleotides encoding the heavy and light chain variable regions of the anti-ROR2 antibodies, as well as full-length heavy and light chain sequences. These polynucleotides are operably linked to promoters, enhancers, secretion signal sequences, and polyadenylation signals in expression vectors suitable for mammalian, insect, or bacterial host systems. The vectors are designed for stable integration into host genomes or for episomal maintenance, enabling high-yield production of recombinant antibodies. In a preferred embodiment, the expression vector contains a genomic IgG1 constant region with intronic sequences to permit alternative splicing and co-expression of membrane-bound and secreted antibody isoforms.

- describe use of antibodies for treating neoplastic disease  
The antibodies and antibody-based conjugates of the invention are used for the treatment of neoplastic diseases characterized by ROR2 overexpression, including breast cancer, renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, colorectal cancer, oral squamous cell carcinoma, pancreatic cancer, and stromal tumors. The antibodies are administered as monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy, radiation, immune checkpoint inhibitors, or other targeted agents. The treatment results in tumor regression, prolonged progression-free survival, and improved overall survival in preclinical models.

- describe method for treating neoplastic disease  
The method comprises administering to a subject diagnosed with a ROR2-expressing neoplasm a therapeutically effective amount of an anti-ROR2 antibody, antibody-drug conjugate, antibody-effector conjugate, or CAR-expressing T cell. The antibody is administered intravenously, subcutaneously, or intratumorally, in a single dose or multiple doses, at intervals ranging from daily to every three weeks. The dosage is determined based on body weight, tumor burden, and pharmacokinetic parameters, and ranges from 0.1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg. The method further includes monitoring ROR2 expression levels in serial biopsies or circulating tumor cells to assess treatment response.

- describe method for detecting neoplastic disease  
The method comprises contacting a biological sample obtained from a subject with an anti-ROR2 antibody, detecting the amount of antibody bound to ROR2 in the sample, and comparing the detected level to a reference level from a healthy control. An elevated level of ROR2 binding indicates the presence of a neoplasm or a disorder associated with ROR2 overexpression. The sample may be a tissue biopsy, blood, serum, urine, or cerebrospinal fluid. Detection is performed by immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, or imaging using a labeled antibody.

### Definitions

- define human antibody  
A human antibody is an antibody in which the variable regions are derived entirely from human immunoglobulin gene sequences, with no non-human amino acid residues introduced during cloning or engineering, and which does not contain murine or chimeric constant regions.

- define antibody-based binding protein  
An antibody-based binding protein is a polypeptide molecule that retains the antigen-binding specificity of an antibody but may lack one or more constant domains, including single-chain variable fragments, nanobodies, dAbs, or Fabs.

- define modified antibody format  
A modified antibody format refers to an antibody molecule that has been structurally altered to improve pharmacokinetics, stability, or effector function, including PEGylated antibodies, Fc-engineered antibodies, or antibody fragments with altered glycosylation patterns.

- define antibody derivative or fragment  
An antibody derivative or fragment is a portion of an intact antibody that retains antigen-binding capability, including Fab, F(ab′)2, Fv, scFv, dsFv, sdAb, or CDR-grafted constructs.

- describe intact antibody structure  
An intact antibody is a full-length immunoglobulin molecule comprising two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, each containing variable and constant domains, linked by disulfide bonds, and capable of mediating effector functions such as complement activation and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

- describe immunoglobulin genes  
Immunoglobulin genes are the genomic loci encoding the variable and constant regions of antibodies, including the heavy chain locus on chromosome 14, the kappa light chain locus on chromosome 2, and the lambda light chain locus on chromosome 22.

- describe heavy and light chain classification  
Heavy chains are classified into five isotypes—IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD, and IgE—based on their constant region structure, while light chains are classified into kappa and lambda types based on their constant domain sequence.

- describe heavy chain constant region  
The heavy chain constant region comprises four domains in IgG: CH1, hinge, CH2, and CH3, with the hinge region conferring flexibility and the CH2 domain mediating Fc receptor and complement binding.

- describe light chain constant region  
The light chain constant region consists of a single domain, CL, which is conserved between kappa and lambda isotypes and contributes to structural stability of the antibody.

- describe variable regions  
Variable regions of the heavy and light chains contain the antigen-binding site and are composed of framework regions and complementarity-determining regions that confer specificity and affinity for the target antigen.

- describe complementarity-determining regions  
Complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) are hypervariable loops within the variable domains of immunoglobulins that directly contact the antigen and determine binding specificity. Three CDRs are present in each heavy and light chain variable region.

- describe framework regions  
Framework regions are conserved sequences between CDRs that maintain the structural scaffold of the variable domain and support proper CDR conformation.

- describe single chain antibody  
A single chain antibody is a recombinant fusion protein comprising a heavy chain variable region and a light chain variable region connected by a flexible peptide linker, forming a single polypeptide chain with antigen-binding capability.

- describe single chain variable region fragment  
A single chain variable region fragment (scFv) is a minimal antigen-binding unit comprising the variable heavy and variable light domains linked by a peptide spacer, typically 10–25 amino acids in length.

- describe antibody-based binding proteins  
Antibody-based binding proteins encompass any engineered polypeptide derived from antibody sequences that retains antigen-binding function, including Fab, scFv, nanobodies, and DARPins.

- describe antigen-binding fragments  
Antigen-binding fragments are portions of an antibody that retain the ability to bind antigen, including Fab, F(ab′)2, Fv, and scFv, but lack the Fc region responsible for effector functions.

- describe Fab fragment  
A Fab fragment is a monovalent antigen-binding fragment comprising one heavy chain variable and constant domain (VH and CH1) and one light chain variable and constant domain (VL and CL), linked by a disulfide bond.

- describe F(ab′)2 fragment  
An F(ab′)2 fragment is a bivalent antigen-binding fragment formed by pepsin cleavage of an antibody, containing two Fab-like units connected by interchain disulfide bonds in the hinge region.

- describe Fd fragment  
An Fd fragment is the portion of a heavy chain comprising the variable domain and the first constant domain (VH and CH1), typically generated by papain digestion.

- describe Fv fragment  
An Fv fragment is the minimal antigen-binding unit comprising only the variable heavy and variable light domains, without the constant domains, held together by non-covalent interactions.

- describe disulfide stabilized Fvs  
Disulfide-stabilized Fvs (dsFvs) are engineered Fv fragments in which a disulfide bond is introduced between the VH and VL domains to enhance stability and prevent dissociation.

- describe single domain antibody  
A single domain antibody is an antigen-binding unit derived from a single variable domain, such as a camelid VHH or a humanized VHH, capable of binding antigen independently of a partner chain.

- describe isolated complementarity determining region  
An isolated complementarity determining region is a peptide sequence corresponding to one of the three CDR loops of a heavy or light chain, capable of binding antigen when grafted into a scaffold or fused to a carrier protein.

- describe methods for generating antibodies  
Methods for generating antibodies include immunization of transgenic animals, phage display, yeast display, ribosome display, and hybridoma technology, with the invention specifically employing immunization of transgenic mice followed by functional screening of cellular libraries.

- describe methods for generating scFv antibodies  
scFv antibodies are generated by PCR amplification of VH and VL genes, followed by fusion via a flexible linker and cloning into expression vectors for bacterial or mammalian production.

- describe methods for generating Fv fragments  
Fv fragments are generated by co-expression of separate VH and VL domains in a host cell, with or without disulfide stabilization, and purified by affinity chromatography.

- describe methods for generating dsFvs  
dsFvs are generated by introducing cysteine residues at conserved positions in VH and VL domains to form an interdomain disulfide bond, enhancing thermal and proteolytic stability.

- describe methods for generating dAbs  
dAbs are generated by cloning single variable domains from camelid or shark antibodies into bacterial expression vectors and selecting binders via phage display.

- describe methods for generating camelid single domain antibodies  
Camelid single domain antibodies are generated by immunizing llamas or alpacas, isolating VHH genes from peripheral blood lymphocytes, and screening libraries via phage display.

- describe conservatively modified variant  
A conservatively modified variant is a polypeptide sequence in which one or more amino acids are substituted with chemically similar residues, such as leucine for isoleucine or aspartic acid for glutamic acid, without altering the antigen-binding function.

- describe silent variations  
Silent variations are nucleotide substitutions in a coding sequence that do not alter the encoded amino acid sequence, often used to optimize codon usage for expression in a particular host system.

- describe percent identity  
Percent identity refers to the percentage of identical amino acid or nucleotide residues between two aligned sequences, calculated over a defined length and used to assess homology to germline or reference sequences.
