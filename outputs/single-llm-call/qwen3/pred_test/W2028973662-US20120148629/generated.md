# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate probiotics to nutritional composition

The present invention relates to a complete nutritional composition comprising a specific probiotic strain of the genus Lactococcus, particularly Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, for use in modulating allergic responses in individuals with established sensitization to allergens. The invention further relates to the integration of this probiotic strain into a nutritionally complete matrix suitable for oral administration, wherein the composition is designed to exert a therapeutic effect on the symptoms of allergic disease without altering the underlying state of allergen sensitization. The nutritional composition may be formulated for human infants, children, adults, or non-human mammals, and may be delivered in the form of infant formula, follow-up formula, weaning foods, yogurts, milk powders, or pet feed. The probiotic is stably incorporated into the matrix in a viable or inactivated form, and may be combined with prebiotics, hydrolyzed proteins, and other bioactive components to enhance its immunomodulatory efficacy within the gastrointestinal tract.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

- describe allergy prevalence

Allergic diseases have become one of the most prevalent chronic conditions worldwide, with a marked increase in incidence over the past several decades, particularly in industrialized nations. The burden of allergic disorders spans multiple organ systems and includes food allergies, atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis, and asthma. These conditions impose significant healthcare costs, reduce quality of life, and often persist into adulthood, creating long-term societal and economic consequences.

- explain atopic march

The progression of allergic disease from early-life food sensitization to respiratory manifestations such as asthma and allergic rhinitis is commonly referred to as the atopic march. This sequential development underscores the importance of early immune modulation, as sensitization to food allergens in infancy often precedes the onset of more severe and chronic allergic conditions later in life. The transition from cutaneous to respiratory allergic phenotypes is driven by persistent immune dysregulation and chronic inflammation, particularly within mucosal tissues.

- discuss immune system development

The development of immune tolerance during early life is a critical determinant of long-term allergic susceptibility. The gut-associated lymphoid tissue plays a central role in shaping immune responses to dietary and environmental antigens. Disruptions in microbial colonization, dietary exposure, and immune signaling during this developmental window can lead to aberrant T helper 2 (Th2) polarization, resulting in the overproduction of cytokines such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, which drive IgE synthesis, eosinophil recruitment, and mast cell activation.

## Prevention of Allergies can be Achieved on Different Levels:

- define primary prevention

Primary prevention refers to interventions aimed at inhibiting the initial development of allergen-specific immune sensitization, typically through avoidance of allergen exposure or immune modulation prior to the first clinical reaction. Such strategies target the immune system during its formative stages, with the goal of promoting tolerance rather than reactivity.

- define secondary prevention

Secondary prevention encompasses therapeutic approaches applied after allergen sensitization has occurred but before the onset of chronic or severe symptoms. These interventions aim to mitigate the clinical manifestations of allergy by modulating inflammatory pathways, suppressing effector cell activity, or reducing the local immune response at sites of allergen exposure, without necessarily reversing the underlying immunological memory.

- discuss modulating sensitization

Modulating sensitization involves altering the immune system’s recognition and response to allergens during the initial encounter, typically through the induction of regulatory T cells, dendritic cell tolerance, or suppression of Th2 differentiation. This approach is most effective when applied prior to or during the first exposure to an allergen.

- discuss modulating symptoms

Modulating symptoms focuses on dampening the downstream inflammatory cascade triggered by allergen re-exposure in already sensitized individuals. This includes reducing the release of histamine, leukotrienes, and cytokines such as IL-13, as well as inhibiting the recruitment and activation of eosinophils, mast cells, and other effector cells involved in allergic tissue damage.

- describe food allergens

Food allergens are proteins or glycoproteins derived from dietary sources such as cow’s milk, hen’s egg, wheat, soy, peanuts, tree nuts, fish, and shellfish. These molecules are resistant to digestion and can cross the intestinal epithelial barrier, where they are captured by antigen-presenting cells and presented to T lymphocytes, initiating an adaptive immune response.

- describe respiratory allergens

Respiratory allergens include airborne proteins from sources such as pollen, dust mites, animal dander, and mold spores. These allergens are inhaled and interact with mucosal surfaces of the upper and lower airways, triggering local IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation and subsequent bronchoconstriction, mucus hypersecretion, and airway remodeling.

- introduce lactococci

Lactococci are Gram-positive, non-motile, non-spore-forming lactic acid bacteria commonly used in dairy fermentation. Historically regarded as food-grade organisms, recent research has revealed their potential to interact with host immune cells and influence cytokine profiles in a strain-specific manner.

- discuss anti-allergic effect

Certain strains of lactococci have demonstrated the capacity to suppress Th2-driven inflammation and promote regulatory immune responses. These effects are not universally observed across all strains and are highly dependent on the bacterial surface components, metabolic byproducts, and interactions with host epithelial and immune cells.

- describe probiotic cultures

Probiotic cultures are defined as live microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confer a health benefit to the host. Their therapeutic potential in allergic disease stems from their ability to modulate dendritic cell function, enhance epithelial barrier integrity, and influence the balance between pro-inflammatory and regulatory cytokines.

- discuss skin allergy suppression

Probiotic administration has been associated with reduced severity of atopic dermatitis in clinical and preclinical models, likely through modulation of systemic immune tone and reduction of local inflammatory mediators in the skin.

- highlight need for reducing allergic reactions

Despite advances in allergen avoidance and pharmacologic therapy, there remains a critical unmet need for safe, non-pharmacological interventions capable of reducing the frequency and intensity of allergic reactions without inducing systemic immunosuppression.

- discuss nutritional needs of infants

Infants have unique nutritional requirements for growth, neurodevelopment, and immune maturation. Complete nutritional compositions must provide balanced macronutrients, essential fatty acids, vitamins, and minerals while minimizing allergenic potential and supporting immune homeostasis.

- describe complex matrices

Nutritional compositions for infants and young children often comprise complex matrices that include hydrolyzed proteins, oligosaccharides, lipids, and micronutrients in carefully calibrated proportions to mimic the composition of human milk and support physiological development.

- discuss need for relief in symptoms

There is a pressing clinical need for nutritional interventions that provide symptomatic relief to children and adults with established food or respiratory allergies, particularly those who continue to experience reactions despite dietary restrictions or pharmacologic treatment.

- discuss need for complete nutritional composition

A complete nutritional composition must not only deliver therapeutic benefits but also satisfy all daily nutritional requirements for the target population, ensuring that immune modulation does not come at the expense of growth, development, or metabolic health.

- discuss need for modulating allergic reactions

Effective modulation of allergic reactions requires targeted action on key cytokines and chemokines involved in tissue inflammation, without broadly suppressing protective immune responses. The ideal intervention selectively dampens pathogenic pathways while preserving immune competence.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- describe complete nutritional composition

The present invention provides a complete nutritional composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, optionally in combination with prebiotic fibers, hydrolyzed proteins, and other bioactive components, formulated for oral administration to individuals with established allergic sensitization. The composition is designed to reduce the clinical manifestations of food and respiratory allergies by modulating local immune responses in the gastrointestinal tract and associated lymphoid tissues, without altering systemic allergen-specific antibody levels or preventing initial sensitization.

- describe composition promoting secondary prevention

The composition of the invention is specifically configured to promote secondary prevention of allergic disease by suppressing the inflammatory cascade triggered by allergen re-exposure in sensitized individuals. It achieves this by downregulating the expression of IL-13 and associated Th2 chemokines such as CCL11 and CCL17 in the ileum, thereby reducing eosinophil recruitment and tissue inflammation without affecting IgE, IgG1, or mast cell protease levels.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- define patients

Patients eligible for treatment with the composition of the invention include individuals with documented clinical or immunological evidence of allergic sensitization to food or respiratory allergens, who continue to experience symptoms upon exposure, regardless of whether they are undergoing pharmacologic management or dietary avoidance.

- define complete nutritional composition

A complete nutritional composition, as defined herein, is a nutritionally balanced formulation that provides all essential macronutrients, micronutrients, and energy requirements for the target population, including proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, vitamins, minerals, and trace elements, in proportions appropriate for age, weight, and physiological status.

- define symptoms of allergies

Symptoms of allergies include, but are not limited to, pruritus, urticaria, angioedema, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, wheezing, coughing, nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, and in severe cases, anaphylaxis. These symptoms arise from the activation of mast cells, eosinophils, and Th2 lymphocytes in response to allergen re-exposure.

- define primary prevention of allergies

Primary prevention of allergies refers to interventions administered prior to the first clinical manifestation of allergy, with the goal of preventing the development of allergen-specific IgE or Th2 sensitization.

- define secondary prevention of allergies

Secondary prevention of allergies refers to interventions administered after allergen sensitization has occurred but prior to the establishment of chronic disease, with the goal of reducing the frequency, severity, or duration of allergic symptoms upon re-exposure.

- define weaning period

The weaning period is the transitional phase during which an infant’s diet shifts from exclusive milk feeding to the introduction of solid foods, typically occurring between four and six months of age, a time of heightened immune plasticity and allergen exposure risk.

- define sensitization

Sensitization is the immunological process by which the immune system recognizes a normally harmless substance as a threat, leading to the production of allergen-specific IgE antibodies and the priming of Th2 lymphocytes, without necessarily resulting in clinical symptoms.

- define immune boost

An immune boost, as used herein, refers to the enhancement of regulatory or anti-inflammatory immune pathways, such as the induction of interleukin-10 or interferon-gamma, without triggering generalized immune activation or systemic inflammation.

- define probiotic

A probiotic is a live or inactivated microorganism that, when administered in adequate amounts, confers a health benefit to the host by modulating immune responses, enhancing barrier function, or influencing microbial ecology.

- define prebiotic

A prebiotic is a non-digestible food ingredient that selectively stimulates the growth or activity of beneficial microorganisms in the gut, thereby conferring a health benefit, and may include oligosaccharides such as fructooligosaccharides and inulin.

### Effect of the Composition:

- describe effect on secondary prevention of allergies

The composition of the invention exerts a significant effect on secondary prevention by reducing the severity of clinical allergic symptoms upon allergen challenge in sensitized individuals, without altering systemic allergen-specific IgE or IgG levels, and without preventing the initial development of sensitization.

- describe reduced symptoms of food allergy

Administration of the composition leads to a clinically meaningful reduction in symptoms of food allergy, including decreased incidence of vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort, and skin reactions, as observed in both human and animal models.

- describe symptoms of allergies

Symptoms of allergies are mediated by the release of histamine, leukotrienes, and cytokines such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, which promote eosinophil infiltration, mucus production, and smooth muscle contraction in affected tissues.

- describe biochemical mediators

Biochemical mediators involved in allergic responses include mast cell-derived proteases such as MMCP-1, IgE-bound FcεRI receptors, eosinophil cationic proteins, and chemokines such as CCL11 and CCL17, which are upregulated in allergic inflammation.

- describe no effect on sensitization

The composition does not alter the level of allergen-specific IgE or the frequency of Th2 cell priming, indicating that it does not interfere with the initial process of sensitization but acts exclusively on the effector phase of the allergic response.

- describe influence on immune response

The composition influences the immune response by selectively downregulating IL-13 production in mesenteric lymph node cells and reducing the expression of IL-13 and its downstream chemokines CCL11 and CCL17 in the ileal mucosa, thereby attenuating local inflammation.

- describe effect on reducing allergy symptoms

The reduction in allergy symptoms is directly correlated with decreased expression of Th2-associated cytokines and chemokines in the ileum, suggesting a localized mechanism of action within the gut-associated lymphoid tissue.

- describe long-term effect on sensitization

The composition has no demonstrable long-term effect on the persistence or magnitude of allergen-specific sensitization, confirming its role as a symptom-modulating agent rather than a tolerance-inducing therapy.

### Composition of the Invention or for Use in the Invention:

- describe complete nutritional composition

The complete nutritional composition comprises a balanced blend of hydrolyzed milk proteins, medium-chain triglycerides, lactose, oligosaccharides, vitamins, minerals, and a therapeutically effective amount of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, formulated to meet the nutritional requirements of infants, children, or adults.

- describe probiotic of Lactococcus genus

The probiotic component of the composition is a strain of the genus Lactococcus, specifically Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, deposited as CNCM I-4154, which has been shown to modulate Th2 cytokine production and reduce allergic symptoms in sensitized individuals.

- describe strain L. lactis NCC 2287 (SL131)

The strain Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, designated SL131, is characterized by its ability to induce interferon-gamma and interleukin-10 in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells and to suppress IL-5 and IL-13 secretion in Th2-skewed cultures.

- describe strain L. lactis NCC 2180 (SL60)

The strain Lactococcus lactis NCC 2180, designated SL60, is a comparator strain that does not exhibit the same immunomodulatory profile as NCC 2287 and is not included in the composition of the invention.

- describe amount of probiotic

The composition contains between 1 × 10⁸ and 1 × 10¹⁰ colony-forming units (CFU) per gram or milliliter of the final product, sufficient to exert a therapeutic effect upon daily administration.

- describe mixing probiotic with composition

The probiotic is mixed with the nutritional matrix under controlled conditions to ensure viability and stability, and may be added prior to or after heat treatment, depending on the formulation.

- describe treatments to improve stability

Treatments to improve stability include lyophilization, spray-drying with protective agents such as maltodextrin or trehalose, encapsulation in enteric-coated microspheres, or the use of inactivated biomass.

- describe inactivated probiotic

The probiotic may be administered in an inactivated form, wherein the bacterial cells are heat-killed or otherwise rendered non-viable but retain their immunomodulatory surface components and bioactive metabolites.

- describe prebiotic

The composition may further comprise a prebiotic selected from the group consisting of fructooligosaccharides, galactooligosaccharides, inulin, and mixtures thereof, to support the local immune-modulating activity of the probiotic.

- describe oligosaccharides

Oligosaccharides are short-chain carbohydrates composed of 2 to 10 monosaccharide units, which are not digested in the upper gastrointestinal tract and serve as substrates for beneficial gut microbiota.

- describe fructooligosaccharide

Fructooligosaccharide is a prebiotic composed of fructose units linked to a sucrose molecule, which enhances the growth of beneficial bacteria and modulates immune cell function in the colon.

- describe mixture of fructooligosaccharide and inulin

A mixture of fructooligosaccharide and inulin in a ratio of 1:1 to 3:1 is preferred to synergistically enhance the immunomodulatory effects of Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287.

- describe amount of prebiotic

The prebiotic is present in the composition at a concentration of 0.5 to 5 grams per 100 kilocalories of energy content.

- describe apple extract

Apple extract, rich in polyphenols and pectin, may be included in the composition to enhance antioxidant capacity and support epithelial barrier integrity.

- describe synergistic effect with probiotic

The inclusion of apple extract results in a synergistic effect with Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287, enhancing the suppression of IL-13 and CCL17 expression in intestinal tissue.

- describe hydrolyzed proteins

Hydrolyzed proteins are derived from milk or other protein sources through enzymatic cleavage, resulting in peptides of low molecular weight that are less allergenic and more easily absorbed.

- describe source of hydrolyzed proteins

The hydrolyzed proteins are derived from bovine milk casein or whey, or from soy, rice, or pea proteins, and are treated with proteases such as trypsin, chymotrypsin, or pepsin to achieve a degree of hydrolysis of at least 15%.

- describe action of proteases

Proteases cleave intact allergenic proteins into smaller peptides that lack conformational epitopes recognized by IgE antibodies, thereby reducing their allergenic potential while preserving nutritional value.

### Target Group:

- describe target group

The target group for the composition includes infants, young children, and adults with documented food or respiratory allergies who continue to experience symptoms despite avoidance or pharmacologic therapy.

- describe young patients with food allergies

Young patients with food allergies are those under the age of five who have experienced at least one clinical reaction to a common food allergen and are at risk of progressing along the atopic march.

- describe patients with airborne or contact allergies

Patients with airborne or contact allergies include those with allergic rhinitis, asthma, or atopic dermatitis triggered by environmental allergens such as pollen, dust mites, or animal dander.

- describe non-human mammals

The composition may also be administered to non-human mammals, including dogs, cats, and horses, suffering from atopic dermatitis or food-induced gastrointestinal inflammation.

- describe infant formula, cereal, and baby food

The composition may be formulated as an infant formula, fortified cereal, or ready-to-eat baby food, suitable for oral consumption during the weaning period.

- describe starter or follow-up infant formula

Starter infant formula is intended for use from birth to six months, while follow-up formula is designed for infants aged six to twelve months and contains higher concentrations of iron, zinc, and other micronutrients.

- describe weaning period

The weaning period is the critical window during which the composition is most effective, as it coincides with the peak of immune plasticity and dietary allergen exposure.

- describe fragile, hospitalized, and elderly adults

Fragile, hospitalized, and elderly adults with chronic allergic conditions, including eosinophilic esophagitis or chronic urticaria, may benefit from the composition as an adjunct to standard care.

## Experimental Results, Section 1:

- test probiotics for allergic sensitization

Multiple probiotic strains were screened for their ability to modulate allergic sensitization using a murine model of food allergy. Only Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 demonstrated a significant effect on symptom severity without altering IgE or MMCP-1 levels.

- develop in vitro system for cytokine profiles

An in vitro system was developed using human peripheral blood mononuclear cells stimulated with allergens and co-cultured with probiotic strains to assess cytokine secretion profiles.

- induce Th2-skewing in human PBMC

Th2-skewing was induced in human PBMCs by culture in the presence of IL-4 and anti-IFN-γ antibodies, resulting in elevated IL-5 and IL-13 production.

- measure cytokine production by ELISA

Cytokine concentrations in culture supernatants were quantified using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays specific for IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-13.

- show L. lactis NCC 2287 induces IFNγ and IL-10

Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 significantly increased the secretion of interferon-gamma and interleukin-10 in Th2-skewed PBMC cultures, indicating a shift toward regulatory immune responses.

- show L. lactis NCC 2287 inhibits IL-5 secretion

Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 reduced IL-5 secretion by more than 60% compared to control cultures, without affecting cell viability.

- confirm results on mRNA level

Real-time PCR confirmed that L. lactis NCC 2287 downregulated mRNA expression of IL-5 and IL-13 in stimulated PBMCs.

- test in vivo effect in mouse food allergy model

In a murine model of ovalbumin-induced food allergy, oral administration of L. lactis NCC 2287 during the management phase significantly reduced clinical symptom scores.

- show L. lactis NCC 2287 reduces clinical symptoms

Mice treated with L. lactis NCC 2287 exhibited a 50% reduction in clinical allergy scores compared to untreated controls, with no effect observed when administered during the sensitization phase.

- measure MMCP-1 serum levels

Serum levels of mouse mast-cell protease-1 were measured by ELISA and found to be unchanged in treated mice, indicating that mast cell degranulation was not inhibited.

- show L. lactis NCC 2287 reduces MMCP-1 levels

No statistically significant reduction in MMCP-1 levels was observed, confirming that the probiotic’s effect is independent of mast cell inhibition.

- measure cytokine production by ex vivo restimulated lymphocytes

Mesenteric lymph node cells were isolated and restimulated with ovalbumin; IL-13 secretion was significantly reduced in cells from probiotic-treated mice.

- show L. lactis NCC 2287 modulates immune responses

Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 modulates immune responses by selectively suppressing IL-13 and Th2 chemokines in the ileum, without affecting systemic antibody production.

## Experimental Results, Section 2:

- evaluate L. lactis NCC 2287 on respiratory allergies

Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 was tested in human PBMCs from grass pollen-allergic donors and non-allergic controls to assess its effect on respiratory allergy pathways.

- show L. lactis NCC 2287 induces IFNγ and IL-10 in PBMC

In PBMCs from both allergic and non-allergic donors, L. lactis NCC 2287 induced interferon-gamma and interleukin-10 production, indicating broad immunomodulatory capacity.

- show L. lactis NCC 2287 inhibits IL-5 levels

IL-5 secretion was significantly reduced in PBMCs from grass pollen-allergic individuals following exposure to L. lactis NCC 2287.

- compare effect on grass pollen allergic and non-allergic individuals

The inhibitory effect on IL-5 was more pronounced in allergic individuals, suggesting a targeted action on dysregulated immune pathways.

- show L. lactis NCC 2287 has beneficial effect on respiratory allergies

The strain demonstrated a consistent ability to suppress Th2 cytokine production in response to respiratory allergens, supporting its use in allergic rhinitis and asthma.

## Analytical Methods:

### Reagents and Bacterial Biomass:

- produce bacterial biomass

Lactococcus lactis NCC 2287 was cultured in M17 broth under anaerobic conditions at 30°C until the stationary phase, harvested by centrifugation, washed in phosphate-buffered saline, and frozen in glycerol for long-term storage.

### Isolation and Culture of Th2-Skewed Human PBMC:

- isolate human PBMC

Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from venous blood using density gradient centrifugation over Ficoll-Paque.

- induce Th2 cytokine phenotype

Cells were cultured in the presence of recombinant human IL-4 and neutralizing anti-IFN-γ antibody for five days to establish a Th2-skewed phenotype.

- culture PBMC with L. lactis NCC 2287

Bacterial biomass was added to cultures at a multiplicity of infection of 10:1 and incubated for 24 to 72 hours.

- isolate PBMC from grass-allergic human donors

PBMCs were isolated from individuals with confirmed IgE-mediated grass pollen allergy and age-matched non-allergic controls.

- culture PBMC with grass allergen pollen extract

Allergen extract was added to cultures at a concentration of 10 μg/mL to stimulate allergen-specific responses.

- culture whole blood from grass-allergic and non-allergic human donors

Whole blood was cultured in the presence of L. lactis NCC 2287 and allergen extract to assess cytokine production in a more physiologically relevant system.

### Cytokine ELISA:

- measure cytokine levels by ELISA

Cytokine concentrations were measured using commercially available ELISA kits for IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, IL-13, and IFN-γ, following manufacturer protocols.

### Evaluation of ELISA Results:

- standardize ELISA results

Results were standardized against a reference standard curve generated from recombinant cytokines and normalized to total protein content.

### Quantitative Gene Expression Levels by Real-Time PCR:

- extract total RNA from stimulated PBMC

Total RNA was extracted using a commercial kit and treated with DNase to remove genomic DNA contamination.

- quantify gene expression by real-time PCR

Gene expression levels of IL-5, IL-13, and housekeeping genes were quantified using TaqMan probes and normalized to β-actin and GAPDH.

## OVA Food Allergy Mouse Model:

- sensitize mice with OVA

BALB/c mice were sensitized orally with ovalbumin and cholera toxin over seven weeks to establish food allergy.

- perform oral challenge and nutritional intervention

Mice were challenged with ovalbumin and simultaneously administered L. lactis NCC 2287 in drinking water during the management phase.

### Serum MMCP-1:

- quantify MMCP-1 in mouse serum by ELISA

MMCP-1 levels were quantified using a commercially available sandwich ELISA kit.

### Isolation and Culture of Mesenteric Lymph Node (MLN) Lymphocytes:

- isolate and culture MLN lymphocytes

Mesenteric lymph nodes were excised, homogenized, and cultured with or without ovalbumin for 72 hours.

### Cytokines in Supernatant of Mesenteric Lymph Node Cell and Splenocyte Cultures:

- measure cytokines in supernatant using multiplex kit

Cytokines IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-13 were measured using a multiplex bead-based immunoassay.

### Low Density Array Analyses of Gene Expression in Intestine:

- extract and quantify RNA from Ileum and Peyer's patch

RNA was extracted from ileal and jejunal tissues using a silica-membrane-based kit and quantified by spectrophotometry.

- perform reverse transcription

Reverse transcription was performed using random hexamers and Multiscribe reverse transcriptase.

- analyze gene expression using Low Density Array

Gene expression of IL-13, CCL11, CCL17, and housekeeping genes was analyzed using TaqMan Low Density Arrays on an ABI 7900HT system.
