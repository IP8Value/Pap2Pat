# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- define technical field of flexible electronic devices

The present invention relates to the field of flexible and stretchable electronic devices, particularly those designed for integration with adhesive surfaces for wearable, biomedical, and industrial sensing applications. The invention encompasses methods and systems for the scalable, high-yield fabrication of micron-scale electronic circuits and sensors on flexible substrates using a novel inverted microfabrication approach that eliminates the need for sacrificial layers and multi-step transfer printing. This technical domain includes the design, fabrication, and deployment of thin-film conductive traces, insulating polymer backbones, and integrated passive or active components that conform to non-planar, dynamic surfaces such as human skin, textiles, or industrial machinery. The invention is particularly suited for applications requiring disposable, single-use electronic systems where rapid, low-cost, and automated production is essential, including but not limited to electrophysiological monitoring, strain sensing, environmental sensing, and Internet of Things (IoT) sensor networks.

## BACKGROUND

- describe flexible electronic devices

Flexible electronic devices have emerged as critical components in next-generation wearable technologies, biomedical monitoring systems, and smart industrial sensors due to their ability to conform to curved, moving, or irregular surfaces without compromising electrical performance. Traditional rigid silicon-based electronics, while highly reliable, are incompatible with such environments due to their mechanical inflexibility and susceptibility to fracture under strain. To overcome these limitations, researchers have developed thin-film circuits composed of metallic traces and polymer insulators deposited on elastomeric substrates, enabling stretchability and conformability. However, the fabrication of these devices has historically relied on complex, multi-step processes such as transfer printing, which involve the deposition of sacrificial layers, solvent-based release, intermediate stamping, and precise alignment procedures. These methods are labor-intensive, time-consuming, and inherently low-yield, especially when scaled to industrial volumes. Furthermore, the reliance on intermediate transfer materials such as polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) introduces variability in pattern fidelity, increases contamination risks, and limits the range of compatible adhesives. As a result, despite the growing demand for flexible sensors in healthcare and consumer markets, widespread adoption remains hindered by manufacturing complexity, high cost, and inconsistent reproducibility.

## SUMMARY

- motivate need for scalable fabrication

The increasing global demand for disposable, wearable, and embedded electronic sensors—driven by the proliferation of the Internet of Things, personalized healthcare, and real-time environmental monitoring—has created an urgent need for fabrication methods that are not only precise and reliable but also scalable to high-volume production. Current microfabrication techniques, while effective in laboratory settings, are ill-suited for mass manufacturing due to their reliance on manual handling, multi-step processing, and expensive cleanroom infrastructure. The inability to integrate sensor fabrication directly with adhesive packaging steps has resulted in fragmented supply chains, elevated production costs, and limited accessibility for emerging markets. A scalable solution must therefore reduce process steps, eliminate intermediate materials, enable automated post-processing, and maintain high device yield without sacrificing performance. The invention addresses this need by introducing a fundamentally new paradigm in flexible electronics fabrication that enables direct, one-step integration of electronic circuits onto adhesive substrates through mechanical peel-off, thereby unlocking the potential for roll-to-roll manufacturing and widespread commercial deployment.

- introduce flexible circuits on flexible substrates

The invention introduces flexible electronic circuits formed on flexible substrates through a novel inverted fabrication scheme that reverses the conventional sequence of layer deposition and transfer. Rather than constructing circuits on rigid donor substrates and subsequently transferring them to flexible or adhesive receiving surfaces, the invention constructs the complete circuit directly on a weakly-adhering donor substrate, with the conductive and insulating layers arranged in an orientation that permits direct, clean detachment upon contact with an adhesive material. This approach results in a fully functional, self-adhering electronic device that requires no additional processing after peeling, enabling seamless integration with commercial adhesive products such as medical tapes, wearable patches, or industrial sensor mounts. The resulting circuits maintain high electrical integrity, precise geometric fidelity, and robust interconnectivity, even when fabricated with micron-scale features and multi-layer architectures.

- describe cost-effective fabrication method

The cost-effective fabrication method disclosed herein eliminates the need for sacrificial layers, solvent baths, intermediate transfer stamps, and precision alignment systems that are standard in conventional transfer printing. By leveraging the inherently weak adhesion between a thin metal film and a specially engineered donor substrate—such as a silicone-coated silicon wafer or a modified polydimethylsiloxane surface—the invention enables a single-step mechanical peel-off process that simultaneously packages the circuit and attaches it to the target adhesive. This reduction in process complexity translates directly into lower material costs, reduced processing time, decreased cleanroom occupancy, and diminished labor requirements. Moreover, the method is compatible with standard photolithography, sputtering, and evaporation equipment already prevalent in semiconductor and microfabrication facilities, allowing for rapid adoption without capital-intensive equipment upgrades.

- outline integration of micron structures

The invention enables the integration of micron-scale electronic structures—including serpentine traces, meandering resistors, capacitive electrodes, and interconnect networks—directly onto adhesive surfaces with sub-micron precision. These structures are patterned using standard photolithographic techniques and etched to form electrically continuous pathways that remain intact during the peel-off process. The use of a photodefinable polyimide as an insulating backbone provides mechanical support, electrical isolation, and thermal stability, while also enabling simultaneous patterning and encapsulation in a single processing step. The resulting micron structures exhibit minimal deformation, no delamination, and consistent electrical performance, even when subjected to bending, stretching, or dynamic mechanical stress during use.

- describe high-volume manufacturing

The disclosed fabrication method is uniquely suited for high-volume manufacturing through roll-to-roll processing. By fabricating circuits on flexible donor substrates such as Kapton or polyimide films, the invention enables continuous, automated production akin to conventional printing or tape-lamination processes. The peel-off step can be mechanized using rollers or tension-controlled peeling systems, allowing for the simultaneous detachment and packaging of thousands of sensors per minute. This capability transforms flexible electronics from a batch-process laboratory curiosity into a scalable industrial commodity, capable of meeting the demands of consumer electronics, point-of-care diagnostics, and large-scale environmental monitoring networks.

- introduce method to fabricate electronic device

The method to fabricate an electronic device comprises the sequential deposition of a weakly-adhesive interface layer on a support substrate, followed by the deposition of an electrically conductive layer, the formation of an insulating layer over the conductive layer, the patterning of the insulating layer to define circuit features, the etching of exposed conductive material to form interconnects, and the final attachment of a flexible adhesive substrate to the circuit structure. Upon application of a controlled peel force, the completed circuit detaches from the donor substrate and adheres permanently to the target adhesive, resulting in a fully functional, self-contained electronic device ready for immediate use.

- describe circuit formation and attachment

Circuit formation is achieved through the precise patterning of a conductive metal layer—such as gold or chromium—using photolithography and wet or dry etching techniques, with the insulating layer formed from a photodefinable polyimide that serves both as a dielectric and a structural backbone. The circuit is not transferred via solvent or stamping but is instead directly attached to the target adhesive through mechanical adhesion during peel-off. The attachment is immediate, uniform, and free of voids or misalignments, as the circuit is formed in intimate contact with the adhesive surface from the moment of detachment. This eliminates the risk of delamination, air pockets, or misregistration that plague conventional transfer methods.

- introduce method to fabricate circuit

The method to fabricate a circuit involves depositing a thin, weakly-adhering polymer layer onto a rigid or flexible support substrate, depositing a conductive metal film over the polymer layer, coating the conductive film with a photodefinable insulating polymer, exposing the insulating layer to ultraviolet light through a photomask to define circuit geometry, developing the exposed regions to reveal underlying conductive material, etching the exposed conductive regions to form circuit traces, and then pressing a flexible adhesive substrate onto the completed circuit structure. Upon application of a controlled tensile force, the circuit is cleanly peeled from the donor substrate, leaving behind a fully formed, self-adhering electronic circuit on the target adhesive.

- describe electronic component placement

Electronic components, including passive elements such as resistors, capacitors, and inductors, as well as active components such as thin-film transistors or integrated circuits, may be placed into pre-formed wells or recesses on the donor substrate prior to the deposition of the conductive layer. These components are embedded within the circuit structure during the subsequent layering and patterning steps, ensuring mechanical stability and electrical connectivity. The method supports both single-layer and multi-layer configurations, allowing for the integration of complex circuitry with minimal post-processing. Components are held in place by the surrounding polymer matrix and are electrically connected via conductive traces formed during the etching process.

- outline applications of disclosed technology

The disclosed technology finds application in a broad range of fields, including continuous health monitoring through wearable electroencephalogram (EEG), electrocardiogram (EKG), and electromyogram (EMG) sensors; real-time strain and pressure sensing in sports equipment, robotics, and industrial machinery; disposable diagnostic patches for point-of-care testing; smart packaging for food and pharmaceutical monitoring; and embedded sensors for structural health monitoring in aerospace and automotive systems. The method’s compatibility with roll-to-roll processing further enables its use in mass-market consumer products such as smart clothing, fitness trackers, and interactive interfaces. The ability to produce high-fidelity, single-use sensors at low cost opens new opportunities for large-scale deployment in low-resource settings, pandemic response, and remote healthcare.
