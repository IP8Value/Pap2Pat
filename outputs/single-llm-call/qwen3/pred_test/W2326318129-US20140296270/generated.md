## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular oncology and medicinal chemistry, specifically to the development of small-molecule inhibitors targeting the Src homology 2 (SH2) domain of signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) and, unexpectedly, STAT1. It further pertains to pharmaceutical compositions, diagnostic kits, and therapeutic methods for the treatment of cancers characterized by aberrant activation of STAT3 and/or STAT1 signaling pathways. The invention also falls within the domain of targeted cancer therapies, particularly those aimed at overcoming chemoresistance and radioresistance in epithelial malignancies such as head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, breast cancer, and other solid tumors with constitutive transcription factor activation. The compounds and methods described herein are designed for use in precision oncology, where inhibition of transcriptional programs driven by STAT proteins is critical to halting tumor progression, eliminating cancer stem cells, and preventing disease recurrence.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) is a cytoplasmic transcription factor that, upon activation by phosphorylation at tyrosine residue 705, dimerizes and translocates to the nucleus to regulate the expression of genes involved in cell proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, immune evasion, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition. Its persistent activation is observed in over half of all human cancers and is frequently associated with poor prognosis, metastatic potential, and resistance to conventional therapies. While numerous upstream kinases—such as EGFR, JAKs, and Src family members—can drive STAT3 activation, direct targeting of STAT3 itself has proven challenging due to the lack of a classical enzymatic active site and the high structural similarity between its SH2 domain and those of other STAT family members, particularly STAT1. Existing inhibitors, including peptidomimetics, oligonucleotide decoys, and covalent small molecules like Stattic, suffer from poor bioavailability, off-target reactivity, or insufficient potency to achieve sustained pathway suppression in vivo. Moreover, many of these agents fail to distinguish between STAT3 and STAT1, despite their divergent roles in tumorigenesis and immune regulation. The clinical need for a non-covalent, orally bioavailable, high-affinity inhibitor capable of selectively disrupting STAT3-mediated transcription without compromising STAT1-dependent antiviral and tumor-suppressive functions has remained unmet. Recent evidence, however, suggests that in certain tumor contexts, including radioresistant head and neck cancers, simultaneous inhibition of both STAT3 and STAT1 may be therapeutically advantageous, necessitating a new class of dual-targeting agents with well-characterized pharmacological profiles.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel class of small-molecule inhibitors derived from a virtual ligand screening campaign targeting the phosphotyrosine-binding pocket of the STAT3 SH2 domain. Through iterative structure-based design, 2D similarity screening, and 3D pharmacophore modeling, lead compounds were optimized to achieve nanomolar binding affinity and potent inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation and nuclear translocation. These compounds, exemplified by C188-9 and its derivatives, bind reversibly and with high specificity to the STAT3 SH2 domain, effectively blocking its interaction with upstream receptor phosphopeptides and preventing downstream transcriptional activation. Unexpectedly, these inhibitors also demonstrate potent activity against STAT1, inhibiting its phosphorylation and nuclear accumulation with comparable efficacy, thereby enabling dual suppression of both transcription factors in tumors where their co-activation drives therapy resistance. The compounds induce apoptosis in multiple cancer cell lines, including those resistant to chemotherapy and radiation, and significantly suppress tumor growth in xenograft models following oral administration. Structural analysis reveals that the inhibitors exploit subtle differences in the electrostatic and hydrophobic architecture of the STAT3 SH2 pocket relative to STAT1, enabling high-affinity binding while maintaining selectivity over other STAT family members. The invention further encompasses general chemical formulas defining the core scaffold of these inhibitors, including substituted N-naphthyl benzenesulfamides with specific functional groups at the 3- and 4-positions of the naphthalene ring that enhance binding energy, solubility, and metabolic stability. These compounds are shown to inhibit the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic shuttling of activated STAT3 and STAT1, destabilize STAT-DNA complexes, and suppress expression of oncogenic and radioresistance-associated genes. The invention provides methods for identifying additional analogs through similarity screening, molecular docking, and structure-activity relationship analysis, and describes their use in combination with radiotherapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted agents to enhance therapeutic outcomes. The compounds are formulated into pharmaceutically acceptable compositions for systemic delivery and are suitable for use in diagnostic kits to assess target engagement in patient biopsies.

- describe objects, features, and advantages of the invention

The primary object of this invention is to provide a new class of orally bioavailable, non-covalent, small-molecule inhibitors that selectively and potently disrupt the function of STAT3 and STAT1 by binding to their SH2 domains. A key feature of the invention is the discovery that certain derivatives of N-naphthyl benzenesulfamide, particularly those bearing bulky hydrophobic substituents at the 3-position of the naphthalene ring, exhibit sub-micromolar inhibition of STAT3 phosphorylation and nanomolar binding affinity as measured by microscale thermophoresis and surface plasmon resonance. Another feature is the unexpected dual inhibition of STAT1, which confers a therapeutic advantage in tumors where both transcription factors contribute to radioresistance and immune evasion. The advantages of the invention include high oral bioavailability, low toxicity in preclinical models, sustained tumor concentration, and the ability to eradicate cancer stem cells in vivo. The compounds overcome the limitations of prior STAT3 inhibitors by avoiding covalent modification, achieving high selectivity over other STAT proteins, and demonstrating efficacy in models of therapy-resistant disease. The invention further enables the development of companion diagnostics to identify patients whose tumors exhibit activated STAT3/STAT1 signaling, thereby facilitating personalized treatment strategies.

- describe Stat3 inhibitors by structure and method of development

The inhibitors of the invention are characterized by a core structure of N-naphth-1-yl benzenesulfamide, with specific substitutions at the 3-position of the naphthalene ring and the 4-position of the phenyl ring that collectively enhance binding to the STAT3 SH2 domain. The 3-substituent is selected from triazole-3-yl-mercapto, binaphthyl, substituted phenyl, or extended aliphatic chains that occupy a hydrophobic sub-pocket adjacent to the phosphotyrosine binding site. The 4-substituent is typically a hydroxyl group or a bioisostere capable of forming a hydrogen bond with Arg-609 and Tyr-640 in the STAT3 SH2 domain. These compounds were developed through a multi-stage process beginning with virtual ligand screening of over 920,000 compounds against the crystal structure of the STAT3 SH2 domain, followed by 2D fingerprint similarity searches using C188 as a query, and subsequent 3D pharmacophore alignment to prioritize compounds with optimal spatial arrangements of hydrogen bond donors, hydrophobic regions, and aromatic planes. The top-ranking compounds were synthesized, tested in binding and cellular assays, and subjected to quantitative structure-activity relationship modeling to define the physicochemical determinants of potency. The resulting inhibitors demonstrate a clear correlation between hydrophobic bulk at the 3-position and increased binding energy, as validated by molecular dynamics simulations and van der Waals interaction energy calculations.

- describe binding-pocket of Stat3 SH2 domain

The binding pocket of the STAT3 SH2 domain is a shallow, solvent-exposed cleft formed by conserved residues including Arg-609, Tyr-640, Ser-611, Lys-591, and Ile-642, which collectively recognize the phosphotyrosine motif of activated receptor tyrosine kinases. The pocket is distinguished by a deep hydrophobic groove adjacent to the pY-binding site that accommodates aromatic and aliphatic side chains of small-molecule inhibitors. Unlike the STAT1 SH2 domain, which has a narrower and more polar entrance due to differences in residue composition at positions 642 and 645, the STAT3 pocket permits greater steric bulk and enhanced hydrophobic interactions. The inhibitors of the invention exploit this difference by introducing bulky 3-substituents that extend into this region, forming favorable van der Waals contacts with Ile-642 and Leu-645 while avoiding steric clashes present in the STAT1 binding site. Molecular modeling reveals that the hydroxyl group at the 4-position of the phenyl ring forms a critical hydrogen bond with the backbone carbonyl of Tyr-640, a feature absent in many prior inhibitors and essential for high-affinity binding.

- describe selectivity of compounds for Stat3 over Stat1

Although the inhibitors demonstrate potent activity against both STAT3 and STAT1, their binding affinity for STAT3 is consistently higher, with dissociation constants in the low nanomolar range compared to slightly higher values for STAT1. This selectivity arises from subtle differences in the amino acid sequence and conformational dynamics of the SH2 domains. Specifically, the STAT3 SH2 domain contains a more flexible loop region near residue 645, allowing for induced-fit accommodation of larger substituents, whereas the corresponding region in STAT1 is more rigid and sterically constrained. Additionally, the electrostatic potential surface of the STAT3 pocket is more favorable for the sulfamide moiety of the inhibitors, which engages in a network of hydrogen bonds not fully replicated in STAT1. Despite this, the inhibitors retain significant activity against STAT1, which is therapeutically beneficial in contexts where both transcription factors drive oncogenesis. The selectivity profile was confirmed through direct binding assays, phosphoflow analysis, and electrophoretic mobility shift assays, demonstrating that the compounds do not inhibit STAT5 or STAT6 at concentrations up to 100 μM.

- describe effects of compounds on breast cancer cell lines

The inhibitors significantly reduce viability and induce apoptosis in multiple breast cancer cell lines, including MDA-MB-468, SUM-149, and BT-549, which exhibit constitutive STAT3 and STAT1 activation. Treatment leads to downregulation of Bcl-xL, survivin, cyclin D1, and VEGF, and upregulation of caspase-3 and PARP cleavage. In cancer stem cell-enriched populations isolated from patient-derived xenografts, the compounds suppress sphere-forming capacity and reduce expression of stemness markers such as CD44, ALDH1, and Nanog. The inhibitors also reverse epithelial-mesenchymal transition, as evidenced by increased E-cadherin and decreased vimentin expression. In vivo, daily oral administration of the lead compound reduces tumor burden and delays recurrence in orthotopic breast cancer models, even after cessation of treatment, suggesting eradication of tumor-initiating cells.

- provide GeneID numbers for human Stat1 and Stat3

The human STAT1 gene is encoded by the GeneID 6772, located on chromosome 2q32.2, and comprises 17 exons encoding a 750-amino acid protein. The human STAT3 gene is encoded by the GeneID 6774, located on chromosome 17q21.2, and consists of 20 exons encoding a 770-amino acid protein. Both genes share approximately 70% sequence identity in their SH2 domains, with key differences at residues 642 (Ile in STAT3, Val in STAT1) and 645 (Leu in STAT3, Arg in STAT1), which are critical for the differential binding of the inhibitors disclosed herein.

### III. EMBODIMENTS FOR TARGETING STAT3

- introduce STAT proteins

The STAT family comprises seven members—STAT1, STAT2, STAT3, STAT4, STAT5a, STAT5b, and STAT6—each activated by distinct cytokine and growth factor receptors to regulate gene expression in immune, hematopoietic, and epithelial cells.

- describe Stat1 and Stat3 functions

STAT3 promotes cell survival, proliferation, and immune suppression, whereas STAT1 mediates antiviral responses, cell cycle arrest, and tumor suppression, though in certain contexts it can also drive inflammation and therapy resistance.

- detail Stat1 and Stat3 isoforms

STAT1 exists as full-length α and truncated β isoforms, while STAT3 exists as α and β isoforms, differing in their C-terminal transactivation domains; STAT3α is the oncogenic isoform, while STAT3β may act as a dominant-negative regulator.

- describe Stat3α and Stat3β differences

STAT3α contains a C-terminal transactivation domain essential for transcriptional activation of oncogenic genes, whereas STAT3β lacks this domain and can compete with STAT3α for DNA binding, thereby attenuating its activity.

- discuss Stat3α role in transformation and cancer

STAT3α is the primary driver of cellular transformation, tumor maintenance, and metastasis in epithelial cancers, and its constitutive activation is sufficient to induce tumorigenesis in transgenic models.

- describe Stat3β antagonizing v-Src transformation

STAT3β can inhibit v-Src-induced transformation by competing with STAT3α for dimerization and DNA binding, thereby suppressing expression of proliferative genes.

- discuss targeting Stat3α while sparing Stat1

The invention provides compounds that preferentially inhibit STAT3α over STAT1, thereby minimizing disruption of STAT1-dependent antitumor immunity while maximizing suppression of oncogenic signaling.

- outline strategy for targeting Stat3α

The strategy involves designing inhibitors that exploit structural differences in the SH2 domain of STAT3α compared to STAT1, particularly in the hydrophobic pocket adjacent to the phosphotyrosine binding site.

- describe biochemical features of Stat3β

STAT3β shares the same SH2 domain as STAT3α but lacks the C-terminal transactivation domain, rendering it incapable of driving transcriptional activation despite retaining dimerization and DNA-binding capacity.

- exploit differential biochemical features

The inhibitors are designed to bind the SH2 domain with high affinity, thereby blocking dimerization regardless of isoform, but their therapeutic effect is primarily mediated through inhibition of STAT3α-driven transcription.

- develop chemical compound targeting Stat3α

The compounds disclosed herein are optimized for binding to the SH2 domain of STAT3α, with modifications that enhance affinity and selectivity over other STAT proteins.

- describe virtual ligand screening approach

Virtual ligand screening was performed using the ICM molecular modeling software, docking over 920,000 compounds from commercial libraries into the crystal structure of the STAT3 SH2 domain, with scoring based on binding energy, solvation, and shape complementarity.

- identify lead small-molecule inhibitors of Stat3

Three lead compounds—C188, C3, and C30—were identified from the screening, with C188 exhibiting the highest potency in inhibiting STAT3 phosphorylation and binding.

- develop 2nd generation Stat3 inhibitors

Second-generation inhibitors were developed by modifying the C188 scaffold through 2D similarity screening and 3D pharmacophore analysis, yielding compounds such as C188-9 with improved binding affinity and cellular potency.

- develop 3rd generation Stat3 inhibitors

Third-generation inhibitors were synthesized by introducing sulfamide modifications, extended aromatic systems, and fluorinated substituents to enhance metabolic stability and tumor penetration.

The strategy involves inhibiting STAT3 signaling to disrupt the transcriptional programs that sustain stemness, thereby depleting the tumor-initiating cell pool.

### Exemplary Materials and Methods

- isolate Stat3 SH2 domain structure

The crystal structure of the human STAT3 SH2 domain (PDB ID: 1BG1) was isolated and prepared for docking using the ICM molecular modeling software.

- convert structure to ICM-compatible system

The structure was converted to an ICM-compatible format, protonated at physiological pH, and energy-minimized using the ICM force field.

- retrieve Stat1 SH2 domain coordinates

The coordinates of the human STAT1 SH2 domain (PDB ID: 1BFH) were retrieved from the Protein Data Bank and aligned with STAT3 for comparative analysis.

- select commercial chemical databases for screening

Eight commercial chemical databases containing over 920,000 compounds were selected for virtual screening, including Life Chemicals, ChemBridge, Enamine, and Maybridge.

- define binding pocket

The binding pocket was defined as a 15 Å radius sphere centered on the phosphotyrosine-binding site, encompassing residues Arg-609, Tyr-640, Ile-642, and Leu-645.

- perform flexible docking calculation

Flexible docking was performed using the ICM docking algorithm with torsional freedom for all ligand rotatable bonds and side-chain flexibility for key residues.

- select compounds for purchase and testing

Top-ranking compounds with docking scores better than −12 kcal/mol and favorable interaction profiles were selected for purchase and biological validation.

- perform Stat3 SH2/pY-peptide binding assay

A surface plasmon resonance assay was performed using biotinylated EGFR pY1068 peptide and recombinant STAT3 SH2 domain to measure inhibition by test compounds.

- perform immunoblot assay

Immunoblotting was performed using antibodies against pSTAT3 (Tyr705), total STAT3, and β-actin to assess inhibition in cell lysates.

- perform similarity screen

A 2D fingerprint similarity screen was performed using the Tanimoto coefficient with C188 as the reference compound.

- perform electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA)

EMSA was performed using radiolabeled hSIE probe and nuclear extracts to assess disruption of STAT3-DNA binding.

- perform molecular modeling

Molecular modeling was performed using Schrödinger Suite 2010, including OPLS-2005 force field minimization and molecular dynamics simulations.

- perform confocal and high-throughput fluorescence microscopy

Confocal microscopy was used to visualize nuclear translocation of STAT3-GFP fusion proteins in live cells treated with inhibitors.

- perform breast cancer cell line apoptosis assay
