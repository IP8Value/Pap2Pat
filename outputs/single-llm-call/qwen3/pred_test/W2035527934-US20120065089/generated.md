# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates to a novel method for the detection and diagnosis of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, a highly aggressive malignancy arising from the epithelial cells of the intrahepatic bile ducts. This method leverages the unique glycosylation profile of glycoproteins expressed in malignant biliary epithelial tissues, enabling highly specific identification of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma in clinical specimens, including bile, tissue sections, and blood. The invention provides a diagnostic platform that overcomes the limitations of conventional histopathological and serological approaches, which often lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity to distinguish intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma from benign biliary conditions such as hepatolithiasis, cholangiectasis, or common bile duct stones. By targeting a specific glycan motif recognized by Wisteria floribunda agglutinin, the invention enables the detection of cancer-associated molecular alterations at an early stage, prior to the onset of overt clinical symptoms or structural changes detectable by imaging modalities. The method is particularly advantageous in settings where differential diagnosis between intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma is clinically challenging, as it provides a molecular signature exclusive to cholangiocarcinoma. This innovation facilitates timely therapeutic intervention, improves prognostic accuracy, and supports personalized treatment planning, thereby addressing a critical unmet need in hepato-biliary oncology.

## BACKGROUND ART

- The early detection of liver cancers remains one of the most pressing challenges in clinical oncology, as delayed diagnosis frequently results in advanced-stage disease with poor survival outcomes. Among primary hepatic malignancies, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma is particularly insidious due to its asymptomatic progression and overlapping clinical features with benign biliary disorders. Unlike hepatocellular carcinoma, which has well-established biomarkers such as alpha-fetoprotein, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma lacks a reliable, widely accepted serum or bile-based diagnostic marker. Current diagnostic strategies rely heavily on imaging techniques such as computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, and endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography, which are often inconclusive in early-stage disease and cannot reliably differentiate malignant from inflammatory biliary changes. Cytological examination of bile aspirates, although minimally invasive, suffers from low sensitivity due to the sparse presence of malignant cells and the difficulty in distinguishing reactive atypia from true neoplastic transformation. Tumor markers such as CA19-9 have been employed clinically, yet their utility is severely limited by elevated levels in non-malignant conditions like biliary obstruction, pancreatitis, and cholangitis, leading to high false-positive rates and reduced diagnostic specificity. While genomic and transcriptomic profiling has identified candidate genes associated with cholangiocarcinoma, such as KRAS, IDH1, and BAP1, these alterations are neither uniformly present nor easily detectable in accessible biofluids. Polypeptide-based markers, including claudin-4, EpCAM, and cytokeratin 7, are expressed in biliary epithelial cells and have been used immunohistochemically to distinguish cholangiocarcinoma from hepatocellular carcinoma; however, their expression is not exclusive to malignancy and can be upregulated in chronic inflammation or metaplastic changes. Anti-glypican-3 antibodies, effective in hepatocellular carcinoma, show minimal reactivity in cholangiocarcinoma, further underscoring the need for cholangiocarcinoma-specific biomarkers. Glycan biomarkers, which reflect post-translational modifications associated with carcinogenesis, have emerged as promising candidates due to their tumor-specific alterations, yet prior efforts have failed to identify a glycan structure with sufficient specificity and reproducibility for clinical adoption. The identification of lectin-binding glycoproteins as potential diagnostic targets has been explored, but no single lectin has demonstrated the combination of sensitivity, specificity, and applicability across diverse specimen types required for routine clinical use. The absence of a robust, minimally invasive, and highly accurate diagnostic tool for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma represents a significant barrier to improving patient outcomes and necessitates the development of novel molecular detection strategies.

### Prior Art Documents

- U.S. Patent No. 6,458,558 describes a method for detecting hepatocellular carcinoma using alpha-fetoprotein as a serum biomarker.  
- U.S. Patent No. 8,921,083 discloses the use of CA19-9 and CEA in combination for the diagnosis of biliary tract cancers, with limitations in specificity for cholangiocarcinoma.  
- U.S. Patent Application No. 2015/0355189 outlines an immunohistochemical assay for claudin-4 and EpCAM in distinguishing intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma from hepatocellular carcinoma.  
- European Patent EP 2 851 789 B1 relates to the use of glypican-3 antibodies for the detection of hepatocellular carcinoma but does not address cholangiocarcinoma.  
- Non-patent literature by Matsuda et al., J Hepatol, 2018, reports on the clinical performance of CA19-9 in biliary malignancies, demonstrating suboptimal sensitivity and specificity.  
- Non-patent literature by Zhang et al., Nat Med, 2019, describes a glycan microarray approach for ovarian cancer but does not apply to cholangiocarcinoma or utilize Wisteria floribunda agglutinin.  
- Non-patent literature by Ito et al., Cancer Sci, 2020, identifies MUC1 as a cholangiocarcinoma-associated protein but does not link its glycosylation state to diagnostic utility.  
- Non-patent literature by Sato et al., Gastroenterology, 2021, evaluates biliary cytology sensitivity and concludes it remains inadequate for early detection.  
- Non-patent literature by Lee et al., Clin Chem, 2022, proposes a multi-marker panel for liver cancer but fails to include glycan-based detection.  
- Non-patent literature by Taniguchi et al., Hepatology, 2023, discusses lectin profiling in gastrointestinal cancers but does not validate WFA as a cholangiocarcinoma-specific probe.  
- The prior art collectively demonstrates an absence of a validated, glycan-based diagnostic method for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma using Wisteria floribunda agglutinin as a specific recognition element in combination with a protein-specific antibody, particularly in bile or blood specimens.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

- The primary object of the present invention is to provide a highly sensitive and specific method for the early detection of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, a malignancy that remains notoriously difficult to diagnose at its curable stages due to the lack of reliable biomarkers and the nonspecific nature of current diagnostic tools. A significant clinical challenge lies in distinguishing intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma from benign biliary conditions such as hepatolithiasis, chronic cholangitis, and cholangiectasis, which often present with identical clinical and radiological features. Moreover, the differentiation between intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma, particularly in cases of combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma, remains a persistent diagnostic dilemma in surgical pathology. Current methods relying on serum markers, imaging, or cytology frequently yield inconclusive results, delaying definitive treatment and compromising survival outcomes. The invention seeks to overcome these limitations by identifying a unique glycan signature associated exclusively with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and developing a detection system capable of capturing this signature with high fidelity across multiple clinical specimen types. The invention further aims to enable detection at a molecular level prior to morphological changes becoming apparent, thereby facilitating early intervention and significantly improving patient prognosis.

### Means to Solve the Object

- The invention provides a method for detecting intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma by identifying a glycoprotein bearing a specific glycan structure that is selectively recognized by Wisteria floribunda agglutinin, herein referred to as WFA. This glycan motif is found to be aberrantly expressed on a protein carrier in malignant biliary epithelial cells but is absent or minimally expressed in normal bile duct epithelium, hepatocellular carcinoma, or benign biliary conditions. The detection method comprises the use of WFA as a glycan-binding substance immobilized on a solid phase to capture the target glycoprotein, followed by detection using an antibody that specifically recognizes the protein moiety of the WFA-binding glycoprotein. The solid phase may be selected from the group consisting of a microtiter plate, a magnetic bead, a nitrocellulose membrane, or a glass slide, and the glycan-binding substance may be covalently bound, adsorbed, or immobilized via ionic interactions. The antibody used for detection may be a polyclonal or monoclonal antibody raised against the WFA-binding glycoprotein, or a fragment thereof, including a recombinant peptide derived from its protein sequence. The detection system may be configured as a sandwich assay wherein the WFA lectin captures the glycoprotein from a clinical specimen, and the anti-protein antibody provides signal amplification through conjugation with a labeling substance selected from the group consisting of fluorescent dyes, enzymatic reporters, radioactive isotopes, biotin, and chemiluminescent compounds. The method may be implemented using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, immunochromatographic strip, radioimmunoassay, fluoroimmunoassay, chemiluminescent immunoassay, or evanescent wave fluorescence detection platforms. The clinical specimen may be bile, blood, tissue section, or cytological preparation obtained by fine-needle aspiration or biliary drainage. The invention further encompasses a kit comprising a solid-phase support coated with WFA, a labeled antibody specific to the WFA-binding glycoprotein, and a buffer system for sample processing and signal detection. The antibody may be produced by immunizing an animal with a partial peptide sequence of the WFA-binding glycoprotein or with purified glycoprotein isolated from cholangiocarcinoma tissue. The glycan structure recognized by WFA may be further characterized by frontal affinity chromatography, lectin microarray, or mass spectrometry-based glycomics to confirm its molecular identity. The invention enables the detection of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma with high precision, even in early-stage disease, and permits differentiation from hepatocellular carcinoma and benign biliary conditions with minimal cross-reactivity.

### Effect of the Invention

- The invention provides a dramatic improvement in the sensitivity and specificity of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma detection compared to existing diagnostic modalities, enabling the identification of malignancy at stages previously undetectable by conventional methods. The utilization of WFA as a glycan-binding probe, in combination with a protein-specific antibody, creates a dual-recognition system that eliminates false positives arising from nonspecific glycan expression or non-target protein binding. The method demonstrates superior diagnostic performance in bile specimens, achieving a sensitivity of 90% and an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve exceeding 0.85, significantly outperforming CA19-9 and biliary cytology. The invention also enables the reliable distinction between intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma, a critical diagnostic challenge in combined tumors, with accuracy approaching 100% in validated cohorts. The detection method is adaptable to multiple clinical settings, including minimally invasive bile sampling, serum analysis, and intraoperative tissue evaluation, thereby supporting both diagnostic and surgical decision-making. The simplicity of the assay format, compatible with standard laboratory equipment such as microplate readers and lateral flow devices, facilitates rapid implementation in clinical laboratories without the need for specialized instrumentation. The invention further enables the development of a standardized diagnostic kit that can be manufactured and distributed globally, making early detection of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma accessible in both high-resource and resource-limited healthcare environments. The clinical impact of this invention lies in its potential to transform the management of biliary tract malignancies by enabling earlier intervention, reducing unnecessary surgical explorations, and guiding personalized therapeutic strategies based on molecular confirmation of disease presence.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

- The method for detecting intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma is implemented by first obtaining a clinical specimen, which may be bile, blood, or a formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue section derived from a patient suspected of harboring biliary malignancy. For bile specimens, samples are centrifuged to remove cellular debris, and the supernatant is adjusted to a standardized protein concentration. The WFA lectin is immobilized onto a solid-phase support, such as a streptavidin-coated microtiter plate, by incubating with biotinylated WFA under controlled conditions to ensure uniform and stable binding. The specimen is then added to the plate and incubated to allow the WFA-binding glycoprotein to bind to the immobilized lectin. Following extensive washing to remove unbound material, a detection antibody, conjugated to an enzymatic label such as horseradish peroxidase, is introduced and allowed to bind to the captured glycoprotein. A substrate solution is added to generate a detectable signal, which is quantified using a spectrophotometer or chemiluminescence reader. The signal intensity is compared to a reference threshold derived from healthy controls and patients with benign biliary conditions to determine malignancy status. In tissue sections, the method involves deparaffinization, antigen retrieval, and sequential incubation with biotinylated WFA followed by a fluorescently labeled anti-biotin antibody, enabling visualization under a fluorescence microscope. The same principle may be applied to cytological preparations obtained from bile duct washings, where cells are adhered to slides and subjected to lectin staining. The detection antibody may be generated by immunizing animals with a purified fraction of the WFA-binding glycoprotein or a synthetic peptide sequence derived from its amino acid composition. The invention further encompasses the use of an automated microarray device capable of simultaneously applying multiple lectins and antibodies to a single specimen, allowing for multiplexed analysis and enhanced diagnostic confidence. The preparation of clinical samples is standardized to ensure reproducibility, with quality controls applied at each step to minimize pre-analytical variability. The method may be further enhanced by incorporating internal standards or reference glycoproteins to normalize for sample-to-sample variation, thereby ensuring consistent diagnostic performance across diverse clinical populations.

## EXAMPLE 1

- Lectin microarray analysis was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections from 45 cases of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and 38 cases of normal bile duct epithelium. Tissue fragments were microdissected under microscopic guidance, and total protein was extracted and labeled with Cy3-succinimidyl ester. Fluorescence signals from 20 different lectins were measured using a GlycoStation scanner, with signal intensities normalized to the highest value observed. Among all lectins tested, Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA) exhibited the most pronounced and statistically significant increase in signal intensity in cancerous lesions compared to normal epithelium, with a tumor-to-normal ratio of 4.6 and a p-value of less than 0.0001. Receiver operating characteristic curve analysis demonstrated an area under the curve of 0.93, with a sensitivity of 87.4% and specificity of 92.1%. The WFA signal was consistently elevated in all cancerous regions regardless of the presence of hepatolithiasis, confirming its association with neoplastic transformation rather than inflammation. No other lectin demonstrated comparable discriminatory power, establishing WFA as the most promising glycan-binding probe for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma detection.

## EXAMPLE 2

- Histochemical staining with biotinylated WFA was performed on 83 intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, 25 hepatocellular carcinoma, 10 combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma, and 25 normal liver tissue sections. After antigen retrieval and blocking, sections were incubated with WFA, followed by streptavidin-peroxidase and diaminobenzidine staining. In 83 out of 83 cases of pure intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (92.2%), strong apical and cytoplasmic WFA staining was observed, while staining was weak or focal in only 8 of 25 normal bile duct epithelia (32.0%). No staining was detected in the hepatocellular components of combined tumors or in pure hepatocellular carcinoma tissues. The intensity and distribution of WFA staining were distinctively different between cholangiocarcinoma and hepatocellular carcinoma, providing a clear histological discriminator. The staining pattern was localized to the luminal surface of malignant bile duct cells, consistent with the apical expression of mucin-type glycoproteins. These findings confirmed the specificity of WFA binding to cholangiocarcinoma-associated glycoconjugates in tissue contexts.

## EXAMPLE 3

- Bile cytology samples from 18 patients with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and 16 with benign biliary disease were examined for WFA binding. After cytological preparation and fixation, slides were stained with biotinylated WFA and fluorescently labeled streptavidin. In 17 of 18 malignant cases, distinct WFA-positive cells were identified among the cytological aggregates, whereas only 2 of 16 benign samples showed minimal, diffuse staining. The sensitivity of WFA staining for detecting malignant cells in bile exceeded that of conventional cytology, which classified only 10 of 18 cases as positive. The presence of WFA-positive cells correlated with histopathological confirmation of malignancy, demonstrating the utility of WFA as a cytomorphological marker in bile samples.

## EXAMPLE 4

- WFA-binding glycoproteins were isolated from cholangiocarcinoma tissue lysates using biotinylated WFA coupled to streptavidin magnetic beads. After affinity capture, proteins were eluted and separated by non-reducing agarose gel electrophoresis. Protein bands were visualized by silver staining, and the most prominent band at approximately 200 kDa was excised and subjected to liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry. Database searching identified the protein as a sialylated form of MUC1, with high Mascot scores and peptide coverage exceeding 40%. No other proteins were consistently identified across multiple samples, confirming MUC1 as the dominant WFA-binding glycoprotein. This identification provided the molecular basis for developing antibody-based detection systems targeting the protein moiety of the WFA-reactive glycoprotein.

## EXAMPLE 5

- Immunohistochemical staining of cholangiocarcinoma tissue sections was performed using antibodies against MUC1, claudin-4, EpCAM, and CD133 in parallel with WFA staining. MUC1 staining co-localized precisely with WFA in the apical membrane of malignant biliary epithelial cells, while claudin-4 and EpCAM were also expressed in normal bile duct epithelium and showed no discriminatory power between benign and malignant conditions. CD133 showed variable expression and was not specific to cholangiocarcinoma. These results confirmed that MUC1, when carrying the WFA-reactive glycans, serves as a unique molecular signature for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, distinguishing it from other epithelial markers that lack cancer-specific glycosylation.

## EXAMPLE 6

- A sandwich ELISA was developed using a WFA-coated plate and the monoclonal antibody MY.1E12, which recognizes MUC1. Bile samples from 30 patients with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and 38 with benign biliary disease were analyzed. The system achieved a sensitivity of 90.0% and specificity of 76.3%, with an area under the ROC curve of 0.86. In contrast, a conventional MY.1E12-MY.1E12 sandwich assay yielded an AUC of 0.75, with lower sensitivity (56.7%). The WFA-MY.1E12 system outperformed CA19-9, which had an AUC of 0.56 in the same cohort. The detection limit was determined to be 0.2 ng/mL of target glycoprotein, demonstrating high analytical sensitivity. The method was reproducible across three independent laboratories, with intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation below 8%.

## EXAMPLE 7

- An improved sandwich ELISA was developed by optimizing the binding buffer composition, incubation times, and blocking agents to minimize nonspecific binding. Biotinylated WFA was immobilized at a density of 1.0 µg/well, and bile samples were diluted to a consistent protein concentration of 10 µg/mL. The MY.1E12 antibody was used at 50 ng/mL, and HRP-conjugated anti-mouse IgG was applied at 1:4000 dilution. Signal amplification was enhanced with tyramide-based deposition, reducing background noise and improving the signal-to-noise ratio. The modified assay maintained 90% sensitivity but increased specificity to 84.2%, yielding an AUC of 0.89. The method was validated on a blinded cohort of 120 patients, confirming its robustness and reliability.

## EXAMPLE 8

- Serum samples from 45 patients with intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and 45 with benign liver disease were analyzed using the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich ELISA. WFA-coated plates were incubated with serum, followed by MY.1E12 and HRP-streptavidin. The assay detected the WFA-binding glycoprotein in 38 of 45 serum samples from cholangiocarcinoma patients (84.4% sensitivity), with 39 of 45 benign controls testing negative (86.7% specificity). The AUC was 0.85, demonstrating that the WFA-reactive glycoprotein can be reliably detected in blood, extending the diagnostic utility of the invention to non-invasive serum testing and enabling longitudinal monitoring of disease progression and treatment response.
