## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Androgen-resistant prostate cancer remains a significant clinical challenge due to its aggressive nature, resistance to conventional hormonal therapies, and limited efficacy of existing chemotherapeutic agents. Despite advances in targeted therapies and immunotherapies, many patients with advanced disease experience rapid disease progression and poor survival outcomes. Current therapeutic modalities, including enzalutamide, abiraterone, and taxane-based chemotherapy, often fail to achieve durable responses, and resistance mechanisms frequently emerge through compensatory signaling pathways, epigenetic reprogramming, and altered cellular uptake dynamics. A critical barrier to the clinical translation of novel molecular therapeutics—particularly those based on peptide nucleic acids, locked nucleic acids, or other oligonucleotide analogs—is the inability to efficiently deliver these agents into the cytoplasm and nucleus of cancer cells. Conventional delivery systems such as lipid nanoparticles or viral vectors suffer from poor tumor specificity, immunogenicity, and systemic toxicity. Moreover, many cell-penetrating peptides previously explored for oligonucleotide delivery exhibit insufficient nuclear localization, endosomal entrapment, or lack of cancer cell selectivity. The absence of a delivery platform capable of simultaneously enabling rapid cellular internalization, bypassing endosomal sequestration, and achieving robust nuclear accumulation has hindered the development of potent transcriptional inhibitors for prostate and pancreatic cancers. There exists a pressing need for a novel class of delivery vehicles that exploit cancer-specific molecular features to facilitate targeted, high-efficiency transport of therapeutic payloads directly into the nucleus of malignant cells.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a novel class of cell-penetrating peptides designed for the targeted delivery of therapeutic payloads into the nuclei of cancer cells, particularly those resistant to conventional therapies. The cell-penetrating peptide comprises a glutamate-rich peptide sequence covalently linked to a nuclear localization signal derived from the Oct6 transcription factor, wherein the glutamate-rich peptide is positioned at the N-terminus of the nuclear localization signal. The glutamate-rich peptide consists of the amino acid sequence EEEAA, and the nuclear localization signal corresponds to the sequence KKRKRRK, which is derived from the Oct6 transcription factor. The glutamate-rich peptide and the nuclear localization signal are connected by a peptide linker comprising alanine or glycine residues, or a combination thereof, to ensure spatial separation and functional independence of each domain. The entire peptide may be labeled at its N-terminus or C-terminus with a fluorescent moiety such as fluorescein isothiocyanate for detection and tracking purposes. A therapeutic payload is covalently attached to the C-terminus of the nuclear localization signal, wherein the therapeutic payload is selected from the group consisting of a peptide nucleic acid, a liposome, a small molecule inhibitor, a diagnostic imaging agent, or a ribonucleic acid molecule. The therapeutic payload is configured to inhibit cell growth by interfering with transcriptional activity, gene expression, or protein synthesis within the nucleus of a cancer cell. The cell-penetrating peptide localizes predominantly within the nucleus of cancer cells following cellular uptake, and the sequence of the peptide is defined as SEQ ID NO: 3, which corresponds to EEEAA-KKRKRRK. The peptide exhibits enhanced uptake and nuclear localization in androgen-resistant prostate cancer cells, pancreatic cancer cells, and other epithelial malignancies expressing glutamate receptors, without requiring serum-free conditions or high concentrations for efficacy. The peptide is not cytotoxic in its unlabeled form and does not induce apoptosis in the absence of a therapeutic payload.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention introduces a novel class of cell-penetrating peptides engineered for the efficient delivery of therapeutic agents into the nuclei of cancer cells. These peptides are constructed by fusing a glutamate-rich motif to the nuclear localization signal of the Oct6 transcription factor, resulting in a chimeric peptide with superior cellular internalization and nuclear targeting properties compared to either component alone. The glutamate-rich segment, consisting of three consecutive glutamic acid residues followed by two alanine residues, is positioned at the N-terminus of the nuclear localization signal to maximize accessibility and interaction with cell surface receptors. The nuclear localization signal, derived from the Oct6 transcription factor, contains a cluster of basic amino acids essential for nuclear import via the importin α/β pathway. The linker region between the glutamate-rich segment and the nuclear localization signal is composed of alanine or glycine residues, which provide flexibility and prevent steric hindrance between the two functional domains. The resulting peptide is capable of rapid internalization into cancer cells, even at low nanomolar concentrations, and achieves near-complete nuclear accumulation within hours of exposure. This enhanced nuclear localization is not observed with the isolated nuclear localization signal or with glutamate-rich peptides lacking the nuclear localization signal. The peptide’s ability to enter cells is dependent on the presence of glutamate residues, as substitution of these residues with phenylalanine, asparagine, or tyrosine abolishes uptake, indicating a sequence-specific interaction with a cell surface receptor. The uptake mechanism is energy-dependent, as evidenced by inhibition in the presence of sodium azide, suggesting an active transport process rather than passive diffusion. The peptide does not accumulate in endosomes but instead translocates directly to the nucleus, avoiding sequestration and degradation. This property distinguishes it from previously described nuclear localization signal peptides, which often remain trapped in endocytic vesicles. The peptide is compatible with a wide range of therapeutic payloads, including peptide nucleic acids, liposomes, and diagnostic molecules, which can be conjugated to its C-terminus without compromising its internalization or nuclear trafficking capabilities. The invention further encompasses the use of this peptide for targeting cancers that overexpress glutamate receptors, including androgen-resistant prostate cancer and pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, where glutamate signaling contributes to tumor progression and survival. The peptide’s specificity for cancer cells, combined with its nuclear targeting efficiency, makes it uniquely suited for the delivery of transcriptional inhibitors, gene silencing agents, and epigenetic modulators.

### Examples

The invention is further illustrated by a series of experiments demonstrating the uptake, localization, and functional efficacy of the novel peptide. Peptides were synthesized using standard solid-phase Fmoc chemistry and purified by high-performance liquid chromatography. Fluorescent labeling with fluorescein isothiocyanate was performed at the N-terminus to enable detection. Live-cell imaging and imaging flow cytometry confirmed that the peptide designated as Glu-Oct6-FITC, corresponding to SEQ ID NO: 3, exhibited significantly greater cellular uptake and nuclear localization in DU-145 and PANC-1 cancer cells compared to control peptides lacking either the glutamate motif or the nuclear localization signal. At concentrations as low as 30 nM, over 60% of cells showed detectable fluorescence, and at 500 nM, nuclear accumulation was nearly complete. Substitution of glutamate residues with phenylalanine, asparagine, or tyrosine resulted in negligible uptake, confirming the necessity of the glutamate sequence. Coincubation experiments with unlabeled mutant peptides did not inhibit uptake of the wild-type peptide, indicating that uptake is not mediated by competitive receptor binding. Temperature dependence studies revealed that uptake was maximal at 37°C and markedly reduced at 4°C, consistent with an energy-dependent process. Inhibition by sodium azide confirmed ATP dependence, distinguishing this mechanism from non-endocytic uptake pathways. Colocalization studies using DRAQ5 nuclear staining demonstrated that over 70% of the peptide signal overlapped with the nuclear compartment. When conjugated to a peptide nucleic acid targeting STAT3, the peptide facilitated nuclear delivery of the PNA, resulting in significant apoptosis in both DU-145 and PANC-1 cells, while the unconjugated PNA showed no effect. A highly fluorescent subpopulation of cells was observed even at low concentrations, suggesting heterogeneous receptor expression or enhanced internalization in a subset of malignant cells. These findings establish the peptide as a potent and specific delivery vehicle for nuclear-targeted therapeutics.

## Experimental Procedures

The peptides used in this invention were synthesized by solid-phase peptide synthesis using Fmoc chemistry on an Applied Biosystems 433A peptide synthesizer. All peptides were purified by reverse-phase high-performance liquid chromatography and characterized by MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry and amino acid sequencing. The fluorescent labels, fluorescein isothiocyanate and carboxydansyl, were conjugated to the N-terminus of each peptide at a 1:1 molar ratio. Peptide nucleic acids were synthesized by BioSynthesis using PNA chemistry, with a lysyl-fluorescein moiety attached to the C-terminus. DU-145, LNCaP, and PANC-1 cancer cell lines were maintained in DMEM/F12 or RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and incubated at 37°C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. For uptake studies, cells were seeded in 12-well plates and incubated with peptides at concentrations ranging from 0 to 1000 nM for 1 to 24 hours. Fluorescence was quantified using flow cytometry on a BD FACScan or BD LSR II instrument, with geometric mean fluorescence intensity calculated for live cells gated by 7-AAD exclusion. Nuclear colocalization was assessed using imaging flow cytometry on an Amnis ImageStream X Mark II with IDEAS software, and live-cell imaging was performed on a Zeiss Axiovert 200 microscope with DRAQ5 nuclear staining. Apoptosis was measured using annexin V and propidium iodide staining followed by flow cytometric analysis. Statistical significance was determined using one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post hoc test in GraphPad InStat software. All experiments were performed in triplicate and repeated at least three times.
