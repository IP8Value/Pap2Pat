# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- relate to recombinant production of IgA proteases

The present invention relates to novel methods for the recombinant production of IgA proteases, particularly serine-type IgA1 proteases derived from bacterial sources, in non-pathogenic host organisms such as Escherichia coli. Specifically, the invention encompasses the expression of truncated polypeptides comprising only the catalytic protease domain of IgA1 protease, without the associated α protein domain or β-core domain, resulting in the efficient generation of soluble, enzymatically active protein in high yield. These methods enable scalable, cost-effective manufacturing of IgA proteases suitable for therapeutic applications, particularly in the treatment of IgA deposition disorders such as IgA nephropathy. The invention further includes the use of optimized expression vectors, host cell strains, and refolding protocols to enhance the solubility, stability, and functional recovery of the recombinant protease, overcoming longstanding limitations in the field of recombinant protease production.

## BACKGROUND

- introduce IgA proteases

IgA proteases are a class of bacterial endopeptidases that specifically recognize and cleave the hinge region of human immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1), leaving other immunoglobulin isotypes such as IgA2, IgG, and IgM intact. These enzymes are produced by a variety of mucosal pathogens including Neisseria meningitidis, Haemophilus influenzae, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and Prevotella species, and are thought to facilitate bacterial immune evasion by disrupting IgA1-mediated mucosal defense mechanisms. The proteolytic activity of these enzymes is highly specific, targeting a unique sequence motif within the proline-rich hinge region of IgA1 that is absent in other immunoglobulins and in IgA from non-primate species.

- describe function of IgA proteases

The primary biological function of IgA proteases is to cleave IgA1 into Fab and Fc fragments, thereby neutralizing its ability to mediate opsonization, complement activation, and transcytosis across epithelial barriers. This cleavage disrupts the structural integrity of IgA1 immune complexes, allowing bacteria to evade host immune surveillance and persist in mucosal environments. The enzymatic activity is mediated by a catalytic domain that is evolutionarily conserved among serine-type IgA proteases, which utilize a serine residue within a catalytic triad to hydrolyze peptide bonds between proline and serine or threonine residues in the IgA1 hinge.

- discuss IgA deposition disorders

IgA deposition disorders are a group of immune-mediated conditions characterized by the abnormal accumulation of IgA1-containing immune complexes in various tissues, most notably the glomeruli of the kidney, where they trigger inflammation, mesangial proliferation, and progressive renal damage. The most prevalent of these disorders is IgA nephropathy, which represents the most common form of primary glomerulonephritis worldwide and is a leading cause of end-stage renal disease. Other conditions associated with IgA1 deposition include dermatitis herpetiformis, Henoch-Schoenlein purpura, and certain forms of liver disease and vasculitis. In all cases, the pathogenicity is linked to the presence of galactose-deficient IgA1 molecules that form circulating immune complexes and deposit in tissues, activating complement and attracting inflammatory cells.

- mention previous attempts at recombinant production

Previous efforts to produce IgA proteases for therapeutic use have relied on isolation from native bacterial sources such as Haemophilus influenzae, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, and Neisseria meningitidis. These approaches are inherently limited by low natural yields, slow growth rates, and the requirement for specialized growth conditions, including the addition of hemin for H. influenzae. Attempts to express full-length IgA protease precursors in Escherichia coli have resulted predominantly in the formation of insoluble inclusion bodies, with minimal recovery of soluble, active enzyme. Even when solubilized and refolded, the resulting protein often exhibits low specific activity due to improper folding or aggregation.

- describe limitations of previous methods

The major limitations of prior methods include the inability to achieve commercially viable yields of soluble, active IgA protease, the reliance on pathogenic organisms that pose biosafety and regulatory challenges, and the structural complexity of the native precursor protein, which requires precise post-translational cleavage events to generate the mature, functional enzyme. Additionally, expression of the full-length precursor in E. coli often leads to misfolding, proteolytic degradation, or sequestration into inclusion bodies, rendering the protein unusable for therapeutic applications. The low solubility and instability of the recombinant protein have precluded its development as a biologic therapeutic despite its demonstrated efficacy in animal models of IgA nephropathy.

- introduce IgA nephropathy

IgA nephropathy is a chronic kidney disease characterized by the deposition of IgA1-containing immune complexes in the mesangial matrix of glomeruli, leading to inflammation, proteinuria, hematuria, and progressive loss of renal function. The disease is often diagnosed incidentally during evaluation for microscopic hematuria or proteinuria and can progress to end-stage renal disease over decades in up to 30% of affected individuals. Current therapeutic strategies, including immunosuppression and renin-angiotensin system blockade, are only partially effective and do not address the underlying pathogenic mechanism of IgA1 deposition.

- discuss treatment of IgA deposition disorders

Therapeutic approaches aimed at removing or degrading deposited IgA1 have been explored, including the use of non-specific proteases such as chymopapain and subtilisin. However, these agents lack specificity and cause collateral damage to other plasma and tissue proteins, limiting their clinical utility. The discovery that IgA1-specific proteases can cleave deposited IgA1 without affecting other immunoglobulins has generated significant interest in their potential as targeted biologic therapies. Yet, the absence of a scalable, safe, and reproducible method for producing sufficient quantities of active IgA1 protease has hindered clinical translation.

- mention use of isolated H. influenzae IgA1 protease

The IgA1 protease derived from Haemophilus influenzae has been shown in preclinical studies to effectively reduce glomerular IgA1 deposits in murine models of IgA nephropathy, demonstrating proof-of-concept for enzyme replacement therapy. However, the natural yield of this enzyme from H. influenzae cultures is extremely low—less than 0.03 mg per liter—and the organism’s pathogenic nature and stringent growth requirements make large-scale production impractical for human therapeutic use.

- summarize need for improved methods

There remains a critical and unmet need for a method to produce recombinant IgA1 protease in high yield, with high solubility, enzymatic activity, and purity, using a non-pathogenic, industrially scalable host system. Such a method would enable the development of a targeted biologic therapy capable of degrading pathogenic IgA1 deposits without inducing systemic proteolytic damage, thereby offering a novel and potentially curative approach to IgA deposition disorders.

## SUMMARY

- introduce methods for producing recombinant IgA proteases

The present invention provides novel methods for the recombinant production of IgA proteases by expressing truncated polypeptides comprising only the catalytic protease domain, devoid of the α protein and β-core domains that are normally present in the native precursor. This approach eliminates the requirement for post-translational cleavage events and allows for the direct synthesis of a stable, soluble, and enzymatically active form of the protease in bacterial host cells. The method enables the production of IgA protease at yields exceeding 100 mg per liter of culture, representing a more than 1,000-fold improvement over prior methods.

- describe expression of IgA protease polypeptides

The IgA protease polypeptides are expressed in recombinant form using expression vectors containing a strong, inducible promoter and a ribosome binding site optimized for high-level translation in Escherichia coli. The coding sequence is designed to encode the mature protease domain with or without an N-terminal signal peptide, depending on the desired subcellular localization. The resulting polypeptide is approximately 100 kDa in molecular weight and retains full IgA1-specific endopeptidase activity without requiring additional processing.

- mention lack of α protein domain and β-core domain

The recombinant IgA protease polypeptides of the invention are engineered to lack the C-terminal α protein domain and β-core domain, which are responsible for membrane anchoring and secretion in the native bacterial context. By excluding these domains, the polypeptide avoids misfolding, aggregation, and sequestration into inclusion bodies, thereby enhancing its solubility and stability in the cytoplasm of the host cell. This structural simplification is key to achieving high yields of functional enzyme.

- describe host cells and vectors

The polypeptides are expressed in genetically engineered strains of Escherichia coli, including but not limited to C41(DE3), C43(DE3), BL21(DE3), and ArcticExpress(DE3), each selected for their ability to support high-level protein synthesis under low-temperature induction conditions. The expression vectors employed are typically derived from the pET or pCold series, featuring T7 or cold-shock promoters, respectively, and are maintained under antibiotic selection. The vectors may also encode a C-terminal hexahistidine tag to facilitate purification by immobilized metal affinity chromatography.

- introduce soluble and active IgA protease

The recombinant IgA protease produced by the methods of the invention is both soluble and enzymatically active, exhibiting specific cleavage of human IgA1 at the hinge region without degrading other immunoglobulin isotypes. The enzyme retains full catalytic activity as confirmed by substrate cleavage assays and mass spectrometry analysis of reaction products. The solubility of the enzyme is achieved without the need for fusion partners, chaperone co-expression, or complex refolding protocols, although such strategies may be optionally employed to further enhance yield.

- describe culturing of host cells

Host cells are cultured in standard nutrient media under controlled temperature and induction conditions. Protein expression is induced by the addition of isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) at concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 1.0 mM, and cultures are incubated at temperatures between 12°C and 25°C for periods of 12 to 36 hours to maximize soluble protein accumulation. The use of low temperature and moderate inducer concentration suppresses inclusion body formation while promoting correct folding.

- mention isolation of inclusion bodies

In embodiments where inclusion bodies are formed, they are isolated by cell lysis and differential centrifugation, followed by washing with detergent-containing buffers to remove contaminating membrane components. The inclusion bodies are then solubilized using chaotropic agents such as urea or guanidine hydrochloride and subsequently refolded under controlled conditions to regenerate the native, active conformation of the protease.

- describe solubilization and refolding of inclusion bodies

Solubilized inclusion bodies are diluted into refolding buffers containing redox agents, arginine, and appropriate pH buffers such as Tris-HCl, CHES, MES, or PBS to facilitate proper disulfide bond formation and tertiary structure recovery. Refolding is performed slowly over 12 to 48 hours at temperatures between 4°C and 20°C, with continuous stirring to minimize aggregation. The refolded protein is then purified by sequential chromatographic steps including affinity, ion-exchange, and size-exclusion chromatography to obtain a homogeneous, active preparation.

- introduce compositions comprising host cells

The invention further encompasses compositions comprising host cells that have been transformed with a recombinant polynucleotide encoding the IgA protease polypeptide as described herein. These compositions may be used for large-scale fermentation processes to produce the protease in quantities sufficient for therapeutic administration.

- describe wet weight of host cells

In industrial-scale production, the wet weight of harvested host cells may exceed 100 grams per liter of culture, yielding approximately 100 mg of total soluble IgA protease per liter of culture medium, with up to 40 mg of purified, active enzyme recovered after downstream processing.

- mention productivity level of soluble protease

The productivity level of soluble IgA protease achieved by the methods of the invention is at least 100 mg per liter of culture, representing a substantial improvement over prior methods that yielded less than 1 mg per liter. This level of productivity is sufficient to support the development of a clinically viable therapeutic product.

- describe volume of culture media

The methods are scalable to volumes exceeding 1,000 liters, enabling the production of kilograms of purified IgA protease per batch, thereby meeting the demand for chronic administration in patients with IgA deposition disorders.

- introduce ratio of soluble to total IgA protease

The ratio of soluble IgA protease to total expressed IgA protease is greater than 80% in optimized conditions, indicating a high proportion of correctly folded, functional enzyme in the soluble fraction.

- describe increased yield of soluble IgA protease

The yield of soluble, active IgA protease is increased by at least 100-fold compared to previous methods, and in some embodiments exceeds 1,000-fold, making therapeutic-scale production feasible for the first time.

- mention total protein productivity level

The total protein productivity level, including both soluble and insoluble forms, may reach up to 200 mg per liter of culture, with the majority of the protein in the soluble, active form.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce recombinant production methods

The recombinant production methods of the invention involve the genetic engineering of non-pathogenic bacterial hosts to express a truncated form of the IgA1 protease that lacks the α protein and β-core domains, thereby enabling high-yield, soluble expression of a fully active enzyme. The method begins with the design and synthesis of a polynucleotide sequence encoding the catalytic domain of the IgA1 protease, optionally fused to a signal peptide for periplasmic targeting or a C-terminal affinity tag for purification. This sequence is cloned into an expression vector under the control of a strong, inducible promoter and introduced into a suitable host strain.

- describe advantages and applications

The advantages of this approach include the elimination of the need for complex post-translational processing, the avoidance of pathogenic bacterial sources, the dramatic increase in soluble protein yield, and the compatibility with established industrial fermentation and purification platforms. The resulting IgA protease is suitable for use as a targeted biologic therapy for IgA nephropathy, dermatitis herpetiformis, Henoch-Schoenlein purpura, and other IgA deposition disorders, offering a mechanism-based treatment that directly addresses the underlying pathogenic agent.

### Representative Embodiments of the Disclosure

- define serine-type IgA protease production method

The serine-type IgA protease production method involves expressing a truncated polypeptide lacking the α and β domains in E. coli C41(DE3) using an IPTG-inducible vector.

- transform host cell with vector

Host cells are transformed with a plasmid encoding the IgA protease polypeptide under control of the T7 promoter.

- express IgA protease polypeptide with modified domains

The polypeptide is expressed without the α protein and β-core domains, and optionally with a C-terminal histidine tag.

- isolate inclusion bodies

Inclusion bodies are isolated by centrifugation after cell lysis and washed with detergent to remove contaminants.

- solubilize inclusion bodies with chaotropic agent

Inclusion bodies are solubilized using 6 M urea or 8 M guanidine hydrochloride.

- refold solubilized inclusion bodies in Tris refolding buffer

Refolding is performed in 50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 8.0, containing 0.5 M arginine and 5 mM reduced glutathione.

- refold solubilized inclusion bodies in CHES refolding buffer

Refolding is performed in 50 mM CHES, pH 9.0, with arginine and redox agents.

- refold solubilized inclusion bodies in MES refolding buffer

Refolding is performed in 50 mM MES, pH 6.5, with arginine and cysteine.

- refold solubilized inclusion bodies in PBS refolding buffer

Refolding is performed in 10 mM PBS, pH 7.4, with arginine and oxidized glutathione.

- add arginine to refolding buffer

Arginine is added at concentrations of 0.2 to 1.0 M to suppress aggregation during refolding.

- refold solubilized inclusion bodies at specific temperature

Refolding is conducted at 4°C to 20°C for 12 to 48 hours.

- control concentration of solubilized inclusion bodies

The concentration of solubilized inclusion bodies is maintained below 0.5 mg/mL to minimize aggregation.

- solubilize inclusion bodies with urea and refold in Tris buffer

Inclusion bodies are solubilized in 6 M urea and refolded by slow dilution into Tris buffer containing arginine.

- wash and purify isolated inclusion bodies

Inclusion bodies are washed with 2% Triton X-100 and 1 M NaCl, followed by centrifugation and resuspension in buffer.

- purify refolded IgA protease

Refolded IgA protease is purified by nickel affinity chromatography, followed by anion-exchange and size-exclusion chromatography.

- ultrafiltrate and diafiltrate refolded IgA protease

Ultrafiltration and diafiltration are performed using 30 kDa cutoff membranes to exchange buffer and concentrate the protein.

- use nickel column for purification

Nickel affinity chromatography is used to capture His-tagged IgA protease using imidazole gradient elution.

- use anion-exchange column for purification

Anion-exchange chromatography on Q Sepharose is used to remove endotoxins and aggregates.

- use cation-exchange column for purification

Cation-exchange chromatography on SP Sepharose is used for further polishing in certain embodiments.

- use hydrophobic-interaction column for purification

Hydrophobic-interaction chromatography is used to remove residual aggregates and misfolded species.

- isolate and purify soluble, active IgA protease polypeptide

Soluble, active IgA protease is isolated and purified to homogeneity using the above chromatographic steps.
