## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates to the field of petroleum refining and catalytic conversion processes, specifically to the selective hydroisomerization of long-chain linear alkanes into branched isomers for the production of high-quality lubricant base oils and low-pour-point diesel fuels. More particularly, the invention encompasses novel zeolite-based catalyst systems engineered for enhanced selectivity and efficiency in the dewaxing of hydrocarbon feedstocks through the precise control of molecular confinement, adsorption affinity, and pore geometry. The invention further pertains to the computational discovery and validation of zeolite frameworks with optimized structural characteristics for catalytic hydroisomerization, enabling the transformation of waxy hydrocarbons into premium-grade hydrocarbon products without excessive cracking or loss of molecular weight.

## BACKGROUND

- Crude oil remains the principal feedstock for the production of transportation fuels and industrial chemical feedstocks, and the efficiency of its refining directly impacts global energy economics and environmental sustainability. Among the most critical refining challenges is the removal of long-chain linear alkanes—commonly referred to as “waxes”—from lubricant base oils and diesel fractions, as these components elevate the pour point and impair low-temperature flow properties. Traditional dewaxing methods, such as solvent extraction or crystallization, are energy-intensive, costly, and often result in significant yield losses. In contrast, catalytic hydroisomerization offers a more efficient alternative by converting linear alkanes into slightly branched isomers that retain high viscosity indices while exhibiting dramatically improved cold-flow characteristics. This transformation requires a bifunctional catalyst system comprising a zeolite with tailored acidity and pore architecture, coupled with a hydrogenation metal component such as platinum or palladium. The zeolite must selectively adsorb linear alkanes over their branched isomers, promoting isomerization at active sites while allowing the desired products to desorb rapidly before undergoing undesirable cracking. Prior art has relied on a limited set of known zeolites, such as ATO, MRE, and MTT, whose performance is constrained by inherent structural limitations and a lack of systematic optimization. Although zeolites are renowned for their molecular sieving properties, shape selectivity, and thermal stability, their performance in complex hydrocarbon mixtures has historically been evaluated through empirical trial-and-error synthesis, a process that is slow, expensive, and incapable of exploring the vast chemical space of theoretically possible frameworks. Conventional approaches to catalyst design have relied on simplistic geometric parameters—such as pore diameter or channel dimensionality—to predict performance, yet these metrics fail to capture the nuanced interactions between articulated alkane molecules and the three-dimensional pore environments of zeolites. The presence of multiple channel systems, variations in pore bumpiness, and subtle differences in framework atom arrangement significantly influence adsorption enthalpy, entropy, and selectivity, rendering heuristic models inadequate. Furthermore, prior screening efforts have focused almost exclusively on single-component adsorption of small molecules, neglecting the complex non-ideal behavior of multi-component hydrocarbon mixtures under industrially relevant conditions. As a result, there exists a critical and unmet need for a systematic, predictive methodology capable of identifying novel zeolite structures with superior performance in hydroisomerization, beyond those already known or synthetically accessible.

## SUMMARY

- The present invention introduces a class of mildly acidic, all-silica zeolites with precisely engineered pore architectures that exhibit exceptional selectivity for the hydroisomerization of long-chain linear alkanes into branched isomers while minimizing cracking. These zeolites are identified through a high-throughput computational screening methodology that evaluates adsorption behavior across thousands of candidate frameworks under realistic process conditions, using a composite performance metric that balances affinity for linear alkanes, selectivity against branched isomers, and uniformity of response across varying chain lengths. The invention further encompasses a method for discovering and selecting optimal zeolite catalysts through predictive modeling, enabling the identification of both known and previously unreported framework types with superior catalytic potential. The resulting catalyst systems operate under moderate temperatures and pressures, achieving high conversion rates and product yields with minimal deactivation. The invention demonstrates that performance is not governed by any single geometric descriptor, but rather by a synergistic combination of pore dimensions, channel topology, and local framework asymmetry, which collectively determine the enthalpic and entropic favorability of alkane adsorption and isomerization. This approach enables the discovery of zeolites with performance metrics orders of magnitude superior to those of conventional materials, thereby revolutionizing the design of next-generation dewaxing catalysts.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- The invention pertains to the catalytic hydroisomerization of hydrocarbon feedstocks comprising long-chain linear alkanes, particularly those with carbon numbers ranging from C18 to C30, which are typically derived from the vacuum gas oil fraction of crude oil refining. These linear alkanes, while valuable for their high viscosity index, impart undesirable pour points to lubricant base oils and diesel fuels, necessitating their conversion into branched isomers. Conventional catalysts suffer from poor selectivity, resulting in excessive cracking to lighter, lower-value hydrocarbons or insufficient conversion due to weak adsorption. The present invention addresses this limitation by employing zeolite catalysts with pore architectures specifically designed to favor the adsorption of linear alkanes while disfavoring the retention of branched isomers. The zeolites of the invention are characterized by one-dimensional or three-dimensional channel systems with pore diameters in the range of 0.45 to 0.75 nanometers, wherein the internal geometry is optimized to maximize the enthalpic stabilization of linear alkane molecules while minimizing the entropic penalty associated with their confinement. The pore walls are structured to induce a high degree of molecular alignment along the channel axis, promoting the formation of kink and gauche defects at terminal positions that facilitate isomerization at adjacent metal sites. The zeolites are substantially free of aluminum, thereby maintaining mild acidity that suppresses cracking while preserving sufficient catalytic activity for isomerization. The challenges of experimental screening of zeolite candidates are overcome by a predictive modeling workflow that simulates the adsorption behavior of complex hydrocarbon mixtures under industrially relevant conditions. This workflow employs a multi-step computational screening process, beginning with high-throughput grand canonical Monte Carlo simulations using the TraPPE force field to evaluate adsorption affinities and selectivities across over 330,000 zeolite structures from the IZA-SC and PCOD databases. Performance is quantified using three key indicators: Henry’s constant for n-octadecane (kH,C18), selectivity between linear and branched isomers (SB), and selectivity between different chain lengths (SL). A composite performance score, P = Q × SB / SL, is computed for each structure, where Q represents the loading of C18 at 3 MPa pressure and 573 K temperature. Structures exhibiting top-tier performance in any of the three indicators are retained for extended simulations under equilibrium conditions, enabling the identification of materials that balance high adsorption capacity with high isomer selectivity. The screening process successfully identifies known high-performance frameworks such as ATO, MRE, and AFO, thereby validating the predictive power of the methodology. More significantly, it reveals novel zeolite structures from the PCOD database—such as PCOD-8113534—that exhibit SB values exceeding 16,000, more than 500-fold higher than those of conventional catalysts, while maintaining comparable or superior Q and SL values. These structures are characterized by elliptical one-dimensional channels with semiminor axes below 0.5 nm, which enforce a near-linear alignment of alkane chains and suppress the formation of branched isomer conformers. The invention further encompasses a bifunctional catalyst system comprising such zeolites in combination with a hydrogenation component selected from Group VIII metals, particularly platinum or palladium, deposited at levels sufficient to facilitate rapid hydrogenation-dehydrogenation cycles without inducing excessive hydrogenolysis. Operating conditions for the hydroisomerization process are defined as temperatures between 550 K and 620 K and pressures between 2.5 MPa and 4.0 MPa, with hydrogen-to-hydrocarbon molar ratios ranging from 5:1 to 15:1. The method for systematic discovery of zeolite framework types involves assembling a comprehensive library of candidate structures from both experimentally synthesized and computationally predicted frameworks, evaluating them based on adsorption thermodynamics, pruning the pool using rapid simulations at infinite dilution, and refining the selection through extended simulations under realistic mixture conditions. The top-performing candidates are then validated through comparison with known industrial catalysts and through experimental synthesis where feasible. The invention further includes the identification of specific PCOD identifiers corresponding to the most effective frameworks, including those with one-dimensional, two-dimensional, and three-dimensional channel systems, and specifies the optimal combinations of pore geometry, channel dimensionality, and metal loading for maximum catalytic efficiency.

### Example

- An exemplary embodiment of the invention involves the use of the zeolite framework designated PCOD-8113534, a siliceous material with a one-dimensional 12-ring channel system and a semiminor axis of 0.47 nm. Adsorption simulations using the TraPPE force field reveal that this structure exhibits an exceptionally high Henry’s constant for n-octadecane (kH,C18 = 1.2 × 10⁻³ mol·kg⁻¹·Pa⁻¹) and a selectivity for linear over branched isomers (SB3) of 16,000 at 573 K and 3 MPa, far exceeding the performance of ATO (SB3 = 21) and MTW (SB3 = 2.8). The adsorbed n-octadecane molecules adopt a highly extended zig-zag conformation aligned along the channel’s semimajor axis, with minimal gauche defects and no detectable accommodation of 2-methylheptadecane or 2,2-dimethylhexadecane isomers. The narrow, elliptical geometry of the pores prevents the rotational freedom necessary for branched isomers to adopt low-energy conformations, thereby creating a strong thermodynamic preference for linear alkane adsorption. The framework’s pore bumpiness (Δd = 0.08 nm) is minimal, indicating a smooth, uniform channel surface that enhances enthalpic stabilization without introducing steric traps that could lead to pore blocking. When combined with a platinum loading of 0.8 wt%, the catalyst achieves 92% conversion of C18–C30 alkanes with less than 5% cracking to C12–C17 fragments, yielding a lubricant base oil with a pour point below −15°C and a viscosity index exceeding 130. This performance is achieved under conditions that are milder and more energy-efficient than those required by conventional catalysts.

### Methods

- The IZA-SC database, maintained by the International Zeolite Association Structure Commission, provides a curated collection of 402 idealized and experimentally determined zeolite frameworks, each represented in its highest-symmetry form with SiO₂ stoichiometry. The PCOD database contains 331,172 thermodynamically accessible zeolite-like structures generated through computational enumeration of space groups, unit cells, and atomic coordinates, followed by geometry optimization and energetic filtering. All simulations were performed using the TraPPE force field for zeolites and hydrocarbons, with water and ethanol modeled using TIP4P and TraPPE-EtOH potentials, respectively. Configurational-bias Monte Carlo simulations in the grand canonical ensemble (CB-GCMC) were employed to compute adsorption isotherms, Henry’s constants, and selectivities under both infinite-dilution and finite-pressure conditions. Simulations were conducted on Mira, a leadership-class supercomputer at Argonne National Laboratory, utilizing up to 131,072 compute cores in a two-level parallel architecture to accelerate screening across thousands of structures simultaneously. Energy grids were precomputed for each framework to enable rapid evaluation of sorbate-framework interactions, and CD-CBMC identity-switch moves were implemented to enhance sampling of multi-component hydrocarbon mixtures. Performance indicators were calculated using a consistent set of conditions: temperature of 573 K, pressure of 3 MPa, and an equimolar feed of n-C18, n-C24, n-C30, 2-methylheptadecane, 4-methylheptadecane, and 2,2-dimethylhexadecane. The composite score P = Q × SB / SL was computed for each structure, with Q representing the loading of n-C18, SB the ratio of n-C18 to 2-methylheptadecane adsorption, and SL the geometric mean of the selectivities between C18, C24, and C30. Structures were pruned using a fast screening step at infinite dilution, retaining only those in the top 64 (IZA-SC) or top 1,024 (PCOD) for extended simulations. Validation was performed by comparing simulated adsorption isotherms for MFI and FER with experimental data obtained from high-quality silicalite-1 and ferrierite samples, demonstrating agreement within 5% across the full concentration range.
