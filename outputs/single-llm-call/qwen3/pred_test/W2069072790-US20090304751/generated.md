# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

- Chlamydia trachomatis is a Gram-negative, obligate intracellular bacterial pathogen that exclusively infects human mucosal epithelial tissues, particularly those of the urogenital tract, conjunctiva, and respiratory passages. It is transmitted primarily through sexual contact and is the most common bacterial sexually transmitted infection worldwide, with an estimated 92 million new cases reported annually by the World Health Organization. The infection is often asymptomatic, especially in women, leading to delayed diagnosis and untreated chronic inflammation that can result in severe reproductive complications including pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal factor infertility, ectopic pregnancy, and chronic pelvic pain. Despite the availability of effective antibiotics such as azithromycin and doxycycline, reinfection rates remain alarmingly high, particularly in populations with limited access to screening and follow-up care. Antibiotic administration fails to address the underlying immune dysregulation caused by persistent infection and does not confer lasting immunity, rendering individuals susceptible to repeated episodes. The absence of a licensed vaccine underscores a critical gap in public health strategy, as current interventions are reactive rather than preventive. The resolution of Chlamydia infection in both humans and animal models is critically dependent on the activation of CD4+ T-helper cells, particularly those of the Th1 subset, which secrete interferon-gamma and coordinate macrophage activation to eliminate intracellular bacteria. While humoral immunity, including the production of immunoglobulins such as IgG and IgA, contributes to neutralization and opsonization, it is insufficient alone to clear established infection. The major outer membrane protein (MOMP), a highly conserved, immunodominant surface antigen of Chlamydia trachomatis, has been extensively studied as a candidate vaccine antigen due to its role in host cell attachment, structural integrity of the elementary body, and capacity to elicit T-cell and antibody responses. However, previous attempts to develop MOMP-based vaccines using soluble protein formulations or traditional adjuvants have failed to generate robust mucosal T-cell immunity, largely due to rapid degradation in the hostile mucosal environment and inadequate delivery to antigen-presenting cells in lymphoid tissues. A critical unmet need exists for a vaccine platform capable of protecting MOMP from proteolytic degradation, facilitating its targeted delivery to dendritic cells within nasal-associated lymphoid tissue, and inducing a durable, tissue-resident Th1 response that extends to distal mucosal sites such as the genital tract. Nasal mucosal administration offers a non-invasive, clinically practical route to stimulate systemic and local immunity simultaneously, leveraging the interconnected nature of mucosal immune networks to generate protective responses at distant sites without the need for direct genital immunization.

## SUMMARY

- The invention provides methods for treating or preventing Chlamydia trachomatis infection through intranasal immunization with recombinant vault-like particles engineered to encapsulate immunogenic fragments of the major outer membrane protein (MOMP). These vault-like particles are self-assembling, non-replicating, non-infectious nanoparticles composed of modified major vault protein (MVP) and optionally fused to a minimal interaction domain derived from poly ADP-ribose polymerase (VPARP), enabling the stable incorporation of MOMP into their internal cavity. The method involves administering a pharmaceutical composition comprising these vault-like particles to the nasal mucosa of a subject, thereby inducing a localized and systemic cell-mediated immune response characterized by the expansion of interferon-gamma-secreting CD4+ T-cells and the generation of MOMP-specific IgG antibodies, which together confer protection against subsequent genital challenge. The invention further encompasses the use of vault-like particles as carriers for other immunogenic peptides derived from Chlamydia species or related pathogens, enabling broad application across multiple mucosal infections. Immunization is achieved without the need for exogenous adjuvants, as the vault structure itself promotes dendritic cell uptake and inflammasome activation, resulting in a balanced immune response that minimizes inflammatory pathology while maximizing pathogen clearance. The composition may be formulated as a sterile, aqueous suspension suitable for nasal spray or drop administration, and may be stored lyophilized for long-term stability. The method is applicable to both prophylactic vaccination and therapeutic intervention in individuals with prior exposure or chronic infection, and is particularly advantageous for use in resource-limited settings due to its stability, ease of delivery, and lack of requirement for cold-chain logistics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- Chlamydia vaccines have historically struggled to induce durable mucosal T-cell immunity due to the instability of protein antigens in mucosal secretions and the inability of traditional delivery systems to target antigen-presenting cells within inductive lymphoid tissues. Vault-like particles, as described herein, are nanoscale ribonucleoprotein structures composed of multiple copies of the major vault protein (MVP), which spontaneously self-assemble into a barrel-shaped architecture with an internal cavity capable of encapsulating large biomolecules. These particles are naturally occurring in eukaryotic cells but can be recombinantly produced in insect cells using the baculovirus expression system, yielding homogeneous, non-pathogenic nanoparticles devoid of endogenous viral or bacterial contaminants. The vault structure is stabilized by the incorporation of a cysteine-rich tag (cp-MVP) that enhances structural integrity without altering the overall morphology or immunogenic properties. The internal cavity of the vault is sufficiently large to accommodate multiple copies of MOMP or other immunogenic peptides, which are fused via a genetically encoded minimal interaction domain (mINT) derived from VPARP, ensuring efficient packaging during particle assembly. The poly ADP-ribosylation reaction catalyzed by VPARP is not required for vault formation; rather, the mINT domain serves solely as a molecular tether that facilitates the recruitment and sequestration of fusion proteins into the vault lumen. The resulting vault-like particle, when administered intranasally, is rapidly internalized by dendritic cells in the nasal mucosa via receptor-mediated endocytosis, bypassing degradation by extracellular proteases. Once internalized, the vault particle triggers inflammasome activation through a TLR-independent mechanism, leading to the maturation of dendritic cells, secretion of IL-12p70 and IL-1β, and upregulation of co-stimulatory molecules such as CD40 and MHC class II. This process promotes the differentiation of naïve T-cells into Th1 effector cells, which migrate to distal mucosal sites including the genital tract, where they provide protective immunity against Chlamydia infection. The vault-like particle may be further modified at its exterior surface by the addition of a staphylococcal protein A-derived peptide (z) to the C-terminus of MVP, which enhances binding to IgG antibodies present in nasal secretions, thereby increasing particle retention and uptake by Fc receptor-expressing dendritic cells. The immunogenic peptides employed in the invention include full-length MOMP or defined fragments thereof, such as the variable domains VD1–VD4, which retain antigenic epitopes recognized by protective T-cell clones. Homologous proteins from other Chlamydia species, including C. pneumoniae and C. psittaci, may be substituted to broaden the scope of protection. The subject refers to any mammal, preferably a human, suffering from or at risk of Chlamydia infection, including individuals with prior exposure, chronic infection, or those in high-risk populations such as adolescents and young adults. The enhanced immunogenicity of the vault platform is theorized to arise from the synergistic effects of antigen encapsulation, prolonged antigen presentation, and the intrinsic adjuvant properties conferred by the vault structure, which mimics the size and physical properties of a pathogen without triggering excessive inflammation. Internalization of the vault-like particle by dendritic cells occurs via clathrin-dependent and -independent pathways, leading to endosomal trafficking and lysosomal processing, which facilitates MHC class II presentation to CD4+ T-cells. The induction of a cell-mediated immune response is characterized by the production of interferon-gamma, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and chemokines such as CXCL10, which recruit additional immune cells to the site of infection. Antibody responses are dominated by IgG subclasses associated with opsonization and complement activation, with a high IgG2a/IgG1 ratio indicative of a Th1-biased response. The protective immunity conferred by this method reduces bacterial burden in genital tissues, prevents ascending infection of the upper reproductive tract, and mitigates immune-mediated tissue damage such as tubal scarring and fibrosis. Nasal immunization is preferred due to its non-invasive nature, its ability to stimulate both local and systemic immunity, and its capacity to induce mucosal IgA and tissue-resident memory T-cells in distal sites. The vault-like particle serves as a versatile carrier molecule capable of delivering not only antigens but also immunomodulatory agents, sensors, or detoxifying enzymes. A targeting moiety may be fused to the MVP or VPARP to direct the particle to specific cell types, such as dendritic cells expressing DEC-205 or CD11c. The Fc binding domain, when incorporated, enhances particle retention in mucosal secretions and promotes uptake via Fcγ receptors. The method of treating or preventing infection involves administering one or more doses of the vault-like particle formulation to the nasal mucosa, with booster immunizations administered at intervals of two to four weeks. The method of reducing infertility is achieved by preventing the establishment of chronic infection and subsequent scarring of fallopian tubes. The method of eliciting an immune response is accomplished without the use of live vectors or chemical adjuvants, relying instead on the innate immunostimulatory properties of the vault structure. The method of preventing damage involves the selective induction of protective immunity without triggering excessive production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-alpha or IL-6, which are associated with immunopathology. Modifications to the vault-like particle include the addition of amino acid sequences to the N- or C-terminus of MVP, such as receptor-binding domains derived from EGF, TAT, or ANT, to enhance cellular uptake. The particle may also be engineered to include polynucleotide-binding domains, heavy metal-binding motifs, or fluorescent sensors for diagnostic applications. The MVP, VPARP, and TEP1 proteins may be derived from human, murine, or other mammalian sources, and their sequences may be codon-optimized for expression in insect cells. The vRNA component, while not essential for particle formation, may be retained or replaced with synthetic RNA for additional immunomodulatory effects. The vault-like particle is defined as a self-assembled structure comprising at least MVP and optionally VPARP or TEP1, with or without vRNA, and containing one or more encapsulated immunogenic peptides. The term “comprise” is used in its inclusive sense, meaning that the described composition may include additional components not explicitly listed. Naturally occurring vaults may be isolated from human tissues and modified ex vivo, but recombinant production is preferred for scalability and purity. The substance to be delivered may be incorporated during particle assembly by co-expression of a fusion protein or by post-assembly loading through membrane permeabilization. Purification is achieved through sucrose gradient centrifugation and ultrafiltration, yielding a preparation free of endotoxin and cellular debris. Administration may be performed via nasal spray, drops, or aerosol, using sterile, isotonic buffers such as phosphate-buffered saline. The dosage of vault-like particles ranges from 10 µg to 500 µg per administration, depending on the age, weight, and immune status of the subject. The MS2 bacteriophage coat protein may be fused to MVP to enable RNA encapsulation or to facilitate purification via affinity tags. The term “human” refers to Homo sapiens, and “organism” encompasses any mammalian species susceptible to Chlamydia infection. The “environmental medium” refers to any biological fluid or tissue into which the vault-like particle is introduced, including nasal secretions, serum, or genital mucosa. “Administering” includes any route of delivery that results in contact with mucosal surfaces, including inhalation, topical application, or injection into mucosal tissues. The dosage may be adjusted based on the immunogenicity of the encapsulated antigen and the desired immune profile. The vault-like particle may be co-administered with additional agents such as cytokines, toll-like receptor agonists, or checkpoint inhibitors to further modulate the immune response. Kits for immunizing or treating subjects include pre-filled nasal applicators containing lyophilized or liquid vault-like particles, along with instructions for use, storage conditions, and safety information. MVP, VPARP, TEP1, and vRNA are defined as the core structural components of the vault, with MVP forming the barrel, VPARP and TEP1 localized at the caps, and vRNA occupying the central lumen. Modifications to MVP, VPARP, and TEP1 include the addition of fusion tags, epitope tags, or enzymatic domains to alter function or enable detection. The vault-like particle may be produced in baculovirus-infected Sf9 insect cells, purified, and loaded with MOMP-mINT fusion protein, resulting in a stable, immunogenic formulation. The method of making vault-like particles involves cloning the modified MVP gene into a baculovirus transfer vector, transfecting insect cells, and harvesting particles from lysates after 48–72 hours. The particles may be purified by differential centrifugation and density gradient ultracentrifugation. The method of delivering substances includes encapsulating not only proteins but also nucleic acids, small molecules, or toxins for therapeutic applications. The method of detecting signals involves incorporating fluorescent or luminescent reporters into the vault structure for real-time tracking in vivo. The method of detoxifying soil involves engineering vault-like particles to express arsenate reductase or other bioremediation enzymes for environmental applications. The method of sequestering substances involves modifying the vault interior to bind heavy metals or toxins for removal from biological fluids. The method of expressing vault-like particles involves transfecting mammalian cells with plasmids encoding modified MVP and co-expressing the antigen of interest. The method of generating bacmid DNA involves recombination in E. coli to produce a baculovirus genome containing the gene of interest. The method of infecting insect cells involves adding recombinant baculovirus to Sf9 cultures at low multiplicity of infection to ensure high particle yield. The method of producing vault-like particles involves harvesting cell lysates, clarifying by low-speed centrifugation, and purifying via sucrose gradients. The method of making vault-like particles with substances involves co-expressing the substance as a fusion protein or incubating pre-formed particles with the substance under controlled conditions. The method of purifying and loading vault-like particles involves chromatographic separation and dialysis to remove unencapsulated material.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- Chlamydia-vault-like particles (CVLPs) were prepared by co-expressing a recombinant fusion protein comprising the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia muridarum linked to the minimal interaction domain (mINT) of VPARP in Sf9 insect cells using a baculovirus expression system. Concurrently, recombinant rat major vault protein (MVP) was expressed in the same cell line under identical conditions. Lysates containing the MOMP-mINT fusion protein were mixed with lysates containing MVP and incubated on ice for 30 minutes to allow for spontaneous encapsulation of the fusion protein within the forming vault structures. The resulting vault-like particles were purified by sequential centrifugation and sucrose density gradient ultracentrifugation, and the integrity of the particles was confirmed by negative stain electron microscopy, which revealed spherical, barrel-shaped structures consistent with previously described vault morphology. Western blot analysis of purified CVLPs using an antibody specific for the VD1 region of MOMP demonstrated the presence of the MOMP-mINT fusion protein within the vault particles, while anti-MVP antibodies confirmed the presence of the vault scaffold. The incorporation mechanism was determined to be dependent on the mINT domain, as deletion of this domain resulted in complete failure of MOMP encapsulation. Immunogenic properties of MOMP were preserved following encapsulation, as evidenced by the ability of anti-MOMP antibodies to recognize the protein in its vault-bound state. The Fc-binding capability of the vault particle was enhanced by incorporating a C-terminal z peptide derived from staphylococcal protein A, which enabled binding to mouse IgG isotypes present in nasal secretions, thereby promoting Fc receptor-mediated uptake by dendritic cells and enhancing the Th1-polarizing capacity of the vaccine.

### Example 2

- Female C57BL/6 mice were immunized intranasally with 200 µg of CVLPs containing MOMP-mINT, or with control vault-like particles containing enhanced green fluorescent protein (GL-vaults), administered three times at two-week intervals. A positive control group received a single intranasal infection with 10³ inclusion-forming units (IFU) of live Chlamydia muridarum. Two weeks after the final immunization, all mice were hormonally synchronized with medroxyprogesterone acetate and challenged intravaginally with 1.5×10⁵ IFU of C. muridarum. Bacterial burden in cervical-vaginal swabs was monitored over 21 days by quantitative culture, revealing that mice immunized with CVLPs exhibited a 1-log reduction in IFU compared to GL-vault controls and achieved bacterial clearance comparable to that observed in mice previously infected with live chlamydiae. Splenocytes and iliac lymph node cells were isolated and restimulated ex vivo with MOMP peptides, followed by intracellular cytokine staining and flow cytometry, which revealed a significant increase in the proportion of IFNγ+IL-4−CD4+CD3+ T-cells in CVLP-immunized mice compared to controls, indicating a dominant Th1 response. The anti-MoPn antibody response was analyzed by ELISA and Western blot, demonstrating high-titer IgG antibodies specific for MOMP in CVLP-immunized mice, with an IgG2a:IgG1 ratio greater than 5, consistent with Th1 bias. No significant IL-4 or IL-17 production was detected, confirming the absence of a Th2 or Th17 response. Cytokine profiling of genital tract homogenates by Luminex assay showed elevated levels of IL-12p70, CXCL10, and IFNγ, but no increase in TNFα or IL-6, indicating a targeted immune response without excessive inflammation. Gel electrophoresis and immunoblotting confirmed the specificity of the antibody response to MOMP and not to vault components, except in CVLP-immunized mice, which developed low-titer anti-MVP antibodies, suggesting antigen-specific breaking of immune tolerance.

### Example 3

- Bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) were generated from C57BL/6 mice and matured under standard conditions. Immature BMDCs were incubated with GL-vaults labeled with enhanced green fluorescent protein (GL) or with FITC-dextran as a control for 30 to 60 minutes at 37°C. Flow cytometric analysis of CD11c+ cells revealed that GL-vaults were internalized with kinetics comparable to FITC-dextran at 60 minutes, but with significantly greater intracellular localization upon confocal microscopy, indicating active endocytosis rather than surface binding. Fluorescent particles were observed concentrated in cytoplasmic vesicles, whereas FITC-dextran remained largely peripherally distributed. The internalization of GL-vaults was inhibited at 4°C, confirming energy-dependent uptake. The process was not dependent on TLR2, TLR4, or TLR9, as vaults failed to activate NF-κB reporter cells expressing these receptors. These findings demonstrate that vault-like particles are efficiently internalized by dendritic cells via a non-TLR-mediated pathway, positioning them as ideal vehicles for antigen delivery to the intracellular antigen-processing machinery.

### Example 4

- Mice immunized intranasally with MOMP-vaults developed robust serum IgG titers against MOMP, as measured by ELISA, with titers comparable to those induced by live chlamydial infection. In contrast, mice immunized with GL-vaults or saline showed no detectable anti-MOMP antibodies. Western blot analysis of serum from MOMP-vault-immunized mice confirmed reactivity exclusively with the 45 kDa MOMP band, demonstrating antigen specificity. Surprisingly, a subset of these mice also developed IgG antibodies against the major vault protein (MVP), as confirmed by immunoblotting against purified MVP particles. This response was not observed in mice immunized with GL-vaults or in mice previously infected with live chlamydiae, indicating that the MOMP antigen, when delivered within the vault structure, uniquely breaks immune tolerance to the self-protein MVP. Flow cytometry and intracellular cytokine staining confirmed that splenocytes from MOMP-vault-immunized mice produced high levels of IFNγ and low levels of IL-4 upon restimulation. The novel vaccine platform described herein enables mucosal immunization without the need for external adjuvants, leveraging the vault’s intrinsic ability to activate inflammasomes and promote dendritic cell maturation. The advantages of vault nanoparticles include their stability, scalability, ability to protect antigens from degradation, and capacity to induce tissue-resident memory T-cells in distal mucosal sites. The reduction in inflammation and immune-mediated pathology is attributed to the absence of TLR activation and the selective induction of Th1 immunity without concomitant neutrophil recruitment. The scope of the invention encompasses the use of vault-like particles for the delivery of any immunogenic peptide, with MOMP serving as a prototype for mucosal vaccine development against intracellular pathogens.
