# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to differentiating human pluripotent stem cells into hypothalamic neurons

The present invention relates to a method for the directed differentiation of human pluripotent stem cells into functional hypothalamic neurons, including those capable of synthesizing and secreting hypothalamic neuropeptides such as arginine vasopressin, oxytocin, corticotropin-releasing hormone, and thyrotropin-releasing hormone. More specifically, the invention provides a chemically defined, stepwise protocol for the efficient generation of spatially organized hypothalamic tissue from human pluripotent stem cells, enabling the production of mature, hormone-secreting neuronal populations that recapitulate key aspects of in vivo hypothalamic development and function. The method further enables the concurrent induction of pituitary tissue from adjacent surface ectoderm, resulting in a structurally and functionally integrated neuroendocrine unit. This technology is particularly useful for modeling human hypothalamic diseases, screening therapeutic agents, and developing cell-based regenerative therapies for disorders such as central diabetes insipidus, adipsic hypernatremia, and other neuroendocrine dysfunctions.

## BACKGROUND ART

- introduce hypothalamus
- limitations of hormone replacement therapy
- previous attempts at differentiating pluripotent stem cells into hypothalamic neurons
- importance of hypothalamic tissue for research and therapy

The hypothalamus is a highly conserved diencephalic structure located at the base of the forebrain that serves as the primary interface between the nervous and endocrine systems. It regulates vital physiological processes including body temperature, circadian rhythms, hunger, thirst, sexual behavior, and fluid homeostasis through the synthesis and release of neurohormones that control pituitary function. Among these, arginine vasopressin, produced by magnocellular neurons in the paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei, is critical for maintaining plasma osmolality by promoting renal water reabsorption. Deficiencies in vasopressin secretion lead to central diabetes insipidus, a condition characterized by excessive urine output and compensatory polydipsia, which, in severe cases, can result in life-threatening dehydration when osmoreceptor function is also impaired. Current treatment strategies rely on synthetic vasopressin analogs such as desmopressin; however, these therapies are associated with a high incidence of hyponatremia and lack the dynamic, stimulus-responsive regulation inherent to native hypothalamic neurons. Moreover, patients with combined osmoreceptor and vasopressin neuron dysfunction often become adipsic, rendering them unable to self-regulate fluid intake and placing them at constant risk of fatal hypernatremia. The absence of a physiologically accurate human model of hypothalamic development has severely limited progress in understanding the pathophysiology of these disorders, screening novel therapeutics, or developing regenerative interventions. While previous efforts have attempted to differentiate human pluripotent stem cells into hypothalamic lineages, these methods have yielded low efficiency, poor maturation, and no demonstrable hormone secretion in response to physiological stimuli. Furthermore, prior protocols failed to recapitulate the spatial organization of hypothalamic progenitors or to induce the concurrent development of pituitary tissue, thereby precluding the formation of a functional neuroendocrine axis in vitro. The ability to generate a self-organizing, integrated hypothalamic-pituitary structure from human pluripotent stem cells represents a critical unmet need in neuroendocrinology and regenerative medicine.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

- limitations of previous differentiation methods
- need for efficient differentiation of human pluripotent stem cells into hypothalamic neurons

Previous methods for deriving hypothalamic neurons from human pluripotent stem cells suffered from multiple critical limitations, including inconsistent aggregate formation, inefficient induction of hypothalamic identity, failure to generate mature, hormone-secreting neurons, and an inability to recapitulate the dorsal-ventral patterning essential for generating distinct neuronal subtypes. These shortcomings rendered prior protocols unsuitable for applications requiring high fidelity, reproducibility, or functional validation. Moreover, none of the existing approaches enabled the simultaneous differentiation of hypothalamic tissue alongside pituitary tissue derived from surface ectoderm, thereby preventing the formation of a physiologically relevant neuroendocrine unit capable of feedback regulation. There was a clear and pressing need for a robust, scalable, and chemically defined method that could reliably produce a high proportion of spatially organized hypothalamic precursor cells, guide their maturation into functional neurosecretory neurons, and induce the adjacent differentiation of pituitary lineages to form an integrated, self-regulating structure. Such a system would enable accurate disease modeling, high-throughput drug screening, and ultimately, cell replacement therapies for neuroendocrine disorders.

The present invention overcomes these limitations by establishing a precisely timed, multi-stage differentiation protocol that recapitulates the developmental sequence of human hypothalamic and pituitary organogenesis. The method begins with the formation of uniform aggregates of human pluripotent stem cells in a serum-free medium supplemented with a minimal concentration of knock-out serum replacement to enable stable aggregation without inducing telencephalic fate. Subsequent exposure to bone morphogenetic protein 4 and sonic hedgehog pathway agonists, at carefully calibrated concentrations, induces a population of RAX-positive hypothalamic progenitors with over 80% efficiency. By modulating the timing and dosage of these signals, the protocol enables selective induction of either dorsal hypothalamic precursors, marked by PAX6 and OTP, or ventral hypothalamic precursors, marked by NKX2.1. The addition of an Akt inhibitor during the early phase enhances rostral identity and promotes the specification of AVP-expressing magnocellular neurons. Following aggregate formation, a transition to gas-liquid interphase culture on Transwell inserts facilitates the emergence of OTP- and BRN2-positive precursors, which are then further matured into functional AVP-secreting neurons through two-dimensional dissociation culture on an extracellular matrix-coated surface. These neurons exhibit morphological features of magnocellular neurons, express neurophysin II and copeptin, and demonstrate stimulus-dependent secretion of vasopressin in response to potassium-induced depolarization. Concurrently, the same protocol induces surface ectoderm expressing E-cadherin and pan-cytokeratin, which differentiates into anterior pituitary tissue under the influence of FGF2 and serum alternatives, giving rise to CRH- and ACTH-producing cells. The resulting three-dimensional cellular structure integrates hypothalamic and pituitary compartments in close anatomical proximity, enabling paracrine signaling and functional feedback. The method further enables the generation of oxytocin-, TRH-, CRH-, NPY-, MCH-, orexin-, AgRP-, and POMC-expressing neurons within the same culture system, all of which are TUJ1-positive and respond to pharmacological stimuli. The entire process is conducted in a chemically defined, serum-free environment, ensuring reproducibility and clinical translatability.

The method for producing a cellular structure including hypothalamic tissue begins with the maintenance of human pluripotent stem cells in a feeder-free, serum-free environment using a basal medium supplemented with non-essential amino acids, pyruvate, and 2-mercaptoethanol. Pluripotency is confirmed through the expression of OCT4, NANOG, and SSEA-4, and the cells are dissociated into single cells using TrypLE Express in the presence of DNase I and the ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 to prevent anoikis. These cells are seeded at a density of 10,000 cells per well into low-adhesion, V-bottom 96-well plates to facilitate the formation of uniform aggregates through rapid reaggregation. The initial differentiation medium contains 5% knock-out serum replacement, which is essential for aggregate stability without inducing telencephalic fate. On day 6, the medium is supplemented with bone morphogenetic protein 4 at concentrations ranging from 0.5 to 2.0 nM and a sonic hedgehog pathway agonist such as smoothened agonist at concentrations between 0.1 and 1.0 μM, which together induce RAX-positive hypothalamic progenitors with over 80% efficiency. In a first embodiment, a concentration of 0.5 nM BMP4 and 0.5 μM SAG promotes dorsal hypothalamic identity, marked by PAX6 and OTP, while in a second embodiment, 1.5 nM BMP4 and 1.0 μM SAG, in combination with 0.5 μM Akt inhibitor, induces ventral hypothalamic identity marked by NKX2.1. The culture is maintained under static conditions in an oxygen-permeable vessel at 40% oxygen partial pressure to enhance cell viability and metabolic activity. After 30 days, aggregates are transferred to Transwell inserts for gas-liquid interphase culture in a medium containing N2, B27, and ciliary neurotrophic factor, which promotes the emergence of OTP- and BRN2-positive precursor cells. Subsequent dissociation into single cells and plating on Matrigel-, poly-D-lysine-, and laminin-coated surfaces in a medium supplemented with BDNF, NT-3, and LM22A-4 enables the maturation of AVP-, OXT-, TRH-, CRH-, NPY-, MCH-, orexin-, AgRP-, and POMC-expressing neurons, all of which are TUJ1-positive and demonstrate stimulus-dependent hormone secretion. Concurrently, the surface ectoderm component of the aggregate, identified by E-cadherin and pan-cytokeratin expression, is maintained under high oxygen conditions and exposed to FGF2 and a serum alternative such as B27 or N2, leading to the differentiation of anterior pituitary cells expressing ACTH and CRH. The resulting three-dimensional structure integrates hypothalamic and pituitary tissues in anatomical proximity, enabling functional neuroendocrine coupling. This structure may be transplanted into the hypothalamic region of animal models or patients with central diabetes insipidus, or used for high-throughput screening of drugs targeting neuroendocrine pathways. The entire process is conducted in a chemically defined, serum-free, and xeno-free environment, ensuring clinical compatibility and reproducibility across multiple human pluripotent stem cell lines, including both embryonic and induced pluripotent stem cells derived from somatic cells of patients with neuroendocrine disorders.

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary

- improve medium composition

The method further enables the simultaneous maturation of hypothalamic and pituitary tissues within a single three-dimensional aggregate through the sequential modulation of signaling pathways and culture conditions. Following the induction of RAX-positive hypothalamic progenitors and E-cadherin/pan-cytokeratin-positive surface ectoderm, the culture medium is supplemented with fibroblast growth factor 2 at a concentration of 10 ng/mL to promote the proliferation and differentiation of anterior pituitary precursors. The oxygen partial pressure is maintained at 40% to support metabolic demands and prevent central necrosis in larger aggregates. After 45 days of suspension culture, the tissue is analyzed by immunofluorescence, revealing the presence of ACTH-expressing corticotrophs and CRH-expressing neurons in close proximity to AVP- and OXT-expressing hypothalamic neurons. The co-localization of these cell types within the same aggregate, without the use of exogenous stromal support or co-culture systems, demonstrates the intrinsic self-organizing capacity of the system. The pituitary tissue exhibits morphological features consistent with in vivo anterior pituitary architecture, including clustering of hormone-producing cells and expression of key transcription factors such as PROP1 and PIT1. Functional validation confirms that these cells release ACTH in response to CRH stimulation, indicating the establishment of a functional hypothalamic-pituitary axis. The medium composition is further optimized by replacing fetal bovine serum with a defined serum alternative such as B27 or N2, eliminating batch variability and enhancing reproducibility. The use of this integrated structure enables the study of paracrine interactions between hypothalamic releasing hormones and pituitary target cells, providing a novel platform for modeling disorders of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis and evaluating the efficacy of drugs designed to modulate neuroendocrine feedback loops.

### 3. Conclusion

- summarize differentiation inducing conditions
- compare with previous report
- conclude novelty of differentiation inducing conditions

The present invention represents a substantial advancement over prior methods for hypothalamic differentiation by establishing a chemically defined, stepwise protocol that achieves unprecedented efficiency, reproducibility, and functional maturity in human pluripotent stem cell-derived hypothalamic and pituitary tissues. Unlike previous approaches that relied on undefined serum components, inconsistent aggregate formation, or failed to demonstrate hormone secretion, this method enables the generation of over 80% RAX-positive hypothalamic progenitors, the selective induction of dorsal or ventral subtypes through precise modulation of BMP4 and Shh signaling, and the maturation of AVP-secreting neurons that respond to depolarizing stimuli with physiologically relevant hormone release. The concurrent induction of pituitary tissue from surface ectoderm, without the need for exogenous scaffolds or co-culture systems, is entirely novel and has not been previously reported. The use of Akt inhibitor to enhance rostral identity, the optimization of oxygen tension to support long-term aggregate viability, and the transition from three-dimensional suspension to two-dimensional dissociation culture for neuronal maturation collectively constitute a unique and non-obvious combination of techniques. These innovations enable the construction of a self-organized, integrated neuroendocrine unit that recapitulates key aspects of human hypothalamic-pituitary development and function, thereby establishing a transformative platform for disease modeling, drug discovery, and regenerative medicine.

### 1. Appearance of Hypothalamic AVP Neuron

- confirm appearance of AVP neurons

The method consistently generates AVP-expressing neurons following the transition to dissociation culture on extracellular matrix-coated surfaces. These neurons exhibit large somatic cell bodies measuring approximately 40 × 20 micrometers, consistent with the morphology of magnocellular neurons in vivo. Immunohistochemical analysis confirms the co-expression of AVP with neurophysin II and copeptin, markers of mature vasopressinergic neurons. The absence of co-expression with CRH confirms the specificity of the magnocellular lineage. Quantitative analysis reveals that up to 15% of TUJ1-positive neurons express AVP, a rate significantly higher than any previously reported method. The neurons remain viable and functional for over 180 days in culture, demonstrating long-term stability and maturation. The expression of AVP is not observed in control cultures lacking BMP4, SAG, or Akt inhibitor, confirming the necessity of the defined signaling conditions. The detection of AVP mRNA and protein in the absence of exogenous growth factors further supports the autonomous differentiation of these neurons from pluripotent precursors.

The pituitary tissue derived from surface ectoderm matures into a structure resembling the anterior pituitary, expressing key transcription factors such as PROP1, PIT1, and SF1, and producing ACTH, prolactin, and growth hormone. When cultured in close proximity to hypothalamic neurons, the pituitary tissue demonstrates enhanced maturation and hormone secretion compared to pituitary tissue grown in isolation. Stimulation of hypothalamic neurons with high potassium induces the release of CRH, which in turn triggers a significant increase in ACTH secretion from adjacent pituitary cells, as measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. This paracrine response is absent in control cultures where hypothalamic and pituitary tissues are physically separated. The integrated structure also responds to pharmacological agents such as dexamethasone, which suppresses ACTH release through negative feedback, mimicking the physiological hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis. The functional coupling between hypothalamic releasing hormones and pituitary target cells confirms the establishment of a self-regulating neuroendocrine unit. This integrated structure represents the first in vitro model of a human hypothalamic-pituitary axis capable of bidirectional signaling and feedback regulation, providing a powerful tool for studying endocrine disorders and testing novel therapeutic interventions.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

- application of cellular structure
- scope of invention

The cellular structures produced by the disclosed method have broad industrial applicability in the fields of biomedical research, pharmaceutical development, and regenerative medicine. These structures serve as a physiologically accurate human model for studying the development and pathophysiology of hypothalamic and pituitary disorders, including central diabetes insipidus, adipsic hypernatremia, Prader-Willi syndrome, and Cushing’s disease. The system enables high-throughput screening of drug candidates for their ability to modulate hormone secretion, restore neuronal function, or correct developmental defects. The chemically defined, xeno-free nature of the protocol allows for the generation of patient-specific cell lines from somatic cells, facilitating personalized medicine approaches and autologous transplantation therapies. The integrated hypothalamic-pituitary structure may be transplanted into animal models of neuroendocrine dysfunction to assess functional recovery, and ultimately, into human patients suffering from irreversible hypothalamic damage. The method is scalable, reproducible, and compatible with automated culture systems, making it suitable for industrial-scale production of therapeutic cell products. The invention thus provides a foundational platform for the development of next-generation diagnostics, therapeutics, and cell-based interventions for neuroendocrine diseases.
