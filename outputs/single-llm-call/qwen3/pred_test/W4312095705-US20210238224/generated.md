# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- Proteolysis Targeting Chimeras (PROTACs) represent a novel class of bifunctional small molecules designed to induce the selective degradation of disease-associated proteins through the ubiquitin-proteasome system. These molecules operate by simultaneously engaging a target protein of interest and an E3 ubiquitin ligase, thereby facilitating the formation of a ternary complex that promotes polyubiquitination of the target protein and its subsequent proteasomal destruction. Unlike conventional inhibitors that merely block protein function, PROTACs act catalytically and irreversibly, enabling sustained biological effects at substoichiometric concentrations. This modality has demonstrated broad applicability across diverse therapeutic areas, particularly in oncology and inflammatory diseases, where traditional pharmacological approaches have been limited by target druggability, resistance mechanisms, or insufficient potency. The present invention pertains to a novel class of PROTACs engineered to concurrently modulate two distinct pathological pathways in chronic kidney disease by targeting the degradation of Smad3 and the stabilization of HIF-2α through a single molecular entity, thereby achieving a synergistic therapeutic outcome not attainable with conventional single-target agents.

## BACKGROUND ART

- Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a progressive, irreversible condition characterized by the accumulation of extracellular matrix, tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and loss of peritubular capillaries, ultimately leading to end-stage renal failure. The pathogenesis of CKD involves sustained activation of profibrotic signaling cascades, with the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β)/Smad3 pathway serving as a central mediator of fibroblast activation, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and collagen deposition. Despite extensive research into therapeutic interventions targeting this axis, clinical translation has been hampered by the pleiotropic roles of Smad3 in physiological homeostasis, resulting in dose-limiting toxicities when using traditional inhibitors that block its phosphorylation or nuclear translocation. Furthermore, the hypoxic microenvironment inherent in fibrotic kidneys triggers compensatory stabilization of hypoxia-inducible factors, particularly HIF-1α and HIF-2α, which exert divergent effects: HIF-1α promotes fibrogenesis through collagen induction, whereas HIF-2α exerts renoprotective actions by enhancing erythropoietin production, angiogenesis, and iron metabolism. Current pharmacological strategies targeting HIF pathways, such as prolyl hydroxylase domain (PHD) inhibitors, stabilize both HIF-α isoforms indiscriminately, leading to unintended activation of pro-fibrotic HIF-1α signaling and limited specificity. Existing PROTAC technologies have primarily focused on degrading single oncogenic or pathogenic proteins, often with limited tissue specificity and without leveraging the potential for simultaneous modulation of complementary biological pathways. Moreover, the recruitment of E3 ligases such as VHL by PROTACs can inadvertently stabilize endogenous ligase substrates, but this phenomenon has historically been regarded as an off-target liability rather than a therapeutically exploitable mechanism. There remains a critical unmet need for a precision therapeutic agent capable of selectively degrading a key profibrotic driver while concurrently enhancing the activity of a protective hypoxia-responsive factor, thereby addressing multiple facets of CKD pathophysiology in a single, coordinated intervention.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention discloses a novel double-target PROTAC molecule designed to simultaneously degrade Smad3 and stabilize HIF-2α by exploiting the natural substrate specificity of the von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) E3 ubiquitin ligase. This PROTAC comprises a first recognition ligand that binds selectively to Smad3, a second recognition ligand that binds selectively to VHL, and a chemically optimized linker connecting these two moieties to enable productive ternary complex formation. The Smad3-binding ligand is derived from a previously identified small molecule compound that interacts with a non-catalytic region of Smad3, thereby avoiding interference with its phosphorylation-dependent activation while enabling efficient ubiquitination. The VHL-binding ligand is a high-affinity small molecule that occupies the oxygen-dependent degradation domain of VHL, thereby competitively inhibiting its interaction with endogenous HIF-2α while permitting recruitment of Smad3 into the ubiquitination machinery. The linker is a succinic acid-based structure that provides optimal spatial orientation and conformational flexibility to facilitate simultaneous engagement of both target proteins within the cellular environment. This double-target PROTAC is constructed through a systematic screening process involving the evaluation of multiple linker lengths, chiral configurations of the Smad3-binding moiety, and binding affinities of both ligands, culminating in the selection of a single molecular species with superior degradation efficiency, target selectivity, and pharmacokinetic properties. The resulting compound is capable of inducing ubiquitin-mediated degradation of Smad3 in renal fibroblasts and tubular cells while preventing the degradation of HIF-2α by occupying VHL, thereby elevating HIF-2α protein levels without affecting HIF-1α, which is degraded through alternative E3 ligases. This dual mechanism of action results in the suppression of extracellular matrix deposition, attenuation of epithelial-mesenchymal transition, reduction of macrophage infiltration, restoration of peritubular capillary density, and enhancement of erythropoietin production and iron mobilization. The invention further encompasses methods of using this PROTAC for the treatment of chronic kidney disease, including its administration via subcutaneous, intraperitoneal, or oral routes, and its use in combination with supportive therapies for renal anemia or fibrosis. The benefits of this invention include a unified therapeutic approach that simultaneously targets multiple pathological drivers of CKD, reduced dosing frequency due to catalytic activity, enhanced tissue specificity conferred by VHL expression patterns, and a favorable safety profile demonstrated in preclinical models. The invention represents a paradigm shift in PROTAC design by intentionally harnessing the stabilization of an endogenous E3 ligase substrate as a therapeutic mechanism, thereby achieving a synergistic, multi-pathway modulation that is not achievable through conventional monotherapies.

## EMBODIMENTS

- The research hypothesis underlying this invention is that a single PROTAC molecule can be engineered to degrade a profibrotic transcription factor while simultaneously stabilizing a renoprotective hypoxia-inducible factor by exploiting the substrate specificity of a shared E3 ligase. This hypothesis is predicated on the observation that Smad3 and HIF-2α are both regulated by ubiquitin-mediated degradation pathways, but through distinct molecular mechanisms: Smad3 is degraded independently of oxygen tension, whereas HIF-2α is constitutively degraded by VHL under normoxic conditions. By designing a PROTAC that recruits VHL to ubiquitinate Smad3, the natural substrate HIF-2α is protected from degradation due to competitive occupancy of the VHL binding pocket. To construct this double-target PROTAC, a small molecule ligand with high affinity for Smad3 was identified through virtual screening and validated by surface plasmon resonance, while a second ligand with high specificity for the VHL protein was selected based on prior structural and biochemical characterization. The binding sites of both ligands were mapped using nuclear magnetic resonance and X-ray crystallography to ensure that conjugation with the linker would not disrupt target engagement. Multiple linker chemistries were synthesized, including polyethylene glycol-based and succinic acid-based variants, to evaluate their impact on ternary complex stability and degradation efficiency. The resulting PROTAC variants were chemically synthesized and purified to homogeneity using high-performance liquid chromatography and confirmed by mass spectrometry. The stereochemical configuration of the Smad3-binding moiety was systematically evaluated, revealing that the S-enantiomer conferred significantly greater potency in both Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization compared to the R-enantiomer. The optimized PROTAC, comprising the S-configured Smad3 ligand, the VHL ligand, and a succinic acid linker, demonstrated nanomolar potency in degrading Smad3 while elevating HIF-2α levels in both rat and human renal cell lines. Western blot analyses confirmed the dose- and time-dependent reduction of Smad3 protein and concomitant increase in HIF-2α, with no effect on HIF-1α, which is degraded by alternative E3 ligases such as RACK1, HAF, and CHIP. In vitro experiments in renal fibroblasts and tubular cells demonstrated that this PROTAC effectively suppressed collagen I and α-SMA expression, reduced inflammatory cytokine secretion, and enhanced endothelial marker expression. These findings validate the feasibility of designing a single molecule that achieves dual therapeutic outcomes by simultaneously hijacking and protecting distinct components of the ubiquitin-proteasome system.

### Example 1

- To identify a suitable Smad3-binding ligand, a library of small molecules was screened using surface plasmon resonance and computational docking against the MH2 domain of Smad3, yielding a lead compound with a dissociation constant in the low micromolar range. The binding site of this compound was confirmed through hydrogen-deuterium exchange mass spectrometry and mutagenesis studies, revealing interactions with a conserved hydrophobic groove distinct from the phosphorylation site. The VHL-binding ligand was selected from a previously published series of small molecules known to bind the β-domain of VHL with high affinity and selectivity. The precise attachment points for linker conjugation on both ligands were determined through chemical derivatization and activity assays to ensure retention of target binding. A series of PROTACs were synthesized by coupling the Smad3 ligand and VHL ligand via linkers of varying length and rigidity, including polyethylene glycol chains and aliphatic dicarboxylic acids. The resulting compounds were purified by preparative HPLC and characterized by high-resolution mass spectrometry to confirm molecular weight and purity. Structural integrity was further verified by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy. Functional activity was assessed in NRK-49F and HK2 cells using Western blot analysis, which revealed that the PROTAC bearing the succinic acid linker (CH2COOH)2 exhibited the most potent degradation of Smad3 at concentrations as low as 25 nM, while simultaneously increasing HIF-2α protein levels by more than two-fold. The S-enantiomer of the Smad3 ligand demonstrated significantly greater efficacy than the R-enantiomer in both degradation and stabilization assays. In vitro ubiquitination assays using ACHN cell lysates confirmed that Smad3 polyubiquitination was dependent on the presence of both the PROTAC and the proteasome, as evidenced by the accumulation of higher molecular weight ubiquitin-conjugated species upon treatment with the proteasome inhibitor MG132. The PROTAC did not induce ubiquitination of unrelated proteins, demonstrating target specificity. Cell viability assays confirmed the absence of cytotoxicity at therapeutic concentrations. These results established the optimized PROTAC as a potent, selective, and safe bifunctional agent capable of simultaneously degrading Smad3 and stabilizing HIF-2α, thereby validating the core invention.
