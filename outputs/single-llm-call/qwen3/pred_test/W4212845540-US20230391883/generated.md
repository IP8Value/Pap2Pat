# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- The present invention relates to monoclonal antibodies directed against the TIGIT protein for the treatment of cancer, particularly those exhibiting elevated expression of TIGIT in the tumor microenvironment. These antibodies are engineered to bind with high specificity and affinity to human TIGIT, thereby disrupting its inhibitory signaling pathways and restoring anti-tumor immune responses. The invention further encompasses antibodies with intact Fc effector functions, which enable critical immune-mediated mechanisms such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, trogocytosis, and activation of myeloid cells, all of which contribute to enhanced therapeutic efficacy. The antibodies described herein are distinct from those with silenced or attenuated Fc domains, as their functional integrity is essential for maximal anti-tumor activity in vivo. The invention also includes combination therapies wherein these TIGIT-targeting antibodies are administered concurrently with other immune checkpoint inhibitors, chemotherapeutic agents, or radiotherapeutic regimens to achieve synergistic clinical outcomes in patients with advanced malignancies.

## BACKGROUND

- TIGIT, also known as VSTM3 or VSIG9, is an immunoglobulin superfamily receptor expressed on activated T cells, natural killer cells, and regulatory T cells in both humans and mice. It functions as a co-inhibitory checkpoint molecule that suppresses immune activation by engaging its ligands, PVR (CD155) and PVRL2 (CD112), which are frequently overexpressed on tumor cells and antigen-presenting cells within the tumor microenvironment. Structurally, TIGIT comprises an extracellular immunoglobulin variable-like domain responsible for ligand binding, a transmembrane region, and an intracellular tail containing an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif and an immunoglobulin tyrosine-based switch motif, both of which mediate downstream inhibitory signaling. In healthy individuals, TIGIT expression is transient and tightly regulated; however, in the context of chronic antigen exposure such as that observed in cancer, TIGIT becomes persistently upregulated on tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, particularly on exhausted CD8+ T cells and immunosuppressive regulatory T cells. This sustained expression correlates with diminished cytokine production, reduced cytotoxic capacity, and poor clinical prognosis across multiple malignancies including non-small cell lung cancer, melanoma, and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. TIGIT competes with the activating receptor DNAM-1 for binding to PVR and PVRL2, thereby outcompeting stimulatory signals and tipping the balance toward immune tolerance. Furthermore, TIGIT engagement promotes the stability and suppressive function of regulatory T cells and induces tolerogenic phenotypes in dendritic cells, further dampening anti-tumor immunity. The clinical success of PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitors has demonstrated the therapeutic potential of reversing T cell exhaustion; however, a substantial proportion of patients exhibit primary or acquired resistance to single-agent blockade, often due to compensatory upregulation of alternative inhibitory receptors such as TIGIT, TIM-3, and LAG-3. This phenomenon underscores the need for combinatorial approaches that simultaneously target multiple immune checkpoints. Preclinical evidence suggests that dual blockade of TIGIT and PD-1 leads to enhanced T cell reactivation, increased interferon-gamma secretion, and superior tumor control compared to either agent alone. Despite the emergence of multiple anti-TIGIT monoclonal antibodies in clinical development, significant variability exists in their Fc domain engineering, with some candidates possessing wild-type IgG1 Fc regions capable of mediating effector functions, while others have been modified to reduce or eliminate FcγR binding. The functional consequences of these structural differences have not been systematically evaluated, leaving critical questions regarding the necessity of Fc-mediated mechanisms for optimal therapeutic efficacy unanswered.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention provides novel monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to human TIGIT with high affinity and are engineered to retain full Fc effector functionality, thereby enabling potent immune-mediated anti-tumor activity through multiple complementary mechanisms. The antibodies of the invention comprise heavy chain variable regions comprising the amino acid sequences of complementarity-determining regions 1, 2, and 3 as defined by the murine precursor clone mu1217, grafted onto human germline framework sequences, and light chain variable regions similarly derived from the same murine source. The heavy chain variable region contains the amino acid sequence of CDR-H1 as SEQ ID NO: 1, CDR-H2 as SEQ ID NO: 2, and CDR-H3 as SEQ ID NO: 3, while the light chain variable region contains CDR-L1 as SEQ ID NO: 4, CDR-L2 as SEQ ID NO: 5, and CDR-L3 as SEQ ID NO: 6. These antibodies bind human TIGIT with a dissociation constant (Kd) of less than 0.2 nM as determined by surface plasmon resonance, and exhibit complete blockade of TIGIT interaction with its ligands PVR and PVRL2. The constant regions of the antibodies are derived from human IgG1 with an intact Fc domain capable of binding FcγRI, FcγRIIA, FcγRIIB, and FcγRIIIA, thereby enabling antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, trogocytosis, and activation of natural killer cells and monocytes. The invention further encompasses the humanized version of the antibody designated hu1217-2-2, which retains the original binding specificity and functional profile of the murine precursor while exhibiting reduced immunogenicity in human subjects. The invention provides a method of treating cancer comprising the administration of an effective amount of the anti-TIGIT antibody described herein, either as a monotherapy or in combination with an anti-PD-1 antibody such as pembrolizumab or nivolumab. The method is applicable to a broad spectrum of solid tumors including non-small cell lung cancer, nasopharyngeal carcinoma, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, cervical cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, and melanoma. The antibody is administered via intravenous infusion at a dose ranging from 1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg every one to three weeks, with preferred regimens including 10 mg/kg every two weeks. The invention further provides for combination therapy with conventional chemotherapy agents such as platinum-based compounds or taxanes, as well as with chemoradiotherapy regimens, to enhance tumor cell killing and immune priming. The dosing regimen may be adjusted based on patient weight, tumor burden, and pharmacokinetic parameters, and may include loading doses followed by maintenance infusions. The antibodies of the invention are formulated as sterile, pyrogen-free solutions suitable for parenteral administration, and may be co-administered with stabilizing agents, buffers, and surfactants to ensure long-term stability and bioavailability.

### Anti-TIGIT Antibodies

- The anti-TIGIT antibodies of the invention are monoclonal antibodies derived from a murine precursor clone, mu1217, which was generated by immunizing mice with recombinant human TIGIT extracellular domain fused to murine IgG2a. The variable regions of the heavy and light chains were cloned, sequenced, and analyzed for homology to human germline immunoglobulin genes. The heavy chain variable region was grafted onto the human IGHV3-15 germline framework, while the light chain variable region was grafted onto the IGKV1-39 framework. A series of humanized variants were generated, with amino acid substitutions introduced into the framework regions to restore binding affinity lost during the humanization process. The final optimized variant, designated hu1217-2-2, exhibits a dissociation constant (Kd) of 0.135 nM for human TIGIT, as determined by surface plasmon resonance, and completely inhibits the binding of TIGIT to PVR and PVRL2 in cell-based assays. The antibody is expressed in Chinese hamster ovary cells as a full-length IgG1 molecule with a kappa light chain. The amino acid sequence of the heavy chain variable region comprises the CDR-H1 sequence of SEQ ID NO: 1, CDR-H2 of SEQ ID NO: 2, and CDR-H3 of SEQ ID NO: 3. The light chain variable region comprises CDR-L1 of SEQ ID NO: 4, CDR-L2 of SEQ ID NO: 5, and CDR-L3 of SEQ ID NO: 6. Mutated versions of the antibody with altered Fc domains, such as the Fc-silent variant BGB-A1217MF with the ELLGG233-237→PAAG- and P329A substitutions, were generated for comparative functional analysis. The hu1217-2-2 antibody retains full binding to FcγRI, FcγRIIA, FcγRIIB, and FcγRIIIA, enabling robust antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, trogocytosis, and activation of natural killer cells and monocytes. The antibody demonstrates no binding to C1q and exhibits no complement-dependent cytotoxicity, indicating selective engagement of FcγRs without triggering complement activation. The antibody is stable under physiological conditions, with a half-life of approximately 14 days in human serum, and exhibits low immunogenicity in preclinical models.

### Anti-PD1 Antibodies

- The invention further encompasses the use of anti-PD-1 antibodies in combination with the anti-TIGIT antibodies described herein. Such anti-PD-1 antibodies include pembrolizumab and nivolumab, both of which are clinically approved humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibodies that bind to the PD-1 receptor and block its interaction with PD-L1 and PD-L2. Pembrolizumab is indicated for the treatment of non-small cell lung cancer, melanoma, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, and other malignancies, while nivolumab is approved for similar indications including renal cell carcinoma and classical Hodgkin lymphoma. These antibodies are administered intravenously at doses ranging from 1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg every two to three weeks. In combination therapy with the anti-TIGIT antibody hu1217-2-2, the anti-PD-1 antibody is administered at the same dose and schedule, either concurrently or sequentially, to achieve synergistic T cell reactivation and enhanced anti-tumor immunity. The combination results in significantly increased interferon-gamma secretion, reduced expression of exhaustion markers such as TIM-3 and LAG-3, and improved tumor control in preclinical models compared to either agent alone. The anti-PD-1 antibody may be formulated in the same infusion solution as the anti-TIGIT antibody or administered separately, depending on clinical protocol and regulatory requirements.

### Further Alteration of the Framework of Fc Region

- The Fc region of the anti-TIGIT antibody may be further modified to enhance or modulate its effector functions. Amino acid residues within the CH2 domain may be substituted to increase affinity for activating Fcγ receptors such as FcγRIIIA (CD16a), thereby enhancing antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity. For example, substitutions such as S298A, E333A, and K334A have been shown to improve binding to FcγRIIIA, as described in U.S. Pat. Nos. 5,624,821 and 5,648,260. Alternatively, mutations such as L234A, L235A, and P329A may be introduced to reduce or abolish binding to FcγRs, thereby minimizing inflammatory side effects in non-target tissues. The ability of the antibody to fix complement may be altered by modifying residues involved in C1q binding, such as K322A and E333A, as disclosed in U.S. Pat. No. 6,194,551. The antibody may also be engineered to enhance antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis by increasing affinity for FcγRI (CD64), as described in PCT Publication WO 94/29351. Allotypic amino acid residues may be introduced to match the patient’s IgG subclass, thereby reducing immunogenicity. The antibody of the invention is of the IgG1 subclass with a kappa isotype, and its Fc region contains binding sites for FcγRI, FcγRII, FcγRIII, and FcRn, ensuring efficient recycling and prolonged serum half-life. Modifications to the Fc region may be made to optimize pharmacokinetics, reduce immunogenicity, or tailor effector function profiles for specific tumor types or patient populations.

### Antibody Production

- The anti-TIGIT antibodies of the invention may be produced by recombinant expression in mammalian host cells, such as Chinese hamster ovary cells, or by chemical synthesis and enzymatic ligation of peptide fragments. Full-length monoclonal antibodies are generated by transfecting expression vectors encoding the heavy and light chain genes into host cells under the control of strong promoters such as the cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter or the elongation factor-1α promoter. The expression vectors contain enhancer elements such as the woodchuck hepatitis virus posttranscriptional regulatory element to maximize transcript stability and protein yield. The antibodies are secreted into the culture medium and purified using protein A affinity chromatography, followed by ion-exchange and size-exclusion chromatography to achieve pharmaceutical-grade purity. Polynucleotides encoding the heavy and light chain variable regions are cloned into expression vectors with high sequence identity (>99%) to the native murine sequences, with human framework regions substituted as described. The nucleic acid sequences are optimized for codon usage in human cells to enhance translation efficiency. The antibodies may also be produced using hybridoma technology, wherein mice immunized with recombinant human TIGIT are sacrificed, splenocytes fused with myeloma cells, and clones screened for high-affinity binding and functional activity. Expression vectors may be designed for transient or stable transfection, and host cells may be selected for high productivity, low endotoxin production, and compatibility with large-scale bioreactor systems. Prokaryotic systems such as E. coli may be used for production of antibody fragments, but are not suitable for full-length IgG due to the absence of glycosylation machinery.

### Methods of Detection and Diagnosis

- The presence of TIGIT in biological samples may be detected using the anti-TIGIT antibodies of the invention in immunoassays such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay, flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, or mass spectrometry. TIGIT expression levels in tumor biopsies, peripheral blood mononuclear cells, or serum-derived exosomes may be compared to levels in healthy control tissues to determine diagnostic or prognostic significance. Elevated TIGIT expression on tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, particularly on regulatory T cells, is indicative of an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and may predict resistance to single-agent PD-1 blockade. The antibody may be conjugated to fluorescent dyes, enzymes, or radionuclides for imaging applications, enabling non-invasive visualization of TIGIT-expressing tumors. Diagnostic kits comprising the antibody, detection reagents, and instructions for use are provided for clinical and research applications.

### Methods of Treatment

- The invention provides a method of treating a TIGIT-associated disorder, including cancer, by administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of the anti-TIGIT antibody described herein, either as a monotherapy or in combination with other therapeutic agents. The antibody is administered parenterally, including intravenously, subcutaneously, or intramuscularly, with intravenous infusion being the preferred route. Dosing regimens may include an initial loading dose of 10 mg/kg followed by maintenance doses of 10 mg/kg every two weeks. The antibody may be formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers such as histidine, sucrose, polysorbate 80, and sodium chloride to ensure stability and solubility. An effective amount of the antibody is that which results in a measurable reduction in tumor volume, prolongation of progression-free survival, or improvement in overall survival relative to untreated controls. Initial candidate dosages are determined based on preclinical toxicology studies and phase I clinical trials, with dose escalation conducted to identify the maximum tolerated dose. Repeated administrations are performed over multiple cycles, typically every two to three weeks, for a duration of at least six months or until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Combination therapy with anti-PD-1 antibodies such as pembrolizumab or nivolumab is administered concurrently, with each agent given at its approved dose and schedule. Chemotherapy agents such as carboplatin and paclitaxel may be administered prior to or following antibody infusion to enhance tumor immunogenicity. Chemoradiotherapy regimens may be employed in locally advanced cancers such as esophageal squamous cell carcinoma or cervical cancer, with the antibody administered during or after completion of radiation therapy to sustain immune activation.

## EXAMPLE

### Example 1: Generation of Anti-TIGIT Monoclonal Antibody

- Anti-TIGIT monoclonal antibodies were generated by immunizing BALB/c mice with recombinant human TIGIT extracellular domain fused to murine IgG2a and administered with Freund’s adjuvant. Splenocytes from immunized mice were fused with the murine myeloma cell line SP2/0 using polyethylene glycol. Hybridoma clones were screened by ELISA for binding to recombinant human TIGIT and by flow cytometry for binding to TIGIT-expressing HEK293 cells. Positive clones were subcloned by limiting dilution and adapted to serum-free culture medium. Conditioned media were tested for functional activity in ligand blockade assays, and the most potent clone, designated mu1217, was selected for further characterization and humanization. The antibody was purified from culture supernatant using protein A affinity chromatography and analyzed for purity by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry.

### Example 2: Cloning and Sequence Analysis of TIGIT Antibodies

- Total RNA was extracted from the mu1217 hybridoma cell line, and cDNA was synthesized using oligo(dT) primers. The variable regions of the heavy and light chains were amplified by PCR using degenerate primers designed to target conserved framework regions. The PCR products were cloned into sequencing vectors and sequenced using Sanger methodology. The heavy chain variable region was found to have 92% homology to the murine IGHV1-18 germline gene, while the light chain variable region showed 89% homology to the IGKV1-13 germline gene. The complementarity-determining regions were identified using the Kabat and IMGT numbering systems, and the sequences were deposited in a public database.

### Example 3: Affinity Determination of Purified Murine Anti-TIGIT Antibodies by SPR

- Binding kinetics of the purified mu1217 antibody were analyzed using a BIAcore T-200 instrument. Human TIGIT-his protein was immobilized on a CM5 sensor chip via amine coupling. Serial dilutions of mu1217 were flowed over the chip at a flow rate of 30 μL/min. Association and dissociation phases were monitored in real time, and data were fitted to a 1:1 Langmuir binding model. The equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd) was calculated to be 0.11 nM, demonstrating high-affinity binding to human TIGIT.

### Example 4: Humanization of the Murine Anti-Human TIGIT mAb mu1217

- The murine variable regions of mu1217 were grafted onto human germline framework sequences with the highest sequence homology: IGHV3-15 for the heavy chain and IGKV1-39 for the light chain. Framework residues critical for maintaining the CDR loop conformation were retained, while non-conserved residues were replaced with human equivalents. A series of humanized variants were generated and expressed in CHO cells. The variant hu1217-2-2 exhibited the highest binding affinity and functional activity, with a Kd of 0.135 nM and complete blockade of TIGIT-PVR interaction. The antibody was further engineered into an IgG1mf format with a wild-type Fc domain and purified to homogeneity.

### Example 5: Binding Activities of Different Versions of 1217 to Native TIGIT

- Flow cytometry was performed on primary human T cells isolated from peripheral blood and tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes from cancer patients. The hu1217-2-2 antibody bound with high specificity to TIGIT-expressing cells, while control antibodies showed no binding. Binding was confirmed on both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, with the highest expression observed on regulatory T cells.

### Example 6: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies Block the Interactions of TIGIT with its Ligands PVR and PVR-L2

- A cell-based competition assay was performed using HEK293 cells stably expressing PVR or PVRL2. Serial dilutions of hu1217-2-2 were pre-incubated with TIGIT-mIgG2a fusion protein, and the ability of the fusion protein to bind to PVR or PVRL2 was measured by flow cytometry. hu1217-2-2 completely inhibited binding at concentrations as low as 1 nM, with IC50 values of 4.53 nM for PVR and 1.69 nM for PVRL2.

### Example 7: Activation of CMV-Specific Human T Cells by Anti-TIGIT Antibodies

- Peripheral blood mononuclear cells from HLA-A2-positive donors were stimulated with CMV pp65 peptide and IL-2 for seven days. The cells were co-cultured with HCT116 target cells pulsed with the same peptide in the presence of hu1217-2-2. IFN-γ secretion was measured by ELISA and found to be significantly increased in a dose-dependent manner, with maximal stimulation at 10 μg/mL.

### Example 8: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies Enhanced NK Cell-Mediated Cytotoxicity

- NK92MI/CD16a-V158 cells were co-cultured with BW5147.3/TIGIT target cells in the presence of hu1217-2-2 or BGB-A1217MF. Cytotoxicity was measured by LDH release assay. hu1217-2-2 induced dose-dependent killing, with 70% lysis at 10 μg/mL, while BGB-A1217MF showed no cytotoxicity. CD107a and perforin expression on NK cells was significantly upregulated only in the presence of hu1217-2-2.

### Example 9: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies can Reduce the Surface Expression of TIGIT Receptor Via FcγR-Mediated Trogocytosis

- Jurkat/TIGIT/DNAM-1 cells were incubated with CF633-labeled hu1217-2-2 or BGB-A1217MF and co-cultured with HEK293 cells expressing FcγRI, FcγRIIA, FcγRIIB, or FcγRIIIA. After 24 hours, TIGIT surface expression on Jurkat cells was measured by flow cytometry. hu1217-2-2 induced significant reduction in TIGIT expression only in the presence of FcγR-expressing acceptor cells, with FcγRI showing the strongest effect. Blocking FcγRI with a specific antibody abolished trogocytosis. Similar results were observed in primary human T cells co-cultured with monocytes or dendritic cells.

### Example 10: ADCC Using Human PBMCs as Target Cells

- Primary PBMCs from lung cancer patients were co-cultured with purified NK cells from healthy donors in the presence of hu1217-2-2 or control antibodies. Flow cytometry was used to quantify Treg depletion. hu1217-2-2 induced a 60% reduction in Treg frequency at 10 μg/mL, while BGB-A1217MF had no effect. Effector CD4+ and CD8+ T cells remained unaffected.

### Example 11: CDC Using Human PBMCs as Target Cells

- Pre-activated human PBMCs were incubated with hu1217-2-2 and fresh autologous sera. Cell lysis was measured by propidium iodide exclusion. No detectable complement-dependent cytotoxicity was observed, even at high antibody concentrations, confirming the absence of C1q binding.

### Example 12: pH Dependent Binding Affinity of hu1217-2-2/IgG1

- Surface plasmon resonance was performed at pH 7.4 and pH 6.0. Binding affinity at pH 6.0 was 3.5-fold higher than at pH 7.4, indicating pH-dependent binding. This property enhances target retention in the acidic tumor microenvironment.

### Example 13: hu1217-2-2 Antibody Toxicology

- Single-dose and repeat-dose toxicology studies were conducted in cynomolgus monkeys. No treatment-related adverse events were observed at doses up to 100 mg/kg. No anti-drug antibodies were detected, and no histopathological changes were observed in major organs.
