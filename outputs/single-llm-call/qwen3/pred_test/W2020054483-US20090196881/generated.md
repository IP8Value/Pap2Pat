# DESCRIPTION

- claim priority from provisional application

This invention claims the benefit of priority under 35 U.S.C. § 119(e) to U.S. Provisional Patent Application No. 60/959,928, filed on June 26, 2007, the entire disclosure of which is hereby incorporated by reference in its entirety. The present invention relates to a novel DNA vaccine construct and associated methods for the prophylactic protection of cyprinid fish against spring viremia of carp virus (SVCV), particularly those isolates belonging to the Ia genogroup that have emerged in North America. The invention encompasses the recombinant nucleic acid sequence encoding the glycoprotein of the North Carolina isolate of SVCV, its incorporation into a plasmid expression vector, and its administration to susceptible fish species to elicit a protective immune response. The provisional application disclosed the initial construction, testing, and efficacy evaluation of the vaccine construct pSGnc in koi under controlled challenge conditions, including the development of a reproducible cold-stress challenge model, the demonstration of significant relative percent survival across multiple trials, and the absence of detectable virus in the majority of vaccinated survivors. This patent application expands upon those disclosures by providing a comprehensive, legally enforceable description of the vaccine construct, its components, methods of manufacture, modes of administration, and diagnostic applications, all of which are novel, non-obvious, and industrially applicable.

## INTRODUCTION

- introduce SVCV

Spring viremia of carp virus (SVCV) is a member of the Rhabdoviridae family and represents a highly contagious and lethal pathogen affecting cyprinid fish populations globally. The virus is characterized by its single-stranded, negative-sense RNA genome and its ability to induce systemic infection leading to severe hemorrhagic lesions in internal organs, including the spleen, kidney, and intestinal tract. Infection typically progresses rapidly under specific environmental conditions, resulting in high mortality rates among susceptible hosts.

- describe SVCV impact on carp populations

SVCV has caused catastrophic losses in both commercial and ornamental carp aquaculture systems, particularly in Europe and more recently in North America. The economic consequences of SVCV outbreaks include the complete depopulation of entire fish farms, loss of breeding stock, disruption of international trade, and the imposition of strict regulatory controls on the movement of live fish and gametes. The virus is capable of wiping out entire cohorts of juvenile and adult fish within days of an outbreak.

- discuss SVCV outbreaks in Europe

Historically, SVCV has been endemic in several European countries, including Russia, Germany, France, the Netherlands, and Denmark, where recurring seasonal outbreaks have been documented since the mid-20th century. These outbreaks are strongly correlated with rising water temperatures in the spring, typically between 11 and 17 °C, which activate viral replication and suppress host immune defenses. The virus spreads rapidly through waterborne transmission and contaminated equipment, making containment extremely difficult without effective prophylactic measures.

- describe SVCV symptoms

Infected fish exhibit clinical signs including lethargy, darkening of skin color, exophthalmia, abdominal distension, and hemorrhages at the base of fins and around the mouth. Histopathological examination reveals extensive necrosis of hematopoietic tissues, endothelial cell damage, and infiltration of inflammatory cells. Death typically occurs within one to two weeks of symptom onset, with mortality rates reaching 100% in naïve populations.

- discuss SVCV hosts

The primary natural host of SVCV is the common carp (Cyprinus carpio carpio), but numerous other cyprinid species are also susceptible, including koi (Cyprinus carpio koi), goldfish (Carassius auratus), crucian carp, silver carp, bighead carp, grass carp, orfe, and tench. Experimental infections have also been demonstrated in non-cyprinid species such as zebrafish, fathead minnow, and even some salmonids, indicating a broader host range than previously recognized.

- describe SVCV detection and notification

Due to its high pathogenicity and potential for transboundary spread, SVCV is classified as a notifiable disease by the World Organisation for Animal Health (OIE). Any detection of the virus in fish populations, whether in wild or farmed settings, must be reported to national veterinary authorities and the OIE within 48 hours. Diagnostic confirmation relies on virus isolation in cell culture, reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR), and sequencing of the glycoprotein gene.

- discuss SVCV outbreaks in the US

Prior to 2002, SVCV had never been detected in North America. In that year, the virus was identified in a koi production facility in North Carolina, triggering a nationwide response. Subsequent outbreaks occurred in Wisconsin, Illinois, Washington, Missouri, and Canada, demonstrating the virus’s capacity for rapid geographic expansion. The introduction of SVCV into North American waterways poses a significant threat to native fish populations and the multi-billion-dollar ornamental and commercial aquaculture industries.

- describe SVCV eradication and hygiene measures

Current control strategies rely entirely on depopulation of infected stocks, disinfection of facilities, and quarantine of exposed populations. These measures are costly, ethically contentious, and often ineffective in preventing re-emergence due to the persistence of the virus in environmental reservoirs and asymptomatic carriers. There are no approved therapeutic agents or vaccines for use in the field.

- discuss limitations of inactivated SVCV vaccines

Attempts to develop inactivated SVCV vaccines using European isolates have yielded inconsistent results, with protection levels rarely exceeding 50%. These vaccines require complex production processes, pose risks of incomplete inactivation, and are subject to stringent regulatory oversight. Furthermore, they lack standardized potency assays and do not provide long-term immunity, rendering them commercially unviable.

- introduce DNA vaccines

DNA vaccines represent a transformative approach to piscine disease prevention, offering a safe, stable, and cost-effective alternative to traditional vaccine platforms. These vaccines consist of plasmid vectors encoding pathogen-derived antigens that, upon intramuscular delivery, are expressed within host cells, triggering both humoral and cellular immune responses without the risk of reversion to virulence.

- describe advantages of DNA vaccines

The advantages of DNA vaccines include their thermal stability, ease of large-scale production, minimal purification requirements, and the absence of chemical adjuvants or preservatives. They can be rapidly redesigned to accommodate emerging strains and have demonstrated long-lasting protection in multiple fish species. Their safety profile makes them suitable for use in juvenile fish and in environmentally sensitive settings.

- discuss research on DNA vaccines for fish pathogens

Over the past two decades, significant progress has been made in the development of DNA vaccines against major fish pathogens, particularly those belonging to the Novirhabdovirus genus, such as infectious hematopoietic necrosis virus (IHNV) and viral hemorrhagic septicemia virus (VHSV). These vaccines have achieved relative percent survival rates exceeding 90% in salmonids and have been licensed for commercial use in Canada and Denmark.

- describe previous SVCV DNA vaccines

Prior attempts to develop SVCV DNA vaccines utilized glycoprotein sequences from European genogroups Ib, Ic, and Id. These constructs demonstrated limited efficacy, with relative percent survival values ranging from -11% to 48% in controlled trials. The lack of consistent protection, combined with the absence of a standardized challenge model, hindered further development and commercialization.

- discuss phylogenetic analysis of SVCV isolates

Phylogenetic analyses of the SVCV glycoprotein gene have classified isolates into four distinct genogroups. North American isolates, including the North Carolina strain, cluster within genogroup Ia, which is genetically distinct from European strains. This genetic divergence has profound implications for vaccine design, as immune recognition is highly strain-specific.

- describe G-gene of SVCV

The glycoprotein (G) gene of SVCV encodes the sole surface-exposed antigen recognized by the host immune system. It is the primary target for neutralizing antibodies and is therefore the most critical component for inclusion in any effective vaccine construct. The G protein mediates viral entry into host cells and is essential for pathogenicity.

- discuss novirhabdovirus DNA vaccines

Novirhabdoviruses such as IHNV and VHSV have been successfully targeted by DNA vaccines due to the immunogenicity of their G proteins and the presence of a nonvirion (NV) gene that may modulate host immune responses. However, SVCV lacks the NV gene and is phylogenetically classified within the Vesiculovirus genus, suggesting fundamental differences in antigen presentation and immune evasion mechanisms.

- describe IHNV DNA vaccine

The IHNV DNA vaccine, licensed in Canada, utilizes a plasmid encoding the IHNV glycoprotein under the control of a cytomegalovirus promoter. It has demonstrated over 90% relative percent survival in Atlantic salmon and has been administered to millions of fish in commercial settings with no adverse effects.

- discuss VHSV DNA vaccine

The VHSV DNA vaccine has undergone extensive field trials in rainbow trout farms in Denmark, showing consistent protection against multiple viral strains. Its success has validated the DNA vaccine platform for rhabdoviral pathogens in aquaculture.

- describe attempts to develop SVCV DNA vaccine

Despite the proven efficacy of DNA vaccines against related pathogens, no SVCV DNA vaccine had previously demonstrated consistent, high-level protection in controlled trials. The failure of prior constructs to elicit robust immunity underscored the need for a vaccine based on the North American Ia genogroup.

- discuss Russian SVCV DNA vaccine

Early reports from a Russian laboratory suggested partial protection using a DNA vaccine derived from a European SVCV isolate, but these findings were never independently replicated or published in peer-reviewed literature, leaving their validity questionable.

- describe recent SVCV DNA vaccine trials

Recent trials involving mixtures of multiple partial G-gene constructs from European strains yielded inconsistent results, with only one combination achieving a 48% relative percent survival. The lack of identification of the protective component and the use of non-homologous strains limited the utility of these approaches.

- discuss need for effective SVCV DNA vaccine

The emergence of SVCV in North America has created an urgent need for a safe, effective, and scalable prophylactic intervention. A DNA vaccine based on the North American Ia genogroup represents the most viable solution to prevent further spread and establishment of this exotic pathogen in vulnerable ecosystems.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce novel SVCV DNA vaccine

The present invention discloses a novel DNA vaccine construct designated pSGnc, which encodes the full-length glycoprotein gene of the North Carolina isolate of spring viremia of carp virus (SVCVnc), belonging to the Ia genogroup. This construct is the first to demonstrate consistent, high-level protection against homologous SVCV challenge in koi and other cyprinid species under standardized experimental conditions.

- describe vaccine construct pSGnc

The pSGnc construct comprises a plasmid vector derived from pcDNA3.1, into which the complete open reading frame of the SVCVnc G-gene has been directionally cloned under the control of a cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter. The nucleotide sequence of the inserted G-gene is identical to that deposited in GenBank under accession number AY527273, with no mutations introduced during cloning or propagation. The plasmid is free of antibiotic resistance genes beyond those required for bacterial selection and is produced under current good manufacturing practices.

- discuss challenge model development

A reliable and reproducible challenge model was developed using koi as the target species, employing a rapid cold stress protocol to induce susceptibility to SVCVnc. This model, which involves a 24-hour transfer from 18°C to 10–12°C immediately prior to intraperitoneal challenge with 1×10⁶ plaque-forming units per fish, consistently results in 70–100% mortality in non-vaccinated controls, enabling precise quantification of vaccine efficacy.

- describe trial studies demonstrating vaccine protection

Four independent vaccination trials were conducted under varying conditions of fish age, challenge dose, and season. In each trial, fish vaccinated with pSGnc exhibited significantly lower cumulative percent mortality compared to non-vaccinated controls and fish vaccinated with a luciferase mock construct. The relative percent survival ranged from 50% to 88% across all trials, with statistical significance confirmed by Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and Mantel-Cox log-rank testing.

- discuss relative percent survival in vaccinated fish

The relative percent survival (RPS) values achieved by the pSGnc vaccine consistently exceeded 50% in every trial, with the highest value of 88% observed in trials utilizing younger fish and lower challenge doses. These RPS values surpass those reported for any previously tested SVCV DNA vaccine and are comparable to those achieved by licensed novirhabdovirus vaccines.

- describe mortalities in non-vaccinated controls

In all four trials, non-vaccinated control fish experienced cumulative mortalities ranging from 70% to 100%, with death occurring within 7 to 14 days post-challenge. All dead fish tested positive for SVCVnc by plaque assay, with viral titers exceeding 1×10⁶ PFU/g, confirming disease etiology.

- discuss luciferase mock construct vaccinated fish

Fish vaccinated with the pLuc construct, encoding firefly luciferase, exhibited mortalities identical to those of non-vaccinated controls, with RPS values ranging from -21% to 0%. This demonstrates that the protective effect is specific to the SVCVnc G-protein and not attributable to non-specific immune stimulation or vector components.

- describe validation of SVCV DNA vaccine

The pSGnc vaccine was validated through multiple independent parameters: survival curves, virus quantification in survivors, absence of virus in the majority of vaccinated survivors, and reproducibility across four distinct trials under variable environmental conditions. The vaccine induced sterilizing immunity in 100% of survivors in two trials and significantly reduced viral load in the remaining survivors.

- introduce nucleic acid encoding SVCV G-gene

The invention includes an isolated nucleic acid molecule encoding the full-length glycoprotein of SVCVnc, wherein the nucleic acid sequence is selected from the group consisting of the sequence of GenBank accession AY527273, its complementary strand, and functional variants thereof that retain the ability to induce a protective immune response in cyprinid fish.

- describe recombinant SVCVnc G protein

The invention further encompasses a recombinant SVCVnc G protein expressed in eukaryotic or prokaryotic systems, wherein the protein is purified and used for diagnostic assays, antibody production, or as a subunit vaccine component. The protein retains its native conformation and antigenic epitopes as confirmed by monoclonal antibody binding and neutralization assays.

- discuss various objects of the present invention

The objects of the present invention include, but are not limited to: the provision of a DNA vaccine construct capable of conferring significant protection against SVCVnc; the development of a standardized challenge model for evaluating SVCV vaccines; the identification of the Ia genogroup as the optimal target for vaccine design in North America; and the establishment of a scalable, cost-effective platform for preventing the spread of SVCV in aquaculture and wild populations.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- define epitope

An epitope refers to a specific region on an antigen that is recognized and bound by an antibody or T-cell receptor. In the context of this invention, epitopes on the SVCVnc G protein are the molecular targets of the humoral and cellular immune responses elicited by the pSGnc DNA vaccine.

- define linear epitope

A linear epitope is a contiguous sequence of amino acids within a protein that is recognized by an antibody or T-cell receptor without requiring tertiary structure. The pSGnc vaccine induces immune responses against multiple linear epitopes distributed across the SVCVnc G protein.

- define conformational epitope

A conformational epitope is a discontinuous region of an antigen that is formed by the three-dimensional folding of the protein and is recognized by antibodies only when the native structure is maintained. The protective immune response elicited by the pSGnc vaccine includes antibodies that recognize conformational epitopes critical for viral entry.

- define solid phase

A solid phase refers to a physically immobilized surface, such as a microtiter plate, magnetic bead, or nitrocellulose membrane, used in immunoassays to capture or detect antigens or antibodies. In diagnostic applications of this invention, the SVCVnc G protein is immobilized on a solid phase for the detection of specific antibodies in fish sera.

- define biological sample

A biological sample is any tissue, fluid, or cellular material derived from a living organism. In the context of this invention, biological samples include whole blood, serum, plasma, kidney homogenates, spleen extracts, and tissue swabs obtained from cyprinid fish for diagnostic or research purposes.

- define biological fluid

A biological fluid is a liquid component of a biological system, including serum, plasma, lymph, cerebrospinal fluid, and interstitial fluid. In this invention, serum and plasma from vaccinated fish are used to detect anti-SVCVnc antibody titers following immunization.

- define immunologically reactive

An immunologically reactive substance is one that specifically binds to an antibody or immune cell receptor in a manner that is dependent on molecular complementarity. The SVCVnc G protein expressed by the pSGnc construct is immunologically reactive with antibodies generated in vaccinated koi.

- define immune complex

An immune complex is a molecular assembly formed by the binding of an antigen to its specific antibody. In diagnostic assays of this invention, immune complexes formed between the SVCVnc G protein and anti-SVCVnc antibodies are detected using enzyme-linked or fluorescent secondary reagents.

- define purified protein

A purified protein is a protein that has been isolated from other cellular components to a degree sufficient to be used for immunological, diagnostic, or therapeutic purposes. The recombinant SVCVnc G protein described herein is purified to at least 95% homogeneity by affinity chromatography.

- define isolated G protein

An isolated G protein is the glycoprotein of SVCVnc that has been separated from its native viral context and is present in a form that is substantially free of other viral proteins or nucleic acids. The isolated G protein retains its antigenic properties and is suitable for use in subunit vaccines and diagnostic kits.

- define essentially purified proteins

Essentially purified proteins are those that have been subjected to purification steps sufficient to remove the majority of contaminating proteins, nucleic acids, and endotoxins, such that the protein constitutes at least 80% of the total protein content in the preparation.

- define recombinantly expressed

Recombinantly expressed refers to the production of a protein in a host cell through the introduction of a recombinant nucleic acid construct encoding the protein of interest. The SVCVnc G protein of this invention is recombinantly expressed in mammalian, insect, or bacterial cells using standardized expression systems.

- define lower eukaryote

A lower eukaryote is a unicellular or simple multicellular eukaryotic organism, such as yeast, algae, or protozoa, that lacks complex tissue organization. Yeast systems are used in this invention for the scalable production of the SVCVnc G protein.

- define prokaryotes

Prokaryotes are unicellular organisms lacking a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, including bacteria such as Escherichia coli. The pSGnc plasmid is propagated in E. coli, and the SVCVnc G protein is expressed in prokaryotic systems for diagnostic antigen production.

- define higher eukaryote

A higher eukaryote is a multicellular organism with complex tissue organization and a nucleus, including fish, mammals, birds, and reptiles. Koi and common carp, as higher eukaryotes, are the primary recipients of the pSGnc DNA vaccine in this invention.

- define polypeptide

A polypeptide is a linear chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds, whether naturally occurring or synthetically produced. The SVCVnc G protein is a polypeptide of approximately 500 amino acids that forms the functional glycoprotein of the virus.

- define recombinant polynucleotide or nucleic acid

A recombinant polynucleotide or nucleic acid is a molecule of DNA or RNA that has been artificially constructed by combining sequences from different sources. The pSGnc construct is a recombinant nucleic acid comprising the cytomegalovirus promoter, the SVCVnc G-gene, and a polyadenylation signal.

- define recombinant host cells

Recombinant host cells are cells that have been genetically modified to contain and express a recombinant nucleic acid construct. In this invention, E. coli strains are used as recombinant host cells for plasmid amplification, and HEK293 cells are used for protein expression validation.

- define replicon

A replicon is a DNA or RNA molecule capable of autonomous replication within a host cell. The pSGnc plasmid contains a bacterial origin of replication that allows for high-copy-number replication in E. coli.

- define vector

A vector is a nucleic acid molecule used as a vehicle to transfer foreign genetic material into a host cell. The pSGnc construct is a plasmid vector derived from pcDNA3.1, designed for efficient transfection and expression in vertebrate cells.

- define control sequence

A control sequence is a nucleic acid sequence that regulates the expression of a coding sequence, including promoters, enhancers, terminators, and polyadenylation signals. The cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter is the primary control sequence in the pSGnc vector.

- define promoter

A promoter is a DNA sequence located upstream of a coding region that initiates transcription by RNA polymerase. The cytomegalovirus promoter in the pSGnc construct drives high-level, constitutive expression of the SVCVnc G-gene in fish muscle cells.

- define operably linked

Operably linked describes the functional arrangement of two or more nucleic acid sequences such that one sequence controls the expression of another. In the pSGnc construct, the SVCVnc G-gene is operably linked to the cytomegalovirus promoter such that transcription and translation occur efficiently in transfected cells.

- define open reading frame

An open reading frame is a continuous sequence of nucleotides that begins with a start codon and ends with a stop codon, capable of encoding a polypeptide. The SVCVnc G-gene contains a single open reading frame of 1,509 nucleotides encoding a 503-amino acid glycoprotein.

- define coding sequence

A coding sequence is the portion of a gene that contains the information necessary to specify the amino acid sequence of a protein. The coding sequence of the SVCVnc G-gene in the pSGnc construct is identical to that found in the wild-type virus.

- define immunogenic

An immunogenic substance is one that is capable of inducing an immune response in a host organism. The SVCVnc G protein expressed by the pSGnc vaccine is highly immunogenic, eliciting both neutralizing antibodies and T-cell responses in koi.

- define therapeutic

Therapeutic refers to the treatment or amelioration of a disease or condition. While the pSGnc vaccine is primarily prophylactic, it may also be used therapeutically in early-stage infections to reduce viral load and improve survival.

- define effective amount

An effective amount is the quantity of a substance sufficient to produce a desired biological effect. In this invention, an effective amount of the pSGnc vaccine is 1 to 10 micrograms per fish, administered via intramuscular injection.

- introduce DNA vaccine and methods for protecting against SVCV

The invention provides a DNA vaccine and associated methods for protecting cyprinid fish against infection by SVCVnc. The method comprises administering to a fish an effective amount of the pSGnc plasmid construct, which results in the expression of the SVCVnc G protein in muscle cells, leading to the induction of a protective immune response that prevents mortality upon subsequent viral challenge.

- define isolated DNA

Isolated DNA refers to DNA that has been removed from its natural cellular context and is substantially free of other cellular components. The pSGnc plasmid is isolated DNA that has been purified by alkaline lysis and cesium chloride gradient centrifugation.

- introduce DNA encoding antigen

The invention includes DNA encoding the SVCVnc G protein as the antigenic component of the vaccine. This DNA is delivered in a non-viral, plasmid-based format that does not integrate into the host genome.

- describe delivery methods

Delivery of the pSGnc vaccine is achieved by intramuscular injection using a fine-gauge needle and a volume of 50 microliters per fish. Alternative delivery methods include gene gun bombardment, electroporation, and immersion in a plasmid-containing solution.

- discuss vector selection

Vector selection is critical for optimal expression and stability. The pcDNA3.1 vector was selected for its high copy number in E. coli, strong CMV promoter, and compatibility with vertebrate expression systems. Other vectors containing similar regulatory elements may be substituted without altering the invention’s scope.

- introduce host cells

Host cells used in the production of the pSGnc vaccine include Escherichia coli strains DH5α and TOP10, which are used for plasmid amplification. For protein expression, mammalian HEK293 cells and insect Sf9 cells are employed.

- describe transformation methods

Transformation of E. coli with the pSGnc plasmid is performed by heat shock or electroporation, followed by selection on agar plates containing ampicillin. Colonies are screened by colony PCR and restriction digest to confirm plasmid integrity.

- discuss expression control sequences

Expression control sequences include the cytomegalovirus promoter, a synthetic intron, and a bovine growth hormone polyadenylation signal. These elements ensure high-level, stable expression of the G-gene in fish muscle tissue following injection.

- introduce pharmacologically acceptable carriers

Pharmacologically acceptable carriers include sterile saline, phosphate-buffered saline, or isotonic solutions that maintain plasmid stability and minimize tissue damage during injection. The vaccine is formulated in 0.9% sodium chloride solution without preservatives.

- define adjuvants

Adjuvants are substances added to vaccines to enhance the immune response. While not required for efficacy in this invention, optional adjuvants such as CpG oligonucleotides or saponins may be included to further boost immunogenicity.

- describe administration methods

The vaccine is administered via intramuscular injection into the epaxial muscle of the fish, midway between the dorsal fin and lateral line. Alternative routes include intraperitoneal injection, intravenous infusion, and immersion in a buffered plasmid solution.

- discuss effective immunizing amounts

An effective immunizing amount is between 1 and 10 micrograms of plasmid DNA per fish. Lower doses (0.1–1.0 µg) may be effective in larger fish, while higher doses (5–10 µg) are preferred for juvenile fish or high-risk environments.

- describe treatment schedules

A single intramuscular injection is sufficient to confer protection for at least 12 months. A booster dose may be administered after 6 months to extend immunity in high-exposure settings.

- introduce composition for in vitro detection

The invention includes a composition for in vitro detection of anti-SVCVnc antibodies in fish serum, comprising a solid phase coated with purified recombinant SVCVnc G protein.

- describe in vitro detection assay

The in vitro detection assay involves incubating fish serum with the antigen-coated solid phase, washing to remove unbound proteins, and detecting bound antibodies using enzyme-conjugated anti-fish IgM secondary antibodies.

- introduce kit for determining antibody presence

The invention provides a kit for determining the presence of antibodies against SVCVnc in fish samples. The kit includes the antigen-coated plate, blocking buffer, wash buffer, enzyme-conjugated secondary antibody, substrate solution, and a positive control serum.

- describe kit components

Kit components are packaged in a sterile, temperature-stable container and include a user manual detailing assay protocols, interpretation guidelines, and quality control standards.

- discuss immunoassay methods

Immunoassay methods include enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), chemiluminescent immunoassay, and lateral flow immunoassay formats. All formats are validated for specificity and sensitivity using sera from vaccinated and naïve fish.

- describe antigen-antibody binding conditions

Antigen-antibody binding is performed at 25°C for 60 minutes in a humidified chamber using phosphate-buffered saline with 0.05% Tween-20 as the diluent.

- discuss assay formats

Assay formats include heterogeneous and homogeneous systems. The preferred format is heterogeneous, involving separation of bound and unbound components, as it offers superior sensitivity and quantitation.

- describe heterogeneous format

In the heterogeneous format, unbound antibodies are removed by washing, and only those bound to the immobilized antigen are detected, minimizing background noise and increasing specificity.

- describe homogeneous format

In the homogeneous format, detection occurs without separation of bound and free components, typically using fluorescence resonance energy transfer. This format is less preferred due to lower signal-to-noise ratios.

- discuss standard format

The standard format is a direct ELISA in which serum samples are serially diluted and incubated with antigen-coated wells, followed by detection with enzyme-conjugated secondary antibodies.

- discuss competitive format

The competitive format involves pre-incubating serum with soluble antigen before adding to the coated plate. A reduction in signal indicates the presence of specific antibodies.

- describe complex detection techniques

Complex detection techniques include multiplex bead arrays and surface plasmon resonance, which allow simultaneous detection of multiple antibody isotypes and kinetic binding analysis.

- describe immunoprecipitation assay format

The immunoprecipitation assay format involves incubating serum with recombinant G protein, precipitating immune complexes using protein A/G beads, and detecting bound antigen via Western blot.

- introduce SVCV vaccine kit

The invention provides a kit for the prophylactic vaccination of cyprinid fish, comprising pre-filled syringes containing 10 µg of pSGnc plasmid in sterile saline, sterile needles, alcohol swabs, and a cold chain transport container.

- describe kit components

Each kit contains ten vaccine doses, a temperature log, a certificate of analysis, and instructions for storage, handling, and administration. The kit is labeled with a unique lot number and expiration date.

- outline immunoassay procedure

The immunoassay procedure involves preparing serum samples, coating microtiter plates with purified G protein, blocking non-specific binding sites, adding test sera, incubating, washing, adding enzyme-conjugated anti-IgM, adding substrate, and measuring absorbance.

- detail G protein antigen preparation

The G protein antigen is produced by transfecting HEK293 cells with the pSGnc plasmid, harvesting culture supernatant, and purifying the secreted protein via nickel-affinity chromatography under denaturing conditions.

- describe monoclonal antibody production

Monoclonal antibodies against the SVCVnc G protein are produced by immunizing mice with purified G protein, fusing splenocytes with myeloma cells, screening hybridomas for reactivity, and expanding positive clones for antibody production.

- outline in vitro diagnosis method

The in vitro diagnosis method involves collecting serum from exposed fish, performing ELISA using the recombinant G protein antigen, and interpreting results based on optical density thresholds established using sera from vaccinated and naïve fish.

- describe kit for in vitro diagnosis

The kit for in vitro diagnosis contains pre-coated plates, conjugates, standards, controls, and a calibration curve, enabling laboratories to determine seroconversion status and vaccine efficacy without requiring live virus.

- discuss therapeutic and prophylactic uses

The pSGnc vaccine is primarily intended for prophylactic use in aquaculture facilities, hatcheries, and ornamental fish operations. It may also be used therapeutically in early-stage infections to reduce viral shedding and mortality.

- outline antibody administration methods

Antibodies generated against the SVCVnc G protein may be administered passively to naïve fish via intraperitoneal injection of hyperimmune serum or purified IgM fractions, providing immediate but temporary protection.

- discuss dosage and response

Dosage is determined by fish weight and age. Juvenile fish (≤10 g) receive 10 µg, while adults (>50 g) may require 5 µg. Immune response is measurable within 14 days and peaks at 28 days post-vaccination.

- describe active immunization

Active immunization refers to the induction of a long-lasting immune response through the administration of an antigen that stimulates the host’s own immune system. The pSGnc vaccine achieves active immunization in cyprinid fish.

- outline vaccine preparation

Vaccine preparation involves plasmid propagation in E. coli, alkaline lysis, phenol-chloroform extraction, ethanol precipitation, and resuspension in sterile saline. Endotoxin levels are kept below 1 EU/mg.

- discuss materials and methods

Materials and methods include fish husbandry protocols, virus propagation in EPC cells, challenge conditions, vaccine administration, and analytical techniques such as plaque assay, RT-PCR, and ELISA.

- describe fish stocks

Fish stocks are sourced from OIE-certified, SVCV-free facilities and maintained under controlled environmental conditions to ensure health and minimize stress prior to vaccination.

- outline virus propagation

Virus is propagated in epithelioma papulosum cyprini (EPC) cells at 20°C in MEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. Titers are determined by plaque assay.

- describe susceptibility challenges

Susceptibility challenges are performed using koi exposed to SVCVnc via intraperitoneal injection or immersion, with mortality recorded over 30 days.

- outline cold stress challenges

Cold stress challenges involve transferring fish from 18°C to 10–12°C within 24 hours prior to virus exposure, a protocol that reliably induces susceptibility without causing non-specific mortality.

- describe vaccine construction

Vaccine construction involves reverse transcription of SVCVnc RNA, PCR amplification of the G-gene, directional cloning into pcDNA3.1, sequence verification, and plasmid purification.

- detail G-gene sequencing

The G-gene was sequenced using Sanger sequencing with primers designed against conserved regions of European strains. The sequence was confirmed to be 100% identical to GenBank AY527273.

- outline cloning and vector preparation

Cloning was performed using the TOPO TA cloning system. The vector was linearized, ligated with the insert, transformed into E. coli, and screened by colony PCR and restriction digest.

- describe vaccine development

Vaccine development included optimization of plasmid concentration, injection volume, and post-vaccination temperature to maximize immune response and minimize stress.

- outline vaccination procedure

Vaccination is performed under anesthesia using a 1 mL syringe with a 27-gauge needle. Each fish receives a single 50 µL intramuscular injection.

- describe virus challenge after vaccination

Twenty-eight days after vaccination, fish are subjected to cold stress and challenged with SVCVnc via intraperitoneal injection of 1×10⁶ PFU/fish.

- outline challenge conditions

Challenge conditions include water temperature of 10–12°C, 24-hour acclimation, and a 28-day observation period with daily mortality recording.

- discuss efficacy assessment

Efficacy is assessed by comparing cumulative percent mortality and relative percent survival between vaccinated and control groups using statistical analysis.

- describe statistical analysis

Statistical analysis is performed using Fisher’s exact test for mortality differences and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis with Mantel-Cox log-rank test for curve comparisons.

- outline survival curve analysis

Survival curves are generated using time-to-death data, with censoring applied for fish that survive the observation period. Curves are compared using the log-rank test.

- discuss virus quantification

Virus quantification is performed by plaque assay on EPC cells, with titers expressed as plaque-forming units per gram of tissue.

- describe sampling and screening

Sampling involves euthanizing dead and surviving fish, homogenizing tissues in PBS, and testing supernatants for virus by plaque assay.

- outline results interpretation

Results are interpreted based on RPS values: RPS ≥50% is considered protective, RPS <20% indicates no protection, and RPS >80% indicates strong protection.

- discuss implications of results

The results demonstrate that the pSGnc vaccine is the first SVCV DNA vaccine to provide consistent, high-level protection against a North American isolate, validating the Ia genogroup as the optimal target for vaccine development.

- describe future directions

Future directions include testing cross-protection against European strains, evaluating long-term immunity beyond 12 months, and developing a thermostable formulation for field use.

- outline patent claims

The patent claims encompass the pSGnc plasmid construct, methods of its production, methods of vaccination, diagnostic kits, and compositions for detection and prophylaxis.

- describe prior art

Prior art includes inactivated SVCV vaccines, DNA vaccines based on European SVCV strains, and vaccines for IHNV and VHSV. None of these provide the same level of protection against North American SVCV isolates.

- discuss advantages of invention

Advantages include high efficacy, low cost, thermal stability, ease of production, no risk of reversion, and compatibility with existing aquaculture practices.

- outline various embodiments

Various embodiments include different promoter systems, alternative delivery methods, combination with adjuvants, and use in multiple cyprinid species.

- describe kit components

Kit components include plasmid DNA, syringes, needles, sterile saline, cold pack, instructions, and certificate of analysis.

- outline method of use

The method of use involves injecting a single dose of pSGnc into the epaxial muscle of fish 28 days prior to anticipated exposure to SVCV.

- discuss therapeutic applications

Therapeutic applications include administration to fish in early stages of infection to reduce viral load and mortality.

- describe prophylactic uses

Prophylactic uses include vaccination of broodstock, juveniles, and fish in high-risk environments such as hatcheries, ornamental ponds, and export facilities.

- outline diagnostic applications

Diagnostic applications include serological monitoring of vaccinated populations and detection of antibody responses in wild fish populations.

- discuss vaccine development

Vaccine development includes scale-up of plasmid production, formulation optimization, and regulatory submission for approval in the United States and Canada.

- describe G-gene sequencing

G-gene sequencing confirms the identity of the North American isolate and ensures the vaccine is matched to circulating strains.

- outline cloning and vector preparation

Cloning and vector preparation ensure the integrity of the insert and the absence of mutations that could compromise immunogenicity.

- describe vaccine construction

Vaccine construction adheres to current good manufacturing practices to ensure batch-to-batch consistency and safety.

- outline patent scope

The patent scope includes all nucleic acid sequences encoding the SVCVnc G protein, all vectors containing such sequences, all methods of administration, and all diagnostic kits utilizing the recombinant G protein.

- discuss future developments

Future developments include multivalent vaccines targeting multiple SVCV genogroups, oral delivery systems, and integration with digital health monitoring platforms for aquaculture.
