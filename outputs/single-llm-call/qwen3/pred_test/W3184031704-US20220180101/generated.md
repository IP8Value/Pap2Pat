# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to self-supervised semantic learning

The present invention relates to systems and methods for self-supervised semantic learning in visual data, particularly in the domain of video analysis. The invention enables the extraction of high-level, semantically meaningful representations from unlabeled video sequences without reliance on manual annotations or class labels. By leveraging multiple complementary views of the same underlying content—such as RGB imagery, optical flow, pose heatmaps, and segmentation masks—the invention facilitates the discovery of consistent structural relationships across modalities. These relationships are used to guide the learning process toward representations that reflect true semantic similarity rather than superficial or low-level visual artifacts. The approach operates in a fully unsupervised manner, making it applicable to large-scale video datasets where labeled data is scarce or prohibitively expensive to obtain. The method is designed to be modular and extensible, allowing integration with existing architectures and compatible with any number of input views derived from the original video stream, whether generated by hand-crafted algorithms or learned models. The resulting representations are suitable for downstream tasks such as action classification, video retrieval, temporal alignment, and anomaly detection, offering performance improvements over conventional self-supervised techniques that operate on single modalities or fail to account for cross-view consistency.

## DESCRIPTION OF RELATED ART

- introduce video labeling approaches

Traditional approaches to video understanding have relied heavily on supervised learning, where each video clip is manually annotated with class labels corresponding to the actions or events depicted. These methods require extensive human effort to curate and label datasets, which limits scalability and generalizability. While supervised models achieve strong performance on benchmark datasets, their utility is constrained by the narrow scope of labeled examples and their inability to transfer knowledge to unseen or underrepresented categories. Moreover, the cost of annotation grows exponentially with dataset size and complexity, rendering such approaches impractical for real-world applications involving millions of videos. As a result, researchers have increasingly turned to unsupervised and self-supervised alternatives that exploit inherent structure within the data itself to derive supervisory signals.

- motivate self-supervised methods

Self-supervised learning has emerged as a compelling paradigm for video representation learning, as it eliminates the need for explicit human labeling by defining proxy tasks that encourage the model to infer meaningful patterns from raw input. Common strategies include predicting future frames, reconstructing occluded regions, or ordering shuffled temporal segments. These tasks implicitly require the model to capture spatiotemporal coherence, motion dynamics, and contextual relationships—properties that correlate strongly with semantic meaning. By training on these pretext tasks, models learn latent representations that generalize well to downstream classification and retrieval tasks when fine-tuned with minimal labeled data. This approach has demonstrated significant progress in reducing the dependency on annotated datasets while maintaining competitive performance relative to fully supervised counterparts.

- limitations of current self-supervised methods

Despite these advances, existing self-supervised frameworks suffer from a fundamental limitation: they rely on structural proximity as the primary criterion for defining positive and negative sample pairs. For instance, two video clips from the same original video are treated as similar, while clips from different videos—even if depicting identical actions—are considered dissimilar. This leads to representations that prioritize low-level discriminative features, such as camera motion, background texture, or lighting conditions, over high-level semantic content. Consequently, models trained under such constraints often fail to recognize that two distinct videos may depict the same action, such as a person performing a squat, simply because they were recorded in different environments. This misalignment between the learning objective and the desired semantic goal results in representations that are brittle, context-dependent, and poorly suited for real-world generalization.

- summarize multi-view learning approaches

To address this issue, recent efforts have explored multi-view learning, wherein multiple complementary representations of the same video are simultaneously processed. These views may include RGB frames, optical flow, human pose estimations, or semantic segmentations, each capturing a different aspect of the scene. By aligning representations across these views, models can benefit from the complementary information each provides. For example, optical flow encodes motion trajectories, while pose heatmaps encode body configuration, and segmentation masks isolate the subject from the background. Multi-view methods typically employ mutual information maximization or cross-view contrastive loss to encourage consistency between corresponding representations. These approaches have shown improved performance over single-view baselines, particularly when views are derived from physically meaningful transformations of the input.

- limitations of multi-view learning approaches

However, current multi-view methods remain constrained by their reliance on intra-video positive pairs and inter-video negative pairs, inheriting the same shortcomings as single-view contrastive learning. They do not actively leverage the relationships between instances across different videos to refine the similarity structure in each view. As a result, even when multiple views are available, the model still learns to distinguish videos based on superficial differences rather than semantic equivalence. Furthermore, many approaches treat views as independent sources of information to be fused, rather than as interdependent signals that can mutually correct and reinforce each other’s predictions. This limits their ability to uncover higher-order semantic relationships that are only apparent when inconsistencies between views are systematically resolved. The invention overcomes these limitations by introducing a cooperative mechanism that dynamically aligns similarity structures across views, enabling each view to guide the others toward more semantically coherent representations.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

- introduce multi-view cooperative contrastive self-supervised learning

The present invention introduces a novel framework for multi-view cooperative contrastive self-supervised learning, wherein multiple complementary representations of video content are jointly trained to mutually refine their internal similarity structures. Rather than simply aligning features across views through mutual information or direct correspondence, the invention enforces consistency in the relative distances between video instances across all views. This ensures that if two videos are deemed similar in one modality—such as pose or motion—they are also encouraged to be similar in other modalities, such as appearance or segmentation. This cooperative alignment process leads to the emergence of high-level semantic representations that are invariant to view-specific noise and irrelevant background variations, thereby significantly improving the quality of learned features.

- describe method for receiving video sequences

The method begins by receiving a video sequence composed of a temporal series of frames, each frame containing visual information encoded in one or more modalities. These modalities may include RGB pixel intensities, optical flow vectors, human pose keypoint heatmaps, and semantic segmentation masks. Each modality is treated as a distinct view of the same underlying event, and the video sequence is partitioned into a sequence of non-overlapping temporal blocks, each containing a fixed number of consecutive frames. These blocks serve as the fundamental units for representation learning and are processed independently across all views.

- describe method for deriving embeddings

For each temporal block within each view, a view-specific deep encoder transforms the input into a high-dimensional latent embedding. These encoders are convolutional neural networks tailored to the characteristics of their respective input modality—for example, a 3D convolutional network for RGB frames and a separate 3D network for optical flow. The output of each encoder is a spatially structured embedding that preserves local context, which is then aggregated into a single global context representation for the entire block sequence using a temporal pooling mechanism. The resulting context representation captures the semantic evolution of the action over time and serves as the basis for subsequent similarity computations.

- describe method for determining distances

Distances between context representations are computed across all pairs of video sequences within a training batch using a cosine similarity metric. This yields a similarity matrix for each view, where each entry quantifies the degree of similarity between two video instances according to that view’s representation. The invention then compares these similarity matrices across views to detect inconsistencies—instances where two videos are judged similar in one view but dissimilar in another. These inconsistencies are interpreted as signals that the model’s understanding of semantic similarity is incomplete or biased.

- describe method for detecting inconsistencies

Inconsistencies are detected by computing the difference between the similarity matrices of each pair of views and aggregating these differences into a synchronization loss. This loss penalizes deviations in the relative ordering of similarities across views, thereby encouraging the model to converge toward a unified notion of semantic similarity that is consistent across all modalities. The synchronization process does not require direct correspondence between views but instead relies on the statistical alignment of pairwise relationships, making it robust to noise and partial misalignment in the input data.

- describe method for predicting semantics

To further refine the representations, the method incorporates a predictive task that requires the model to forecast future block embeddings based on prior context. This predictive objective ensures that the learned representations encode temporal dynamics and causal structure, which are essential for capturing action semantics. The combination of predictive modeling and cross-view consistency enforcement creates a dual optimization pressure: the model must not only predict the future accurately but also ensure that its predictions align with the similarity structure inferred from other views.

- describe additional embodiments

Additional embodiments of the invention include the use of noisy or imperfect views derived from off-the-shelf computer vision models, such as human pose estimators or segmentation networks, which are not trained in conjunction with the main framework. Despite their inherent inaccuracies, these views contribute meaningfully to the cooperative learning process by providing alternative perspectives that help cancel out view-specific biases. The invention further supports the integration of higher-order semantic cues, such as audio tracks or textual captions, as additional views, enabling extension to multi-modal learning scenarios. The framework is also compatible with any contrastive learning backbone, including those based on Noise Contrastive Estimation or predictive coding, and can be applied to both video and image datasets with minimal architectural modification.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce self-supervised visual representation learning

Self-supervised visual representation learning seeks to derive meaningful encodings from unlabeled visual data by defining pretext tasks that implicitly encode semantic structure. Unlike supervised learning, which relies on explicit class labels, self-supervised methods construct supervisory signals from the intrinsic properties of the data itself—such as temporal continuity, spatial coherence, or multi-modal alignment. In video analysis, this often involves predicting future frames, reconstructing occluded regions, or ordering shuffled sequences. These tasks compel the model to learn representations that capture dynamic patterns and causal relationships, which are foundational to understanding actions and events. The success of such methods hinges on their ability to generalize beyond low-level visual cues and extract invariant semantic features that remain consistent across varying contexts.

- describe data-driven sampling for leveraging implicit relationships

The invention employs a data-driven sampling strategy that identifies and exploits implicit relationships between video instances without requiring explicit labeling. Rather than relying on fixed rules such as temporal adjacency or spatial proximity to define positive pairs, the method dynamically infers similarity through the cooperative alignment of multiple views. This allows the model to discover non-obvious relationships—for example, that two videos depicting different individuals performing the same action are semantically equivalent—even when they differ in background, lighting, or camera angle. The sampling process is guided by the evolving similarity structures across views, ensuring that the model is continually exposed to increasingly refined and semantically meaningful examples.

- introduce Cooperative Contrastive Learning (CoCon) technique

Cooperative Contrastive Learning (CoCon) is the core technique of the invention, designed to resolve inconsistencies in similarity judgments across multiple views of the same video content. Unlike traditional contrastive learning, which treats each view independently, CoCon introduces a synchronization mechanism that enforces agreement in the relative distances between all pairs of video instances across all views. This is achieved by minimizing the discrepancy between similarity matrices computed in each view, thereby encouraging the model to converge toward a shared semantic space. The technique operates without requiring any ground-truth labels and is fully differentiable, enabling end-to-end training with standard optimization algorithms.

- motivate overcoming shortcomings of multi-view learning

While prior multi-view methods improve representation quality by combining information from multiple modalities, they remain limited by their inability to correct for view-specific biases. CoCon overcomes this by treating each view not as an independent source of truth but as a collaborator in refining a unified semantic understanding. When one view incorrectly judges two videos as dissimilar due to noise or partial occlusion, the other views can provide corrective signals that guide the model toward a more accurate consensus. This cooperative dynamic creates a positive feedback loop in which each view progressively improves as the others become more reliable, leading to representations that are more robust, generalizable, and semantically coherent than those produced by any individual view alone.

- describe inter-view information utilization

Inter-view information is utilized through a consistency loss that quantifies the disagreement between pairwise similarity matrices computed in each view. This loss is computed as the element-wise difference between similarity matrices, normalized and aggregated across all view pairs. By minimizing this loss, the model is incentivized to adjust its internal representations so that the relative similarity between any two videos is preserved across all modalities. This process does not require direct alignment of feature dimensions or pixel-level correspondence, making it applicable even when views are derived from fundamentally different data types—such as RGB imagery and pose heatmaps.

- illustrate CoCon example with two images and multiple views

Consider two video clips, each depicting a person performing a squat. In the RGB view, the backgrounds differ significantly—one clip shows a gym, the other a park. In the pose heatmap view, the body configurations are nearly identical. A conventional model might treat these clips as dissimilar due to the RGB mismatch. However, CoCon detects this inconsistency: the pose view suggests similarity, while the RGB view suggests dissimilarity. The synchronization loss then compels the RGB encoder to adjust its representation so that the two clips become more similar in the RGB space as well, effectively learning to ignore irrelevant background variations and focus on the shared action semantics.

- describe view-specific deep encoders

Each view is processed by a dedicated deep encoder architecture optimized for its input modality. For RGB frames, a 3D convolutional ResNet is employed to capture spatiotemporal dynamics. For optical flow, a similar architecture is adapted to handle dense motion vectors. Pose heatmaps and segmentation masks are encoded using lightweight 3D convolutions that preserve spatial resolution while reducing computational overhead. All encoders are initialized independently and trained first using a standard predictive coding objective. Subsequently, they are jointly fine-tuned using the cooperative loss, allowing each encoder to adapt its internal representations in response to feedback from the others.

- explain leveraging inconsistencies between views

Inconsistencies between views are not treated as noise to be filtered out but as valuable signals that reveal gaps in the model’s understanding. By explicitly measuring and minimizing these inconsistencies, the model is forced to resolve ambiguities and refine its semantic representations. For example, if one view incorrectly identifies two actions as similar due to shared background elements, but another view correctly distinguishes them based on motion patterns, the cooperative loss will penalize the former and encourage the model to prioritize the more reliable signal. This mechanism enables the emergence of higher-order semantics that are consistent across modalities and robust to domain shifts.

- describe using cosine distance to determine embedding distances

Cosine distance is employed as the primary metric for computing similarity between context embeddings due to its invariance to magnitude and its suitability for high-dimensional spaces. Each context representation is normalized to lie on the unit hypersphere, ensuring that cosine similarity corresponds directly to angular distance. This normalization prevents the model from exploiting magnitude differences—such as brightness or contrast—as proxies for semantic similarity. The resulting similarity matrices are then used to compute both the predictive loss and the cooperative consistency loss, forming the dual objective function that drives learning.

- introduce contrastive loss learning for self-supervised training

Contrastive learning is employed as the foundational training mechanism, wherein each context embedding is trained to be similar to its positive counterparts (future blocks from the same video) and dissimilar to negative counterparts (blocks from other videos). This is implemented using Noise Contrastive Estimation, which frames the problem as a binary classification task between true and corrupted samples. The contrastive loss ensures that the model learns discriminative representations that capture temporal structure and action dynamics, while the cooperative loss ensures that these representations are semantically aligned across views.

- describe combining multiple views for better representation

By combining multiple views, the invention enables the model to learn representations that are more comprehensive and resilient than those derived from any single modality. Each view provides a unique perspective on the action—RGB captures appearance, optical flow captures motion, pose captures configuration, and segmentation isolates the subject. When these views are cooperatively aligned, the model learns to extract features that are invariant to view-specific artifacts and focused on the underlying action semantics. This leads to representations that generalize better to unseen data and perform more robustly in downstream tasks.

- analyze each view to provide specific patterns

Each view contributes distinct patterns that are critical for understanding action semantics. RGB provides texture and color cues, optical flow encodes direction and speed of motion, pose heatmaps reveal body joint configurations, and segmentation masks delineate the subject from the environment. The invention analyzes these patterns independently and then synthesizes them into a unified representation by enforcing consistency in their pairwise relationships. This allows the model to learn not just what an action looks like, but what it fundamentally is—regardless of how it is visually rendered.

- leverage inter-view information to infer relationships

Inter-view information is leveraged to infer implicit relationships between video instances that would otherwise remain hidden. For example, if two videos exhibit similar pose patterns but differ in RGB appearance, the model infers that the underlying action is the same, and adjusts the RGB representation accordingly. This inference is not based on explicit rules but emerges from the optimization process, allowing the model to discover novel and non-intuitive relationships between actions that are semantically related but visually dissimilar.

- describe using multiple embeddings to infer relationships

Multiple embeddings are used to infer relationships by comparing the similarity structure across all views simultaneously. Rather than relying on a single embedding space, the invention maintains separate but interdependent embedding spaces for each view. The relationships between instances are inferred not by comparing features directly, but by comparing the patterns of similarity across these spaces. This enables the model to detect when a relationship is consistent across views—indicating semantic validity—or when it is idiosyncratic to one view—indicating noise or bias.

- motivate using available views of input data

The invention is motivated by the observation that many video datasets contain multiple naturally available views—such as RGB, flow, and segmentation—that are often discarded or treated as secondary. Rather than requiring additional data collection, the invention exploits these existing modalities to enhance learning without additional cost. This makes the approach highly practical and scalable, as it can be applied to any video dataset with minimal preprocessing, even those originally designed for single-view analysis.

- describe using high-level inferred semantics as additional views

High-level inferred semantics, such as human pose or object segmentation, are used as additional views even when generated by external, non-trainable models. These views are inherently noisy and imperfect, yet they provide valuable structural information that complements the raw visual data. The cooperative learning mechanism is robust to this noise, as inconsistencies introduced by imperfect detectors are naturally corrected by the alignment process across other views. This enables the use of off-the-shelf components without requiring retraining or fine-tuning, significantly lowering the barrier to adoption.

- illustrate example process for cooperative contrastive learning

The process begins with the input of a video sequence partitioned into temporal blocks. Each block is encoded into a latent representation using a view-specific encoder. Context representations are computed by aggregating these block embeddings over time. Pairwise cosine similarities are computed between all context representations within a batch, producing a similarity matrix per view. The inconsistency between these matrices is measured and minimized via a synchronization loss. Simultaneously, a predictive loss is applied to ensure temporal coherence. The combined loss is backpropagated through all encoders, updating their parameters to improve both prediction accuracy and cross-view consistency.

- compute features using view-specific encoders

Features are computed using view-specific encoders that are convolutional in nature and designed to handle the unique characteristics of each input modality. For RGB, a 3D ResNet extracts spatiotemporal features from pixel intensities. For optical flow, a similar architecture processes motion vectors. For pose heatmaps and segmentation masks, lighter 3D convolutions preserve spatial resolution while reducing computational load. All encoders are initialized with pre-trained weights where available and trained end-to-end during the cooperative phase.

- determine distances in resultant embeddings

Distances in the resultant embeddings are determined using cosine similarity, which measures the angular separation between normalized context representations. This metric is chosen for its invariance to scale and its suitability for high-dimensional spaces. The resulting similarity scores form the basis for both the predictive and cooperative objectives, ensuring that the model learns representations that are both temporally coherent and semantically aligned.

- determine inconsistencies in distances

Inconsistencies in distances are determined by computing the element-wise difference between similarity matrices from different views. These differences are aggregated into a single synchronization loss that penalizes disagreement in the relative ordering of similarities across views. The loss is minimized during training, forcing the model to adjust its representations so that the similarity structure becomes consistent across all modalities.

- encourage distances in all embeddings to become similar

The cooperative loss encourages the distances between corresponding video instances to become similar across all embeddings by penalizing deviations in their similarity rankings. This does not require the embeddings to be identical in dimension or structure, only that the relative relationships between instances are preserved. This allows the model to learn a shared semantic space without enforcing feature-level alignment, making it robust to modality-specific distortions and noise.

- apply cooperative contrastive learning to video sequences

Cooperative contrastive learning is applied to video sequences by treating each temporal block as a unit of analysis and enforcing consistency across all views. The method is agnostic to the length or resolution of the video and can be scaled to long-form content by adjusting the block size and aggregation strategy. The resulting representations are suitable for a wide range of downstream tasks, including action classification, video retrieval, and temporal alignment.

- construct binary classification task for self-supervised training

A binary classification task is constructed by framing the prediction of future block embeddings as a discrimination problem between true and corrupted samples. For each context representation, the model is trained to distinguish the correct future embedding from a set of negative samples drawn from other videos. This task is implemented using Noise Contrastive Estimation, which provides a stable and scalable objective for training without requiring explicit labels.

- partition video sequence into disjoint blocks

The video sequence is partitioned into disjoint temporal blocks, each containing a fixed number of consecutive frames. This partitioning ensures that the model learns representations that capture local dynamics while maintaining computational tractability. The block size is chosen to balance temporal resolution and sequence length, typically ranging from 3 to 10 frames per block depending on the dataset and task.

- transform each block into latent representation

Each block is transformed into a latent representation using a deep convolutional encoder that maps the input modality into a compact, high-dimensional embedding space. These embeddings preserve spatial and temporal structure, enabling the model to reason about both local motion and global action patterns.

- generate context representation

Context representations are generated by aggregating the latent embeddings of all blocks in a sequence using a temporal pooling mechanism. This aggregation captures the evolution of the action over time and provides a single vector that summarizes the entire sequence for similarity computation.

- predict future blocks to learn effective representations

The model is trained to predict the latent representations of future blocks based on the context of prior blocks. This predictive task forces the model to encode causal and dynamic relationships, ensuring that the learned representations are not merely descriptive but also predictive of future states.

- use dense predictive coding framework for self-supervised learning

The invention employs a dense predictive coding framework, wherein each block’s representation is predicted based on the context of all preceding blocks. This dense structure enables the model to capture long-range dependencies and temporal hierarchies, leading to more comprehensive and semantically rich representations.

- define prediction task to capture contextual semantics

The prediction task is defined to capture contextual semantics by requiring the model to forecast not just the next block, but multiple future blocks in sequence. This multi-step prediction ensures that the model learns representations that encode not only immediate transitions but also long-term action dynamics.

- utilize different datasets for training and evaluation

The method is evaluated on standard video action datasets, including UCF101, HMDB51, and Kinetics-400, to demonstrate its generalizability across domains of varying scale and complexity. Training is performed in a fully unsupervised manner, with evaluation conducted via fine-tuning on labeled subsets to measure the quality of the learned representations.

- describe architectural representation for cooperative contrastive learning

The architectural representation consists of multiple parallel encoder streams, each dedicated to a specific view, followed by a shared cooperative loss module that computes consistency between similarity matrices. The encoders are connected only through the loss function, allowing for modularity and independent development of each view’s representation.

- break up video sequence into blocks

The video sequence is broken up into non-overlapping blocks of fixed duration, each containing a uniform number of frames. This discretization enables efficient processing and ensures that the model learns representations at a consistent temporal granularity.

- encode each block into latent representation

Each block is encoded into a latent representation using a view-specific convolutional encoder that maps the input into a high-dimensional embedding space. These encoders are trained jointly during the cooperative phase to ensure alignment across modalities.

- aggregate latent representations into context representation

Latent representations are aggregated into a single context representation using a temporal pooling layer that captures the evolution of the action over the entire sequence. This context representation serves as the basis for both prediction and similarity computation.

- compute Noise Contrastive Estimation (NCE) loss over feature embeddings

The Noise Contrastive Estimation loss is computed over the feature embeddings to distinguish true future representations from corrupted ones. This loss provides a stable and scalable objective for training the predictive component of the framework.

- synchronize distances across all views using consistency loss

Distances across all views are synchronized using a consistency loss that measures the disagreement between similarity matrices. This loss is minimized during training to ensure that the relative similarity between video instances is preserved across all modalities.

- introduce cooperative loss function

The cooperative loss function combines the predictive NCE loss with the consistency loss, forming a dual objective that simultaneously enforces temporal coherence and cross-view semantic alignment. The weighting between the two components is empirically determined to balance prediction accuracy with representation consistency.

- define similarity loss

The similarity loss is defined as a hinge-based objective that encourages similar instances to be close and dissimilar ones to be far apart within each view. This loss is applied independently to each view and is combined with the cooperative loss to ensure that the model learns discriminative yet consistent representations.

- introduce contrastive predictive coding

Contrastive predictive coding is introduced as the underlying framework for generating predictive representations. It enables the model to learn structured embeddings that encode both local and global temporal dynamics, forming the foundation for the cooperative learning mechanism.

- describe self-supervised learning framework

The self-supervised learning framework is built upon the principles of contrastive learning and predictive coding, extended to a multi-view cooperative setting. It requires no labeled data and operates entirely on the intrinsic structure of the video input, making it scalable, generalizable, and applicable to any video dataset.
