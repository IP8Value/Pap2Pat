# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- Peptidomimetic foldamers represent a class of synthetic molecular architectures designed to emulate the structural and functional properties of natural peptides while overcoming their inherent pharmacological limitations. These foldamers are composed of non-natural backbone units that adopt well-defined secondary conformations, such as helices, turns, or sheets, through intramolecular interactions that mimic those found in polypeptides. Unlike traditional peptides, which are susceptible to rapid enzymatic degradation and poor bioavailability, peptidomimetic foldamers are engineered to maintain high target affinity and selectivity while exhibiting enhanced metabolic stability, membrane permeability, and prolonged in vivo half-life. Among the most promising subclasses of foldamers are oligoureas, which feature a urea-based backbone that preserves the spatial orientation of side chains and the helical propensity of α-helical peptides, enabling seamless integration into biological recognition interfaces. The development of hybrid molecules that combine segments of natural amino acid sequences with oligourea units has emerged as a powerful strategy to engineer bioactive compounds with tailored pharmacokinetic profiles, particularly for targets that require precise molecular recognition, such as G-protein coupled receptors and protein-protein interaction interfaces.

## BACKGROUND

- Protein-protein interactions (PPIs) constitute a fundamental class of molecular events that regulate nearly all cellular processes, including signal transduction, immune response, apoptosis, and metabolic homeostasis. Consequently, the modulation of PPIs has become a major focus in drug discovery, particularly for diseases where aberrant interactions drive pathogenesis, such as cancer, autoimmune disorders, and metabolic syndromes. Despite their therapeutic potential, targeting PPIs with conventional small molecules has proven challenging due to the large, flat, and often hydrophobic interfaces involved, which lack well-defined pockets suitable for high-affinity binding. Peptides, by virtue of their ability to present extended surfaces and diverse side-chain functionalities, have emerged as promising candidates for disrupting or stabilizing such interactions. However, native peptides suffer from critical drawbacks that severely limit their utility as therapeutics, including rapid proteolytic degradation in biological fluids, poor oral bioavailability, low membrane permeability, and short plasma half-lives. These limitations have prompted extensive efforts to develop peptidomimetic alternatives that retain the molecular recognition capabilities of peptides while enhancing their drug-like properties. Foldamers—synthetic oligomers capable of folding into stable, predictable conformations—have risen as a leading solution to this challenge. Among foldamers, oligoureas have demonstrated exceptional promise due to their structural similarity to peptide backbones, resistance to proteases, and ability to propagate α-helical geometry without disrupting native protein folding patterns. Oligoureas are composed of repeating urea units linked by methylene spacers, forming a backbone that closely mimics the hydrogen-bonding network and helical pitch of α-helical peptides. Importantly, oligoureas can be synthesized using standard solid-phase techniques, enabling rapid diversification and precise control over sequence and stereochemistry. Despite these advantages, the full potential of oligoureas has remained underexploited in the context of complex peptide therapeutics due to uncertainties regarding their compatibility with native side-chain architectures and their functional performance in vivo. Prior efforts to replace peptide segments with oligourea motifs have yielded mixed results, often resulting in significant loss of biological activity due to misalignment of side-chain vectors or disruption of critical intermolecular contacts. There remains a critical unmet need for rationally designed, sequence-specific peptidomimetic compounds that can replace defined segments of bioactive peptides with oligourea units while preserving or even enhancing their biological function, stability, and duration of action.

## SUMMARY

- The present invention discloses a novel class of peptide-amino urea hybrid foldamers that integrate defined segments of oligourea units into the backbone of bioactive peptides to enhance their pharmacological properties without compromising biological activity. These hybrid compounds consist of a parental peptide sequence in which one or more consecutive α-amino acid residues are substituted with amino urea residues, each comprising a urea linkage flanked by methylene groups and a side chain attached at a position that optimally mimics the spatial projection of the native amino acid side chain. The amino urea residue, designated as Xu, is structurally configured such that its side chain is attached to the second methylene carbon of the urea backbone, enabling precise alignment with the side-chain orientation of the replaced α-amino acid. In certain embodiments, the Xu residue may be substituted with Xuα, wherein the side chain is attached to the adjacent methylene carbon, allowing for fine-tuning of side-chain geometry to accommodate residues with distinct steric or electronic requirements. The hybrid foldamers are described by Formula I, wherein the backbone comprises alternating peptide segments and amino urea segments, and R groups represent side chains derived from natural or non-natural amino acids, including but not limited to alanine, glutamic acid, tyrosine, phenylalanine, isoleucine, aspartic acid, and their analogs. In a preferred embodiment, the hybrid foldamers are represented by Formula II, wherein the oligourea segment consists of three consecutive amino urea residues (Xu3) replacing four consecutive α-amino acid residues, thereby preserving the helical turn geometry and inter-residue spacing of the native peptide. The hybrid compounds exhibit enhanced resistance to proteolytic degradation, particularly by neutral endopeptidase 24.11 and pancreatin, while maintaining high affinity for their target receptors, as exemplified by their potent agonism of the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor (GLP-1R). These compounds are capable of inducing cAMP production with EC50 values in the low nanomolar range, comparable to or exceeding that of native GLP-1. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising one or more of these hybrid foldamers in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, wherein the effective amount of the compound is sufficient to elicit a therapeutic response in a subject suffering from a metabolic disorder, including but not limited to type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, or cognitive dysfunction. The hybrid foldamers may be further modified through covalent attachment of fatty acid chains, polyethylene glycol linkers, or other protraction moieties to extend plasma half-life and enable once-weekly or once-daily dosing regimens. The compounds may be formulated as acid or base salts, solvates, zwitterions, enantiomers, diastereoisomers, or prodrugs to optimize solubility, stability, and bioavailability. The invention also provides methods for treating metabolic disorders by administering an effective amount of the hybrid foldamer to a subject in need thereof, as well as methods for manufacturing and purifying these compounds using solid-phase synthesis techniques.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- The detailed description herein provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the structure, function, and utility of the peptide-amino urea hybrid foldamers disclosed herein, with particular reference to their application in the modulation of glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor signaling. For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “peptide” refers to a chain of two or more amino acids linked by amide bonds, whereas “amino acid” denotes a molecule containing both an amino group and a carboxylic acid group, with the side chain determining its chemical identity. The term “amino urea residue” or “Xu residue” refers to a non-natural building block comprising a central urea group flanked by two methylene units, with a side chain attached to the second methylene carbon, thereby enabling the mimicry of α-amino acid side-chain projection in a helical context. The urea group, defined as a functional group with the structure –NH–C(=O)–NH–, confers conformational rigidity and hydrogen-bonding capacity essential for helix stabilization. The term “oligourea” refers to a polymer composed of multiple consecutive amino urea residues, capable of adopting a helical conformation with a pitch and diameter closely matching that of an α-helix. The hybrid foldamers of the invention are defined as compounds in which one or more contiguous segments of the parental peptide backbone are replaced by oligourea segments, wherein the number of amino urea residues is selected to preserve the spatial relationship between side chains and backbone torsional angles of the native sequence. In a preferred embodiment, four consecutive α-amino acid residues are replaced by three consecutive amino urea residues (Xu3), a substitution that maintains the helical turn geometry and minimizes distortion of the peptide’s binding interface. The side chains of the Xu residues may be identical to those of the replaced amino acids or may be substituted with analogs to optimize binding affinity, solubility, or metabolic stability. The hybrid compounds may contain one or more Xu residues, with preferred embodiments containing between one and six consecutive amino urea residues. The hybrid foldamers exhibit a stable helical conformation in solution, as confirmed by circular dichroism spectroscopy and molecular modeling, and retain the ability to bind and activate the GLP-1 receptor with high potency, as demonstrated by cAMP accumulation assays in transfected cells. The compounds demonstrate marked resistance to degradation by neutral endopeptidase 24.11 and pancreatin, with half-lives in plasma exceeding those of native GLP-1 by more than threefold in certain embodiments. The invention further encompasses the use of these compounds in pharmaceutical compositions for oral, parenteral, subcutaneous, or intranasal administration, formulated as tablets, capsules, syrups, suspensions, lyophilisates, or controlled-release matrices. The compounds may be administered as single entities or in combination with other therapeutic agents, including antidiabetic drugs, anti-obesity agents, or insulin sensitizers, to achieve synergistic therapeutic effects. The hybrid foldamers may be synthesized using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, employing Fmoc-protected amino acids and activated amino urea monomers, followed by cleavage from resin, deprotection, and purification by preparative HPLC. The compounds may be isolated as racemates, enantiomers, or diastereoisomers, and may be converted into pharmaceutically acceptable salts using acids such as hydrochloric, sulfuric, or citric acid, or bases such as sodium hydroxide, potassium carbonate, or triethylamine. The invention further provides methods for determining the efficacy of these compounds in vivo, including measurement of blood glucose levels, intraperitoneal glucose tolerance tests, and pharmacokinetic profiling in rodent models. The compounds are particularly useful for the treatment of metabolic disorders characterized by impaired insulin secretion or action, including type 2 diabetes mellitus, prediabetes, obesity, and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. The hybrid foldamers may also be modified with fatty acid chains, such as C18 alkyl groups, to promote binding to serum albumin and extend plasma half-life, thereby enabling infrequent dosing regimens. The invention further includes methods for manufacturing these compounds at scale, including optimization of coupling efficiency, resin loading, and purification protocols to ensure high purity and yield. The compounds may be formulated with excipients such as stabilizers, surfactants, buffers, and preservatives to enhance shelf-life and compatibility with delivery systems. The invention further encompasses the use of these compounds in diagnostic applications, including their use as radiolabeled or fluorescent probes to visualize GLP-1 receptor expression in tissues. The hybrid foldamers represent a transformative approach to peptide therapeutics, enabling the design of molecules with superior pharmacokinetic profiles while retaining the high specificity and potency characteristic of native peptides.

### Examples

- The invention is further illustrated by the synthesis and biological evaluation of a series of GLP-1-derived peptide-amino urea hybrid foldamers, each designed to replace specific tetrapeptide segments within the α-helical domain of GLP-1 with oligourea triads. The parental sequence, GLP-1-G2-NH2 (1), was selected as the starting point due to its resistance to dipeptidyl peptidase-4 cleavage conferred by the glycine substitution at position 2. A systematic replacement of four consecutive amino acid residues with three consecutive amino urea residues (Xu3) was performed at multiple positions along the helical segment spanning residues 9 to 31, with side chains of the Xu residues selected to mirror the native amino acid side chains or optimized for improved binding. The compound GG2[YuEuAu]14–17 (9), in which residues 14–17 were replaced by a triad of tyrosine, glutamic acid, and alanine amino urea residues, exhibited an EC50 of 0.18 nM in a cAMP production assay, surpassing the potency of the parental peptide (0.24 nM). This compound also demonstrated a plasma half-life of 171 minutes, over tenfold longer than that of GLP-1-G2 (16 minutes), and retained significant activity in vivo, as evidenced by sustained reductions in blood glucose levels for up to four hours following intravenous administration. The hybrid GG2[AuAuAu]28–31 (22), which replaced residues 28–31 with three alanine amino urea units, showed a 19% reduction in potency relative to GLP-1-G2 but exhibited a plasma half-life exceeding 60 minutes and maintained robust glucose-lowering activity for up to six hours in intraperitoneal glucose tolerance tests. The hybrid GG2[YuEuAuAuAuAu]14–21 (23), containing two consecutive triads replacing eight amino acid residues, retained 76% of the parental peptide’s potency and demonstrated a plasma half-life of 39 minutes, with activity persisting for over four hours in vivo. These results demonstrate that the substitution of tetrapeptide segments with oligourea triads can significantly enhance metabolic stability without compromising receptor activation. The hybrid 24, a semaglutide analogue in which the C-terminal four residues were replaced by an oligourea triad, retained full agonist activity in vitro and exhibited a pancreatin degradation half-life of 3.2 hours, compared to 0.63 hours for semaglutide, suggesting enhanced resistance to gastrointestinal proteolysis and potential for oral delivery. The compounds were synthesized using microwave-assisted solid-phase synthesis with Fmoc-protected amino acids and azide-functionalized amino urea monomers, followed by reduction of azides to amines, cleavage from resin, and purification by preparative HPLC. All compounds were characterized by mass spectrometry and analytical HPLC, with purity exceeding 95%. The biological activity of each compound was confirmed in duplicate assays using βTC6 cells expressing the human GLP-1 receptor, with cAMP production measured via HTRF technology. The results demonstrate that the peptide-amino urea hybrid foldamer approach enables the rational design of peptide therapeutics with dramatically improved pharmacokinetic properties, extended duration of action, and potential for novel routes of administration.
