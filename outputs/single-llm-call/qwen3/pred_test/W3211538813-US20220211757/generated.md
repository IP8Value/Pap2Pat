## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cellular immunotherapy, specifically to engineered gamma delta T cells derived from pluripotent or hematopoietic stem cells for the treatment of cancer and infectious diseases. The invention further pertains to methods for the in vitro generation, expansion, and functional characterization of allogeneic gamma delta T cells with enhanced antitumor activity, reduced immunogenicity, and intrinsic safety mechanisms. The disclosed compositions and methods are particularly useful for the development of off-the-shelf cellular therapies that do not require patient-specific customization, thereby overcoming logistical, temporal, and economic limitations inherent in autologous cell therapies. The invention also encompasses the genetic modification of these cells to express chimeric antigen receptors, suicide genes, or immune-modulatory transgenes, enabling tailored therapeutic applications across a broad spectrum of malignancies and immune-responsive conditions.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Gamma delta T cells are a subset of T lymphocytes that bridge innate and adaptive immunity through their ability to recognize stress-induced ligands, phosphoantigens, and non-peptide antigens presented by non-classical MHC molecules. Unlike conventional alpha beta T cells, gamma delta T cells do not require antigen processing and presentation via classical HLA class I or II molecules, allowing them to detect and eliminate transformed or infected cells in an HLA-independent manner. These cells are naturally enriched in epithelial tissues and exhibit potent cytotoxic activity against a wide array of tumor cell lines, including those resistant to conventional T cell therapies. Despite their therapeutic promise, the clinical translation of gamma delta T cells has been hindered by their low frequency in peripheral blood, limited ex vivo expansion capacity, and heterogeneity in functional phenotype. Conventional methods for generating gamma delta T cells rely on the isolation and activation of endogenous populations from donor blood using phosphoantigens such as zoledronate or synthetic ligands, followed by cytokine-driven expansion. These approaches yield inconsistent cell numbers, often contaminated with bystander alpha beta T cells or regulatory subsets, and are not scalable for widespread clinical use. Furthermore, the resulting cell products are typically autologous or allogeneic but retain surface expression of HLA class I and II molecules, rendering them susceptible to host immune rejection and limiting their utility as universally applicable therapies. There remains a critical need for a robust, reproducible, and scalable method to generate large quantities of homogeneous, functionally potent, and immunologically inert gamma delta T cells suitable for off-the-shelf administration to any patient regardless of HLA type.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a novel platform for the generation of engineered gamma delta T cells from genetically modified pluripotent or hematopoietic stem cells, enabling the production of off-the-shelf cellular therapeutics with enhanced antitumor efficacy, reduced immunogenicity, and built-in safety controls. The invention discloses a method for producing functional gamma delta T cells by transducing human hematopoietic stem cells with nucleic acid sequences encoding a gamma delta T cell receptor, followed by in vitro differentiation under defined culture conditions that mimic thymic development. The resulting gamma delta T cells uniformly express the transgenic receptor, exhibit a mature cytotoxic phenotype, and demonstrate potent tumor-killing activity against multiple cancer types. The invention further encompasses the genetic ablation of HLA class I and II molecules through targeted gene editing, resulting in universal gamma delta T cells that evade host T cell and NK cell-mediated rejection without requiring immunosuppressive preconditioning. Compositions of matter include populations of engineered gamma delta T cells expressing chimeric antigen receptors, cytokines, or suicide genes, all derived from a single clonal stem cell source. The invention enables the scalable production of therapeutic-grade gamma delta T cell products from a single donor batch, yielding up to 10^12 to 10^13 cells suitable for thousands of clinical doses. These cells can be cryopreserved with retained viability and function, stored in sterile, isotonic solutions containing cryoprotectants, and administered intravenously or intratumorally to patients with solid or hematologic malignancies. The engineered gamma delta T cells exhibit a unique gene expression profile characterized by high expression of NK-activating receptors, tissue-homing chemokine receptors, and effector molecules, while simultaneously downregulating HLA and ligands for NK inhibitory receptors. This combination of features confers superior tumor targeting, enhanced persistence, and resistance to allorejection, distinguishing the invention from all prior approaches to gamma delta T cell therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Gamma delta T cells represent a unique lymphocyte population capable of recognizing antigens independent of classical MHC presentation, making them ideal candidates for broad-spectrum cancer immunotherapy. Unlike conventional alpha beta T cells, which are restricted by HLA polymorphism and prone to inducing graft-versus-host disease in allogeneic settings, gamma delta T cells exhibit intrinsic tolerance to mismatched HLA environments and do not mount alloreactive responses. However, their natural scarcity in peripheral blood and inability to be expanded reliably in vitro have historically limited their therapeutic application. The present invention overcomes these limitations by leveraging the multipotentiality of human hematopoietic stem cells, which can be genetically modified to express a defined gamma delta T cell receptor and then directed through an artificial thymic organoid system to differentiate into a homogeneous population of functional gamma delta T cells. This method enables the production of large-scale, clonally pure gamma delta T cell products that are free from contaminating alpha beta T cells and exhibit consistent phenotypic and functional characteristics across multiple donors. The transduced stem cells are cultured in a serum-free, feeder-independent medium supplemented with specific cytokines and Notch ligands to support T cell lineage commitment and maturation. The resulting gamma delta T cells express high levels of CD161, NKG2D, DNAM-1, and inflammatory homing receptors such as CCR5 and CXCR3, while displaying minimal expression of HLA class I and II molecules, a feature that confers resistance to host immune surveillance. The invention further encompasses the use of CRISPR-Cas9 or other gene-editing technologies to disrupt the B2M and CIITA genes, thereby completely ablating surface HLA expression and generating universal gamma delta T cells that are compatible with any recipient without risk of rejection. These cells can be further engineered to express chimeric antigen receptors targeting tumor-associated antigens such as BCMA, CD19, or GD2, enabling dual recognition mechanisms that combine innate tumor sensing with adaptive antigen specificity. The engineered gamma delta T cells retain their ability to be activated by phosphoantigens such as zoledronate, allowing for in vivo modulation of their activity. A suicide gene, such as the sr39TK variant of herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase, is co-expressed with the gamma delta TCR to enable selective ablation of the therapeutic cells in the event of adverse events, using the prodrug ganciclovir. The cells are produced in a closed, GMP-compliant system using defined media components including B-27 supplement, GS21, and recombinant human cytokines such as IL-7, IL-15, and FLT3-L, ensuring clinical-grade consistency. The final cell product is cryopreserved in a solution containing dextrose, electrolytes, albumin, dextran, and dimethyl sulfoxide, maintaining viability and function upon thawing. The invention enables the generation of a single batch of engineered gamma delta T cells from one donor that can yield up to 10^13 cells, sufficient for tens of thousands of therapeutic doses, thereby transforming gamma delta T cell therapy into a commercially viable off-the-shelf modality. The cells demonstrate superior antitumor efficacy in vivo compared to conventional NK cells or CAR-T cells, with no evidence of graft-versus-host disease or cytokine release syndrome in preclinical models. The invention further includes methods for isolating and enriching the engineered cells using magnetic or fluorescence-based sorting techniques, and for co-culturing them with feeder cells or artificial stromal layers to enhance expansion and maturation. The resulting population of engineered gamma delta T cells is characterized by a stable, non-alloreactive phenotype, high cytotoxic potential, and long-term persistence in vivo, making it uniquely suited for the treatment of solid tumors, hematologic malignancies, and persistent viral infections.

## EXAMPLES

Human hematopoietic stem cells were isolated from cord blood or G-CSF-mobilized peripheral blood and transduced with a lentiviral vector encoding a human Vγ9Vδ2 T cell receptor linked to a suicide gene, sr39TK. Transduced cells were cultured in an artificial thymic organoid system composed of MS5-DLL4 stromal cells in a serum-free medium supplemented with IL-7, FLT3-L, and ascorbic acid phosphate for eight weeks. Following differentiation, cells were expanded in the presence of zoledronate and IL-15 for an additional two weeks, yielding a population of over 98% gamma delta T cells with uniform TCR expression and negligible contamination by alpha beta T cells. These cells exhibited high expression of NK-activating receptors, produced large quantities of IFN-γ and perforin upon stimulation, and killed a broad panel of tumor cell lines including melanoma, leukemia, and multiple myeloma in vitro. When cryopreserved and thawed, the cells retained full cytotoxic function, unlike conventional NK cells. The same cells were further engineered with a BCMA-targeting CAR, resulting in a triple-mechanism tumor-targeting cell product capable of killing via gamma delta TCR, CAR, and NK receptor pathways. Gene editing using CRISPR-Cas9 to disrupt B2M and CIITA resulted in complete loss of HLA-I and HLA-II expression, and these universal cells demonstrated no response in mixed lymphocyte reactions against allogeneic PBMCs. In vivo xenograft models showed that the engineered gamma delta T cells eradicated tumors more effectively than CAR-T or NK cells, with no signs of graft-versus-host disease. Administration of ganciclovir led to complete depletion of the engineered cells in all tissues, confirming the functionality of the suicide switch. The entire manufacturing process, from stem cell transduction to final cell product, was completed in 10–12 weeks and produced over 10^12 cells per donor, sufficient for tens of thousands of clinical doses.

## ILLUSTRATIVE VECTOR SEQUENCES

The lentiviral vector used to deliver the gamma delta T cell receptor comprises a tricistronic cassette encoding the human Vγ9 and Vδ2 TCR chains separated by P2A and F2A ribosomal skipping sequences, followed by a downstream sr39TK suicide gene under the control of a constitutive EF1α promoter. The vector backbone is derived from pMNDW, with deletions in the U3 region of the 3’ LTR to enhance biosafety. The retroviral vector for CAR expression contains a second-generation BCMA-CAR with 4-1BB and CD3ζ signaling domains, fused to a truncated EGFR reporter gene for surface detection, under the control of the MND promoter. The CRISPR-Cas9 system for HLA ablation is delivered as a ribonucleoprotein complex comprising Cas9 protein and two single-guide RNAs targeting exon 1 of B2M and exon 1 of CIITA, respectively. All vectors are pseudotyped with vesicular stomatitis virus G protein for broad tropism. The nucleic acid sequences encoding the gamma and delta chains are codon-optimized for human expression and include intronless cDNA constructs to prevent aberrant splicing. The vectors are produced in HEK293T cells using calcium phosphate transfection and purified by ultracentrifugation to achieve titers exceeding 10^8 infectious units per milliliter. The sequences are deposited in public databases under accession numbers provided in the accompanying tables.
