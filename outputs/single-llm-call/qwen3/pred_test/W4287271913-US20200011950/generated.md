## BACKGROUND

- Cancer is a systemic disease characterized by heterogeneous growth patterns that extend beyond the anatomical boundaries visible on conventional imaging. In solid tumors, particularly in aggressive malignancies such as glioblastoma, the influence of the tumor is not confined to the region of gross abnormality but permeates into adjacent tissues that appear structurally normal on radiological scans. This phenomenon, known as the tumor field effect, manifests as biomechanical and molecular alterations in the peritumoral parenchyma, driven by invasive cell migration, mass effect, and disruption of local tissue architecture. The clinical consequence of this field effect is profound: recurrence is nearly universal in glioblastoma despite maximal surgical resection, and survival outcomes are strongly correlated with the extent of microscopic infiltration beyond visible margins. Conventional diagnostic and prognostic approaches have historically relied on tumor volume, contrast enhancement, and histopathological grading, all of which fail to capture the latent, subvisual signatures of tumor aggressiveness embedded in the surrounding brain tissue. As a result, patients with similar radiographic appearances often exhibit divergent clinical trajectories, highlighting a critical gap in the ability to predict overall survival with precision. Existing methods lack the sensitivity to detect subtle, spatially distributed deformations in the peritumoral region that reflect the biomechanical forces exerted by the tumor, and they overlook the synergistic relationship between textural heterogeneity within the tumor and morphological perturbations in the adjacent parenchyma. Without a comprehensive framework that integrates both local tissue disruption and global biomechanical strain, current models remain inadequate for stratifying patients into distinct risk categories with meaningful clinical utility.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- Glioblastoma is the most aggressive primary brain tumor in adults, characterized by rapid proliferation, diffuse infiltration, and resistance to therapy. Its clinical behavior is governed not only by the intrinsic properties of the tumor core but also by its interaction with the surrounding brain parenchyma, which undergoes structural deformation due to mass effect, edema, and mechanical displacement. This interaction, termed the tumor field effect, creates a zone of altered tissue architecture that extends far beyond the enhancing lesion visible on magnetic resonance imaging. Traditional approaches to prognostication have focused exclusively on features derived from the tumor itself—such as necrosis, contrast enhancement, and texture heterogeneity—while neglecting the information encoded in the deformation patterns of the peritumoral region. These methods are inherently limited because they treat the tumor as an isolated entity, ignoring the systemic biomechanical consequences of its growth. Radiomic analyses, though capable of extracting hundreds of quantitative features from imaging data, have largely confined their scope to the tumor and immediate peritumoral boundary, failing to capture the graded, distance-dependent changes in tissue morphology that occur as one moves from the tumor margin into the healthy parenchyma. The present invention overcomes this limitation by introducing a novel integrated descriptor, r-DepTH, which unifies biomechanical deformation metrics derived from the brain around tumor region with textural heterogeneity measures from the tumor and peritumoral compartments. This descriptor is computed through a multi-stage computational pipeline that begins with the acquisition of a three-dimensional radiological image of the brain, followed by precise segmentation of the tumor into its enhancing and non-enhancing components. A peritumoral region is then defined through morphological dilation of the tumor boundary, creating a series of concentric annular sub-volumes that extend radially outward at uniform intervals up to 60 millimeters. Each of these sub-volumes is analyzed for deformation magnitude using non-rigid registration between the patient’s brain scan and a healthy atlas, enabling the quantification of voxel-wise displacement vectors that reflect the cumulative mechanical strain induced by the tumor. Concurrently, a three-dimensional gradient-based texture descriptor, CoLIAGe, is computed within the tumor and peritumoral regions to capture local anisotropy in tissue structure through statistical analysis of gradient orientation co-occurrence matrices. The resulting deformation and textural feature sets are concatenated into a single high-dimensional descriptor, r-DepTH, which encapsulates both the spatial extent of biomechanical perturbation and the microstructural heterogeneity of the tumor habitat. This integrated descriptor is then provided as input to a machine learning classifier trained to distinguish between long-term survivors and short-term survivors based on overall survival outcomes. The classifier, implemented through a regularized Cox regression model with LASSO feature selection, identifies a minimal set of discriminative features that collectively predict survival probability with high accuracy. The output of the classifier is a continuous risk score and a categorical classification, which may be displayed alongside the original radiological image and the computed descriptors to guide clinical decision-making. The system is further capable of generating personalized treatment plans by correlating the predicted survival risk with established therapeutic protocols, including the extent of surgical resection, radiation field planning, and adjuvant chemotherapy regimens. Embodiments of the invention include apparatuses comprising a processor, memory, and input/output circuits configured to execute the full computational workflow, from image acquisition to risk stratification and display. The method is applicable to standard clinical MRI sequences, including T1-weighted, T2-weighted, and FLAIR images, and does not require contrast agents or specialized acquisition protocols. The invention also encompasses a training framework in which a cohort of labeled radiological images is used to optimize the parameters of the machine learning classifier, including the number of annular bands, the depth of feature extraction, and the selection of statistical metrics such as skewness, kurtosis, and standard deviation of deformation magnitude. Receiver operating characteristic curves are generated to evaluate discriminative performance, and area under the curve values exceeding 0.75 are consistently achieved across independent validation cohorts. The invention further includes a networked system in which radiological images are transmitted from remote imaging centers to a centralized processing server, where the r-DepTH descriptor is computed and returned to the clinician as a diagnostic aid. The system is implemented on a non-transitory computer-readable storage medium containing executable instructions that, when executed by a processor, cause the system to access a radiological image, segment the tumoral region, define the peritumoral region through morphological dilation, register the parenchymal region to a healthy brain atlas, compute deformation heterogeneity features, extract 3D gradient-based textural descriptors, generate the r-DepTH descriptor, provide it to a trained machine learning classifier, receive a probability of survival classification, and display the resulting classification to the user. The invention enables a paradigm shift in neuro-oncology by transforming routine clinical imaging into a dynamic biomarker of tumor aggressiveness, thereby facilitating earlier intervention, more precise surgical planning, and improved patient stratification for clinical trials. The technical improvement lies in the ability to extract prognostic information from regions previously considered benign, thereby increasing the sensitivity and specificity of survival prediction beyond what is achievable with tumor-centric features alone.

- The invention further comprises a method for distinguishing long-term survivors from short-term survivors of glioblastoma by analyzing radiological images acquired via magnetic resonance imaging. The method begins with the acquisition of a three-dimensional radiological image of a patient’s brain, which is then preprocessed to correct for intensity inhomogeneities and aligned to a standardized anatomical space. The tumor region is segmented into two distinct compartments: the enhancing lesion and the T2/FLAIR hyperintense peritumoral component, using manual or semi-automated delineation by a trained radiologist. A peritumoral region is defined by applying a morphological dilation operation to the tumor boundary, generating a series of concentric annular sub-regions at fixed radial intervals—typically 5 millimeters—extending from the tumor margin up to a maximum distance of 60 millimeters. Each annular sub-region is treated as an independent volume of interest for subsequent analysis. The parenchymal region corresponding to each annular band is then registered to a healthy brain atlas using a diffeomorphic non-rigid registration algorithm, which computes a voxel-wise deformation field that quantifies the displacement of each voxel in the patient’s brain relative to its corresponding position in the atlas. The magnitude of this displacement is calculated for every voxel within each annular sub-region, and first-order and higher-order statistical features—including mean, median, standard deviation, skewness, and kurtosis—are computed to characterize the distribution of deformation magnitudes. Simultaneously, a three-dimensional gradient-based texture descriptor is computed for both the enhancing lesion and the peritumoral region using a CoLIAGe algorithm, which calculates local gradient orientations in three dimensions, constructs co-occurrence matrices of these orientations, and extracts Haralick statistics to quantify textural heterogeneity. The resulting deformation features and textural features are concatenated into a unified feature vector, known as the r-DepTH descriptor, which contains 320 distinct radiomic parameters. This descriptor is then provided as input to a machine learning classifier trained on a retrospective cohort of glioblastoma patients with known overall survival outcomes. The classifier, implemented as a LASSO-penalized Cox proportional hazards model, selects a subset of the most predictive features and generates a continuous risk score. A threshold is determined using X-tile software to dichotomize patients into high-risk and low-risk groups, and survival analysis is performed using Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests to validate the prognostic significance of the classification. The system is validated on multiple independent datasets, demonstrating consistent performance across institutions and scanner platforms. The invention further includes a display circuit that renders the original radiological image overlaid with the computed r-DepTH descriptor, the risk classification, and a color-coded heatmap of deformation magnitude across the peritumoral region, enabling the clinician to visually correlate structural abnormalities with predicted survival outcomes. The method is implemented on a specialized apparatus comprising a processor, memory, input/output interface, and dedicated circuits for image acquisition, region definition, descriptor computation, classification, and display. The apparatus may be integrated into a clinical workflow as a computer-aided diagnosis system or deployed as a standalone device for remote analysis. The invention also encompasses a personalized medicine system that, upon receiving the classification, generates a tailored treatment plan that recommends surgical resection margins, radiation dose distribution, and chemotherapy regimens based on the predicted risk profile. The system is capable of continuous learning, wherein new patient data is periodically incorporated to refine the classifier and improve predictive accuracy over time. The technical advancement lies in the integration of biomechanical deformation metrics with textural heterogeneity features to create a holistic biomarker of tumor field effect, thereby enabling a level of prognostic precision previously unattainable through conventional imaging analysis.

- The invention further includes a computer-readable storage medium encoded with instructions that, when executed by a processor, perform the steps of accessing a radiological image of a patient diagnosed with glioblastoma, segmenting the tumoral region into enhancing and non-enhancing components, defining a peritumoral region through morphological dilation of the tumor boundary, generating a set of annular sub-regions extending radially outward from the tumor margin, registering the parenchymal region within each annular sub-region to a healthy brain atlas to compute a deformation field, calculating the magnitude of deformation for each voxel within each annular sub-region, computing first-order and higher-order statistical features from the deformation magnitudes, extracting a three-dimensional gradient-based textural descriptor from the enhancing lesion and peritumoral region, concatenating the deformation and textural features into a single r-DepTH descriptor, providing the r-DepTH descriptor to a trained machine learning classifier, receiving a probability of survival classification from the classifier, generating a classification of the patient as either a long-term survivor or a short-term survivor, and displaying the classification to a user. The method further includes training the machine learning classifier by acquiring a training dataset of radiological images with known survival outcomes, computing the r-DepTH descriptor for each image in the dataset, selecting a subset of features using LASSO regression, and optimizing the classifier parameters to maximize the concordance index between predicted and observed survival times. The invention also encompasses a system for generating a personalized glioblastoma treatment plan by correlating the classification output with clinical guidelines, and displaying the recommended therapeutic strategy on a graphical interface. The system is configured to operate in a networked environment, allowing radiological images to be transmitted from remote imaging centers to a central processing server, where the r-DepTH descriptor is computed and returned to the clinician via a secure communication channel. The invention further includes a digital whole slide scanner adapted for radiological image analysis, a computer-aided diagnosis system integrated into a hospital information system, and a networked environment comprising multiple remote computers configured to access the system via a secure web portal. The invention provides a technical improvement in the field of oncological imaging by enabling the extraction of prognostic information from regions of the brain previously considered non-informative, thereby increasing the accuracy, reproducibility, and clinical utility of survival prediction in glioblastoma.
