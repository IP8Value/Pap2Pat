# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to mitochondrial uncoupling

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for modulating mitochondrial uncoupling in living organisms to achieve therapeutic benefits, particularly in the context of aging, metabolic regulation, intestinal homeostasis, and cancer. Specifically, the invention encompasses the activation of mitochondrial uncoupling through pharmacological, genetic, or physiological means to extend lifespan, reduce cellular proliferation, preserve tissue integrity, and inhibit tumor progression. The invention is grounded in the discovery that disruption of circadian repressor proteins, particularly Period, leads to constitutive upregulation of endogenous mitochondrial uncoupling proteins, resulting in a metabolic state characterized by increased respiration, reduced mitochondrial membrane potential, diminished reactive oxygen species production, and enhanced resilience to age-related and pathological tissue dysfunction. This state, once considered merely a byproduct of metabolic inefficiency, is now recognized as a potent regulator of organismal health and longevity. The invention provides novel strategies to induce this beneficial uncoupling state in a controlled and therapeutically relevant manner, without reliance on dietary restriction or modulation of canonical longevity pathways such as insulin or TOR signaling. The compounds, methods, and biological systems described herein are applicable across a broad spectrum of species, including mammals, and are particularly suited for the prevention and treatment of conditions associated with mitochondrial dysfunction, stem cell overproliferation, intestinal barrier failure, and neoplastic transformation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce circadian rhythm

Circadian rhythms are endogenous, approximately 24-hour biological cycles that regulate a wide array of physiological and behavioral processes, including sleep-wake cycles, hormone secretion, metabolism, and cellular repair. These rhythms are generated by molecular feedback loops composed of transcriptional activators and repressors that oscillate in expression to synchronize internal physiology with external environmental cues, primarily light and darkness. In many organisms, including Drosophila melanogaster and Homo sapiens, core components of this system include the transcriptional activators CLOCK and CYCLE, which drive the expression of repressor proteins such as PERIOD and TIMELESS. These repressors accumulate over time, inhibit their own transcription by suppressing CLOCK-CYCLE activity, and are subsequently degraded, allowing the cycle to restart. This tightly regulated feedback mechanism ensures that metabolic and proliferative activities occur at optimal times of day, minimizing oxidative stress and maintaining tissue homeostasis. Disruption of these rhythms, whether through genetic mutation, environmental perturbation, or aging, has been historically associated with metabolic disease, neurodegeneration, and shortened lifespan.

- describe circadian clock components

The core circadian clock in Drosophila consists of a transcription-translation feedback loop wherein the heterodimeric transcription factor CLOCK-CYCLE binds to E-box promoter elements to activate transcription of the period and timeless genes. The resulting PER and TIM proteins form complexes in the cytoplasm, translocate into the nucleus, and directly inhibit CLOCK-CYCLE activity, thereby repressing their own expression. This repression is relieved upon phosphorylation and proteasomal degradation of PER and TIM, allowing a new cycle to begin. In mammals, homologous proteins—CLOCK or NPAS2, BMAL1, PER1–3, and CRY1–2—perform analogous functions. The circadian system is not confined to the central pacemaker in the brain; peripheral clocks exist in virtually all tissues, including the intestine, liver, and adipose tissue, where they locally regulate gene expression in coordination with systemic cues. These peripheral clocks are particularly critical for metabolic regulation, and their desynchronization has been linked to obesity, diabetes, and cancer. Notably, while disruption of the activator components (e.g., CLOCK or CYCLE) consistently results in metabolic dysfunction and reduced lifespan, the functional consequences of losing repressor components have been less clear and context-dependent, with some studies reporting detrimental effects and others suggesting potential benefits.

- discuss impact of circadian regulation on lifespan

Aging is accompanied by a progressive decline in circadian rhythmicity, manifesting as dampened oscillations in gene expression, altered sleep patterns, and disrupted metabolic homeostasis. It has long been hypothesized that this loss of circadian control contributes directly to the aging process, and that restoring rhythmicity may extend healthspan. However, experimental evidence has revealed a more nuanced relationship: while loss of circadian activators such as CYCLE or CLOCK invariably shortens lifespan, loss of repressors like PER or TIM in Drosophila can paradoxically extend lifespan by up to 20%, even under standard dietary conditions. This extension occurs independently of known longevity pathways such as dietary restriction, reduced insulin signaling, or inhibition of TOR activity. The mechanism underlying this phenomenon remained elusive until the discovery that PER loss leads to constitutive mitochondrial uncoupling, a process that increases energy expenditure without ATP production but simultaneously reduces mitochondrial reactive oxygen species generation. This uncoupling state, when confined to specific tissues such as the intestine, preserves epithelial integrity, suppresses age-related hyperproliferation of stem cells, and delays the onset of barrier dysfunction—a key predictor of mortality. These findings challenge the traditional view that circadian disruption is universally deleterious and instead suggest that targeted disruption of specific repressor components can induce a beneficial metabolic reprogramming that enhances resilience to aging.

- summarize previous studies on circadian transcriptional repressors

Previous investigations into the role of circadian repressors in longevity have yielded conflicting results. Studies in mice have shown that loss of PER2 leads to increased susceptibility to tumorigenesis and metabolic dysregulation, while other reports indicate that PER2-deficient mice exhibit improved glucose tolerance and reduced adiposity under ad libitum feeding. In Drosophila, early studies reported that per01 mutants exhibit shortened lifespan, but these findings were often confounded by high-protein diets or uncontrolled microbial loads. More recent work has demonstrated that under controlled environmental conditions, per01 and tim01 mutants consistently live longer than wild-type controls, regardless of dietary protein content. These long-lived mutants display a hypermetabolic phenotype characterized by increased food intake, reduced lipid stores, elevated CO2 production, and faster recovery from cold stress—all hallmarks of mitochondrial uncoupling. Importantly, this phenotype is not rescued by inhibition of insulin-like signaling or TOR activity, indicating a novel pathway to longevity. Furthermore, the lifespan extension is abolished when mitochondrial uncoupling is genetically or pharmacologically blocked, and is recapitulated by overexpression of the mitochondrial uncoupling protein UCP4C. These findings collectively suggest that circadian repressors, far from being mere transcriptional brakes, actively constrain a metabolic state that, when released, promotes longevity through tissue-specific mitochondrial remodeling.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce mitochondrial uncoupling benefits

Mitochondrial uncoupling refers to the dissipation of the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane without concomitant ATP synthesis, resulting in the conversion of metabolic energy into heat. While traditionally viewed as an inefficient and potentially harmful process, the present invention demonstrates that mild, sustained mitochondrial uncoupling confers profound benefits on organismal health and longevity. Specifically, uncoupling reduces the mitochondrial membrane potential, which in turn lowers the production of reactive oxygen species—a major driver of cellular damage, stem cell dysfunction, and tissue degeneration during aging. This reduction in oxidative stress preserves intestinal barrier integrity, suppresses aberrant proliferation of intestinal stem cells, and delays the onset of age-related pathologies. Importantly, these benefits are achieved without reducing caloric intake or altering canonical longevity pathways, making uncoupling a uniquely potent and independent mechanism for extending healthspan. The invention further reveals that uncoupling can be induced pharmacologically, genetically, or through targeted disruption of circadian repressor proteins, providing multiple avenues for therapeutic intervention.

- describe method of extending lifespan

The invention provides a method for extending the lifespan of a subject by inducing mitochondrial uncoupling in a tissue-specific manner, particularly within the intestine. This is accomplished by either genetically disrupting the expression of a circadian transcriptional repressor such as PERIOD, or by administering a compound that activates mitochondrial uncoupling. In both cases, the resulting uncoupling state reduces mitochondrial membrane potential and reactive oxygen species levels in intestinal stem cells and their progeny, thereby preserving epithelial homeostasis and delaying age-related functional decline. The method is effective in diverse organisms, including insects, rodents, and primates, and does not require dietary restriction or suppression of insulin or TOR signaling. Lifespan extension is observed even in the presence of normal or elevated nutrient intake, and is associated with improved resistance to stress, maintenance of tissue architecture, and reduced incidence of neoplastic transformation.

- describe method of decreasing cellular proliferation

The invention provides a method for decreasing aberrant cellular proliferation in tissues prone to age-related hyperproliferation, such as the intestinal epithelium, by inducing mitochondrial uncoupling. This method involves the administration of a mitochondrial uncoupling agent or the genetic activation of endogenous uncoupling proteins, which reduces mitochondrial membrane potential and suppresses the production of reactive oxygen species. The lowered oxidative environment inhibits mitogenic signaling in stem and progenitor cells, preventing their uncontrolled division and differentiation. This effect is particularly pronounced in the intestine, where uncoupling suppresses the overproliferation of intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts, thereby maintaining tissue architecture and preventing dysplasia. The method is effective in both aged subjects and those with genetically induced proliferative disorders, and does not require inhibition of growth factor pathways or immune modulation.

- describe method of restoring intestinal homeostasis

The invention provides a method for restoring intestinal homeostasis in subjects exhibiting age-related or pathology-induced disruption of the intestinal barrier, stem cell regulation, or epithelial integrity. This is achieved by inducing mitochondrial uncoupling within the intestinal epithelium, specifically in intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts. Uncoupling reduces mitochondrial reactive oxygen species, which are known to drive aberrant signaling that leads to stem cell overactivation, misdifferentiation, and loss of barrier function. By maintaining a low-oxidative state in these cells, the method preserves the balance between proliferation and differentiation, prevents the infiltration of microbial products into systemic circulation, and restores the structural and functional integrity of the intestinal epithelium. This restoration is demonstrated by reduced “smurf” phenotype incidence, decreased epithelial permeability, and normalized expression of junctional proteins, all of which correlate with improved survival and healthspan.

- describe method of slowing tumor growth

The invention provides a method for slowing the growth of tumors, particularly those arising from epithelial stem cells such as intestinal adenomas, by inducing mitochondrial uncoupling in the tumor microenvironment. This method involves the administration of a mitochondrial uncoupling agent to a subject with a developing or established neoplasm. The uncoupling agent reduces mitochondrial membrane potential and reactive oxygen species levels within tumor-initiating cells, thereby suppressing their proliferative capacity and delaying tumor progression. This effect is observed even in genetically driven tumor models, such as those induced by loss of Notch signaling, and occurs independently of immune activation or cytotoxic chemotherapy. The method is particularly effective in tissues with high stem cell turnover, where uncoupling acts as a metabolic brake on neoplastic transformation.

- describe method of preventing or treating cancer

The invention provides a method for preventing or treating cancer by inducing mitochondrial uncoupling in tissues susceptible to epithelial malignancies. This method involves the systemic or localized administration of a mitochondrial uncoupling agent to a subject at risk for or diagnosed with cancer, particularly gastrointestinal, skin, or lung cancers characterized by stem cell hyperproliferation. The uncoupling agent reduces mitochondrial membrane potential and reactive oxygen species production in pre-malignant and malignant cells, thereby inhibiting the signaling cascades that drive uncontrolled division, evasion of apoptosis, and metabolic reprogramming. The method is effective as a prophylactic intervention in aging populations and as an adjunctive therapy in combination with conventional treatments, with minimal toxicity due to the selective metabolic vulnerability of rapidly dividing cells to mild uncoupling.

- discuss administration of mitochondrial uncoupling activator

The invention provides methods for the administration of a mitochondrial uncoupling activator to a subject in need thereof, wherein the activator is delivered in a form suitable for oral, topical, intravenous, or inhalation routes. The activator may be administered continuously or intermittently, and may be formulated to target specific tissues such as the intestine, liver, or skin. The dosage is selected to achieve a mild, non-toxic level of uncoupling sufficient to reduce mitochondrial membrane potential and reactive oxygen species without inducing hyperthermia or systemic metabolic collapse. Administration may be initiated in pre-symptomatic individuals to prevent age-related decline or in patients with early-stage disease to slow progression. The activator may be co-administered with other therapeutic agents, including chemotherapeutics, antioxidants, or anti-inflammatory compounds, to enhance efficacy and reduce side effects.

- describe types of agents for mitochondrial uncoupling

The invention encompasses a variety of agents capable of activating mitochondrial uncoupling, including small molecule protonophores such as 2,4-dinitrophenol, stearic acid, and butylated hydroxytoluene; salicylanilide derivatives such as niclosamide and nitazoxanide; carbonyl cyanide phenylhydrazones such as BAM15; membrane-active peptides such as alamethicin and mastoparan; and nucleic acid constructs encoding endogenous uncoupling proteins such as UCP4C, UCP1, or UCP2. These agents function by either dissipating the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane (protonophores), activating endogenous uncoupling proteins (fatty acids), or forming pores in the membrane (peptides). The agents may be synthetic, semi-synthetic, or naturally derived, and are selected based on their ability to induce uncoupling without causing acute toxicity. The invention further includes recombinant vectors for the tissue-specific expression of uncoupling proteins, enabling genetic induction of uncoupling in defined cell populations.

- introduce kits for practicing the invention

The invention further provides kits for practicing the methods described herein, comprising one or more mitochondrial uncoupling agents, instructions for administration, and optionally, diagnostic reagents for assessing mitochondrial membrane potential or reactive oxygen species levels in biological samples. The kits may include pharmaceutical compositions in unit dosage forms, lyophilized powders for reconstitution, or sustained-release formulations. Additional components may include buffers for mitochondrial isolation, fluorescent dyes for membrane potential or ROS detection, antibodies for immunohistochemical analysis of proliferation markers, and protocols for tissue-specific gene delivery. The kits are designed for use in clinical, research, or veterinary settings and are suitable for both prophylactic and therapeutic applications.

### Activation of Mitochondrial Uncoupling has Beneficial Effects

- introduce beneficial effects of mitochondrial uncoupling

The activation of mitochondrial uncoupling produces a constellation of beneficial physiological effects that collectively enhance organismal health and longevity. These include a reduction in mitochondrial membrane potential, a decrease in the production of reactive oxygen species, an increase in basal metabolic rate without increased caloric intake, and a preservation of tissue integrity in high-turnover epithelia. These effects are not merely correlative but are causally linked, as genetic or pharmacological inhibition of uncoupling abolishes the observed benefits. The uncoupling state acts as a metabolic rheostat, dampening oxidative stress while maintaining energy flux, thereby protecting cells from damage without compromising viability.

- describe delayed aging and extended lifespan

The induction of mitochondrial uncoupling delays the onset of aging-related phenotypes and extends the median and maximum lifespan of subjects across multiple species. In Drosophila, this extension is observed even in the absence of dietary restriction or reduced insulin signaling, demonstrating that uncoupling operates through a distinct pathway. The lifespan extension is accompanied by improved locomotor activity, enhanced stress resistance, and delayed decline in organ function, particularly in the intestine and nervous system. In mammalian models, similar effects are achieved through the administration of low-dose uncoupling agents, suggesting evolutionary conservation of the mechanism.

- describe increased intestinal homeostasis

Mitochondrial uncoupling significantly enhances intestinal homeostasis by reducing oxidative stress in intestinal stem cells and their progeny. This leads to a more balanced rate of proliferation and differentiation, preservation of epithelial barrier integrity, and reduced translocation of microbial products into systemic circulation. The result is a marked reduction in age-related “smurf” phenotype incidence, decreased intestinal permeability, and normalized expression of tight junction proteins such as septate junction components in Drosophila or claudins in mammals.

- describe decreased cellular proliferation

Uncoupling suppresses the hyperproliferation of stem and progenitor cells in tissues such as the intestine, skin, and hematopoietic system. This effect is mediated by the reduction in mitochondrial reactive oxygen species, which act as signaling molecules that drive proliferation through pathways such as JNK, NF-κB, and MAPK. By lowering ROS levels, uncoupling restores quiescence in stem cell populations and prevents the accumulation of dysplastic clones, thereby reducing the risk of tissue overgrowth and neoplastic transformation.

- describe slowed tumor growth

In models of genetically induced or spontaneous tumorigenesis, mitochondrial uncoupling slows tumor growth by impairing the metabolic flexibility of cancer cells. Tumor-initiating cells, which rely on high mitochondrial membrane potential and elevated ROS for survival and proliferation, become metabolically compromised under uncoupling conditions. This leads to reduced clonal expansion, delayed tumor formation, and extended survival in tumor-bearing subjects, even in the absence of cytotoxic therapy.

- describe prevention and treatment of cancer

The induction of mitochondrial uncoupling provides a novel strategy for the prevention and treatment of cancer, particularly epithelial cancers arising from stem cell compartments. By creating a metabolic environment that is unfavorable for hyperproliferation and oxidative stress-driven mutagenesis, uncoupling agents can prevent the initiation of tumors and suppress the progression of existing lesions. This approach is especially promising for cancers of the colon, esophagus, and skin, where stem cell dynamics are central to disease pathogenesis.

- summarize beneficial effects

In summary, the activation of mitochondrial uncoupling produces a broad spectrum of beneficial effects, including extended lifespan, enhanced intestinal homeostasis, suppressed cellular proliferation, delayed tumor growth, and improved resilience to metabolic and oxidative stress. These effects are achieved without the need for caloric restriction, pharmacological inhibition of growth pathways, or immune modulation, making uncoupling a uniquely powerful and broadly applicable therapeutic strategy.

### Methods and Compositions for Activating Mitochondrial Uncoupling

- motivate mitochondrial uncoupling

Mitochondrial uncoupling has long been considered a wasteful process that dissipates energy as heat. However, the present invention demonstrates that mild, sustained uncoupling is a highly beneficial physiological state that enhances longevity and tissue integrity by reducing oxidative damage. This paradigm shift motivates the development of targeted strategies to induce uncoupling in a controlled and safe manner, particularly in aging populations or individuals at risk for age-related diseases.

- describe benefits of mitochondrial uncoupling

The benefits of mitochondrial uncoupling include a reduction in mitochondrial membrane potential, a decrease in reactive oxygen species production, an increase in metabolic rate without increased caloric intake, preservation of stem cell quiescence, maintenance of epithelial barrier function, and suppression of neoplastic transformation. These benefits are synergistic and cumulative, leading to improved healthspan and extended lifespan across diverse species.

- introduce mitochondrial uncouplers

Mitochondrial uncouplers are agents that facilitate the movement of protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, thereby dissipating the proton gradient without ATP synthesis. These agents include protonophores, fatty acids, peptides, and endogenous uncoupling proteins. The invention encompasses both synthetic and naturally derived uncouplers, each with distinct pharmacological profiles and tissue-specific effects.

- exemplify mifepristone (RU486)

Mifepristone (RU486), a synthetic steroid receptor modulator, is employed in the invention as an inducer of gene expression in genetically modified organisms. When used in conjunction with the GeneSwitch system, RU486 activates the expression of uncoupling proteins such as UCP4C in Drosophila, thereby inducing mitochondrial uncoupling and extending lifespan. While RU486 itself is not a direct uncoupler, it serves as a critical tool for controlled, tissue-specific induction of uncoupling in experimental models.

- exemplify stearic acid

Stearic acid, a saturated long-chain fatty acid, functions as a natural activator of endogenous uncoupling proteins such as UCP1 and UCP4C. When administered orally or incorporated into the diet, stearic acid binds to and activates these proteins, leading to increased proton leak, reduced mitochondrial membrane potential, and decreased ROS production. This makes stearic acid a safe and dietary-compatible agent for inducing uncoupling in humans and other mammals.

- exemplify 2,4-dinitrophenol (2,4 DNP or DNP)

2,4-Dinitrophenol (DNP) is a potent protonophore that shuttles protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, effectively uncoupling oxidative phosphorylation. At low, non-toxic doses (0.01–0.1 mM), DNP extends lifespan in Drosophila and reduces intestinal ROS levels without causing hyperthermia. The invention provides methods for the safe, controlled administration of DNP in humans for the prevention of aging-related diseases and cancer.

- exemplify butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT)

Butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), a synthetic antioxidant, also functions as a mitochondrial uncoupler at low concentrations. BHT reduces mitochondrial membrane potential and suppresses age-related intestinal hyperproliferation in Drosophila and murine models. Its dual role as an antioxidant and uncoupler makes it a uniquely effective agent for preserving tissue homeostasis.

- describe general structural requirements for uncoupling activity

Effective mitochondrial uncouplers typically possess a hydrophobic aromatic ring system, a weakly acidic protonatable group, and sufficient lipophilicity to partition into the mitochondrial membrane. These structural features enable the molecule to bind protons on one side of the membrane, diffuse across the lipid bilayer, and release the proton on the other side, thereby dissipating the gradient.

- exemplify substituted phenols

Substituted phenols, including salicylic acid derivatives and nitrophenols, are potent uncouplers due to their ability to act as proton carriers. The presence of electron-withdrawing groups such as nitro or halogen substituents enhances acidity and membrane permeability, increasing uncoupling efficacy.

- describe importance of hydrophobicity, acidity, and stability

Hydrophobicity enables uncouplers to penetrate the lipid bilayer of the mitochondrial membrane. Acidity determines the pKa of the protonatable group, ensuring efficient proton binding and release within the physiological pH range. Stability ensures that the compound remains intact during administration and does not degrade into toxic metabolites.

- exemplify salicylanilide derivatives

Salicylanilide derivatives, such as niclosamide and nitazoxanide, are clinically approved anthelmintic drugs that also function as mitochondrial uncouplers. These compounds contain a salicylic acid moiety linked to an anilide group, conferring both protonophoric activity and high oral bioavailability.

- describe structure-activity relationships with salicylanilide derivatives

Structure-activity relationship studies reveal that the hydroxyl group on the salicylate ring and the halogen substitution on the anilide ring are critical for uncoupling activity. Removal of the hydroxyl group abolishes protonophoric function, while increased halogen size enhances membrane partitioning and potency.

- exemplify niclosamide

Niclosamide, a salicylanilide derivative approved for the treatment of tapeworm infections, induces mitochondrial uncoupling at low micromolar concentrations. It reduces mitochondrial membrane potential, suppresses intestinal stem cell proliferation, and extends lifespan in Drosophila. The invention provides methods for repurposing niclosamide for the prevention of aging-related diseases and cancer.

- describe niclosamide's safety profile

Niclosamide has a well-established safety profile in humans, with minimal systemic toxicity at therapeutic doses. It is poorly absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract, limiting off-target effects, and is excreted largely unchanged. These properties make it ideal for oral administration targeting the intestinal epithelium.

- exemplify nitazoxanide

Nitazoxanide, another salicylanilide derivative, exhibits similar uncoupling activity to niclosamide and is approved for the treatment of protozoal infections. The invention demonstrates that nitazoxanide extends lifespan and preserves intestinal homeostasis in aging models, supporting its use as a mitochondrial uncoupling agent.

- describe carbonyl cyanide phenylhydrazones

Carbonyl cyanide phenylhydrazones (CCPs) are synthetic uncouplers that disrupt the proton gradient by facilitating proton transport. BAM15, a newer CCP derivative, exhibits high selectivity for mitochondria and low toxicity, making it a preferred agent for long-term administration.

- exemplify BAM15

BAM15 is a potent, selective mitochondrial uncoupler with minimal off-target effects. It reduces mitochondrial membrane potential, increases oxygen consumption, and suppresses ROS production without inducing hyperthermia. The invention provides methods for administering BAM15 to extend lifespan and prevent cancer in mammalian models.

- describe other types of agents which activate uncoupling

Other agents capable of activating uncoupling include mitochondrial-targeted peptides, small molecule activators of endogenous UCPs, and compounds that modulate fatty acid metabolism to enhance UCP activity. These agents offer diverse routes of administration and tissue specificity.

- describe membrane-active peptides

Membrane-active peptides such as alamethicin and mastoparan form pores in the mitochondrial membrane, allowing proton leakage. These peptides are effective uncouplers but require targeted delivery to avoid systemic toxicity.

- exemplify alamithicin and mastoparan

Alamethicin, a fungal peptide, and mastoparan, a wasp venom peptide, both induce mitochondrial uncoupling by forming voltage-dependent ion channels. When delivered via liposomal encapsulation or tissue-specific promoters, these peptides can be used to induce localized uncoupling without systemic effects.

- describe effect of peptides on mitochondrial integrity

While peptides can effectively uncouple mitochondria, their pore-forming activity may compromise membrane integrity if not carefully controlled. The invention provides methods for conjugating peptides to targeting moieties or encapsulating them in nanoparticles to limit their action to specific cell types.

- describe signal peptide-induced uncoupling

Signal peptides, normally involved in mitochondrial protein import, can induce mild uncoupling when overexpressed due to their ability to disrupt membrane potential during translocation. The invention includes methods for engineering synthetic signal peptides to enhance this effect.

- describe nucleic acid encoding one or more uncoupling proteins

Nucleic acid constructs encoding endogenous uncoupling proteins such as UCP1, UCP2, UCP4C, or UCP5 can be delivered to tissues via viral vectors or transgenic expression. These constructs enable long-term, tissue-specific uncoupling without the need for continuous drug administration.

- describe delivery methods for vectors

Delivery methods for nucleic acid vectors include adeno-associated virus (AAV), lentivirus, lipid nanoparticles, and electroporation. Tissue-specific promoters such as intestinal epithelial cell-specific enhancers ensure that uncoupling is restricted to the desired organ, minimizing systemic effects.

Nasal and pulmonary administration are suitable for agents targeting the respiratory epithelium or requiring rapid systemic absorption. Nebulizers and inhalers are employed to deliver fine particulate formulations.

- describe vaginal administration

Vaginal administration is used for localized treatment of gynecological conditions or for systemic absorption via the vaginal mucosa.

- describe parenteral administration

Parenteral administration includes intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and intraperitoneal injection. This route is used when rapid systemic delivery or bypass of the gastrointestinal tract is required.

Parenteral vehicles include sterile water, saline, dextrose solutions, and lipid emulsions. Stabilizers such as albumin or polysorbates may be included to prevent aggregation.

- describe delivery vehicles

Delivery vehicles include liposomes, nanoparticles, micelles, and dendrimers that encapsulate uncoupling agents to enhance bioavailability, reduce toxicity, and enable targeted delivery.

Liposomes are spherical vesicles composed of phospholipid bilayers that encapsulate hydrophilic or hydrophobic agents. They can be surface-modified with ligands to target specific tissues, such as intestinal epithelial cells.

Liposomes are formed by hydration of phospholipids followed by extrusion or sonication. They are used to deliver uncoupling agents such as BAM15 or niclosamide with enhanced stability and reduced off-target effects.

- describe nanocapsule or nanoparticle formulations

Nanocapsules and nanoparticles are polymeric or lipid-based carriers with diameters below 200 nm. They protect uncoupling agents from degradation and enable controlled release, particularly in the gastrointestinal tract.

The therapeutically effective amount is selected based on the agent’s potency, the subject’s weight, age, and health status, and the desired biological endpoint. For example, 2,4-DNP is administered at 0.01–0.1 mM in diet, while niclosamide is given at 1–10 mg/kg/day orally.

Factors affecting dose include the agent’s bioavailability, half-life, tissue distribution, metabolic clearance, and potential for toxicity. Dose escalation studies are conducted to determine the maximum tolerated dose and the minimum effective dose.

The uncoupling agent may be administered concurrently with chemotherapeutics, antioxidants, anti-inflammatory agents, or metabolic modulators to enhance efficacy and reduce side effects. For example, uncoupling enhances the efficacy of radiation therapy by reducing tumor antioxidant capacity.

Subjects who benefit from mitochondrial uncoupling include aging individuals, those with metabolic syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease, intestinal dysplasia, or early-stage cancer. Healthy subjects may also benefit from prophylactic administration to delay age-related decline.

Healthy subjects benefit from mitochondrial uncoupling through enhanced metabolic flexibility, reduced oxidative stress, improved mitochondrial quality control, and delayed onset of age-related pathologies.

Subjects with cancer benefit from mitochondrial uncoupling through suppression of tumor-initiating cell proliferation, reduced metastatic potential, and increased sensitivity to conventional therapies.

Subjects with intestinal dysfunction, including inflammatory bowel disease, leaky gut syndrome, or age-related barrier failure, benefit from restored epithelial integrity, reduced microbial translocation, and normalized immune activation.

Subjects with harmful cellular proliferation, such as polyps, adenomas, or premalignant lesions, benefit from the suppression of stem cell overactivation and the restoration of normal differentiation programs.

### Example 1

- introduce Fly Srains

Drosophila melanogaster strains including per01, tim01, cyc01, daGS-GAL4, UAS-Ucp4C, UAS-Per, UAS-DN-Clock, esg-GAL4, and UAS-Notch-RNAi were obtained from established stock centers and outcrossed for at least 12 generations into a w1118 background to minimize genetic variability.

- describe UAS-CRISPR line construction

CRISPR/Cas9 constructs targeting the period gene were assembled using pCFD6 plasmid and four guide RNAs designed to span critical exons. Constructs were microinjected into embryos at the Su(Hw)attP5 locus and verified by Sanger sequencing.

- describe fly media

Standard fly media contained 3.8% glucose, 1.9% sucrose, 3% yeast extract, 6.5% cornmeal, 0.8% agar, 1.5% methylparaben, and 1% propionic acid. Adult media was supplemented with varying concentrations of yeast extract (0.01–10%) to test dietary effects.

- describe drug supplementation in media

Mitochondrial uncouplers including 2,4-DNP (0.01–0.1 mM), BHT (0.1–1 mM), niclosamide (10–100 µM), and RU486 (5–100 µg/mL) were dissolved in ethanol and added to cooled media immediately before use. Vehicle controls received equivalent volumes of ethanol.

- describe lifespan analysis and starvation

Newly eclosed male flies were collected and maintained in vials at 25°C under a 12:12 light-dark cycle. Survival was monitored daily, with live flies transferred to fresh food every 2–3 days. Starvation resistance was assessed by transferring flies to 1% agar and recording time to death.

- describe Western blot analysis

Whole-fly lysates were prepared in RIPA buffer, separated by SDS-PAGE, and probed with antibodies against phospho-AKT, phospho-S6K, GFP, and actin. Signal was detected using ECL and quantified by densitometry.

- describe quantitative real-time PCR

RNA was extracted from whole flies or dissected intestines using TRIzol, reverse-transcribed, and amplified using gene-specific primers. Expression levels were normalized to Actin5C and calculated using the ΔΔCt method.

- describe cold shock recovery assay

Flies were chilled at 4°C for 1 hour and then transferred to 25°C. Recovery was defined as the time required to achieve three consecutive beam breaks in a DAM monitor.

- describe quantification of triglycerides levels

Lipids were extracted from five flies using chloroform:ethanol (2:1), separated by thin-layer chromatography, and quantified by densitometry after staining with Amido Black.

- describe intestinal barrier dysfunction assay

Flies were fed blue food dye (2.5% FD&C Blue No. 1) for 24 hours and scored as “smurfs” if dye was visible outside the digestive tract.

- describe feeding assay

Capillary feeding (CAFE) assays were performed using ten flies per vial with calibrated capillaries containing blue-dyed food. Consumption was measured by capillary volume change over 24 hours.

- describe CO2 respiration assay

Flies were placed in sealed respirometers containing soda lime, and CO2 production was measured by the displacement of blue dye in a capillary over 2 hours.

- describe mitochondrial purification for downstream assays

Fifty flies were homogenized in mitochondrial isolation medium and centrifuged at 1000×g and then 4000×g to isolate mitochondria. Protein concentration was determined by Bradford assay.

- describe mitochondrial oxygen consumption measurements

Mitochondria were resuspended in MiR05 buffer and oxygen consumption was measured using an Oroboros Oxygraph-2k. Sequential additions of substrates and inhibitors were used to assess respiratory states.

- describe membrane potential JC-1 assay

Mitochondria were loaded with JC-1 dye and fluorescence at 530 nm and 590 nm was measured using a plate reader. The ratio of red to green fluorescence indicated membrane potential.

- describe blue-native PAGE and in-gel activity assay

Mitochondria were solubilized in digitonin, loaded onto NativePAGE gels, and electrophoresed. Complex I activity was visualized by NADH and nitroblue tetrazolium staining.

- describe circadian locomotor activity

Flies were monitored in DAM systems under 12:12 light-dark cycles and then in constant darkness. Rhythmicity and period were analyzed using ClockLab software.

- describe TMRE/MitoSOX intestinal staining

Intestines were dissected and stained with TMRE (75 nM) or MitoSOX (50 µM) for 10 minutes, washed, and imaged using confocal microscopy. Fluorescence intensity was quantified using ImageJ.

- describe phospho-histone H3 immunostaining

Intestines were fixed, permeabilized, and stained with anti-phospho-H3 antibody. Nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst. pHH3-positive cells were counted and normalized to tissue area.

- describe Esets>UAS-Notch-RNAi intestine staining

Flies expressing UAS-Notch-RNAi under esg-GAL4 were fed 2,4-DNP or vehicle. Intestines were stained for GFP and area of GFP expression was quantified as a measure of tumor burden.

- describe statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using Prism software. Lifespan curves were compared using log-rank tests, group comparisons using t-tests or ANOVA, and significance was set at p < 0.05. Error bars represent SEM.

- describe fly strains used

Strains included per01, tim01, cyc01, daGS-GAL4, UAS-Ucp4C, UAS-Per, UAS-DN-Clock, esg-GAL4, UAS-Notch-RNAi, UAS-reaper, dilp2-GAL4, and UAS-GFP-Atg8.

- describe reagents used

Reagents included 2,4-DNP, BHT, niclosamide, RU486, JC-1, TMRE, MitoSOX, rotenone, oligomycin, palmitate, GTP, and DAPI.

Software included ClockLab, ImageJ, GraphPad Prism, and Fiji.

Data were analyzed for normality using Shapiro-Wilk tests. Parametric tests were used for normally distributed data; non-parametric tests were used otherwise. All experiments were replicated at least three times.
