# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- claim benefit of prior patent application  
This application claims the benefit of priority under 35 U.S.C. §119(e) to U.S. Provisional Patent Application No. 62/XXX,XXX, filed on [Insert Date], the entire disclosure of which is hereby incorporated by reference in its entirety. The present invention builds upon prior disclosures related to metabolic biomarkers in psychiatric disorders, extending those teachings to the specific identification and validation of isocitrate and associated enzymatic regulators as diagnostic and therapeutic indicators for bipolar disorder.  

- introduce bipolar disorder  
Bipolar disorder is a chronic and recurrent psychiatric condition characterized by distinct episodes of mania or hypomania alternating with periods of depression, often separated by intervals of relative emotional stability known as euthymia. The disorder typically manifests in late adolescence or early adulthood and persists throughout the lifespan, necessitating long-term clinical management and frequent medical intervention.  

- describe economic burden of bipolar disorder  
The societal and economic impact of bipolar disorder is substantial, encompassing direct healthcare expenditures, lost productivity, disability payments, and increased utilization of emergency and inpatient services. Studies estimate the annual per-patient cost exceeds twenty-eight thousand euros in certain healthcare systems, with aggregate national costs in the United States reaching over one hundred fifty billion dollars annually, reflecting the profound strain this condition imposes on public health infrastructure and workforce stability.  

- discuss genetic and environmental factors  
While familial and twin studies demonstrate a strong heritable component to bipolar disorder, the precise interplay between genetic susceptibility and environmental triggers remains incompletely understood. The disorder does not follow a simple Mendelian inheritance pattern, and no single gene variant has been conclusively shown to account for a significant proportion of disease risk. Environmental stressors, including sleep disruption, psychosocial trauma, and circadian rhythm disturbances, are known to precipitate or exacerbate episodes, suggesting a complex etiology involving both constitutional vulnerability and external modulators.  

- introduce metabolomics and its advantages  
Metabolomics offers a powerful systems-level approach to disease characterization by directly quantifying the small-molecule metabolites that reflect the functional output of cellular processes, integrating information from genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic activity as well as post-translational and environmental influences. Unlike other omics technologies, metabolomics captures the dynamic biochemical state of an organism in real time, providing a sensitive and physiologically relevant readout of pathological alterations that may not be evident at the level of gene expression or protein abundance.  

- highlight need for new methods of diagnosing and treating bipolar disorder  
Current diagnostic criteria for bipolar disorder rely exclusively on clinical interviews and behavioral assessments, which are subjective, prone to misclassification, and often delayed due to the episodic nature of symptoms. There is no objective laboratory test to confirm diagnosis, predict relapse, or guide therapeutic selection. Furthermore, existing treatments—including mood stabilizers, antipsychotics, and antidepressants—are associated with significant side effects, variable efficacy, and prolonged trial-and-error periods before optimal regimens are identified. There is therefore an urgent and unmet need for objective, biologically grounded biomarkers that can facilitate early and accurate diagnosis, stratify disease subtypes, monitor treatment response, and identify novel therapeutic targets.

## Non-Patent Literature

- list references related to bipolar disorder  
Numerous peer-reviewed publications have explored the clinical phenomenology, epidemiology, and treatment outcomes of bipolar disorder, including longitudinal cohort studies, randomized controlled trials of pharmacological agents, and meta-analyses of symptom trajectories. These works collectively underscore the heterogeneity of the disorder and the limitations of current diagnostic frameworks.  

- describe studies on metabolomics and bipolar disorder  
Prior metabolomic investigations have examined post-mortem brain tissue and peripheral blood samples from individuals with bipolar disorder, identifying alterations in metabolites related to energy metabolism, oxidative stress, and neurotransmitter synthesis. However, post-mortem brain studies are confounded by post-mortem interval effects, while blood-based analyses may not accurately reflect central nervous system biochemistry due to the blood-brain barrier.  

- discuss genetic studies on bipolar disorder  
Large-scale genome-wide association studies have identified multiple loci with nominal associations to bipolar disorder, yet none have demonstrated consistent, replicable effects across diverse populations. The absence of high-penetrance variants suggests a polygenic architecture with low individual effect sizes, limiting the utility of genetic testing for clinical prediction.  

- introduce capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry  
Capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry is a high-resolution analytical technique capable of simultaneously quantifying hundreds of charged, low-molecular-weight metabolites in complex biological matrices with exceptional sensitivity and reproducibility. This method is particularly suited for analyzing cerebrospinal fluid due to its ability to resolve structurally similar compounds and detect low-abundance metabolites without extensive sample pre-fractionation.  

- describe studies on biomarkers for bipolar disorder  
Previous efforts to identify biomarkers for bipolar disorder have focused on inflammatory cytokines, neurotrophic factors, and oxidative stress markers in serum or plasma. While some associations have been reported, these markers lack specificity, show inconsistent replication, and are influenced by comorbid conditions such as metabolic syndrome or substance use.  

- discuss limitations of current methods  
Existing diagnostic and monitoring approaches for bipolar disorder are entirely dependent on clinical observation, which is inherently subjective and temporally delayed. No biomarker has been validated for routine clinical use, and no assay can reliably distinguish bipolar disorder from unipolar depression or other psychiatric conditions with overlapping symptomatology.  

- highlight need for new methods  
There remains a critical gap in the clinical management of bipolar disorder for an objective, biologically validated, and reproducible diagnostic and prognostic tool. Such a method must be grounded in a pathophysiologically relevant mechanism, detectable in accessible biofluids, independent of medication status, and capable of guiding therapeutic decisions.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

- introduce method for diagnosing bipolar disorder  
The present invention provides a method for diagnosing bipolar disorder by measuring the concentration of specific metabolites in cerebrospinal fluid or serum, comparing those concentrations to established control ranges, and identifying statistically significant deviations indicative of the disorder.  

- describe method for confirming diagnosis of bipolar disorder  
The method further includes confirmation of a clinical diagnosis by correlating the measured biomarker profile with standardized psychiatric assessments, thereby enhancing diagnostic accuracy and reducing false positives or misclassifications.  

- describe method for determining predisposition to bipolar disorder  
The invention enables the determination of predisposition to bipolar disorder in asymptomatic individuals by detecting subclinical alterations in metabolite levels that precede the onset of overt symptoms, allowing for early intervention strategies.  

- introduce method for identifying compounds for treating bipolar disorder  
The invention further encompasses a method for identifying novel therapeutic compounds by assessing their ability to normalize the aberrant metabolite profile associated with bipolar disorder in cellular or animal models.  

- describe measurement of biomarkers in cerebrospinal fluid  
Biomarker levels are measured in cerebrospinal fluid obtained via lumbar puncture during a euthymic state, ensuring that the metabolic signature reflects the underlying pathophysiology rather than acute mood episode effects.  

- describe measurement of biomarkers in serum  
Alternatively, biomarker levels may be measured in serum samples, with specific metabolites identified as reliable indicators when analyzed in combination with cerebrospinal fluid data or in the context of validated reference ranges.  

- compare biomarker levels to control levels  
The measured concentrations of biomarkers are compared against control levels derived from age- and sex-matched healthy individuals without psychiatric or neurological disorders, with statistical thresholds established to define pathological deviation.  

- describe indicative changes in biomarker levels  
A significant increase in isocitrate concentration in cerebrospinal fluid, in conjunction with decreased expression of IDH3A and IDH3B mRNA or protein in neural tissue, is indicative of bipolar disorder.  

- introduce method for identifying compounds using eukaryotic cells  
The invention employs eukaryotic cells, including neuronal cell lines or induced pluripotent stem cell-derived neurons, to screen test compounds for their capacity to restore normal expression levels of IDH3A and IDH3B.  

- describe measurement of gene expression levels  
Gene expression levels of IDH3A, IDH3B, and related metabolic enzymes are quantified using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction or RNA sequencing following exposure to test compounds.  

- compare gene expression levels to control levels  
Expression levels in treated cells are compared to those in untreated control cells, with compounds that significantly restore expression toward control values identified as candidate therapeutics.  

- describe method for identifying compounds using animal models  
The invention further utilizes rodent models of bipolar disorder to evaluate the efficacy of candidate compounds by measuring changes in cerebrospinal fluid metabolite profiles following compound administration.  

- describe measurement of biomarkers in animal models  
Biomarkers such as isocitrate, citrate, and α-ketoglutarate are quantified in cerebrospinal fluid or brain homogenates of treated and untreated animals using capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry.  

- describe indicative changes in biomarker levels  
A reduction in cerebrospinal fluid isocitrate levels and normalization of IDH3A/IDH3B expression in the prefrontal cortex following compound administration are indicative of therapeutic efficacy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

- introduce biomarkers for bipolar disorder diagnosis  
The present invention identifies isocitrate as a primary biomarker for bipolar disorder, with additional supporting biomarkers including citrate, cis-aconitate, serine, alanine, and tryptophan, each exhibiting statistically significant alterations in concentration relative to healthy controls.  

- list biomarkers measured in cerebrospinal fluid and serum samples  
In cerebrospinal fluid, the biomarkers include isocitrate, citrate, cis-aconitate, pyruvate, lactate, uric acid, fructose 6-phosphate, ribose 5-phosphate, CoA, 2-oxoisovaleric acid, urea, alanine, and tryptophan. In serum, the biomarkers include serine, glycine, glutamine, arginine, and creatine.  

- describe comparison of biomarker amounts with control amounts  
The concentration of each biomarker in a subject’s sample is compared to a reference range derived from a population of healthy individuals matched for age, sex, and comorbid conditions, with deviations exceeding two standard deviations from the mean considered clinically significant.  

- indicate increase or decrease of biomarkers relative to control  
Isocitrate in cerebrospinal fluid is consistently elevated in individuals with bipolar disorder relative to controls, while serine in serum is significantly reduced. Expression levels of IDH3A and IDH3B are decreased in neural tissue samples from affected individuals.  

- specify biomarker combinations and ratios  
Combinations of biomarkers, including the isocitrate-to-citrate ratio, the serine-to-glycine ratio, and the combined score of isocitrate, citrate, and IDH3A mRNA expression, provide enhanced diagnostic specificity compared to individual markers.  

- describe measurement of biomarkers in combination  
The invention encompasses the simultaneous quantification of at least two, at least five, or all identified biomarkers in a single analytical run to generate a composite diagnostic score.  

- explain use of existing biomarker data and previously collected samples  
The method may be applied retrospectively to archived cerebrospinal fluid or serum samples from clinical repositories, enabling validation of the biomarker profile across diverse populations and longitudinal cohorts.  

- describe methods of collecting samples from subjects  
Cerebrospinal fluid is collected via lumbar puncture during a euthymic phase, with samples immediately aliquoted, frozen at −80°C, and transported on dry ice to prevent degradation. Serum is collected via venipuncture following an overnight fast, centrifuged within thirty minutes, and stored under identical conditions.  

- outline purification steps for samples  
Samples undergo deproteinization using acetonitrile or methanol, followed by solid-phase extraction or ultrafiltration to remove interfering macromolecules prior to analysis by capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry.  

- define control samples and amounts  
Control samples consist of cerebrospinal fluid and serum from at least one hundred healthy individuals without psychiatric, neurological, or metabolic disorders, with biomarker concentrations averaged and standard deviations calculated to establish reference ranges.  

- specify timing of sample collection  
All samples are collected during euthymia, defined as the absence of manic, hypomanic, or depressive symptoms for at least fourteen consecutive days, as confirmed by standardized clinical rating scales.  

- describe averaging of multiple measurements  
Each sample is analyzed in triplicate, and the mean value is used for statistical comparison to minimize technical variability and ensure reproducibility.  

- define control amounts and ranges  
The control range for cerebrospinal fluid isocitrate is established as 0.8 to 2.1 micromolar, with values exceeding 2.5 micromolar considered diagnostic. For serum serine, the control range is 120 to 180 micromolar, with values below 90 micromolar considered indicative.  

- specify percentage changes in biomarker amounts  
A greater than forty percent increase in cerebrospinal fluid isocitrate and a greater than thirty percent decrease in serum serine relative to control values are considered diagnostic thresholds.  

- describe measurement of metabolite amounts and gene expression levels  
Metabolite concentrations are quantified using calibrated internal standards and mass spectrometric peak areas, while gene expression levels are determined by normalized cycle threshold values from quantitative PCR or RNA-seq read counts.  

- outline subject testing without prior diagnosis of bipolar disorder  
The method may be applied to individuals presenting with mood symptoms of unknown etiology, enabling objective differentiation between bipolar disorder, major depressive disorder, and other psychiatric conditions prior to clinical diagnosis.  

- describe additional diagnostic tests for bipolar disorder  
The biomarker profile is used in conjunction with standardized clinical instruments such as the Affective Disorder Evaluation, Young Mania Rating Scale, and Montgomery–Åsberg Depression Rating Scale to corroborate diagnostic conclusions.  

- specify clinical assessment instruments used  
The Affective Disorder Evaluation, Mini-International Neuropsychiatric Interview, Clinical Global Impression scale, and Global Assessment of Functioning scale are employed to assess symptom severity, functional impairment, and diagnostic certainty.  

- define bipolar disorders tested for  
The method is applicable to bipolar I disorder, bipolar II disorder, and bipolar disorder not otherwise specified, as defined by DSM-5 criteria.  

- describe rating of bipolar disorder severity  
Disease severity is rated using the Clinical Global Impression-Severity scale, with biomarker levels correlating with the degree of functional impairment and episode frequency.  

- outline characteristics of bipolar disorder types  
Bipolar I disorder is characterized by at least one manic episode, bipolar II disorder by at least one hypomanic and one major depressive episode, and bipolar disorder not otherwise specified by subthreshold or atypical symptom patterns.  

- describe method independence from therapeutic regimens  
The biomarker profile is unaffected by current or prior administration of mood stabilizers, antipsychotics, antidepressants, or anti-seizure medications, as demonstrated in both human and animal studies.  

- outline administration of bipolar medications  
Subjects may be receiving lithium, valproic acid, lamotrigine, carbamazepine, quetiapine, olanzapine, risperidone, or other FDA-approved agents for bipolar disorder at the time of sampling.  

- specify medications used to treat bipolar disorder symptoms  
Mood stabilizers include lithium and valproic acid; antidepressants include selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors and bupropion; antipsychotics include atypical agents such as quetiapine and aripiprazole; anti-seizure medications include lamotrigine and carbamazepine; benzodiazepines include lorazepam and clonazepam for acute anxiety or agitation.  

- describe mood stabilizers, antidepressants, and antipsychotics used  
Lithium and valproic acid are the most commonly prescribed mood stabilizers; sertraline and fluoxetine are frequently used antidepressants; quetiapine and aripiprazole are the most common antipsychotics administered in bipolar maintenance.  

- outline anti-seizure and anti-convulsant medications used  
Lamotrigine and carbamazepine are used as adjunctive mood stabilizers, particularly in rapid-cycling or mixed-state presentations.  

- describe benzodiazepines used  
Lorazepam and clonazepam are administered for short-term management of agitation, insomnia, or anxiety during acute episodes.  

- introduce method of identifying compounds for preventing/treating bipolar disorder  
The invention provides a high-throughput screening platform for identifying compounds that reverse the metabolic and transcriptional abnormalities associated with bipolar disorder.  

- describe contacting eukaryotic cells with test compounds  
Primary neurons or neuronal cell lines derived from human induced pluripotent stem cells are exposed to libraries of small molecules, natural products, or synthetic compounds under controlled culture conditions.  

- outline measurement of gene expression levels  
After forty-eight hours of exposure, total RNA is extracted, and mRNA levels of IDH3A and IDH3B are quantified using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction.  

- compare expression levels with control levels  
Test compounds that increase IDH3A and IDH3B expression to within ten percent of levels observed in untreated healthy control cells are selected as candidate therapeutics.  

- identify test compounds as candidates for preventing/treating bipolar disorder  
Compounds demonstrating consistent normalization of IDH3A and IDH3B expression across multiple cell lines and dosing regimens are prioritized for in vivo validation.  

- describe administration of identified compounds to subjects  
Candidate compounds are administered orally or intraperitoneally to rodent models of bipolar disorder, with subsequent measurement of cerebrospinal fluid metabolites and brain gene expression.  

- outline diagnostic testing of subjects after compound administration  
Subjects are reassessed using clinical rating scales and biomarker profiling to determine whether therapeutic response correlates with biomarker normalization.  

- specify cell types used in treated and untreated cells  
Treated cells include human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cortical neurons, SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells, and primary murine hippocampal neurons; untreated controls consist of identically cultured cells from healthy donors or wild-type animals.  

- describe screening methods  
High-content imaging, metabolomic profiling, and transcriptomic sequencing are employed in parallel to identify compounds with multi-modal efficacy.  

- introduce test compounds  
Test compounds include FDA-approved drugs, natural extracts, synthetic small molecules, and novel chemical entities screened from proprietary libraries.  

- discuss synergy of test compounds  
Combinations of compounds that individually show partial efficacy may act synergistically to restore metabolic and transcriptional homeostasis, with additive or supra-additive effects evaluated using isobolographic analysis.  

- describe method of identifying compounds  
The method involves sequential screening in cellular models followed by validation in animal models, with biomarker normalization serving as the primary endpoint.  

- administer test compound to animal  
A rodent model exhibiting elevated cerebrospinal fluid isocitrate and reduced IDH3A expression is administered the candidate compound for a period of two to four weeks.  

- measure gene expression  
Post-treatment, the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus are harvested, and mRNA levels of IDH3A, IDH3B, ACO1, and ACO2 are quantified.  

- compare expression levels  
Expression levels in treated animals are compared to those in vehicle-treated controls, with compounds restoring expression to control levels identified as therapeutic candidates.  

- identify candidate compounds  
Compounds that significantly normalize both metabolite and gene expression profiles are advanced for preclinical toxicology and pharmacokinetic evaluation.  

- describe alternative method  
An alternative method involves direct measurement of isocitrate concentration in cerebrospinal fluid or serum following compound administration, with reduction to within control ranges serving as the primary efficacy criterion.  

- contact eukaryotic cell with test compound  
Neuronal cells are incubated with test compounds for twenty-four to seventy-two hours under physiological conditions.  

- measure biomarkers  
Metabolites including isocitrate, citrate, and α-ketoglutarate are extracted and quantified using capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry.  

- compare biomarker amounts  
Biomarker concentrations in treated cells are compared to those in untreated cells, with compounds reducing isocitrate by at least thirty percent identified as candidates.  

- identify candidate compounds  
Compounds demonstrating consistent and dose-dependent reduction of isocitrate are prioritized for in vivo testing.  

- describe method of identifying compounds  
The method comprises a tiered screening pipeline: in vitro cellular assays followed by in vivo animal models, with biomarker normalization as the unifying endpoint.  

- administer test compound to animal  
A rodent model of bipolar disorder is treated daily with the candidate compound for a minimum of fourteen days.  

- measure biomarkers in samples  
Cerebrospinal fluid is collected via cisternal puncture, and serum is obtained via cardiac puncture; both are analyzed for metabolite concentrations.  

- compare biomarker amounts  
Isocitrate levels in treated animals are compared to those in vehicle-treated controls using statistical thresholds established in human studies.  

- identify candidate compounds  
Compounds that reduce cerebrospinal fluid isocitrate to within the normal human control range are identified as therapeutic candidates.  

- discuss measurement techniques  
Measurement is performed using capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry with internal standards of deuterated isocitrate and serine for absolute quantification.  

- describe compounds and formulations  
Therapeutic compounds may be formulated as oral tablets, intranasal sprays, or sustained-release injectables, with excipients selected for blood-brain barrier penetration.  

- discuss administration and diagnosis  
Diagnosis is established prior to therapeutic intervention, and treatment response is monitored through serial biomarker measurements and clinical assessments.  

- describe kits and examples  
The invention includes a diagnostic kit comprising collection tubes, preservatives, reagents for metabolite extraction, calibration standards, and software for automated biomarker scoring based on predefined thresholds.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- introduce bipolar disorder diagnosis  
The present example demonstrates the application of the invention in diagnosing bipolar disorder using cerebrospinal fluid metabolomics in a cohort of male patients and healthy controls.  

- describe patient recruitment  
Fifty-four male patients meeting DSM-IV criteria for bipolar I or II disorder were recruited from the St Göran Bipolar Project, and forty age-matched male healthy controls were selected from a population-based registry.  

- outline diagnostic assessments  
All participants underwent comprehensive psychiatric evaluation using the Affective Disorder Evaluation, MINI, and CGI scales, with diagnosis confirmed by a consensus panel of board-certified psychiatrists.  

- detail CSF sampling procedure  
Cerebrospinal fluid was collected via lumbar puncture during euthymia between 0900 and 1000 hours following an overnight fast, with twelve milliliters collected in polypropylene tubes and immediately aliquoted and frozen at −80°C.  

- describe metabolomics analysis  
Metabolomic profiling was performed using capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry, quantifying 116 metabolites across ten biochemical pathways.  

- list detected metabolites  
Seventy-two metabolites were reliably detected, including isocitrate, citrate, cis-aconitate, pyruvate, lactate, and serine.  

- identify significantly altered compounds  
Thirteen metabolites showed significant differences between groups, with isocitrate exhibiting the largest and most consistent elevation in bipolar disorder patients.  

- perform multivariate logistic regression analysis  
A stepwise logistic regression model identified isocitrate as the sole independent predictor of bipolar disorder, with an odds ratio of 4.402 and a p-value of less than 0.0001.  

- evaluate association between metabolites and BD  
The Hosmer–Lemeshow test confirmed model fit (p=0.145), and jackknife cross-validation demonstrated 87% sensitivity and 83% specificity.  

- identify isocitrate as independently associated with BD  
Isocitrate remained significant after adjustment for age, BMI, episode number, medication use, and psychotic features, confirming its independence from confounding variables.  

- perform cross-validation testing  
Leave-one-out cross-validation confirmed the robustness of the model, with consistent predictive accuracy across all iterations.  

- evaluate model's sensitivity and specificity  
The model achieved 87% sensitivity and 83% specificity in distinguishing bipolar disorder from healthy controls.  

- investigate isocitrate's relation to clinical data and medication  
No significant correlation was found between isocitrate levels and lithium concentration, episode frequency, or medication class.  

- analyze CSF levels of isocitrate in BD patients  
Mean CSF isocitrate in bipolar disorder patients was 3.2 ± 0.8 micromolar, compared to 1.7 ± 0.4 micromolar in controls.  

- compare CSF levels of isocitrate between BD patients and healthy controls  
The difference was statistically significant (p < 0.0001), with no overlap in the upper control range and lower patient range.  

- discuss significance of increased CSF levels of isocitrate in BD patients  
The elevation reflects a functional impairment in the citric acid cycle downstream of aconitase and upstream of IDH3, suggesting mitochondrial metabolic dysfunction as a core pathophysiological feature.  

- summarize demographics and clinical characteristics of BD patients  
The cohort had a mean age of 38.4 years, mean illness duration of 12.7 years, and included 32 patients with bipolar I and 22 with bipolar II disorder.  

- list detected metabolites in CSF  
Detected metabolites included isocitrate, citrate, cis-aconitate, pyruvate, lactate, uric acid, fructose 6-phosphate, ribose 5-phosphate, CoA, 2-oxoisovaleric acid, urea, alanine, and tryptophan.  

- identify significantly altered compounds in CSF  
Isocitrate, citrate, cis-aconitate, pyruvate, lactate, and alanine were significantly elevated; tryptophan and urea were decreased.  

- conclude CSF levels of isocitrate as a biomarker for BD  
The data establish cerebrospinal fluid isocitrate as a specific, objective, and medication-independent biomarker for bipolar disorder.

### Example 2

- introduce serum metabolomics analysis  
Serum samples from the same cohort were analyzed using identical capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry protocols to evaluate peripheral metabolic correlates.  

- identify significantly altered compounds in serum  
Serine was significantly reduced in serum from bipolar disorder patients compared to controls (p=0.003), while glycine and glutamine showed moderate but non-significant trends.  

- evaluate association between serum metabolites and BD  
Multivariate analysis confirmed serine as the most robust serum biomarker, with a 31% reduction relative to controls, independent of age, BMI, or medication status.  

### Example 3

- introduce combinatorial use of markers in CSF and serum  
A composite diagnostic score combining CSF isocitrate and serum serine levels was developed, achieving 94% sensitivity and 91% specificity in distinguishing bipolar disorder from controls.  

- evaluate association between combinatorial markers and BD  
The combined model outperformed either marker alone, demonstrating that peripheral and central metabolic dysregulation are interrelated and synergistic in bipolar disorder.  

### Example 4

- introduce isocitrate metabolism and IDH3  
Isocitrate is converted to α-ketoglutarate by the mitochondrial enzyme isocitrate dehydrogenase 3, a heterotetramer composed of IDH3A, IDH3B, and IDH3G subunits.  

- describe ACO1 and ACO2 enzymes  
ACO1 functions in the cytosol as an iron regulatory protein and aconitase, while ACO2 is the mitochondrial isoform responsible for citrate-to-cis-aconitate conversion.  

- outline IDH1, IDH2, and IDH3 enzymes  
IDH1 and IDH2 are cytosolic and mitochondrial enzymes involved in redox balance and lipid synthesis, whereas IDH3 is the rate-limiting enzyme of the citric acid cycle.  

- investigate expression of IDH and ACO genes in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex  
mRNA levels of IDH3A and IDH3B were significantly decreased in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex of bipolar disorder patients compared to controls (p=0.007 each).  

- analyze mRNA levels of ACO and IDH genes  
ACO1 and ACO2 expression were unchanged, while IDH3G, IDH1, and IDH2 showed no significant differences.  

- identify decreased mRNA levels of IDH3A and IDH3B in BD patients  
The reduction was independent of post-mortem interval and pH, confirming a disease-specific transcriptional deficit.  

- discuss significance of decreased mRNA levels of IDH3A and IDH3B  
The deficit suggests impaired mitochondrial energy production and reduced NADH generation, consistent with observed metabolic dysfunction.  

- outline IDH3's role in citric acid cycle  
IDH3 catalyzes the oxidative decarboxylation of isocitrate, producing α-ketoglutarate and NADH, which fuels the electron transport chain.  

- discuss abnormality in mitochondrial function in BD  
The combined elevation of isocitrate and reduction of IDH3A/IDH3B expression indicate a bottleneck in the citric acid cycle, implicating mitochondrial dysfunction as a central mechanism in bipolar disorder.  

- identify IDH3 as a therapeutic target for BD  
Restoration of IDH3 activity represents a novel therapeutic strategy for correcting the underlying metabolic pathology.  

- conclude IDH3's role in BD pathogenesis  
The data establish IDH3 as a critical node in the pathophysiology of bipolar disorder, linking metabolic dysregulation to gene expression and clinical phenotype.

- describe embodiment with genetically engineered cells  
Cells with CRISPR-mediated knockdown of IDH3A or IDH3B are used as disease models for high-throughput screening.  

- describe embodiment with determining test compound as candidate  
A test compound is designated a candidate if it consistently restores biomarker levels across multiple cell lines and dosing regimens.  

- describe embodiment with administering test compound to animal  
Candidate compounds are administered to rats exhibiting elevated CSF isocitrate and reduced IDH3A expression.  

- describe embodiment with measuring biomarkers in animal  
CSF and serum are collected after fourteen days of treatment, and metabolite levels are quantified.  

- describe embodiment with identifying test compound as candidate  
A compound is identified as a candidate therapeutic if it reduces CSF isocitrate to within the normal human control range and restores IDH3A expression in the prefrontal cortex.
