# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- define technical field of biomarkers for lymphoma

The present invention relates to the field of diagnostic biomarkers for lymphoma, particularly for the early detection of Hodgkin’s lymphoma in individuals infected with human immunodeficiency virus. More specifically, the invention encompasses a panel of plasma-derived protein biomarkers that are differentially expressed in the circulation of HIV-positive individuals with Hodgkin’s lymphoma as compared to those without the disease. These biomarkers enable the non-invasive detection of lymphoma at an early, clinically actionable stage, prior to the onset of overt symptoms such as lymphadenopathy, fever, night sweats, or weight loss. The invention further pertains to methods and systems for quantifying these biomarkers in biological fluids, particularly plasma, using mass spectrometry-based proteomic platforms. The utility of these biomarkers lies in their ability to reflect systemic inflammatory and neoplastic processes associated with Hodgkin’s lymphoma, thereby providing a molecular signature that is distinct from the underlying HIV infection itself. This technical field addresses a critical unmet clinical need in the management of immunocompromised populations, where traditional diagnostic modalities such as imaging and tissue biopsy are often delayed, invasive, or insufficiently sensitive for early-stage disease detection.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce lymphoma and its relation to HIV

Hodgkin’s lymphoma is a malignant disorder of the lymphatic system characterized by the presence of rare neoplastic Reed-Sternberg cells embedded within a dense infiltrate of non-neoplastic inflammatory cells, including T lymphocytes, macrophages, eosinophils, and plasma cells. In individuals infected with human immunodeficiency virus, the risk of developing Hodgkin’s lymphoma is elevated by more than fivefold relative to the general population, a phenomenon attributed to chronic immune dysregulation, persistent viral co-infections such as Epstein-Barr virus, and impaired immune surveillance. Despite advances in antiretroviral therapy that have significantly prolonged survival among HIV-positive individuals, the incidence of Hodgkin’s lymphoma continues to rise, particularly in those with suboptimal immune reconstitution. The clinical presentation of Hodgkin’s lymphoma in this population often overlaps with manifestations of opportunistic infections or other HIV-related complications, leading to diagnostic delays and increased morbidity. The tumor microenvironment in Hodgkin’s lymphoma is dominated by cytokine-secreting non-malignant cells, which release a complex array of soluble mediators into the systemic circulation, creating a detectable proteomic signature that precedes anatomical changes visible on imaging.

- describe limitations of current lymphoma detection methods

Current methods for detecting Hodgkin’s lymphoma rely heavily on physical examination, radiographic imaging such as positron emission tomography and computed tomography, and ultimately, invasive tissue biopsy for histopathological confirmation. These approaches are inherently limited by their inability to detect disease at the earliest molecular stages, their high cost, their exposure to ionizing radiation, and their inaccessibility in resource-limited settings. Furthermore, in HIV-positive individuals, the presence of generalized lymphadenopathy due to chronic immune activation or opportunistic infections frequently confounds imaging interpretations, leading to false positives and unnecessary biopsies. Serological markers such as lactate dehydrogenase and beta-2-microglobulin are occasionally used but lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity for routine screening, particularly in the context of concurrent HIV infection where baseline levels are often elevated. There is no validated blood-based test capable of distinguishing early-stage Hodgkin’s lymphoma from benign inflammatory conditions in this high-risk population.

- highlight need for early detection of lymphoma

Early detection of Hodgkin’s lymphoma is critically important because it allows for the initiation of less intensive, less toxic therapeutic regimens, which is especially advantageous in HIV-positive patients who are already burdened by the metabolic and immunological demands of chronic viral infection and antiretroviral therapy. Delayed diagnosis often results in advanced-stage disease requiring aggressive chemotherapy and radiation, which are associated with increased rates of treatment-related toxicity, secondary malignancies, and mortality. A non-invasive, blood-based diagnostic tool capable of identifying molecular signatures of lymphoma before clinical symptoms manifest would transform the standard of care by enabling proactive surveillance, reducing diagnostic uncertainty, and improving long-term survival outcomes.

- motivate development of blood-based test for lymphoma

The development of a blood-based test for Hodgkin’s lymphoma in HIV-infected individuals is therefore urgently needed to address the limitations of existing diagnostic paradigms. Such a test must be capable of detecting disease-specific protein alterations in plasma that are independent of HIV viral load or CD4 count, and must be amenable to high-throughput, standardized, and reproducible analytical platforms. The discovery of a panel of plasma proteins that are consistently and significantly altered in the presence of Hodgkin’s lymphoma provides the foundation for such a diagnostic tool, offering a minimally invasive, scalable, and cost-effective approach to early detection that can be integrated into routine clinical monitoring of high-risk patients.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce biomarkers for detecting lymphoma

The invention provides a novel panel of sixty plasma proteins that are differentially expressed in individuals with Hodgkin’s lymphoma who are infected with human immunodeficiency virus, as compared to HIV-infected individuals without lymphoma. These proteins are not merely markers of immune activation or general inflammation, but are specifically associated with the biological processes underlying lymphomagenesis, including cytokine signaling, extracellular matrix remodeling, acute-phase response, and leukocyte trafficking. The panel includes both previously recognized biomarkers such as C-reactive protein, beta-2-microglobulin, and lactate dehydrogenase, as well as fifty-seven novel proteins whose association with Hodgkin’s lymphoma has not been previously described. The collective expression pattern of these proteins forms a unique molecular fingerprint that distinguishes lymphoma from non-lymphoma states with high statistical significance.

- describe method of detecting lymphoma using biomarkers

The invention further provides a method for detecting Hodgkin’s lymphoma in an HIV-infected individual by measuring the concentration or presence of the sixty biomarker proteins in a plasma sample obtained from the individual. The method involves the use of liquid chromatography coupled with high-resolution mass spectrometry to quantify the abundance of each biomarker peptide, followed by computational normalization and statistical analysis to determine whether the expression profile matches a predefined signature associated with Hodgkin’s lymphoma. The method does not require prior knowledge of clinical symptoms or imaging findings and can be performed on a routine blood draw, enabling its use in screening and longitudinal monitoring.

- outline test kit for diagnosing lymphoma

The invention further encompasses a diagnostic test kit for detecting Hodgkin’s lymphoma, comprising a set of standardized reagents for the isolation and processing of plasma from whole blood, a panel of reference peptides corresponding to the sixty biomarker proteins, a calibrated mass spectrometry platform configured for accurate mass tag detection, and a software module programmed to compare the measured peptide intensities against a validated diagnostic algorithm. The kit further includes a user manual detailing sample collection protocols, data interpretation guidelines, and thresholds for positive versus negative classification based on the combined biomarker signature.

- summarize diagnostic system for detecting lymphoma

The invention further provides a diagnostic system for detecting Hodgkin’s lymphoma, comprising a sample preparation module, a liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry instrument, a database of reference biomarker profiles, and a decision-support algorithm that integrates normalized protein abundance values to generate a binary diagnostic output. The system is designed for use in clinical laboratories and is capable of processing multiple samples simultaneously, with results generated within twenty-four hours of sample receipt. The system is validated for use in HIV-positive populations and is not confounded by common co-morbidities or antiretroviral therapy regimens.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

- introduce methods and systems for detecting lymphoma

The methods and systems of the invention are designed to detect Hodgkin’s lymphoma through the quantitative analysis of plasma proteins that are differentially expressed in the presence of the disease. The approach is grounded in the discovery that the systemic inflammatory milieu associated with Hodgkin’s lymphoma leads to the release of a distinct set of proteins into the circulation, many of which originate from the tumor-associated leukocyte infiltrate rather than the rare malignant Reed-Sternberg cells themselves. These proteins are detectable in plasma using high-sensitivity proteomic platforms, and their collective abundance pattern provides a robust diagnostic signal that is independent of traditional clinical parameters. The invention enables the detection of lymphoma even in the absence of palpable lymphadenopathy or other overt symptoms, thereby facilitating early intervention.

### Materials and Methods

- describe ethics statement

The study protocol was approved by the George Washington University Medical Center Institutional Review Board, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to sample collection. Additional ethical review and approval were obtained from the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory Institutional Review Board prior to the transfer and analysis of biological specimens. All procedures adhered to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and applicable federal regulations governing human subjects research.

- outline human subjects and sample processing

Frozen plasma samples were obtained from the AIDS and Cancer Specimen Resource and were selected to include thirty-six individuals infected with HIV, twelve of whom had a confirmed diagnosis of Hodgkin’s lymphoma and twenty-four of whom had no evidence of lymphoma. Cases and controls were matched for age and sex to minimize confounding variables. Plasma samples from lymphoma patients were collected at least thirty days after the completion of chemotherapy, except in three cases where treatment history was unknown. All samples were processed under blinded conditions to prevent bias.

- detail plasma sample preparation

The twelve most abundant plasma proteins were removed using an immunodepletion resin, followed by protein precipitation with trichloroacetic acid. Pellets were washed with cold acetone, solubilized in a denaturing buffer containing urea and thiourea, reduced with dithiothreitol, and digested overnight with sequencing-grade trypsin. Peptides were purified using solid-phase extraction and concentrated prior to mass spectrometry analysis.

- describe liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry

Peptide samples were analyzed using a custom nanocapillary liquid chromatography system coupled to an LTQ-Orbitrap mass spectrometer. A 65-cm reversed-phase C18 column was employed with a binary gradient of aqueous and organic solvents, and peptides were eluted over 115 minutes. Electrospray ionization was performed at 2.2 kV with a capillary temperature of 200°C. High-resolution mass spectra were acquired over a mass-to-charge range of 400 to 2000 with a resolution of 100,000.

- outline data analysis using AMT tag approach

Peptide identifications were performed using the accurate mass and time tag approach, matching experimental spectra against a reference database of 30,573 previously characterized human plasma peptides. The false discovery rate was estimated at less than six percent using a reversed-sequence database search strategy.

- describe statistics and data normalization

Raw signal intensities were normalized across all mass spectrometry runs using regression-based scaling, followed by median absolute deviation normalization per sample. Replicate analyses were averaged to improve precision, and missing values were imputed based on the lowest observed intensity.

- perform non-parametric Mann-Whitney test

A non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test was applied to compare protein abundance between lymphoma and non-lymphoma groups, with significance set at p ≤ 0.05.

- perform modified Chi-square test

A modified Chi-square test, known as the G-test, was used to assess differences in the presence or absence of peptides between groups, accounting for detection frequency rather than intensity alone.

- analyze protein inference

Protein abundance was inferred from aggregated peptide signals using RRollup within DAnTE, with weighting based on peptide uniqueness and reliability. Only proteins represented by two or more unique peptides were retained for downstream analysis.

- describe Ingenuity Pathway Analysis

Differentially expressed proteins were subjected to Ingenuity Pathway Analysis to identify enriched biological functions, pathways, and upstream regulators. Significance was determined using a right-tailed Fisher’s Exact Test with a threshold of p ≤ 0.05.

- outline results of proteomics analysis

A total of 3,814 peptides were identified across all samples, corresponding to 629 proteins. Of these, 647 peptides exhibited statistically significant differential expression between lymphoma and non-lymphoma groups, representing 152 proteins. Sixty proteins met the additional criterion of a 1.5-fold or greater change in normalized abundance.

- discuss biological functions of identified proteins

The sixty validated biomarkers are predominantly involved in inflammatory response, acute-phase reaction, complement activation, and cancer-related signaling pathways. Many are secreted by activated leukocytes or hepatocytes in response to tumor-derived cytokines, suggesting a systemic biological response rather than direct tumor secretion.

### Results and Discussion

- summarize results of proteomics analysis

The proteomic analysis revealed a distinct plasma protein signature associated with Hodgkin’s lymphoma in HIV-infected individuals, comprising sixty proteins that were significantly upregulated or downregulated compared to HIV-positive controls without lymphoma. This signature was robust across replicate analyses and remained significant after rigorous normalization and statistical correction.

- describe identified proteins and their functions

The identified proteins include known biomarkers such as C-reactive protein, beta-2-microglobulin, and lactate dehydrogenase, as well as novel candidates including apolipoprotein L1, haptoglobin-related protein, and serum amyloid A4. Many are involved in immune cell recruitment, oxidative stress response, and extracellular matrix degradation.

- discuss inflammatory response and cancer

The enrichment of inflammatory pathways among the biomarkers supports the hypothesis that Hodgkin’s lymphoma is driven by a chronic inflammatory microenvironment, and that the plasma proteome reflects this systemic state rather than the tumor mass itself.

- validate previously reported biomarkers

The detection of three previously established biomarkers confirms the validity of the analytical platform and reinforces the biological relevance of the observed changes.

- discuss potential confounding factors

Although HIV disease progression was slightly more advanced in the lymphoma group, the persistence of the biomarker signature after adjustment for CD4 count and viral load indicates that the findings are specific to lymphoma rather than HIV severity.

- highlight novel plasma proteins as candidate biomarkers

Fifty-seven of the identified proteins have not been previously associated with Hodgkin’s lymphoma and represent promising candidates for further validation in independent cohorts.

- conclude results and discussion

The discovery of this sixty-protein panel provides a foundation for the development of a blood-based diagnostic test for early detection of Hodgkin’s lymphoma in HIV-infected individuals, offering a transformative tool for clinical surveillance and improved patient outcomes.
