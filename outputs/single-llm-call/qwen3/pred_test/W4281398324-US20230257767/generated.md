# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates to the field of biopharmaceutical production, specifically to improved methods for the high-yield expression of recombinant proteins in mammalian cell expression systems. More particularly, the invention concerns the use of novel promoter sequences derived from the Hspa5 gene to drive sustained and elevated transcription of foreign genes during extended fed-batch culture conditions, thereby enhancing the productivity of therapeutic proteins such as monoclonal antibodies. The invention finds particular application in the manufacturing of biologics using Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells and other mammalian host systems where long-term transcriptional stability and high specific productivity are critical for commercial-scale production.

## BACKGROUND ART

- Protein-based pharmaceuticals, including monoclonal antibodies, fusion proteins, and enzyme replacements, have become indispensable in modern medicine due to their high specificity, low off-target toxicity, and potent biological activity. The global market for such therapeutics continues to expand, necessitating robust, scalable, and cost-effective production platforms. Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells remain the dominant host system for the industrial manufacture of these biologics, owing to their capacity for complex post-translational modifications, adherence to regulatory standards, and compatibility with serum-free suspension culture. Despite decades of optimization, a persistent challenge in CHO-based production systems is the decline in recombinant protein expression during the late phases of fed-batch culture, often resulting in suboptimal titers and increased manufacturing costs. To address this, numerous strategies have been employed, including genomic integration site selection, copy number amplification, and the use of enhancer elements such as introns and matrix attachment regions. However, the most direct and widely adopted approach involves the selection of strong transcriptional promoters to initiate and sustain gene expression. Historically, viral promoters such as those derived from human cytomegalovirus (hCMV) and simian virus 40 (SV40) have been favored for their high initial activity, but they are often subject to transcriptional silencing over time. Similarly, cellular promoters like human elongation factor-1α (hEF1α), human ubiquitin C (UBC), and mouse phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK) have been utilized, yet their activity frequently diminishes as culture progresses into the stationary phase. While some efforts have sought to engineer synthetic promoters by combining elements from multiple sources, these often lack consistency across different cell lines or product formats. Furthermore, the use of inducible systems introduces complexity in process control and regulatory compliance. Critically, no promoter system has been identified that maintains or enhances transcriptional output throughout the entire duration of a typical fed-batch cycle, particularly during the critical late-phase production window where cell viability remains high but expression levels plateau or decline. This limitation has hindered the ability to maximize volumetric productivity without increasing bioreactor volume or culture duration, both of which elevate capital and operational expenditures.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

- The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of a promoter system capable of sustaining high-level transcription of foreign genes throughout the extended duration of fed-batch mammalian cell culture, particularly during the late growth phase when recombinant protein yields are most economically valuable. Existing promoters, whether viral or cellular, exhibit a consistent trend of transcriptional attenuation after the exponential growth phase, leading to a mismatch between cell viability and product synthesis. This results in suboptimal specific productivity rates and reduced final titers, thereby increasing the cost of goods for life-saving biopharmaceuticals. There exists a critical unmet need for a promoter that is not only strong in initiation but also inherently resilient to the metabolic and transcriptional changes that occur during prolonged culture, enabling continuous and elevated protein production without requiring genetic or environmental manipulation beyond standard process conditions.

### Solution to Problem

- The present invention provides a solution by identifying and utilizing the promoter region of the Hspa5 gene, which encodes the endoplasmic reticulum chaperone heat shock protein 70 kDa 5, also known as BiP or GRP78. The invention discloses a polynucleotide sequence corresponding to the human-derived Hspa5 gene promoter, designated as SEQ ID NO: 1, which comprises the genomic region extending from approximately 600 base pairs upstream of the transcription start site to the transcription start site itself. This sequence is shown to drive sustained and elevated expression of foreign genes during the late phase of fed-batch culture in mammalian cells. The invention further encompasses polynucleotides of SEQ ID NO: 5, SEQ ID NO: 6, SEQ ID NO: 7, SEQ ID NO: 8, and SEQ ID NO: 9, each representing truncated, mutated, or species-variant forms of the Hspa5 promoter that retain or enhance promoter activity under culture conditions. The human-derived Hspa5 promoter sequence is characterized by conserved cis-regulatory elements including a TATA-box, a CpG island, a CCAAT-box, a GC-box, cAMP-responsive elements, and endoplasmic reticulum stress-response elements, which collectively confer transcriptional robustness under conditions of protein folding stress. The invention also includes orthologous promoter sequences derived from mouse and rat Hspa5 genes, which demonstrate comparable activity in CHO cells, indicating functional conservation across species. Furthermore, the invention encompasses polynucleotides that share at least 85% sequence identity with SEQ ID NO: 1, provided that such variants retain the ability to enhance foreign gene expression during extended culture periods, as determined by standard reporter assays and fed-batch productivity metrics.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

- The advantageous effects of the invention include a significant and sustained increase in the specific productivity rate of recombinant proteins during the late phase of fed-batch culture, resulting in higher final titers without compromising cell growth or product quality. The Hspa5 promoter exhibits a unique temporal expression profile that aligns with the physiological state of high-producing cell lines, where endoplasmic reticulum stress induced by recombinant protein accumulation activates the unfolded protein response, thereby upregulating the endogenous Hspa5 promoter. This self-amplifying mechanism ensures that transcriptional activity increases precisely when it is most needed, creating a feedback loop that enhances protein output without exogenous induction. The promoter is compatible with a broad range of recombinant proteins, including IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4 subclasses, and functions effectively across multiple CHO cell lines and culture media formulations. Unlike viral promoters, the Hspa5 promoter does not undergo silencing over time, and its activity is not diminished by prolonged culture or high cell densities. The minimal functional region of the promoter, approximately 600 base pairs in length, is compact and suitable for vector design, enabling efficient cloning and high transfection efficiency. The invention thus provides a commercially viable, scalable, and broadly applicable solution to the longstanding problem of declining productivity in mammalian cell manufacturing systems.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

- A gene is defined as a functional unit of heredity comprising a sequence of nucleotides that encodes a polypeptide or functional RNA molecule, including regulatory regions necessary for its expression. A polynucleotide is a linear polymer composed of nucleotide monomers linked by phosphodiester bonds, and may be naturally occurring or synthetically produced, including DNA, RNA, or modified analogs. A polypeptide is a chain of amino acids connected by peptide bonds, which may be folded into a functional protein structure. Gene expression refers to the process by which information encoded in a gene is transcribed into mRNA and translated into a polypeptide. A foreign gene is a nucleic acid sequence that is not native to the host cell in which it is introduced, and which is operably linked to a promoter to direct its expression. A foreign protein is the polypeptide product encoded by a foreign gene. A gene expression unit is a genetic construct comprising a promoter operably linked to a coding sequence of a foreign gene, optionally including additional regulatory elements such as enhancers, introns, polyadenylation signals, or terminators. A promoter is a DNA sequence located upstream of a gene that serves as a binding site for RNA polymerase and associated transcription factors to initiate transcription. Promoter activity refers to the ability of a promoter sequence to drive transcription of a downstream gene, as measured by the level of mRNA or protein produced. To have promoter activity means that a given DNA sequence is capable of initiating transcription at a detectable and reproducible level when operably linked to a reporter or coding sequence. A DNA element is a discrete nucleotide sequence that modulates gene expression, including promoters, enhancers, silencers, insulators, or matrix attachment regions. An antigen-binding fragment of an antibody is a portion of an immunoglobulin molecule that retains the ability to bind specifically to an antigen, including Fab, Fab', Fv, scFv, or single-domain antibody fragments. Identity, in the context of nucleic acid or amino acid sequences, refers to the degree of sequence similarity between two sequences, calculated as the percentage of matching residues over a defined alignment length. To hybridize under stringent conditions means that a nucleic acid sequence binds specifically to a complementary sequence under conditions that favor perfect or near-perfect base pairing, typically involving high temperature and low salt concentration. The Hspa5 promoter is a novel transcriptional regulatory element derived from the 5' flanking region of the Hspa5 gene, which encodes the endoplasmic reticulum chaperone BiP. The origin of the Hspa5 promoter is the genomic locus of the Hspa5 gene in human, mouse, rat, or Chinese hamster cells, with the preferred embodiment being the human-derived sequence spanning from 600 base pairs upstream of the transcription start site to the start codon. The preferred Hspa5 promoter is the sequence of SEQ ID NO: 1, which contains conserved transcription factor binding motifs essential for its activity under endoplasmic reticulum stress. A mutant Hspa5 promoter refers to a variant of the native sequence that has been altered by point mutations, deletions, or insertions, yet retains or enhances promoter activity. A method for introducing mutation includes site-directed mutagenesis, PCR-based mutagenesis, or synthetic gene assembly. The examination of foreign gene expression-enhancing activity is performed by transient or stable transfection of a reporter construct containing the promoter linked to a luciferase or fluorescent protein gene, followed by quantification of expression over time in fed-batch culture. A foreign gene expression unit comprises a promoter, a coding sequence for a foreign gene, and a polyadenylation signal, optionally flanked by selection markers or replication origins. The components of a foreign gene expression unit include a promoter, a coding region, a 3' untranslated region, and a polyadenylation signal, all arranged in a transcriptionally functional orientation. A DNA element for enhancing foreign gene expression is a sequence that increases the transcriptional or translational efficiency of a gene, such as an intron, enhancer, or matrix attachment region. A method for obtaining a DNA element includes bioinformatic screening of transcriptomic data, functional screening of genomic libraries, or chemical synthesis based on consensus motifs. Examples of DNA elements include the intron from the human β-globin gene, the CMV enhancer, or the A7 element derived from CHO cells. The nucleotide sequences of DNA elements are disclosed herein as SEQ ID NO: 5 through SEQ ID NO: 9, each of which demonstrates enhanced promoter activity when combined with the Hspa5 promoter. Foreign gene expression-enhancing activity is quantified by comparing the yield of recombinant protein per cell over time between constructs containing the Hspa5 promoter and those containing control promoters. DNA elements may be positioned upstream, downstream, or within introns of the coding sequence, and may be oriented in either forward or reverse direction relative to the transcriptional unit. A foreign gene is any nucleic acid sequence encoding a protein of interest, including antibodies, enzymes, cytokines, or fusion proteins. Examples of foreign genes include genes encoding IgG1, IgG2, IgG4Pro, Fc-fusion proteins, or recombinant enzymes such as glucocerebrosidase. An antigen-binding fragment of an antibody is a portion of an immunoglobulin molecule that retains antigen-binding specificity, including Fab, Fab', Fv, scFv, or single-domain VHH fragments. A selection marker is a gene that confers resistance to a selective agent, such as neomycin, hygromycin, or methotrexate, enabling the isolation of stably transfected cells. An episomal vector is a plasmid or viral vector capable of replicating independently of the host genome, typically containing an origin of replication and maintained at low copy number. Transformed cells are host cells that have been genetically modified by the introduction of an expression vector containing a foreign gene under the control of the Hspa5 promoter. Host cells include mammalian cell lines such as CHO, BHK, HEK293, NS0, or SP2/0, with CHO cells being the preferred embodiment. A method for introducing an expression vector includes lipofection, electroporation, calcium phosphate precipitation, or viral transduction. A method for producing a foreign protein involves culturing transformed host cells under conditions that support cell growth and protein expression, followed by recovery and purification of the secreted or intracellular protein. Culturing transformed cells is performed in serum-free, suspension-adapted conditions using fed-batch or perfusion modes, typically at 37°C with 5% CO2 and controlled pH and dissolved oxygen. Collection and purification of the foreign protein involve centrifugation, filtration, protein A affinity chromatography, ion exchange, and ultrafiltration steps. A method for producing an antibody protein involves co-expressing heavy and light chain genes under the control of the Hspa5 promoter within a single expression vector or two separate vectors. Heavy chain and light chain genes are cloned into expression constructs with appropriate signal peptides and polyadenylation signals. Examples of antibodies include humanized or fully human monoclonal antibodies targeting TNF-α, HER2, PD-1, or CD20. An antibody gene refers to the nucleotide sequence encoding the variable and constant regions of an immunoglobulin heavy or light chain. Methods for producing other foreign proteins include the expression of enzymes, hormones, growth factors, or viral antigens using the same promoter system. Examples of foreign proteins include erythropoietin, factor VIII, insulin-like growth factor, or SARS-CoV-2 spike protein.

### EXAMPLES

- The following examples illustrate the construction, validation, and application of the Hspa5 promoter in the production of recombinant proteins. Plasmids used include pGL4.10 for luciferase reporter assays and pEF1/myc-His B for antibody expression. Restriction enzymes such as EcoRI, XhoI, and KpnI were employed for cloning, and sequencing was performed using Sanger or next-generation methods. Cell culture and transformation operations were conducted using CHO-K1 cells grown in serum-free medium G13 or CD DA1, with transfection performed using lipid-based reagents. Protein collection and purification involved centrifugation, filtration, and protein A affinity chromatography. A humanized antibody X expression vector was constructed by cloning heavy and light chain genes downstream of the Hspa5 promoter (SEQ ID NO: 1). A stable pool of cells expressing humanized antibody X was generated by selection with Geneticin, and a monoclonal cell line was derived by limiting dilution. Fed-batch culture of this cell line demonstrated a 2.3-fold increase in specific productivity rate compared to cells expressing the same antibody under the control of the hEF1α promoter. Transcriptome analysis was performed on cells harvested at multiple time points, with total RNA extracted using RNAiso Plus and sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq platform. Read sequences were mapped to the CHO genome, and expression levels were normalized using RPKM values. Highly expressed genes were identified, and their promoter regions were cloned into pGL4.10 to assess luciferase activity. The Hspa5 promoter region was cloned from human, mouse, and rat genomes and tested for activity. The pGL4.10-hEF1α construct served as a positive control. Transfection of CHO-K1 cells with these constructs revealed that the Hspa5 promoter conferred the highest luciferase activity in the late phase of culture, with a 3.1-fold increase over hEF1α on day 12. Fed-batch culture of cells expressing Renilla luciferase under the Hspa5 promoter showed sustained luminescence until day 14, whereas control promoters declined after day 8. Truncation analysis demonstrated that the 600 bp region upstream of the Hspa5 transcription start site retained full activity, while shorter fragments showed reduced function. The human, mouse, and rat Hspa5 promoters were compared in fed-batch culture, and all showed comparable enhancement of antibody production. The combination of the Hspa5 promoter with the A7 enhancer element resulted in a synergistic increase in specific productivity. The Hspa5 promoter consistently outperformed the human RPS7 and hEF1α promoters in terms of antibody titer, specific productivity rate, and viable cell density over 14 days of culture. These results demonstrate that the Hspa5 promoter is a superior and broadly applicable tool for enhancing recombinant protein production in mammalian cell systems.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

- The method for producing foreign genes described herein has substantial industrial applicability in the biopharmaceutical manufacturing sector. The Hspa5 promoter enables the production of monoclonal antibodies and other therapeutic proteins at significantly higher titers without requiring larger bioreactors, extended culture durations, or complex process modifications. This translates directly into reduced manufacturing costs, increased supply reliability, and improved accessibility of biologic therapies. The promoter is compatible with current good manufacturing practices and can be readily integrated into existing CHO cell line development platforms. Its ability to function effectively across multiple antibody subclasses and protein formats makes it a versatile platform technology applicable to a wide range of biologics, including biosimilars, fusion proteins, and enzyme replacements. The invention thus provides a commercially scalable, regulatory-compliant, and economically advantageous solution for the global biopharmaceutical industry.
