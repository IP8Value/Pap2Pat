# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to deep-learning

The present invention pertains to the field of deep-learning systems designed for the reliable transmission of digital information over noisy communication channels. Specifically, the invention encompasses neural network architectures and training methodologies that emulate the principles of classical channel coding through end-to-end learning frameworks. These frameworks leverage autoencoder structures to jointly optimize encoding and decoding functions without reliance on predefined mathematical models of channel behavior. The system operates by transforming discrete binary message sequences into continuous-valued representations suitable for transmission over analog channels, and subsequently reconstructing the original message from corrupted observations using learned decoding rules. Unlike conventional coding schemes that depend on algebraic or probabilistic constructions, this approach derives coding strategies directly from data-driven optimization, enabling adaptation to arbitrary channel statistics, including those that are non-stationary, non-Gaussian, or otherwise intractable to model analytically. The invention further extends to the integration of list decoding paradigms within neural network decoders, allowing the system to generate multiple plausible reconstructions of the transmitted message and select the most likely candidate based on auxiliary validation criteria. This methodology fundamentally redefines the design of communication systems by replacing rule-based encoding and decoding with learned mappings that are trained to minimize end-to-end error rates under realistic noise conditions.

## SUMMARY

- motivate reliable transmission

Reliable transmission of digital data across imperfect communication channels remains a foundational challenge in information theory and telecommunications. The presence of noise, interference, and signal distortion during transmission introduces uncertainty in the received signal, which can lead to errors in the reconstructed message. Traditional approaches to mitigating these errors rely on carefully constructed channel codes that introduce structured redundancy into the transmitted data, enabling the receiver to detect and correct errors through deterministic or probabilistic decoding algorithms. However, these methods are often constrained by assumptions about the statistical nature of the channel, such as additive white Gaussian noise, and require extensive mathematical analysis to ensure optimality. The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a deep-learning-based framework that learns to encode and decode messages directly from observed input-output pairs, thereby eliminating the need for explicit channel modeling and enabling robust performance under a broad spectrum of real-world impairments.

- introduce channel coding

Channel coding is the process of augmenting a message with redundant information to enable recovery from transmission errors. In classical systems, this redundancy is introduced through deterministic mappings governed by algebraic or combinatorial principles, such as convolutional codes, low-density parity-check codes, or polar codes. These codes are designed to maximize the minimum distance between codewords or to exploit the structure of iterative decoding algorithms. The present invention reimagines channel coding as a learned transformation, wherein neural networks are trained to approximate optimal encoding and decoding functions without explicit adherence to classical code structures. The encoder network maps binary message vectors into continuous-valued signal constellations, while the decoder network reverses this process by estimating the original message from a noisy observation. This paradigm shift allows the system to discover coding strategies that may not be derivable through conventional analytical methods, particularly in scenarios where the channel model is unknown, time-varying, or highly complex.

- summarize Shannon's work

Claude Shannon’s seminal work established the theoretical limits of reliable communication over noisy channels, demonstrating that for any given channel, there exists a capacity beyond which error-free transmission is impossible. He further proved that, with sufficiently long codes, it is possible to approach this capacity arbitrarily closely using randomly generated codebooks and maximum likelihood decoding. While Shannon’s results provided a theoretical foundation for modern communications, they did not offer practical methods for constructing such codes. The present invention operationalizes Shannon’s insights by employing deep-learning techniques to implicitly construct codebooks that approximate capacity-achieving performance. Rather than relying on random ensembles or structured algebraic designs, the system learns to generate codewords through gradient-based optimization, effectively discovering high-dimensional representations that are resilient to noise and optimized for the specific characteristics of the transmission medium.

- describe landmark codes

Landmark codes such as Turbo codes, low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes, and polar codes have dominated the field of practical channel coding for decades due to their near-capacity performance and efficient decoding algorithms. Turbo codes leverage iterative decoding between constituent convolutional encoders, LDPC codes exploit sparse parity-check matrices and belief propagation, and polar codes utilize channel polarization to create synthetic channels of varying reliability. Each of these codes represents a triumph of mathematical ingenuity and engineering precision. The present invention does not seek to replicate these structures but rather to surpass them through a data-driven alternative. By training neural networks to emulate the behavior of these codes while adapting to their limitations—such as sensitivity to channel mismatch or high decoding complexity—the invention achieves comparable or superior performance with greater flexibility and reduced reliance on human-designed constraints.

- introduce system embodiment

The system embodiment of the invention comprises a neural autoencoder architecture consisting of an encoder network and a decoder network, both implemented as deep neural networks with learnable parameters. The encoder transforms a binary message word of length K into a real-valued codeword of length N, which is then transmitted over a noisy channel. The decoder receives the corrupted version of the codeword and outputs a reconstructed message word. The system further incorporates a list decoding mechanism, wherein the decoder produces not a single estimate but a ranked list of L candidate message words. A selection mechanism, such as a cyclic redundancy check (CRC) or a genie-aided oracle, is then employed to identify the most likely candidate from this list. The entire system is trained end-to-end using a loss function that encourages the true message to appear among the top candidates in the list, thereby optimizing for block error rate rather than bit-level accuracy.

- describe receiver circuit

The receiver circuit of the invention comprises a neural network decoder that processes the received noisy signal and generates a list of candidate message words. This decoder is composed of multiple interconnected layers, including convolutional, recurrent, or fully connected units, arranged to capture both local and global dependencies in the received signal. The output of the decoder is a matrix of size K × L, where each column represents a candidate message word with continuous-valued probabilities for each bit. These values are subsequently processed by a selection module that applies a validation criterion, such as CRC verification, to filter out invalid candidates. The remaining candidates are ranked by their likelihood scores, and the highest-ranked valid candidate is selected as the final decoded message. The receiver circuit is implemented in digital electronic circuitry, software, firmware, or a combination thereof, and is capable of operating in real time on embedded platforms.

- describe decoder circuit

The decoder circuit is configured to receive a noisy channel output and produce a list of candidate decoded messages. It comprises a sequence of neural network blocks, each of which refines the estimate of the transmitted message based on partial observations of the received signal. These blocks are arranged in an iterative architecture, where the output of one block serves as the input to the next, allowing for progressive refinement of the candidate list. Each block may include interleavers and deinterleavers to simulate the effect of code interleaving, thereby enhancing the diversity of error correction. The decoder circuit further incorporates a sigmoid activation function at its final stage to constrain the output values to the interval [0,1], facilitating probabilistic interpretation of the bit estimates. The weights of the decoder circuit are trained using backpropagation through time, with gradients computed with respect to a loss function that penalizes the absence of the true message from the candidate list.

- describe training process

The training process involves the generation of a large dataset of binary message words, their corresponding encoded representations, and the associated noisy channel outputs. The encoder and decoder networks are trained jointly in an alternating fashion, wherein the encoder weights are frozen while the decoder is optimized, and vice versa, to prevent convergence to suboptimal local minima. During each training iteration, a batch of message words is encoded, transmitted through a simulated channel with additive noise, and decoded. The loss function is computed based on the proximity of the true message to at least one candidate in the decoder’s output list. Gradients are computed using automatic differentiation, and the network parameters are updated via stochastic gradient descent with adaptive learning rates. The training is conducted over a range of signal-to-noise ratios to ensure robustness across varying channel conditions.

- compute loss function

The loss function is computed as the minimum binary cross-entropy between the true message word and each candidate in the decoder’s output list. For a given message word and a list of L candidate words, the loss is defined as the smallest cross-entropy value among all L candidates. This formulation ensures that the network is incentivized to place the true message among the top candidates, even if it is not the single most probable output. The use of the minimum operator introduces non-differentiable points, but these occur with negligible probability during training and do not impede gradient propagation. The loss function is computed over a batch of samples and averaged to produce a scalar value that guides the optimization process.

- select candidate message word

The candidate message word is selected from the list of L candidates by applying a validation criterion, such as a cyclic redundancy check (CRC), to each candidate. Only those candidates that satisfy the CRC condition are considered valid. Among the valid candidates, the one with the highest cumulative probability score is selected as the final decoded message. If no candidate satisfies the CRC condition, the candidate with the highest overall likelihood is chosen as a fallback. This selection process emulates the behavior of a genie-aided decoder during training and a practical CRC-aided decoder during inference, ensuring consistency between training and deployment.

- describe loss function options

Alternative loss functions may be employed to further refine the training objective. One variant replaces the minimum operation with a soft-minimum approximation to ensure differentiability throughout the entire domain. Another variant incorporates a weighted sum of the cross-entropy values, where weights are assigned based on the position of the candidate in the list. A third variant introduces a penalty term proportional to the distance between the true message and the closest candidate, encouraging the network to produce tightly clustered candidate lists. These options provide flexibility in balancing convergence speed, training stability, and final performance.

- describe BCE loss function

The binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss function is used to quantify the discrepancy between the predicted bit probabilities and the ground truth binary values. For a single bit, the BCE is defined as the negative logarithm of the predicted probability if the true bit is one, or the negative logarithm of one minus the predicted probability if the true bit is zero. This function is applied element-wise across all K bits of the message word and averaged over the batch. The BCE loss is particularly well-suited for this application because it provides a smooth, convex measure of error that is compatible with gradient-based optimization and naturally aligns with the probabilistic interpretation of neural network outputs.

- describe decoder sub-decoders

The decoder is composed of multiple sub-decoders, each responsible for processing a subset of the received signal. These sub-decoders operate in sequence, with the output of one serving as the input to the next. Each sub-decoder is trained to refine the candidate list based on increasingly detailed observations of the channel output. The sub-decoders may differ in architecture, with earlier stages employing simpler models and later stages incorporating more complex, high-capacity networks. This hierarchical structure enables the system to first identify broad regions of plausible messages and then narrow down to precise estimates, mimicking the behavior of iterative decoding in classical codes.

- perform sigmoid function

A sigmoid activation function is applied to the final output of the decoder to constrain the predicted bit values to the interval [0,1]. This function transforms the raw network outputs into probabilities, enabling the interpretation of each bit as a likelihood of being zero or one. The sigmoid function is differentiable and smooth, making it ideal for use in backpropagation. It also ensures that the outputs are compatible with the binary cross-entropy loss function and the CRC validation mechanism, which operate on discrete binary values derived by thresholding the sigmoid outputs.
