# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

Microfluidic devices are miniaturized systems designed to manipulate and control small volumes of fluids through channels with dimensions typically ranging from tens to hundreds of micrometers. These devices integrate multiple laboratory functions—such as sample preparation, reaction, separation, and detection—onto a single substrate, enabling precise manipulation of biological and chemical processes under highly controlled conditions. The architecture of microfluidic devices is engineered to exploit physical phenomena at small scales, including surface tension, viscous dominance, and laminar flow, which collectively allow for enhanced reproducibility, reduced reagent consumption, and improved spatial resolution compared to conventional macroscale systems. Microfluidic platforms are particularly well-suited for applications involving living cells, where environmental parameters such as fluid shear stress, nutrient gradients, and waste removal must be tightly regulated to maintain physiological relevance. The integration of biological systems within these devices has led to the emergence of organ-on-a-chip technologies, which aim to replicate the structural and functional complexity of human tissues in vitro.

### Description of Related Art

The demand for physiologically accurate in vitro models has driven the widespread adoption of microfluidic devices in biomedical research, particularly in drug screening, toxicology, and personalized medicine. These systems offer a significant advantage over traditional cell culture methods by enabling dynamic perfusion of culture media, thereby mimicking the continuous flow of blood or interstitial fluid encountered in vivo. However, most existing microfluidic platforms rely on external pumps—such as syringe pumps, peristaltic pumps, or pressure controllers—to drive fluid through the channels. These systems introduce several practical limitations, including the need for complex tubing connections, susceptibility to bubble formation and leakage, and the requirement for specialized training to operate and maintain equipment. Furthermore, the use of external pumps increases the risk of contamination, particularly when handling infectious agents or patient-derived samples, and imposes substantial capital and operational costs that limit accessibility to well-funded laboratories. Alternative approaches have been proposed to circumvent these issues, including gravity-driven systems such as rocking platforms, which induce oscillatory flow by tilting the device. While these methods eliminate the need for electronic pumps, they generate non-uniform and transient shear stress profiles that compromise the reproducibility of cellular responses. Other solutions, such as hydrostatic pressure gradients or modified siphon systems, suffer from limited flow control, inability to sustain long-term perfusion, or incompatibility with standard cell culture incubators. None of these approaches provide a simple, robust, and tunable method for achieving continuous, stable, and physiologically relevant fluid flow without reliance on external equipment.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention introduces a novel apparatus for generating controlled, pumpless fluid flow within microfluidic devices through the strategic use of evaporation-driven capillary wicking. The apparatus comprises a solid substrate defining a microfluidic channel, a fluid reservoir connected to one end of the channel for containing cell culture medium, and an evaporation reservoir positioned at the opposite end of the channel, which contains a hydrophilic structure capable of wicking fluid away from the channel. The hydrophilic structure, such as a thread or fibrous material, is configured to extend from the outlet of the microfluidic channel into the evaporation reservoir, where ambient evaporation under controlled environmental conditions generates a sustained capillary pressure gradient. This gradient drives continuous, unidirectional flow of fluid through the channel without the need for external pumps, power sources, or tubing. The flow rate is precisely adjustable by modifying the physical dimensions of the hydrophilic structure, including its length, diameter, and material composition, thereby enabling tailored control over fluid shear stress experienced by cultured cells. The apparatus operates autonomously within standard cell culture incubators, maintaining sterile conditions and eliminating the risks associated with external fluid handling systems.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention encompasses a microfluidic apparatus designed for the autonomous perfusion of cell cultures without the use of external pumps or mechanical components. The apparatus comprises a solid substrate fabricated from a biocompatible, optically transparent material such as polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) or glass, which defines a microfluidic channel with a defined cross-sectional geometry. The channel is connected at one end to a fluid reservoir containing cell culture medium and at the opposite end to an evaporation reservoir, which houses a hydrophilic, wicking structure. The hydrophilic structure is composed of a fibrous material, such as a spunlace thread, engineered to exhibit high capillary action and consistent fluid transport properties. The structure is positioned such that it extends from the outlet of the microfluidic channel into the evaporation reservoir without contacting the bottom surface of the channel, thereby preventing complete drainage of the medium while permitting continuous evaporation-driven flow. The apparatus is designed to operate within a controlled environment, such as a cell culture incubator maintained at 37 °C, 5% CO₂, and 90% relative humidity, where the evaporation of fluid from the distal end of the hydrophilic structure generates a steady capillary pressure differential that drives fluid through the channel. The flow rate is determined by the interplay between the evaporation rate and the wicking capacity of the hydrophilic structure, both of which are modulated by adjusting the length and diameter of the thread. Flow rates ranging from 0.15 µL min⁻¹ to 6 µL min⁻¹ have been achieved, enabling precise tuning of fluid shear stress to match the physiological requirements of diverse cell types, including intestinal Caco-2 and lung A549 epithelial cells. The apparatus is fabricated using standard soft lithography techniques, wherein a master mold is created via photolithography on a silicon wafer, followed by casting and curing of PDMS, which is then bonded to a glass coverslip to form a sealed microfluidic system. The fluid reservoir and evaporation reservoir are formed by punching holes into the PDMS layer, with the evaporation reservoir sized to accommodate the hydrophilic structure and retain sufficient fluid volume to sustain prolonged operation. The hydrophilic structure is treated to enhance wicking efficiency through plasma oxidation or mercerization, removing surface impurities and increasing hydrophilicity. The apparatus may incorporate optional features such as a dead volume reservoir to buffer fluctuations in fluid volume or a lid to minimize evaporation from the inlet reservoir. The design enables the fabrication of multichannel configurations, allowing for the simultaneous culture of multiple cell populations under distinct flow conditions within a single device. This capability is particularly valuable for high-throughput screening of shear stress-dependent cellular responses. The apparatus is entirely equipment-free, requiring no external power, pumps, or tubing, and can be operated by non-specialist users with minimal training. Its simplicity, low cost, and compatibility with standard laboratory incubators make it uniquely suited for adoption in diverse research settings, including clinical laboratories, small biotech firms, and educational institutions. The apparatus supports long-term culture of epithelial monolayers for up to five days, maintaining stable flow conditions and promoting the development of physiologically relevant tissue phenotypes, including tight junction formation, mitochondrial activity, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition markers. The absence of tubing and external connections significantly reduces the risk of contamination, enabling the entire device to be contained within a sterile Petri dish during operation. The apparatus is scalable and adaptable to a wide range of microfluidic architectures, including constant-width channels, Hele-Shaw geometries, and multiplexed designs, and is suitable for use with primary cells, organoids, and patient-derived samples.

### Examples

The apparatus was fabricated using standard soft lithography techniques. A master mold was created by spin-coating SU-8 photoresist onto a silicon wafer and patterning it using a mask writer to define channels with a height of 150 µm. Polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) was cast onto the mold at a 10:1 base-to-curing agent ratio and cured at 65 °C for two hours. The cured PDMS slab was peeled from the mold and punched to create an 8 mm inlet reservoir and a 1.5 mm outlet reservoir. The outlet reservoir was sized to accommodate a superhydrophilic spunlace thread without allowing it to contact the glass substrate. The PDMS slab was then oxygen plasma-treated and permanently bonded to a glass coverslip. The resulting device was sterilized by exposure to 70% ethanol and phosphate-buffered saline before coating the channel surface with Matrigel Basement Membrane Matrix. Human intestinal epithelial Caco-2 cells and human lung epithelial A549 cells were seeded into the devices at a density of 2 × 10⁵ cells per cm² and allowed to adhere for one hour. A superhydrophilic thread with a diameter of 1.5 mm and a length of 15 mm was inserted into the outlet reservoir, extending into a separate evaporation reservoir containing culture medium. The entire device was placed in a sterile Petri dish and maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C and 5% CO₂. Fluorescent nanoparticles were introduced into the channel to verify flow dynamics, and flow rates were quantified by tracking particle displacement using time-lapse microscopy. The Caco-2 cells formed confluent monolayers with robust expression of ZO-1 tight junction proteins and dense F-actin networks after five days of culture under a shear stress of 0.02 dyn cm⁻². A549 cells cultured under higher shear stress (0.15 dyn cm⁻²) exhibited reduced cytokeratin expression and increased vimentin fluorescence, consistent with epithelial-mesenchymal transition. The apparatus was successfully adapted to a multiplexed design with five parallel channels, each connected to a thread of different length, enabling the simultaneous application of five distinct flow rates within a single device. The flow behavior in each channel was verified independently using nanoparticle tracking, confirming independent control over shear stress conditions. The apparatus demonstrated sustained operation over five days without interruption, with no evidence of channel drying, bubble formation, or contamination. The hydrophilic thread was characterized using scanning electron microscopy, revealing a fibrous structure with fiber diameters of approximately 15 µm, and capillary rise assays confirmed reproducible wicking kinetics across multiple thread dimensions. The invention enables the reliable, reproducible, and accessible culture of epithelial cells under physiologically relevant fluid shear stress conditions without reliance on external equipment.
