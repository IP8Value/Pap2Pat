# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to plant hormone sensing

The present invention relates to the field of plant physiology and agricultural diagnostics, specifically to the non-invasive sensing of plant-derived volatile signaling molecules indicative of pathogenic infection. More particularly, the invention concerns the detection of methyl salicylate, a key phytohormone derivative released during the early stages of biotic stress, through selective chemical recognition and signal transduction using boron-containing organic compounds. The invention enables real-time, in situ monitoring of plant immune responses without requiring tissue destruction, sample extraction, or laboratory instrumentation, thereby facilitating the early identification of crop infections prior to visible symptom development.

## BACKGROUND ART

- describe plant defense mechanisms

Plants possess sophisticated innate immune systems that activate upon recognition of pathogenic invaders such as bacteria, fungi, and viruses. Upon detection of pathogen-associated molecular patterns, plants initiate a cascade of biochemical responses culminating in the production of antimicrobial compounds and signaling molecules. One of the most conserved defense pathways involves the accumulation of salicylic acid, which triggers systemic acquired resistance across distal tissues. This pathway is critically amplified through the enzymatic conversion of salicylic acid into methyl salicylate, a volatile organic compound that serves as a mobile alarm signal between neighboring cells and plants. The release of methyl salicylate into the surrounding air occurs within hours of infection, long before any macroscopic signs of disease—such as necrosis, chlorosis, or wilting—become apparent.

- describe plant hormone synthesis

The biosynthesis of methyl salicylate in plants is catalyzed by salicylic acid methyltransferase, an enzyme induced during pathogen challenge. This enzyme transfers a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine to salicylic acid, yielding methyl salicylate as the primary volatile product. Unlike salicylic acid, which remains largely confined within plant tissues, methyl salicylate diffuses readily through stomata and cuticular pores into the atmosphere. Its volatility enables long-distance signaling and makes it an ideal biomarker for non-invasive detection. The concentration of methyl salicylate in the headspace above infected plants increases rapidly and reproducibly during the first 24 to 72 hours post-inoculation, preceding measurable changes in gene expression or visible tissue damage.

- describe plant hormone signaling

Methyl salicylate functions not only as an intraplant signal but also as an interplant communication molecule, priming neighboring plants for enhanced defense readiness. Its signaling role is tightly regulated by esterases that hydrolyze it back to salicylic acid upon reaching target tissues. This dynamic equilibrium ensures spatial and temporal control over immune activation. The emission profile of methyl salicylate is highly specific to biotic stress, distinguishing it from other volatile organic compounds released under abiotic conditions such as drought or heat. This specificity renders methyl salicylate an ideal molecular fingerprint for the early detection of infectious disease in agricultural settings.

- describe prior art in plant hormone sensing

Prior methods for detecting plant hormones such as salicylic acid and methyl salicylate have relied on destructive sampling techniques, including gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) and liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS), which require leaf homogenization, solvent extraction, and complex instrumentation. These approaches are unsuitable for field deployment due to their cost, time requirements, and need for trained personnel. Alternative methods involving electronic noses or optical sensors have been explored, but suffer from poor selectivity, cross-reactivity with other volatiles, and lack of quantification capability. No prior art discloses a chemical sensor capable of selectively binding methyl salicylate in ambient air and converting this interaction into a measurable optical or electrochemical signal with sufficient sensitivity for early-stage pathogen detection.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

- state technical problem

A persistent technical problem in modern agriculture is the inability to detect pathogenic infections in crops at a presymptomatic stage, when intervention is most effective yet most difficult to implement. Current diagnostic tools are either too invasive, too slow, or too insensitive to provide actionable data before widespread infection occurs. There exists a critical unmet need for a simple, robust, and field-deployable sensing platform that can detect the earliest biochemical indicators of plant disease—specifically, the volatile emission of methyl salicylate—with high specificity, reproducibility, and quantitative accuracy under real-world environmental conditions.

### Solution to Problem

- describe solution to technical problem

The present invention solves this problem through the development of a chemosensory system based on boron-oxygen-containing organic compounds that selectively bind methyl salicylate via reversible esterification, resulting in a measurable change in fluorescence or electrochemical properties. Specifically, the invention employs a class of boron-based receptors including diboroxane, phenylboronic acid, and boroxine derivatives that form cyclic ester adducts with methyl salicylate upon molecular recognition. This binding event alters the electronic structure of the boron center, inducing a detectable shift in fluorescence emission intensity or a measurable change in current response when integrated into an electrochemical cell. The receptor is immobilized on a porous hydrogel or solid substrate, enabling direct exposure to ambient air above plant canopies without requiring sample collection or preparation.

### Advantageous Effect of Invention

- describe advantageous effect of invention

The invention provides a transformative advantage by enabling the non-invasive, real-time, and quantitative detection of methyl salicylate directly in the field, thereby permitting the identification of pathogenic infection within hours of its initiation—long before visible symptoms emerge. This early detection capability allows for targeted application of control measures, reducing unnecessary pesticide use, minimizing crop loss, and preventing disease spread across entire fields. The sensor system is low-cost, requires no external power source for optical detection, and is compatible with existing agricultural infrastructure. Furthermore, the boron-based recognition element is stable under ambient conditions, resistant to humidity fluctuations, and exhibits minimal cross-reactivity with other plant volatiles, ensuring high specificity and reliability in complex environmental matrices.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

- introduce receptor for methyl salicylate

The invention employs a molecular receptor designed to selectively recognize methyl salicylate through the formation of a stable, five-membered cyclic ester complex between the boron atom and the ortho-hydroxyl group of methyl salicylate. This binding mechanism is highly specific due to the geometric and electronic complementarity between the boron center and the salicylate moiety, which is not replicated by other common plant volatiles such as jasmonic acid methyl ester or green leaf volatiles. The receptor is synthesized as a functionalized organic compound bearing one or more boron-oxygen moieties, enabling covalent immobilization on solid supports or incorporation into hydrogel matrices for sustained exposure to airborne analytes.

- describe diboroxane derivative

A preferred embodiment utilizes a diboroxane derivative comprising two boron atoms linked through oxygen bridges, each boron center functionalized with an electron-withdrawing aryl group. This structure enhances Lewis acidity and promotes cooperative binding with methyl salicylate, resulting in a pronounced fluorescence enhancement upon complexation. The diboroxane derivative is synthesized via condensation of phenylboronic acid with a diol linker under anhydrous conditions, followed by purification and immobilization on a cellulose-based substrate. The resulting material exhibits a detection limit for methyl salicylate below 1 part per billion in air, making it suitable for early-stage pathogen detection.

- describe phenylboronic acid derivative

Another embodiment employs a phenylboronic acid derivative substituted at the para-position with a fluorophore such as naphthalimide or coumarin. Upon binding methyl salicylate, the boron center transitions from trigonal planar to tetrahedral geometry, disrupting intramolecular charge transfer and triggering a significant increase in fluorescence quantum yield. This derivative is incorporated into a porous polymer matrix or coated onto a glass slide, enabling direct exposure to plant headspace. The fluorescence response is linear over a concentration range spanning two orders of magnitude, allowing for quantitative estimation of methyl salicylate levels.

- describe boroxine derivative

A third embodiment utilizes a cyclic trimeric boroxine structure derived from 3,5-difluorophenylboronic acid. The boroxine ring exhibits enhanced stability and selectivity for methyl salicylate due to steric constraints and electronic tuning of the boron centers. When exposed to methyl salicylate vapor, the boroxine undergoes ring-opening and reorganization into a dimeric ester complex, accompanied by a red-shift in emission wavelength. This structural transition is detectable using both optical and electrochemical readouts, providing dual-mode confirmation of analyte binding.

- describe reaction of receptor and methyl salicylate

The interaction between the boron-based receptor and methyl salicylate proceeds via nucleophilic attack of the phenolic oxygen on the electron-deficient boron atom, forming a tetrahedral boronate ester. This reaction is reversible and occurs spontaneously under ambient conditions without the need for catalysts or elevated temperatures. The equilibrium constant for binding is optimized to ensure rapid association and dissociation kinetics, enabling continuous monitoring of changing methyl salicylate concentrations over time. The reaction is highly selective, with no significant response observed to structurally similar compounds such as methyl jasmonate, acetic acid, or ethanol.

- describe fluorescence emission phenomenon

Upon binding methyl salicylate, the receptor undergoes a conformational change that alters its electronic transition properties, resulting in a measurable increase in fluorescence intensity at a characteristic emission wavelength. This phenomenon is observed when the receptor is excited by ultraviolet light at a wavelength between 300 and 330 nm. The fluorescence emission peak shifts from approximately 400 nm in the unbound state to 430–450 nm upon complexation, with an intensity increase of up to 12-fold. The fluorescence signal is stable for several hours under ambient lighting and is unaffected by ambient temperature fluctuations between 15°C and 35°C.

- describe excitation light

The excitation light used to activate the fluorescence response is provided by a low-power ultraviolet light-emitting diode (UV-LED) emitting at 310 nm, chosen for its optimal overlap with the absorption maximum of the boron-methyl salicylate complex. This wavelength is selected to minimize interference from natural plant autofluorescence and to avoid photodegradation of the receptor. The light source is integrated into a handheld or fixed sensor unit and is activated only during measurement cycles to conserve energy and extend device longevity.

- describe detection of fluorescence

Fluorescence emission is detected using a photodiode or complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) sensor positioned perpendicular to the excitation path, with an optical filter centered at 440 nm to isolate the signal from background noise. The sensor is enclosed in a housing that directs ambient air from the plant canopy toward the receptor surface, ensuring continuous exposure. The detected signal is digitized and processed to determine the relative fluorescence intensity, which correlates directly with methyl salicylate concentration.

- describe comparison with reference value

The detected fluorescence intensity is compared against a pre-established reference value derived from control plants grown under identical environmental conditions but free of pathogen exposure. A statistically significant increase in fluorescence intensity—defined as exceeding the mean background signal by more than three standard deviations—is interpreted as evidence of methyl salicylate emission and, by extension, the presence of an active pathogenic infection.

- describe electrochemical behavior

In an alternative embodiment, the receptor is immobilized on an electrode surface, where binding of methyl salicylate induces a measurable change in electron transfer kinetics. The boron center, upon complexation, alters the local dielectric environment and reduces the reorganization energy required for redox processes, resulting in an increase in Faradaic current. This electrochemical response is monitored using cyclic voltammetry or amperometry at a fixed potential of +0.4 V versus Ag/AgCl.

- describe measurement of current

The current generated by the electrochemical reaction is measured using a potentiostat connected to a working electrode coated with the boron-based receptor, a counter electrode, and a reference electrode. The electrode is housed in a microfluidic chamber that allows ambient air to diffuse through a porous membrane, exposing the receptor to methyl salicylate vapor. The current is recorded at regular intervals and integrated over time to produce a cumulative signal proportional to analyte concentration.

- describe detection of change in current value

A significant increase in current value—defined as a rise exceeding 15% above baseline levels measured over a 24-hour period—is indicative of methyl salicylate emission. The rate of current increase is correlated with the severity and progression of infection, enabling not only detection but also temporal tracking of disease development.

- describe comparison with reference value

The electrochemical signal is compared against a reference baseline established from healthy plants under identical environmental conditions. Deviations beyond the statistically defined threshold trigger an alert, indicating the presence of pathogenic infection.

- describe electrochemical cell

The electrochemical cell comprises a three-electrode configuration housed in a sealed, weather-resistant enclosure with a hydrophobic gas-permeable membrane. The membrane allows selective diffusion of methyl salicylate while excluding water droplets, dust, and larger organic molecules. The cell is powered by a low-voltage battery and communicates wirelessly with a central monitoring system.

- describe method for detecting methyl salicylate

The method for detecting methyl salicylate involves placing a sensor unit containing the boron-based receptor in proximity to a plant canopy, exposing the receptor to ambient air for a period of 10 to 60 minutes, and measuring either fluorescence intensity or electrochemical current. The sensor is calibrated using known concentrations of methyl salicylate vapor generated in a controlled chamber. The resulting signal is processed using a proprietary algorithm to determine the presence or absence of infection.

- describe methyl salicylate sensor

The methyl salicylate sensor comprises a recognition section containing the boron-based receptor, a detection section comprising either an optical detector or an electrochemical transducer, and a signal processing unit. The sensor is mounted on a support structure that positions it at a fixed height above the crop canopy, ensuring consistent exposure to headspace volatiles.

- describe recognition section

The recognition section consists of a porous substrate—such as cellulose, silica gel, or polymeric foam—impregnated with the boron-based receptor. The substrate is designed to maximize surface area and facilitate rapid diffusion of methyl salicylate molecules to the active binding sites.

- describe detection section

The detection section includes either a photodetector for fluorescence measurement or an electrochemical transducer for current measurement. Both are integrated into a single housing and calibrated to operate autonomously under field conditions.

- describe optical detection

Optical detection is performed using a UV-LED excitation source and a silicon photodiode with a narrow-band filter centered at 440 nm. The system is housed in a light-tight enclosure to eliminate ambient interference.

- describe electrochemical detection

Electrochemical detection is performed using a three-electrode system with a gold working electrode coated with the receptor, a platinum counter electrode, and a silver/silver chloride reference electrode. The system operates at a fixed potential and measures current changes over time.

- describe computer program

A computer program stored on a non-transitory memory module processes the raw signal data, applies noise reduction algorithms, compares results to reference thresholds, and generates binary alerts (infected/uninfected) or quantitative concentration estimates. The program is capable of remote data transmission via Wi-Fi or cellular networks.

- describe analysis of signal

Signal analysis involves baseline subtraction, moving average filtering, and statistical outlier detection to distinguish true methyl salicylate signals from environmental noise. Machine learning models trained on field-collected data further refine classification accuracy.

- describe output of analysis result

The output of analysis is displayed on a local interface or transmitted to a cloud-based agricultural dashboard, where it is visualized as color-coded alerts, trend graphs, or geolocated infection maps for farm management.

- describe detection of methyl salicylate

Detection of methyl salicylate is achieved through the selective binding event between the boron-based receptor and the ortho-hydroxy methyl ester group of methyl salicylate, resulting in a quantifiable change in optical or electrochemical properties.

- describe measurement of concentration

Concentration of methyl salicylate is measured by calibrating the sensor response against known vapor concentrations generated in a controlled environment. A linear regression model is used to convert signal intensity into parts per billion equivalents.

- describe use in pathogen infection detection

The detection of methyl salicylate serves as a direct proxy for the activation of systemic acquired resistance in plants, enabling the identification of pathogenic infection prior to symptom development.

- describe installation near crop

The sensor is installed within 10 to 50 centimeters above the crop canopy, mounted on stakes or suspended from overhead structures, ensuring consistent exposure to headspace volatiles without physical contact with plant tissues.

- describe examples of crops and diseases

The sensor is applicable to solanaceous crops such as tomato, potato, and eggplant infected with Ralstonia solanacearum; brassicaceous crops such as cabbage and broccoli infected with Xanthomonas campestris; and cereals such as wheat and rice infected with Magnaporthe oryzae.

### <Fluorescence Emission Phenomenon>

- describe fluorescence emission phenomenon

The fluorescence emission phenomenon arises from the restriction of intramolecular rotation and enhanced rigidity of the receptor upon methyl salicylate binding, which reduces non-radiative decay pathways and increases quantum yield. This effect is most pronounced in phenylboronic acid derivatives bearing extended π-conjugated systems.

- describe excitation light

Excitation light is delivered at a wavelength of 310 nm using a low-power UV-LED, selected to match the absorption maximum of the receptor-methyl salicylate complex while minimizing interference from chlorophyll fluorescence.

- describe detection of fluorescence

Fluorescence is detected using a photodiode with a 440 nm bandpass filter, positioned at a 90-degree angle to the excitation beam to eliminate scattered light. The signal is amplified and digitized for processing.

- describe comparison with reference value

The fluorescence intensity is compared against a reference value derived from healthy plants under identical environmental conditions. A deviation exceeding three standard deviations triggers an infection alert.

### <Electrochemical Behavior>

- describe electrochemical behavior

The electrochemical behavior is characterized by an increase in Faradaic current upon methyl salicylate binding, due to enhanced electron transfer efficiency at the boron-modified electrode surface.

- describe measurement of current

Current is measured using a potentiostat operating in amperometric mode at +0.4 V versus Ag/AgCl, with sampling every 30 seconds over a 60-minute exposure period.

- describe detection of change in current value

A sustained increase in current exceeding 15% above baseline is detected as a positive signal for methyl salicylate emission.

- describe comparison with reference value

The current value is compared against a reference baseline established from control plants. Statistical thresholds are set using a 99% confidence interval.

- describe electrochemical cell

The electrochemical cell is constructed from a waterproof polymer housing with a gas-permeable membrane, three embedded electrodes, and an integrated microcontroller for signal acquisition and wireless transmission.

### <Methyl Salicylate Sensor>

- describe recognition section

The recognition section is composed of a cellulose fiber matrix impregnated with a phenylboronic acid derivative, providing high surface area and rapid analyte diffusion.

- describe detection section

The detection section integrates a UV-LED excitation source and a silicon photodiode for fluorescence readout, or a three-electrode system for electrochemical detection.

- describe optical detection

Optical detection is implemented using a collimated UV-LED and a filtered photodiode arranged in a right-angle geometry to eliminate stray light.

- describe electrochemical detection

Electrochemical detection employs a gold working electrode coated with the receptor, a platinum counter electrode, and a silver/silver chloride reference electrode, all housed in a sealed microfluidic chamber.

- describe computer program

The computer program executes signal filtering, baseline correction, threshold comparison, and alert generation, and supports remote data upload via cellular or LoRaWAN networks.

- describe analysis of signal

Signal analysis employs a moving median filter to remove transient noise, followed by Z-score calculation to determine deviation from baseline.

- describe output of analysis result

The output is presented as a visual indicator on a display screen or as an automated alert sent to a mobile application used by farm managers.

- describe detection of methyl salicylate

Detection is achieved through the irreversible formation of a boronate ester complex between the receptor and methyl salicylate, resulting in a measurable change in optical or electrical properties.

- describe measurement of concentration

Concentration is quantified using a calibration curve generated from known methyl salicylate vapor standards, with a detection limit of 0.5 ppb.

- describe use in pathogen infection detection

The sensor is used to detect the onset of pathogen infection by monitoring the temporal profile of methyl salicylate emission, enabling intervention before disease spread.

- describe installation near crop

The sensor is installed at a height of 20 cm above the crop canopy, spaced at intervals of 10 meters across the field, and powered by a solar-rechargeable battery.

- describe examples of crops and diseases

The sensor is effective for detecting Ralstonia solanacearum in tomato, Xanthomonas axonopodis in citrus, and Fusarium oxysporum in banana.

### <Method for Early Detection of Pathogen Infection in Crop>

- describe method for early detection

The method involves deploying a methyl salicylate sensor in proximity to a crop, exposing the sensor to ambient air for a minimum of 30 minutes, measuring the fluorescence or electrochemical response, and comparing the result to a predefined threshold to determine infection status.

- describe installation of sensor

Sensors are installed at regular intervals across the field, mounted on non-metallic poles to avoid interference, and positioned to avoid direct rainfall exposure.

- describe detection of methyl salicylate

Detection is based on the selective binding of methyl salicylate to the boron-based receptor, resulting in a quantifiable signal change.

## EXAMPLES

- introduce examples of the present invention

The following examples illustrate the preparation, operation, and performance of the methyl salicylate sensor using various boron-based receptors under controlled and field-relevant conditions.

### Example 1

- prepare filter paper containing TBO and detect fluorescence emission

Filter paper impregnated with tetrakis(4-boronic acid phenyl)ethylene (TBO) was exposed to methyl salicylate vapor at a concentration of 10 ppb for 30 minutes. Upon excitation at 310 nm, a distinct fluorescence peak at 445 nm was observed, with an intensity increase of 8.7-fold compared to untreated control.

### Comparative Example 1

- prepare filter paper containing TBO and detect no fluorescence emission

Filter paper impregnated with TBO was exposed to ambient air containing no exogenous methyl salicylate. No significant fluorescence signal was detected above background levels, confirming the specificity of the response.

### Example 2

- prepare filter paper containing TBO and detect fluorescence emission as a volatile signal

Filter paper containing TBO was placed above a tomato plant infected with Ralstonia solanacearum for 24 hours. Fluorescence emission at 445 nm increased by 11.3-fold, correlating with LC-MS/MS detection of methyl salicylate in headspace samples.

### Example 3

- prepare filter paper containing PB and detect fluorescence emission

### Comparative Example 2

### Example 4
