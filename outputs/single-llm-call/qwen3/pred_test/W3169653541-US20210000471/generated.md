# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- Arteriovenous grafts are synthetic vascular conduits designed to establish a direct, high-flow connection between an artery and a vein in patients requiring chronic hemodialysis. These devices are typically constructed from biocompatible, non-thrombogenic materials such as expanded polytetrafluoroethylene and are surgically implanted to provide reliable, repeated access for the extracorporeal circulation of blood through a hemodialysis machine. The graft serves as a durable conduit that permits percutaneous cannulation at multiple sites along its length, enabling the efficient removal of metabolic waste products and excess fluid from the bloodstream in individuals with end-stage renal disease. Arteriovenous grafts are particularly indicated when autogenous arteriovenous fistulas are not feasible due to inadequate vein caliber, prior venous cannulation, or other anatomical limitations. The most common configuration involves the connection of the brachial artery to the axillary vein in the upper arm, a site selected for its accessibility, sufficient vessel diameter, and compatibility with the seated posture during dialysis sessions. The structural integrity and hemodynamic performance of these grafts are critical determinants of long-term patency and patient outcomes, as failure of the graft necessitates urgent intervention, repeated surgical procedures, and increased morbidity.

## BACKGROUND

- Hemodialysis is a life-sustaining procedure for patients with end-stage renal disease, replacing the lost filtration function of the kidneys by removing uremic toxins, electrolyte imbalances, and excess fluid from the circulating blood. Without regular dialysis, accumulation of metabolic waste leads to uremia, cardiac arrhythmias, pulmonary edema, and ultimately death. Access to the vascular system for dialysis requires a high-flow, low-resistance pathway capable of sustaining blood flow rates of 300 to 600 milliliters per minute over prolonged periods. While autogenous arteriovenous fistulas are the preferred modality due to superior long-term patency and lower complication rates, a significant proportion of patients lack suitable native vasculature for fistula creation. In such cases, synthetic arteriovenous grafts serve as the primary alternative, offering a reliable, immediately usable conduit that can be cannulated within weeks of implantation. Despite widespread clinical use, arteriovenous grafts suffer from high rates of early and late failure, with primary patency rates below 60% at one year and secondary patency rates rarely exceeding 80%. The predominant cause of graft failure is thrombosis, frequently initiated by the development of intimal hyperplasia at the venous anastomosis. This pathological remodeling is driven by abnormal hemodynamic forces, including regions of excessively low or high wall shear stress, which trigger endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and smooth muscle cell proliferation. Current surgical techniques for anastomosis rely heavily on intraoperative discretion, with no standardized guidance for optimal angle or geometry. Surgeons often prioritize ease of closure, graft alignment, or tissue tension over hemodynamic optimization, resulting in highly variable anastomotic configurations that predispose patients to flow disturbances. The absence of a geometrically optimized design has perpetuated a cycle of recurrent interventions, increased healthcare costs, and diminished quality of life for dialysis-dependent patients.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

- The invention comprises a novel arteriovenous graft engineered with a specifically optimized venous anastomosis geometry to mitigate pathological wall shear strain rates and thereby enhance long-term patency. The graft is characterized by a tapered cylindrical body that transitions from a smaller diameter at the arterial end to a larger diameter at the venous end, with the venous anastomosis configured at a fixed angle of approximately 30 degrees relative to the longitudinal axis of the vein. This angle is selected to minimize the extent of the venous wall exposed to shear strain rates below 50 s⁻¹ or above 1000 s⁻¹ during the cardiac cycle, conditions known to promote thrombus formation and intimal hyperplasia. The venous end of the graft is further modified with a beveled or contoured attachment surface that incorporates micro-digit grooves and a lachrymiform indent to further modulate the local flow field, reduce flow separation, and promote laminar transition. The graft is pre-formed to maintain this geometry during implantation, eliminating variability introduced by surgical technique and ensuring consistent hemodynamic performance across patients. The design enables improved flow distribution, reduced endothelial injury, and decreased incidence of thrombotic occlusion, thereby extending graft patency and reducing the need for reintervention.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- The present disclosure introduces a surgically implantable arteriovenous graft with a geometrically optimized venous anastomosis designed to reduce the risk of thrombosis and improve long-term patency in patients undergoing chronic hemodialysis. The graft is manufactured from a biocompatible, non-thrombogenic polymer, preferably expanded polytetrafluoroethylene, with a wall thickness sufficient to resist collapse under physiological pressure gradients while permitting percutaneous cannulation. The graft is configured as a continuous, tapered conduit with an arterial inlet diameter ranging from 3.5 to 4.5 millimeters and a venous outlet diameter ranging from 6.5 to 7.5 millimeters, with a total length of 145 to 155 millimeters to accommodate subcutaneous tunneling. The venous anastomosis is formed at a fixed angle of 30 degrees ± 2 degrees relative to the longitudinal axis of the recipient vein, a configuration determined through computational fluid dynamics simulations to minimize the area of the venous wall exposed to pathologically low or high wall shear strain rates. The anastomotic junction is beveled to facilitate seamless integration with the venous lumen, and the inner surface of the venous end is contoured with a series of micro-digit grooves, each measuring 0.1 to 0.3 millimeters in width, 0.05 to 0.15 millimeters in depth, and spaced at intervals of 0.2 to 0.5 millimeters along the circumferential perimeter. Adjacent to these grooves, a lachrymiform indent is integrally formed at the distal tip of the anastomosis, with a major axis of 1.5 to 2.5 millimeters and a minor axis of 0.8 to 1.2 millimeters, oriented to direct flow smoothly into the venous lumen without abrupt redirection. The combination of the optimized anastomosis angle, micro-digit grooves, and lachrymiform indent acts synergistically to stabilize the flow field, reduce recirculation zones, and prevent stagnation, thereby minimizing endothelial activation and the initiation of thrombotic cascades. The graft is pre-formed during manufacturing to maintain these geometric features under physiological stress, ensuring that the hemodynamic advantages are preserved regardless of surgical implantation technique. The dimensions and geometry are calibrated to match the typical diameter of the axillary vein in adult patients, with the graft’s taper designed to match the natural flow impedance between the brachial artery and the venous system. The graft may be implanted using standard surgical techniques, with the arterial anastomosis performed end-to-side or end-to-end, and the venous anastomosis performed at the predetermined angle without requiring intraoperative adjustment. The absence of variability in anastomotic geometry eliminates a major source of procedural inconsistency, thereby standardizing outcomes across clinical settings.

- The venous anastomosis angle is a critical determinant of the wall shear strain rate distribution along the vein wall. Angles greater than 40 degrees result in high-velocity impingement of arterial flow against the distal venous wall, generating shear rates exceeding 1000 s⁻¹, which promote platelet adhesion and endothelial denudation. Conversely, angles less than 20 degrees cause flow separation and recirculation zones immediately distal to the anastomosis, where shear rates fall below 50 s⁻¹, facilitating fibrin deposition and thrombus nucleation. The 30-degree angle represents a biomechanical equilibrium, where the momentum of the incoming arterial flow is gradually redirected into the venous lumen, minimizing both high-shear and low-shear regions. Computational simulations demonstrate that at this angle, the area of the venous wall exposed to pathological shear rates is reduced by more than 60% compared to conventional 90-degree anastomoses. The micro-digit grooves serve to disrupt the boundary layer and promote turbulent mixing at low Reynolds numbers, preventing the formation of stagnant pockets. The lachrymiform indent functions as a flow diffuser, smoothing the transition from the graft’s conical geometry to the cylindrical vein, reducing flow separation and vortex shedding. Together, these features create a hemodynamically favorable environment that preserves endothelial integrity and delays the onset of intimal hyperplasia. The graft is manufactured using precision molding or 3D printing techniques to ensure reproducibility of the anastomotic geometry, and may be supplied in multiple sizes to accommodate variations in venous diameter among patient populations. The material composition is selected to resist calcification, infection, and mechanical fatigue over prolonged exposure to pulsatile flow.

- The graft may be used in conjunction with standard dialysis cannulation protocols, and its geometry is compatible with both needle and catheter-based access systems. The pre-formed anastomosis eliminates the need for intraoperative shaping, reducing surgical time and the risk of technical error. The design is applicable to other venous targets beyond the axillary vein, including the cephalic, basilic, or subclavian veins, provided that the venous diameter and flow characteristics are within the calibrated range. The inclusion of the micro-digit grooves and lachrymiform indent does not compromise the structural integrity of the graft or its ability to withstand repeated cannulation. The surface texture is engineered to be non-porous and resistant to protein adsorption, minimizing the risk of biofilm formation. The graft may be coated with antithrombotic agents such as heparin or endothelial progenitor cell-seeding matrices to further enhance patency, though such coatings are not required for the core functionality of the invention. The invention provides a standardized, reproducible solution to a persistent clinical problem, offering a tangible improvement in graft survival and a reduction in the burden of interventional procedures for dialysis patients.

### EXAMPLES

- Computational fluid dynamics simulations were performed to model the pulsatile flow of blood through an idealized brachio-axillary arteriovenous graft system. Blood was modeled as a non-Newtonian fluid using the Bird-Carreau constitutive law, with a density of 1060 kg/m³, zero-shear viscosity of 0.0056 Pa·s, infinite-shear viscosity of 0.0035 Pa·s, relaxation time of 3.313 seconds, and power index of 0.3568. The arterial inlet velocity waveform was derived from clinical ultrasound measurements of the brachial artery and reconstructed as a Fourier series with eight harmonics to represent the pulsatile nature of flow. The venous outlet was maintained at a constant centerline velocity of 0.15 m/s to simulate axillary vein flow. The graft was modeled with an arterial inlet diameter of 4 mm, a venous outlet diameter of 7 mm, and a length of 150 mm. A series of simulations were conducted with venous anastomosis angles ranging from 13° to 90°, with mesh refinement ensuring convergence and boundary layer resolution. The computational mesh consisted of 200,000 to 250,000 elements with inflation layers of seven prism elements at the wall, achieving a maximum aspect ratio of 10:1. The Navier-Stokes equations were solved using ANSYS Fluent with the PISO algorithm and hybrid initialization. Flow fields were analyzed over a complete cardiac cycle, and wall shear strain rates were mapped across the venous lumen. Metrics Γ_low and Γ_high were defined to quantify the extent of pathological shear regions, with Γ_low integrating the inverse of shear rate over areas below 50 s⁻¹ and Γ_high integrating shear rate over areas above 1000 s⁻¹. Results demonstrated that at 30°, both Γ_low and Γ_high reached their minimum values, indicating the most favorable hemodynamic environment. Cross-sectional velocity profiles at peak systole and mid-diastole confirmed reduced flow separation and diminished high-shear zones at this angle. The addition of micro-digit grooves and the lachrymiform indent further reduced the area of low-shear regions by 22% and high-shear regions by 18% compared to a smooth-walled 30° anastomosis. These findings were consistent across multiple simulations with varying flow rates, confirming the robustness of the design. The optimized geometry was validated against clinical outcomes data, demonstrating a projected 45% reduction in thrombotic failure over a 12-month period. The invention is thus supported by quantitative, reproducible, and physiologically relevant computational evidence.
