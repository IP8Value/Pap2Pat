# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to boiling systems

The present invention relates to systems and methods for the active, reversible, and spatially precise control of boiling phenomena in liquids through the application of electric fields to surfaces in contact with charged surfactant solutions. Specifically, the invention encompasses tunable boiling systems that enable dynamic modulation of bubble nucleation, heat transfer performance, and thermal response by electrostatically regulating the adsorption and desorption of ionic surfactants at the solid–liquid interface. These systems are particularly useful in applications requiring adaptive thermal management, including power generation, electronics cooling, microfluidic actuation, and industrial heat exchange processes where conventional boiling systems lack responsiveness to changing thermal loads. The invention distinguishes itself from prior art by eliminating the need for engineered surface textures or microstructures, instead leveraging the intrinsic electrostatic interaction between charged surfactants and conductive surfaces under low-voltage electric fields to achieve real-time control over boiling behavior. This approach enables a paradigm shift from static, passive boiling surfaces to responsive, field-programmable thermal interfaces capable of self-optimizing heat transfer characteristics in accordance with operational demands.

## BACKGROUND ART

- introduce boiling research

Boiling is a widely utilized heat transfer mechanism in energy systems, industrial processes, and electronic cooling applications due to its exceptional capacity to remove large heat fluxes at relatively low temperature differences. For decades, research has focused on enhancing boiling performance through surface modifications such as microchannels, porous coatings, and nanostructured textures, all aimed at increasing nucleation site density and promoting stable vapor formation. Despite these advances, conventional boiling systems remain fundamentally static in their operational characteristics, incapable of adapting to transient thermal loads or varying environmental conditions without mechanical or structural intervention.

- describe wettability and bubble generation

The initiation of bubble nucleation during boiling is critically dependent on the wettability of the heating surface, which governs the contact angle between the liquid and the solid interface. Hydrophobic surfaces promote bubble formation by reducing the energy barrier for vapor nucleation, whereas hydrophilic surfaces suppress nucleation by enhancing liquid spreading and increasing the required superheat. This relationship between surface energy and boiling behavior has been well documented, yet prior efforts to manipulate wettability have relied on permanent surface treatments or chemical coatings that lack reversibility and temporal control.

- explain boiling curve and heat transfer coefficient

The performance of boiling systems is typically quantified using boiling curves, which plot heat flux against surface superheat, and the associated heat transfer coefficient (HTC), which reflects the efficiency of thermal energy transfer from the surface to the fluid. These curves delineate distinct regimes—nucleate boiling, critical heat flux (CHF), and film boiling—each with characteristic thermal behaviors. The ability to shift these curves dynamically, thereby altering the HTC and CHF in real time, has remained an unmet challenge in thermal engineering, as existing systems are constrained by fixed material properties and irreversible surface modifications.

- discuss limitations of static boiling systems

Static boiling systems suffer from inherent inflexibility: once fabricated, their thermal response cannot be adjusted to accommodate fluctuating heat loads, leading to suboptimal performance under non-steady-state conditions. This limitation is particularly acute in applications such as dispatchable power plants, variable-load electronics, and pulsed thermal systems, where rapid adaptation is essential to prevent thermal runaway or inefficient operation. Furthermore, attempts to modulate boiling via external forces such as electric fields have been hindered by reliance on high-voltage electrohydrodynamic (EHD) effects or electrolytic decomposition, both of which introduce instability, material degradation, and energy inefficiency.

- describe surface roughness and wettability modifications

While surface roughness and chemical patterning have been employed to enhance nucleation, these approaches are static, irreversible, and often compromise mechanical durability or long-term stability. Moreover, they do not permit fine-grained, real-time control over the spatial distribution of boiling activity, limiting their utility in multi-zone thermal management systems.

- highlight need for tunable boiling systems

There exists a critical and unmet need for boiling systems capable of dynamic, reversible, and spatially resolved control over nucleation and heat transfer performance without requiring structural alterations to the surface. Such systems must operate at low voltages, avoid electrochemical side reactions, and leverage widely available, non-toxic, and thermally stable surfactants to enable practical deployment across diverse industrial and technological domains.

## SUMMARY OF EMBODIMENTS

- introduce tunable boiling system

The present invention provides a tunable boiling system that enables active, reversible, and spatially selective control of bubble nucleation and heat transfer by applying low-voltage electric fields to a conductive boiling surface immersed in a liquid containing charged surfactants. The system operates through the electrostatic adsorption or desorption of surfactant molecules at the solid–liquid interface, thereby modulating surface wettability and consequently altering the nucleation characteristics of the boiling fluid. This mechanism permits real-time tuning of the boiling curve, heat transfer coefficient, and critical heat flux without requiring changes to surface geometry or composition.

- describe method of selectively boiling a fluid

A method of selectively boiling a fluid is disclosed, wherein an electric potential is applied between a conductive boiling surface and a counter electrode immersed in the fluid. The potential is configured to induce electrostatic adsorption of oppositely charged surfactants onto the surface, thereby reducing wettability and enhancing nucleation, or to repel surfactants away from the surface, increasing wettability and suppressing nucleation. This method enables the selective activation or deactivation of boiling in specific regions of the surface, allowing for spatially resolved thermal control.

- describe method of forming a tunable boiling system

A method of forming a tunable boiling system comprises selecting a conductive substrate, coating or integrating it with a heating element, immersing it in a liquid containing a low concentration of ionic surfactant below the critical micelle concentration, and electrically connecting the substrate and a counter electrode to a programmable voltage source. The system is configured such that the applied voltage remains within a capacitive regime, avoiding redox reactions and ensuring reversible surfactant adsorption/desorption. The resulting system exhibits tunable boiling behavior responsive to changes in voltage magnitude and polarity.

- highlight features of the system

Key features of the system include its operation at voltages below 2.0 V, its compatibility with common aqueous solvents and surfactants, its absence of microfabricated nucleation structures, its sub-second temporal response, and its capability for spatial patterning using independently addressable electrodes. The system is scalable, robust over extended operational periods, and exhibits no measurable degradation under repeated cycling. It enables precise control over both the intensity and location of boiling activity, making it uniquely suited for adaptive thermal management applications.

## DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS

- describe tunable boiling system configuration

The tunable boiling system comprises a conductive boiling surface, an integrated resistive heater for uniform heat input, a counter electrode positioned within the boiling fluid, and a voltage control circuit capable of applying a precisely regulated electric potential between the surface and the counter electrode. The boiling surface is in contact with a liquid containing a low concentration of ionic surfactant, such as sodium dodecyl sulfate or dodecyl trimethylammonium bromide, which adsorbs electrostatically to the surface in response to the applied field. The system is enclosed in a sealed chamber to maintain saturated conditions and prevent evaporation loss, with a reflux condenser to recycle vapor and maintain constant fluid volume.

- introduce working electrode and counter electrode

The working electrode is formed from a conductive material such as silver, gold, copper, or platinum, and serves as the boiling interface. The counter electrode is typically a mesh or rod of titanium, stainless steel, or another inert conductive material, placed at a defined distance from the boiling surface to ensure uniform electric field distribution. The electrodes are connected to a programmable voltage source that can deliver constant, square wave, or triangular wave potentials with amplitudes ranging from −0.1 V to −2.0 V, selected to remain within the capacitive charging regime of the electric double layer.

- explain application of voltage to affect bubble formation

Application of a negative potential to a surface in contact with a negatively charged surfactant causes electrostatic repulsion of the surfactant molecules from the interface, increasing surface wettability and suppressing bubble nucleation. Conversely, application of a less negative or positive potential (relative to the surfactant charge) promotes surfactant adsorption, reducing wettability and enhancing nucleation. The same principle applies in reverse for positively charged surfactants. This reversible adsorption/desorption mechanism directly modulates the energy barrier for vapor nucleation, thereby controlling the onset of boiling and the associated heat transfer coefficient.

- describe surface tension and surfactant properties

The surfactants employed are ionic and possess a hydrophilic head group and a hydrophobic tail, enabling them to align at the liquid–solid interface and alter the effective contact angle. The concentration of surfactant is maintained below the critical micelle concentration to prevent bulk aggregation and preserve the electrostatic nature of the interaction. The adsorption isotherm of the surfactant is monotonic with respect to potential, resulting in a continuous, non-hysteretic modulation of wettability and nucleation behavior.

- discuss electrochemical considerations

The applied voltage is carefully selected to avoid Faradaic reactions, ensuring that the system behaves as a capacitive interface rather than an electrochemical cell. Cyclic voltammetry confirms the absence of redox peaks within the operational range, and current densities remain below 60 μA/cm², consistent with double-layer charging. This capacitive behavior ensures long-term stability and reversibility, with no observed degradation over extended operation exceeding ten hours.

- highlight importance of surfactant adsorption

Surfactant adsorption is the sole mechanism responsible for the observed modulation of boiling behavior. Control experiments with non-ionic surfactants and inert salts show no response to applied voltage, confirming that electrostatic interaction, not bulk property changes or EHD effects, drives the tunability. Contact angle measurements under boiling conditions directly correlate with nucleation behavior, demonstrating that surface wettability is the primary determinant of boiling performance.

- describe spatial and temporal control of boiling

The system enables spatial control by patterning the boiling surface into independently addressable electrode regions, each capable of receiving a distinct voltage. By switching individual electrodes between “on” and “off” states, boiling can be selectively activated or suppressed in discrete zones with sub-second precision. Temporal control is achieved through square wave or triangular wave voltage modulation, which induces corresponding oscillations in surface temperature and heat flux, enabling dynamic thermal regulation synchronized with external demands.

- introduce examples of materials and solvents

Suitable conductive materials for the boiling surface include silver, gold, copper, platinum, and titanium. The liquid medium may be deionized water or aqueous solutions with low ionic strength. Ionic surfactants such as SDS, DTAB, S10S, and analogous compounds with alkyl chain lengths between C8 and C16 are preferred. Non-ionic surfactants and salts such as NaBr and MEGA-10 serve as negative controls and are excluded from functional embodiments.

- discuss advantages of tunable boiling system

The system offers unprecedented advantages over conventional boiling technologies: it requires no micro- or nano-scale surface structuring, operates at low power, exhibits rapid response times, is fully reversible, and is compatible with scalable fabrication techniques. It enables optimization of heat transfer performance across a wide range of operating conditions, allowing a single device to function efficiently under both low- and high-heat-flux regimes by simply adjusting the applied voltage.

- describe boiling curves and heat transfer coefficient

Boiling curves for ionic surfactant solutions exhibit significant shifts in heat flux at fixed superheat when voltage is varied. For SDS, a more negative potential increases wettability and reduces nucleation, shifting the curve to higher superheat and lowering HTC. For DTAB, the same potential enhances nucleation, shifting the curve to lower superheat and increasing HTC. The vertical separation between curves at fixed superheat demonstrates tunability exceeding 1000% in HTC, while horizontal separation reveals temperature modulation exceeding 2°C at fixed heat flux.

- highlight tunability of boiling performance

The tunability of boiling performance is not limited to a single operating point but extends across the entire nucleate boiling regime. The system enables continuous adjustment of the boiling curve, allowing the operator to select optimal performance characteristics for specific thermal loads. This adaptability permits the same surface to serve as a high-efficiency cooler under low load and a high-CFH stabilizer under high load, a capability absent in all prior boiling systems.

- discuss potential applications

Potential applications include thermal management of high-power electronics, adaptive heat exchangers in renewable energy systems, distributed power generation, microfluidic pumps and valves, and industrial boiling processes requiring dynamic control. The system is particularly advantageous in systems with intermittent or variable thermal demands, where static surfaces lead to inefficiency or failure.

- describe system configuration and components

The system includes a conductive boiling substrate, a resistive heating element bonded to the backside of the substrate, a fluid containment chamber, a counter electrode, a programmable voltage source, thermocouples for temperature measurement, and a data acquisition system for monitoring current, voltage, and thermal response. The substrate may be fabricated via standard metallization and lithographic techniques on silicon or glass substrates, enabling integration with existing microsystems.

- explain experimental setup and procedure

Experiments are conducted in a pool boiling rig with a sealed chamber, reflux condenser, and temperature-controlled environment. Heat flux is applied via cartridge or thin-film heaters, and surface temperature is measured using embedded thermocouples. Voltage is applied between the boiling surface and counter electrode, and boiling curves are obtained by quasi-statically varying heater power while recording superheat and heat flux. High-speed imaging captures bubble dynamics, and contact angles are measured using a bubble sliding method.

- discuss results and observations

Results confirm that ionic surfactants enable reversible, voltage-controlled modulation of nucleation and HTC, while non-ionic additives show no response. The system exhibits hysteresis-free behavior within the capacitive regime, and spatial control is demonstrated with eight independently addressable electrodes. Thermal response times are under 600 ms, and no degradation is observed after prolonged operation.

- highlight significance of results

The significance lies in the demonstration that boiling, a process long considered inherently passive, can be actively and reversibly controlled using simple, low-power electric fields and common surfactants. This discovery overturns conventional assumptions about boiling system design and opens a new paradigm for adaptive thermal management.

## EXAMPLES

- introduce experimental setup and procedure

Experimental validation was conducted using a pool boiling apparatus with a 2 cm × 2 cm silver surface, a titanium counter electrode, and a programmable voltage source capable of delivering square wave and triangular wave potentials between −0.1 V and −2.0 V. The fluid consisted of deionized water with 2.6 mM concentrations of SDS, DTAB, NaBr, or MEGA-10. Heat flux was maintained at 60 W, and surface temperature was recorded using four embedded thermocouples. Current was measured using a current follower op-amp, and boiling behavior was imaged using a high-speed camera at 120 fps.

- describe surfactant properties and concentrations

All surfactants were sourced from Sigma-Aldrich and dissolved in deionized water at 173 mM stock concentration, then diluted to 2.6 mM for testing. SDS and DTAB are ionic surfactants with critical micelle concentrations above 8 mM. NaBr is a non-surfactant salt, and MEGA-10 is a non-ionic surfactant. All solutions were sonicated for one hour at 40°C to ensure homogeneity.

- explain boiling curve measurements

Boiling curves were obtained by slowly decreasing heater power from a superheated state, allowing the surface to cool while recording heat flux and surface temperature. Each curve represents a descending measurement over a period of approximately 30 minutes, with cooling rates of 1–2°C per minute. Measurements were repeated at two voltage conditions: −0.1 V and −2.0 V.

- discuss results for NaBr and MEGA-10 solutions

For NaBr and MEGA-10, no significant change in boiling curve, surface temperature, or HTC was observed between −0.1 V and −2.0 V. Contact angles remained constant, and current responses showed no capacitive signature, confirming that electrostatic surfactant adsorption is necessary for tunability.

- discuss results for DTAB and SDS solutions

For SDS, increasing the negative potential from −0.1 V to −2.0 V increased surface wettability and suppressed nucleation, shifting the boiling curve to higher superheat and reducing HTC by up to 96%. For DTAB, the same potential increased nucleation, shifting the curve to lower superheat and increasing HTC by up to 1100%. The thermal response was inversely correlated with voltage for SDS and directly correlated for DTAB, confirming the role of surfactant charge.

- describe photographs of boiling system

Photographs captured under boiling conditions show a dramatic reduction in bubble density at −2.0 V for SDS and a marked increase for DTAB. In spatially controlled experiments, bubbles were observed only over electrodes set to the “on” state, with complete suppression over “off” electrodes, demonstrating precise spatial control.

- explain experimental setup and components

The spatially controlled system employed a silicon wafer with eight gold electrodes, each insulated by a 1 μm silicon dioxide layer and heated by a platinum thin-film resistor. Each electrode was independently connected to a voltage switch controlled by a data acquisition system, allowing real-time selection between −0.1 V and −2.0 V.

- discuss thermal response to voltage changes

Thermal response to square wave voltage switching showed a clear lag between voltage transition and temperature change, with rise and fall times of approximately 300–600 ms, consistent with surfactant adsorption/desorption kinetics. Temperature oscillations mirrored the voltage waveform, confirming direct coupling between electric field and thermal output.

- describe square wave voltage application

Square wave voltage with 60-second periods was applied to SDS and DTAB solutions. Current response confirmed capacitive behavior, and temperature oscillations were in-phase with HTC for DTAB and out-of-phase for SDS, validating the proposed mechanism.

- discuss results for DTAB and SDS solutions

DTAB exhibited a strong positive correlation between voltage magnitude and HTC, while SDS exhibited a strong negative correlation. No such correlation was observed for non-ionic or ionic non-surfactant controls.

- highlight significance of results

The results conclusively demonstrate that boiling can be turned on and off with sub-second precision using low-voltage electric fields and ionic surfactants. This represents the first demonstration of true, reversible, and spatially programmable boiling control without surface structuring.

- introduce spatially controlled boiling system

A spatially controlled boiling system was fabricated with eight independently addressable electrodes on a silicon substrate. Each electrode could be switched between “on” (−2.0 V for DTAB) and “off” (−0.1 V) states, enabling selective activation of boiling in discrete regions.

- describe experimental setup and components

The setup included a custom voltage switching matrix, a high-power supply for uniform heating, and a high-speed imaging system. The electrodes were spaced 5 mm apart, and each was heated with a constant 1.5 W/cm² flux.

- explain voltage control and switching

Voltage control was achieved using SPDT relays driven by digital outputs from a LabJack data acquisition unit. Each electrode received either −0.1 V or −2.0 V based on programmed sequences, enabling complex boiling patterns to be generated.

- describe photographs of boiling system

Photographs show boiling confined exclusively to electrodes set to the “on” state, with adjacent “off” electrodes remaining completely bubble-free. The transition between states occurred within 500 ms, demonstrating precise spatial and temporal control.

- discuss results and observations

Boiling was activated and deactivated in any sequence of electrodes without cross-talk or thermal interference. The system maintained stability over 1000 switching cycles with no degradation in performance.

- highlight significance of results

The ability to spatially control boiling at the millimeter scale using simple voltage switching represents a transformative advancement in thermal management, enabling next-generation cooling systems with programmable heat dissipation patterns.

- conclude examples

The examples confirm that the invention enables unprecedented control over boiling phenomena through electrostatic modulation of surfactant adsorption. The system is robust, scalable, reversible, and applicable across a broad range of thermal management challenges.
