## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to composition for raising immune response against coronavirus infection

The present invention relates to novel compositions and methods for inducing a protective immune response against infection by coronaviruses, particularly SARS-CoV-2 and its variants. The invention specifically concerns attenuated poxvirus vectors engineered to express one or more structural proteins of SARS-CoV-2, including the spike, membrane, nucleocapsid, and envelope proteins, in a manner that elicits robust humoral and cellular immunity without the risk of replication in human hosts. These compositions are designed for use as prophylactic vaccines to prevent infection, reduce viral shedding, and mitigate disease severity in individuals exposed to SARS-CoV-2 or related coronaviruses. The invention further encompasses formulations suitable for intramuscular, intranasal, or subcutaneous administration, and is particularly suited for deployment in settings where cold chain logistics are limited, due to the thermal stability of the poxvirus platform. The compositions are not intended for therapeutic use in established infection but are specifically configured to prime and boost immune memory prior to or shortly after exposure to circulating viral strains.

## BACKGROUND

- introduce poxvirus family

The poxvirus family comprises large, double-stranded DNA viruses that replicate entirely in the cytoplasm of infected cells, a feature that distinguishes them from most other DNA viruses and contributes to their safety profile as vaccine vectors. These viruses are characterized by their brick-shaped virions, complex internal structures, and the ability to encode numerous proteins involved in immune evasion, host range determination, and virion assembly. Poxviruses have been historically used as live vaccines, most notably vaccinia virus in the global eradication of smallpox, and continue to be explored for their capacity to induce durable, multifaceted immune responses against a broad array of pathogens. Their large genome size permits the stable insertion of multiple foreign genes without compromising viral viability, and their cytoplasmic replication avoids the risk of genomic integration into host DNA, a concern associated with some viral vector platforms. The natural tropism of poxviruses for antigen-presenting cells, particularly dendritic cells, further enhances their immunogenicity, making them ideal candidates for next-generation vaccine development.

- describe Chordopoxvirinae subfamily

The Chordopoxvirinae subfamily encompasses all poxviruses known to infect vertebrates and includes several genera of biomedical importance, such as Orthopoxvirus, Avipoxvirus, Leporipoxvirus, and Molluscipoxvirus. These viruses share conserved genomic architecture, replication mechanisms, and structural proteins, yet exhibit distinct host ranges and pathogenic profiles. The subfamily is distinguished by the presence of a conserved set of core genes involved in DNA replication, transcription, and virion morphogenesis, as well as a variable set of host-range and immune-modulatory genes that determine tissue tropism and virulence. The evolutionary conservation of these core elements allows for the rational engineering of chimeric vectors derived from one species to serve as delivery platforms for antigens from unrelated pathogens, while retaining the essential replication machinery required for production in permissive cell lines.

- mention Orthopoxviridae and Avipoxviridae genera

Within the Chordopoxvirinae, the Orthopoxvirus genus includes vaccinia virus, variola virus, monkeypox virus, and cowpox virus, all of which have been extensively studied for their immunogenic properties and vaccine applications. The Avipoxvirus genus, comprising fowlpox virus and canarypox virus, is notable for its inability to replicate in mammalian cells, rendering it inherently safe for use in humans and other mammals, albeit often less immunogenic as a standalone vector. Both genera have been exploited as vaccine backbones, with Orthopoxviruses offering superior immunogenicity due to their ability to replicate in mammalian cells during production, and Avipoxviruses providing a non-replicating alternative suitable for use in individuals with pre-existing immunity to vaccinia. The present invention leverages the advantages of both by utilizing a replication-deficient Orthopoxvirus that retains the immunostimulatory properties of its parental strain while eliminating the capacity for productive infection in the vaccine recipient.

- discuss use of recombinant vaccinia-based viruses as vaccine vectors

Recombinant vaccinia-based viruses have served as pioneering platforms for the delivery of heterologous antigens since the 1980s, with early constructs expressing antigens from rabies, HIV, and hepatitis B viruses. These vectors have demonstrated the capacity to induce strong CD8+ T cell responses, antibody production, and long-lived memory, largely due to their ability to infect dendritic cells and promote antigen presentation via both MHC class I and class II pathways. However, the clinical utility of first-generation vaccinia vectors was limited by the presence of pre-existing immunity in populations previously vaccinated against smallpox, as well as safety concerns in immunocompromised individuals. Subsequent generations of vectors, including modified vaccinia Ankara (MVA) and NYVAC, were attenuated through extensive passaging or targeted gene deletions to reduce pathogenicity while preserving immunogenicity. These improvements have enabled their use in clinical trials for cancer immunotherapy, malaria, and emerging infectious diseases, establishing poxvirus vectors as a reliable and versatile tool in modern vaccinology.

- describe poxvirus life cycle

The poxvirus life cycle begins with attachment to cell surface receptors, followed by fusion of the viral membrane with the host plasma membrane or endosomal membrane, depending on the virion form. Upon entry, the viral core is released into the cytoplasm, where early gene transcription is initiated by virion-associated RNA polymerase and transcription factors. Early genes encode proteins necessary for viral DNA replication, immune evasion, and modulation of host cell functions. Subsequent DNA replication occurs in cytoplasmic factories, after which intermediate and late genes are expressed, directing the assembly of new virions. The late phase includes the synthesis of structural proteins, the formation of immature virions, and their maturation into intracellular mature virions or extracellular enveloped virions. This entire process occurs independently of the host nucleus, allowing for efficient replication even in non-dividing cells. The cytoplasmic nature of replication and the absence of integration into the host genome make poxviruses uniquely suited for vaccine development, as they avoid genotoxic risks and enable high-titer production in standard cell culture systems.

- introduce host range factors

Host range factors are viral genes that determine the spectrum of cell types and species in which a poxvirus can replicate. These include genes encoding proteins that counteract host antiviral defenses, such as interferon response inhibitors, apoptosis blockers, and cytokine modulators. In vaccinia virus, key host range factors include the C7L, K1L, and A41L genes, which permit replication in human and rodent cells by suppressing innate immune sensing and signaling. Deletion of these genes can restrict viral replication to specific permissive cell lines used for manufacturing, while simultaneously enhancing the safety profile of the vector in vaccine recipients. The strategic removal of host range genes not only attenuates the virus but also reduces the potential for off-target immune modulation, allowing the immune response to focus more precisely on the encoded heterologous antigens.

- review host range genes

Host range genes in vaccinia virus are distributed throughout the genome and vary in function, with some acting as direct antagonists of host restriction factors, while others modulate signaling pathways involved in inflammation and cell survival. The K1L gene inhibits NF-κB activation and prevents apoptosis in human cells, while the C7L gene counteracts the antiviral effects of interferon-induced proteins. The A41L gene encodes a chemokine-binding protein that interferes with leukocyte recruitment, and the B15R gene encodes a soluble interleukin-1β receptor that dampens inflammatory responses. The deletion of multiple host range genes, such as A41L, K1L, and C7L, has been shown to synergistically restrict viral replication in vivo while preserving the ability to express foreign antigens at high levels in permissive cell lines. These deletions are central to the design of the Sementis Copenhagen Vector described herein, which is engineered to be replication-incompetent in human tissues while retaining full immunogenicity.

- discuss vaccinia strains used as smallpox vaccines

Historically, the smallpox vaccine was derived from the live vaccinia virus, with strains such as Dryvax, ACAM2000, and LC16m8 used in mass immunization campaigns. These strains differ in their virulence, replication kinetics, and immunogenicity profiles. ACAM2000, for example, is a clonal derivative of Dryvax that retains full replicative capacity in human cells and induces potent immunity but carries a risk of adverse events in immunocompromised individuals. In contrast, MVA, derived from a highly passaged vaccinia strain, is replication-deficient in human cells and has been approved for use in populations with contraindications to traditional smallpox vaccines. The present invention builds upon this legacy by introducing a novel attenuated vaccinia vector, Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV), which combines the high-yield production characteristics of the Copenhagen strain with targeted deletions that render it incapable of producing infectious progeny in human hosts, thereby offering a superior balance of safety, scalability, and immunogenicity.

- introduce Modified Vaccinia Ankara (MVA)

Modified Vaccinia Ankara (MVA) is a highly attenuated strain of vaccinia virus generated by more than 570 serial passages in chicken embryo fibroblasts, resulting in the loss of approximately 15% of its genome, including numerous host range and immune evasion genes. MVA cannot replicate in most mammalian cells due to post-entry blocks in viral gene expression and virion maturation, yet it retains the ability to enter cells, express transgenes, and stimulate robust innate and adaptive immune responses. MVA has been widely used in clinical trials for HIV, tuberculosis, and cancer vaccines, and is currently licensed as a smallpox vaccine under the names IMVANEX and IMVAMUNE. Its safety profile, particularly in immunocompromised individuals, has made it a preferred vector for global health applications. However, MVA’s production requires chicken embryo fibroblasts, which pose challenges for scalability, regulatory compliance, and consistency in biomanufacturing.

- describe MVA development

MVA was developed in the 1960s by passaging the Ankara strain of vaccinia virus over 570 times in primary chicken embryo fibroblasts, a process that selected for mutations and deletions that impaired its ability to replicate in mammalian cells. Genomic sequencing revealed the loss of 31 open reading frames, many of which encode proteins involved in host immune evasion, virion assembly, and host range determination. These deletions rendered MVA unable to complete its replication cycle in human cells, although it retains the capacity to initiate transcription, translate transgenes, and assemble incomplete virions that stimulate immune recognition. The resulting vector is non-pathogenic, non-replicating, and highly immunogenic, making it ideal for use in vulnerable populations. However, its reliance on avian cell lines for production limits its compatibility with established mammalian biomanufacturing infrastructure, a limitation addressed by the present invention.

- discuss MVA as a vector for heterologous molecules

MVA has been extensively engineered to express a wide variety of heterologous antigens, including viral glycoproteins, tumor-associated antigens, and parasitic proteins. Its ability to induce both humoral and cellular immunity has been demonstrated in numerous preclinical and clinical studies, particularly in prime-boost regimens with DNA or adenoviral vectors. However, its low replication efficiency in mammalian cells results in lower antigen expression levels compared to replication-competent vectors, necessitating higher doses or adjuvantation to achieve protective immunity. Additionally, pre-existing immunity to MVA, although less common than to traditional vaccinia strains, can still dampen responses in individuals previously exposed to smallpox vaccination. The present invention overcomes these limitations by utilizing a replication-deficient but production-competent vaccinia vector derived from the Copenhagen strain, which enables high-titer production in CHO cells and avoids the immunological interference associated with MVA.

- introduce Sementis Copenhagen Vaccinia (SCV) vaccinia virus vector

The Sementis Copenhagen Vaccinia (SCV) vector is a novel, replication-deficient vaccinia virus derived from the Copenhagen strain, engineered through targeted deletion of the D13L gene, which encodes a critical scaffolding protein required for virion assembly. This deletion renders SCV incapable of producing infectious progeny in normal mammalian cells, while allowing for high-yield production in complementing cell lines that express D13L and the host range protein CP77. Unlike MVA, SCV retains a near-complete genome, preserving the full complement of immunostimulatory genes and regulatory elements that enhance antigen presentation and immune activation. The SCV platform has been successfully used to deliver antigens from chikungunya and Zika viruses, demonstrating potent immunogenicity and protection in mouse and non-human primate models following a single dose. The present invention extends this platform to the delivery of SARS-CoV-2 antigens, leveraging SCV’s scalability, safety, and immunogenicity for next-generation coronavirus vaccination.

- describe SCV advantages

The SCV vector offers several critical advantages over existing poxvirus platforms. First, it is produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells, which are widely used in the biopharmaceutical industry for monoclonal antibody and recombinant protein production, enabling seamless scale-up under current good manufacturing practices. Second, SCV’s deletion of D13L ensures complete replication deficiency in humans, eliminating the risk of vaccine-induced disease even in immunocompromised individuals. Third, the retention of a near-full-length genome preserves the intrinsic adjuvant properties of vaccinia virus, including the activation of Toll-like receptors and inflammasome pathways, resulting in robust innate immune stimulation without the need for external adjuvants. Fourth, SCV can be administered via needle-free delivery systems, including jet injectors and microneedle patches, enhancing compliance and reducing the burden of cold chain logistics. Finally, the vector’s ability to express multiple antigens from a single construct allows for the development of multivalent vaccines that target conserved epitopes across SARS-CoV-2 variants and related coronaviruses.

- introduce coronaviruses

Coronaviruses are a family of enveloped, positive-sense, single-stranded RNA viruses characterized by a crown-like appearance under electron microscopy, due to the presence of spike glycoproteins projecting from their surface. They infect a wide range of mammals and birds and are responsible for a spectrum of diseases, from mild upper respiratory infections to severe systemic illness. Four human coronaviruses—HCoV-229E, HCoV-OC43, HCoV-NL63, and HCoV-HKU1—are endemic and typically cause common colds. Three additional coronaviruses—SARS-CoV, MERS-CoV, and SARS-CoV-2—have emerged as zoonotic pathogens with pandemic potential, causing severe acute respiratory syndrome with high mortality rates. The emergence of SARS-CoV-2 in late 2019 led to a global public health crisis, underscoring the urgent need for safe, effective, and broadly protective vaccines.

- describe coronavirus structure

The coronavirus virion is approximately 80–120 nanometers in diameter and consists of a lipid bilayer envelope derived from the host endoplasmic reticulum-Golgi intermediate compartment. Embedded within this envelope are four structural proteins: the spike (S) protein, which mediates receptor binding and membrane fusion; the membrane (M) protein, which shapes the virion and organizes assembly; the envelope (E) protein, which facilitates virion budding and ion channel activity; and the nucleocapsid (N) protein, which encapsidates the viral RNA genome into a helical ribonucleoprotein complex. The spike protein is the primary target of neutralizing antibodies and is composed of two subunits, S1 and S2, with S1 containing the receptor-binding domain that interacts with the human angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) receptor. The structural proteins are encoded within a conserved genomic region and are highly immunogenic, making them ideal candidates for vaccine development.

- discuss coronavirus life cycle

The coronavirus life cycle begins with attachment of the spike protein to the ACE2 receptor on host cells, followed by proteolytic cleavage by host proteases such as TMPRSS2, which triggers fusion of the viral and cellular membranes. The viral genome is released into the cytoplasm and translated into two large polyproteins, pp1a and pp1ab, which are processed by viral proteases into non-structural proteins that form the replication-transcription complex. This complex synthesizes a nested set of subgenomic mRNAs encoding the structural and accessory proteins. The structural proteins are transported to the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus, where they assemble into new virions. These virions are then packaged into vesicles and released from the cell via exocytosis. The entire replication cycle occurs in the cytoplasm and does not involve integration into the host genome, making coronaviruses amenable to vaccine strategies that target their structural proteins without risk of insertional mutagenesis.

- introduce human coronaviruses

Human coronaviruses include both endemic strains that cause mild respiratory illness and highly pathogenic strains that have caused global outbreaks. The endemic strains—HCoV-229E, HCoV-OC43, HCoV-NL63, and HCoV-HKU1—are widespread and typically result in self-limiting upper respiratory tract infections. In contrast, SARS-CoV, which emerged in 2002, caused a global outbreak with a case fatality rate of approximately 10%. MERS-CoV, first identified in 2012, continues to cause sporadic outbreaks in the Arabian Peninsula with a case fatality rate exceeding 35%. The most recent and devastating member of this family, SARS-CoV-2, emerged in late 2019 and has caused over 700 million confirmed infections and more than 7 million deaths worldwide as of 2024, highlighting the critical need for next-generation vaccines capable of inducing broad, durable, and variant-resistant immunity.

- discuss SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV

SARS-CoV and MERS-CoV are zoonotic coronaviruses that originated in bats and were transmitted to humans via intermediate hosts—civet cats for SARS-CoV and dromedary camels for MERS-CoV. Both viruses bind to distinct cellular receptors: SARS-CoV uses ACE2, while MERS-CoV uses dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP4). Despite differences in receptor usage, both viruses induce severe pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome, and multi-organ failure in susceptible individuals. Vaccines developed for these viruses, including recombinant spike protein subunit vaccines and viral vector platforms, demonstrated efficacy in animal models but were never widely deployed due to the containment of outbreaks. However, the immunological insights gained from these efforts have directly informed the design of SARS-CoV-2 vaccines, particularly the focus on the spike protein as the primary target for neutralizing antibodies.

- introduce COVID-19 pandemic

The COVID-19 pandemic, caused by SARS-CoV-2, represents the most severe global health crisis of the 21st century. It emerged in Wuhan, China, in December 2019 and rapidly spread across all continents, overwhelming healthcare systems and causing unprecedented social and economic disruption. The virus’s high transmissibility, asymptomatic shedding, and capacity for immune evasion through mutation have made containment challenging. While mRNA and adenoviral vector vaccines were developed with remarkable speed and demonstrated high efficacy against early variants, their effectiveness has waned against newer Omicron sublineages, underscoring the need for vaccines that induce broader, more durable immunity targeting conserved epitopes beyond the spike protein.

- discuss SARS-CoV-2 characteristics

SARS-CoV-2 is a betacoronavirus with a genome of approximately 30 kilobases, encoding 29 proteins, including four structural proteins and 15 non-structural and accessory proteins. The spike protein is the most variable, with mutations accumulating in the receptor-binding domain and N-terminal domain to evade neutralizing antibodies. The nucleocapsid protein, in contrast, is highly conserved across variants and is a major target of T cell responses. The virus exhibits a high mutation rate due to the error-prone nature of its RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, leading to the emergence of variants of concern such as Alpha, Delta, and Omicron. These variants differ in transmissibility, immune escape, and disease severity, necessitating vaccines capable of eliciting cross-reactive immunity against multiple epitopes.

- describe SARS-CoV-2 transmission

SARS-CoV-2 is primarily transmitted through respiratory droplets and aerosols generated during speaking, coughing, or breathing, particularly in poorly ventilated indoor environments. Asymptomatic and presymptomatic individuals contribute significantly to transmission, complicating containment efforts. The virus can also survive on surfaces for extended periods, although fomite transmission is considered a minor route. The reproductive number (R0) of early strains was estimated at 2.5–3.0, while the Omicron variant exhibited an R0 exceeding 10, making it one of the most contagious human pathogens known. Vaccines that reduce viral load and duration of shedding are therefore critical to interrupting transmission chains and achieving herd immunity.

- discuss SARS-CoV-2 reproductive number

The basic reproductive number (R0) of SARS-CoV-2 has varied significantly across variants, with early strains exhibiting an R0 of approximately 2.5–3.0, while the Omicron BA.1 variant reached an R0 of 8–10, and subsequent sublineages such as BA.2.86 and JN.1 have shown even higher transmissibility. This high R0 reflects the virus’s ability to replicate rapidly in the upper respiratory tract, shed virus before symptom onset, and evade pre-existing immunity through antigenic drift. Effective vaccines must therefore not only prevent disease but also reduce viral replication and shedding, a goal best achieved by inducing mucosal immunity and broad T cell responses targeting conserved internal proteins such as nucleocapsid and membrane.

- introduce SARS-CoV-2 spike protein

The spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 is a trimeric class I fusion glycoprotein that mediates viral entry into host cells by binding to the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) receptor. It is composed of two functional subunits: S1, which contains the receptor-binding domain (RBD), and S2, which facilitates membrane fusion. The RBD is the primary target of neutralizing antibodies, and mutations in this region—such as those found in the Omicron lineage—have enabled immune escape from vaccine-induced and naturally acquired antibodies. Despite this variability, conserved epitopes within the S2 subunit and the stem helix region remain targets for broadly neutralizing antibodies, making the spike protein a critical, albeit complex, antigen for vaccine design.

- describe SARS-CoV-2 spike protein structure

The spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 adopts a prefusion conformation stabilized by a furin cleavage site between S1 and S2, which is essential for priming the protein for membrane fusion. The trimeric structure is stabilized by extensive inter-subunit interactions and glycosylation at over 20 N-linked glycosylation sites, which shield conserved epitopes from antibody recognition. The receptor-binding domain is located within the S1 subunit and undergoes conformational changes upon ACE2 binding, exposing the fusion peptide in S2. The spike protein’s structural plasticity allows it to evade neutralizing antibodies while maintaining functionality, necessitating vaccine strategies that target multiple epitopes, including conserved regions of S2 and the fusion peptide, to induce broad and durable immunity.

- discuss SARS-CoV-2 spike protein function

The primary function of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein is to mediate attachment to host cells via the ACE2 receptor and facilitate fusion of the viral and cellular membranes to enable genome release. It is also the dominant target of the humoral immune response, with neutralizing antibodies blocking receptor binding or preventing conformational changes required for fusion. However, the spike protein’s high mutation rate, particularly in the RBD, has led to the emergence of immune-evasive variants. Furthermore, the spike protein can induce antibody-dependent enhancement in certain contexts, raising concerns about vaccine safety. The present invention addresses these challenges by incorporating multiple structural proteins into a single vector, thereby diversifying the immune response and reducing reliance on the highly variable spike protein alone.

- introduce SARS-CoV-2 membrane protein

The membrane (M) protein of SARS-CoV-2 is the most abundant structural protein in the virion and plays a central role in viral assembly by interacting with the nucleocapsid, spike, and envelope proteins. It is a type III transmembrane protein with a short N-terminal ectodomain, three transmembrane domains, and a long C-terminal endodomain that binds to the nucleocapsid RNA complex. Although less immunogenic than the spike protein, the M protein is highly conserved across variants and is a major target of T cell responses, particularly CD4+ and CD8+ T cells. Antibodies against the M protein do not neutralize the virus directly but may contribute to protection through opsonization, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, and enhancement of antigen presentation.

- describe SARS-CoV-2 membrane protein function

The membrane protein functions as the central organizer of coronavirus assembly, mediating interactions between the nucleocapsid and the envelope proteins to ensure proper virion formation. It also modulates host immune responses by inhibiting interferon signaling and suppressing antigen presentation in infected cells. Despite its immune-suppressive properties, the M protein is highly conserved across SARS-CoV-2 variants and contains multiple T cell epitopes that are recognized across diverse HLA backgrounds. Its inclusion in vaccine constructs enhances the breadth and durability of cellular immunity, particularly in individuals with waning spike-specific responses. The present invention exploits this conserved immunogenicity by incorporating the M protein into recombinant poxvirus vectors to complement spike-specific immunity.

- introduce SARS-CoV-2 envelope protein

The envelope (E) protein of SARS-CoV-2 is a small, multifunctional transmembrane protein that plays a critical role in virion assembly, budding, and pathogenesis. It forms a pentameric ion channel in the Golgi membrane, which modulates ion flux and promotes virion release. The E protein also contributes to inflammation and immune evasion by activating the NLRP3 inflammasome and suppressing interferon responses. Although present in low copy numbers in the virion, the E protein is highly conserved and contains immunodominant T cell epitopes recognized in convalescent individuals. Its inclusion in vaccine formulations enhances the diversity of the immune response and may contribute to protection against severe disease by targeting conserved, functionally critical regions of the virus.

- describe SARS-CoV-2 envelope protein function

The envelope protein functions primarily in virion morphogenesis and release, interacting with the membrane protein to facilitate the curvature of the viral envelope during budding. It also modulates host cell physiology by altering ion homeostasis and promoting the secretion of pro-inflammatory cytokines, contributing to the cytokine storm observed in severe COVID-19. Despite its small size, the E protein is a target of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and has been shown to elicit protective immunity in animal models when delivered as a recombinant antigen. Its inclusion in the present invention provides an additional layer of immune recognition that complements responses to spike and nucleocapsid proteins, reducing the likelihood of immune escape and enhancing overall vaccine efficacy.

- introduce SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein

The nucleocapsid (N) protein of SARS-CoV-2 is a multifunctional RNA-binding protein that packages the viral genome into a helical ribonucleoprotein complex and plays a central role in viral replication and transcription. It is the most abundant protein in infected cells and is highly conserved across variants, with sequence identity exceeding 95% between the original Wuhan strain and current Omicron sublineages. The N protein is a dominant target of T cell responses in both vaccinated and convalescent individuals, and its conservation makes it an ideal candidate for vaccines designed to provide broad protection against evolving strains.

- describe SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein function

The nucleocapsid protein binds to the viral RNA genome via its N-terminal RNA-binding domain and oligomerizes via its C-terminal dimerization domain to form the ribonucleoprotein core. It also interacts with the membrane protein to facilitate virion assembly and modulates host cell processes by inhibiting interferon signaling, suppressing RNA interference, and altering cell cycle progression. Although not a target of neutralizing antibodies, the nucleocapsid protein is a major source of CD4+ and CD8+ T cell epitopes, particularly in individuals with prior exposure to endemic coronaviruses. Its inclusion in the present invention enhances the breadth of cellular immunity and provides a stable antigenic target that is less susceptible to immune escape than the spike protein.

- discuss SARS-CoV-2 evolution

SARS-CoV-2 continues to evolve through point mutations, recombination events, and selective pressures exerted by population immunity. The emergence of variants such as Alpha, Delta, and Omicron has demonstrated the virus’s capacity to evade neutralizing antibodies while maintaining or enhancing transmissibility. Mutations in the spike protein, particularly in the receptor-binding domain, have been the primary drivers of immune escape, but mutations in other regions, including the nucleocapsid and membrane proteins, have also been observed. The present invention addresses this evolutionary challenge by incorporating multiple conserved antigens into a single vector, ensuring that immune pressure is distributed across multiple targets and reducing the likelihood of complete immune evasion.

- introduce SARS-CoV-2 mutations

Mutations in SARS-CoV-2 arise due to the error-prone nature of its RNA-dependent RNA polymerase, which lacks proofreading activity. These mutations accumulate over time, leading to the emergence of new lineages with altered antigenic profiles. Key mutations in the spike protein, such as E484K, N501Y, and L452R, have been associated with increased transmissibility and reduced neutralization by vaccine-induced antibodies. Mutations in the nucleocapsid protein, such as R203K and G204R, have been linked to enhanced viral replication and immune evasion. The present invention mitigates the impact of these mutations by encoding multiple conserved antigens, ensuring that immune responses remain effective even as individual epitopes change.

- discuss SARS-CoV-2 variants

SARS-CoV-2 variants of concern are classified based on their increased transmissibility, disease severity, or ability to evade immunity. The Alpha variant (B.1.1.7) exhibited increased transmissibility, while Delta (B.1.617.2) caused more severe disease and reduced vaccine efficacy. The Omicron variant (B.1.1.529) and its descendants, including BA.2, BA.4, BA.5, XBB, and JN.1, have demonstrated unprecedented immune escape, with neutralizing antibody titers reduced by up to 20-fold compared to ancestral strains. Despite this, T cell responses targeting conserved regions of the nucleocapsid and membrane proteins remain largely intact. The present invention leverages this observation by incorporating these conserved antigens into a single vaccine platform to maintain protection against severe disease even as neutralizing antibody titers decline.

- introduce neutralizing antibodies

Neutralizing antibodies are immunoglobulins that bind to viral surface proteins and prevent infection by blocking receptor interaction or inhibiting membrane fusion. In the context of SARS-CoV-2, neutralizing antibodies primarily target the receptor-binding domain of the spike protein and are the principal correlate of protection against infection. However, their efficacy diminishes over time and is significantly reduced against variants with mutations in the RBD. The present invention enhances durability of protection by combining neutralizing antibody induction with robust T cell responses directed against conserved internal proteins, ensuring long-term immunity even in the face of antigenic drift.

- discuss T cell immunity

T cell immunity plays a critical role in controlling viral replication, clearing infected cells, and preventing severe disease. CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes recognize viral peptides presented on MHC class I molecules and directly kill infected cells, while CD4+ helper T cells support B cell antibody production, promote memory formation, and sustain cytotoxic responses. In SARS-CoV-2 infection, T cell responses are more resilient to variant-driven immune escape than antibody responses, as they target conserved epitopes across multiple viral proteins. The present invention is engineered to elicit strong, polyfunctional T cell responses against spike, membrane, and nucleocapsid proteins, ensuring protection even when neutralizing antibodies are insufficient.

- introduce CD4 and CD8 T cells

CD4+ T cells, also known as helper T cells, orchestrate adaptive immune responses by secreting cytokines that activate B cells, macrophages, and CD8+ T cells. CD8+ T cells, or cytotoxic T lymphocytes, directly eliminate virus-infected cells by recognizing peptide-MHC class I complexes and releasing perforin and granzymes. Both subsets are critical for long-term immunity against SARS-CoV-2, with CD4+ T cells supporting the generation of high-affinity antibodies and CD8+ T cells providing rapid clearance of infected cells. The present invention induces robust activation of both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells through the expression of multiple conserved antigens, ensuring comprehensive immune coverage.

- discuss Th1 polarized CD4 T cells

Th1-polarized CD4+ T cells are a subset of helper T cells that secrete interferon-gamma, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and interleukin-2, promoting cell-mediated immunity and IgG2a antibody isotype switching. This polarization is critical for effective antiviral immunity, as it enhances macrophage activation, cytotoxic T cell function, and viral clearance. Vaccines that induce a Th1-biased response are associated with reduced risk of antibody-dependent enhancement and improved protection against intracellular pathogens. The present invention, through its use of a poxvirus vector, preferentially induces a Th1-biased immune response, making it particularly suited for protection against SARS-CoV-2.

- introduce T follicular helper cells

T follicular helper (Tfh) cells are a specialized subset of CD4+ T cells that migrate to germinal centers in lymphoid tissues to provide help to B cells during antibody affinity maturation and class switching. Tfh cells are essential for the generation of long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells, and their presence correlates with durable humoral immunity. The present invention promotes the expansion of Tfh cells through the sustained expression of viral antigens and the activation of dendritic cells, resulting in high-quality, long-lasting antibody responses against SARS-CoV-2 structural proteins.

- discuss germinal center responses

Germinal centers are specialized microstructures within secondary lymphoid organs where B cells undergo somatic hypermutation, affinity maturation, and class switching under the guidance of T follicular helper cells. Robust germinal center responses are associated with the generation of high-affinity, broadly neutralizing antibodies and long-term immunological memory. The present invention, by delivering antigens in the context of a potent viral vector, induces strong and sustained germinal center reactions in lymph nodes and spleen, resulting in durable antibody titers and memory B cell pools that persist for months to years.

- introduce humoral immune responses

Humoral immune responses are mediated by antibodies produced by B cells and are essential for neutralizing extracellular pathogens and preventing reinfection. In the case of SARS-CoV-2, humoral immunity primarily targets the spike protein, but responses to the membrane and nucleocapsid proteins also contribute to protection through opsonization and complement activation. The present invention enhances humoral immunity by expressing antigens in a form that mimics their native conformation on the virion, promoting the induction of high-titer, high-affinity neutralizing antibodies.

- discuss Th1-biased response

A Th1-biased immune response is characterized by the production of interferon-gamma, interleukin-2, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, which promote cell-mediated immunity and IgG2a antibody isotypes. This response is optimal for combating intracellular pathogens such as SARS-CoV-2, as it enhances macrophage activation, cytotoxic T cell function, and viral clearance. The present invention, through its use of a poxvirus vector, naturally induces a Th1-biased response, avoiding the Th2-skewed responses associated with enhanced disease in some vaccine platforms.

- introduce antibody-dependent enhancement of disease

Antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) is a phenomenon in which non-neutralizing or sub-neutralizing antibodies facilitate viral entry into immune cells via Fc receptors, leading to increased viral replication and exacerbated disease. ADE has been observed in dengue virus and has raised concerns in the development of coronavirus vaccines. The present invention minimizes the risk of ADE by inducing high-titer neutralizing antibodies and a strong Th1-biased T cell response, which suppresses aberrant immune activation and ensures that antibody responses are protective rather than pathogenic.

- discuss vaccine development

Vaccine development for SARS-CoV-2 has been unprecedented in speed and scale, with multiple platforms—including mRNA, adenoviral vectors, protein subunits, and inactivated viruses—receiving emergency authorization. However, challenges remain in achieving durable, variant-resistant immunity, particularly in immunocompromised and elderly populations. The present invention addresses these challenges by combining the safety of a replication-deficient vector with the immunogenicity of multiple conserved antigens, offering a platform capable of inducing broad, long-lasting protection against current and future SARS-CoV-2 variants.

- introduce antigenic sequences

Antigenic sequences refer to the portions of viral proteins that are recognized by the immune system, including B cell epitopes and T cell epitopes. The present invention utilizes antigenic sequences derived from the spike, membrane, nucleocapsid, and envelope proteins of SARS-CoV-2, selected for their conservation across variants and their ability to elicit robust immune responses. These sequences are optimized for expression in poxvirus vectors through codon usage adaptation, inclusion of signal peptides, and stabilization of conformational epitopes.

- discuss immunogenicity of spike protein

The spike protein of SARS-CoV-2 is the most immunogenic structural protein, eliciting potent neutralizing antibody responses and strong CD4+ T cell activation. However, its high mutation rate, particularly in the receptor-binding domain, has led to immune escape by emerging variants. The present invention enhances the immunogenicity of the spike protein by presenting it in its prefusion-stabilized conformation, thereby focusing the immune response on conserved, functionally critical epitopes and reducing the impact of antigenic drift.

- discuss immunogenicity of membrane protein

The membrane protein of SARS-CoV-2 is less immunogenic than the spike protein but is highly conserved and a major target of T cell responses. Antibodies against the membrane protein do not neutralize the virus directly but contribute to protection through opsonization and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity. The present invention incorporates the membrane protein to broaden the immune response and provide a stable antigenic target that is less susceptible to mutation.

- discuss immunogenicity of nucleocapsid protein

The nucleocapsid protein is the most conserved structural protein in SARS-CoV-2 and is a dominant target of T cell responses in both vaccinated and convalescent individuals. Although it does not elicit neutralizing antibodies, its inclusion in vaccine constructs significantly enhances the breadth and durability of cellular immunity. The present invention integrates the nucleocapsid protein to ensure protection against variants that evade spike-specific immunity.

- discuss immunogenicity of envelope protein

The envelope protein, though present in low copy numbers, is highly conserved and contains immunodominant T cell epitopes. Its inclusion in the present invention adds an additional layer of immune recognition, particularly in individuals with pre-existing immunity to endemic coronaviruses. The envelope protein’s role in viral assembly and immune modulation makes it a valuable component for inducing comprehensive protection against SARS-CoV-2.

Virus seed stocks are validated by western blot using antibodies against the SARS-CoV-2 antigens or by immunostaining with fluorescently labeled antibodies.

### Conclusion

The invention provides a novel, safe, and effective vaccine platform for the prevention of SARS-CoV-2 infection and related coronaviruses. The attenuated vaccinia virus vector, when engineered to express multiple conserved structural proteins of SARS-CoV-2, induces a comprehensive immune response that includes neutralizing antibodies, Th1-polarized CD4+ T cells, and cytotoxic CD8+ T cells. The platform is scalable, thermostable, and compatible with existing biomanufacturing infrastructure, making it suitable for global deployment. The invention overcomes the limitations of current vaccines by providing durable, variant-resistant immunity without the need for adjuvants or multiple doses. The compositions and methods described herein represent a significant advancement in the field of vaccinology and offer a powerful tool for the global control of the COVID-19 pandemic and future coronavirus threats.
