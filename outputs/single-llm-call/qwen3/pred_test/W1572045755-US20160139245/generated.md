## BACKGROUND

The passive localization and tracking of acoustic sources in underwater environments presents a persistent and technically demanding challenge in naval surveillance, oceanographic monitoring, and maritime security. Sound propagation in shallow water is inherently complex due to multipath reflections from the sea surface, seafloor, and other boundaries, which generate interference patterns that distort the spatial signature of the source. These distortions render traditional localization techniques unreliable, particularly when multiple sources are present or when the environmental model used to predict acoustic behavior deviates from actual conditions. Matched-field processing has long served as the primary methodology for estimating source locations by comparing measured acoustic fields with replicas generated from a theoretical propagation model. However, the accuracy of matched-field processing is critically dependent on the fidelity of the model, and even minor discrepancies in bathymetry, sound speed profiles, or source characteristics can produce misleading ambiguity surfaces riddled with false peaks. Furthermore, the computational burden of evaluating dense grids of potential source locations across multiple frequencies renders real-time implementation impractical. Matched-field tracking extends this approach by sequentially constructing ambiguity surfaces over time and linking peaks across consecutive frames to form trajectories. Yet this method suffers from combinatorial explosion in the number of possible track connections, necessitating restrictive kinematic assumptions that limit adaptability to unpredictable source motion. Bayesian methods have been proposed to incorporate prior knowledge of source dynamics, but they rely on computationally intensive Markov Chain Monte Carlo sampling, which is unsuitable for online deployment. Sparsity-driven Kalman filters attempt to exploit the fact that only a small subset of grid points correspond to actual sources, but their state-space dimensionality remains prohibitively large for practical use. Relevance vector machine approaches offer probabilistic sparsity modeling but require iterative covariance matrix updates that scale poorly with grid size and frequency resolution. None of these methods simultaneously enable broadband coherent processing, enforce temporal consistency across successive time frames, and maintain computational efficiency in high-dimensional, model-mismatched environments. There remains a critical unmet need for an algorithm that can robustly localize and track multiple moving acoustic sources in real time using passive sensor arrays, even under conditions of severe environmental uncertainty and sparse, noisy measurements.

The invention introduces a sparsity-driven framework for broadband acoustic source localization and tracking that leverages the inherent structure of source activity across frequency and time. At each time instant, a source location map is constructed per frequency band, where only those grid points corresponding to active sources contain nonzero values, and all other entries are constrained to zero. Coherence across frequency bands is enforced by requiring that the support of these maps—i.e., the set of locations with nonzero values—remain identical across all frequencies, reflecting the physical reality that a single source emits energy simultaneously across its spectral bandwidth. The estimation problem is formulated as a convex optimization task that simultaneously minimizes the residual error between measured and modeled Fourier coefficients while penalizing the group sparsity of rows in a regression coefficient matrix, where each row corresponds to a tentative source location and each column to a frequency. A temporal regularization term further constrains the solution to remain close to the previous estimate, thereby encoding memory of prior source positions and promoting trajectory continuity. The resulting optimization problem is solved using a proximal gradient algorithm that exploits the separable structure of the objective function to enable parallel updates across grid locations. The algorithm operates iteratively, updating each row of the coefficient matrix using a gradient descent step followed by a proximal operator that enforces group sparsity via a soft-thresholding operation on the L2 norm of each row. The proximal operator ensures that only those locations with sufficient evidence from both current measurements and prior estimates retain nonzero values, effectively suppressing noise and model mismatch artifacts. The final source location estimates are derived from the nonzero rows of the converged matrix, with their spatial coordinates mapped to a predefined grid of tentative source positions. The entire process is implemented as a modular system operating in the frequency domain, transforming raw hydrophone time-series data into Fourier coefficient vectors, constructing replica matrices using an acoustic propagation model, and iteratively refining the source location map until convergence criteria are met. The method is scalable to arbitrary grid densities and number of frequencies, and it does not rely on assumptions regarding source kinematics such as constant velocity or bounded acceleration. The system may be deployed on embedded hardware with sufficient computational capability to perform real-time matrix operations, and the resulting location estimates may be output to visualization modules, tracking interfaces, or decision-making systems for surveillance or environmental monitoring applications.
