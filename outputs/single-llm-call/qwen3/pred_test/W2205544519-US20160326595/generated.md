# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Prostate cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting men worldwide, with approximately one in eight men receiving a diagnosis during their lifetime. In the United States, it is the second leading cause of cancer-related death among males, accounting for over 34,000 fatalities annually. Disparities in incidence, aggressiveness, and mortality are starkly evident when comparing populations of African descent to those of Caucasian descent, with men of African ancestry experiencing nearly double the mortality rate despite similar or lower incidence rates in some regions. These disparities cannot be fully explained by socioeconomic factors alone, including access to healthcare, screening practices, or treatment adherence. While disparities in care contribute to outcomes, mounting evidence indicates that biological and molecular differences underpin the more aggressive clinical behavior observed in prostate cancers arising in men of African descent. Genetic studies have identified recurrent somatic alterations in genes such as ERG, PTEN, SPOP, and CHD1; however, the majority of these findings derive from cohorts composed predominantly of men of European ancestry. As a result, the genomic architecture of prostate cancer in men of African descent remains incompletely characterized, leading to a critical gap in understanding the drivers of disease progression in this high-risk population. The prevailing reliance on biomarkers and therapeutic targets identified in non-diverse populations has limited the development of precision medicine strategies tailored to the unique molecular profiles of African descent patients. There is an urgent and unmet need to identify novel, ethnically specific genomic alterations that can serve as diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic biomarkers to guide clinical decision-making and improve outcomes for underserved populations.

## SUMMARY

A novel genomic rearrangement involving the ZBTB20 and LSAMP genes on chromosome 3q13.31 has been identified as a recurrent, somatic alteration specifically enriched in prostate cancers of men of African descent. This rearrangement manifests as a deletion or duplication event that fuses sequences from the ZBTB20 locus with those of the LSAMP gene, resulting in the formation of a chimeric transcript and, potentially, a truncated or aberrant polypeptide product. The presence of this genomic rearrangement is strongly associated with aggressive disease phenotypes, including biochemical recurrence following radical prostatectomy and advanced pathological staging, particularly in men of African descent. Detection of this rearrangement provides a molecular marker for risk stratification and prognosis, enabling clinicians to identify patients at elevated risk for disease progression who may benefit from intensified monitoring or early intervention. The invention encompasses methods for detecting the ZBTB20/LSAMP genomic rearrangement through the analysis of genomic DNA, messenger RNA, or complementary DNA derived from prostate tissue or bodily fluids. Compositions for detection include polynucleotide probes and primers specifically designed to hybridize to or amplify sequences spanning the fusion junction, including novel exon-exon boundaries formed by the rearrangement, such as those involving exon 3* of LSAMP or exon 0* of LSAMP. The invention further includes isolated antibodies capable of binding to epitopes uniquely present on the chimeric ZBTB20/LSAMP fusion protein or to truncated forms of LSAMP resulting from the rearrangement. These antibodies may be used in immunoassays for diagnostic or prognostic purposes. The invention also encompasses kits comprising combinations of polynucleotide probes, primers, antibodies, and instructional materials for the detection of the ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement in clinical samples. Additionally, the invention recognizes that the absence of PTEN deletion, a common event in prostate cancers of Caucasian descent, is a distinguishing feature of prostate cancers in men of African descent. This inverse relationship enables the development of differential diagnostic algorithms that combine detection of ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement with assessment of PTEN status to guide therapeutic selection. In particular, patients exhibiting ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement and intact PTEN are unlikely to benefit from therapies targeting the PI3K/PTEN/Akt/mTOR pathway, and such treatments may be contraindicated in this subgroup. The invention therefore provides a framework for ethnically informed precision oncology, enabling the selection of targeted therapies based on the molecular profile of the tumor.

### Examples

Comparative genomic DNA analysis of prostate cancer samples from seven African American and seven Caucasian American patients revealed a novel, recurrent genomic rearrangement involving the ZBTB20 and LSAMP genes on chromosome 3q13.31. Whole genome sequencing was performed on tumor and matched normal tissues, with alignment to the GRCh37 reference genome and somatic variant calling using Strelka, BreakDancer, and cn.MOPs. In three of seven African American cases, a deletion spanning approximately 1–23 Mb was identified, removing the intervening sequence between ZBTB20 and LSAMP and resulting in a fusion transcript. This rearrangement was absent in all seven Caucasian American cases. RNA sequencing confirmed the presence of chimeric transcripts containing ZBTB20 exon sequences fused to LSAMP exon sequences, including novel junctions involving exon 3* and exon 0* of LSAMP. The fusion transcripts were validated by 5′-RACE and Sanger sequencing. Fluorescent in situ hybridization using locus-specific probes confirmed the deletion in tumor nuclei and was absent in benign prostatic tissue. Analysis of an independent cohort of 320 prostate cancer cases from The Cancer Genome Atlas showed that LSAMP deletions occurred in 27% of African American tumors compared to 13% of Caucasian American tumors (p = 0.023). Validation in a tissue microarray of 101 prostate cancer specimens confirmed a significantly higher frequency of LSAMP deletion in African American patients (26%) versus Caucasian American patients (7%) (p = 0.007). Furthermore, LSAMP deletion was associated with biochemical recurrence (p = 0.05) and advanced pathological stage. In contrast, PTEN deletion was detected in only 15% of African American tumors compared to 63% of Caucasian American tumors (p = 1E-06), with the most pronounced difference observed in Gleason 6 tumors (7% vs. 53%). These findings establish the ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement as a distinct molecular signature of aggressive prostate cancer in men of African descent and demonstrate that PTEN deletion is a rare event in this population. The invention provides a method for distinguishing between molecular subtypes of prostate cancer based on ethnicity-specific genomic alterations, enabling personalized diagnostic and therapeutic strategies. Primers and probes were designed to detect the fusion junctions, and antibodies were generated against the chimeric protein to enable immunoassay-based detection in clinical samples. Kits were assembled containing all necessary reagents for detection of the rearrangement and PTEN status, along with interpretive guidelines for clinical use.
