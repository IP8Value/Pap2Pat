# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- Porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) is a small, non-enveloped, circular single-stranded DNA virus belonging to the genus Circovirus within the family Circoviridae. It is one of the most economically significant pathogens affecting swine populations globally, primarily associated with postweaning multisystemic wasting syndrome, dermatitis and nephropathy syndrome, and reproductive failure. The virus exhibits high transmission efficiency through oral-nasal routes and via contaminated bodily secretions, leading to persistent infection cycles in commercial swine herds despite biosecurity measures. Its genome encodes a single major capsid protein of approximately 27 kDa, which is the principal antigenic determinant capable of eliciting neutralizing antibodies and protective immunity in infected animals. The global swine industry suffers annual losses exceeding billions of dollars due to PCV-2-related morbidity, mortality, and reduced growth performance, necessitating effective and scalable intervention strategies.

- Despite the availability of commercial vaccines, there remains a critical unmet need for next-generation vaccine platforms that offer enhanced safety, improved immunogenicity, and reduced production costs. Current licensed vaccines against PCV-2 are predominantly based on either inactivated whole-virus preparations or recombinant subunit formulations expressing the capsid protein. While these vaccines have significantly reduced disease incidence, they are limited by high manufacturing complexity, reliance on expensive mammalian or insect cell culture systems, and challenges in achieving consistent product quality at scale. Inactivated vaccines require handling of live virus under high-containment conditions, posing biosafety risks during production, while subunit vaccines often fail to fully recapitulate the native conformational epitopes necessary for robust neutralizing antibody responses. Furthermore, purification processes for recombinant capsid proteins frequently involve multi-step chromatographic techniques that increase production costs and reduce yield, limiting accessibility in resource-constrained regions.

- Existing vaccine production systems, including bacterial, yeast, and insect cell platforms, are constrained by high operational expenditures, stringent sterility requirements, and the risk of contamination with adventitious animal pathogens. These systems demand specialized infrastructure, costly growth media, and extensive downstream processing to isolate and purify the target antigen. Additionally, the incorporation of affinity tags to facilitate purification often alters protein folding or immunogenicity, requiring additional enzymatic cleavage steps that further complicate manufacturing. The absence of a scalable, cost-effective, and biosafe platform for producing authentic virus-like particles has hindered the development of more efficacious and widely accessible PCV-2 vaccines, particularly in developing countries where swine production is expanding but biomanufacturing capacity remains limited.

- Pseudovirions, or virus-like particles (PsVs), represent a promising alternative to traditional vaccine modalities by mimicking the structural integrity and antigenic presentation of native virions without containing infectious genetic material. These particles self-assemble from recombinant capsid proteins and retain the three-dimensional conformational epitopes critical for inducing potent and durable humoral immune responses. When produced in plant-based expression systems, pseudovirions benefit from the inherent advantages of plant cells, including the capacity for complex posttranslational modifications, absence of human or animal pathogens, and scalability through agronomic cultivation. Plant systems eliminate the need for sterile bioreactors and reduce reliance on expensive fermentation media, enabling decentralized and sustainable production models that are particularly suited for veterinary vaccine deployment in low-resource settings.

- The use of plant-based transient expression systems for the production of PCV-2 pseudovirions offers a transformative solution to the limitations of conventional platforms. By leveraging the natural ability of plant cells to synthesize and assemble complex protein structures, this approach enables the cost-effective generation of immunologically authentic particles without the need for chemical cross-linking, affinity purification, or viral inactivation steps. The scalability of plant cultivation, combined with simplified downstream processing via density gradient centrifugation, allows for the production of high-yield, high-purity pseudovirions suitable for both prophylactic vaccination and diagnostic applications. This innovation not only reduces manufacturing costs but also enhances vaccine stability, safety, and accessibility, positioning plant-derived PCV-2 pseudovirions as a next-generation platform for global swine health management.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- A novel method for producing porcine circovirus type 2 pseudovirions in plant cells is disclosed, wherein the viral capsid polypeptide is expressed from a recombinant expression cassette introduced into plant tissue via agroinfiltration, leading to the spontaneous assembly of virus-like particles that structurally and immunologically mimic native PCV-2 virions. This method eliminates the need for live virus handling, chemical inactivation, or chromatographic purification, enabling a streamlined, scalable, and biosafe production process entirely within a plant-based system.

- The method involves the transient expression of a PCV-2 capsid polypeptide in plant cells using a plant-optimized nucleic acid sequence encoding the full-length ORF2 gene, which is codon-adapted for efficient translation in Nicotiana benthamiana. The polypeptide is expressed under the control of a plant-specific promoter and terminator, and is targeted to the cytoplasmic compartment where it self-assembles into morphologically intact, non-infectious pseudovirions with a diameter of approximately 16 to 24 nanometers, as confirmed by transmission electron microscopy and buoyant density analysis.

- The expression system employs a replicating vector derived from the Bean yellow dwarf virus (BeYDV), which encodes a replication initiator protein (Rep/RepA) that enables rolling-circle amplification of the capsid-encoding transgene within plant nuclei. This results in high-copy-number accumulation of the transgene, significantly enhancing the yield of capsid protein and facilitating the formation of densely packed pseudovirions without the need for exogenous replication machinery or viral helper elements.

- The assembled pseudovirions are capable of encapsidating endogenous plant-derived nucleic acids, including circular amplicons generated during rolling-circle replication, thereby forming structurally complete particles that resemble native PCV-2 virions in size, morphology, and antigenic presentation. These pseudovirions are purified from plant tissue extracts through a single-step sucrose density gradient centrifugation, yielding a highly enriched fraction of particles without the use of affinity tags, chromatography resins, or enzymatic cleavage steps.

- A novel pseudovirion-based neutralization assay is disclosed, wherein plant-produced PCV-2 pseudovirions are combined with a biological sample suspected of containing anti-PCV-2 antibodies, such as porcine or murine serum, and then introduced to a mammalian cell line permissive for PCV-2 infection. The inhibition of pseudovirion entry or intracellular gene expression is measured by assaying the expression of a reporter polypeptide encoded within the pseudovirion, enabling quantitative determination of neutralizing antibody titers in a high-throughput, non-infectious format.

- The method further comprises the step of contacting mammalian cells with a composition comprising the purified pseudovirions and a reporter construct, wherein the reporter polypeptide is selected from the group consisting of yellow fluorescent protein, luciferase, β-galactosidase, or green fluorescent protein. Expression of the reporter is quantified using fluorescence microscopy, luminescence detection, enzymatic activity assays, or flow cytometry, providing a sensitive and reproducible readout for serological testing and vaccine efficacy evaluation.

- The invention further comprises the PCV-2 pseudovirion per se, characterized by a diameter of 12 to 24 nanometers, a buoyant density of approximately 1.56 g/cm³ in cesium chloride, and the absence of infectious viral DNA, wherein the particle is composed exclusively of the PCV-2 capsid polypeptide and encapsidated plant-derived nucleic acid fragments. The pseudovirion is non-infectious, non-replicating, and capable of eliciting a neutralizing antibody response in a mammalian host upon immunization.

- A composition comprising the PCV-2 pseudovirion and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier is disclosed, wherein the carrier is selected from the group consisting of phosphate-buffered saline, aluminum hydroxide, squalene-based emulsions, or mineral oil adjuvants. The composition is formulated for parenteral administration and is stable for at least six months when stored at 2–8°C.

- A method for producing the PCV-2 pseudovirion is disclosed, comprising the steps of transforming Agrobacterium tumefaciens with a binary vector containing a plant-optimized PCV-2 capsid gene under the control of a CaMV 35S promoter, co-infiltrating Nicotiana benthamiana leaves with the transformed Agrobacterium and a second vector expressing the BeYDV Rep/RepA protein, harvesting plant tissue 4 to 7 days post-infiltration, homogenizing the tissue in a buffered saline solution, clarifying the extract by centrifugation, and purifying pseudovirions via sucrose density gradient centrifugation.

- The invention further includes an embodiment wherein the replicating vector encodes a viral replication regulatory protein selected from the group consisting of BeYDV Rep, BeYDV RepA, or a functional homolog thereof, which is expressed in trans to drive amplification of the capsid-encoding transgene through rolling-circle replication.

- An additional embodiment comprises a pseudovirion comprising a heterologous polypeptide fused to the PCV-2 capsid protein, wherein the heterologous polypeptide is selected from the group consisting of a B-cell epitope from a different porcine pathogen, a T-cell epitope, or a molecular adjuvant, thereby enabling the pseudovirion to function as a multivalent vaccine platform.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention relates to a novel method for the production, purification, and application of porcine circovirus type 2 pseudovirions derived from plant-based transient expression systems. The invention is illustrated in the accompanying figures, wherein Figure 1 depicts the expression and purification profile of PCV-2 capsid protein from infiltrated Nicotiana benthamiana leaves, Figure 2 shows the buoyant density profile of purified particles in cesium chloride gradients, Figure 3 presents transmission electron micrographs confirming the morphology of assembled pseudovirions, and Figure 4 demonstrates the immunogenicity of plant-derived pseudovirions in a murine model. The scope of the invention encompasses all methods, compositions, and uses described herein, including variations in expression vectors, host plants, purification protocols, and diagnostic applications.

- For the purposes of this disclosure, singular forms such as “a,” “an,” and “the” are intended to include plural referents unless the context clearly dictates otherwise. The term “comprising” is used in its open-ended sense, meaning that the recited elements are included but not limited to those specified. The terms “polypeptide,” “protein,” and “capsid protein” are used interchangeably to refer to the amino acid sequence encoded by the PCV-2 ORF2 gene, including naturally occurring variants, fragments, and fusion constructs that retain the ability to self-assemble into virus-like particles.

- Porcine circovirus type 2 particles are non-enveloped, icosahedral virions approximately 17 nanometers in diameter, composed of a single capsid protein that forms a T=1 symmetry lattice. The capsid protein contains immunodominant epitopes that elicit neutralizing antibodies in swine, making it the primary target for vaccine development. The immune response to native PCV-2 is often suboptimal due to immune evasion mechanisms, whereas pseudovirions assembled in plant cells present these epitopes in a native, multivalent conformation that enhances antigen presentation and B-cell activation.

- The production of pseudovirions in plants is motivated by the need to circumvent the limitations of traditional expression systems, including high costs, biosafety risks, and inconsistent yields. Unlike insect or mammalian cell cultures, plant systems do not harbor mammalian pathogens, do not require sterile bioreactors, and can be scaled through agricultural practices. The use of Agrobacterium-mediated transient expression enables rapid, high-level protein production without genomic integration, making it ideal for vaccine development.

- The method described herein differs from prior art in that it does not rely on affinity-tagged purification, chromatographic separation, or chemical cross-linking to achieve particle integrity. Instead, the capsid protein self-assembles into authentic pseudovirions driven by its intrinsic structural properties, and is purified by a single-step density gradient centrifugation, resulting in a product that is structurally and immunologically equivalent to native virions.

- The method for producing PCV-2 pseudovirions in plant cells comprises the introduction of a first polynucleotide encoding the PCV-2 capsid polypeptide into a plant cell via a binary vector, wherein the polynucleotide is operably linked to a plant-specific promoter and terminator. A second polynucleotide encoding the BeYDV Rep/RepA replication protein is introduced in trans, enabling rolling-circle amplification of the capsid-encoding sequence and resulting in high-copy-number accumulation of the transgene within the plant nucleus.

- The term “vector” refers to a nucleic acid molecule capable of replicating in a host cell and delivering a transgene, including plasmids, viral vectors, and artificial chromosomes. The term “cassette” refers to a functional unit comprising a promoter, coding sequence, and terminator, arranged to enable transcription and translation in the host. The term “amplicon” refers to a circular, single-stranded DNA molecule generated by rolling-circle replication, which may be encapsidated within the pseudovirion during assembly.

- Self-assembly of the capsid protein into virus-like particles occurs spontaneously in the cytoplasm of plant cells following translation, driven by hydrophobic and electrostatic interactions between monomeric subunits. The resulting particles are morphologically indistinguishable from native PCV-2 virions and are stabilized by the encapsidation of plant-derived nucleic acids, which serve as surrogate genomes.

- The pseudovirions produced by this method are non-infectious because they lack the full viral genome and do not encode replication proteins. They are, however, fully capable of binding to cellular receptors and triggering immune recognition, making them ideal for vaccine and diagnostic applications.

- The replicating vector employed in the invention comprises the BeYDV Rep/RepA protein, which initiates rolling-circle replication of the transgene, leading to exponential amplification of the capsid-encoding sequence. This results in a 10- to 50-fold increase in protein yield compared to non-replicating systems.

- The invention further includes embodiments wherein a heterologous polypeptide is fused to the N- or C-terminus of the PCV-2 capsid protein, enabling the display of additional antigens on the pseudovirion surface. The reporter polypeptide, when encoded within the pseudovirion, serves as a marker for infectivity in mammalian cells, allowing for the development of high-throughput neutralization assays.

- The pseudovirion neutralization assay comprises contacting a biological sample with the plant-produced pseudovirion, incubating with permissive mammalian cells, and measuring reporter gene expression. Inhibition of reporter expression indicates the presence of neutralizing antibodies, enabling serological surveillance and vaccine potency testing.

- The plant-based production system offers significant advantages over conventional methods, including lower capital investment, reduced risk of contamination, scalability through greenhouse cultivation, and elimination of chromatographic purification. The resulting pseudovirions are stable, immunogenic, and suitable for use in both prophylactic vaccines and diagnostic kits.

### Example 1

- A plant expression vector was designed to encode the codon-optimized PCV-2 ORF2 gene under the control of the CaMV 35S promoter and the NOS terminator. A yellow fluorescent protein (YFP) gene was cloned downstream of the capsid gene to enable visualization of expression. The BeYDV Rep/RepA gene was cloned into a separate expression cassette and introduced into the same vector backbone. The entire replicating module was transferred into the pEAQ-HT expression vector, which contains the 35S enhancer and the 5′ untranslated region from the tobacco mosaic virus. Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404 was transformed with the construct and selected on kanamycin and rifampicin media. Transformants were confirmed by colony PCR using primers specific to the pEAQ-HT backbone. Agrobacterium cultures were grown to an OD600 of 1.0, infiltrated into Nicotiana benthamiana leaves, and harvested at 4 days post-infiltration. Plant tissue was homogenized in DB150 buffer, clarified by centrifugation, and filtered through Miracloth. The supernatant was loaded onto a sucrose gradient and centrifuged at 120,000 g for 4 hours. The pellet was resuspended in PBS and analyzed by SDS-PAGE, revealing a 27 kDa band corresponding to the PCV-2 capsid protein. Immunoblotting confirmed reactivity with anti-PCV-2 antisera. Transmission electron microscopy revealed spherical particles of 16–24 nm diameter. The pseudovirions were tested for infectivity in PK-15 cells transfected with a reporter plasmid, and fluorescence was detected only in cells exposed to pseudovirions, confirming successful delivery and expression of the reporter gene.

### Discussion

- The geminivirus-based replicating vector system enables high-level accumulation of the capsid protein through rolling-circle replication, a mechanism not previously exploited for PCV-2 pseudovirion production. The formation of amplicons in plant nuclei was confirmed by rolling circle amplification and restriction digestion, demonstrating that the replication machinery is functional and drives transgene amplification. The YFP gene was successfully expressed and visualized in planta, confirming the functionality of the expression cassette. Co-infiltration with the Rep/RepA gene significantly enhanced capsid protein yield and particle assembly. Electron microscopy confirmed the formation of virus-like particles with native morphology. The pseudovirions were infectious in mammalian cells, delivering the reporter gene and enabling detection of neutralizing antibodies. The plant-produced pseudovirions induced robust immune responses in mice comparable to a commercial subunit vaccine, validating their potential as a next-generation vaccine platform.
