# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

- Porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) is a highly contagious coronavirus that has caused devastating economic losses to the global swine industry since its emergence in Europe in the 1970s, followed by widespread outbreaks in Asia and North America beginning in 2010 and 2013, respectively. The virus primarily targets mature intestinal enterocytes, leading to severe villus atrophy, malabsorptive diarrhea, vomiting, and dehydration, with mortality rates in neonatal piglets reaching up to 100% within days of infection. The acute nature of the disease, coupled with the vulnerability of suckling piglets whose immune systems are not yet fully developed, has rendered traditional control measures insufficient. Current vaccines approved for use in North America are based on inactivated virus or viral RNA, yet their efficacy remains marginal, often failing to prevent clinical signs or reduce mortality in challenged litters. These limitations stem from an inability to induce robust mucosal immunity, poor antigen stability during storage and transport, and the necessity for parenteral administration, which requires individual handling of animals and increases labor costs. Furthermore, the immunogenicity of these vaccines is inconsistent across herds, and they do not reliably confer passive immunity to offspring through colostrum or milk. The absence of a vaccine that is both highly effective and logistically feasible for large-scale use has created a critical gap in disease prevention, necessitating the development of a novel immunization strategy that overcomes these persistent shortcomings.

- The limitations of existing PEDV vaccines are multifaceted and deeply rooted in both biological and practical constraints. Inactivated virus vaccines elicit primarily systemic immune responses that are poorly translocated to the intestinal mucosa, the primary site of viral entry and replication. This disconnect between the site of immunity and the site of infection significantly diminishes protective efficacy. Additionally, these vaccines often require adjuvants such as aluminum hydroxide and mineral oil, which can induce local inflammation and reduce animal welfare. The production of these vaccines involves high-containment facilities, costly purification processes, and cold-chain logistics, all of which elevate production expenses and limit accessibility in resource-constrained regions. RNA-based vaccines, while offering faster development timelines, suffer from instability under ambient conditions and require specialized delivery systems to prevent degradation in the gastrointestinal tract. Moreover, neither platform has demonstrated consistent induction of neutralizing antibodies in milk, which is the critical route of passive immunity for neonatal piglets. The failure to generate durable mucosal immunity, coupled with the logistical burden of injection-based delivery, has rendered current vaccines inadequate for controlling the rapid spread of PEDV in commercial swine operations. Consequently, there exists an urgent and unmet need for a vaccine that is orally administered, heat-stable, cost-effective, and capable of inducing high-titer neutralizing antibodies in sow milk to protect nursing piglets without requiring direct immunization of the vulnerable neonates.

## SUMMARY

- The present invention introduces a novel plant-based vaccine for the prevention of porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) infection, wherein the critical immunogenic component is the S1 subunit of the PEDV spike protein, expressed at high levels within the endosperm and embryo tissues of genetically modified maize grain. This vaccine is designed for oral administration to pregnant sows, enabling the passive transfer of protective immunity to their nursing piglets through colostrum and milk. Unlike conventional vaccines, this platform leverages the natural bioencapsulation properties of maize seed tissue to protect the antigen from degradation in the gastrointestinal tract, thereby facilitating efficient uptake and immune stimulation at mucosal surfaces. The S1 antigen accumulates stably within the grain at concentrations sufficient to elicit robust neutralizing antibody responses without requiring purification, extraction, or refrigeration, making it uniquely suited for deployment in large-scale swine production systems.

- The vaccine is produced through the genetic transformation of maize using a codon-optimized nucleic acid sequence encoding the S1 region of the PEDV spike protein, under the control of seed-preferred promoters that drive high-level expression specifically in the developing endosperm and embryo. The transformed maize plants are cultivated under controlled agricultural conditions, and the harvested grain is processed into a stable, dry meal form that retains antigenic integrity for years at ambient temperatures. This process eliminates the need for cold-chain storage, lyophilization, or complex downstream purification, dramatically reducing production and distribution costs. The resulting product is formulated as a feed additive, administered orally to gestating sows during critical windows of immune priming to maximize antibody transfer to colostrum and milk. The simplicity of administration—mixing the antigen-containing meal into standard feed—enables mass vaccination without individual animal handling, significantly improving compliance and scalability.

- The benefits of this invention include unparalleled stability, cost-effectiveness, ease of use, and superior efficacy in conferring passive immunity to neonatal piglets. The maize-based platform ensures that the S1 antigen remains bioactive for extended periods, enabling long-term storage and global distribution without refrigeration. Oral delivery induces a mucosal immune response in the sow’s gut-associated lymphoid tissue, leading to the secretion of virus-specific IgA and IgG antibodies into milk, which directly protects suckling piglets during their most vulnerable period. Animal trials demonstrate that low-dose oral administration results in significantly higher survival rates of piglets following lethal PEDV challenge compared to both control groups and high-dose oral groups, suggesting an optimal immunomodulatory window that avoids oral tolerance. Furthermore, this vaccine reduces systemic inflammation, as evidenced by markedly lower levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as GM-CSF in both serum and milk of vaccinated sows, correlating directly with improved piglet survival.

- Experimental results from controlled challenge studies show that piglets born to sows fed the low-dose maize-produced S1 antigen exhibited a survival rate of 53% at 11 days post-challenge, compared to only 10% in non-vaccinated controls and 7% in high-dose oral groups. The survival advantage was statistically significant and reproducible across multiple litters. Neutralizing antibody titers in milk were significantly elevated in the low-dose group, while serum neutralizing antibodies were also increased, indicating systemic immune activation. Critically, milk neutralizing antibody levels showed a strong positive correlation with piglet survival, whereas elevated levels of GM-CSF in milk were inversely correlated with survival, suggesting a dual mechanism of protection involving both antibody-mediated neutralization and immunomodulation of inflammatory pathways.

- The vaccine exerts a significant immunomodulatory effect by suppressing the production of GM-CSF, a cytokine associated with inflammatory pathology in coronavirus infections. In vaccinated sows, particularly those receiving the low-dose oral regimen, serum and milk concentrations of GM-CSF were significantly reduced compared to controls, and this reduction was predictive of improved piglet outcomes. This finding suggests that the S1 antigen not only acts as a neutralizing immunogen but also modulates the maternal immune environment to create a less inflammatory milieu in milk, thereby enhancing neonatal resilience. The dual function of the antigen—as both a target for neutralizing antibodies and a regulator of inflammatory signaling—represents a novel paradigm in coronavirus vaccine design.

- The invention further proposes a diagnostic application based on the correlation between milk neutralizing antibody titers and GM-CSF levels and piglet survival. Measurement of these biomarkers in sow milk can be used to predict the likelihood of protection in a litter prior to viral exposure, enabling proactive herd management decisions. This diagnostic utility extends beyond vaccination evaluation, offering a tool for monitoring herd immunity, identifying non-responsive sows, and optimizing vaccination protocols in real time.

## DESCRIPTION

- Porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) is a member of the Alphacoronavirus genus within the family Coronaviridae, characterized by a single-stranded, positive-sense RNA genome approximately 28 kilobases in length. The virus is enveloped and possesses four major structural proteins: the spike (S), membrane (M), nucleocapsid (N), and envelope (E) proteins, along with several accessory proteins encoded by open reading frames including ORF3. PEDV primarily infects the epithelial cells lining the small intestine, where it induces cytopathic effects leading to villous atrophy, impaired nutrient absorption, and severe secretory diarrhea. The clinical presentation is most severe in neonatal piglets, with mortality rates approaching 100% in the absence of maternal immunity. The virus spreads rapidly through fecal-oral transmission, and its environmental stability allows for prolonged persistence in barns, transport vehicles, and fomites, contributing to its high transmissibility.

- PEDV was first identified in England in 1971 and remained relatively contained until its re-emergence in China in 2010, where it rapidly evolved into highly virulent strains. These strains were introduced into the United States in 2013, triggering a nationwide epidemic that resulted in the loss of millions of piglets and billions of dollars in economic damage. Subsequent genetic analyses revealed that the U.S. outbreak strains belong to the G2 genogroup, distinct from the earlier European G1 strains, and exhibit mutations in the spike protein that enhance receptor binding and immune evasion. The virus continues to circulate globally, with new variants emerging periodically, underscoring the need for adaptable vaccine platforms capable of rapid response to antigenic drift.

- PEDV shares structural and functional homology with other coronaviruses, including transmissible gastroenteritis virus (TGEV), porcine deltacoronavirus (PDCoV), and human pathogens such as SARS-CoV-1, MERS-CoV, and SARS-CoV-2. All these viruses utilize the spike protein as the primary mediator of host cell entry, binding to specific receptors on the apical surface of intestinal or respiratory epithelial cells. The spike protein is cleaved into two functional subunits, S1 and S2, with S1 responsible for receptor recognition and S2 mediating membrane fusion. The S1 subunit contains multiple neutralizing epitopes, making it the dominant target for protective antibodies. The core neutralizing epitope (COE), located within residues 499–638 of the S1 domain, has been identified as a critical region for eliciting virus-neutralizing responses across multiple strains.

- The PEDV spike protein is a trimeric glycoprotein that protrudes from the viral envelope and is essential for initiating infection. The S1 region contains the receptor-binding domain (RBD), which interacts with porcine aminopeptidase N (pAPN) as the primary cellular receptor. The S2 region contains the fusion peptide, heptad repeats, and transmembrane domain necessary for merging the viral and host membranes. Cleavage of the spike protein by host proteases such as furin or trypsin is required for activation of membrane fusion and viral entry. The presence of a conserved GPRLQPY motif near the S1/S2 cleavage site has been implicated in enhancing cleavage efficiency and viral infectivity, and variations in this region correlate with strain virulence.

- The S gene exhibits significant sequence diversity among PEDV strains, particularly within the S1 domain, leading to the classification of strains into two major genogroups: G1 and G2. G1 strains are largely ancestral and less virulent, while G2 strains, which dominate current outbreaks, contain deletions and substitutions in the S1 region that enhance immune escape and pathogenicity. The M protein, a transmembrane glycoprotein, plays a key role in virion assembly and budding, while the N protein encapsulates the viral RNA and modulates host immune responses. The ORF3 accessory protein, though non-essential for replication, has been linked to increased virulence through its role in modulating ion channel activity and suppressing interferon responses.

- Clinical signs of PEDV infection include sudden onset of watery diarrhea, vomiting, dehydration, and lethargy, with mortality highest in piglets under seven days of age. Gross pathology reveals thinning of the intestinal wall, fluid distension of the small intestine, and flattened villi upon histological examination. The disease is most severe in naive herds, where lack of maternal antibodies leaves piglets unprotected. In contrast, sows with prior exposure or vaccination develop robust immunity, which is transferred to offspring via colostrum and milk, providing critical passive protection during the first weeks of life.

- The genetic diversity of PEDV strains has necessitated the development of vaccines capable of inducing broad cross-reactive immunity. While inactivated and live-attenuated vaccines have been developed, their efficacy is inconsistent, particularly against emerging G2 strains. The present invention addresses this challenge by focusing on the S1 subunit, which contains conserved neutralizing epitopes across multiple genogroups, and by expressing it in a plant system that preserves native conformational structures critical for antibody recognition.

- The S1 polypeptide used in this invention is derived from the full-length spike protein of a virulent U.S. PEDV strain (USA/NC/49469/2013) and is expressed in maize under the control of seed-specific promoters that direct high-level accumulation in the embryo and endosperm. Codon optimization for plant expression enhances translation efficiency without altering the amino acid sequence, ensuring antigenic fidelity. The resulting protein retains its native glycosylation patterns and conformational epitopes, enabling recognition by neutralizing antibodies induced in vaccinated sows.

- The nucleic acid construct encoding the S1 polypeptide is synthesized de novo using degenerate codon substitutions optimized for maize codon usage, cloned into a plant expression vector containing a CaMV 35S enhancer, a maize glutamine synthetase promoter, and a barley alpha-amylase signal peptide to facilitate endoplasmic reticulum targeting. The construct is introduced into maize via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation of immature embryos, followed by selection on bialaphos-containing media and regeneration of transgenic plants. The resulting transgenic maize lines produce S1 protein at concentrations exceeding 50 mg per kilogram of dry grain, as confirmed by Western blot and ELISA.

- The S1 polypeptide is expressed as a full-length, non-cleaved protein that is retained within the protein bodies of the maize endosperm, where it is protected from proteolytic degradation. This bioencapsulation allows the antigen to survive passage through the sow’s gastrointestinal tract, where it is taken up by gut-associated lymphoid tissue and presented to the immune system. Oral administration of the maize meal to gestating sows triggers a robust mucosal and systemic immune response, resulting in high titers of neutralizing antibodies in milk.

- The invention encompasses the use of conservatively modified variants of the S1 polypeptide, including amino acid substitutions that preserve structural integrity while enhancing stability or solubility. Silent nucleotide substitutions are employed to optimize codon usage without altering the encoded protein sequence. The polypeptide may also be fused to transit peptides or endoplasmic reticulum retention signals to enhance intracellular trafficking and accumulation. Functional fragments of the S1 region, including the core neutralizing epitope (COE), may be expressed independently or as part of chimeric proteins to broaden immune recognition.

- The expression vector utilized in this invention includes selectable markers such as bar or nptII, regulatory elements for seed-specific expression, and transcriptional terminators derived from the nopaline synthase or rbcS genes. The vector is delivered into maize cells via Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404 or EHA101, using modified Ishida protocols that employ Hi-II maize lines and bialaphos selection. Transformed plants are grown to maturity, and grain is harvested, dried, and milled into a uniform meal suitable for feed incorporation.

- The invention further includes methods for producing variant proteins through site-directed mutagenesis, domain swapping, or fusion with immunomodulatory domains such as cytokines or TLR agonists. Purification of the S1 antigen is not required, as the whole grain meal is administered orally, eliminating the need for costly downstream processing. The resulting product is stable for years at ambient temperature, requires no cold chain, and can be stored and transported using standard agricultural logistics.

- The method of administration involves feeding the maize meal to pregnant sows during late gestation, typically during three 3-day periods spaced at 28-day intervals beginning at day 57 of gestation. Each feeding provides a defined dose of S1 antigen, ranging from 10 to 50 mg per kilogram of feed. The low-dose regimen (10 mg/kg) has been shown to be optimal for inducing protective immunity without inducing oral tolerance, while higher doses result in diminished efficacy.

- The invention also encompasses the use of the S1-expressing maize as a diagnostic tool. Measurement of neutralizing antibody titers and GM-CSF levels in sow milk provides a predictive biomarker panel for assessing the likelihood of piglet survival following PEDV challenge. This diagnostic application enables real-time monitoring of herd immunity and informs vaccination timing and dosage adjustments.

## EXAMPLES

- The spike protein of PEDV serves as the primary immunogen in this invention, with the S1 subunit being the focus due to its possession of multiple neutralizing epitopes and its role as the receptor-binding domain. Prototype candidates based on the full-length S protein, the S1 moiety, the S2 moiety, and the core neutralizing epitope (COE) have been evaluated, with the S1 fragment demonstrating superior immunogenicity and stability when expressed in maize. The S1 antigen produced in this system retains its native conformation and glycosylation, enabling recognition by neutralizing antibodies from convalescent pigs and monoclonal antibody panels.

- The production of the S protein at high levels in recombinant systems has historically been challenging due to its large size, complex folding requirements, and dependence on post-translational modifications. Expression in bacterial systems results in insoluble aggregates, while mammalian cell cultures are prohibitively expensive and lack scalability. The plant-based system described herein overcomes these limitations by leveraging the endoplasmic reticulum’s natural protein-folding machinery and the maize grain’s ability to sequester and stabilize recombinant proteins within protein bodies.

- Oral administration of vaccines is motivated by the fact that PEDV initiates infection at the intestinal mucosa, and systemic vaccines fail to induce sufficient mucosal immunity. Precedent for oral immunization exists in studies using Lactobacillus-expressed PEDV antigens, which induced partial protection, but these systems lack the stability and yield of plant-based platforms. The maize grain system provides a scalable, cost-effective, and safe vehicle for delivering intact antigen to the gut-associated lymphoid tissue.

- Maize grain is preferred as the production platform due to its high biomass yield, established agricultural infrastructure, and intrinsic ability to bioencapsulate proteins. The recombinant S1 antigen accumulates in the embryo and endosperm at levels exceeding 50 mg per kg of dry grain, remains stable for over three years at room temperature, and requires no purification. The antigen is delivered in a non-diluted, feed-compatible form that integrates seamlessly into existing swine feeding protocols.

- Early studies with the TGEV spike protein expressed in maize demonstrated successful induction of neutralizing antibodies in sows and protection of piglets upon challenge. These findings provided the foundation for applying the same platform to PEDV. The present invention builds upon this precedent by expressing the more complex and virulent PEDV S1 antigen and demonstrating its efficacy in conferring passive immunity to neonates.

- The maize-produced S1 antigen has been shown to elicit high titers of neutralizing antibodies in sow milk, with titers correlating strongly with piglet survival. In animal trials, piglets from sows fed the low-dose oral vaccine exhibited a 53% survival rate after lethal challenge, compared to 10% in controls. The high-dose group showed reduced efficacy, suggesting an immunomodulatory threshold beyond which tolerance may be induced.

- The vaccine candidate was administered to naïve sows during gestation, with no adverse effects observed on litter size, birth weight, or maternal health. The antigen was detected in colostrum and milk, and its presence coincided with a significant reduction in pro-inflammatory cytokines, particularly GM-CSF, which was inversely correlated with piglet survival.

- Constructs used for production include a synthetic gene encoding the S1 region (residues 1–789) of the PEDV USA/NC/49469/2013 strain, codon-optimized for maize, cloned into the pSB11 vector under the control of the glutamine synthetase promoter and fused to the barley alpha-amylase signal peptide. Expression was confirmed by Western blot using anti-S1 monoclonal antibodies, with a single band at approximately 90 kDa corresponding to the full-length glycosylated protein.

- Western blot analysis of maize grain extracts revealed consistent expression across multiple generations, with no loss of antigenicity. The material was prepared for animal trials by grinding the grain to pass through a 20-mesh screen, ensuring uniform particle size and antigen distribution. The low-dose formulation contained 10 mg of S1 per kg of feed, while the high-dose contained 50 mg/kg.

- Animal trials involved 16 sows divided into four treatment groups: intramuscular vaccine, non-vaccinated control, low-dose oral, and high-dose oral. Piglets were challenged with 10⁴ TCID₅₀/mL of live PEDV at days 3–5 postpartum. Daily clinical scoring, survival monitoring, and sample collection for neutralizing antibody and cytokine analysis were performed. Statistical analysis confirmed that the low-dose oral group had significantly higher survival rates than all other groups.

- The results demonstrate that the maize-produced S1 antigen, when administered orally at a low dose, provides superior passive immunity to nursing piglets compared to both conventional injectable vaccines and higher oral doses. The invention establishes a new standard for PEDV vaccination based on mucosal immunization, plant-based antigen production, and biomarker-guided protection.
