## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The developmental origin of human primordial germ cells has long remained one of the most elusive questions in reproductive biology, owing to the inaccessibility of early post-implantation human embryos and the ethical constraints surrounding their study. While murine models have provided a detailed framework for understanding germ cell specification, the molecular and cellular mechanisms governing human primordial germ cell formation diverge significantly from those observed in mice, necessitating a human-specific investigative platform. Previous studies have inferred human germ cell development through extrapolation from nonhuman primates and in vitro differentiation of pluripotent stem cells, yet these approaches have yielded inconsistent results due to the absence of a robust, reproducible, and physiologically relevant in vitro system. The prevailing models have proposed that human primordial germ cells arise either from the posterior epiblast, the primitive streak, or the dorsal amnion, but no consensus has emerged due to the lack of definitive lineage tracing and molecular characterization in human tissues. Moreover, prior in vitro methods for generating primordial germ cell-like cells relied on complex three-dimensional aggregation protocols requiring prolonged culture durations, high concentrations of exogenous growth factors, and genetically modified reporter lines, which limited scalability, reproducibility, and applicability across diverse genetic backgrounds. These methods also failed to resolve the critical temporal dynamics of signaling pathways such as WNT and BMP, which are now understood to be essential for lineage bifurcation but were previously treated as static inputs. The inability to distinguish between true primordial germ cells and contaminating somatic lineages, particularly mesoderm and endoderm derivatives, further obscured the fidelity of these systems. Consequently, there has been a persistent and unmet need for a simplified, two-dimensional, serum-free, monolayer-based platform capable of efficiently and reproducibly generating human primordial germ cells with high purity, without reliance on transgenic reporters, and with defined temporal control over key signaling events. Such a platform would not only resolve longstanding controversies regarding human germ cell origins but also enable the systematic study of germline specification, the modeling of infertility disorders, and the eventual development of cell-based therapies for reproductive medicine.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

A method for forming human primordial germ cells in vitro is disclosed, comprising the sequential exposure of a pluripotent stem cell population to a WNT agonist and a TGFβ agonist for a defined period, followed by the removal of these agonists and the subsequent contact of the resulting posterior epiblast cell population with a WNT inhibitor. This method enables the efficient and reproducible generation of primordial germ cells within a 3.5-day time frame under serum-free, two-dimensional culture conditions. A method for isolating primordial germ cells is further disclosed, wherein the cells are purified by fluorescence-activated cell sorting based on a surface marker profile comprising the co-expression of CXCR4 and the absence of PDGFRα and GARP. A method for forming primordial germ cells is provided, wherein the pluripotent stem cell population is first contacted with Activin-A and CHIR99021 to induce posterior epiblast formation, followed by treatment with XAV939, BMP-4, SCF, and EGF to specify germ cell fate. A method for isolating primordial germ cells is detailed, wherein the population is enriched by sorting for cells that are CXCR4-positive, PDGFRα-negative, and GARP-negative, thereby achieving a purity of greater than 95 percent. A method for treating infertility is described, wherein primordial germ cells generated by the disclosed method are transplanted into a subject with impaired gametogenesis to restore or enhance reproductive potential. Various aspects of primordial germ cells are described, including their continuous expression of pluripotency transcription factors OCT4 and NANOG throughout the transition from pluripotent stem cells to germ cell fate, their transcriptional similarity to early human fetal germ cells, and their capacity to be generated across multiple human embryonic and induced pluripotent stem cell lines without genetic modification.

### Methods

A two-dimensional in vitro platform for generating human primordial germ cells is disclosed, comprising the stepwise exposure of a pluripotent stem cell population to a WNT agonist and a TGFβ agonist for a duration of 12 hours, followed by the removal of these agonists and the subsequent contact with a WNT inhibitor, BMP-4, SCF, and EGF for a total of 3.5 days. The production of human primordial germ cells for drug discovery is simplified by enabling high-throughput screening in monolayer culture without the need for 3D aggregation or transgenic reporters. The production of human primordial germ cells for disease modeling is streamlined by providing a reproducible, serum-free system compatible with patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cells. The production of human primordial germ cells for infertility treatments is facilitated by generating cells with transcriptional fidelity to early fetal germ cells, suitable for transplantation or in vitro gametogenesis. A research tool to investigate key lineage intermediates is provided by the identification of a posterior epiblast population co-expressing pluripotency factors and primitive streak markers. A research tool to investigate extracellular signals in primordial germ cell specification is enabled by the temporal control of WNT and BMP pathways, revealing that WNT inhibition is required for germ cell commitment. Primordial germ cells are formed in vitro by first contacting a pluripotent stem cell population with a WNT agonist and a TGFβ agonist, thereby inducing a posterior epiblast state, then removing the agonists and exposing the cells to a WNT inhibitor, which triggers germ cell specification. A posterior epiblast cell population is formed by exposing pluripotent stem cells to Activin-A and CHIR99021 for 12 hours in serum-free medium. The posterior epiblast cell population is contacted with a WNT inhibitor, such as XAV939, to suppress somatic differentiation and promote germ cell fate. The WNT agonist and TGFβ agonist are removed from the culture medium after 12 hours of exposure, prior to the addition of the WNT inhibitor. Primordial germ cells are formed by maintaining cells in medium containing BMP-4, SCF, EGF, and XAV939 for 72 hours following the removal of the initial agonists. Primordial germ cells are isolated using fluorescence-activated cell sorting based on a surface marker profile of CXCR4-positive, PDGFRα-negative, and GARP-negative cells.
