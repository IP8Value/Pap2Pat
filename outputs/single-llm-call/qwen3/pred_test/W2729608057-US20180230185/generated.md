## TECHNICAL FIELD

- introduce peptidomimetics for HER2-overexpressed cancers

The present invention relates to novel peptidomimetic compounds designed to selectively target and inhibit the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) in cancers characterized by its overexpression. These compounds are engineered to disrupt critical protein-protein interactions that drive oncogenic signaling cascades, particularly those involving HER2 heterodimerization with HER3 or EGFR. Unlike conventional therapeutic modalities such as monoclonal antibodies or small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors, these peptidomimetics are structurally optimized to mimic key residues at the dimerization interface while exhibiting enhanced metabolic stability, membrane permeability, and target specificity. Their design leverages conformational constraints derived from cyclic peptide scaffolds incorporating non-natural amino acids and stereochemically defined proline motifs, enabling high-affinity binding to domain IV of HER2 without cross-reactivity to other members of the EGFR family. This class of compounds represents a new therapeutic paradigm for the treatment of breast, lung, ovarian, and other HER2-driven malignancies where resistance to existing therapies has emerged as a major clinical challenge.

## BACKGROUND ART

- motivate EGFRs in human cancers

Dysregulated signaling through the epidermal growth factor receptor family is a well-established driver of tumorigenesis across multiple epithelial cancers, including invasive breast carcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and ovarian adenocarcinoma. These receptors function as receptor tyrosine kinases that, upon ligand-induced activation, initiate downstream pathways governing cell proliferation, survival, migration, and resistance to apoptosis. Overexpression or mutation of these receptors leads to constitutive signaling, uncontrolled cell cycle progression, and evasion of normal growth controls, making them compelling targets for therapeutic intervention. Among the four members of this family—EGFR (HER1), HER2, HER3, and HER4—HER2 stands out due to its ligand-independent activation potential and its central role as a preferred dimerization partner for other family members, thereby amplifying oncogenic signals even in the absence of direct ligand binding.

- describe EGFR family and signaling

The EGFR family comprises transmembrane glycoproteins composed of an extracellular ligand-binding domain, a single transmembrane helix, and an intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. Upon dimerization—either homodimerization or heterodimerization—these receptors undergo autophosphorylation of specific tyrosine residues within their cytoplasmic tails, creating docking sites for adaptor proteins such as Grb2 and Shc, which activate the RAS-MAPK and PI3K-AKT pathways. HER2, unlike its counterparts, lacks a known ligand but is constitutively primed for dimerization due to its extended extracellular conformation. This renders HER2-overexpressing tumors particularly aggressive, as even low levels of ligands such as neuregulin-1 can trigger robust heterodimerization with HER3, leading to sustained activation of survival pathways and therapeutic resistance.

- limitations of existing cancer therapies

Current standard-of-care treatments for HER2-positive cancers include monoclonal antibodies like trastuzumab and pertuzumab, as well as small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as lapatinib and neratinib. While these agents have improved survival outcomes, their clinical utility is significantly limited by intrinsic and acquired resistance mechanisms. Trastuzumab, which binds to domain IV of HER2, often fails to fully suppress downstream signaling due to incomplete inhibition of heterodimerization or compensatory activation of alternative pathways. Pertuzumab, targeting domain II, inhibits dimerization but does not induce receptor internalization or antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity as effectively as desired. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors, while orally bioavailable, are frequently rendered ineffective by point mutations in the kinase domain, such as the T790M gatekeeper mutation, which sterically hinders drug binding. Moreover, all these modalities suffer from poor tissue penetration, high immunogenicity, limited oral bioavailability, and substantial manufacturing costs.

- discuss HER2-overexpressed cancers

HER2 overexpression occurs in approximately 15–20% of breast cancers and is associated with poor prognosis, rapid disease progression, and increased metastatic potential. It is also observed in subsets of gastric, ovarian, and non-small cell lung cancers, where it correlates with resistance to chemotherapy and targeted agents. The oncogenic potency of HER2 stems not only from its overexpression but from its ability to form heterodimers with HER3, which possesses multiple PI3K-binding sites, thereby creating a potent survival signal that is difficult to suppress with single-agent therapies. Consequently, there remains a critical unmet need for agents that can simultaneously and selectively inhibit multiple dimerization interfaces with high affinity, metabolic stability, and minimal off-target effects.

- summarize existing approaches to cancer therapy

Existing therapeutic strategies have largely focused on either blocking ligand binding, inhibiting kinase activity, or promoting receptor degradation via immune-mediated mechanisms. Monoclonal antibodies are effective but require intravenous administration, have limited tumor penetration, and may elicit immune responses upon repeated dosing. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors are orally administered but suffer from narrow therapeutic windows and rapid development of resistance. Combination therapies, while sometimes synergistic, increase toxicity and complicate patient management. Peptide-based inhibitors have been explored but are typically susceptible to proteolytic degradation, poor cellular uptake, and low oral bioavailability, rendering them unsuitable for systemic administration.

- limitations of antibodies and tyrosine kinase inhibitors

Monoclonal antibodies are large molecules that do not readily cross biological membranes, limiting their efficacy against intracellular signaling nodes. Their immunogenicity necessitates frequent dosing and can lead to infusion reactions or neutralizing antibody formation. Tyrosine kinase inhibitors, while cell-permeable, lack specificity and often inhibit unrelated kinases, resulting in off-target toxicities. Furthermore, their binding to the ATP-binding pocket is vulnerable to mutations that alter steric or electrostatic complementarity, leading to clinical resistance. Neither modality effectively targets the extracellular dimerization interface with the precision required to disrupt HER2:HER3 or HER2:EGFR heterodimers without affecting physiological signaling.

- prior art on peptidomimetics for HER2 binding

Previous attempts to develop peptidomimetic inhibitors of HER2 have yielded linear peptides with moderate affinity and poor stability. These compounds often rely on natural amino acid sequences that are rapidly cleaved by serum proteases, limiting their in vivo utility. Some have incorporated D-amino acids or cyclization to improve stability, but none have achieved the combination of high binding affinity, structural rigidity, and selectivity for domain IV of HER2 that is demonstrated by the compounds of the present invention. Moreover, prior peptidomimetics have not been shown to effectively inhibit HER2:HER3 heterodimerization in physiologically relevant cellular models or to suppress tumor growth in vivo with a favorable safety profile.

- targeting HER2 domain IV and II

Domain IV of HER2 is a critical region for heterodimerization with HER3 and EGFR, as it contains key hydrophobic and electrostatic residues that mediate intermolecular contact. While pertuzumab binds domain II and sterically blocks dimerization, domain IV offers a more direct interface for competitive inhibition. Targeting domain IV allows for direct occlusion of the dimerization interface rather than allosteric modulation, potentially yielding more complete suppression of downstream signaling. Prior efforts to target this domain have been hindered by the lack of high-resolution structural data for HER2 heterodimers and the difficulty in designing small molecules that mimic the large, irregular interface of protein-protein interactions.

- peptides targeting HER2

Several linear peptides derived from HER2-binding motifs have been reported, but they exhibit weak binding affinities (micromolar range), poor pharmacokinetic profiles, and negligible in vivo efficacy. Their flexibility allows for multiple conformations, reducing the entropic advantage upon binding. Cyclization has been attempted to constrain conformation, but without strategic incorporation of non-natural amino acids or stereochemical control, these analogs fail to achieve the necessary binding orientation or stability.

- cyclic peptide incorporating D-Pro and L-Pro

Cyclic peptides incorporating D-Pro and L-Pro sequences have been shown to stabilize β-turn conformations in solution, which are critical for mimicking protein interaction motifs. However, prior applications have not exploited the chiral switch between L-Pro-D-Pro and D-Pro-L-Pro to modulate spatial orientation of pharmacophoric groups. The specific stereochemical arrangement of these proline residues in conjunction with β-amino acid side chains has not been previously recognized as a determinant of binding specificity to HER2 domain IV or as a means to achieve nanomolar inhibitory potency.

- unfilled need for improved treatments

There remains a significant and unfilled need for orally bioavailable, metabolically stable, highly selective, and potent inhibitors of HER2 heterodimerization that can overcome the limitations of current therapies. Such agents must bind with high affinity to domain IV of HER2, inhibit both HER2:HER3 and HER2:EGFR heterodimers, suppress downstream kinase phosphorylation, demonstrate robust antitumor activity in vivo, and exhibit minimal toxicity. The compounds described herein fulfill this need by combining a symmetric cyclic architecture, stereochemically defined proline motifs, and optimized hydrophobic and cationic pharmacophores to achieve unprecedented target engagement and therapeutic efficacy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce novel peptidomimetics for HER2-overexpressed cancers

The present invention provides a novel class of cyclic peptidomimetic compounds specifically designed to inhibit HER2-mediated oncogenic signaling by disrupting heterodimerization with HER3 and EGFR. These compounds are characterized by a symmetric, macrocyclic backbone that incorporates a conserved Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp sequence repeated on either side of a central L-Pro-D-Pro dipeptide motif, which enforces a stable β-hairpin conformation essential for high-affinity binding to domain IV of HER2. The compounds are not antibodies, nor are they traditional peptides; they are synthetic, non-natural molecular entities engineered to mimic the structural and electrostatic features of the dimerization interface while resisting proteolytic degradation and exhibiting favorable pharmacokinetic properties.

- describe peptidomimetics binding to HER2 protein

The peptidomimetics of the invention bind with high specificity to the extracellular domain IV of HER2, as demonstrated by surface plasmon resonance and competitive binding assays. Binding occurs at a site overlapping the dimerization interface used by HER3 and EGFR, thereby directly preventing heterodimer formation. The compounds do not bind significantly to EGFR, HER3, or HER4, indicating exceptional selectivity for HER2. This binding is mediated by a combination of hydrophobic interactions involving β-naphthyl and phenyl side chains, electrostatic interactions with Arg residues and acidic residues on HER2, and conformational complementarity conferred by the L-Pro-D-Pro turn.

- inhibit protein-protein interactions and signaling

By occupying the dimerization interface on HER2, these compounds prevent the formation of HER2:HER3 and HER2:EGFR heterodimers, thereby blocking the transphosphorylation of the intracellular kinase domains. This results in the suppression of downstream signaling cascades, including the PI3K-AKT and RAS-MAPK pathways, leading to cell cycle arrest, induction of apoptosis, and inhibition of tumor cell proliferation. The compounds are effective at nanomolar concentrations in HER2-overexpressing cell lines and show minimal activity in HER2-low or HER2-negative cells, confirming their mechanism is dependent on HER2 expression.

- advantages of peptidomimetics over antibodies and peptides

Compared to monoclonal antibodies, these peptidomimetics are significantly smaller, enabling better tissue penetration and potential for oral administration. Unlike antibodies, they are not immunogenic, do not require refrigerated storage, and can be synthesized at scale using solid-phase peptide synthesis. Compared to linear peptides, they exhibit dramatically improved metabolic stability in serum, with half-lives exceeding 48 hours, and enhanced binding affinity due to pre-organization of the bioactive conformation. Their synthetic nature allows for precise structural modifications to optimize pharmacokinetics, solubility, and bioavailability.

- peptidomimetics bind to domain IV of HER2

The compounds bind specifically to domain IV of HER2, as confirmed by surface plasmon resonance using recombinant HER2 extracellular domain and domain IV fragments. Binding is not observed with other EGFR family members, nor with domain II or III of HER2. The binding site corresponds to a hydrophobic pocket formed by residues Pro543, Phe573, Pro584, and Pro590, with key electrostatic contacts involving Asp570, Glu544, and Gly581. This site is distinct from the epitopes of trastuzumab and pertuzumab, allowing for potential combination therapy without steric interference.

- antiproliferative activity and kinase domain phosphorylation

The compounds demonstrate potent antiproliferative activity in HER2-overexpressing cell lines such as SKBR-3, BT-474, and Calu-3, with IC50 values ranging from 18 to 194 nM. In contrast, activity is negligible in HER2-negative lines such as MCF-7 and MCF-10A. Treatment with the compounds leads to dose-dependent reduction in phosphorylation of HER2 at tyrosine residues 1221/1222 and 1248, as well as downstream effectors such as AKT and ERK, confirming inhibition of signaling at the kinase level.

- tumor suppression in xenograft animal model

In a xenograft model of HER2-positive breast cancer using BT-474 cells implanted in athymic nude mice, administration of the lead compound resulted in significant suppression of tumor growth compared to vehicle control, with tumor volumes reduced by over 60% after 19 days of treatment. Western blot analysis of excised tumors confirmed decreased HER2 phosphorylation and reduced HER2:HER3 heterodimerization as detected by proximity ligation assay. No signs of systemic toxicity were observed in histopathological examination of vital organs.

- embodiments of peptidomimetics in consumable products

The compounds may be formulated into oral dosage forms such as tablets, capsules, or liquid suspensions for chronic administration. They may also be incorporated into transdermal patches, inhalable powders, or injectable solutions for localized or systemic delivery. The compounds are stable under standard pharmaceutical storage conditions and may be combined with excipients, stabilizers, or penetration enhancers to optimize bioavailability.

- exemplary Compound 18 and its properties

Compound 18 is a symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic with the sequence cyclo[Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp-L-Pro-D-Pro-Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp], where Anapa is (2R,3S)-2-amino-3-(2-naphthyl)propanoic acid. It has a molecular weight of approximately 1425.7 Da, a calculated logP of 3.2, and exhibits >95% purity by HPLC. Its binding affinity to HER2 domain IV is characterized by a Kd of 125 nM, and it demonstrates an IC50 of 18 nM in Calu-3 lung cancer cells. It is stable in mouse serum for over 48 hours and suppresses tumor growth in vivo at a dose of 4 mg/kg administered intratumorally twice weekly.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

- illustrate Compound 18 structure

Compound 18 is a macrocyclic molecule formed by head-to-tail cyclization between the C-terminal carboxyl group of the second Asp residue and the N-terminal amine of the first Arg residue. The backbone contains two identical Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp segments connected by a central L-Pro-D-Pro dipeptide linker. The β-naphthyl side chains of the Anapa residues project outward from the cyclic core, oriented to engage a hydrophobic cleft on HER2 domain IV. The Arg side chains are positioned to form salt bridges with acidic residues on HER2, while the Phe and Asp residues contribute to van der Waals and hydrogen bonding interactions. The L-Pro-D-Pro sequence enforces a right-handed β-turn that rigidifies the entire structure, locking the pharmacophores into the optimal spatial arrangement for binding.

- generalize Compound 18 structure

The general structure of the compounds of the invention is represented by the formula: cyclo[Arg-Xaa-Phe-Asp-L-Pro-D-Pro-Arg-Xaa-Phe-Asp], wherein Xaa is a β-amino acid selected from (2R,3S)-2-amino-3-(2-naphthyl)propanoic acid, (2S,3R)-2-amino-3-(2-naphthyl)propanoic acid, or analogs thereof substituted at the 3-position with aryl, heteroaryl, or substituted alkyl groups. The cyclic backbone may be modified by replacement of one or more amino acids with non-natural analogs, including N-methylated, D-amino acid, or α,α-disubstituted variants, provided the overall conformation and binding affinity are preserved.

- describe stereoisomers of Compound 18

The stereochemistry of the Anapa residue and the Pro-Pro sequence critically determines biological activity. The most active isomer, Compound 18, contains the (2R,3S)-Anapa configuration and the L-Pro-D-Pro sequence. Diastereomers with (2S,3R)-Anapa and L-Pro-D-Pro, or (2R,3S)-Anapa and D-Pro-L-Pro, exhibit significantly reduced potency, demonstrating a chiral switch phenomenon. The spatial orientation of the β-naphthyl groups relative to the HER2 binding pocket is altered in these isomers, leading to suboptimal hydrophobic contacts and loss of inhibitory activity.

- describe optional substitutions on naphthyl group

The 2-naphthyl group of the Anapa residue may be substituted at the 1-, 3-, 4-, 5-, 6-, or 7-positions with alkyl, halo, alkoxy, cyano, nitro, or aryl groups. Substitutions such as 3-methyl, 4-fluoro, or 6-trifluoromethyl enhance lipophilicity and binding affinity without compromising conformational stability. Larger substituents such as biphenyl or naphthyl extensions may be tolerated if they maintain the planar geometry required for insertion into the hydrophobic pocket.

- show additional compounds within scope of invention

Additional compounds within the scope of the invention include analogs in which the Arg residue is replaced with Lys, Orn, or homoArg; the Asp residue is replaced with Glu, phosphoAsp, or carboxyethylcysteine; the Phe residue is replaced with Tyr, Trp, or 2-naphthylalanine; and the Anapa residue is replaced with β-homoPhe, β-(3-indolyl)alanine, or β-(4-pyridyl)alanine. All such analogs retain the symmetric cyclic architecture and the L-Pro-D-Pro turn.

- describe optional replacements of amino acids

The Arg, Asp, and Phe residues may be replaced with non-proteinogenic amino acids bearing modified side chains, including those with extended π-systems, charged groups, or hydrogen-bonding functionalities. The cyclic backbone may be expanded to include additional amino acids such as Gly, Ala, or Sar to modulate ring size and flexibility, provided the distance between the two Anapa residues remains compatible with HER2 binding.

- describe exemplary substituents

Exemplary substituents include methyl, ethyl, propyl, isopropyl, fluoro, chloro, bromo, iodo, methoxy, ethoxy, cyano, nitro, trifluoromethyl, phenyl, pyridyl, thienyl, and benzyl. These may be attached to the naphthyl ring, the Arg guanidinium, the Asp carboxylate, or the Phe phenyl ring to modulate solubility, binding affinity, or metabolic stability.

- describe modifications to Compound 18 backbone

The backbone may be modified by N-methylation of amide bonds to reduce hydrogen bonding potential and enhance membrane permeability. The amide bonds may be replaced with ester, urea, thiourea, or sulfonamide linkages to improve metabolic stability. The cyclization may occur via lactam, lactone, or triazole linkages formed by click chemistry or native chemical ligation, provided the overall topology of the pharmacophores is maintained.

### Example 1. Compound 18: Synthesis and Characterization

- synthesize compound 18

Compound 18 was synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis on 2-chlorotrityl chloride resin. Fmoc-Pro-OH was loaded onto the resin, followed by sequential coupling of Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-OH, Fmoc-(2R,3S)-Anapa-OH, Fmoc-Phe-OH, and Fmoc-Asp(OtBu)-OH. The sequence was repeated in reverse order, followed by coupling of L-Pro and D-Pro. After full chain assembly, the peptide was cleaved from the resin using 2,2,2-trifluoroethanol:dichloromethane (1:1), and the linear precursor was cyclized in solution using PyAOP and DIEA in tetrahydrofuran/dimethylformamide (4:1). The cyclic product was deprotected with TFA/water/TIPS (95:2.5:2.5), precipitated in cold diethyl ether, and purified by preparative HPLC.

- characterize compound 18 by LC-MS

Compound 18 was characterized by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry using a C18 column and a gradient of water/acetonitrile containing 0.1% formic acid. The observed m/z for [M+H]+ was 1425.7, matching the calculated mass of 1425.6.

- characterize compound 18 by 1H 2D-NMR

1H 2D-NMR spectra (TOCSY, NOESY, ROESY) were acquired in DMSO-d6 at 500 MHz. Resonances were assigned for all protons, and key NOE contacts confirmed the β-hairpin conformation, including inter-residue contacts between Anapa Hα and Phe Hδ.

- describe compound 18 structure

Compound 18 is a symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic with the sequence cyclo[Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp-L-Pro-D-Pro-Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp]. The L-Pro-D-Pro sequence enforces a right-handed β-turn, positioning the two Anapa β-naphthyl groups in a parallel orientation suitable for insertion into the HER2 hydrophobic pocket.

- describe β-naphthyl moiety

The β-naphthyl moiety of the Anapa residue is a planar, hydrophobic group that mimics the side chain of tryptophan and inserts into a cleft formed by Pro543, Phe573, Pro584, and Pro590 on HER2 domain IV.

- describe stereoisomers of compound 18

Stereoisomers of compound 18 include the (2S,3R)-Anapa variant and the D-Pro-L-Pro variant, both of which exhibit reduced binding affinity and antiproliferative activity due to altered spatial orientation of the β-naphthyl groups.

- summarize compound 18 properties

Compound 18 has a molecular weight of 1425.7 Da, a logP of 3.2, a solubility in water of 12 µg/mL, and a half-life in mouse serum of >48 hours. It is stable at room temperature for at least six months when stored as a lyophilized powder.

### Example 2. Inhibition Testing

- test compound 18 for antiproliferative activity

Compound 18 was tested for antiproliferative activity in HER2-overexpressing cell lines SKBR-3, BT-474, Calu-3, and HER2-negative lines MCF-7 and MCF-10A using the CellTiter-Glo assay. After 72 hours of exposure, IC50 values were determined.

- summarize IC50 values for different cancer cell lines

In SKBR-3 cells, IC50 was 194 nM; in BT-474, 210 nM; in Calu-3, 18 nM; in MCF-7, >50 µM; and in MCF-10A, 40 µM. The compound demonstrated over 200-fold selectivity for HER2-overexpressing cells.

### Example 5. Dose-Response Curves for Heterodimerization

- describe dose-response curve for heterodimerization

Dose-response curves for HER2:HER3 heterodimerization were generated using the PathHunter assay in U2OS cells transfected with HER2 and HER3. Luminescence was measured after 24 hours of compound exposure.

- describe inhibition of HER2-HER3 heterodimerization

Compound 18 inhibited HER2:HER3 heterodimerization with an IC50 of 38 nM, while compound 21 (an analog) had an IC50 of 120 nM. Neuregulin-1 induced dimerization with an EC50 of 0.15 nM, and the control compound showed no inhibition.

- describe PathHunter assay

The PathHunter assay utilizes enzyme fragment complementation: HER2 is fused to a β-galactosidase acceptor fragment, HER3 to a donor fragment. Dimerization brings fragments together, reconstituting enzyme activity, which is detected by luminescence.

- describe dose-response curve for compound 18

The dose-response curve for compound 18 showed a sigmoidal inhibition profile with an IC50 of 38 nM and a Hill slope of 1.1, indicating cooperative binding.

- describe dose-response curve for compound 21

Compound 21 exhibited a shallower curve with an IC50 of 120 nM, indicating reduced potency.

- describe dose-response curve for neuregulin-1

Neuregulin-1 induced dimerization with an EC50 of 0.15 nM, confirming ligand-dependent activation.

- describe dose-response curve for control compound

The control compound showed no inhibition of dimerization up to 10 µM, confirming specificity.

- describe inhibition of HER2-HER3 heterodimerization by compound 18

Compound 18 inhibited HER2:HER3 heterodimerization in a concentration-dependent manner, with near-complete inhibition at 1 µM.

- describe inhibition of HER2-HER3 heterodimerization by compound 21

Compound 21 inhibited heterodimerization but with reduced efficacy, consistent with its lower binding affinity.

- describe inhibition of HER2-HER3 heterodimerization by neuregulin-1

Neuregulin-1 induced robust heterodimerization, serving as a positive control for pathway activation.

- describe inhibition of HER2-HER3 heterodimerization by control compound

The control compound had no effect on heterodimerization, confirming lack of non-specific inhibition.
