# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- introduce Chlamydia antigen MOMP

The present invention relates to recombinant vaccine antigens derived from the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia trachomatis and related species, and more specifically to methods for the recombinant expression, purification, and formulation of MOMP as a subunit vaccine candidate for the prevention and treatment of chlamydial infections. MOMP is a highly conserved, immunodominant β-barrel outer membrane protein that constitutes approximately sixty percent of the total protein mass of the infectious elementary body form of Chlamydia. Due to its surface exposure, structural stability, and capacity to elicit both humoral and cellular immune responses, MOMP has long been recognized as a principal target for vaccine development against Chlamydia-associated pathologies, including genital tract infections, pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal infertility, ectopic pregnancy, neonatal conjunctivitis, and infant pneumonia. The invention provides a novel platform for the production of structurally authentic, immunogenic MOMP in a heterologous bacterial host, enabling scalable, cost-effective manufacturing of a vaccine antigen that retains key conformational epitopes critical for protective immunity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce Chlamydia trachomatis

Chlamydia trachomatis is an obligate intracellular, Gram-negative bacterium that infects human mucosal epithelial surfaces and is responsible for the most prevalent bacterial sexually transmitted infection worldwide. It exists in two distinct developmental forms: the metabolically inert, extracellular elementary body (EB), which is infectious and adapted for host cell entry, and the replicative, intracellular reticulate body. The bacterium is classified into multiple serovars based on antigenic variation in its major outer membrane protein, with serovars A through C causing trachoma, serovars D through K associated with urogenital disease, and serovars L1 through L3 responsible for lymphogranuloma venereum. The global burden of C. trachomatis infection is immense, with an estimated 127 million new cases annually, leading to significant morbidity, reproductive health complications, and economic strain on healthcare systems.

- describe pathologies caused by Chlamydia

In women, untreated genital infection can ascend into the upper reproductive tract, triggering inflammation of the cervix, endometrium, fallopian tubes, and ovaries, often without overt symptoms. This silent progression can result in tubal scarring, obstruction, and subsequent infertility or ectopic pregnancy. In men, infection may lead to urethritis, epididymitis, and reactive arthritis. Neonates born to infected mothers are at risk of developing conjunctivitis and pneumonia. The chronic inflammatory sequelae of repeated or persistent infection underscore the urgent need for an effective prophylactic vaccine to interrupt transmission and prevent long-term complications.

- motivate need for Chlamydia vaccine

Despite the availability of antibiotics for treatment, reinfection rates remain high due to asymptomatic carriage and limited screening programs. Antibiotics do not prevent reinfection or reverse existing tissue damage, and their widespread use contributes to antimicrobial resistance. A vaccine capable of inducing durable, protective immunity would represent a transformative public health intervention, reducing the incidence of infection and its devastating reproductive consequences. The development of such a vaccine has been hindered by the complex immunobiology of Chlamydia and the failure of early whole-cell or inactivated preparations to confer lasting protection.

- describe prior art Chlamydia vaccines

Historical vaccine efforts have included formalin-inactivated elementary bodies, live attenuated strains, and subunit antigens derived from purified native MOMP. While native MOMP isolated from infected cell cultures demonstrated partial protective efficacy in murine challenge models, its production is inherently limited by the low yield of EBs, the labor-intensive and costly purification process, and the inability to scale manufacturing to meet global demand. Additionally, native MOMP preparations often contain contaminating host cell components and endotoxins that complicate regulatory approval and safety profiling.

- describe limitations of prior art vaccines

Recombinant expression of MOMP in Escherichia coli has been attempted as an alternative, but early efforts resulted in the accumulation of misfolded protein within cytoplasmic inclusion bodies. These aggregates required denaturation and refolding procedures that frequently failed to restore the native β-barrel conformation, leading to reduced immunogenicity and diminished protective capacity in animal models. Furthermore, attempts to express MOMP in the periplasm or outer membrane were hampered by host toxicity, low expression yields, and inefficient translocation due to incompatible signal sequences or excessive transcriptional burden. The lack of a robust, reproducible, and scalable method for producing structurally authentic MOMP has impeded clinical translation.

- motivate new vaccine candidates

There remains a critical unmet need for a recombinant MOMP-based vaccine that is produced in a manner that preserves its native conformation, minimizes host-derived contaminants, and enables high-yield manufacturing under Good Manufacturing Practice conditions. The invention addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive method for the optimized recombinant expression of MOMP directly within the outer membrane of E. coli, utilizing codon harmonization, tailored expression vectors, and precise control of physiological parameters to achieve high levels of properly folded, immunologically active antigen.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce recombinant MOMP expression method

The invention provides a method for the recombinant expression of Chlamydia trachomatis major outer membrane protein (MOMP) in Escherichia coli such that the protein is correctly inserted into the bacterial outer membrane, retains its native β-barrel conformation, and elicits a protective immune response upon administration. This method enables the production of a vaccine antigen that is structurally and immunologically comparable to native MOMP purified from elementary bodies, while overcoming the limitations of traditional purification and refolding techniques.

- describe transforming E. coli host cells

The method comprises the transformation of a suitable E. coli host strain with an expression vector containing a nucleic acid sequence encoding a Chlamydia MOMP polypeptide, operatively linked to a promoter and a secretion leader sequence. The host strain is selected for its compatibility with outer membrane protein biogenesis and its capacity to support low-to-moderate levels of transcription and translation without inducing excessive cellular stress or lysis.

- describe culturing transformed cells

The transformed host cells are cultured in a defined growth medium under controlled conditions, including temperature, cell density at induction, and duration of induction, to maximize the surface expression of MOMP while maintaining host viability. Induction is performed during mid-logarithmic phase at a temperature between 25°C and 30°C, and the culture medium is optimized to support membrane protein synthesis without promoting inclusion body formation.

- describe producing recombinant MOMP

Recombinant MOMP is produced as a full-length, integral outer membrane protein with its N-terminal leader sequence cleaved by endogenous signal peptidases, resulting in a mature protein that is stably integrated into the outer membrane. The expressed protein is not aggregated in the cytoplasm, nor is it retained in the periplasm; instead, it is efficiently translocated and assembled into the outer membrane via the Bam complex, preserving its native tertiary structure.

- describe optional purification step

Following expression, the recombinant MOMP may be purified from the outer membrane fraction by sequential detergent extraction and chromatographic methods, including size exclusion and ion exchange chromatography, to yield a preparation of high purity suitable for pharmaceutical formulation. The purification process removes host cell proteins, endotoxins, and other contaminants while preserving the structural integrity of the MOMP antigen.

- describe codon harmonization

The nucleic acid sequence encoding MOMP is codon-harmonized to approximate the synonymous codon usage profile of the native Chlamydia host, rather than being fully optimized for E. coli codon bias. This approach incorporates rare codons that are naturally present in the Chlamydia genome, thereby modulating translation elongation rates to facilitate co-translational folding and membrane insertion, resulting in higher yields of functional protein.

- describe codon optimization

In alternative embodiments, the nucleic acid sequence may be codon-optimized for E. coli to maximize translation efficiency, provided that the resulting sequence does not disrupt critical folding intermediates or induce ribosomal stalling. Codon optimization may be applied selectively to regions of the gene that are not associated with structural domains critical for β-barrel assembly.

- describe leader sequence options

The expression vector includes a secretion leader sequence that directs the nascent MOMP polypeptide across the inner membrane and into the periplasmic space. Suitable leader sequences include those derived from E. coli OmpA, OmpP, or Shigella flexneri SopA, which have been shown to enhance outer membrane localization of MOMP more effectively than native Chlamydia leader sequences. The leader sequence is fused directly to the N-terminus of the mature MOMP coding region, with or without a short spacer of one to five amino acids to facilitate signal peptidase cleavage.

- describe promoter options

The expression vector employs a promoter of moderate strength, such as the λPL promoter, to avoid transcriptional overload and maintain host cell fitness. Strong promoters, such as T7, are avoided as they lead to rapid mRNA accumulation, ribosomal traffic jams, and increased misfolding. The promoter is operatively linked to the nucleic acid sequence encoding MOMP and is inducible by isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) or other chemical inducers.

- describe transcription/translation rate control

Transcription and translation rates are further controlled by the use of low-copy-number plasmid backbones, such as pACYC or pAVE029, and by the selection of host strains with attenuated RNA polymerase activity. Induction is performed at a cell density corresponding to an OD600 of approximately 0.5, ensuring that protein synthesis occurs under conditions of balanced metabolic capacity.

- describe recombinant MOMP production

The recombinant MOMP produced by this method is a full-length, post-translationally processed protein that is embedded in the outer membrane of E. coli and retains the characteristic β-sheet secondary structure of native MOMP, as confirmed by circular dichroism spectroscopy. The protein is capable of binding conformation-dependent antibodies raised against native Chlamydia elementary bodies, indicating preservation of key epitopes.

- describe pharmaceutical composition

The invention further encompasses a pharmaceutical composition comprising the recombinant MOMP antigen in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier and an immunostimulatory adjuvant. The composition is formulated for parenteral administration and may include saponin-based, aluminum-based, or ISCOM-type adjuvants to enhance the magnitude and quality of the immune response.

- describe treatment or prophylaxis of Chlamydia

The recombinant MOMP composition is intended for the prophylaxis or treatment of Chlamydia trachomatis infection in humans and animals. Administration of the composition induces a humoral immune response characterized by neutralizing antibodies against elementary bodies and a Th1-biased cellular immune response, as evidenced by interferon-gamma production and cytotoxic T cell activation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce MOMP as vaccine target

The major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of Chlamydia trachomatis is a structurally conserved, surface-exposed β-barrel porin that serves as the primary target for neutralizing antibodies and T cell recognition. Its high immunogenicity, abundance on the elementary body surface, and conservation across serovars make it an ideal candidate for subunit vaccine development. Despite decades of research, no licensed MOMP-based vaccine has reached clinical use due to challenges in producing a properly folded, stable, and immunogenic form of the protein.

- limitations of native MOMP purification

Purification of native MOMP from infected cell cultures is inherently inefficient, requiring large-scale propagation of Chlamydia in eukaryotic cells, followed by laborious detergent extraction, gradient centrifugation, and chromatographic separation. The resulting preparations are contaminated with host cell proteins, lipids, and endotoxins, and the yield is insufficient for global vaccine deployment. Furthermore, batch-to-batch variability and the risk of residual infectivity limit regulatory acceptance.

- recombinant expression as alternative

Recombinant expression in bacterial hosts offers a scalable, cost-effective, and controllable alternative. However, previous attempts to express MOMP in E. coli resulted in cytoplasmic aggregation, misfolding, and poor immunogenicity. The present invention overcomes these limitations by directing MOMP expression to the outer membrane, where it is naturally folded and assembled by the host’s own machinery.

- previous attempts at recombinant expression

Earlier studies expressed MOMP in the cytoplasm, leading to inclusion body formation. Attempts to refold the protein in vitro yielded aggregates with reduced conformational integrity and diminished protective efficacy. Other efforts to express MOMP in the periplasm resulted in low yields and instability. Surface expression was rarely achieved, and when it was, it was accompanied by severe host toxicity and rapid cell lysis.

- invention provides method for recombinant expression

The invention provides a method for recombinant expression of MOMP in the outer membrane of E. coli that achieves high yields without compromising host viability. This is accomplished through a combination of codon harmonization, moderate promoter strength, low-copy-number vectors, optimized leader sequences, and precisely controlled induction conditions.

- expression in E. coli outer membrane

The recombinant MOMP is expressed as a full-length, membrane-integrated protein with its N-terminal signal sequence cleaved, resulting in a mature protein that is stably inserted into the outer membrane. This localization ensures that the protein adopts its native β-barrel conformation, as confirmed by circular dichroism and antibody binding assays.

- method comprises transformation, culturing, and purification

The method comprises three essential steps: transformation of E. coli with a recombinant expression vector encoding MOMP, culturing the transformed cells under optimized conditions to induce surface expression, and purification of the antigen from the outer membrane fraction using detergent extraction and chromatography.

- E. coli outer membrane expressed MOMP elicits protection

Mice immunized with recombinant MOMP expressed on the E. coli outer membrane develop high-titer antibodies that bind native Chlamydia elementary bodies and exhibit significant protection against intravaginal challenge, as measured by reduced bacterial shedding. The level of protection is comparable to that achieved with native MOMP.

- limitations of heterologous protein expression

Expression of complex outer membrane proteins in heterologous hosts is often hindered by mismatched codon usage, inefficient translocation, and host toxicity. These limitations are particularly acute for cysteine-rich β-barrel proteins like MOMP, which require precise folding kinetics and disulfide bond formation.

- codon redundancy and preference

Codon usage varies significantly between Chlamydia and E. coli, with certain codons being preferred in one organism and rare in the other. This disparity can lead to translational pausing, misfolding, and degradation of the nascent polypeptide.

- codon harmonization for optimal expression

Codon harmonization involves replacing codons in the MOMP gene with those that are statistically similar in frequency between Chlamydia and E. coli, rather than replacing all codons with the most abundant E. coli equivalents. This preserves natural translation kinetics, allowing the protein to fold correctly as it emerges from the ribosome.

- codon optimization for high expression

Codon optimization, which replaces rare codons with the most frequently used synonyms in the host, may increase expression levels but often results in misfolding. The invention demonstrates that codon harmonization yields higher functional protein than full optimization.

- methods for codon harmonization and optimization

Codon harmonization is performed by aligning the codon usage profiles of Chlamydia and E. coli, identifying synonymous codons with similar frequencies, and replacing codons accordingly. Optimization is performed by selecting the most abundant codons for each amino acid, with careful exclusion of regions critical for folding.

- creating synthetic genes for MOMP expression

Synthetic genes encoding codon-harmonized or codon-optimized MOMP sequences are chemically synthesized and cloned into expression vectors using restriction sites such as NdeI and XhoI, avoiding native restriction sites that may interfere with cloning.

- variations in codon optimization procedure

Variations include partial optimization of non-structural regions, retention of rare codons in transmembrane domains, and use of codon bias indices to predict folding efficiency.

- expression vector with leader sequence and MOMP

The expression vector contains a nucleic acid cassette comprising a promoter, a secretion leader sequence, and the MOMP coding sequence, all arranged in a single transcriptional unit.

- leader sequence for targeting MOMP to outer membrane

The leader sequence is selected from E. coli OmpA, OmpP, or Shigella flexneri SopA, each of which directs efficient translocation to the periplasm and subsequent outer membrane insertion.

- additional amino acid residues between leader and MOMP

One to five amino acid residues may be inserted between the leader sequence and the MOMP coding region to facilitate signal peptidase cleavage and prevent steric interference.

- expression of MOMP fusion proteins

MOMP may be expressed as a fusion protein with a heterologous polypeptide, such as a carrier protein or epitope tag, attached to the N- or C-terminus to enhance solubility, immunogenicity, or purification.

- additional polypeptide attached to MOMP

The additional polypeptide may be a dimerization domain, a T-cell epitope, or a molecular adjuvant, designed to enhance the immune response without altering the core structure of MOMP.

- attachment of additional polypeptide to N- or C-terminus

The additional polypeptide may be fused to either the N-terminus, downstream of the leader sequence, or to the C-terminus, provided that the fusion does not disrupt the β-barrel folding or membrane insertion.

- nucleic acid molecule with leader, MOMP, and additional polypeptide

The nucleic acid molecule encodes a single open reading frame comprising the leader sequence, the MOMP coding region, and the additional polypeptide sequence, all in-frame and under the control of a single promoter.

- MOMP comprises Chlamydia trachomatis or muridarum sequence

The MOMP sequence may be derived from any Chlamydia trachomatis serovar (D, E, F, G, etc.) or from Chlamydia muridarum, with preference for sequences that are conserved across clinically relevant strains.

- MOMP derivatives with deletions, insertions, or substitutions

Derivatives of MOMP may include deletions of non-conserved loops, insertions of flexible linkers, or substitutions of surface-exposed residues to enhance stability or reduce immunodominance of non-protective epitopes.

- leader sequence comprises Shigella flexneri SopA or other sequences

The leader sequence may be derived from Shigella flexneri SopA, Salmonella enterica PgtE, Yersinia pestis Pla, or E. coli OmpP, each of which has been shown to support higher surface expression than native Chlamydia leaders.

- promoter operatively linked to nucleic acid molecule

The promoter is operatively linked to the nucleic acid molecule such that transcription is initiated at a controlled rate, avoiding overexpression that leads to toxicity and misfolding.

- low or moderate strength promoter preferred

Promoters of low or moderate strength, such as λPL, are preferred over strong promoters like T7, as they allow sufficient time for co-translational folding and membrane insertion.

- expression vector with constrainable transcription/translation rate

The expression vector is designed to allow fine-tuning of transcription and translation rates through inducible promoters, host strain selection, and plasmid copy number control.

- inducing transformed host cell with IPTG

Induction is achieved by addition of isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) at a concentration between 0.1 mM and 1 mM, with 0.4 mM being optimal.

- inducing at specific temperature and cell density

Induction is performed when the culture reaches an OD600 of approximately 0.5, at a temperature between 25°C and 30°C, to balance protein synthesis with cellular health.

- recombinant MOMP and derivatives produced by the methods

The recombinant MOMP and its derivatives produced by the methods described herein are structurally authentic, immunogenic, and suitable for use in pharmaceutical compositions.

## EXAMPLE 1

### Expression of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP.

- motivate codon harmonization

Codon harmonization was motivated by the observation that standard codon optimization of MOMP resulted in low surface expression and high cellular toxicity, suggesting that translation rate was too rapid for proper folding.

- evaluate codon selection effect

Comparison of codon-harmonized and codon-optimized MOMP constructs revealed a two-fold increase in surface expression by flow cytometry when using the harmonized sequence.

- describe codon harmonization results

The codon-harmonized MOMP gene exhibited higher geometric mean fluorescence intensity in whole-cell assays, indicating greater antigen presentation on the bacterial surface.

- motivate expression vector optimization

To further enhance expression, a panel of expression vectors was evaluated to identify those that supported high MOMP surface expression without inducing cell lysis.

- evaluate expression vector panel

Vectors with high copy number and strong promoters (e.g., pET, pJ831) resulted in minimal surface expression and high cell death. Low-copy vectors with moderate promoters (e.g., pAVE029) yielded the highest expression.

- describe optimal expression vector

The pAVE029 vector, with its λPL promoter and low-copy origin of replication, was identified as optimal for MOMP expression.

- motivate secretion leader sequence optimization

Multiple leader sequences were tested to determine which best directed MOMP to the outer membrane without causing periplasmic accumulation or toxicity.

- evaluate secretion leader sequences

SopA, OmpA, and OmpP leaders resulted in high surface expression, whereas native Chlamydia leaders failed to direct expression.

- describe optimal secretion leader sequence

The SopA leader sequence from Shigella flexneri was found to be optimal, yielding the highest level of surface-expressed MOMP.

- motivate optimization of expression conditions

Expression conditions were optimized to balance protein yield with bacterial viability, as high-level expression was linked to membrane stress.

- summarize optimized expression conditions

Optimal conditions included induction at OD600 = 0.5, 30°C, 4 hours, in Cinnabar medium, with 0.4 mM IPTG.

## EXAMPLE 2

### Purification of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP.

- describe cell disruption and membrane fractionation

Cells were disrupted by microfluidization, and the membrane fraction was isolated by differential centrifugation, with outer membrane pellets collected after ultracentrifugation.

- describe detergent evaluation for rMOMP extraction

Detergents including Triton X-100, β-octyl-glucoside, sarkosyl, and foscholine-14 were tested for their ability to solubilize MOMP without denaturation.

- describe rMOMP purification and characterization

Sarkosyl was found to be the most effective detergent for extraction. Purified MOMP was further purified by size exclusion and ion exchange chromatography, yielding a product with approximately 70% purity and undetectable endotoxin levels.

- immunize mice with nMOMP or rMOMP

- analyze post-immunization mouse serum by ELISA
