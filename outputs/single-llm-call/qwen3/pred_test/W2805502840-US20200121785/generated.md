# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- introduce recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector  
The present invention relates to a recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector engineered for the expression of heterologous antigens derived from avian pathogens, particularly for use as a live attenuated vaccine in poultry. The vector is derived from the SB-1 strain of Gallid herpesvirus 3, a naturally attenuated avian herpesvirus licensed for use in commercial Marek’s disease vaccines. This recombinant platform enables the stable integration and sustained expression of foreign immunogenic polynucleotides within nonessential intergenic regions of the viral genome, thereby preserving viral replication competence while eliciting robust immune responses against co-administered or co-targeted pathogens. The vector system is designed to function independently or in combination with other herpesvirus-based vectors, such as herpesvirus of turkeys, without inducing immune interference, thus offering a novel and synergistic approach to multivalent avian vaccination.

## BACKGROUND

- describe Marek's disease  
Marek’s disease is a highly contagious lymphoproliferative disorder affecting chickens and other avian species, caused by infection with Marek’s disease virus serotype 1. The disease is characterized by the development of T-cell lymphomas in peripheral nerves, gonads, iris, and visceral organs, often accompanied by paralysis, weight loss, and sudden death. It remains one of the most economically significant diseases in the global poultry industry despite decades of vaccination efforts.

- describe Marek's disease virus-1  
Marek’s disease virus-1, also known as Gallid herpesvirus 2, is an oncogenic alphaherpesvirus that establishes lifelong latent infection in infected birds. It replicates in epithelial cells of the feather follicles, where it produces large quantities of infectious virions shed into the environment via dander and dust. The virus is exceptionally stable in poultry house environments and is transmitted primarily through airborne particles, making biosecurity measures alone insufficient for disease control.

- describe Gallid herpesvirus 2  
Gallid herpesvirus 2 is the official designation for Marek’s disease virus-1 and is classified within the genus Mardivirus of the family Herpesviridae. Its genome is approximately 170 kilobases in length and encodes over 100 open reading frames, including genes involved in immune evasion, latency, and tumorigenesis. The virus has evolved in parallel with commercial poultry production, leading to the emergence of increasingly virulent strains that overcome the protective efficacy of older vaccine strains.

- describe MDV-1 replication  
Replication of Marek’s disease virus-1 occurs in a biphasic manner: initial lytic replication in respiratory epithelial cells followed by dissemination to lymphoid tissues, where the virus establishes latency in CD4+ T lymphocytes. Reactivation from latency leads to renewed shedding and transmission. The efficiency of this replication cycle underpins the virus’s high transmissibility and its ability to persist in flocks despite vaccination.

- describe MD mortality rate  
Mortality rates associated with Marek’s disease vary widely depending on viral strain virulence, host genetics, age at infection, and vaccination status. In unvaccinated flocks, mortality can reach 100%, while in vaccinated populations, rates typically range between 10% and 30%. Even subclinical infections contribute to reduced productivity, poor feed conversion, and increased culling, resulting in substantial economic losses.

- describe live vaccines against MD  
Live attenuated vaccines have been the cornerstone of Marek’s disease control since the 1970s. These include the attenuated MDV-1 strain Rispens, the non-oncogenic MDV-2 strain SB-1, and the herpesvirus of turkeys strain FC126. These vaccines are administered to day-old chicks either in ovo or subcutaneously and induce long-lasting immunity by mimicking natural infection without causing disease.

- describe HVT as a recombinant vaccine vector  
Herpesvirus of turkeys has been extensively engineered as a recombinant vaccine vector to express antigens from avian pathogens such as infectious bursal disease virus, Newcastle disease virus, and avian influenza. Recombinant HVT vaccines are commercially available and demonstrate efficacy even in the presence of maternal antibodies. However, their utility is constrained by the inability to combine multiple recombinant HVT constructs due to competitive interference during replication.

- describe limitations of HVT vector  
The primary limitation of recombinant HVT vectors lies in their mutual interference when co-administered. Multiple recombinant HVT strains expressing different antigens fail to elicit optimal immune responses, likely due to competition for cellular replication machinery or immune modulation. This restricts the development of polyvalent vaccines and necessitates sequential administration, increasing cost and logistical complexity.

- describe GaHV3 as a vaccine vector  
Gallid herpesvirus 3, particularly the SB-1 strain, has been used for over four decades as a safe and effective component of bivalent vaccines in combination with HVT. Unlike HVT, SB-1 does not interfere with HVT-based vaccines; instead, it enhances protection against Marek’s disease through synergistic immune activation. These properties position SB-1 as an ideal platform for recombinant antigen expression without compromising the efficacy of existing HVT-based immunization programs.

- describe SB-1 strain of GaHV3  
The SB-1 strain of Gallid herpesvirus 3 is a naturally attenuated, non-oncogenic virus that replicates efficiently in chicken embryonic fibroblasts and induces strong cellular and humoral immune responses. Its genome is structurally similar to Marek’s disease virus-1 but lacks oncogenic genes, making it inherently safer for use in young chicks. The strain has been successfully propagated in bacterial artificial chromosome systems, enabling precise genetic manipulation.

- describe bivalent vaccines containing SB-1 and HVT  
Commercial bivalent vaccines combining SB-1 and HVT are widely used in North America, South America, and Asia. These formulations provide superior protection against Marek’s disease compared to either component alone, particularly in chicks with maternal antibodies. The complementary immune profiles of the two viruses—HVT inducing strong antibody responses and SB-1 promoting robust T-cell activation—underlie their synergistic efficacy.

- describe need for additional vector platforms  
Given the limitations of HVT-based vectors and the rising prevalence of multiple avian pathogens requiring simultaneous protection, there is an urgent need for alternative recombinant vaccine platforms that do not interfere with existing vaccination regimens. SB-1 represents such a platform, offering compatibility with HVT and the potential for multivalent antigen delivery.

- describe challenges in controlling IBDV  
Infectious bursal disease virus is a highly contagious birnavirus that targets the bursa of Fabricius in young chickens, causing immunosuppression, increased susceptibility to secondary infections, and mortality rates up to 80% in unvaccinated flocks. The emergence of very virulent strains, antigenic drift, and interference from maternal antibodies have complicated control efforts. Attenuated live vaccines can cause subclinical damage to the bursa, and in ovo administration is often contraindicated due to embryonic toxicity.

- describe need for bivalent MDV/IBDV vaccines  
The simultaneous occurrence of Marek’s disease and infectious bursal disease in commercial flocks necessitates a combined vaccination strategy. A single vaccine capable of protecting against both pathogens would reduce labor, cost, and stress on chicks, while improving compliance and efficacy. The development of a recombinant SB-1 vector expressing IBDV antigens fulfills this unmet need.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- describe recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector  
The invention provides a recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector derived from the SB-1 strain, wherein one or more heterologous polynucleotides encoding immunogenic antigens of avian pathogens are stably inserted into nonessential intergenic regions of the viral genome. The vector retains full replication competence in chicken embryonic fibroblasts and induces protective immunity against both Marek’s disease and the targeted pathogen.

- describe insertion of heterologous polynucleotides  
The heterologous polynucleotides are inserted into intergenic loci between UL3 and UL4, UL10 and UL11, or UL21 and UL22 of the SB-1 genome using homologous recombination or site-specific recombinase technology. These loci are nonessential for viral replication and permit high-level expression of foreign genes without compromising viral fitness.

- describe expression of at least one antigen  
The recombinant vector expresses at least one immunogenic protein or peptide derived from an avian pathogen, including but not limited to the VP2 capsid protein of infectious bursal disease virus, the hemagglutinin of avian influenza virus, the fusion protein of Newcastle disease virus, or the glycoprotein E of infectious laryngotracheitis virus.

- describe preferred antigens  
The preferred antigen is the VP2 capsid protein of infectious bursal disease virus, as it is the major immunogenic component responsible for inducing neutralizing antibodies. Other preferred antigens include the hemagglutinin of avian influenza subtypes H5, H7, and H9, the fusion and hemagglutinin-neuraminidase proteins of Newcastle disease virus, and the S1 glycoprotein of infectious bronchitis virus.

- describe expression cassette  
The expression cassette comprises a promoter operably linked to the coding sequence of the antigen, followed by a transcription termination and polyadenylation signal. The promoter may be derived from murine cytomegalovirus immediate early protein 2, simian virus 40 early promoter, or a viral early/late promoter from Gallid herpesvirus 3 itself.

- describe promoter options  
Suitable promoters include the murine cytomegalovirus immediate early promoter, the simian virus 40 early promoter, the glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase promoter, and endogenous Gallid herpesvirus 3 promoters such as those from UL55 or UL56. The choice of promoter influences the timing and level of antigen expression during the viral replication cycle.

- describe vaccine composition  
The vaccine composition comprises the recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector suspended in a pharmaceutically or veterinarily acceptable carrier, optionally with an adjuvant, stabilizer, or buffer system to maintain viability during storage and administration.

- describe further Marek's disease virus vector  
The invention further encompasses a recombinant Marek’s disease virus vector expressing the same or a different antigen, which may be co-administered with the recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector to enhance immune responses without interference.

- describe recombinant Marek's disease virus vector  
A recombinant Marek’s disease virus vector may be engineered to express an antigen distinct from that expressed by the recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector, enabling a prime-boost strategy or simultaneous dual-pathogen protection with complementary immune profiles.

- describe administration of vaccine  
The vaccine is administered to avian species via subcutaneous injection, in ovo injection, or through drinking water, with dosages ranging from 100 to 10,000 plaque-forming units per bird, depending on age, species, and pathogen target.

- describe isolated DNA encoding vector  
The invention includes an isolated nucleic acid molecule encoding the full-length genome of the recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector, which is capable of being transfected into permissive cells to reconstitute infectious virus.

- describe bacterial artificial chromosome  
The recombinant genome is maintained as a bacterial artificial chromosome in Escherichia coli, allowing for precise genetic manipulation, sequence verification, and scalable production of the viral vector.

- describe use of vaccine in avian species  
The vaccine is intended for use in domesticated avian species, particularly chickens, turkeys, quail, and pheasants, for the prevention of Marek’s disease and one or more additional avian viral diseases.

- describe protection against diseases  
The recombinant vector provides protection against Marek’s disease, infectious bursal disease, Newcastle disease, avian influenza, infectious laryngotracheitis, and infectious bronchitis, either singly or in combination, depending on the inserted antigen.

- describe administration routes  
Administration routes include subcutaneous injection, in ovo injection at 18–19 days of embryonation, and oral delivery via drinking water. In ovo administration is preferred for mass vaccination in commercial hatcheries.

- describe method of treating avian species  
The method of treating an avian species involves administering the recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector to a bird at an early age, prior to exposure to the target pathogen, thereby inducing active immunity that prevents clinical disease and reduces viral shedding.

- describe recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector with markers  
The recombinant vector may further comprise a selectable marker gene, such as galK or GFP, inserted transiently during vector construction to facilitate isolation of recombinant clones, which may be subsequently excised using site-specific recombinase systems.

### Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) Vector

- introduce recombinant GaHV3 vector  
The recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector is constructed by inserting one or more heterologous polynucleotides encoding immunogenic antigens into the genome of the SB-1 strain of Gallid herpesvirus 3, using bacterial artificial chromosome technology and homologous recombination.

- describe vector composition  
The vector composition includes the full-length SB-1 genome, a heterologous expression cassette inserted into an intergenic locus, and optionally a selectable marker gene, all maintained within a bacterial artificial chromosome.

- specify intergenic loci UL3/UL4 and UL21/UL22  
The preferred intergenic loci for insertion are the regions between UL3 and UL4, and between UL21 and UL22, both of which have been demonstrated to tolerate foreign gene insertion without impairing viral replication.

- define heterologous polynucleotide insertion  
Heterologous polynucleotide insertion refers to the targeted integration of a non-native DNA sequence into the viral genome, resulting in stable expression of the encoded antigen during viral replication.

- list avian pathogens  
Avian pathogens for which antigens may be expressed include infectious bursal disease virus, avian influenza virus, Newcastle disease virus, infectious laryngotracheitis virus, and infectious bronchitis virus.

- specify IBDV as preferred antigen  
The preferred antigen is the VP2 capsid protein of infectious bursal disease virus, which is the primary target of neutralizing antibodies and confers complete protection against lethal challenge.

- describe VP2 protein of IBDV  
The VP2 protein of infectious bursal disease virus is a 42-kDa structural protein that forms the viral capsid and contains the major neutralizing epitopes. Its expression in the recombinant vector induces high-titer neutralizing antibodies.

- define sequence similarity and identity  
Sequence similarity and identity refer to the degree of homology between the inserted VP2 gene and reference sequences, with the inserted sequence exhibiting at least 95% identity to the VP2 gene of IBDV strain Faragher 52/70.

- specify SEQ ID NO: 10 and SEQ ID NO: 6  
The VP2 gene inserted into the vector is encoded by SEQ ID NO: 10, with the promoter and regulatory elements provided by SEQ ID NO: 6.

- describe immunogenic fragment  
An immunogenic fragment of VP2 is a portion of the protein that retains the ability to induce neutralizing antibodies, including residues 150–380, which encompass the major conformational epitope.

- specify ILTV glycoprotein E, I, and B  
The glycoproteins E, I, and B of infectious laryngotracheitis virus are expressed as immunogenic antigens in alternative embodiments, with respective sequences provided by SEQ ID NO: 31, SEQ ID NO: 32, and SEQ ID NO: 33.

- describe sequence similarity and identity

- describe immunogenic fragment  
Immunogenic fragments of ILTV glycoproteins include the ectodomain of glycoprotein E (residues 1–450), the transmembrane domain of glycoprotein I (residues 100–300), and the cytoplasmic tail of glycoprotein B (residues 50–200).

- specify NDV fusion protein and hemagglutinin neuraminidase

### Methods for Producing a Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) Vector

- introduce method for producing recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector  
The method for producing the recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector involves the introduction of a heterologous polynucleotide into the SB-1 genome via homologous recombination in bacterial artificial chromosome form, followed by transfection into permissive cells to reconstitute infectious virus.

- describe step of providing Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector  
The step of providing the Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector involves isolating the full-length SB-1 genome maintained as a bacterial artificial chromosome in Escherichia coli.

- describe step of inserting heterologous polynucleotide(s)  
The step of inserting heterologous polynucleotides involves designing homologous arms flanking the target intergenic locus and using recombineering techniques to replace a selectable marker with the antigen expression cassette.

- describe step of amplifying Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector  
The step of amplifying the recombinant vector involves transfecting the modified bacterial artificial chromosome into chicken embryonic fibroblasts, allowing viral replication, and harvesting progeny virus through multiple passages.

- specify Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector used  
The Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector used is the SB-1 strain, with genome sequence accession number AB049735.1.

- describe alternative method of producing recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector  
An alternative method involves the use of site-specific recombinase systems, such as Cre-loxP or Flp-FRT, to excise a marker gene and replace it with an antigen cassette in a single step.

- describe step of replacing marker(s) with expression cassette  
The step of replacing marker(s) with an expression cassette involves co-transfecting the recombinant BAC with a donor plasmid containing the antigen cassette flanked by recombinase recognition sites.

- describe step of amplifying Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector  
Following replacement, the modified BAC is transfected into chicken embryonic fibroblasts, and recombinant virus is amplified through serial passage.

- specify site-specific recombinase technology used  
The site-specific recombinase technology used is Cre-loxP, with loxP sites flanking the selectable marker and a Cre recombinase plasmid introduced during transfection.

- describe construction of recombinant Gallid herpesvirus-3 vector  
The construction involves cloning the antigen expression cassette into a shuttle vector, transforming E. coli containing the SB-1 BAC, selecting recombinant clones, and verifying insertion by PCR and sequencing.

- describe use of bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) or P1-derived artificial chromosome (PAC)  
The SB-1 genome is maintained as a bacterial artificial chromosome for stability and ease of manipulation; alternatively, a P1-derived artificial chromosome may be used for larger inserts.

- describe steps of cloning and transfecting  
The steps include amplifying the antigen cassette by PCR, cloning into a shuttle vector, transforming E. coli harboring the SB-1 BAC, selecting recombinants on galactose-containing media, and transfecting purified BAC DNA into chicken embryonic fibroblasts.

- describe steps of isolating and amplifying recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector

## EXAMPLES

- clone galK expression cassette into SB-1 virus genome  
A galK expression cassette was cloned into the intergenic loci between UL3 and UL4, UL10 and UL11, and UL21 and UL22 of the SB-1 bacterial artificial chromosome using homologous recombination, with selection for galactose utilization.

- describe homologous recombination technique  
Homologous recombination was performed using a shuttle plasmid containing 500 base pair homology arms flanking the target locus and the galK gene under control of the mCMV IE2 promoter.

- exemplify insertion sites UL3/4, UL10/11 and UL21/22  
The galK cassette was successfully inserted into all three intergenic loci, with recombinant clones confirmed by PCR amplification and sequencing.

- clone IBDV VP2 expressing cassette into SB-1 virus genome  
The galK cassette was replaced with an expression cassette encoding the VP2 protein of IBDV under control of the mCMV IE2 promoter and SV40 polyA signal.

- replace galK expression cassette with VP2 expression cassette  
Replacement was achieved by recombineering with a donor plasmid containing the VP2 cassette flanked by homology arms identical to those used for galK insertion.

- confirm integration of VP2 expression cassette  
Integration was confirmed by PCR amplification of the VP2 gene, sequencing of the insertion junctions, and immunofluorescence staining of infected cells with anti-VP2 monoclonal antibody.

### Cell Culture and Virus Propagation

- prepare chicken embryonic fibroblasts (CEF)  
Chicken embryonic fibroblasts were prepared from 9–10-day-old specific-pathogen-free Rhode Island Red embryos and maintained in E199 medium supplemented with 5% fetal bovine serum.

- propagate DF-1 and DT40 cells  
DF-1 cells were propagated in DMEM with 10% serum, while DT40 cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 with 10% serum, both supplemented with antibiotics.

- transfect BAC DNA into CEF  
BAC DNA purified from E. coli was transfected into confluent CEF monolayers using Lipofectamine 2000 according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

- titrate SB-1 vaccine viruses  
Virus titers were determined by plaque assay on CEF monolayers, with plaques visualized by immunostaining using anti-gB and anti-VP2 monoclonal antibodies.

### Virus Growth Curve Studies

- quantify SB-1 genome copy numbers using real-time PCR  
Viral genome copy numbers were quantified using a TaqMan assay targeting the UL55 gene, with standard curves generated from serial dilutions of the SB-1 BAC plasmid.

### Serum Neutralization Test

- perform serum neutralization test using D78 strain of IBDV  
Serum samples were incubated with 100 TCID50 of IBDV D78 strain, added to DF-1 monolayers, and scored for cytopathic effect after four days to determine neutralizing antibody titers.

### Statistical Analysis

- analyze data using linear model and ANOVA tests  
Data were analyzed using two-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test for multiple comparisons, and survival curves were compared using the Mantel-Cox log-rank test.

### Analysis of Different Loci to Insert an Expression Cassette

The SB-1 strain was evaluated as a recombinant vector platform by inserting the VP2 expression cassette into three intergenic loci and comparing replication kinetics and antigen expression levels.

All three loci supported insertion, but the UL3/UL4 locus yielded the highest VP2 expression and minimal disruption of adjacent gene transcription.

### Vaccine Development Against IBDV: Immunization Study

- introduce experimental design  
One-day-old SPF chicks were divided into four groups and vaccinated with either recombinant SB-1-UL3/4VP2, SB-1-UL21/22VP2, SB-1-UL10/11VP2, or commercial Vaxxitek HVT+IBD.

- describe animal housing and care  
Birds were housed in isolators under specific-pathogen-free conditions, with ad libitum access to feed and water, and monitored daily for clinical signs.

- outline vaccination procedure  
Each bird received 3,000 plaque-forming units subcutaneously in the neck region on day one of life.

- detail blood sampling schedule  
Serum samples were collected at weeks 2, 3, and 4 post-vaccination for neutralization assays.

- describe immunogenicity assessment  
Neutralizing antibody titers were determined using the D78 strain of IBDV in DF-1 cells, with titers defined as the highest serum dilution preventing cytopathic effect.

- present results of neutralizing antibody production  
All vaccinated groups developed detectable neutralizing antibodies by week four, with no significant difference between recombinant vectors and the commercial vaccine.

- compare antibody levels between groups  
The mean neutralizing antibody titers were highest in the SB-1-UL3/4VP2 group, followed by SB-1-UL10/11VP2 and SB-1-UL21/22VP2, with no statistical difference among groups.

- summarize findings  
All recombinant vaccines induced protective levels of neutralizing antibodies comparable to the commercial vaccine.

- introduce Example 4  
A challenge study was conducted to evaluate protection against very virulent IBDV UK661.

- describe challenge study design  
Birds were challenged at 28 days of age with 10^4.3 TCID50 of IBDV UK661 via intranasal inoculation.

- outline vaccination procedure  
Vaccination was performed as described in Example 3, with one group receiving parental SB-1 as a control.

- detail challenge procedure  
Challenge was administered in 100 µL divided between both nostrils, and birds were monitored for clinical signs every six hours for seven days.

- describe clinical sign scoring  
Clinical signs were scored using a standardized system based on depression, ruffled feathers, diarrhea, and ataxia, with a maximum score of 12.

- present results of challenge study  
All birds vaccinated with recombinant SB-1 vectors survived, while 100% of control birds died by 56 hours post-challenge.

- summarize findings  
The recombinant SB-1 vector conferred 100% protection against lethal IBDV challenge, independent of serum neutralizing antibody titer.

- introduce Example 5  
An interference study was conducted to assess compatibility with HVT-based vaccines.

- describe interference study design  
Chicks were vaccinated with recombinant SB-1-UL3/4VP2 and Vaxxitek HVT+IBD simultaneously or sequentially.

- outline vaccination procedure  
Groups received either recombinant SB-1 alone, HVT+IBD alone, or both in combination.

- detail blood sampling schedule  
Serum was collected at weeks 2, 3, and 4 for neutralization assays.

- describe virus neutralization assay  
Neutralizing antibodies against IBDV were measured using the D78 strain in DF-1 cells.

- present results of virus neutralization assay  
No significant reduction in neutralizing antibody titers was observed in the combined vaccination group compared to single-vaccine groups.

- compare antibody levels between groups  
Antibody levels were equivalent between single and combined vaccination groups, indicating no interference.

- summarize findings  
The recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 vector does not interfere with HVT-based vaccines, supporting its use in bivalent or polyvalent vaccination programs.
