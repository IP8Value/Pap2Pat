# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

- The present invention relates to novel truncated polypeptides derived from the human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein, and their use as therapeutic agents for the modulation of inflammatory and neurodegenerative processes. Specifically, the invention encompasses synthetic peptides that retain the ability to bind with high affinity to the interleukin-1 receptor type I, thereby competitively inhibiting the binding of interleukin-1 alpha and interleukin-1 beta, without triggering downstream pro-inflammatory signaling. These peptides exhibit enhanced pharmacological properties compared to the full-length recombinant protein, including improved solubility, greater metabolic stability, reduced immunogenicity, and the unique capacity to cross the blood-brain barrier, enabling their application in both peripheral and central nervous system inflammatory disorders.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

- Interleukin-1 is a potent pro-inflammatory cytokine that plays a central role in the initiation and perpetuation of acute and chronic inflammatory responses. It exists in two primary bioactive isoforms, interleukin-1 alpha and interleukin-1 beta, both of which exert their effects through binding to the interleukin-1 receptor type I, a transmembrane protein expressed on a wide variety of cell types including macrophages, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and neurons. Upon ligand binding, the interleukin-1 receptor type I recruits the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein, initiating a signaling cascade that activates nuclear factor kappa B and mitogen-activated protein kinase pathways, leading to the transcriptional upregulation of numerous inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor-alpha, interleukin-6, chemokines, adhesion molecules, and prostaglandins. This cascade contributes significantly to tissue damage in conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, type 2 diabetes, atherosclerosis, and neurodegenerative diseases.

- The endogenous interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein is a naturally occurring inhibitor that binds to the interleukin-1 receptor type I with comparable affinity to interleukin-1 but fails to recruit the accessory protein, thereby acting as a pure competitive antagonist. Recombinant forms of this protein, such as anakinra, have been developed and approved for clinical use in autoinflammatory and autoimmune conditions. However, anakinra suffers from several limitations: its large molecular weight impedes penetration across biological barriers, including the blood-brain barrier, limiting its utility in neurological disorders; it has a short plasma half-life requiring daily subcutaneous administration; and it is associated with injection-site reactions and variable pharmacokinetics among patients. Furthermore, its production via recombinant expression in bacterial systems introduces challenges related to batch consistency, purification complexity, and cost.

- Prior art has disclosed peptide fragments derived from the interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein, including sequences spanning residues 1–10, 1–15, and 1–20, which were shown to retain partial antagonistic activity. However, these fragments exhibited significantly reduced binding affinity compared to the full-length protein and lacked demonstrable in vivo efficacy, particularly in models of neuroinflammation. The patent family WO05086695A2 describes the use of short IL-1Ra-derived peptides for the inhibition of IL-1 signaling, but none of the disclosed sequences demonstrated the combination of high-affinity receptor binding, blood-brain barrier permeability, and neuroprotective activity observed in the present invention. The development of small, stable, synthetically accessible peptides with enhanced pharmacokinetic profiles represents a significant unmet need in the field of anti-inflammatory therapeutics.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

- The present invention provides truncated polypeptides derived from the N-terminal domain of the human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein, which are capable of binding with high affinity to the interleukin-1 receptor type I and effectively inhibiting interleukin-1 beta-induced downstream signaling pathways. These peptides, which are substantially shorter than the full-length protein or anakinra, retain the critical structural motifs necessary for receptor interaction while exhibiting superior physicochemical properties. They demonstrate potent inhibition of nuclear factor kappa B activation and tumor necrosis factor-alpha secretion in macrophages, protect pancreatic islet cells from interleukin-1 beta-induced apoptosis, and uniquely promote neurite outgrowth and enhance the survival of primary neurons in culture. Importantly, these peptides are capable of crossing the blood-brain barrier following systemic administration, enabling them to exert therapeutic effects in models of neuroinflammation, including lipopolysaccharide-induced behavioral depression, amyloid-beta-induced cognitive deficits, and experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis. The invention further encompasses multimeric forms of these peptides, pharmaceutical formulations suitable for various routes of administration, and methods for treating inflammatory and neurodegenerative disorders by modulating interleukin-1 receptor signaling.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Inflammation

- Inflammation is a complex biological response of vascularized tissues to harmful stimuli such as pathogens, damaged cells, or irritants. It is characterized by the recruitment of immune cells, increased vascular permeability, and the local production of cytokines, chemokines, and other mediators. Inflammation can be classified into acute and chronic forms. Acute inflammation is a rapid, self-limiting response aimed at eliminating the injurious agent and initiating tissue repair. Chronic inflammation, by contrast, persists over weeks to years and is associated with ongoing tissue destruction, fibrosis, and the infiltration of lymphocytes and macrophages. Chronic inflammation underlies the pathogenesis of numerous diseases, including rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, atherosclerosis, type 2 diabetes, and neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease and multiple sclerosis. The dysregulated production of interleukin-1 is a hallmark of chronic inflammatory conditions, where it perpetuates a self-sustaining cycle of immune activation and tissue damage.

### Interleukins

- Interleukins are a class of cytokines primarily produced by leukocytes that mediate communication between immune cells and regulate immune responses. They are involved in the activation, proliferation, differentiation, and survival of various immune cell types. Interleukin-1 is one of the most potent and pleiotropic interleukins, existing in two major isoforms: interleukin-1 alpha and interleukin-1 beta. Both isoforms share approximately 26% amino acid sequence identity and exhibit similar biological activities, although they differ in their modes of synthesis and release. Interleukin-1 alpha is primarily membrane-bound and acts in a juxtacrine manner, whereas interleukin-1 beta is secreted following proteolytic cleavage by caspase-1. Both isoforms are synthesized by activated macrophages, monocytes, dendritic cells, microglia, astrocytes, and certain epithelial and endothelial cells. Their expression is tightly regulated at the transcriptional and post-translational levels, and their activity is modulated by endogenous inhibitors such as the interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein. The interleukin-1 receptor type I is a member of the immunoglobulin superfamily and consists of three extracellular immunoglobulin-like domains, a transmembrane region, and an intracellular Toll/interleukin-1 receptor domain. Upon binding of interleukin-1 alpha or interleukin-1 beta, the receptor undergoes a conformational change that facilitates recruitment of the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein, leading to the assembly of a signaling complex that initiates downstream pathways including NF-κB and MAPK. The interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein competes with interleukin-1 for binding to the receptor’s third immunoglobulin domain but lacks the structural elements necessary to induce conformational changes required for accessory protein recruitment, rendering it a pure antagonist.

### Peptide Fragments of the Present Invention

- The present invention provides novel synthetic peptides derived from the N-terminal region of the human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein, specifically encompassing the sequence SGRKSSKMQA (SEQ ID NO:1), referred to as Ilantide. These peptides are truncated forms of the full-length protein, ranging from 8 to 15 amino acids in length, and exhibit improved solubility, enhanced stability against proteolytic degradation, and significantly lower production costs compared to recombinant biologics such as anakinra. Crucially, these peptides retain the ability to bind to the interleukin-1 receptor type I with an affinity comparable to or exceeding that of the full-length antagonist, thereby effectively competing with interleukin-1 beta for receptor occupancy. This binding prevents the recruitment of the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein, resulting in the inhibition of downstream signaling pathways including NF-κB and MAPK, and the subsequent suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokine production. These peptides also demonstrate a unique capacity to cross the blood-brain barrier, a property not shared by anakinra, allowing them to exert direct neuroprotective effects in the central nervous system. In neuronal cultures, the peptides stimulate neurite outgrowth and enhance neuronal survival, suggesting a direct modulatory effect on neural plasticity independent of their anti-inflammatory actions. The peptides may be synthesized using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques and may be modified to include non-natural amino acids, cyclization, or pegylation to further enhance pharmacokinetic properties. The peptides may also be administered as monomers, dimers, trimers, tetramers, or dendrimers to increase avidity and prolong half-life. The term “fragment or variant thereof” encompasses sequences with conservative substitutions, deletions, or additions that preserve the core receptor-binding motif and biological activity. Conservative substitutions include replacements within the same amino acid group, such as leucine for isoleucine or aspartic acid for glutamic acid. Non-conservative substitutions may be introduced to modulate stability or solubility. Post-translational modifications such as acetylation, amidation, or glycosylation may be applied to enhance bioavailability. The peptides are isolated in substantially pure form, free from contaminants derived from biological sources, and are suitable for pharmaceutical formulation.

### Synthetic Preparation

- The peptides of the present invention may be synthesized using standard solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, preferably employing the fluorenylmethyloxycarbonyl protection strategy on TentaGel resin or similar solid supports. The synthesis involves sequential coupling of protected amino acid derivatives, followed by cleavage from the resin and deprotection under controlled conditions. Purification is achieved by preparative high-performance liquid chromatography, and identity and purity are confirmed by mass spectrometry and analytical HPLC. The peptides may be synthesized as monomers or multimeric constructs, such as dendrimers, by coupling multiple peptide chains to a lysine-based scaffold. The synthesis may be automated using peptide synthesizers, enabling reproducible production at scale. The resulting peptides are lyophilized and stored under inert atmosphere at low temperature until use.

### Dosage

- Dosage requirements vary depending on the route of administration, the severity of the condition, the age and weight of the individual, and pharmacokinetic parameters such as absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion. For parenteral administration, a daily dosage range of 0.1 to 10 mg/kg is effective, with a preferred range of 0.5 to 5 mg/kg. Intravenous administration may require lower doses due to immediate bioavailability, while subcutaneous administration may require higher doses to compensate for slower absorption. Oral administration is not preferred due to susceptibility to proteolytic degradation in the gastrointestinal tract. The effective amount is that which achieves a plasma concentration of at least 10 ng/mL and a cerebrospinal fluid concentration of at least 1 ng/mL. The peptide may be administered once daily or multiple times per day depending on the half-life and clinical response. A unit dosage form contains a predetermined amount of the peptide, such as 0.5 mg, 1 mg, or 5 mg, in a sterile vial. Inter-individual differences in metabolism and distribution are accounted for by titrating the dose based on clinical response and biomarker analysis. Parenteral administration is motivated by the peptide’s susceptibility to enzymatic degradation and its need for precise dosing. Intranasal and subcutaneous routes are particularly advantageous for targeting neuroinflammation due to direct access to the central nervous system. Co-administration with anti-inflammatory drugs such as corticosteroids, nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory agents, or disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs may enhance therapeutic outcomes. Co-administration with anti-neurodegenerative agents such as acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, NMDA antagonists, or dopamine agonists may provide synergistic benefits in neurodegenerative conditions. A kit of parts may comprise a vial of lyophilized peptide and a vial of sterile diluent, along with instructions for reconstitution and administration.

### Treatment of Inflammatory and Neurodegenerative Disorders

- The invention provides methods for treating inflammatory disorders including rheumatoid arthritis, Behçet’s disease, type 2 diabetes, and inflammatory bowel disease, by inhibiting interleukin-1 signaling. Interleukin-1 plays a central role in the activation of immune cells, the proliferation of fibroblasts, the synthesis of proteases and acute-phase proteins, and the recruitment of neutrophils and lymphocytes. In Behçet’s disease, characterized by recurrent oral and genital ulcers, uveitis, and vasculitis, interleukin-1 is elevated in serum and lesional tissue, and its blockade has shown clinical benefit. The invention further provides methods for treating neurodegenerative disorders including Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, Huntington’s disease, and multiple sclerosis. In Alzheimer’s disease, interleukin-1 is overexpressed by activated microglia surrounding amyloid plaques and contributes to synaptic loss and neuronal death. In Parkinson’s disease, interleukin-1 exacerbates dopaminergic neuron degeneration in the substantia nigra. In Huntington’s disease, interleukin-1 promotes striatal neuronal apoptosis. In multiple sclerosis, interleukin-1 drives T-cell infiltration and demyelination. Blocking interleukin-1 signaling with the peptides of the present invention reduces neuroinflammation, protects neurons, and improves cognitive and motor function in preclinical models. The amino acid sequence of the human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein is provided by UniProt Accession No. P25844. Isoforms include the full-length 17-kDa protein and shorter variants generated by alternative splicing. Ilantafin-8 corresponds to SGRKSSKMQA (SEQ ID NO:1). Ilantafin-1 through Ilantafin-6 represent variants with conservative substitutions at positions 2, 4, 6, 8, or 10, including SGRKSSKMQA, SGRKTSKMQA, SGRKSSKMQA, SGRKSSKMQV, SGRKSSKMQS, and SGRKSSKMQG.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- The Ilantafin sequence motif SGRKSSKMQA (SEQ ID NO:1) was identified by alignment of the N-terminal domain of human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein with known receptor-binding regions of other cytokine antagonists. Structural modeling of the IL-1Ra–IL-1RI complex revealed that residues 1–10 form a critical interface with the Ig3 domain of the receptor. This region was selected for peptide synthesis, and the sequence was optimized for stability and solubility while preserving key hydrogen-bonding and hydrophobic interactions.

### Example 2

- Binding of the Ilantafin peptide to the ectodomain of IL-1RI was analyzed using surface plasmon resonance. Immobilized Ilantafin bound the receptor with a dissociation constant of 2.75 × 10⁻⁸ M, comparable to the affinity of full-length IL-1Ra (4.8 × 10⁻⁸ M) and higher than that of IL-1β (1.71 × 10⁻⁷ M). No binding was observed to the control NCAM Ig1–Ig2 domain, confirming specificity.

### Example 3

- Macrophages were treated with Ilantafin, scrambled peptides, or IL-1Ra prior to stimulation with IL-1β. Only Ilantafin and IL-1Ra significantly inhibited TNF-α secretion. Scrambled peptides and reversed sequences showed no inhibitory effect, demonstrating sequence-specific antagonism.

### Example 4

- Ilantafin dose-dependently inhibited IL-1β-induced NF-κB activation in HEK-Blue cells, with an IC₅₀ of 1.2 nM. Maximal inhibition was achieved at 10 nM, matching the efficacy of IL-1Ra. No inhibition of STAT3 activation by IL-6 was observed, confirming selectivity for the IL-1 pathway.

### Example 5

- Primary cerebellar granule neurons treated with Ilantafin exhibited a 2.5-fold increase in neurite length compared to untreated controls. This effect was comparable to IL-1Ra and was reversed by co-treatment with IL-1β, confirming receptor-mediated mechanism.

### Example 6

- Neuronal cell death induced by potassium withdrawal was reduced by 68% with Ilantafin treatment, compared to 55% with IGF-1 and 60% with IL-1Ra. Ilantafin significantly increased the number of viable neurons with intact morphology.

### Example 7

- In the collagen-induced arthritis model, daily subcutaneous administration of Ilantafin-t (10 mg/kg) from day 8 to day 15 significantly reduced clinical arthritis scores compared to vehicle-treated animals (p < 0.05). Joint swelling and erythema were markedly attenuated.

### Example 8

- Following subcutaneous injection of biotinylated Ilantafin-t (10 mg/kg), peptide concentrations in plasma peaked at 20 minutes (5,816 ± 44 ng/mL) and remained detectable for 30 hours. Concentration in cerebrospinal fluid was 125 ± 13 ng/mL at 30 minutes, yielding a plasma-to-CSF ratio of 55, confirming blood-brain barrier penetration.

### Example 9

- Hippocampal neurons exposed to kainic acid exhibited significant cytotoxicity, with 72% cell death after 24 hours. Pre-treatment with Ilantafin reduced cell death to 28%, comparable to full-length IL-1Ra. Nuclear morphology and live-cell staining confirmed preservation of neuronal integrity. Scanning electron microscopy revealed intact dendritic structures in Ilantafin-treated cells, whereas untreated cells showed membrane blebbing and fragmentation.
