## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of microbiology and infectious disease therapeutics, specifically to novel small-molecule compounds that modulate quorum sensing in pathogenic Vibrio species by inhibiting the ATPase activity of the NtrC-type response regulator LuxO. The invention further encompasses methods for the diagnosis, prophylaxis, and treatment of bacterial infections caused by Vibrio cholerae, Vibrio parahaemolyticus, Vibrio vulnificus, and other Vibrio species that utilize LuxO as a central regulator of virulence and biofilm formation through quorum sensing pathways.

## BACKGROUND

Quorum sensing is a conserved mechanism of bacterial communication that enables populations of microorganisms to coordinate gene expression in response to changes in cell density through the production, detection, and response to extracellular signaling molecules known as autoinducers. This process regulates a wide array of collective behaviors including bioluminescence, sporulation, motility, biofilm formation, and the production of virulence factors. In many Gram-negative pathogens, quorum sensing systems are intricately linked to the pathogenic lifecycle, often repressing virulence at high cell density to facilitate dissemination from the host. This regulatory logic presents a unique therapeutic opportunity: rather than killing bacteria or inhibiting growth, agents that artificially activate quorum sensing can prematurely suppress virulence programs, thereby attenuating disease without imposing strong selective pressure for resistance.

Vibrio cholerae, the causative agent of cholera, employs a sophisticated quorum sensing circuit that integrates two autoinducers—CAI-1 and AI-2—detected by the membrane-bound receptors CqsS and LuxPQ, respectively. At low cell density, these receptors function as kinases that phosphorylate the cytoplasmic response regulator LuxO. Phosphorylated LuxO activates transcription of four small regulatory RNAs, Qrr1–4, which, in conjunction with the RNA chaperone Hfq, repress translation of the master regulator HapR while stabilizing AphA. The absence of HapR permits expression of genes essential for colonization, including those encoding the toxin-coregulated pilus and cholera toxin. Upon reaching high cell density, autoinducer binding switches the receptors to phosphatase activity, leading to LuxO dephosphorylation, cessation of Qrr expression, derepression of HapR translation, and subsequent repression of virulence and biofilm genes. This regulatory switch ensures that virulence is expressed during initial infection and silenced upon population saturation to promote environmental dispersal.

Other pathogenic Vibrio species, including Vibrio parahaemolyticus and Vibrio vulnificus, utilize homologous quorum sensing systems in which LuxO orthologs control analogous virulence outputs through conserved regulatory cascades. Despite the clinical significance of these organisms, therapeutic strategies targeting quorum sensing have been limited to antagonists of acyl-homoserine lactone systems, leaving non-HSL pathways such as the Vibrio system largely unexploited. Moreover, while inhibitors of histidine kinases have been pursued, targeting downstream response regulators remains a largely uncharted frontier due to perceived functional specificity and structural complexity. To date, no small molecule has been reported to selectively inhibit the ATPase activity of an NtrC-family response regulator, despite the high conservation of this domain across bacterial two-component systems. The absence of chemical tools capable of modulating LuxO activity has hindered both mechanistic understanding and therapeutic development for vibriosis and related infections.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention discloses novel strategies for activating quorum sensing in Vibrio species by pharmacologically inhibiting the ATPase activity of the NtrC-type response regulator LuxO, thereby inducing premature expression of the HapR transcriptional repressor and suppressing virulence factor production. The invention further provides a class of small-molecule compounds characterized by a 5-thio-6-azauracil core and specific hydrophobic side chain substitutions that function as uncompetitive inhibitors of LuxO ATPase activity. These compounds activate quorum sensing in Vibrio cholerae and related species regardless of autoinducer status, effectively bypassing upstream signaling components and directly modulating the central regulatory node of the pathway. The invention encompasses methods for prophylactic and therapeutic treatment of bacterial infections by administering these LuxO inhibitors to prevent or reduce colonization, biofilm formation, and toxin production. The compounds are shown to be effective against multiple Vibrio species, including V. parahaemolyticus and V. vulnificus, and demonstrate low cytotoxicity in mammalian cells, supporting their potential as anti-virulence agents. The invention further provides methods for identifying additional inhibitors of NtrC-family response regulators by exploiting the conserved ATPase domain architecture shared among these proteins.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention identifies a novel class of small-molecule inhibitors that activate quorum sensing in Vibrio species by directly targeting the ATPase domain of the NtrC-type response regulator LuxO. These compounds, exemplified by structures bearing a 5-thio-6-azauracil scaffold with branched alkyl side chains such as tert-butyl carbinol or neopentyl groups, function as uncompetitive inhibitors that bind to the LuxO-ATP complex and suppress ATP hydrolysis without interfering with ATP binding or DNA recognition. Structural analysis of inhibitor-resistant LuxO mutants reveals that these compounds interact with a region adjacent to the Walker B motif, near conserved residues I211, L215, L242, and V294, which are critical for coupling ATP hydrolysis to conformational changes required for transcriptional activation. The inhibition of ATPase activity prevents LuxO from facilitating open complex formation at qrr promoter regions, leading to derepression of HapR and premature transition to the high-cell-density gene expression program. This mechanism results in the downregulation of virulence factors such as cholera toxin and toxin-coregulated pilus in V. cholerae and Type Three Secretion System effectors in V. parahaemolyticus, thereby attenuating pathogenicity without affecting bacterial viability.

The invention further classifies these compounds into a distinct chemical class defined by the 5-thio-6-azauracil core and specific stereochemical and steric properties of the side chain substituents. Compounds with bulky, hydrophobic terminal moieties such as tert-butyl, neopentyl, isobutyl, cyclobutylmethyl, cyclopropylmethyl, and 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-yl exhibit enhanced potency, while linear or polar side chains result in diminished activity. The structure-activity relationship demonstrates that optimal inhibition correlates with increased steric bulk and hydrophobicity at the terminal position of the side chain, suggesting a constrained binding pocket adjacent to the ATP-binding site. These compounds are water-soluble, stable under physiological conditions, and cell-permeable, enabling effective intracellular delivery and sustained target engagement. The invention further encompasses synthetic methods for the preparation of these compounds, including the synthesis of neopentyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate, isobutyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate, cyclobutylmethyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate, cyclopropylmethyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate, 3-methylbutan-2-yl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate, (trimethylsilyl)methyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate, 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-yl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate, and 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate, each synthesized via nucleophilic substitution of 6-chloro-5-thio-6-azauracil with corresponding alkyl halides under basic conditions, followed by purification by column chromatography and characterization by NMR spectroscopy and high-resolution mass spectrometry.

The invention demonstrates that these compounds activate quorum sensing across multiple Vibrio species, including V. harveyi and V. parahaemolyticus, by inducing bioluminescence and switching colony morphology from translucent to opaque, respectively, in strains genetically locked in the low-cell-density state. In vivo cytotoxicity assays using mammalian cell lines show that treatment with these compounds significantly reduces bacterial-mediated host cell lysis, confirming that suppression of virulence factor expression in vitro translates to functional attenuation of pathogenicity. The compounds are non-bactericidal, do not impair bacterial growth, and exhibit no measurable cytotoxicity in mammalian cells at concentrations exceeding 500 µM, supporting their suitability for prophylactic use. The invention further contemplates the use of these LuxO inhibitors as broad-spectrum agents against other NtrC-type response regulators that share structural homology in the ATPase domain, including those involved in nitrogen metabolism, motility, and virulence in other bacterial pathogens. The identification of a conserved binding pocket adjacent to the Walker B motif opens a new avenue for rational design of inhibitors targeting AAA+ ATPase domains across diverse bacterial and eukaryotic systems.

### Materials and Methods

Bacterial strains used in this invention include V. cholerae C6706str derivatives BH1578 (ΔcqsA ΔluxS pBB1), BH1651 (luxO D47E pBB1), and ΔluxO strains, as well as V. harveyi BB120 and V. parahaemolyticus strains LM4476 (luxO*) and LM9688 (ΔluxO). All strains were cultured in LB or LM medium under standard conditions at 30°C or 37°C with appropriate antibiotic selection. Screening for quorum sensing-activating molecules was performed using a 90,000-compound library in 384-well microtiter plates, with bioluminescence measured after 6 hours of incubation. Bioluminescence assays were conducted using Envision Multilabel Readers with OD600 normalization. LuxO D47E protein was overexpressed in E. coli BL21 Gold (DE3) using pET28B plasmid and purified via Ni-NTA affinity chromatography. ATPase activity was measured using a coupled enzyme assay monitoring NADH oxidation at 340 nm, with kinetic parameters determined by nonlinear regression fitting. DNA binding was assessed by electrophoretic mobility shift assays and fluorescence anisotropy using fluorescein-labeled qrr4 promoter fragments. Mutants resistant to compound 12 were identified by random mutagenesis of luxO D47E cloned into pEVS143, followed by selection on compound-containing agar and sequencing of resistant clones. Western blot analysis of TcpA and VopD was performed using standard protocols with specific antibodies. Cytotoxicity was quantified using lactate dehydrogenase release assays in HeLa cells infected with V. parahaemolyticus. Chemical synthesis of the disclosed compounds was carried out under anhydrous conditions using sodium hydride as base and alkyl halides as electrophiles, with purification by silica gel chromatography and structural confirmation by ¹H and ¹³C NMR and high-resolution mass spectrometry.

### Chemical Reactions

The synthesis of neopentyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate was achieved by reacting 6-chloro-5-thio-6-azauracil with neopentyl bromide in dimethylformamide in the presence of sodium hydride at 60°C for 12 hours, followed by aqueous workup and purification by column chromatography. Isobutyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate was prepared analogously using isobutyl bromide. Cyclobutylmethyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate was synthesized from cyclobutylmethyl bromide under identical conditions. Cyclopropylmethyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate was obtained from cyclopropylmethyl bromide. 3-Methylbutan-2-yl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate was prepared using 2-bromo-3-methylbutane. (Trimethylsilyl)methyl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate was synthesized using chloromethyltrimethylsilane. 3,3-Dimethylbutan-2-yl 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate was prepared from 2-bromo-3,3-dimethylbutane. 2-((3,5-dioxo-2,3,4,5-tetrahydro-1,2,4-triazin-6-yl)thio)acetate was synthesized using bromoacetic acid ethyl ester followed by saponification. All compounds were purified by flash chromatography on silica gel and characterized by NMR and mass spectrometry.

## Results

The invention identifies two classes of quorum sensing-activating molecules, with Class 2 compounds exhibiting potent and selective inhibition of LuxO ATPase activity. Compound 11 and its analog compound 12, bearing a tert-butyl carbinol side chain, activate quorum sensing in both CqsA/LuxS-deficient and luxO D47E strains of V. cholerae, indicating action downstream of the receptors. These compounds reduce transcription of qrr sRNAs without altering LuxO DNA binding, and inhibit ATP hydrolysis in a dose-dependent manner with uncompetitive kinetics. A genetic screen for resistant mutants identified four amino acid substitutions—I211F, L215F, L242F, and V294L—that confer resistance to inhibition while preserving ATPase function, mapping to a region adjacent to the Walker B motif. These mutations abolish compound binding without affecting ATP binding, confirming an allosteric mechanism. The compounds activate quorum sensing in V. harveyi and induce opaque colony formation in V. parahaemolyticus luxO* mutants, demonstrating broad activity across Vibrio species. Treatment with compound 12 reduces TcpA production in V. cholerae and VopD secretion in V. parahaemolyticus, and significantly attenuates cytotoxicity in mammalian cell infection models. The compounds exhibit no cytotoxicity at concentrations up to 500 µM and do not affect bacterial growth, supporting their potential as anti-virulence agents.

## Discussion Of Results

The invention discloses the first class of small molecules that selectively inhibit the ATPase activity of an NtrC-type response regulator, establishing LuxO as a viable target for anti-virulence therapy. The 5-thio-6-azauracil scaffold represents a novel chemotype for modulating bacterial signaling, with potency dictated by steric and hydrophobic features of the side chain. The uncompetitive inhibition mechanism, coupled with binding to a region adjacent to the Walker B motif, distinguishes these compounds from previously described response regulator inhibitors that target phosphorylation or DNA binding. The identification of inhibitor-resistant mutants provides a structural blueprint for rational drug design and confirms a conserved binding pocket that may be exploited for broader inhibition of NtrC-family proteins. The broad spectrum activity across Vibrio species underscores the conservation of LuxO and its central role in virulence regulation. The ability of these compounds to reduce virulence without affecting viability positions them as ideal candidates for prophylactic use, particularly in settings of cholera outbreaks or seafood-borne infections. The low cytotoxicity and favorable pharmacokinetic properties support further development into clinical candidates. Moreover, the discovery of this chemical class opens the door to the development of inhibitors targeting AAA+ ATPase domains in other bacterial and eukaryotic systems, providing a new paradigm for antimicrobial and therapeutic discovery.
