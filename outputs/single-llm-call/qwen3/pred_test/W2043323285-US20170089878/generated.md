## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of this invention pertains to autonomous, instrumented devices designed for in situ measurement of physical forces experienced by organisms or objects passing through high-energy hydraulic environments, particularly those associated with hydroelectric turbines and spillway structures. The operation of hydropower facilities often subjects aquatic life, especially migratory fish species, to extreme mechanical and hydrodynamic stresses, including rapid pressure fluctuations, intense shear forces, turbulent eddies, and direct impact with rotating turbine components. These conditions can result in significant injury or mortality, raising ecological concerns and regulatory challenges for dam operators and environmental agencies. While live fish studies have historically provided critical insights into biological outcomes, they are inherently limited in their ability to quantify the precise hydraulic parameters—such as acceleration, rotation, pressure, and temperature—that directly cause injury. The inability to pinpoint the location, timing, and magnitude of deleterious forces within the turbine passage impedes the rational design of fish-friendly infrastructure. Prior art sensor devices, including earlier generations of instrumented fish analogs, have been deployed to bridge this gap, yet they suffer from critical shortcomings. These include insufficient pressure measurement ranges for high-head facilities, limited shock survivability under blade strike conditions, inadequate data storage and sampling rates, unreliable recovery mechanisms, and high manufacturing costs that restrict widespread deployment. Furthermore, many prior devices were not optimized for use in diverse turbine types such as Francis or pump-storage units, which exhibit different flow dynamics and higher fatality rates than Kaplan turbines. A new generation of sensor device is therefore needed that can withstand extreme mechanical loads, operate reliably across a broader range of hydraulic conditions, collect high-fidelity multidimensional data at elevated sampling frequencies, and be economically scalable for international deployment. The present invention addresses these deficiencies by introducing a compact, robust, neutrally buoyant, autonomous sensor platform engineered to survive the harshest turbine environments while delivering precise, time-synchronized measurements of linear and angular motion, pressure, orientation, and temperature. The device is uniquely configured with redundant recovery systems, low-power electronics, and wireless beaconing capabilities to ensure high retrieval rates, enabling comprehensive data collection that directly informs turbine redesign, operational modifications, and ecological risk assessment. This disclosure presents a novel sensor fish system that overcomes the limitations of prior technology and establishes a new standard for in situ hydraulic characterization in hydropower systems.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a next-generation autonomous sensor device, referred to as the Gen 2 Sensor Fish, designed to replicate the size, density, and hydrodynamic behavior of juvenile salmonid fish while instrumenting them with a suite of high-precision sensors to measure the physical stresses encountered during passage through hydroelectric turbines and other hydraulic structures. The device is engineered to be neutrally buoyant in freshwater, with a streamlined cylindrical housing measuring 24.5 millimeters in diameter and 89.9 millimeters in length, and a total mass of approximately 42.1 grams, ensuring it behaves similarly to a natural fish during turbulent flow. The sensor suite includes a triaxial accelerometer with a ±200 g full-scale range and 10,000 g shock survival rating, a triaxial gyroscope with a ±2000 degrees per second range, a triaxial magnetometer and secondary accelerometer integrated into an eCompass module, a pressure sensor with a 12-bar operational range and 30-bar overpressure tolerance, and a precision temperature sensor calibrated for in situ water temperature measurement. All sensors are mounted on a flexible circuit board positioned at the device’s center of mass to minimize measurement artifacts from inertial coupling. A low-power 16-bit microcontroller with an internal low-frequency oscillator governs data acquisition at a sampling rate of 2048 Hz, storing up to five minutes of high-resolution sensor data in non-volatile flash memory. The device is powered by a rechargeable lithium-ion battery, regulated by a low-quiescent-current LDO to maximize operational endurance. Upon completion of data collection, a recovery mechanism is activated via the controlled severing of nichrome wire tension lines that hold two weighted components in place, causing the device to become positively buoyant and ascend to the surface. Once at the surface, four high-intensity orange LEDs and a radiofrequency beacon operating at 146 MHz activate intermittently to facilitate visual and electronic recovery. The device is activated by proximity to a magnetic switch and undergoes a self-diagnostic sequence prior to deployment. After retrieval, data are downloaded through a docking station equipped with a TTL-to-USB interface, enabling batch processing of multiple units simultaneously. Calibration protocols and firmware logic ensure measurement accuracy across all sensor channels, and the system is designed for rapid reconfiguration and reuse. The purpose of this summary is to provide a concise overview of the invention’s structure, function, and operational workflow, without limiting the scope of the claims that follow.

## DESCRIPTION

The purpose of this disclosure is to describe in detail the structure, components, and operational logic of the Gen 2 Sensor Fish, an autonomous instrumented device engineered for the precise measurement of hydraulic forces experienced by aquatic organisms during passage through hydropower infrastructure. The invention encompasses a fully integrated system designed to survive extreme mechanical environments while delivering high-fidelity, time-synchronized data on linear acceleration, rotational velocity, orientation, pressure, and temperature. The scope of the invention includes all embodiments of the device as described herein, including variations in housing materials, sensor configurations, power systems, recovery mechanisms, and communication protocols, provided they retain the essential functional architecture and operational principles disclosed. Embodiments may include modifications to the sensor array for use in saltwater environments, adaptations for different fish sizes, integration with external telemetry systems, or deployment in non-turbine hydraulic structures such as locks, fish ladders, or pump storage systems. The housing of the Gen 2 Sensor Fish is constructed from a durable, corrosion-resistant polymer composite that encloses all internal components in a sealed, pressure-tolerant envelope. The device’s dimensions are optimized to match the hydrodynamic profile of a yearling salmon smolt, ensuring representative behavior in turbulent flows. Internal components are arranged to maintain a center of gravity coincident with the geometric center of the device, minimizing rotational artifacts during free-fall and turbulent passage. The primary accelerometer, a chip-scale analog device with a ±200 g full-scale range and a 10,000 g overload survival rating, is mounted at the center of a flex circuit board, aligned with the device’s principal axes to ensure accurate measurement of impulsive forces. The accelerometer’s bandwidth is programmable between 0.5 Hz and 1300 Hz for the x- and y-axes, and between 0.5 Hz and 1000 Hz for the z-axis, allowing adaptation to varying flow regimes. The gyroscope, a digital three-axis MEMS device with a ±2000 degrees per second full-scale range, features an integrated temperature sensor and a 16-bit analog-to-digital converter, and is mounted adjacent to the accelerometer to minimize thermal and mechanical drift. The eCompass module integrates a three-axis digital accelerometer with a ±16 g range and a three-axis magnetometer with a ±8.1 gauss range, both connected via an I²C interface, providing orientation data that complement the gyroscope’s angular velocity measurements. The pressure sensor is a micromachined silicon Wheatstone bridge device with a 12-bar absolute range and a 30-bar overpressure tolerance, mounted on a flexible substrate in direct thermal contact with the housing via conductive epoxy to ensure accurate water temperature correlation. The primary temperature sensor is an analog device with a linear output of 6.25 mV per degree Celsius, calibrated over the range of -40 to +125 degrees Celsius, and positioned proximal to the pressure sensor to capture ambient water temperature with minimal thermal lag. The recovery mechanism consists of two spring-loaded weights, each secured by a fishing line looped over a nichrome wire segment. A microcontroller-controlled transistor circuit delivers a brief, high-current pulse to the nichrome wire, heating it to sever the line and release the weight, thereby inducing positive buoyancy. The process is repeated at the opposite end of the device to ensure reliable surfacing regardless of orientation. Upon activation, a magnetic reed switch initiates a self-check sequence that verifies battery voltage and flash memory status, followed by a configurable delay before data acquisition begins. Data are sampled at 2048 Hz and stored in internal flash memory with SPI communication to the microcontroller. After data collection, a configurable resurface delay precedes activation of the recovery mechanism. Once surfaced, a 146 MHz RF beacon and four high-intensity orange LEDs emit periodic signals to aid in visual and electronic recovery. The device is powered by a rechargeable battery regulated by a low-quiescent-current LDO to extend operational life. The microcontroller is a 16-bit PIC device with an internal low-frequency oscillator, eliminating the need for an external crystal and reducing power consumption. A 12-bit successive approximation ADC digitizes analog sensor outputs, and all sensor supply voltages and grounds are independently regulated to minimize noise. The device includes a bi-color LED for system status indication, with yellow indicating standby, green indicating full charge, and red indicating failure. The recovery mechanism is controlled by a dedicated circuit comprising a capacitor bank for energy storage, bleeder resistors for safe discharge, and MOSFET transistors for precise current control. The nichrome wires are embedded within the housing and insulated to prevent unintended activation. The docking station facilitates simultaneous charging and data download of up to four units via a TTL-to-USB interface, and communicates with proprietary software that converts raw binary data into calibrated, time-stamped CSV files with physical units. Firmware logic governs the entire sequence of operations, including sensor initialization, data logging, recovery timing, beacon activation, and error handling, ensuring consistent performance across all operational environments. The device is constructed from two primary printed circuit boards—an analog board housing the sensors and a digital board housing the microcontroller and communication modules—connected via adapter boards that maintain signal integrity and mechanical alignment. All components are selected for reliability under extreme hydraulic conditions, and the entire system has been validated through laboratory calibration and field deployment in high-head and low-head hydropower facilities.
