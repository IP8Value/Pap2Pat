# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods for identifying patients at risk of schizophrenic relapse through the systematic evaluation of specific clinical symptoms and biomarker levels, enabling timely intervention to prevent full psychotic decompensation. The invention provides a clinically actionable framework for detecting early signs of relapse in individuals diagnosed with schizophrenia, leveraging a targeted subset of symptom indicators derived from the Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) in combination with quantifiable biological markers. This approach is designed to overcome the limitations of infrequent clinical assessments and subjective global evaluations that currently dominate standard psychiatric care. By focusing on a narrow, empirically validated set of symptom domains that demonstrate the most consistent and predictive escalation prior to relapse, the invention enables the development of scalable, technology-enabled monitoring systems capable of detecting incipient relapse with sufficient lead time to permit preventive therapeutic adjustments. The method is particularly suited for deployment in outpatient settings where access to frequent psychiatric evaluation is limited, and where early detection can significantly alter disease trajectory, reduce hospitalization rates, and improve long-term functional outcomes.

## BACKGROUND

Schizophrenia is a chronic and severe neuropsychiatric disorder characterized by persistent disturbances in thought, perception, emotion, and behavior. Core symptoms include positive manifestations such as delusions, hallucinations, and disorganized speech, as well as negative symptoms like emotional withdrawal, avolition, and social isolation, alongside general psychopathological features including anxiety, tension, and impaired insight. The disease typically follows a relapsing-remitting course, with the majority of patients experiencing recurrent episodes of acute psychosis over their lifetime. Each relapse is associated with progressive neurobiological deterioration, cognitive decline, functional impairment, and increased risk of suicide, with mortality rates significantly elevated compared to the general population. Despite the availability of antipsychotic medications, treatment adherence remains suboptimal, and relapse rates remain high, particularly when symptom monitoring is limited to sporadic outpatient visits occurring at intervals of four to twelve weeks. Current clinical practice relies heavily on global assessments such as the Clinical Global Impression scale or subjective clinician judgment, which lack the granularity to detect subtle but clinically significant symptom fluctuations that precede full relapse. These assessments are often conducted too infrequently to capture the rapid onset of symptom escalation, which has been shown in recent studies to occur within days rather than weeks prior to decompensation. As a result, many patients present to emergency departments or inpatient units only after significant deterioration has occurred, rendering early intervention impossible. The absence of objective, quantifiable, and frequently measurable indicators of impending relapse has created a critical gap in the management of schizophrenia, necessitating a paradigm shift toward data-driven, symptom-specific, and technology-integrated monitoring systems capable of identifying at-risk individuals before irreversible clinical consequences unfold.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention introduces a novel method for identifying patients at risk of schizophrenic relapse through the concurrent assessment of a defined set of interview-based clinical symptoms and quantifiable biomarker levels. The method utilizes a targeted subset of seven PANSS items—P1 (delusions), P2 (conceptual disorganization), P3 (hallucinations), P4 (excitement), P6 (suspiciousness), G2 (anxiety), and G4 (tension)—as primary indicators of impending relapse, based on their consistent and statistically significant escalation in the period immediately preceding psychotic decompensation. These symptoms are selected not for their prevalence, but for their predictive power, temporal trajectory, and sensitivity to early change. In addition to symptom monitoring, the method incorporates the measurement of specific biomarkers, including but not limited to cortisol levels, inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and neurochemical metabolites detectable in serum or cerebrospinal fluid, whose fluctuations correlate with the onset of symptom exacerbation. The administration of a pharmaceutical agent, such as an antipsychotic medication or an adjunctive anxiolytic or mood-stabilizing compound, is initiated or adjusted upon detection of a significant increase in the specified symptom profile or biomarker levels, with the explicit goal of reducing the likelihood of full relapse. The invention enables proactive, personalized intervention prior to the occurrence of hospitalization, suicidal ideation, or aggressive behavior, thereby preserving functional capacity and reducing the burden on healthcare systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The method is applicable to adult patients diagnosed with schizophrenia according to DSM-5 or ICD-11 criteria who have achieved a period of clinical stability following acute exacerbation and are receiving maintenance pharmacotherapy. Symptom severity is measured using a structured clinical interview that evaluates the seven identified PANSS items, with scoring conducted on a seven-point ordinal scale ranging from absent (1) to extreme (7). The assessment may be administered by a trained clinician, a psychiatric nurse, or via a validated digital interface designed for remote use. The PANSS assessment is administered at intervals no longer than seven days to ensure sufficient temporal resolution to detect the rapid symptom escalation observed prior to relapse. The identification of biomarkers is performed through routine blood sampling or, in clinical research settings, through cerebrospinal fluid analysis, with levels compared against established reference ranges and individual baseline values. The administration of a pharmaceutical agent is guided by predefined thresholds of symptom severity or biomarker elevation, with dose escalation, medication switching, or the addition of adjunctive therapy initiated in accordance with clinical guidelines. Remote detection of symptom severity is enabled through secure electronic platforms that deliver standardized questionnaires to patients via smartphones, tablets, or wearable devices, allowing for daily or twice-daily self-reporting of the seven key symptoms. The system automatically transmits responses to a secure healthcare portal, where algorithms compare current scores to historical baselines and generate alerts when predefined escalation criteria are met. These alerts trigger notifications to treating clinicians, prompting timely clinical review and intervention before full relapse occurs.

### PANSS

The Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) is a standardized instrument used to evaluate the severity of symptoms in schizophrenia through 30 individual items grouped into three subscales: positive, negative, and general psychopathology. Each item is rated on a seven-point scale based on a semi-structured clinical interview incorporating patient self-report and collateral information from caregivers or family members. The seven PANSS items central to this invention—P1 (delusions), P2 (conceptual disorganization), P3 (hallucinations), P4 (excitement), P6 (suspiciousness), G2 (anxiety), and G4 (tension)—were identified through longitudinal analysis of relapse events across multiple clinical trials as exhibiting the most consistent and earliest increases prior to decompensation. While the full PANSS is comprehensive, its administration is time-intensive and impractical for frequent use in routine care. The selected subset of items retains high predictive validity while minimizing patient burden and administrative overhead. The administration of this subset can be completed in under five minutes, making it feasible for daily use in both clinical and remote settings. The predictive value of this subset lies in its ability to detect symptom escalation with high sensitivity and specificity, as demonstrated by non-linear mixed effects modeling showing that these items begin to rise approximately seven to ten days before relapse, with a one-point increase occurring on average within one day of the event. Limitations of the full PANSS, such as its susceptibility to rater bias and dependence on clinician availability, are mitigated by the focused use of this subset, which can be reliably self-reported and digitally captured. The use of this subset enables the development of automated, algorithm-driven monitoring systems that can operate continuously and without direct clinician input, thereby expanding access to early detection in underserved populations. Remote detection of symptom severity is facilitated through secure mobile applications that present the seven-item questionnaire in a user-friendly format, with response data encrypted and transmitted in real time to a central clinical dashboard. Comparison of current scores to historical baselines is performed using machine learning algorithms that account for individual variability and seasonal fluctuations. When a statistically significant upward trend is detected, the system generates an alert to the treating clinician, who may then initiate a telehealth consultation or adjust medication. The increased frequency of assessments enabled by this method allows for the detection of relapse risk at a stage when pharmacological intervention is most effective, thereby preventing hospitalization and preserving functional outcomes.

## EXAMPLES

### PANSS: Methods

Data were pooled from three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials evaluating the efficacy of paliperidone formulations in delaying psychotic relapse. These studies included a total of 907 patients with schizophrenia who had achieved clinical stability during an open-label run-in phase and were subsequently randomized to receive either active medication or placebo. PANSS assessments were conducted at regular intervals, with the frequency varying between weekly and biweekly during the initial phase and every four weeks thereafter. Relapse events were prospectively defined using standardized criteria including hospitalization, significant increases in PANSS scores, or the emergence of suicidal or aggressive behavior. The dataset included 267 patients who experienced relapse during the double-blind phase, with detailed PANSS scores available for analysis at each assessment point prior to relapse.

### PANSS: Definition of Relapse

The primary efficacy variable in all three studies was time to first relapse, defined by any one of four criteria: (1) psychiatric hospitalization, (2) deliberate self-injury or clinically significant suicidal or homicidal ideation, (3) a 25% increase in PANSS total score over two consecutive assessments less than seven days apart for patients with a baseline score above 40, or a 10-point increase for those with a baseline score of 40 or below, or (4) a sustained increase to a score of 5 or higher on any of the pre-specified PANSS items (P1, P2, P3, P6, P7, G8) for patients whose baseline score was 3 or less, or to a score of 6 or higher if baseline was 4. Patients who met relapse criteria but were not formally classified as such during the trial were retrospectively reclassified, and those who did not meet the criteria but were labeled as relapsed were excluded. A total of 267 patients met the defined relapse criteria and were included in the analysis.

### PANSS: Statistical Analysis

Individual PANSS items were ranked according to the magnitude of change from randomization to relapse. Linear and non-linear mixed effects models were applied to estimate the trajectory of symptom change over time, with observations aligned relative to the date of relapse rather than scheduled visit dates. The non-linear model employed an exponential function to capture the accelerating nature of symptom escalation, with parameters a, b, and c representing the rate of increase, the time point at which a one-point increase occurred relative to relapse, and the baseline symptom level, respectively. Subject-level random effects were included to account for inter-individual variability. Model selection was based on Akaike Information Criterion, and all analyses were conducted using SAS 9.4.

### PANSS: Demographics and Characteristics of Patients Experienced a Relapse During Double-Blind Phase

Among the 267 patients who experienced relapse, the mean age was 38.4 years, 59.2% were male, and the majority identified as White. Baseline PANSS total scores were significantly higher in relapsed patients compared to non-relapsed controls (54.9 vs. 52.5, p=0.002), indicating a higher symptom burden even prior to relapse. No other demographic or clinical characteristics differed significantly between groups.

### Example 5

Analysis of symptom trajectories revealed that the seven PANSS items—P1, P2, P3, P4, P6, G2, and G4—demonstrated the most consistent and pronounced increases at the time of relapse. Non-linear modeling indicated that these items began to escalate approximately 7–10 days before relapse, with a one-point increase occurring on average between 0.33 and 1.17 days prior to the event. Trajectories were consistent across treatment groups and relapse definitions, confirming the robustness of the findings. Individual patient trajectories showed marked heterogeneity, but the seven-item subset remained the most predictive across all subgroups. The results support the implementation of a monitoring protocol centered on these items, with remote electronic assessment enabling daily tracking and automated alerting to clinicians upon detection of clinically significant escalation.
