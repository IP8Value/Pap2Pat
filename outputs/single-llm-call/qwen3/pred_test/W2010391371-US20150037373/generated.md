# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to compositions and methods utilizing extracts derived from pinecones, particularly those obtained from species of the genus Pinus, for the modulation of immune responses in mammals. Specifically, the invention encompasses purified polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complexes (PPC) isolated from pinecone tissue and their use as biological adjuvants to enhance the magnitude, duration, and quality of T cell-mediated immunity. The invention further includes formulations for oral, parenteral, or mucosal administration of PPC to stimulate innate and adaptive immune pathways, particularly in the context of vaccination, immunotherapy, and the treatment of infectious or neoplastic diseases. The compositions are characterized by their ability to influence dendritic cell maturation, promote antigen-specific T cell expansion during the primary immune response, and modulate cytokine profiles without altering the fundamental kinetics of activation-induced cell death. The invention is not limited to any particular species of pine, but is exemplified by extracts from Pinus sylvestris, and is applicable across a broad range of mammalian species including humans.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The medicinal use of pinecone extracts dates back to antiquity, with documented applications in ancient Greek pharmacology as recorded by Pedanius Dioscorides in his seminal work De Materia Medica, composed around AD 65. Dioscorides, a physician and botanist who traveled extensively throughout the Roman Empire, described the use of pinecone preparations for the treatment of renal disorders, gastrointestinal disturbances, and cutaneous conditions resembling psoriasis. These early observations were not isolated; in the island of Kyushu, Japan, indigenous populations have for generations consumed aqueous infusions of pinecones as a traditional remedy for a wide spectrum of ailments, including chronic infections, inflammatory conditions, and malignancies. The breadth of reported therapeutic effects suggested a systemic immunomodulatory mechanism rather than a localized or symptomatic action. Modern scientific inquiry into these traditional remedies has revealed that the bioactive components responsible for these effects are complex polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide structures, collectively termed PPC. Previous studies have demonstrated that orally administered PPC biases the immune response toward a T helper type 1 (Th1) phenotype, suppresses the development of Th2-driven allergic responses, and significantly enhances the generation of antigen-specific cytotoxic CD8+ T cells during secondary immune responses. In vitro, PPC has been shown to induce the differentiation of monocytes and bone marrow progenitors into dendritic-like cells exhibiting enhanced expression of co-stimulatory molecules and cytokine secretion profiles consistent with immune activation. Despite these empirical observations, the precise molecular and cellular mechanisms by which PPC influences T cell dynamics—particularly during the critical phases of expansion and contraction—remained poorly understood. The absence of a mechanistic framework has hindered the rational optimization of PPC-based therapies and limited its integration into modern immunological practice. There exists a clear need for a scientifically validated composition and method that elucidates how PPC enhances T cell immunity, enables reproducible clinical application, and provides a foundation for its use as a stand-alone or adjunctive immunomodulatory agent.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and non-obvious understanding of the immunological effects of pinecone-derived polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC), demonstrating that its primary mechanism of action lies in the enhancement of the proliferative phase of the primary T cell response to antigenic stimulation, rather than in the modulation of apoptosis or the contraction phase. The invention discloses that oral administration of PPC, when co-administered with an antigenic stimulus, significantly increases the number of antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cells during the early expansion phase, resulting in a quantitatively larger pool of effector cells that subsequently contributes to a more robust secondary response. This effect is not attributable to inhibition of activation-induced cell death, suppression of reactive oxygen species, or upregulation of anti-apoptotic proteins such as Bcl-2, as previously hypothesized. Instead, the invention reveals that PPC acts upstream of T cell proliferation, likely through the promotion of dendritic cell maturation and migration to secondary lymphoid tissues, thereby increasing the frequency and duration of antigen presentation to naïve T cells. The invention further encompasses methods for the preparation of standardized, purified PPC extracts suitable for pharmaceutical use, as well as compositions for use in vaccination protocols, cancer immunotherapy, and the treatment of chronic infections. The invention also includes diagnostic and monitoring methods to assess the immunological impact of PPC administration through quantification of antigen-specific T cell populations, cytokine profiles, and dendritic cell activation markers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

The invention builds upon prior observations that PPC enhances antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses following vaccination, and now provides a mechanistic framework for this phenomenon through detailed in vivo analysis of T cell dynamics during the primary immune response. In a model using Staphylococcal enterotoxin B (SEB) as a superantigen, oral administration of PPC was shown to double the number of CD4+/Vβ8+ and CD8+/Vβ8+ T cells in the draining lymph nodes at 48 hours post-stimulation, without altering the rate of subsequent contraction. This increase was not observed in mice treated with PPC alone, indicating that the effect is antigen-dependent. The expansion was specific to T cells bearing the Vβ8 T cell receptor, confirming that PPC does not induce generalized lymphocyte proliferation. The enhanced expansion was accompanied by a shift in systemic cytokine production, with elevated serum levels of interferon-gamma and reduced levels of interleukin-2, suggesting a Th1-polarized environment conducive to cytotoxic T cell development. Crucially, the contraction phase proceeded normally in PPC-treated mice, with CD4+/Vβ8+ T cells declining to baseline levels by 96 hours, while CD8+/Vβ8+ T cells remained elevated due to their greater initial expansion. Apoptosis, as measured by Annexin V binding, was not suppressed by PPC, and intracellular levels of the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2 were similarly reduced in both SEB-treated and SEB+PPC-treated T cells, indicating that PPC does not inhibit activation-induced cell death. Reactive oxygen species levels in activated T cells were diminished in PPC-treated mice, yet this reduction did not correlate with altered Bcl-2 expression, suggesting that PPC’s antioxidant properties are insufficient to override the pro-apoptotic signaling cascade triggered by SEB. Gene expression analysis revealed no significant modulation of Bcl-2, Bcl-xL, or Bax mRNA, but a marked increase in Bim expression in both SEB and SEB+PPC groups, consistent with ongoing apoptosis. Notably, PPC alone induced a transient increase in Bcl-3 mRNA, a regulator of T cell survival, but this effect was absent during antigen-driven activation, further supporting the conclusion that PPC’s primary action is on early T cell expansion. The invention further posits that PPC enhances dendritic cell maturation and trafficking to lymph nodes, thereby increasing the number of antigen-presenting cells available to stimulate naïve T cells. This hypothesis is supported by preliminary data showing increased CD11c+ cell accumulation in lymph nodes of PPC-treated animals. The invention encompasses methods for administering PPC orally, subcutaneously, intranasally, or intravenously, in combination with vaccines, tumor antigens, or pathogen-derived epitopes, to amplify the magnitude of the primary T cell response and thereby improve long-term immunological memory.

## EXAMPLES

### Preparation of PPC

Pinecones harvested from Pinus sylvestris were washed in deionized water to remove surface contaminants, then mechanically shredded into uniform fragments. The shredded material was loaded into a stainless steel extraction vessel and subjected to aqueous extraction at a pH of 12.0 and a temperature of 121°C for a duration of 30 minutes. The resulting extract was filtered through a 0.45 μm membrane to remove particulates, then concentrated under reduced pressure. The concentrate was standardized by ultraviolet spectrophotometry at 280 nm using a predetermined extinction coefficient of 22.8 L·g⁻¹·cm⁻¹ for polyphenylpropanoids. The extract was tested for the absence of endotoxins, heavy metals, pesticides, and microbial contaminants using validated assays. The purified liquid was spray-dried to produce a stable, odorless, dark brown powder with a moisture content below 5%. The powder was suspended in sterile distilled water to a concentration of 25 mg/mL, centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 20 minutes, and filtered through a 0.2 μm nylon membrane to yield a sterile, particle-free stock solution. Working dilutions of 200 μg/mL were prepared weekly and stored at 4°C.

### In Vivo Immune Stimulation

Six- to seven-week-old male Balb/c mice were obtained from Charles River Laboratories and housed under specific pathogen-free conditions. On Day 0, mice were injected intraperitoneally with 100 μg of Staphylococcal enterotoxin B (SEB) in 100 μL phosphate-buffered saline, or with an equivalent volume of saline as a control. Mice designated for PPC treatment received 100 μL of a 200 μg/mL PPC solution via oral gavage three times daily on Days 0 and 1, for a total of six doses. Additional mice received PPC in their drinking water at a concentration of 200 μg/mL ad libitum. Mice were euthanized at 48 or 96 hours post-injection, and inguinal lymph nodes and spleens were harvested for cellular and molecular analysis.

### ELISA Assays

Serum samples were collected at the time of euthanasia and stored at −80°C. Commercially available enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits specific for murine IL-2, IFNγ, and IL-12p70 were used according to manufacturer instructions. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a microplate spectrophotometer, and cytokine concentrations were calculated from standard curves generated using GraphPad Prism software.

### Lymph Node and Spleen Cell Isolation

Lymph nodes and spleens were mechanically homogenized in Hank’s Balanced Salt Solution using a Seward Stomacher®80 at medium speed for 60 seconds. Cell suspensions were pelleted by centrifugation at 300 × g for 5 minutes, resuspended in appropriate volumes of HBSS, and viable cell counts were determined using a Millipore Muse Cell Analyzer. Cells were resuspended in complete RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin at a concentration of 4 × 10⁶ cells/mL.

### Flow Cytometry

One million lymph node cells per sample were stained with fluorescently labeled monoclonal antibodies against CD3 (APC), CD4 (FITC), CD8 (PE), and Vβ8 (PerCP-eFluor®710), along with appropriate isotype controls. Cells were incubated on ice for 30 minutes, washed twice with FACS buffer, and analyzed on a BD FACSCalibur flow cytometer using CellQuest Pro software. Data were analyzed using FloJo software to quantify the percentage and absolute number of CD3+/Vβ8+, CD4+/Vβ8+, and CD8+/Vβ8+ T cell populations.

### Detection of Apoptosis

Cells were stained with APC-labeled anti-CD3, PerCP-eFluor®710-labeled anti-Vβ8, and FITC-labeled Annexin V according to the manufacturer’s protocol. After a 10-minute incubation at room temperature, cells were washed and analyzed by flow cytometry to determine the percentage of Annexin V-positive T cells.

### Detection of Reactive Oxygen Species

Lymph node cells were incubated with 2 μL of 50 μM CM-H2DCFDA for 15 minutes at 37°C, followed by washing and analysis by flow cytometry. Fluorescence intensity was measured in CD3+ T cells to determine the percentage of ROS-positive cells and the mean fluorescence intensity as an indicator of intracellular ROS levels.

### Detection of Intracellular Bcl-2

After surface staining, cells were fixed and permeabilized with 0.03% saponin in HBSS, then incubated with FITC-labeled anti-Bcl-2 antibody for 60 minutes on ice. Cells were washed twice with saponin-containing buffer and once with HBSS, then analyzed by flow cytometry to determine Bcl-2 expression levels in CD3+/Vβ8+ and CD3+/Vβ8− populations.

### Analysis of Gene Expression

Total RNA was extracted from lymph node cells using the RNeasy Plus Mini Kit. One microgram of RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA using the QuantiTect Reverse Transcription Kit. Real-time quantitative PCR was performed using SABiosciences RT2 Profiler SYBR Green Master Mix and validated primer sets for Bcl-2, Bcl-3, Bcl-xL, Bax, and Bim. Reactions were run on a BioRad iCycler with an initial denaturation at 95°C for 10 minutes, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 seconds and 60°C for 1 minute. mRNA expression levels were normalized to β-actin and analyzed using the ΔΔ-Ct method. Statistical significance was determined by one-way ANOVA followed by Newman-Keuls multiple comparison test.

### Describe Example I: oral delivery of PPC enhances T cell response to SEB

Oral administration of PPC in combination with SEB resulted in a statistically significant doubling of CD4+/Vβ8+ and CD8+/Vβ8+ T cells in the inguinal lymph nodes at 48 hours post-injection compared to SEB alone (p < 0.01). No increase was observed in mice treated with PPC alone, confirming the antigen-dependent nature of the effect.

### Describe Example II: PPC enhances CD8+ T cell survival during contraction phase

At 96 hours post-SEB injection, CD8+/Vβ8+ T cell numbers remained elevated in both SEB and SEB+PPC groups compared to naïve controls, with the SEB+PPC group showing a statistically significant increase over the SEB-only group (p < 0.05). CD4+/Vβ8+ T cells had contracted to baseline levels in both groups, indicating no protective effect of PPC on CD4+ T cell survival.

### Describe Example III: PPC affects cytokine production

Serum levels of IFNγ were significantly elevated in the SEB+PPC group compared to SEB alone (p < 0.005), while IL-2 levels were significantly reduced (p < 0.05). IL-12p70 levels remained unchanged, indicating a selective modulation of Th1-associated cytokines.

### Describe Example IV: PPC reduces ROS levels in T cells

The percentage of CD3+/ROS+ T cells and the mean fluorescence intensity of ROS were both significantly lower in the SEB+PPC group compared to the SEB group (p < 0.01), demonstrating a measurable antioxidant effect in vivo.

### Describe Example V: PPC's effect on Bcl-2 expression

Intracellular Bcl-2 protein levels in CD3+/Vβ8+ T cells were equally reduced in both SEB and SEB+PPC groups compared to naïve controls, with no difference in mean fluorescence intensity between the two groups, indicating that PPC does not rescue Bcl-2 expression.

### Describe Example VI: PPC's effect on Bcl-3, Bim, Bax, and Bcl-xL expression

Bim mRNA expression was increased nearly fourfold in both SEB and SEB+PPC groups compared to naïve mice, while Bcl-2 and Bcl-xL mRNA were suppressed. Bcl-3 mRNA was elevated in mice treated with PPC alone but not in the SEB+PPC group. Bax expression was unchanged.

### Describe Example VII: PPC does not suppress SEB-enhanced apoptosis

Annexin V staining revealed a significant increase in apoptotic Vβ8+ T cells in the SEB group compared to naïve mice (p < 0.001), with no difference between the SEB and SEB+PPC groups (p > 0.05), confirming that PPC does not inhibit activation-induced apoptosis.

### Summarize results of Example I

Oral PPC administration significantly enhances the expansion of antigen-specific CD4+ and CD8+ T cells during the primary response to SEB, doubling their numbers without inducing proliferation in the absence of antigen.

### Summarize results of Example II

PPC enhances the persistence of CD8+ T cells during the contraction phase not by preventing apoptosis, but by increasing the initial pool of activated cells, resulting in a larger residual population.

### Summarize results of Example III

PPC shifts the cytokine milieu toward a Th1 profile by increasing IFNγ and decreasing IL-2, which may promote cytotoxic T cell differentiation and limit Th2-mediated immune deviation.

### Summarize results of Example IV

PPC reduces reactive oxygen species in activated T cells, but this effect is insufficient to alter downstream apoptotic signaling or Bcl-2 expression.

### Summarize results of Example V

PPC does not restore Bcl-2 protein expression in activated T cells, indicating that its antioxidant properties do not translate into anti-apoptotic activity in this context.

### Summarize results of Example VI

PPC does not modulate the expression of key apoptosis-related genes during antigen-driven activation, despite inducing Bcl-3 expression in naïve T cells, suggesting context-dependent gene regulation.

### Summarize results of Example VII

PPC does not reduce SEB-induced apoptosis, as confirmed by Annexin V staining, confirming that its immunostimulatory effects are not mediated through inhibition of cell death.

### Discuss implications of results

The findings demonstrate that PPC functions as a potent adjuvant by amplifying the primary T cell response through enhanced antigen presentation, not by altering cell survival. This positions PPC as a unique immunomodulator suitable for use in vaccines requiring strong cytotoxic T cell responses.

### Discuss limitations of study

The study was conducted in mice using a superantigen model; human translation requires validation in clinical settings. The precise molecular target of PPC on dendritic cells remains unidentified.

### Suggest future directions

Future studies should investigate the effect of PPC on dendritic cell migration, antigen cross-presentation, and T cell priming in tumor and viral infection models. Clinical trials evaluating PPC as an adjuvant in cancer vaccines are warranted.

### Conclude with summary of findings

The invention demonstrates that pinecone-derived PPC enhances antigen-specific T cell expansion during the primary immune response through mechanisms involving dendritic cell activation, without altering apoptosis or cytokine-driven cell death. This discovery enables the development of novel immunotherapeutic strategies based on PPC for the treatment of infectious diseases and cancer.
