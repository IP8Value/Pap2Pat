# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The Akt protein, also known as protein kinase B (PKB), is a serine/threonine kinase that plays a central role in regulating cellular processes essential for survival, proliferation, metabolism, and growth. It functions as a key node in the phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) signaling pathway, which is one of the most frequently dysregulated pathways in human cancers. Activation of Akt occurs through a highly coordinated sequence of molecular events initiated by extracellular growth factors binding to receptor tyrosine kinases. This triggers the recruitment and activation of PI3K at the plasma membrane, leading to the generation of phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate (PIP3). PIP3 serves as a critical second messenger that facilitates the translocation of Akt from the cytosol to the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane via its pleckstrin homology (PH) domain. Once localized to the membrane, Akt undergoes dual phosphorylation at two conserved residues: threonine 308 within the activation loop and serine 473 in the hydrophobic motif. Phosphorylation at Thr308 is primarily mediated by phosphoinositide-dependent kinase 1 (PDK1), while phosphorylation at Ser473 is attributed to the mammalian target of rapamycin complex 2 (mTORC2), although other kinases such as DNA-dependent protein kinase (DNA-PK) and integrin-linked kinase (ILK) have also been proposed as potential contributors under specific cellular contexts. The full activation of Akt requires both phosphorylation events, which induce a conformational change that enables substrate recognition and catalytic activity. The activity of the Akt pathway is tightly regulated by the tumor suppressor phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN), which dephosphorylates PIP3 to phosphatidylinositol (4,5)-bisphosphate, thereby terminating Akt membrane recruitment and activation. Loss-of-function mutations, deletions, or epigenetic silencing of PTEN are among the most common genetic alterations observed in a wide spectrum of malignancies, including glioblastoma, prostate, endometrial, and breast cancers, leading to constitutive Akt signaling. Akt promotes tumorigenesis by phosphorylating downstream effectors that suppress apoptosis, enhance protein synthesis, stimulate cell cycle progression, and support metabolic reprogramming. For instance, Akt inhibits pro-apoptotic proteins such as Bad and FoxO transcription factors, activates the mTORC1 pathway via TSC2 phosphorylation, and enhances glucose uptake and glycolysis through modulation of GLUT4 and hexokinase activity. The persistent activation of Akt confers a survival advantage to cancer cells, rendering them resistant to conventional therapies such as chemotherapy and radiation. Consequently, Akt has emerged as a compelling therapeutic target in oncology. Numerous strategies have been pursued to inhibit Akt activity, including the development of ATP-competitive kinase inhibitors, allosteric modulators, and agents targeting upstream regulators such as PI3K or mTOR. However, many of these compounds suffer from poor selectivity, off-target effects, or limited efficacy in vivo due to compensatory signaling pathways or inadequate pharmacokinetic properties. Lipid-based inhibitors like perifosine, which interfere with Akt membrane localization, have been evaluated in clinical trials but demonstrated limited therapeutic windows due to toxicity and lack of robust biomarker-driven patient selection. Despite considerable efforts, there remains a critical unmet need for a potent, selective, and orally bioavailable small molecule inhibitor capable of effectively suppressing Akt signaling without disrupting other essential kinase pathways, particularly in tumors harboring hyperactivated Akt due to PTEN loss or activating mutations.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

Compounds and compositions are disclosed that selectively inhibit the Akt signaling pathway by directly binding to the pleckstrin homology domain of Akt, thereby preventing its translocation to the plasma membrane and subsequent activation. A novel small molecule, designated API-1, is described as a potent and selective inhibitor of Akt kinase activity in living cells. The chemical structure of API-1 is characterized as a pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidine derivative with a molecular formula that enables specific interaction with the PH domain of Akt without competing with ATP or substrate binding. API-1 effectively suppresses the phosphorylation of Akt at both Thr308 and Ser473 in a concentration-dependent manner, leading to the inhibition of downstream signaling cascades involving GSK3β, mTOR, FOXO3a, and Bad. Importantly, API-1 does not inhibit upstream regulators such as PI3K or PDK1, nor does it affect the activity of other AGC family kinases including PKA, PKC, and SGK, demonstrating exceptional selectivity. The compound inhibits the growth and induces apoptosis in cancer cells exhibiting elevated Akt activity due to PTEN deficiency, Akt amplification, or gain-of-function mutations such as AKT1-E17K, while having minimal impact on cells with low basal Akt signaling. This selective cytotoxicity translates into significant antitumor efficacy in vivo, where API-1 administration results in marked suppression of tumor growth in xenograft models derived from Akt-hyperactivated cancer cells, with no observable systemic toxicity at therapeutic doses. The invention encompasses methods for inhibiting the survival or proliferation of cancer cells by administering API-1 or structurally related analogs to subjects harboring tumors with aberrant Akt activation. The therapeutic utility of API-1 is further enhanced by its ability to overcome resistance mechanisms associated with constitutively active Akt mutants, making it a promising candidate for precision oncology applications. API-1 represents a novel class of Akt inhibitors that function through a unique mechanism of action—blocking membrane translocation rather than kinase catalysis—thereby offering a distinct pharmacological profile compared to existing agents and establishing its potential as a targeted anti-cancer agent with a favorable safety and efficacy profile.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Compounds and compositions are provided that are effective in inhibiting the Akt/PKB signaling pathway through direct interaction with the pleckstrin homology domain of Akt. The general structure of these compounds is based on a pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidine scaffold, which may be substituted at various positions with alkyl, alkoxy, halogen, or optionally substituted aryl groups to modulate potency, solubility, and pharmacokinetic properties. Embodiments of the compounds include derivatives wherein the pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidine core is substituted at the 2-, 4-, or 6-position with groups such as methyl, ethyl, methoxy, chloro, bromo, or phenyl, each of which may be optionally substituted with hydroxyl, amino, or carboxyl moieties. The compounds inhibit the Akt/PKB pathway by binding to the PH domain of Akt, thereby preventing its association with phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate at the plasma membrane and blocking the conformational changes necessary for dual phosphorylation and full activation. This mechanism distinguishes the disclosed compounds from ATP-competitive inhibitors and allows for selective inhibition of Akt even in the presence of constitutively active mutants such as E17K-AKT1, which exhibits aberrant membrane localization due to altered PH domain affinity. Pharmaceutical compositions are formulated to deliver these compounds via oral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, or topical routes, depending on the clinical indication. Routes of administration are selected based on the nature of the tumor, its anatomical location, and the desired pharmacokinetic profile. Pharmaceutically useful compositions are prepared using excipients such as lactose, starch, cellulose derivatives, magnesium stearate, or polysorbates, and may include stabilizers, buffers, or surfactants to ensure chemical integrity and bioavailability. Formulations for administration include solid dosage forms such as tablets, capsules, pills, and troches, which may contain binders like hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, disintegrants such as croscarmellose sodium, and lubricants including stearic acid. Liquid carriers or vehicles suitable for parenteral administration include sterile water, saline, dextrose solutions, or ethanol-water mixtures, optionally containing tonicity modifiers and preservatives. Sterile injectable solutions are prepared under aseptic conditions and may be lyophilized into powders for reconstitution prior to use. Topical formulations may incorporate solid carriers such as polyethylene glycols or liquid carriers such as propylene glycol, along with thickeners like carbomers or cellulose gums to enhance adherence and localized delivery. Adjuvants such as penetration enhancers or anti-inflammatory agents may be included to augment therapeutic effect. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of the compounds include hydrochlorides, sulfates, acetates, citrates, and maleates, which improve solubility and stability. Hydrates and solvates of the compounds are encompassed within the scope of the invention, as are prodrugs designed to enhance absorption or metabolic stability. Analogs of the parent compound are prepared through synthetic organic chemistry methods including Suzuki coupling, nucleophilic substitution, reductive amination, and heterocycle cyclization, allowing for systematic structure-activity relationship studies. Stereoisomers, including enantiomers and diastereomers, are individually isolated and evaluated for biological activity, with specific configurations shown to confer enhanced potency or reduced toxicity. Therapeutic applications include the treatment of oncological disorders characterized by hyperactivation of the Akt pathway, including but not limited to breast, ovarian, prostate, endometrial, glioblastoma, and pancreatic cancers, particularly those associated with PTEN loss, PIK3CA mutations, or AKT1-E17K mutations. The compounds exhibit potent anti-proliferative properties by inducing G1 cell cycle arrest and triggering caspase-dependent apoptosis in tumor cells with elevated Akt signaling, while sparing normal cells with physiological Akt activity. Therapeutic methods involve administering an effective amount of the compound to a subject in need, with dosages extrapolated from in vitro IC50 values and in vivo efficacy studies in murine xenograft models. Pharmaceutical compositions are formulated to deliver daily doses ranging from 0.1 mg/kg to 50 mg/kg, depending on route, formulation, and patient characteristics. Combination therapy with chemotherapeutic agents such as paclitaxel, cisplatin, doxorubicin, or 5-fluorouracil, or immunotherapeutic agents such as immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-1, PD-L1, or CTLA-4, is disclosed to enhance antitumor efficacy and overcome resistance. Methods for inhibiting the Akt/PKB pathway involve contacting a cell with the compound under conditions that permit binding to the PH domain of Akt, thereby preventing membrane localization and downstream signaling. Dosage levels are adjusted based on pharmacokinetic parameters including half-life, clearance, and tissue distribution, with monitoring of biomarkers such as phospho-Akt, phospho-GSK3β, and cleaved PARP to assess target engagement. Kits comprising the compound in sealed containers with instructions for use, storage conditions, and diagnostic criteria for patient selection are provided to facilitate clinical implementation. Mammalian species benefiting from the methods include humans, dogs, cats, horses, cattle, pigs, sheep, goats, rabbits, and non-human primates. In vitro methods are employed for screening tumor cell lines for Akt dependency and for evaluating compound efficacy prior to in vivo testing. The term “patient” refers to any mammalian subject diagnosed with an oncological disorder exhibiting aberrant Akt activation, and the term “subject” includes both human and veterinary patients.

### MATERIALS AND METHODS

Cell lines and the NCI Diversity Set were utilized to identify compounds capable of selectively inhibiting the growth of Akt-transformed cells. Screening was performed by plating AKT2-transformed NIH3T3 cells and control LXSN-transfected NIH3T3 cells in 96-well plates and exposing them to 5 µM concentrations of each compound from the NCI/DTP Open Chemical Repository. Cell viability was assessed using the CellTiter 96 One Solution Cell Proliferation Assay. Compounds demonstrating selective inhibition of Akt-transformed cells over control cells were prioritized for further characterization. In vitro protein kinase assays were conducted using recombinant Akt, PDK1, and SGK enzymes incubated with substrate peptides and [γ-32P]ATP in the presence of test compounds. Apoptosis was quantified using annexin V staining followed by flow cytometry. Antitumor activity was evaluated in a nude mouse tumor xenograft model, wherein OVCAR3, PANC-1, OVCAR5, and COLO357 cells were injected subcutaneously into the flanks of 8-week-old female athymic mice. Once tumors reached approximately 100 mm³, animals were randomized and treated daily via intraperitoneal injection with either vehicle (20% DMSO) or API-1 at 10 mg/kg/day. Tumor volumes were measured twice weekly, and tissues were harvested for immunoblotting to assess phosphorylation status of Akt and downstream targets.

### EXAMPLE 1

The development of a small molecule Akt inhibitor was motivated by the high frequency of Akt pathway activation in human cancers and the clinical limitations of existing inhibitors. Screening of the NCI Diversity Set identified API-1 as a compound that selectively inhibited the proliferation of AKT2-transformed NIH3T3 cells while sparing control cells. API-1 was characterized as a potent inhibitor of Akt kinase activity and phosphorylation at both Thr308 and Ser473 in multiple cancer cell lines, with an IC50 of approximately 0.8 µM for phospho-Ser473. Importantly, API-1 did not directly inhibit recombinant Akt kinase activity in vitro, even at concentrations exceeding 50 µM, indicating a mechanism distinct from ATP-competitive inhibition. These findings established API-1 as a selective cellular Akt inhibitor that acts indirectly by targeting upstream regulatory events.

### EXAMPLE 2

To determine whether API-1 inhibits upstream activators of Akt, the effect of API-1 on PI3K and PDK1 activity was evaluated. In vitro PI3K kinase assays using immunoprecipitated p110α from HEK293 cells showed no inhibition by API-1, whereas wortmannin completely abrogated activity. Similarly, PDK1 kinase activity measured using SGK as a substrate remained unaffected by API-1, and phosphorylation of PDK1 at Ser241 was unchanged in treated cells. These results confirmed that API-1 does not interfere with PI3K or PDK1, establishing its specificity for Akt.

### EXAMPLE 3

The selectivity of API-1 for Akt over other AGC kinases was assessed using in vitro kinase assays for PKA, PKC, and SGK. API-1 showed no inhibition of PKA or SGK activity at concentrations up to 50 µM, whereas specific inhibitors of these kinases produced robust suppression. In cells, API-1 did not alter phosphorylation of PKC or PKA substrates, nor did it affect the activation status of ERK, JNK, p38, or STAT3, confirming its high degree of selectivity for the Akt pathway.

### EXAMPLE 4

API-1 was tested for its ability to inhibit constitutively active forms of Akt, including myristoylated Akt and the E17K mutant. API-1 effectively suppressed kinase activity and phosphorylation of myr-Akt1, myr-AKT2, Akt1-E40K, and AKT1-E17K in transfected cells. This demonstrated that API-1’s mechanism of action extends beyond inhibition of growth factor-induced activation and is effective against oncogenic mutants that bypass normal regulatory controls.

### EXAMPLE 5

API-1 was evaluated for its effects on cell growth and apoptosis in cancer cell lines with varying levels of Akt activation. Cells with elevated Akt activity, including OVCAR3, MCF7, and MDA-MB-468, exhibited significant growth inhibition and apoptosis induction upon API-1 treatment, whereas cells with low Akt activity such as OVCAR5 and MDA-MB-435s showed minimal responses. API-1 induced PARP cleavage and annexin V positivity in Akt-hyperactive cells at concentrations as low as 5 µM, confirming its selective pro-apoptotic activity.

### EXAMPLE 6

A nude mouse xenograft model was employed to evaluate the in vivo efficacy of API-1. Tumors derived from Akt-hyperactivated cells (OVCAR3 and PANC-1) showed a 70% and 50% reduction in volume, respectively, following daily intraperitoneal administration of API-1 at 10 mg/kg/day. In contrast, tumors from cells with low Akt activity (OVCAR5 and COLO357) exhibited no significant growth inhibition. Immunoblotting of excised tumors revealed a 70% reduction in phospho-Akt levels without changes in total Akt expression, confirming target engagement. No weight loss, behavioral changes, or hematologic abnormalities were observed, indicating a favorable safety profile.
