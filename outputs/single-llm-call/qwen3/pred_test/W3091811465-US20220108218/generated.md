## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to quantum-assisted machine learning systems and methods, particularly those leveraging tensor network architectures for efficient generative modeling on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) hardware. The invention encompasses apparatuses, algorithms, and compilation techniques designed to translate classically trained tensor network models—specifically matrix product states—into executable quantum circuits that operate under the constraints of real-world quantum processors. These constraints include limited qubit connectivity, restricted native gate sets, elevated error rates in multi-qubit operations, and the absence of mid-circuit measurement and reset capabilities in many current platforms. The disclosed methods enable the faithful representation of complex probability distributions over binary data vectors using minimal quantum resources, making them particularly suited for applications in unsupervised learning, data synthesis, and probabilistic inference on near-term quantum devices. The invention further provides a framework for optimizing the mapping of abstract tensor operations onto physical qubit topologies through gauge transformations, basis permutations, and greedy compilation heuristics that prioritize circuit depth and entangling gate count, thereby enhancing model fidelity in the presence of hardware noise.

## BACKGROUND

Current quantum computing platforms, while advancing rapidly in qubit count and control fidelity, remain fundamentally constrained by the noisy, intermediate-scale nature of their hardware. These NISQ devices lack the error correction and qubit coherence times necessary for large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computation, rendering traditional quantum algorithms—such as Shor’s or Grover’s—impractical for deployment. Consequently, the field has shifted toward identifying application domains where quantum advantage may be achieved despite these limitations. Machine learning has emerged as a promising candidate, particularly in generative modeling tasks where quantum circuits can encode high-dimensional probability distributions with exponential expressivity using relatively few qubits. However, existing approaches to quantum machine learning often rely on abstract circuit models that are compiled using generic quantum compilers, which produce deep, resource-intensive circuits unsuitable for NISQ hardware. These compilers typically treat quantum operations as black-box unitaries, ignoring the structural properties of the underlying model, such as sparsity, isometric constraints, or gauge freedom inherent in tensor network representations. As a result, they frequently generate circuits with excessive entangling gates, leading to error accumulation that overwhelms any potential quantum benefit. Furthermore, most prior methods do not account for the physical topology of qubit connectivity or the asymmetry in error rates between single- and two-qubit gates, nor do they exploit the inherent redundancies in the representation of ancillary degrees of freedom that are never measured. This results in suboptimal circuit implementations that fail to maximize fidelity under realistic noise conditions, limiting the practical utility of quantum-assisted machine learning on existing hardware.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The invention introduces a novel quantum-assisted machine learning framework that integrates tensor network-based generative modeling with hardware-aware quantum compilation to enable high-fidelity learning on NISQ devices. At its core, the method encodes classical binary data vectors into unentangled product states of qubits, then learns a matrix product state (MPS) model using classical optimization techniques grounded in the minimization of negative log-likelihood. The learned MPS is then transformed into a sequential preparation scheme involving a single physical qubit and a low-dimensional ancillary system, where each step is represented by an isometric operator. Crucially, the invention employs a diagonal gauge transformation that exploits the unitary freedom in the MPS bond basis to render these isometries as close to diagonal as possible, thereby minimizing the need for entangling operations during quantum compilation. A greedy compilation heuristic is then applied to map each isometry into a sequence of native single-qubit rotations and two-qubit entangling gates, using a cost function that penalizes deviation from the target isometry while favoring circuits with fewer entangling operations. The resulting quantum circuits are optimized for the specific topology and gate set of the target hardware, such as IBM’s superconducting qubit architectures, and are designed to operate without requiring mid-circuit measurement or reset. This end-to-end workflow enables the deployment of highly resource-efficient quantum models capable of generating samples from complex data distributions with fidelity exceeding that of generic compilation methods, even under significant hardware noise.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention provides a comprehensive system and method for quantum-assisted machine learning using tensor networks on noisy intermediate-scale quantum hardware. The process begins with the encoding of classical data vectors into quantum states via a binary embedding, wherein each element of a binary vector is mapped directly to the state of a qubit, forming a product state across a register of N qubits. This encoding ensures that any entanglement introduced during learning arises solely from correlations within the data ensemble, not from prior assumptions about individual data points. A matrix product state (MPS) is then trained as a generative model using a classical optimization procedure based on gradient descent of the negative log-likelihood, where the MPS tensors are updated in a sweeping manner using single- or two-site updates derived from singular value decomposition. The resulting MPS, representing a quantum Born machine, is converted into a sequential preparation scheme by transforming it into left-canonical form, yielding a sequence of isometric operators that act on a single physical qubit and a χ-dimensional ancillary system. To facilitate efficient quantum compilation, the isometries are subjected to a diagonal gauge transformation, wherein the ancilla basis is permuted using unitary transformations derived from the polar decomposition of the ancilla-only overlap matrix. This step ensures that the isometries are as diagonal as possible, reducing the number of quantum operations required to implement them. Following this, a greedy compilation heuristic is employed to map each isometry into a sequence of native quantum gates. The heuristic constructs a tree of possible gate sequences, beginning with single-qubit rotations and iteratively inserting entangling gates at allowed qubit pairs, while evaluating each candidate circuit using a cost function that measures the Frobenius norm difference between the target isometry and the compiled unitary, restricted to non-zero elements of the isometry. The optimization employs a priority queue strategy, limiting the number of candidate circuits at each level to prevent combinatorial explosion, and incorporates penalty terms for longer gate sequences that require multiple entangling operations. The resulting quantum circuits are further refined by collecting adjacent single-qubit rotations, removing negligible rotations below a threshold, and decomposing parameterized multi-qubit gates into native CNOT and single-qubit rotations. The compiled circuits are then mapped onto the physical qubit layout of the target hardware, with virtual qubits assigned to physical qubits based on connectivity constraints. The entire workflow is implemented on a computing apparatus comprising a processor configured to execute the classical optimization and compilation algorithms, memory for storing the classical data, trained MPS tensors, and compiled quantum circuits, and a quantum processing unit capable of executing the compiled circuits on a qubit topology with defined connectivity and gate set. The method is applicable to any binary data domain, including image features, genomic sequences, or financial time series, and is particularly effective when the data exhibits localized correlations that can be captured by low-bond-dimension MPS models. The invention further includes a noise-aware performance assessment protocol that employs measurement error mitigation, jackknife resampling, and Kullback-Leibler divergence metrics to quantify model fidelity under realistic hardware conditions, demonstrating superior performance over generic compilation methods even when operating with limited qubit resources and high gate error rates.
