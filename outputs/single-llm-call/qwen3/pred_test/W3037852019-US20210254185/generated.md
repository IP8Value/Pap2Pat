# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to method for detecting undifferentiated cells

The present invention relates to a method for detecting undifferentiated cells, particularly residual pluripotent stem cells, within populations of differentiated cells derived from induced pluripotent stem cells. The invention provides a highly sensitive and broadly applicable diagnostic approach for identifying trace contamination of undifferentiated cells in differentiated cell products intended for regenerative medicine, cell transplantation, and tissue engineering applications. This method is specifically designed to overcome the limitations of existing marker systems that exhibit cross-reactivity or insufficient sensitivity across multiple germ layer lineages. By leveraging a defined set of molecular markers expressed exclusively in the undifferentiated state and rapidly silenced upon lineage commitment, the invention enables reliable, quantitative, and reproducible detection of undifferentiated cells at frequencies as low as 0.005%, even in complex, heterogeneous cell populations derived from endodermal, mesodermal, and ectodermal lineages. The method is applicable across diverse differentiation protocols and cell types, including hepatocytes, pancreatic islet-like cells, endothelial cells, mesenchymal stromal cells, neural stem cells, and neuronal derivatives, thereby offering a universal standard for safety assessment in cell-based therapies.

## BACKGROUND ART

- introduce conventional methods
- limitations of conventional methods

Prior methods for detecting residual undifferentiated cells in differentiated cell populations have relied primarily on the expression of well-established pluripotency-associated genes such as OCT4, NANOG, SOX2, and LIN28A, as well as cell surface markers like TRA-1-60 and SSEA-4. These approaches have been implemented through immunocytochemistry, flow cytometry, quantitative PCR, and colony-forming assays following re-cultivation under pluripotency-maintaining conditions. While these techniques have demonstrated utility in specific contexts, they suffer from critical limitations that compromise their reliability in clinical and translational settings. Many of these markers are not exclusively restricted to the undifferentiated state; for instance, LIN28A is transiently expressed during early endodermal and neural differentiation, leading to false-positive signals that obscure the presence of actual contaminating pluripotent cells. Similarly, SOX2 and NANOG, though considered canonical pluripotency factors, exhibit residual expression in certain progenitor populations, resulting in elevated detection thresholds that fail to identify low-level contamination. Furthermore, conventional methods often require extensive culture periods—up to seven days or more—to allow undifferentiated cells to form visible colonies, rendering them impractical for rapid quality control in manufacturing environments. Additionally, the sensitivity of these assays is frequently insufficient to detect contamination levels below 0.1%, a threshold deemed unsafe for clinical transplantation due to the risk of teratoma formation. The lack of a universally applicable marker system across all three germ layers has necessitated the development of lineage-specific protocols, increasing complexity, cost, and variability in safety validation across different therapeutic cell products.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

### Problem for Solution by the Invention

- state problem to be solved

A critical and unresolved challenge in the clinical translation of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived therapies is the inability to reliably detect and quantify residual undifferentiated cells at clinically relevant thresholds in heterogeneous differentiated cell populations. Existing detection methods are plagued by insufficient sensitivity, lack of specificity across germ layer lineages, and high false-positive rates due to the aberrant expression of traditional pluripotency markers during differentiation. This results in an unacceptable risk of teratoma formation upon transplantation and impedes the standardization of safety assessments required for regulatory approval. There exists a pressing need for a universal, highly sensitive, and lineage-independent method capable of identifying trace contamination of undifferentiated cells in any cell product derived from pluripotent stem cells, regardless of the differentiation protocol or target tissue origin.

The present invention solves the aforementioned problem by identifying and validating a novel panel of marker genes—ESRG, SFRP2, VSNL1, THY1, SPP1, USP44, and CNMD—that are highly and specifically expressed in undifferentiated pluripotent stem cells and rapidly silenced upon initiation of lineage commitment across all three germ layers. These genes meet the criteria of an ideal undifferentiation marker: they exhibit minimal to no expression in differentiated progeny, high baseline expression in pluripotent cells, and a steep dynamic range between undifferentiated and differentiated states. Unlike conventional markers such as LIN28A, SOX2, or NANOG, which retain residual expression in developing endodermal, mesodermal, or ectodermal lineages, the identified genes are robustly downregulated during differentiation, enabling detection of residual undifferentiated cells at concentrations as low as 0.005%. The invention provides a method for detecting undifferentiated cells by measuring the expression level of one or more of these marker genes using quantitative nucleic acid amplification, single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization, or protein detection assays. The method further enables the selection of highly safe undifferentiated cell clones by screening for clones exhibiting complete and irreversible silencing of these markers upon differentiation induction, thereby ensuring the absence of residual pluripotent potential. The invention also encompasses a method for detecting undifferentiated cells in tissue sections or organoids by spatially resolving marker gene expression using in situ hybridization or immunohistochemistry, allowing for direct visualization of contaminating cells within complex 3D structures. The marker genes serve as definitive molecular indicators of pluripotency status and are employed in diagnostic kits comprising primers, probes, antibodies, and reference controls for standardized detection. The kit includes reagents for RNA extraction, reverse transcription, qPCR amplification with gene-specific primers and UPL probes, branched DNA probes for single-molecule FISH, and monoclonal antibodies against the encoded proteins, all accompanied by validated positive and negative controls derived from well-characterized pluripotent and differentiated cell lines.

### Effect of the Invention

- describe effect of present invention

The effect of the present invention is the provision of a universal, highly sensitive, and reproducible method for detecting residual undifferentiated cells in any human pluripotent stem cell-derived product, irrespective of differentiation lineage or protocol. This significantly enhances the safety profile of cell therapies by enabling the detection of contamination at levels previously undetectable, thereby reducing the risk of teratoma formation to negligible levels. The method eliminates the need for lineage-specific validation protocols, streamlining quality control across diverse therapeutic applications. The use of the identified marker genes allows for rapid, high-throughput screening compatible with manufacturing timelines, and facilitates the selection of clonal populations with complete epigenetic silencing of pluripotency pathways. The invention further enables the direct visualization of contaminating cells in tissue contexts, providing unprecedented spatial resolution for safety assessment in organoid and engineered tissue applications. The accompanying diagnostic kit ensures standardization, regulatory compliance, and scalability for clinical implementation, representing a transformative advancement in the field of regenerative medicine.

The method for detecting undifferentiated cells involves the quantification of mRNA or protein expression levels of ESRG, SFRP2, VSNL1, THY1, SPP1, USP44, and CNMD in a sample of differentiated cells suspected of containing residual pluripotent stem cells. The undifferentiated cell targets include human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) and embryonic stem cells (ESCs) maintained under standard pluripotency conditions. The differentiated cell population encompasses any cell type generated through directed differentiation protocols, including endodermal derivatives such as hepatic endoderm, immature hepatocytes, and pancreatic progenitors; mesodermal derivatives such as septum transversum mesenchyme, mesenchymal stromal cells, vascular endothelial cells, and cardiomyocytes; and ectodermal derivatives such as neural stem cells, neural crest cells, and mature neurons. In a representative example, undifferentiated iPSC contamination in hepatic endoderm cells is detected by extracting total RNA, performing reverse transcription, and conducting quantitative PCR using primers specific for ESRG and CNMD; a signal above a predefined threshold indicates contamination. Similarly, in mesenchymal cells derived from iPSCs, expression of SFRP2 and THY1 is measured to confirm absence of residual pluripotent cells, with no detectable signal confirming purity. In vascular endothelial cells, VSNL1 and USP44 expression is assessed via digital droplet PCR to achieve single-cell sensitivity. In neural stem cells and neural crest cells, SPP1 and CNMD expression is evaluated using single-molecule FISH, revealing discrete positive foci corresponding to contaminating undifferentiated cells. The measurement of marker gene expression is performed using quantitative reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), digital droplet PCR (ddPCR), single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization (smFISH), or immunoblotting with specific antibodies. Detection of undifferentiated cells is confirmed when expression levels exceed the background threshold established using fully differentiated control samples. Selection of highly safe undifferentiated cell clones is achieved by subjecting candidate clones to differentiation induction and measuring the silencing kinetics of the marker genes; clones exhibiting complete and irreversible downregulation within 72 hours are selected for expansion. The detection of undifferentiated cells in tissue sections is performed by embedding tissue in paraffin or cryo-sectioning, followed by in situ hybridization with labeled probes targeting ESRG or CNMD mRNA, and visualization via fluorescence microscopy. The marker genes serve as definitive indicators of pluripotency status and are used in diagnostic kits comprising gene-specific primers, UPL probes, anti-ESRG, anti-SFRP2, anti-VSNL1, anti-THY1, anti-SPP1, anti-USP44, and anti-CNMD monoclonal antibodies, RNA extraction reagents, reverse transcriptase, PCR master mix, positive controls (undifferentiated iPSCs), negative controls (fully differentiated hepatocytes or neurons), and written instructions detailing sample preparation, amplification cycles, threshold determination, and interpretation criteria. Reagents for measuring promoter activity include luciferase or GFP reporter vectors driven by the promoters of ESRG, CNMD, or SFRP2, transfected into cells to monitor silencing dynamics. All reagents are labeled with fluorophores, biotin, or enzymatic tags for enhanced detection sensitivity. Detection of target molecules employs primary antibodies raised against the marker proteins, followed by secondary antibodies conjugated to horseradish peroxidase or fluorescent dyes. Reporter proteins such as GFP, mCherry, and luciferase are used in stable reporter cell lines to enable real-time monitoring of pluripotency exit. Vectors for measuring promoter activity include lentiviral or episomal constructs containing the 5’ regulatory regions of the marker genes fused to reporter genes. Additional components of the kit include RNA stabilization buffers, DNase treatment reagents, calibration standards, and a digital reader for qPCR or ddPCR analysis. The kit is accompanied by detailed written instructions, including protocols for sample handling, data normalization using housekeeping genes, and interpretation of results based on predefined cutoff values derived from spike-in experiments. Control samples include known concentrations of undifferentiated cells spiked into differentiated populations to establish detection limits and dynamic range. Instruments included in the kit are microcentrifuges, thermal cyclers, fluorescence microscopes, flow cytometers, and droplet digital PCR systems. The best mode for carrying out the invention is achieved through the integrated use of these markers, reagents, and instruments in a standardized workflow that ensures reproducibility, sensitivity, and regulatory compliance across all applications.

The invention is further illustrated through a series of examples demonstrating its efficacy across multiple cell lineages. In Example 1, human iPSC lines were differentiated into hepatic endoderm, mesenchymal, and neural lineages using established protocols. Residual undifferentiated cells were quantified by re-seeding and colony formation assays, and gene expression was analyzed by qPCR. ESRG and CNMD showed the strongest correlation with colony counts, while LIN28A exhibited persistent expression in hepatic progenitors, confirming its unsuitability. Spike-in experiments revealed detection limits of 0.005% for ESRG and 0.025% for CNMD, far surpassing the sensitivity of SOX2 or NANOG. In Example 2, reporter constructs containing the ESRG promoter fused to GFP were introduced into iPSCs; upon differentiation, GFP fluorescence was extinguished within 48 hours, whereas control constructs driven by OCT4 retained signal. In Example 3, clonal iPSC lines were screened for complete silencing of the marker genes following differentiation; clones exhibiting no detectable expression after 72 hours were selected for therapeutic use, demonstrating elimination of teratoma risk in vivo. In Example 4, cells differentiated using the STEMdiff Trilineage Kit were analyzed, and the marker panel successfully detected residual undifferentiated cells in all three germ layers, validating its universal applicability. Statistical analysis confirmed significant differences between undifferentiated and differentiated samples (p < 0.001), and no false positives were observed in fully differentiated controls. The results demonstrate that the marker panel enables reliable, sensitive, and lineage-independent detection of residual pluripotent cells, fulfilling the critical need for safety assurance in regenerative medicine.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

- application of invention

The invention has substantial industrial applicability in the fields of regenerative medicine, cell therapy manufacturing, pharmaceutical safety testing, and personalized medicine. It is directly applicable in biomanufacturing facilities producing iPSC-derived hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, pancreatic beta cells, neural progenitors, and other therapeutic cell types for clinical transplantation. The method and accompanying kit enable standardized, high-throughput quality control that meets regulatory requirements set forth by the FDA, EMA, and PMDA. The invention facilitates the development of off-the-shelf cell products with guaranteed safety profiles, reducing the risk of clinical failure due to tumorigenic contamination. It is also applicable in academic and industrial research laboratories for validating differentiation protocols, screening clonal lines, and monitoring epigenetic stability during long-term culture. The kit format allows for deployment in GMP-compliant environments, and the method is compatible with automation platforms for integration into closed-system manufacturing workflows. Its broad applicability across all germ layers renders it indispensable for the commercialization of next-generation cell therapies, organoids, and tissue-engineered constructs, positioning it as a foundational tool for the safe and scalable translation of pluripotent stem cell technologies into clinical practice.
