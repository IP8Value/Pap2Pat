# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to systems and methods for treating cholangiocarcinoma through the combined application of cold atmospheric plasma and a modified FOLFIRINOX chemotherapeutic regimen. This invention specifically addresses the clinical challenge of chemoresistance and high recurrence rates in biliary tract malignancies by introducing a novel, non-thermal, localized therapeutic approach that enhances the cytotoxic efficacy of standard chemotherapy while minimizing systemic toxicity. The method integrates physical plasma-mediated cellular disruption with pharmacological intervention to achieve synergistic tumor cell elimination, particularly in settings where surgical resection is feasible but incomplete due to microscopic residual disease. The invention encompasses both the therapeutic protocol and the specialized medical device used to deliver cold atmospheric plasma directly to surgical margins or tumor beds, ensuring precise, controlled, and reproducible application without thermal damage to adjacent healthy tissue.

### Brief Description of the Related Art

Cholangiocarcinoma is a highly aggressive malignancy originating from the epithelial cells of the biliary tree, with a dismal prognosis characterized by a five-year survival rate below twenty percent. Most patients present at advanced stages, rendering curative surgical resection or liver transplantation viable only in a small subset of cases. Even following complete resection, recurrence rates remain unacceptably high due to the presence of microscopic residual disease and the intrinsic chemoresistance of cholangiocarcinoma cells. Standard first-line chemotherapies such as gemcitabine and fluorouracil have demonstrated limited efficacy, and while the FOLFIRINOX regimen—comprising fluorouracil, leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin—has shown promise in pancreatic cancer, its application in cholangiocarcinoma remains investigational. Clinical trials have revealed that the full-dose FOLFIRINOX protocol is often poorly tolerated, with significant hematologic toxicities including neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, and febrile neutropenia, leading to dose reductions, treatment delays, or discontinuation. Modified versions of FOLFIRINOX, including FOLFOX-4, FOLFOX-5, and FOLFOX-7, have been explored in other gastrointestinal cancers to mitigate toxicity, but none have been validated specifically for cholangiocarcinoma with consistent clinical benefit. The need for a complementary, non-systemic adjuvant therapy that can enhance tumor cell kill without exacerbating systemic side effects remains unmet. Cold atmospheric plasma, a partially ionized gas generated at near-ambient temperatures, has emerged as a promising physical modality in oncology due to its ability to selectively induce apoptosis and DNA damage in malignant cells while sparing normal tissue. Preclinical studies have demonstrated that cold atmospheric plasma can effectively reduce viability in cholangiocarcinoma cell lines without harming adjacent hepatocytes, suggesting a favorable therapeutic window. Indirect application of cold atmospheric plasma via gas flow has been shown to penetrate cell culture media and elicit cytotoxic effects, and recent device innovations have enabled direct, controlled delivery to surgical sites. Furthermore, preliminary evidence indicates that cold atmospheric plasma may potentiate the effects of chemotherapeutic agents through mechanisms involving oxidative stress, membrane permeabilization, and disruption of cellular repair pathways. However, no prior study has investigated the combination of cold atmospheric plasma with the FOLFIRINOX regimen in cholangiocarcinoma, nor has any system been developed to integrate this combination into a clinically translatable surgical protocol.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

Cholangiocarcinoma continues to present formidable therapeutic challenges due to its late diagnosis, aggressive biology, and resistance to conventional chemotherapy. The FOLFIRINOX regimen, while potent, is limited by its systemic toxicity, which restricts its utility in patients with compromised performance status or those requiring adjuvant therapy after resection. The present invention introduces a novel, synergistic treatment paradigm that combines a low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen with localized application of cold atmospheric plasma to enhance tumor cell eradication while reducing overall chemotherapeutic burden. Cold atmospheric plasma, generated at temperatures below thirty-five degrees Celsius, is applied directly to the surgical margins immediately following tumor resection, targeting residual microscopic disease without damaging surrounding healthy tissue. The invention demonstrates that the combination of cold atmospheric plasma and FOLFIRINOX produces a synergistic reduction in cholangiocarcinoma cell viability that exceeds the sum of either treatment alone, enabling the use of significantly lower drug doses while maintaining or improving therapeutic efficacy. Preferred embodiments of the invention include a precisely calibrated FOLFIRINOX regimen consisting of 6.7 micromolar fluorouracil, 1.7 micromolar leucovorin, 0.6 micromolar irinotecan, and 0.5 micromolar oxaliplatin, administered prior to or concurrently with cold atmospheric plasma exposure. The invention further encompasses a specialized cold atmospheric plasma generator designed for intraoperative use, featuring a compact, sterile, handheld applicator connected to a controlled gas and power delivery system capable of producing a stable, non-thermal plasma jet with consistent energy output. This system enables reproducible, time-controlled plasma delivery to resection beds, ensuring uniform treatment of tumor margins and minimizing operator variability. The invention also includes methods for determining optimal treatment duration, drug sequencing, and plasma parameters to maximize synergistic cell death while preserving tissue integrity. This approach represents a paradigm shift in the management of cholangiocarcinoma, offering a targeted, minimally invasive, and highly effective adjuvant strategy that can be readily integrated into existing surgical workflows.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

Cholangiocarcinomas are malignant tumors of the biliary epithelium that exhibit high rates of recurrence following surgical resection due to the persistence of microscopic disease at resection margins. Cold atmospheric plasma, a non-equilibrium ionized gas generated at atmospheric pressure and temperatures below thirty-five degrees Celsius, has been shown to selectively induce apoptosis and oxidative stress in cancer cells while leaving normal cells largely unaffected. The present invention leverages this selective cytotoxicity by applying cold atmospheric plasma directly to the surgical bed immediately after tumor resection, thereby eliminating residual malignant cells that may not be visible to the naked eye. The FOLFIRINOX regimen, consisting of fluorouracil, leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin, is a multi-agent chemotherapeutic protocol that has demonstrated efficacy in pancreatic cancer but is limited in cholangiocarcinoma by its systemic toxicity. The invention defines a low-dosage FOLFIRINOX regimen comprising 6.7 micromolar fluorouracil, 1.7 micromolar leucovorin, 0.6 micromolar irinotecan, and 0.5 micromolar oxaliplatin, which, when administered prior to cold atmospheric plasma exposure, produces a synergistic reduction in cell viability exceeding that of either modality alone. The method of treatment involves the intravenous or intra-arterial administration of the low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen within twenty-four hours prior to surgical resection, followed by immediate application of cold atmospheric plasma to the resection margins using a specialized generator and applicator. Surgical removal of the primary tumor is performed using standard oncologic principles, with careful attention to achieving negative margins. Following resection, the cold atmospheric plasma applicator is positioned at a fixed distance of one point five centimeters above the resection bed, and a helium carrier gas is delivered at a constant flow rate of three liters per minute while an electrical discharge of 120p (28.7 watts) is applied for durations ranging from one to five minutes. The generator is housed in a sterile, compact unit with a touchscreen interface for setting treatment parameters, including plasma duration, gas flow, and power output. The housing contains a central processing unit, memory module, and power supply capable of maintaining stable output over extended periods. A gas control module regulates helium flow with precision, while a power module delivers high-frequency electrical energy to the plasma applicator, which is connected via a sealed, disposable tubing system to prevent contamination. Alternative arrangements for delivery of the carrier gas and electrical energy include integrated catheter-based systems for endoscopic or laparoscopic applications, or modular attachments for use in robotic-assisted surgical platforms. The cold atmospheric plasma produced by this system remains below thirty-five degrees Celsius throughout application, ensuring no thermal injury to adjacent bile ducts, vasculature, or parenchymal tissue. The plasma induces intracellular accumulation of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, disrupts mitochondrial membrane potential, and enhances the permeability of cancer cell membranes, thereby potentiating the intracellular uptake and efficacy of FOLFIRINOX components. This dual mechanism results in enhanced DNA damage, cell cycle arrest, and apoptotic cell death, as confirmed by confocal microscopy, Ki-67 staining, and live-cell imaging systems. The generator is designed for single-use disposables, including the plasma applicator tip and gas tubing, to ensure sterility and compliance with surgical aseptic protocols.

### Experiments

Cell culture studies were conducted using the human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma cell line KKU-055, maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with fetal bovine serum and antibiotics. Cells were seeded in twelve-well plates and allowed to adhere for twenty-four hours prior to treatment. FOLFIRINOX drugs were diluted in dimethyl sulfoxide and combined in a stock solution at the clinical dose ratio, then serially diluted in culture medium to achieve concentrations corresponding to 0.8 to 53.8 micromolar fluorouracil. Cold atmospheric plasma was generated using a high-frequency electrosurgical generator paired with a helium-based plasma scalpel, operated at 120p power and a helium flow rate of three liters per minute. Treatment durations ranged from one to seven minutes, with the plasma tip held at a constant distance of one point five centimeters above the cell monolayer. After plasma exposure, cells were returned to the incubator for forty-eight hours before viability assessment via MTT assay. For combination treatments, cells were exposed to FOLFIRINOX for twenty-four hours prior to plasma application. Cell viability was quantified spectrophotometrically at five hundred seventy nanometers, and statistical significance was determined using two-way analysis of variance followed by Bonferroni-corrected post hoc tests. Confocal microscopy was performed using Ki-67 and DAPI staining to assess proliferation, while live-cell imaging was conducted using the IncuCyte Live-Cell Analysis System with fluorescent cell cycle reporters to track phase distribution over seventy-two hours. All experiments were repeated a minimum of three times with duplicate wells per condition.

### Results

FOLFIRINOX treatment alone reduced cell viability in a dose-dependent manner, with statistically significant reductions observed at concentrations of 3.4 micromolar fluorouracil and above. Cold atmospheric plasma alone reduced viability to as low as three percent after seven minutes of exposure. When combined, the low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen of 6.7 micromolar fluorouracil with five minutes of plasma exposure reduced viability to one percent, a level significantly lower than either treatment alone. Two-way ANOVA confirmed a statistically significant interaction between FOLFIRINOX and plasma treatments, indicating synergy rather than mere additivity. Ki-67 staining revealed a marked reduction in proliferating cells following combination treatment, with a seventy percent decrease in Ki-67-positive nuclei compared to controls. Live-cell imaging demonstrated a cessation of cell confluence and rapid accumulation of cellular debris within twenty-four hours of combination treatment. Cell cycle analysis showed a significant increase in cells arrested in G1 and sub-G1 phases, consistent with apoptosis induction. Morphological changes observed under phase contrast microscopy included cell shrinkage, membrane blebbing, and detachment, all hallmarks of programmed cell death. Statistical analysis confirmed that the combination treatment was significantly more effective than either modality alone at all timepoints, with p-values below zero point zero zero five for the most effective dose combinations.

### Discussion

The findings presented herein establish the first evidence of synergistic interaction between cold atmospheric plasma and the FOLFIRINOX regimen in cholangiocarcinoma. While FOLFIRINOX has demonstrated clinical potential, its utility has been constrained by toxicity, and cold atmospheric plasma has remained largely investigational. This invention bridges these two modalities by demonstrating that a subtherapeutic dose of FOLFIRINOX, when combined with localized plasma exposure, achieves near-complete tumor cell eradication. The mechanism likely involves plasma-induced membrane disruption enhancing intracellular drug uptake, coupled with oxidative stress amplifying DNA damage and impairing repair mechanisms. The absence of thermal damage to normal tissue and the ability to target microscopic disease at surgical margins make this approach uniquely suited for intraoperative adjuvant therapy. The results support the clinical translation of this combination, particularly in patients who cannot tolerate full-dose chemotherapy or in whom recurrence risk is high. Future studies should explore in vivo models and clinical trials to validate these findings in human subjects.

### Conclusion

The combination of cold atmospheric plasma and a low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen represents a transformative approach to the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma. This invention demonstrates that synergistic tumor cell kill can be achieved with significantly reduced chemotherapeutic exposure, thereby minimizing systemic toxicity while maximizing local efficacy. The integration of this method into surgical workflows offers a promising strategy to reduce recurrence rates and improve survival outcomes. Future research should focus on optimizing delivery parameters, validating safety in animal models, and initiating clinical trials to establish this as a standard of care for resectable cholangiocarcinoma.
