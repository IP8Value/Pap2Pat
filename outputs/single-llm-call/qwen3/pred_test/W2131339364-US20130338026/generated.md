# DESCRIPTION

- introduce endometrial cancer diagnosis method  
The present invention introduces a novel diagnostic method for endometrial carcinoma that leverages the molecular signatures of chromosomal amplifications and gene expression profiles to accurately identify tumors with aggressive biological behavior. This method enables the differentiation of endometrial carcinomas into distinct molecular subtypes based on the presence or absence of specific genomic alterations, particularly amplifications at chromosomal regions 3q26.32 and 12p12.1, and the concomitant overexpression of the STMN1 gene or its encoded protein. Unlike conventional histopathological assessments that rely solely on morphological features such as grade and stage, this diagnostic approach integrates molecular data to reveal underlying biological drivers of tumor aggressiveness. The method is applicable to formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue samples, fresh frozen biopsies, and liquid biopsy-derived nucleic acids, thereby facilitating integration into routine clinical workflows. By identifying tumors with elevated STMN1 expression in the context of 3q26.32 amplification, the invention provides a robust framework for distinguishing indolent from high-risk endometrial cancers at the time of initial diagnosis, enabling earlier intervention and personalized therapeutic planning. The diagnostic utility of this method has been validated across multiple independent patient cohorts, demonstrating consistent reproducibility and superior predictive power compared to existing clinical parameters.

- outline various aspects of the invention  
The invention encompasses a comprehensive suite of diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic stratification tools grounded in the molecular characterization of endometrial carcinoma. Central to the invention is the identification of STMN1 as a critical biomarker whose expression level correlates strongly with chromosomal instability, PI3K pathway activation, and poor clinical outcomes. The invention further defines a molecular signature composed of co-amplified regions on 3q26.32 and 12p12.1, which together serve as a genomic fingerprint for aggressive endometrial tumors. This signature is not merely correlative but functionally linked to tumor progression, as demonstrated by its association with increased recurrence rates, metastatic potential, and resistance to conventional therapies. The invention also integrates the assessment of PI3K pathway activity through a quantitative scoring system derived from the expression levels of key downstream effectors, thereby enabling a systems-level evaluation of oncogenic signaling. Additionally, the invention provides for the simultaneous detection of STMN1 expression alongside genomic alterations, creating a multi-dimensional diagnostic platform that enhances sensitivity and specificity. The method is adaptable to various analytical platforms including microarray hybridization, next-generation sequencing, quantitative PCR, and immunohistochemistry, ensuring broad accessibility across clinical laboratories. The invention further extends to the development of standardized kits for in vitro diagnostic use, enabling consistent and scalable implementation in both academic and community healthcare settings.

## PRIOR ART

- introduce endometrial cancer statistics  
Endometrial carcinoma represents the most prevalent gynecologic malignancy in developed nations, with an estimated lifetime risk of approximately 2 to 3 percent among women. The majority of cases are diagnosed at an early stage, often presenting with abnormal uterine bleeding, and are treated with surgical resection. Despite high initial cure rates for localized disease, a significant subset of patients—approximately 15 to 20 percent—experience disease recurrence, frequently in the form of distant metastases that are refractory to conventional chemotherapy and radiation therapy. The prognosis for patients with recurrent or metastatic endometrial cancer remains dismal, with median survival times often less than one year following disease progression. The incidence of endometrial cancer continues to rise, paralleling increases in obesity and metabolic syndrome, further underscoring the urgent need for improved diagnostic and therapeutic strategies.

- describe limitations of current treatments  
Current therapeutic paradigms for endometrial carcinoma are largely based on histopathological classification, including tumor grade, depth of myometrial invasion, and lymphovascular space involvement. These parameters, while useful, exhibit substantial inter-observer variability and lack the molecular precision required to predict individual patient outcomes or response to therapy. Standard chemotherapy regimens, such as carboplatin-paclitaxel combinations, demonstrate limited efficacy in advanced or recurrent disease, with response rates often below 30 percent and durable remissions being exceedingly rare. Furthermore, there are no validated biomarkers to guide the selection of patients most likely to benefit from specific cytotoxic agents or targeted therapies. As a result, many patients are subjected to overtreatment with associated toxicities, while others with aggressive disease are undertreated due to the absence of reliable prognostic indicators.

- summarize molecular basis of type I and II cancers  
Endometrial carcinomas are broadly categorized into two molecular subtypes: Type I, which is estrogen-driven, endometrioid in histology, and typically characterized by PTEN inactivation, microsatellite instability, and low-grade histology; and Type II, which includes serous and clear cell carcinomas, is often TP53-mutated, exhibits chromosomal instability, and is associated with poor prognosis. While Type I tumors generally respond well to hormonal and surgical interventions, Type II tumors are inherently more aggressive and resistant to conventional therapies. However, recent genomic analyses have revealed a spectrum of molecular alterations that transcend these traditional categories, with a subset of high-grade endometrioid tumors exhibiting molecular features akin to Type II cancers, including frequent amplifications of 3q26.32 and overexpression of STMN1. This molecular heterogeneity challenges the utility of histology alone and necessitates a more nuanced classification system grounded in functional genomic data.

- discuss prognostic value of molecular alterations  
Molecular alterations such as mutations in PTEN, PIK3CA, and KRAS, as well as copy number variations, have been increasingly recognized as critical determinants of clinical behavior in endometrial carcinoma. While PTEN loss is common in low-grade tumors and associated with favorable outcomes, amplifications of oncogenic loci such as 3q26.32 and 12p12.1 are strongly linked to aggressive disease, metastasis, and reduced survival. The integration of these molecular markers into prognostic models has been shown to significantly improve risk stratification beyond traditional clinical parameters. However, no single alteration has yet been widely adopted in clinical practice due to inconsistencies in detection methods, lack of standardized thresholds, and insufficient validation across diverse populations.

- introduce gene expression signature of PTEN tumor suppressant pathway activity  
The transcriptional activity of the PTEN tumor suppressor pathway has been shown to correlate with a distinct gene expression signature that reflects downstream inhibition of the PI3K-AKT-mTOR axis. Tumors with intact PTEN function exhibit downregulation of genes involved in cell proliferation, survival, and metabolic reprogramming, whereas loss of PTEN leads to a transcriptional program characterized by upregulation of ribosomal biogenesis, protein synthesis, and glycolytic enzymes. This signature has been used to infer PTEN pathway activity even in the absence of direct mutational or deletion analysis, providing a functional readout of pathway status. However, the utility of this signature in predicting response to targeted therapies has not been fully explored, particularly in the context of concurrent alterations such as STMN1 overexpression.

- hypothesize underlying genetic alterations in aggressive tumors  
It is hypothesized that the most aggressive forms of endometrial carcinoma arise from a convergence of genomic amplifications, particularly at 3q26.32, and the consequent dysregulation of key oncogenic drivers such as STMN1 and PIK3CA. These alterations are not isolated events but are part of a coordinated molecular program that promotes chromosomal instability, evasion of apoptosis, and enhanced motility. The co-occurrence of 3q26.32 amplification with elevated PI3K pathway activity and STMN1 overexpression defines a molecular phenotype that is highly predictive of early recurrence and resistance to taxane-based chemotherapy. This phenotype is frequently observed in tumors that are histologically classified as high-grade endometrioid but behave clinically like serous carcinomas, suggesting that molecular profiling is essential to accurately define tumor biology and guide therapeutic decisions.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

- diagnose endometrial carcinoma using chromosomal regions and gene expression  
The present invention provides a method for diagnosing endometrial carcinoma by detecting amplifications in chromosomal regions 3q26.32 and 12p12.1 in conjunction with elevated expression levels of the STMN1 gene or its encoded protein. This diagnostic method enables the identification of tumors with a high-risk molecular profile that are indistinguishable from low-grade tumors by conventional histopathology alone. The combination of genomic and transcriptomic data improves diagnostic accuracy, reduces misclassification, and facilitates the early recognition of aggressive disease.

- evaluate probability of survival using chromosomal regions and gene expression  
The invention further enables the evaluation of a patient’s probability of survival by integrating the presence of 3q26.32 amplification, 12p12.1 amplification, and STMN1 expression levels into a predictive algorithm. This algorithm generates a risk score that correlates strongly with recurrence-free survival and overall survival, providing clinicians with a quantitative tool to counsel patients regarding prognosis and the necessity for adjuvant therapy.

- stratify therapy using STMN1 gene or protein expression  
Therapeutic decisions for endometrial carcinoma are stratified based on the expression level of STMN1, with high expression indicating a higher likelihood of resistance to taxane-based regimens and a greater potential benefit from PI3K pathway inhibitors. Patients with elevated STMN1 expression are directed toward targeted therapies such as mTOR or Akt inhibitors, while those with low expression may be candidates for standard chemotherapy.

- monitor therapy efficacy using STMN1 gene or protein expression  
The invention provides a method for monitoring the efficacy of therapeutic interventions by serially measuring STMN1 expression in tumor tissue or circulating nucleic acids. A reduction in STMN1 expression following treatment is indicative of a favorable response, whereas persistent or increasing expression suggests therapeutic resistance and the need for regimen modification.

- monitor progression of cancer using STMN1 gene or protein expression  
Progression of endometrial carcinoma can be tracked over time through repeated assessment of STMN1 expression levels, allowing for the early detection of disease recurrence or metastatic spread prior to clinical or radiographic manifestation. This enables timely intervention and improves the likelihood of successful salvage therapy.

- provide kit for diagnosis, stratification, and monitoring  
The invention includes a diagnostic kit comprising reagents for the detection of chromosomal amplifications at 3q26.32 and 12p12.1, as well as probes or antibodies for quantifying STMN1 gene or protein expression. The kit further includes standardized protocols, reference controls, and software for data analysis, enabling consistent implementation across clinical laboratories.

- predict response to taxanes based on STMN1 gene or protein expression  
High expression of STMN1 is predictive of poor response to taxane-based chemotherapy, whereas low expression is associated with increased sensitivity. This predictive capability allows for the avoidance of ineffective and toxic treatments in patients unlikely to benefit, thereby optimizing therapeutic outcomes and reducing unnecessary side effects.

- stratify therapy using PI3K inhibitors, Akt inhibitors, mTOR inhibitors, or PTEN activators  
Patients exhibiting high STMN1 expression in the context of PI3K pathway activation are stratified for treatment with PI3K, Akt, or mTOR inhibitors, or agents that restore PTEN function. This targeted approach improves therapeutic precision and enhances the likelihood of durable clinical responses.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

- relate to diagnosis or differentiation of endometrial carcinoma  
The invention is directed to a method for diagnosing or differentiating endometrial carcinoma by analyzing genomic and transcriptomic alterations in tumor tissue. This method distinguishes between indolent and aggressive subtypes based on the presence of specific chromosomal amplifications and the expression level of STMN1, thereby overcoming the limitations of histological classification.

- determine alterations of chromosomal regions 3q26.32 and 12p12.1  
Alterations in chromosomal regions 3q26.32 and 12p12.1 are determined using fluorescence in situ hybridization, array comparative genomic hybridization, or next-generation sequencing techniques. Amplification of these regions is defined as a copy number gain exceeding a threshold of 1.5-fold relative to a diploid reference.

- determine gene expression profile of specific genes  
The gene expression profile of STMN1, PIK3CA, PTEN, and other genes within the PI3K pathway is determined using RNA sequencing, quantitative real-time PCR, or microarray hybridization. Expression levels are normalized to housekeeping genes and compared against established reference ranges.

- identify two clusters of tumors with different clinical and histopathologic characteristics  
Tumors are classified into two distinct molecular clusters based on hierarchical clustering of gene expression and copy number data. Cluster 1 is characterized by low STMN1 expression, intact PTEN, and favorable clinical outcomes, while Cluster 2 exhibits high STMN1 expression, PI3K pathway activation, and aggressive clinical behavior.

- associate cluster 2 with more aggressive tumors and poorer recurrence-free survival  
Cluster 2 tumors are consistently associated with higher histological grade, deeper myometrial invasion, lymph node metastasis, and significantly shorter recurrence-free survival compared to Cluster 1 tumors. This association is independent of traditional clinical variables.

- determine PI3K activity in patients with aggressive endometrial carcinoma  
PI3K activity is quantified using a composite score derived from the expression levels of downstream target genes, including AKT1, mTOR, and S6K1. High PI3K activity is strongly correlated with the presence of 3q26.32 amplification and STMN1 overexpression.

- determine expression status of STMN1 gene or protein  
The expression status of STMN1 is assessed at both the mRNA and protein levels using quantitative RT-PCR and immunohistochemistry, respectively. Protein expression is scored using a semi-quantitative system based on staining intensity and percentage of positive tumor cells.

- diagnose and differentiate endometrial carcinoma based on STMN1 expression  
Endometrial carcinoma is diagnosed and differentiated into molecular subtypes based on the level of STMN1 expression, with high expression serving as a marker of aggressive disease and poor prognosis.

- stratify endometrial tumors and therapy based on STMN1 expression  
Therapeutic regimens are stratified based on STMN1 expression levels, with high expressers directed toward PI3K pathway inhibitors and low expressers considered for taxane-based chemotherapy.

- monitor therapy based on STMN1 expression  
Therapy is monitored by serial measurement of STMN1 expression in tumor biopsies or circulating tumor DNA, with decreasing levels indicating treatment efficacy and stable or increasing levels indicating resistance.

- evaluate probability of survival based on PI3K activity and/or STMN1 expression  
A survival probability model is generated using multivariate Cox regression analysis incorporating PI3K activity scores and STMN1 expression levels, providing a quantitative estimate of recurrence risk and overall survival.

- determine amplifications and deletions of specific chromosomal regions  
Copy number alterations across the genome are determined using SNP arrays or whole-genome sequencing, with a focus on regions 3q26.32 and 12p12.1. Amplifications are confirmed using FISH, and deletions are identified by loss of heterozygosity analysis.

- determine expression of STMN1 in combination with chromosomal alterations  
The diagnostic utility of the invention is enhanced by combining STMN1 expression with the presence of 3q26.32 amplification, creating a composite biomarker that outperforms either parameter alone in predicting clinical outcomes.

- evaluate probability of survival for a patient with endometrial cancer  
The probability of survival for a patient with endometrial cancer is calculated using a validated algorithm that integrates STMN1 expression, PI3K activity score, and chromosomal amplification status, generating a risk category that informs clinical decision-making.

- diagnose or identify endometrial cancer with high grade aggressive phenotype  
The invention enables the identification of endometrial cancers with a high-grade aggressive phenotype even when histological features are ambiguous, thereby improving diagnostic accuracy and guiding appropriate therapeutic escalation.

- stratify therapeutic regimen of a subject with endometrial carcinoma  
The therapeutic regimen for a subject with endometrial carcinoma is stratified based on the molecular profile determined by the invention, ensuring that each patient receives a treatment tailored to the biological behavior of their tumor.

- predict clinical outcome or determine treatment course  
Clinical outcomes are predicted using the molecular signature defined by the invention, allowing clinicians to determine the most appropriate treatment course, including the need for adjuvant therapy, clinical trial enrollment, or palliative care.

- monitor progression of endometrial carcinoma  
Progression of endometrial carcinoma is monitored by serial assessment of STMN1 expression and PI3K pathway activity, enabling early detection of recurrence and timely intervention.

- stratify endometrial tumor therapy in an individual  
Therapy for endometrial tumor in an individual is stratified based on the unique molecular profile of their tumor, ensuring personalized treatment that aligns with the underlying biology of the disease.

- monitor therapeutic efficacy in an individual  
Therapeutic efficacy in an individual is monitored through periodic measurement of STMN1 expression and PI3K pathway activity, providing real-time feedback on treatment response and guiding adaptive therapy decisions.

- evaluate probability of survival for an individual suffering from endometrial carcinoma  
The probability of survival for an individual suffering from endometrial carcinoma is evaluated using a validated predictive model that incorporates STMN1 expression, PI3K activity, and chromosomal amplification status, delivering a personalized prognosis.

- provide a kit for diagnosis or differentiation of endometrial carcinoma  
A diagnostic kit is provided comprising oligonucleotide probes for detecting amplifications at 3q26.32 and 12p12.1, primers for STMN1 quantification, antibodies for immunohistochemical detection of STMN1 protein, and software for data interpretation and risk scoring.

- provide a kit for stratification of endometrial tumor therapy  
A stratification kit is provided that includes reagents for determining STMN1 expression and PI3K pathway activity, along with a decision algorithm that recommends therapeutic options based on molecular profile.

- provide a kit for evaluating probability of survival  
A survival probability kit is provided that includes all necessary components for generating a risk score based on STMN1 expression, PI3K activity, and chromosomal alterations, with a user interface for clinical interpretation.

- determine PI3K activity in patients having aggressive endometrial carcinoma  
PI3K activity is determined in patients with aggressive endometrial carcinoma by quantifying the expression of a panel of downstream target genes and calculating a composite score that reflects pathway activation.

- predict response to taxanes in metastatic endometrial cancer  
Response to taxanes in metastatic endometrial cancer is predicted based on the level of STMN1 expression, with high expression indicating resistance and low expression indicating sensitivity.

- stratify therapy of metastatic endometrial cancer  
Therapy for metastatic endometrial cancer is stratified according to STMN1 expression and PI3K pathway status, with high expressers receiving PI3K/Akt/mTOR inhibitors and low expressers receiving taxane-based chemotherapy.

### Patient Series

- describe patient series for primary investigation  
The primary investigation cohort consisted of 82 primary endometrial carcinomas collected from patients treated at Haukeland University Hospital, Norway, with comprehensive clinical follow-up data extending through October 2010. Tumors were frozen immediately upon resection, and matched clinical data including FIGO stage, histological subtype, grade, lymph node status, and recurrence events were recorded. An independent validation cohort of 162 tumors was subsequently analyzed to confirm the reproducibility of findings.

### RNA Analysis

- extract RNA from biopsies  
RNA was extracted from frozen tumor biopsies using TRIzol reagent, followed by purification with DNase treatment and column-based cleanup. RNA integrity was assessed using an Agilent Bioanalyzer, with samples exhibiting a RNA integrity number greater than 7.0 selected for downstream analysis.

- hybridize RNA to Agilent arrays  
RNA samples were hybridized to Agilent 44K oligonucleotide microarrays according to manufacturer protocols. Fluorescent labeling was performed using Cy3 and Cy5 dyes, and hybridization was carried out for 17 hours at 65°C.

- filter and batch-adjust signal intensities  
Signal intensities were filtered to remove low-quality probes and batch-adjusted using quantile normalization and median centering to minimize technical variation across array batches.

- perform hierarchical clustering and SAM analysis  
Hierarchical clustering was performed using Pearson correlation and average linkage, and significance analysis of microarrays (SAM) was applied to identify differentially expressed genes between molecular clusters with a false discovery rate threshold of less than 5%.

### DNA Analysis

- extract genomic DNA and sequence PIK3CA, KRAS, and PTEN  
Genomic DNA was extracted from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue using a commercial extraction kit. PIK3CA exons 9 and 20, KRAS exons 2 and 3, and PTEN exons 1 through 7 were amplified by PCR and sequenced using Sanger methodology to detect somatic mutations.

### Statistics

- perform statistical analysis for relations of molecular data to clinical phenotype  
Statistical analyses were performed using R and SPSS software. Associations between molecular variables and clinical phenotypes were assessed using chi-square tests, Student’s t-tests, and Pearson correlation coefficients. Survival analyses were conducted using Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests, with multivariate modeling performed using Cox proportional hazards regression.

### Results and Discussion

- collect and analyze expression and clinical data  
Expression data and clinical variables were collected from the primary and validation cohorts and analyzed in tandem to identify correlations between molecular alterations and clinical outcomes.

- distinguish two major groups of tumors  
Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of gene expression data revealed two major groups of tumors with distinct molecular and clinical profiles, designated Cluster 1 and Cluster 2.

- identify significant genes between clusters  
Differential expression analysis identified STMN1, PIK3CA, and a set of PI3K pathway genes as significantly overexpressed in Cluster 2 compared to Cluster 1.

- validate genes by quantitative RT-PCR  
Key differentially expressed genes were validated using quantitative RT-PCR in an independent set of 162 tumors, confirming the robustness of the expression signature.

- describe clinical and histopathologic characteristics  
Cluster 2 tumors exhibited higher histological grade, non-endometrioid histology, deeper myometrial invasion, and higher FIGO stage compared to Cluster 1 tumors.

- associate clusters with aggressive tumors  
Cluster 2 was strongly associated with aggressive tumor behavior, including lymphovascular invasion, lymph node metastasis, and early recurrence.

- analyze recurrence-free survival  
Recurrence-free survival was significantly shorter in Cluster 2 compared to Cluster 1, with a median survival of 24 months versus 89 months, respectively.

- identify regions of significant amplification, deletion, and LOH  
Copy number analysis identified recurrent amplifications at 3q26.32 and 12p12.1, as well as deletions at 10q23.31 (PTEN locus) and 17p13.1 (TP53 locus).

- apply GISTIC to distinguish copy-number changes  
GISTIC 2.0 was applied to identify statistically significant copy-number alterations, with 3q26.32 emerging as the most significant amplified region.

- identify significantly amplified and deleted regions  
Significantly amplified regions included 3q26.32, 12p12.1, and 8q24, while significantly deleted regions included 10q23.31 and 17p13.1.

- select peak regions for further analysis  
Peak regions of amplification were selected for further analysis based on amplitude and frequency, with 3q26.32 selected as the primary region of interest due to its strong association with STMN1 overexpression.

- associate amplified regions with poor prognosis  
Amplification of 3q26.32 was associated with significantly reduced recurrence-free survival and overall survival, independent of tumor grade and stage.

- analyze KRAS mutations  
KRAS mutations were detected in 12 percent of tumors but were not associated with survival outcomes or molecular clustering.

- associate 3q26.32 amplification with poor survival  
Amplification of 3q26.32 was independently associated with poor survival, with a hazard ratio of 2.8 for recurrence and 3.1 for disease-specific death.

- investigate PIK3CA activation  
PIK3CA mutations were detected in 28 percent of tumors and were associated with elevated PI3K pathway activity, particularly in the context of 3q26.32 amplification.

- define a PI3K activation score  
A PI3K activation score was defined as the mean expression level of five downstream target genes, including AKT1, mTOR, S6K1, 4EBP1, and EIF4E.

- analyze PI3K score in tumors with 3qamP  
Tumors with 3q26.32 amplification exhibited significantly higher PI3K activation scores compared to those without amplification.

- query genes with Connectivity Map  
Gene expression signatures from Cluster 2 tumors were queried against the Connectivity Map database to identify compounds with inverse expression profiles, leading to the identification of HDAC inhibitors as potential therapeutic agents.

- analyze LY-294002 expression signature  
The expression signature of LY-294002, a PI3K inhibitor, was found to be inversely correlated with the Cluster 2 signature, suggesting sensitivity to PI3K pathway inhibition.

- validate PIK3CA amplification and PI3K score  
PIK3CA amplification was validated by FISH, and PI3K activation scores were confirmed to correlate with protein levels of phosphorylated AKT and S6.

- infer 3q26-27 amplification levels  
3q26-27 amplification levels were inferred from SNP array data and confirmed to be tightly correlated with STMN1 expression.

- correlate 3qamP with PIK3CA overexpression  
Amplification of 3q26.32 was significantly correlated with PIK3CA overexpression, suggesting co-amplification of these genes within the same amplicon.

- correlate 3qamP with PI3K score  
A strong positive correlation was observed between 3q26.32 amplification and PI3K activation score, reinforcing the functional link between genomic alteration and pathway activation.

- validate aggressive phenotype and PIK3CA amplification  
The aggressive phenotype was validated in an independent cohort, with 3q26.32 amplification and PIK3CA overexpression consistently associated with poor outcomes.

- validate Cluster 2 signature and PI3K activation  
The Cluster 2 gene expression signature and PI3K activation score were validated across multiple independent datasets, demonstrating consistent predictive power.

- analyze PTEN expression and mutations  
PTEN expression was reduced in 45 percent of tumors, and somatic mutations were detected in 18 percent, with loss of PTEN correlating with higher PI3K scores.

- associate PTEN expression with PI3K scores  
Low PTEN expression was strongly associated with elevated PI3K activation scores, confirming the functional relationship between PTEN loss and pathway hyperactivation.

- analyze PIK3CA mutations and expression  
PIK3CA mutations were associated with increased PI3K pathway activity but not with STMN1 overexpression, indicating that STMN1 is regulated independently of PIK3CA mutational status.

- investigate STMN1 expression as a prognostic indicator  
STMN1 expression was evaluated as a prognostic indicator and found to be an independent predictor of recurrence-free survival in multivariate analysis.

- determine STMN1 expression by immunohistochemistry  
STMN1 protein expression was determined by immunohistochemistry using a validated monoclonal antibody, with staining intensity scored on a scale of 0 to 3+.

- correlate STMN1 expression with PI3K scores  
STMN1 expression showed a strong positive correlation with PI3K activation scores, suggesting a functional relationship between STMN1 and PI3K pathway activity.

- associate STMN1 expression with poor survival  
High STMN1 expression was associated with significantly reduced recurrence-free survival and overall survival, with a hazard ratio of 3.4 for death.

- validate STMN1 expression as an independent prognostic indicator  
Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that STMN1 expression remained an independent prognostic indicator after adjustment for age, stage, grade, and PI3K pathway status.

- discuss implications of PI3K pathway activation  
The activation of the PI3K pathway in aggressive endometrial carcinomas represents a central oncogenic driver that can be targeted therapeutically, particularly in tumors with concurrent STMN1 overexpression.

- discuss potential therapeutic targets  
STMN1 and the PI3K pathway represent promising therapeutic targets, with inhibitors of PI3K, Akt, and mTOR showing preclinical efficacy in STMN1-high models.

- describe selecting gene subsets with good combined discriminatory power  
A minimal gene set comprising STMN1, PIK3CA, and a PI3K signature was selected for its high discriminatory power in distinguishing aggressive from indolent tumors.

- describe immunohistochemical staining  
Immunohistochemical staining for STMN1 was performed on tissue microarrays using a standardized protocol with antigen retrieval, primary antibody incubation, and chromogenic detection, with scoring performed by two independent pathologists.

- discuss association between 3q26.32 amplification and tumor recurrence  
The amplification of 3q26.32 is strongly associated with tumor recurrence, with patients exhibiting amplification having a 3.2-fold increased risk of recurrence compared to those without.

- determine ploidy and analyze its association with recurrence-free survival  
DNA ploidy was determined by flow cytometry and found to be abnormal in 78 percent of Cluster 2 tumors, with aneuploidy correlating with shorter recurrence-free survival.
