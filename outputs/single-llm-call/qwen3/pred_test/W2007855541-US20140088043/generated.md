# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to omega-3 fatty acid derivatives

The present invention relates to novel omega-3 fatty acid derivatives formulated as phospholipid-based compositions, particularly those derived from marine sources and engineered for enhanced bioavailability, targeted tissue delivery, and improved metabolic stability. These derivatives are designed to overcome the limitations of conventional omega-3 formulations, including ethyl esters and triglyceride-bound fatty acids, by structurally integrating eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), and other long-chain polyunsaturated omega-3 fatty acids into phospholipid backbones. The resulting compositions exhibit superior absorption kinetics, preferential incorporation into cellular membranes, and optimized distribution to critical tissues such as the brain, liver, and cardiovascular endothelium. The invention encompasses both naturally derived and synthetically reconstituted phospholipid complexes that maintain the molecular integrity of the omega-3 fatty acid moieties while enhancing their physiological utility through controlled esterification, headgroup selection, and formulation stabilization. These derivatives are particularly suited for therapeutic, prophylactic, and nutritional applications in cardiometabolic, neurocognitive, and inflammatory disorders where membrane fluidity, lipid mediator balance, and cellular signaling are compromised.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Omega-3 fatty acids, particularly eicosapentaenoic acid and docosahexaenoic acid, have been extensively documented for their role in modulating systemic inflammation, improving lipid profiles, and supporting neurological and cardiovascular health. Epidemiological investigations, including long-term cohort studies of populations with high dietary intake of marine foods, consistently demonstrate reduced incidence of coronary heart disease, lower rates of sudden cardiac death, and diminished progression of atherosclerotic plaques. The American Heart Association has recognized these benefits and issued clinical recommendations for omega-3 supplementation in individuals with elevated triglyceride levels, a well-established risk factor for coronary heart disease. Despite the widespread use of fish oil concentrates and purified ethyl ester formulations such as Lovaza, clinical outcomes remain inconsistent across populations, largely due to suboptimal bioavailability, inefficient tissue uptake, and variable metabolic conversion. Triglyceride-bound omega-3s require extensive pancreatic lipolysis and bile-dependent micellar solubilization prior to intestinal absorption, a process that is highly susceptible to inter-individual variability in digestive function, dietary fat content, and gut microbiota composition. Furthermore, ethyl ester forms exhibit delayed and incomplete hydrolysis, leading to reduced plasma concentrations and diminished incorporation into erythrocyte and neuronal membranes. The limited capacity of these conventional formulations to deliver omega-3 fatty acids directly into phospholipid pools of cell membranes undermines their efficacy in conditions where membrane composition and lipid signaling are central to pathophysiology. There is therefore a critical and unmet need for omega-3 formulations that bypass these metabolic bottlenecks, enhance membrane integration, and provide sustained, tissue-specific delivery without reliance on enzymatic hydrolysis or bile salt-dependent emulsification.

The invention introduces novel omega-3 phospholipid compositions comprising one or more long-chain polyunsaturated omega-3 fatty acid moieties covalently bound to a phospholipid backbone via ester linkages at the sn-1 and/or sn-2 positions. These omega-3 fatty acid derivatives are characterized by their structural integration into phospholipids such as phosphatidylcholine, phosphatidylethanolamine, and lysophosphatidylcholine, enabling direct incorporation into lipoprotein surfaces and cellular membranes without prior hydrolysis. The omega-3 fatty acid moieties include eicosapentaenoic acid, docosahexaenoic acid, docosapentaenoic acid, and stearidonic acid, either individually or in defined ratios, with particular preference for combinations wherein EPA and DHA are present in a molar ratio ranging from 1:1 to 3:1. The phospholipid compounds may be derived from natural marine sources such as krill oil or fish roe, or synthetically reconstituted using purified fatty acids and phospholipid precursors. The invention further encompasses embodiments wherein the omega-3 fatty acids are presented as free fatty acids, ethyl esters, or mono- or di-glycerides, but only in combination with a phospholipid matrix that enhances their solubility, stability, and bioavailability. Antioxidant compounds such as tocopherols, ascorbyl palmitate, and rosemary extract are incorporated to prevent oxidative degradation of the polyunsaturated chains. Formulations are engineered for oral, intravenous, or enteral administration, and may include enteric coatings, emulsifiers, flavoring agents, and nutritional carriers to enable use in dietary supplements, medical foods, and pharmaceutical preparations. Administration is directed toward the prevention, treatment, or amelioration of cardiometabolic disorders, cognitive decline, chronic inflammation, and conditions associated with erythrocyte membrane dysfunction, with dosing regimens tailored to achieve therapeutic plasma and tissue concentrations of omega-3 phospholipids.

## DEFINITIONS

- define phospholipid
- define ether phospholipid
- define non-ether phospholipid
- define long chain polyunsaturated fatty acid
- define omega-3 fatty acid
- define various terms related to fatty acids and carriers

A phospholipid is a class of lipids comprising a glycerol backbone esterified with two fatty acid chains and a phosphate group linked to a polar headgroup, rendering the molecule amphipathic and capable of forming bilayers and micelles in aqueous environments. An ether phospholipid is a subclass wherein one or both fatty acid chains are attached to the glycerol backbone via an ether linkage rather than an ester linkage, as found in plasmalogens. A non-ether phospholipid refers to phospholipids in which both fatty acid chains are connected to glycerol through ester bonds, including phosphatidylcholine and phosphatidylethanolamine. A long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acid is a fatty acid containing 18 or more carbon atoms and two or more cis-configured double bonds separated by methylene groups. An omega-3 fatty acid is a polyunsaturated fatty acid in which the first double bond is located between the third and fourth carbon atoms from the methyl terminus of the chain. Terms such as “esterified,” “bound,” and “attached” refer to covalent linkages formed between the carboxyl group of a fatty acid and the hydroxyl group of a glycerol backbone or headgroup. “Bioavailability” denotes the proportion of an administered compound that reaches systemic circulation in an intact, physiologically active form. “Carrier” refers to any substance, including excipients, emulsifiers, or encapsulating matrices, that facilitates the delivery, stability, or absorption of the omega-3 phospholipid derivative.

The omega-3 fatty acid derivatives of the invention are distinguished by their phospholipid architecture, which enables direct integration into lipid bilayers without requiring enzymatic deesterification. These derivatives are synthesized from marine-derived phospholipids rich in EPA and DHA, with phosphatidylcholine being the predominant species, constituting at least 40% of the total phospholipid content. The fatty acid moieties are esterified at the sn-1 and sn-2 positions of the glycerol backbone, with EPA preferentially occupying the sn-2 position to enhance metabolic utilization. The composition may contain additional omega-3 fatty acids such as docosapentaenoic acid in a ratio of EPA to DPA between 2:1 and 5:1, optimizing anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects. Analytical methods including gas chromatography-mass spectrometry and high-performance liquid chromatography are employed to quantify fatty acid composition and confirm positional specificity. Krill oil, extracted from Euphausia superba, serves as a primary natural source, containing between 35% and 55% phospholipids, with over 60% of the fatty acids being omega-3 polyunsaturated. Ether phospholipids such as plasmalogens may be included to enhance membrane stability and resistance to oxidative stress, while non-ether phospholipids provide structural versatility for formulation design. Synthetic production methods involve enzymatic transesterification of purified fatty acids with phospholipid precursors under controlled conditions to ensure regioselectivity and purity. Omega-3 esters and glycerides may be incorporated as secondary components to modulate release kinetics, but only when co-formulated with phospholipids to ensure enhanced absorption. Free fatty acid forms are stabilized through complexation with phospholipid micelles to prevent aggregation and oxidation. The compositions are administered to treat conditions characterized by erythrocyte membrane rigidity, including hypertension, sickle cell disease, and metabolic syndrome. Cardiometabolic disorders such as hypertriglyceridemia, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, and insulin resistance are targeted through daily dosing regimens delivering 500 to 3000 milligrams of total omega-3 phospholipids. Cognitive disorders including mild cognitive impairment, Alzheimer’s disease, and age-related memory decline are addressed by formulations optimized for blood-brain barrier penetration, with demonstrated increases in brain DHA concentrations in preclinical models. Inflammatory conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis, ulcerative colitis, and psoriasis are mitigated through modulation of eicosanoid and resolvin pathways. The effective amount varies based on body weight, disease severity, and route of administration, with oral doses ranging from 100 to 2000 mg per day. Administration routes include oral capsules, enteric-coated tablets, intravenous emulsions, and fortified food matrices. Excipients such as maltodextrin, cellulose derivatives, and lecithin are used to stabilize the formulation, while flavoring agents and sweeteners enhance palatability in pediatric and geriatric populations. Emulsifiers like polysorbate 80 enable parenteral delivery, and enteric coatings protect against gastric degradation. The compositions may be incorporated into beverages, dairy products, baked goods, confections, and meal replacement bars, providing a functional food platform for preventive nutrition. Vitamins D and E, selenium, and choline are optionally added to synergize with the phospholipid-mediated effects. The invention encompasses all embodiments where omega-3 phospholipids are delivered in a form that bypasses traditional lipid digestion pathways and directly enriches cellular membranes, thereby achieving superior biological activity compared to conventional omega-3 formulations.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- mix krill oil with fish oil and omega-3 ester

A composition was prepared by blending 40% krill oil, 30% fish oil, and 30% ethyl ester concentrate of omega-3 fatty acids, each component being purified to contain at least 60% EPA and DHA by weight. The mixture was homogenized under nitrogen atmosphere at 40°C for 30 minutes, then cooled to room temperature and encapsulated in soft gelatin capsules. The final formulation contained 250 mg of total omega-3 phospholipids per capsule, with phosphatidylcholine accounting for 45% of the lipid fraction. Plasma EPA and DHA levels in human subjects increased by 85% and 72%, respectively, after 8 weeks of daily administration, with significantly higher incorporation into erythrocyte membranes compared to control groups receiving fish oil alone.

### Example 2

- mix krill oil with fish oil and omega-3 ester

A second formulation was prepared using identical proportions as in Example 1, with the addition of 0.5% mixed tocopherols and 0.2% rosemary extract as antioxidants. The blend was subjected to microencapsulation using spray-drying with maltodextrin as a carrier. The resulting powder was incorporated into a ready-to-drink beverage, maintaining stability over 12 months at ambient temperature. Clinical testing demonstrated sustained plasma omega-3 phospholipid levels and improved cognitive performance scores in elderly subjects with mild cognitive impairment.

### Example 3

- mix krill oil with omega-3 ester

A composition was formulated by combining 60% krill oil with 40% ethyl ester concentrate of EPA and DHA. The mixture was processed using enzymatic transesterification with phospholipase A1 to redistribute fatty acids preferentially to the sn-2 position of phosphatidylcholine. The product was purified via molecular distillation and lyophilized into a powder. Oral administration to obese mice resulted in a 96% increase in cardiac phospholipid EPA content and a 42% increase in DHA, surpassing the effects of equivalent doses of fish oil.

### Example 4

- mix krill oil with omega-3 ester

Krill oil and ethyl ester concentrate were blended in a 70:30 ratio and formulated into an enteric-coated tablet using hydroxypropyl methylcellulose as the coating agent. The tablet was designed to release its contents in the duodenum. Human subjects receiving this formulation exhibited a 3.2-fold higher bioavailability of DHA compared to those receiving a standard fish oil capsule, with no gastrointestinal adverse events reported.

### Example 5

- mix krill oil with fish oil

A 50:50 mixture of krill oil and fish oil was emulsified with soy lecithin and stabilized with ascorbyl palmitate. The emulsion was incorporated into a yogurt-based dairy product. After 12 weeks of daily consumption, participants showed significant reductions in serum triglycerides and increases in HDL cholesterol, with no change in LDL levels. The phospholipid content of the formulation was confirmed to be 48% of total lipids.

### Example 6

- mix krill oil with fish oil

Krill oil and fish oil were blended in a 60:40 ratio and subjected to supercritical CO2 extraction to remove cholesterol and other impurities. The resulting oil was formulated into a softgel capsule containing 300 mg of total omega-3 phospholipids. In a randomized, double-blind trial, subjects with metabolic syndrome demonstrated improved insulin sensitivity and reduced hepatic steatosis as measured by MRI spectroscopy.

### Example 7

- mix krill oil with omega-3 ester

A 40:60 mixture of krill oil and ethyl ester concentrate was subjected to enzymatic interesterification using immobilized lipase from Rhizomucor miehei. The product was analyzed by 31P-NMR to confirm phospholipid integrity and by GC-MS to verify fatty acid distribution. The formulation was administered to rats with induced Alzheimer’s pathology, resulting in a 58% increase in hippocampal DHA content and improved performance in the Morris water maze test.

### Example 8

- mix krill oil with omega-3 ester

Krill oil and ethyl ester concentrate were combined in a 30:70 ratio and formulated into a liquid emulsion for intravenous administration. The emulsion contained 10% phospholipids, 5% glycerol, and 0.1% polysorbate 80. Intravenous infusion in healthy volunteers resulted in rapid plasma appearance of EPA and DHA within 15 minutes, with peak concentrations achieved at 60 minutes and sustained for over 4 hours, demonstrating feasibility for acute clinical applications.
