# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology, specifically to the diagnosis and management of epithelial thyroid neoplasms. It encompasses molecular diagnostic methods and associated compositions for distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid lesions, including those with indeterminate cytological features, and further enables the differentiation of noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP) from invasive follicular variant of papillary thyroid carcinoma (FVPTC) and other malignant subtypes. The invention is particularly directed to the detection of specific splice variants of a defined panel of gene transcripts in fine needle aspiration samples, enabling improved preoperative risk stratification and guiding therapeutic decision-making in thyroid cancer care.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Thyroid cancer is one of the most rapidly increasing malignancies in the United States and globally, with incidence rates nearly quadrupling over the past two decades. The majority of these cases arise from epithelial cells of the thyroid follicle, with papillary thyroid carcinoma being the most prevalent subtype. Preoperative evaluation of thyroid nodules is routinely performed using fine needle aspiration (FNA) cytology, which remains the standard of care due to its minimally invasive nature and high negative predictive value. However, a substantial proportion of FNA samples—up to 30%—yield indeterminate results under the Bethesda classification system, including categories such as atypia of undetermined significance, follicular lesion of undetermined significance, follicular neoplasm, and suspicious for malignancy. These indeterminate findings create significant clinical uncertainty, often leading to diagnostic lobectomies that may be unnecessary in benign cases.

The recent reclassification of a subset of follicular variant papillary thyroid carcinomas as noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP) has further complicated diagnostic interpretation. NIFTP is a histologically distinct entity characterized by follicular growth pattern and nuclear features of papillary carcinoma but lacking capsular or vascular invasion, and it carries an extremely low risk of recurrence or metastasis. Importantly, NIFTP cannot be reliably distinguished from invasive FVPTC by cytology or imaging, and definitive diagnosis requires histopathologic evaluation of the resected specimen. Consequently, patients with NIFTP are frequently subjected to the same aggressive surgical and adjuvant treatments as those with invasive carcinoma, despite the indolent nature of the lesion.

Molecular diagnostic tests have been developed to supplement cytological evaluation and reduce the rate of diagnostic uncertainty. Commercially available platforms, including gene expression classifiers and mutation panels, have demonstrated utility in identifying malignant lesions. However, these tests were largely developed and validated prior to the formal recognition of NIFTP as a separate diagnostic category. As a result, many of these assays misclassify NIFTP as malignant, thereby diminishing their specificity and positive predictive value in contemporary clinical practice. For instance, gene expression-based classifiers and next-generation sequencing panels continue to report NIFTP as “suspicious” or “positive” for malignancy, leading to overtreatment and unnecessary surgical interventions. There remains a critical unmet need for a molecular diagnostic tool capable of accurately distinguishing NIFTP from true malignant thyroid neoplasms while maintaining high sensitivity and specificity in FNA samples with variable cellular content and potential contamination by non-thyroidal cells such as peripheral blood mononuclear cells.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides an improved molecular diagnostic test for the preoperative evaluation of thyroid nodules that overcomes the limitations of existing methods by leveraging a novel panel of five splice-variant-specific gene transcripts whose expression patterns reliably differentiate benign lesions—including NIFTP—from malignant thyroid tumors. This diagnostic gene panel, comprising specific isoforms of CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1, is quantified using reverse transcription followed by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) in fine needle aspiration samples, with expression levels normalized to a thyrocyte-specific load control marker, TPO1, which is absent in peripheral blood cells and stably expressed across differentiated thyroid neoplasms. The invention achieves high sensitivity and specificity, enabling accurate classification of thyroid lesions as benign or malignant with a negative predictive value exceeding 90% and a positive predictive value approaching 75% in validation cohorts. The method is particularly valuable for identifying NIFTP, which is correctly classified as benign by this panel, thereby preventing overtreatment of this indolent neoplasm. The invention further enables the application of this diagnostic approach to high-risk populations, including patients with indeterminate cytology, those undergoing preoperative risk assessment, and individuals with a family history of thyroid cancer or prior radiation exposure. The method involves the extraction of nucleic acid from FNA samples, reverse transcription to generate complementary DNA, amplification of the five target transcripts and the TPO1 reference gene using isoform-specific primers, and quantification of expression levels via real-time PCR. The relative expression of each target transcript is calculated as the difference in cycle threshold values compared to TPO1, transformed into a standardized z-score, and summed to generate a composite diagnostic score. A predefined threshold of this composite score allows for the binary classification of the lesion as benign or malignant. The invention further encompasses the use of this diagnostic method to guide clinical management, including the selection of less invasive surgical approaches for benign lesions and the initiation of targeted therapies for confirmed malignancies. The invention also includes compositions and kits containing the necessary reagents, primers, controls, and instructions for performing the assay in clinical laboratories.

### II. Sample Preparation for Detection of Expression

A sample is obtained from a subject suspected of having a thyroid neoplasm, typically via fine needle aspiration performed under ultrasound guidance. The aspirate is collected into a preservative solution such as 95% ethanol or RNAlater and stored at -80°C until processing. Alternatively, tissue samples obtained during surgical resection are snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored under similar conditions. Nucleic acid is extracted from the sample using a combination of mechanical disruption and chemical lysis, followed by purification using silica-membrane columns or magnetic bead-based systems to isolate total RNA. In cases where cellular content is low or contamination by peripheral blood mononuclear cells is suspected, the sample may be pre-enriched using antibody-coated magnetic beads targeting thyrocyte-specific surface markers such as CD56 or thyroglobulin. The purified RNA is quantified spectrophotometrically or fluorometrically, and integrity is assessed using capillary electrophoresis. For reverse transcription, 100–500 ng of total RNA is converted to complementary DNA using a reverse transcriptase enzyme and random hexamers or gene-specific primers. In samples with limited material, such as single-pass FNAs, the entire eluate from the RNA isolation step is used for cDNA synthesis to maximize target recovery. The resulting cDNA is diluted in nuclease-free water and stored at -20°C until use in amplification reactions. In some embodiments, protein extraction is performed in parallel using lysis buffers containing protease inhibitors, and expression levels of corresponding proteins are assessed via immunoassay for correlative validation. The sample may be processed without prior enrichment, provided that the inclusion criteria based on TPO1 expression are met to ensure adequate thyrocyte content.

### III. Detection of Target Nucleic Acid

The expression levels of the five target gene transcripts—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—are quantified using reverse transcription followed by real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Isoform-specific primers are designed to span unique exon-exon junctions that distinguish the diagnostic splice variants from other transcript isoforms or genomic DNA contamination. Each reaction is performed in duplicate using a SYBR Green-based detection system on a real-time thermal cycler, with amplification cycles set to 40 and an annealing temperature optimized for each primer pair. A reference gene, TPO1, is co-amplified in parallel to normalize for variations in thyrocyte content across samples. The cycle threshold (Ct) values for each target and reference are recorded, and the relative expression is calculated as the difference in Ct values (∆Ct = Ct_target – Ct_TPO1). Samples with a TPO1 Ct value exceeding 30 are excluded from analysis due to insufficient thyrocyte content. The ∆Ct values are then z-transformed to standardize expression levels across samples and genes. The composite diagnostic score is generated by summing the z-transformed ∆Ct values of the five target transcripts. A predefined threshold of -1 for the composite score is used to classify samples as benign (score ≥ -1) or malignant (score < -1). Alternative detection methods include the use of TaqMan probes with fluorophore-labeled hydrolysis probes, digital PCR for absolute quantification, or multiplexed microfluidic platforms capable of simultaneous detection of all six targets. Amplification may be performed without prior target enrichment, and no pre-amplification steps are required. The assay is validated for linearity, reproducibility, and limit of detection using serial dilutions of known RNA concentrations. The use of hot-start polymerase and optimized buffer conditions minimizes non-specific amplification and enhances signal-to-noise ratio.

### IV. Detection of Target Protein(s)

In alternative embodiments, the expression levels of the protein products encoded by the five target genes are measured using immunoassay techniques. Antibodies specific to the unique epitopes of each splice variant are generated by immunizing animals with synthetic peptides corresponding to the variant-specific amino acid sequences. These antibodies are then purified, conjugated to detectable labels such as horseradish peroxidase or fluorescent dyes, and used in enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), Western blotting, or immunohistochemistry. For high-throughput applications, multiplexed electrochemiluminescent platforms or bead-based flow cytometry systems are employed to simultaneously quantify all five proteins in a single sample. Capture agents are immobilized on solid-phase substrates such as microbeads or microarrays, and binding of target proteins is detected via labeled secondary antibodies or direct labeling. Competitive binding assays may be used to enhance specificity, particularly in samples with high background protein content. The detection of protein expression provides a complementary method to nucleic acid analysis, enabling validation of transcriptional findings and offering potential for point-of-care diagnostic applications. The use of aptamers as alternative binding agents is also contemplated, wherein nucleic acid-based ligands are selected via SELEX to bind with high affinity to the target proteins. These aptamers may be labeled and used in lateral flow assays or biosensor formats for rapid clinical testing.

### V. Treatment of Patients Guided by Target Biomarker Panel

Patients classified as malignant by the diagnostic method of this invention are candidates for definitive surgical intervention, including total or near-total thyroidectomy, with or without lymph node dissection, depending on tumor size, extrathyroidal extension, and nodal involvement. Following surgery, patients may receive radioactive iodine ablation, thyroid hormone suppression therapy, or external beam radiation if indicated by histopathological findings. For patients with confirmed BRAF V600E mutations or other MAPK pathway alterations, targeted inhibition using BRAF inhibitors such as vemurafenib or dabrafenib, or MEK inhibitors such as trametinib or cobimetinib, may be initiated in cases of advanced or metastatic disease. In patients with TERT promoter mutations, investigational TERT inhibitors may be administered as part of clinical trials. Patients classified as benign, including those with NIFTP, are managed conservatively with active surveillance, including serial ultrasound and clinical evaluation, avoiding total thyroidectomy and radioactive iodine therapy. In select cases, lobectomy may be performed for diagnostic or symptomatic reasons, but adjuvant therapies are not indicated. Additional therapeutic agents, including acivicin, aclarubicin, acodazole hydrochloride, acronine, adozelesin, aldesleukin, altretamine, ambomycin, aminoglutethimide, amsacrine, anastrozole, and anthramycin, may be considered in refractory or metastatic cases, particularly in combination with molecularly targeted agents. The diagnostic method enables personalized therapeutic decision-making by identifying patients who will benefit from aggressive intervention and those who can safely avoid it, thereby reducing morbidity, healthcare costs, and psychological burden.

### VI. Kits for Detection of Target Biomarkers

The invention includes a diagnostic kit for performing the method described herein. The kit comprises a set of primers specific to the five target splice variants (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1) and the reference gene TPO1, each primer pair supplied in lyophilized or aqueous form at optimized concentrations. The kit further includes a master mix containing a thermostable DNA polymerase, dNTPs, reaction buffer, and SYBR Green dye, sufficient for at least 20 reactions. A positive control sample containing known concentrations of the target transcripts and a negative control sample lacking thyroid-derived nucleic acid are included. Instructions for use are provided in printed or electronic form, detailing sample preparation, reverse transcription, amplification conditions, data analysis, and interpretation of the composite score. The kit may be configured for use with standard real-time PCR instruments and includes a data analysis software module for automated calculation of the z-transformed composite score and classification output. In alternative embodiments, the kit includes immunoassay components such as capture antibodies, detection antibodies, enzyme conjugates, and substrate solutions for protein-based detection. The kit may be packaged in a sterile, temperature-controlled container with desiccant and stored at -20°C. Optional components include magnetic beads for thyrocyte enrichment, RNA stabilization reagents, and calibration standards for absolute quantification. The kit is intended for use in CLIA-certified laboratories and is suitable for integration into routine clinical pathology workflows.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and Methods

Clinical samples were collected from patients undergoing thyroid surgery at Johns Hopkins Hospital under Institutional Review Board approval. Fine needle aspiration specimens were obtained intraoperatively using a 25-gauge needle and preserved in RNAlater or 95% ethanol. Frozen tumor tissues were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen. Total RNA was extracted using Trizol and RNeasy Mini Kit protocols. cDNA was synthesized using SuperScript III reverse transcriptase. Reverse transcription-PCR was performed using Platinum Taq DNA polymerase, and products were analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis. Real-time qPCR was conducted on a Bio-Rad iQ5 system using Power SYBR Green Master Mix. Primer sequences are listed in Supplemental Table S1. Cycle threshold values were averaged from duplicate reactions. TPO1 expression was used to exclude samples with insufficient thyrocyte content (Ct > 30). Expression levels were normalized to TPO1, and ∆Ct values were z-transformed. Receiver operating characteristic analysis was performed to determine diagnostic performance, and Bayes Rule was applied to calculate predictive values based on published prevalence rates.

### Results

Five splice variants—CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1—were identified as differentially expressed between benign and malignant thyroid tumors. None were detectable in peripheral blood mononuclear cells. TPO1 was selected as the load control due to its thyrocyte-specific expression and stability across tumor subtypes. In 137 qualifying FNA samples, the composite z-score of the five transcripts achieved an AUC of 0.86 for distinguishing benign from malignant lesions. At a threshold of -1, sensitivity was 75% and specificity was 91%, yielding a negative predictive value of 91% and a positive predictive value of 74%. The panel successfully distinguished NIFTP from invasive FVPTC and PTC with statistical significance (p < 0.05). The method demonstrated robust performance across sample types and operators.

### Discussion

The invention addresses a critical gap in thyroid cancer diagnostics by providing a molecular tool that accurately classifies NIFTP as benign, thereby preventing overtreatment. The use of TPO1 as a load control eliminates confounding effects from non-thyroidal cell contamination. The five-gene panel is highly specific, with no cross-reactivity to blood-derived transcripts. The method is compatible with standard clinical workflows and requires minimal sample volume. Limitations include the retrospective nature of the cohort and the need for prospective validation in multicenter trials.

### Conclusions

The invention provides a novel, clinically applicable molecular diagnostic method for thyroid nodules that accurately differentiates benign from malignant lesions, including NIFTP. The method improves diagnostic precision, reduces unnecessary surgeries, and enables personalized management. The associated kit facilitates widespread adoption in clinical laboratories.

## Example 3: Development of a High Sensitivity and Specificity Test to Evaluate Preoperative Thyroid Fine Needle Aspiration Cytology Material.

The goal was to develop a molecular test capable of accurately classifying thyroid FNA samples with indeterminate cytology, with particular emphasis on distinguishing NIFTP from malignant tumors. The original 12-gene panel was refined to focus on splice variants not expressed in peripheral blood. TPO1 was selected as the load control after demonstrating absence in PBMCs and stable expression across tumor subtypes. The final five-gene panel was tested on 159 archival FNA samples, achieving 86.2% success rate in sample inclusion. The panel correctly classified 91% of benign cases and 75% of malignant cases. Shifting from GapDH to TPO1 as the load control significantly improved diagnostic accuracy, particularly in samples with low thyrocyte content. The method demonstrated superior performance over existing commercial assays in distinguishing NIFTP from invasive carcinoma.
