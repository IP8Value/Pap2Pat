# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- define technical field of bacterial capsular saccharides

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to the preparation, characterization, and use of synthetic capsular saccharide conjugates derived from bacterial pathogens for the induction of protective immune responses. More particularly, the invention concerns novel glycoconjugate vaccines comprising chemically modified capsular polysaccharides of *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup A, designed to overcome the inherent instability of native polysaccharide antigens while retaining or enhancing their immunogenic properties. The invention further encompasses methods for the synthesis, purification, conjugation, and formulation of these stabilized antigens, as well as pharmaceutical compositions and kits for their administration as prophylactic or therapeutic agents against meningococcal disease. The technical domain includes the application of organic synthesis, carbohydrate chemistry, bioconjugation techniques, and immunological assays to generate structurally defined, stable, and immunologically active vaccine candidates that surpass the limitations of conventional polysaccharide-based or even native conjugate vaccines.

## BACKGROUND ART

- introduce capsular saccharides in vaccines

Capsular saccharides are surface-expressed polysaccharide structures produced by many pathogenic bacteria, serving as critical virulence factors that shield the microorganism from host immune recognition and phagocytic clearance. These glycans have long been recognized as key targets for vaccine development due to their high conservation across clinical isolates and their essential role in bacterial survival within the human host. Vaccines based on purified capsular polysaccharides were among the first subunit vaccines to achieve widespread clinical use, particularly against *Haemophilus influenzae* type b, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, and *Neisseria meningitidis*. However, these vaccines exhibit significant limitations, including poor immunogenicity in infants and immunocompromised individuals, lack of immunological memory, and inability to elicit T cell-dependent responses, which are necessary for sustained protection and booster effects.

- motivate conjugation to carriers

To overcome these shortcomings, conjugation of capsular polysaccharides to immunogenic carrier proteins has been established as the gold standard in modern vaccinology. This strategy converts the T cell-independent immune response elicited by pure polysaccharides into a T cell-dependent response, enabling robust IgG antibody production, class switching, affinity maturation, and long-term immunological memory. Carrier proteins such as tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, and the non-toxic mutant CRM197 have been successfully employed in licensed vaccines, demonstrating enhanced safety, durability, and efficacy across all age groups, including neonates.

- summarize Hib conjugate vaccine

The success of the *Haemophilus influenzae* type b (Hib) conjugate vaccine, which couples the polyribosylribitol phosphate (PRP) capsular polysaccharide to CRM197 or other carrier proteins, revolutionized pediatric immunization and led to near-eradication of invasive Hib disease in vaccinated populations. This paradigm established the feasibility and clinical value of glycoconjugate vaccines, prompting extensive efforts to replicate this success for other encapsulated pathogens, including *N. meningitidis* serogroups A, C, W, and Y.

- list serogroups of N. meningitidis

*Neisseria meningitidis* is classified into at least thirteen serogroups based on antigenic differences in their capsular polysaccharides. Of these, six—A, B, C, W, Y, and X—are responsible for over 90% of invasive meningococcal disease worldwide. Serogroup A has historically dominated epidemics in the African meningitis belt, while serogroups C, W, and Y are prevalent in Europe, the Americas, and parts of Asia. Serogroup X, though less frequently reported, has emerged as a significant cause of outbreaks in sub-Saharan Africa and poses an increasing public health threat due to its potential for rapid spread and high case fatality rate.

- describe tetravalent vaccine

Tetravalent conjugate vaccines combining capsular polysaccharides from serogroups A, C, W, and Y have been developed and licensed for broad-spectrum protection. These vaccines have significantly reduced the burden of meningococcal disease in regions where multiple serogroups co-circulate. However, the inclusion of serogroup A remains particularly challenging due to the inherent instability of its capsular polysaccharide.

- limitations of polysaccharide vaccines

Native polysaccharide vaccines suffer from multiple drawbacks: they are poorly immunogenic in children under two years of age, do not induce immunological memory, and exhibit batch-to-batch variability due to heterogeneity in molecular weight, acetylation patterns, and impurities. Furthermore, their immunogenicity is often transient, requiring frequent revaccination, and they are susceptible to degradation during storage, especially under suboptimal temperature conditions, which compromises vaccine potency and shelf life.

- introduce conjugate vaccines against serogroup C

Conjugate vaccines targeting serogroup C meningococcus have demonstrated high efficacy and long-lasting protection, leading to the near-elimination of serogroup C disease in countries with routine immunization programs. These vaccines rely on the covalent linkage of serogroup C polysaccharide to CRM197 or other carrier proteins, resulting in strong IgG responses and herd immunity. However, the chemical instability of the phosphodiester backbone in serogroup A polysaccharide has prevented the development of similarly stable and effective conjugate formulations.

- describe mixtures of conjugates

Modern meningococcal vaccines often combine multiple glycoconjugates to provide broad protection. For example, quadrivalent conjugate vaccines contain separate conjugates of serogroups A, C, W, and Y, each linked to a carrier protein, either the same or different, to ensure optimal immunogenicity for each component. The formulation of such mixtures requires careful optimization of conjugation chemistry, antigen-to-carrier ratios, and stability profiles to prevent interference between components and ensure consistent immune responses.

- introduce serogroup X capsular polysaccharide

Serogroup X capsular polysaccharide is composed of repeating units of (1→6)-linked 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-α-D-mannopyranosyl phosphate, structurally analogous to serogroup A but with distinct immunological properties. Although less prevalent than serogroup A, serogroup X has been associated with explosive outbreaks in previously unaffected regions, and its inclusion in next-generation vaccines is increasingly recognized as a critical public health priority.

- motivate need for vaccine against serogroup X

The emergence of serogroup X as a cause of epidemic meningococcal disease in sub-Saharan Africa, coupled with the absence of a licensed conjugate vaccine, underscores an urgent unmet medical need. Current tetravalent vaccines do not include serogroup X, leaving vulnerable populations at risk. The development of a stable, immunogenic serogroup X conjugate vaccine is therefore essential to achieve comprehensive protection against meningococcal disease.

- describe structure of group X capsular polysaccharide

The serogroup X capsular polysaccharide consists of a linear homopolymer of α-(1→6)-linked N-acetyl-D-mannosamine-1-phosphate repeating units, with variable degrees of O-acetylation at the C-3 and C-4 hydroxyl positions. This phosphodiester-linked backbone is inherently labile in aqueous environments, particularly under physiological pH and temperature conditions, leading to depolymerization via hydrolysis of the anomeric phosphodiester bond. The presence of acetyl esters modulates this instability, but even acetylated forms degrade over time, compromising vaccine efficacy.

- motivate need for aqueous formulations

The instability of serogroup X polysaccharide necessitates storage in lyophilized form, requiring reconstitution prior to administration—a logistical barrier in resource-limited settings. The development of a stable, liquid, ready-to-use formulation would greatly enhance vaccine accessibility, reduce cold chain dependency, and improve compliance. Achieving such a formulation requires not only chemical stabilization of the polysaccharide but also preservation of its immunogenic conformation during storage.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

- introduce serogroup X capsular polysaccharide conjugates

The present invention provides novel glycoconjugates comprising a chemically stabilized analog of the serogroup X capsular polysaccharide covalently linked to a carrier protein. These conjugates are designed to retain the immunogenic epitopes of the native polysaccharide while exhibiting enhanced hydrolytic stability, enabling their formulation in liquid, shelf-stable preparations without loss of potency. The stabilized analogs are synthesized using organic chemistry to replace the labile ring oxygen of the mannose residue with a methylene group, thereby generating a non-hydrolysable carba analog of the natural repeating unit.

- describe immunogenicity of conjugates

The serogroup X carba-polysaccharide conjugates elicit robust, T cell-dependent immune responses in animal models, characterized by high-titer IgG antibodies that cross-react with the native serogroup X capsular polysaccharide and mediate complement-dependent bactericidal activity against live *N. meningitidis* serogroup X strains. The immune response is dose-dependent, booster-responsive, and persists for extended periods, demonstrating the induction of immunological memory. The conjugates are capable of inducing functional antibodies that recognize both acetylated and deacetylated forms of the native polysaccharide, indicating broad epitope recognition.

- discuss combination with other antigens

The serogroup X glycoconjugates of the invention may be formulated in combination with other bacterial, viral, or parasitic antigens to create multivalent vaccines. These combinations may include conjugates of serogroups A, C, W, and Y of *N. meningitidis*, as well as antigens from *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Salmonella typhi*, or respiratory syncytial virus. The co-formulation does not compromise the immunogenicity of any individual component, and the use of distinct carrier proteins for each conjugate may further enhance immune responses and reduce antigenic competition.

- highlight stability of serogroup X capsular polysaccharides

The carba analogs of serogroup X capsular polysaccharide exhibit exceptional hydrolytic stability compared to their native counterparts. Accelerated stability studies conducted at 37°C in physiological buffers demonstrate no detectable depolymerization over periods exceeding eight weeks, whereas native polysaccharides degrade significantly within days. This stability is attributable to the replacement of the ring oxygen with a methylene group, which eliminates the susceptibility of the phosphodiester linkage to acid- or base-catalyzed hydrolysis. The stabilized analogs retain their conformational integrity and immunoreactivity, making them ideal candidates for liquid vaccine formulations.

- introduce first aspect of invention: conjugate of serogroup X capsular polysaccharide and carrier molecule

The first aspect of the invention is a glycoconjugate comprising a serogroup X capsular polysaccharide analog covalently attached to a carrier protein, wherein the analog is a synthetic oligosaccharide comprising at least four repeating units of a non-hydrolysable carba-mannosyl phosphate structure, and wherein the carrier protein is selected from CRM197, tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, or a fragment thereof. The conjugate is characterized by a saccharide-to-protein molar ratio of between 5:1 and 50:1, and exhibits no detectable free polysaccharide following purification.

- describe process for preparing conjugate

The process for preparing the conjugate involves the chemical synthesis of the carba-serogroup X oligosaccharide using phosphoramidite-based solid-phase methodology, followed by selective deprotection to expose a terminal amine group for conjugation. The oligosaccharide is then activated by reaction with a bifunctional linker, such as di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, and subsequently coupled to the carrier protein under controlled pH and molar excess conditions to ensure efficient and reproducible conjugation.

- introduce second aspect of invention: processes for preparing conjugate

The second aspect of the invention encompasses novel chemical processes for the preparation of the serogroup X capsular polysaccharide analogs and their conjugates. These processes include oxidation, reductive amination, and direct coupling methods, each optimized for yield, purity, scalability, and reproducibility. The processes enable the production of conjugates with defined degrees of polymerization, controlled acetylation patterns, and minimal impurities, suitable for clinical manufacturing.

- describe first embodiment of second aspect: oxidation and coupling

In a first embodiment, the process comprises the oxidation of primary hydroxyl groups on the terminal sugar residue of the carba-polysaccharide to generate aldehyde functionalities, followed by direct coupling to lysine residues on the carrier protein via reductive amination. This method avoids the use of external linkers and results in conjugates with high homogeneity and minimal structural perturbation.

- describe advantages of first embodiment

The advantages of this embodiment include the absence of synthetic linkers, which eliminates the risk of anti-linker immune responses, simplifies purification, and reduces manufacturing complexity. The resulting conjugates exhibit superior stability, consistent saccharide-to-protein ratios, and high immunogenicity in vivo, with minimal batch-to-batch variation.

- introduce third aspect of invention: pharmaceutical composition

The third aspect of the invention is a pharmaceutical composition comprising the glycoconjugate of the first aspect, a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, and optionally an adjuvant. The composition is formulated as a sterile, isotonic, aqueous solution suitable for parenteral administration, or as a lyophilized powder for reconstitution. The composition is stable for at least 24 months when stored at 2–8°C and retains full immunogenicity after reconstitution.

- describe composition

The composition contains between 1 and 20 μg of the serogroup X glycoconjugate per dose, dissolved in a buffer system comprising sodium phosphate, sodium chloride, and polysorbate 80, adjusted to a pH of 6.5 to 7.5. The composition may further contain aluminum phosphate as an adjuvant at a concentration of 0.1 to 0.5 mg of aluminum per dose. The formulation is free of preservatives and is suitable for single-dose vials or multi-dose containers.

- introduce other aspects of invention: intermediates and uses

Other aspects of the invention include novel synthetic intermediates useful in the preparation of the glycoconjugates, such as protected carba-mannosyl phosphoramidites, partially deprotected oligomers, and linker-derivatized saccharides. The invention further encompasses the use of the glycoconjugates in the manufacture of a medicament for the prevention or treatment of meningococcal disease caused by *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup X.

- describe first embodiment of second aspect: oxidation step

In the first embodiment of the process, the oxidation step is carried out using TEMPO-mediated oxidation in the presence of sodium hypochlorite and sodium bromide, under controlled pH and temperature conditions, to selectively convert terminal primary hydroxyl groups to aldehydes without affecting secondary hydroxyls or phosphodiester linkages. The extent of oxidation is limited to less than 10% of available hydroxyl groups to preserve structural integrity.

- describe first embodiment of second aspect: coupling step

The coupling step involves the reaction of the oxidized oligosaccharide with the carrier protein in an aqueous buffer at pH 7.0–7.5, in the presence of sodium cyanoborohydride, to reduce the Schiff base formed between the aldehyde and lysine ε-amino groups. The reaction is monitored by SDS-PAGE and MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry to confirm conjugation and determine saccharide-to-protein ratios.

- introduce second embodiment of second aspect: reductive amination

The second embodiment of the process employs reductive amination between a terminal amine on the carba-polysaccharide and an aldehyde-functionalized carrier protein. The carrier protein is first modified by periodate oxidation to generate aldehyde groups on its carbohydrate moieties, if present, or through chemical introduction of aldehyde-containing linkers. The oligosaccharide amine is then coupled under reductive conditions to form a stable secondary amine linkage.

- describe second embodiment of second aspect: advantages

The advantages of this embodiment include the ability to utilize carrier proteins with native glycosylation, such as CRM197, without prior chemical modification. The method allows for site-specific conjugation, minimizes protein aggregation, and results in conjugates with high purity and reproducible immunogenic profiles.

- introduce third embodiment of second aspect: reduction, oxidative cleavage, and reductive amination

The third embodiment involves the reduction of the carba-polysaccharide to generate a terminal alcohol, followed by oxidative cleavage of the terminal residue using sodium periodate to produce an aldehyde, and subsequent reductive amination with the carrier protein. This sequence enables controlled depolymerization and functionalization at the reducing end, yielding homogeneous conjugates with defined molecular weights.

- describe third embodiment of second aspect: advantages

This embodiment provides precise control over the size and functionalization of the saccharide antigen, allowing for the generation of conjugates with tailored immunogenicity. The method is particularly suitable for producing conjugates with low molecular weight oligosaccharides that retain high epitope density and binding affinity.

- introduce fourth embodiment of second aspect: reduction, oxidative cleavage, and direct coupling

The fourth embodiment combines reduction and oxidative cleavage as in the third embodiment, but replaces reductive amination with direct coupling via a bifunctional linker. The aldehyde generated by periodate cleavage is reacted with a diamine linker, such as ethylenediamine, followed by conjugation to the carrier protein via NHS ester chemistry.

- describe fourth embodiment of second aspect: advantages

The advantages of this embodiment include the ability to introduce a spacer between the saccharide and protein, which may enhance epitope accessibility and reduce steric hindrance. The method allows for modular conjugation strategies and facilitates purification by exploiting the linker’s physicochemical properties.

- introduce method for assaying serogroup X capsular polysaccharide

The invention further provides a method for assaying the content and integrity of serogroup X capsular polysaccharide in fermentation broths, purification intermediates, or final conjugates. The method involves acid hydrolysis of the polysaccharide to release monomeric units, followed by liquid chromatography with pulsed amperometric detection (HPAEC-PAD) to quantify N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate or its carba analog.

- describe method for assaying serogroup X capsular polysaccharide

The method comprises treating the sample with 0.1 M hydrochloric acid at 80°C for 2 hours to cleave glycosidic and phosphodiester bonds, neutralizing the hydrolysate, and injecting the sample onto a high-performance anion-exchange chromatography column equipped with a PAD detector. The quantification is performed using a calibration curve generated with synthetic N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate or its carba analog as an internal standard, enabling precise determination of saccharide concentration and degradation extent.

- introduce processes and reagents for preparing N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate

The invention further provides novel synthetic processes for the preparation of N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate and its carba analogs, which serve as reference standards for analytical assays. These processes involve regioselective phosphorylation of protected N-acetylglucosamine derivatives using phosphoramidite or phosphotriester chemistry, followed by global deprotection and purification by ion-exchange chromatography.

- describe structure of serogroup X capsular polysaccharide

The serogroup X capsular polysaccharide is a linear homopolymer of (1→6)-linked α-N-acetyl-D-mannosamine-1-phosphate, with variable O-acetylation at the C-3 and C-4 hydroxyl positions. The natural polymer has an average degree of polymerization ranging from 10 to 20, but its instability renders it unsuitable for long-term storage. The synthetic analogs of the invention retain this repeating unit structure but replace the ring oxygen with a methylene group, resulting in a non-hydrolysable backbone.

- describe purification of capsular polysaccharide

Purification of the capsular polysaccharide from bacterial fermentation broths involves centrifugation, ultrafiltration, ethanol precipitation, and sequential chromatography on anion-exchange and size-exclusion resins. The purified polysaccharide is characterized by NMR, HPAEC-PAD, and SDS-PAGE to confirm identity, purity, and molecular weight distribution.

- describe modification of capsular polysaccharide

Modification of the capsular polysaccharide includes selective O-acetylation using acetic anhydride and imidazole under controlled conditions to achieve an acetylation level of 50–80%, mimicking the natural antigen. The acetylation pattern is confirmed by 1H and 13C NMR spectroscopy and mass spectrometry.

- describe fragmentation of capsular polysaccharide

Fragmentation of the polysaccharide is achieved by controlled acid hydrolysis or enzymatic digestion to obtain oligomers of defined length. The fragments are purified by gel filtration chromatography and characterized by MALDI-TOF and HPAEC-PAD to determine degree of polymerization and purity.

- describe sizing of polysaccharide

Sizing of the polysaccharide is performed by ultrafiltration using membranes with molecular weight cut-offs of 10 kDa, 30 kDa, or 100 kDa to isolate fractions with desired molecular weight distributions. The size distribution is confirmed by SEC-MALS and dynamic light scattering.

- introduce carrier molecules

Carrier molecules suitable for use in the invention include CRM197, tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, outer membrane protein complex, flagellin, and recombinant protein fragments containing T-helper epitopes. The carrier must be non-toxic, immunogenic, and capable of forming stable covalent bonds with the saccharide.

- describe preferred carrier proteins

The preferred carrier protein is CRM197, a non-toxic mutant of diphtheria toxin, due to its proven safety profile, high immunogenicity, and compatibility with existing vaccine manufacturing platforms. Other preferred carriers include tetanus toxoid and recombinant detoxified diphtheria toxin.

- describe other suitable carrier proteins

Other suitable carrier proteins include outer membrane vesicles, recombinant hepatitis B surface antigen, and synthetic peptides containing CD4+ T-cell epitopes from tetanus or diphtheria toxins. These carriers may be used in combination or sequentially to enhance immune responses in populations with pre-existing immunity to traditional carriers.

- describe oxidation step

The oxidation step is performed using TEMPO (2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidin-1-oxyl) in combination with sodium hypochlorite and sodium bromide as co-catalysts, in an aqueous buffer at pH 6.5–7.5, at 0–25°C for 1–4 hours. The reaction is monitored by HPLC and stopped by addition of sodium sulfite.

- conclude disclosure of invention

The invention thus provides a novel class of stabilized, synthetic glycoconjugate vaccines for the prevention of meningococcal disease caused by *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup X. The conjugates exhibit superior stability, reproducible immunogenicity, and compatibility with liquid formulation, enabling their deployment in global immunization programs without reliance on cold chain infrastructure.

- introduce oxidation of hydroxyl groups

Oxidation of hydroxyl groups on the saccharide is achieved using TEMPO-based systems, periodate, or other selective oxidants to generate aldehyde or carboxylic acid functionalities for subsequent coupling. The reaction is performed under mild conditions to avoid degradation of the phosphodiester backbone.

- describe TEMPO-mediated oxidation

TEMPO-mediated oxidation selectively targets primary hydroxyl groups, converting them to aldehydes without affecting secondary hydroxyls or ester linkages. The reaction is highly reproducible, scalable, and compatible with aqueous environments, making it ideal for conjugation chemistry.

- outline oxidative cleavage

Oxidative cleavage is performed using sodium periodate to cleave vicinal diols, generating aldehyde termini on saccharide fragments. This method is particularly useful for generating defined oligomers from longer polysaccharide chains.

- describe reduction of polysaccharides

Reduction of polysaccharides is carried out using sodium borohydride to convert aldehydes to alcohols, or to reduce imine intermediates formed during conjugation. The reduction is performed in aqueous buffer at pH 6–8 to preserve saccharide integrity.

- outline reductive amination

Reductive amination is a key conjugation strategy in the invention, involving the formation of a Schiff base between an aldehyde on the saccharide and a primary amine on the carrier protein, followed by reduction with sodium cyanoborohydride to form a stable secondary amine linkage.

- describe reductive amination in third embodiment

In the third embodiment, reductive amination is performed after oxidative cleavage of the terminal residue, generating an aldehyde at the reducing end of the oligosaccharide. The aldehyde is then coupled to lysine residues on CRM197 under controlled pH and molar excess conditions.

- describe reductive amination in fourth embodiment

In the fourth embodiment, reductive amination is performed between a linker molecule bearing a primary amine and an aldehyde generated on the saccharide, followed by activation of the linker’s second amine with NHS ester chemistry for coupling to the carrier protein.

- outline coupling to a carrier molecule

Coupling to a carrier molecule is achieved through covalent bonds formed between functional groups on the saccharide and amino, thiol, or hydroxyl groups on the carrier protein. The coupling is performed in aqueous buffer at physiological pH to preserve protein structure.

- describe direct coupling

Direct coupling refers to the covalent attachment of the saccharide to the carrier protein without the use of a bifunctional linker. This is achieved via reductive amination between aldehyde groups on the saccharide and lysine residues on the protein.

- describe coupling via a linker

Coupling via a linker involves the use of a bifunctional molecule that contains one reactive group for the saccharide and another for the carrier protein. The linker may be homobifunctional or heterobifunctional, and may contain spacers to enhance epitope accessibility.

- outline attachment of polysaccharide or linker-derivatised polysaccharide

Attachment of the polysaccharide or linker-derivatized polysaccharide to the carrier protein is confirmed by SDS-PAGE, MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry, and size-exclusion chromatography. The degree of conjugation is quantified by UV spectroscopy, BCA assay, and HPAEC-PAD.

- describe reductive amination for direct coupling

Reductive amination for direct coupling is performed by incubating the oxidized saccharide with the carrier protein in the presence of sodium cyanoborohydride at pH 7.0–7.5 for 16–24 hours. The reaction is quenched by dialysis or ultrafiltration to remove unreacted components.

- describe coupling via a linker in first embodiment

In the first embodiment, coupling via a linker is achieved by reacting the saccharide with di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, followed by incubation with CRM197. The linker provides a six-carbon spacer between the saccharide and protein, minimizing steric interference.

- describe coupling via a linker in second embodiment

In the second embodiment, the linker is a heterobifunctional molecule containing an NHS ester and a maleimide group. The saccharide is first modified with a thiol-containing moiety, then coupled to the maleimide, and finally conjugated to a cysteine-engineered carrier protein.

- describe coupling via a linker in third embodiment

In the third embodiment, the linker is a polyethylene glycol derivative bearing an NHS ester and a hydrazide group. The saccharide is oxidized to generate aldehydes, which react with the hydrazide, and the NHS ester is then used to couple to lysine residues on the carrier protein.

- outline bifunctional linkers

Bifunctional linkers are molecules possessing two distinct reactive groups, enabling sequential conjugation of the saccharide to the carrier protein. These linkers may be homobifunctional, containing two identical reactive groups, or heterobifunctional, containing two different reactive groups.

- describe homobifunctional linkers

Homobifunctional linkers, such as di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, contain two identical NHS ester groups and are used to crosslink primary amines on the saccharide and carrier protein. These linkers are simple to use but may lead to polymerization if not carefully controlled.

- outline other bifunctional linkers

Other bifunctional linkers include succinimidyl ester-thiol, isothiocyanate-azide, and click chemistry-compatible pairs such as alkyne-azide. These allow for bioorthogonal conjugation strategies that minimize side reactions.

- describe linker moiety

The linker moiety is a chemical spacer that connects the saccharide to the carrier protein. It is selected to be non-immunogenic, water-soluble, and of sufficient length to prevent steric hindrance. Preferred linkers are alkyl chains of 2–12 carbons, polyethylene glycol segments, or amino acid sequences.

- outline particularly useful linker moiety

A particularly useful linker moiety is di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, which provides a six-carbon spacer, is commercially available, and has been extensively validated in licensed vaccines. Its use ensures regulatory compliance and manufacturing consistency.

- describe other chemistries for attaching a linker

Other chemistries for attaching a linker include click chemistry, enzymatic ligation, and metal-catalyzed coupling. These methods offer high specificity and are suitable for complex saccharide structures.

The glycoconjugates of the invention have a polysaccharide-to-protein molar ratio ranging from 5:1 to 50:1, with preferred ratios between 10:1 and 30:1. This range ensures optimal immunogenicity without inducing carrier suppression or excessive aggregation.

## Particular Embodiments of the Invention

- define conjugate of Neisseria meningitidis serogroup X capsular polysaccharide and carrier molecule

The conjugate comprises a synthetic carba analog of the serogroup X capsular polysaccharide, wherein the analog is an oligomer of at least six repeating units of (1→6)-linked 2-acetamido-2-deoxy-β-D-mannopyranosyl phosphate with a methylene substitution at the ring oxygen, covalently linked to CRM197 via a reductive amination linkage.

- describe process for obtaining conjugate

The process involves synthesis of the carba oligomer using phosphoramidite chemistry, oxidation of terminal hydroxyls to aldehydes, and coupling to CRM197 in the presence of sodium cyanoborohydride.

- specify oxidation of primary hydroxyl group in capsular polysaccharide

Oxidation is limited to the terminal primary hydroxyl group of the reducing end, using TEMPO/NaOCl under controlled pH.

- specify extent of oxidation

The extent of oxidation is less than 10% of available hydroxyl groups, as determined by NMR and HPLC.

- specify TEMPO-mediated oxidation

TEMPO-mediated oxidation is performed in aqueous buffer at pH 7.0 using 0.1 mM TEMPO, 10 mM NaBr, and 50 mM NaOCl for 2 hours at 25°C.

- specify direct coupling

Direct coupling is achieved without the use of a bifunctional linker, by reductive amination between the aldehyde on the saccharide and lysine residues on CRM197.

- specify reductive amination

Reductive amination is performed using sodium cyanoborohydride at a 5-fold molar excess over aldehyde groups, at pH 7.2 for 16 hours.

- describe alternative process for obtaining conjugate

An alternative process involves the use of a bifunctional linker, such as di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, to couple the saccharide to CRM197.

- specify coupling via linker

Coupling via linker involves first reacting the saccharide with the NHS ester of the linker, then reacting the activated linker with CRM197.

- describe alternative process for obtaining conjugate

An alternative process involves reductive amination between a terminal amine on the saccharide and an aldehyde introduced on CRM197 via periodate oxidation.

- specify location of primary amine group

The primary amine group is introduced at the reducing end of the oligosaccharide by reductive amination of a terminal aldehyde with ammonia.

- specify reductive amination with bifunctional linker

Reductive amination with a bifunctional linker involves coupling the saccharide to one end of the linker via reductive amination, and the other end to CRM197 via NHS ester chemistry.

- specify X₁ and X₂ groups

X₁ is a carboxylic acid group, and X₂ is an NHS ester group, connected by a six-carbon alkyl chain.

- specify coupling via linker

Coupling via linker is performed in two sequential steps: first, the saccharide is activated with the linker; second, the activated saccharide is mixed with CRM197.

- specify bifunctional linker

The bifunctional linker is di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate, with the structure HOOC-(CH₂)₄-COO-NHS.

- specify homobifunctional linker

The homobifunctional linker contains two identical NHS ester groups and is used to crosslink two primary amines.

- specify N-oxysuccinimide

N-oxysuccinimide is the leaving group in NHS ester chemistry, facilitating nucleophilic attack by amines.

- specify L

L is a linker moiety comprising a six-carbon aliphatic chain terminated by NHS ester groups.

- specify L²

L² is a polyethylene glycol chain of 12 ethylene oxide units, terminated by an NHS ester and a thiol group.

- specify capsular polysaccharide as oligosaccharide

The capsular polysaccharide is an oligosaccharide with a degree of polymerization of 6 to 10.

- specify degree of polymerisation

The degree of polymerization is determined by MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry and is between 6 and 10 repeating units.
