## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to magnetic nanoparticles coated with amphiphilic block copolymers, and more specifically to magnetic nanoparticle polymer complexes that can be remotely actuated by low or super-low frequency alternating magnetic fields to selectively disrupt the cytoskeletal architecture of cancerous cells while sparing non-malignant cells. The invention encompasses compositions and methods for delivering these complexes to tumor sites, activating them through non-thermal magnetic stimulation, and leveraging the differential mechanical properties of cancer versus healthy cells to induce targeted cell death. The technology is particularly useful in oncology for the treatment of solid tumors, including but not limited to breast, lung, pancreatic, and prostate cancers, where conventional therapies are limited by systemic toxicity or resistance. The magnetic nanoparticle polymer complexes are engineered to accumulate intracellularly, form dense physical networks within lysosomal compartments, and transmit mechanical forces to surrounding cytoskeletal elements upon exposure to magnetic fields that are too low to induce significant heating. This approach represents a paradigm shift in cancer therapy by replacing chemical or thermal cytotoxicity with physical disruption, thereby minimizing off-target effects and overcoming mechanisms of drug resistance.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Magnetic hyperthermia has long been explored as a non-invasive modality for cancer treatment, relying on the conversion of electromagnetic energy into heat by magnetic nanoparticles under alternating magnetic fields. While clinically promising, this approach is fundamentally limited by the requirement for high-frequency fields and high magnetic field amplitudes to generate sufficient thermal energy, which often leads to non-specific heating of healthy tissues, poor penetration depth, and inconsistent temperature distribution within heterogeneous tumors. Furthermore, the therapeutic window is narrow, as excessive heating can cause collateral damage to vasculature and surrounding organs, while insufficient heating fails to induce meaningful cell death. Alternative strategies have sought to exploit the mechanical properties of magnetic nanoparticles, such as torque-induced rotation or oscillation, to disrupt cellular structures without relying on thermal effects. However, these approaches have been hindered by the inability to generate sufficient force at clinically relevant field parameters, the lack of intracellular targeting, and the failure to achieve selectivity between malignant and non-malignant cells. Previous studies have demonstrated that magnetic nanoparticles can influence cytoskeletal dynamics when conjugated to signaling proteins or when exposed to high-frequency gradient fields, but these methods require complex functionalization, high field intensities, or specialized equipment incompatible with clinical deployment. Moreover, the use of large nanoparticles (>100 nm) has been associated with rapid clearance, poor tissue penetration, and limited intracellular accumulation. There remains a critical unmet need for a system that can deliver magnetic nanoparticles to cancer cells with high efficiency, retain them within subcellular compartments, and activate them using low-frequency, low-amplitude magnetic fields to selectively induce mechanical failure in cancer cells based on their inherent biophysical properties, without triggering heat-mediated damage or lysosomal rupture.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel class of polymer-coated magnetic nanoparticles capable of being remotely actuated by low or super-low frequency alternating magnetic fields to selectively disrupt the cytoskeleton of cancer cells while leaving non-malignant cells intact. The magnetic nanoparticle polymer complex comprises a core of iron oxide nanoparticles coated with a pentablock copolymer comprising two polyanionic blocks flanking a central hydrophobic poly(ethylene oxide)-poly(propylene oxide)-poly(ethylene oxide) block, wherein the hydrophobic segment promotes intracellular aggregation and physical anchoring within lysosomes. The complex self-assembles into stable micellar structures with a hydrodynamic diameter between 30 and 70 nanometers, enabling efficient cellular uptake without opsonization or rapid clearance. Upon internalization, the nanoparticles accumulate in lysosomal compartments, where the hydrophobic domains of the copolymer interact to form a dense, physically crosslinked nanogel network that couples the magnetic moment of the iron oxide cores to the surrounding cytoskeletal matrix. When exposed to alternating magnetic fields at frequencies between 30 and 300 Hz and field strengths between 10 and 100 kA/m, the aggregated nanoparticles experience torque and oscillatory motion, transmitting mechanical stress to actin filaments and microtubules anchored to the lysosomal membrane. This mechanical perturbation induces fatigue failure in the cytoskeleton of mechanically compliant cancer cells, leading to loss of structural integrity, cell cycle arrest, and apoptosis, while the stiffer cytoskeleton of healthy cells remains resistant to such forces. The invention further encompasses methods for increasing cellular uptake through optimization of the hydrophilic-lipophilic balance of the copolymer, methods for incorporating therapeutic agents within the micellar core, and methods for obtaining high-contrast biomedical images via the magnetic or fluorescent labeling of the complex. The system enables theranostic applications, combining targeted cancer cell killing with real-time monitoring of nanoparticle distribution and treatment response.

A “polymer” is a macromolecule composed of repeating structural units, and a “block copolymer” is a polymer comprising two or more distinct polymer segments covalently linked together. An “amphiphilic block copolymer” possesses both hydrophilic and hydrophobic segments, enabling self-assembly in aqueous environments. The magnetic nanoparticle polymer complex is defined as a core of iron oxide nanoparticles, typically magnetite or maghemite, coated with a block copolymer that enables colloidal stability, cellular uptake, and mechanical coupling to intracellular structures. The iron oxide nanoparticles have a primary diameter between 5 and 15 nanometers, and the complex as a whole exhibits a hydrodynamic diameter between 30 and 70 nanometers, with a polydispersity index less than 0.2. The block copolymer is a pentablock structure comprising two polyanionic poly(acrylic acid) blocks separated by a central poly(ethylene oxide)-poly(propylene oxide)-poly(ethylene oxide) block, wherein the poly(propylene oxide) segment has a molecular weight between 3,000 and 6,000 Daltons. The polyanionic blocks are covalently attached to the nanoparticle surface via carboxylate or phosphonate anchor groups, and the hydrophobic core facilitates aggregation within lysosomes. The complex forms micelles with a core-shell architecture, wherein the iron oxide nanoparticles are embedded in a hydrophobic domain surrounded by a hydrophilic corona. The grafting density of the copolymer on the nanoparticle surface ranges from 0.5 to 2.0 chains per square nanometer, ensuring sufficient coverage to prevent aggregation while allowing mobility for mechanical coupling. The complex may be prepared in aqueous media by ligand exchange, wherein pre-synthesized iron oxide nanoparticles are incubated with the block copolymer under controlled pH and ionic strength conditions to promote surface binding and self-assembly. The resulting magnetic nanoparticle polymer complex may be further modified with targeting ligands, fluorescent dyes, or therapeutic agents to enhance specificity, imaging capability, or therapeutic potency. The complex may be administered via intravenous, intratumoral, or inhalation routes, and may be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions suitable for parenteral, oral, or topical delivery. The complex is actuated by exposing the subject to an alternating magnetic field at a frequency between 30 and 300 Hz and a field strength between 10 and 100 kA/m for durations ranging from 10 to 60 minutes, either continuously or in pulsed cycles. The field parameters are selected to avoid bulk heating, with temperature increases in the tissue not exceeding 1°C. The mechanism of action involves the generation of mechanical torque on the aggregated nanoparticles, which is transmitted to the actin cytoskeleton, resulting in fatigue-induced filament rupture and subsequent apoptosis. This effect is selective for cancer cells due to their reduced mechanical stiffness compared to non-malignant cells, as demonstrated in breast cancer models including MDA-MB-231 and BT474, while non-tumorigenic MCF10A cells remain viable under identical conditions. The complex may be used for both therapeutic and diagnostic purposes, enabling real-time monitoring of nanoparticle distribution via magnetic resonance imaging or fluorescence imaging, and correlating signal intensity with therapeutic response. The invention further includes a kit comprising the magnetic nanoparticle polymer complex in lyophilized or liquid form, along with instructions for reconstitution, administration, and magnetic field application, suitable for use in clinical or preclinical settings.
