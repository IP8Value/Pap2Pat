## FIELD

The invention relates to novel small molecule modulators of the C-X-C chemokine receptor type 4 (CXCR4), including agonists, antagonists, and inverse agonists, and to their use in the modulation of CXCR4-mediated signaling pathways. More particularly, the invention encompasses chemically distinct, synthetically accessible compounds that bind to the CXCR4 receptor with high specificity and modulate its functional activity in a manner distinct from known ligands such as stromal cell-derived factor-1 (SDF-1) or AMD3100. These compounds are useful in the treatment of diseases characterized by aberrant CXCR4 signaling, including cancer metastasis, inflammatory disorders, autoimmune conditions, and hematopoietic stem cell mobilization deficiencies. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions containing these compounds, methods of administering them, and kits for their diagnostic or therapeutic use.

## BACKGROUND

The C-X-C chemokine receptor type 4, or CXCR4, is a G protein-coupled receptor that plays a central role in embryonic development, immune cell trafficking, and tissue homeostasis. Its natural ligand, the chemokine CXCL12 (also known as SDF-1), binds with high affinity to CXCR4 and triggers intracellular signaling cascades involving Gαi proteins, β-arrestins, and downstream effectors such as ERK, AKT, and calcium flux. This receptor-ligand pair is essential for the homing of hematopoietic stem cells to the bone marrow, the migration of lymphocytes during immune surveillance, and the establishment of neural circuits during development. Dysregulation of the CXCR4/CXCL12 axis has been implicated in numerous pathological states, including the metastatic spread of solid tumors, the retention of malignant cells in protective niches, chronic inflammation, and HIV-1 entry into host cells.

To date, only one small molecule CXCR4 antagonist, plerixafor (AMD3100), has received regulatory approval for clinical use, specifically for the mobilization of hematopoietic stem cells in patients with non-Hodgkin lymphoma or multiple myeloma undergoing autologous transplantation. Despite its efficacy, AMD3100 exhibits limited oral bioavailability, requires subcutaneous administration, and is associated with transient gastrointestinal and cardiovascular side effects. Moreover, no small molecule agonist of CXCR4 has been previously reported in the scientific literature, despite the potential therapeutic utility of such agents in conditions requiring enhanced stem cell homing, tissue repair, or neuroprotection. Conventional approaches to CXCR4 ligand discovery have relied heavily on structure-based design informed by the crystallographic binding mode of peptide ligands or the known pharmacophore of AMD3100, which primarily targets a deep, polar pocket within the transmembrane helices of the receptor. However, these strategies have failed to yield compounds capable of inducing agonist activity, likely due to an incomplete understanding of the conformational changes required for receptor activation versus inhibition.

Chemokine signaling is highly context-dependent, with differential engagement of G protein versus β-arrestin pathways leading to divergent biological outcomes. The ability to selectively modulate these pathways—whether by promoting, inhibiting, or biasing receptor signaling—represents a major unmet need in GPCR pharmacology. Previous efforts to identify CXCR4 modulators have focused almost exclusively on antagonists, leaving a significant gap in the therapeutic arsenal for conditions where receptor activation is beneficial. Furthermore, the large size and complex binding interface of the natural ligand SDF-1 have made it difficult to design small molecules that mimic its full functional profile. As a result, there remains a critical need for novel, drug-like CXCR4 modulators with distinct pharmacological profiles, improved pharmacokinetic properties, and the capacity to elicit agonist, inverse agonist, or biased signaling responses.

## SUMMARY

The invention provides a class of novel small molecule modulators of the CXCR4 receptor, including compounds that function as agonists, antagonists, and inverse agonists, with unprecedented selectivity and functional efficacy. These compounds are represented by the general chemical structure of Formula (1), wherein the core scaffold comprises a substituted piperidine or piperazine ring fused to a heteroaryl or aryl moiety, and wherein specific substituents at defined positions confer the ability to bind CXCR4 and modulate its activity. The compounds of Formula (1) bind to the CXCR4 receptor at a site distinct from the orthosteric binding pocket occupied by SDF-1, yet they are capable of eliciting full agonist responses, including intracellular calcium mobilization, ERK phosphorylation, receptor internalization, and chemotaxis, in a manner fully inhibitable by known CXCR4 antagonists. The invention further encompasses specific compounds such as NUCC-390, NUCC-388, NUCC-397, and their pharmaceutically acceptable salts, which demonstrate potent and selective modulation of CXCR4 activity in vitro and in cellular models. The compounds are useful in methods of treating diseases involving dysregulated CXCR4 signaling, including but not limited to metastatic cancer, autoimmune disorders, ischemic injury, and hematopoietic stem cell mobilization failure. The invention also includes methods of modulating CXCR4 activity in a subject by administering an effective amount of a compound of Formula (1), thereby either activating or inhibiting receptor signaling depending on the pharmacological profile of the administered compound.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention is directed to novel small molecule modulators of the CXCR4 receptor that exhibit unique pharmacological profiles, including agonist, antagonist, and inverse agonist activities, which were previously unattainable with existing chemotypes. These compounds are structurally distinct from both the endogenous ligand SDF-1 and the clinically approved antagonist AMD3100, and they interact with CXCR4 at a site that overlaps partially, but is not identical to, the orthosteric binding pocket. The discovery of these compounds was enabled by a dual in silico screening strategy combining ligand-based pharmacophore modeling and structure-based virtual docking, which together identified a set of chemically diverse hits with high predicted binding affinity and functional relevance. Subsequent in vitro validation confirmed that several of these compounds, including NUCC-390, act as potent agonists of CXCR4, inducing intracellular calcium flux, ERK phosphorylation, receptor internalization, and chemotaxis—all hallmark responses of CXCR4 activation. Importantly, these agonist effects are fully reversible and inhibitable by known CXCR4 antagonists, confirming receptor specificity. The compounds of the invention are represented by Formula (1), wherein the core structure comprises a substituted piperidine or piperazine ring linked to a heteroaryl or aryl moiety through a methylene or ethylene bridge. Variable substituents at positions R1 through R6 are selected from hydrogen, alkyl, haloalkyl, hydroxy, alkoxy, amine, amide, carbonyl, cyano, and heterocyclic groups, each of which can be optionally substituted. The ring systems designated as J and L may be selected from phenyl, pyridyl, pyrimidinyl, thiazolyl, oxazolyl, indolyl, quinolinyl, and tetrahydroquinolinyl, each of which may be substituted with one or more functional groups. The linker group G may be a direct bond, —CH2—, —CH2CH2—, —O—, —S—, or —NH—, and the substituent X may be selected from halogen, alkyl, alkoxy, amino, nitro, cyano, and trifluoromethyl. The compounds of Formula (1) are synthesized through multi-step organic transformations involving nucleophilic substitution, reductive amination, amide coupling, and ring-forming cyclizations, with purification by chromatography and characterization by NMR, MS, and HPLC. The compounds are formulated into pharmaceutical compositions comprising a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as lactose, microcrystalline cellulose, starch, polyethylene glycol, or polysorbate 80, and may be administered orally, intravenously, subcutaneously, or via inhalation. Dosage ranges for therapeutic use are determined based on in vivo efficacy in murine models of metastasis and stem cell mobilization, with typical daily doses ranging from 0.1 mg/kg to 100 mg/kg. The compounds may be used as monotherapies or in combination with other agents, including chemotherapeutics, immunomodulators, or radiation therapy. Pharmaceutical kits are provided containing the compound in unit dosage form, along with instructions for use in treating cancer, inflammation, or hematopoietic disorders. The invention further includes methods of modulating CXCR4 activity by administering a compound of Formula (1) to a subject in need thereof, thereby either enhancing or suppressing receptor signaling depending on the compound’s intrinsic activity profile. The compounds of the invention represent the first small molecule agonists of CXCR4 ever identified, and their unique pharmacology opens new avenues for therapeutic intervention in diseases where receptor activation, rather than inhibition, is beneficial.

### EXPERIMENTAL

All compounds used in the experimental studies were obtained from ChemBridge Corporation and were confirmed to be at least 95% pure by analytical liquid chromatography/mass spectrometry (LC/MS) using UV detection at 254 nm. Each compound exhibited a molecular ion peak consistent with its intended structure. Calcium imaging assays were performed using the human melanoma cell line C8161, which expresses high levels of endogenous CXCR4. Cells were loaded with the fluorescent calcium indicator fura-2 AM and stimulated with SDF-1 or test compounds in a fluorometric plate reader. Responses were quantified as the ratio of fluorescence intensities at 340 nm and 380 nm excitation wavelengths, with a response defined as a signal exceeding two standard deviations above baseline. Dose-response curves were generated using concentrations ranging from 1 nM to 100 μM and fitted to the Hill equation using IGOR Pro 6.12 software to determine EC50 or IC50 values. Western blot analysis was performed to assess ERK phosphorylation; cells were treated for 30 minutes with compounds, lysed, and proteins separated by SDS-PAGE before transfer to nitrocellulose membranes and probing with phospho-ERK and total ERK antibodies. Receptor internalization was assessed in HEK293 cells stably expressing CXCR4-YFP fusion protein using confocal microscopy following 2-hour treatment with SDF-1 or NUCC-390. Chemotaxis was measured using a modified Boyden chamber assay with C8161 cells migrating through 8-μm pore membranes toward a chemoattractant gradient. Binding competition assays were performed using 125I-labeled SDF-1α on human Chem-1 cells, with displacement measured by gamma counting. All assays were conducted in triplicate, and data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance was determined using Student’s t-test or one-way ANOVA with post-hoc Tukey correction.

### Annotated Database Creation

The ChemBridge GPCR-focused library, comprising approximately 13,000 compounds, was selected as the primary screening set. Each compound was subjected to conformational analysis using the ConFirm/CatConf module of Discovery Studio 3.1, with energy minimization performed using a modified CHARMm force field and a poling technique that biased sampling toward energetically accessible, non-local-minimum conformations. A maximum energy cutoff of 10 kcal/mol was applied, resulting in an average of 100 low-energy conformers per compound. These conformers were then used as input for subsequent pharmacophore modeling and virtual screening.

### EXAMPLE 2

A set of 162 CXCR4 antagonists with reported IC50 values between 1 nM and 10 μM was compiled from the ChEMBL database (version 13). Cluster analysis using Discovery Studio 3.1 grouped the compounds into five distinct chemotypes based on structural similarity. Two representative compounds from each cluster were selected to form a training set (n=10), and an additional ten compounds formed a test set for validation. A common-feature pharmacophore model was generated using the HypoGen algorithm, resulting in ten hypotheses. The optimal model consisted of two hydrophobic features, two hydrogen bond acceptors, and one positive ionizable feature, with a fit value of 100% for perfect matches. This model was applied to screen the annotated database, yielding 26 compounds with fit values exceeding 85% and conformational energies below 5 kcal/mol. Six compounds were selected for purchase based on synthetic accessibility and commercial availability.

### EXAMPLE 3

The CXCR4 crystal structures (PDB codes 3ODU and 3OE0) were prepared using Prime version 3.8, with corrections to side-chain orientations, protonation states, and charge assignments. A 12 Å³ grid box centered on the bound ligand was generated for Glide docking. The Surflex docking engine was configured with a ligand-based protomol derived from IT1t. Both Glide and Surflex were used to dock 20 known antagonists to validate the docking protocols. The GPCR-focused library of 13,000 compounds was then docked using identical parameters. Compounds with Glide scores below −6.0 and Surflex total scores above 6.0 were retained. Twenty-two compounds showed consistent binding poses and interactions with key residues (Tyr255, Glu288, Asp97, and His281) in both docking engines. Nine of these were selected for purchase based on structural novelty, absence of toxicophores, and synthetic feasibility.

### EXAMPLE 4

Known antagonists were docked into the CXCR4 binding site using Glide-XP and Surflex, and their binding poses were analyzed to identify critical interactions. The same docking protocols were applied to the 13,000-compound library. Hits were filtered for favorable interactions with Tyr255, Glu288, and Asp97, and for low predicted metabolic liability. Fifty-two compounds were selected from Glide and 48 from Surflex, with 22 overlapping. Nine of these were purchased and tested in vitro.

### EXAMPLE 5

Calcium imaging assays were performed on C8161 cells using fura-2 AM. Compounds were tested at 10 μM for initial screening. Four compounds (NUCC-388, NUCC-392, NUCC-397, and NUCC-51420) inhibited SDF-1-induced calcium flux, indicating antagonist activity. Three compounds (NUCC-390, NUCC-398, and NUCC-54118) induced calcium flux independently, indicating agonist activity. Dose-response curves were generated for lead compounds, yielding IC50 values of 0.3 μM for NUCC-388 and EC50 values of 0.8 μM for NUCC-390. All agonist effects were blocked by AMD3100 and NUCC-388, confirming CXCR4 specificity.

### EXAMPLE 6

C8161 cells were treated with SDF-1 or NUCC-390 for 30 minutes, lysed, and analyzed by Western blot for phospho-ERK levels. Both SDF-1 and NUCC-390 induced significant increases in ERK phosphorylation compared to vehicle controls. The magnitude of phosphorylation induced by NUCC-390 was comparable to that induced by SDF-1, confirming downstream signaling activation.

### EXAMPLE 7

HEK293 cells expressing CXCR4-YFP were treated with SDF-1 or NUCC-390 for 2 hours. Confocal microscopy revealed that both ligands induced pronounced internalization of CXCR4-YFP from the plasma membrane into cytoplasmic vesicles. Co-treatment with AMD-3100 prevented internalization and restored membrane localization, suggesting inverse agonist activity. In the absence of any ligand, basal membrane localization was observed, indicating constitutive receptor activity. Chemotaxis assays using Boyden chambers demonstrated that NUCC-390 induced migration of C8161 cells with potency comparable to SDF-1. Compound synthesis followed general procedures involving amide coupling, reductive amination, and cyclization. Intermediates were characterized by MS and 1H NMR. Final compounds were purified by preparative HPLC and confirmed by analytical LC/MS. Synthesis of NUCC-390 involved the coupling of a substituted piperidine carboxylic acid with a pyridyl amine via EDC/HOBt activation, followed by deprotection and purification. MS data for compound 10 showed [M+H]+ at m/z 412.2. 1H NMR data confirmed the structure with characteristic peaks at δ 7.85 (d, J=8.2 Hz), 7.42 (t, J=7.5 Hz), and 3.21 (s, 2H). Compound 11 was synthesized via reductive amination of a pyridyl aldehyde with a piperazine derivative. Compound 12 was prepared by ester hydrolysis and amide formation. General Procedure IV was used to synthesize compound 13 via Suzuki coupling. General Procedure V enabled the synthesis of compound 14a through nucleophilic aromatic substitution. Compound 15 was prepared by cyclization under acidic conditions. Compound 16 was synthesized via catalytic hydrogenation.
