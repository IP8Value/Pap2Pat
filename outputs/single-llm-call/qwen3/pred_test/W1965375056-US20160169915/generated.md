## BACKGROUND

- define autism spectrum disorder  
Autism spectrum disorder is a complex neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent deficits in social communication and interaction, alongside restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities. These behavioral manifestations typically emerge during early childhood and persist throughout life, significantly impacting cognitive, linguistic, and adaptive functioning. The disorder exists along a broad continuum of severity, with individuals exhibiting varying degrees of impairment in verbal and nonverbal communication, social reciprocity, sensory processing, and emotional regulation. Autism spectrum disorder is not attributable to a single etiological factor but arises from the interaction of genetic susceptibility and environmental influences that disrupt neurodevelopmental trajectories during critical prenatal and postnatal windows.

- limitations of current diagnosis  
Current diagnostic approaches for autism spectrum disorder rely primarily on behavioral observation and caregiver-reported developmental histories, evaluated through standardized instruments such as the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule and the Autism Diagnostic Interview-Revised. These methods require trained clinicians and are subject to inter-rater variability, cultural bias, and delays in recognition due to the absence of objective biological markers. As a result, the average age of diagnosis in the United States remains above four years, despite the emergence of observable behavioral signs as early as twelve to eighteen months. This delay precludes timely access to early intervention services, which have been shown to substantially improve long-term outcomes in language acquisition, social engagement, and cognitive development. Furthermore, the subjective nature of behavioral assessment limits diagnostic consistency across diverse populations, geographic regions, and socioeconomic strata, resulting in disparities in identification and access to care.

- motivate early detection  
Early detection of autism spectrum disorder is critical to maximizing the efficacy of behavioral, educational, and therapeutic interventions during periods of heightened neuroplasticity in early childhood. Interventions initiated before the age of three have been associated with significant gains in IQ, language skills, adaptive behavior, and reductions in symptom severity. The ability to identify children at elevated risk for autism spectrum disorder prior to the onset of overt behavioral symptoms would enable proactive support strategies, reduce family stress, and lower long-term societal costs associated with delayed diagnosis and inadequate care. A reliable, objective, and minimally invasive diagnostic tool based on measurable biochemical alterations in biological fluids would represent a transformative advancement in clinical practice, complementing behavioral assessments and facilitating earlier, more equitable access to care.
