## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- The invention pertains to the field of nucleic acid-based molecular recognition and targeted protein analysis, specifically to single-stranded DNA aptamers that bind with high specificity and affinity to the nuclear factor kappa B subunit RelA. These aptamers are engineered to function as selective molecular probes capable of distinguishing between inactive and activated forms of RelA, enabling precise detection, isolation, and quantification of the transcriptionally active state of this key regulatory protein. The technology finds particular utility in the context of cellular signaling pathways governing inflammation, immune response, and oncogenesis, where dynamic changes in RelA activity serve as critical biomarkers. The invention further extends to analytical platforms that integrate aptamer-mediated enrichment with mass spectrometric quantification to achieve unprecedented sensitivity and specificity in measuring low-abundance, post-translationally modified forms of RelA within complex biological matrices such as nuclear and cytoplasmic extracts from human cells.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- Single-stranded DNA aptamers are synthetic oligonucleotides selected through iterative in vitro evolution processes to bind target molecules with high affinity and specificity, often rivaling or exceeding the performance of antibodies. These molecules possess distinct advantages including chemical stability, ease of synthesis and modification, low immunogenicity, and the ability to be engineered for diverse applications ranging from diagnostics to therapeutics. In the context of intracellular signaling proteins, aptamers have been employed to modulate protein function, capture specific protein conformations, and enable the isolation of transiently activated complexes that are otherwise inaccessible to conventional affinity reagents. Previous efforts to target the nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) pathway have yielded a range of nucleic acid ligands, including double-stranded oligonucleotides, thioate-modified aptamers, and RNA-based binders, each designed to interfere with DNA binding or protein-protein interactions. U.S. Patent Application No. 20080261225 discloses the use of modified oligonucleotides to inhibit NF-κB DNA binding activity in vitro and in cell-based assays, but does not describe the selective recognition of activated RelA or the quantification of its post-translationally modified forms. U.S. Pat. No. 6,867,289 describes the use of DNA decoys to sequester NF-κB dimers and suppress gene expression, yet fails to provide a method for isolating or quantifying the specific activated species of RelA within cellular extracts. U.S. Pat. No. 7,309,786 details the application of aptamer-based biosensors for detecting protein biomarkers in serum, but does not address the challenges of intracellular protein state discrimination or the integration of aptamers with stable isotope dilution mass spectrometry for absolute quantification of low-abundance transcription factors. None of these prior disclosures provide a single-stranded DNA aptamer capable of selectively binding the non-IκBα-associated, phosphorylated, and transcriptionally active form of RelA, nor do they describe a method for enriching and quantifying this specific molecular state using tandem affinity purification and selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The invention introduces a novel single-stranded DNA aptamer, designated as SEQ ID NO: 9, which binds with high affinity and specificity to the activated form of the nuclear factor kappa B subunit RelA, but does not recognize RelA when complexed with its inhibitory protein IκBα. This aptamer enables the selective isolation of RelA in its post-translationally modified state, including phosphorylation at serine residues 536 and 276, and its association with the p300 coactivator. The invention further encompasses methods for quantifying the abundance of activated RelA in cellular extracts through the integration of aptamer-based affinity enrichment with stable isotope dilution selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry, thereby allowing for the absolute determination of activated RelA molecules per cell. The aptamer exhibits homology to SEQ ID NO: 21, which represents a conserved structural motif within the variable region critical for binding. The invention provides in vitro and in vivo applications for the aptamer, including its use as a competitive antagonist of RelA-DNA interactions, its ability to inhibit tumor necrosis factor alpha-induced gene expression, and its capacity to block RelA binding to endogenous promoters of inflammatory genes. The invention further describes methods for isolating and purifying activated RelA complexes through aptamer-tandem affinity purification, followed by on-bead trypsin digestion and liquid chromatography–selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry analysis. The method includes the addition of biotin-conjugated aptamer sequences to cellular extracts, capture of bound RelA on streptavidin-coated magnetic beads, release of the bound complex under denaturing conditions, and subsequent quantification of released RelA peptides by mass spectrometry. The invention also introduces a method for improving the sensitivity and specificity of selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry analysis by reducing sample complexity through aptamer-mediated enrichment, thereby enhancing the signal-to-noise ratio and enabling detection at femtomolar concentrations. The invention further provides a method for modulating the innate immune response by administering compositions comprising the aptamer, thereby inhibiting NF-κB-mediated gene expression and suppressing downstream inflammatory signaling.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- The invention is directed to a novel class of single-stranded DNA aptamers that selectively bind to the activated form of the nuclear factor kappa B subunit RelA, enabling the isolation, quantification, and functional modulation of this critical transcriptional regulator. The term “activated RelA” refers to the form of the protein that is free of IκBα inhibition, phosphorylated at key serine residues, and capable of binding to κB DNA motifs and recruiting transcriptional coactivators such as p300. The term “aptamer” refers to a single-stranded oligonucleotide of defined sequence selected through systematic evolution of ligands by exponential enrichment (SELEX) to bind a specific molecular target with high affinity and selectivity. The aptamer designated as P028F4, corresponding to SEQ ID NO: 9, was identified through twelve rounds of selection against recombinant human RelA (amino acids 1–313) and exhibits a dissociation constant of approximately 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ M as determined by surface plasmon resonance. This aptamer binds specifically to RelA in its activated conformation, as demonstrated by its inability to interact with RelA when complexed with IκBα, and its colocalization with nuclear RelA following tumor necrosis factor alpha stimulation. The aptamer competes directly with natural double-stranded κB DNA sequences for binding to RelA, as confirmed by electrophoretic mobility shift assays and competition studies using radiolabeled probes. The invention demonstrates that P028F4 can be used as an affinity reagent to enrich activated RelA complexes from cellular extracts, including those containing phosphorylated RelA and associated p300, while excluding IκBα-bound complexes. This enrichment step is integrated into a quantitative analytical workflow that employs stable isotope-labeled internal standards and selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry to achieve absolute quantification of activated RelA in subcellular fractions. The method involves the addition of biotinylated P028F4 to cell lysates, capture on streptavidin magnetic beads, washing to remove non-specifically bound proteins, on-bead trypsin digestion, and analysis by liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry. The invention further provides for the use of this method to determine the fraction of activated RelA in unstimulated cells, revealing that approximately five percent of total cytoplasmic RelA exists in an activated state even in the absence of external stimuli. The invention also encompasses the use of P028F4 as a therapeutic agent to inhibit NF-κB-dependent gene expression, either by direct transfection into cells or by encapsulation within lipid nanoparticles to enhance cellular uptake. The aptamer may be conjugated to effector molecules such as cytotoxic agents, fluorescent dyes, or contrast agents for imaging applications in diseases characterized by aberrant NF-κB activation, including cancer, autoimmune disorders, and chronic inflammatory conditions. The invention further includes alternative aptamers with binding specificity to RelA, including those sharing homology with SEQ ID NO: 21, and methods for optimizing their binding affinity through sequence truncation, chemical modification, or secondary structure stabilization. The invention provides a comprehensive platform for the detection, quantification, and functional inhibition of activated RelA, representing a significant advancement over prior methods that lack the ability to distinguish between inactive and active forms of this pivotal signaling protein.
