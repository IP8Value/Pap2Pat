## TECHNICAL FIELD

- introduce corneal endothelial cells

Corneal endothelial cells constitute a single layer of specialized epithelial cells lining the posterior surface of the cornea, intimately adherent to Descemet’s membrane. These cells are essential for maintaining corneal transparency by actively regulating the hydration state of the corneal stroma through ion transport and fluid pump mechanisms. Unlike many other epithelial cell types, human corneal endothelial cells exhibit minimal proliferative capacity in vivo following postnatal development, rendering them particularly vulnerable to cumulative loss due to aging, trauma, or disease. Their inability to regenerate effectively under pathological conditions often leads to corneal edema, loss of visual acuity, and ultimately corneal decompensation. The functional integrity of the corneal endothelium is therefore critical to ocular health, and its preservation or restoration represents a central challenge in ophthalmic medicine. The unique anatomical position, limited regenerative potential, and specialized physiological function of these cells necessitate precise methods for their identification, characterization, and therapeutic application. Current clinical strategies for replacing dysfunctional endothelium rely almost exclusively on donor tissue transplantation, a solution constrained by global shortages of viable corneal grafts and the inherent risks of immune rejection. Consequently, there exists a compelling need for alternative approaches that enable the generation of functional corneal endothelial cells from renewable cellular sources, such as pluripotent stem cells, and for reliable biomarkers to assess their quality and suitability for clinical use.

## BACKGROUND ART

- describe corneal endothelial cell damage

Corneal endothelial cell damage arises from a variety of etiologies, including Fuchs’ endothelial corneal dystrophy, pseudoexfoliation syndrome, trauma during intraocular surgery, chronic inflammation, and congenital anomalies. These insults lead to a progressive decline in endothelial cell density, which compromises the barrier and pump functions of the monolayer. As cell density falls below a critical threshold, typically estimated at 500 to 800 cells per square millimeter, the remaining cells are unable to maintain stromal deturgescence, resulting in fluid accumulation, stromal swelling, loss of corneal clarity, and the formation of characteristic guttae or excrescences on Descemet’s membrane. The resulting visual impairment manifests as glare, halos, blurred vision, and, in advanced cases, painful epithelial bullae. The irreversible nature of this damage underscores the urgency for regenerative therapies, as the native regenerative capacity of human corneal endothelial cells is negligible in adulthood. While surgical interventions such as penetrating keratoplasty and endothelial keratoplasty remain the standard of care, they are limited by donor tissue availability, surgical complexity, long-term graft survival rates, and the necessity for lifelong immunosuppression in some cases. The lack of effective pharmacological agents to stimulate endogenous repair or prevent further cell loss further exacerbates the clinical burden, leaving transplantation as the only definitive solution for advanced disease.

- limitations of current corneal endothelium regeneration therapy

Current approaches to corneal endothelial regeneration are fundamentally constrained by their dependence on donor tissue, which is in critically short supply worldwide, particularly in low- and middle-income countries. Even in regions with robust eye banking systems, the quality and viability of donor corneas vary significantly, and the time-sensitive nature of tissue procurement imposes logistical challenges. Ex vivo expansion of primary human corneal endothelial cells has proven exceedingly difficult due to their tendency to undergo endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition in culture, leading to loss of characteristic morphology, downregulation of functional markers such as Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and ZO-1, and acquisition of fibroblastic properties. This phenotypic instability renders cultured cells unsuitable for transplantation. Alternative strategies involving transdifferentiation of iris or stromal cells have shown limited success and scalability. While recent advances in stem cell biology have enabled the derivation of corneal endothelial-like cells from embryonic and induced pluripotent stem cells, the absence of robust, universally accepted molecular markers for assessing the fidelity of differentiation has hindered standardization and clinical translation. Without reliable methods to confirm the identity, maturity, and functional competence of derived cells, the risk of transplanting heterogeneous or incompletely differentiated populations remains high, potentially leading to graft failure or aberrant tissue formation. Furthermore, no standardized protocol exists for the production of cell preparations in forms suitable for clinical delivery, such as suspensions, sheets, or three-dimensional spheres, nor are there established criteria for determining when a cell population has reached sufficient maturity for therapeutic administration.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

- identify cells suitable for corneal transplantation

The primary technical problem addressed by this invention is the inability to reliably identify and select a population of cells that possess the phenotypic and functional characteristics of mature, viable human corneal endothelial cells for the purpose of transplantation. Existing methods rely on morphological assessment, which is subjective and insufficiently precise, or on the detection of a limited set of surface markers that are not exclusive to the corneal endothelial lineage and may be expressed in other ocular or non-ocular cell types. This lack of specificity results in the potential transplantation of cells that are immature, partially differentiated, or contaminated with non-endothelial lineages, thereby compromising graft integrity and long-term visual outcomes. There is a critical unmet need for objective, molecularly defined criteria that can unequivocally distinguish a therapeutically competent corneal endothelial cell population from other cell types or transitional states, particularly in the context of cell products derived from pluripotent or progenitor sources.

### Solution to Problem

- provide means for identifying cells suitable for corneal transplantation

The invention provides a novel and objective means for identifying cells suitable for corneal transplantation by measuring the expression levels of specific genes whose transcriptional activity is uniquely and consistently associated with mature human corneal endothelial cells. This method enables the determination of cellular suitability based on the quantitative expression profile of a defined set of genetic markers, thereby eliminating reliance on subjective morphological criteria or non-specific surface markers. The identification of these markers is grounded in genome-wide promoter activity profiling, which reveals transcriptional signatures that are exclusive to corneal endothelial cells across diverse human tissues and cell types.

- provide means for producing cells suitable for corneal transplantation

The invention further provides a means for producing a population of cells suitable for corneal transplantation by culturing pluripotent or progenitor cells under defined conditions that promote differentiation along the corneal endothelial lineage, and by monitoring the expression of the identified genetic markers to determine when the population has achieved a state of maturity equivalent to native corneal endothelial cells. This approach allows for the controlled and reproducible generation of transplantable cell products from renewable sources, circumventing the limitations of donor tissue scarcity.

- define method for producing cell population

The method for producing a cell population suitable for transplantation comprises the steps of initiating culture of stem cells under conditions that support proliferation, followed by transition to conditions that induce differentiation into corneal endothelial cells, and continuing culture until the expression levels of specific genes reach a predetermined reference value indicative of cellular maturity.

- measure gene expression

Gene expression is measured using quantitative techniques capable of detecting transcript abundance with high sensitivity and specificity, including reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction, digital PCR, RNA sequencing, or hybridization-based assays.

- define method for determining cell population suitability

Suitability of the cell population for transplantation is determined by comparing the measured expression levels of the defined set of genes against a reference value derived from the expression profile of native human corneal endothelial cells. A cell population is deemed suitable when the expression levels of the specified genes fall within a defined range that corresponds to the mature endothelial state.

- culture stem cells

Stem cells selected for this process include induced pluripotent stem cells, embryonic stem cells, embryonic germ cells, germline stem cells, and somatic stem cells derived from ocular or neural crest-derived tissues.

- continue culturing until gene expression reaches reference value

Culture is maintained under differentiation-inducing conditions until the expression of the specified genes reaches or exceeds a reference value established from the analysis of native corneal endothelial cells, at which point the population is harvested for transplantation.

- select genes for measurement

The genes selected for measurement include POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B, each of which demonstrates highly specific and elevated expression in mature human corneal endothelial cells relative to all other human tissues and cell types.

- prepare cell population for transplantation

The cell population is prepared for transplantation by harvesting, washing, and formulating into a suspension, a sheet, or a three-dimensional sphere, optionally in combination with a biocompatible scaffold material.

- administer cell population to patient

The prepared cell population is administered to a patient in need of corneal endothelial regeneration via direct injection into the anterior chamber, placement onto Descemet’s membrane, or transplantation as a bioengineered sheet.

- produce suspension, sheet, or sphere

The cell population may be formulated as a suspension in a physiologically compatible medium, as a cohesive sheet supported by a biodegradable substrate, or as a three-dimensional sphere aggregated under low-adhesion conditions, each form optimized for delivery and integration into the host cornea.

- treat patient in need of corneal transplantation

The method is applied to patients suffering from corneal endothelial dysfunction due to dystrophy, trauma, surgical complication, or age-related degeneration, with the goal of restoring corneal transparency and visual function.

- use genes for determining cell population suitability

The genes POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B are used as the primary indicators for determining the suitability of a cell population for transplantation, with POU6F2 serving as the most definitive marker of terminal differentiation.

- provide means for efficiently producing cell population

The invention provides a means for efficiently producing a homogeneous, clinically relevant population of corneal endothelial cells by integrating molecular quality control into the differentiation protocol, thereby reducing trial-and-error culture cycles and improving yield and consistency.

- provide means for determining cell population suitability

The invention provides a means for determining cell population suitability through objective, quantitative, and reproducible measurement of gene expression, enabling standardized evaluation across laboratories and clinical settings.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The invention provides a transformative advancement in corneal regenerative medicine by introducing a molecularly defined, objective, and highly specific method for identifying and producing transplantable corneal endothelial cells. By leveraging the uniquely restricted expression of POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B, the invention enables the precise selection of mature, functional cells while excluding immature or off-target populations, thereby significantly enhancing the safety and efficacy of cell-based therapies. This approach eliminates the subjectivity and variability inherent in morphological assessments, facilitates the standardization of manufacturing protocols, and accelerates the transition from research to clinical application. The ability to monitor differentiation in real time through gene expression profiling allows for dynamic adjustment of culture conditions, optimizing yield and reducing production time. Furthermore, the use of these markers permits the development of quality control assays compatible with regulatory requirements, enabling scalable, GMP-compliant production of corneal endothelial cell products. Ultimately, this invention offers a viable, sustainable, and reliable alternative to donor corneal transplantation, addressing the global shortage of grafts and improving access to sight-restoring therapy for millions of patients worldwide.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

- define cell population

The cell population referred to in this invention consists of a homogeneous or enriched group of cells derived from pluripotent, embryonic, or somatic stem cells that have undergone directed differentiation toward a corneal endothelial cell fate, and which express a defined set of molecular markers indicative of functional maturity.

- explain conditions for inducing differentiation into corneal endothelial cells

Conditions suitable for inducing differentiation into corneal endothelial cells involve the use of serum-free culture media supplemented with specific growth factors and extracellular matrix components that mimic the in vivo microenvironment of the developing corneal endothelium, including laminin-511E8 as a substrate and the absence of serum-derived differentiation inducers that promote mesenchymal transition.

- describe medium used for inducing differentiation

The medium used for inducing differentiation is a defined, serum-free formulation containing a basal nutrient base such as Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium/F12, supplemented with a serum replacement, essential amino acids, antioxidants, and a defined concentration of fibroblast growth factor to support survival and lineage commitment without inducing aberrant differentiation.

- specify serum-free medium

The medium is substantially free of fetal bovine serum or any other animal-derived serum, thereby minimizing batch-to-batch variability and reducing the risk of immunogenic contamination or unintended differentiation signals.

- list examples of usable media

Examples of usable media include Knockout Serum Replacement-supplemented DMEM/F12, StemPro-34, and E8 medium, each of which has been modified to include laminin-511E8 coating and controlled concentrations of bFGF.

- describe serum replacement

The serum replacement is a chemically defined substitute for serum, comprising insulin, transferrin, selenium, and other essential nutrients, and is free of undefined growth factors or lipids that could interfere with lineage specification.

- list substances added to medium for cell maintenance and growth

Substances added to the medium for cell maintenance and growth include L-glutamine, non-essential amino acids, 2-mercaptoethanol, penicillin, streptomycin, and recombinant human basic fibroblast growth factor at concentrations ranging from 1 to 10 ng/mL.

- describe differentiation inducers

Differentiation inducers refer to exogenous agents such as transforming growth factor-beta, bone morphogenetic proteins, or Wnt pathway modulators that are capable of directing pluripotent cells toward neural crest or mesodermal fates, which are precursors to corneal endothelial lineage.

- specify substantially free of differentiation inducers

The culture medium is substantially free of exogenous differentiation inducers during the maintenance phase, and only introduced in a controlled, time-limited manner during the induction phase to ensure precise temporal regulation of differentiation.

- describe differentiation induction promoters

Differentiation induction promoters include extracellular matrix proteins such as fibronectin, collagen IV, or vitronectin, which may enhance cell adhesion and signaling during the early stages of lineage specification.

- specify substantially free of differentiation induction promoters

During the final maturation phase, the culture environment is substantially free of extracellular matrix promoters other than laminin-511E8 to prevent phenotypic drift and ensure the acquisition of authentic corneal endothelial characteristics.

- describe preferred medium composition

The preferred medium composition consists of DMEM/F12 supplemented with 20% Knockout Serum Replacement, 2 mM L-glutamine, 1% non-essential amino acids, 100 μM 2-mercaptoethanol, 50 U/mL penicillin, 50 μg/mL streptomycin, and 4 ng/mL bFGF, maintained on a laminin-511E8-coated surface.

- specify pH and temperature of medium

The culture medium is maintained at a pH of 7.2 to 7.4 and a temperature of 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% carbon dioxide.

- describe culture time period

The culture time period for differentiation ranges from 14 to 42 days, depending on the cell source and initial density, with gene expression monitoring initiated at day 7 and continued until the reference expression levels are achieved.

- list examples of culture containers

Examples of culture containers include tissue culture flasks, multi-well plates, cell culture dishes, and bioreactor systems, each of which may be coated with laminin-511E8 to support adherence and differentiation.

- describe coating of culture container inner surface

The inner surface of the culture container is coated with laminin-511E8 at a concentration of 0.05 to 0.2 μg/cm², either by direct adsorption or through pre-coated commercial substrates, to provide a physiologically relevant substrate for corneal endothelial cell attachment and polarization.

- specify type of cells to be cultured

The type of cells to be cultured includes human induced pluripotent stem cells, human embryonic stem cells, human embryonic germ cells, human germline stem cells, and somatic stem cells isolated from neural crest-derived tissues.

- describe obtaining iPS cells

Induced pluripotent stem cells are obtained by reprogramming somatic cells such as dermal fibroblasts or peripheral blood mononuclear cells using defined transcription factors, including OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, and c-MYC, delivered via viral vectors, episomal plasmids, or mRNA transfection.

- describe obtaining ES cells, EG cells, GS cells, and somatic stem cells

Embryonic stem cells are derived from the inner cell mass of blastocyst-stage embryos, embryonic germ cells are isolated from primordial germ cells of fetal gonads, germline stem cells are obtained from testicular or ovarian tissues, and somatic stem cells are isolated from the corneal limbus, iris, or neural crest-derived mesenchyme using enzymatic digestion and fluorescence-activated cell sorting.

- define conditions suitable for culturing corneal endothelial cells

Conditions suitable for culturing corneal endothelial cells include a serum-free, chemically defined medium, a laminin-511E8-coated substrate, a temperature of 37°C, a 5% CO₂ atmosphere, and the absence of exogenous differentiation inducers beyond those required for initial lineage specification.

- specify examples of conditions suitable for culturing corneal endothelial cells

Examples of suitable conditions include culture on laminin-511E8 in Knockout Serum Replacement-containing medium supplemented with bFGF, or culture on FNC coating mix in a medium containing low concentrations of fetal bovine serum during the maturation phase.

- describe isolation of corneal endothelial cells from a cornea

Corneal endothelial cells are isolated from donor corneoscleral rims by enzymatic dissociation using Accutase, followed by mechanical stripping of Descemet’s membrane, and subsequent plating on laminin-511E8-coated surfaces in serum-free medium.

- introduce POU6F2 gene

The POU6F2 gene encodes a POU-domain transcription factor that is specifically and highly expressed in mature human corneal endothelial cells, with expression levels significantly higher than in any other human tissue or cell type, and is upregulated during terminal differentiation.

- introduce LMX1B gene

The LMX1B gene encodes a LIM-homeodomain transcription factor that is expressed in neural crest-derived ocular tissues, including the corneal endothelium, trabecular meshwork, and iris stroma, and is involved in the early specification of the corneal endothelial lineage.

- introduce TFAP2B gene

The TFAP2B gene encodes a transcription factor of the AP-2 family that is expressed in the developing corneal endothelium and other neural crest-derived structures, and is required for the initial commitment of periocular mesenchyme to the endothelial fate.

- describe preparation of cell population cultured under conditions suitable for inducing differentiation into corneal endothelial cells

The preparation of the cell population involves the sequential culture of stem cells under proliferation conditions followed by transition to differentiation conditions, with continuous monitoring of gene expression to determine the optimal time for harvest.

- specify forms of cell population cultured under conditions suitable for inducing differentiation into corneal endothelial cells

The cell population may be cultured as a monolayer, as a three-dimensional aggregate, or as a sheet attached to a biodegradable scaffold, each form being suitable for subsequent transplantation.

- describe measurement of gene expression in cell population

Measurement of gene expression is performed using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, digital PCR, RNA sequencing, or microarray analysis, with normalization to stable reference genes such as GAPDH or ACTB.

- specify genes to be measured

The genes to be measured include POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B, with POU6F2 being the most critical indicator of terminal maturation.

- describe quantitative measurement of gene expression

Quantitative measurement of gene expression is performed using standardized protocols with calibrated standards, internal controls, and triplicate technical replicates to ensure accuracy and reproducibility.

- specify preferred genes to be measured

The preferred genes to be measured are POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B, with POU6F2 expression being the primary determinant of suitability for transplantation.

- describe use of gene expression as indication of suitability for transplantation

The level of expression of POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B serves as a direct molecular indication of the suitability of the cell population for transplantation, with elevated expression of POU6F2 correlating strongly with functional maturity and endothelial pump activity.

- describe determination of suitability for transplantation based on gene expression

Suitability for transplantation is determined by comparing the measured expression levels of the specified genes to a reference threshold derived from the expression profile of native human corneal endothelial cells, with a population deemed suitable when expression levels meet or exceed 80% of the reference value.

- describe further culture of cell population under conditions suitable for inducing differentiation into corneal endothelial cells

If the expression levels of the specified genes have not reached the reference threshold, the cell population is subjected to further culture under the same differentiation-inducing conditions until the required expression levels are achieved.

- describe use of cell population as cell preparation for regenerating corneal endothelium

The cell population, once confirmed to express the specified genes at reference levels, is used as a cell preparation for regenerating the corneal endothelium in patients with endothelial dysfunction.

- specify components of cell preparation

The cell preparation comprises the differentiated cell population suspended in a physiologically compatible medium, optionally combined with a scaffold material, a solubilizing agent, or a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.

- describe scaffold materials and components

Scaffold materials include biodegradable polymers such as polyglycolic acid, polylactic acid, or collagen membranes, which provide structural support and facilitate cell delivery and integration into the host cornea.

- describe solubilizing agents

Solubilizing agents include hyaluronic acid, methylcellulose, or carboxymethylcellulose, which enhance cell suspension stability and reduce aggregation during injection.

- describe other pharmaceutically acceptable carriers

Other pharmaceutically acceptable carriers include balanced salt solutions, phosphate-buffered saline, or Ringer’s lactate, each formulated to maintain cell viability and osmotic balance during administration.

- specify subjects to whom cell preparation is administered

The cell preparation is administered to human subjects suffering from corneal endothelial decompensation due to Fuchs’ dystrophy, post-surgical endothelial failure, trauma, or congenital anomalies.

- describe administration form of cell preparation

The administration form of the cell preparation is selected from a suspension injected into the anterior chamber, a sheet transplanted onto Descemet’s membrane, or a three-dimensional sphere implanted via minimally invasive techniques.

- introduce Method B for determining suitability for transplantation

Method B for determining suitability for transplantation involves the quantitative measurement of gene expression in a cell population derived from stem cells, followed by comparison to a reference expression profile established from native human corneal endothelial cells.

- describe step of measuring gene expression in Method B

The step of measuring gene expression in Method B involves extracting total RNA from the cell population, synthesizing complementary DNA, and quantifying transcript levels of POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B using digital PCR or RNA sequencing.

- specify genes to be measured in Method B

The genes to be measured in Method B are POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B, with POU6F2 being the most predictive of functional maturity.

- describe use of gene expression as indication of suitability for transplantation in Method B

In Method B, the expression levels of POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B serve as a molecular signature indicating the degree of differentiation and functional readiness of the cell population for transplantation.

- describe determination of suitability for transplantation based on gene expression in Method B

Suitability for transplantation in Method B is determined when the expression levels of the three genes collectively exceed a predefined threshold derived from the median expression observed in native corneal endothelial cells.

- describe further culture of cell population under conditions suitable for inducing differentiation into corneal endothelial cells in Method B

If the gene expression levels do not meet the threshold, the cell population is returned to differentiation conditions for an additional period, with re-assessment performed at intervals not exceeding seven days.

- describe use of cell population as cell preparation for regenerating corneal endothelium in Method B

The cell population, upon confirmation of gene expression levels meeting the reference threshold, is formulated into a cell preparation and administered to a patient to regenerate the corneal endothelium.

- conclude Method B

Method B provides a standardized, objective, and reproducible protocol for determining the suitability of stem cell-derived corneal endothelial cells for transplantation, thereby ensuring the clinical safety and efficacy of regenerative therapies.
