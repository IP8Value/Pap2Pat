# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

DNA repair mechanisms are fundamental to the maintenance of genomic integrity across all living organisms. In mammalian cells, the faithful preservation of genetic information is continuously challenged by endogenous and exogenous sources of DNA damage, including ionizing radiation, reactive oxygen species, and replication errors. To counteract these threats, evolution has conserved multiple DNA repair pathways that act in concert to detect, signal, and resolve DNA lesions. Among these, the repair of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) is particularly critical, as unrepaired or misrepaired DSBs can lead to chromosomal rearrangements, loss of genetic material, cellular senescence, or malignant transformation. Two primary pathways mediate the repair of DSBs: non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) and homology-directed repair (HDR). NHEJ operates throughout the cell cycle but is most dominant in the G1 phase, where it directly ligates broken DNA ends without requiring a homologous template. This process is rapid and efficient but inherently error-prone, often resulting in small insertions or deletions at the break site due to end processing prior to ligation. The core components of classical NHEJ include the Ku70 and Ku80 heterodimer, which bind and stabilize broken DNA ends, and DNA ligase IV (LIG4), which catalyzes the final ligation step in complex with XRCC4 and XLF. In contrast, HDR is a high-fidelity repair mechanism that utilizes a homologous DNA sequence—typically the sister chromatid—as a template to restore the original sequence with precision. HDR is restricted to the S and G2 phases of the cell cycle when a sister chromatid is available and requires a suite of proteins including BRCA1, BRCA2, RAD51, RAD50, and CtIP to facilitate end resection, strand invasion, and DNA synthesis. Despite its accuracy, HDR is significantly less efficient than NHEJ in most mammalian somatic cells, limiting its utility for precise genome editing applications. The imbalance between these two pathways has long been recognized as a major bottleneck in the development of therapeutic genome editing strategies, where the goal is to achieve targeted gene correction rather than random mutagenesis. While chemical inhibitors of NHEJ components, cell cycle synchronization, and fusion proteins that tether Cas9 to HDR-promoting factors have been explored, these approaches often suffer from off-target effects, transient activity, cytotoxicity, or limited applicability in vivo. There remains a critical need for a genetically encoded, programmable, and reversible system that can simultaneously enhance HDR while suppressing NHEJ, without compromising cell viability or requiring exogenous small molecules.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides novel compositions and methods for enhancing homology-directed repair (HDR) efficiency in mammalian cells through the coordinated modulation of key DNA repair pathway components using catalytically inactive guide RNAs and a single active Cas9 nuclease. The invention comprises a set of engineered vectors designed to simultaneously activate genes involved in HDR and repress genes involved in NHEJ, thereby shifting the balance of DSB repair toward precise genome editing. A first vector comprises a dead guide RNA (dgRNA) scaffold fused to MS2 RNA aptamers, operably linked to a promoter capable of driving expression in eukaryotic cells, and further encoding a fusion protein comprising the MS2 coat protein fused to the transcriptional activation domains P65 and HSF1, such that the dgRNA directs the activation complex to the promoter region of an HDR-related gene, thereby upregulating its transcription. A second vector comprises a dgRNA scaffold fused to Com RNA aptamers, operably linked to a promoter, and encoding a fusion protein comprising the Com protein fused to the KRAB repressive domain, such that the dgRNA directs the repression complex to the promoter region of an NHEJ-related gene, thereby downregulating its transcription. A third vector comprises a nonfunctional green fluorescent reporter gene containing a frameshift mutation within its coding sequence, flanked by a constitutive promoter and a polyadenylation signal, such that restoration of the reading frame via HDR results in detectable fluorescence. A fourth vector comprises a reverse tetracycline-controlled transactivator (rtTA) sequence under the control of a constitutive promoter, operably linked to a TRE3G promoter that drives expression of either the dgRNA-MS2:MPH or dgRNA-Com:CK constructs in the presence of doxycycline. A fifth vector comprises a dgRNA targeting the coding region or promoter of CDK1 or CDK2, fused to MS2 aptamers and linked to the MPH activation complex, to enhance end resection and HDR initiation. A sixth composition comprises the above vectors in combination with a separate expression vector encoding a functional Cas9 nuclease and a single guide RNA (sgRNA) targeting a genomic locus of interest. A seventh composition comprises a eukaryotic cell stably transfected or transduced with one or more of the above vectors, wherein the cell exhibits enhanced HDR efficiency upon induction of Cas9-mediated double-strand break formation. A method of enhancing HDR in a cell comprises introducing into the cell a first vector encoding a dgRNA-MS2:MPH complex targeting an HDR gene, a second vector encoding a dgRNA-Com:CK complex targeting an NHEJ gene, and a third vector encoding Cas9 and an sgRNA targeting a genomic site, thereby increasing the frequency of precise gene editing events. A further method comprises introducing an activation plasmid encoding dgRNA-MS2:MPH targeting CDK1 and an repression plasmid encoding dgRNA-Com:CK targeting KU80, followed by induction of Cas9 activity. A method utilizing both activation and repression plasmids together achieves synergistic enhancement of HDR efficiency beyond that observed with either plasmid alone. A composition comprising two distinct vectors—one encoding dgRNA-MS2:MPH targeting an HDR gene and the other encoding dgRNA-Com:CK targeting an NHEJ gene—further enhances the precision and magnitude of HDR modulation. A kit for performing precise genome editing comprises one or more of the above vectors, a Cas9 expression vector, an HDR donor template, and instructional material detailing the sequence of the dgRNAs, the timing of transfection, the use of inducible systems, and methods for quantifying HDR efficiency.

### Description

CRISPR systems have revolutionized the field of genome engineering by enabling precise, programmable manipulation of DNA sequences. The Cas9 endonuclease, guided by a single guide RNA (sgRNA), induces a site-specific double-strand break that is subsequently repaired by either non-homologous end joining or homology-directed repair. While NHEJ is the dominant repair pathway in most mammalian cell types, resulting in unpredictable insertions or deletions, HDR enables precise gene correction when a homologous donor template is provided. However, the inefficiency of HDR relative to NHEJ has severely limited the therapeutic potential of CRISPR-based gene editing. Conventional strategies to enhance HDR have relied on chemical inhibitors of NHEJ components, synchronization of the cell cycle, or fusion of Cas9 with HDR-promoting proteins. These approaches are often transient, cytotoxic, or incompatible with in vivo delivery. The present invention overcomes these limitations by repurposing the CRISPR system itself to simultaneously modulate the expression of key DNA repair genes. By engineering catalytically inactive guide RNAs (dgRNAs) that retain target binding but lack cleavage activity, the invention enables the recruitment of transcriptional activators or repressors to the promoters of HDR and NHEJ genes, respectively. This dual modulation—activation of HDR-promoting genes such as CDK1 and CtIP, and repression of NHEJ-promoting genes such as KU70, KU80, and LIG4—shifts the cellular repair machinery toward high-fidelity repair without the need for exogenous small molecules. The use of a single active Cas9 nuclease to perform both genome editing and transcriptional regulation represents a significant advance in genetic control, enabling multiplexed, temporally coordinated manipulation of multiple pathways with minimal genetic payload. This approach is broadly applicable across cell types and can be adapted to inducible systems for precise temporal control, making it ideal for both ex vivo cell therapies and in vivo applications.

### Compositions

The invention encompasses a series of engineered nucleic acid compositions designed to enhance homology-directed repair through transcriptional modulation of DNA repair genes. A first composition comprises a vector encoding a dead guide RNA (dgRNA) scaffold containing MS2 RNA aptamers, operably linked to a U6 or H1 promoter for RNA polymerase III-driven expression, and further encoding a fusion protein comprising the MS2 coat protein fused to the transcriptional activation domains P65 and HSF1 (MPH). This vector is configured to target the promoter region of an HDR-related gene, such as CDK1, CtIP, BRCA1, BRCA2, RAD51, or RAD50, thereby increasing their transcriptional output. A second composition comprises a vector encoding a dgRNA scaffold containing Com RNA aptamers, operably linked to a U6 or H1 promoter, and encoding a fusion protein comprising the Com protein fused to the KRAB repressive domain (CK), configured to target the promoter region of an NHEJ-related gene, such as LIG4, KU70, KU80, or XRCC4, thereby reducing their expression. A third composition comprises an inducible version of either the activation or repression vector, wherein the expression of the MPH or CK fusion protein is under the control of a TRE3G promoter, which is activated by the reverse tetracycline-controlled transactivator (rtTA) in the presence of doxycycline. The rtTA is expressed from a constitutive promoter such as CMV or EF1α, enabling reversible, dose-dependent control of HDR and NHEJ modulation. A fourth composition comprises a traffic light reporter vector containing a nonfunctional green fluorescent protein variant (bf-Venus) with a frameshift mutation in codons 53–63, fused in-frame to a self-cleaving T2A peptide and a red fluorescent protein (fs-mCherry) containing a two-base-pair frameshift mutation. This reporter enables simultaneous quantification of HDR (restoration of Venus fluorescence) and NHEJ (restoration of mCherry fluorescence) outcomes following Cas9-mediated cleavage. The entire cassette is flanked by a CMV promoter and SV40 polyadenylation signal for robust expression in mammalian cells. Suitable promoters for bacterial expression include T7, T3, and SP6, while eukaryotic expression is enabled by CMV, EF1α, CAG, or PGK promoters. Additional components of the vectors include nuclear localization signals (NLS) fused to the Cas9 or fusion proteins, flexible peptide linkers between domains, and polyadenylation signals to ensure mRNA stability. The vectors may be delivered as plasmids, viral vectors, or synthetic RNA constructs, and may be packaged into lentiviral, adeno-associated viral, or adenoviral systems for efficient delivery into primary cells or in vivo tissues.

### Methods

The invention provides methods for enhancing homology-directed repair and/or decreasing non-homologous end joining in a cell by introducing a composition comprising a first vector encoding a dgRNA-MS2:MPH complex targeting an HDR gene, a second vector encoding a dgRNA-Com:CK complex targeting an NHEJ gene, and a third vector encoding a functional Cas9 nuclease and a single guide RNA targeting a genomic locus of interest. The method further comprises transfecting or transducing the cell with these vectors, followed by induction of Cas9 activity through expression or delivery of a Cas9 expression plasmid or ribonucleoprotein complex. The dgRNAs are designed to bind within 200 base pairs upstream of the transcription start site of the target gene, ensuring effective modulation without disrupting coding sequences. The activation plasmid may target CDK1, CtIP, BRCA1, or RAD51, while the repression plasmid may target KU70, KU80, or LIG4. The method may further comprise the use of an inducible system, wherein the expression of the MPH or CK fusion proteins is controlled by a TRE3G promoter activated by doxycycline, allowing temporal control over HDR enhancement. The method may further comprise delivering the vectors via viral transduction, electroporation, lipofection, or microinjection. The Cas9 protein may be derived from Streptococcus pyogenes, Staphylococcus aureus, or other orthologs, and may be fused to nuclear localization signals or fluorescent tags for tracking. The sgRNA may be delivered as a plasmid, RNA, or ribonucleoprotein complex. The method may further comprise co-delivery of a homologous donor template containing the desired sequence modification flanked by homology arms of at least 400 base pairs. The method may be performed in vitro, ex vivo, or in vivo, and may be applied to primary human cells, stem cells, or somatic tissues. The method may further comprise analyzing HDR efficiency by flow cytometry, PCR, or next-generation sequencing to confirm precise integration of the donor sequence. The method may further comprise administering the composition to a subject, wherein the subject is a mammal, and wherein the composition is delivered by intravenous, intramuscular, or local injection. The method may further comprise isolating cells from a subject, modifying them ex vivo using the composition, and reintroducing them into the subject as an autologous cell therapy.

## Experimental Examples

### Example 1

The invention was tested by constructing a dgRNA-MS2:MPH vector targeting the promoter region of CDK1 and a dgRNA-Com:CK vector targeting the promoter region of KU80. The MS2 and Com binding loops were engineered into the dgRNA scaffold using synthetic DNA fragments cloned into pUC57 backbone vectors. These constructs were first validated in an EGFP reporter system, where dgRNA targeting EGFP resulted in robust transcriptional activation or repression, confirming functionality. Subsequent testing on endogenous genes ASCL1 and HBG1 demonstrated significant upregulation upon dgRNA-MS2:MPH delivery and downregulation upon dgRNA-Com:CK delivery. The system was then applied to modulate HDR and NHEJ genes: CDK1 and CtIP were activated, while LIG4, KU70, and KU80 were repressed, resulting in three-fold upregulation of CDK1 and CtIP mRNA and 40–50% reduction in LIG4, KU70, and KU80 mRNA levels. A traffic light reporter (TLR) system was stably integrated into HEK293 cells expressing Cas9, comprising a broken Venus gene, T2A peptide, and frameshifted mCherry. Upon Cas9-mediated cleavage at the Venus site, HDR restoration of Venus fluorescence was quantified alongside NHEJ restoration of mCherry. In the control group, 2.42% of cells exhibited HDR (Venus+) and 6.82% exhibited NHEJ (mCherry+). Upon delivery of dgCDK1-2:MS2:MPH and dgKU80-1:Com:CK, HDR increased to 15.4%, representing a six-fold enhancement, while NHEJ remained unchanged, confirming selective enhancement of HDR. Genomic sequencing of Venus+ clones confirmed precise repair without indels, while mCherry+ clones showed characteristic NHEJ indels. The system was then applied to the endogenous AAVS1 locus using a SA-T2A-EGFP donor template. In HEK293 cells, HDR efficiency increased from 2.09% to 15.3% with dual modulation, a seven-fold enhancement. Similar results were observed in HEK293T and HeLa cells, with three- to five-fold increases. Inducible systems using rtTA and TRE3G promoters enabled doxycycline-dependent activation, with four-fold enhancement upon induction. Lentiviral delivery of dgCDK1-MS2:MPH into HEK293FT-Cas9 cells followed by HDR donor transfection resulted in consistent HDR enhancement, demonstrating compatibility with viral packaging systems. These results confirm that simultaneous activation of HDR genes and repression of NHEJ genes using dgRNA-CRISPRa/i systems significantly enhances precise genome editing efficiency across multiple cell lines and loci.
