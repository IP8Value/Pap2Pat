# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention resides in the field of immunopharmacology and oncological therapeutics, specifically in the use of opioid receptor antagonists to modulate innate immune signaling pathways through the selective inhibition of toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9) activity. This invention pertains to novel therapeutic applications of naltrexone and its pharmaceutically acceptable analogues in the treatment of disorders characterized by aberrant TLR9 overexpression or overactivity, including but not limited to solid and hematological malignancies, chronic inflammatory conditions, and autoimmune diseases. The invention further encompasses the use of such compounds as immunomodulatory agents in cancer immunotherapy regimens, vaccine adjuvants, and supportive care interventions aimed at restoring immune homeostasis in tumor-bearing hosts. The compositions and methods described herein are grounded in the unexpected discovery that naltrexone, traditionally recognized for its opioid receptor antagonism, directly and selectively interferes with TLR9-mediated signal transduction in human immune cells, thereby suppressing pro-inflammatory and tumor-promoting cytokine production without inducing cytotoxicity or apoptosis.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce naltrexone

Naltrexone is a synthetic opioid receptor antagonist that has been clinically utilized for decades in the management of opioid and alcohol dependence. It is administered orally or via extended-release injectable formulations and is well tolerated in human subjects, with a documented safety profile established through extensive clinical use. Naltrexone is chemically classified as a 17-cyclopropylmethyl-3,14-dihydroxy-4,5α-epoxy-6,14-ethano-morphinan derivative, and its molecular structure confers high affinity for mu, delta, and kappa opioid receptors, with minimal activity at non-opioid targets under standard therapeutic dosing.

- describe chemical structure

The chemical structure of naltrexone hydrochloride consists of a rigid morphinan core with a cyclopropylmethyl substitution at the nitrogen atom, a hydroxyl group at the 3-position, and an additional hydroxyl group at the 14-position, with an epoxide bridge between carbons 4 and 5. The hydrochloride salt form enhances aqueous solubility and facilitates oral bioavailability, making it suitable for formulation in tablets, capsules, and sustained-release preparations. The stereochemistry of naltrexone is critical to its biological activity, with the (-)-isomer exhibiting significantly greater receptor affinity than its enantiomer.

- discuss off-label use

Despite its approved indications, naltrexone has been increasingly employed off-label at low doses—typically ranging from 1.5 mg to 4.5 mg daily—to modulate immune function in patients with chronic inflammatory and autoimmune conditions. These low-dose regimens, referred to as low-dose naltrexone (LDN), have been associated with anecdotal and clinical improvements in symptoms of multiple sclerosis, fibromyalgia, Crohn’s disease, and other immune-mediated disorders, although the underlying mechanisms remained poorly understood until the present discovery.

- summarize cancer treatment research

Emerging clinical reports have suggested that LDN may exert beneficial effects in patients with various cancers, including lymphoma, pancreatic carcinoma, and melanoma, often in combination with nutritional or immunological adjuvants. These observations, while not supported by large-scale controlled trials, have prompted renewed interest in the potential immunomodulatory properties of naltrexone beyond opioid receptor blockade.

- explain role of LDN in cancer treatment

Low-dose naltrexone is hypothesized to transiently inhibit opioid receptors, triggering a compensatory upregulation of endogenous opioid peptides and opioid receptor expression, which may indirectly influence immune cell activity. However, these mechanisms fail to fully account for the rapid and specific suppression of inflammatory cytokines observed in vitro and in vivo following LDN administration. The present invention reveals a direct and previously unrecognized pharmacological interaction between naltrexone and TLR9, providing a mechanistic foundation for its observed anti-tumor and immunoregulatory effects.

- discuss TLR4 antagonism

Prior studies had indicated that naltrexone could inhibit TLR4 signaling in microglial and HEK-293 reporter cell lines, suggesting a broader role in innate immune modulation. These findings were attributed to binding of naltrexone to the MD2 co-receptor, a key component of the TLR4/MD2 complex responsible for lipopolysaccharide recognition. However, the relevance of this mechanism in human immune cells, particularly in the context of cancer, remained uncertain, and no evidence existed to suggest that naltrexone could target other TLR family members, especially those localized intracellularly.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- summarize naltrexone's TLR9 antagonism

The present invention discloses that naltrexone functions as a direct and selective antagonist of toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9), a pattern recognition receptor located within endosomal compartments of immune cells. Unlike its previously described interaction with TLR4, which requires the MD2 co-receptor, naltrexone inhibits TLR9 signaling independently of MD2, directly interfering with the receptor’s ability to recognize and respond to CpG-containing DNA motifs. This antagonism results in the suppression of downstream MyD88-dependent signaling, leading to reduced production of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α, particularly in plasmacytoid dendritic cells and B lymphocytes.

- outline novel therapeutic applications

This discovery enables novel therapeutic applications for naltrexone and its structural analogues in the treatment of cancers and inflammatory disorders driven by TLR9 overactivity. These include, but are not limited to, melanoma, prostate cancer, pancreatic adenocarcinoma, B-cell lymphomas, and autoimmune conditions such as psoriasis and systemic lupus erythematosus, where aberrant TLR9 activation contributes to chronic inflammation, immune evasion, and tumor progression. The invention further encompasses the use of naltrexone as an adjuvant in cancer vaccines to enhance cytotoxic T-cell priming by dampening TLR9-mediated immunosuppressive signals within the tumor microenvironment.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce naltrexone as antagonist of TLR9

Naltrexone has been identified as a potent and selective antagonist of TLR9, capable of inhibiting ligand-induced signaling in human immune cells at pharmacologically relevant concentrations. This activity is distinct from its opioid receptor antagonism and occurs independently of opioid receptor expression. Naltrexone does not interfere with TLR4, TLR7, or TLR8 signaling in all contexts, but demonstrates consistent and reproducible suppression of TLR9-mediated cytokine production in primary human monocytes, B cells, and plasmacytoid dendritic cells.

- describe TLR9 location and function

Toll-like receptor 9 is an endosomal transmembrane protein expressed predominantly in B lymphocytes, plasmacytoid dendritic cells, and certain subsets of monocytes. It functions as a sensor for unmethylated cytosine-phosphate-guanine (CpG) dinucleotide motifs commonly found in bacterial and viral DNA, as well as in self-DNA released during cellular stress or necrosis. Upon ligand binding, TLR9 undergoes conformational changes that initiate downstream signaling cascades essential for the activation of innate and adaptive immune responses.

- explain TLR9 recognition of CpG DNA

TLR9 specifically recognizes CpG DNA sequences through its leucine-rich repeat ectodomain, which binds to the phosphate-sugar backbone and nucleotide bases of the ligand within the acidic environment of the endosome. This interaction triggers receptor dimerization and recruitment of the adaptor protein MyD88, initiating a signaling cascade that culminates in the activation of transcription factors such as NF-κB and IRF7, leading to the production of type I interferons, IL-6, TNF-α, and other inflammatory mediators.

- describe TLR9-mediated signalling and downstream effects

TLR9-mediated signaling proceeds via the MyD88-dependent pathway, resulting in the phosphorylation of IRAK kinases, activation of TRAF6, and subsequent induction of MAPK and NF-κB pathways. These events drive the transcription of genes encoding pro-inflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and co-stimulatory molecules. In cancer, persistent TLR9 activation in tumor-infiltrating immune cells can promote an immunosuppressive microenvironment, enhance angiogenesis, and facilitate metastatic spread through the upregulation of matrix metalloproteinases and vascular endothelial growth factor.

- introduce pDC and their role in immune response

Plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) are specialized immune cells that serve as primary producers of type I interferons in response to viral nucleic acids. They express high levels of TLR7 and TLR9 and are critical for linking innate and adaptive immunity. In the context of cancer, pDCs can acquire tolerogenic properties when chronically exposed to self-DNA or tumor-derived nucleic acids, leading to the induction of regulatory T cells and suppression of cytotoxic T-cell responses.

- describe TLR9 overactivity and its effects on immune response

Overactivity of TLR9, whether due to genetic polymorphisms, chronic tissue damage, or dysregulated cell death, leads to sustained production of inflammatory mediators and immune cell exhaustion. In malignancies such as melanoma and prostate cancer, elevated TLR9 expression in tumor cells and tumor-associated immune cells correlates with advanced disease stage, resistance to therapy, and poor prognosis. This overactivity contributes to immune evasion by promoting the secretion of IL-10, TGF-β, and other immunosuppressive factors.

- explain how TLR9 antagonism can be beneficial in cancer treatment

Inhibition of TLR9 signaling interrupts this pathological loop, restoring immune surveillance and enhancing the efficacy of cytotoxic T lymphocytes and natural killer cells. By reducing the production of immunosuppressive cytokines and dampening chronic inflammation, TLR9 antagonism creates a more favorable environment for anti-tumor immunity, potentiating the effects of chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy.

- describe LDN-mediated inhibition of TLR9

Low-dose naltrexone (LDN), administered at concentrations between 1.5 mg and 4.5 mg daily, achieves plasma levels sufficient to inhibit TLR9 signaling in human immune cells without inducing opioid receptor blockade. This inhibition is reversible, dose-dependent, and occurs without cytotoxicity, making it suitable for long-term administration. LDN effectively reduces IL-6 and TNF-α secretion from pDCs and B cells exposed to CpG DNA, thereby mitigating the immunosuppressive signals that support tumor progression.

- introduce naltrexone and its analogues

Naltrexone and its pharmaceutically acceptable analogues, including nalmefene, naloxone, and structurally modified derivatives with enhanced TLR9 affinity or bioavailability, are encompassed by this invention. Modifications may include alterations to the N-substituent, hydroxylation patterns, or formulation with delivery enhancers to optimize tissue penetration and duration of action.

- define agonist and antagonist

An agonist is a molecule that binds to a receptor and activates it, eliciting a biological response. An antagonist is a molecule that binds to a receptor without activating it, thereby preventing the binding and action of an agonist. In the context of this invention, naltrexone acts as a competitive antagonist of TLR9, preventing CpG DNA from inducing receptor activation.

- define overexpression of TLR9

Overexpression of TLR9 refers to the abnormal increase in the quantity of TLR9 protein or mRNA in a cell or tissue relative to normal physiological levels, as determined by quantitative PCR, immunohistochemistry, or flow cytometry.

- define overactivity of TLR9-mediated signalling

Overactivity of TLR9-mediated signaling denotes the persistent or excessive activation of downstream signaling pathways, even in the absence of exogenous ligands, resulting in elevated cytokine production, chronic inflammation, and immune dysfunction.

- describe techniques to determine TLR9 expression and signalling

TLR9 expression can be assessed by quantitative reverse transcription PCR, Western blotting, flow cytometry using TLR9-specific antibodies, or immunohistochemical staining of tissue sections. Signaling activity can be measured by ELISA for downstream cytokines, phospho-flow cytometry for phosphorylated IRAK1 or IκBα, or reporter assays using NF-κB or IRF7-driven luciferase constructs.

- define treating and treatment

Treating refers to the administration of a therapeutic agent to a subject for the purpose of ameliorating, preventing, or delaying the progression of a disease or disorder. Treatment encompasses both curative and palliative approaches, including the reduction of symptoms, improvement of quality of life, and extension of survival.

- describe supportive care

Supportive care refers to interventions aimed at managing symptoms, reducing treatment-related toxicity, improving immune function, and enhancing overall well-being in patients with chronic or advanced disease. In the context of this invention, supportive care includes the use of naltrexone to reduce systemic inflammation, alleviate fatigue, and improve response to other therapies.

- define cancer and cancerous

Cancer refers to a group of diseases characterized by the uncontrolled proliferation of cells, invasion of surrounding tissues, and potential for metastasis. Cancerous denotes cells or tissues exhibiting the phenotypic and genetic hallmarks of malignancy, including genomic instability, evasion of apoptosis, and sustained angiogenesis.

- define tumour and neoplasm

A tumor is an abnormal mass of tissue resulting from unregulated cell growth, while a neoplasm is a new and abnormal growth of tissue that serves no physiological purpose and may be benign or malignant.

- define tumourigenic

Tumourigenic refers to the capacity of a substance, cell, or process to initiate or promote the formation of tumors.

- define tumour/cancer microenvironment

The tumor microenvironment encompasses the cellular and molecular components surrounding a tumor, including immune cells, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, extracellular matrix, cytokines, and signaling molecules, all of which influence tumor progression and therapeutic response.

- define subject

A subject refers to a human or non-human mammal requiring medical intervention, particularly a patient diagnosed with a disorder characterized by TLR9 overexpression or overactivity.

- define immunomodulator

An immunomodulator is a substance that alters the immune response, either by enhancing, suppressing, or redirecting immune activity.

- describe disorders characterised by TLR9 overexpression and/or overactivity

Disorders characterized by TLR9 overexpression and/or overactivity include melanoma, prostate cancer, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, chronic lymphocytic leukemia, systemic lupus erythematosus, psoriasis, and inflammatory bowel disease.

- describe diagnostic tests for TLR9 expression and signalling

Diagnostic tests include molecular assays for TLR9 mRNA levels, immunohistochemical staining of tumor biopsies for TLR9 protein, serum cytokine profiling for IL-6 and TNF-α, and functional assays measuring CpG-induced cytokine secretion in patient-derived peripheral blood mononuclear cells.

- introduce pharmaceutical compositions of the invention

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise naltrexone or its analogues in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or delivery systems, formulated for oral, subcutaneous, intravenous, or sustained-release administration.

- describe use of pharmaceutical compositions in treating disorders

These compositions are used for the treatment of disorders characterized by TLR9 overactivity, including cancers and autoimmune diseases, either as monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy, or vaccines.

- describe optional features of pharmaceutical compositions

Optional features include the inclusion of vitamin D3, Mycobacterium vaccae, or other immunostimulatory agents to enhance therapeutic efficacy, or the use of enteric coatings to protect naltrexone from gastric degradation.

- describe preferred features of pharmaceutical compositions

Preferred features include a daily dose of 1.5 mg to 4.5 mg of naltrexone hydrochloride, administered orally once daily at bedtime, in a tablet or capsule formulation with a bioavailability-enhancing excipient such as hydroxypropyl methylcellulose.

- introduce method of treating a subject

The method of treating a subject involves administering an effective amount of naltrexone or its analogue to a subject diagnosed with a disorder characterized by TLR9 overexpression or overactivity, thereby reducing cytokine production, inhibiting tumor progression, and improving survival outcomes.

- describe optional features of method of treating

Optional features include concurrent administration of a vaccine, checkpoint inhibitor, or radiation therapy, or monitoring of serum cytokine levels to titrate dosage.

- describe preferred features of method of treating

Preferred features include daily oral administration of 3 mg naltrexone hydrochloride for a period of at least six months, with clinical response assessed by imaging, tumor biomarker analysis, and immune cell profiling.

- introduce method of providing supportive care

The method of providing supportive care involves administering naltrexone to a subject undergoing cancer therapy to reduce systemic inflammation, alleviate fatigue, and improve quality of life without compromising anti-tumor efficacy.

- describe optional features of method of providing supportive care

Optional features include co-administration with nutritional supplements, sleep hygiene protocols, or stress-reduction interventions.

- describe preferred features of method of providing supportive care

Preferred features include daily administration of 3 mg naltrexone hydrochloride beginning one week prior to initiation of chemotherapy and continuing throughout the treatment course, with documented improvement in patient-reported outcomes and reduced incidence of treatment-related complications.

- describe administration of pharmaceutical compositions

Pharmaceutical compositions may be administered orally, subcutaneously, intravenously, or via transdermal patch, with oral administration being the most preferred route due to established safety and patient compliance.

- describe dose of naltrexone

The effective dose of naltrexone ranges from 1.5 mg to 4.5 mg per day, with 3 mg being the most preferred daily dose for long-term administration in adults.

- describe route of administration

The preferred route of administration is oral, with administration at bedtime to align with circadian rhythms of endogenous opioid release and maximize immunomodulatory effects.

- introduce naltrexone as vaccine adjuvant

Naltrexone may be co-administered with cancer vaccines to enhance the priming of cytotoxic T lymphocytes by suppressing TLR9-mediated tolerogenic signals in dendritic cells, thereby promoting a more robust and durable anti-tumor immune response.

- describe stimulation of cytotoxic T cells

By inhibiting TLR9-driven IL-10 and TGF-β secretion, naltrexone promotes dendritic cell maturation and antigen presentation, leading to increased activation and proliferation of tumor-specific CD8+ T cells.

- introduce vaccine compositions

Vaccine compositions of the invention comprise tumor-associated antigens, pathogen-derived peptides, or neoantigens combined with naltrexone or its analogues as an immunomodulatory adjuvant.

- describe Example 1 - identification of naltrexone as antagonist of TLR9

In a series of in vitro assays using transfected HEK-293 cells expressing human TLR7, TLR8, TLR9, and NOD receptors, naltrexone selectively inhibited TLR9-mediated NF-κB activation in a dose-dependent manner, with no effect on TLR4, TLR7, TLR8, or NOD2 signaling. In primary human PBMCs, naltrexone reduced IL-6 and TNF-α secretion following CpG stimulation by over 60% at 200 µM, without affecting cell viability or inducing apoptosis.

- describe Example 2 - LDN in treatment of melanoma

In a cohort of patients with advanced melanoma, daily administration of 3 mg naltrexone hydrochloride in combination with vitamin D3 resulted in prolonged stabilization of disease, reduced serum IL-6 levels, and increased tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells in biopsy specimens, with no evidence of disease progression in 60% of patients at 12 months.

- describe Example 3 - LDN in treatment of prostate cancer

In patients with castration-resistant prostate cancer, daily administration of 3 mg naltrexone hydrochloride with Mycobacterium vaccae led to a significant decline in prostate-specific antigen velocity, improved performance status, and increased frequency of tumor-reactive T cells in peripheral blood, with a median progression-free survival of 14 months compared to 7 months in controls.

- conclude invention

This invention establishes naltrexone as a novel and selective TLR9 antagonist with broad therapeutic potential in oncology and immunology, offering a safe, well-tolerated, and cost-effective strategy to modulate immune responses in cancer and inflammatory disease.

- provide final remarks

The discovery that naltrexone directly antagonizes TLR9 represents a paradigm shift in the understanding of its pharmacological profile and opens new avenues for the repurposing of an established drug in precision immunotherapy. The methods and compositions described herein provide a robust foundation for clinical development and regulatory approval of naltrexone-based therapies targeting TLR9-driven pathologies.
