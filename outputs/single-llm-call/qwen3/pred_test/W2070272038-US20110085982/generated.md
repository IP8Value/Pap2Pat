## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of oncology, specifically to the development and use of human-derived cell lines for the study of aggressive breast cancer subtypes. The invention is directed to a novel human triple-negative breast cancer cell line, designated DKAT, which was isolated from a malignant pleural effusion of a patient with rapidly progressive, chemotherapy-resistant metastatic disease. This cell line exhibits a unique combination of molecular and phenotypic characteristics that distinguish it from all previously established breast cancer models. Unlike conventional cell lines that maintain a fixed epithelial or mesenchymal identity, the DKAT cell line demonstrates dynamic and reversible transitions between epithelial and mesenchymal states in response to environmental cues, mirroring the phenotypic heterogeneity observed in clinical samples of triple-negative breast cancer. The cell line retains key features of the original tumor, including loss of estrogen and progesterone receptor expression, absence of HER2 amplification, elevated phospho-AKT signaling, and a CD44high/CD24low stem-like phenotype. Its capacity to recapitulate the morphological and molecular diversity of human breast cancer progression renders it a uniquely valuable tool for investigating the biological mechanisms underlying metastasis, treatment failure, and tumor recurrence.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer is a highly heterogeneous disease characterized by diverse histological, molecular, and clinical behaviors. Gene expression profiling has identified distinct molecular subtypes, including luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched, normal-like, and basal-like tumors, each with unique prognostic implications and therapeutic vulnerabilities. Among these, triple-negative breast cancers—defined by the absence of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 amplification—are particularly aggressive, frequently metastasize early, and lack targeted therapeutic options. These tumors are often enriched for markers associated with epithelial-mesenchymal transition, a developmental program that confers increased motility, invasiveness, and resistance to apoptosis. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition involves the downregulation of epithelial adhesion molecules such as E-cadherin and the upregulation of mesenchymal markers including vimentin and N-cadherin, accompanied by a shift from a cobblestone to a spindle-shaped morphology. The reverse process, mesenchymal-epithelial transition, has also been observed in metastatic sites, suggesting a dynamic, reversible plasticity rather than a unidirectional transition. However, the relevance of these processes in human tumors remains controversial, as most clinical samples do not exhibit a complete shift to a mesenchymal phenotype, and metastases typically retain epithelial characteristics. This paradox has led to the hypothesis that transient, partial epithelial-mesenchymal transitions may be critical for dissemination and colonization. Furthermore, triple-negative breast cancers are frequently associated with cancer-initiating cells, often identified by the CD44high/CD24low surface marker profile, which exhibit enhanced tumorigenic potential and resistance to conventional therapies. Despite these insights, the study of these phenomena has been severely limited by the lack of appropriate in vitro and in vivo models that faithfully recapitulate the phenotypic plasticity observed in patients. Existing cell lines such as MDA-MB-231 and MDA-MB-468 are widely used but exhibit fixed phenotypes and fail to demonstrate reversible transitions between epithelial and mesenchymal states under physiological conditions. Other models, including MCF-7 and SUM-149, are epithelial and do not acquire mesenchymal traits in response to environmental stimuli. The inability of these lines to model dynamic transitions has hindered the identification of molecular regulators of plasticity and the development of therapies targeting metastatic resilience. There remains a critical unmet need for a human-derived, tumorigenic cell line that recapitulates the full spectrum of epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity observed in aggressive triple-negative breast cancers.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel human triple-negative breast cancer cell line designated DKAT, which was established from the malignant pleural effusion of a patient with metastatic, treatment-resistant disease. The DKAT cell line exhibits a unique and reproducible ability to reversibly transition between epithelial and mesenchymal phenotypes in response to changes in culture medium, a property not observed in any other established breast cancer cell line. When cultured in serum-free mammary epithelial growth medium, DKAT cells display an epithelial morphology, express E-cadherin, cytokeratins 5 and 18, and localize E-cadherin to the cell membrane. Upon exposure to serum-containing stromal cell growth medium supplemented with insulin, fibroblast growth factor-2, and fetal bovine serum, DKAT cells undergo a rapid and sustained morphological transformation to a spindle-shaped, mesenchymal phenotype, accompanied by downregulation of epithelial markers and upregulation of vimentin, N-cadherin, and lysyl oxidase. This transition is reversible: when mesenchymal DKAT cells are returned to epithelial growth conditions, they regain epithelial characteristics, including membrane-localized E-cadherin and reduced vimentin expression. The DKAT cell line has been deposited with the American Type Culture Collection under Accession No. PTA-10322 and is maintained as a continuous, tumorigenic cell line capable of indefinite propagation in vitro. The cell line harbors a missense mutation in exon 8 of the TP53 gene at codon 273 (CGT > CAT), consistent with loss of p53 function, and demonstrates high levels of phosphorylated Akt at Serine 473, despite the absence of mutations in PIK3CA exons 9 or 20. The DKAT cell line expresses the CD44high/CD24low phenotype, indicative of cancer stem cell enrichment, and forms solid tumors in immunocompromised mice following subcutaneous or intramammary injection. These tumors recapitulate the phenotypic heterogeneity of the original human tumor, containing regions with epithelial, mesenchymal, and mixed characteristics. The DKAT cell line is capable of generating tumorspheres in non-adherent conditions, each containing both epithelial and mesenchymal subpopulations derived from a single cell. The invention further encompasses the use of the DKAT cell line for identifying genes and proteins modulated during epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition, invasion, and metastasis. The invention also provides methods for evaluating the efficacy of chemotherapeutic agents and biological therapies on tumor growth, invasion, and phenotypic plasticity using the DKAT cell line in vitro and in vivo.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The DKAT cell line represents a transformative advancement in the modeling of triple-negative breast cancer due to its unprecedented capacity for reversible epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity. Unlike previously established models, which are locked into a single phenotypic state, DKAT cells dynamically respond to environmental signals by altering their gene expression, protein composition, and morphology in a manner that closely mirrors the heterogeneity observed in clinical specimens. The cell line was derived from a malignant pleural effusion of a 35-year-old woman with a history of aggressive, chemotherapy-resistant triple-negative breast cancer that metastasized to the lung, liver, bone, and bone marrow. The primary tumor exhibited multiple distinct foci, some expressing epithelial markers such as E-cadherin and cytokeratins, while others expressed mesenchymal markers including vimentin and N-cadherin, with intermediate phenotypes observed in chest wall recurrences. This phenotypic diversity was preserved in the DKAT cell line, which, when cultured under standard conditions, displays a mixed epithelial-mesenchymal profile. Upon switching to serum-containing media, DKAT cells rapidly downregulate epithelial junction proteins and upregulate mesenchymal cytoskeletal components, acquiring a migratory phenotype within hours. This transition is not due to selection of a pre-existing subpopulation, as clonal populations derived from single DKAT cells exhibit identical plasticity. When returned to serum-free conditions, these cells revert to an epithelial state, demonstrating that the plasticity is an intrinsic property of the cell line rather than an artifact of culture adaptation. The DKAT cell line maintains a hyperdiploid karyotype with complex chromosomal rearrangements, including a double minute, and carries a known pathogenic TP53 mutation at codon 273, contributing to its tumorigenic potential. The cell line expresses basal epithelial markers such as cytokeratin 5 and p63, luminal markers such as cytokeratin 18, and stem cell markers including CD44 and ALDH1A3. It is negative for estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 amplification, fulfilling the diagnostic criteria for triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line is tumorigenic in immunocompromised mice, forming solid tumors with histological features mirroring the original tumor, including infiltrative growth, intraductal spread, and mixed epithelial-mesenchymal differentiation. These tumors contain both human p53-positive cells expressing epithelial markers and those expressing mesenchymal markers, confirming that phenotypic heterogeneity originates from the implanted DKAT cells and not host stromal contamination. The DKAT cell line is uniquely suited for studying the molecular mechanisms of phenotypic plasticity, identifying novel therapeutic targets, screening for agents that block metastasis, and evaluating the impact of microenvironmental cues on tumor behavior. Its ability to model reversible transitions between epithelial and mesenchymal states provides a powerful platform for understanding the biological basis of treatment resistance and metastatic recurrence in triple-negative breast cancer.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The DKAT cell line was established from a malignant pleural effusion obtained from a 35-year-old female patient with metastatic triple-negative breast cancer who had received multiple lines of chemotherapy, including cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, 5-fluorouracil, taxotere, and navelbine, with no clinical response prior to death. The pleural fluid was centrifuged at 300 × g for 10 minutes, and the cellular pellet was resuspended in Mammary Epithelial Growth Medium (MEGM) supplemented with bovine pituitary extract, insulin, epidermal growth factor, and hydrocortisone. Cells were plated in T25 flasks and maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂. Within 48 hours, adherent cells began proliferating with a doubling time of approximately 24 hours. The cell line was passaged every 3–4 days using trypsin-EDTA and cryopreserved at passage 5. Mycoplasma testing was performed monthly using a PCR-based assay and confirmed negative throughout culturing. The patient’s primary tumor was a 4 cm, lymph node-negative, ER/PR-negative, HER2-negative carcinoma with basal-like features, and the pleural effusion contained malignant cells with mixed epithelial and mesenchymal morphology.

### Example 2

Other breast cancer cell lines, including MDA-MB-231, MCF-7, SUM-149, SUM-190, and hTERT-immortalized HMECs, were maintained under standard conditions as described in the literature. MDA-MB-231 cells were cultured in αMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. MCF-7, SUM-149, and SUM-190 cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. HMEC-hTERT cells were cultured in MEGM without serum. All cell lines were passaged every 3–4 days and tested for mycoplasma contamination monthly. None of these cell lines exhibited phenotypic plasticity when switched between serum-containing and serum-free media, as evidenced by stable expression of E-cadherin and vimentin over 14 days of culture.

### Example 3

Cytogenetic analysis of the DKAT cell line was performed at passage 3 using spectral karyotyping (SKY). Twenty-five metaphase spreads were analyzed, revealing a modal chromosome number of 56. Three distinct clones were identified: a stemline with 56 chromosomes, a sideline with an additional double minute, and a near-tetraploid sideline with 104–109 chromosomes. Two additional cells exhibited hyperploid karyotypes with 117 and 127 chromosomes, respectively. G-banding confirmed the presence of structural abnormalities including translocations and deletions. No common chromosomal aberrations were observed in other triple-negative breast cancer lines, indicating a unique genomic landscape for DKAT.

### Example 4

Immunocytochemistry was performed on DKAT cells fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100. Cells were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against E-cadherin (1:200, BD Biosciences), vimentin (1:200, Sigma), CK5 (1:100, DAKO), and p63 (1:100, Santa Cruz). Secondary antibodies conjugated to horseradish peroxidase were applied for 1 hour at room temperature, followed by development with DAB substrate. DKAT cells exhibited strong membranous E-cadherin staining, weak cytoplasmic vimentin, and robust expression of CK5 and p63. Control MDA-MB-231 cells showed diffuse vimentin and absent E-cadherin staining.

### Example 5

Immunofluorescence staining was performed on DKAT cells grown on coverslips. Cells were fixed in methanol, blocked with 5% goat serum, and incubated with primary antibodies against E-cadherin (1:200), vimentin (1:200), CK18 (1:200), and CD44 (1:100) overnight at 4°C. Secondary antibodies conjugated to Alexa Fluor 488 and 594 were applied for 1 hour at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. Images were acquired using a Zeiss Axio Observer A1 fluorescence microscope. Under MEGM conditions, E-cadherin was localized to the cell membrane, while vimentin was sparse. Under SCGM conditions, E-cadherin was internalized and vimentin formed dense cytoplasmic filaments. Co-staining for CD44 and CK18 revealed a heterogeneous population of cells with dual epithelial and stem-like characteristics.

### Example 6

Western blotting was performed using lysates from DKAT cells cultured in MEGM or SCGM for 14 days. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to nitrocellulose membranes. Membranes were probed with antibodies against E-cadherin (1:1000), vimentin (1:1000), N-cadherin (1:1000), claudin-1 (1:1000), and β-actin (1:5000). Densitometric analysis revealed a 10-fold increase in vimentin and N-cadherin expression and a 7-fold decrease in E-cadherin and claudin-1 in SCGM compared to MEGM. Phospho-Akt (Ser473) was constitutively elevated, while total Akt levels remained unchanged.

### Example 7

Genomic DNA was extracted from DKAT cells at passage 20 using a Qiagen DNeasy kit. Exons 5–9 of the TP53 gene were amplified by PCR using primers previously described in the IARC TP53 database. PCR products were purified and sequenced bidirectionally on an ABI 3730xl sequencer. A heterozygous point mutation was identified at codon 273 in exon 8, resulting in a CGT to CAT substitution and an arginine to histidine amino acid change (R273H). This mutation was confirmed in a second independent DNA preparation and is known to disrupt p53 DNA-binding function.

### Example 8

Total RNA was extracted from DKAT cells cultured in MEGM or SCGM using the RNeasy Mini Kit. RNA integrity was confirmed by agarose gel electrophoresis. cDNA synthesis was performed using the SuperScript III First-Strand Synthesis System. Gene expression profiling was conducted using Affymetrix HG-U133 Plus 2.0 microarrays. Data were normalized using RMA algorithm and analyzed using Cluster 3.0 and TreeView software. Hierarchical clustering revealed distinct gene expression signatures between MEGM- and SCGM-cultured cells. Genes downregulated in SCGM included KRT5, KRT17, TP63, and CLDN1, while genes upregulated included TGFA, FGF2, LOX, and CDH2. Validation by qRT-PCR confirmed fold-changes of 37-fold for KRT5, 7.8-fold for KRT17, and 5.6-fold for CDH2.

### Example 9

Invasion assays were performed using Transwell chambers with 8-μm pores coated with Matrigel. DKAT cells were seeded in serum-free MEGM in the upper chamber, and SCGM was placed in the lower chamber as a chemoattractant. After 24 hours, non-invading cells were removed with a cotton swab, and invading cells on the lower membrane were fixed, stained with crystal violet, and counted under a microscope. DKAT cells cultured in SCGM exhibited a 4.5-fold increase in invasion compared to those in MEGM. EMT assays were performed by switching cells from MEGM to SCGM and analyzing morphology and marker expression after 14 days. Flow cytometry was performed using APC-conjugated CD44 and PE-conjugated CD24 antibodies. DKAT cells showed 82% CD44high/CD24low population. Mouse xenografts were established by injecting 10⁴ DKAT cells in 50 µL of 1:1 PBS:Matrigel into the mammary fat pad of NOD-SCID mice. Tumors became palpable within 14 days and were harvested at 10 weeks. Tumors were fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with H&E and immunohistochemistry for CK5, vimentin, and human p53. Tumors exhibited heterogeneous staining, with regions positive for both epithelial and mesenchymal markers, confirming in vivo phenotypic plasticity.
