# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE

- relate to VEGF inhibitors

The present disclosure relates to novel compounds and methods for inhibiting the interaction between vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165) and neuropilin-1 (NRP-1), with particular utility in the modulation of pathological angiogenesis. These compounds are designed to interfere with signaling pathways critical to tumor growth, metastasis, and other angiogenesis-dependent diseases. The invention encompasses a class of peptidomimetic molecules that exhibit high affinity and specificity for the NRP-1 receptor, thereby disrupting the VEGF-A165/NRP-1 complex formation without directly targeting VEGFR-2 or other tyrosine kinase receptors. The disclosed compounds are distinguished by the presence of an N-terminal cysteine residue positioned in close proximity to a C-terminal arginine-containing motif, a structural feature that confers unexpectedly potent inhibitory activity. This innovation enables the development of therapeutics with improved pharmacological profiles over existing agents, including enhanced binding kinetics, metabolic stability, and selectivity for the NRP-1 binding pocket. The compounds are particularly suited for use in oncology, ophthalmology, and inflammatory conditions where aberrant angiogenesis drives disease progression.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

- introduce NRP-1 and VEGF-A165 interaction

Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) is a transmembrane receptor that functions as a co-receptor for multiple signaling ligands, including vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165), a key mediator of angiogenesis in both physiological and pathological contexts. The interaction between VEGF-A165 and NRP-1 enhances the binding affinity and downstream signaling of VEGF-A165 through its primary receptor, VEGFR-2, thereby amplifying endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and vascular permeability. Unlike VEGFR-2, NRP-1 lacks intrinsic tyrosine kinase activity but plays a critical structural role in stabilizing the VEGF-A165/VEGFR-2 complex and facilitating its internalization. Overexpression of NRP-1 is frequently observed in a wide range of human cancers, including breast, lung, prostate, and colorectal carcinomas, and correlates strongly with aggressive tumor phenotypes, increased microvessel density, and poor patient prognosis. The binding interface between VEGF-A165 and NRP-1 is localized to the b1 domain of NRP-1 and is primarily mediated by the C-terminal hexapeptide sequence of VEGF-A165, specifically the residues CDKPRR, which conform to a well-characterized C-end rule (CendR) motif.

- describe VEGF signalling pathway

The VEGF signaling pathway is a central regulator of vascular homeostasis and angiogenesis. Upon binding to VEGFR-2, VEGF-A165 triggers dimerization and autophosphorylation of the receptor, initiating a cascade of intracellular events involving phospholipase Cγ, protein kinase C, mitogen-activated protein kinases, and endothelial nitric oxide synthase. NRP-1 augments this signaling by increasing the local concentration of VEGF-A165 at the cell surface and promoting the formation of a ternary complex with VEGFR-2. This cooperative interaction results in sustained activation of pro-angiogenic signals, including those driving endothelial cell survival and vascular remodeling. In pathological settings such as solid tumors, chronic inflammation, and retinopathies, dysregulated VEGF-A165 expression leads to excessive and disorganized neovascularization, contributing to tumor hypoxia, immune evasion, and metastatic dissemination. Targeting this pathway has therefore become a cornerstone of anti-angiogenic therapy.

- discuss current anti-angiogenic strategies

Current anti-angiogenic strategies primarily rely on monoclonal antibodies such as bevacizumab, which neutralizes VEGF-A, or tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as sunitinib and sorafenib, which target VEGFR signaling. While these agents have demonstrated clinical benefit in several cancers, their efficacy is often limited by compensatory mechanisms, acquired resistance, and significant systemic toxicities including hypertension, proteinuria, and impaired wound healing. Moreover, these approaches do not selectively disrupt the NRP-1-mediated enhancement of VEGF-A165 signaling, leaving a critical node of angiogenic amplification unaddressed. Peptide-based inhibitors derived from the C-terminal region of VEGF-A165, such as the heptapeptide A7R (ATWLPPR), have been explored as alternatives, but their low binding affinity, poor metabolic stability, and lack of structural optimization have hindered clinical translation.

- motivate need for new compounds

There remains a substantial unmet need for small, stable, high-affinity inhibitors that selectively target the VEGF-A165/NRP-1 interface without perturbing other VEGF family members or their receptors. Such agents would offer improved specificity, reduced off-target effects, and greater potential for oral or localized delivery. The discovery of structural motifs that enhance binding to NRP-1 beyond the established CendR rule represents a pivotal opportunity for innovation in this field.

- summarize prior art peptides

Prior art has identified several peptidic ligands that bind NRP-1 via the CendR motif, including A7R, tuftsin (TKPR), and the C-terminal hexapeptide CDKPRR of VEGF-A165. These peptides rely on a terminal arginine or lysine residue for receptor engagement, with the free carboxyl group of this residue being essential for binding. Modifications such as amidation or extension of the C-terminus abolish activity, underscoring the strict structural requirements of the CendR rule. However, none of these peptides exploit the potential of an N-terminal cysteine residue to augment binding affinity or stability.

- highlight limitations of prior art

Despite their mechanistic insight, prior art peptides suffer from low potency (IC50 values in the micromolar range), rapid degradation in biological fluids, and insufficient bioavailability. The introduction of non-natural amino acids or branched architectures has improved stability but not sufficiently enhanced affinity. Crucially, the role of the cysteine residue, which is naturally conserved in the N-terminal region of VEGF-A165’s NRP-1-binding domain, has been largely overlooked in the design of synthetic inhibitors. The present invention addresses these limitations by demonstrating that the strategic placement of a cysteine residue immediately upstream of the CendR motif dramatically enhances binding affinity, resulting in compounds with nanomolar inhibitory potency and improved resistance to proteolytic degradation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSURE

- introduce novel VEGF-A165/NRP-1 inhibitors

The present invention discloses a novel class of VEGF-A165/NRP-1 inhibitors characterized by the presence of an N-terminal cysteine residue adjacent to a C-terminal arginine residue, forming a structural motif that significantly enhances binding affinity to the b1 domain of NRP-1. Unlike prior art peptides that rely solely on the CendR motif for receptor engagement, these inhibitors exploit an additional interaction mediated by the thiol group of the cysteine residue, which is proposed to form hydrogen bonds or sulfur–π interactions with aromatic residues within the NRP-1 binding pocket. This newly identified rule, termed the “CysR” motif, enables the design of compounds with potency orders of magnitude greater than previously known ligands.

### Methods for Preparing Compounds of Formula (I)

- define first preparation method

The first preparation method involves solid-phase peptide synthesis using Fmoc chemistry on Wang resin, wherein the C-terminal arginine residue is pre-loaded onto the resin, followed by sequential coupling of amino acids in the order Dab, hArg, and Cys, with selective deprotection of side-chain protecting groups.

- specify group A as group (A1)

In the first preparation method, group A is defined as A1, and the cysteine residue is attached to the ε-amino group of lysine via a side-chain coupling strategy using Alloc-protected lysine.

- describe R1 and R4 options

R1 is cysteine and R4 is hydrogen, with the cysteine residue introduced as Fmoc-Cys(Trt)-OH during the final coupling step.

- describe R2 and R5 options

R2 is homoarginine and R5 is hydrogen, with the homoarginine residue introduced as Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH.

- specify A as formula A1′

Group A is represented as A1′, wherein the linkage between the cysteine side chain and the lysine backbone is formed via an amide bond between the ε-amino group of lysine and the α-carboxyl group of cysteine.

- outline steps for preparing compound of formula (I)

The steps include: (a) deprotection of the Fmoc group from the N-terminus using piperidine; (b) coupling of the next amino acid using HATU and DIPEA; (c) washing with DMF; (d) repetition of deprotection and coupling until the full sequence is assembled; (e) selective deprotection of the Alloc group using Pd(0) and PhSiH3; (f) cleavage of the peptide from the resin using TFA with scavengers; and (g) purification by preparative HPLC.

- describe step a) alpha amine group deprotection

Step a) involves treatment of the resin-bound peptide with 20% piperidine in DMF for 20 minutes to remove the Fmoc protecting group from the α-amino terminus.

- describe step b) alpha amine group deprotection

Step b) is repeated after each coupling to expose the α-amino group for the next amino acid addition.

- describe step c) alpha amine group deprotection

Step c) is performed after each coupling cycle to ensure complete deprotection prior to the next elongation step.

- describe step d) alpha amine group deprotection

Step d) includes monitoring of deprotection efficiency using the Kaiser test or chloranil test to confirm free amine formation.

- describe step e) epsilon amine group deprotection

Step e) involves selective removal of the Alloc group from the ε-amino group of lysine using tetrakis(triphenylphosphine)palladium(0) and phenylsilane in dichloromethane.

- describe step f) removing compound from solid support

Step f) involves cleavage of the fully assembled peptide from the Wang resin using a mixture of TFA, water, triisopropylsilane, and dithiothreitol, followed by precipitation in cold diethyl ether.

- define second preparation method

The second preparation method involves solid-phase synthesis on Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin, with the cysteine residue introduced at the N-terminus as the first amino acid, followed by sequential addition of Dab, hArg, and Lys.

- specify group A as group (A2)

In the second preparation method, group A is defined as A2, and the cysteine residue is attached to the α-amino group of the peptide backbone, forming a linear structure.

- describe R1 and R4 options

R1 is cysteine and R4 is hydrogen, with the cysteine residue introduced as Fmoc-Cys(Trt)-OH at the beginning of the synthesis.

- describe R2 and R5 options

R2 is homoarginine and R5 is hydrogen, with the homoarginine residue introduced as Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH.

- specify A as formula A2

Group A is defined as A2, representing a direct α-amino linkage between the cysteine residue and the remainder of the peptide sequence.

- outline steps for preparing compound of formula (I)

The steps include: (a) deprotection of Fmoc from cysteine; (b) coupling of Dab; (c) coupling of hArg; (d) coupling of Lys; (e) final deprotection; and (f) cleavage from resin.

Steps a) to e) involve sequential deprotection and coupling of each amino acid, with monitoring of coupling efficiency by Kaiser test and final deprotection of side-chain protecting groups.

### Example

- introduce synthesis of peptide VEGF-A165/NRP-1 inhibitors

The synthesis of compounds of formula (I) was carried out using solid-phase peptide synthesis on Fmoc-L-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin, with side-chain protection using standard Fmoc chemistry and selective deprotection of Alloc and Pbf groups.

- describe solid phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) procedure

Solid-phase peptide synthesis was performed manually on a 0.5 g scale of Fmoc-L-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin with a loading capacity of 0.39 mmol/g, using HATU and DIPEA for coupling, and 20% piperidine in DMF for Fmoc deprotection.

- specify protecting groups for functional groups

The thiol group of cysteine was protected with Trt, the guanidinium group of arginine and homoarginine with Pbf, and the ε-amino group of lysine with Alloc.

- describe Wang resin for peptide synthesis

Wang resin was selected for its acid-labile linker, enabling clean cleavage of the peptide from the solid support using TFA-based cocktails without side-chain deprotection.

- specify coupling reagents for peptide bonds

Coupling reagents included HATU (1.0 eq), DIPEA (5.0 eq), and DMF as solvent, with coupling times of 60 minutes per residue.

- describe cleavage of peptide from resin

Cleavage was performed using a mixture of TFA:DTT:H2O:TIS (88:5:5:2, v:v:v:v) for three hours at room temperature, followed by precipitation in cold diethyl ether.

- specify purification method using HPLC

Purification was carried out using preparative reversed-phase HPLC on a Phenomenex Jupiter Proteo C12 column with a gradient of water/acetonitrile containing 0.1% TFA.

- convert to pharmaceutically acceptable salt

The purified peptide was converted to the trifluoroacetate salt by lyophilization from aqueous TFA solution, or to the hydrochloride salt by ion exchange chromatography.

- introduce solid phase synthesis of peptides of formula (I)

Solid-phase synthesis of peptides of formula (I) was performed using two distinct strategies: one with cysteine at the N-terminus and another with cysteine attached to the side chain of lysine.

- describe synthesis of compound of formula (I) H-Cys-Lys(hArg)-Dab-ΔPro-Arg-OH

The compound H–Cys–Lys(hArg)–Dab–ΔPro–Arg–OH was synthesized by sequential coupling of Fmoc-ΔPro-OH, Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH, Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-OH, Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH, and Fmoc-Cys(Trt)-OH onto Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin.

- specify Fmoc-L-Arg(Pbf) Wang resin

Fmoc-L-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin was used as the starting material, with the arginine residue pre-loaded to ensure C-terminal anchoring.

- describe Fmoc-deprotection step

Fmoc deprotection was performed with 20% piperidine in DMF for 20 minutes, followed by washing with DMF and methanol.

- specify Kaiser test for monitoring Fmoc cleavage

The Kaiser test was used to confirm complete deprotection prior to each coupling step, with a positive blue color indicating free amine formation.

- describe coupling of Fmoc-ΔPro-OH

Fmoc-ΔPro-OH was coupled using HATU/DIPEA in DMF for 90 minutes, with coupling efficiency confirmed by chloranil test.

- specify acetaldehyde/chloranil test for monitoring coupling reaction

The chloranil test was used to detect free amines after coupling, with a blue-green color indicating incomplete coupling.

- describe coupling of Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH

Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH was coupled using the same conditions, with the Boc group remaining intact until final cleavage.

- describe Fmoc-deprotection and washing steps

After each coupling, the resin was washed with DMF and methanol, and Fmoc deprotection was repeated as necessary.

- describe coupling of Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-OH

Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-OH was coupled using HATU/DIPEA, followed by Alloc deprotection using Pd(0)/PhSiH3 in DCM.

- describe Alloc-deprotection step

Alloc deprotection was performed with 0.1 eq tetrakis(triphenylphosphine)palladium(0) and 20 eq phenylsilane in DCM for 1 hour.

- describe coupling of Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH

Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH was coupled using HATU/DIPEA, followed by Fmoc deprotection and washing.

- describe Fmoc-deprotection and washing steps

Final Fmoc deprotection was performed, followed by thorough washing with DMF and methanol prior to cleavage.

- introduce example 3: solid phase synthesis of compound of formula (I) H-Lys(Cys-hArg)-Dab-ΔPro-Arg

The compound H–Lys(Cys–hArg)–Dab–ΔPro–Arg–OH was synthesized by first loading Fmoc-Arg(Pbf) onto Wang resin, followed by coupling of Fmoc-ΔPro-OH, Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH, and Boc-Lys(Fmoc)-OH.

- describe synthesis of compound of formula (I) H-Lys(Cys-hArg)-Dab-ΔPro-Arg-OH

After coupling of Boc-Lys(Fmoc)-OH, the Fmoc group was removed, and Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH was coupled to the ε-amino group of lysine, followed by Fmoc-Cys(Trt)-OH to form the branched structure.

- specify Fmoc-L-Arg(Pbf) Wang resin

Fmoc-L-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin was used as the starting material, with the arginine residue pre-loaded.

- describe Fmoc-deprotection and washing steps

Fmoc deprotection was performed after each coupling, with washing steps between each reaction.

- describe coupling of Fmoc-ΔPro-OH

Fmoc-ΔPro-OH was coupled using HATU/DIPEA for 90 minutes.

- describe coupling of Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH

Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH was coupled under the same conditions, with chloranil test confirmation.

- describe coupling of Boc-Lys(Fmoc)-OH

Boc-Lys(Fmoc)-OH was coupled to the growing chain, with the Boc group remaining intact until final cleavage.

- describe coupling of Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH

Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH was coupled to the ε-amino group of lysine after Alloc deprotection.

- describe Fmoc-deprotection and washing steps

Final Fmoc deprotection was performed, followed by washing and cleavage.

- describe final steps of synthesis

Final steps included cleavage with TFA/scavenger mixture, precipitation in ether, purification by HPLC, and lyophilization.

- describe purification and analysis of final product

Purified compounds were analyzed by analytical HPLC and high-resolution mass spectrometry, confirming molecular weights within 0.1 Da of theoretical values and purity greater than 95%.
