# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapy, specifically to novel therapeutic strategies targeting the chemokine CCL20 and its receptor CCR6 for the treatment of malignant tumors. The invention encompasses the use of CCL20-neutralizing antibodies as potent agents for inhibiting tumor growth, vascularization, invasion, and metastasis in a broad spectrum of human cancers. These cancers include those of epithelial, mesenchymal, and hematopoietic origin that exhibit aberrant expression of CCL20 and/or its receptor CCR6, particularly in contexts where such expression is driven by or correlated with elevated levels of the chemokine receptor CXCR4. The invention provides a mechanistic and therapeutic framework for interrupting a previously unrecognized signaling axis that contributes to tumor progression, offering a targeted approach to cancer intervention that is distinct from conventional cytotoxic or broad-spectrum immunotherapies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- Chemokines are a family of small, secreted signaling proteins that orchestrate the directed migration of immune cells through gradients of concentration, a process known as chemotaxis. Beyond their canonical roles in immune surveillance and inflammation, chemokines participate in a wide array of physiological processes including embryonic development, hematopoiesis, lymphopoiesis, and tissue homeostasis. In the context of malignancy, however, chemokines and their receptors are frequently hijacked by tumor cells to facilitate survival, proliferation, and dissemination. Among the most extensively studied chemokine receptors in oncology is CXCR4, which is expressed across a diverse array of human cancers including gliomas, breast carcinomas, leukemias, uterine cancers, neuroblastomas, and pancreatic adenocarcinomas. Its ligand, CXCL12, has been shown to promote tumor cell migration, enhance angiogenesis, and support metastatic seeding in distant organs. Despite extensive efforts to inhibit the CXCR4/CXCL12 axis, clinical outcomes have been limited, suggesting the existence of compensatory or downstream pathways that sustain tumor progression. Another chemokine, CCL20, and its sole receptor CCR6, have been implicated in inflammatory conditions and mucosal immunity, but their role in cancer remains controversial. Some studies suggest that CCL20 may recruit immature dendritic cells to the tumor microenvironment, potentially enhancing antitumor immunity, while others report that CCL20 secretion by tumor cells directly stimulates proliferation and adhesion of CCR6-expressing cancer cells, thereby promoting tumor aggressiveness. This duality has led to conflicting interpretations in the literature and has hindered the development of targeted therapies. Prior art has focused primarily on blocking CXCR4 or other chemokine receptors, but no therapeutic agent has been developed that specifically neutralizes CCL20 to inhibit tumor growth. Consequently, there remains a significant unmet medical need for therapies that target the CCL20/CCR6 axis, particularly in cancers where CCL20 expression is elevated and correlates with advanced disease stage, poor prognosis, or resistance to existing treatments.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention provides a composition for the treatment of cancer comprising a CCL20-neutralizing antibody, wherein the antibody specifically binds to human CCL20 and blocks its interaction with the CCR6 receptor, thereby inhibiting tumor growth, vascularization, and metastasis. This invention is based on the surprising discovery that CCL20 functions not merely as a chemoattractant but as a direct autocrine and paracrine growth and adhesion factor for a variety of human cancer cells, particularly those expressing CCR6 and/or CXCR4. The inventors have demonstrated that CCL20 promotes the proliferation and adhesion of cancer cells to extracellular matrix components such as collagen I, and that its overexpression enhances tumor vascularization and invasiveness in vivo. Neutralization of CCL20 with specific monoclonal antibodies results in significant inhibition of tumor growth, induction of tumor regression, and suppression of metastatic spread in multiple preclinical models. The invention encompasses a pharmaceutical composition comprising a CCL20-neutralizing antibody, wherein the antibody may be a monoclonal, chimeric, humanized, or fully human antibody, or any functional fragment thereof capable of specifically antagonizing the CCL20/CCR6 interaction. The invention further encompasses methods for treating cancer by administering such an antibody to a subject diagnosed with a CCL20-dependent tumor, including but not limited to prostate cancer, glioma, leukemia, uterine cancer, colon cancer, and melanoma. The method of treatment involves inhibiting tumor growth and progression, reducing tumor vascularization, and inducing tumor regression through the blockade of CCL20 signaling. The invention also includes a pharmaceutical pack or kit comprising the CCL20-neutralizing antibody and instructions for use in the treatment of cancers exhibiting CCL20 and/or CCR6 expression.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- The invention is directed to the use of anti-CCL20 antibodies for the treatment of cancer, particularly cancers that exhibit expression of CCR6 and/or CXCR4, which are associated with aggressive disease phenotypes and poor clinical outcomes. The method of treating CCL20-dependent cancer involves administering a therapeutically effective amount of a CCL20-neutralizing antibody to a subject diagnosed with a tumor that expresses CCL20 and/or CCR6, wherein the antibody binds to CCL20 with high specificity and prevents its interaction with CCR6. The invention specifically targets tumors that co-express CCR6 and CXCR4, as these tumors demonstrate heightened dependence on the CCL20 pathway for growth and survival. The method for inhibiting tumor growth and/or progression involves the administration of the antibody to reduce the proliferation of cancer cells and to suppress the autocrine signaling loop driven by CCL20 secretion. The method for inhibiting tumor vascularization involves the disruption of CCL20-mediated angiogenic signaling, leading to reduced formation of functional tumor vasculature and increased tumor hypoxia and necrosis. The method for inducing or enhancing tumor regression involves the sustained blockade of CCL20, resulting in decreased tumor cell survival, increased apoptosis, and loss of invasive capacity. The invention is applicable to a range of cancer types, including glioma, leukemia, uterine cancer, prostate cancer, colon cancer, pancreatic cancer, and melanoma, all of which have been shown to express CCL20 and/or CCR6. The antibody MAB360, a monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to human CCL20 and neutralizes its biological activity, is a preferred embodiment of the invention. Administration of the antibody is performed by injection or infusion, either subcutaneously, intravenously, or intratumorally, depending on the tumor location and clinical context. The method for inhibiting metastasis of CCL20-dependent tumors involves the systemic administration of the antibody to prevent the seeding and colonization of distant sites by circulating tumor cells. The subject to be treated is one who has been diagnosed with a tumor expressing CCR6 and/or CXCR4, as determined by immunohistochemical analysis, flow cytometry, or molecular profiling. The pharmaceutical composition comprises a CCL20-neutralizing antibody in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, formulated for stability and bioavailability. The medicament is designed for inhibiting tumor progression, growth, or vascularization, and may also be used to reduce tumor size or inhibit invasiveness. A pharmaceutical kit is provided, comprising the CCL20-neutralizing antibody in a sterile container along with written instructions for administration in the treatment of CCL20-dependent cancers. The antibodies used in the invention specifically antagonize the interaction between CCL20 and CCR6, and may be full-length immunoglobulins, Fab fragments, Fv fragments, single-chain variable fragments (scFv), or other antigen-binding domains derived from monoclonal or recombinant sources. The antibody may be chimeric, humanized, or fully human, and may be produced using phage display technology, transgenic mice, or recombinant DNA techniques. The antigen-binding domain of the antibody recognizes a conformational epitope on human CCL20 that is critical for CCR6 binding. The invention includes isolated CDR peptides, humanized antibodies generated by CDR grafting, and mutated human antibodies with enhanced affinity or stability. The antibody may be formulated as a liquid solution, lyophilized powder, liposomal suspension, or transmucosal or transdermal delivery system. The pharmaceutical composition may include excipients such as albumin, polysorbate, sucrose, or histidine to ensure stability and solubility. Administration routes include intravenous, subcutaneous, intradermal, intratumoral, pulmonary, or local delivery. The therapeutically effective amount is determined based on tumor burden, antibody pharmacokinetics, and subject weight, and may be administered in single or multiple doses per week. The invention further encompasses the use of gene therapy or cell therapy to deliver CCL20-neutralizing molecules, and the use of delivery systems such as nanoparticles or viral vectors for sustained release. The invention provides a medicament for the treatment of cancer, identified by its ability to neutralize CCL20 and inhibit tumor growth, vascularization, and metastasis in vivo.

## EXAMPLES

### Experimental Procedures

- Human prostate cancer cell lines PC3, LNCaP, 22RV1, and DU145 were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO2. PC3 cells were transduced with a lentiviral vector encoding human CXCR4 to generate stable CXCR4-overexpressing clones, designated PC3-CXCR4.5. Single-cell clones were isolated by limiting dilution and validated by flow cytometry and quantitative RT-PCR. Secreted CCL20 protein levels in culture supernatants were quantified using a commercially available human CCL20 ELISA kit. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent, reverse transcribed into cDNA, and analyzed by semi-quantitative and real-time PCR using primers specific for CCL20, CCR6, CXCR4, and β-actin. Cell proliferation was assessed by propidium iodide staining and flow cytometry, and by BrdU incorporation assays. Cell adhesion to fibronectin and collagen I was measured by seeding cells onto coated plates, washing away non-adherent cells, and quantifying adherent cells by cell counting. Pertussis toxin was used to inhibit G-protein signaling. CXCR4 expression was silenced using Dicer-substrate siRNA, and knockdown efficiency was confirmed by flow cytometry and qPCR. Tumor xenografts were established by subcutaneous injection of 5×10⁶ cancer cells into SCID/bg or nude mice. Tumor volume was calculated using caliper measurements, and tumor weight was recorded at endpoint. Histological sections of excised tumors were stained with hematoxylin and eosin for morphological analysis. In vivo vascular functionality was assessed using functional MRI to measure changes in tumor oxygen saturation (ΔSo2) during carbogen inhalation. Statistical significance was determined using two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test.

### Example 1

- Expression of CCR6 and CCL20 was characterized in four human prostate cancer cell lines. RT-PCR and flow cytometry revealed that only PC3 cells expressed CCR6 at the mRNA and cell surface level. ELISA analysis confirmed that only PC3 cells secreted detectable levels of CCL20 under basal conditions. Stimulation with CXCL12 increased CCL20 mRNA and protein secretion in a dose-dependent manner in PC3 and PC3-CXCR4.5 cells. Silencing CXCR4 with siRNA reduced both constitutive and CXCL12-induced CCL20 secretion by more than 80%. Treatment with JAK2 inhibitor AG-490 abolished CCL20 secretion, while MEK inhibition had no consistent suppressive effect. These results demonstrate that CXCR4 signaling regulates CCL20 expression via a JAK/STAT-dependent, G-protein-independent pathway.

### Example 2

- PC3 cells stably overexpressing CCL20 (PC3-CCL20.30) were injected subcutaneously into SCID/bg mice. Tumors derived from these cells were significantly larger and more vascularized than those from control PC3 cells. Histological analysis revealed invasive growth into surrounding muscle tissue and extensive aberrant vascularization. MRI analysis of ΔSo2 values demonstrated significantly higher functional vessel density in CCL20-overexpressing tumors compared to controls. These findings confirm that CCL20 promotes tumor vascularization and invasive growth in vivo.

### Example 3

- Mice bearing PC3-CCL20.30 xenografts were treated with either a neutralizing anti-human CCL20 antibody (MAB360) or an isotype control. Treatment with MAB360 resulted in a marked reduction in tumor growth, with 50% of treated mice developing only small, non-progressive tumors. Histological examination of treated tumors revealed extensive necrosis and reduced microvessel density. In vitro, MAB360 completely blocked CCL20-induced adhesion of PC3-CCL20.30 cells to collagen I. These results demonstrate that neutralizing CCL20 inhibits tumor growth and vascularization in vivo.

### Example 4

- Immunohistochemical analysis of 52 human prostate cancer tissue samples revealed that 96% expressed CCR6 and 65% expressed CCL20. Co-expression of CCL20 and CCR6 was observed in the majority of positive cases. High CCL20 expression correlated significantly with higher Gleason scores (p=0.02). In a separate cohort of 48 samples, 79.5% of tumors exhibited co-expression of CXCR4 and CCL20 in the same cancer cells. These findings establish the clinical relevance of the CCL20/CCR6 axis in human prostate cancer and support its targeting for therapeutic intervention.
