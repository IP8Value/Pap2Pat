# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to process for pretreating lignocellulosic material

The present invention relates to an improved process for the pretreatment of lignocellulosic material to enhance its enzymatic digestibility and facilitate the subsequent production of biofuels and biochemicals. Specifically, the invention pertains to a mechanically assisted alkaline pretreatment method that employs a specially configured twin-screw extruder to simultaneously apply shear forces, compressive stress, and thermal energy to lignocellulosic feedstock in the presence of an alkaline aqueous solution. This process enables efficient disruption of the lignin-carbohydrate complex without requiring external heating beyond the heat generated internally through mechanical friction, thereby reducing energy consumption and operational complexity. The method is particularly suited for high-solid-content feedstocks and is designed to be scalable for industrial integration with existing pulp and biofuel production systems.

## BACKGROUND

- introduce renewable energy sources

The global transition toward sustainable energy systems has intensified interest in renewable alternatives to fossil fuels, driven by environmental concerns, geopolitical instability, and the finite nature of petroleum reserves. Renewable energy sources such as solar, wind, and biomass offer viable pathways to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and promote energy security. Among these, biomass-derived fuels represent a uniquely versatile option, capable of replacing liquid transportation fuels and serving as feedstocks for a broad spectrum of chemical products.

- motivate use of biofuels

Biofuels derived from lignocellulosic biomass are particularly attractive due to their non-food origin, abundant global availability, and potential for carbon neutrality. Unlike first-generation biofuels produced from starch or sugar crops, lignocellulosic biofuels utilize agricultural residues, forestry waste, and dedicated energy crops that do not compete with food production. Their deployment supports circular economy principles by converting low-value waste streams into high-value energy carriers.

- describe advantages of lignocellulosic material

Lignocellulosic materials, including corn stover, wheat straw, rice husk, bagasse, and wood chips, are composed primarily of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin. These components form a highly ordered, recalcitrant matrix that resists enzymatic breakdown. Despite this structural complexity, lignocellulosic biomass offers high carbohydrate content, low cost, and minimal preprocessing requirements compared to other feedstocks. Its widespread distribution and seasonal abundance make it an ideal candidate for decentralized biofuel production systems.

- summarize conversion methods for lignocellulosic materials

The conversion of lignocellulosic biomass into fermentable sugars typically involves four sequential steps: pretreatment, hydrolysis, fermentation, and product recovery. Pretreatment is the most critical stage, as it determines the accessibility of cellulose and hemicellulose to enzymatic action. Various pretreatment strategies have been developed, including acid hydrolysis, steam explosion, ammonia fiber expansion, organosolv extraction, and alkaline treatments. Each method presents trade-offs between efficiency, cost, environmental impact, and compatibility with downstream processes.

- describe production of bio-ethanol

Bio-ethanol remains the most commercially advanced biofuel derived from lignocellulosic feedstocks. Its production requires the efficient release of glucose and xylose from polysaccharides, followed by microbial fermentation. However, the natural recalcitrance of lignocellulosic material severely limits sugar yields unless the structural barriers imposed by lignin and hemicellulose are effectively disrupted. Conventional pretreatment methods often require high temperatures, extended reaction times, or corrosive chemicals, resulting in high capital and operational expenditures.

- introduce pretreatment step

Pretreatment serves to solubilize or restructure hemicellulose and lignin, increase porosity, reduce crystallinity of cellulose, and enhance surface area accessible to hydrolytic enzymes. The efficacy of subsequent enzymatic hydrolysis is directly correlated with the degree of structural modification achieved during pretreatment. Therefore, optimizing pretreatment conditions is essential to maximizing sugar recovery while minimizing degradation of fermentable carbohydrates.

- categorize pretreatment methods

Pretreatment methods can be broadly classified into physical, chemical, physicochemical, and biological categories. Chemical methods, particularly alkaline treatments using sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide, or lime, are widely studied due to their effectiveness in lignin removal and minimal carbohydrate degradation under controlled conditions. Alkaline pretreatments are especially advantageous because they preserve cellulose integrity and generate lignin streams suitable for valorization.

- describe alkali pretreatments

Alkaline pretreatment operates by cleaving ester and ether linkages between lignin and polysaccharides, solubilizing lignin, and swelling the biomass matrix. Sodium hydroxide is the most commonly employed agent due to its high solubility, low cost, and strong delignification capacity. However, traditional alkaline methods often require long residence times, high liquid-to-solid ratios, and elevated temperatures, leading to increased water usage, energy consumption, and wastewater treatment burdens.

- summarize prior art of alkaline pretreatment methods

Prior art in alkaline pretreatment includes batch reactors, continuous stirred-tank systems, and steam-assisted reactors. These systems typically operate at temperatures exceeding 100°C, necessitating pressurized vessels and external heating. Some approaches have attempted to reduce energy input by using ambient temperature treatments, but these suffer from incomplete delignification and prolonged reaction times. Other methods employ mechanical agitation to improve reagent penetration, yet lack the intensity and uniformity required for consistent performance across diverse feedstocks.

- describe radio-frequency-assisted alkali pretreatment

Radio-frequency-assisted alkaline pretreatment has been proposed to enhance reagent diffusion through dielectric heating. While this method reduces thermal gradients and improves uniformity, it requires specialized equipment and consumes significant electrical energy. Its scalability remains unproven, and integration with existing industrial infrastructure is limited.

- describe high solids alkaline pretreatment

High solids alkaline pretreatment aims to minimize water usage by operating at solid loadings above 15%. This approach reduces downstream processing costs but introduces challenges in mixing, heat transfer, and mass transfer. Conventional mixers struggle to achieve homogeneity under such conditions, leading to uneven pretreatment and variable sugar yields.

- describe cold sodium hydroxide/urea based pretreatment

Cold sodium hydroxide/urea pretreatment has been explored to reduce energy demand by operating at ambient temperatures. While effective for certain feedstocks, this method requires extended reaction durations and often fails to achieve sufficient delignification for high-efficiency enzymatic hydrolysis. The addition of urea improves lignin solubility but increases chemical cost and complicates recovery.

- describe comparison of different pretreatment methods

Comparative analyses of pretreatment technologies reveal that no single method satisfies all criteria for commercial viability: high sugar yield, low energy input, minimal chemical consumption, rapid processing, and compatibility with existing infrastructure. Acid-based methods generate inhibitors, steam explosion demands high-pressure vessels, and ammonia-based systems require complex chemical recovery loops. Alkaline methods offer a favorable balance but have been constrained by inefficiencies in mixing and heat generation.

- describe Chinese Patent Application CN101121175A

Chinese Patent Application CN101121175A describes a method for alkaline pretreatment of crop residues using a screw conveyor with external heating. The process operates at temperatures above 100°C and requires pre-grinding of feedstock to particle sizes below 5 mm. While achieving moderate delignification, the method relies on continuous external energy input and does not exploit mechanical friction for thermal generation.

- describe Chinese Patent Application CN101255479

Chinese Patent Application CN101255479 discloses a two-stage alkaline treatment process involving soaking and mechanical pressing. The method improves reagent penetration through pressure differentials but still requires prolonged soaking times and does not integrate heat generation within the pretreatment unit. The process lacks the continuous, high-shear environment necessary for uniform and rapid pretreatment.

- motivate need for new pretreatment process

There remains a critical need for a pretreatment process that combines the delignification efficiency of alkaline chemistry with the energy efficiency of mechanical activation, operates at high solid loadings without pre-grinding, eliminates the need for external heating, and integrates seamlessly into existing biorefinery workflows. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing a novel twin-screw extrusion system that generates sufficient internal heat through frictional forces to enable effective alkaline pretreatment under mild external conditions.

## SUMMARY

- introduce pretreatment process

The present invention introduces a novel process for pretreating lignocellulosic material using a specially designed twin-screw extruder that simultaneously applies mechanical shear, compression, and internally generated thermal energy in the presence of an alkaline aqueous solution. This process achieves substantial delignification and structural modification of the biomass without requiring external heating beyond ambient conditions, thereby significantly reducing energy consumption and operational complexity.

- describe process for pretreating lignocellulosic material

The process comprises feeding lignocellulosic material into a co-rotating twin-screw extruder, where it is first dewatered and de-aired to enhance the penetration of an alkaline aqueous solution. The solution is introduced in stages, and the biomass is subjected to intense mechanical shear and compression as it passes through alternating transport and reversed screw elements. The friction generated between the biomass fibers and the screw surfaces elevates the temperature of the mixture to between 90°C and 105°C, enabling effective chemical reactions without external heating. The resulting aqueous slurry is then subjected to a controlled heat preservation step to complete delignification and structural rearrangement.

- highlight advantages of embodiments

The embodiments of the invention demonstrate multiple advantages over prior art methods. First, the process operates at high solid-to-liquid ratios (1:1 to 1:3), minimizing water usage and downstream wastewater treatment costs. Second, the absence of external heating reduces energy consumption and capital expenditure. Third, the process accommodates raw feedstock cut into 2–5 cm pieces without requiring size reduction, simplifying feedstock preparation. Fourth, the generated lignin stream exhibits high purity and molecular weight, suitable for valorization into resins or adhesives. Fifth, the process achieves total sugar yields exceeding 75% with minimal carbohydrate degradation, enabling efficient downstream fermentation. Finally, the system is scalable and compatible with existing pulp mill infrastructure, facilitating rapid industrial adoption.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- define lignocellulosic material

Lignocellulosic material refers to plant-derived biomass composed primarily of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin, along with minor amounts of extractives and ash. Such materials include agricultural residues such as corn stover, wheat straw, rice straw, and sugarcane bagasse; forestry residues such as sawdust, bark, and wood chips; and energy crops such as switchgrass, miscanthus, and poplar. These materials are characterized by a highly ordered, recalcitrant structure in which cellulose microfibrils are embedded within a matrix of hemicellulose and lignin, forming a protective barrier that impedes enzymatic access.

- describe sources of lignocellulosic material

Lignocellulosic material may be sourced from a wide variety of non-food agricultural and forestry waste streams, including but not limited to corn stover collected after grain harvest, rice straw from paddy fields, wheat straw from cereal cultivation, sugarcane bagasse from sugar extraction, and wood residues from logging and sawmills. These materials are typically available in large quantities at low cost and are often considered waste products requiring disposal. Their use as feedstock for biofuel production transforms them into valuable resources, contributing to waste reduction and circular economic models.

- specify lignin content range

The lignin content of the lignocellulosic material used in the process typically ranges from 15% to 30% on a dry weight basis. Materials with lignin content below 15% may not benefit significantly from delignification, while those exceeding 30% may require higher alkali dosages or extended residence times to achieve sufficient structural modification. Preferred feedstocks exhibit lignin content between 18% and 26%, as these levels provide an optimal balance between recalcitrance and treatability.

- specify cellulose content range

The cellulose content of the lignocellulosic material generally ranges from 35% to 50% on a dry weight basis. Higher cellulose content correlates with greater potential sugar yield following pretreatment and hydrolysis. Materials with cellulose content below 35% are less economically viable due to lower fermentable sugar output. Preferred feedstocks contain between 40% and 48% cellulose, ensuring maximum conversion efficiency and economic return.

- describe washing of lignocellulosic material

Prior to pretreatment, the lignocellulosic material is washed with water to remove soluble impurities such as dust, dirt, soluble sugars, and inorganic salts. Washing is performed using a spray or immersion system at ambient temperature for a duration sufficient to reduce ash content to less than 5% on a dry weight basis. The washed material is then dewatered using mechanical presses or centrifuges to reduce free moisture content, enhancing the efficiency of subsequent alkali penetration.

- describe dewatering and de-airing of lignocellulosic material

Dewatering reduces the moisture content of the lignocellulosic material to between 30% and 50% wet basis, while de-airing removes entrapped air pockets that impede uniform reagent distribution. This is achieved through compression within the initial feed zone of the twin-screw extruder, where rotating screws apply axial pressure and reduce void volume. De-airing is critical to ensure intimate contact between the alkaline solution and biomass fibers during subsequent mixing.

- specify solid to liquid weight ratio of feed

The solid-to-liquid weight ratio of the lignocellulosic material and alkaline aqueous solution at the point of introduction into the extruder is maintained between 1:1 and 1:3. A ratio below 1:1 results in excessive dilution, increasing downstream water treatment burden, while a ratio above 1:3 leads to insufficient reagent penetration and heterogeneous pretreatment. The preferred ratio is 1:2, which balances chemical efficiency with process economy.

- define alkaline aqueous solution

The alkaline aqueous solution is a liquid medium containing a dissolved alkali agent selected from the group consisting of sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide, calcium hydroxide, and sodium carbonate. The solution is prepared by dissolving the alkali agent in deionized or tap water to achieve a target pH and concentration suitable for delignification without excessive carbohydrate degradation.

- specify pH range of alkaline aqueous solution

The pH of the alkaline aqueous solution prior to mixing with the lignocellulosic material is maintained between 12.5 and 14.0. A pH below 12.5 results in insufficient lignin solubilization, while a pH above 14.0 increases the risk of carbohydrate degradation and corrosion of equipment. The preferred pH is 13.2, which provides optimal delignification efficiency with minimal sugar loss.

- describe alkaline pretreatment agents

The primary alkaline pretreatment agent is sodium hydroxide, due to its high solubility, low cost, and proven efficacy in lignin removal. Alternative agents such as potassium hydroxide may be used where sodium silicate formation is a concern, particularly in high-silica feedstocks like rice straw or wheat straw. Sodium carbonate may be substituted in systems where causticizing is impractical, though it requires higher dosages to achieve equivalent delignification.

- specify concentration range of sodium hydroxide

The concentration of sodium hydroxide in the alkaline aqueous solution is maintained between 1.5% and 5.0% by weight. A concentration below 1.5% results in inadequate delignification, while concentrations above 5.0% increase the risk of carbohydrate degradation and chemical recovery costs. The preferred concentration is 3.2%, which corresponds to a sodium hydroxide loading of 0.04 to 0.08 grams per gram of oven-dry biomass.

- describe mixing of lignocellulosic material and alkaline aqueous solution

The lignocellulosic material and alkaline aqueous solution are introduced into the twin-screw extruder through separate inlets and mixed within the barrel under controlled shear and pressure conditions. The mixing occurs in a series of alternating transport and reversed screw elements, which subject the material to repeated compression, elongation, and shear deformation. This mechanical action ensures uniform distribution of the alkaline solution throughout the biomass matrix, enhancing chemical penetration and reaction kinetics.

- specify solid to liquid weight ratio of mixture

The solid-to-liquid weight ratio of the mixture within the extruder barrel is maintained between 1:1 and 1:3, consistent with the feed ratio. This range ensures sufficient liquid to facilitate chemical reactions while maintaining the mechanical integrity of the biomass matrix for effective shear transfer. Deviations outside this range compromise either reagent availability or mechanical efficiency.

- describe addition of alkaline aqueous solution and/or water

The alkaline aqueous solution is introduced in two or more stages along the length of the extruder barrel to ensure progressive saturation of the biomass. An additional stream of water may be introduced downstream to modulate temperature and viscosity, facilitating slurry formation and reducing mechanical load on the extruder. This staged addition prevents localized over-concentration of alkali and ensures uniform reaction throughout the material.

- describe types of mixers

The process employs a twin-screw extruder as the primary mixing device, though other high-shear mixers such as planetary mixers, ribbon blenders, or high-intensity dispersers may be adapted for laboratory-scale applications. The twin-screw extruder is preferred for its continuous operation, scalability, and ability to generate internal heat through friction.

- define shear mixer

A shear mixer is a mechanical device designed to apply high shear forces to a material by creating relative motion between adjacent layers. In the context of this invention, the shear mixer refers to the twin-screw extruder, where the intermeshing screws generate shear rates exceeding 500 s⁻¹, sufficient to disrupt fiber bundles and expose internal surfaces.

- describe application of shear energy

Shear energy is applied continuously as the lignocellulosic material passes through the extruder, with the energy input determined by the screw geometry, rotational speed, and material viscosity. The shear forces cause fibrillation of cellulose fibers, separation of lignin-carbohydrate complexes, and reduction of particle size without the need for pre-grinding.

- specify shear energy range

The shear energy applied during mixing ranges from 50 to 200 kJ/kg of dry biomass. A shear energy below 50 kJ/kg results in insufficient structural disruption, while values above 200 kJ/kg increase mechanical wear and energy consumption without proportional gains in delignification. The preferred shear energy is 120 kJ/kg.

- describe screw mixer

The screw mixer comprises a pair of co-rotating, intermeshing screws housed within a cylindrical barrel. Each screw is composed of alternating transport screw elements and reversed screw elements, which create zones of compression and decompression to enhance mixing and heat generation.

- describe extruder

The extruder is a continuous processing unit designed to convey, compress, and shear the lignocellulosic material while simultaneously introducing chemical reagents. It is constructed from corrosion-resistant materials such as stainless steel or nickel alloys to withstand alkaline conditions.

- describe twin screw extruder

The twin-screw extruder utilized in the invention features co-rotating screws with a L/D ratio of at least 20:1, enabling sufficient residence time for chemical and mechanical reactions. The screws are configured with four transport screw elements followed by four reversed screw elements, repeated in sequence along the barrel length. This configuration generates repeated compression and decompression cycles, enhancing both mixing and heat generation.

- describe generation of friction heat

Friction heat is generated internally through the mechanical interaction between the lignocellulosic fibers and the screw surfaces, as well as between the fibers themselves. This heat is sufficient to elevate the temperature of the mixture from ambient to between 90°C and 105°C without the need for external heating, thereby enabling effective alkaline delignification under mild thermal conditions.

- describe use of external heating

External heating is not required during the mixing phase of the process. The temperature rise is entirely attributable to mechanical friction. However, optional external heating may be applied during the subsequent heat preservation step to accelerate reaction kinetics, though it is not essential for achieving high delignification.

- specify temperature increase range

The temperature of the lignocellulosic material increases by 40°C to 70°C above ambient temperature during passage through the extruder. This increase is sufficient to accelerate the alkaline reaction rate without causing thermal degradation of carbohydrates. The final temperature within the extruder barrel is maintained between 95°C and 100°C.

- describe dewatering and de-airing in shear mixer

Dewatering and de-airing occur concurrently during the initial transport phase of the extruder, where the screws compress the biomass and expel free water and entrapped air. This step is critical to ensure that subsequent introduction of the alkaline solution occurs into a low-moisture, high-surface-area matrix, promoting rapid and uniform chemical penetration.

- describe addition of alkaline aqueous solution in stages

The alkaline aqueous solution is introduced at two or more points along the extruder barrel, with the first addition occurring after initial dewatering and the second addition occurring after the first reversed screw element. This staged addition prevents localized over-concentration of alkali and ensures progressive saturation of the biomass, leading to more uniform pretreatment.

- describe removal of water and/or residual alkaline aqueous solution

After pretreatment, the aqueous slurry is passed through a dewatering unit such as a screw press, belt press, or centrifuge to remove excess liquid. The recovered alkaline solution may be partially recycled after neutralization and purification, reducing chemical consumption and wastewater volume.

- specify temperature range for mixing

The temperature of the lignocellulosic material and alkaline solution during mixing within the extruder is maintained between 90°C and 105°C. This range is achieved solely through frictional heating and is sufficient to promote lignin solubilization without degrading cellulose or hemicellulose.

- specify pressure range for mixing

The pressure within the extruder barrel during mixing ranges from 0.5 MPa to 2.0 MPa. Higher pressures enhance mechanical compression and improve reagent penetration, while pressures below 0.5 MPa reduce shear efficiency. The preferred pressure is 1.2 MPa.

- specify residence time range of lignocellulosic material

The residence time of the lignocellulosic material within the extruder barrel ranges from 20 to 60 seconds. A residence time below 20 seconds results in incomplete pretreatment, while times exceeding 60 seconds increase energy consumption without significant improvement in delignification. The preferred residence time is 30 seconds.

- describe production of aqueous slurry

The output of the extruder is a homogeneous aqueous slurry comprising the pretreated lignocellulosic material suspended in alkaline solution. The slurry is characterized by a uniform fiber morphology, reduced lignin content, and increased porosity, as confirmed by scanning electron microscopy.

- specify dry matter content range of aqueous slurry

The dry matter content of the aqueous slurry exiting the extruder ranges from 25% to 40% by weight. This concentration is optimal for downstream heat preservation and facilitates handling in continuous processing systems.

- specify pH range of aqueous slurry

The pH of the aqueous slurry immediately after extrusion ranges from 12.8 to 13.8. This alkalinity is sufficient to sustain delignification during the subsequent heat preservation step and ensures complete solubilization of lignin fragments.

- describe heat-treatment of aqueous slurry

Following extrusion, the aqueous slurry is transferred to a holding vessel where it is maintained at a temperature between 80°C and 100°C for a period of 1 to 10 hours. This heat-treatment step completes the delignification process and allows for structural rearrangement of the biomass matrix. The heat may be supplied by external sources or retained from the extruder’s residual thermal energy.

- specify temperature range for heat-treatment

The temperature during heat-treatment is maintained between 85°C and 98°C. Temperatures below 85°C slow the reaction kinetics excessively, while temperatures above 98°C risk carbohydrate degradation. The preferred temperature is 92°C.

- specify pressure range for heat-treatment

The pressure during heat-treatment is maintained at atmospheric pressure or slightly above, up to 0.2 MPa. Elevated pressure is not required and may introduce unnecessary safety and equipment costs.

- describe sequence of mixing and heat-treating

The process follows a sequential arrangement in which mechanical mixing in the extruder precedes thermal holding in a separate vessel. This sequence allows for independent optimization of shear and thermal parameters. Alternatively, the heat-treatment may be conducted in-line within an insulated conveyor system immediately following extrusion.

- describe simultaneous mixing and heat-treating

In an alternative embodiment, mixing and heat-treatment may occur simultaneously within a single reactor equipped with both mechanical agitation and external heating. However, this configuration is less energy-efficient and does not fully exploit the frictional heating advantage of the twin-screw extruder.

- describe lignocellulosic material properties

The lignocellulosic material used in the process exhibits a moisture content of 8% to 15% prior to washing, a bulk density of 120 to 200 kg/m³, and a particle size distribution with 90% of particles between 1 cm and 5 cm in length. The material is free of metal contaminants and exhibits low ash content.

- describe alkaline aqueous solution properties

The alkaline aqueous solution is clear, colorless, and free of particulate matter. It is prepared using deionized water and analytical-grade sodium hydroxide. The solution is stored in corrosion-resistant tanks and delivered via calibrated pumps to ensure precise dosing.

- describe mixer properties

The twin-screw extruder is constructed of 316L stainless steel with hardened screw surfaces to resist wear. The barrel is jacketed for optional temperature control, though not required. The drive system provides variable speed control from 100 to 400 rpm, with a torque capacity of at least 500 N·m.

- describe process conditions

The process conditions are optimized to achieve a lignin reduction of 65% to 85%, a glucan recovery of 95% or higher, and a total sugar yield of 75% to 82%. These conditions are reproducible across multiple feedstocks and scale levels.

- describe resulting aqueous slurry

The resulting aqueous slurry is characterized by a fibrous, porous structure with visible delamination and fiber separation under scanning electron microscopy. The lignin content is reduced by at least 70%, and the cellulose crystallinity is decreased by 15% to 25%. The slurry is stable for up to 24 hours at ambient temperature.

- describe one-step process

In a one-step process embodiment, the extrusion and heat-treatment occur in a single continuous unit, with the extruder barrel extended to include a heated section downstream of the mixing zone. This configuration reduces equipment footprint and capital cost but requires precise thermal control.

- describe two-step process

The preferred embodiment is a two-step process comprising mechanical pretreatment in the twin-screw extruder followed by thermal holding in a separate vessel. This separation allows for independent optimization of shear and thermal parameters, resulting in superior delignification and sugar yield.

- specify mixer preferences

The twin-screw extruder is the preferred mixer due to its continuous operation, scalability, and ability to generate internal heat. Single-screw extruders and batch mixers are less effective due to lower shear intensity and poor homogeneity.

- specify residence time in mixer

The residence time of the lignocellulosic material within the extruder mixer is maintained at 30 seconds. This duration is sufficient to achieve complete mixing and sufficient frictional heating without inducing mechanical degradation of the fibers.

- specify temperature and pressure in two-step process

In the two-step process, the extruder operates at a temperature of 95°C and a pressure of 1.2 MPa, while the heat-treatment vessel operates at 92°C and atmospheric pressure. These conditions are maintained throughout the process to ensure consistency and reproducibility.

- describe advantages of two-step process

The two-step process offers superior control over reaction parameters, minimizes mechanical wear on the extruder, and allows for flexible integration with existing industrial systems. It also enables the recovery and reuse of alkaline solution between batches, enhancing process economics.

- specify residence time in reaction vessel

The residence time of the aqueous slurry in the heat-treatment vessel ranges from 1 to 6 hours, with a preferred duration of 2 hours. This time is sufficient to achieve maximum delignification without significant carbohydrate degradation.

- describe transporting means

The aqueous slurry is transported from the extruder to the heat-treatment vessel via insulated pipelines or a continuous conveyor system. The transport system is designed to minimize heat loss and maintain slurry homogeneity.

- describe reaction vessel options

The reaction vessel may be a jacketed tank, a continuous flow reactor, or an insulated conveyor belt. The vessel is constructed of stainless steel and equipped with temperature sensors and agitation systems to ensure uniform thermal exposure.

- specify heat sources

Heat sources for the heat-treatment vessel may include steam coils, electric heaters, or waste heat recovery systems. In the preferred embodiment, heat is supplied by the residual thermal energy of the extruded slurry, eliminating the need for external heating.

- describe mechanical displacement means

Mechanical displacement means include screw conveyors, piston pumps, and positive displacement pumps used to move the slurry between process units. These devices are selected for their ability to handle high-viscosity, fibrous slurries without clogging.

- describe use of multiple reaction vessels

Multiple reaction vessels may be connected in series to extend residence time and improve delignification uniformity. This configuration is particularly useful for high-throughput industrial applications where continuous operation is required.

- describe heat-treated aqueous slurry

The heat-treated aqueous slurry exhibits a lignin content reduced by 70% to 85%, a cellulose crystallinity index reduced by 20%, and a porosity increased by 300% compared to untreated material. The slurry is ready for neutralization and enzymatic hydrolysis.

- describe subsequent processing steps

Following heat-treatment, the slurry is washed with water to remove residual alkali and solubilized lignin. The washed solid is then subjected to enzymatic hydrolysis using cellulase and β-glucosidase enzymes to release fermentable sugars. The hydrolysate is fermented to produce ethanol or other alkanols, followed by distillation and purification.

- describe conversion to biofuel and/or biochemical

The sugars released during hydrolysis are converted into biofuels such as ethanol, butanol, or biodiesel, or into biochemicals such as lactic acid, succinic acid, or xylitol, depending on the microbial strain and fermentation conditions employed.

- describe neutralization step

The pretreated slurry is neutralized to a pH of 4.5 to 5.5 using sulfuric acid, phosphoric acid, or carbon dioxide. Neutralization is performed prior to enzymatic hydrolysis to ensure optimal enzyme activity and prevent inhibition.

- describe conversion to ethanol

The hydrolysate is fermented using Saccharomyces cerevisiae or engineered strains capable of co-fermenting glucose and xylose. Fermentation is conducted at 30°C to 37°C for 48 to 72 hours, yielding ethanol concentrations of 6% to 8% by volume.

- describe process for producing alkanol

The process for producing alkanol includes fermentation of the hydrolysate using genetically modified microorganisms such as Zymomonas mobilis or Clostridium acetobutylicum, followed by distillation and dehydration to produce anhydrous ethanol, butanol, or other alkanols.

- describe removing water and lignin

Water is removed from the pretreated slurry via dewatering, and lignin is recovered from the spent liquor by precipitation, ultrafiltration, or spray drying. The recovered lignin exhibits a molecular weight of 20,000 to 25,000 Da and a polydispersity index below 1.5, making it suitable for use in adhesives, resins, or carbon fibers.

- describe washing and neutralizing

Washing is performed with deionized water until the effluent pH reaches 6.5 to 7.0. Neutralizing is then conducted using dilute acid to adjust the pH to 4.8, the optimal range for cellulase activity.

- describe hydrolysis step

Hydrolysis is performed using a mixture of cellulase and β-glucosidase enzymes at a loading of 20 FPU and 5 IU per gram of dry biomass, respectively. The reaction is carried out at 50°C for 48 to 72 hours with continuous agitation.

- describe fermentation step

Fermentation is conducted in a bioreactor under anaerobic conditions using yeast or bacterial strains capable of fermenting both hexose and pentose sugars. The process yields ethanol concentrations of up to 8% by volume.

- describe retrieving alkanol

Ethanol and other alkanols are recovered by distillation using a fractional column, followed by molecular sieve dehydration to achieve fuel-grade purity. The recovered alkanol is blended with gasoline or used as a standalone fuel.

- describe blending with other components

The alkanol may be blended with petroleum-derived fuels in proportions ranging from 5% to 85%, depending on regulatory requirements and engine specifications. The blend is stabilized with additives to prevent phase separation and improve combustion properties.

- describe process for producing fuel

The overall process for producing fuel comprises pretreatment, washing, neutralization, hydrolysis, fermentation, distillation, and blending. The system is designed for continuous operation and may be integrated into existing ethanol production facilities.

- describe wetting and washing lignocellulosic material

The lignocellulosic material is first wetted with water to initiate swelling and then washed to remove soluble impurities. This step ensures that the material is clean and uniformly moistened prior to alkaline treatment.

- describe mixing with alkaline aqueous solution

The washed material is fed into the extruder, where it is mixed with the alkaline aqueous solution under high shear. The mixing is completed within 30 seconds, resulting in a homogeneous slurry with uniform chemical distribution.

- describe heat-treating aqueous slurry

The slurry is transferred to a holding vessel and maintained at 92°C for 2 hours. During this time, residual lignin is solubilized, and the cellulose structure is further modified to enhance enzymatic accessibility.

- describe purification and neutralization

The pretreated solid is washed with water until neutral pH is achieved, then neutralized with dilute sulfuric acid to prepare for enzymatic hydrolysis.

- describe hydrolysis

Enzymatic hydrolysis is performed using commercial cellulase and β-glucosidase preparations. The reaction proceeds for 72 hours, releasing glucose and xylose at yields exceeding 80% of theoretical maximum.

- describe fermentation

The hydrolysate is inoculated with Saccharomyces cerevisiae and fermented for 48 hours. Ethanol concentrations reach 7.2% by volume, with minimal byproduct formation.

- describe distillation

Ethanol is recovered by distillation using a three-column system, achieving a purity of 99.5% after dehydration. The process yields approximately 210 liters of ethanol per ton of dry biomass.

- describe ethanol production

Ethanol production is quantified based on the mass balance of carbohydrates in the original biomass and the yield of ethanol from fermentation. The process achieves a total ethanol yield of 200 to 230 liters per ton of dry corn stover.

- describe other products

In addition to ethanol, the process generates a high-purity lignin stream, a sodium silicate-rich spent liquor, and a nutrient-rich wash water that may be recycled or treated for agricultural use.

- describe non-limiting examples

The invention is not limited to corn stover. Similar results are achieved with wheat straw, rice straw, sugarcane bagasse, and poplar wood, demonstrating broad applicability across lignocellulosic feedstocks.

- describe weight percentages

The dry weight percentages of glucan, xylan, and lignin in untreated corn stover are approximately 42%, 24%, and 22%, respectively. After pretreatment, these values shift to 41%, 16%, and 5%, indicating substantial delignification and hemicellulose removal.

- conclude description

The described process represents a significant advancement in lignocellulosic pretreatment technology, offering a scalable, energy-efficient, and economically viable method for converting abundant agricultural residues into fermentable sugars. By integrating mechanical and chemical pretreatment within a single, friction-heated system, the invention eliminates the need for external heating, reduces water consumption, and enhances sugar yields, thereby enabling the commercial production of biofuels and biochemicals from non-food biomass.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- prepare corn stover

Corn stover was collected from fields in Qingdao, China, and cut into pieces measuring 2 to 5 cm in length. The material was air-dried to a moisture content of 12% and stored in sealed bags until use.

- add sodium hydroxide solution

A 3.2% (w/w) aqueous solution of sodium hydroxide was prepared and introduced into a twin-screw extruder at a solid-to-liquid ratio of 1:2. The sodium hydroxide loading was 0.06 g per gram of oven-dry biomass.

- set twin screw extruder conditions

The twin-screw extruder was operated at a rotational speed of 325 rpm, with a barrel temperature maintained at 95°C through frictional heating alone. The residence time of the material within the extruder was 30 seconds.

- collect and analyze pretreated material

The pretreated slurry was collected and immediately transferred to plastic bags for heat preservation at ambient temperature for 1 hour. The solid fraction was then washed with tap water until neutral pH was achieved and dried at 45°C to constant weight.

- hydrolyze pretreated material

The dried pretreated material was subjected to enzymatic hydrolysis using a mixture of Celluclast 1.5 L and Novozyme 188 at a loading of 20 FPU and 5 IU per gram of dry biomass, respectively. Hydrolysis was conducted at 50°C for 48 hours in a shaking incubator.

- analyze hydrolysis product

The hydrolysate was analyzed by HPLC for glucose and xylose content. The glucan conversion was 83%, and the xylan conversion was 89%. The total sugar yield was 77.6%, demonstrating the effectiveness of the pretreatment.

### Example 2

- prepare corn stover

Corn stover was prepared as described in Example 1.

- add sodium hydroxide solution

A 3.2% (w/w) sodium hydroxide solution was added at a loading of 0.08 g NaOH per gram of dry biomass, maintaining a solid-to-liquid ratio of 1:2.

- collect and analyze pretreated material

The pretreated slurry was collected and heat-preserved for 1 hour. The solid fraction was washed, dried, and analyzed. Lignin content was reduced by 80%, and glucan recovery was 96%.

### Example 3

- prepare poplar wood pieces

Poplar wood chips, measuring 2 to 5 cm in length, were obtained from a local sawmill and dried to 10% moisture content.

- add sodium hydroxide solution

A 3.2% sodium hydroxide solution was introduced at a loading of 0.06 g NaOH per gram of dry biomass, with a solid-to-liquid ratio of 1:2.

- set twin screw extruder conditions

The extruder was operated at 325 rpm for 30 seconds, achieving a temperature of 97°C through frictional heating.

- collect and analyze pretreated material

The pretreated material was heat-preserved for 2 hours, washed, and dried. Lignin reduction was 78%, and enzymatic hydrolysis yielded 75% total sugar recovery, confirming the process’s applicability to woody biomass.
