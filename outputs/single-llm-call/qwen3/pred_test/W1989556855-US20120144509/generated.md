# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- relate to Helicobacter pylori strains

The present invention relates to novel, naturally occurring, and genetically engineered strains of *Helicobacter pylori* that exhibit enhanced capacity to colonize the gastric mucosa of mammalian hosts without inducing acute pathology, and which are uniquely suited for the targeted delivery of biologically active agents to the gastrointestinal tract. These strains possess specific genetic and phenotypic characteristics that distinguish them from conventional clinical isolates and attenuated laboratory strains, particularly in their ability to establish persistent, low-pathogenicity infections in the absence of host adaptation. The invention further encompasses the use of such strains as live vectors for the sustained expression and delivery of therapeutic proteins, antigens, nucleic acids, or immunomodulatory molecules directly to the gastric epithelium and associated lymphoid tissues. The strains are selected based on their capacity for natural transformation, stable genomic integration of exogenous genetic material, and long-term colonization of the gastric niche under physiologically relevant conditions, including acidic pH and immune surveillance. This technology enables a new class of oral therapeutics that leverage the natural tropism of *H. pylori* for the human stomach, circumventing the limitations of systemic administration and offering localized, prolonged, and highly efficient delivery of biological agents.

## BACKGROUND

- introduce live bacteria as delivery vehicles

Live bacterial vectors have long been explored as platforms for the delivery of therapeutic molecules due to their ability to self-replicate, persist in target tissues, and elicit robust immune responses. Bacteria such as *Salmonella*, *Listeria*, and *Lactobacillus* have been engineered to express antigens or cytokines, yet their clinical utility has been limited by poor colonization efficiency, rapid clearance by the host immune system, or unintended systemic dissemination. The gastric environment presents a unique opportunity for bacterial delivery due to its relative sterility compared to the lower gastrointestinal tract and its direct interface with mucosal immune structures. However, few bacterial species are naturally adapted to survive and persist in this niche without causing disease. The development of safe, effective, and controllable bacterial delivery systems for gastric targeting has remained an unmet challenge in mucosal immunotherapy and precision medicine.

- motivate advantages of bacterial delivery

Bacterial delivery systems offer several compelling advantages over conventional pharmaceutical approaches. They can produce therapeutic proteins continuously at the site of action, reducing the need for repeated dosing. They can penetrate mucus layers and adhere to epithelial surfaces, ensuring localized concentration of the delivered agent. They can stimulate both innate and adaptive immunity, enhancing the immunogenicity of co-delivered antigens. Furthermore, they can be genetically programmed to respond to environmental cues such as pH, nutrient availability, or inflammation, enabling dynamic control over gene expression. These features make bacterial vectors particularly attractive for treating chronic gastric conditions, including *H. pylori*-associated gastritis, peptic ulcer disease, and gastric cancer precursors, where sustained local modulation of the mucosal environment is critical.

- summarize limitations of current systems

Current bacterial delivery systems suffer from multiple limitations. Many strains are too attenuated to colonize effectively, resulting in transient presence and insufficient therapeutic exposure. Others retain residual virulence, triggering inflammation or systemic immune activation that compromises safety. Genetic instability is common, with plasmid-based expression systems frequently lost during replication. Additionally, most strains require artificial genetic modifications that impair fitness, or they depend on host immunosuppression to establish colonization, which is clinically untenable. None of the existing systems have demonstrated the combination of long-term persistence, low pathogenicity, and high transformability required for reliable, repeatable, and scalable therapeutic application in immunocompetent hosts.

- describe problems with attenuated bacteria

Attenuated bacterial strains, whether derived from chemical mutagenesis, gene deletion, or chemical inactivation, often lose their ability to compete with the native microbiota or to withstand gastric acidity and host immune clearance. Many such strains fail to colonize even in germ-free animal models, rendering them ineffective as delivery vehicles. Furthermore, attenuation frequently disrupts metabolic pathways essential for survival in the gastric niche, leading to rapid decline in bacterial load and loss of therapeutic function. Even when colonization is achieved, it is often transient, necessitating frequent re-administration, which increases the risk of immune tolerance or adverse reactions. The inability to integrate foreign DNA stably into the chromosome further limits their utility, as extrachromosomal elements are prone to segregation and loss during cell division.

- introduce Helicobacter pylori as solution

*Helicobacter pylori* is uniquely positioned to overcome these limitations. It is the only bacterium known to chronically colonize the human stomach in the absence of immunosuppression, persisting for decades in over half the global population. It possesses intrinsic mechanisms to survive extreme acidity, evade immune detection, and modulate host inflammatory responses. Crucially, *H. pylori* is naturally transformable, allowing for efficient, homologous recombination-based integration of exogenous DNA into its genome without reliance on plasmids or antibiotic selection markers. Recent discoveries have identified clinical isolates that colonize murine models without prior adaptation, retain low pathogenicity despite robust colonization, and induce durable immune responses without eliciting severe gastritis. These strains represent a breakthrough in bacterial vector design, offering a platform that is both biologically sophisticated and clinically translatable.

## SUMMARY

- introduce isolated H. pylori strains

The present invention provides novel, isolated strains of *Helicobacter pylori* that have been identified and characterized for their ability to colonize the gastric mucosa of mammalian hosts with minimal inflammation and long-term stability. These strains are derived from human clinical isolates and have been selected through rigorous screening for natural transformability, gastric colonization efficiency, and immunological compatibility. Unlike conventional laboratory strains, these isolates do not require genetic manipulation for adaptation to murine hosts, and they maintain stable colonization over extended periods without inducing significant tissue damage or systemic immune activation.

- describe characteristics of H. pylori strains

The isolated strains exhibit a distinct phenotype characterized by high urease activity, intact flagellar motility, and the absence of virulence determinants associated with severe gastritis or gastric cancer, including CagA and VacA. They are capable of natural transformation with linear DNA fragments, allowing for precise, marker-free integration of heterologous genes into chromosomal loci. These strains colonize the gastric mucosa at high density, persist for months to years in immunocompetent mice, and induce a balanced Th1/Th2 immune response that supports bacterial persistence without pathology. Their genomes are stable over multiple passages, and they retain their colonization capacity even after repeated in vivo passaging.

- motivate use of H. pylori as delivery vehicle

The unique biological properties of these strains make them ideal vehicles for the delivery of biologically active agents to the gastric mucosa. Their natural tropism for the stomach ensures targeted delivery, while their ability to persist without causing disease enables sustained therapeutic expression. Their capacity for natural transformation allows for the stable integration of genes encoding therapeutic proteins, antigens, or immunomodulators directly into the chromosome, eliminating the risk of plasmid loss. These strains can be used to deliver vaccines against gastric pathogens, to modulate the local immune environment in autoimmune or inflammatory conditions, or to express tumor-suppressive or anti-inflammatory proteins in the context of premalignant gastric lesions.

- introduce second aspect of invention

A second aspect of the invention relates to the use of these strains for the induction of antigen-specific immune responses in the gastric mucosa and associated lymphoid tissues. The strains, when engineered to express foreign antigens, elicit robust and durable humoral and cellular immune responses that are localized to the gastric compartment and extend to systemic circulation. This feature enables their use as oral vaccines against infectious agents, including *H. pylori* itself, or as platforms for tolerance induction in autoimmune disorders affecting the gastrointestinal tract.

- describe characteristics of second aspect

The second aspect is characterized by the ability of the strains to induce high-titer, antigen-specific IgG and IgA antibodies in serum and gastric secretions, respectively, without triggering excessive inflammation. The immune response is sustained over time, correlating with persistent bacterial colonization. The strains do not induce regulatory T-cell exhaustion or immune tolerance, but rather promote a balanced, adaptive immune state that supports long-term antigen presentation. This makes them particularly suitable for therapeutic vaccination strategies requiring durable immunity without systemic side effects.

- introduce specific H. pylori strains

The invention further provides specific, named strains of *H. pylori* that have been isolated, characterized, and deposited for public access. These strains include OND737, OND738, OND739, OND740, OND248, and OND256, each of which has been demonstrated to colonize multiple mouse strains with high efficiency and low pathogenicity.

- describe H. pylori strain OND737

Strain OND737 is a clinical isolate derived from a patient with non-ulcer dyspepsia. It exhibits high urease activity, robust motility, and the absence of CagA and VacA genes. It colonizes C57BL/6, DBA/2J, and FVB/n mice with densities exceeding 10⁶ CFU/g of gastric tissue at 10 months post-inoculation. It is naturally transformable with linear DNA fragments and integrates exogenous sequences with >95% efficiency. It induces moderate IgG titers against its own antigens without elevating pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β or TNF-α.

- describe H. pylori strain OND738

Strain OND738 was isolated from a healthy individual with no history of gastric disease. It possesses a unique genomic signature characterized by a truncated *hopQ* allele and an intact *babA* gene. It colonizes C3H, 129/s, and Swiss mice with high efficiency, achieving stable colonization for over 12 months. It demonstrates enhanced natural transformation efficiency compared to reference strain X47 and integrates heterologous DNA into the *ureA* locus without disrupting urease function. It elicits a Th2-skewed immune response with elevated IL-4 and IgG1 production, making it ideal for vaccine applications requiring antibody-mediated immunity.

- describe H. pylori strain OND739

Strain OND739 is characterized by its ability to colonize immunocompromised and immunocompetent hosts with equal efficiency. It lacks the *cag* pathogenicity island and expresses a novel variant of the outer membrane protein HopZ. It achieves colonization densities of 10⁷ CFU/g in C57BL/6 mice and maintains these levels for 18 months. It is highly stable in culture and retains its colonization phenotype after 20 serial passages. It is capable of expressing and secreting recombinant proteins under the control of the *ureA* promoter, enabling pH-responsive expression in the acidic gastric environment.

- describe H. pylori strain OND740

Strain OND740 is a low-pathogenicity isolate that induces minimal gastric inflammation despite persistent colonization. It carries a mutation in the *flaA* gene that reduces motility by 40% but does not impair colonization. It is uniquely capable of colonizing aged mice and exhibits enhanced biofilm formation on gastric epithelial cells. It is highly transformable and supports stable integration of large DNA fragments (>10 kb). It induces a regulatory immune phenotype characterized by elevated IL-10 and TGF-β, making it suitable for applications in immune tolerance induction.

- describe H. pylori strain OND248

Strain OND248 was isolated from a patient with gastric adenocarcinoma and exhibits an intermediate phenotype between pathogenic and commensal strains. It colonizes CBA and DBA/2 mice with moderate efficiency and induces a balanced Th1/Th2 response. It expresses a novel urease surface epitope that enhances binding to CD74 on dendritic cells, promoting antigen presentation without inflammation. It is capable of delivering tumor-associated antigens and has been shown to reduce tumor burden in murine models of gastric cancer.

- describe H. pylori strain OND256

Strain OND256 is distinguished by its exceptional stability in acidic conditions and its ability to colonize mice fed a casein-rich diet with significantly higher efficiency than other strains. It possesses a unique regulatory element upstream of the *napA* gene that enhances expression under low-nutrient conditions. It colonizes all tested mouse strains, including immunodeficient models, and maintains colonization for over two years. It is the most transformable strain identified, with integration efficiency exceeding 98% for linear DNA fragments. It is the preferred strain for large-scale therapeutic applications due to its robustness, genetic stability, and consistent immunogenicity.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

- introduce patent application

This patent application describes novel strains of *Helicobacter pylori* and their use as delivery vehicles for biologically active agents in the gastrointestinal tract. The invention provides a platform for the development of oral therapeutics that exploit the natural biology of *H. pylori* to achieve targeted, sustained, and localized delivery of proteins, nucleic acids, and immunomodulators. The strains disclosed herein are isolated from human clinical samples, rigorously characterized for colonization capacity and pathogenicity, and demonstrated to be genetically stable and transformable. The invention further encompasses methods for their use in vaccination, immune modulation, and targeted gene delivery.

- define scope of invention

The scope of the invention includes all isolated strains of *H. pylori* possessing the phenotypic and genotypic characteristics described herein, including but not limited to OND737, OND738, OND739, OND740, OND248, and OND256. The invention further includes any derivatives, mutants, or recombinant strains thereof that retain the ability to colonize the gastric mucosa of mammals with low pathogenicity and to stably integrate exogenous genetic material. The invention also includes methods of using these strains for the delivery of therapeutic agents, the induction of immune responses, and the treatment of gastric and systemic diseases.

- incorporate prior art by reference

All methods of bacterial culture, genetic manipulation, mouse infection, ELISA, and histological analysis described herein are consistent with standard techniques in the field and are incorporated by reference from established protocols in microbiology, molecular biology, and immunology, including those described in the Journal of Clinical Microbiology, Infection and Immunity, and Nature Protocols.

- describe conventional techniques

Conventional techniques for the isolation and propagation of *H. pylori* involve culture on blood agar under microaerobic conditions, selection using urease-based media, and confirmation by PCR for virulence genes. Genetic manipulation typically relies on plasmid-based transformation, antibiotic selection, and repeated passaging, which often results in loss of colonization capacity. Mouse infection models traditionally use adapted laboratory strains such as X47 or SS1, which require prior passage in mice and frequently induce severe gastritis.

- define terminology

As used herein, “isolated” means a strain that has been separated from its original biological source and purified to homogeneity, or genetically modified in a controlled manner. “Natural transformation” refers to the uptake and integration of exogenous DNA into the bacterial genome via homologous recombination without the use of artificial vectors or electroporation. “Low pathogenicity” denotes the absence of histological signs of active gastritis, neutrophil infiltration, or epithelial damage in the gastric mucosa despite persistent colonization. “Chronic infection” refers to colonization lasting longer than three months in the absence of host immunosuppression. “Mammalian subject” includes humans, non-human primates, rodents, and livestock. “Biologically active agent” includes proteins, peptides, nucleic acids, small molecules, and immunomodulators capable of eliciting a biological effect upon delivery.

- describe characteristics of H. pylori strains

The strains of the invention are characterized by their ability to colonize the gastric mucosa of immunocompetent mammals without inducing significant inflammation, their capacity for natural transformation, their genetic stability over multiple generations, and their retention of urease activity and motility. They lack key virulence factors such as CagA and VacA, and they do not elicit elevated levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-8, TNF-α, or IFN-γ. Their colonization density remains stable over time, and they are capable of expressing heterologous genes under endogenous promoters without loss of fitness.

- define low pathogenicity

Low pathogenicity, as defined herein, refers to the absence of histological evidence of active gastritis, including the lack of neutrophil infiltration, epithelial erosion, or glandular atrophy in gastric biopsies, despite the presence of high bacterial loads. Strains exhibiting low pathogenicity do not induce elevated serum levels of inflammatory markers, and they do not cause weight loss or behavioral changes in infected animals.

- describe virulence factors

Virulence factors refer to bacterial molecules that contribute to tissue damage, immune evasion, or systemic dissemination. In *H. pylori*, these include CagA, VacA, BabA, SabA, and OipA. The strains of the invention are devoid of functional CagA and VacA genes and express non-functional or truncated variants of other adhesins, thereby minimizing their capacity to induce inflammation or epithelial disruption.

- define chronic infection

Chronic infection, as used herein, refers to the persistent colonization of the gastric mucosa for a duration exceeding three months in immunocompetent hosts, with bacterial densities remaining above 10⁴ CFU/g of gastric tissue throughout the infection period. Chronic infection is associated with the establishment of a stable host-pathogen equilibrium characterized by immune modulation rather than immune elimination.

- describe natural transformation

Natural transformation is the process by which *H. pylori* takes up free DNA from its environment and incorporates it into its genome via homologous recombination. This process is mediated by the ComEC and ComF proteins and is highly efficient in the strains of the invention, allowing for the integration of DNA fragments up to 15 kb in length without the need for selectable markers or artificial delivery systems.

- define mammalian subject

A mammalian subject includes any warm-blooded vertebrate of the class Mammalia, including humans, non-human primates, rodents, rabbits, dogs, cats, pigs, and livestock. The strains of the invention are effective in all tested mammalian species, with particular utility in humans due to their natural adaptation to the gastric niche.

- describe ability to infect mice without host adaptation

The strains of the invention are capable of colonizing naïve, immunocompetent mice without prior passage or adaptation in the host. Unlike conventional laboratory strains, which require multiple rounds of mouse passage to regain colonization ability, these strains establish infection upon first inoculation, demonstrating intrinsic fitness for the murine gastric environment.

- identify clinical isolates of H. pylori

Clinical isolates were obtained from gastric biopsies of patients undergoing endoscopy for dyspepsia or screening. Isolates were cultured on selective media, genotyped for virulence markers, and screened for natural transformability. Strains that colonized mice without adaptation and induced minimal inflammation were selected for further characterization.

- describe mutants or derivatives of H. pylori strains

Mutants or derivatives of the disclosed strains include those with targeted insertions, deletions, or substitutions in chromosomal genes, provided that the resulting strain retains the ability to colonize the gastric mucosa with low pathogenicity and to stably express heterologous genes. Derivatives may include strains expressing fluorescent reporters, antibiotic resistance markers, or therapeutic proteins under the control of inducible promoters.

- describe delivery of biologically active agents

Biologically active agents are delivered by integrating nucleic acid sequences encoding the agent into the chromosome of the strain via natural transformation. The agent is expressed constitutively or under the control of a gastric pH-responsive promoter, ensuring localized production within the stomach. Agents include antigens, cytokines, antibodies, enzymes, and RNA molecules.

- define isolated nucleic acid

An isolated nucleic acid is a DNA or RNA molecule that has been separated from its native genomic context and is operably linked to regulatory elements enabling its expression in *H. pylori*. It may encode a protein, peptide, or regulatory RNA and may be derived from any source, including human, viral, bacterial, or synthetic origins.

- describe methods for isolating nucleic acids

Nucleic acids are isolated using standard molecular biology techniques, including PCR amplification, restriction digestion, and gel purification. Sequences are cloned into transformation vectors flanked by homologous regions for integration into the *H. pylori* genome. Transformation is performed by co-incubation of purified DNA with bacterial lawns under microaerobic conditions.

- describe biologically active agents

Biologically active agents include, but are not limited to, tumor antigens, allergens, autoantigens, immunomodulatory cytokines such as IL-10 or TGF-β, neutralizing antibodies, CRISPR-Cas components, and antimicrobial peptides. These agents are expressed at high levels within the gastric lumen and mucosa, enabling localized therapeutic effects.

- describe pharmaceutical use of recombinant H. pylori strains

The recombinant strains are formulated as oral suspensions in buffered saline or lyophilized powders for administration to mammals. They are administered in single or multiple doses and are stable at 4°C for extended periods. They are suitable for use in prophylactic or therapeutic settings for gastric cancer, peptic ulcer disease, autoimmune gastritis, and infectious diseases.

- describe method of inducing antibody response

Antibody responses are induced by administering the recombinant strain to a mammalian subject, allowing it to colonize the gastric mucosa and express the antigen of interest. The antigen is presented to antigen-presenting cells in the gastric lymphoid tissue, leading to the activation of B cells and the production of antigen-specific IgG and IgA antibodies detectable in serum and gastric secretions.

- describe integration of isolated nucleic acid into genome

Integration is achieved by homologous recombination between the flanking regions of the nucleic acid construct and the chromosomal locus of choice. Selection is performed by phenotypic screening or PCR verification. Integration is stable over multiple generations and does not require antibiotic selection.

- describe method of identifying suitable H. pylori strains

Suitable strains are identified by isolating clinical isolates, screening for natural transformability, testing colonization in multiple mouse strains, and assessing histological inflammation. Strains that colonize efficiently, persist long-term, and induce minimal inflammation are selected.

- describe testing of isolated strains

Testing involves inoculation of mice with bacterial suspensions, monitoring colonization by plating gastric homogenates, measuring antibody titers by ELISA, and performing histopathological analysis of gastric tissue. Strains are tested for genetic stability over 20 passages.

- describe ability to naturally transform and colonise mouse stomach mucosa

The strains are capable of naturally transforming with linear DNA and colonizing the mouse stomach mucosa at densities exceeding 10⁶ CFU/g for over 12 months, with no evidence of tissue damage or systemic inflammation.

- describe designation of suitable strains

Suitable strains are designated based on their colonization efficiency, genetic stability, transformability, and low pathogenicity. Strains OND737, OND738, OND739, OND740, OND248, and OND256 meet all criteria and are designated as preferred embodiments.

- introduce examples

The following examples illustrate the isolation, characterization, and application of the strains of the invention.

- describe isolation of clinical strains

Clinical isolates were obtained from gastric biopsies of patients undergoing endoscopy. Samples were plated on BHI agar supplemented with horse blood and antibiotics. Colonies were picked, genotyped for CagA and VacA, and tested for urease activity and motility.

- describe resurrect isolates from glycerol stocks

Glycerol stocks were thawed and plated on selective media. Colonies were subcultured three times to ensure purity and viability.

- describe grow isolates on selective blood agar plates

Isolates were grown on BHI agar supplemented with 5% horse blood, 10 µg/mL erythromycin, and 10 µg/mL streptomycin under microaerobic conditions at 37°C for 72 hours.

- describe genotype isolates

Genomic DNA was extracted and analyzed by PCR for the presence of *cagA*, *vacA*, *babA*, and *hopQ*. Strains lacking *cagA* and *vacA* were retained.

- screen isolates for natural transformation

Transformability was tested by adding linear DNA encoding a FLAG-tagged urease fragment to bacterial lawns. Transformants were selected on acidified urea plates and confirmed by PCR and Western blot.

### Example 2

- test clinical isolates in mouse model

C57BL/6 mice were orogastrically inoculated with 1×10⁹ CFU of each isolate. Stomachs were harvested at 3, 6, and 10 weeks for bacterial enumeration.

- challenge mice with bacteria

Mice received a single orogastric inoculation of bacterial suspension in PBS.

- determine level of colonisation

Stomachs were homogenized, serially diluted, and plated on selective media. Colonies were counted after five days.

- identify colonising strains

Strains achieving >10⁵ CFU/g at 10 weeks were designated colonizers.

### Example 3

- further investigate robustness of colonising strains

Colonizing strains were passaged five times in mice to assess stability of colonization and phenotype.

- select true colonising strains

Strains maintaining colonization across passages were selected as true colonizers.

### Example 4

- test long-term colonisation

Mice were monitored for 12 months. Bacterial load and histology were assessed at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months.

- identify strains that establish chronic infection

Strains maintaining >10⁴ CFU/g at 12 months were classified as chronic colonizers.

### Example 5

- determine immunogenicity of colonising H. pylori strains

Serum was collected from infected mice and tested for anti-*H. pylori* IgG by ELISA.

- test IgG antibody response in DBA/2J mouse model

DBA/2J mice were infected with K6 and K8 strains. IgG titers were measured at 4, 8, and 12 weeks.

- measure H. pylori-specific IgG antibodies by ELISA

Plates were coated with whole-cell lysate, blocked with BSA, and incubated with serum. Bound IgG was detected with HRP-conjugated anti-mouse IgG.

- show immunogenicity of K6 and K8 strains

K6 and K8 induced high IgG titers without inflammation, confirming immunogenicity without pathology.

### Example 6

- screen H. pylori strains in C57BL/6 mouse model

Multiple strains were tested for colonization efficiency in C57BL/6 mice.

- identify K4 and K12 strains as colonisers

K4 and K12 achieved >10⁶ CFU/g at 10 weeks and were selected for further study.

### Example 7

Mice were fed either standard chow or a casein-rich diet.

- test effect of diet on colonisation

Casein-rich diet significantly increased colonization density across all strains.

- show improved colonisation with casein-rich diet

Colonization increased 10- to 100-fold in mice fed casein-rich diet compared to standard chow.
