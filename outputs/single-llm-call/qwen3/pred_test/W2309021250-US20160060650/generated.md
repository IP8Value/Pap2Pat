## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention

The present invention resides in the field of plant biotechnology and crop improvement, specifically in the genetic modification of solanaceous crops to overcome reproductive barriers and enhance breeding potential. More particularly, the invention concerns the targeted expression of a microbial cellulose binding domain protein in potato plants to restore female fertility, enable the production of viable seeds through cross-pollination, and facilitate the transfer of complex agronomic traits such as disease resistance and tuber coloration into otherwise sterile commercial cultivars. This field encompasses the application of recombinant DNA technology to manipulate plant development, the engineering of plant cell wall architecture through heterologous protein expression, and the strategic use of transgenic lines as breeding platforms to expand genetic diversity in clonally propagated crops.

- describe relevant art of cellulose binding domains

Cellulose binding domains are modular protein sequences commonly found in microbial cellulolytic enzymes, where they mediate the physical association of the enzyme with insoluble cellulose substrates. These domains, classified into families based on sequence homology and structural features, are known to enhance enzymatic efficiency by concentrating catalytic activity at the surface of cellulose microfibrils. While prevalent in saprophytic fungi and bacteria, cellulose binding domains are notably absent from plant genomes and from the secretomes of most plant pathogens, suggesting evolutionary divergence in substrate recognition strategies. One exception is the cellulose binding domain 1 (CBD1) derived from the oomycete pathogen Phytophthora infestans, which is tightly associated with the hyphal cell wall and may contribute to structural integrity or evasion of host defense mechanisms. Prior studies have demonstrated that CBD1 can bind to cellulose in vitro and localize to plant cell walls when expressed heterologously, but no prior work has linked its expression to alterations in plant reproductive development or fertility.

- motivate need for new potato breeding strategies

The potato cultivar Bintje, developed in the early 20th century, remains one of the most widely cultivated varieties in Europe due to its exceptional yield stability, cooking quality, and adaptability to diverse environmental conditions. However, Bintje is genetically sterile as a female parent, with flowers that undergo premature abscission following bloom, rendering it incapable of producing seed berries or participating in conventional breeding programs. This sterility has persisted for over a century, severely limiting the ability to introduce new traits—such as resistance to late blight, enhanced nutritional content, or improved tuber morphology—into the Bintje gene pool through sexual reproduction. Current breeding strategies rely on labor-intensive, multi-generational crosses with wild relatives or other cultivars, followed by extensive backcrossing to recover the Bintje phenotype, a process that is inefficient, time-consuming, and often fails to retain the desired organoleptic qualities.

- discuss limitations of current potato industry

The global potato industry faces mounting challenges from evolving pathogen strains, climate variability, and consumer demand for improved nutritional profiles and aesthetic qualities. The reliance on clonal propagation of elite cultivars like Bintje perpetuates genetic uniformity, increasing vulnerability to widespread disease outbreaks. Traditional breeding is hindered by the sterility of many high-performing cultivars, while genetic engineering efforts have historically focused on direct trait insertion, often resulting in transgenic lines with unintended phenotypic consequences or regulatory barriers to commercialization. There exists a critical unmet need for a breeding platform that enables the introgression of complex, multigenic traits into sterile cultivars without permanently altering their genetic identity or requiring permanent transgene retention in the final commercial product.

- introduce idea of expressing CBD1 protein in potato plants

The present invention introduces the novel concept of expressing the Phytophthora infestans cellulose binding domain 1 (CBD1) protein in the potato cultivar Bintje to overcome its inherent female sterility. Contrary to expectations that CBD1 might influence pathogen resistance or cell wall degradation, its expression in transgenic Bintje plants was found to induce the development of persistent seed berries, even in the absence of pollination, and to restore the capacity for successful fertilization when crossed with fertile pollen donors. This unexpected outcome reveals a previously unknown role for cellulose-binding proteins in modulating ovary development and reproductive competence in solanaceous species, and provides a powerful tool for revitalizing the genetic potential of one of the world’s most important potato cultivars.
