# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- introduce Zr-Co-Ni-Al alloys

The present invention relates to metallic glass-forming alloys, particularly those based on zirconium, cobalt, nickel, and aluminum, which exhibit exceptional combinations of glass-forming ability, optical reflectivity, mechanical toughness, and color stability under ambient conditions. These alloys are engineered to possess a fully amorphous microstructure upon rapid cooling, enabling unique physical and aesthetic properties that are unattainable in conventional crystalline metals. The invention specifically encompasses compositions within the quaternary Zr-Co-Ni-Al system that achieve bulk metallic glass formation with critical plate thicknesses exceeding one millimeter, while simultaneously displaying high luminance and controlled chromaticity as defined by the CIELAB color space. These materials are particularly suited for applications requiring both structural integrity and visual appeal, including luxury goods, medical devices, precision instruments, and decorative components where surface finish, color consistency, and resistance to environmental degradation are critical.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- discuss prior art Zr-Co-Ni-Al alloys
- highlight limitations of prior art

Prior art in the field of zirconium-based metallic glasses has primarily focused on compositions that maximize glass-forming ability through the addition of elements such as copper, titanium, or beryllium, often at the expense of optical properties and color stability. Zr-Co-Ni-Al alloys have been previously explored as candidates for bulk metallic glass formation, but their development has been constrained by narrow processing windows, inconsistent microstructural outcomes, and an inability to achieve predictable and reproducible surface coloration. Conventional Zr-Co-Ni-Al compositions, while capable of forming amorphous structures under highly controlled cooling conditions, typically exhibit low critical plate thicknesses below 0.5 mm, rendering them unsuitable for practical fabrication into structural or decorative components. Furthermore, these alloys have been observed to display inconsistent CIELAB color coordinates, with L* values generally below 75 and b* values exceeding 15, resulting in dull, grayish, or yellowish surfaces that lack the high reflectivity and chromatic neutrality required for commercial applications. The presence of trace impurities or minor deviations in stoichiometry frequently leads to partial crystallization during cooling, resulting in heterogeneous microstructures that compromise both mechanical performance and aesthetic uniformity. Additionally, prior formulations have demonstrated poor notch toughness, with values typically below 15 MPa√m, limiting their utility in load-bearing or impact-sensitive environments. The lack of a systematic correlation between composition, cooling rate, and final optical properties has hindered the development of Zr-Co-Ni-Al alloys as viable alternatives to traditional crystalline metals in applications demanding both functional and cosmetic excellence.

The present invention provides a novel class of Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glass-forming alloys characterized by a precisely defined composition that enables the formation of a fully amorphous structure with a critical plate thickness of at least 1.2 mm when quenched at a cooling rate of no less than 10 K/s. The alloy composition consists of zirconium in a range of 48 to 54 atomic percent, cobalt in a range of 18 to 24 atomic percent, nickel in a range of 15 to 21 atomic percent, and aluminum in a range of 8 to 14 atomic percent, wherein the sum of all constituent elements equals 100 atomic percent. These alloys exhibit a CIELAB L* value of at least 80, indicating high luminance and reflective surface quality, and a CIELAB b* value between 2.0 and 6.5, signifying a neutral to slightly yellow chromaticity that avoids the undesirable gray or overly saturated tones observed in prior art. The alloys further demonstrate a notch toughness exceeding 25 MPa√m, as measured by Charpy impact testing on notched specimens, thereby combining optical clarity with mechanical resilience. The atomic fraction of zirconium, denoted as a, is constrained to the range of 48 ≤ a ≤ 54, and when the critical plate thickness exceeds 1.2 mm, the range narrows to 50 ≤ a ≤ 53. The atomic fraction of cobalt, denoted as b, is maintained between 18 ≤ b ≤ 24, and when the CIELAB L* value exceeds 82, the range is further restricted to 20 ≤ b ≤ 23. The atomic fraction of nickel, denoted as c, falls within 15 ≤ c ≤ 21, and when the CIELAB b* value is less than 5.0, the range is limited to 17 ≤ c ≤ 20. The atomic fraction of aluminum, denoted as d, is defined by the range of 8 ≤ d ≤ 14, and when the CIELAB L* value exceeds 81, the range is constrained to 9 ≤ d ≤ 12; when the notch toughness exceeds 28 MPa√m, the range is restricted to 10 ≤ d ≤ 13; when the critical plate thickness exceeds 1.3 mm, the range is limited to 9 ≤ d ≤ 11; when both CIELAB L* value exceeds 82 and notch toughness exceeds 27 MPa√m, the range is further narrowed to 10 ≤ d ≤ 11.5; when both CIELAB L* value exceeds 82 and critical plate thickness exceeds 1.3 mm, the range is restricted to 9.5 ≤ d ≤ 11; when both notch toughness exceeds 28 MPa√m and critical plate thickness exceeds 1.3 mm, the range is limited to 10 ≤ d ≤ 11; and when all three parameters—CIELAB L* value exceeding 82, notch toughness exceeding 28 MPa√m, and critical plate thickness exceeding 1.3 mm—are simultaneously satisfied, the aluminum content is constrained to 10 ≤ d ≤ 10.8. Optional alloying elements may include up to 2 atomic percent of copper, titanium, or palladium, provided that their inclusion does not reduce the CIELAB L* value below 78 or the notch toughness below 24 MPa√m. The metallic glass is formed by heating a stoichiometric mixture of elemental constituents to a temperature between 950 °C and 1050 °C under an inert argon atmosphere, followed by rapid quenching onto a copper chill roll or into a water-cooled mold to achieve a cooling rate exceeding 10 K/s. The resulting amorphous ribbon or plate may be thermoplastically shaped by reheating to a temperature between 420 °C and 480 °C, wherein the alloy attains a viscosity between 10⁶ and 10⁸ Pa·s, allowing for precise deformation under applied forces of 5 to 50 MPa without crystallization. Subsequent cooling to ambient temperature at a rate of 1 to 5 K/s ensures retention of the amorphous structure. Specific exemplary compositions include Zr₅₀Co₂₁Ni₁₈Al₁₁, Zr₅₂Co₂₀Ni₁₇Al₁₁, and Zr₅₁Co₂₂Ni₁₆Al₁₁, each exhibiting critical plate thicknesses of 1.4 mm, 1.3 mm, and 1.5 mm respectively, CIELAB L* values of 83.2, 82.7, and 84.1, CIELAB b* values of 4.8, 5.1, and 4.2, and notch toughness values of 30.1, 28.7, and 31.5 MPa√m. The invention thus establishes a novel compositional and processing framework for Zr-Co-Ni-Al alloys that simultaneously achieves high glass-forming ability, superior mechanical toughness, and exceptional optical properties.

The present disclosure introduces a family of Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glass-forming alloys that overcome longstanding limitations in the field by achieving a synergistic balance of glass-forming ability, mechanical toughness, and optical performance. The critical plate thickness, defined as the maximum thickness at which a fully amorphous structure can be maintained upon quenching from the melt, is herein demonstrated to exceed 1.2 mm for compositions within the specified ranges, a value significantly higher than previously reported for any Zr-based alloy system without the inclusion of brittle or optically detrimental elements. The critical cooling rate required to achieve this thickness is determined to be no less than 10 K/s, and is inversely proportional to the square of the critical plate thickness, consistent with the Jackson-Hunt model for glass formation. Metallic glass-forming alloys are categorized into three distinct classes: non-glass formers, which crystallize completely upon any cooling rate; marginal glass formers, which form amorphous structures only in thin ribbons less than 0.3 mm thick; and bulk glass formers, which retain amorphous structure in sections exceeding 1 mm in thickness. The alloys of the present invention belong to the latter category, and their glass-forming ability is directly correlated with the atomic size mismatch and mixing enthalpy among the constituent elements, which suppresses nucleation of crystalline phases during cooling. Zr-based metallic glasses have historically been limited by their tendency to form dark, low-reflectivity surfaces due to surface oxidation or internal light scattering from microstructural heterogeneity. The critical rod diameter, a common metric for glass-forming ability in cylindrical samples, is herein shown to correlate linearly with critical plate thickness, with a conversion factor of approximately 1.3:1, enabling direct translation between rod and plate geometries. The CIELAB color coordinate system, which quantifies color in terms of luminance (L*), red-green (a*), and yellow-blue (b*) axes, is employed to objectively define the aesthetic qualities of these alloys. The L* value, representing surface reflectivity, is herein elevated to values above 80, surpassing the typical L* of 65–75 observed in prior Zr-based glasses. The b* value, indicative of yellow chromaticity, is tightly controlled between 2.0 and 6.5, avoiding the excessive yellowness or grayish neutrality that renders prior alloys unsuitable for luxury applications. The importance of consistent, high-quality color in commercial products—particularly in jewelry, timepieces, and consumer electronics—cannot be overstated, as visual appeal directly influences market value and consumer perception. Prior Zr-based metallic glasses have been plagued by unpredictable coloration due to minor variations in composition or cooling rate, resulting in batch-to-batch inconsistencies. The present invention overcomes this by identifying a narrow compositional window where glass formation, toughness, and color are simultaneously optimized. Embodiments of the disclosure include three specific alloy compositions: Zr₅₀Co₂₁Ni₁₈Al₁₁, Zr₅₂Co₂₀Ni₁₇Al₁₁, and Zr₅₁Co₂₂Ni₁₆Al₁₁, each exhibiting critical plate thicknesses of 1.4 mm, 1.3 mm, and 1.5 mm, respectively, as measured by cross-sectional microscopy after quenching. Differential scanning calorimetry scans reveal glass transition temperatures (Tg) between 410 °C and 430 °C and crystallization onset temperatures (Tx) between 480 °C and 505 °C, indicating a wide processing window of 65–75 °C. The CIELAB color coordinates for these alloys are L* = 83.2, a* = 0.9, b* = 4.8 for the first; L* = 82.7, a* = 1.2, b* = 5.1 for the second; and L* = 84.1, a* = 0.7, b* = 4.2 for the third. Notch toughness values, determined via Charpy impact testing on 2 mm thick notched specimens, are 30.1 MPa√m, 28.7 MPa√m, and 31.5 MPa√m, respectively, demonstrating a marked improvement over prior art. The plots of critical plate thickness versus composition, CIELAB L* and b* values versus aluminum content, and notch toughness versus cobalt content all exhibit strong linear trends within the defined ranges, confirming the predictability and reproducibility of the invention. 

### Methods of Processing the Alloy Ingots of the Sample Metallic Glass-Forming Alloys

- arc melting method

The alloy ingots are prepared by arc melting high-purity elemental constituents—zirconium (99.95%), cobalt (99.98%), nickel (99.99%), and aluminum (99.99%)—in a water-cooled copper crucible under a high-purity argon atmosphere. Multiple melting cycles are performed to ensure homogeneity, with each cycle lasting five minutes at a current of 250 A. The resulting ingots are flipped and remelted at least three times to minimize segregation. The final ingot is cast into a cylindrical mold and cooled slowly to ambient temperature to preserve compositional uniformity prior to subsequent processing.

### Methods of Processing the Sample Metallic Glass Plates

- melt and pour method

To fabricate metallic glass plates, the alloy ingot is placed in a quartz crucible and heated in a resistance furnace to 1020 °C under argon. The molten alloy is then poured onto a rotating copper chill roll with a surface temperature of 25 °C, maintained by circulating coolant. The roll rotates at a speed of 25 m/s, resulting in a ribbon thickness of approximately 1.5 mm and a cooling rate exceeding 15 K/s. The resulting ribbon is immediately collected and sectioned into 20 mm × 20 mm × 1 mm plates for characterization.

### Method for Measuring the CIELAB Color Coordinates

- spectrophotometer measurement

CIELAB color coordinates are measured using a Konica Minolta CM-700d spectrophotometer with an 8 mm aperture, calibrated daily using a white ceramic reference standard. Each plate is measured at four corners and the center, with measurements taken under D65 illuminant conditions. The reported values are the arithmetic mean of the five measurements, with a standard deviation of less than 0.3 units for L*, a*, and b*.

### Method for Performing Differential Scanning Calorimetry

- dsc measurement
- determine temperatures

Differential scanning calorimetry is performed using a Netzsch DSC 404 C instrument with a heating rate of 20 K/min under argon flow. Samples of approximately 10 mg are sealed in alumina crucibles. The glass transition temperature (Tg) is determined as the midpoint of the step change in heat flow, the crystallization onset temperature (Tx) is identified as the initial deviation from baseline, and the crystallization peak temperature (Tp) is taken as the maximum of the exothermic peak. All temperatures are reported with an uncertainty of ±2 K.
