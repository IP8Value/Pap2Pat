# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- introduce immune-checkpoint inhibitors for blood-borne cancers

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic strategies for the treatment of blood-borne cancers through the modulation of immune-checkpoint pathways that suppress cytotoxic lymphocyte activity. Specifically, the invention encompasses the use of inhibitors targeting the poliovirus receptor (PVR), poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2), T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT), Galectin-9, and TIM-3 to restore anti-tumor immune responses in hematological malignancies. These agents are particularly effective in acute myeloid leukemia and other leukemias characterized by aberrant expression of immune-regulatory ligands on malignant blasts. The invention further provides for the combination of such inhibitors with adoptive cellular therapies, including chimeric antigen receptor T cells and bispecific T cell engagers, to achieve synergistic enhancement of tumor cell lysis. This approach represents a paradigm shift in the immunotherapeutic management of blood-borne cancers by directly counteracting the immunosuppressive microenvironment established by leukemic cells through the upregulation of multiple, previously underappreciated checkpoint molecules.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- motivate immune-checkpoint blockade

The immune system possesses the intrinsic capacity to recognize and eliminate malignant cells, yet neoplastic cells frequently evade immune surveillance by exploiting physiological mechanisms designed to maintain self-tolerance and prevent immunopathology. In the context of hematological malignancies, this immune escape is often mediated by the overexpression of inhibitory ligands on leukemic blasts that engage receptors on cytotoxic T cells and natural killer cells, thereby inducing a state of functional exhaustion. The clinical success of immune-checkpoint blockade in solid tumors has demonstrated that reversing this suppression can lead to durable remissions, yet similar strategies have shown limited efficacy in blood-borne cancers due to the distinct immunobiology of the bone marrow and peripheral blood microenvironments. There remains a critical unmet need for novel targets and combinatorial approaches capable of overcoming the unique immunosuppressive networks that protect leukemic cells from immune-mediated destruction.

- describe immune-checkpoint pathways

Immune-checkpoint pathways are evolutionarily conserved regulatory systems that fine-tune the amplitude and duration of T cell activation to prevent autoimmunity and excessive inflammation. These pathways involve receptor-ligand interactions that either co-stimulate or co-inhibit T cell responses. While the CTLA-4/CD80-CD86 and PD-1/PD-L1 axes have been extensively characterized and clinically targeted, recent evidence has revealed a broader network of inhibitory interactions that contribute to immune evasion in hematologic malignancies. These include the TIGIT/PVR/PVRL2 axis, the Galectin-9/TIM-3 pathway, and other emerging ligand-receptor pairs that collectively establish a multi-layered barrier to effective anti-leukemic immunity.

- explain T cell activation

T cell activation requires two distinct signals: the first is delivered through the T cell receptor upon recognition of peptide-major histocompatibility complex on the surface of antigen-presenting cells or malignant cells, and the second is provided by co-stimulatory molecules such as CD28 binding to CD80 or CD86. Without this second signal, T cells enter a state of anergy or dysfunction. In the tumor microenvironment, however, the balance is skewed toward inhibition, as malignant cells express ligands that engage inhibitory receptors such as TIGIT, TIM-3, and PD-1, thereby overriding co-stimulatory cues and suppressing cytolytic function. This results in diminished cytokine production, reduced proliferation, and impaired ability to lyse tumor targets.

- discuss B7 family members

The B7 family of ligands includes CD80, CD86, PD-L1, PD-L2, and other related molecules that bind to receptors on T cells to either activate or inhibit immune responses. While CD80 and CD86 are primarily associated with co-stimulation via CD28, their homologs PD-L1 and PD-L2 serve as ligands for the inhibitory receptor PD-1. In acute myeloid leukemia, the expression of PD-L1 is often inducible rather than constitutive, and its blockade alone has demonstrated only modest clinical benefit. This suggests that other, more consistently expressed inhibitory ligands may play a dominant role in immune suppression in this disease.

- describe TNF family members

Members of the tumor necrosis factor superfamily, including OX40, 4-1BB, and GITR, are known to provide co-stimulatory signals that enhance T cell survival and effector function. However, their ligands are not typically overexpressed by leukemic blasts, limiting their utility as direct therapeutic targets. In contrast, the ligands targeted by the present invention—PVR, PVRL2, Galectin-9, and TIM-3—are highly and consistently expressed on primary AML cells, making them more reliable and broadly applicable targets for intervention.

- explain cytokine signals

Cytokines such as interleukin-2, interferon-gamma, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha are critical mediators of T cell effector function and are often suppressed in the presence of inhibitory checkpoint signaling. The blockade of PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, or TIM-3 leads to a measurable increase in the secretion of these cytokines, indicating a reversal of T cell dysfunction. This restoration of cytokine production is directly correlated with enhanced cytotoxicity against leukemic targets, as demonstrated by increased Granzyme B release and target cell lysis in vitro and in vivo.

- discuss tumor immune resistance

Tumor immune resistance arises through multiple mechanisms, including the downregulation of antigen presentation, recruitment of immunosuppressive cell populations, and the upregulation of inhibitory ligands. In acute myeloid leukemia, the expression of PVR and PVRL2 on leukemic stem cells and progenitor blasts creates a persistent barrier to T cell-mediated killing, even in the presence of potent immunotherapeutic agents such as bispecific T cell engagers. This resistance is not overcome by targeting PD-1 or CTLA-4 alone, underscoring the necessity of addressing alternative checkpoint pathways that are more dominantly expressed in this disease context.

- introduce cancer immunotherapy

Cancer immunotherapy has evolved from a theoretical concept into a clinical reality, with approved agents now standard of care in several malignancies. However, the application of these strategies to blood-borne cancers has been hindered by the unique biology of the hematopoietic system, where immune cells and malignant cells coexist in close proximity within the bone marrow niche. The present invention addresses this challenge by identifying novel, highly expressed immune checkpoints on leukemic cells and providing specific inhibitors capable of restoring immune function without inducing systemic autoimmunity.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce immunomodulating compounds

The invention provides novel immunomodulating compounds designed to disrupt the interaction between immune-checkpoint ligands expressed on leukemic cells and their cognate receptors on cytotoxic lymphocytes. These compounds include monoclonal antibodies, antibody fragments, soluble receptor decoys, small molecule inhibitors, and nucleic acid-based agents that specifically target PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3. Each of these agents is capable of reversing the immunosuppressive signals transmitted through these pathways, thereby reactivating cytotoxic T cells and natural killer cells to eliminate malignant blasts.

- describe pharmaceutical composition with CAR T cells

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising chimeric antigen receptor T cells engineered to recognize antigens such as CD33, CD19, or Flt3, in combination with one or more inhibitors of PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, or TIM-3. This combination enhances the persistence, expansion, and cytotoxic efficacy of CAR T cells in the leukemic microenvironment, overcoming the functional exhaustion typically induced by inhibitory ligand expression on target cells.

- describe pharmaceutical composition with antibody construct

Pharmaceutical compositions are also provided that combine bispecific antibody constructs, such as CD3/CD33 or CD3/CD19 T cell engagers, with inhibitors of the PVR/PVRL2/TIGIT or Galectin-9/TIM-3 axes. These compositions demonstrate synergistic enhancement of leukemic cell lysis, with significantly greater cytotoxicity than either agent alone, as evidenced by increased target cell death, elevated Granzyme B secretion, and prolonged survival in preclinical models.

- motivate need for new immune-checkpoints

Despite the clinical success of PD-1 and CTLA-4 blockade, a substantial proportion of patients with acute myeloid leukemia do not respond to these therapies, and many who initially respond eventually relapse. This underscores the need for alternative immune-checkpoint targets that are more consistently and highly expressed on leukemic cells. The identification of PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3 as dominant inhibitory ligands in AML provides a compelling rationale for their targeting as novel therapeutic strategies.

- introduce CD112, CD155, TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3

The invention identifies CD112 (PVRL2), CD155 (PVR), TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3 as key immune-checkpoint molecules that are overexpressed on primary acute myeloid leukemia blasts and leukemic stem cells. These molecules engage receptors on cytotoxic lymphocytes to suppress activation, reduce cytokine production, and inhibit target cell lysis. Their expression is associated with poor clinical outcomes, and their blockade restores T cell function in both cell line and primary patient-derived models.

- describe inhibitors against CD112, CD155, TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3

Inhibitors against CD112, CD155, TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3 are provided in the form of neutralizing monoclonal antibodies, antibody fragments, soluble receptor fusion proteins, small molecules, and gene-silencing agents. These inhibitors are capable of blocking the interaction between the respective ligand and its receptor, thereby preventing the transmission of inhibitory signals to T cells and natural killer cells.

- describe combination with CAR T cells

The combination of these inhibitors with CAR T cells results in enhanced persistence and cytotoxic activity of the engineered cells in the presence of leukemic blasts. This is particularly significant in cases where CAR T cells alone are rendered dysfunctional by the inhibitory microenvironment. The addition of checkpoint inhibitors prevents this exhaustion, leading to sustained tumor control.

- describe combination with antibody construct

When combined with bispecific antibody constructs such as CD3/CD33 BiTE® molecules, the inhibitors significantly augment the degree of target cell lysis beyond what is achievable with either modality alone. This synergy is observed across multiple AML cell lines and primary patient samples, indicating broad applicability.

- describe inhibitor against CD112

An inhibitor against CD112 is provided in the form of a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds with high affinity to the extracellular domain of PVRL2, preventing its interaction with TIGIT and DNAM-1. This antibody is capable of restoring T cell-mediated lysis of CD112-expressing leukemic cells in vitro and in vivo.

- describe inhibitor against CD155

An inhibitor against CD155 is provided as a fully human monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to PVR with nanomolar affinity, blocking its interaction with TIGIT and CD96. This agent enhances the cytotoxicity of both endogenous T cells and CAR T cells against PVR-positive AML blasts.

- describe inhibitor against TIGIT

An inhibitor against TIGIT is provided as a monoclonal antibody that binds to the IgV domain of TIGIT, preventing its engagement with PVR and PVRL2. This antibody restores T cell effector function and increases Granzyme B secretion in co-culture assays with AML cells.

- describe inhibitor against Galectin-9

An inhibitor against Galectin-9 is provided as a soluble form of TIM-3 fused to an Fc domain, which acts as a competitive decoy receptor. This agent neutralizes Galectin-9-mediated suppression of T cell function and enhances the activity of cytotoxic lymphocytes against Galectin-9-expressing leukemic cells.

- describe inhibitor against TIM-3

An inhibitor against TIM-3 is provided as a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds to the IgV domain of TIM-3, preventing its interaction with Galectin-9 and phosphatidylserine. This inhibition reverses T cell exhaustion and improves survival in murine xenograft models of AML.

- describe antibody construct

An antibody construct is provided that contains two distinct antigen-binding domains: one targeting CD3 on T cells and another targeting CD33, CD19, or Flt3 on leukemic cells. This bispecific construct facilitates the formation of a cytolytic synapse between T cells and malignant blasts, leading to targeted lysis.

- describe CAR T cell

A CAR T cell is provided that expresses a chimeric antigen receptor comprising an extracellular antigen-binding domain specific for CD33, CD19, or Flt3, a transmembrane domain, and intracellular signaling domains derived from CD3ζ and one or more co-stimulatory molecules such as 4-1BB or CD28. These cells are engineered to resist suppression by PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, or TIM-3 when co-administered with corresponding inhibitors.

- describe immunostimulant

An immunostimulant is provided in the form of a toll-like receptor agonist, cytokine, or adjuvant that enhances dendritic cell maturation and promotes the priming of T cell responses against leukemic antigens. This agent is administered in conjunction with checkpoint inhibitors to amplify the magnitude and durability of the anti-leukemic immune response.

- describe iRNA

An interfering RNA molecule is provided that is designed to silence the expression of CD112, CD155, TIGIT, Galectin-9, or TIM-3 in leukemic cells. This molecule is delivered via lipid nanoparticles or viral vectors and results in reduced ligand expression, thereby diminishing the immunosuppressive capacity of the tumor microenvironment.

- describe CRISPR/cas9 technique

The CRISPR/Cas9 technique is employed to genetically ablate the genes encoding CD112, CD155, TIGIT, Galectin-9, or TIM-3 in leukemic cell lines or primary patient-derived cells. This genetic knockout results in increased susceptibility to T cell-mediated lysis and validates the functional importance of these molecules as immune checkpoints.

- describe modulation of intracellular signaling

Modulation of intracellular signaling pathways downstream of TIGIT, TIM-3, or Galectin-9 is achieved through the use of small molecule inhibitors that block phosphorylation events or disrupt adaptor protein recruitment. This approach provides an alternative to antibody-based blockade and enables oral administration of checkpoint inhibition.

- describe pharmaceutical composition

A pharmaceutical composition is provided that contains a therapeutically effective amount of one or more inhibitors of CD112, CD155, TIGIT, Galectin-9, or TIM-3, formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for parenteral, intravenous, or subcutaneous administration. The composition may further include CAR T cells, bispecific antibody constructs, or immunostimulants to achieve synergistic therapeutic effects.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- disclaim prior art

The prior art describes immune-checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-1, PD-L1, and CTLA-4 in the context of solid tumors, but fails to recognize the dominant role of PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3 in the immune evasion of acute myeloid leukemia. While bispecific T cell engagers have been developed for CD33 and CD19, their efficacy is limited by the immunosuppressive ligands expressed on leukemic blasts. No prior disclosure teaches the combination of these engagers with inhibitors of the PVR/PVRL2/TIGIT or Galectin-9/TIM-3 axes, nor does any prior art demonstrate the prognostic significance of these molecules in AML patient cohorts.

- introduce immune-checkpoint inhibitors

Immune-checkpoint inhibitors are defined as agents that interfere with the binding of inhibitory ligands to their cognate receptors on T cells, thereby preventing the transmission of negative regulatory signals. These agents include monoclonal antibodies, antibody fragments, soluble receptors, small molecules, and nucleic acid-based silencing agents. The inhibitors of the present invention are specifically designed to target PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3, which are not effectively addressed by existing therapies.

- motivate treatment of blood-borne cancers

The treatment of blood-borne cancers, particularly acute myeloid leukemia, remains challenging due to the high rate of relapse and resistance to conventional chemotherapy. The development of immunotherapeutic strategies that directly counteract the immune-suppressive mechanisms employed by leukemic cells offers a promising avenue for achieving durable remissions. The present invention provides such a strategy by targeting the PVR/PVRL2/TIGIT and Galectin-9/TIM-3 axes, which are consistently overexpressed in primary AML samples and correlate with poor survival.

- describe CD112 and CD155 as targets

CD112 (PVRL2) and CD155 (PVR) are cell surface glycoproteins that are highly expressed on the majority of primary AML blasts and leukemic stem cells. These molecules bind with high affinity to TIGIT and CD96 on T cells and natural killer cells, delivering potent inhibitory signals that suppress cytotoxic function. Their expression is significantly higher on AML cells than on normal hematopoietic cells, making them ideal targets for selective immune modulation.

- describe Galectin-9 and TIM-3 as targets

Galectin-9 is a β-galactoside-binding lectin that is secreted by leukemic blasts and binds to TIM-3 on exhausted T cells, inducing apoptosis and functional impairment. TIM-3 is upregulated on T cells in the bone marrow microenvironment of AML patients and is associated with a terminally exhausted phenotype. The blockade of this interaction restores T cell viability and effector function, as demonstrated by increased cytokine production and target cell lysis.

- summarize negative prognostic impact of PVR and PVRL2

High expression of PVR and PVRL2 at the mRNA and protein levels is associated with significantly shorter event-free survival, relapse-free survival, and overall survival in two independent cohorts of AML patients. Multivariate analysis confirms that PVR expression is an independent negative prognostic factor, even after adjusting for age, karyotype, and FLT3 mutation status.

- describe blockage of PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT

Blockade of PVR, PVRL2, or TIGIT using neutralizing antibodies or soluble decoys results in a significant increase in the lysis of AML cell lines and primary patient-derived blasts by cytotoxic T cells. This effect is observed both in the presence and absence of bispecific T cell engagers, demonstrating that checkpoint inhibition can enhance the efficacy of existing immunotherapies.

- summarize contradictory data on Galectin-9 and TIM-3

While some studies suggest that Galectin-9 and TIM-3 may have context-dependent roles in immune regulation, the data presented herein demonstrate a consistent and dominant immunosuppressive function in AML. Inhibition of this axis leads to measurable improvements in T cell function and survival in preclinical models, resolving prior contradictions through the use of primary patient samples and in vivo validation.

- describe blockage of Galectin-9 and TIM-3

Blockade of Galectin-9 or TIM-3 using neutralizing antibodies or soluble TIM-3-Fc fusion proteins results in a significant reduction in T cell apoptosis and an increase in Granzyme B secretion during co-culture with AML cells. This effect is synergistic when combined with CD33/CD3 bispecific engagers, leading to enhanced tumor clearance.

- summarize protein expression of PVR and PVRL2

Flow cytometric analysis reveals that PVR and PVRL2 are expressed on over 90% of AML cell lines and primary blasts from untreated patients. The median fluorescence intensity of expression is significantly higher than that observed on normal peripheral blood mononuclear cells, indicating selective upregulation by malignant cells.

- compare protein density on AML cell lines and PBMCs

The density of PVR and PVRL2 on AML cell lines is approximately tenfold higher than on PBMCs from healthy donors. This differential expression provides a therapeutic window for selective targeting of leukemic cells while sparing normal hematopoietic populations.

- describe protein expression on primary blasts of AML patients

In bone marrow aspirates from newly diagnosed AML patients, PVR and PVRL2 are expressed on CD34+/CD38− leukemic stem cells, as well as on more differentiated CD33+ blasts. This indicates that the checkpoint axis is active across the leukemic hierarchy, supporting its utility as a therapeutic target.

- summarize immunosuppressive signal of PVR and PVRL2

The binding of PVR and PVRL2 to TIGIT delivers a dominant inhibitory signal that overrides co-stimulatory cues from CD28 and DNAM-1. This results in decreased T cell proliferation, reduced cytokine secretion, and impaired cytolytic granule exocytosis.

- summarize immunosuppressive signal of Galectin-9 and TIM-3

The interaction between Galectin-9 and TIM-3 induces calcium flux and caspase activation in T cells, leading to apoptosis and functional exhaustion. This pathway is particularly active in the bone marrow niche, where Galectin-9 is secreted by leukemic blasts and stromal cells.

### Examples

- introduce examples of specific embodiments

Specific embodiments of the invention include a humanized monoclonal antibody against PVR with a dissociation constant of 0.8 nM, a bispecific T cell engager targeting CD3 and CD33, and a CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout of PVRL2 in AML cell lines, each demonstrating enhanced T cell-mediated lysis in vitro and in vivo.

- analyze patient samples for immune checkpoint molecules

Patient samples from two independent cohorts of AML patients were analyzed for the expression of PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3 using flow cytometry, RT-qPCR, and immunohistochemistry, revealing consistent overexpression on leukemic blasts.

- correlate expression with patient demographics and clinical survival data

High expression of PVR and PVRL2 was significantly correlated with older age, adverse karyotype, and FLT3 mutations, and independently predicted shorter overall survival, event-free survival, and relapse-free survival.

- identify independent prognostic markers

Multivariate Cox regression analysis identified high PVR expression as an independent negative prognostic marker for overall survival, even after adjusting for age, karyotype, and FLT3 mutation status.

- study therapeutic effect of PVR and PVRL2 blockade

In vitro cytotoxicity assays demonstrated that blockade of PVR and PVRL2 with neutralizing antibodies increased the lysis of AML cell lines and primary blasts by up to 4.5-fold, with synergistic enhancement observed when combined with CD33/CD3 BiTE® constructs.

- perform in vitro killing assays

In vitro killing assays were performed using CFSE-labeled AML cells co-cultured with autologous or allogeneic T cells in the presence or absence of inhibitors, with target cell death quantified by flow cytometry using 7-AAD or Annexin V staining.

- confirm specificity of approach using CRISPR/Cas-9

CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout of PVR and PVRL2 in MV4-11 cells resulted in increased susceptibility to T cell-mediated lysis, confirming that the observed effects of antibody blockade were specifically due to disruption of these ligands.

- exclude effects caused by antibody dependent cellular cytotoxicity

Control experiments using purified T cells and isotype control antibodies confirmed that the enhanced lysis was mediated by T cell cytotoxicity and not by antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

- describe prognostic impact of PVR, PVRL2 and Galectin-9 in AML

High expression of PVR, PVRL2, and Galectin-9 was associated with significantly shorter overall survival in two independent AML cohorts, with hazard ratios ranging from 1.52 to 3.39, confirming their role as negative prognostic biomarkers.

- analyze mRNA expression of PVR, PVRL2 and Galectin-9

mRNA expression levels of PVR, PVRL2, and Galectin-9 were measured by RT-qPCR in 139 AML patients, with high expression defined as above the median, and correlated with clinical outcomes.

- correlate gene expression with patient demographics and clinical survival data

Gene expression levels of PVR, PVRL2, and Galectin-9 were stratified by age, karyotype, and FLT3 status, and multivariate analysis confirmed their independent prognostic significance.

- describe protein expression of PVR, PVRL2 and Galectin-9

Protein expression was confirmed by flow cytometry on primary AML blasts, with median fluorescence intensities significantly higher than on normal PBMCs, validating the translational relevance of mRNA findings.

- describe T-cell induced AML cell lysis

T-cell induced lysis of AML cells was significantly enhanced in the presence of inhibitors of PVR, PVRL2, TIGIT, Galectin-9, or TIM-3, as measured by increased Granzyme B secretion and target cell death in co-culture assays.
