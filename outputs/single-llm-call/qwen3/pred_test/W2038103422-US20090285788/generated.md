## FIELD OF INVENTION

- introduce RNA molecule invention

The present invention relates to novel RNA molecules characterized by a double-stranded variable region flanked by non-self-complementary sequences and a loop-forming region, and to recombinant expression vectors capable of stably expressing such RNA molecules in eukaryotic cells. These RNA molecules are designed to function as potent and selective modulators of gene expression through RNA interference and potentially non-canonical mechanisms, including translational activation or repression depending on cellular context. The invention further encompasses libraries of such RNA molecules, each member of which contains a substantially random sequence within the double-stranded region, enabling unbiased functional screening for phenotypic outcomes such as cell survival, differentiation, or resistance to viral infection. The RNA molecules are expressed from polymerase III promoters in retroviral or lentiviral vectors, allowing for stable genomic integration and long-term expression in target cells. The design of these molecules permits the incorporation of mismatches within the double-stranded stem, mimicking the structural heterogeneity of endogenous microRNAs and enhancing functional potency beyond that of conventional short-interfering RNAs. The invention enables the discovery of RNA sequences that confer biological effects without prior knowledge of molecular targets, thereby circumventing the limitations of rational design and enabling the identification of therapeutics that act through multi-gene or non-canonical pathways.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- limitations of conventional RNA library synthesis

Conventional approaches to RNA interference library construction rely on rational design principles that target individual mRNA transcripts with high complementarity, typically using 19- to 21-nucleotide duplexes modeled after synthetic siRNAs. These libraries are labor-intensive to generate, require extensive bioinformatic prediction of target sites, and are inherently biased toward canonical RNAi mechanisms involving perfect or near-perfect seed region complementarity. Furthermore, the use of siRNA-encoding constructs limits the length of the double-stranded region, reducing potency compared to longer hairpin structures that more closely resemble endogenous microRNAs. Existing random libraries often employ opposing promoters to transcribe complementary strands from a single insert, resulting in linear duplexes that lack the structural stability and processing efficiency conferred by hairpin conformations. These designs also preclude the introduction of mismatches between strands, which are known to enhance the functional diversity and potency of natural miRNAs. Additionally, cloning procedures in bacterial systems frequently eliminate sequences with internal complementarity or secondary structure, leading to significant bias and loss of functional diversity. The inability to generate stable, long-stemmed shRNA constructs with randomized stems and non-complementary loops has hindered the development of unbiased, high-throughput functional screens capable of identifying RNA molecules that act through non-canonical mechanisms, such as translational activation or modulation of multiple targets simultaneously. Moreover, the reliance on single-gene knockdown as a screening endpoint fails to capture the polygenic effects that underlie complex phenotypes such as cell differentiation, pluripotency maintenance, or resistance to apoptosis. There remains a critical need for a method to generate, express, and screen large libraries of RNA molecules with randomized, long double-stranded stems and structurally defined loops, enabling the discovery of therapeutic RNA sequences that function independently of pre-defined target predictions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- provide expression vectors for RNA molecules

The invention provides recombinant expression vectors comprising a nucleic acid sequence encoding an RNA molecule that forms a double-stranded region with a variable, non-self-complementary sequence, a loop-forming region, and a polymerase III transcription termination signal. The vector is operably linked to a promoter that drives constitutive expression of the RNA molecule in eukaryotic cells, preferably a U6 or H1 RNA polymerase III promoter. The vector is configured for stable integration into the host genome via retroviral or lentiviral packaging systems, enabling sustained expression in dividing and non-dividing cells. The expression vector includes flanking restriction sites that permit seamless insertion of synthetic DNA cassettes encoding the RNA molecule, and is engineered to contain a poly-pyrimidine tract upstream of the transcription start site to enhance transcriptional efficiency. The vector backbone is optimized for high-titer viral production and low cytotoxicity, and includes a fluorescent marker gene for tracking transduction efficiency. The expression vector may be further modified to include a polyadenylation signal or intronic sequences to enhance RNA stability and nuclear export, although such modifications are not required for functional activity.

- define RNA molecule structure

The RNA molecule comprises a first region of nucleotides, a second region of nucleotides that is substantially complementary to the first region, and a non-self-complementary loop region connecting the first and second regions, thereby forming a hairpin structure. The first and second regions together form a double-stranded stem of between 25 and 29 nucleotides in length, with at least one mismatch between complementary positions in the stem. The loop region is non-complementary to any portion of the stem and contains between 4 and 12 nucleotides, with a preferred sequence of CTAAAC. The double-stranded stem is derived from a substantially random sequence of nucleotides, such that each RNA molecule in a library encodes a unique sequence capable of forming a distinct secondary structure. The RNA molecule is transcribed as a single-stranded precursor that folds intramolecularly into the hairpin conformation, which is subsequently processed by Dicer into a functional RNAi effector. The RNA molecule may further include a 3′ terminal sequence of three or more uridines to serve as a transcription termination signal for RNA polymerase III.

- describe set or library of recombinant expression vectors

The invention provides a set or library of recombinant expression vectors, each member of which contains a unique RNA molecule encoding a different double-stranded stem sequence of 25 to 29 nucleotides, with a non-self-complementary loop of defined length and sequence. The library contains at least 10⁴ distinct vector constructs, each representing a unique combination of nucleotides within the double-stranded region, thereby ensuring comprehensive coverage of all possible seed sequences of six to eight nucleotides. The library is generated by inserting a synthetic DNA cassette encoding the RNA molecule into a common vector backbone using standardized cloning procedures. The library is packaged into retroviral or lentiviral particles for delivery into target cells, and is maintained as a pooled population to enable functional selection based on phenotypic outcomes. The library may be sub-divided into smaller sub-libraries for iterative screening or optimization, and may be archived as bacterial stocks or plasmid preparations for future use.

- motivate method of generating RNA molecules

The method of generating these RNA molecules is motivated by the need to overcome the limitations of rational design and single-gene targeting in RNA interference screening. By introducing randomness into the stem region of a long-stemmed shRNA, the method enables the discovery of RNA sequences that exert biological effects through mechanisms not predictable by existing algorithms, including multi-gene modulation, non-canonical activation, or context-dependent translational regulation. The inclusion of mismatches within the stem mimics the natural heterogeneity of microRNAs and enhances the functional diversity of the library. The use of a polymerase III promoter ensures high-level, constitutive expression without the need for inducible systems, and the retroviral delivery system allows for stable integration and long-term phenotypic analysis. This approach enables the identification of RNA molecules that confer survival, differentiation, or resistance phenotypes without prior assumptions about molecular targets.

- describe method of generating RNA molecules

The method comprises synthesizing a single-stranded DNA molecule containing a constant region, a substantially random sequence of 25 to 29 nucleotides, and a second constant region flanked by restriction enzyme recognition sites. A primer is annealed to the constant region and extended using a DNA polymerase with strand-displacement activity to generate a complementary strand, forming a double-stranded intermediate. A hairpin-shaped linker nucleic acid molecule is ligated to the recessed end of the extended strand to form a loop structure. The resulting nucleic acid intermediate is digested with a nicking endonuclease to expose a single-stranded region, followed by extension with a strand-displacing polymerase to generate a full-length double-stranded intermediate. The intermediate is then digested with a restriction enzyme that cuts asymmetrically due to the presence of phosphorothioate linkages, generating compatible ends for ligation into a vector downstream of a polymerase III promoter. The ligated product is transformed into bacterial cells, and the resulting plasmid pool is purified and packaged into viral particles for delivery into target cells.

- detail nucleic acid intermediate II

Nucleic acid intermediate II is a single-stranded DNA molecule comprising a 5′ constant region, a variable region of 25 to 29 nucleotides, a non-self-complementary loop sequence, and a 3′ constant region. The variable region is synthesized using an oligonucleotide synthesizer with controlled incorporation of all four nucleotides to ensure randomness, and the loop sequence is designed to prevent self-complementarity and to facilitate intramolecular folding. The intermediate is generated by annealing a primer to a template oligonucleotide, extending the primer with a DNA polymerase, and ligating a hairpin-shaped linker to the 3′ end of the extended strand. The intermediate is purified by gel electrophoresis or column chromatography and used as a substrate for subsequent enzymatic steps.

- detail double-stranded intermediate III

Double-stranded intermediate III is a fully double-stranded DNA molecule generated by extending the nucleic acid intermediate II using a strand-displacing DNA polymerase in the presence of a second primer complementary to the 3′ constant region. The resulting molecule contains a double-stranded stem formed by the variable region and its reverse complement, a loop region connecting the two strands, and flanking sequences that include restriction enzyme sites for cloning. The double-stranded intermediate is resistant to degradation by exonucleases and is suitable for direct digestion and ligation into expression vectors. The molecule may contain one or more mismatches within the stem region, introduced either during synthesis or during polymerase extension, to enhance functional diversity.

- describe restriction enzyme site asymmetry

The restriction enzyme sites flanking the variable region are engineered to produce asymmetric cleavage products by incorporating phosphorothioate linkages at specific positions within the recognition sequence. This modification renders one strand resistant to cleavage while allowing the other strand to be cut, generating a recessed end and a protruding end that are incompatible with blunt-end ligation. This asymmetry ensures directional cloning of the insert into the vector and prevents self-ligation of the vector backbone. The restriction enzyme used is selected from the group consisting of BtgZI, Nb.BbvCI, and Nt.BstNB I, each of which recognizes a sequence modified by phosphorothioate substitution to produce the desired asymmetry.

- provide alternative method of generating RNA molecules

An alternative method involves synthesizing two separate oligonucleotides: one encoding the sense strand of the variable region and the loop, and a second encoding the antisense strand of the variable region. The two oligonucleotides are annealed to form a double-stranded stem with a loop, and the ends are ligated to flanking sequences containing restriction sites. The resulting construct is amplified by PCR and cloned into the expression vector. This method avoids the need for nicking and strand displacement but requires careful design to prevent intermolecular hybridization and to ensure proper loop formation.

- describe synthesizing nucleotide intermediate I

Nucleotide intermediate I is a single-stranded DNA molecule synthesized by solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry, comprising a 5′ constant region, a variable region of 25 to 29 nucleotides, and a 3′ constant region. The variable region is synthesized using a mixture of phosphoramidites in which each nucleotide is spiked with minor amounts of the other three to introduce controlled randomness. The synthesis is performed on an automated oligonucleotide synthesizer, and the product is purified by HPLC or PAGE to remove failure sequences. The 5′ end is phosphorylated and the 3′ end is protected with a CPG linker for subsequent ligation steps.

- describe synthesizing second nucleotide molecule

The second nucleotide molecule is a complementary strand synthesized by annealing a primer to nucleotide intermediate I and extending it using a DNA polymerase with high processivity and strand-displacement activity. The primer is designed to hybridize to the 5′ constant region and is extended in the presence of dNTPs to generate a full-length complementary strand. The extension reaction is carried out at elevated temperature to prevent secondary structure formation, and the resulting double-stranded molecule is purified by phenol-chloroform extraction and ethanol precipitation.

- describe annealing nucleotide intermediate I

Nucleotide intermediate I is annealed to a primer complementary to its 5′ constant region under controlled temperature conditions to ensure specific hybridization. The annealing is performed in a buffer containing magnesium chloride and sodium chloride, with a gradual cooling ramp from 95°C to 25°C over two hours to promote stable duplex formation. The annealed product is then used as a template for enzymatic extension or ligation.

- provide set or library of recombinant viruses

The invention provides a set or library of recombinant retroviral or lentiviral particles, each particle containing a single copy of a recombinant expression vector encoding a unique RNA molecule with a randomized double-stranded stem. The library contains at least 10⁵ distinct viral particles, each capable of transducing a target cell and integrating the expression cassette into the host genome. The viral particles are produced by co-transfection of packaging cells with the vector library and a packaging plasmid encoding gag, pol, and env proteins. The viral titer is quantified by flow cytometry using a fluorescent marker encoded in the vector, and the library is stored as frozen aliquots for long-term use.

- provide expression vector for RNA molecule

The expression vector is a plasmid backbone derived from a retroviral or lentiviral vector system, comprising a polymerase III promoter, a multiple cloning site downstream of the promoter, a fluorescent marker gene, a polyadenylation signal, and viral LTRs for integration. The cloning site is flanked by restriction enzyme sites compatible with the asymmetric digestion products of the nucleic acid intermediate. The vector is devoid of bacterial antibiotic resistance genes that may interfere with mammalian cell function and includes a bacterial origin of replication for propagation in E. coli.

- describe method of conferring protection against viral infection

The method comprises transducing a population of cells with the library of recombinant viruses, exposing the cells to a cytopathic virus, and selecting for surviving cells. Genomic DNA is extracted from surviving cells, and the integrated RNA molecule-encoding sequences are amplified by PCR and sequenced to identify those that confer resistance. The identified sequences are re-introduced into naïve cells to confirm protection, and the mechanism of resistance is determined by analyzing changes in host gene expression or viral replication kinetics.

- describe method of inhibiting viral replication

The method comprises transducing cells with the library of recombinant viruses, infecting the cells with a virus of interest, and monitoring viral replication over time. Cells exhibiting reduced viral load or delayed cytopathic effect are isolated, and the integrated RNA molecule sequences are retrieved and sequenced. The sequences are validated by re-expression in naïve cells and testing for inhibition of viral entry, transcription, or assembly.

- describe method of inducing cell differentiation

The method comprises transducing progenitor or stem cells with the library of recombinant viruses, culturing the cells under conditions that promote differentiation, and isolating cells exhibiting a differentiated phenotype by flow cytometry or morphological analysis. The integrated RNA molecule sequences are retrieved from the differentiated cells, cloned into individual vectors, and re-introduced into naïve progenitor cells to confirm the ability to induce differentiation. The method enables the identification of RNA molecules that drive lineage commitment without the use of exogenous transcription factors.
