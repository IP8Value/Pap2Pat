## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to modified recombinant binding proteins

The present invention relates to modified recombinant binding proteins engineered for high-affinity, specific, and stable interaction with vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) splice variants, particularly VEGF-Axxx isoforms, and their use in the inhibition of pathological angiogenesis and vascular permeability. These binding proteins are derived from non-immunoglobulin protein scaffolds and are designed to function as potent antagonists of VEGF-A signaling without eliciting immune effector functions. The invention encompasses recombinant polypeptides comprising defined repeat domains, optionally conjugated to polymer moieties to enhance pharmacokinetic properties, and formulated for therapeutic or diagnostic application in ocular, oncological, and inflammatory diseases characterized by aberrant VEGF-A activity. The binding proteins are produced using recombinant DNA technology, exhibit exceptional thermal and structural stability, and are suitable for formulation in high-concentration solutions, sustained-release systems, or topical delivery vehicles, including eye drops, thereby enabling novel routes of administration that circumvent the limitations of conventional biologics.

Angiogenesis is a tightly regulated physiological process essential for tissue development, wound healing, and reproductive functions, but when dysregulated, it contributes to the pathogenesis of numerous diseases including cancer, diabetic retinopathy, age-related macular degeneration, and inflammatory disorders. Among the key mediators of pathological angiogenesis, vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) stands out as the most potent and central regulator, acting through binding to its primary receptor, VEGF receptor 2, a receptor tyrosine kinase expressed predominantly on vascular endothelial cells. VEGF-A also interacts with co-receptors such as neuropilin-1 and neuropilin-2, which modulate its signaling potency and specificity. The human VEGF-A gene undergoes alternative splicing to generate multiple isoforms, the most clinically relevant being the pro-angiogenic VEGF-Axxx variants, such as VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121, which are overexpressed in neovascular eye diseases and solid tumors. In contrast, the anti-angiogenic VEGF-Axxxb isoforms exert opposing effects but are typically underrepresented in pathological contexts. Current therapeutic strategies targeting VEGF-A include monoclonal antibodies, antibody fragments, and aptamers, all of which bind to VEGF-A and prevent its interaction with VEGFR-2. Despite their clinical utility, these agents suffer from significant limitations, including short intraocular half-lives necessitating frequent intravitreal injections, susceptibility to aggregation or denaturation under stress, high manufacturing costs, and limited capacity for topical delivery due to poor stability and penetration. Furthermore, existing therapies often require complex purification processes and are incompatible with high-concentration formulations needed for sustained-release delivery systems. Designed repeat proteins, such as those described in WO 02/20565, represent a class of non-immunoglobulin binding molecules constructed from tandem repeats of structural units that fold into stable, soluble, and highly specific ligand-binding domains. These proteins offer advantages over antibodies, including absence of disulfide bonds, resistance to aggregation, and compatibility with cytoplasmic expression systems. However, prior art has not disclosed designed repeat proteins with the combination of picomolar affinity for VEGF-Axxx, robust stability under physiological conditions, and suitability for both intravitreal and topical administration. There remains a critical unmet need for next-generation anti-angiogenic agents that combine superior potency, prolonged ocular retention, formulation flexibility, and safety profiles suitable for repeated or non-invasive delivery.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- describe recombinant binding protein

The invention provides a recombinant binding protein comprising a designed ankyrin repeat domain specifically engineered to bind with high affinity and selectivity to pro-angiogenic VEGF-Axxx isoforms, wherein the binding domain exhibits a dissociation constant (Kd) for VEGF-A165 of less than 50 picomolar, and wherein the protein is free of immunoglobulin fold structures, immunoglobulin-like domains, and free cysteine residues. The recombinant binding protein is produced by recombinant DNA technology in bacterial expression systems, resulting in a soluble, monomeric polypeptide with exceptional thermal stability and resistance to aggregation. Optionally, the binding protein is conjugated to a proteinaceous or non-proteinaceous polymer moiety to extend its half-life in vivo, enhance solubility, or facilitate sustained delivery. The binding protein is capable of inhibiting VEGF-A-induced endothelial cell sprouting and vascular permeability in vitro and in vivo, and is suitable for formulation in pharmaceutical compositions for intravitreal injection, topical ocular application, or systemic administration. The invention further encompasses methods for producing, purifying, and characterizing such binding proteins, as well as their use in the treatment of conditions driven by pathological VEGF-A signaling, including diabetic macular edema, wet age-related macular degeneration, corneal neovascularization, and tumor angiogenesis.

VEGF-Axxx isoforms refer to the pro-angiogenic splice variants of vascular endothelial growth factor A, including but not limited to VEGF-A121, VEGF-A165, and VEGF-A189, which contain exon 8a and are characterized by their ability to bind heparin and activate VEGFR-2 signaling. The binding domain of the recombinant binding protein is specifically selected to recognize and bind with high affinity to these isoforms while exhibiting minimal or no cross-reactivity with VEGF-B, VEGF-C, VEGF-D, placental growth factor, or platelet-derived growth factor. The protein is defined as a polypeptide chain encoded by recombinant DNA and expressed in a heterologous host system, wherein the polypeptide comprises a defined structural domain responsible for target recognition. A protein domain is a discrete, independently folding unit within a polypeptide that confers a specific function, such as ligand binding. Recombinant refers to the process of constructing nucleic acid molecules in vitro using molecular cloning techniques, and recombinant DNA technology encompasses the manipulation of DNA sequences to express desired proteins in host organisms such as Escherichia coli. A polypeptide tag is an amino acid sequence fused to the recombinant protein to facilitate purification or detection, and examples include the RGS-His tag, FLAG tag, and Strep-tag II. A polypeptide linker is a flexible sequence connecting functional domains, and examples include (Gly-Gly-Gly-Gly-Ser)n repeats, where n is an integer from 1 to 5. A polypeptide is a linear chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds, and the minimum length of the binding domain is 80 amino acid residues. A proteinaceous polymer moiety is a naturally derived polymeric structure, such as albumin or elastin-like polypeptides, that enhances solubility and plasma half-life, and has a molecular weight ranging from 10 kDa to 50 kDa. A non-proteinaceous polymer moiety is a synthetic or semi-synthetic polymer conjugated to the binding protein, and examples include polyethylene glycol (PEG), polyvinyl alcohol, and dextran. PEGylation refers to the covalent attachment of polyethylene glycol chains to the binding protein, which reduces renal clearance, increases circulation time, and minimizes immunogenicity. The binding protein is defined as a monomeric, non-antibody molecule comprising a designed ankyrin repeat domain that binds VEGF-Axxx with high specificity. Components of the binding protein include the repeat domain, optional terminal capping modules, and optional polymer conjugates. Multimerization moieties, such as Fc domains or trimerization motifs, may be fused to the binding domain to enhance avidity, although the preferred embodiment is monomeric. The molecular weight of the binding protein ranges from 15 kDa to 40 kDa. The binding domain is defined as the region of the protein directly responsible for interaction with VEGF-Axxx, and the protein scaffold is the structural framework upon which the binding domain is built, with examples including ankyrin repeats, fibronectin type III domains, and leucine-rich repeats. A predetermined property refers to a defined characteristic such as thermal stability, solubility, or binding affinity, and screening and selection methods include ribosome display, phage display, and yeast two-hybrid systems. The invention explicitly excludes antibodies, antibody fragments, immunoglobulin folds, and immunoglobulin-like domains. The anti-angiogenic effects are demonstrated by inhibition of endothelial cell sprouting in a human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) spheroid assay, where the binding domain reduces sprout length by at least 70% at concentrations below 100 pM. The binding domain has a length of between 85 and 120 amino acids. The binding protein is engineered to contain no free cysteine residues to prevent unwanted disulfide bonding or aggregation. The repeat domain consists of multiple tandem ankyrin repeats, each approximately 33 amino acids in length, flanked by N-terminal and C-terminal capping modules that stabilize the overall fold. The designed ankyrin repeat domain is a non-naturally occurring sequence derived from rational design and directed evolution, and repeat proteins are defined as proteins composed of multiple repeating structural units. Each repeat unit consists of a conserved helix-loop-helix structural motif, and examples include the consensus sequence of ankyrin repeats from human ankyrin or GA-binding protein. The repeat sequence motif is a conserved pattern of amino acids that maintains the structural integrity of the repeat, and the folding topology is a right-handed superhelical arrangement that creates a continuous binding surface. The capping module is a non-repeating structural element at the termini of the repeat domain that prevents unfolding and enhances stability. The target is VEGF-Axxx, and the binding domain is defined by its ability to inhibit VEGF-A binding to VEGFR-2 with a dissociation constant (Kd) of less than 50 pM, as determined by surface plasmon resonance. The preferred binding domain exhibits a Kd for VEGF-A165 of less than 10 pM. PBS refers to phosphate-buffered saline, and the binding protein maintains structural integrity and binding function in PBS at 37°C for at least 14 days. An embodiment of the binding protein exhibits a midpoint denaturation temperature (Tm) greater than 75°C and shows no detectable aggregation upon storage at 4°C or 25°C for six months. HUVEC refers to human umbilical vein endothelial cells, and the sprouting assay measures the length of endothelial cell projections from spheroids embedded in collagen matrix after stimulation with VEGF-A. The IC50 value is the concentration of binding protein required to inhibit 50% of sprout formation, and the preferred IC50 is less than 20 pM. The monomeric binding protein exhibits an IC50 value below 20 pM in the HUVEC spheroid assay. The binding domain exhibits negligible binding to VEGF-B, VEGF-C, VEGF-D, PIGF, or PDGF with Kd values greater than 10 nM. The recombinant binding protein comprises a designed ankyrin repeat domain expressed in E. coli and purified to homogeneity. The conjugation of a PEG moiety to the binding domain is achieved via site-specific attachment to a genetically introduced lysine or cysteine residue, resulting in a mono-PEGylated species with a PEG molecular weight of 5 kDa to 40 kDa. Competition assays confirm that the binding protein displaces VEGF-A from VEGFR-2 with higher potency than ranibizumab. The recombinant binding protein with repeat domain and PEGylation exhibits an intravitreal half-life exceeding 14 days in rabbit models. Pharmaceutical and diagnostic compositions comprise the binding protein in a buffered aqueous solution containing carriers such as mannitol, sucrose, or histidine, and excipients such as polysorbate 80 or arginine. Edema is defined as pathological fluid accumulation in tissues, and its consequences include vision loss, tissue hypoxia, and organ dysfunction. Angiogenesis is the formation of new blood vessels from pre-existing vasculature, and pathological angiogenesis occurs in conditions such as diabetic retinopathy, age-related macular degeneration, and tumor growth. Retinal ischemic diseases arise from insufficient vascular supply to the retina, and diabetic retinopathy and macular degeneration are leading causes of blindness. Diabetic macular edema results from VEGF-A-mediated vascular leakage in the macula. The biochemical mechanisms involve upregulation of VEGF-A in response to hyperglycemia and hypoxia, leading to endothelial permeability and neovascularization. Pulmonary hypertension and chronic inflammation are also driven by VEGF-A overexpression. Tumor formation depends on angiogenesis for nutrient supply, and solid tumors such as renal cell carcinoma and glioblastoma rely on VEGF-A for growth and metastasis. Binding proteins are used to inhibit VEGF-A signaling in tumors by blocking receptor activation. The pharmaceutical composition includes stabilizers such as glycine, antioxidants, and tonicity agents. Combination formulations may include inhibitors of PIGF, Ang-2, or integrins. The preferred pharmaceutical composition for eye diseases is a sterile, isotonic, preservative-free solution suitable for intravitreal injection or topical instillation. Administration methods include intravitreal injection, subconjunctival delivery, sustained-release implants, continuous infusion via microcatheters, and topical administration as eye drops. Sustained-release preparations include biodegradable polymers, hydrogels, or osmotic pumps. An intraocular implant may be composed of a silicone or polymeric matrix loaded with the binding protein. Dosage and mode of administration vary depending on the route, with intravitreal doses ranging from 10 to 200 μg and topical doses from 0.1 to 50 mg/mL. Continuous infusion may be delivered via a reservoir system for up to six months. Administration by eye drops enables non-invasive treatment of anterior and posterior segment diseases. Combination therapy with a PIGF inhibitor or other anti-angiogenic agent enhances efficacy and reduces resistance. The method of treating retinopathy involves administering the binding protein to a subject with diabetic retinopathy, macular degeneration, or retinal vein occlusion. The method of inhibiting VEGF-A biological activity involves contacting VEGF-A with the binding protein to prevent its interaction with VEGFR-2. The method of treating a subject with a condition involves identifying a subject with a VEGF-A-driven disease and administering an effective amount of the binding protein. Conditions suitable for treatment include diabetic macular edema, wet age-related macular degeneration, corneal neovascularization, tumor angiogenesis, and inflammatory retinopathies. Preferred conditions are those requiring long-term suppression of VEGF-A activity. The recombinant binding protein is obtained by cloning the gene encoding the designed repeat domain into an expression vector, transforming E. coli, inducing expression, lysing cells, and purifying the protein by affinity and size-exclusion chromatography. The evolving recombinant binding protein is generated by constructing a library of ankyrin repeat variants, performing multiple rounds of ribosome display selection against immobilized VEGF-A165, and isolating clones with improved affinity and specificity. The library of ankyrin repeat proteins is generated by randomizing key residues in the repeat units while preserving the structural scaffold. Selection or screening of VEGF-Axxx-specific DARPins is performed using ELISA, surface plasmon resonance, and functional cellular assays. Modular assembly of repeat domains allows for the precise addition or removal of repeat units to fine-tune binding affinity and stability. The general outline of the invention is the design, production, and application of a novel class of recombinant, non-antibody binding proteins with exceptional affinity, stability, and delivery versatility for the treatment of VEGF-A-mediated diseases.
