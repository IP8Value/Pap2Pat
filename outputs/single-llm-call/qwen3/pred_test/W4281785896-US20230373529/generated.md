## BACKGROUND

- motivate trajectory planning

The development of autonomous vehicles has fundamentally shifted the paradigm of mobility from human-driven operation to machine-mediated decision-making, where the core challenge lies not in sensing the environment or executing low-level controls, but in generating trajectories that reflect the nuanced, context-sensitive behaviors of expert human drivers. Traditional motion planning systems rely heavily on rule-based architectures that encode driving behavior through manually designed cost functions, heuristic thresholds, and predefined behavioral templates. While these approaches offer interpretability and deterministic guarantees, they suffer from an inherent inflexibility: each parameter must be tuned in isolation, often leading to suboptimal performance in dynamic, unstructured environments where interactions between vehicles are complex, unpredictable, and non-linear. The inability of such systems to generalize beyond predefined scenarios—particularly in dense urban settings characterized by erratic cut-ins, abrupt braking, and ambiguous right-of-way conventions—has limited their deployment to controlled, low-complexity domains. Furthermore, the manual engineering of features such as time-to-collision thresholds, minimum headway distances, and jerk limits introduces a combinatorial explosion of tuning parameters, each of which can degrade performance in one aspect of driving while improving another. This fragility undermines the scalability and robustness required for real-world deployment. Consequently, there exists a critical need for a planning architecture that decouples the generation of plausible trajectories from the evaluation of their behavioral appropriateness, enabling the system to learn from expert demonstrations without requiring exhaustive hand-crafting of behavioral rules. The motivation for trajectory planning, therefore, is not merely to navigate from point A to point B, but to do so in a manner that is safe, comfortable, socially compliant, and indistinguishable from human driving behavior—even under conditions of uncertainty, partial observability, and adversarial agent behavior. This requires a framework that can infer the underlying reward structure of expert driving from observed trajectories, while simultaneously enforcing hard safety constraints that prevent catastrophic outcomes regardless of the learned model’s confidence or ambiguity. The integration of inverse reinforcement learning with a recursive safety filter represents a principled approach to this challenge, shifting the burden of behavioral modeling from manual specification to data-driven learning, while preserving the reliability expected of safety-critical systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce description purpose

The purpose of this detailed description is to provide a comprehensive and enabling disclosure of a system and method for autonomous vehicle trajectory planning that combines a machine learning-based scoring mechanism with a deterministic safety filter to ensure both behavioral realism and operational safety. This invention is not merely an algorithmic improvement but a structural reorganization of the planning pipeline, wherein trajectory generation and safety validation are handled by reliable, interpretable components, while the complex, context-dependent task of evaluating behavioral quality is delegated to a learned model trained on expert driving data. The architecture is designed to maximize the utility of limited training data by focusing model capacity exclusively on the aspects of driving that are difficult to specify explicitly—such as subtle speed modulation, clearance preferences, and temporal coordination with surrounding agents—while eliminating the need for the model to also generate feasible trajectories or avoid obvious collisions. This separation of concerns ensures that the system remains robust to model uncertainty, overfitting, and distributional shift, as the safety filter operates independently of the learning component and enforces a recursive guarantee that any selected trajectory permits a safe continuation under worst-case assumptions about other agents. The description further clarifies the interplay between hardware components, software modules, and environmental representations that collectively enable real-time, closed-loop operation on public roads, providing sufficient detail to allow a person of ordinary skill in the art to implement the invention without undue experimentation.

- clarify specific details

Specific details of the invention include the use of a trajectory generator that synthesizes a diverse set of dynamically feasible ego motion paths based on the vehicle’s current state, a high-definition map, and a specified route, ensuring that all generated trajectories satisfy constraints on curvature, acceleration, and continuity required by the vehicle’s low-level controllers. Each trajectory is represented as a discrete sequence of states over a fixed time horizon, with each state encoding position, heading, speed, and acceleration. The safety filter then applies a recursive safety check to each trajectory, assuming that all non-ego vehicles will perform a hard brake at a predefined deceleration rate within a fixed time window, and evaluates whether the ego vehicle can safely come to a stop or maintain a safe distance if such an event occurs. Trajectories that fail this check are discarded before being passed to the scoring model. The scoring model, implemented as a deep neural network, receives as input a set of hand-engineered features derived from each trajectory, including time-to-collision estimates, adaptive cruise control parameters, maximum jerk and lateral acceleration, speed limit adherence, and a concatenation of past ego states. These features are processed through separate neural pathways, normalized, and then interact via a masked self-attention mechanism to produce a scalar score for each trajectory. The model is trained using maximum entropy inverse reinforcement learning on expert driving demonstrations, optimizing for the likelihood that the expert’s trajectory would be selected over alternatives given the observed scene context. The highest-scoring trajectory that passes the safety filter is then transmitted to the vehicle’s control system for execution.

- illustrate block diagrams

The system is organized into a hierarchical architecture comprising an environment perception module, a trajectory generation module, a safety filter module, a trajectory scoring module, and a vehicle control interface. The environment perception module ingests data from LiDAR, radar, cameras, and microphones to detect and track surrounding vehicles, pedestrians, and static objects, while simultaneously localizing the ego vehicle within a high-definition map. This information is passed to the trajectory generation module, which produces a set of candidate trajectories consistent with the vehicle’s dynamics and the road geometry. Each candidate trajectory is then evaluated by the safety filter, which recursively modifies the trajectory to simulate a hard brake scenario and checks for collisions under worst-case assumptions. Trajectories that survive this filter are forwarded to the scoring module, which computes a behavioral score using a deep neural network trained on expert demonstrations. The highest-scoring safe trajectory is selected and transmitted to the vehicle’s drive-by-wire system, which executes the trajectory through coordinated control of the powertrain, steering, and braking actuators. The entire pipeline operates in real time, with all components synchronized to a common clock and updated at a fixed frequency. The safety filter operates as a hard constraint, meaning that no trajectory is ever executed unless it passes all safety checks, regardless of its score.

- explain schematic element ordering

The schematic elements of the system are ordered to enforce a strict data flow that prioritizes safety over performance. The trajectory generation module precedes the safety filter, ensuring that only physically realizable paths are considered. The safety filter precedes the scoring module, ensuring that no unsafe trajectory is ever scored, thereby preventing the learning model from being exposed to or influenced by potentially hazardous behaviors. The scoring module precedes the selection and execution stage, ensuring that the final trajectory is not only safe but also behaviorally optimal according to the learned expert model. This ordering is not arbitrary but is foundational to the invention’s safety guarantee: by filtering out unsafe trajectories before scoring, the system eliminates the risk that a high-scoring but unsafe trajectory could be selected due to model error or data bias. The ordering also ensures computational efficiency, as the computationally expensive scoring model operates only on a reduced set of trajectories that have already passed a lightweight safety check.

- clarify connecting elements

The connecting elements between system components are defined as bidirectional communication channels that transmit structured data packets containing state vectors, feature arrays, and control signals. The trajectory generator connects to the safety filter via a data stream of trajectory sequences, each annotated with metadata such as duration, speed profile, and curvature. The safety filter connects to the scoring module via a filtered trajectory set, where each trajectory is accompanied by its associated feature vector computed from the environment and the trajectory itself. The scoring module connects to the trajectory selector via a scalar score for each trajectory, and the selector connects to the vehicle control system via a single selected trajectory and its associated timestamped state sequence. All connections are implemented over a high-bandwidth, low-latency bus architecture that ensures deterministic timing and synchronization across all modules. The communication protocol includes error-checking mechanisms and fallback procedures to handle transmission failures or timing mismatches.

- define terminology

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “trajectory” refers to a discrete sequence of ego vehicle states over a fixed time horizon, each state comprising position, heading, speed, and acceleration in a global coordinate frame. The term “safety filter” refers to a deterministic algorithm that evaluates whether a trajectory permits a safe continuation under worst-case assumptions regarding the behavior of surrounding agents. The term “scoring model” refers to a deep neural network trained to assign a scalar value to each trajectory based on how closely it matches expert driving behavior. The term “expert demonstration” refers to a recorded sequence of ego vehicle states and corresponding environmental conditions collected from a skilled human driver operating the vehicle under real-world conditions. The term “recursive safety guarantee” refers to the property that, if the first segment of a trajectory is executed and a hard brake is applied thereafter, the resulting motion remains collision-free under specified assumptions.
