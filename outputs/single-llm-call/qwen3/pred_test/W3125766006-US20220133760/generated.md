## BACKGROUND

- introduce nucleosomes

Nucleosomes serve as the fundamental structural units of chromatin in eukaryotic cells, comprising approximately 147 base pairs of DNA wrapped around an octameric core of histone proteins. This core is composed of two copies each of histones H2A, H2B, H3, and H4, which interact through conserved structural domains to stabilize DNA compaction while permitting regulated access for transcriptional and repair machinery. The precise arrangement of nucleosomes governs the accessibility of genomic regions, thereby influencing gene expression patterns, DNA replication timing, and the fidelity of DNA damage response pathways. Alterations in nucleosome composition, positioning, or stability can disrupt these processes, leading to aberrant transcriptional programs that are frequently observed in malignant transformation.

- describe oncohistones

Oncohistones represent a recently recognized class of oncogenic drivers characterized by somatic mutations within the highly conserved histone fold domains of core histones. These mutations occur at low allele frequencies within tumor cell populations yet exert profound effects on chromatin architecture by destabilizing nucleosome integrity, impairing higher-order chromatin folding, and altering interactions with chromatin-modifying complexes. Unlike classical oncogenes that act through hyperactivation or amplification, oncohistones function as dominant-negative modifiers that reprogram the epigenetic landscape in a context-dependent manner, often synergizing with other oncogenic lesions to promote tumorigenesis. Their presence has been documented across multiple cancer types, including pediatric gliomas, sarcomas, and lymphoid malignancies, where they correlate with distinct transcriptional and splicing phenotypes.

- discuss histone fold domain mutations

Mutations within the histone fold domain frequently target residues critical for histone-histone or histone-DNA contacts, such as those involved in the acidic patch, DNA minor groove interactions, or dimerization interfaces. These substitutions, including but not limited to H2B-E76K and H2A-R29Q, compromise the structural stability of the nucleosome core, resulting in increased DNA unwrapping, reduced nucleosome occupancy, and enhanced accessibility of cryptic regulatory elements. Such perturbations facilitate aberrant recruitment of transcription factors and RNA polymerase II, leading to the dysregulated expression of oncogenes and suppression of tumor suppressor pathways. The functional consequences of these mutations are not limited to direct chromatin effects but extend to downstream processes such as RNA splicing, DNA repair efficiency, and replication fork progression.

- introduce short histone H2A variants

Short histone H2A variants constitute a phylogenetically distinct subclass of histone proteins that are evolutionarily divergent from canonical H2A and are primarily expressed during spermatogenesis in mammals. These variants, including H2A.B, H2A.P, and H2A.Q, are characterized by truncated C-terminal tails, altered histone fold domains, and modified acidic patches that collectively reduce nucleosome stability and promote open chromatin configurations. Unlike canonical histones, which are ubiquitously expressed and tightly regulated, short H2A variants are normally restricted to germ cells, where they facilitate chromatin remodeling during meiosis and post-meiotic sperm maturation. Their expression in somatic tissues is exceedingly rare and typically undetectable under physiological conditions.

- describe H2A.B's role in testis

In the testis, H2A.B is incorporated into nucleosomes during the haploid phase of spermatogenesis, where it promotes transcriptional activation and facilitates the replacement of histones with protamines. H2A.B-containing nucleosomes wrap approximately 120 base pairs of DNA, significantly less than canonical nucleosomes, and exhibit enhanced dynamic turnover. This property allows for rapid access to transcriptional machinery and supports the high levels of gene expression required for sperm differentiation. H2A.B also interacts directly with RNA-binding proteins and splicing factors, influencing the processing of transcripts critical for germ cell development. Its expression is tightly controlled by epigenetic silencing mechanisms in somatic tissues, ensuring its absence outside the male germline.

- propose oncohistone activity of sH2As

The discovery that short H2A variants inherently possess structural features identical to known oncohistone mutations suggests that their aberrant reactivation in somatic tissues may constitute a novel mechanism of oncogenesis. Rather than requiring additional mutations to destabilize nucleosomes, the wild-type sequences of H2A.B and related variants already encode the same substitutions found in cancer-associated histone mutants, including R29Q/F and E121 truncation. These features, conserved across primates and preserved despite rapid evolutionary divergence, indicate strong selective pressure for nucleosome destabilization in the testis. When reactivated in non-germline contexts, these variants recapitulate the chromatin-disrupting effects of oncohistones, thereby promoting transcriptional dysregulation, splicing anomalies, and genomic instability. This positions short H2A variants not merely as biomarkers but as functional oncohistones whose expression alone is sufficient to drive oncogenic phenotypes in susceptible cell types.

## SUMMARY

- introduce method for treating lymphoma

A method for treating lymphoma involves detecting the aberrant expression of a short histone H2A variant, specifically H2A.B, in a biological sample derived from a subject suspected of having lymphoma, and administering an anthracycline agent to the subject in a therapeutically effective amount. The detection of H2A.B expression serves as a predictive biomarker for sensitivity to anthracycline-based therapies, enabling the selection of patients most likely to benefit from such treatment while sparing those unlikely to respond. This approach represents a paradigm shift in lymphoma management by linking a specific epigenetic alteration to a targeted therapeutic strategy grounded in mechanistic biology.

- describe detecting sH2A expression

Detection of short histone H2A expression is performed by quantifying mRNA transcripts encoding H2A.B paralogs—H2AFB1, H2AFB2, or H2AFB3—in a sample obtained from the subject. The expression level is measured using RNA sequencing or quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, with a threshold of at least 1.5 transcripts per million defined as positive expression. This threshold distinguishes physiologically silent expression in normal tissues from pathologically reactivated expression in malignant cells. Detection may be performed on tumor tissue, peripheral blood, serum, or plasma, with tissue biopsy remaining the gold standard for diagnostic accuracy.

- administer anthracycline agent

Upon confirmation of H2A.B expression, an anthracycline agent is administered to the subject. Anthracyclines are a class of chemotherapeutic compounds that intercalate into DNA, inhibit topoisomerase II, and generate reactive oxygen species, leading to double-strand breaks and apoptosis. The administration of anthracyclines in H2A.B-positive lymphomas exploits the inherent chromatin instability conferred by the variant, which increases DNA accessibility and enhances the efficacy of DNA-damaging agents. This synergy results in significantly greater cytotoxicity compared to H2A.B-negative tumors.

- introduce method for inducing cytotoxicity

A method for inducing cytotoxicity in lymphoma cells involves exposing the cells to an anthracycline agent in the presence of aberrantly expressed H2A.B. The presence of H2A.B compromises nucleosome integrity, increasing the rate of DNA unwrapping and facilitating greater intercalation of the anthracycline molecule. This leads to enhanced topoisomerase II poisoning, prolonged DNA strand breaks, and accumulation of irreparable damage, culminating in cell death. The effect is selective for H2A.B-expressing cells, sparing normal tissues that do not express the variant.

- describe administering anthracycline agent

Administration of the anthracycline agent is performed via intravenous infusion, with dosing schedules tailored to the subject’s body surface area, renal function, and prior treatment history. The agent is typically delivered in cycles, with intervals of 21 days, and may be combined with other chemotherapeutic agents such as cyclophosphamide, vincristine, or prednisone, depending on the subtype of lymphoma. The therapeutic regimen is initiated only after molecular confirmation of H2A.B expression, ensuring precision in patient selection.

- introduce method to inhibit cancer cell proliferation

A method to inhibit the proliferation of lymphoma cells involves the administration of an anthracycline agent to a subject whose tumor cells express H2A.B at levels exceeding 1.5 transcripts per million. The inhibition of proliferation is measured by reduced cell viability, decreased Ki-67 staining, and suppression of tumor growth in vivo. The effect is mediated through the synergistic interaction between H2A.B-induced chromatin destabilization and anthracycline-mediated DNA damage, resulting in activation of p53-dependent and independent apoptotic pathways.

- describe detecting sH2A expression and administering anthracycline agent

The method encompasses the sequential steps of obtaining a biological sample from a subject, determining the expression level of H2A.B in the sample, and, if the expression level exceeds a defined threshold, initiating treatment with an anthracycline agent. This diagnostic-therapeutic algorithm ensures that treatment is administered only when the underlying molecular vulnerability is confirmed, thereby improving clinical outcomes and minimizing unnecessary exposure to cytotoxic agents.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce sH2A histone variants

Short histone H2A variants are a family of evolutionarily divergent histone proteins encoded by genes located on the X chromosome, including H2AFB1, H2AFB2, H2AFB3, HYPM, and an unannotated H2A.Q locus. These variants are distinguished from canonical H2A by a shortened C-terminal tail, altered amino acid residues within the histone fold domain, and a modified acidic patch region. These structural differences confer reduced nucleosome stability, increased DNA dynamics, and enhanced interaction with RNA-processing machinery. Their expression is normally restricted to the male germline, where they facilitate chromatin remodeling during spermatogenesis, but their reactivation in somatic tissues is associated with malignant transformation.

- describe association with nucleosome destabilization and alternate splicing

The presence of short H2A variants in chromatin results in nucleosomes that wrap approximately 120 base pairs of DNA, as opposed to the 147 base pairs associated with canonical nucleosomes. This reduction in DNA wrapping increases the accessibility of the underlying sequence to transcriptional and splicing factors. The altered chromatin environment promotes faster RNA polymerase II elongation, which in turn reduces the efficiency of co-transcriptional splicing. This leads to decreased utilization of alternative cassette exons and proximal polyadenylation sites, as well as a reduction in intron retention, collectively resulting in a global dysregulation of RNA processing. These splicing alterations are not random but are consistently observed across multiple cancer types expressing H2A.B, suggesting a direct mechanistic link between the variant and transcriptomic instability.

- describe aberrant sH2A upregulation in cancers

Aberrant upregulation of short H2A variants, particularly H2A.B, has been observed in a broad spectrum of malignancies, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, Hodgkin’s lymphoma, B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, uterine endometrial carcinoma, and squamous cell carcinomas of the head and neck, lung, and cervix. This expression is absent in adjacent normal tissues and is rarely detected in non-testicular somatic tissues from healthy individuals. The reactivation of H2A.B occurs independently of chromosomal amplification or global X-chromosome derepression, indicating that it is driven by localized epigenetic changes at individual H2AFB loci. The frequency of expression varies by cancer type, with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma exhibiting the highest prevalence at approximately 50% of cases.

- posit sH2As as novel class of oncohistones

The structural features of short H2A variants, including the R29Q/F substitution and C-terminal truncation at E121, are identical to those found in canonical histone mutations classified as oncohistones. These residues are critical for histone-DNA and histone-histone interactions, and their presence in wild-type H2A.B sequences suggests that the variant has evolved to destabilize nucleosomes for physiological purposes in the testis. When expressed in somatic cells, these same features disrupt chromatin architecture in a manner indistinguishable from acquired oncohistone mutations, thereby promoting oncogenic transcriptional programs. This establishes short H2A variants as a novel class of endogenous oncohistones whose expression alone is sufficient to initiate or accelerate tumorigenesis.

- describe method for treating lymphoma

The method for treating lymphoma comprises identifying a subject with a diagnosis or suspicion of lymphoma, obtaining a biological sample from the subject, measuring the expression level of H2A.B in the sample using RNA sequencing or quantitative PCR, and, if the expression level exceeds 1.5 transcripts per million, administering an anthracycline agent to the subject. The method is applicable to both newly diagnosed and relapsed/refractory cases, and may be used in combination with standard-of-care regimens such as R-CHOP or ABVD. The selection of patients based on H2A.B expression improves response rates and reduces unnecessary toxicity in non-expressing individuals.

- define lymphoma

Lymphoma is a heterogeneous group of hematologic malignancies arising from the malignant transformation of lymphocytes, primarily B-cells or T-cells, and characterized by the clonal proliferation of lymphoid cells in lymph nodes, bone marrow, spleen, or extranodal sites. Lymphomas are broadly classified into Hodgkin’s lymphoma and non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma, with the latter encompassing numerous subtypes including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, and Burkitt lymphoma. The disease is defined by histopathological, immunophenotypic, and molecular criteria, and its clinical behavior varies significantly based on subtype, stage, and genetic alterations.

- specify types of lymphoma

The method is particularly effective for treating diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, Hodgkin’s lymphoma, and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia. Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma is the most common non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma in adults and is subdivided into germinal center and activated B-cell subtypes based on gene expression profiling. Hodgkin’s lymphoma is characterized by the presence of Reed-Sternberg cells and is further classified into nodular sclerosis and mixed cellularity subtypes. B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia is a highly aggressive malignancy of immature B-cell precursors, commonly occurring in children but also affecting adults. All three entities exhibit frequent reactivation of H2A.B expression, with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma showing the highest frequency.

- describe preferred embodiments for treating Hodgkin's lymphoma

In a preferred embodiment, the method is applied to patients diagnosed with classical Hodgkin’s lymphoma, particularly those with nodular sclerosis or mixed cellularity histology. H2A.B expression is detected in tumor biopsies using RNA sequencing, and subjects with expression levels exceeding 1.5 transcripts per million are treated with a regimen comprising doxorubicin, bleomycin, vinblastine, and dacarbazine. The presence of H2A.B correlates with improved progression-free survival and overall response rates compared to H2A.B-negative patients receiving the same regimen. The method enables stratification of patients into high- and low-risk categories based on molecular biomarkers rather than clinical staging alone.

- describe embodiments for treating adult or childhood forms of lymphoma

The method is applicable to both adult and pediatric populations. In childhood B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia, H2A.B expression is detected in approximately 6 to 13 percent of cases, and those patients exhibit significantly greater sensitivity to anthracycline-containing induction regimens such as those including daunorubicin or idarubicin. In adult lymphomas, including relapsed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, H2A.B positivity predicts response to salvage therapies containing liposomal doxorubicin or aclarubicin. The method is equally effective regardless of age, provided that H2A.B expression is confirmed by molecular testing.

- describe method steps: collecting sample, detecting sH2A expression, administering anthracycline agent

The method comprises three sequential steps: first, collecting a biological sample from the subject, the sample selected from the group consisting of peripheral blood, serum, plasma, or solid tumor tissue; second, detecting the expression level of H2A.B by quantifying mRNA transcripts encoding H2AFB1, H2AFB2, or H2AFB3 using RNA sequencing or quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, with a threshold of 1.5 transcripts per million defining positivity; and third, administering an anthracycline agent to the subject if the expression level exceeds the threshold. The administration is performed under medical supervision, with monitoring for hematologic toxicity and cardiac function.

- specify sH2A variant: H2A.B

The short histone H2A variant is H2A.B, encoded by the H2AFB1, H2AFB2, or H2AFB3 genes. H2A.B is the most extensively characterized member of the short H2A family and is the primary variant associated with oncogenic activity in lymphoid and epithelial malignancies. Its expression is not correlated with other testis-specific histone variants such as TH2A or TH2B, indicating specificity of the oncohistone phenotype.

- describe amino acid sequences of H2A.B variants

The H2A.B protein sequence is characterized by the substitution of arginine at position 29 with glutamine or phenylalanine, and the absence of the C-terminal glutamic acid residue at position 121, which corresponds to a truncation of the canonical H2A tail. The amino acid sequence of H2A.B.1.1 (H2AFB2) is represented by SEQ ID NO: 1, H2A.B.1.2 (H2AFB3) by SEQ ID NO: 2, and H2A.B.2 (H2AFB1) by SEQ ID NO: 3. These sequences are substantially identical to the oncohistone mutations found in canonical H2A in cancer, including R29Q, R29F, and E121del.

- define "having" or "suspected of having" lymphoma

A subject “having” lymphoma is one who has been diagnosed with lymphoma based on histopathological examination, immunophenotyping, and/or molecular profiling consistent with established diagnostic criteria. A subject “suspected of having” lymphoma is one who presents with clinical signs or imaging findings suggestive of lymphoma, including lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, B-symptoms, or abnormal blood counts, and for whom a definitive diagnosis has not yet been established but for whom biopsy or molecular testing is clinically indicated.

- describe sample types: blood, serum, plasma, tissue

Biological samples suitable for detecting H2A.B expression include formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tumor tissue, fresh frozen tumor tissue, peripheral blood mononuclear cells, serum, and plasma. Tissue samples provide the highest sensitivity and specificity, while liquid biopsies offer a non-invasive alternative for monitoring disease progression or relapse. RNA extracted from plasma or serum may contain cell-free transcripts derived from circulating tumor cells or apoptotic tumor fragments, enabling detection without invasive biopsy.

- define detecting sH2A expression level

Detecting the expression level of a short histone H2A variant refers to the quantitative measurement of mRNA transcripts encoding H2AFB1, H2AFB2, or H2AFB3 in a biological sample, using methods such as RNA sequencing, quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction, digital PCR, or microarray analysis. The expression level is normalized to total RNA content and expressed as transcripts per million mapped reads. A value of at least 1.5 transcripts per million is defined as positive expression, distinguishing it from background noise and low-level expression in non-malignant tissues.

- describe methods for detecting sH2A expression

Methods for detecting H2A.B expression include RNA sequencing using Illumina or Ion Torrent platforms, followed by alignment to the human reference genome and quantification using RSEM or Salmon. Quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction may be performed using TaqMan probes specific to H2AFB1, H2AFB2, or H2AFB3, with normalization to housekeeping genes such as GAPDH or ACTB. Digital PCR provides absolute quantification without reliance on reference standards and is particularly useful for low-abundance transcripts in liquid biopsies.

- describe threshold for detection

The threshold for defining H2A.B expression as positive is set at 1.5 transcripts per million, a value derived from population-level analysis of normal tissues and cancer samples. This threshold is statistically validated to distinguish H2A.B-positive tumors from H2A.B-negative tumors with high sensitivity and specificity, and corresponds to the 95th percentile of expression in non-testicular somatic tissues. Samples with expression levels below 0.5 transcripts per million are considered negative, while those between 0.5 and 1.5 are excluded from analysis due to inconclusive signal-to-noise ratios.

- describe administering anthracycline agent

The anthracycline agent is administered parenterally, typically via intravenous infusion, over a period of 15 to 60 minutes, depending on the specific agent and patient tolerance. Administration is performed in cycles, with intervals of 21 days, and is often combined with other chemotherapeutic agents such as cyclophosphamide, vincristine, prednisone, or rituximab, depending on the lymphoma subtype. The timing of administration is initiated following confirmation of H2A.B expression and is not administered in the absence of molecular evidence of variant expression.

- define anthracycline agent

An anthracycline agent is a chemotherapeutic compound belonging to the class of anthraquinone derivatives that intercalate into DNA and inhibit topoisomerase II, resulting in DNA double-strand breaks and apoptosis. Anthracyclines include, but are not limited to, doxorubicin, daunorubicin, epirubicin, idarubicin, aclarubicin, and mitoxantrone, as well as liposomal or pegylated formulations thereof. The term encompasses both natural and synthetic analogs that retain the core mechanism of action involving DNA intercalation and topoisomerase II poisoning.

- describe mechanisms of action of anthracyclines

Anthracyclines exert their cytotoxic effects through three primary mechanisms: intercalation between DNA base pairs, inhibition of topoisomerase II by stabilizing the cleavage complex, and generation of reactive oxygen species through redox cycling. These actions result in persistent DNA damage, activation of the DNA damage response, mitochondrial dysfunction, and induction of apoptosis. In H2A.B-expressing cells, the destabilized chromatin structure enhances the accessibility of DNA to anthracycline molecules, thereby amplifying each of these mechanisms and increasing the likelihood of irreversible cellular damage.

- describe examples of anthracyclines and analogs

Examples of anthracyclines include doxorubicin hydrochloride, daunorubicin hydrochloride, epirubicin, idarubicin, aclarubicin, and mitoxantrone. Liposomal formulations such as Doxil (pegylated liposomal doxorubicin) and Myocet (non-pegylated liposomal doxorubicin) are also encompassed. Analogs include synthetic derivatives such as valrubicin, amrubicin, and pirarubicin, as well as prodrugs designed to improve tumor targeting or reduce cardiotoxicity. All such agents are suitable for use in the method provided that they retain the ability to intercalate DNA and inhibit topoisomerase II.

- describe therapeutically effective dose of anthracycline agent

A therapeutically effective dose of an anthracycline agent is an amount sufficient to induce cytotoxicity in H2A.B-expressing lymphoma cells while minimizing systemic toxicity. For doxorubicin, the dose ranges from 50 to 75 mg/m² per cycle; for daunorubicin, 45 to 60 mg/m²; for idarubicin, 10 to 12 mg/m²; and for aclarubicin, 80 to 120 mg/m². The dose is adjusted for body surface area, age, renal function, and prior exposure to anthracyclines. Cumulative lifetime doses are monitored to prevent cardiotoxicity, with a maximum recommended dose of 550 mg/m² for doxorubicin.

- describe dosing schedules

Dosing schedules are typically administered every 21 days, with cycles repeated for a total of 6 to 8 cycles in frontline therapy or 2 to 4 cycles in salvage regimens. In pediatric populations, dosing may be more frequent, such as every 7 to 14 days, depending on tolerance and disease aggressiveness. Dose reductions are implemented in the presence of neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, or cardiac dysfunction. The schedule is individualized based on molecular response, clinical progression, and toxicity profile.

- describe routes of administration

The anthracycline agent is administered via intravenous infusion. Alternative routes, including intra-arterial delivery for localized disease or intrathecal administration for central nervous system involvement, may be employed in specific clinical contexts. Oral formulations of anthracyclines are not available and are not contemplated. Administration is performed in a clinical setting with cardiac monitoring, particularly for patients with pre-existing cardiac conditions or prior anthracycline exposure.

- describe method of inducing cytotoxicity in lymphoma cell

The method of inducing cytotoxicity in a lymphoma cell involves exposing the cell to an anthracycline agent in the presence of aberrantly expressed H2A.B, wherein the variant causes nucleosome destabilization that increases DNA accessibility, thereby enhancing the binding of the anthracycline molecule to DNA and potentiating topoisomerase II inhibition. This results in an increased number of persistent DNA double-strand breaks, activation of the ATM/ATR-p53 axis, mitochondrial membrane depolarization, caspase activation, and apoptotic cell death. The cytotoxic effect is significantly greater in H2A.B-expressing cells compared to H2A.B-negative cells exposed to the same concentration of anthracycline agent.

### EXAMPLES

- introduce examples

The following examples are provided to illustrate the practice of the invention and are not intended to limit its scope in any way.

- describe aberrant sH2A upregulations in cancers

Analysis of transcriptomic data from over 10,000 tumor samples across 33 cancer types revealed that H2A.B expression was detected in 50% of diffuse large B-cell lymphomas, 70% of Hodgkin’s lymphoma cell lines, and 13% of pediatric B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia cases. Expression was absent in matched normal tissues and in non-testicular somatic tissues from the GTEx database.

- compare sH2A sequences to canonical H2A

Alignment of H2A.B sequences with canonical H2A revealed that the R29Q/F substitution and E121 truncation, both common oncohistone mutations in cancer, are naturally encoded in all H2A.B variants. These residues are conserved across primates, indicating evolutionary selection for nucleosome destabilization.

- analyze oncohistone features in sH2As

The acidic patch of H2A.B is modified by substitutions that disrupt histone-histone interactions, mirroring mutations found in H2A-E64K and H2B-E76K. These changes reduce nucleosome stability in vitro and correlate with increased DNA accessibility in vivo.

- show conservation of oncohistone-like changes

Phylogenetic analysis of H2A.B across 12 primate species demonstrated that the R29Q/F and E121 truncation are conserved in all species, indicating strong selective pressure and functional importance.

- imply functional consequences of oncohistone-like changes

The conservation of these residues implies that they confer a selective advantage in the testis, likely by facilitating chromatin decompaction during spermatogenesis. When expressed in somatic tissues, these same features promote oncogenesis.

- describe H2A.B reactivation in various cancers

H2A.B expression was detected in uterine endometrial carcinoma, cervical squamous cell carcinoma, lung squamous cell carcinoma, and bladder carcinoma, with expression levels ranging from 1.5 to over 100 transcripts per million.

- analyze H2A.B expression in TCGA dataset

In the TCGA dataset, H2A.B was expressed in 50% of DLBCLs, 9.5% of UCECs, and 4.7% of BLCA samples, with no expression in adjacent normal tissues.

- show H2A.B expression in DLBCLs

Of 74 DLBCL samples analyzed, 37 expressed H2A.B at levels exceeding 1.5 TPM, with no correlation to known molecular subtypes such as EZH2 or CREBBP mutations.

- analyze H2A.B expression in B-ALL datasets

In four independent B-ALL datasets, 6–13% of cases expressed H2A.B, with higher expression correlating with improved response to anthracycline-based induction therapy.

- show H2A.B expression in CCLE dataset

Of 1,000 cancer cell lines screened, 70% of Hodgkin’s lymphoma lines and 25% of non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma lines expressed H2A.B, while epithelial lines showed lower frequencies.

- compare H2A.B expression across cancers

H2A.B expression was highest in lymphoid malignancies, followed by squamous carcinomas and endometrial cancers, with minimal expression in adenocarcinomas and gliomas.

- investigate causes of H2A.B induction in cancer

No correlation was found between H2A.B expression and X-chromosome ploidy, copy number alterations, or global epigenetic changes, suggesting localized transcriptional activation.

- analyze X-chromosome ploidy in H2A.B-expressing samples

X-chromosome to autosome transcript ratios were comparable between H2A.B-positive and negative samples, indicating that expression is not driven by chromosomal gain.

- investigate expression profiles of individual H2AFB loci

Each H2AFB paralog (H2AFB1, H2AFB2, H2AFB3) was independently upregulated in different tumors, with no co-expression of neighboring genes, indicating locus-specific activation.

- show local activation of individual H2AFB paralogues

RNA-seq coverage plots confirmed that transcriptional activation occurred exclusively at the H2AFB loci, with no spread to flanking regions.

- compare transcriptomes of H2AFB1-reactivated and H2AFB2/3-reactivated samples

Transcriptomic profiles of H2AFB1-positive and H2AFB2/3-positive tumors were highly similar, with overlapping gene expression signatures across cancer types.

- identify commonly up- or down-regulated genes

A core set of 146 genes were consistently upregulated and 90 downregulated in H2A.B-positive cancers, including multiple cancer-testis antigens.

- analyze expression of other genes associated with H2A.B expression

Other histone variants, including H2A.Z, H3.3, and H2A.X, were not consistently upregulated, nor were testis-specific variants such as TH2A or TH2B.

- show lack of co-upregulation of other testis-specific histone variants

No correlation was observed between H2A.B expression and expression of TH2A, TH2B, or H2A.1, indicating specificity of the oncohistone phenotype.

- analyze expression of histone chaperone NAP1

NAP1L1 expression was elevated in H2A.B-positive tumors, particularly in DLBCL, suggesting a role in H2A.B chromatin incorporation.

- investigate chromatin consequences of NAP1 expression

Higher NAP1 expression correlated with increased H2A.B occupancy in chromatin, as measured by ChIP-seq, but did not alter global nucleosome density.

- identify Cancer-Testis Antigens associated with H2A.B expression

Twelve of the 146 commonly upregulated genes were Cancer-Testis Antigens, including CTAG1B and CT45A5.

- analyze CTA expression in H2A.B-reactivated cancers

CTA scores were significantly higher in H2A.B-positive HNSC, LUSC, and UCEC samples, but not in DLBCL or sarcoma.

- show CTA upregulation in H2A.B-expressing HNSCs, LUSCs, and UCECs

H2A.B-positive tumors in these cancers exhibited a 2.5-fold increase in CTA expression compared to negative counterparts.

- analyze CTA expression in B-ALL datasets

CTA expression was elevated in H2A.B-positive B-ALL samples, with NY-ESO-1 and CT45A5 showing the highest fold-changes.

- investigate association of H2A.B expression with immune infiltrates

No consistent enrichment of immune cell markers was observed in H2A.B-positive tumors, and in some cases, immune infiltration was reduced.

- analyze immune cell markers in H2A.B-expressing tumors

Markers for B-cells, T-cells, NK cells, monocytes, and macrophages showed no significant difference between H2A.B-positive and negative samples.
