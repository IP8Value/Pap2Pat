## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of vector-borne disease control, specifically to the use of microbial agents for the suppression of mosquito-borne pathogens. It further pertains to the fields of microbiology, entomology, infectious disease epidemiology, and biotechnological applications involving bacterial metabolites with anti-pathogen properties. The invention encompasses compositions and methods for reducing the transmission of dengue virus and Plasmodium species by targeting the mosquito vector through colonization, metabolic inhibition, or direct pathogen neutralization. It also extends to the development of biocontrol products, insecticidal formulations, and transmission-blocking agents derived from a novel bacterial strain isolated from the midgut of disease-transmitting mosquitoes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Mosquito-borne diseases such as dengue fever and malaria continue to pose a significant global health burden, affecting hundreds of millions of individuals annually and resulting in substantial morbidity and mortality, particularly in tropical and subtropical regions. Conventional control strategies, including chemical insecticides and environmental management, have been increasingly compromised by the emergence of insecticide resistance, ecological side effects, and logistical challenges in delivery. While recent advances have identified the mosquito gut microbiota as a modulator of vector competence, few practical interventions have emerged from this knowledge. The presence of certain bacterial species within the mosquito midgut has been associated with reduced infection intensity of Plasmodium parasites and dengue virus, yet these associations have not been harnessed for scalable, reproducible, or durable disease control. There remains a critical need for novel, environmentally sustainable, and highly specific agents capable of disrupting pathogen transmission without inducing resistance or harming non-target organisms. The present invention addresses this unmet need by introducing a previously unrecognized bacterial strain with potent, broad-spectrum anti-pathogen activity and entomopathogenic properties that can be deployed directly within mosquito populations.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention introduces Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P), a novel bacterial isolate with exceptional capacity to colonize the midgut of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, the primary vectors of malaria and dengue, respectively. Csp_P exhibits potent anti-pathogen activity against both Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus serotype 2, both in vivo within the mosquito host and in vitro independent of the mosquito. This activity is mediated by stable, heat-sensitive, biofilm-associated metabolites that inhibit parasite development and abolish viral infectivity without sequestering viral particles or altering host cell susceptibility. Csp_P also demonstrates strong entomopathogenic effects, causing significant mortality in both larval and adult stages of these mosquito species upon ingestion or exposure. The bacterium is capable of forming robust biofilms in vitro, and these biofilms retain anti-pathogen activity even after desiccation, suggesting resilience under environmental stress. The invention proposes the use of Csp_P as a live biocontrol agent delivered via sugar baits, larval breeding sites, or residual sprays, as well as the use of its purified metabolites, supernatants, or biofilm extracts as transmission-blocking pharmaceuticals. The broad-spectrum antibacterial activity of Csp_P further enhances its utility by suppressing competing midgut microbiota that may otherwise interfere with pathogen blocking. The invention encompasses compositions comprising Csp_P in whole-cell, filtrate, supernatant, or biofilm form, and methods for administering these compositions to mosquitoes to reduce or eliminate their ability to transmit human pathogens.

### II. Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P)

Chromobacterium sp. Panamam, designated herein as Csp_P, is a Gram-negative, non-spore-forming, rod-shaped bacterium originally isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes in Panama. The bacterium grows optimally at 30°C on Luria-Bertani agar, forming flat, opaque, tan-colored colonies that darken with age under light exposure. Phylogenetic analysis based on 16S rRNA gene sequencing reveals a 98% sequence identity to Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum, yet Csp_P is distinguished by its unique metabolic profile, lack of violacein production, and exceptional entomopathogenic and anti-pathogen activities. Csp_P demonstrates potent insecticidal activity against both larval and adult stages of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti, with exposure to as few as 10^6 colony-forming units per milliliter resulting in rapid mortality. The bacterium is capable of colonizing the mosquito midgut within 24 hours of ingestion, achieving prevalence rates exceeding 95% in antibiotic-treated populations. Extracts derived from Csp_P cultures, including supernatants and biofilm preparations, retain insecticidal activity even after removal of viable cells, indicating that the activity is mediated by secreted factors rather than direct infection. Extraction procedures involve culturing Csp_P under static conditions to promote biofilm formation, followed by harvesting, centrifugation, filtration, and lyophilization. Effective insect control amounts range from 10^4 to 10^10 CFU per mosquito, depending on life stage, exposure route, and environmental conditions. Compositions incorporating Csp_P may be formulated as aqueous suspensions, sugar-based baits, or solid matrices for sustained release in mosquito habitats.

### III. Administration of Compositions Comprising Csp_P to Insects

Compositions comprising Csp_P may be administered to mosquitoes through a variety of exposure routes designed to ensure ingestion, contact, or environmental persistence. In one embodiment, adult mosquitoes are exposed to a sugar solution containing Csp_P at concentrations ranging from 10^6 to 10^10 colony-forming units per milliliter, which is absorbed during feeding and results in midgut colonization. The composition may be linked to a food component such as sucrose, honey, or fruit extract to enhance palatability and uptake. Oral introduction is achieved by impregnating cotton wicks, filter paper, or sponge matrices with the bacterial suspension, which are then placed within mosquito cages. The composition may also be incorporated directly into the larval breeding medium, such as standing water containing organic detritus, where larvae ingest Csp_P during feeding. Bait formulations may be designed in solid, gel, or liquid forms and may include attractants such as lactic acid, carbon dioxide, or octenol to increase mosquito visitation. These baits may be placed within traps or housing units designed to retain mosquitoes for prolonged exposure, including sticky-lined surfaces, funnel traps, or oviposition sites. The composition may be delivered via aerial spraying, ground misting, or residual application to surfaces where mosquitoes rest, using oil-based, water-based, or encapsulated formulations to enhance stability and longevity. Multiple formulations may be combined to achieve synergistic effects, such as pairing a slow-release biofilm matrix with a fast-acting supernatant spray. The term “insect” as used herein includes all species of Culicidae, particularly Anopheles, Aedes, and Culex genera, and the term “pest” encompasses any arthropod whose presence or activity contributes to human disease transmission or economic loss.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Chromobacterium Csp_P Reduces Malaria and Dengue Infection in Vector Mosquitoes

Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) was introduced into laboratory-reared populations of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti through sugar feeding and blood meal supplementation. Antibiotic-treated mosquitoes were exposed to Csp_P at concentrations of 10^8 CFU/mL and 10^6 CFU/mL, respectively, and midgut colonization was confirmed by plating, PCR, and fluorescence in situ hybridization. Within 24 hours, Csp_P was detected in over 90% of individuals, with bacterial loads exceeding 10^4 CFU per midgut. Mosquitoes exposed to Csp_P prior to feeding on Plasmodium falciparum-infected blood exhibited a 92% reduction in oocyst prevalence and a 95% reduction in mean oocyst intensity compared to controls. Similarly, dengue virus infection in Ae. aegypti was reduced by 98% when mosquitoes were pre-exposed to Csp_P. In vitro assays demonstrated that Csp_P biofilm supernatant and desiccated biofilm completely abolished dengue virus infectivity in BHK-21 and C6/36 cells, while asexual, ookinete, and gametocyte stages of P. falciparum were inhibited by 85–100%. Heat inactivation of the biofilm eliminated anti-pathogen activity, confirming the role of thermolabile metabolites. No sequestration of viral particles was observed, and pH changes induced by the biofilm were insufficient to account for viral inactivation. Csp_P induced upregulation of immune genes including Cecropin E and Defensin C in Ae. aegypti midguts, but the anti-pathogen effect persisted in immunocompromised mosquito lines, indicating direct action. The bacterium also exhibited broad-spectrum inhibition of other midgut-derived bacteria, including Serratia, Enterobacter, and Pseudomonas species. These results demonstrate that Csp_P is a potent, multi-modal agent capable of simultaneously reducing mosquito survival and blocking pathogen transmission.

### Example 2: Live Chromobacterium Csp_P is a General Mosquitocidal, and Malaria and Dengue Transmission-Blocking Agent

Live Csp_P was delivered to adult An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti via sugar baits, blood meals, and environmental spraying. Mortality rates reached 90% within 72 hours in An. gambiae and 85% in Ae. aegypti following ingestion of 10^8 CFU/mL. Larvae exposed to Csp_P in breeding water exhibited 100% mortality within 48 hours. When Csp_P was introduced into the mosquito midgut prior to pathogen exposure, infection rates for both Plasmodium and dengue virus were reduced by over 90% compared to untreated controls. Other Chromobacterium strains, including C. violaceum, failed to replicate this effect. Secreted metabolites from Csp_P culture supernatants, even in the absence of live cells, retained full anti-pathogen activity. Biofilm preparations were stable for over 30 days at room temperature and remained effective after lyophilization. Field trials in simulated outdoor environments demonstrated that Csp_P-laced sugar baits reduced mosquito survival and pathogen prevalence by 80% over a two-week period. The bacterium was not pathogenic to non-target organisms including bees, fish, or mammals. These findings establish Csp_P as a uniquely effective, broad-spectrum, and environmentally compatible agent for integrated vector management.

## CONCLUSION

Insect-microbe interactions play a decisive role in determining vector competence for human pathogens. The present invention identifies Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) as a novel bacterial agent with unparalleled capacity to colonize the mosquito midgut, induce rapid mortality, and block the development of Plasmodium and dengue virus through the secretion of stable, biofilm-associated metabolites. Unlike previous microbial candidates, Csp_P achieves all three critical objectives—vector suppression, pathogen inhibition, and environmental resilience—within a single organism. Its activity is independent of mosquito immune activation, resistant to desiccation, and effective against multiple pathogen life stages. The invention provides a foundation for the development of next-generation biocontrol products that can be deployed in the field through low-cost, scalable methods, offering a sustainable alternative to chemical insecticides. The potential for isolating and synthesizing its active metabolites further opens avenues for pharmaceutical development. Csp_P represents a transformative tool in the global effort to eliminate mosquito-borne diseases.
