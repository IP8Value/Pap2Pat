# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel monoclonal antibodies specific for MCR proteins and methods for detecting the presence of these proteins in biological and environmental samples. More particularly, the invention provides highly specific immunological reagents capable of identifying the enzymatic products of mobile colistin resistance genes, including but not limited to MCR-1, MCR-2, and related variants. These antibodies enable direct detection of the functional resistance determinant, bypassing the limitations of nucleic acid-based assays and phenotypic susceptibility testing. The invention further encompasses immunoassay formats, diagnostic kits, and detection protocols designed for rapid, sensitive, and reliable identification of colistin-resistant bacteria in clinical, agricultural, and food safety contexts.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global rise in antimicrobial resistance has severely compromised the efficacy of last-resort antibiotics, particularly colistin, a polymyxin-class agent used to treat life-threatening infections caused by multidrug-resistant Gram-negative pathogens. Colistin exerts its bactericidal effect by binding to the anionic lipid A component of lipopolysaccharide in the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria, displacing divalent cations and inducing membrane disruption. Historically, resistance to colistin was considered rare and primarily mediated by chromosomal mutations that alter lipid A structure, limiting its transmission between bacterial strains. However, the discovery of the plasmid-borne mcr-1 gene in 2015 marked a paradigm shift, revealing a transmissible mechanism of resistance capable of rapid horizontal dissemination across species and geographic boundaries. Subsequent identification of additional mcr gene variants—designated mcr-2 through mcr-8—has further expanded the scope of this public health threat, with some variants exhibiting higher prevalence in food animal reservoirs than mcr-1 itself. These genes encode phosphoethanolamine transferases that modify lipid A by adding a cationic phosphoethanolamine group, thereby neutralizing the negative charge of the bacterial surface and preventing colistin binding. The clinical and epidemiological significance of these genes lies not merely in their presence, but in their expression as functional proteins capable of conferring phenotypic resistance. Current detection methods for colistin resistance rely heavily on phenotypic broth microdilution assays, which are labor-intensive, subject to technical variability due to colistin’s polycationic nature, and require extended incubation times. Alternatively, molecular methods such as PCR and whole-genome sequencing detect the genetic determinants but cannot distinguish between transcriptionally inactive genes, non-functional alleles, or cryptic sequences that may yield false-positive results without corresponding protein expression. These limitations create a critical gap in surveillance capability, particularly in resource-limited settings where advanced genomic infrastructure is unavailable. There exists a persistent and unmet need for a direct, rapid, and robust method to detect the MCR protein itself as a biomarker of active resistance, enabling accurate risk assessment, timely intervention, and effective infection control measures in both clinical and food production environments.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses the aforementioned challenges by providing novel monoclonal antibodies that specifically recognize conserved and variant epitopes within the catalytic domain of MCR proteins, including MCR-1 and MCR-2. These antibodies are capable of detecting the functional enzyme responsible for colistin resistance, irrespective of the underlying genetic variant, thereby enabling direct, phenotypic-level detection. The invention further encompasses immunoassay methods, particularly sandwich enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), that utilize these monoclonal antibodies in combination with polyclonal capture antibodies to achieve high sensitivity and specificity in complex matrices such as meat, fecal samples, and clinical isolates. Hybridoma cell lines producing these monoclonal antibodies have been generated and are stably maintained, ensuring reproducible and scalable antibody production. The antibodies may be conjugated to detectable labels including enzymes, fluorophores, chemiluminescent substrates, or biotin to facilitate signal amplification and compatibility with standard laboratory instrumentation. The invention includes a diagnostic kit comprising the monoclonal antibodies, capture antibodies, labeled detection reagents, buffer systems, and written instructions for use in detecting MCR proteins in food, environmental, and clinical samples. The disclosed methods offer significant advantages over existing technologies: they provide direct evidence of functional resistance, eliminate false positives arising from non-expressed genes, demonstrate high tolerance to complex sample matrices, achieve detection limits below 0.01 ng/mL for MCR-1, and enable quantification of resistance burden without requiring bacterial culture or nucleic acid extraction. The invention further enables source-tracking of specific MCR variants through the use of MCR-1-specific monoclonal antibodies, while cross-reactive antibodies support broad surveillance of emerging mcr gene variants. The utility of this invention spans public health surveillance, food safety monitoring, clinical diagnostics, and antimicrobial stewardship programs, providing a critical tool for mitigating the spread of colistin resistance in an era of dwindling therapeutic options.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The term “antibody” refers to a polypeptide produced by the immune system that specifically binds to an antigen, including full-length immunoglobulins, fragments thereof such as Fab, F(ab’)2, and scFv, and recombinant or engineered variants. A “monoclonal antibody” is an antibody produced by a single clone of cells, exhibiting uniform specificity and affinity for a single epitope. The term “MCR protein” encompasses all phosphoethanolamine transferases encoded by mcr genes, including MCR-1, MCR-2, MCR-3, and other variants, whether derived from natural isolates or recombinant expression systems. The “catalytic domain” of an MCR protein refers to the C-terminal region responsible for enzymatic transfer of phosphoethanolamine to lipid A, typically comprising residues 212–541 of MCR-1 and 212–538 of MCR-2. The term “chimeric” describes an antibody comprising sequences derived from more than one species, such as a humanized antibody containing murine complementarity-determining regions grafted onto a human immunoglobulin framework. “Isolated” means that the antibody or protein has been separated from components of its natural environment, including other cellular components or contaminants, and is substantially free of other substances with which it is naturally associated. “Detecting the formation of the immunological complex” refers to the identification of a specific binding event between an antibody and its target antigen, achieved through direct or indirect signal generation. “Effective amount” denotes the quantity of antibody or reagent sufficient to produce a detectable signal above background in a given assay under defined conditions. “Humanized” refers to an antibody in which non-human sequences have been modified to resemble human immunoglobulin sequences while retaining antigen-binding specificity. “Consisting essentially of” indicates that the claimed composition or method may include additional components that do not materially affect the basic and novel characteristics of the invention. “About” is used to indicate a range of ±10% around a stated value unless otherwise specified. “Resistance to colistin” is defined as the phenotypic ability of a bacterial strain to grow in the presence of colistin at concentrations exceeding the clinical breakpoint, conferred by the expression of an MCR protein. The invention encompasses monoclonal antibodies developed against the catalytic domain of MCR-1 and MCR-2, which were generated by immunizing mice with recombinant, purified proteins lacking the N-terminal transmembrane domain. These antibodies were selected based on their high binding affinity, specificity for MCR proteins over unrelated bacterial antigens, and performance in sandwich ELISA formats. The development process included screening over one thousand hybridoma clones, followed by multiple rounds of limiting dilution to ensure clonality. Antibodies were characterized by Western blot, direct and sandwich ELISA, and cross-reactivity profiling against MCR-1, MCR-2, and related variants. The catalytic domains of MCR-1 and MCR-2 were expressed in E. coli as His-tagged recombinant proteins, purified via immobilized metal affinity chromatography and size-exclusion chromatography, and confirmed for structural integrity by mass spectrometry. Polyclonal antibodies raised in rabbits against cMCR-1 were used as capture agents in sandwich ELISA, while monoclonal antibodies served as detection reagents. The resulting assay demonstrated a limit of detection of 0.01 ng/mL for MCR-1 and 0.1 ng/mL for MCR-2 in buffer, with linear dynamic ranges extending up to 50 ng/mL. The assay retained sensitivity in complex matrices such as ground beef, pork, and chicken, where detection of MCR-1 was achieved at bacterial loads as low as 0.4 cfu per gram of tissue following enrichment. The monoclonal antibodies were labeled with horseradish peroxidase, alkaline phosphatase, biotin, or fluorescent dyes such as FITC and Alexa Fluor 647, enabling compatibility with luminescent, colorimetric, or fluorescent detection platforms. The invention further includes a diagnostic kit comprising a first antibody immobilized on a solid support, a second labeled antibody, a buffer system, positive and negative controls, and written instructions detailing sample preparation, incubation times, and interpretation criteria. The kit may be configured for use in microtiter plates, lateral flow devices, or automated immunoassay systems. The antibodies and methods described herein are not limited to the specific embodiments disclosed and encompass all functional equivalents, modifications, and applications within the scope of the claims.

### Materials and Methods

All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Western Regional Research Center, under approved protocol numbers. E. coli strains AR-Bank #0346 and AR-Bank #0349, harboring the mcr-1 gene on a multicopy plasmid, were obtained from the FDA-CDC Antimicrobial Resistance Bank. ATCC25922 and ATCC29425 served as negative controls. Recombinant MCR-1 and MCR-2 catalytic domains were expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) using pET-based vectors with N-terminal His-tags. Protein expression was induced with IPTG at 16°C for 20 hours. Cells were lysed by sonication in buffer containing protease inhibitors, and soluble fractions were purified using Ni-NTA affinity chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography. Protein concentration was determined by absorbance at 280 nm using theoretical extinction coefficients. Full-length proteins were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and confirmed by Western blot using anti-His antibodies. Mass spectrometry was performed at the UC Berkeley Proteomics/Mass Spectrometry Laboratory to verify protein integrity. Polyclonal antibodies were generated in two rabbits immunized with cMCR-1 at 3-week intervals over four injections. Sera were pooled and IgG purified via Protein-A affinity chromatography. Monoclonal antibodies were produced by fusing splenocytes from immunized BALB/c mice with SP2/0 myeloma cells using polyethylene glycol. Hybridomas were screened by direct ELISA using cMCR-1-coated plates, and positive clones were subcloned by limiting dilution. Antibodies were purified from culture supernatants using Protein-G Sepharose and isotype determined using commercial isotype-specific secondary antibodies. Sandwich ELISA was performed by coating microtiter plates with purified rabbit anti-cMCR-1 IgG, blocking with non-fat dry milk in TBST, adding standards or samples, incubating with monoclonal antibody MCR-1-7, and detecting with HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG. Signal was quantified using chemiluminescent substrate and a plate reader. Limit of detection was calculated as mean background plus three standard deviations. Intra- and inter-day precision were assessed using replicate measurements across multiple days. Western blotting was performed using NuPAGE gels and PVDF membranes, with detection via ECL reagent. Meat samples were spiked with bacterial inocula at 10 cfu per 25 g, enriched in tryptic soy broth containing colistin, and processed for ELISA after cell lysis with B-PER reagent.

### Results and Discussion

Recombinant cMCR-1 and cMCR-2 proteins were successfully expressed and purified, with molecular weights consistent with predicted sizes. Mass spectrometry confirmed the integrity of the full-length MCR-1 protein, with 60.6% sequence coverage. Polyclonal antibodies exhibited high titers and specific binding to MCR-1 in both purified protein and bacterial lysates, with no cross-reactivity to MCR-negative strains. Nine monoclonal antibody clones were isolated, six of which cross-reacted with both MCR-1 and MCR-2, while three were specific for MCR-1. Monoclonal antibody MCR-1-7 demonstrated the highest binding affinity and signal intensity in both direct and sandwich ELISA formats. The sandwich ELISA using rabbit anti-cMCR-1 as capture and MCR-1-7 as detection antibody achieved a limit of detection of 0.01 ng/mL for cMCR-1 and 0.1 ng/mL for cMCR-2, with intra-day and inter-day coefficients of variation below 5% and 15%, respectively. The assay showed excellent linearity (R² > 0.98) across the quantifiable range. When applied to spiked meat samples, the assay reliably detected MCR-1 in ground beef, chicken, and pork at inoculum levels of 10 cfu per 25 g, despite matrix-induced signal attenuation. No false positives were observed in negative controls, including samples containing non-MCR-producing strains or blank matrix. The results demonstrate that the invention provides a robust, sensitive, and specific method for detecting the functional MCR protein in real-world samples, offering a significant advancement over nucleic acid-based detection methods that cannot confirm active resistance. The use of monoclonal antibodies ensures reproducibility, scalability, and suitability for high-throughput screening in food safety and public health laboratories worldwide. The invention is not limited to the specific antibodies or formats described and encompasses all variants, conjugates, and applications that utilize the functional binding properties of the disclosed monoclonal antibodies.
