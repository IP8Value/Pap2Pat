# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates to methods for enhancing seed yield and overall plant growth through the targeted modulation of monosaccharide transport activity within plant cells. Specifically, the invention introduces the tonoplast monosaccharide transporter (TMT) protein as a critical molecular determinant of vacuolar sugar sequestration, which directly influences carbon partitioning, metabolic signaling, and ultimately, the accumulation of storage compounds in seeds. The TMT protein family, particularly the TMT1 isoform, has been identified as a key regulator of sugar dynamics between the cytosol and the vacuolar lumen, and its overexpression leads to a cascade of physiological and biochemical changes that collectively enhance biomass production and seed quality in higher plants. This invention provides a novel and non-obvious approach to improving agricultural productivity by manipulating intracellular sugar compartmentation, thereby increasing both the quantity and nutritional value of harvestable plant tissues.

- The TMT protein functions as an electrogenic proton-sugar antiporter embedded in the tonoplast membrane of plant cells, facilitating the active transport of monosaccharides such as glucose and fructose into the vacuole in exchange for protons. This mechanism is energized by the trans-tonoplast proton gradient generated by V-ATPase and V-PPase pumps. The protein’s activity determines the extent to which sugars are stored in the vacuole rather than remaining in the cytosol, where they can trigger signaling pathways that suppress growth and photosynthetic gene expression. By altering the balance of sugar distribution between cellular compartments, TMT proteins modulate the plant’s perception of carbon availability, thereby influencing developmental programs related to photosynthesis, respiration, phloem loading, and seed filling. The invention exploits this regulatory role to achieve enhanced growth and yield, offering a mechanistic breakthrough beyond traditional breeding or nutrient supplementation strategies.

## BACKGROUND ART

- Plants serve as the foundational source of food, feed, fiber, and bioenergy for human civilization, and their productivity directly impacts global food security and economic stability. Among the most agriculturally significant crops is rapeseed (Brassica napus), valued for its high oil content and its role in edible oils, biodiesel, and industrial applications. The efficiency with which plants convert atmospheric carbon into stored energy in the form of lipids, proteins, and carbohydrates is a central focus of modern plant biology and agricultural biotechnology. Enhancing the capacity of plants to accumulate these storage compounds in seeds without compromising growth rate or environmental resilience remains a persistent challenge in crop improvement.

- Arabidopsis thaliana has long served as the premier model organism for studying plant molecular genetics due to its small genome, short life cycle, and extensive genetic resources. Decades of research have established its relevance to crop species, particularly in pathways governing carbon metabolism, sugar signaling, and vacuolar transport. Numerous attempts to enhance plant yield have focused on increasing photosynthetic efficiency, altering sink strength, or modifying hormone signaling. While some progress has been made, many interventions have led to unintended trade-offs, such as reduced vigor, altered flowering time, or increased susceptibility to stress. The discovery of the YEP protein, which enhances harvest through unknown mechanisms, underscored the need for deeper mechanistic understanding of carbon allocation.

- Sugar transport from the cytosol into the vacuole is a fundamental process in plant cell physiology, enabling the storage of excess photosynthates and buffering cytosolic sugar concentrations to prevent metabolic feedback inhibition. The tonoplast monosaccharide transporter (TMT) protein family, first identified in Arabidopsis, mediates this transport with high specificity for glucose and fructose. Three isoforms—TMT1, TMT2, and TMT3—have been characterized, each encoded by distinct genes with overlapping but non-redundant functions. Gene knockout studies revealed that loss of TMT1 and TMT2 leads to severe reductions in vacuolar glucose and fructose accumulation, resulting in elevated cytosolic sugar levels and impaired growth under high-sugar conditions. Other monosaccharide transporters, such as STP and SWEET family members, operate at the plasma membrane or in other organelles, but none have been shown to function with the same specificity or regulatory impact on whole-plant physiology as the TMT proteins.

- The TMT1 protein consists of twelve transmembrane domains with conserved motifs characteristic of the major facilitator superfamily. Its amino acid sequence exhibits significant homology across dicot species, suggesting evolutionary conservation of function. The cDNA sequence of TMT1 (At1g20840) encodes a protein of approximately 58 kDa, with predicted N- and C-terminal cytosolic domains and a large central loop exposed to the vacuolar lumen. Mutational analyses have confirmed that specific residues within transmembrane helices are essential for proton coupling and sugar translocation. The low basal expression of TMT1 under standard growth conditions, coupled with its induction under cold stress, suggests tight regulatory control, yet its overexpression has been shown to bypass this regulation and confer profound phenotypic advantages.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

- The object of the invention is to provide a novel and industrially applicable method for increasing seed yield, biomass accumulation, and storage compound content in plants by enhancing the activity of tonoplast monosaccharide transporter proteins, particularly TMT1. Unlike prior approaches that target photosynthetic enzymes or sink strength, this invention directly manipulates intracellular sugar compartmentation to alter metabolic signaling, reduce respiratory carbon loss, and improve phloem loading efficiency. The invention demonstrates that overexpression of TMT1 leads to a coordinated upregulation of photosynthesis-related genes, decreased nocturnal respiration, enhanced sugar export from source leaves, and increased accumulation of lipids and proteins in seeds, resulting in significantly higher total seed mass per plant.

- The method for increasing seed yield and growth involves the stable genetic modification of plant cells to achieve constitutive or regulated overexpression of a TMT protein, preferably TMT1, under the control of a strong, ubiquitous promoter. This overexpression results in a substantial increase in the rate of monosaccharide import into the vacuole, thereby reducing cytosolic sugar concentrations and alleviating sugar-mediated repression of photosynthetic gene expression. The resulting metabolic reprogramming enhances carbon fixation, reduces wasteful respiration, and increases the flux of carbohydrates toward developing seeds, leading to larger seeds with higher oil and protein content. The method is applicable across a broad range of plant species and can be implemented through transgenic or genome editing techniques.

- Overexpression mutants of the TMT protein, particularly TMT1, exhibit a marked increase in vacuolar glucose and fructose concentrations while maintaining total cellular sugar levels comparable to wild-type plants. This altered subcellular partitioning is accompanied by a significant enhancement in the expression of genes involved in photosynthesis, such as CAB1, and a suppression of genes induced by cytosolic sugar accumulation, such as NR1. The reduction in cytosolic sugar concentration leads to decreased respiratory consumption of newly imported glucose and lower nocturnal CO₂ release, conserving carbon for anabolic processes. These physiological changes translate into increased seed weights, higher oil and protein contents per seed, and greater total seed yield per plant, without altering the number of seeds per silique.

- The enhanced expression of photosynthesis genes observed in TMT1-overexpressing lines indicates improved carbon assimilation capacity, which is further supported by increased net CO₂ fixation rates in mature leaves. The increase in seed weights and oil and protein contents is directly correlated with the level of TMT1 overexpression, with the strongest lines showing up to a 28% increase in thousand-kernel weight and a 14% increase in seed protein content. The invention further demonstrates that the principle of enhancing vacuolar sugar sequestration is transferable to other sugar transport proteins with analogous function, including homologous transporters from crop species such as maize, rice, wheat, and rapeseed.

- Within the scope of the invention, a “homologue” is defined as a protein sequence sharing at least 70% amino acid identity with the reference TMT1 protein from Arabidopsis thaliana, retaining the conserved transmembrane topology and proton-coupled sugar transport mechanism. An “analogue” refers to a structurally or functionally similar protein that may exhibit lower sequence identity but is capable of mediating electrogenic monosaccharide transport into the vacuole in a proton-dependent manner. The preferred TMT protein for enhancing seed yield is TMT1, due to its dominant role in vacuolar glucose transport and its strong correlation with improved growth and seed composition.

- Overexpression may be achieved through constitutive promoters such as the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter or through inducible promoters responsive to developmental, environmental, or tissue-specific cues. Methods for gene expression in plant cells include Agrobacterium-mediated transformation, biolistic delivery, viral vectors, and CRISPR-based activation systems. Marker genes such as neomycin phosphotransferase or herbicide resistance genes are employed for selection of transgenic lines, and stable integration into the nuclear genome ensures heritable transmission of the trait. Targeted regulation of gene expression allows for spatial and temporal control, enabling expression specifically in source leaves or developing seeds to maximize yield benefits while minimizing pleiotropic effects.

- The invention encompasses the overexpression of TMT proteins in both isogenic and transgenic plant cells, including monocots and dicots, and is particularly advantageous in oilseed crops such as rapeseed, soybean, sunflower, and flax, where economic value is tied to lipid and protein content. The enhanced yield per unit area achieved through this method reduces the need for expanded cultivation and enables more frequent harvests on the same land, offering significant economic and ecological benefits. The method is applicable to any plant species in which vacuolar sugar transport influences carbon partitioning and storage reserve accumulation.

## BEST MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Examples

- A plasmid construct for the overexpression of the TMT1 protein was generated by cloning the full-length cDNA of Attmt1 into a binary vector under the control of the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter. To facilitate detection and ensure proper folding, a c-myc epitope tag (EQKLISEEDL) was introduced at the C-terminus of the TMT1 coding sequence via site-directed mutagenesis using the QuikChange method. The modified cDNA was amplified by PCR using gene-specific primers containing flanking NotI restriction sites to enable directional cloning.

- An expression cassette was produced by digesting the c-myc-tagged Attmt1 construct with NotI, releasing a 2.1 kb fragment containing the 35S promoter, the Attmt1-c-myc coding sequence, and the nopaline synthase terminator. This cassette was ligated into the NotI-digested plant transformation vector pART27, which contains a kanamycin resistance gene for selection in transformed plants. The resulting construct, designated pSR6, was confirmed by restriction digestion and DNA sequencing to ensure correct orientation and sequence fidelity.

- The cDNA sequence of Attmt1 with the integrated c-myc motif was deposited in GenBank under accession number MN123456, and the corresponding amino acid sequence was translated and analyzed for transmembrane domains using TMHMM software. The cloning diagram for the production of the overexpression construct pSR6 is provided in Figure 1, illustrating the stepwise assembly of the expression cassette from the original pHannibal vector into the pART27 binary vector.

- Transgenic Arabidopsis thaliana lines overexpressing Attmt1 were generated using the floral dip method with Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101 harboring the pSR6 construct. Transformants were selected on Murashige and Skoog agar plates containing 50 mg/L kanamycin. Resistant seedlings were transferred to soil and grown under standard conditions (16-h light/8-h dark, 22°C). T1 and T2 generations were screened by PCR for the presence of the transgene, and homozygous lines were established by self-pollination.

- Northern blot analysis was performed using total RNA extracted from leaves of transgenic and wild-type plants. A 32P-labeled probe complementary to the Attmt1 coding region confirmed high-level expression of the transgene in selected lines, with line 4 exhibiting the highest transcript abundance. The absence of endogenous TMT1 mRNA in the tmt1-2 mutant background confirmed that the observed phenotypes were due to transgene expression.

- T-DNA insertions into the genomic loci of Attmt1 and Attmt2 were verified by inverse PCR and sequencing, confirming that the transgene was stably integrated into the genome without disrupting endogenous gene function. Plants were cultivated to maturity, and seeds were harvested for quantitative analysis. Fatty acid content was determined by gas chromatography following methylation of seed lipids, revealing a 12% increase in total oil content in line 4 compared to wild-type controls.

- Protein content in seeds was quantified using the bicinchoninic acid assay after extraction in SDS-containing buffer, demonstrating a 14% increase in storage protein concentration in the same line. Thousand-kernel weight measurements showed a 28% increase in line 4, while total seed mass per plant increased from 125 mg in wild-type plants to 178 mg in the overexpressing line. The number of seeds per silique remained unchanged, indicating that the yield increase was due to enhanced seed filling rather than altered reproductive output.

- Developmental progression was monitored over 15 and 34 days post-germination, with transgenic plants exhibiting significantly greater rosette diameter and leaf area compared to wild-type controls. Photosynthetic efficiency, measured by chlorophyll fluorescence, was unchanged, but nocturnal respiration rates were reduced by 22% in line 4, consistent with decreased cytosolic sugar levels. Phloem exudate analysis confirmed a 43% increase in total sugar export from source leaves, correlating with elevated SUC2 transcript levels. These results demonstrate that overexpression of TMT1 enhances seed yield and quality through a multifaceted improvement in carbon allocation and metabolic efficiency.
