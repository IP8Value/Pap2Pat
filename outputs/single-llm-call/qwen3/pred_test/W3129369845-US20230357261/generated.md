## BACKGROUND

Necrotizing enterocolitis is a severe inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract that predominantly affects premature infants, often resulting in tissue necrosis, systemic infection, and high mortality rates. The disease typically manifests within the first few weeks of life following the initiation of enteral feeding and bacterial colonization of the immature intestinal mucosa. Clinical presentation includes abdominal distension, feeding intolerance, bloody stools, and systemic signs of sepsis. Despite decades of research, the underlying pathophysiology remains incompletely understood, and current therapeutic strategies remain largely supportive, including bowel rest, antibiotics, and surgical intervention in advanced cases. Preventive measures such as the use of breast milk, probiotics, and delayed feeding have shown modest benefit but fail to address the fundamental molecular drivers of disease. There remains a critical unmet need for targeted interventions capable of modulating the aberrant immune responses that characterize this condition, particularly those that can be administered prior to or immediately after birth to mitigate disease onset and progression.

## SUMMARY

Aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist compounds have been identified as potent modulators of intestinal inflammation and as effective agents for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis. The compound of formula (I) is defined as a benzimidazole derivative having the general structure wherein X₁ is selected from the group consisting of oxygen, sulfur, and nitrogen; Y₁ is selected from the group consisting of a substituted or unsubstituted phenyl ring, a substituted or unsubstituted pyridine ring, and a substituted or unsubstituted thiophene ring; R₁ is selected from the group consisting of hydrogen, halogen, alkyl, alkoxy, cyano, nitro, and trifluoromethyl; R₂ is selected from the group consisting of hydrogen, lower alkyl, hydroxyalkyl, and aryl; R₃ and R₄ are independently selected from the group consisting of hydrogen, halogen, alkyl, alkoxy, amino, and aryl. Specific embodiments of formula (I) include compounds wherein X₁ is oxygen, Y₁ is a 4-(2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy)pyridin-2-yl group, R₁ is fluoro, R₂ is methyl, and R₃ and R₄ are both hydrogen. Additional embodiments include compounds wherein R₁ is chloro and R₂ is ethyl, or wherein R₃ is methoxy and R₄ is hydrogen. A further embodiment of the invention is a compound of formula (II), defined as a quinazoline derivative having the general structure wherein the quinazoline core is substituted at the 4-position with a thioether or sulfonyl linkage to a substituted benzimidazole ring. Specific examples of formula (II) include 4-(((1H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-yl)methyl)thio)quinazoline, 4-((1-methyl-1H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-yl)methoxy)quinazoline, and 2-ethyl-4-(((1H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-yl)methoxy)quinazoline. A compound of formula (III) is defined as a pyridine N-oxide derivative having the general structure wherein the pyridine ring is substituted at the 4-position with an alkoxy, aryloxy, or benzyloxy group and at the 2-position with a methylenehydroxy substituent. Specific examples of formula (III) include 2-methylenehydroxy-3-methyl-4-(2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy)pyridine N-oxide, 2-methylenehydroxy-3-methyl-4-methoxypyridine N-oxide, and 2-methylenehydroxy-3-methyl-4-phenoxypyridine N-oxide. These compounds are characterized by their ability to activate the aryl hydrocarbon receptor and suppress toll-like receptor 4 signaling in the intestinal epithelium.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic approaches for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis through the targeted activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor. The detailed description that follows provides a comprehensive framework for the composition, administration, and use of aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonists in the context of inflammatory disorders of the neonatal intestine. This description is intended to enable a person of ordinary skill in the art to practice the invention without undue experimentation and to fully appreciate the scope and utility of the claimed subject matter. The invention is grounded in the discovery that aryl hydrocarbon receptor signaling in the intestinal epithelium serves as a critical regulatory node in the pathogenesis of necrotizing enterocolitis, and that pharmacological activation of this pathway can profoundly attenuate disease severity by suppressing aberrant inflammatory responses mediated by toll-like receptor 4. The compounds disclosed herein are designed to mimic endogenous ligands derived from maternal diet and breast milk, thereby providing a pharmacological strategy to compensate for the developmental deficiency in aryl hydrocarbon receptor activity observed in premature infants.

### A. Compounds

The compounds of formula (I) are characterized by a central heterocyclic scaffold that facilitates high-affinity binding to the aryl hydrocarbon receptor ligand-binding domain. In specific embodiments, the variable X₁ is oxygen, which enhances metabolic stability and receptor affinity. Y₁ is preferably a pyridine ring substituted at the 4-position with a 2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy group, which confers increased lipophilicity and membrane permeability. R₁ is most commonly fluoro or chloro, positioned to optimize electronic interactions with key residues in the receptor pocket. R₂ is typically methyl or ethyl, providing steric bulk without compromising solubility. R₃ and R₄ are independently hydrogen or halogen, allowing for fine-tuning of receptor activation kinetics. Specific compounds of formula (I) include 5-fluoro-2-(((3-methyl-4-(2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy)pyridin-2-yl)methyl)thio)benzimidazole, 5-methyl-2-(((3-methyl-4-(2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy)pyridin-2-yl)methyl)thio)benzimidazole, and 5-fluoro-2-(((3-methyl-4-methoxypyridin-2-yl)methyl)thio)benzimidazole. Compounds of formula (II) feature a quinazoline core linked via a methylene bridge to a substituted benzimidazole moiety, with the thioether or sulfonyl linker enhancing binding affinity and metabolic resistance. Examples include 4-(((1H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-yl)methyl)sulfonyl)quinazoline and 2-hydroxy-4-(((1H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-yl)methoxy)quinazoline. Compounds of formula (III) are pyridine N-oxides with a methylenehydroxy group at the 2-position and an alkoxy or aryloxy substituent at the 4-position, enabling selective activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor without off-target effects on cytochrome P450 enzymes. Examples include 2-methylenehydroxy-3-methyl-4-benzyloxypyridine N-oxide and 2-methylenehydroxy-3-methyl-4-phenoxypyridine N-oxide. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts of these compounds include hydrochloride, sulfate, mesylate, and citrate salts, which enhance aqueous solubility and bioavailability for enteral or parenteral administration.

### B. Methods of Treatment

The methods of treatment disclosed herein involve the administration of an aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist to a subject at risk of or suffering from an inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract, particularly necrotizing enterocolitis. Activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor leads to the transcriptional upregulation of cytochrome P450 enzymes, including CYP1A1, which in turn suppresses the expression and signaling of toll-like receptor 4 in the intestinal epithelium. Inflammatory disorders encompass conditions characterized by dysregulated innate immune responses, including but not limited to necrotizing enterocolitis, inflammatory bowel disease, and sepsis-associated intestinal injury. The subject is preferably a human infant, particularly a premature infant born before 37 weeks gestational age. The term newborn refers to an infant within the first 28 days of life, and a premature infant is defined as one born prior to 37 completed weeks of gestation. Treatment is administered either prophylactically to reduce the risk of disease onset or therapeutically to reduce disease severity. Enteral administration is preferred, including oral gavage or incorporation into infant formula. An effective amount is that which results in a measurable reduction in intestinal epithelial apoptosis, decreased expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-6 and TNF-α, and improved histological integrity of the intestinal mucosa. Combination therapy with other agents such as probiotics, breast milk, or anti-inflammatory compounds is contemplated, and may be administered simultaneously or sequentially. Synergistic effects are observed when the aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist is co-administered with breast milk, resulting in a synergy index greater than 1.5. Antagonism is avoided by selecting compounds with high specificity for the aryl hydrocarbon receptor. A synergistically effective amount is determined by in vivo models of necrotizing enterocolitis and is defined as the lowest dose that produces a statistically significant improvement in disease score compared to either agent alone. Prophylactic treatment is administered to pregnant mothers to enable transplacental transfer of the agonist to the fetal circulation, thereby priming the developing intestinal epithelium for immune tolerance. This approach reduces both the incidence and severity of necrotizing enterocolitis in premature offspring.

### C. Infant Nutritional Formula Comprising Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) Agonists for the Prevention and Treatment of Inflammatory Disorders

The invention further encompasses infant nutritional formulas formulated to contain an aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist in therapeutically effective concentrations. The formula composition includes a base of hydrolyzed protein, lactose, vegetable oils, and essential vitamins and minerals, with the addition of a compound of formula (I), (II), or (III) at a concentration ranging from 0.1 to 100 micrograms per milliliter. The formula may be provided in liquid form for direct enteral administration or as a powdered concentrate for reconstitution. Commercially available formulas such as Similac Advance, Enfamil, and Nutramigen may be modified by the addition of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist. The formulation is stable under standard storage conditions and maintains biological activity upon reconstitution. Enteral administration is achieved via nasogastric or orogastric tube in premature infants, or by bottle feeding in term infants at risk for gastrointestinal inflammation. The agonist is uniformly dispersed in the formula to ensure consistent dosing and bioavailability.

### D. Maternal Administration of an Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) Agonist

The invention provides a method for preventing inflammatory disorders in a newborn by administering an aryl hydrocarbon receptor agonist to a pregnant mother during the third trimester of gestation. The agonist is delivered orally, intravenously, or via transdermal patch at a dose sufficient to achieve detectable concentrations in maternal serum, amniotic fluid, and breast milk. Administration begins no later than 28 weeks gestational age and continues through delivery. The compound crosses the placental barrier and activates aryl hydrocarbon receptor signaling in the fetal intestinal epithelium, leading to upregulation of protective genes such as CYP1A1 and suppression of TLR4 expression. This prenatal priming reduces the susceptibility of the neonate to postnatal inflammatory triggers such as bacterial colonization and formula feeding. The method is particularly effective in pregnancies complicated by preterm labor, maternal infection, or history of prior neonatal intestinal disease.

### E. Pharmaceutical Compositions and Administration

The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of formula (I), (II), or (III) in association with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts include hydrochloride, sulfate, acetate, citrate, and maleate salts for acid addition, and sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium salts for base addition. The formulations may be prepared as tablets, capsules, dragees, suspensions, emulsions, or injectable solutions. Modes of administration include systemic delivery via oral, intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous routes, as well as topical, transmucosal, nasal, or inhalation delivery for localized effects. Timed-release formulations are employed to maintain sustained receptor activation over 12 to 24 hours. Parenteral delivery is preferred in critically ill neonates unable to tolerate enteral feeding. Transmucosal administration via buccal or rectal routes is utilized when rapid absorption is required. Nasal or inhalation delivery is employed for systemic distribution in neonates with compromised gut integrity. Effective amounts range from 0.01 to 10 mg/kg body weight per day, depending on the route of administration and the specific compound. Pharmaceutical compositions are prepared using standard techniques including wet granulation, spray drying, lyophilization, and homogenization. Oral preparations include tablets containing microcrystalline cellulose, croscarmellose sodium, and magnesium stearate; dragees coated with hydroxypropyl methylcellulose; and capsules filled with powdered compound and lactose. Each formulation is validated for stability, dissolution, and bioavailability.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples are provided to illustrate the preparation and use of the claimed compounds and methods, and are not intended to limit the scope of the invention.

### Example 1

Abbreviations used include: NEC for necrotizing enterocolitis, AHR for aryl hydrocarbon receptor, I3C for indole-3-carbinol, TLR4 for toll-like receptor 4, Cyp1a1 for cytochrome P450 family 1 subfamily A member 1, and qRT-PCR for quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction. The current dogma holds that NEC arises exclusively from postnatal environmental triggers, including formula feeding and bacterial colonization. However, emerging evidence suggests that in utero signaling pathways may modulate disease susceptibility. Maternal dietary intake of AHR ligands such as I3C has been shown to reduce the incidence of premature birth and NEC. Administration of I3C during pregnancy induces Cyp1a1 expression in the fetal intestine, confirming transplacental activation of AHR. In murine models, maternal I3C supplementation reduces NEC severity, decreases intestinal epithelial apoptosis, and suppresses TLR4-mediated cytokine production. AHR expression is significantly reduced in the intestinal epithelium of human, murine, and porcine NEC patients compared to controls. Genetic deletion of Ahr in mice results in markedly more severe NEC, whereas deletion of Ahr specifically from the intestinal epithelium, but not from myeloid cells, abolishes protection. Administration of I3C to neonatal mice reduces NEC severity only in the presence of intact intestinal epithelial AHR signaling. Breast milk activates AHR in enteroids and reduces TLR4 signaling in an AHR-dependent manner. The compound A18, identified as a potent AHR agonist, activates the receptor in human ileal explants and reduces TNF-α expression. Maternal administration of A18 during pregnancy induces Cyp1a1 in offspring and protects against NEC. These findings establish that AHR signaling in the intestinal epithelium is both necessary and sufficient for protection against NEC, and that pharmacological activation of this pathway during pregnancy or in the neonatal period represents a novel therapeutic strategy.

### Example 2

The compound designated as A18 was synthesized by a multi-step process beginning with the preparation of 5-fluoro-1,2-diaminobenzene, followed by cyclization to form 5-fluoro-2-thiobenzimidazole. This intermediate was alkylated with 4-(2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy)-2-bromomethylpyridine under basic conditions to yield the target compound. Analogues were prepared by varying the pyridine substituent to include methoxy, ethoxy, phenyloxy, and benzyloxy groups. The synthesis of compound 169 involved the substitution of the thiobenzimidazole with a sulfonyl linker to a quinazoline core. SAR studies revealed that the 2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy group at the 4-position of the pyridine ring conferred optimal AHR binding affinity and metabolic stability. Representative compounds were purified by column chromatography using silica gel and eluted with gradients of ethyl acetate in hexane. Analytical methods included ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectroscopy, high-resolution mass spectrometry, and infrared spectroscopy. Reaction conditions employed anhydrous solvents under nitrogen atmosphere at temperatures between 60 and 120°C. Quenching was performed with saturated aqueous ammonium chloride, followed by extraction with ethyl acetate and drying over sodium sulfate. NMR spectra confirmed the structure of all compounds, with characteristic shifts for aromatic protons and fluorinated alkyl chains. HRMS data matched theoretical masses within 0.01 Da. IR spectra confirmed the presence of C=O, C-N, and C-F bonds. Synthesis of additional analogues included the preparation of 2-methylenehydroxy-3-methyl-4-(2,2,2-trifluoroethoxy)pyridine N-oxide and its derivatives. Purification was achieved via preparative HPLC, and identity was confirmed by melting point determination and elemental analysis. Spectral data for all compounds were consistent with published literature for analogous structures.

### Example 3

AHR activation was demonstrated in vitro using an AHR-luciferase reporter cell line derived from IEC-6 enterocytes. Screening of a clinical compound library identified A18 as the most potent agonist, with an EC₅₀ of 1.2 μM. Administration of A18 to neonatal mice induced Cyp1a1 expression in the ileum and reduced LPS-induced TNF-α and IL-6 levels. Structural analogues were synthesized and tested for potency, with compound #169 showing a 3-fold increase in AHR binding affinity compared to A18. Compound #243 demonstrated enhanced oral bioavailability, while compound #247 exhibited prolonged half-life in plasma. In vivo testing in a murine NEC model confirmed that compound #263, a sulfonyl-linked quinazoline derivative, reduced NEC severity by 85% compared to vehicle control. RNA isolation and quantification confirmed upregulation of Cyp1a1 and downregulation of Tlr4 mRNA in intestinal tissue. These results validate the therapeutic potential of novel AHR agonists for the prevention and treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis.
