# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

Metabolic disorder diseases represent a broad category of physiological dysfunctions characterized by aberrant energy homeostasis, impaired glucose regulation, and lipid metabolism disturbances. Among these, type 2 diabetes mellitus stands as the most prevalent and clinically significant condition, affecting hundreds of millions of individuals globally. It arises from a complex interplay between diminished insulin secretion from pancreatic β-cells and reduced insulin sensitivity in peripheral tissues such as skeletal muscle, liver, and adipose tissue. This dual defect leads to chronic hyperglycemia, which, if unmanaged, initiates a cascade of secondary complications including cardiovascular disease, renal failure, retinopathy, and neuropathy. Insulin resistance, a hallmark of type 2 diabetes, impedes the normal translocation of glucose transporter type 4 (GLUT4) to the cell membrane, thereby limiting cellular glucose uptake even in the presence of elevated circulating insulin. Adiponectin, an adipokine secreted predominantly by adipose tissue, plays a pivotal role in enhancing insulin sensitivity, promoting fatty acid oxidation, and suppressing hepatic gluconeogenesis. Its circulating levels are inversely correlated with body fat mass and insulin resistance, making it a critical biomarker and therapeutic target in metabolic syndrome. Current pharmacological interventions for type 2 diabetes—including sulfonylureas, metformin, thiazolidinediones, and α-glucosidase inhibitors—often provide partial glycemic control but are associated with significant limitations such as hypoglycemia, weight gain, gastrointestinal distress, hepatotoxicity, and lactic acidosis. Moreover, most of these agents act through a single molecular pathway, necessitating polypharmacy to achieve adequate metabolic control, which increases the risk of drug interactions and patient noncompliance. In response to these shortcomings, there has been a resurgence of interest in ethnobotanical remedies, particularly those derived from traditional medicinal systems in Asia, where plant-based therapies have been used for centuries to manage glucose dysregulation. Among these, Curculigo latifolia, a perennial shrub native to Southeast Asia and commonly known as Lemba in Malaysia, has emerged as a promising candidate. Despite its traditional use as a natural sweetener and its documented antioxidant properties, no prior scientific investigation has systematically evaluated its potential to modulate insulin signaling, adiponectin secretion, or glucose uptake in the context of metabolic dysfunction. The unique phytochemical profile of this plant, rich in phenolic compounds and bioactive glycosides, suggests a multi-targeted mechanism of action that may address the core pathophysiological defects of type 2 diabetes without the adverse effects associated with synthetic drugs.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The invention disclosed herein comprises the use of aqueous extracts derived from the fruit and root tissues of Curculigo latifolia for the treatment and amelioration of metabolic disorders, particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus and associated dyslipidemia. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that a 1:1 aqueous extract of C. latifolia fruit and root, when administered orally, significantly reduces fasting blood glucose levels, improves glucose tolerance, enhances insulin and adiponectin secretion, and restores lipid homeostasis in a diet- and chemically-induced model of type 2 diabetes. This effect is mediated through the coordinated upregulation of key genes involved in insulin signaling, glucose transport, and lipid metabolism, including IRS-1, IGF-1, GLUT4, PPARγ, PPARα, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, LPL, and lipase. Unlike conventional antidiabetic agents that target isolated pathways, the C. latifolia extract exerts pleiotropic effects that simultaneously enhance insulin sensitivity, stimulate insulin secretion, promote adiponectin production, and facilitate fatty acid oxidation, thereby addressing multiple facets of metabolic dysfunction in a single therapeutic intervention. The extract demonstrates no observable toxicity in vivo and does not induce weight gain, a common side effect of insulin-sensitizing agents such as thiazolidinediones. The invention further encompasses the use of the extract in formulations suitable for oral administration, including capsules, tablets, syrups, and functional foods, for long-term management of metabolic disorders with improved safety and efficacy profiles compared to existing pharmaceuticals.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The method for ameliorating elevated blood glucose levels in a subject suffering from metabolic disorder involves the oral administration of an aqueous extract prepared from the fruit and root tissues of Curculigo latifolia in a ratio of 1:1 by weight. The extract is administered daily at a dosage ranging from 50 mg/kg to 200 mg/kg of body weight, depending on the severity of hyperglycemia and the metabolic profile of the subject. The extract is prepared by macerating fresh fruit and dried root material in distilled water for 24 hours under continuous stirring at ambient temperature, followed by filtration through Whatman No. 1 filter paper and lyophilization to yield a dry powder. This powder is reconstituted in sterile water or saline immediately prior to administration. The extract may be administered alone or in combination with dietary modification and physical activity regimens to enhance therapeutic outcomes. In vitro studies were conducted using 3T3-L1 adipocytes and L6 myotubes, which were exposed to varying concentrations of the extract to assess cytotoxicity and metabolic activity. Cell viability assays performed via MTT and trypan blue exclusion demonstrated no significant cytotoxicity at concentrations up to 500 μg/mL, confirming the extract’s safety profile in mammalian cell lines. Cell counting and differentiation protocols confirmed that the extract did not interfere with adipogenic or myogenic differentiation, and instead enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in both cell types. Glucose uptake assays using 2-deoxyglucose tracer revealed a dose-dependent increase in basal and insulin-mediated glucose transport, with maximal stimulation observed at 100 μg/mL. Insulin secretion assays using INS-1 pancreatic β-cells showed a significant increase in insulin release under high-glucose conditions, indicating that the extract possesses insulin secretagogue properties. Adiponectin secretion was measured in differentiated 3T3-L1 adipocytes and found to be significantly elevated following 48 hours of extract exposure, suggesting direct modulation of adipokine production. In vivo studies employed a high-fat diet combined with low-dose streptozotocin-induced diabetic rat model, which closely recapitulates the progressive insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction observed in human type 2 diabetes. Animals were divided into seven groups, including normal controls, obese controls, diabetic controls, and treatment groups receiving either the extract at 50, 100, or 200 mg/kg/day or glibenclamide at 10 mg/kg/day. Oral glucose tolerance tests performed after four weeks of treatment demonstrated significantly improved glucose clearance in all extract-treated groups, with the 200 mg/kg dose achieving a 64.5% reduction in postprandial glucose levels compared to diabetic controls. Fasting blood glucose levels were similarly reduced, with no hypoglycemic episodes observed. Plasma insulin and adiponectin concentrations were significantly elevated in extract-treated animals, while lipid profiles showed marked reductions in total cholesterol, triglycerides, and low-density lipoprotein, accompanied by increased high-density lipoprotein. Histological and biochemical analyses of liver and kidney tissues revealed no signs of toxicity, and gene expression profiling confirmed upregulation of IRS-1, IGF-1, GLUT4, PPARγ, PPARα, AdipoR1, AdipoR2, LPL, and lipase mRNA in both adipose and skeletal muscle tissues. These findings collectively demonstrate that the C. latifolia extract improves metabolic function through a multi-pathway mechanism that enhances insulin sensitivity, stimulates insulin and adiponectin secretion, and promotes lipid catabolism without inducing adverse effects.

### Example 1

The aqueous extract of Curculigo latifolia fruit was prepared by harvesting fully ripe berries from plants cultivated in Beranang, Selangor, Malaysia, washing them under running tap water, blotting excess moisture, and storing at −20°C until processing. The fruit tissue was manually mashed using a sterile mortar and pestle and then extracted with 2000 mL of distilled water per 50 g of fresh tissue. The mixture was stirred continuously for 24 hours at room temperature, filtered through Whatman No. 1 filter paper, and the filtrate was lyophilized to obtain a dry powder. The root tissue was obtained from mature plants, washed thoroughly, air-dried overnight at 40°C, ground into a fine powder using an electric grinder, and stored at 4°C. Fifty grams of root powder was soaked in 2000 mL of distilled water under continuous stirring for 24 hours, filtered, and lyophilized to yield a dry extract. The fruit and root extracts were combined in a 1:1 weight ratio to form the final therapeutic formulation, which was stored at −80°C until use. Each batch was characterized for total phenolic content using the Folin-Ciocalteu method and antioxidant capacity via DPPH radical scavenging assay, confirming consistency across preparations.

### Example 2

3T3-L1 preadipocytes and L6 myoblasts were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37°C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. Upon reaching 90% confluence, preadipocytes were induced to differentiate using a hormonal cocktail containing insulin, dexamethasone, and isobutylmethylxanthine for seven days, while myoblasts were differentiated into myotubes by switching to low-serum medium. Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay after 24, 48, and 72 hours of exposure to extract concentrations ranging from 10 to 500 μg/mL. No significant reduction in mitochondrial activity was observed at any concentration, indicating non-toxicity. Cell counting via hemocytometer confirmed that extract treatment did not alter proliferation rates. Differentiation efficiency, evaluated by Oil Red O staining in adipocytes and myosin heavy chain expression in myotubes, remained unchanged, confirming that the extract supports rather than inhibits terminal differentiation.

### Example 3

INS-1 insulinoma cells were seeded in 24-well plates and incubated in low-glucose (5.5 mM) or high-glucose (25 mM) medium for 24 hours. Cells were then treated with the C. latifolia extract at concentrations of 10, 50, and 100 μg/mL for an additional 24 hours. Supernatants were collected and analyzed for insulin content using a rat-specific ELISA kit. Compared to untreated controls, extract-treated cells exhibited a dose-dependent increase in insulin secretion under high-glucose conditions, with maximal stimulation at 100 μg/mL, indicating direct potentiation of β-cell function.

### Example 4

Glucose uptake was measured in differentiated 3T3-L1 adipocytes and L6 myotubes using 2-deoxy-[³H]glucose as a tracer. Cells were serum-starved for four hours, then incubated with or without insulin (100 nM) in the presence of extract at 10, 50, and 100 μg/mL for 30 minutes. Radioactivity incorporated into cells was quantified via scintillation counting. The extract significantly enhanced basal glucose uptake in both cell types, and further potentiated insulin-stimulated uptake by up to 85% at 100 μg/mL, demonstrating insulin-sensitizing and insulin-mimetic properties.

### Example 5

Differentiated 3T3-L1 adipocytes were treated with the C. latifolia extract at 10, 50, and 100 μg/mL for 48 hours. Culture supernatants were collected and analyzed for adiponectin concentration using a commercially available rat adiponectin ELISA kit. Extract treatment resulted in a statistically significant, dose-dependent increase in adiponectin secretion, with a 2.8-fold elevation observed at 100 μg/mL compared to untreated controls, indicating direct modulation of adipokine production.

### Example 6

Forty-two male Sprague-Dawley rats were divided into seven groups of six animals each. The high-fat diet was formulated by blending 50% standard rat chow, 24% corn oil, 20% full-cream milk powder, and 6% sucrose. After four weeks of high-fat feeding, obesity was confirmed, and diabetes was induced via a single intravenous injection of 40 mg/kg streptozotocin. Rats with fasting blood glucose exceeding 170 mg/dL after seven days were included in the study. Treatment groups received daily oral gavage of the C. latifolia fruit:root extract at 50, 100, or 200 mg/kg/day, or glibenclamide at 10 mg/kg/day, for four weeks. Oral glucose tolerance tests were performed after an overnight fast, with blood glucose measured at 0, 30, 60, and 120 minutes following a 2 g/kg glucose load. Plasma was analyzed for glucose, insulin, adiponectin, lipids, urea, creatinine, ALT, and GGT. Tissues were harvested for RNA extraction and gene expression analysis using the GenomeLab GeXP system. The extract significantly improved glucose tolerance, increased insulin and adiponectin levels, normalized lipid profiles, reduced liver and kidney injury markers, and upregulated key metabolic genes in both adipose and muscle tissues. No mortality or behavioral abnormalities were observed, confirming the safety of the extract.
