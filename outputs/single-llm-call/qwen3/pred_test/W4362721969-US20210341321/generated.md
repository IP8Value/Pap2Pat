# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to microfluidic systems and, more specifically, to a novel flow rate sensor capable of detecting and quantifying fluid flow within microscale channels through the mechanical deformation of a nanoscale membrane coupled to a microwave resonator. This sensor is designed for integration directly onto microfluidic chips, enabling real-time, in situ measurement of flow rates without requiring external instrumentation or moving parts. The invention finds particular utility in biomedical applications such as organ-on-a-chip platforms, digital microfluidics, targeted drug delivery systems, and cell culture environments where precise control and monitoring of fluid dynamics are critical to experimental reproducibility and biological fidelity. The sensor operates passively, leveraging the intrinsic mechanical response of a thin, rigid yet compliant membrane to fluid-induced forces, and translates this deformation into an electrical signal via microwave resonance modulation. This approach overcomes longstanding limitations of existing flow sensing technologies by combining high sensitivity, rapid response, biocompatibility, and compatibility with both liquid and gaseous media within a single, monolithic device architecture.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION (PRIOR ART)

The accurate measurement of fluid flow rates within microfluidic channels is a fundamental requirement for advancing precision biomedical technologies. Fluid-structure interactions in confined geometries give rise to complex hydrodynamic behaviors that influence cellular behavior, chemical reaction kinetics, and particle sorting efficiency. In microfluidic applications, the delivery of nutrients, drugs, or signaling molecules must be tightly controlled to maintain physiological relevance, particularly in systems modeling human tissues or organs. Conventional flow control systems rely on syringe pumps or peristaltic pumps, which introduce significant temporal delays, pressure fluctuations, and hysteresis due to their mechanical nature and the compliance of tubing. These limitations render them unsuitable for dynamic, high-resolution applications requiring sub-second temporal fidelity. While commercial flow sensors are available, they are typically designed for macroscopic tubing and cannot be miniaturized or integrated directly onto microfluidic chips, thereby preventing localized, channel-specific flow measurement. Thermal flow sensors, though widely adopted, depend on the thermal properties of the fluid medium and suffer from reduced accuracy when fluid composition varies—such as in mixtures containing cells, proteins, or polymers—due to unaccounted heat transfer pathways. Coriolis-based flow meters offer broader dynamic ranges but require oscillating channels, increasing fabrication complexity and introducing unwanted heating effects that may compromise biological samples. Capacitive sensors have been employed to detect membrane deflection induced by fluid drag, but these systems often require perforated structures that obstruct flow or suffer from low sensitivity and slow recovery times. Optical methods, while precise, necessitate bulky external equipment and are incompatible with opaque or scattering media. Recently, elastomeric polymer-based sensors using polydimethylsiloxane have demonstrated microwave-based flow detection, but their slow mechanical relaxation—on the order of minutes—renders them unsuitable for rapid or repeated measurements. These limitations underscore a critical gap in the field: the absence of a robust, integrable, high-sensitivity, and fast-recovery flow sensor capable of operating across diverse fluid types and flow regimes within a microfluidic chip.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a nanomembrane-based flow sensor that integrates a thin, crystalline silicon nitride membrane directly into a microfluidic channel and couples it to a coplanar waveguide microwave resonator for real-time, label-free flow rate detection. The sensor operates by detecting minute changes in the capacitance of the microwave resonator caused by fluid-induced deformation of the membrane, which alters the gap between the signal and ground electrodes of the resonator. This change in capacitance results in a measurable shift in the resonance frequency and phase of the microwave signal, providing a direct, quantitative readout of flow rate. The invention further reveals a secondary, dynamic sensing mechanism wherein steady fluid flow induces periodic pulsations of the membrane, the frequency and amplitude of which are strongly correlated with the flow rate. The use of a 220 nm thick silicon nitride membrane, as opposed to elastomeric materials, enables rapid mechanical recovery, resistance to fatigue, and compatibility with both aqueous and gaseous fluids. The microwave resonator is fabricated using standard microfabrication techniques, ensuring scalability and compatibility with existing lab-on-a-chip platforms. The invention achieves sensitivity below 0.5 µL/min for liquids and demonstrates linear response over a wide dynamic range, extending into the milliliter-per-minute regime for gas flow. The dual-mode sensing capability—quasi-static deformation and dynamic pulsation—provides redundant, complementary measurement pathways, enhancing reliability and enabling operation across a broad spectrum of flow conditions. The invention is further distinguished by its ability to function without external optical or mechanical actuators, relying solely on passive fluid-membrane interaction and microwave transduction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The flow rate sensor comprises a microfluidic channel formed above a nanoscale silicon nitride membrane, which is electrically integrated with a coplanar waveguide microwave resonator fabricated directly on the membrane surface. The membrane, approximately 220 nm in thickness and ranging from 300 µm to 1.5 mm in lateral dimensions, is suspended over an etched cavity in a silicon substrate, leaving a free-standing, rigid yet flexible diaphragm. The microwave resonator consists of gold signal and ground electrodes patterned in a coplanar geometry, with a narrow gap of 20 µm positioned directly beneath the membrane, creating a localized sensing region. When fluid flows through the channel, hydrodynamic forces cause the membrane to deflect, reducing the distance between the signal and ground electrodes and thereby increasing the local capacitance. This capacitance change shifts the resonance frequency of the microwave structure, which is detected using a phase-locked loop circuit that tracks deviations from a reference phase condition. The sensor exhibits two distinct sensing mechanisms. The first is a quasi-static response, wherein the magnitude of the frequency shift upon reaching a steady flow rate is proportional to the flow rate itself, enabling absolute quantification. The second mechanism arises from the nonlinear fluid-membrane interaction, which under constant flow induces periodic oscillations of the membrane, characterized by distinct pulsation cycles. The period of these pulsations decreases monotonically with increasing flow rate, allowing frequency-based flow determination. The amplitude of the pulsation also correlates with flow rate, providing a third independent sensing parameter. The silicon nitride membrane’s high elastic modulus, low damping, and chemical inertness enable rapid recovery—within seconds—after flow cessation, unlike elastomeric alternatives that require minutes to relax. Fabrication begins with a silicon wafer comprising a silicon dioxide layer and a 220 nm silicon nitride top layer. Backside etching using inductively coupled plasma and potassium hydroxide wet etching creates the membrane cavity. Gold electrodes are deposited and patterned via photolithography, followed by bonding of a polydimethylsiloxane microchannel aligned over the membrane. Experimental validation demonstrates that the sensor responds to step changes in flow with a time resolution of seconds, achieves a baseline sensitivity of 0.5 µL/min, and maintains linearity across flow rates from 0.1 µL/min to 120 µL/min for liquids and up to 4 bar for gases. Optical microscopy confirms cyclic membrane deformation synchronized with microwave phase modulation. Experiments with varying membrane sizes reveal that smaller membranes exhibit higher sensitivity at low flow rates, while larger membranes extend the measurable range to higher flows. The phase response of the resonator, rather than frequency alone, enables continuous monitoring during the recovery phase, permitting uninterrupted operation. The sensor’s response to abrupt flow changes is reproducible and reversible, with no measurable hysteresis or drift after multiple cycles. In air flow experiments, the frequency shift remains linear with pressure over two orders of magnitude, confirming the sensor’s versatility. The crystalline nature of silicon nitride ensures long-term mechanical stability and resistance to degradation under repeated actuation. The sensor’s design eliminates the need for calibration against fluid composition, as the mechanical transduction is independent of thermal or optical properties. The system’s performance is validated through simultaneous comparison with commercial thermal flow sensors, demonstrating agreement within measurement uncertainty. The invention thus provides a unified, robust, and highly sensitive platform for on-chip flow sensing across diverse biomedical and diagnostic applications.
