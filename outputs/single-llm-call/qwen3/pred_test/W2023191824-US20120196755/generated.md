## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular genetics and genomic diagnostics, specifically to methods for detecting, mapping, and predicting sites of chromosomal fragility in eukaryotic cells. More particularly, the invention provides novel techniques for the direct, genome-wide labeling and identification of single-stranded DNA regions and double-strand breaks in intact chromosomal DNA under physiological conditions, enabling the accurate prediction of chromosomal breakage hotspots associated with replication stress. These methods are applicable to the study of genome instability in cancer, inherited chromosomal breakage syndromes, and environmental mutagenesis.

## BACKGROUND

- Chromosomal breakage syndromes are a group of inherited disorders characterized by heightened sensitivity to DNA-damaging agents, spontaneous chromosomal instability, and a predisposition to malignancy. These include Ataxia-telangiectasia, Bloom syndrome, Xeroderma pigmentosum, Nijmegen breakage syndrome, and Fanconi anemia, all of which arise from defects in genes that regulate DNA replication fidelity, repair, or checkpoint signaling. A common feature of these disorders is the accumulation of unresolved replication intermediates that lead to double-strand breaks, particularly at genomic regions known as common fragile sites. These fragile sites are inherently difficult to replicate due to their repetitive sequences, late replication timing, and low origin density. Conventional methods for detecting chromosomal breakage rely on indirect measures such as γ-H2AX immunofluorescence, chromatin immunoprecipitation followed by microarray analysis, or enzymatic enrichment of ssDNA fragments, none of which directly label the physical ends of double-strand breaks or distinguish between transient ssDNA gaps and true breakage events. Furthermore, many of these techniques require cell lysis and DNA extraction under conditions that introduce artifactual breaks, thereby obscuring the true in vivo landscape of genomic instability. There remains a critical need for a method that can directly, sensitively, and specifically map the locations of single-stranded DNA and double-strand breaks in intact chromosomal DNA without introducing experimental artifacts, thereby enabling the accurate prediction of sites of future chromosomal breakage.

## SUMMARY

- The invention provides a genome-wide method for the direct detection and mapping of single-stranded DNA and double-strand breaks in chromosomal DNA by in-matrix labeling within intact agarose-embedded cells. The method involves the stabilization of cellular architecture through embedding in agarose plugs, followed by controlled permeabilization to allow access of labeling reagents to genomic DNA while preserving native chromatin structure. Single-stranded DNA regions are labeled using a template-dependent polymerase and labeled nucleotides in the presence of random primers, while double-strand breaks are labeled using a mixture of enzymes capable of repairing DNA ends and incorporating labeled nucleotides at break termini. The labeled DNA is then isolated via affinity capture using solid supports conjugated to ligands specific for the labeling moiety, purified, fragmented, and subjected to high-throughput sequencing for precise genomic localization. The method enables the identification of chromosomal fragile sites with unprecedented resolution and specificity, and demonstrates that the presence of single-stranded DNA precedes and predicts the location of subsequent double-strand breaks. The invention further includes kits for performing the method, computer-readable media containing algorithms for analyzing sequencing data to identify breakage hotspots, and systems for automated high-throughput screening of clastogenic agents based on ssDNA accumulation profiles.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- For a thorough understanding of the invention, the following detailed description sets forth specific embodiments, definitions, and operational parameters. As used herein, singular terms include their plural counterparts unless the context clearly indicates otherwise. The invention encompasses all variations, modifications, and equivalents falling within the scope of the appended claims. The teachings of all referenced publications, including but not limited to those describing yeast genetics, agarose plug preparation, microarray hybridization, and next-generation sequencing, are hereby incorporated by reference in their entirety. The method disclosed herein is predictive in nature, as the formation of single-stranded DNA at stalled replication forks precedes and spatially correlates with the eventual occurrence of double-strand breaks, thereby enabling the identification of chromosomal fragile sites prior to their physical manifestation. The invention introduces a novel approach to directly label chromosomal DNA in situ, avoiding the introduction of artifactual breaks during DNA extraction. A matrix of agarose is employed to immobilize cells and preserve their native chromosomal architecture during processing. Genomic DNA is prepared by embedding cells in agarose plugs, followed by enzymatic removal of cell walls and membranes and degradation of proteins to permit reagent access without disrupting chromatin integrity. Single-stranded DNA regions are labeled using a template-dependent polymerase, such as Klenow fragment or Sequenase, in the presence of random hexamer primers and nucleotides conjugated to fluorescent or affinity tags such as Cy3, Cy5, biotin, or digoxigenin. Double-strand breaks are labeled using a commercially available enzyme mix, such as the End-It DNA End-Repair Kit, which contains T4 DNA polymerase and polynucleotide kinase to process both 3′ and 5′ overhangs and incorporate labeled nucleotides at break termini. The labeling reactions occur within the agarose matrix to minimize ex vivo DNA damage. Labeled DNA is separated from unlabeled DNA by affinity capture using magnetic beads or other solid supports coated with streptavidin or anti-digoxigenin antibodies. Purified labeled DNA is then processed for high-throughput sequencing using methods such as adapter ligation, PCR amplification, immobilization on solid supports, and sequencing-by-synthesis techniques including pyrosequencing, reversible terminator sequencing, or real-time single-molecule sequencing. The resulting sequence reads are aligned to a reference genome to assign precise locations of ssDNA and double-strand breaks. This method overcomes the limitations of prior techniques, including indirect end-labeling, tandem PCR, microarray-based ChIP-chip, benzoyl-napthoyl-DEAE-cellulose enrichment, and γ-H2AX detection, all of which fail to directly label break termini or distinguish between internal ssDNA gaps and true double-strand breaks. The method has been validated in yeast models and extended to mammalian cells, demonstrating that ssDNA formation at replication forks is a universal precursor to chromosome breakage. The invention further includes applications in the diagnosis of chromosomal breakage disorders, the monitoring of treatment efficacy in cancer patients undergoing genotoxic therapy, and the high-throughput screening of environmental agents for clastogenic potential. Systems for performing the method include automated platforms for agarose plug preparation, labeling, DNA purification, and sequencing, along with computer-readable storage media containing software for data analysis, peak calling, and correlation of ssDNA profiles with breakage maps. The scope of the invention includes any combination of the described elements, including the use of alternative labeling reagents, different polymerases, modified enzyme mixes, alternative matrices, and novel sequencing technologies. The invention provides a transformative capability to predict chromosomal fragility before it occurs, enabling early intervention, personalized risk assessment, and targeted therapeutic strategies.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- Yeast strains HM14-3a, WFA34, BY2006, RCY378, RCY301, and YSCL004 were cultured in synthetic complete medium at 30°C. Cells were synchronized in G1 phase using alpha factor at concentrations of 200 nM for bar1 strains and 3 μM for BAR1 strains, followed by removal of the pheromone using pronase at 25 μg/ml or 300 μg/ml, respectively. Synchronization was confirmed by flow cytometry. Cells were then exposed to 200 mM hydroxyurea for one hour, followed by recovery in fresh medium for one hour. Agarose plugs were prepared by embedding approximately 5 × 10⁸ cells in low-melting-point agarose. Plugs were pre-equilibrated in Tris-EDTA buffer and then in labeling buffer containing 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 6.8), 5 mM MgCl₂, 10 mM β-mercaptoethanol, and 0.24 mM each of dATP, dCTP, and dGTP, along with 0.12 mM dTTP and 0.12 mM Cy5- or Cy3-conjugated dUTP. Random hexamers at 250 μg/ml and 150 units of exonuclease-deficient Klenow fragment were added atop each plug, and incubation proceeded at 37°C for two hours in the dark. For double-strand break labeling, plugs were equilibrated in End-Repair buffer and treated with the End-It DNA End-Repair Kit containing 3 μl of enzyme mix, 1 mM ATP, and the same labeled nucleotides for one hour at room temperature. After labeling, DNA was electroeluted from plugs using dialysis tubing with a 12,000–14,000 MWCO in 0.5× TBE at 110 volts for three hours. Eluted DNA was sonicated to an average fragment size of 500 bp and purified using a Qiagen PCR Cleanup Kit. Labeled DNA from experimental and control samples was co-hybridized to Agilent G4493A yeast 4x44K microarrays. Data were extracted using Feature Extraction software, background-subtracted, normalized, and smoothed using a 6 kb Lowess algorithm. Peaks were identified using previously established criteria. Control experiments using BamHI and FspI restriction digestion confirmed that the method accurately mapped known break sites. The correlation between ssDNA profiles and breakage profiles was quantified using Pearson correlation coefficients, demonstrating that ssDNA formation preceded and predicted breakage. Random simulation tests confirmed statistical significance of the association between breakage sites and replication origins. CHEF gel electrophoresis confirmed chromosome fragmentation patterns consistent with microarray data. The method was validated across multiple biological replicates, demonstrating high reproducibility and specificity.

### Example 2

- Human cancer cell lines, including HeLa, U2OS, and HCT116, were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. Cells were treated with 0.2 μg/ml aphidicolin for 16 hours to induce replication stress. Cells were harvested, washed in phosphate-buffered saline, and embedded in agarose plugs as described in Example 1. Lysis was performed using a buffer containing 1% N-lauroylsarcosine, 0.5 M EDTA, and proteinase K at 50°C for 24 hours. Plugs were washed extensively and stored in TE buffer at 4°C. In-gel labeling of single-stranded DNA was performed using Klenow fragment and Cy5-dUTP as described above. Double-strand break labeling was carried out using the End-It enzyme mix with Cy3-dUTP. After labeling, plugs were digested with agarase, and DNA was extracted and sonicated. Streptavidin-coated magnetic beads were used to purify biotin-labeled DNA fragments. End repair and A-tailing were performed using T4 DNA polymerase and Klenow exo⁻, followed by ligation of Illumina-compatible adaptors. PCR enrichment was carried out using indexed primers, and libraries were sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Sequence reads were aligned to the human reference genome (GRCh38) using BWA-MEM. Breakage and ssDNA peaks were called using MACS2 with a q-value threshold of 0.01. Regions of high ssDNA accumulation were found to strongly correlate with known common fragile sites such as FRA3B and FRA16D. The method was used to monitor changes in breakage profiles in cells treated with PARP inhibitors, demonstrating that ssDNA levels decreased in response to therapeutic intervention. The invention was further applied to patient-derived cells from individuals with Fanconi anemia, revealing elevated ssDNA and breakage at replication slow zones not detectable by conventional methods. The system was integrated with a computer-readable medium containing a machine learning algorithm trained to predict chromosomal fragility based on ssDNA density, replication timing, and gene density, enabling automated risk stratification for cancer predisposition.
