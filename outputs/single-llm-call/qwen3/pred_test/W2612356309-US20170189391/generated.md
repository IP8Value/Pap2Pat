# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

- relate to method

The present invention relates to a method for the selective inhibition of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) cell proliferation through the targeted modulation of protein kinase C (PKC) signaling pathways. Specifically, the invention encompasses a therapeutic strategy that exploits the differential expression and functional dependency of specific PKC isozymes in TNBC cells compared to non-TNBC breast cancer subtypes. The method involves the administration of a PKC inhibitor, particularly chelerythrine, to patients diagnosed with TNBC to induce selective apoptotic cell death and suppress tumor growth without significant cytotoxic effects on hormone receptor-positive or HER2-positive breast cancer cells. This approach represents a novel and precise intervention tailored to the molecular profile of TNBC, a subtype of breast cancer historically resistant to endocrine and targeted therapies. The method further includes the identification and validation of PKC isozymes, including PKN2 and PRKCA, as critical mediators of TNBC cell survival and proliferation, thereby establishing a mechanistic foundation for the therapeutic application of PKC inhibition. The invention also extends to diagnostic and prognostic applications involving the detection of elevated PKC isozyme expression levels in tumor tissue or circulating biomarkers as indicators of TNBC susceptibility to PKC-targeted therapy.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

- introduce breast cancer

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women globally, accounting for a substantial proportion of cancer-related morbidity and mortality. Despite advances in early detection and multimodal treatment strategies, a significant subset of breast cancer patients continues to experience poor clinical outcomes due to the aggressive nature and therapeutic resistance of certain tumor subtypes. The heterogeneity of breast cancer is well characterized by the expression status of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2, which collectively define clinically distinct subgroups with divergent prognoses and treatment responses.

- classify breast cancer

Among these subgroups, triple-negative breast cancer is defined by the absence of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 protein overexpression. This molecular phenotype, which constitutes approximately 10 to 15 percent of all breast cancer cases, is associated with a more aggressive clinical course, higher rates of distant metastasis, particularly to the lungs and central nervous system, and a significantly shorter overall survival compared to other subtypes. Due to the lack of established molecular targets, systemic treatment for triple-negative breast cancer has remained largely confined to conventional cytotoxic chemotherapy, which often yields transient responses followed by rapid disease recurrence.

- limitations of treatments

Current therapeutic regimens for triple-negative breast cancer are limited by their nonselective cytotoxicity, high rates of acquired resistance, and inability to eradicate cancer stem cell populations responsible for tumor relapse and metastasis. Although numerous investigational agents targeting alternative signaling pathways—such as PARP, EGFR, PI3K, and mTOR—have been evaluated in clinical trials, none have yet achieved regulatory approval as standard-of-care therapies for this subtype. The persistent absence of targeted interventions underscores an urgent and unmet medical need for novel therapeutic strategies that exploit the unique molecular vulnerabilities of triple-negative breast cancer. Furthermore, the lack of predictive biomarkers to guide patient selection for existing or emerging therapies contributes to suboptimal treatment efficacy and unnecessary exposure to toxic regimens in non-responsive patients.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

- introduce method

The present invention introduces a novel method for the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer by selectively inhibiting protein kinase C activity through the administration of a pharmacological agent capable of suppressing multiple PKC isozymes, thereby inducing apoptosis and suppressing proliferation specifically in triple-negative breast cancer cells while sparing non-TNBC cells. This method is grounded in the discovery that certain PKC isoforms, including PKN2 and PRKCA, are overexpressed and functionally essential in triple-negative breast cancer cell lines and patient-derived tumor samples, rendering them uniquely susceptible to pan-PKC inhibition. The method enables the selective targeting of triple-negative breast cancer based on the molecular signature of PKC isozyme overexpression, providing a precision medicine approach that addresses the fundamental therapeutic gap in this aggressive cancer subtype.

- embodiment of method

An embodiment of the method involves the administration of chelerythrine, a naturally derived benzophenanthridine alkaloid with high specificity for the catalytic domain of PKC isozymes, to a patient diagnosed with triple-negative breast cancer. The administration may be performed via intravenous, intraperitoneal, or oral routes, depending on formulation and clinical context, at a therapeutically effective dose sufficient to inhibit PKC-mediated survival signaling without inducing systemic toxicity. In a preferred embodiment, the method further includes the prior determination of elevated mRNA or protein expression levels of PKN2 and/or PRKCA in tumor tissue or circulating tumor cells, thereby identifying patients most likely to respond to PKC inhibition. The method may be administered as a monotherapy or in combination with conventional chemotherapeutic agents such as taxanes or platinum compounds, where synergistic enhancement of tumor cell death is observed without increased toxicity to normal tissues. The method further encompasses the use of chelerythrine or structurally related analogs to suppress colony formation, induce G2/M or sub-G0/G1 cell cycle arrest, and activate caspase-dependent apoptosis in triple-negative breast cancer cells, as confirmed by the detection of cleaved poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase and morphological hallmarks of apoptosis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

- define "comprising"

As used herein, the term “comprising” is intended to be open-ended and inclusive, meaning that the described method, composition, or system may include the recited elements or steps, but is not limited to those explicitly listed and may encompass additional components, modifications, or procedures not specifically enumerated. This term is used throughout the specification to describe the scope of the invention broadly and without exclusion of other elements that may contribute to the intended function or therapeutic outcome.

- introduce protein kinase C (PKC) as a target agent for TNBC treatment

Protein kinase C represents a family of serine/threonine kinases that regulate diverse cellular processes including proliferation, survival, migration, and apoptosis. In the context of breast cancer, PKC signaling has been implicated in the development of endocrine resistance, tumor aggressiveness, and stem cell maintenance. The present invention identifies PKC as a central signaling node whose activity is indispensable for the survival of triple-negative breast cancer cells, thereby establishing it as a viable and specific therapeutic target for this molecularly distinct subtype.

- describe the role of PKC in various disease processes

PKC isozymes participate in the pathogenesis of numerous human diseases, including cardiovascular disorders, neurodegenerative conditions, autoimmune diseases, and metabolic syndromes. Within the oncogenic context, PKC activation promotes tumor progression through the modulation of downstream effectors such as MAPK, AKT, and NF-κB, and by influencing cytoskeletal dynamics and cell adhesion. In breast cancer, PKC activity is frequently elevated in malignant tissues compared to normal mammary epithelium, and its overexpression correlates with poor clinical outcomes.

- discuss the inverse relationship between ER and PKC activity and abundance

An inverse correlation exists between estrogen receptor expression and PKC activity in breast cancer cell lines and clinical specimens. Estrogen receptor-positive tumors typically exhibit low levels of PKC isozymes, whereas estrogen receptor-negative tumors, including triple-negative subtypes, demonstrate marked upregulation of PKC signaling components. This reciprocal relationship suggests that the loss of hormonal regulation in triple-negative breast cancer creates a permissive environment for PKC-driven oncogenic signaling.

- describe the elevation of PKC in malignant versus normal breast tissue

Comparative analyses of malignant and non-malignant breast tissue have consistently demonstrated elevated expression levels of multiple PKC isozymes in tumor specimens. This overexpression is not merely a bystander phenomenon but is functionally linked to enhanced cell survival, resistance to anoikis, and increased metastatic potential, establishing PKC as a driver of malignancy rather than a passive marker.

- associate PRKCA with antiestrogen resistance and tumor aggressiveness

The PRKCA isoform, commonly referred to as PKCα, has been previously associated with resistance to antiestrogen therapies and increased tumor aggressiveness in hormone receptor-positive breast cancers. Its overexpression in triple-negative breast cancer cell lines and patient-derived xenografts further implicates it as a key mediator of disease progression in the absence of hormonal signaling.

- identify PRKCA as a central signaling node and therapeutic target for breast cancer stem cells

PRKCA has been identified as a critical regulator of breast cancer stem cell maintenance, a population responsible for tumor initiation, therapy resistance, and recurrence. Its inhibition leads to a reduction in stem cell markers and sphere-forming capacity, suggesting that targeting PRKCA may address the root cause of therapeutic failure in triple-negative breast cancer.

- imply PKC as a potential specific therapeutic target agent for TNBC

The collective evidence of PKC overexpression, functional dependency, and association with aggressive phenotypes in triple-negative breast cancer strongly implies that PKC inhibition represents a viable and specific therapeutic strategy for this subtype, distinct from approaches applicable to other breast cancer classifications.

- introduce chelerythrine as a specific PKC inhibitor

Chelerythrine is a naturally occurring benzophenanthridine alkaloid derived from the plant Chelidonium majus, known for its potent and selective inhibitory activity against the catalytic domain of PKC isozymes. Unlike many kinase inhibitors that target specific ATP-binding pockets, chelerythrine binds to a conserved region shared among multiple PKC isoforms, enabling broad yet selective suppression of PKC signaling without significant off-target effects on unrelated kinases.

- describe the anti-cancer effect of chelerythrine against TNBC cell lines

Chelerythrine demonstrates a pronounced and selective anti-proliferative effect against triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, including MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and MDA-MB-468, with IC50 values consistently below 5 μM. In contrast, non-TNBC cell lines such as MCF7, ZR-75-1, SK-BR-3, and MDA-MB-453 exhibit minimal growth inhibition even at concentrations exceeding 10 μM, indicating a highly selective therapeutic window.

- outline materials and methods

The efficacy of chelerythrine was evaluated using standardized in vitro and in vivo methodologies, including sulforhodamine B proliferation assays, colony formation assays, flow cytometric analysis of cell cycle and apoptosis, Western blotting for protein expression, quantitative real-time PCR for mRNA quantification, and xenograft tumor models in immunodeficient mice. These methods were employed to rigorously establish the specificity, potency, and mechanistic basis of chelerythrine’s activity.

- list reagents and antibodies used

Reagents included chelerythrine, taxol, trichloroacetic acid, propidium iodide, Hoechst 33258, Triton X-100, sulforhodamine B, and crystal violet, all obtained from commercial suppliers. Antibodies utilized for detection included those targeting cleaved PARP, PRKCA, PKN2, β-actin, and horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies.

- describe cell culture conditions

Breast cancer cell lines were maintained in Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin-glutamine, under controlled conditions of 37°C and 5% CO2. All cell lines were authenticated and tested for mycoplasma contamination prior to experimentation.

- outline in vitro cell proliferation assay (SRB assay)

Cell proliferation was quantified using the sulforhodamine B colorimetric assay, wherein cells were seeded in 96-well plates, treated with serial dilutions of chelerythrine for 72 hours, fixed with trichloroacetic acid, stained with SRB, and absorbance measured at 515 nm to determine growth inhibition and calculate IC50 values.

- describe colony formation assay

For colony formation assays, cells were plated at low density following chelerythrine exposure, allowed to grow for 7 to 10 days in drug-free medium, fixed, stained with crystal violet, and colonies counted manually to assess long-term clonogenic survival.

- outline flow-cytometric analysis of cell cycle

Cell cycle distribution was analyzed by propidium iodide staining of ethanol-fixed cells, followed by flow cytometry to determine the proportion of cells in sub-G0/G1, G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases, with data analyzed using FlowJo software.

- outline flow-cytometric analysis of apoptosis

Apoptosis was assessed using Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide dual staining, with cells analyzed by flow cytometry to quantify early and late apoptotic populations, as defined by membrane phosphatidylserine externalization and membrane integrity loss.

- describe assessment of cell morphological changes

Apoptotic morphology was visualized using Hoechst 33258 nuclear staining under fluorescence microscopy, with characteristic features such as chromatin condensation and nuclear fragmentation documented in treated cells.

- outline retrieval of gene expression data from CCLE database

Gene expression profiles for 12 PKC isozymes across 56 breast cancer cell lines were extracted from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia database, with log2 fold changes calculated between triple-negative and non-triple-negative subgroups to identify differentially expressed isoforms.

- describe quantitative real-time PCR

Total RNA was isolated, reverse transcribed, and amplified using SYBR Green-based quantitative real-time PCR with primers specific for PKC isozymes, with expression normalized to EIF3S5 or TIF as reference genes.

- outline Western blotting

Protein lysates were prepared using RIPA buffer, separated by SDS-PAGE, transferred to nitrocellulose membranes, and probed with primary antibodies against PKC isoforms, followed by horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies and chemiluminescent detection.

- describe dual-drug combination assay

Chelerythrine was combined with taxol at fixed ratios, and cell viability was assessed via SRB assay. Combination indices were calculated using CompuSyn software to determine synergistic, additive, or antagonistic interactions.

- introduce results

Results demonstrated that chelerythrine selectively inhibited proliferation and induced apoptosis in triple-negative breast cancer cell lines, with minimal effects on non-TNBC cells. IC50 values for TNBC lines ranged from 2.6 to 4.2 μM, while non-TNBC lines exhibited IC50 values exceeding 10 μM.

- describe chelerythrine's selective inhibition of proliferation of TNBC cell lines

Chelerythrine induced a dose- and time-dependent suppression of proliferation in all four TNBC cell lines tested, with near-complete inhibition observed at 5 μM after 72 hours. In contrast, non-TNBC cell lines retained robust proliferation even at the highest concentrations tested.

- show IC50 values of chelerythrine on TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines

The mean IC50 values for TNBC cell lines were 2.6 μM (BT-549), 3.0 μM (MDA-MB-231), 3.6 μM (HCC1937), and 4.2 μM (MDA-MB-468), whereas non-TNBC cell lines showed IC50 values greater than 10 μM, with two lines exhibiting no inhibition at all.

- describe time-dependent inhibition of cell growth by chelerythrine

Time-course experiments revealed progressive growth suppression in TNBC cells over 72 hours, with significant reductions observed as early as 24 hours post-treatment, whereas non-TNBC cells showed negligible changes throughout the time course.

- confirm selective anti-proliferative activity of chelerythrine by colony formation assay

Colony formation was reduced by over 90% in TNBC cells after 6 hours of chelerythrine exposure, while non-TNBC cells retained more than 70% of their colony-forming capacity even after 48 hours of treatment.

- describe chelerythrine's differential induction of cell cycle arrest in TNBC cell lines

Chelerythrine induced cell cycle arrest in a cell line-dependent manner, with MDA-MB-468 showing G2/M arrest and HCC1937 exhibiting G0/G1 accumulation, while MDA-MB-231 and BT-549 displayed minimal cell cycle perturbation, indicating that apoptosis, rather than cell cycle arrest, was the primary mechanism of growth inhibition.

- show cell cycle distribution of TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines after chelerythrine treatment

Flow cytometry analysis confirmed a significant increase in the sub-G0/G1 population in TNBC cells, indicative of apoptotic DNA fragmentation, while non-TNBC cells maintained normal cell cycle profiles.

- describe chelerythrine's selective induction of apoptosis in TNBC cell lines

Chelerythrine triggered robust apoptosis in all TNBC cell lines, as evidenced by morphological changes, cleavage of PARP, and Annexin V positivity, whereas non-TNBC cells remained largely unaffected.

- show morphological characteristics of apoptosis in TNBC cells

Fluorescence microscopy revealed chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, and membrane blebbing in TNBC cells treated with chelerythrine, consistent with classical apoptotic morphology, absent in non-TNBC controls.

- detect cleaved nuclear poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (cPARP) in TNBC cell lines

Western blot analysis confirmed the presence of cleaved PARP, a hallmark of caspase-mediated apoptosis, exclusively in TNBC cell lines following chelerythrine treatment, with no detectable signal in non-TNBC lines.

- quantify apoptotic cell fractions by flow cytometry

Quantitative flow cytometry revealed apoptotic fractions of 67.8%, 51.0%, 22.2%, and 35.3% in MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and MDA-MB-468, respectively, compared to less than 5% in non-TNBC lines.

- describe time-dependent induction of apoptosis by chelerythrine

Apoptosis increased progressively over time in MDA-MB-231 cells, with Annexin V-positive cells rising from 12% at 6 hours to over 65% at 24 hours, confirming a time-dependent apoptotic response.

- discuss multiple PKC isozymes mediating the selective inhibitory activity of chelerythrine

The selective activity of chelerythrine is mediated by the co-overexpression of multiple PKC isozymes in TNBC cells, including PRKCA, PKN1, and PKN2, with PKN2 identified as the most consistently elevated isoform across all TNBC lines.

- measure mRNA expression level of PRKCA in breast cancer cell lines

Quantitative PCR revealed elevated PRKCA mRNA in MDA-MB-231 and BT-549, but not in HCC1937 or MDA-MB-468, indicating heterogeneity in PRKCA dependency among TNBC subtypes.

- confirm protein expression level of PRKCA by western blotting

Western blot analysis corroborated elevated PRKCA protein expression in MDA-MB-231 and BT-549, supporting its role in these specific lines.

- identify PKN1 and PKN2 as PKC subtypes overexpressed in TNBC cell lines

Analysis of CCLE data and validation by qPCR and Western blotting identified PKN2 as the most uniformly overexpressed PKC isoform across all four TNBC cell lines, with PKN1 also elevated in a subset.

- analyze mRNA expression levels of PKN1 and PKN2 in breast cancer cell lines

PKN2 mRNA expression was significantly higher in TNBC cell lines compared to non-TNBC lines (p < 0.01), while PKN1 showed moderate elevation in some lines.

- identify PRKCD, PRKCH, and PRKCZ as PKC isozymes less expressed in TNBC cells

PRKCD, PRKCH, and PRKCZ were found to be expressed at lower levels in TNBC cells relative to non-TNBC cells, suggesting that their downregulation may contribute to the distinct signaling phenotype of this subtype.

- describe chelerythrine's enhancement of chemotherapy activity

Combination of chelerythrine with taxol resulted in synergistic or additive inhibition of TNBC cell proliferation, with combination indices ranging from 0.75 to 1.14, indicating enhanced efficacy without increased cytotoxicity.

- discuss the potential of chelerythrine as a promising regimen for TNBC tumors

The selective cytotoxicity, mechanistic clarity, and synergistic potential of chelerythrine establish it as a promising therapeutic candidate for triple-negative breast cancer, offering a novel strategy to overcome the limitations of current chemotherapy regimens and address the unmet clinical need for targeted interventions in this aggressive disease.
