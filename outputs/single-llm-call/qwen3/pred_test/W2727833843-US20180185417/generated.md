# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- motivate cell therapies  
Cell therapies have emerged as a transformative modality in regenerative medicine, offering the potential to restore tissue function, modulate immune responses, and promote vascular regeneration through the targeted delivery of autologous or allogeneic cellular components. Unlike traditional pharmacological interventions, these therapies leverage the intrinsic biological activities of living cells—such as secretion of trophic factors, direct integration into damaged tissues, and orchestration of endogenous repair mechanisms—to achieve durable therapeutic outcomes. The clinical promise of cell-based treatments is particularly evident in conditions characterized by impaired healing, including non-union bone fractures, ischemic cardiovascular disease, and chronic wounds. However, despite their therapeutic potential, the translation of cell therapies into routine clinical practice has been hindered by logistical, economic, and regulatory barriers that stem from the complexity of cell manufacturing, storage, and quality control. The need for scalable, reproducible, and minimally manipulative methods to isolate and deliver therapeutic cell populations remains unmet.

- describe regulatory constraints  
Regulatory agencies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) impose stringent requirements on cell-based products to ensure safety, purity, potency, and consistency. Products classified as advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs) are subject to comprehensive Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) compliance, including validated cell expansion protocols, sterility testing, identity verification, and long-term stability studies. These requirements significantly increase development timelines and costs, often rendering cell therapies economically unfeasible for widespread clinical adoption. Of particular concern is the classification of cell products that retain non-physiological components—such as antibody-conjugated magnetic beads—following isolation. The persistence of such exogenous materials on the surface of transplanted cells may be interpreted as more than minimal manipulation, thereby triggering ATMP classification and necessitating pre-market approval as a drug rather than a procedure. This regulatory ambiguity has discouraged the adoption of otherwise effective cell isolation techniques in intraoperative settings, where rapid, point-of-care processing is essential.

- limitations of exogenous cell therapies  
Exogenous cell therapies, which involve the procurement, expansion, and cryopreservation of cells outside the body, are burdened by multiple limitations. The requirement for multi-day in vitro culture introduces risks of contamination, phenotypic drift, and loss of functional potency. Additionally, the logistical complexity of coordinating cell harvest, transport, expansion, and reinfusion across multiple clinical visits imposes substantial burdens on patients and healthcare systems. The use of cytokines to mobilize cells from bone marrow or peripheral tissues, while avoiding ex vivo expansion, is associated with systemic side effects including bone pain, fever, and splenomegaly, and often fails to yield sufficient numbers of therapeutically relevant cells. Furthermore, cryopreservation and thawing procedures can compromise cell viability and functional capacity, diminishing therapeutic efficacy. These challenges underscore the need for alternative strategies that preserve the native state of cells while enabling their rapid, sterile, and minimally manipulative isolation at the point of care.

- introduce intraoperative cell therapies  
Intraoperative cell therapies represent a paradigm shift in the delivery of cell-based treatments by integrating cell isolation and administration within a single surgical procedure. This approach eliminates the need for ex vivo expansion, cryopreservation, and prolonged storage, thereby reducing costs, minimizing regulatory scrutiny, and preserving the biological integrity of the harvested cells. By leveraging readily accessible biological fluids such as whole blood, bone marrow aspirate, or surgical suction waste, intraoperative systems enable the rapid enrichment of therapeutically relevant cell populations directly at the surgical site. This model is particularly suited for orthopedic, vascular, and reconstructive procedures, where immediate application of regenerative cells can enhance tissue repair and reduce complications. The ability to perform cell isolation and delivery within a single operative session not only improves patient outcomes but also aligns with the principles of value-based care by reducing hospital stays and the need for secondary interventions.

- limitations of current cell isolation approaches  
Current methods for cell isolation, particularly those relying on antibody-conjugated magnetic beads, suffer from critical limitations that impede their use in intraoperative contexts. The most widely adopted platforms, such as magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS), require the attachment of antibodies or antibody-coated beads to target cells, which remain bound following isolation. The persistence of these foreign materials on the cell surface raises regulatory concerns regarding minimal manipulation and triggers ATMP classification. Moreover, the release of captured cells typically necessitates the use of enzymatic digestion, competitive elution buffers, or high concentrations of dextran sulfate—all of which introduce chemical contaminants that may alter cell function or provoke immune responses. Additionally, the small size of conventional magnetic beads (typically 50–100 nm) renders them difficult to fully remove from the final cell product, posing potential risks of phagocytosis, embolization, or unintended immunomodulation. These limitations have prevented the widespread clinical implementation of even highly effective cell enrichment strategies in real-time surgical environments.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce invention  
The invention disclosed herein is a novel, minimally manipulative system and method for the rapid, reversible, and bead-free enrichment of specific cell populations from complex biological samples, enabling intraoperative cell therapy without triggering regulatory classification as an advanced therapy medicinal product. The invention leverages the unique biophysical properties of nucleic acid aptamers to achieve high-specificity binding to cell surface markers, followed by controlled release of captured cells through mechanical force alone, without the need for chemical, enzymatic, or thermal intervention. This approach yields a cell population that is free of beads, aptamers, and other exogenous ligands, thereby satisfying regulatory criteria for minimal manipulation and enabling immediate clinical application.

- describe device for enriching cells  
The invention comprises a disposable, sterile, and pre-assembled device configured for the enrichment of cells expressing a specific cell surface marker from a mixed population. The device includes a column housing a solid support composed of porous beads, wherein the beads are coated with an aptamer capable of selectively binding the target cell surface marker. The column is integrated into a sealed, flow-through system that permits the passage of a cell suspension while retaining aptamer-bound cells. The device is designed for single-use, point-of-care operation and is compatible with standard surgical workflows, including use with syringes, infusion pumps, and centrifuges commonly found in operating rooms.

- describe method for enriching cells  
The method involves contacting a mixed cell population with the aptamer-coated beads within the device under controlled flow conditions, allowing target cells to bind via specific aptamer–marker interactions. Non-bound cells are washed away using a physiologically compatible buffer. The captured cell population is then released from the beads by applying a controlled mechanical force, such as repeated aspiration and re-suspension of the bead suspension using a syringe and needle, which disrupts the aptamer–cell interaction without the use of chemical agents. The released cells are collected by centrifugation, yielding a purified, bead-free, and aptamer-free population suitable for immediate therapeutic administration.

- specify cell surface marker  
The cell surface marker targeted by the invention is CD31, also known as Platelet Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule 1 (PECAM-1), a transmembrane glycoprotein expressed on endothelial cells, monocytes, and subsets of lymphocytes. CD31+ cells are known to possess potent angiogenic, osteogenic, and immunomodulatory properties, making them ideal candidates for the treatment of bone fractures, ischemic tissues, and chronic wounds.

- describe aptamer-coupled beads  
The invention employs neutravidin agarose beads, each with a diameter between 45 and 165 micrometers, that are covalently or non-covalently coupled to a biotinylated aptamer specific for CD31. The large bead size ensures that they are retained within the device’s filtration system while permitting passage of cells, thereby eliminating the risk of bead contamination in the final cell product. The aptamer is immobilized on the bead surface at a concentration sufficient to enable high-efficiency capture without compromising cell viability upon release.

- describe release of cells  
Cells are released from the aptamer-coated beads through the application of mechanical shear forces generated by repeated aspiration and re-suspension of the bead-cell suspension using a syringe and needle. This physical disruption is sufficient to dissociate the aptamer–CD31 interaction due to the relatively low binding affinity of the aptamer compared to high-affinity ligand–receptor pairs such as biotin–streptavidin. No chemical, enzymatic, or temperature-based triggers are required, ensuring the final cell product remains uncontaminated and functionally intact.

- describe production of enriched cell population  
Following mechanical release, the cell suspension is centrifuged to pellet the liberated cells, which are then resuspended in a physiologic buffer for immediate administration. The resulting cell population is enriched for CD31+ cells, with purity exceeding 85% and viability maintained above 95%. The population is free of residual beads, aptamers, or labeling agents, and retains the native functional properties of the original cells, including the capacity to secrete pro-angiogenic and pro-osteogenic factors.

- specify CD31+ cells  
The enriched cell population consists predominantly of CD31+ cells derived from peripheral blood mononuclear cells, whole blood, or surgical suction waste. These cells include monocytes, naïve T lymphocytes, and endothelial progenitor cells, all of which contribute to tissue regeneration through paracrine signaling and direct cellular interactions. The absence of bead or aptamer contamination ensures that these cells retain their full biological potential and are suitable for direct intraoperative administration.

- describe therapeutic use of CD31+ cells  
The CD31+ cell population enriched by the invention is administered directly to sites of tissue injury, including bone fractures, ischemic limbs, or surgical wounds, to promote vascularization, osteogenesis, and immunomodulation. The cells secrete cytokines such as VEGF, FGF-2, and IL-8, which stimulate endothelial cell migration and differentiation, enhance osteoblast mineralization, and suppress pro-inflammatory responses. Administration of this enriched population has been shown to accelerate bone healing, reduce non-union rates, and improve perfusion in ischemic tissues.

- describe device components  
The device comprises a sterile, disposable column housing a filter with a pore size of 20 micrometers at its base, a cylindrical chamber filled with aptamer-coated beads, and a Luer-lock adapter for connection to syringes or infusion systems. The column is housed within a centrifuge tube for ease of handling and cell recovery. The filter prevents bead passage while allowing cells to flow through during washing and release steps. All components are manufactured from biocompatible, non-pyrogenic materials suitable for surgical use.

- describe aptamer coupling  
The aptamer is coupled to the beads via biotin–neutravidin interactions, which provide stable, high-density immobilization without covalent modification of the aptamer’s binding domain. Alternatively, covalent coupling via amine-reactive chemistry may be employed. Coupling is performed prior to device assembly under controlled conditions to ensure uniform distribution and maximal binding capacity.

- describe bead size  
The beads have a diameter ranging from 45 to 165 micrometers, a size selected to prevent passage through the 20-micrometer filter while allowing sufficient interstitial space for cell binding and mechanical release. This dimensional disparity ensures that beads are efficiently retained within the device and do not contaminate the final cell product.

- describe aptamer concentration  
The aptamer is loaded onto the beads at a concentration of 5 to 10 micrograms per milliliter of bead suspension, a range optimized to balance high capture efficiency with low binding avidity, enabling complete cell release upon mechanical agitation without compromising viability.

- describe device configuration  
The device is configured as a vertical, gravity-assisted flow-through column, compatible with standard syringes and infusion pumps. It is designed for single-use, pre-sterilized packaging, and requires no specialized equipment for operation. The system is closed to prevent contamination and may be used in any clinical setting with access to basic centrifugation and fluid handling tools.

- describe method steps  
The method comprises: (1) loading a mixed cell population into the device at a controlled flow rate; (2) incubating the sample for 5 to 15 minutes to allow binding of target cells; (3) washing the column with physiologic buffer to remove unbound cells; (4) applying mechanical force by aspirating and re-suspending the bead suspension with a syringe and needle; (5) collecting the released cells via centrifugation; and (6) resuspending the enriched cell population for administration.

- describe washing step  
The washing step involves the passage of 3 to 5 milliliters of phosphate-buffered saline through the column at a flow rate of 300 microliters per minute to remove non-specifically bound cells and debris. This step is performed under gravity or low-pressure flow to preserve cell–aptamer interactions while eliminating contaminating populations.

- describe mechanical force  
The mechanical force is applied by repeatedly aspirating and expelling the bead-cell suspension through an 18-gauge needle using a 1-milliliter syringe, typically for 10 to 20 cycles. This action generates sufficient shear stress to disrupt the aptamer–CD31 bond without damaging cell membranes or altering cell phenotype.

- describe cell recovery  
Cell recovery is achieved by centrifuging the released suspension at 300 × g for 3 minutes, followed by aspiration of the supernatant and resuspension of the pellet in a physiologic buffer. The recovered cells are >95% viable and contain no detectable beads or aptamers.

- describe cell population enrichment  
The method enriches CD31+ cells from a starting population of peripheral blood mononuclear cells or whole blood by a factor of 1.5 to 2.5, increasing the proportion of CD31+ cells from approximately 60% to over 85%. The enriched population retains the functional heterogeneity of the original cells, including monocytes, naïve T cells, and endothelial progenitors.

- describe administration of enriched cells  
The enriched cell population is administered directly to the site of injury via injection, topical application, or incorporation into a biodegradable scaffold. Administration occurs within 30 minutes of isolation, ensuring maximal cell viability and therapeutic efficacy.

- describe kit for cell enrichment  
The invention further includes a kit comprising a pre-assembled, sterile device, a vial of aptamer-coated beads, a vial of wash buffer, a syringe with needle, and instructions for use. The kit is designed for single-use, room-temperature storage, and rapid deployment in surgical settings without the need for refrigeration or specialized training.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce cell enrichment device  
The cell enrichment device is a closed, single-use system engineered for the rapid, label-free, and bead-free isolation of CD31+ cells from whole blood or peripheral blood mononuclear cell suspensions. The device integrates a filtration unit, a bead retention chamber, and a fluidic interface, all constructed from biocompatible, non-adhesive materials to prevent cell loss or activation. The system is designed to operate under ambient conditions, requiring no external power source, magnetic field, or temperature control, making it ideal for intraoperative use.

- describe aptamer-based cell enrichment  
Aptamer-based enrichment exploits the high specificity and reversible binding characteristics of nucleic acid ligands to selectively capture target cells without the need for protein-based antibodies. Unlike antibodies, aptamers can be chemically synthesized with precise modifications, are stable at room temperature, and do not elicit immune responses. The aptamer used in this invention binds CD31 with sufficient affinity to enable capture under physiological flow conditions but with low enough avidity to permit release through mechanical agitation alone.

- define terms used in the invention  
For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “aptamer” refers to a single-stranded oligonucleotide, typically 20 to 80 nucleotides in length, that folds into a three-dimensional structure capable of binding a specific molecular target with high affinity and selectivity. The term “cell surface marker” refers to a protein or glycoprotein expressed on the plasma membrane of a cell that serves as a distinguishable identifier for a specific cell type. The term “bead-free” refers to a cell population from which solid support particles, including beads, have been completely removed and are not detectable by microscopy or flow cytometry. The term “intraoperative” refers to procedures performed during the course of a surgical intervention, without requiring cell storage or transport.

- describe CD31+ cells and their characteristics  
CD31+ cells are a heterogeneous population of leukocytes and endothelial progenitors that express the transmembrane glycoprotein CD31, also known as PECAM-1. These cells exhibit potent angiogenic activity through the secretion of vascular endothelial growth factor, fibroblast growth factor, and matrix metalloproteinases. They also promote osteogenesis by stimulating mesenchymal stromal cell differentiation and mineralization, and modulate inflammation by suppressing T-cell activation and promoting regulatory macrophage polarization. CD31 is expressed on approximately 70% of circulating monocytes and a subset of naïve T lymphocytes, and its expression is preserved with aging and does not vary significantly by sex.

- discuss limitations of traditional cell purification methods  
Traditional methods for isolating CD31+ cells rely on antibody-conjugated magnetic beads, which bind irreversibly or require chemical elution for cell release. These methods result in residual bead or antibody contamination, which may trigger immune responses or alter cell function. Additionally, the small size of magnetic beads (50–100 nm) makes complete removal impossible, raising regulatory concerns. The requirement for refrigerated storage, specialized equipment, and lengthy processing times renders these methods unsuitable for intraoperative use.

- introduce aptamer-based cell enrichment method  
The aptamer-based method described herein enables the capture of CD31+ cells through reversible, non-covalent interactions that are disrupted solely by mechanical force. This eliminates the need for chemical elution, enzymatic digestion, or temperature shifts, ensuring the final cell product is free of exogenous ligands and beads. The method is rapid, scalable, and compatible with standard surgical workflows.

- describe advantages of aptamer-based cell enrichment  
Aptamers offer multiple advantages over antibodies: they are chemically synthesized with batch-to-batch consistency, are stable at room temperature for extended periods, can be modified with functional groups for immobilization without loss of activity, and are non-immunogenic. Their lower binding affinity compared to antibody–antigen pairs is advantageous in this context, as it permits release under mild mechanical stress without compromising cell viability.

- provide example of bone healing application  
In a clinical scenario involving a patient with a delayed union femoral fracture, whole blood is drawn preoperatively and processed through the device to enrich CD31+ cells. The enriched population is then injected directly into the fracture site during open reduction and internal fixation. Postoperative imaging and biochemical markers demonstrate accelerated callus formation and reduced inflammation compared to controls, without evidence of bead retention or adverse immune reactions.

- describe CD31+ cells in bone healing  
CD31+ cells contribute to bone healing by promoting vascular ingrowth into the fracture callus, enhancing the recruitment of osteoprogenitor cells, and secreting cytokines that stimulate osteoblast differentiation. Their presence in the early phase of healing correlates with improved outcomes in both animal models and human trials.

- discuss importance of rapid cell enrichment  
Rapid enrichment is critical in intraoperative settings to maintain cell viability and function. Delays beyond 30 minutes result in decreased metabolic activity and reduced secretion of regenerative factors. The invention enables isolation and administration within 20 to 30 minutes, preserving the therapeutic potential of the cells.

- describe aptamer characteristics  
The aptamer used in the invention is a 60-nucleotide DNA sequence selected for high specificity to human CD31, with a dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 1.14 × 10⁻⁹ M. It is biotinylated at the 5′ end for immobilization and lacks fluorescent or chemical modifications that could interfere with binding.

- discuss advantages of aptamers over antibodies  
Aptamers are more stable than antibodies under thermal and storage stress, can be produced synthetically at scale, and do not require animal immunization. They are less prone to denaturation, aggregation, or batch variability, and their small size allows for higher ligand density on bead surfaces.

- describe SELEX method for generating aptamers  
The aptamer was generated using Systematic Evolution of Ligands by EXponential enrichment (SELEX), a process involving iterative rounds of binding, partitioning, and amplification of nucleic acid libraries against the target antigen. After multiple cycles, high-affinity binders were isolated and sequenced.

- discuss counter-SELEX method for improving aptamer specificity  
Counter-SELEX was employed to eliminate aptamers that bind to non-target antigens expressed on other leukocyte subsets. Libraries were pre-incubated with CD31-negative cells, and non-binding sequences were discarded, enhancing specificity for CD31+ cells.

- describe tag and probe components for aptamer immobilization  
The aptamer is biotinylated to serve as a tag, enabling high-affinity binding to neutravidin-coated beads, which act as the probe. This non-covalent interaction provides stable immobilization while preserving the aptamer’s binding domain.

### II. Aptamers

- introduce aptamers  
Aptamers are synthetic, single-stranded nucleic acids that fold into specific three-dimensional structures capable of binding molecular targets with high affinity and selectivity.

- describe aptamer characteristics  
They are typically 20–80 nucleotides long, chemically stable, and can be modified with functional groups for immobilization or detection.

- discuss advantages of aptamers  
Aptamers are non-immunogenic, can be produced synthetically, are stable at room temperature, and exhibit batch-to-batch reproducibility.

- describe aptamer binding to cell surface markers  
The aptamer binds specifically to CD31 on the surface of monocytes and lymphocytes, with minimal cross-reactivity to other leukocyte markers.

- introduce SELEX method for generating aptamers  
SELEX involves repeated cycles of binding, partitioning, and amplification to isolate high-affinity binders from a random oligonucleotide library.

- describe SELEX process  
A library of 10¹⁴ unique sequences is incubated with recombinant CD31 protein; bound sequences are recovered, amplified by PCR, and subjected to additional rounds of selection.

- discuss counter-SELEX method for improving aptamer specificity  
Counter-SELEX involves pre-incubating the library with CD31-negative cells to remove sequences that bind non-target antigens, thereby enhancing specificity.

- describe aptamer synthesis  
The aptamer is synthesized via solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry, purified by HPLC, and biotinylated at the 5′ end.

- discuss aptamer stability  
The aptamer remains stable for over 12 months at room temperature and withstands multiple freeze-thaw cycles without loss of function.

- describe aptamer binding affinity  
The dissociation constant (Kd) for the aptamer–CD31 interaction is 1.14 × 10⁻⁹ M, sufficient for capture but low enough for mechanical release.

- discuss aptamer advantages over antibodies  
Unlike antibodies, aptamers do not require animal immunization, are less expensive to produce, and are not subject to batch variability.

- introduce tag and probe components for aptamer immobilization  
The biotin tag on the aptamer binds to neutravidin on the bead surface, forming a stable, reversible linkage.

- describe tag characteristics  
The biotin tag is a small, stable moiety that does not interfere with aptamer folding or binding.

- describe probe characteristics  
The neutravidin-coated agarose bead serves as the probe, providing high-capacity binding sites for biotinylated aptamers.

- discuss aptamer immobilization on beads  
Aptamers are immobilized at a density of 5–10 µg per milliliter of bead suspension, maximizing capture efficiency while preserving release kinetics.

- describe aptamer-based cell enrichment method  
Cells are passed through a column containing aptamer-coated beads; target cells bind, non-target cells are washed away, and bound cells are released by mechanical agitation.

- discuss advantages of aptamer-based cell enrichment  
The method is rapid, chemical-free, scalable, and yields a purified, functional cell population suitable for intraoperative use.

- provide example of aptamer-based cell enrichment  
In a clinical case, 100 mL of whole blood was processed in 25 minutes, yielding 15 million CD31+ cells with 87% purity and 97% viability.

- describe aptamer-based cell enrichment for therapeutic use  
The enriched cells are administered directly to the site of injury, promoting angiogenesis and osteogenesis without the need for ex vivo expansion.

- discuss importance of rapid aptamer-based cell enrichment  
Rapid enrichment preserves cell function and enables same-session therapy, reducing costs and improving outcomes.

- conclude aptamer section  
The aptamer-based enrichment method described herein represents a significant advancement in cell therapy technology, enabling the isolation of therapeutically potent cells in a manner that is safe, efficient, and compliant with regulatory standards.

### Materials and methods

- prepare PBMCs  
Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from whole blood using density gradient centrifugation with Ficoll-Paque Plus. Cells were washed twice in PBS and resuspended at 2.5 × 10⁷ cells/mL in binding buffer.

- validate aptamer specificity  
Aptamer binding was validated by flow cytometry using biotinylated aptamer and anti-CD31 antibody. Double-positive and double-negative populations were quantified.

- perform magnetic bead studies  
Commercial CD31 magnetic beads (Miltenyi Biotec) were used as a benchmark for comparison.

- analyze cell populations using flow cytometry  
Cells were stained for CD31, CD14, CD45, CD3, and CD19 and analyzed on a BD LSRFortessa.

- assemble system for cell enrichment  
Columns were assembled by attaching a 20-micrometer filter to the base of a microcentrifuge tube and packing with aptamer-coated beads.

- isolate CD31+ cells using system  
PBMCs were passed through the column at 50 µL/min for 10 minutes, washed, and cells were released by mechanical agitation.

- prepare conditioned medium  
Conditioned medium was collected after 24 hours of cell culture in EBM or DMEM media.

- assess angiogenic potential  
Tube formation assays were performed using HUVECs and conditioned medium on Matrigel.

- assess osteogenic potential  
Mesenchymal stromal cells were cultured in osteogenic medium supplemented with conditioned medium and stained with Alizarin Red.

- prepare aptamer coated beads  
Beads were incubated with 5 µg/mL aptamer for 20 minutes at 4°C, washed, and resuspended.

- optimize ligand concentration  
Aptamer concentrations of 0.5, 2, 5, and 10 µg/mL were tested for yield and viability.

- analyze cell adhesion and release  
Cell adhesion was measured by flow cytometry; release was assessed by comparing pre- and post-release populations.

- validate aptamer specificity using flow cytometry  
Flow cytometry confirmed high specificity and sensitivity for CD31+ cells.

- evaluate aptamer capacity to concentrate CD31+ cells  
Enrichment increased CD31+ cells from 64% to 87%.

- benchmark aptamer-based isolation to commercial antibody isolation  
Aptamer-based isolation achieved comparable enrichment to commercial magnetic beads.

- summarize aptamer validation results  
The aptamer demonstrated high specificity, sensitivity, and functional equivalence to antibodies.
