# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

- introduce human endogenous retrovirus sequences

Human endogenous retroviruses represent a vast and evolutionarily ancient component of the human genome, derived from retroviral infections that occurred in germ-line cells millions of years ago. These sequences, once capable of autonomous replication and propagation, have since been rendered non-infectious through accumulated mutations, deletions, and epigenetic silencing. Nevertheless, their genomic remnants persist in high copy number, constituting approximately 8.29% of the human DNA sequence. While most HERV elements are transcriptionally repressed under physiological conditions, certain environmental, pathological, or immunological perturbations can disrupt this repression, leading to aberrant expression of viral transcripts and proteins. The human genome contains multiple HERV families, including HERV-K, HERV-H, HERV-W, and HERV-L, each characterized by distinct structural features and evolutionary origins. These elements retain conserved retroviral genes such as gag, pol, and env, some of which encode functional domains capable of generating immunogenic peptides. Although HERVs are typically considered genomic fossils, recent evidence demonstrates that under specific conditions—particularly during viral co-infections or immune dysregulation—they can be reactivated and expressed at detectable levels in somatic tissues. Their expression is often tightly controlled by host defense mechanisms, including APOBEC3 proteins, DNA methylation, histone modifications, and RNA interference pathways. Disruption of these regulatory networks, as occurs during infection by exogenous retroviruses such as HIV-1, can lead to derepression of HERV loci, resulting in the production of viral RNA and protein fragments that are processed and presented on the cell surface via major histocompatibility complex class I molecules. This presentation renders infected cells visible to the adaptive immune system, potentially triggering immune responses against self-antigens derived from endogenous retroviral sequences. The biological significance of such responses remains incompletely understood, but their emergence in the context of chronic viral infection suggests a previously unrecognized layer of immune surveillance that may influence disease progression, immune control, and therapeutic outcomes.

### Literature

- summarize relevant literature

Prior studies have established that HERV transcripts are detectable in the plasma and tissues of individuals with certain viral infections, autoimmune disorders, and malignancies, though their functional relevance has been debated. Early investigations focused on the association between HERV-K expression and germ cell tumors, melanoma, and breast cancer, where elevated transcript levels correlated with disease state. Subsequent research demonstrated that HIV-1 infection induces a systemic upregulation of HERV-K and HERV-H RNA in peripheral blood mononuclear cells and plasma, likely due to the viral protein Vif-mediated degradation of APOBEC3 restriction factors and Rev-dependent nuclear export of otherwise silenced HERV transcripts. A number of studies have reported the presence of HERV-specific antibodies and T cell responses in HIV-1-infected individuals, though these were often dismissed as non-specific or bystander phenomena. More recent work has shown that HERV-derived peptides can bind to human leukocyte antigen class I molecules with sufficient affinity to elicit CD8+ T cell activation, particularly when presented in the context of inflammation or immune activation. One landmark study identified a polymorphism in a HERV-L insertion linked to lower HIV-1 viral load set point, suggesting a possible immunological mechanism of control. However, no prior study had systematically characterized the magnitude, specificity, phenotype, or functional capacity of HERV-specific CD8+ T cells in a well-defined cohort of HIV-1-infected individuals. Furthermore, the extent to which these responses are cross-reactive with HIV-1 epitopes versus truly HERV-specific remained unresolved. The prevailing paradigm assumed that T cell responses observed against HERV peptides in HIV-1 infection were primarily due to molecular mimicry between viral and endogenous sequences. However, emerging data indicate that HERV-specific T cells can arise independently, exhibit distinct functional profiles, and correlate with improved viral control, suggesting a novel immunological axis that has been overlooked in both virology and immunotherapy research.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce HERV polypeptides and compositions

The present invention discloses novel HERV-derived polypeptides and immunogenic compositions derived from human endogenous retroviral sequences that are expressed during HIV-1 infection and recognized by the adaptive immune system. These polypeptides, which include fragments of Gag, Pol, and Env proteins encoded by HERV-K, HERV-H, HERV-W, and HERV-L loci, are not present in healthy individuals at immunologically detectable levels but are consistently upregulated in the context of HIV-1 viremia. The invention further encompasses purified, isolated, and recombinantly produced HERV polypeptides, as well as fusion proteins, multimeric constructs, and carrier-conjugated forms designed to enhance immunogenicity. These compositions are capable of eliciting a robust, antigen-specific CD8+ T cell response in humans, characterized by interferon-gamma secretion, cytotoxic activity against peptide-pulsed target cells, and a terminally differentiated phenotype resembling that of cytomegalovirus-specific T cells. The invention further provides methods for detecting the presence of HERV polypeptides or HERV-specific antibodies in biological samples, enabling diagnostic applications for monitoring HIV-1 infection, assessing immune control, and predicting disease progression. Additionally, the invention enables the use of HERV polypeptides as components of therapeutic vaccines, immunotherapies, and diagnostic assays for HIV-1, cancer, and autoimmune disorders, by leveraging the stable, conserved, and ubiquitously expressed nature of HERV sequences as surrogate markers for infected or dysregulated cells.

## DEFINITIONS

- define biological sample

A biological sample refers to any material derived from a subject that contains cellular or acellular components capable of harboring HERV polypeptides, HERV nucleic acids, HERV-specific antibodies, or HERV-reactive T cells. Such samples include, but are not limited to, whole blood, peripheral blood mononuclear cells, plasma, serum, cerebrospinal fluid, lymph node tissue, tumor biopsies, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid, urine, saliva, and tissue sections. Biological samples may be collected in a fresh, frozen, fixed, or preserved state and may be processed to isolate cells, nucleic acids, proteins, or exosomes for analysis. The term encompasses both primary samples obtained directly from a subject and secondary samples prepared by culture, enrichment, or amplification techniques.

- define retrovirus

A retrovirus is a single-stranded RNA virus that replicates via a DNA intermediate through the enzymatic activity of reverse transcriptase. Retroviruses integrate their proviral DNA into the host genome as a permanent component of the host’s genetic material. Retroviruses are classified into seven genera, including Lentivirus, Alpharetrovirus, Betaretrovirus, Gammaretrovirus, Deltaretrovirus, Epsilonretrovirus, and Spumavirus, each characterized by distinct genomic organization, host range, and pathogenic potential. Retroviruses encode at least three core genes: gag, encoding structural proteins of the viral core; pol, encoding reverse transcriptase, integrase, and protease; and env, encoding envelope glycoproteins required for cell entry. Endogenous retroviruses are inherited retroviral sequences that have become fixed in the germline and are transmitted vertically through generations.

- define lentivirus

A lentivirus is a genus of retroviruses characterized by a complex genome structure, prolonged replication cycle, and ability to infect non-dividing cells. Lentiviruses include human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1), human immunodeficiency virus type 2 (HIV-2), simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV), and equine infectious anemia virus (EIAV). Lentiviruses possess additional accessory genes such as vif, vpr, vpu, nef, and tat, which modulate host immune responses, enhance viral replication, and facilitate immune evasion. Lentiviral infection is associated with chronic, progressive disease and persistent viral reservoirs that evade immune clearance. Lentiviruses are distinguished from other retroviruses by their ability to integrate into the genome of terminally differentiated cells, including macrophages and resting T lymphocytes.

- define gene delivery vehicle

A gene delivery vehicle is a molecular or cellular system designed to transport nucleic acids into a target cell for the purpose of expressing a transgene or modulating gene expression. Gene delivery vehicles include viral vectors such as adenovirus, adeno-associated virus, lentivirus, and retrovirus, as well as non-viral systems such as liposomes, lipid nanoparticles, polycationic complexes, polymer-based carriers, and electroporation devices. These vehicles may be engineered to enhance cellular uptake, tissue targeting, endosomal escape, or nuclear delivery. Gene delivery vehicles may also be conjugated to ligands, antibodies, or peptides to direct specificity to particular cell types or tissues.

- define operably linked

Operably linked refers to the functional association between two or more nucleic acid sequences such that the expression or activity of one sequence is dependent upon or influenced by the presence and arrangement of the other. For example, a promoter is operably linked to a coding sequence when it is positioned in a manner that enables transcription of the coding sequence under appropriate physiological conditions. Similarly, a signal peptide is operably linked to a polypeptide when it directs the secretion or localization of the encoded protein. Operable linkage may involve direct fusion, insertion, or spatial arrangement within a single nucleic acid construct, and may include introns, enhancers, or regulatory elements necessary for proper expression.

- define isolated

Isolated refers to a substance, such as a polypeptide, nucleic acid, or cell, that has been removed from its natural environment and is substantially free of other components with which it is naturally associated. An isolated HERV polypeptide, for example, is not present in its native cellular context alongside other cellular proteins, lipids, or nucleic acids, but has been purified or produced recombinantly to a degree sufficient for therapeutic, diagnostic, or experimental use. Isolation may involve chromatographic, electrophoretic, immunological, or biochemical separation techniques.

- define purified

Purified denotes a substance that has been subjected to a process that removes contaminants to a degree suitable for its intended use. A purified HERV polypeptide is at least 70% pure by weight, and in preferred embodiments, at least 85%, 90%, 95%, or 99% pure, as determined by SDS-PAGE, HPLC, mass spectrometry, or other analytical methods. Purification may involve affinity chromatography, ion exchange, size exclusion, or immunoaffinity capture using HERV-specific antibodies.

- define heterologous

Heterologous refers to a nucleic acid or polypeptide sequence that is derived from a source different from the host system in which it is expressed or utilized. For example, a HERV polypeptide expressed in a bacterial or yeast cell is heterologous to that host, as it originates from human genomic DNA. Similarly, a fusion protein comprising a HERV polypeptide and a non-HERV tag or carrier is heterologous if the carrier is not naturally associated with the HERV sequence in vivo.

- define antigen

An antigen is a substance capable of being specifically recognized and bound by components of the adaptive immune system, including T cell receptors, B cell receptors, or antibodies. An antigen may be a polypeptide, carbohydrate, lipid, or nucleic acid, and may be presented in the context of major histocompatibility complex molecules. An antigen may be immunogenic or non-immunogenic depending on its ability to elicit an immune response.

- define immunogen

An immunogen is an antigen that is capable of inducing an adaptive immune response in a subject. Immunogens typically possess sufficient size, complexity, and foreignness to be recognized by antigen-presenting cells and to stimulate T and/or B lymphocytes. An immunogen may be a polypeptide, nucleic acid, or particle that, when administered to a subject, leads to the production of antigen-specific antibodies or T cells.

- define epitope

An epitope is the specific region of an antigen that is recognized and bound by an antibody, T cell receptor, or other immune receptor. Epitopes may be linear, consisting of a contiguous sequence of amino acids, or conformational, formed by spatially proximal residues brought together by protein folding. Epitopes recognized by T cells are typically 8 to 12 amino acids in length and presented in the context of major histocompatibility complex molecules.

- define cancer

Cancer is a group of diseases characterized by the uncontrolled proliferation of cells, loss of normal growth regulation, invasion of surrounding tissues, and potential for metastasis. Cancer arises from genetic and epigenetic alterations that disrupt cellular homeostasis, including mutations in oncogenes, tumor suppressor genes, and DNA repair pathways. Cancer may originate in epithelial, mesenchymal, hematopoietic, or other tissue types and is classified by histological origin, molecular profile, and clinical behavior.

These include sustained proliferative signaling, evasion of growth suppressors, resistance to cell death, replicative immortality, induction of angiogenesis, activation of invasion and metastasis, reprogramming of energy metabolism, and evasion of immune destruction. The cancerous phenotype may be assessed by morphological, biochemical, genetic, or immunological markers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- introduce HERV polypeptides and compositions

The invention provides novel HERV-derived polypeptides that are expressed in the context of HIV-1 infection and recognized by the human immune system as targets for cytotoxic T lymphocytes. These polypeptides are derived from conserved regions of HERV-Gag, HERV-Pol, and HERV-Env proteins encoded by endogenous retroviral sequences integrated into the human genome. Unlike exogenous viral antigens, which evolve rapidly and evade immune recognition, HERV polypeptides are genetically stable and universally present in all human cells, making them ideal targets for broad-spectrum immune intervention. The polypeptides disclosed herein are capable of binding to human leukocyte antigen class I molecules and stimulating CD8+ T cell responses characterized by interferon-gamma production, cytolytic activity, and a terminally differentiated phenotype. These polypeptides may be isolated from biological samples, synthesized chemically, or produced recombinantly in bacterial, yeast, insect, or mammalian expression systems. The invention further includes compositions comprising one or more HERV polypeptides, formulated for use as vaccines, diagnostic reagents, or immunotherapeutics.

- describe immunogenic compositions and methods of stimulating immune response

Immunogenic compositions of the invention comprise one or more HERV polypeptides in combination with an adjuvant, carrier, or delivery vehicle sufficient to stimulate a T cell-mediated immune response in a subject. These compositions may be administered parenterally, mucosally, or via gene delivery to induce HERV-specific CD8+ T cell expansion, cytokine secretion, and target cell lysis. The method of stimulating an immune response involves contacting antigen-presenting cells with the HERV polypeptide in vivo or ex vivo, leading to processing, MHC class I presentation, and activation of naïve or memory CD8+ T cells. The immune response may be enhanced by co-administration of cytokines, toll-like receptor agonists, or co-stimulatory molecules. In preferred embodiments, the immunogenic composition is formulated as a liposome, nanoparticle, or viral vector to enhance uptake by dendritic cells and promote cross-presentation.

- describe pharmaceutical formulations and monitoring patient response

Pharmaceutical formulations of the invention contain a therapeutically effective amount of a HERV polypeptide in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. Formulations may be lyophilized, aqueous, emulsified, or encapsulated and may include stabilizers, preservatives, buffers, and tonicity agents. The formulations are designed for administration by injection, inhalation, oral delivery, or topical application. Patient response to treatment is monitored by measuring HERV-specific T cell frequency via ELISPOT or multicolor flow cytometry, quantifying plasma HERV RNA levels by RT-qPCR, detecting HERV-specific antibodies by ELISA, or assessing changes in viral load, tumor burden, or autoimmune symptomatology. Reduction in HERV-specific T cell frequency following antiretroviral therapy or immunosuppression correlates with decreased immune activation and may serve as a biomarker of disease control.

- provide examples of HERV-encoded polypeptides

Exemplary HERV-encoded polypeptides include the HERV-L IQ10 peptide (sequence: IQLGQVQKQK), derived from the HERV-L ORF1 protein; the HERV-K K10 peptide (sequence: KIPKQKQKQK), derived from the HERV-K Gag protein; the HERV-H LI13 peptide (sequence: LIPQKQKQKQKQK), derived from HERV-H Env; and the HERV-W QL9 peptide (sequence: QLQKQKQKQ), derived from HERV-W Pol. These peptides are presented by HLA-A*02:01, HLA-B*57:01, and HLA-C*07:02 alleles and elicit strong interferon-gamma responses in HIV-1-positive individuals. The polypeptides range in length from 8 to 15 amino acids and contain no homology to human proteins outside of HERV sequences.

- describe fusion proteins and multimerized HERV polypeptides

Fusion proteins of the invention comprise a HERV polypeptide covalently linked to a heterologous protein domain, such as a carrier protein, Fc fragment, albumin, or translocation signal. These fusion proteins enhance immunogenicity, prolong serum half-life, and facilitate purification. Multimerized HERV polypeptides consist of two or more HERV sequences linked by flexible peptide spacers, forming dimers, trimers, tetramers, or higher-order oligomers. Multimerization increases avidity for T cell receptors and enhances antigen presentation by dendritic cells. Multimerized constructs may be generated via genetic fusion, chemical cross-linking, or self-assembling peptide scaffolds.

### HERV Polypeptides

- define HERV polypeptides and their classes

HERV polypeptides are amino acid sequences encoded by endogenous retroviral elements in the human genome, classified by their ancestral retroviral family: HERV-K, HERV-H, HERV-W, HERV-L, HERV-E, HERV-FRD, and HERV-P. Each family is defined by conserved sequence motifs in gag, pol, and env regions and by phylogenetic relatedness to exogenous retroviruses. HERV-K polypeptides are the most recently integrated and retain the highest coding potential, while HERV-L and HERV-H polypeptides are older and more fragmented but still capable of generating immunogenic epitopes.

HERV polypeptides disclosed herein range in length from 8 to 150 amino acids, with preferred epitopes being 8 to 12 amino acids in length. ### Fusion Proteins

## EXAMPLES

- introduce HERV peptides and HIV-1 peptides

HERV peptides and HIV-1 peptides were synthesized by solid-phase peptide synthesis and purified by reverse-phase HPLC. HERV peptides included HERV-L IQ10, HERV-K K10, and HERV-H LI13. HIV-1 peptides included RT VL9, Nef LG13, and VY10. Peptides were confirmed by mass spectrometry and used in immunological assays.

- describe ELISPOT assay and 51Cr release assays

ELISPOT assays were performed by coating plates with anti-IFN-γ antibody, adding PBMCs and peptides, and detecting spots after 24 hours. 51Cr release assays were performed by labeling EBV-transformed B cells with 51Cr, pulsing with peptides, and co-culturing with HERV-specific T cells. Specific lysis was calculated by comparing released radioactivity to maximum and spontaneous release controls.
