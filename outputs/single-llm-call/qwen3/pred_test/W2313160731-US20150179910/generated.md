# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to energy conversion

The present invention relates to systems and methods for the efficient conversion of thermal energy into electrical energy, particularly in the context of solar-powered thermoelectric generation. The invention encompasses novel configurations of heat transfer fluid circulation, thermoelectric generator design, and solar concentrator integration to maximize thermal energy capture and electrical output while minimizing energy losses and operational complexity. The systems described are especially suited for deployment in terrestrial and marine environments where solar irradiance is abundant and conventional power generation infrastructure is limited or economically unfeasible. The invention further includes embodiments that utilize advanced heat retention media and modular thermoelectric architectures to enable scalable, continuous, and self-regulating energy production without reliance on moving parts or external fuel sources.

## BACKGROUND ART

- describe thermoelectric generators

Thermoelectric generators are solid-state devices that convert a temperature gradient directly into electrical energy through the Seebeck effect. These devices consist of semiconductor pairs, typically n-type and p-type materials, connected electrically in series and thermally in parallel, forming thermocouples. When one side of the device is heated and the other cooled, charge carriers migrate from the hot junction to the cold junction, generating a voltage proportional to the temperature differential. While thermoelectric generators offer advantages such as silent operation, long-term reliability, and minimal maintenance, their widespread adoption has been hindered by low conversion efficiencies, typically below 10%, and the difficulty in maintaining sufficient and sustained temperature gradients under real-world conditions. Conventional systems often rely on active cooling mechanisms or high-conductivity heat sinks, which add cost, weight, and complexity.

- discuss prior art solar energy conversion systems

Prior art solar energy conversion systems have predominantly relied on photovoltaic panels or concentrated solar power plants employing steam turbines. Photovoltaic systems suffer from low efficiency under diffuse light conditions, degradation over time due to thermal cycling, and significant land use requirements. Concentrated solar power systems, while capable of higher thermal efficiencies, require large-scale infrastructure including mirrors, boilers, turbines, and complex fluid handling systems. These systems are capital-intensive, require regular maintenance, and are often impractical for decentralized or mobile applications. Additionally, many prior systems depend on heat transfer fluids with suboptimal thermal properties, leading to increased pumping power, reduced thermal storage capacity, and elevated operational risks due to fluid degradation at high temperatures.

- highlight limitations of prior art systems

The limitations of existing systems include inefficient thermal coupling between solar concentrators and heat transfer media, inadequate heat retention during periods of low solar irradiance, and the inability to maintain stable temperature differentials across thermoelectric modules. Many systems fail to account for the thermal expansion and contraction of fluids, resulting in pressure fluctuations and mechanical stress. Furthermore, prior art systems rarely integrate feedback loops for fluid recycling or fail to optimize the geometry of heat exchange surfaces, leading to significant thermal losses. The absence of adaptive solar tracking, modular scalability, and hybrid energy retention mechanisms further restricts their applicability in variable environmental conditions. Most critically, existing systems do not combine the advantages of high-stability organic heat transfer fluids with submerged thermoelectric arrays and passive cooling via ambient water bodies, thereby leaving a critical gap in the field of compact, efficient, and resilient solar energy conversion technologies.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

- introduce system for converting thermal energy into electrical energy

The invention introduces a novel system for converting thermal energy into electrical energy through the synergistic integration of a solar concentrator, a closed-loop heat transfer fluid circuit, and a thermoelectric generator submerged in a thermal sink medium. The system operates without combustion, moving turbines, or external power inputs, relying solely on solar radiation and natural convective cooling to generate electricity. The core innovation lies in the combination of a high-efficiency organic heat transfer fluid with a thermoelectric module designed for direct immersion in a liquid thermal sink, enabling sustained temperature differentials that maximize the Seebeck effect. This configuration eliminates the need for bulky heat exchangers and external cooling systems, significantly reducing system complexity and increasing overall energy conversion efficiency.

- describe solar concentrator for directing sunlight to heat transfer fluid

The system employs a parabolic solar concentrator configured to focus incident solar radiation onto a vacuum tubing assembly located at its focal point. The vacuum tubing, constructed of high-temperature resistant glass with an inner coating of selective absorber material, minimizes radiative and convective heat losses while maximizing thermal absorption. The concentrator is aligned to track the sun’s diurnal path, ensuring continuous and optimal energy capture throughout the day. The absorbed thermal energy is transferred to a heat transfer fluid flowing through the vacuum tube, which is maintained at a temperature range between 150°C and 300°C, depending on ambient conditions and fluid composition. The fluid is selected from a class of aryl ether derivatives exhibiting high thermal stability, low viscosity, and exceptional specific heat capacity, enabling efficient energy transport with minimal pumping requirements.

- detail conveyance for transferring heat transfer fluid to thermoelectric generator

The heat transfer fluid is conveyed from the solar concentrator via a dual-duct system comprising an inner duct and an outer duct, both configured with triangular cross-sectional geometries to optimize structural integrity and thermal contact. The inner duct carries the heated fluid, while the outer duct serves as an insulating barrier and structural support. The ducts are connected end-to-end through precisely engineered outlet tubes and engagement regions, allowing for modular extension and seamless fluid flow across multiple units. The conveyance system is insulated with high-temperature resistant materials and includes control valves to regulate flow rate and pressure, ensuring optimal thermal delivery to the thermoelectric generator.

- describe thermoelectric generator submerged in water

The thermoelectric generator is fully submerged in a body of water, such as a reservoir, lake, or ocean, which acts as a passive and inexhaustible thermal sink. The generator is encased in a hermetically sealed housing with a thermally conductive outer surface, allowing direct heat exchange between the heat transfer fluid and the surrounding water. The submerged configuration leverages the high specific heat and thermal conductivity of water to maintain a consistent cold junction temperature, thereby maximizing the temperature differential across the thermoelectric modules. This design eliminates the need for active cooling systems and reduces thermal cycling stress on the semiconductor materials.

- outline feedback loop for recycling heat transfer fluid

A closed-loop feedback system is implemented to continuously circulate the heat transfer fluid between the solar concentrator and the thermoelectric generator. After transferring its thermal energy to the submerged generator, the cooled fluid is returned to the concentrator via a secondary conveyance line. Temperature and flow sensors monitor the fluid state in real time, and a control unit adjusts the pump speed and valve positions to maintain optimal thermal gradients. This feedback loop ensures continuous operation, minimizes thermal losses, and prevents fluid stagnation or overheating, thereby extending the operational lifespan of the system components.

- describe alternative embodiment with steam generator and turbine

In an alternative embodiment, the system incorporates a steam generator and turbine for hybrid energy conversion. In this configuration, the heat transfer fluid passes through a heat exchanger where it vaporizes water into high-pressure steam, which drives a microturbine connected to an electrical generator. The steam is then condensed and returned to the heat exchanger, forming a secondary closed loop. This hybrid system enables dual-mode operation: thermoelectric generation during daylight hours and turbine-based generation during periods of high thermal accumulation, thereby increasing total energy output and system flexibility.

- detail thermoelectric generator configuration

The thermoelectric generator is composed of multiple thermoelectric modules arranged in series and connected electrically in parallel to maximize voltage and current output. Each module is fabricated from bismuth telluride-based semiconductors and encapsulated in a ceramic substrate with high thermal conductivity. The modules are mounted onto a metallic heat spreader plate that interfaces directly with the inner duct, ensuring uniform heat distribution and minimizing hot spots. The entire assembly is designed for long-term immersion in aqueous environments, with corrosion-resistant coatings and hermetic sealing to prevent fluid ingress.

- describe array of fins on outer surface of thermoelectric generator

An array of thermally conductive fins is affixed to the outer surface of the thermoelectric generator housing, extending into the surrounding water to increase the effective heat exchange surface area. The fins are constructed from copper or aluminum alloys and are shaped with tapered profiles to enhance natural convection currents. Their arrangement is optimized to minimize flow resistance while maximizing turbulent mixing, thereby accelerating heat dissipation and maintaining a stable cold junction temperature. The fin geometry is determined through computational fluid dynamics simulations to ensure maximum thermal transfer under varying water flow conditions.

- outline automatic orientation adjustment of solar concentrator

The solar concentrator is mounted on a dual-axis tracking system that automatically adjusts its orientation to follow the sun’s path across the sky. The tracking system is powered by a small photovoltaic panel integrated into the concentrator frame and regulated by a microprocessor that receives input from solar position sensors and ambient light detectors. The system recalibrates its position at intervals of no more than five minutes to ensure continuous maximum irradiance capture, even under partially cloudy conditions. The tracking mechanism is designed for low torque operation and requires no external power source beyond the energy generated by the system itself.

- describe artificial island embodiment

The invention further includes an artificial island embodiment, wherein the entire system is mounted on a buoyant platform constructed from composite materials and anchored to a seabed or lakebed. The island is designed to float on the water surface, with the thermoelectric generators submerged below the platform and the solar concentrators mounted above. The platform incorporates a central storage tank for the heat transfer fluid and a control hub for system monitoring. The artificial island is self-sustaining, requiring no land-based infrastructure, and is particularly suited for deployment in offshore or remote inland water bodies where grid connectivity is absent.
