# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Protein interaction networks form the foundational architecture of cellular function, enabling the coordination of biochemical processes essential for life. These networks are not randomly organized but exhibit a small-world topology, characterized by high clustering among local nodes and short path lengths between distant nodes, which allows for rapid signal propagation and robustness against random perturbations. This structural property is particularly pronounced in disease-associated networks, where key hub proteins serve as convergence points for multiple pathogenic insults, including bacterial toxins, viral proteins, and endogenous dysregulatory signals. The interconnectedness of these pathways means that targeting a single component often yields limited therapeutic benefit due to compensatory mechanisms or pathway redundancy. Consequently, a multiplex approach that simultaneously disrupts multiple nodes within a shared network architecture offers a superior strategy for therapeutic intervention. By identifying and inhibiting central hub proteins exploited by diverse pathogenic agents, it becomes possible to develop broad-spectrum countermeasures that are less susceptible to resistance and more effective in complex, multi-factorial disease states. Such an approach shifts the paradigm from pathogen-centric therapeutics to host-oriented interventions, leveraging the conserved nature of host cellular machinery to achieve wider applicability across infectious and inflammatory conditions.

## SUMMARY

The invention encompasses a method for inhibiting pathogenic agents by targeting host caspase proteins that are co-opted by a broad range of microbial toxins and viruses to induce programmed cell death. This method involves the administration of Bithionol, a small molecule previously approved for antihelminthic use, which acts as a potent and selective inhibitor of caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9. By suppressing the proteolytic activity of these executioner and inflammatory caspases, Bithionol prevents the downstream cellular dismantling that is essential for the cytotoxic effects of diverse pathogenic agents, including bacterial exotoxins, plant toxins, and enveloped viruses. The invention further includes a pharmaceutical composition comprising Bithionol as the active ingredient, formulated for systemic delivery via oral, parenteral, or other clinically viable routes, and optionally combined with antibiotics or antiviral agents to enhance therapeutic efficacy. This composition enables the rapid deployment of a repurposed drug with established safety profiles for the treatment of acute intoxications and emerging infectious threats, thereby circumventing the lengthy timelines associated with de novo drug development.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention utilizes a cell-based multiplex approach to identify host proteins that are simultaneously exploited by multiple unrelated pathogenic agents, thereby revealing conserved vulnerabilities in human cellular machinery. Through parallel screening of cytotoxicity and proteolytic activity across diverse toxins—including Pseudomonas exotoxin A, anthrax lethal factor, ricin, botulinum neurotoxin A, and diphtheria toxin—Bithionol was identified as a compound capable of preserving cell viability by inhibiting the activation of key caspases without directly interacting with the toxins themselves. This mechanism distinguishes the invention from conventional antimicrobial strategies that target pathogen-specific structures, instead focusing on the host’s own proteolytic machinery that pathogens hijack to execute cell death. The term “pathogen” refers to any biological entity, including bacteria, fungi, plants, or viruses, capable of causing disease through the production of toxic proteins or the induction of host cell dysfunction. A “pathogenic agent” encompasses any molecular component of such a pathogen, including exotoxins, endotoxins, or viral proteins, that initiates or propagates cellular damage. The term “prevent” denotes the inhibition of disease onset prior to or during early exposure, while “treat” refers to the amelioration of symptoms or progression of disease following established infection or intoxication.

Bithionol inhibits caspase-dependent pathogenic agents by directly binding to and suppressing the enzymatic activity of caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9, as demonstrated through fluorogenic substrate cleavage assays using purified recombinant human enzymes. This inhibition is dose-dependent and occurs at micromolar concentrations consistent with achievable plasma levels following oral administration in humans. Bithionol does not inhibit other caspases such as -2, -4, -5, -8, or -10, nor does it interfere with the proteolytic activity of bacterial or plant toxins themselves, as confirmed by direct substrate cleavage assays using anthrax lethal factor, botulinum neurotoxin A light chain, and ricin. In vivo analysis of Bithionol demonstrates its ability to confer complete protection against lethal doses of botulinum neurotoxin A in murine models when administered orally, with no observable toxicity at therapeutic doses. The dosage of Bithionol required for efficacy ranges from 1 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg body weight in mammals, with optimal results observed at 6 mg/kg, corresponding to serum concentrations of approximately 100–200 μM, which exceeds the in vitro EC50 values by more than tenfold. The method of inhibiting caspase-dependent pathogenic agents involves the administration of an effective amount of Bithionol to a subject at risk of or suffering from intoxication by a pathogenic agent whose cytotoxicity is mediated through caspase activation.

The composition for inhibiting caspase-dependent pathogenic agents comprises Bithionol as the sole active ingredient or in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or stabilizers suitable for oral, intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous delivery. The composition may be formulated as tablets, capsules, solutions, suspensions, or injectable emulsions, with Bithionol present in concentrations ranging from 1% to 50% by weight. Administration of the Bithionol composition is achieved by delivering the formulation to a mammalian subject via any route that achieves systemic bioavailability, including oral ingestion, intravenous infusion, intramuscular injection, or transdermal delivery. The composition is stable under standard pharmaceutical storage conditions and retains inhibitory activity against caspases for at least two years when stored at room temperature in a dry, light-protected environment.

### Combination Compositions and Treatments

The invention further contemplates the combination of Bithionol with antibiotics such as ciprofloxacin, vancomycin, or tetracycline to enhance therapeutic outcomes in infections where both bacterial proliferation and toxin-mediated tissue damage contribute to disease severity. The composition including Bithionol and an antibiotic may be formulated as a fixed-dose combination tablet or as separate units administered concurrently, with each component present at its respective therapeutic dose. The mixture of Bithionol and antibiotic may be dissolved in a sterile aqueous solution for parenteral administration, ensuring simultaneous systemic delivery of both agents. Parenteral administration of the composition is achieved via intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous injection, with dosing schedules tailored to the clinical context, such as acute intoxication or sepsis. The dosage of the combination composition is adjusted such that Bithionol is administered at 1–10 mg/kg and the antibiotic at its standard clinical dose, with no observed pharmacokinetic interference between the two agents. The method of treating specific pathogenic agents includes the administration of the combination composition to subjects infected with Bacillus anthracis, Clostridium botulinum, or Pseudomonas aeruginosa, where toxin production is a primary driver of morbidity. Reference to the patent for Octodrine, U.S. Patent No. 4,585,789, is provided to illustrate prior art in the use of small molecules for host-directed modulation of cellular responses, though Octodrine does not inhibit caspases or exhibit the broad-spectrum anti-toxin activity described herein.

### Administration of the Bithionol Composition

Parenteral administration refers to any route of drug delivery that bypasses the gastrointestinal tract, including intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, intra-arterial, and intrathecal routes. Systemic administration denotes delivery of a compound into the circulatory system such that it reaches target tissues throughout the body, including the central nervous system and viscera. The administering of the Bithionol composition is performed by introducing the formulation into the bloodstream or interstitial fluid of a subject in need, with dosing frequency determined by pharmacokinetic half-life and disease progression. The preparation of pharmaceutical doses involves the dissolution or suspension of Bithionol in a biocompatible vehicle such as saline, dextrose, polysorbate 80, or cyclodextrin derivatives, followed by sterilization by filtration or autoclaving and encapsulation in vials or prefilled syringes for clinical use. The final formulation is adjusted to a pH between 6.0 and 8.0 to ensure stability and minimize tissue irritation upon injection.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Identification of Host Hub Proteins Exploited by Multiple Pathogenic Toxins

Host hub proteins were identified by analyzing the convergence of cytotoxic pathways activated by diverse bacterial and plant toxins, including Pseudomonas exotoxin A, anthrax lethal factor, ricin, and botulinum neurotoxin A. Genetic association studies in human B-lymphoblastoid cell lines revealed that polymorphisms in caspase genes correlated with differential sensitivity to these toxins, suggesting that caspases serve as central nodes in a shared pathogenic network. These proteins were subsequently selected as targets for high-throughput drug screening to identify compounds capable of broadly suppressing toxin-induced cell death.

### Example 2. The Effect of Caspase Mutations on the Sensitivity of Human B-Cells to P. aeruginosa Exotoxin A

Human B-cells derived from 234 individuals across four distinct populations were exposed to serial dilutions of Pseudomonas exotoxin A, and cell viability was quantified using Alamar Blue fluorescence. A 200-fold variation in LD20 was observed, with significant associations found between specific single nucleotide polymorphisms in CASP3 and CASP7 and increased toxin sensitivity. Parent-child trio analysis confirmed heritability of this trait, establishing caspase activity as a genetically determined determinant of susceptibility to bacterial toxins.

### Example 3. A Multiplexed Cellular Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Cytotoxic Activities of Bacterial Toxins Exploiting Unique but Interconnected Host Pathways

A Clinical Compound Library of 1,280 compounds was screened for the ability to protect RAW264.7 and C32 cells from death induced by Pseudomonas exotoxin A, cholera toxin, and diphtheria toxin. Two compounds, Bithionol and Pyrogallol, were identified as multiplex hits, each increasing cell survival by more than 20 standard deviations above control levels across all three toxins. These compounds were selected for further mechanistic investigation.

### Example 4. A Multiplex Protein Function-Based Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Proteolytic Activities of Host Caspase-3, -6, and -7

Fluorogenic FRET substrates specific for caspase-3, caspase-6, and caspase-3/7 were incubated with recombinant human caspases and the CCL. Bithionol emerged as one of three compounds capable of inhibiting the proteolytic activity of all three caspases by more than 80% at 33 μM. This dual identification in both cellular and biochemical screens confirmed Bithionol as a direct caspase inhibitor with broad-spectrum potential.

### Example 5. Bithionol Reduces the Pathogenicity of a Range of Toxins by Inhibiting Host Caspases

Bithionol reduced cytotoxicity of Pseudomonas exotoxin A, diphtheria toxin, and cholera toxin in RAW264.7 and C32 cells with EC50 values of 10 μM. In biochemical assays, Bithionol inhibited caspase-3, -6, and -3/7 with IC50 values of 21, 13, and 11 μM, respectively. The drug also protected human B-cell lines from toxin-induced death at concentrations achievable in human serum, confirming its therapeutic relevance.

### Example 6. Bithionol Inhibits Cytotoxic Activity of Anthrax Toxins

In RAW264.7 cells treated with anthrax lethal factor and protective antigen, Bithionol (33 μM) prevented cell death and suppressed caspase-1 activation without inhibiting furin or cathepsin-B activity. Western blot analysis confirmed preservation of MAPKK2 integrity, indicating that Bithionol does not interfere with the toxin’s primary proteolytic function but instead blocks downstream apoptotic signaling.

### Example 7. Bithionol Inhibits Ricin and Botulinum Neurotoxin A-Induced Death In Vitro and In Vivo

Bithionol reduced ricin-induced cytotoxicity in K562 cells with an EC50 of 10 μM. In mice challenged with a lethal dose of botulinum neurotoxin A, oral administration of Bithionol at 6 mg/kg resulted in 100% survival, with no signs of paralysis or weight loss. FRET assays confirmed that Bithionol did not inhibit BoNT/A’s proteolytic cleavage of SNAP-25, demonstrating that protection was mediated through host caspase inhibition.

### Example 8. Bithionol Acts as a Zika Virus Inhibitor

Bithionol inhibited replication of both African and Asian lineages of Zika virus in Vero E6 cells and human astrocytes, with EC50 values of 5.5–6.7 μM. High-content imaging revealed a dose-dependent reduction in viral envelope protein expression, with no cytotoxicity observed at therapeutic concentrations. These findings establish Bithionol as a host-directed antiviral agent capable of suppressing Zika virus propagation.

### Example 9. Material and Methods

Chemicals and reagents were sourced from commercial vendors including Sigma-Aldrich, List Biological Laboratories, and BioVision. Cell lines were maintained under standard conditions in DMEM, RPMI, or MEM supplemented with fetal bovine serum and antibiotics. Human B-lymphocytes were treated with Pseudomonas exotoxin A for 48 hours, and viability was assessed via Alamar Blue fluorescence. Cellular drug screens were conducted in 96-well plates using MTT and Alamar Blue assays. Caspase activity was measured using fluorogenic FRET substrates and quantified via kinetic fluorescence readings analyzed with Microsoft Excel LINEST function. Western blot analyses were performed using Odyssey infrared imaging. In vivo studies utilized Swiss Webster mice treated orally with Bithionol and botulinum neurotoxin A, with survival monitored over seven days. Zika virus infection was quantified using high-content imaging and immunofluorescence staining with anti-flavivirus envelope antibodies. Statistical significance was determined using GraphPad Prism software with a threshold of p < 0.05.
