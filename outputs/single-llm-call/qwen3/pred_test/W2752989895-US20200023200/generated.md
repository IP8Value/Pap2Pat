## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- The invention relates to the field of radiation oncology, specifically to the delivery of electron beam therapy for the treatment of superficial and shallow-seated malignancies. Electron beams are uniquely suited for targeting tissues within a few centimeters of the skin surface, offering sharp dose fall-off beyond the target volume and minimizing exposure to deeper organs. The energy range of therapeutic electron beams typically spans from 4 MeV to 20 MeV, corresponding to penetration depths of approximately 1 cm to 8 cm in water-equivalent tissue. While x-ray and proton therapies benefit from advanced intensity modulation techniques, electron therapy has historically been limited to uniform field delivery due to the physical challenges of controlling electron scattering and maintaining field conformity. Other radiation sources, including photons, protons, and brachytherapy isotopes, have been successfully modulated using multileaf collimators, scanned beams, or source positioning systems, but none provide the same depth-dose characteristics as electrons for superficial targets. Radiation dose is defined as the energy deposited per unit mass of tissue, and its spatial distribution is governed by the principles of charged particle transport, including multiple Coulomb scattering, energy loss, and lateral diffusion. Scattering of electron beams occurs both upstream and downstream of any intervening material, leading to broadening of the beam profile and blurring of sharp dose gradients. Control of the spatial distribution of beam intensity is therefore essential to achieve homogeneous dose coverage within irregularly shaped target volumes while sparing adjacent healthy tissue. Deep-seated planning target volumes are generally unsuitable for electron therapy due to insufficient penetration, whereas superficial planning target volumes, such as those in the skin, chest wall, head and neck, and extremities, are ideal candidates. Electron conformal therapy has emerged as a method to shape the electron field to match the contour of the target, but without intensity modulation, it remains incapable of correcting for surface irregularities or achieving uniform dose within complex geometries. The limitations of current methods stem from the inability to locally attenuate or transmit electron fluence with sufficient precision, resulting in hot and cold spots that compromise therapeutic efficacy. The present invention introduces a passive method for achieving precise, patient-specific intensity modulation in electron beams, overcoming the longstanding technical barriers that have prevented widespread clinical adoption of modulated electron therapy.

## BACKGROUND AND DESCRIPTION OF PRIOR ART

- Electron conformal therapy has been developed to conform the edge of an electron field to the shape of a superficial target, typically using custom-cut acrylic bolus or cut-out collimators. However, these methods provide only geometric shaping and do not modulate the fluence within the field, leading to dose inhomogeneities when the target surface is irregular or curved. Attempts to deliver electron intensity modulation have focused on adapting multileaf collimator technology from photon therapy, but the large air gaps inherent in conventional multileaf collimators cause excessive electron scattering, resulting in unacceptably broad penumbras and loss of field definition. Electron multileaf collimators have been proposed and prototyped, incorporating thinner leaves and reduced air gaps, yet these systems remain prohibitively expensive, mechanically complex, and incompatible with existing treatment planning systems. Proton beam intensity modulation, which relies on pencil beam scanning and energy modulation, is not applicable to electrons due to their significantly higher scattering rates and lower penetration depth. Current methods for electron intensity modulation, whether active or passive, have failed to achieve clinical utility due to impractical implementation, insufficient dose calculation accuracy, or lack of integration into standard clinical workflows. The absence of a simple, low-cost, and reliable method for electron intensity modulation has left a critical gap in the treatment of superficial cancers, where dose homogeneity is paramount for local control and toxicity reduction. No prior art has demonstrated a passive, physically realizable device capable of generating spatially varying electron fluence distributions through the strategic placement of small, isolated attenuating or transmitting elements within the beam path. The invention disclosed herein fills this gap by introducing a novel architecture that leverages the natural scattering properties of electrons to reconstruct a smooth, modulated dose profile from discrete, precisely arranged elements.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The invention introduces a novel method for passive radiotherapy intensity modulation for electron beams, referred to as PRIME—Passive Radiotherapy Intensity Modulator for Electrons. This method utilizes an array of small, isolated electron-blocking structures, termed Island Blocks, and/or electron-transparent pathways, termed Island Apertures, arranged within the collimating plane of an electron therapy system to create a spatially modulated fluence distribution. The intensity of the electron beam is controlled by varying the size, shape, spacing, and density of these elements, which are designed to attenuate or transmit electrons in proportion to the desired dose pattern. The invention enables precise, patient-specific modulation without requiring complex mechanical systems, real-time beam scanning, or helium-filled treatment heads. The method is low-cost, compatible with existing treatment planning systems, and can be fabricated using readily available materials and manufacturing techniques. The Island Blocks and Island Apertures are embedded within or integrated into a collimating insert, and their spatial arrangement is determined by an inverse planning algorithm that accounts for electron multiple Coulomb scattering to ensure the resulting dose distribution at depth matches the prescribed target. The PRIME method transforms the challenge of intensity modulation from an active, dynamic process into a passive, static design problem, making it practical for routine clinical use. The invention enables the delivery of homogeneous dose distributions to irregularly shaped superficial targets, thereby improving therapeutic outcomes in electron conformal therapy applications such as bolus ECT, segmented-field therapy, and modulated electron radiation therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- The method of selecting Island Blocks or Island Apertures begins with the determination of the desired intensity modulation pattern at the depth of the planning target volume, typically derived from a treatment plan that accounts for patient anatomy and dose constraints. The spatial distribution of these elements is then optimized using an inverse planning algorithm that calculates the required fractional attenuation at each point in the modulation plane, translating this into a corresponding diameter and spacing of either Island Blocks or Island Apertures. The shape of the Island Blocks and Island Apertures is preferably circular, as this geometry minimizes the surface-to-area ratio and reduces the likelihood of electron scattering from lateral walls. The separation between adjacent Island Blocks or Island Apertures is selected to ensure that the electron multiple Coulomb scattering occurring in air and tissue downstream is sufficient to blend the individual beamlets into a smooth, continuous dose distribution. The location of the Island Blocks or Island Apertures is positioned within the collimating insert, immediately upstream of the final beam aperture, such that their central axes align with the diverging rays emanating from the virtual source of the electron beam. The Island Blocks are implemented as solid, electron-blocking posts fabricated from a high-density, medically non-hazardous material such as tungsten alloy or lead-copper composite, while the Island Apertures are defined as precisely machined openings in the collimating insert itself. The collimating insert is constructed from a material thick enough to fully attenuate the highest energy electron beam used clinically, typically 20 MeV, and is designed to maintain structural integrity under repeated use. The height, shape, and spacing of the Island Blocks may vary across the modulation plane to achieve the desired intensity gradient, with larger blocks used for greater attenuation and smaller blocks for partial attenuation. Similarly, the height, shape, and spacing of the Island Apertures are varied to control the transmission of electrons, with larger apertures allowing more fluence and smaller apertures restricting it. The substrate supporting the Island Blocks is composed of a low-density, machineable foam such as polystyrene or polyurethane, which provides mechanical stability while contributing negligibly to electron attenuation or scattering. The central axes of both the Island Blocks and Island Apertures are oriented to coincide with the diverging rays from the virtual source, minimizing oblique incidence and reducing edge scattering effects. When the electron beam passes through the modulator, the Island Blocks absorb the majority of incident electrons, while the Island Apertures allow electrons to pass through with minimal perturbation. The modulation of electron beam intensity arises from the spatial variation in the fraction of the beam area blocked or transmitted, with the resulting fluence distribution at depth being reconstructed through the natural broadening effect of multiple Coulomb scattering. FIG. 1 illustrates a hexagonal grid of circular Island Blocks embedded in a foam substrate, positioned within the collimating insert. FIG. 2 depicts the relative electron fluence profiles at varying distances downstream, demonstrating the broadening of the attenuation kernel due to scattering. FIG. 3 shows the resulting intensity profile across a half-field of Island Blocks, illustrating how the local intensity reduction correlates with the fractional area blocked. Multiple Coulomb scattering causes the electron beamlets emerging from each Island Aperture or around each Island Block to recombine downstream, forming a continuous dose distribution that approximates the prescribed modulation. The effect of Island Blocks is to locally reduce the fluence, while the effect of Island Apertures is to locally increase it, with the combined use of both enabling full-range modulation from 0% to 100%. The distance between the modulator and the calculation plane is controlled to ensure sufficient recombination of beamlets, typically between 5 cm and 15 cm, depending on electron energy and desired smoothness. The materials used in the construction of the modulator are selected for their radiological properties, mechanical stability, and compatibility with clinical sterilization and handling protocols. The substrate is low-density and non-toxic, ensuring patient safety and ease of fabrication. The Island Blocks and Island Apertures are designed to be interchangeable and reusable across multiple patients, with each modulator calibrated to a specific treatment plan.

### EXAMPLE 1

- A prototype electron beam intensity modulator was constructed using cylindrical lead wires of 0.2 cm diameter and 2.0 cm thickness, embedded at regular intervals in a 2.0 cm thick sheet of polystyrene foam arranged in a hexagonal grid with a center-to-center spacing of 0.5 cm. The foam substrate was cut to fit within the collimating insert of a clinical electron accelerator, positioned immediately upstream of the final trimmer. The lead wires were inserted into pre-drilled holes in the foam, ensuring precise alignment and secure retention. The entire assembly was mounted in the treatment head of an Elekta Infinity linear accelerator, and relative dose measurements were acquired at a depth of 2.0 cm in a water phantom using a p-type electron diode detector with a 0.2 cm active diameter. The modulator was exposed to a 16 MeV electron beam with a 10 × 10 cm² field size at a source-to-surface distance of 100 cm. The measured dose profile along the central axis showed a consistent reduction in intensity of approximately 15% in the regions corresponding to the Island Blocks, with minimal variation between adjacent measurement points. The profile exhibited a smooth transition between blocked and unblocked regions, confirming that multiple Coulomb scattering had effectively blended the individual beamlets into a continuous modulation pattern. The prototype demonstrated that the PRIME method is physically realizable using simple, low-cost materials and can be integrated into existing clinical infrastructure without modification to the accelerator or treatment planning system.

### EXAMPLE 2

- The relative intensity profile of the prototype modulator was calculated using the Hogstrom pencil beam algorithm under identical conditions to the experimental setup. The algorithm modeled the electron fluence distribution generated by each Island Block as a Gaussian kernel, with the magnitude of attenuation determined by the fractional area blocked. The composite intensity profile was generated by summing the contributions of all individual kernels across the modulation plane. The calculated profile was compared with the measured profile obtained from the diode detector, showing an agreement within 1% across the entire modulated region. The minimum relative dose measured was 0.82, closely matching the theoretical value of 0.85 predicted by the formula for hexagonally packed circular blocks. The profile exhibited no significant oscillations or artifacts, confirming that the selected spacing and diameter were within the clinically acceptable range for achieving uniform intensity reduction. The close agreement between calculation and measurement validates the underlying physical model and establishes the feasibility of using inverse planning algorithms to design patient-specific modulators with high accuracy.

### EXAMPLE 3

- A patient-specific intensity modulator was designed for the treatment of a right buccal mucosa carcinoma using bolus electron conformal therapy. The desired intensity modulation pattern was derived from a prior treatment plan that required a 25% reduction in dose over a 5 cm × 5 cm region to correct for surface irregularities and achieve homogeneous target coverage. An inverse planning algorithm was employed to determine the optimal arrangement of circular Island Blocks on a hexagonal grid with a spacing of 0.5 cm. The algorithm calculated the required diameter of each block to achieve the local intensity reduction factor, resulting in a spatially varying pattern of 127 blocks with diameters ranging from 0.12 cm to 0.31 cm. The modulator was fabricated using a CNC-machined polystyrene substrate into which tungsten alloy pins were inserted with sub-millimeter precision. The completed modulator was verified using a 16 MeV electron beam and a 2D detector array, showing an average deviation of less than 2% from the planned intensity distribution at a depth of 2 cm. The modulator was successfully used in clinical treatment, resulting in improved dose homogeneity within the planning target volume and reduced hot spots in the underlying mucosal tissue. This example demonstrates the clinical applicability of the PRIME method for real-world patient treatments and establishes a pathway for routine implementation in radiation oncology departments.
