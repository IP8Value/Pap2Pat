## TECHNICAL FIELD

- The present invention relates generally to the field of artificial intelligence and natural language processing, and more specifically to systems and methods for reducing hallucinations in abstractive text summarization models through contrastive parameter ensembling. The invention provides a novel computational framework that enhances the factual accuracy of generated summaries without compromising lexical fluency, information recall, or computational efficiency, thereby addressing a critical limitation in state-of-the-art neural summarization architectures trained on noisy datasets.

## BACKGROUND

- Abstractive summarization systems have demonstrated remarkable progress in generating concise, human-like summaries from lengthy source documents, achieving high scores on traditional metrics such as ROUGE that measure lexical and syntactic overlap with reference summaries. However, these systems frequently produce content that is factually inconsistent with the source material—a phenomenon known as hallucination—where entities, relationships, or events are invented, misattributed, or distorted. Such errors are pervasive in models trained on widely used datasets such as XSUM and CNN/DM, where the reference summaries themselves contain factual inaccuracies or excessive abstraction. Human evaluations have consistently shown that even the most advanced models generate summaries with substantial factual errors, undermining their reliability in real-world applications such as legal documentation, medical reporting, and journalistic summarization.

- Prior approaches to mitigating hallucinations have relied primarily on post-hoc correction techniques, data filtering, or constrained decoding. Data filtering, for instance, involves removing training samples deemed factually inconsistent based on automated metrics, but this approach drastically reduces training data size and degrades summary quality by eliminating valuable linguistic patterns and diverse expression styles. Post-hoc correction methods require additional models or reranking mechanisms that increase inference latency and memory overhead, making them impractical for real-time deployment. Constrained decoding techniques impose rigid constraints during generation but often fail to generalize across diverse document types and lack the flexibility to adapt to nuanced factual relationships. Furthermore, existing ensemble methods that average model outputs or parameters do not distinguish between beneficial and harmful learned behaviors, resulting in marginal improvements at significant computational cost. These limitations highlight a fundamental gap: there is no effective mechanism to simultaneously leverage both clean and noisy training data to improve factual consistency while preserving overall summary quality.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

- The invention comprises a neural summarization framework that introduces a Contrastive Parameter Ensembling (CaPE) architecture, which refines a base summarization model by integrating parameters from two specialized models: an expert model and an anti-expert model. The base summarization model is first trained on the full training dataset, capturing general summarization patterns, fluency, and information coverage. Subsequently, an expert model is derived by fine-tuning the base model on a subset of training samples identified as having high factual consistency, as determined by automated factual metrics such as entity overlap precision and dependency arc entailment. Conversely, an anti-expert model is derived by fine-tuning the base model on a subset of training samples identified as having low factual consistency, characterized by high rates of entity mismatches or unentailed dependency arcs. These two fine-tuned models retain the same architecture and initialization as the base model, ensuring alignment in optimization trajectories and enabling meaningful parameter manipulation.

- Hallucinations in abstractive summarization arise when models learn to reproduce factual inaccuracies present in training data, particularly when reference summaries contain entities not mentioned in the source document or misrepresent semantic relationships. The invention recognizes that the anti-expert model, trained on the most erroneous samples, internalizes these hallucinatory patterns to a greater degree than the base model, while the expert model, trained on the most accurate samples, suppresses such errors. By contrastively combining the parameters of these models with the base model, the invention effectively amplifies the beneficial behaviors encoded in the expert while suppressing the detrimental behaviors encoded in the anti-expert. This is achieved through a linear combination of parameters, wherein the expert’s parameters are added to the base model and the anti-expert’s parameters are subtracted, resulting in a net adjustment that corrects for systematic hallucinations without introducing new distortions.

- The mixing coefficient, a scalar parameter, controls the relative influence of the expert and anti-expert contributions, allowing for fine-grained trade-offs between factual accuracy and other summary qualities such as ROUGE scores and information recall. For example, a higher mixing coefficient increases factual consistency at the expense of slight reductions in summary length or lexical diversity, while a lower coefficient preserves fluency with moderate gains in accuracy. This tunability distinguishes the invention from rigid filtering or post-processing methods that impose fixed constraints. The framework is agnostic to the specific factual metric used for data selection, enabling the use of entity overlap precision, dependency arc entailment, or other automated factuality indicators, and further allows for cross-metric ensembling—such as combining an expert trained on entity precision with an anti-expert trained on dependency entailment—to achieve broader factual robustness.

- The base summarization model is implemented using a transformer-based architecture pretrained on large-scale text corpora and fine-tuned for summarization tasks, such as BART or T5. The expert and anti-expert models are initialized from the same pre-trained weights as the base model and fine-tuned for a single epoch on their respective subsets, ensuring computational efficiency and parameter compatibility. The ensembling operation is performed offline during model training, resulting in a single consolidated model that requires no additional inference-time computation, unlike ensemble methods that require multiple forward passes. The final model is stored as a single set of weights and deployed like any standard summarization model, maintaining compatibility with existing infrastructure.

- FIG. 1 illustrates the overall architecture of the CaPE framework, depicting the flow from the original training dataset through data selection, expert and anti-expert fine-tuning, and parameter ensembling to produce the final model. The data selection module applies automated factual metrics to classify training samples into clean and noisy subsets, using predefined thresholds for entity overlap precision and dependency arc entailment error counts. The fine-tuning module applies gradient descent to update the base model parameters on each subset, preserving the original architecture. The ensembling module computes the final parameters by applying the mixing coefficient to the difference between the expert and anti-expert parameters and adding this delta to the base model parameters.

- Factual consistency is quantified using multiple automated metrics, including entity overlap precision (E-Psrc), which measures the proportion of named entities in the summary that appear in the source document, and dependency arc entailment (DAE), which evaluates whether grammatical relationships in the summary are logically supported by the source. These metrics are used not only for data selection but also for evaluation, alongside complementary metrics such as BERTScore, QuestEval, QAFactEval, and MNLI entailment scores. The invention demonstrates that the CaPE framework consistently improves performance across all these metrics, even those not used during training, indicating that the contrastive ensembling induces a generalizable reduction in hallucination tendencies.

- The system is implemented on a computing device comprising a processor, memory, and machine-readable storage medium containing executable instructions that, when executed, cause the device to perform the steps of training the base model, selecting clean and noisy samples, fine-tuning the expert and anti-expert models, computing the ensembled parameters, and storing the final model. The framework may be deployed in a networked environment where a server processes training data, performs model training, and distributes the final model to user devices via a network interface. The Summarization module includes submodules for Base Training, Data Filtering, Fine-Tuning, and Mixing Experts, each implemented as executable code modules that interact through defined data interfaces. The system supports integration with external databases containing training corpora, user inputs, and model outputs, and can be accessed via a user interface application for monitoring performance, adjusting the mixing coefficient, or retraining with new data.

- Alternative ensembling methods, such as attention-weighted parameter averaging or dynamic coefficient scheduling, are contemplated but found to be less effective than the proposed contrastive subtraction mechanism. The invention has been validated on benchmark datasets including XSUM and CNN/DM, demonstrating superior performance over baseline models, ensembles, and post-processing methods across all factual consistency metrics while maintaining or improving ROUGE scores and information recall. Human evaluations confirm that summaries generated by the CaPE model are rated significantly more factually accurate than those from baseline systems, with high inter-annotator agreement.

- The invention provides a scalable, efficient, and generalizable solution to the problem of hallucination in abstractive summarization, enabling the faithful reproduction of factual content from source documents without sacrificing fluency or requiring costly inference-time interventions. The framework is applicable to any neural text generation system where training data contains systematic errors, and its parameter-space ensembling approach may be extended to other tasks such as question answering, dialogue generation, and machine translation.
