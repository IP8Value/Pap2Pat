## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to noninvasive optical systems and methods for simultaneously measuring tissue oxygenation and cellular metabolic state in living organisms, particularly for clinical monitoring of cerebral and organ function. More specifically, the invention encompasses apparatuses and techniques utilizing a super-continuum laser-based infrared spectroscopy system to detect and quantify the redox state of cytochrome c oxidase alongside concentrations of oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin, enabling real-time assessment of metabolic demand and hemodynamic supply in brain tissue and other organs. The invention is particularly suited for deployment in emergency, critical care, surgical, and neurological monitoring environments where continuous, noninvasive, and artifact-resistant measurement of tissue viability is essential for guiding therapeutic interventions and predicting clinical outcomes.

## BACKGROUND

Tissue oxygenation is a fundamental physiological parameter that reflects the balance between oxygen delivery and consumption at the cellular level. In clinical settings, the assessment of oxygenation status has long relied on hemodynamic indicators derived from changes in blood volume and composition, particularly through functional magnetic resonance imaging and functional near-infrared spectroscopy. Functional magnetic resonance imaging exploits the blood oxygenation level-dependent signal to infer regional neural activity by detecting shifts in the magnetic properties of hemoglobin as it transitions between oxygenated and deoxygenated states. Similarly, functional near-infrared spectroscopy measures the differential absorption of near-infrared light by oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin to estimate changes in tissue perfusion and oxygen utilization. Despite their widespread adoption, these techniques are fundamentally limited in that they provide only indirect surrogates of cellular metabolism, measuring vascular responses rather than the biochemical activity of mitochondria themselves. This limitation becomes critically significant in pathological conditions such as traumatic brain injury, hemorrhagic shock, or ischemia, where cellular metabolic dysfunction may occur independently of hemodynamic changes, leading to false-negative assessments and delayed intervention.

Cytochrome c oxidase, the terminal enzyme of the mitochondrial electron transport chain, plays a central role in cellular energy production by catalyzing the reduction of molecular oxygen to water during adenosine triphosphate synthesis. The redox state of cytochrome c oxidase—the ratio of its oxidized to reduced forms—serves as a direct, real-time indicator of mitochondrial metabolic activity and cellular energy demand. When cellular ATP consumption increases, such as during neuronal activation, cytochrome c oxidase becomes increasingly reduced as electrons accumulate in the enzyme’s active centers, resulting in a measurable decrease in its oxidized spectral signature. Conversely, when oxygen delivery is compromised, the enzyme remains in a reduced state due to substrate limitation, providing an early and specific biomarker of impending cellular distress. The spectroscopic signature of cytochrome c oxidase, particularly within the near-infrared and short-wave infrared range between 700 and 900 nanometers, is distinct from that of hemoglobin but overlaps sufficiently to create significant spectral interference, necessitating high-resolution, high-signal-to-noise measurement capabilities to isolate its contribution from background noise and competing chromophores.

Historically, attempts to measure cytochrome c oxidase noninvasively have been constrained by the low brightness and broad spectral emission profiles of conventional light sources such as tungsten-halogen lamps. These sources suffer from poor coupling efficiency into optical fibers, limited spectral power density, and susceptibility to environmental interference, resulting in insufficient sensitivity to detect the weak, broad, and featureless absorption features of cytochrome c oxidase against the dominant signals of hemoglobin and tissue scattering. Prior art systems have also lacked the temporal and spectral resolution required to distinguish true metabolic signals from motion artifacts, physiological drift, or extracranial contamination. Moreover, existing approaches typically rely on single-arm measurement configurations that are vulnerable to laser intensity fluctuations, temperature variations, and optical path instabilities, further degrading measurement fidelity. Consequently, there exists a critical and unmet need for a noninvasive, robust, and high-fidelity system capable of simultaneously and quantitatively resolving the redox state of cytochrome c oxidase with high signal-to-noise ratio, independent of confounding hemodynamic signals, and suitable for continuous monitoring in dynamic clinical environments.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel apparatus and method for noninvasively measuring the metabolic and oxygenation state of biological tissue through the simultaneous detection of cytochrome c oxidase redox dynamics and hemoglobin concentration changes using a super-continuum laser-based infrared spectroscopy system. The apparatus comprises a high-brightness, all-fiber integrated super-continuum laser source that generates a coherent, broadband optical output spanning the near-infrared and short-wave infrared spectrum from approximately 670 to 2500 nanometers, with sufficient spectral power density to enable detection of cytochrome c oxidase absorption features that are otherwise obscured by noise in conventional systems. The laser output is amplified through a multi-stage fiber amplifier configuration, including a preamplifier optimized for noise suppression and a power amplifier designed to minimize nonlinear distortion, resulting in a stable, high-average-power output with excellent spatial coherence and spectral flatness. The amplified light is directed through a tunable spectrometer, an optical chopper, and a polarizer to ensure polarization stability and temporal modulation of the light source, followed by a beam splitter configured in a dual-arm differential configuration to generate a reference beam and a sample beam.

The sample beam is delivered to the target tissue via a fiber-optic probe with a fixed inter-fiber spacing, while the reference beam is routed through a matched optical path containing a control medium to compensate for source fluctuations and environmental interference. Reflected light from the tissue is collected by the same probe and combined with the reference signal at a pair of silicon photodetectors, each coupled to a synchronized lock-in amplifier tuned to the chopper frequency. The outputs of the lock-in amplifiers are processed by a programmable processor executing machine-readable instructions that apply a modified Beer-Lambert law algorithm to calculate absolute concentration changes of oxygenated hemoglobin, deoxygenated hemoglobin, and cytochrome c oxidase based on wavelength-dependent attenuation profiles. The system achieves a substantial improvement in signal-to-noise ratio through the combined effects of the super-continuum laser’s high brightness, polarization filtering, dual-arm differential subtraction, and lock-in detection, enabling the isolation of the cytochrome c oxidase signal from hemodynamic artifacts, motion noise, and extracranial interference. The apparatus further enables the determination of tissue metabolic state by quantifying the redox state of cytochrome c oxidase independently of oxygenation changes, thereby distinguishing between metabolic demand and oxygen supply, and permits the identification of organ or tissue functionality through the correlation or dis-correlation of these two physiological parameters.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention provides a comprehensive system for noninvasive interrogation of tissue metabolic and oxygenation states through the simultaneous measurement of cytochrome c oxidase redox dynamics and hemoglobin concentration changes. Cytochrome c oxidase, as the terminal enzyme in the mitochondrial electron transport chain, directly reflects the cellular demand for adenosine triphosphate, with its redox state shifting toward the reduced form during increased metabolic activity and remaining reduced during oxygen deprivation. In contrast, oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin serve as indicators of vascular supply and perfusion, with their relative concentrations governed by blood flow, oxygen extraction, and vascular tone. The key innovation of the present invention lies in its ability to resolve these two distinct physiological signals—metabolic demand and hemodynamic supply—simultaneously and independently, overcoming the fundamental limitation of prior hemodynamic imaging modalities that conflate the two.

The super-continuum laser infrared spectroscopy system employed in this invention utilizes a modulational instability-initiated super-continuum source based on a master oscillator power amplifier architecture, wherein a nanosecond-pulsed semiconductor seed laser is amplified through ytterbium-doped fiber stages before being coupled into a cascade of nonlinear fibers. The first segment of standard single-mode fiber induces modulational instability, converting the pump pulses into a train of ultra-short solitons, while a subsequent nonlinear fiber generates spectral broadening through self-phase modulation, four-wave mixing, and the Raman effect, producing a continuous spectrum extending from 670 to 2500 nanometers. This all-fiber design eliminates free-space components, reduces alignment sensitivity, and enhances system robustness, making it suitable for clinical deployment. The high brightness and spatial coherence of the super-continuum output enable efficient coupling into single-mode delivery fibers, achieving spectral power densities nearly an order of magnitude greater than conventional lamp-based systems, which is critical for detecting the weak absorption features of cytochrome c oxidase.

The dual-arm differential measurement configuration, combined with a polarizer and lock-in amplifiers, effectively cancels out source intensity drift, environmental noise, and detector instability, while the temporal modulation introduced by the optical chopper allows the system to reject ambient light and other non-synchronous interference. The use of a 99:1 beam splitter ensures that the majority of optical power is directed to the tissue, while the reference arm provides a stable baseline for normalization. The fiber-based probe, with a fixed inter-fiber spacing of approximately 2 to 3 centimeters, enables deep tissue penetration into the cerebral cortex or other organs, and the system’s sensitivity permits the detection of concentration changes in cytochrome c oxidase at levels below 10% of those of hemoglobin, a feat previously unattainable with noninvasive methods. The system’s ability to distinguish cytochrome c oxidase signals from hemodynamic responses, skin interference, and motion artifacts arises from the high spectral resolution, the differential measurement architecture, and the precise temporal synchronization of acquisition with the modulated light source.

Clinical applications of the invention include the early detection of traumatic brain injury, stroke, and hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy, where metabolic dysfunction precedes structural damage and hemodynamic changes. The system is also applicable in the operating room for monitoring cerebral metabolism during neurosurgery, in the intensive care unit for assessing organ viability during shock or sepsis, and in emergency settings for triaging patients with suspected internal hemorrhage. The system’s portability, enabled by its all-fiber design and integrated cart-based configuration, allows for transport between clinical departments and deployment in field or pre-hospital environments. The invention further extends to other tissues and organs, including the kidney, liver, and skeletal muscle, where simultaneous assessment of oxygenation and metabolism can guide resuscitation strategies and predict recovery potential. The system’s high signal-to-noise ratio and artifact rejection capabilities enable reliable measurements even in motion-prone environments, and its ability to quantify absolute concentration changes provides a quantitative metric for longitudinal monitoring and therapeutic evaluation.

The empirical validation of the invention includes laboratory measurements of purified cytochrome c oxidase, demonstrating spectral absorption features consistent with published literature and confirming the system’s specificity. Human pilot studies during physiological challenges such as breath-holding and cognitive attention tasks demonstrate that the system accurately captures the expected hemodynamic response in oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin while simultaneously revealing a decrease in the oxidized form of cytochrome c oxidase during increased neural activity, consistent with elevated mitochondrial ATP demand. The system’s performance exceeds that of conventional functional near-infrared spectroscopy in both sensitivity and specificity, particularly in distinguishing true metabolic signals from vascular artifacts. The cart-based prototype integrates the laser, spectrometer, lock-in amplifiers, and processing unit into a single, mechanically stabilized unit capable of withstanding transport and clinical handling, with software providing real-time visualization of hemodynamic and metabolic parameters. The invention thus provides a transformative tool for noninvasive, continuous, and quantitative assessment of tissue viability, enabling earlier diagnosis, more precise intervention, and improved clinical outcomes across a broad spectrum of medical conditions.

### Empirical Examples

The invention was empirically validated through a series of controlled laboratory and human subject experiments. In vitro measurements of bovine heart cytochrome c oxidase in oxidized and reduced states were conducted using a laboratory configuration comprising the super-continuum laser source, a tunable spectrometer, an optical chopper, a polarizer, a 50:50 beam splitter, and dual lock-in amplifiers synchronized to the chopper frequency. The absorption spectrum of cytochrome c oxidase, measured between 750 and 900 nanometers, exhibited a broad, featureless peak centered near 825 nanometers, with spectral characteristics closely matching published reference data. Temporal drift in the absorption peak under sealed conditions suggested sensitivity to subtle changes in redox equilibrium, confirming the system’s capacity to resolve dynamic enzyme states.

For human testing, a modified fiber probe with a 2-millimeter diameter borosilicate fiber bundle and a 3-centimeter source-detector separation was employed to measure cerebral responses during physiological and cognitive challenges. A blood pressure occlusion test on the forearm demonstrated that while oxygenated hemoglobin decreased and deoxygenated hemoglobin increased during cuff inflation, the cytochrome c oxidase redox state remained statistically unchanged, consistent with the low metabolic demand of resting forearm muscle and validating the system’s ability to distinguish tissue-specific metabolic responses. In a breath-holding protocol on the forehead, the system recorded a significant increase in oxygenated hemoglobin concurrent with a measurable decrease in the oxidized form of cytochrome c oxidase, aligning with known physiological responses to voluntary apnea and confirming the system’s sensitivity to cerebral metabolic activation.

A pilot cognitive attention test involving 25 healthy human participants was conducted using an interleaved probe configuration that co-registered measurements with a commercial functional near-infrared spectroscopy system. After stringent data screening to exclude motion artifacts and outlier participants, the remaining data revealed a consistent and significant increase in oxygenated hemoglobin during attention task periods, accompanied by a corresponding decrease in the oxidized cytochrome c oxidase signal. This dis-correlation between hemodynamic supply and metabolic demand was observed across multiple trials and was not reliably detectable using conventional fNIRS alone. The system’s ability to resolve this metabolic signature with high fidelity, even under suboptimal acquisition conditions, demonstrates its superiority in detecting true cellular activity. The results were further corroborated by comparing the system’s performance against arterial blood gas measurements and mean arterial pressure in swine models of hemorrhagic shock, where the cytochrome c oxidase signal exhibited predictable shifts during partial aortic occlusion and reperfusion, validating its utility in real-time clinical monitoring. These empirical examples collectively confirm the invention’s capacity to provide simultaneous, quantitative, and artifact-resistant measurements of tissue oxygenation and metabolic state, establishing its clinical relevance and technical feasibility.
