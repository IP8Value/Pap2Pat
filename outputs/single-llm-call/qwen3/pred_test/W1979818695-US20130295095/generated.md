# DESCRIPTION

Alzheimer disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the gradual decline of cognitive functions, including memory, language, and executive reasoning, ultimately leading to profound impairment in daily functioning and independence. It is the most common cause of dementia among older adults, affecting millions worldwide, with prevalence expected to rise dramatically as global populations age. The disease manifests through a combination of behavioral, psychological, and neurological symptoms, often beginning with mild forgetfulness and progressing to severe disorientation, loss of emotional regulation, and motor dysfunction. Pathologically, Alzheimer disease is defined by the accumulation of extracellular amyloid plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein, alongside widespread synaptic loss, neuronal death, and chronic neuroinflammation. Despite decades of research, the precise sequence of molecular events initiating the disease remains incompletely understood, and no curative therapy currently exists. The clinical diagnosis remains challenging, often relying on exclusionary criteria and late-stage symptomatology, as biomarkers for early detection have been limited in specificity and accessibility. The lack of reliable tools for preclinical identification has hindered timely intervention and the development of disease-modifying treatments, underscoring an urgent need for novel diagnostic and therapeutic strategies targeting the earliest pathogenic drivers of neurodegeneration.

The importance of amyloid-β (Aβ) oligomers in Alzheimer disease pathology lies in their established role as the primary neurotoxic species responsible for synaptic dysfunction and neuronal loss prior to the formation of insoluble amyloid plaques. While amyloid plaques have long been considered the hallmark of Alzheimer disease, accumulating evidence indicates that soluble, prefibrillar aggregates of Aβ—particularly low-molecular-weight oligomers—are more closely correlated with cognitive decline than plaque burden itself. These oligomeric forms of Aβ disrupt synaptic transmission, impair long-term potentiation, induce oxidative stress, activate microglia and astrocytes, and trigger inflammatory cascades that exacerbate neuronal damage. Importantly, these toxic oligomers are not merely intermediates in the pathway to fibril formation but are stable, bioactive entities capable of propagating pathological changes across neural networks. Their ability to spread between neurons and seed further aggregation suggests a prion-like mechanism underlying disease progression. Furthermore, oligomers can accumulate intracellularly within neuronal processes and dendritic spines, where they interfere with intracellular signaling, mitochondrial function, and protein homeostasis, contributing to early synaptic failure before extracellular plaques become detectable. The selective toxicity of these soluble assemblies, combined with their presence in the earliest stages of the disease, positions them as critical targets for both diagnostic detection and therapeutic intervention.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Alzheimer disease is a complex, multifactorial neurodegenerative condition marked by the progressive accumulation of misfolded proteins, primarily amyloid-β and tau, leading to synaptic degeneration and neuronal death. Its clinical presentation includes insidious memory loss, disorientation, language deficits, and eventual loss of autonomy, with a pathological triad consisting of extracellular amyloid plaques, intracellular neurofibrillary tangles, and chronic neuroinflammation. The amyloid-β peptide, derived from the sequential proteolytic cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein by β- and γ-secretases, exists in multiple isoforms, with Aβ40 and Aβ42 being the most prevalent. Among these, Aβ42 exhibits a greater propensity for aggregation due to its hydrophobic C-terminal residues, making it the primary component of amyloid deposits. However, the full-length Aβ peptides are not the sole contributors to pathology; N-truncated variants, particularly those beginning at position 3 with a pyroglutamate modification, have emerged as highly pathogenic species. These N-terminally modified peptides, termed AβpE3, arise through enzymatic cyclization of glutamate residues by glutaminyl cyclase, a process that stabilizes the peptide against degradation and dramatically enhances its aggregation kinetics. AβpE3 peptides have been detected in human Alzheimer disease brains at elevated levels compared to age-matched controls, and their presence correlates strongly with disease severity and early cognitive impairment. The pyroglutamate modification confers resistance to aminopeptidases and promotes the formation of stable, neurotoxic oligomers that are more aggregation-prone than their full-length counterparts. These oligomers exhibit distinct structural properties, forming compact, β-sheet-rich assemblies that resist clearance and propagate pathology through seeding mechanisms. Glutaminyl cyclase, the enzyme responsible for generating AβpE3, is upregulated in Alzheimer disease brains and localized to neurons containing intracellular Aβ accumulations, suggesting a direct role in disease progression. Therapeutic strategies targeting glutaminyl cyclase have been explored to reduce AβpE3 formation, yet these approaches have shown limited efficacy in clinical trials, likely because they do not neutralize preformed toxic oligomers or clear existing aggregates. Transgenic mouse models overexpressing mutant human amyloid precursor protein, such as APP/PS1KI and 5XFAD mice, recapitulate key aspects of human Alzheimer disease, including age-dependent accumulation of intraneuronal Aβ, synaptic dysfunction, and behavioral deficits. These models have been instrumental in demonstrating that intraneuronal Aβ oligomers precede extracellular plaque deposition and correlate with early cognitive decline. Soluble oligomers, particularly those containing β-sheet structures, are now widely regarded as the most neurotoxic forms of Aβ, capable of disrupting membrane integrity, inducing calcium dyshomeostasis, and activating microglial inflammatory responses. In contrast, mature amyloid fibrils, while histologically prominent, appear to be relatively inert endpoints of aggregation. Current therapeutic approaches, including monoclonal antibodies targeting full-length Aβ or small molecule inhibitors of secretases, have largely failed to halt cognitive decline in clinical trials, largely because they do not selectively neutralize the most pathogenic oligomeric species. The failure of these strategies underscores the need for novel agents capable of specifically recognizing and neutralizing AβpE3 oligomers, which represent a critical, previously underappreciated node in the pathogenic cascade.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The technical problem addressed by this invention is the lack of specific diagnostic tools and therapeutic agents capable of detecting and neutralizing the most neurotoxic forms of amyloid-β in Alzheimer disease, particularly the N-terminally truncated and pyroglutamate-modified oligomers of AβpE3, which are not effectively targeted by existing methods. The solution lies in the identification and development of novel binding molecules, particularly monoclonal antibodies, that selectively recognize and bind to low-molecular-weight oligomeric assemblies of AβpE3 without cross-reacting with full-length Aβ1-42 or other non-pathogenic Aβ species. These binding molecules enable the detection of AβpE3 oligomers in biological samples, allowing for the early and accurate diagnosis of Alzheimer disease and related amyloidopathies, as well as the monitoring and prediction of therapeutic efficacy. The invention further provides a method for the passive immunization of subjects with these binding molecules, resulting in the reduction of AβpE3 oligomers, decreased amyloid plaque burden, and normalization of behavioral deficits in preclinical models. The antibodies 9D5H6 and 8C4D2, exemplified herein, specifically bind to a conformational epitope unique to AβpE3 oligomers, enabling their detection in neurons, cerebrovascular walls, and biological fluids such as plasma, while showing no reactivity with monomeric or fibrillar forms of Aβ1-42. These antibodies serve as powerful diagnostic tools for differentiating Alzheimer disease from healthy aging and other neurodegenerative conditions, and as therapeutic agents capable of inhibiting the further aggregation of AβpE3, neutralizing its toxicity, and promoting its clearance. The invention also encompasses the use of these antibodies in sandwich ELISA assays to quantify AβpE3 oligomer levels in plasma, where reduced concentrations correlate with disease progression, providing a novel biomarker signature. The method of diagnosing a subject suspected of suffering from an amyloid-related disorder involves determining the amount of AβpE3 oligomers in a biological sample and comparing it to a reference amount, wherein a statistically significant deviation indicates the presence of the disorder. Similarly, the efficacy of a treatment can be monitored by tracking changes in AβpE3 oligomer levels over time, and the predicted efficacy of a therapeutic intervention can be inferred from the magnitude of reduction observed in early treatment phases. The invention further introduces binding molecules capable of inhibiting Aβ-oligomerization, including not only antibodies but also peptides, peptidomimetics, and small molecules, all of which can be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions for the treatment and prevention of amyloid-related disorders. The advantage of this invention over existing glutaminyl cyclase inhibitors is that it directly targets and neutralizes the toxic oligomers themselves, rather than merely preventing their formation, thereby offering a more immediate and potent therapeutic effect. The invention thus provides a paradigm-shifting approach to Alzheimer disease by focusing on a specific, highly pathogenic Aβ species that has been previously overlooked, enabling both precise diagnosis and effective intervention.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The binding molecules of the present invention, particularly the monoclonal antibodies 9D5H6 and 8C4D2, are characterized by their exclusive recognition of low-molecular-weight oligomeric forms of AβpE3-42, with no detectable cross-reactivity to monomeric Aβ1-42, fibrillar Aβ, or other N-truncated variants such as AβpE1-42. Immunohistochemical analysis of brain tissue from APP/PS1KI and 5XFAD transgenic mice reveals abundant intraneuronal staining with these antibodies, beginning at six months of age and progressively increasing with age, while extracellular plaque staining remains minimal or absent until later stages, consistent with the intracellular origin of these toxic oligomers. In human post-mortem brain specimens from patients with sporadic and familial Alzheimer disease, strong immunoreactivity is observed within neurons of the hippocampus, cortex, and brainstem, as well as in cerebral blood vessels affected by cerebral amyloid angiopathy, whereas healthy control specimens show negligible or no staining. The staining pattern is distinct from that of antibodies targeting full-length Aβ, such as 4G8 or W0-2, which label both plaques and diffuse deposits, whereas 9D5H6 and 8C4D2 specifically highlight intraneuronal accumulations and vascular deposits associated with early pathology. These antibodies recognize AβpE3-42 oligomers in SDS-resistant complexes as demonstrated by Western blot and dot blot assays, where a single band of approximately 10–20 kDa is observed under denaturing conditions, corresponding to oligomeric assemblies, with no signal detected in samples containing only Aβ1-42. This specificity renders them uniquely suited for diagnostic assays, including sandwich ELISA, in which 9D5H6 serves as the capture antibody and a biotinylated antibody targeting the pyroglutamate residue (e.g., 2-48) serves as the detection reagent, enabling quantification of AβpE3 oligomers in plasma, cerebrospinal fluid, and brain homogenates. The levels of these oligomers in plasma from Alzheimer disease patients are significantly reduced compared to age-matched healthy controls, likely due to their sequestration within neurons and vascular walls, providing a novel biomarker for disease detection. The method of diagnosing a subject suspected of suffering from an amyloid-related disorder involves obtaining a biological sample, such as plasma, cerebrospinal fluid, or a tissue biopsy, determining the concentration of AβpE3 oligomers using the binding molecules of the invention, and comparing this value to a reference amount derived from a population of healthy individuals or a validated baseline. A statistically significant deviation from this reference, whether elevated or reduced, is indicative of the presence of the disorder. The method of monitoring the efficacy of a treatment involves serial determination of AβpE3 oligomer levels in the same subject over time, with a reduction in concentration correlating with therapeutic benefit. Similarly, the method of predicting the efficacy of a treatment involves measuring the initial change in oligomer levels following the initiation of therapy, where a rapid and substantial decrease predicts a favorable clinical outcome. The binding molecules of the invention are capable of inhibiting Aβ-oligomerization by binding to the nucleation sites of AβpE3 monomers and preventing their assembly into higher-order toxic aggregates, as demonstrated in Thioflavin T aggregation assays and neurotoxicity models. The pharmaceutical composition of the invention comprises one or more of these binding molecules in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for systemic or intracerebral administration, and may include stabilizers, preservatives, and excipients appropriate for parenteral delivery. The binding molecule may be an antibody, antibody fragment, peptide, peptidomimetic, or small molecule, with the preferred embodiment being a fully humanized monoclonal antibody derived from the murine 9D5H6 and 8C4D2 sequences. The variable regions of these antibodies contain specific complementarity-determining regions that confer their unique specificity, and nucleic acid sequences encoding these regions are provided for recombinant expression in mammalian, insect, or bacterial host cells. The invention further encompasses kits for diagnosis, comprising the binding molecule, detection reagents, buffers, and instructions for use, as well as vectors and host cells for the production of therapeutic quantities of the antibody. The method of treating or preventing an amyloid-related disorder involves administering an effective dose of the binding molecule to a subject, resulting in the reduction of intraneuronal and vascular AβpE3 oligomers, decreased amyloid plaque load, and improved cognitive and behavioral outcomes, as demonstrated in transgenic mouse models following passive immunization. The invention thus provides a comprehensive platform for the detection, monitoring, prediction, and treatment of Alzheimer disease and related amyloidopathies through the selective targeting of a previously unrecognized pathogenic entity.

### EXAMPLES

Antibodies 9D5H6 and 8C4D2 were generated by immunizing Balb/c mice with synthetic AβpE3-38 peptide, followed by hybridoma generation and screening for selective binding to AβpE3-42 oligomers. Hybridoma clones were selected based on ELISA reactivity against AβpE3-42 and absence of binding to Aβ1-42 or AβpE1-42. Size-exclusion chromatography of synthetic AβpE3-42 revealed distinct oligomeric peaks at 4–10-mers, which were selectively recognized by 9D5H6 in dot blot and Western blot assays, whereas Aβ1-42 fractions showed no signal. Under denaturing conditions, 9D5H6 detected a single band at approximately 10–20 kDa corresponding to AβpE3-42 oligomers, with no cross-reactivity to Aβ1-42. In Thioflavin T aggregation assays, AβpE3-42 exhibited rapid oligomer formation without a prolonged lag phase, and the addition of 9D5H6 at a 1:76 molar ratio significantly suppressed the formation of higher-order aggregates while leaving early oligomers unaffected. Neurotoxicity assays using SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells demonstrated that AβpE3-42 induced significant cell death, which was completely abrogated by co-incubation with 9D5H6, whereas Aβ1-42 toxicity was unaffected by the antibody. Immunohistochemistry of 5XFAD mouse brains at 3, 6, and 12 months of age revealed age-dependent intraneuronal accumulation of AβpE3 oligomers, with no extracellular plaque staining until late stages. Double-labeling with Iba-1 confirmed co-localization of 9D5H6-positive material within microglia, suggesting phagocytic clearance, while GFAP staining showed no association with astrocytes. In human Alzheimer disease brain tissue, 9D5H6 exhibited robust intraneuronal and vascular staining in sporadic and familial cases, including those with APP or presenilin mutations, whereas healthy controls showed no reactivity. A sandwich ELISA using 9D5H6 as capture and 2-48 as detection antibody revealed a 46% reduction in plasma AβpE3 oligomer levels in Alzheimer disease patients compared to healthy controls. Passive immunization of 4.5-month-old 5XFAD mice with 9D5H6 for six weeks resulted in a significant reduction of AβpE3, Aβ40, and Aβ42 plaque load in the hippocampus and cortex, accompanied by normalization of anxiety-like behavior in the elevated plus maze test. Brain lysates from treated mice showed decreased levels of soluble AβpE3 in both TBS and SDS fractions, confirming intracellular clearance. No significant changes were observed in PBS-injected controls. Cross-reactivity studies in non-human primates and other organs confirmed specificity for AβpE3 oligomers in the central nervous system. These results establish the diagnostic and therapeutic utility of the binding molecules of the invention.
