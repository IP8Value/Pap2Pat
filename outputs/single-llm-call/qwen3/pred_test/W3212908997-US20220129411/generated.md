## BACKGROUND

Quantum circuits form the foundational architecture for executing quantum algorithms on physical quantum hardware, encoding unitary transformations as sequences of elementary quantum gates. These circuits are composed of single-qubit and two-qubit operations that manipulate the quantum state of a system through coherent evolution. Among the most widely used gate sets are those derived from the Clifford group, which includes the Hadamard (H), Phase (S), and controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates, along with the Pauli operators X, Y, and Z. The Clifford group is of particular significance because it preserves the stabilizer formalism under conjugation, enabling efficient classical simulation of certain quantum processes and forming the backbone of many quantum error correction protocols. In practical quantum computing systems, such as those based on superconducting qubits or trapped ions, two-qubit entangling gates like CNOT and CZ are significantly more error-prone and slower than single-qubit gates. Consequently, minimizing the number of these entangling operations is critical to improving circuit fidelity and reducing overall computational latency. Despite the theoretical understanding of Clifford circuits and their asymptotic synthesis bounds, the practical challenge of generating minimal-depth, low-gate-count implementations for real-world applications remains unsolved at scale. Conventional approaches to circuit optimization have relied heavily on template matching, a technique that identifies recurring gate patterns corresponding to the identity operation and replaces them with shorter equivalent sequences. While effective in reducing redundancy, this method suffers from a fundamental limitation: it operates locally and cannot account for global dependencies introduced by entangling gates that couple distant qubits. Furthermore, template matching is often applied without regard to the temporal ordering of operations or the structural constraints imposed by the Clifford group’s algebraic properties, leading to suboptimal reductions in gate count.

Another widely adopted optimization strategy is peephole optimization, which examines small, localized subcircuits—typically spanning two or three qubits—and replaces them with precomputed optimal equivalents drawn from a lookup table. This method has proven successful in classical reversible computing and has been adapted for quantum circuits. However, its applicability to Clifford circuits is severely constrained by the exponential growth of the Clifford group with the number of qubits. For systems beyond six qubits, it becomes computationally infeasible to enumerate and store all possible optimal subcircuit realizations, rendering traditional peephole optimization ineffective for larger, industrially relevant circuits. Moreover, conventional peephole techniques assume that the subcircuit being optimized is completely decoupled from the rest of the system, an assumption that rarely holds in practice due to the presence of entangling gates that connect the local region to external qubits. When such entangling gates are present, the unitary implemented by the subcircuit cannot be isolated, and the optimization process fails to produce a valid global transformation. As a result, existing methods are unable to scale beyond small problem sizes, leaving a critical gap between theoretically optimal synthesis and practically deployable circuit implementations. This limitation is especially acute in applications such as quantum error correction, where large-scale Clifford circuits are routinely required to encode logical qubits, and even minor reductions in gate count can yield substantial improvements in fault-tolerance thresholds. Without a method that can systematically handle entangling connections across qubit partitions and optimize non-local subcircuits without exhaustive precomputation, the full potential of Clifford-based quantum algorithms remains unrealized.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel system and method for optimizing Clifford quantum circuits by combining partitioned template matching with symbolic peephole optimization, thereby overcoming the scalability and effectiveness limitations of prior approaches. The invention operates on a quantum circuit represented as a sequence of Clifford gates and systematically reduces its total number of entangling gates, particularly CNOT and CZ gates, while preserving the overall unitary transformation. The system comprises a partition component that restructures the input circuit into three distinct stages: a computation stage containing only non-Pauli Clifford gates, a swap stage containing all SWAP operations, and a Pauli stage containing all single-qubit Pauli operators. This partitioning is achieved by leveraging the stabilizer formalism to push Pauli and SWAP gates to the end of the circuit without altering the underlying unitary, thereby isolating the entangling gate structure for focused optimization. A template component then applies a curated library of Clifford-specific templates to the computation stage, replacing redundant gate sequences with shorter equivalents while respecting the algebraic constraints of the group. A floating component facilitates the relocation of Hadamard and Phase gates to maximize template applicability, and a SWAP component optimizes the swap stage by aligning SWAP gates with adjacent entangling operations to reduce their effective cost. A symbolic component introduces symbolic Pauli gates to represent the influence of entangling gates connecting a selected subset of qubits to the remainder of the system, enabling the optimization of otherwise intractable subcircuits. A peephole component then performs dynamic programming-based optimization on these symbolic subcircuits, minimizing the combined cost of entangling gates and symbolic Pauli gates while preserving temporal order and phase consistency. The optimized circuit is then reconstructed by lifting the symbolic transformations back into physical gates. The invention may be implemented as a computer-implemented method, a software system executed on a classical processor, or as a hardware-accelerated module integrated into a quantum compiler stack. Alternative embodiments include iterative execution of the entire optimization pipeline, randomized resynthesis of the initial circuit followed by repeated optimization, and integration with fault-tolerant quantum error correction protocols to reduce the overhead of logical qubit encoding. The invention is further embodied as a computer-readable storage medium storing program instructions that, when executed by one or more processors, cause the performance of the described optimization steps. The resulting optimized circuits exhibit significantly reduced gate counts compared to conventional synthesis methods, enabling more efficient execution on current and near-term quantum hardware platforms.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

It is understood that the following description is not intended to limit the scope of the invention but rather to provide a detailed exemplification of its preferred embodiments. The invention is not restricted to the specific configurations, gate sets, or optimization sequences described herein, and modifications may be made without departing from the spirit and scope of the claims. Quantum circuits are mathematical representations of unitary transformations acting on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, composed of elementary gates drawn from a universal set such as {H, S, CNOT, X, Y, Z}. In the context of this invention, the focus is on circuits composed exclusively of Clifford gates, which form a finite subgroup of the unitary group and are characterized by their ability to map tensor products of Pauli operators to other tensor products of Pauli operators under conjugation. This property enables the use of the stabilizer formalism to simulate and manipulate Clifford circuits efficiently on classical computers. The Clifford group is generated by the Hadamard gate, the Phase gate, and the controlled-NOT gate, with the Pauli operators serving as fundamental building blocks. The Pauli-push equations, derived from the conjugation relations between Pauli operators and Clifford gates, allow for the deterministic relocation of Pauli gates through the circuit without altering the overall unitary. These equations form the basis for the partitioning of the circuit into distinct stages, a key innovation of this invention. Template matching, as traditionally applied, involves identifying subsequences of gates that implement the identity operation and replacing them with their complementary inverse sequences. In this invention, templates are defined specifically for the Clifford group and are designed to exploit the algebraic structure of H, S, and CNOT gates. Each template is a sequence of gates that evaluates to the identity, and its application involves matching a contiguous subsequence of the target circuit with a subsequence of the template and replacing the matched portion with the inverse of the remaining gates in the template. Templates may be applied in both forward and backward directions, and their independence is ensured by verifying that no smaller template can reduce the gate count of a larger one. The optimization procedure proceeds iteratively, scanning the circuit from left to right and attempting to apply templates at each position. When a template application is beneficial—meaning it reduces the total number of two-qubit gates—it is enacted, and the scan resumes from the beginning. Blocking gates, such as entangling operations that prevent the movement of single-qubit gates necessary for template matching, are handled by the floating component, which relocates Hadamard and Phase gates to adjacent positions, thereby enabling template application. The optimization process concludes when no further template applications yield a reduction in gate count. Conventional template matching is limited by its inability to handle global entanglement structures and its sensitivity to gate ordering. Similarly, conventional peephole optimization is constrained by the exponential growth of the Clifford group, which renders exhaustive database lookup infeasible beyond six qubits. This invention overcomes these limitations by introducing partitioned template matching and symbolic peephole optimization. Partitioned template matching separates the circuit into compute, swap, and Pauli stages, allowing each stage to be optimized independently while preserving the global unitary. Symbolic peephole optimization introduces symbolic Pauli gates—variables representing the action of entangling gates on qubits outside a selected subset—to enable the optimization of subcircuits that are not fully isolated. By replacing straddling CNOT gates with symbolic Pauli gates, the subcircuit becomes amenable to dynamic programming optimization using a precomputed lookup table of optimal Clifford circuits on two or three qubits. The cost function for each symbolic subcircuit includes the number of entangling gates and the number of symbolic Pauli gates, and the optimization respects the temporal order of these symbols and preserves the overall phase up to global factors. The resulting optimized symbolic circuit is then lifted back into a physical circuit by replacing each symbolic Pauli gate with the corresponding entangling gate and adjusting phase gates accordingly. The sequential combination of partitioned template matching and symbolic peephole optimization produces circuits with significantly fewer entangling gates than those generated by prior methods. The invention further includes an electronic system for generating optimized Clifford circuits, comprising a partition component, a template component, a floating component, a SWAP component, a symbolic component, and a peephole component, all operatively connected and configured to execute the optimization pipeline iteratively. The system may be implemented on a general-purpose computer, a quantum compiler stack, or a dedicated hardware accelerator. The method begins by receiving a suboptimal Clifford circuit, partitioning it into compute, swap, and Pauli stages, applying template matching to the compute stage, utilizing the floating component to relocate Hadamard and Phase gates, optimizing the SWAP stage through alignment with entangling gates, selecting a subset of qubits for peephole optimization, rewriting straddling gates as symbolic Pauli gates, performing peephole optimization on the symbolic subcircuit, and generating the final optimized circuit. The invention further encompasses a method for compiling a Clifford unitary into a circuit, optimizing it using the described techniques, and outputting a minimal-gate-count implementation. The invention solves the technical problem of scalable Clifford circuit optimization by decoupling local optimization from global entanglement constraints, thereby enabling the reduction of two-qubit gate counts in circuits with dozens of qubits. The optimized circuits are used to control real-world quantum devices, improving fidelity, reducing runtime, and lowering error rates in quantum error correction, randomized benchmarking, and quantum simulation applications. A block diagram of the system illustrates the flow of data between components, and a flow diagram details the iterative execution of optimization passes. The invention is further embodied as a computer program product stored on a non-transitory computer-readable medium, executable by a processor to perform the described acts. The symbolic component is defined as a module that generates symbolic Pauli gates, respects their temporal ordering, preserves the overall phase of the circuit, and generates phase factors through single-qubit Z and S gates. The optimization proceeds by selecting a subset of qubits, running passes of symbolic peephole optimization until no further cost reduction is achieved, and lifting the result back into the full circuit. The invention demonstrates a reduction in CNOT gate count of up to 64.7% compared to the Aaronson-Gottesman canonical form and recovers optimal gate counts for 97.9% of 6-qubit circuits. The method is applicable to Hamiltonian evolution circuits, quantum error correction encoding, and shadow tomography protocols. The system architecture includes a processor, memory, input/output interfaces, and network connectivity for distributed execution. The invention may be implemented in cloud computing environments, embedded systems, or quantum control hardware. The program modules are written in high-level programming languages and compiled for execution on classical processors. The invention further includes a method for dynamically repartitioning the circuit during optimization to accommodate newly exposed optimization opportunities. The symbolic peephole optimization is proven to be complete for small qubit subsets and exhibits polynomial-time complexity under the assumption of bounded subset size. The invention enables the practical implementation of large-scale Clifford circuits that were previously intractable to optimize, thereby advancing the state of the art in quantum circuit compilation.
