# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Avian influenza virus (AIV) is a highly contagious pathogen that continues to pose a severe threat to global poultry production and public health. Outbreaks of AIV, particularly those caused by high-pathogenicity strains, result in mass mortality among domesticated birds and necessitate the culling of entire flocks to prevent further spread. These events lead to catastrophic economic losses, with global expenditures exceeding billions of dollars annually due to trade restrictions, destruction of livestock, and the cost of emergency response measures. The poultry industry, which relies heavily on large-scale, high-density farming systems, is especially vulnerable to rapid transmission and sustained viral shedding within flocks. The virus spreads efficiently through respiratory secretions and fecal contamination, with infected birds capable of shedding infectious particles for prolonged periods, even before clinical signs manifest. This silent transmission complicates early detection and containment efforts, allowing outbreaks to escalate before intervention is possible.

Current vaccination strategies for AIV are fundamentally limited in their ability to provide timely, broad-spectrum protection. Most commercially available vaccines require subcutaneous or intramuscular administration, which is labor-intensive and impractical for large commercial flocks. Furthermore, protective immunity typically takes two to three weeks to develop following vaccination, rendering these interventions ineffective during the critical early phase of an outbreak. Vaccine efficacy is also constrained by the necessity for strict antigenic matching between the vaccine strain and the circulating field virus, as antibodies induced by vaccination are largely subtype-specific. This requirement forces producers and regulatory agencies to maintain vast, rapidly expiring stockpiles of vaccines, each tailored to specific hemagglutinin and neuraminidase combinations. The constant antigenic drift and shift of AIV strains render these stockpiles obsolete within months, creating a reactive and unsustainable cycle of vaccine development, production, and deployment. In addition, improper storage, handling, or administration further diminishes vaccine effectiveness, contributing to inconsistent protection across flocks.

The emergence of drug-resistant AIV variants has intensified concerns regarding the long-term viability of antiviral chemotherapies. While neuraminidase inhibitors and other small-molecule antivirals have been explored, their use in poultry is limited by cost, regulatory restrictions, and the risk of selecting for resistant mutants. Moreover, the widespread use of such agents in food-producing animals raises concerns about the transfer of resistance genes to human pathogens. The lack of a universal prophylactic strategy that can be rapidly deployed, does not require antigenic matching, and provides immediate protection has created an urgent need for innovative approaches to AIV control. Current vaccination practices also interfere with serological surveillance, as vaccinated birds produce antibodies indistinguishable from those generated by natural infection. This complicates the implementation of DIVA (Differentiating Infected from Vaccinated Animals) strategies, which are critical for maintaining international trade and accurately assessing outbreak dynamics. The cumulative effect of these limitations underscores a critical gap in avian disease management: the absence of a scalable, rapid-acting, broad-spectrum, and non-interfering prophylactic platform capable of mitigating AIV transmission at the population level.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention comprises a novel prophylactic and therapeutic platform for the prevention and reduction of avian influenza virus infection in avian species, utilizing a transkingdom RNA interference (tkRNAi) delivery system based on genetically engineered nonpathogenic Escherichia coli. This system is designed to deliver short hairpin RNA (shRNA) molecules directly into avian mucosal epithelial cells, where they induce sequence-specific silencing of essential viral genes, thereby inhibiting viral replication and reducing infectious shedding. The engineered E. coli bacterium expresses two key genetic components: an invasion factor that facilitates receptor-mediated endocytosis into host epithelial cells, and a cytoplasm-targeting toxin that enables the release of shRNA from endosomal vesicles into the cytosol. Once released, the shRNA is processed by the host cell’s RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), leading to the degradation of complementary viral mRNA transcripts derived from conserved regions of the AIV genome.

The shRNA molecules are specifically designed to target highly conserved sequences within the nucleoprotein (NP), polymerase acidic (PA), polymerase basic 1 (PB1), and polymerase basic 2 (PB2) genes of avian influenza virus, which are critical for viral replication and transcription. These target regions exhibit minimal variation across multiple AIV subtypes, including H5N1, H7N9, H6N2, and H8N4, enabling the platform to provide broad-spectrum protection against diverse strains without requiring strain-specific reformulation. The invention further encompasses a cocktail approach in which multiple shRNAs targeting distinct conserved regions of NP and PA are co-delivered within a single bacterial vector or in combination with separate vectors, thereby reducing the likelihood of viral escape through mutation. The shRNA expression cassettes are driven by T7, T3, or SP6 promoters integrated into a stable plasmid vector, ensuring constitutive and high-level transcription within the bacterial host. The engineered E. coli strain is a diaminopimelic acid (Dap) auxotroph and is kanamycin resistant, ensuring environmental containment and controlled propagation.

The invention includes a pharmaceutical composition comprising two distinct populations of the engineered E. coli bacterium: one expressing shRNA targeting the NP gene and another expressing shRNA targeting the PA gene, each capable of independent delivery and intracellular unloading. This composition may be administered via intranasal, oral, or aerosolized routes, enabling mass application to poultry flocks through spraying, nebulization, or water-based delivery systems. The method of treatment involves the direct application of the composition to the respiratory mucosa of susceptible avian hosts, where the bacteria invade epithelial cells, release shRNA, and initiate gene silencing prior to or immediately following viral exposure. This approach provides rapid, non-immunogenic protection within hours of administration, circumventing the delayed immune response associated with conventional vaccines. The invention further encompasses methods for reducing the risk of AIV transmission by suppressing viral shedding in treated birds, thereby interrupting the chain of infection within flocks and reducing environmental contamination.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

The development of this invention is motivated by the persistent and escalating threat posed by avian influenza virus to global food security, economic stability, and zoonotic public health. Conventional vaccination strategies, while widely used, suffer from critical shortcomings: they require invasive administration, take weeks to induce immunity, are strain-specific, and interfere with diagnostic surveillance. RNA interference (RNAi) has long been recognized as a potent mechanism for silencing viral gene expression, yet its clinical application has been hindered by the inability to deliver RNA molecules efficiently and safely into target cells. Synthetic carriers often induce cytotoxicity, require high dosages, and lack tissue specificity, while viral vectors raise concerns about insertional mutagenesis and immune activation. The invention overcomes these limitations by introducing a transkingdom RNAi (tkRNAi) delivery platform that leverages the natural biology of nonpathogenic bacteria to transport therapeutic RNA into avian epithelial cells.

The tkRNAi system is composed of a genetically modified Escherichia coli strain engineered to express two essential functional elements: the invasin gene (inv) and the listeriolysin O gene (hlyA). The invasin protein, expressed on the bacterial surface, binds with high affinity to β(1) integrin receptors abundantly present on the apical membranes of avian mucosal epithelial cells, initiating receptor-mediated endocytosis. Following internalization, the bacterium resides within an endosomal compartment, where the expression of listeriolysin O, a pore-forming toxin, disrupts the endosomal membrane, allowing the release of shRNA into the cytoplasm. The shRNA, transcribed from a plasmid under the control of a T7 promoter, is processed by the host cell’s Dicer enzyme into small interfering RNA (siRNA) duplexes, which are then loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). The siRNA guides RISC to complementary sequences in viral mRNA transcripts of NP, PA, PB1, or PB2, resulting in their cleavage and degradation, thereby halting viral replication.

This system was validated in an avian epithelial cell model using chicken LMH hepatocellular carcinoma cells, which express β(1) integrin and are permissive to AIV infection. The tkRNAi vectors were shown to invade these cells with efficiencies exceeding 74%, as confirmed by fluorescent microscopy and flow cytometry using RFP-tagged constructs. The maximum non-cytotoxic bacterial concentration was determined to be 7.8 × 10⁵ CFU/mL, a dose that enabled robust intracellular delivery without inducing cell damage. shRNA sequences targeting conserved regions of the NP and PA genes were designed to exhibit 100% complementarity across over 10,000 AIV strains, including H5N1, H7N9, and H9N2, while showing no significant homology to any known chicken gene sequence, thereby minimizing off-target effects. When LMH cells were pre-treated with anti-AIV vectors expressing NP- or PA-targeting shRNA and subsequently challenged with H6N2 or H8N4 viruses, infectious viral titers were reduced by up to four orders of magnitude compared to untreated controls. The most potent effect was observed with a cocktail of both NP- and PA-targeting vectors, demonstrating synergistic suppression of viral shedding.

The invention demonstrates that tkRNAi provides a superior alternative to vaccination by acting directly on viral RNA rather than relying on adaptive immune responses. Unlike vaccines, this platform does not induce antibody production, eliminating interference with serological surveillance and enabling true DIVA compliance. The bacterial vectors are non-conjugative, non-colonizing, and incapable of integrating into the host genome, ensuring biosafety. Administration is non-invasive and scalable, suitable for mass delivery via aerosol or spray systems targeting the respiratory tract, including the Harderian gland and tracheal mucosa, where AIV replication is initiated. The system can be manufactured at low cost using standard fermentation techniques, stored for extended periods, and deployed rapidly during outbreaks. Furthermore, the platform is adaptable: new shRNA sequences can be synthesized and incorporated into the plasmid within days to respond to emerging variants, providing a dynamic defense against antigenic evolution.

The invention further addresses the limitations of mammalian cell models in predicting efficacy in avian hosts. By validating vector uptake and antiviral activity directly in avian epithelial cells, the invention establishes a biologically relevant platform for avian disease intervention. The tkRNAi system was shown to reduce viral shedding not only by silencing viral genes but also by potentially enhancing innate immune responses through bacterial component recognition, such as lipopolysaccharide interaction with Toll-like receptor 4, which may prime antiviral defenses without inducing pathological inflammation. This dual mechanism—direct RNA silencing and mild immune stimulation—contributes to a robust and sustained reduction in infectious virus production. The technology is applicable to all avian species susceptible to AIV, including chickens, turkeys, ducks, and quail, and represents a transformative solution for controlling outbreaks in commercial poultry operations.

## GLOSSARY OF CLAIM TERMS

Administration refers to the introduction of a composition into a subject via a route that enables delivery to mucosal epithelial tissues, including intranasal, oral, aerosol, or topical application, such that the composition reaches the respiratory or gastrointestinal tract lining.

Composition refers to a pharmaceutical formulation comprising one or more nonpathogenic, genetically engineered Escherichia coli bacteria capable of expressing short hairpin RNA targeting avian influenza virus mRNA, wherein the bacteria further express an invasion factor and a cytoplasm-targeting toxin to facilitate cellular uptake and intracellular release of the RNA.

Therapeutically effective amount refers to the quantity of the composition sufficient to reduce viral replication, suppress infectious virus shedding, or prevent clinical disease in an avian subject, as measured by reduction in tissue viral titers, decreased mortality, or diminished transmission to contact animals.

Treating AIV refers to the reduction, suppression, or elimination of avian influenza virus infection, replication, or shedding in an avian subject, whether administered prophylactically or therapeutically.

Treatment refers to the process of administering a composition to an avian subject for the purpose of preventing, reducing, or ameliorating symptoms, viral load, or transmission associated with avian influenza virus infection.

Subject in need of treatment refers to an avian animal, including but not limited to chickens, turkeys, ducks, or quail, that is at risk of or is currently exposed to avian influenza virus, or that is housed in an environment where AIV transmission is likely.

Pharmaceutically acceptable refers to a formulation, carrier, or excipient that is compatible with the biological system of the avian subject and does not induce undue toxicity, immunopathology, or adverse physiological effects upon administration.

Safe and effective amount refers to the dosage range of the composition that achieves the desired antiviral outcome without causing significant cytotoxicity, systemic inflammation, or disruption of normal host physiology.

A and an refer to one or more of the recited elements, unless the context clearly indicates otherwise.

And/or refers to either one or both of the listed elements, encompassing all possible combinations.

Comprising refers to an open-ended term indicating that the recited elements are included, but additional elements may also be present without departing from the scope of the invention.

Consisting essentially of refers to a term indicating that the composition includes the specified components and may include additional components that do not materially affect the basic and novel characteristics of the invention.

Consisting of refers to a closed term indicating that only the specified components are present, with no additional elements allowed.

Invasive refers to the capacity of a microorganism to penetrate host cell membranes and enter the intracellular space via receptor-mediated mechanisms, without causing lysis or overt cytopathic effects.

Invasive microorganisms refer to nonpathogenic bacteria genetically modified to express surface proteins that mediate binding and internalization into host epithelial cells, such as invasin.

Invasion factor refers to a protein expressed on the surface of a bacterial vector that binds to host cell receptors, triggering endocytosis; in the preferred embodiment, this is the invasin protein derived from Yersinia pseudotuberculosis.

Cytoplasm-targeting gene refers to a gene encoding a toxin that disrupts endosomal membranes to facilitate the release of therapeutic nucleic acids into the host cell cytoplasm; in the preferred embodiment, this is the listeriolysin O gene (hlyA).

Preferred embodiment of invasive microorganism refers to a nonpathogenic, diaminopimelic acid auxotrophic, kanamycin-resistant Escherichia coli strain engineered to express invasin and listeriolysin O, and to constitutively transcribe shRNA targeting conserved regions of avian influenza virus genes.

Additional microorganisms refer to other bacterial species capable of being genetically modified to express the same functional components, including but not limited to Lactobacillus, Salmonella, or Pseudomonas strains, provided they retain nonpathogenicity, mucosal tropism, and compatibility with shRNA expression.

Factors to consider for additional bacterial species include mucosal adherence capability, endosomal escape efficiency, genetic stability of the expression plasmid, safety profile in avian hosts, and scalability of production.

Avian influenza refers to infection caused by any strain of influenza A virus that naturally infects birds, including low-pathogenicity and high-pathogenicity variants, and is characterized by respiratory and systemic disease in poultry.

Disease prevention refers to the administration of a composition prior to viral exposure to reduce the likelihood of infection, replication, or transmission.

Disease treatment refers to the administration of a composition after exposure or during early infection to reduce viral load, clinical signs, or shedding duration.

Symptoms of avian influenza include respiratory distress, decreased feed and water consumption, reduced egg production, swelling of the head and comb, neurological signs, and sudden death, depending on viral strain and host species.

Kits for practicing the methods refer to packaged units containing one or more vials of the engineered bacterial composition, instructions for administration, dilution buffers, and application devices such as nebulizers or spray nozzles.

Kit components include the bacterial vector, a diluent suitable for aerosolization, a calibration guide for dosage, and a certificate of analysis verifying viability, purity, and shRNA expression.

Conventional techniques refer to methods of vaccination, antiviral drug administration, biosecurity measures, or culling practices used for the control of avian influenza prior to the use of the claimed tkRNAi platform.

Scope of the invention encompasses all methods, compositions, and uses described herein, including variations in shRNA sequence, bacterial strain, administration route, and combination with other prophylactic agents, provided the core mechanism of tkRNAi-mediated viral gene silencing is retained.
