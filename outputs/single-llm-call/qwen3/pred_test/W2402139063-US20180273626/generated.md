# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- define technical field

The present invention relates to the field of immunotherapy, specifically to novel methods and compositions for the treatment of IgE-mediated allergic disorders. The invention encompasses the use of engineered IgE variants that are resistant to neutralization by omalizumab, in combination with omalizumab itself, to achieve sustained modulation of IgE receptor occupancy on effector cells without inducing pathological IgE depletion. This approach represents a paradigm shift in anti-IgE therapy by replacing endogenous allergen-specific IgE with non-pathogenic, omalizumab-resistant IgE fragments that maintain receptor binding while preventing downstream activation signals. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions, dosing regimens, and kits designed for the safe and effective administration of these engineered IgE variants in human patients suffering from allergic asthma, atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis, and other IgE-dependent conditions.

## BACKGROUND

- introduce allergic diseases

Allergic diseases represent a pervasive global health burden, affecting hundreds of millions of individuals worldwide. These conditions arise from an aberrant immune response to otherwise harmless environmental antigens, leading to the overproduction of immunoglobulin E (IgE) antibodies that bind with high affinity to receptors expressed on mast cells and basophils. Upon re-exposure to the allergen, cross-linking of receptor-bound IgE triggers the release of inflammatory mediators such as histamine, leukotrienes, and cytokines, resulting in symptoms ranging from rhinorrhea and wheezing to life-threatening anaphylaxis. The chronic nature of these disorders often leads to progressive tissue remodeling, reduced quality of life, and significant economic strain on healthcare systems.

- describe IgE antibodies

Immunoglobulin E is a unique class of antibody characterized by its exceptionally high affinity for the high-affinity Fc receptor, FcεRI, expressed on the surface of mast cells and basophils. Unlike other immunoglobulin isotypes, IgE has a prolonged serum half-life when bound to its receptor, enabling persistent sensitization of effector cells even in the absence of circulating free IgE. This receptor-bound IgE serves as the central molecular trigger for type I hypersensitivity reactions and is therefore a critical therapeutic target in allergic disease.

- explain FcεRI receptors

The high-affinity IgE receptor, FcεRI, is a tetrameric complex composed of an IgE-binding α-chain, a β-chain, and two disulfide-linked γ-chains. The α-chain contains two extracellular immunoglobulin-like domains that directly engage the Cε3 domain of IgE, forming a stable complex that primes the cell for activation upon allergen encounter. The receptor’s expression on mast cells and basophils is tightly regulated by IgE occupancy, with receptor levels being stabilized and upregulated in the presence of bound IgE. This homeostatic mechanism ensures that effector cells remain responsive to allergens but also creates a vulnerability that can be exploited therapeutically.

- discuss IgE-mediated antigen presentation

Beyond its role in immediate hypersensitivity, IgE also participates in antigen presentation through its interaction with the low-affinity receptor CD23, expressed primarily on B cells and dendritic cells. CD23 binds IgE in a manner distinct from FcεRI and facilitates the internalization and processing of allergens for presentation to T cells, thereby perpetuating the Th2-driven immune response characteristic of allergic inflammation. The dual engagement of IgE with both FcεRI and CD23 thus creates a self-reinforcing loop that sustains allergic sensitization and chronic inflammation.

- introduce omalizumab

Omalizumab is a humanized monoclonal antibody approved for the treatment of moderate-to-severe persistent allergic asthma and chronic idiopathic urticaria. It functions by binding to the Cε3 domain of free IgE, thereby preventing its interaction with FcεRI and CD23. This mechanism leads to a progressive reduction in free serum IgE levels and, over time, a decline in receptor-bound IgE on effector cells, resulting in decreased cellular activation and symptom control.

- describe omalizumab's mechanism

Omalizumab binds with high specificity to a conformational epitope on the Cε3 domain of IgE that partially overlaps with the binding sites for both FcεRI and CD23. Structural studies reveal that omalizumab sterically blocks receptor access by occupying a broad surface on the IgE-Fc region, with its heavy and light chain complementarity-determining regions forming an extensive network of hydrogen bonds, salt bridges, and hydrophobic interactions. Notably, omalizumab does not induce conformational changes in IgE but instead prevents receptor binding through direct physical occlusion, particularly at site 2 of the FcεRI interaction interface.

- discuss limitations of omalizumab

Despite its clinical utility, omalizumab therapy is associated with several limitations. First, it does not eliminate pre-existing IgE already bound to FcεRI on mast cells and basophils, which persist for weeks to months due to the slow turnover of these cells. Second, the depletion of IgE disrupts homeostatic feedback loops mediated by CD23, leading to compensatory increases in IgE synthesis and potential rebound effects upon treatment cessation. Third, some patients exhibit incomplete clinical responses, possibly due to residual receptor occupancy or heightened basophil sensitivity per unit of bound IgE. Finally, omalizumab is ineffective against IgE that has already engaged its receptor, limiting its ability to rapidly disarm sensitized cells during acute allergic episodes.

- introduce DARPin inhibitors

Designed Ankyrin Repeat Proteins (DARPins) represent a novel class of non-immunoglobulin binding proteins engineered to bind with high affinity and specificity to target antigens. Certain DARPins have been shown to bind IgE in a manner that accelerates the dissociation of preformed IgE:FcεRI complexes, offering a mechanism distinct from omalizumab’s passive blockade. These agents can actively displace IgE from effector cells, potentially enabling faster therapeutic effects and enhanced clearance of allergen-specific IgE.

- highlight need for better treatments

There remains a critical unmet need for next-generation anti-IgE therapies that overcome the limitations of current approaches by not only neutralizing free IgE but also actively replacing receptor-bound IgE with non-pathogenic variants that maintain receptor occupancy without triggering activation. Such a strategy would preserve the homeostatic stabilization of FcεRI and CD23 while eliminating allergen responsiveness, thereby providing a more durable and comprehensive suppression of allergic inflammation.

## SUMMARY

- introduce omalizumab-resistant IgE variants

The present invention introduces a novel class of engineered IgE variants that are resistant to neutralization by omalizumab while retaining the ability to bind with high affinity to both FcεRI and CD23. These variants are generated through targeted amino acid substitutions within the omalizumab epitope, most notably the substitution of arginine at position 419 with asparagine (R419N), which introduces a novel N-linked glycosylation site that sterically and chemically disrupts omalizumab binding without impairing receptor interaction.

- describe IgE variant structure

The omalizumab-resistant IgE variant comprises a truncated or full-length IgE heavy chain in which the Cε3 domain contains at least one amino acid substitution that abrogates binding to omalizumab while preserving structural integrity and receptor-binding capacity. The R419N substitution is particularly preferred, as it results in the addition of a glycan moiety at the core of the omalizumab epitope, effectively masking the antibody-binding interface. The variant may be expressed as an IgE-Fc3–4 fragment, which retains the critical receptor-binding domains while minimizing potential immunogenicity associated with the full-length molecule.

- introduce composition with omalizumab-resistant IgE variant

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant in combination with omalizumab. This dual-agent composition enables the simultaneous neutralization of endogenous free IgE by omalizumab and the competitive exchange of receptor-bound IgE with the resistant variant, thereby achieving a state of receptor occupancy without allergen responsiveness.

- describe composition with additional agents

In preferred embodiments, the composition may further include one or more additional therapeutic agents selected from the group consisting of corticosteroids, leukotriene receptor antagonists, antihistamines, biologics targeting IL-4, IL-5, IL-13, or TSLP, and other IgE-targeting agents such as DARPin inhibitors. These adjunctive agents may be administered concurrently or sequentially to enhance the overall therapeutic effect, reduce inflammation, or modulate immune cell function.

- introduce method of anti-IgE therapy

The invention provides a method of anti-IgE therapy comprising the administration of omalizumab and the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant to a subject suffering from an IgE-mediated disorder. This method is distinguished from prior approaches by its intent to replace, rather than merely deplete, receptor-bound IgE, thereby maintaining the physiological stabilization of FcεRI and CD23 while eliminating allergen-triggered activation.

- describe administration of omalizumab-resistant IgE variant

The omalizumab-resistant IgE variant is administered parenterally, preferably by subcutaneous or intravenous injection, in a dosage regimen designed to achieve and maintain sufficient serum concentrations to compete with endogenous IgE for receptor binding. The variant may be administered simultaneously with omalizumab or following a priming period of omalizumab monotherapy to reduce circulating IgE levels prior to exchange.

- explain therapeutic effect

The therapeutic effect of this method is characterized by the sustained occupancy of FcεRI and CD23 by the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant, which prevents allergen-induced cross-linking and subsequent degranulation while simultaneously preserving receptor expression levels and suppressing homeostatic feedback loops that drive IgE overproduction. This results in a profound and durable suppression of allergic inflammation without the rebound effects observed with IgE depletion alone.

- introduce multiple dosing regimens

The invention encompasses multiple dosing regimens for the administration of the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant, including daily, weekly, biweekly, or monthly administration, depending on the pharmacokinetic profile of the variant and the clinical condition being treated. The variant may be administered in fixed doses or adjusted based on serum IgE levels, body weight, or biomarkers of disease activity.

- describe administration timing

In certain embodiments, the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant is administered after a period of omalizumab monotherapy sufficient to reduce circulating IgE levels by at least 70%. In other embodiments, both agents are administered concurrently to facilitate immediate exchange of receptor-bound IgE. The timing of administration is optimized to maximize receptor occupancy by the resistant variant while minimizing the risk of immune recognition or clearance.

- introduce combination therapy

Combination therapy with the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant and omalizumab is particularly effective in reducing basophil and mast cell activation in response to allergen challenge, as demonstrated by ex vivo studies showing synergistic inhibition of CD63 expression and histamine release. This synergy arises not only from competitive displacement of native IgE but also from the potential of the resistant variant to act as a non-activating ligand that sequesters signaling components proximal to FcεRI.

- describe kit composition

The invention further provides a kit for the treatment of IgE-mediated disorders, comprising a first container holding a pharmaceutical composition of omalizumab and a second container holding a pharmaceutical composition of the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant. The kit may further include a package insert detailing administration instructions, dosing schedules, and safety information, as well as syringes, needles, or other delivery devices.

- introduce omalizumab-resistant IgE variant for treatment

The omalizumab-resistant IgE variant is specifically intended for use in the treatment of IgE-mediated disorders, including allergic asthma, atopic dermatitis, allergic rhinitis, food allergy, and chronic urticaria. Its use is contraindicated in patients with a known hypersensitivity to any component of the formulation, and its administration is monitored for potential immunogenicity or unintended immune activation.
