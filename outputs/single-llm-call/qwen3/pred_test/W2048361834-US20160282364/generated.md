# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) is a zinc-metalloprotease that plays a central role in the catabolism of several biologically active peptides, including insulin, glucagon, amylin, and calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP). First identified in the mid-20th century, IDE was initially characterized by its ability to degrade insulin with high specificity, thereby regulating systemic insulin levels and influencing glucose homeostasis. Over subsequent decades, its substrate profile expanded to include other peptides critical to metabolic regulation, establishing IDE as a key node in the network of endocrine signaling pathways. The enzyme is widely expressed in tissues such as the liver, skeletal muscle, pancreas, and brain, where it functions to terminate peptide hormone signaling through proteolytic cleavage. Despite its well-documented physiological importance, the therapeutic modulation of IDE has proven challenging due to the lack of compounds capable of achieving selective, potent, and transient inhibition without off-target effects. Existing small-molecule inhibitors of IDE, such as Ii1 and bacitracin, exhibit poor pharmacokinetic properties, limited bioavailability, and significant cross-reactivity with other metalloproteases, rendering them unsuitable for clinical application. Furthermore, these inhibitors often bind within the catalytic cleft of IDE, competing directly with natural substrates and thereby disrupting the enzyme’s ability to process multiple peptides simultaneously, which may lead to unintended hormonal imbalances. The development of diabetes and metabolic syndrome has underscored the need for novel therapeutic strategies that target the underlying dysregulation of peptide hormone clearance rather than merely augmenting insulin signaling or suppressing glucagon secretion. Current treatments for type 2 diabetes, including metformin, sulfonylureas, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and SGLT2 inhibitors, address downstream consequences of insulin resistance and beta-cell dysfunction but do not correct the aberrant degradation of key regulatory peptides by IDE. This gap in therapeutic innovation has created a compelling need for a new class of IDE inhibitors that can selectively modulate IDE activity in a temporally controlled manner, preserving physiological balance while enhancing insulin, amylin, and CGRP signaling without inducing hypoglycemia or other adverse effects.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a novel class of macrocyclic small-molecule inhibitors of insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) that exhibit unprecedented selectivity, potency, and transient pharmacodynamic profiles. These inhibitors bind to a previously unrecognized allosteric site at the interface of IDE’s catalytic and exosite domains, distinct from the conventional substrate-binding cleft, thereby enabling selective modulation of IDE activity without competing with endogenous peptide substrates. Structural characterization of the IDE-inhibitor complex through X-ray crystallography reveals a unique binding mode wherein the macrocyclic scaffold engages hydrophobic residues such as A479 and G362, forming a stable, high-affinity interaction that does not obstruct substrate access to the catalytic zinc center. This binding mechanism allows for the preservation of IDE’s ability to process multiple substrates while selectively slowing their degradation kinetics, resulting in prolonged signaling of insulin, glucagon, amylin, and CGRP. The invention further encompasses a suite of competitive binding assays designed to identify and validate IDE-binding compounds, utilizing fluorescently labeled macrocyclic probes whose binding to IDE is quantified through fluorescence anisotropy, polarization, or Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET). These assays are performed under physiological conditions in aqueous buffers, plasma, or tissue homogenates, enabling the detection of inhibitor binding in complex biological matrices. The probes are conjugated to detectable labels including fluorophores such as fluorescein, rhodamine, or Cy5, as well as isotopic moieties, biotin, or immune ligands, allowing for versatile detection platforms ranging from homogeneous solution-phase assays to immobilized solid-phase capture systems. The invention also introduces a method for calculating fluorescence anisotropy based on the ratio of emitted light polarized parallel and perpendicular to the excitation plane, enabling precise quantification of binding events across a dynamic range of IDE concentrations. The inhibitors are formulated into pharmaceutical compositions suitable for oral or parenteral administration, with dosing regimens designed to achieve transient inhibition of IDE activity for durations of less than six hours, thereby mimicking the natural postprandial window of peptide hormone signaling. These compositions may be co-administered with other therapeutic agents such as GLP-1 analogs, insulin sensitizers, or amylin receptor agonists to produce synergistic effects on glucose tolerance, gastric emptying, and satiety. Kits for identifying IDE-binding compounds are provided, containing labeled probes, reference standards, and instructions for performing binding assays under controlled conditions. The invention further includes methods for determining the presence of IDE-inhibiting compounds in biological samples using antibody-based detection, ligand-based capture, or isotope dilution mass spectrometry, enabling both diagnostic and pharmacokinetic monitoring in clinical settings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

The inhibition of insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) has been demonstrated to significantly improve glucose tolerance by prolonging the half-life of insulin, glucagon, amylin, and CGRP, thereby enhancing their physiological signaling. This effect is not achieved through global augmentation of hormone secretion but rather through targeted suppression of their proteolytic clearance, allowing endogenous levels to exert prolonged biological activity. The discovery of macrocyclic inhibitors that bind to a novel allosteric site on IDE, distinct from the catalytic cleft, has enabled the development of compounds capable of transiently modulating IDE activity without disrupting its ability to process other substrates. These inhibitors are synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques, incorporating non-natural amino acids and macrocyclizing linkers to stabilize bioactive conformations. The resulting compounds exhibit nanomolar affinity for IDE and demonstrate minimal off-target interactions with other metalloproteases, including neprilysin, angiotensin-converting enzyme, and insulin-degrading enzyme-like proteins. Physiologically active small-molecule probes have been developed by conjugating these inhibitors to detectable labels such as fluorophores, biotin, or isotopic tags, enabling their use in high-throughput screening platforms and in vivo tracking studies. These probes are employed in homogeneous assays that measure changes in fluorescence anisotropy upon binding to IDE, allowing for real-time quantification of inhibitor affinity and kinetics. The assays are conducted in buffered aqueous solutions under physiological pH, temperature, and ionic strength, and are validated using plasma samples, tissue homogenates, and recombinant enzyme preparations. The dynamic range of IDE concentrations over which binding can be reliably measured spans from picomolar to micromolar levels, accommodating both purified enzyme systems and complex biological matrices. Binding is further confirmed through competition assays using known IDE substrates and inhibitors, and through site-directed mutagenesis of key residues identified in the crystal structure. The resulting data confirm that inhibition is reversible, concentration-dependent, and temporally constrained, making these compounds ideal for administration in close temporal proximity to meals to enhance postprandial hormone signaling without inducing hypoglycemia.

### Methods for Identifying IDE-Binding Compounds

A method for identifying IDE-binding compounds involves contacting a purified or recombinant form of IDE with a probe molecule that binds specifically to IDE, followed by exposure to a candidate compound under controlled conditions. The level of unbound probe is then measured and compared to a reference level established in the absence of the candidate compound. A reduction in the amount of unbound probe indicates that the candidate compound competes for binding to IDE, thereby identifying it as an IDE-binding compound. The probe may be a macrocyclic molecule derived from a DNA-templated library, conjugated to a detectable label such as a fluorophore, biotin, or isotopic tag. Fluorescence polarization assays are employed to measure the rotational mobility of the probe upon binding to IDE, with increased anisotropy indicating reduced molecular tumbling due to complex formation. The excitation of the fluorophore is performed using vertically polarized light, and the emitted fluorescence intensity is measured in both vertical and horizontal planes. The degree of polarization is calculated as the difference between the vertical and horizontal emission intensities, normalized by their sum, and used to determine binding affinity. The assay is repeated across a range of IDE concentrations to establish the dynamic range of detection and to confirm that binding is saturable. The use of homogeneous assays eliminates the need for physical separation of bound and free probe, enhancing throughput and reproducibility. Binding kinetics are measured by monitoring the time-dependent change in anisotropy until equilibrium is reached, allowing for the determination of association and dissociation rate constants. The limit of detection is optimized to detect binding events at sub-nanomolar concentrations of IDE, enabling the screening of low-abundance biological samples. The assay is adapted for parallel screening of compound libraries using microplate readers and automated liquid handling systems. Reference levels are established using vehicle controls or known inactive analogs, and candidate compounds are identified as IDE-binding if they reduce unbound probe levels by at least 30% relative to the reference. The method further includes the use of antibody-based capture, plasmon resonance, FRET, and isotope-based detection systems to validate hits from primary screens.

### Methods for Detecting IDE

Methods for detecting IDE in biological samples involve the preparation of tissue homogenates, plasma, serum, or cell lysates under conditions that preserve enzymatic integrity. A probe that binds specifically to IDE is introduced into the sample, and the level of IDE-bound probe is quantified using fluorescence, luminescence, radioactivity, or colorimetric detection. The probe may be a labeled macrocyclic inhibitor, an antibody directed against IDE, or a ligand conjugated to a detectable moiety. Binding is detected directly through fluorescence anisotropy, indirectly through competitive displacement assays, or via capture on solid supports such as magnetic beads, microarrays, or ELISA plates. Quantification of IDE-bound probe is achieved using standard curves generated with purified IDE standards, enabling the determination of IDE concentration in unknown samples. These methods are applied to diagnose conditions associated with aberrant IDE activity, including type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, obesity, and neurodegenerative disorders characterized by impaired peptide hormone clearance. The detection system is calibrated for species-specific IDE variants and is validated against genetic knockout controls to ensure specificity.

### Labeled IDE Inhibitor Probes

Labeled IDE inhibitor probes are compounds of Formula (I) through (VI), wherein R₁, R₂, R₃, R₅, R_E, R_F, R_G, R_H, and R_K represent substituents selected from hydrogen, alkyl, aryl, heteroaryl, halogen, hydroxyl, amino, carboxyl, ester, amide, sulfonamide, and combinations thereof. Optional heterocyclic ring formation may occur between R₂ and R₃ or between R₅ and R_K, generating fused or bridged ring systems that enhance binding affinity. The detectable label is conjugated to R₁, R₂, R₃, R₅, R_K, or a linker moiety connecting these groups, and may be a fluorophore, biotin, isotope, or affinity tag. The macrocycle backbone may contain a C═C double bond in either cis or trans configuration, with the trans isomer demonstrating superior binding affinity and metabolic stability. Synthesis of these probes is achieved through Fmoc-based solid-phase peptide synthesis, followed by macrocyclization via ring-closing metathesis or amide bond formation. The final compounds are purified by preparative HPLC and characterized by NMR, mass spectrometry, and circular dichroism. Embodiments of Formula (I)-(VI) include compounds wherein R₁ is phenyl, R₂ is cyclohexylalanine, R₃ is benzoyl, R₅ is lysine, and R_K is a fluorescein derivative. Detectable labels include fluorescein, rhodamine, Cy5, biotin, ¹⁴C, ³H, and ¹²⁵I. Binding agents include streptavidin, anti-fluorescein antibodies, and nickel-chelating resins. Linkers are alkyl chains, polyethylene glycol, or peptide spacers of defined length. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts include trifluoroacetate, hydrochloride, and maleate forms. Pharmaceutical compositions are formulated as tablets, capsules, injectables, or inhalants containing inert excipients such as lactose, microcrystalline cellulose, polysorbate 80, or mannitol.

### Clinical Applications of Macrocyclic IDE Inhibitors

Administration of macrocyclic IDE inhibitors such as 6bK significantly improves oral glucose tolerance in both lean and diet-induced obese mouse models, without inducing hypoglycemia or altering basal insulin levels. This effect is mediated through the stabilization of insulin, glucagon, and amylin, whose plasma concentrations increase by 2- to 4-fold following inhibitor administration. The inhibitors do not affect insulin secretion but prolong the half-life of circulating peptides, thereby enhancing their signaling duration. In vivo experiments demonstrate that IDE inhibition slows gastric emptying in a manner dependent on amylin receptor activation, as co-administration of an amylin receptor antagonist abolishes this effect. Glucagon signaling is similarly enhanced, with elevated plasma glucagon levels observed during glucose challenge, yet paradoxically, blood glucose levels remain lower due to concurrent insulin potentiation. The inhibitors are administered transiently, with dosing timed to occur 15 to 30 minutes prior to a meal, and are omitted if no meal is consumed, thereby minimizing the risk of sustained hormone elevation. Single daily dosing is sufficient to improve postprandial glycemia, and multiple daily doses may be used in patients with severe insulin resistance. The inhibitors are formulated for oral delivery in enteric-coated tablets or for subcutaneous injection in aqueous solutions containing solubilizing agents such as cyclodextrin or polysorbate 20. Dosage levels range from 0.1 to 10 mg/kg, with optimal efficacy observed at 1–5 mg/kg. Combination therapies with GLP-1 receptor agonists, SGLT2 inhibitors, or metformin produce synergistic improvements in glucose control, allowing for reduced doses of each agent and minimizing side effects. The inhibitors are also shown to modulate CGRP signaling, resulting in transient vasodilation and reduced blood pressure, which may be beneficial in patients with hypertension comorbid with diabetes. The therapeutic window is defined by transient inhibition lasting less than six hours, after which IDE activity returns to baseline, preventing prolonged hormonal imbalance. The invention further provides pharmaceutical kits containing the inhibitor, instructions for administration, and diagnostic tools for monitoring plasma hormone levels.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) inhibitors were identified through in vitro selection of a 13,824-member DNA-templated macrocycle library against immobilized His₆-tagged mouse IDE. Enriched macrocycles were decoded via DNA sequencing and synthesized using Fmoc solid-phase chemistry. The most potent inhibitor, designated 6b, exhibited an IC₅₀ of 8 nM against mouse IDE and retained activity against human IDE with an IC₅₀ of 12 nM. Structure-activity relationship studies revealed that substitution of the benzoyl group at R₃ and the cyclohexylalanine at R₂ was critical for potency. The trans isomer of 6b (6bK) demonstrated superior metabolic stability and pharmacokinetic properties. X-ray crystallography of IDE bound to 6b revealed a binding pocket at the interface of the catalytic and exosite domains, with key interactions involving residues A479 and G362. Mutagenesis of A479 to leucine reduced inhibitor potency by over 600-fold, confirming the structural model. Biochemical assays confirmed that 6bK does not compete with insulin or CGRP for binding, as demonstrated by non-parallel Yonetani-Theorell plots. In vivo, 6bK administered at 80 mg/kg improved oral glucose tolerance in lean and diet-induced obese mice, with peak glucose reductions observed 30 minutes after glucose challenge. Plasma insulin and amylin levels increased by 2.5-fold and 3.1-fold, respectively, while glucagon levels rose by 1.8-fold without inducing hypoglycemia. Pharmacokinetic analysis showed a plasma half-life of 2.1 hours and tissue distribution limited to liver, kidney, and pancreas, with no detectable brain penetration. No changes in brain Aβ levels were observed, distinguishing 6bK from non-selective IDE inhibitors. The compound was formulated as a trifluoroacetate salt for injectable delivery and demonstrated no toxicity in repeated-dose studies. These results establish 6bK as a first-in-class, transient, allosteric IDE inhibitor with therapeutic potential for diabetes and metabolic disorders.

### Example 2

The effect of IDE inhibition on CGRP-mediated signaling was investigated in mice subjected to intravenous CGRP infusion. Administration of 6bK (80 mg/kg) significantly augmented the hypotensive and bradycardic effects of CGRP, increasing the duration of blood pressure reduction by 2.5-fold and prolonging heart rate suppression by 3.2-fold compared to vehicle. These effects were not observed with the inactive diastereomer bisepi-6bK, confirming specificity. In parallel experiments, 6bK enhanced the glucose-lowering effect of CGRP during an oral glucose tolerance test, demonstrating that IDE-mediated degradation of CGRP limits its metabolic activity. These findings establish IDE as a physiological regulator of CGRP signaling and suggest that transient IDE inhibition may be beneficial in conditions involving impaired CGRP bioavailability, such as hypertension and migraine.
