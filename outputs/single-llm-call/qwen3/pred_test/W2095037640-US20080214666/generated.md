# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to mmBCFA discovery

The present invention relates to the discovery of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) as essential lipid mediators in eukaryotic organisms, particularly their critical role in growth, development, and metabolic homeostasis. Specifically, the invention concerns the identification of C15ISO and C17ISO as biologically active mmBCFAs whose presence is indispensable for normal larval progression, reproductive competence, and cellular integrity in multicellular eukaryotes. The invention further encompasses the molecular pathways responsible for the biosynthesis, regulation, and functional utilization of these fatty acids, including the discovery of novel enzymes, transcriptional regulators, and feedback mechanisms that govern their production and metabolic integration. This discovery fundamentally alters the understanding of lipid metabolism in higher organisms by establishing mmBCFAs not as incidental metabolites but as non-redundant signaling and structural components required for viability.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce fatty acids

Fatty acids are fundamental lipid molecules that serve as primary energy sources, structural components of cellular membranes, and precursors to bioactive signaling molecules. Their chain length, degree of saturation, and branching pattern determine their physical properties and biological functions. While the biosynthesis and roles of straight-chain saturated and unsaturated fatty acids have been extensively characterized across species, the physiological significance of branched-chain variants, particularly those with monomethyl substitution at the iso or anteiso position, has remained poorly understood in eukaryotes despite their detection in multiple tissues.

- describe mmBCFA in bacteria

In prokaryotes, monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids are well-documented constituents of membrane lipids, where they contribute to fluidity modulation under varying environmental conditions such as temperature fluctuations. Their biosynthesis in bacteria is initiated through the decarboxylative condensation of branched-chain α-keto acids derived from leucine, isoleucine, and valine, followed by elongation via fatty acid synthase systems. These pathways are conserved across diverse bacterial genera and are essential for maintaining membrane integrity and function under stress.

- describe mmBCFA in eukaryotes

Although mmBCFAs have been detected in various eukaryotic tissues—including skin, brain, blood, and cancer cells—their biosynthetic origin, metabolic regulation, and physiological necessity have remained speculative. Their presence in mammals has been noted for decades, yet no functional studies have established their requirement for development or survival. Prior assumptions suggested they might serve as inert structural components or metabolic byproducts, with no evidence supporting a regulatory or essential role in cellular physiology.

- discuss mmBCFA functions

Prior to this invention, proposed functions of mmBCFAs in eukaryotes were limited to anecdotal associations with hydrophobicity in hair fibers, binding to cholesterol in protein biosynthesis, or correlations with disease states such as cancer and neurological disorders. No mechanistic link had been established between mmBCFA levels and cellular processes such as growth arrest, developmental timing, or metabolic adaptation. Their absence from standard nutritional models and lack of targeted genetic tools prevented systematic investigation into their biological necessity.

- discuss mmBCFA biosynthesis

The enzymatic machinery required for mmBCFA biosynthesis in eukaryotes had never been identified. While bacterial pathways were well characterized, homologous systems in animals or nematodes were unknown. The elongation of branched-chain primers into long-chain mmBCFAs was assumed to occur via conventional fatty acid elongation enzymes, but no specific isoforms had been linked to this function. The origin of the branched-chain acyl-CoA precursors in eukaryotes remained unverified, and the potential involvement of conserved complexes such as branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase had not been experimentally tested in vivo.

- highlight knowledge gaps

Critical knowledge gaps persisted regarding whether mmBCFAs are synthesized de novo in eukaryotes, whether their levels are actively regulated, whether they are required for viability, and if so, at what developmental stage and through what molecular mechanism. There was no evidence that their deficiency could be rescued by exogenous supplementation, nor any understanding of how their biosynthesis might be integrated with broader metabolic networks such as insulin signaling, nutrient sensing, or lipid homeostasis. The absence of a genetically tractable model system with conserved mmBCFA metabolism had impeded progress in this field.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce non-human animal model

The invention employs the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans as a genetically tractable, non-human animal model to elucidate the biosynthesis, regulation, and physiological necessity of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids. This model system enables precise genetic manipulation, high-throughput phenotypic screening, and biochemical analysis of lipid metabolism in a multicellular organism with conserved metabolic pathways.

- describe protein modifications

The invention identifies specific protein modifications associated with mmBCFA biosynthesis, including the post-translational regulation of key enzymes and transcription factors in response to lipid availability. These modifications include changes in subcellular localization, stability, and transcriptional activity of proteins involved in fatty acid elongation and metabolic signaling.

- introduce isolated cell model

The invention further utilizes isolated cell cultures derived from eukaryotic organisms to study the uptake, elongation, and incorporation of exogenous mmBCFAs into membrane and storage lipids. These models allow for controlled manipulation of nutrient availability and direct assessment of lipid metabolism without confounding systemic influences.

- describe cell modifications

Cellular modifications induced by mmBCFA deficiency include alterations in membrane fluidity, organelle integrity, and metabolic flux through lipid synthesis pathways. These modifications are reversible upon supplementation with specific mmBCFAs, indicating a direct causal relationship between lipid composition and cellular function.

- introduce method to identify compounds

The invention provides a method for identifying compounds that modulate the biosynthesis, metabolism, or function of mmBCFAs through genetic screening in C. elegans. This includes RNA interference, CRISPR-mediated gene knockout, and chemical library screening in the context of defined lipid environments.

- describe compound identification

Compounds identified through this method include small molecules, peptides, and nucleic acid-based agents that either enhance or suppress the expression or activity of enzymes involved in mmBCFA biosynthesis, such as ELO-5, ELO-6, ACS-1, and PNK-1. These compounds are validated by their ability to rescue or exacerbate phenotypes associated with mmBCFA deficiency.

- introduce method to regulate mmBCFA

The invention introduces a method for regulating mmBCFA levels in a eukaryotic organism by modulating the expression or activity of key biosynthetic enzymes or their transcriptional regulators, including LPD-1, NHR-49, and SREBP homologs.

- describe compound detection

Detection of regulatory compounds is achieved through gas chromatography-mass spectrometry analysis of fatty acid profiles in treated organisms, coupled with phenotypic scoring of developmental progression, reproductive output, and viability. Changes in mmBCFA abundance correlate directly with functional outcomes.

- introduce method to identify compounds

The invention provides a method for identifying compounds that restore mmBCFA homeostasis in genetically compromised organisms by supplementing exogenous mmBCFAs or their derivatives, such as methyl esters, and measuring rescue of developmental arrest or metabolic dysfunction.

- describe compound detection

Detection of effective compounds is performed using quantitative lipidomics, reporter gene expression, and behavioral assays to confirm restoration of normal physiology. Compounds that restore C17ISO levels and rescue L1 arrest are prioritized for further development.

- introduce method to regulate mmBCFA

The invention describes a method for regulating mmBCFA biosynthesis by modulating the activity of pantothenate kinase, acyl-CoA synthetase, or sterol regulatory element-binding protein homologs through pharmacological or genetic intervention.

- describe compound detection

Compound detection involves measuring changes in the abundance of C15ISO and C17ISO in lipid extracts using gas chromatography, alongside transcriptional profiling of downstream targets such as pnk-1 and sod-3 to confirm pathway modulation.

- introduce method to identify compounds

The invention provides a method for identifying compounds that alter the ratio of branched-chain to straight-chain fatty acids by screening libraries in organisms with genetically engineered lipid profiles.

- describe compound detection

Detection is achieved through comparative lipidomics and phenotypic analysis, with compounds that shift the mmBCFA/straight-chain FA balance and restore developmental competence selected for further characterization.

- introduce method to regulate mmBCFA

The invention introduces a method for regulating mmBCFA metabolism by modulating the activity of the insulin-signaling pathway through genetic or pharmacological manipulation of DAF-2 or DAF-16.

- describe compound detection

Compound detection involves monitoring nuclear translocation of DAF-16, expression of target genes such as sod-3 and pnk-1, and rescue of dauer-like arrest phenotypes in mmBCFA-deficient animals.

- introduce method to identify compounds

The invention provides a method for identifying compounds that enhance the elongation of short-chain mmBCFAs into longer-chain derivatives by screening in organisms expressing heterologous elongases.

- describe compound detection

Detection is performed by measuring the appearance of C17ISO in organisms fed C15ISO, using gas chromatography to quantify product formation and confirm enzymatic activity.

- introduce method to regulate mmBCFA

The invention describes a method for regulating mmBCFA biosynthesis by controlling the expression of elongase genes ELO-5 and ELO-6 through promoter-driven transcriptional activators or repressors.

- describe compound detection

Compound detection involves fluorescent reporter assays under controlled nutrient conditions, with compounds that alter GFP expression from elo-5 or elo-6 promoters selected for their ability to modulate mmBCFA output.

The invention provides a method for identifying compounds that interfere with the feedback regulation of mmBCFA biosynthesis by screening for agents that disrupt the repression of pnk-1 or acs-1 under high mmBCFA conditions.

Detection is achieved through quantitative RT-PCR and promoter-reporter assays, with compounds that prevent transcriptional downregulation of pnk-1 in the presence of excess mmBCFA identified as potential disruptors of homeostatic control.

The invention introduces a method for regulating mmBCFA levels by modulating the activity of the TOR signaling pathway, which transcriptionally suppresses elo-5 expression.

- describe compound detection

Compound detection involves measuring changes in elo-5 promoter activity and C15ISO/C17ISO levels following treatment with TOR inhibitors or activators, with compounds that restore elongase expression under nutrient-replete conditions selected for therapeutic potential.

The formulation comprises C15ISO, C17ISO, or their methyl ester derivatives in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, including liposomes, emulsions, or lipid nanoparticles, designed for oral, topical, or parenteral administration.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce mmBCFA in eukaryotes

Monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids are not merely trace contaminants in eukaryotic tissues but are endogenously synthesized, tightly regulated, and physiologically indispensable lipid species. Their presence in multiple tissues across diverse species suggests a conserved evolutionary role, yet their functional significance had remained unrecognized until the discovery that their deficiency leads to complete and reversible developmental arrest.

- describe biosynthesis of mmBCFA in C. elegans

In Caenorhabditis elegans, mmBCFAs are synthesized de novo from branched-chain amino acid precursors via a conserved pathway involving the branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase complex, followed by elongation mediated by two distinct fatty acid elongases, ELO-5 and ELO-6. ELO-5 is responsible for the initial elongation step that generates C15ISO, while ELO-6 extends C15ISO to form C17ISO, the primary bioactive species.

- show essentiality of mmBCFA for C. elegans growth and development

Suppression of ELO-5 or ELO-6 through RNA interference or genetic deletion results in a complete and penetrant arrest at the first larval stage, which is not observed with suppression of any other fatty acid elongase. This arrest is fully reversible upon supplementation with C17ISO, demonstrating that mmBCFAs are not merely metabolic byproducts but essential regulators of developmental progression.

- describe mmBCFA with different length carbon backbones

Among the mmBCFAs tested, only C15ISO and C17ISO, with 15 and 17 carbon backbones respectively, are capable of rescuing the developmental arrest caused by ELO-5 deficiency. Shorter or longer chain analogs, including C13ISO, C18ISO, and C19ISO, fail to restore viability, indicating a strict structural requirement for biological activity.

- show correlation between mmBCFA supplement and rescue ability

The rescue of developmental arrest by C17ISO is dose-dependent and non-linear, with optimal restoration occurring at concentrations between 10 and 50 μM. Higher concentrations do not enhance rescue and may induce subtle delays in maturation, suggesting a narrow physiological window for optimal function.

- describe ACS-1 requirement for embryogenesis

The acyl-CoA synthetase ACS-1 is required for the activation of mmBCFAs into their CoA derivatives, which are necessary for incorporation into phospholipids and other lipid classes. Suppression of ACS-1 results in embryonic lethality and defective eggshell formation, indicating that mmBCFA activation is essential for early developmental processes.

- show genetic interaction between zen-4 and acs-1

Genetic analysis reveals a functional interaction between ACS-1 and the cytokinesis regulator ZEN-4, wherein loss of ACS-1 phenocopies the membrane defects observed in zen-4 mutants. This interaction suggests that mmBCFA-CoA esters are required for proper membrane remodeling during cell division.

- describe effect of ACS-1 on eggshell and adult cuticle formation

Suppression of ACS-1 leads to osmotic sensitivity in embryos and fragility in the adult cuticle, indicating that mmBCFA-derived lipids are critical structural components of protective extracellular layers. These defects are rescued by supplementation with C17ISO, confirming the specificity of the lipid requirement.

- show relationship between mmBCFA and DAF pathway

Deficiency in mmBCFAs leads to constitutive nuclear translocation of the FOXO transcription factor DAF-16, mimicking the response to nutrient deprivation. This indicates that mmBCFAs act as signaling molecules that modulate the insulin/IGF-1-like pathway, linking lipid metabolism to developmental timing and stress response.

- describe genetic interaction between acs-1 and DAF-2/DAF-16 pathway

Loss of ACS-1 enhances the nuclear localization of DAF-16 even under nutrient-rich conditions, and this effect is suppressed by mutations in daf-2, the insulin receptor homolog. This demonstrates that mmBCFA metabolism directly interfaces with the insulin-signaling cascade to regulate developmental decisions.

- show effect of mmBCFA deficiency on DAF-16 nuclear translocation

In the absence of C17ISO, DAF-16 accumulates in the nucleus regardless of food availability, triggering expression of stress-response genes such as sod-3 and pnk-1. This transcriptional reprogramming underlies the developmental arrest observed in mmBCFA-deficient animals.

- describe connection of mmBCFA to insulin-signaling pathway

The insulin-signaling pathway is not merely responsive to glucose or amino acid levels but also to the availability of specific lipid species. mmBCFAs serve as ligands or co-regulators that maintain DAF-2 activity, thereby suppressing DAF-16 and permitting continuous development under favorable conditions.

- show effect of mmBCFA deficiency on dauer formation

Under conditions of nutrient stress, mmBCFA-deficient animals exhibit enhanced dauer formation, even in the presence of food. This suggests that mmBCFAs are necessary to signal nutrient sufficiency and prevent entry into diapause, positioning them as key sensors in the decision-making circuitry for developmental arrest.

- describe link between mmBCFA biosynthesis and protein uptake

Disruption of the oligopeptide transporter PEP-2 reduces mmBCFA levels and causes slow postembryonic development, indicating that amino acid uptake is required for the generation of mmBCFA precursors. This establishes a direct link between protein nutrition and lipid biosynthesis.

- show transcriptional suppression of elo-5 by TOR pathway

Inhibition of the TOR kinase pathway leads to downregulation of elo-5 expression, demonstrating that nutrient-sensing mechanisms directly control mmBCFA biosynthesis. This places mmBCFA production under the same regulatory hierarchy as protein and carbohydrate metabolism.

- describe effect of exogenous mmBCFA on pnk-1 expression

Supplementation with exogenous C17ISO leads to transcriptional repression of pnk-1, a gene encoding pantothenate kinase, a key enzyme in coenzyme A biosynthesis. This indicates that mmBCFAs exert feedback control over central metabolic cofactor production.

- show negative feedback control of pnk-1 by mmBCFA

High intracellular levels of C17ISO suppress pnk-1 transcription, while low levels induce it, forming a homeostatic loop that balances lipid availability with coenzyme A synthesis. This feedback mechanism ensures metabolic coordination between lipid and energy metabolism.

- describe role of mmBCFA in coordination of food signaling and energy expenditure

mmBCFAs integrate signals from nutrient availability, insulin signaling, and mitochondrial function to regulate energy allocation between growth, reproduction, and maintenance. Their absence triggers a catabolic state even in the presence of food, indicating their role as metabolic gatekeepers.

- show elongation of C13ISO into C15ISO and C17ISO in mammalian cells

Human cell lines are capable of elongating exogenous C13ISO into C15ISO and C17ISO, demonstrating conservation of the elongation machinery across species. This suggests that mammals possess the enzymatic capacity to synthesize bioactive mmBCFAs from dietary precursors.

- describe identification of key enzymes and regulatory proteins in mmBCFA biosynthesis

The invention identifies ELO-5, ELO-6, ACS-1, PNK-1, LPD-1, and NHR-49 as core components of the mmBCFA biosynthetic and regulatory network. These proteins form a conserved module that links amino acid metabolism, lipid elongation, and transcriptional control.

- show potential feedback regulation of mmBCFA biosynthesis

The biosynthesis of mmBCFAs is subject to multiple layers of feedback regulation, including transcriptional repression of elongases by high lipid levels, suppression of pnk-1 by C17ISO, and induction of lpd-1 under deficiency, creating a dynamic equilibrium system.

- describe involvement of SREBP and pantothenate kinase in mmBCFA biosynthesis

The SREBP homolog LPD-1 directly activates transcription of elo-5 and elo-6, while PNK-1 provides the necessary coenzyme A for acyl-CoA synthetase activity. This dual dependence on transcriptional and metabolic regulators ensures precise control over mmBCFA production.

- show elo-5 and elo-6 as targets of LPD-1

Reporter assays demonstrate that LPD-1 binds to the promoters of elo-5 and elo-6 and is required for their expression in intestinal and neuronal tissues. Loss of LPD-1 phenocopies elo-5 deficiency, confirming its role as a master transcriptional regulator.

- describe physiological roles of C13ISO, C15ISO, and C17ISO

C13ISO serves as a precursor but lacks biological activity on its own. C15ISO contributes to membrane composition and is partially functional in rescue assays. C17ISO is the primary bioactive species, essential for developmental progression, reproductive competence, and metabolic homeostasis.

- show biological activity of mmBCFA methyl esters

Methyl esters of C15ISO and C17ISO exhibit enhanced solubility and membrane permeability compared to free acids, and are equally effective in rescuing developmental arrest. This makes them superior candidates for therapeutic and nutritional applications.

- describe use of mmBCFA methyl esters as food supplements

The methyl ester derivatives of C17ISO and C15ISO can be incorporated into dietary formulations for human or animal consumption, providing a stable, bioavailable source of essential lipids for metabolic support.

- show better solubility and permeability of mmBCFA methyl esters

Compared to free fatty acids, methyl esters demonstrate improved absorption in intestinal models and greater stability in aqueous environments, making them ideal for encapsulation in lipid-based delivery systems.

- describe absorption of dietary mmBCFA by animal cells

Orally administered mmBCFA methyl esters are efficiently absorbed by intestinal epithelial cells, hydrolyzed intracellularly, and incorporated into phospholipids and triglycerides, demonstrating their bioavailability and metabolic integration.

- show incorporation of mmBCFA into lipid fractions

Exogenous mmBCFAs are incorporated into membrane phospholipids, neutral lipids, and lipid droplets, replacing endogenous straight-chain fatty acids without disrupting membrane integrity or function.

- describe lack of toxic effect of mmBCFA in eukaryotic organisms

Administration of high concentrations of mmBCFAs or their esters does not induce cytotoxicity, inflammation, or metabolic dysfunction in any tested eukaryotic model, indicating a favorable safety profile.

- show extraction of mmBCFA from bacterial species

mmBCFAs can be efficiently extracted from bacterial strains such as Stenotrophomonas maltophilia, which naturally overproduce C15ISO and C15anteISO, enabling scalable production for commercial applications.

- describe use of mmBCFA-carrying bacteria as sources of mmBCFA

Live or lysed bacterial cultures enriched in mmBCFAs can be used as probiotic supplements or feed additives to enhance lipid profiles in animals and humans.

- show use of fatty acid methyl esters in various applications

mmBCFA methyl esters are suitable for inclusion in infant formulas, therapeutic diets, topical creams, and injectable lipid emulsions, expanding their utility beyond nutritional supplementation.

- describe potential physiological value of mmBCFA methyl esters

The physiological value of mmBCFA methyl esters lies in their ability to restore developmental competence, enhance metabolic resilience, and modulate insulin signaling without the need for endogenous biosynthesis.

- show anteISO branched-chain FA physiological potency

Anteiso isomers of mmBCFAs, such as C17anteISO, exhibit comparable biological activity to their iso counterparts, broadening the range of structurally active compounds for therapeutic use.

- describe model system for identifying regulators of mmBCFA

The C. elegans system provides a genetically tractable platform for high-throughput screening of compounds that modulate mmBCFA biosynthesis, using RNAi libraries, chemical libraries, and reporter strains.

- show modification of mmBCFA biosynthetic pathway components

Targeted deletion or knockdown of elo-5, acs-1, or lpd-1 creates defined lipid-deficient phenotypes that serve as sensitive readouts for identifying compounds that restore mmBCFA homeostasis.

- describe evaluation of effects of modification on mmBCFA biosynthesis

Effects of genetic modifications are evaluated through gas chromatography, transcriptomics, and phenotypic scoring, enabling quantitative assessment of lipid profile changes and functional outcomes.

- show use of organisms with intact mmBCFA biosynthesis capacity

Wild-type organisms are used as controls to establish baseline lipid profiles and to assess the specificity of compound effects, distinguishing true regulators from general metabolic perturbations.

- describe evaluation of effects of putative regulators on mmBCFA biosynthesis

Putative regulators are tested for their ability to restore C17ISO levels and rescue developmental arrest in elo-5-deficient animals, with efficacy quantified by larval progression and reproductive output.

- show use of functional and non-functional mmBCFA systems

Functional systems (wild-type) and non-functional systems (elo-5 mutants) are used in parallel to identify compounds that specifically target the mmBCFA pathway without off-target effects.

- describe modification of organisms to produce mmBCFA

Organisms are genetically modified to overexpress ELO-5, ELO-6, or ACS-1 to create hyperproducing strains for lipid extraction or to serve as biosensors in screening assays.

- show use of bacterial cells and eukaryotic organisms

Both bacterial and eukaryotic systems are employed in tandem: bacteria for scalable production, and eukaryotes for functional validation of bioactivity and safety.

- describe genetic modification of organisms

Genetic modification includes CRISPR/Cas9-mediated insertion of promoter-reporter fusions, RNAi knockdown, and overexpression of biosynthetic genes under inducible promoters.

- show deletion or inactivation of proteins involved in mmBCFA biosynthesis

Deletion of acs-1, lpd-1, or pnk-1 results in severe lipid depletion and developmental arrest, confirming their essential roles and validating them as therapeutic targets.

- describe use of RNA interference to modify mmBCFA biosynthesis

RNA interference is used to transiently suppress gene expression in a controlled manner, allowing for reversible phenotyping and high-throughput screening of gene function.

- show feeding of RNAi to C. elegans to disrupt mmBCFA biosynthesis

Feeding C. elegans bacteria expressing dsRNA targeting elo-5, acs-1, or lpd-1 results in specific lipid depletion and developmental arrest, providing a robust and reproducible model for compound screening.

- describe use of invertebrate systems to elucidate biochemical pathways

Invertebrate models such as C. elegans enable rapid, cost-effective dissection of conserved metabolic pathways that are difficult to study in mammals due to genetic redundancy or ethical constraints.

- show use of SREBP expression system to screen for regulators of mmBCFA biosynthesis

A yeast or mammalian SREBP expression system is used to screen for compounds that activate or inhibit transcription of mmBCFA biosynthetic genes, enabling cross-species validation of regulatory mechanisms.

Genetically modified organisms with enhanced mmBCFA production are combined with dietary supplementation to achieve synergistic effects in metabolic enhancement and disease resistance.

- introduce mmBCFA system

The mmBCFA system comprises a network of biosynthetic enzymes, transcriptional regulators, transporters, and feedback controllers that collectively maintain lipid homeostasis and coordinate development with nutrient availability.

- describe mmBCFA biosynthesis and metabolism

mmBCFA biosynthesis begins with the transamination and decarboxylation of branched-chain amino acids to generate α-keto acids, which are then converted to acyl-CoA primers and elongated by ELO-5 and ELO-6. The resulting C15ISO and C17ISO are activated by ACS-1 and incorporated into complex lipids.

- relate mmBCFA to growth, development, and reproduction

mmBCFAs are required for progression through larval stages, gonad maturation, oogenesis, and embryonic viability. Their absence halts development at L1, while their presence permits continuous growth and successful reproduction.

- introduce methods to evaluate pharmaceutical and nutraceutical compounds

Methods include lipidomic profiling, developmental scoring, gene expression analysis, and behavioral assays to assess the efficacy of compounds intended to modulate mmBCFA levels.

- describe targets for pharmaceutical and nutraceutical compounds

Primary targets include ELO-5, ELO-6, ACS-1, LPD-1, PNK-1, NHR-49, and DAF-2, all of which are druggable enzymes or transcription factors whose modulation can restore mmBCFA homeostasis.

- introduce long chain fatty acid elongation enzymes ELO-5 and ELO-6

ELO-5 and ELO-6 are specialized fatty acid elongases that catalyze the sequential addition of two-carbon units to branched-chain acyl-CoA primers, generating C15ISO and C17ISO, respectively.

- describe ELO-5 and ELO-6 biological activity

ELO-5 exhibits substrate specificity for C13ISO and C15ISO, while ELO-6 preferentially elongates C15ISO to C17ISO. Both enzymes are expressed in the intestine and neurons, and their activity is regulated by LPD-1 and nutrient status.

- introduce mmBCFA-specific acetyl-CoA synthetase ACS-1

ACS-1 is a very-long-chain acyl-CoA synthetase that activates mmBCFAs for incorporation into phospholipids and other lipid classes, and is required for embryonic viability and cuticle integrity.

- describe ACS-1 biological activity

ACS-1 catalyzes the ATP-dependent ligation of mmBCFAs to coenzyme A, enabling their use in membrane synthesis and signaling. Its loss results in embryonic lethality and osmotic sensitivity, which is rescued by C17ISO supplementation.

- introduce lipid depleted 1 LPD-1

LPD-1 is the Caenorhabditis elegans homolog of mammalian SREBP-1c and functions as a master transcriptional regulator of lipid biosynthesis, including both straight-chain and branched-chain fatty acids.

- describe LPD-1 biological activity

LPD-1 binds to sterol response elements in the promoters of elo-5, elo-6, and other lipogenic genes, activating their transcription in response to nutrient availability. Its suppression leads to profound lipid depletion and developmental arrest.

- introduce nuclear hormone receptor 49 NHR-49

NHR-49 is a nuclear hormone receptor homologous to mammalian HNF4 and PPARα, involved in fatty acid oxidation and lipid homeostasis.

- describe NHR-49 biological activity

NHR-49 regulates the expression of genes involved in β-oxidation and lipid desaturation. Its suppression leads to increased fat accumulation and altered mmBCFA levels, indicating a role in balancing lipid synthesis and degradation.

- introduce RuvB-like DNA binding protein

RuvB-like DNA binding protein, encoded by C27H6.2, is a conserved chromatin remodeler implicated in transcriptional regulation and DNA repair.

- describe RuvB-like DNA binding protein biological activity

C27H6.2 modulates the expression of monounsaturated fatty acids in response to mmBCFA deficiency, suggesting a role in membrane adaptation and lipid homeostasis.

- introduce pantothenate kinase PNK-1

PNK-1 is the first enzyme in the coenzyme A biosynthesis pathway, catalyzing the phosphorylation of pantothenate to 4'-phosphopantothenate.

- describe PNK-1 biological activity

PNK-1 expression is transcriptionally repressed by high levels of mmBCFAs, forming a feedback loop that coordinates lipid availability with cofactor synthesis. Its upregulation under deficiency supports metabolic adaptation.

- introduce branched-chain α-keto-acid dehydrogenase BCKAD

BCKAD is a multi-subunit mitochondrial complex that converts branched-chain amino acid-derived α-keto acids into acyl-CoA primers for mmBCFA biosynthesis.

- describe BCKAD biological activity

BCKAD activity is essential for generating the initial precursors of mmBCFAs. Its suppression reduces C15ISO and C17ISO levels, phenocopying elo-5 deficiency and confirming its role in eukaryotic mmBCFA biosynthesis.

- introduce oligopeptide transporter PEP-2

PEP-2 is a transmembrane transporter responsible for the uptake of small peptides and amino acids in the intestine.

- describe PEP-2 biological activity

PEP-2 is required for the supply of branched-chain amino acids necessary for mmBCFA biosynthesis. Its suppression reduces mmBCFA levels and causes developmental delay, linking protein nutrition to lipid metabolism.

- introduce phosphoinositide-dependent protein kinase 1 PDK-1

PDK-1 is a kinase that activates AGC family kinases, including AKT, downstream of insulin signaling.

- describe PDK-1 biological activity

PDK-1 activity is modulated by lipid composition, and its inhibition enhances DAF-16 nuclear translocation, suggesting that mmBCFAs influence insulin signaling through upstream kinase regulation.

- introduce DAF-2 insulin receptor

DAF-2 is the Caenorhabditis elegans insulin/IGF-1 receptor homolog that negatively regulates the FOXO transcription factor DAF-16.

- describe DAF-2 biological activity

DAF-2 signaling suppresses DAF-16 nuclear translocation under nutrient-rich conditions. mmBCFAs are required to maintain DAF-2 activity, and their absence leads to constitutive DAF-16 activation and developmental arrest.

- introduce POD-1

POD-1 is a transcription factor involved in epidermal development and cuticle formation.

- describe POD-1 biological activity

POD-1 is required for the structural integrity of the eggshell and adult cuticle. Its function is compromised in the absence of mmBCFAs, indicating a lipid-dependent role in extracellular matrix formation.

- introduce SOD-3

SOD-3 is a superoxide dismutase expressed in the intestine and involved in oxidative stress response.

- describe SOD-3 biological activity

SOD-3 expression is upregulated under mmBCFA deficiency, serving as a biomarker of metabolic stress and DAF-16 activation.

- introduce ZEN-4

ZEN-4 is a kinesin-like protein required for cytokinesis and membrane remodeling during cell division.

- describe ZEN-4 biological activity

ZEN-4 function is impaired in the absence of mmBCFA-CoA esters, leading to multinucleated cells and embryonic lethality, demonstrating a direct structural role for mmBCFAs in cell division.

- relate targets to mmBCFA biosynthesis and function

All identified targets—ELO-5, ELO-6, ACS-1, LPD-1, PNK-1, NHR-49, BCKAD, PEP-2, DAF-2, and ZEN-4—are functionally interlinked in a network that governs mmBCFA biosynthesis, activation, regulation, and utilization.

- describe results of microarray analysis

Results confirm that mmBCFA deficiency induces lpd-1 transcription, particularly in neuronal tissues.

- test LPD-1 regulation by mmBCFA in amphid neurons

GFP expression from lpd-1Prom::GFP is significantly enhanced in amphid neurons under mmBCFA deficiency.

- describe results of LPD-1 regulation in amphid neurons

Neuronal induction suggests that mmBCFAs act as neuro-metabolic signals influencing systemic development.

- introduce screening for additional genes involved in mmBCFA homeostasis

Screening is performed using transcriptomic profiling of mmBCFA-deficient animals to identify feedback regulators.

- describe microarray analysis to identify candidate genes

Microarray analysis identifies 25 candidate genes differentially expressed under deficiency.

- describe selection criteria for candidate genes

Candidates are selected based on fold-change >1.5, p < 0.01, and absence of change in spt-1(RNAi) controls.

- introduce four candidate genes involved in mmBCFA homeostasis

The four candidates are pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2.

- describe RNAi analysis of candidate genes

RNAi of each candidate alters mmBCFA levels, confirming functional involvement.

- describe results of RNAi analysis

RNAi of pnk-1 and acs-1 reduces mmBCFAs, while RNAi of nhr-49 increases them, indicating opposing roles.

- analyze candidate genes for feedback regulation of mmBCFA biosynthesis

Feedback regulation is confirmed by upregulation of pnk-1 and acs-1 under deficiency and downregulation under supplementation.

- describe reporter strains to test acs-1 and pnk-1 regulation

Reporter strains with acs-1Prom::GFP and pnk-1Prom::GFP show increased fluorescence under deficiency.

- describe results of reporter strain analysis

Results confirm transcriptional upregulation of both genes in response to lipid depletion.

- discuss potential link to DAF-2/insulin signaling

Both acs-1 and pnk-1 are known DAF-16 targets, linking mmBCFA homeostasis to insulin signaling and longevity.

- introduce C27H6.2 as a potential regulator of monounsaturated FA levels

C27H6.2, a RuvB-like protein, is downregulated under mmBCFA deficiency and upregulates monounsaturated fatty acids.

- describe potential adaptive effect of C27H6.2 down-regulation

Downregulation may compensate for membrane fluidity loss by increasing monounsaturated fatty acids.

- conclude importance of mmBCFA for growth and development

mmBCFAs are essential for growth, development, and reproduction, acting as metabolic signals that integrate nutrient status with developmental progression.

- discuss potential regulatory role of mmBCFA on growth and development

mmBCFAs regulate the timing of larval transitions and reproductive maturation through DAF-16 and LPD-1.

- discuss potential mechanisms of mmBCFA action

Mechanisms include membrane remodeling, transcriptional regulation, and signaling through nuclear receptors and kinases.

- introduce potential for further investigation of mmBCFA biosynthesis

Further investigation will identify additional enzymes, transporters, and regulatory proteins in the pathway.

- introduce potential for further investigation of mmBCFA transport and signaling

Transport mechanisms and receptor interactions remain to be characterized, offering new therapeutic targets.

- introduce potential for further investigation of mmBCFA physiological functions

Physiological functions in mammals, including brain development, skin barrier function, and immune modulation, warrant clinical investigation.
