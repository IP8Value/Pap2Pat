# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global burden of HIV-1 infection remains one of the most formidable challenges in modern public health, with millions of new infections occurring annually despite decades of research and intervention efforts. A critical barrier to the development of an effective preventive vaccine lies in the inability of conventional immunogens to consistently elicit broadly neutralizing antibodies capable of recognizing and neutralizing the vast diversity of circulating viral strains. The primary target for such antibodies is the envelope glycoprotein complex, or Env spike, which protrudes from the surface of the virion and mediates the essential steps of viral entry into host cells. This trimeric structure, composed of three gp120-gp41 heterodimers, undergoes dramatic conformational changes during fusion and is heavily shielded by glycans and structural constraints that limit antibody access to conserved epitopes. Soluble forms of the Env glycoprotein, particularly truncated gp140 constructs lacking the transmembrane and cytoplasmic domains, have been pursued as vaccine candidates due to their ability to be secreted and purified in large quantities. However, these soluble constructs typically adopt non-native, open conformations that expose immunodominant but non-neutralizing epitopes while concealing the vulnerable sites targeted by broadly neutralizing antibodies. As a result, immune responses elicited by these antigens are often misdirected, leading to the production of strain-specific or non-neutralizing antibodies that fail to confer broad protection. To overcome these limitations, engineered stabilization strategies have been developed, including the introduction of a disulfide bond between gp120 and gp41 ectodomain (SOS) and a proline substitution at position 559 in gp41 (I559P), which together promote a closed, pre-fusion trimeric conformation closely resembling the native viral spike. These modifications, collectively termed SOSIP, have enabled the production of homogeneous, native-like trimers that display key bNAb epitopes while minimizing exposure of non-neutralizing epitopes. The BG505 SOSIP.664 trimer, derived from a subtype A transmitted/founder virus, represents the most extensively characterized and structurally validated version of this class of immunogens. It has been shown to adopt a native-like morphology under electron microscopy, exhibit high thermal stability, and bind selectively to broadly neutralizing antibodies while remaining inert to non-neutralizing ones. Despite these advances, the practical utility of BG505 SOSIP.664 and similar trimers has been constrained by reliance on transient transfection systems, which yield low and variable quantities of protein, are labor-intensive, and are unsuitable for scalable manufacturing under Good Manufacturing Practice standards. Furthermore, achieving complete proteolytic cleavage between gp120 and gp41—essential for structural integrity and antigenic authenticity—requires co-expression of the cellular protease furin, adding complexity to expression systems. The absence of a stable, clonally derived mammalian cell line capable of consistently producing high yields of fully cleaved, structurally authentic SOSIP trimers has thus represented a major bottleneck in advancing these immunogens toward clinical evaluation.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel expression vector designed for the stable, high-yield production of cleaved HIV-1 Env glycoprotein trimers in mammalian cell lines. This vector enables the coordinated, balanced expression of a modified HIV-1 Env glycoprotein and the furin protease under the control of distinct promoters, thereby ensuring efficient cleavage and structural stability of the resulting trimer. The invention further encompasses a genetically engineered mammalian cell line, derived through Flp-recombinase-mediated integration of the expression vector into a defined genomic locus, which constitutively secretes fully cleaved, native-like Env trimers without degradation or aggregation over multiple passages. The method of the invention involves transfecting a mammalian host cell with the expression vector and a Flp recombinase-expressing plasmid, selecting for stable integrants using a selectable marker, and propagating clonal populations that exhibit sustained, high-level expression of the Env glycoprotein. The resulting cell lines produce Env trimers in quantities substantially exceeding those obtained via transient transfection, with biochemical, antigenic, and structural properties indistinguishable from those derived from transient systems. This approach eliminates batch-to-batch variability, enables long-term production from a single cell line, and provides a scalable platform suitable for GMP-compliant manufacturing of HIV-1 vaccine immunogens.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The expression vector of the invention is a recombinant plasmid construct comprising a Flp Recombination Target (FRT) site linked to a selectable marker gene, enabling site-specific integration into a host genome containing a complementary FRT locus. The vector includes a strong constitutive promoter, selected from the group consisting of the cytomegalovirus immediate-early enhancer/promoter, driving the expression of a modified HIV-1 Env glycoprotein gene. The Env glycoprotein is encoded by a sequence derived from the BG505 or B41 HIV-1 strain, modified to include the SOS disulfide bond and the I559P substitution in the gp41 ectodomain, along with a truncation at residue 664 to remove the transmembrane and cytoplasmic domains. The vector further contains a second transcriptional unit under the control of a weaker promoter, such as the elongation factor 1 alpha promoter, which drives expression of the furin protease gene. This promoter configuration ensures sufficient furin activity to achieve complete cleavage of the Env precursor while minimizing cytotoxicity associated with overexpression. The vector includes a synthetic polyadenylation signal derived from the herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase gene, positioned downstream of the furin coding sequence to ensure efficient mRNA termination and stability. The Env glycoprotein is further engineered to contain a C-terminal Foldon trimerization domain to enhance trimer stability and a deletion of the V3 loop variable region to reduce proteolytic susceptibility. The expression vector may be introduced into mammalian cells via transfection using lipofection, electroporation, or viral transduction, followed by selection with hygromycin B to isolate stably transfected clones. The preferred host cells are human embryonic kidney 293T cells or Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells, each pre-engineered to contain a single integrated FRT site. Integration of the expression vector into this site occurs via Flp recombinase-mediated recombination, ensuring consistent genomic context and copy number across all clones. The resulting cell lines are propagated under standard culture conditions in serum-containing or serum-free media, with periodic monitoring of Env expression by flow cytometry, dot blot, or ELISA. The Env glycoproteins are secreted into the culture supernatant and purified by affinity chromatography using the broadly neutralizing antibody 2G12, followed by size exclusion chromatography to isolate the trimeric fraction. The purified trimers exhibit a single, sharp thermal transition in differential scanning calorimetry, indicating homogeneity, and bind selectively to quaternary-dependent bNAbs such as PGT145 while showing negligible reactivity with non-neutralizing antibodies like F105. Negative-stain electron microscopy confirms their native-like morphology, and reducing and non-reducing SDS-PAGE analyses demonstrate complete cleavage into gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits with no evidence of degradation, aggregation, or V3 clipping. The cell lines remain stable for over ten passages, maintaining consistent Env expression levels and trimer quality, and are adaptable to suspension culture and bioreactor systems for large-scale production.

### EXAMPLE 1

The expression vector designated pAM/C BG505 was constructed by cloning the BG505 SOSIP.664 env gene under the control of the cytomegalovirus immediate-early promoter into the pcDNA5/FRT backbone, replacing the native promoter and polyadenylation elements. The furin gene was inserted downstream of the EF1α promoter, positioned between the HindIII and NotI cloning sites, to ensure its expression at a level sufficient to support Env cleavage without inducing cellular stress. The resulting plasmid contained no epitope tags, allowing for the production of unmodified, native-like trimers suitable for structural and immunological studies. The vector was verified by restriction digest and sequencing to confirm the correct orientation and integrity of all components.

### EXAMPLE 2

The pAM/C BG505 vector was co-transfected with pOG44, a plasmid encoding Flp recombinase, into Flp-In 293T and CHO cells. After selection with hygromycin B, colonies were expanded and subjected to limiting dilution to obtain clonal populations. FACS analysis using FITC-labeled 2G12 confirmed high and uniform Env expression in selected clones, with clone 13 #3-5 (293T) and clone B-D7 (CHO) exhibiting the highest levels of secreted trimers as measured by ELISA using 2G12 capture and PGT145 detection.

### EXAMPLE 3

Intracellular Env expression was monitored over ten consecutive passages using flow cytometry after fixation and permeabilization with Brefeldin A. The fluorescence intensity remained constant across passages, indicating no loss of transgene expression or genetic instability. Cell viability, assessed by trypan blue exclusion, remained above 90% throughout the culture period, demonstrating that neither Env nor furin expression compromised cell health.

### EXAMPLE 4

Trimers purified from stable cell lines and transient transfections were analyzed by BN-PAGE and SEC, revealing identical trimeric profiles with minimal dimer or monomer contamination. Reducing SDS-PAGE confirmed complete cleavage, with no detectable degradation products when probed with ARP 3119 or HIV-Ig. In contrast, monomeric gp120 and uncleaved gp140 controls showed multiple cleavage fragments and aggregates. Western blotting confirmed the absence of V3 clipping in stable cell-derived trimers, even after prolonged culture.

### EXAMPLE 5

Surface plasmon resonance and ELISA assays were developed to assess the antigenicity of non-tagged trimers. PGT145 bound robustly to trimers from both stable and transient sources, while F105 binding was negligible, confirming the structural authenticity of the trimers. Binding kinetics were indistinguishable between cell line-derived and transiently produced trimers.

### EXAMPLE 6

Negative-stain electron microscopy revealed that trimers from stable cell lines adopted a native-like morphology with 80–100% of particles exhibiting the characteristic three-lobed structure. Class averages were virtually identical to those derived from transient transfection, confirming that the production method does not alter trimer conformation.

### EXAMPLE 7

Differential scanning calorimetry demonstrated a single, sharp thermal transition at 66.3°C for CHO-derived trimers and 66.9°C for 293T-derived trimers, comparable to values obtained for transiently produced trimers, indicating high structural homogeneity and stability.

### EXAMPLE 8

The SOSIP modifications were successfully applied to the B41 Env gene from subtype B, generating B41 SOSIP.664-D7324 trimers. Similar modifications were introduced into a subtype C Env gene to produce monomeric gp120 and trimeric constructs, demonstrating the modularity of the vector system for diverse HIV-1 strains.

### EXAMPLE 9

Transient transfection of HEK293T cells with pAM/C BG505 and furin in the presence of fetal calf serum resulted in V3-clipped gp120 fragments, detectable by Western blot. When the same transfection was performed in serum-free medium, no clipping was observed. Treatment of purified trimers with thrombin induced V3 cleavage, confirming that serum-derived proteases, not cellular machinery, were responsible. This demonstrated that the stable cell lines produce Env trimers resistant to proteolytic degradation due to their native-like structure, not due to absence of protease activity.
