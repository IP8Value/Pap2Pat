# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- introduce engineered polypeptides

The present invention relates to engineered polypeptides comprising a modified Fc region derived from human immunoglobulin G, specifically designed to exhibit reduced or abolished effector function while preserving critical pharmacokinetic and developability properties. These polypeptides are constructed through precise amino acid substitutions within the Fc domain of IgG1, enabling their use as therapeutic agents in conditions where immune activation is undesirable, such as in T cell-engaging bispecific antibodies, autoimmune disease treatments, and targeted delivery systems requiring minimal inflammatory response. The engineered polypeptides retain high-affinity binding to their intended antigens, maintain structural integrity under physiological and manufacturing stress conditions, and demonstrate enhanced stability profiles compared to conventional Fc-silenced variants. The modifications are strategically positioned to disrupt interactions with activating Fc gamma receptors and the complement component C1q, without compromising binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) or protein A, thereby ensuring prolonged serum half-life and compatibility with standard purification workflows. These polypeptides are suitable for incorporation into monoclonal antibodies, Fc fusion proteins, and multispecific constructs, offering a versatile platform for next-generation biologic therapeutics with improved safety and manufacturability.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce biologics

Biologics represent a class of therapeutic agents derived from living organisms, including proteins, antibodies, and nucleic acids, that are used to treat a wide spectrum of diseases such as cancer, autoimmune disorders, and chronic inflammatory conditions. Unlike small molecule drugs, biologics are characterized by their high specificity, large molecular size, and complex three-dimensional structures, which enable precise targeting of disease-associated molecules with minimal off-target effects. Among biologics, monoclonal antibodies have emerged as one of the most successful therapeutic modalities due to their ability to bind antigens with exceptional affinity and selectivity, mediate immune responses, and achieve long circulation times in vivo.

- describe therapeutic monoclonal antibodies

Therapeutic monoclonal antibodies are engineered proteins designed to recognize and bind specific epitopes on target cells or soluble mediators with high fidelity. Their clinical utility stems from their capacity to neutralize pathogenic factors, block receptor-ligand interactions, and recruit immune effector mechanisms to eliminate diseased cells. However, while these effector functions—such as antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cell-mediated phagocytosis (ADCP), and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC)—are beneficial in oncology, they can lead to unintended tissue damage, cytokine release syndromes, and systemic inflammation in other contexts, particularly when targeting immune cells or tissues with high antigen expression.

- explain Fc region of an antibody

The Fc region of an antibody, located at the carboxy-terminal end of the heavy chains, is responsible for mediating interactions with various components of the immune system, including Fc receptors expressed on immune cells and the complement cascade. This domain is composed of two homologous CH2 and CH3 subdomains connected by a flexible hinge region, and its structure is stabilized by conserved disulfide bonds and a central N-linked glycan at position N297. The conformational integrity of the Fc region is critical not only for effector function but also for maintaining the overall stability and solubility of the antibody molecule.

- describe Fc receptors

Fc receptors are transmembrane proteins expressed on the surface of immune cells such as macrophages, natural killer cells, dendritic cells, and neutrophils. These receptors recognize and bind to the Fc region of IgG antibodies, triggering intracellular signaling pathways that result in cellular activation, phagocytosis, degranulation, or cytotoxicity. The activating Fc gamma receptors, including FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIa (CD32a), and FcγRIIIa (CD16a), are key mediators of ADCC and ADCP, while the inhibitory FcγRIIb modulates immune responses to prevent excessive activation.

- explain effector function

Effector function refers to the biological activities elicited by the interaction of the antibody Fc region with immune effector molecules and cells. These include ADCC, wherein natural killer cells lyse antibody-coated target cells; ADCP, wherein macrophages engulf and destroy opsonized targets; and CDC, wherein the complement cascade is activated to form membrane attack complexes that perforate target cell membranes. While these functions are advantageous in cancer immunotherapy, they are often detrimental in therapies targeting immune cells or in settings where immune activation must be minimized to avoid toxicity.

- describe IgG binding to FcRs

Human IgG subclasses exhibit distinct binding affinities for Fc receptors, with IgG1 demonstrating the strongest interactions with activating FcγRs and C1q, making it the most potent mediator of effector functions. The binding interface for FcγRs is localized primarily to the lower hinge and upper CH2 domains, with residues L234 and L235 playing a central role in contact formation. Mutations at these sites have been shown to significantly reduce or eliminate binding, thereby attenuating downstream immune activation.

- introduce FcRn

The neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) is a pH-dependent receptor expressed in endothelial and hematopoietic cells that binds IgG in acidic endosomal compartments and recycles it back to the bloodstream, thereby extending its serum half-life to approximately 21 days in humans. Unlike activating FcγRs, FcRn binds to the CH2-CH3 interface of the Fc region, a site distinct from the FcγR and C1q binding epitopes, allowing for independent modulation of half-life and effector function.

- describe IgG Fc region variants

IgG Fc region variants are engineered versions of the native Fc domain that have been altered through site-directed mutagenesis to alter binding properties, stability, or developability. These variants are designed to retain desirable attributes such as high expression yield, solubility, and resistance to aggregation while minimizing undesirable interactions with immune effector molecules. Common strategies include substitution of key residues in the lower hinge, CH2 domain, or glycosylation sites to disrupt FcγR and C1q binding without affecting FcRn or protein A interactions.

- summarize known modifications

Numerous Fc modifications have been described in the literature, including L234A/L235A (LALA), L234A/L235A/G237A (LALAGA), P329G, P331S, D265A, and N297A. While some of these variants effectively reduce effector function, many compromise biophysical stability, leading to increased aggregation under acidic conditions, reduced melting temperature, or altered hydrophobicity profiles that hinder large-scale manufacturing and formulation.

- describe substitution of human IgG1 and IgG2 residues

Substitution of residues from IgG2 or IgG4 into IgG1 has been explored to confer reduced effector function, as these subclasses naturally exhibit lower binding to FcγRs and C1q. However, such chimeric constructs often introduce structural instability, particularly in the CH2 domain, resulting in lower thermal melting temperatures and increased sensitivity to pH stress, which compromises their suitability for clinical development.

- explain ADCC and CDC

Antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) occurs when the Fc region of an antibody bound to a target cell engages FcγRIIIa on natural killer cells, triggering the release of cytotoxic granules containing perforin and granzymes that induce apoptosis. Complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) is initiated when the Fc region binds the C1q complex, activating the classical complement pathway that culminates in the formation of the membrane attack complex, leading to osmotic lysis of the target cell. Both mechanisms are potent but can cause off-target tissue damage when not appropriately controlled.

- motivate need for antibodies with decreased effector function

There is a critical unmet need for therapeutic antibodies that retain full antigen-binding capacity, prolonged serum half-life, and favorable developability characteristics while exhibiting minimal or no effector function. Such molecules are particularly valuable in T cell-engaging bispecifics, where Fc-mediated activation of immune cells can lead to excessive cytokine release and neurotoxicity, and in autoimmune applications where immune cell depletion must be avoided. The ideal Fc variant would completely abrogate binding to FcγRs and C1q, preserve FcRn and protein A binding, and maintain structural stability equivalent to wild-type IgG1.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce polypeptides with variant Fc region

The present invention provides novel engineered polypeptides comprising a variant Fc region derived from human IgG1, wherein specific amino acid substitutions at positions L234, L235, and P329 substantially reduce or eliminate binding to activating Fc gamma receptors and the complement protein C1q, while preserving binding to FcRn and protein A. These polypeptides exhibit biophysical properties comparable to wild-type IgG1, including high thermal stability, low aggregation propensity under acidic conditions, and favorable hydrophobicity profiles, making them suitable for use in a broad range of therapeutic applications.

- describe Fc variants with reduced affinity

The Fc variants of the invention are characterized by a combination of substitutions at residues L234 and L235 in the lower hinge region, paired with a substitution at residue P329 in the CH2 domain. These substitutions, specifically L234A; L235A; P329A (LALA-PA) and L234A; L235E; P329G (LALE-PG), result in complete abrogation of binding to FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa, and C1q under physiologically relevant conditions, as demonstrated by surface plasmon resonance, cell-based binding assays, and functional cytotoxicity assays. Importantly, these variants retain binding affinity for FcRn and protein A at levels indistinguishable from wild-type IgG1.

- introduce LALA-PA polypeptide

The LALA-PA polypeptide comprises the amino acid substitutions L234A, L235A, and P329A within the Fc region of human IgG1. This variant demonstrates no detectable binding to any activating Fc gamma receptor in both low- and high-avidity binding assays, including when the antibody is pre-complexed with its target antigen to simulate immune complex formation. Furthermore, LALA-PA exhibits no measurable C1q binding in a sensitive bead-based complement activation assay, while maintaining a thermal melting temperature (Tm) of approximately 67.5°C and an aggregation profile under pH 3.5 stress that is equivalent to wild-type IgG1.

- describe properties of LALA-PA polypeptide

The LALA-PA polypeptide retains full antigen-binding capacity, as confirmed by biolayer interferometry and surface plasmon resonance, and demonstrates no reduction in expression yield or purification efficiency when produced in mammalian cell systems. Its hydrophobic interaction chromatography retention time, polyspecificity reagent binding, and self-interaction propensity, as measured by AC-SINS, are within the range of wild-type IgG1, indicating minimal perturbation of surface properties. The variant is stable during long-term storage and resistant to acid-induced aggregation, making it ideal for use in therapeutic formulations requiring low pH hold steps during viral inactivation.

- introduce LALE-PG polypeptide

The LALE-PG polypeptide comprises the amino acid substitutions L234A, L235E, and P329G within the Fc region of human IgG1. This variant exhibits complete loss of binding to all activating Fc gamma receptors and C1q, even under highly avid conditions, and maintains binding to FcRn and protein A at wild-type levels. The LALE-PG variant demonstrates superior developability characteristics compared to other Fc-silenced variants, including D265A and P331S, which are associated with reduced thermal stability and increased aggregation.

- describe properties of LALE-PG polypeptide

The LALE-PG polypeptide exhibits a thermal melting temperature of 67.2°C, identical to wild-type IgG1, and shows no increase in aggregation under pH 3.5 stress conditions. Its hydrophobic interaction chromatography retention time and self-interaction profile are indistinguishable from those of the wild-type molecule, and it demonstrates no increase in polyspecificity reagent binding, indicating that its surface charge and hydrophobicity remain unaltered. The variant is efficiently produced in HEK293 and CHO cell systems, purified via protein A chromatography with yields comparable to wild-type IgG1, and remains stable during formulation and lyophilization.

- describe optional features of polypeptides

The polypeptides of the invention may be incorporated into full-length immunoglobulins, Fc fusion proteins, or antigen-binding fragments such as Fab, scFv, or diabodies. They may be further modified to include non-natural amino acids, site-specific conjugations, or glycosylation modifications without compromising the effector-silenced phenotype. The polypeptides may be fused to cytokines, toxins, enzymes, or other therapeutic moieties to create multifunctional biologics. The Fc region may be derived from human IgG1 and may be optionally humanized or chimerized with non-human framework regions for improved immunogenicity profiles.

- describe pharmaceutical compositions

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the engineered polypeptides of the invention in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, buffer, or excipient. These compositions may be formulated for intravenous, subcutaneous, intramuscular, or intrathecal administration and may include stabilizers such as sucrose, histidine, or polysorbate 80. The compositions may be provided as lyophilized powders or liquid solutions, and may be packaged in single-dose or multi-dose containers suitable for clinical use.

- describe methods of producing polypeptides

The polypeptides of the invention may be produced by recombinant expression in mammalian cell lines such as HEK293 or CHO cells, using plasmid vectors encoding the heavy and light chain sequences under the control of constitutive or inducible promoters. The expressed polypeptides are purified from culture supernatants using protein A affinity chromatography, followed by ion exchange, hydrophobic interaction, or size exclusion chromatography as needed. The final product is formulated, filtered, and tested for purity, potency, and stability according to regulatory guidelines.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- define technical terms

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “polypeptide” refers to a linear chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds, whether naturally occurring or synthetically produced, and includes full-length antibodies, Fc fusion proteins, and antigen-binding fragments thereof. The term “Fc region” denotes the carboxy-terminal portion of the heavy chain of an immunoglobulin, encompassing the CH2 and CH3 domains and the lower hinge region, excluding the variable domains. The term “variant Fc” refers to an Fc region that has been modified by one or more amino acid substitutions relative to the wild-type human IgG1 sequence.

- specify meaning of "about"

The term “about” when used in reference to a numerical value or range indicates a variation of ±10% from the stated value, unless otherwise specified, and encompasses values that are functionally equivalent due to experimental variability, measurement error, or biological heterogeneity.

- explain EU numbering system for amino acid residues

The EU numbering system, as defined by Kabat et al., is a standardized method for numbering amino acid residues in immunoglobulin heavy and light chains based on alignment with the consensus sequence of human IgG1. Residues are numbered sequentially from the N-terminus of the variable domain through the constant domains, with the hinge region and CH2 domain residues assigned specific positions such as L234, L235, and P329, which correspond to the lower hinge and CH2 domains critical for Fc receptor and complement binding.

- clarify "comprising," "consisting," and "consisting essentially of" aspects

The term “comprising” is used in its open-ended sense to include any additional elements, components, or steps beyond those explicitly recited. The term “consisting of” denotes a closed group that includes only the recited elements, excluding any others. The term “consisting essentially of” indicates that the claimed invention includes the specified elements and may include additional elements that do not materially affect the basic and novel characteristics of the invention.

- introduce variant Fc domain and its advantages

The variant Fc domain of the invention provides a unique combination of complete effector silencing and preserved developability, overcoming the limitations of prior art variants that sacrifice stability for reduced function. This domain enables the development of safer, more manufacturable biologics with predictable pharmacokinetics and reduced risk of aggregation-related immunogenicity.

- define "variant Fc" and its modifications

A “variant Fc” is an Fc region containing one or more amino acid substitutions that alter its interaction profile with Fc receptors, complement proteins, or other binding partners. The variant Fc of the invention includes substitutions at L234, L235, and P329, and may further include substitutions at S267K, D270A, or other residues that do not compromise the core silencing phenotype or biophysical stability.

- describe Fc region and its domains

The Fc region consists of two identical CH2 and CH3 domains arranged in a dimeric structure, stabilized by interchain disulfide bonds and a conserved N-glycan at N297. The CH2 domain contains the primary binding interface for FcγRs and C1q, while the CH3 domain contributes to FcRn and protein A binding. The lower hinge region, extending from residues 230 to 237, provides structural flexibility and contains key residues that directly contact FcγRs.

- explain "lower hinge" of Fc region

The lower hinge region refers to the segment of the Fc domain encompassing amino acid residues 230–237, which connects the CH1 domain to the CH2 domain. This region is critical for positioning the CH2 domain to enable optimal interaction with FcγRs and C1q. Substitutions at L234 and L235 disrupt the hydrophobic interface required for receptor binding, thereby abolishing effector function.

- define "parent Fc" and its preparation

The term “parent Fc” refers to the unmodified Fc region derived from wild-type human IgG1, which serves as the reference sequence for introducing substitutions. The parent Fc may be expressed as part of a full-length antibody or as a standalone Fc fragment and is prepared by recombinant expression in mammalian cells followed by purification using protein A affinity chromatography.

- introduce Fc variants with decreased effector function

The Fc variants of the invention are specifically engineered to decrease or eliminate effector function by disrupting interactions with activating Fc gamma receptors and C1q, while maintaining binding to FcRn and protein A. These variants are distinguished from prior art by their ability to achieve complete silencing without compromising thermal stability or developability.

- define "effector function" and its types

Effector function refers to the biological activities mediated by the Fc region of an antibody, including antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cell-mediated phagocytosis (ADCP), and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). These functions are triggered upon binding of the Fc region to FcγRs or C1q, leading to immune cell activation and target cell destruction.

- explain Fc gamma receptor and its family

The Fc gamma receptor family comprises several transmembrane proteins, including FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIa (CD32a), FcγRIIb (CD32b), FcγRIIc (CD32c), and FcγRIIIa (CD16a), which bind IgG with varying affinities and trigger distinct intracellular signaling cascades. Activating receptors (FcγRI, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa) contain immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs (ITAMs), while the inhibitory receptor FcγRIIb contains an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibition motif (ITIM).

- define "FcRn" and its protein structure

The neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) is a heterodimeric protein composed of a major histocompatibility complex class I-like heavy chain and β2-microglobulin. It binds IgG in acidic endosomes (pH <6.5) and recycles it to the cell surface, where neutral pH promotes release into the bloodstream, thereby extending serum half-life. FcRn binds to the CH2-CH3 interface of IgG, a site distinct from the FcγR and C1q binding regions.

- describe Fc variants with desirable therapeutic properties

The Fc variants of the invention exhibit desirable therapeutic properties including complete abrogation of ADCC, ADCP, and CDC; retention of FcRn-mediated half-life extension; preservation of protein A binding for purification; and maintenance of biophysical stability equivalent to wild-type IgG1, as demonstrated by thermal melting, aggregation assays, and hydrophobicity profiling.

- explain antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC)

Antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) is a mechanism by which immune cells, particularly natural killer cells, recognize and lyse target cells coated with IgG antibodies. This process is initiated when FcγRIIIa on natural killer cells binds to the Fc region of the antibody, triggering the release of cytotoxic granules that induce apoptosis in the target cell.

- describe antibody-dependent cell-mediated phagocytosis (ADCP)

Antibody-dependent cell-mediated phagocytosis (ADCP) is a process by which phagocytic cells, such as macrophages and neutrophils, internalize and degrade antibody-opsonized target cells. This occurs upon engagement of FcγRI and FcγRIIa on phagocytes with the Fc region of the bound antibody, leading to actin remodeling and engulfment of the target.

- explain complement dependent cytotoxicity (CDC)

Complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) is a lytic mechanism initiated when the C1q complex binds to the Fc region of two or more closely spaced IgG molecules on a target cell surface. This triggers the classical complement cascade, leading to the formation of the membrane attack complex (C5b-9), which creates pores in the target cell membrane, resulting in osmotic lysis.

- describe in vitro and in vivo cytotoxicity assays

In vitro cytotoxicity assays measure target cell lysis in the presence of effector cells and test antibodies, using methods such as chromium release, flow cytometry-based viability staining, or impedance-based real-time monitoring. In vivo cytotoxicity assays evaluate target cell depletion in animal models, typically by quantifying reduction in target cell numbers in peripheral blood or tissues following antibody administration.

- introduce Fc receptor binding assays

Fc receptor binding assays are used to quantify the interaction between the Fc region of an antibody and recombinant or cell-expressed FcγRs. These assays include surface plasmon resonance, biolayer interferometry, flow cytometry, and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, and are performed under conditions that mimic physiological pH and ionic strength.

- describe ADCC activity assessment methods

ADCC activity is assessed by co-culturing target cells, effector cells (e.g., peripheral blood mononuclear cells or NK cell lines), and test antibodies, followed by measurement of target cell death using luminescent, fluorescent, or radiolabeled readouts. The percentage of specific lysis is calculated relative to spontaneous and maximum lysis controls.

- explain CDC activity assessment methods

CDC activity is assessed by incubating target cells with test antibodies and human serum as a source of complement, followed by quantification of cell lysis using dye exclusion, flow cytometry, or lactate dehydrogenase release assays. The degree of lysis is compared to controls lacking antibody or complement.

- introduce L234A; L235A; P329A (LALA-PA) and L234A; L235E; P329G

The L234A; L235A; P329A (LALA-PA) and L234A; L235E; P329G (LALE-PG) substitutions represent two novel Fc variants that completely eliminate binding to activating Fc gamma receptors and C1q, while preserving FcRn and protein A binding. These variants were identified through systematic screening of Fc mutations and demonstrate superior developability profiles compared to previously described silencing mutations.

- describe CH2 domain of human IgG Fc region

The CH2 domain of human IgG Fc is a conserved structural unit that contains the N297 glycosylation site and forms the primary interface for FcγR and C1q binding. Residues surrounding N297, including P329, D270, and K322, are critical for maintaining the structural integrity and functional activity of this domain. Substitutions at P329 disrupt the local conformation required for receptor engagement without destabilizing the overall fold.

- explain testing for reduced FcR binding and developability

Testing for reduced FcR binding involves quantitative binding assays using purified receptors or cell lines expressing FcγRs, while developability is assessed through a panel of biophysical assays including thermal melting, size exclusion chromatography, hydrophobic interaction chromatography, and polyspecificity reagent binding. The combination of these assays ensures that silencing is achieved without compromising manufacturability or stability.

- summarize results of LALA-PA and LALE-PG substitutions

The LALA-PA and LALE-PG substitutions result in complete loss of binding to all activating FcγRs and C1q, as confirmed by multiple orthogonal assays, while maintaining wild-type levels of FcRn and protein A binding. These variants exhibit no increase in aggregation under acidic conditions, retain full thermal stability, and demonstrate no adverse effects on hydrophobicity or self-interaction, making them ideal for clinical development.

- define Fc fusion protein

An Fc fusion protein is a recombinant molecule consisting of a protein of interest fused to the Fc region of an immunoglobulin, typically IgG1, to confer extended serum half-life, improved solubility, and ease of purification. The Fc region may be modified as described herein to silence effector function while retaining FcRn-mediated recycling.

- define antibody

An antibody is a Y-shaped glycoprotein composed of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, each containing variable and constant domains. The variable domains form the antigen-binding site, while the constant domains mediate effector functions and interactions with other immune components.

- define monoclonal antibody

A monoclonal antibody is a population of identical antibody molecules derived from a single clone of B cells, recognizing a single epitope on a target antigen with high specificity and uniformity.

- define antigen-binding fragment

An antigen-binding fragment is a portion of an antibody that retains the ability to bind its target antigen, including Fab, F(ab’)2, scFv, sdAb, and nanobody formats, which may be fused to the variant Fc region of the invention.

- describe antigen-binding fragments

Antigen-binding fragments are smaller, engineered versions of full-length antibodies that retain antigen-binding capability but lack the Fc region or contain modified Fc regions. These fragments may be fused to the variant Fc region of the invention to confer extended half-life and improved pharmacokinetics without inducing effector function.

- define multispecific antibody

A multispecific antibody is an engineered antibody molecule capable of binding to two or more distinct antigens or epitopes, including bispecific, trispecific, or tetravalent constructs, which may incorporate the variant Fc region of the invention to silence effector function across all binding arms.

- define bispecific antibody

A bispecific antibody is a monoclonal antibody engineered to bind two different antigens simultaneously, often used to redirect immune cells to tumor cells. The variant Fc region of the invention is particularly suited for use in bispecifics where effector function must be eliminated to prevent cytokine release and toxicity.

- define epitope

An epitope is the specific region or molecular structure on an antigen that is recognized and bound by an antibody or T cell receptor. Epitopes may be linear or conformational and may be located on cell surface proteins, soluble cytokines, or pathogens.

- describe epitopes

Epitopes are discrete regions of an antigen that interact with the complementarity-determining regions of an antibody’s variable domains. Epitopes may be classified as continuous (linear) or discontinuous (conformational), and their accessibility, charge, and hydrophobicity influence binding affinity and specificity.

- describe antibody structure

The antibody structure consists of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, each composed of variable and constant domains. The variable domains of the heavy and light chains form the antigen-binding site, while the constant domains of the heavy chains form the Fc region responsible for effector function and half-life extension.

- describe heavy chain variable region

The heavy chain variable region (VH) is the N-terminal domain of the heavy chain that, together with the light chain variable region, forms the antigen-binding site. It contains three complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) that confer specificity and affinity for the target antigen.

- describe light chain variable region

The light chain variable region (VL) is the N-terminal domain of the light chain that pairs with the heavy chain variable region to form the antigen-binding site. It contains three CDRs that contribute to the shape, charge, and hydrophobicity of the paratope.

- describe CDRs and FRs

Complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) are hypervariable loops within the variable domains of heavy and light chains that directly contact the antigen. Framework regions (FRs) are the more conserved segments that provide structural support for the CDRs and maintain the overall immunoglobulin fold.

- describe antibody classes

Antibody classes, or isotypes, include IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD, and IgE, each with distinct effector functions, tissue distributions, and half-lives. The invention is directed to IgG1-derived polypeptides, although the Fc modifications may be transplanted to other isotypes.

- describe developability

Developability refers to the suitability of a biologic molecule for large-scale manufacturing, formulation, and clinical use, encompassing properties such as expression yield, solubility, stability, aggregation propensity, and resistance to environmental stressors.

- describe desirable developability characteristics

Desirable developability characteristics include high expression titers in mammalian cell culture, low viscosity in concentrated formulations, resistance to aggregation under thermal or pH stress, minimal polyspecificity, and compatibility with standard purification and filtration processes.

- describe undesirable developability characteristics

Undesirable developability characteristics include low expression yield, high viscosity, increased aggregation under acidic or elevated temperature conditions, elevated polyspecificity reagent binding, and poor recovery during protein A chromatography.

- describe methods to ascertain developability

Developability is ascertained through a panel of analytical assays including size exclusion chromatography for aggregation, hydrophobic interaction chromatography for hydrophobicity, affinity-capture self-interaction nanoparticle spectroscopy for self-interaction, differential scanning fluorimetry for thermal stability, and polyspecificity reagent binding assays for off-target interactions.

- describe polyspecific antibodies

Polyspecific antibodies are those that bind to multiple unrelated antigens or cellular components, potentially leading to off-target effects and immunogenicity. The variant Fc region of the invention does not increase polyspecificity, as confirmed by binding assays against a panel of membrane proteins.

- describe improved expressability

Improved expressability refers to the ability of a polypeptide to be produced at high yields in recombinant expression systems without compromising quality. The variant Fc region of the invention supports expression levels comparable to wild-type IgG1 in both CHO and HEK293 cell lines.

- provide examples

Examples of polypeptides incorporating the variant Fc region include anti-CD3ε antibodies, anti-CD20 antibodies, anti-TNFα antibodies, and Fc-fusion proteins comprising interleukin-2 or cytotoxic domains, all of which demonstrate silenced effector function and enhanced developability.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Antibodies

- generate CD3 antibody variants

Antibodies targeting the CD3ε subunit of the T cell receptor complex were generated by cloning the variable regions of the humanized anti-CD3ε antibody ADI-26906 into expression vectors encoding human IgG1 heavy and kappa light chains. The Fc region was modified to contain the LALA-PA or LALE-PG substitutions using site-directed mutagenesis. The resulting constructs were transiently transfected into HEK293 cells, and the antibodies were purified from culture supernatants using protein A affinity chromatography. All variants were confirmed to bind CD3ε with affinity equivalent to the wild-type parent antibody by biolayer interferometry.

### Example 2: THP-1 Cell Binding and C1q Binding to Antibodies

- test antibody variants for THP-1 cell binding

THP-1 cells, which express FcγRI and FcγRII, were incubated with wild-type and variant antibodies under standard and highly avid conditions using CD3ε-BSA immune complexes. Binding was detected using a fluorescently labeled secondary antibody and analyzed by flow cytometry. The LALA-PA and LALE-PG variants showed no detectable binding under either condition, whereas wild-type IgG1 and LALA variants exhibited significant binding under avid conditions.

- test antibody variants for C1q binding

Antibodies were immobilized on streptavidin-coated beads via a biotinylated CD3ε peptide and incubated with human C1q and complement. Binding of C1q was detected using an APC-conjugated anti-C1q antibody. The LALA-PA and LALE-PG variants showed no detectable C1q binding, whereas wild-type IgG1 exhibited strong signal. Other variants such as D265A and P331S showed partial reduction, but not complete ablation.

- analyze binding data

Binding data were normalized to wild-type IgG1 and analyzed for statistical significance using one-way ANOVA with post-hoc Tukey testing. Both LALA-PA and LALE-PG showed binding levels indistinguishable from negative controls (p > 0.05), whereas all other variants showed significantly higher binding.

- summarize binding results

The LALA-PA and LALE-PG variants completely abrogated binding to both FcγRs on THP-1 cells and to C1q, demonstrating superior effector silencing compared to all other tested variants.

### Example 3: FcγR Binding by Antibodies

- measure FcγR binding by surface plasmon resonance

Binding kinetics of the variant antibodies to recombinant human FcγRI, FcγRIIa, and FcγRIIIa were measured using surface plasmon resonance. Antibodies were captured on a CM5 chip via anti-human Fc, and FcγRs were injected at concentrations ranging from 1 nM to 1000 nM. No binding was detected for LALA-PA or LALE-PG to any FcγR, even at the highest concentration tested.

- analyze binding responses

Sensorgrams were fitted to a 1:1 binding model. The association and dissociation rates for LALA-PA and LALE-PG were indistinguishable from buffer-only controls, indicating no measurable interaction.

- summarize binding results

The LALA-PA and LALE-PG variants demonstrated complete absence of binding to all activating FcγRs, confirming their effector-silenced phenotype.

- compare binding responses between variants

All other variants, including LALA and LALE, showed measurable binding to FcγRIIIa and FcγRIIa under high-concentration conditions, whereas LALA-PA and LALE-PG showed no binding even at 1000 nM, demonstrating superior silencing.

### Example 4: Developability Assays

- prepare polyspecificity reagent (PSR) binding reagent

CHO cell membranes were isolated, biotinylated, and mixed with soluble cytosolic proteins to create a polyspecificity reagent. Antibodies were incubated with the reagent, and binding was detected via flow cytometry using streptavidin-PE and anti-human light chain-FITC.

- prepare soluble membrane proteins

Soluble membrane proteins were extracted from CHO cells using detergent lysis and centrifugation, followed by concentration and filtration.

- biotinylate enriched membrane fraction

The membrane fraction was labeled with NHS-LC-Biotin, quenched, and purified by size exclusion chromatography.

- perform PSR binding analyses

Antibodies were incubated with PSR, washed, and analyzed by flow cytometry. Binding scores were normalized to a three-point calibration curve. LALA-PA and LALE-PG showed scores of 0.38 and 0.41, respectively, matching wild-type IgG1 (0.39).

- analyze PSR binding data

No significant differences were observed between the variant and wild-type antibodies (p > 0.05), indicating no increase in polyspecificity.

- perform HIC retention time analyses

Antibodies were loaded onto a HIC butyl-NP5 column and eluted with an ammonium sulfate gradient. Retention times were recorded at 280 nm.

- analyze HIC retention time data

Retention times for LALA-PA and LALE-PG were 14.2 and 14.1 minutes, respectively, identical to wild-type IgG1 (14.2 min).

- perform AC-SINS self-interaction analyses

Antibodies were incubated with gold nanoparticles coated with anti-human Fc, and wavelength shifts were measured spectrophotometrically.

- analyze AC-SINS data

Maximum wavelength shifts for LALA-PA and LALE-PG were 1.8 nm and 1.9 nm, respectively, comparable to wild-type IgG1 (1.7 nm).

- perform ProA SEC analyses

Antibodies were analyzed by size exclusion chromatography after protein A binding and elution. Peak area and shape were assessed.

- analyze ProA SEC data

All variants showed single, symmetric peaks with >98% monomer content, indicating no aggregation or fragmentation.

- perform pH stress analyses

Antibodies were incubated at pH 3.5 for one hour, then diluted and analyzed by SEC.

- analyze pH stress data

LALA-PA and LALE-PG showed Δ% monomer of +0.8% and +1.2%, respectively, versus wild-type IgG1 (+0.5%), indicating no significant increase in aggregation.
