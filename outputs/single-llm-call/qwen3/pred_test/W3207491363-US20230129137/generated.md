# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Heart disease remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with myocardial infarction accounting for a substantial proportion of cardiac-related deaths due to the limited regenerative capacity of adult human cardiomyocytes. Following ischemic injury, the heart responds with fibrotic scarring rather than functional tissue replacement, resulting in progressive ventricular remodeling, systolic dysfunction, and eventual heart failure. Current therapeutic strategies, including reperfusion, pharmacological modulation of neurohormonal pathways, and mechanical support devices, address symptoms and slow disease progression but fail to restore lost myocardial tissue. Surgical interventions such as apical resection have demonstrated in preclinical models that the neonatal heart retains an intrinsic ability to regenerate through cardiomyocyte proliferation, suggesting that reactivating latent regenerative programs in the adult heart may offer a transformative approach to cardiac repair. Central to this regenerative potential is the Hippo signaling pathway, which acts as a master regulator of organ size during development and becomes highly active in postnatal cardiomyocytes, suppressing proliferation by promoting cytoplasmic sequestration and degradation of the transcriptional coactivators YAP and TAZ. Pharmacological agents that inhibit Hippo signaling or activate YAP/TAZ have shown promise in promoting cardiomyocyte cell cycle re-entry; however, the transient nature of systemic drug delivery has limited their efficacy. TT-10, a small-molecule fluorinated compound identified as a potent modulator of YAP signaling, has been shown to reduce infarct size and transiently improve cardiac function in murine models of myocardial infarction when administered via intraperitoneal injection. Despite these early benefits, the therapeutic window of TT-10 is narrow, with rapid clearance and insufficient tissue retention leading to a failure to sustain functional improvements over time. This limitation underscores the need for a delivery platform capable of providing sustained, localized exposure of TT-10 to the injured myocardium, thereby maximizing its regenerative potential while minimizing systemic side effects and dosage requirements.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses novel nanoparticle compositions comprising poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) encapsulating TT-10 for the targeted and sustained delivery of this therapeutic agent to the myocardium following ischemic injury. These compositions are designed to enhance the potency and durability of TT-10’s cardioprotective and regenerative effects by enabling controlled release over a period of up to nine days, thereby maintaining therapeutic concentrations within the peri-infarct region during the critical window of cardiomyocyte response to injury. The method of improving cardiac function involves the direct intramyocardial administration of these nanoparticle compositions into the border zone surrounding the infarct, resulting in significant reductions in infarct size, improved left ventricular ejection fraction, enhanced fractional shortening, and decreased end-systolic and end-diastolic dimensions compared to untreated controls or those receiving free TT-10. The administration is performed within 24 to 72 hours following myocardial infarction induction and is delivered in a single dose, with the nanoparticle composition remaining bioactive for a duration sufficient to promote cardiomyocyte proliferation, suppress apoptosis, and stimulate angiogenesis without inducing persistent cell cycle activity that could compromise contractile integrity. Additional features and advantages of the invention include the use of biocompatible and biodegradable polymers that eliminate the need for surgical removal, the ability to administer the composition via minimally invasive catheter-based or echo-guided techniques, and the compatibility of the formulation with low-dose regimens that reduce the risk of off-target effects. The invention further enables the modulation of Hippo/YAP signaling in a spatially and temporally controlled manner, thereby recapitulating the natural regenerative dynamics observed in neonatal hearts while avoiding the arrhythmogenic risks associated with prolonged cardiomyocyte proliferation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention encompasses a class of biodegradable nanoparticle compositions specifically engineered for the treatment of myocardial infarction through the sustained delivery of TT-10 to the injured myocardium. These nanoparticles are composed of poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid), a copolymer derived from the condensation polymerization of lactic acid and glycolic acid monomers, which is widely recognized for its safety profile and established use in clinical drug delivery systems. The molecular weight of the PLGA employed in the nanoparticles ranges from approximately 10,000 to 30,000 Daltons, providing an optimal balance between degradation kinetics and drug encapsulation efficiency. The ratio of lactide to glycolide in the copolymer is maintained between 50:50 and 75:25, which ensures a predictable and tunable release profile of TT-10 over a period of up to nine days, with approximately 47% of the encapsulated drug released within the first 24 hours and 90% released by day nine. The nanoparticles encapsulate TT-10 at a loading efficiency of approximately 2.5 micrograms per milligram of PLGA, resulting in a final composition wherein the average diameter of the nanoparticles is between 140 and 170 nanometers, as determined by nanoparticle tracking analysis and scanning electron microscopy. The nanoparticle composition includes TT-10 uniformly dispersed within the polymeric matrix, with the drug molecules protected from premature degradation and clearance by the surrounding PLGA shell. The method for improving cardiac function involves the direct administration of these TT-10-loaded nanoparticles into the myocardial tissue surrounding the infarct zone, preferably via intramyocardial injection at three distinct sites within the peri-infarct border zone. The target site for administration is the region of myocardium immediately adjacent to the necrotic core, where viable cardiomyocytes remain in a metabolically stressed state and are most responsive to regenerative stimuli. The duration of nanoparticle action is sufficient to sustain therapeutic concentrations of TT-10 for at least seven days, thereby covering the critical early phase of post-infarction inflammation and the initiation of cardiomyocyte cell cycle re-entry. The dosage of the nanoparticle composition is administered in a single bolus, with the total amount of TT-10 delivered ranging from 50 to 100 nanograms per mouse, which corresponds to a dose significantly lower than that required for systemic administration of free TT-10. The effect of this method on cardiac function is characterized by a marked improvement in left ventricular ejection fraction and fractional shortening, accompanied by a significant reduction in infarct size as measured by histological quantification of fibrotic scar tissue. Left ventricular end-systolic volume is reduced, and left ventricular end-diastolic volume is stabilized, indicating attenuation of adverse remodeling. The method improves multiple cardiac functions and properties, including contractility, compliance, and ventricular geometry, all of which contribute to enhanced overall cardiac performance. Infarct size is defined as the percentage of the left ventricular circumference occupied by fibrotic tissue, and in treated subjects, this value is reduced by at least 15% compared to control groups. Left ventricular ejection fraction is improved by a minimum of 10 percentage points, and left ventricular fractional shortening increases by at least 8 percentage points. Heart damage following myocardial infarction is caused by ischemia-induced cardiomyocyte death, mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and subsequent inflammatory infiltration, all of which trigger fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix deposition. TT-10 is a fluorinated small molecule that promotes cardiomyocyte survival and proliferation by inhibiting the Hippo signaling pathway, leading to nuclear translocation of YAP and subsequent transcriptional activation of genes involved in cell cycle progression and anti-apoptotic signaling. Its use in treating myocardial infarction is enhanced by encapsulation within PLGA nanoparticles, which prolong its bioavailability and localize its action to the site of injury.

### ASPECTS

The invention includes a nanoparticle composition comprising TT-10 encapsulated within a biocompatible polymer matrix, wherein the polymer is poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) with a lactide-to-glycolide ratio of 75:25 and a molecular weight of 20,000 Daltons. The nanoparticle composition is characterized by an average diameter of 155 nanometers and a TT-10 loading efficiency of 2.5 micrograms per milligram of polymer. The invention further includes a method for improving cardiac function in a subject following myocardial infarction by administering the nanoparticle composition directly into the myocardial tissue surrounding the infarct zone. The method results in increased expression of cardiomyocyte proliferation markers, including Ki67, phospho-histone H3, and nuclear YAP, and a reduction in apoptotic cardiomyocytes as measured by TUNEL staining. The method further includes the administration of the nanoparticle composition within 72 hours of myocardial infarction induction, with a single dose sufficient to sustain therapeutic concentrations for at least seven days. The method improves cardiac properties by increasing left ventricular ejection fraction by at least 10 percentage points and reducing infarct size by at least 15% compared to untreated controls. The nanoparticle composition is administered via intramyocardial injection at three distinct sites within the peri-infarct border zone, with each injection delivering 8 microliters of a suspension containing 22.4 nanograms of TT-10. The method enhances cardiomyocyte proliferation without inducing persistent cell cycle activity beyond the first week post-treatment, thereby minimizing the risk of arrhythmia. The method promotes angiogenesis in the peri-infarct region, as evidenced by increased expression of CD31 and α-smooth muscle actin. The nanoparticle composition is biodegradable and fully resorbed within four weeks, leaving no residual material in the myocardium. The method is applicable to both murine and porcine models of myocardial infarction and is translatable to human clinical settings using minimally invasive delivery techniques.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the preparation, characterization, and therapeutic efficacy of the TT-10-loaded PLGA nanoparticles as disclosed herein. Example 1 demonstrates that TT-10 promotes cell cycle activity in human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes, as evidenced by increased expression of Ki67, BrdU incorporation, and phospho-histone H3, with maximal effects observed at 10 to 20 micromolar concentrations. TT-10 also reduces apoptosis as measured by TUNEL staining and increases nuclear translocation of YAP, consistent with inhibition of Hippo signaling. Example 2 describes the preparation of PLGA nanoparticles using a single-emulsion solvent evaporation technique, with an average particle size of 155.4 ± 0.7 nanometers and a TT-10 loading efficiency of 2.50 ± 0.08 micrograms per milligram of polymer. In vitro release studies show that 47% of TT-10 is released within the first 24 hours and 90% by day nine. In a murine model of myocardial infarction, intramyocardial injection of TT-10-loaded nanoparticles significantly improves left ventricular ejection fraction and fractional shortening at both one and four weeks post-infarction, while infarct size is reduced by 20% compared to controls. Cardiomyocyte proliferation markers are elevated one week after treatment, but decline by four weeks, indicating transient but sufficient regenerative stimulation. Apoptosis is significantly reduced, and vascular density in the peri-infarct region is increased. Example 3 details the preparation of PLGA nanoparticles, characterization by scanning electron microscopy and nanoparticle tracking analysis, and confirmation of cellular uptake in human cardiomyocytes using fluorescently labeled NPs. In a mouse MI model, echocardiography confirms improved LVEF and LVFS, while histological analysis reveals reduced infarct size, increased Ki67+ and PH3+ cardiomyocytes, and decreased TUNEL+ cells. Western blot analysis confirms decreased phosphorylation of YAP and increased total YAP nuclear localization. In a porcine model of myocardial infarction, intramyocardial injection of TT-10-NPs via catheter-based delivery results in improved left ventricular function as assessed by echocardiography and cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, with reduced late gadolinium enhancement and increased cardiomyocyte cell-cycle activity. All studies were conducted under approved institutional protocols, and statistical significance was determined using appropriate parametric and non-parametric tests. Embodiments of the present disclosure include variations in PLGA molecular weight, lactide-to-glycolide ratio, nanoparticle size, TT-10 loading concentration, and administration route, all of which are within the scope of the invention. Modifications to the formulation, including the addition of targeting ligands, surface coatings, or co-encapsulated agents, are also contemplated without departing from the essential features of the invention.
