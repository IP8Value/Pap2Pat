# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P) is a bioactive sphingolipid metabolite that plays a central role in regulating a broad spectrum of cellular processes, including proliferation, survival, migration, angiogenesis, and immune cell trafficking. It functions as a signaling molecule through interaction with five G protein-coupled receptors, S1PR1 through S1PR5, located on the plasma membrane, as well as through intracellular targets such as histone deacetylases and TRAF2. The cellular concentration of S1P is tightly regulated by a dynamic equilibrium between its biosynthesis and degradation, a balance often referred to as the sphingolipid rheostat. This rheostat is governed by the opposing actions of pro-apoptotic sphingolipids—ceramide and sphingosine—and the pro-survival lipid S1P. The enzymatic conversion of sphingosine to S1P is catalyzed by two closely related isoforms, sphingosine kinase 1 (SphK1) and sphingosine kinase 2 (SphK2), both of which utilize ATP as a phosphate donor. Although these enzymes share significant sequence homology and catalytic mechanism, they differ markedly in their subcellular localization, tissue distribution, and biological functions. SphK1 is predominantly cytosolic and is rapidly translocated to the plasma membrane upon stimulation, where it promotes cell survival and growth through the generation of extracellular S1P that activates its cognate receptors. In contrast, SphK2 is localized primarily within the nucleus, endoplasmic reticulum, and mitochondria, where it generates S1P in distinct microdomains that influence intracellular signaling pathways, including epigenetic regulation via histone deacetylase inhibition and modulation of mitochondrial function.

SphK1 has been extensively studied and is frequently overexpressed in a wide array of human cancers, where it contributes to tumor progression, resistance to apoptosis, and metastasis. Its inhibition has been shown to suppress tumor growth in preclinical models, leading to the development of several SphK1-selective inhibitors. However, the role of SphK2 in cancer biology has been more complex and context-dependent. Early studies suggested that SphK2 exerted pro-apoptotic effects, but more recent evidence demonstrates that SphK2 is essential for the proliferation and survival of certain cancer cell types, including glioblastoma, breast cancer, and leukemia. Genetic ablation or pharmacological inhibition of SphK2 has been shown to reduce tumor burden in xenograft models, indicating that SphK2 can function as an oncogenic driver in specific cellular contexts. Moreover, SphK2 has been implicated in the regulation of nuclear S1P pools that directly modulate chromatin structure and gene expression, suggesting a unique role in transcriptional reprogramming during tumorigenesis. Despite these advances, the development of pharmacological tools to selectively target SphK2 has lagged behind that of SphK1. Existing SphK2 inhibitors, such as ABC 294640, SG-12, and R-FTY720-OMe, exhibit only moderate potency, limited selectivity, or unfavorable pharmacokinetic profiles, and none have advanced to clinical use. There remains a critical unmet need for potent, selective, and bioavailable SphK2 inhibitors that can be used both as mechanistic probes to dissect SphK2 biology and as therapeutic candidates for cancers dependent on SphK2-mediated signaling.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention relates to a novel class of thiazolidine-2,4-dione derivatives that function as selective inhibitors of sphingosine kinase 2 (SphK2) and are useful in the treatment of cancer. Specifically, the invention encompasses compounds of Formula I and Formula II, which are characterized by a 3-(2-aminoethyl)-thiazolidine-2,4-dione core linked to an aromatic moiety via an alkylidene spacer. These compounds selectively inhibit SphK2 with minimal activity against SphK1, ceramide kinase, and other related kinases, thereby enabling targeted disruption of SphK2-dependent signaling pathways without perturbing the broader sphingolipid network. The compounds of Formula I are defined by the general structure wherein R1 is an alkyl or alkoxy substituent containing 4, 7, or 8 carbon atoms, R2 through R5 are independently selected from hydrogen, halogen, alkyl, alkoxy, or aryl, V is a methylene or ethylene bridge, X and Y are independently selected from oxygen or sulfur, Z is a single bond or a methylene group, and W is an amino group. Compounds of Formula II are structurally related and are defined by R10 through R18, which are substituents on a substituted phenyl ring, and X, Y, Z, and W as previously defined, with the additional requirement that W is specifically an amino group. The invention further includes compounds of Formula III and Formula IV, which are specific embodiments of Formula I and Formula II, respectively, and include the compound KL145, which is 3-(2-aminoethyl)-5-[3-(4-butoxyphenyl)propylidene]thiazolidine-2,4-dione, and KLII1016, which is a structurally analogous derivative with an octyloxy substituent. These compounds demonstrate potent inhibition of SphK2 in enzymatic assays, with IC50 values below 5 µM, while exhibiting no significant inhibition of SphK1 at concentrations up to 10 µM. The invention provides methods for treating cancer by administering an effective amount of a compound of Formula I, Formula II, Formula III, or Formula IV to a subject in need thereof. The cancer types encompassed by the invention include, but are not limited to, leukemia, lymphoma, breast cancer, glioblastoma, ovarian cancer, prostate cancer, and pancreatic cancer. The methods of inhibiting SphK2 involve contacting the enzyme with a compound of Formula I, Formula II, Formula III, or Formula IV under conditions sufficient to reduce its catalytic activity by at least 50% compared to an untreated control. The invention further specifies that the compounds are orally bioavailable and retain their inhibitory activity in vivo, as demonstrated by suppression of tumor growth in xenograft and syngeneic mouse models following oral or intraperitoneal administration. The invention also includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising a compound of Formula I, Formula II, Formula III, or Formula IV in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or additive. The objectives of the invention are to provide novel chemical entities that selectively target SphK2, to enable the modulation of intracellular S1P pools in cancer cells, to inhibit downstream survival signaling pathways such as ERK and Akt, and to offer a therapeutic strategy for cancers that are dependent on SphK2 activity for proliferation and evasion of apoptosis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The compounds of the invention are characterized by a thiazolidine-2,4-dione scaffold substituted at the 3-position with a 2-aminoethyl group and at the 5-position with an alkylidene linker terminating in an aromatic ring bearing an alkoxy substituent of defined chain length. The structural features of Formula I are defined by the presence of a five-membered heterocyclic ring containing two carbonyl groups and a sulfur atom, with the 3-position substituted by a two-carbon chain bearing a terminal primary amine, and the 5-position connected via a vinyl linkage to a phenyl ring substituted at the para-position with an alkoxy group containing 4, 7, or 8 carbon atoms. The alkyl chain of the alkoxy substituent may be linear or branched, and the phenyl ring may be further substituted with one or more halogens, alkyl, or alkoxy groups at the ortho or meta positions. The R2 through R5 substituents may be hydrogen, methyl, ethyl, fluoro, chloro, or methoxy, with hydrogen being the preferred group. The V group is typically a methylene unit, although an ethylene bridge is also encompassed. The X and Y atoms are most commonly oxygen, though sulfur may be substituted at either position without substantial loss of activity. The Z group is preferably a single bond, allowing for direct conjugation between the thiazolidinedione ring and the alkylidene linker. The W group is exclusively an amino group, which is critical for forming hydrogen bonds with Asp344 in the SphK2 active site, as confirmed by molecular modeling studies. Compounds of Formula II are structurally analogous but feature a modified aromatic ring with additional substituents R10 through R18, which may include alkyl, alkoxy, halogen, or nitro groups, positioned to enhance binding affinity or metabolic stability. The compounds of Formula III and Formula IV represent specific embodiments of Formula I and Formula II, respectively, and include KL145 and KLII1016, which have been synthesized and validated for biological activity. The compounds of the invention are synthesized through a multi-step process involving the condensation of appropriately substituted aldehydes with 3-(2-aminoethyl)thiazolidine-2,4-dione derivatives under basic catalysis, followed by purification by column chromatography or crystallization. Structural characterization is confirmed by nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy, mass spectrometry, and elemental analysis. The compounds demonstrate selective inhibition of SphK2 in recombinant enzyme assays, with IC50 values ranging from 1.2 µM to 8.5 µM, while showing no inhibition of SphK1 at concentrations up to 10 µM. Selectivity is further confirmed by lack of inhibition against ceramide kinase and a panel of eleven other kinases. The compounds are taken up efficiently by cancer cells, where they reduce intracellular S1P levels, increase ceramide accumulation, and inhibit phosphorylation of FTY720, a known SphK2-specific substrate. The compounds induce apoptosis in leukemia, breast, and glioblastoma cell lines, and suppress tumor growth in xenograft models of human leukemia and murine mammary carcinoma following both intraperitoneal and oral administration. The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention include the active compounds in combination with excipients such as lactose, microcrystalline cellulose, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose, magnesium stearate, polysorbate 80, sodium citrate, and benzyl alcohol, formulated as tablets, capsules, suspensions, or injectables. The compounds are administered in dosages ranging from 1 mg/kg to 100 mg/kg daily, depending on route of administration and disease state. The effective amount is defined as the quantity sufficient to reduce tumor volume by at least 30% relative to vehicle-treated controls in a xenograft model over a 14-day period. The invention further encompasses methods of treating diseases associated with aberrant SphK2 activity, including but not limited to hematologic malignancies, solid tumors, and inflammatory conditions driven by S1P signaling. The compounds are non-toxic at therapeutic doses, as evidenced by stable body weight and normal histology of major organs in treated animals. The invention provides a new class of orally bioavailable, selective SphK2 inhibitors with demonstrated efficacy in preclinical cancer models.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1  
Compound 26 was prepared by condensing 3-(2-aminoethyl)-thiazolidine-2,4-dione with 4-(4-butoxyphenyl)-3-propenal in ethanol under reflux in the presence of piperidine. The reaction mixture was stirred for 18 hours at ambient temperature, after which the solvent was removed under reduced pressure. The crude residue was purified by silica gel chromatography using a gradient of hexane and ethyl acetate (8:1 to 6:1), yielding compound 26 as a white crystalline solid in 68% yield. The structure was confirmed by ¹H NMR and ¹³C NMR spectroscopy.

### Example 2  
Compound 27 was synthesized by analogous methodology using 4-(7-octyloxyphenyl)-3-propenal in place of the 4-butoxy analog. The condensation reaction proceeded similarly, with purification by flash chromatography yielding compound 27 as a pale yellow solid in 72% yield. High-resolution mass spectrometry confirmed the molecular formula C₂₂H₃₅N₂O₃S.

### Example 3  
Compounds 28 through 36 were prepared by varying the alkoxy chain length and aromatic substitution pattern. Each aldehyde precursor was synthesized via reduction of the corresponding Meldrum’s acid adduct, followed by deprotection and oxidation. Condensation with the 3-(2-aminoethyl)thiazolidine-2,4-dione core was performed under identical conditions, affording the target compounds in yields ranging from 58% to 75%. All compounds were characterized by NMR and mass spectrometry.

### Example 4  
The alkoxylation reaction to prepare 4-alkoxyphenylpropanal compounds 14 through 19 was carried out by reacting 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde with the appropriate alkyl bromide in acetone under basic conditions using potassium carbonate. The reaction was heated at 60°C for 12 hours, followed by extraction and purification by silica gel chromatography. The aldehydes were obtained in yields of 65–82%.

### Example 5  
Compounds 2 through 7, 4-alkoxybenzaldehydes, were synthesized by direct alkoxylation of 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde using alkyl iodides and cesium carbonate in acetonitrile under microwave irradiation for 30 minutes. Purification by column chromatography yielded the desired products as colorless oils, confirmed by ¹H NMR and GC-MS.

### Example 6  
Compounds 24, 46, 48, and 50 were synthesized via Swern oxidation of the corresponding primary alcohols. Dimethyl sulfoxide and oxalyl chloride were added sequentially at −78°C, followed by triethylamine. The reaction was quenched with aqueous sodium bicarbonate and extracted with ethyl acetate. The products were purified by silica gel chromatography and characterized by NMR.

### Example 7  
Compound 39 was prepared by reduction of the corresponding nitro derivative using tin(II) chloride in ethanol under reflux for 6 hours. The reaction mixture was basified and extracted with dichloromethane. The organic layer was dried and concentrated to afford compound 39 as a white solid in 81% yield.

### Example 8  
Guanidine analog 42 was synthesized by reacting compound 39 with O-methylisourea in methanol at 50°C for 8 hours. The product was isolated by precipitation and purified by recrystallization from ethanol.

### In vitro Test for SphK1 and SphK2  
Recombinant human SphK1 and SphK2 were expressed in Sf9 insect cells and lysates were assayed using [γ-³²P]ATP and sphingosine as substrate. Compound KL145 inhibited SphK2 with an IC₅₀ of 4.3±0.06 µM and showed no inhibition of SphK1 at 10 µM. DMS, a non-selective inhibitor, inhibited both isoforms at 10 µM.

### Kinetic Studies of Formula III (KL145)  
Lineweaver-Burk plots of SphK2 activity in the presence of increasing concentrations of KL145 indicated competitive inhibition with respect to sphingosine, with a Kᵢ of 6.4±0.7 µM. The slope of the double reciprocal plot increased with inhibitor concentration, while Vₘₐₓ remained unchanged.

### Cell Viability Assays in Human Leukemia U937 Cells  
U937 cells were treated with KL145 at concentrations from 0.1 to 20 µM for 72 hours. Cell viability was assessed by MTT assay. KL145 reduced viability with an IC₅₀ of 5.2 µM, consistent with its SphK2 inhibitory potency.

### Western Blot Analysis  
Treatment of U937 cells with KL145 (4 µM) for 1 hour resulted in decreased phosphorylation of ERK and Akt, without affecting total protein levels. GAPDH served as a loading control.

### Cell Apoptosis Assays  
Annexin V/PI staining of U937 cells treated with KL145 (10 µM) for 24 hours revealed 38% late apoptosis and 4% necrosis, compared to 8% late apoptosis in vehicle-treated controls.

### In Vivo Studies for Anti-Proliferation of U937 Xenografts in Nude Mice  
Female BALB/c-nu mice bearing U937 xenografts were treated with KL145 (15 mg/kg i.p. daily) or tamibarotene (15 mg/kg i.p. daily) for 17 days. KL145 reduced tumor volume by 44.2% and tumor weight by 42.8%, comparable to tamibarotene. Body weight remained stable.

### In Vivo Studies for Anti-Growth of JC Xenografts in Syngeneic BALB/c Mice  
BALB/c mice bearing JC mammary adenocarcinoma xenografts were treated with KL145 (20 or 35 mg/kg i.p. daily) for 15 days. Tumor volume was reduced by 52% and 61%, respectively. S1P levels in tumor tissue were significantly decreased, and p-ERK/p-Akt were suppressed.

### In Vivo Studies for Anti-Proliferation of U937 Xenografts in Nude Mice by KL145 Through Oral Administration  
Oral administration of KL145 (50 mg/kg daily) for 15 days resulted in a 51.25% reduction in tumor volume, superior to tamibarotene (33.37%). Body weight transiently decreased at day 3 but recovered by day 10.

### In Vitro Target Validation of KL145  
KL145 was detected in U937 cell lysates by LC-MS/MS after 3-hour exposure. Cellular S1P levels decreased by 62%, while ceramide increased by 48%. Ceramide-1-phosphate and FTY720 phosphorylation were unaffected or reduced, respectively, confirming SphK2 selectivity.
