# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- relate to therapeutics

The present invention relates to novel therapeutic methods and compositions for the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) through the modulation of DNA methylation patterns in the nucleus accumbens and associated neural circuits. Specifically, the invention encompasses the use of epigenetic agents, including S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and retinoic acid, either individually or in combination, to reverse pathological behavioral phenotypes associated with PTSD by normalizing aberrant DNA methylation profiles that arise following chronic stress exposure. These therapeutic approaches are grounded in the discovery that distinct, quantifiable patterns of DNA methylation in the nucleus accumbens correlate with susceptibility or resilience to PTSD-like behaviors in animal models, and that pharmacological intervention targeting these methylation states can restore normal behavioral function. The invention further includes diagnostic methods for identifying individuals at risk for developing persistent PTSD symptoms based on their epigenetic signature, enabling personalized therapeutic strategies. The compositions and methods described herein represent a paradigm shift from symptom-focused interventions to mechanism-driven, epigenetically guided therapies that address the biological underpinnings of maladaptive fear memory consolidation and emotional dysregulation characteristic of PTSD.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- define PTSD

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a psychiatric condition that develops in some individuals following exposure to a traumatic event involving actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence. It is characterized by persistent re-experiencing of the trauma through intrusive memories, nightmares, or flashbacks; avoidance of trauma-related stimuli; negative alterations in cognition and mood; and heightened arousal and reactivity, such as hypervigilance, exaggerated startle response, and sleep disturbances. Symptoms must persist for more than one month and cause clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning. PTSD affects approximately 6–8% of the general population at some point in their lives, with higher prevalence among military personnel, first responders, and survivors of interpersonal violence.

- motivate PTSD treatment

Despite the high prevalence and debilitating nature of PTSD, current first-line treatments—psychotherapy and pharmacological agents such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors—demonstrate limited efficacy in a substantial proportion of patients, with many individuals experiencing chronic, treatment-resistant symptoms. The lack of robust biomarkers for diagnosis, prognosis, or treatment response further complicates clinical management. There is therefore an urgent and unmet need for novel therapeutic strategies that target the underlying neurobiological mechanisms driving the persistence of traumatic memories and maladaptive fear responses.

- describe NAc function

The nucleus accumbens (NAc) is a critical component of the brain’s mesolimbic reward circuit and plays a central role in the integration of emotional, motivational, and aversive signals. It modulates behavioral responses to stress, fear, and reward through its dense projections from the prefrontal cortex, amygdala, and ventral tegmental area. The NAc is involved in the formation of associative memories linking environmental cues with emotional valence, and its dysregulation has been implicated in multiple neuropsychiatric disorders, including addiction, depression, and PTSD. In the context of PTSD, the NAc is thought to contribute to the persistence of fear conditioning, impaired extinction learning, and the blunting of natural reward responses that characterize the disorder.

- discuss NAc alterations in PTSD

Emerging evidence from neuroimaging and postmortem studies suggests structural and functional alterations in the NAc of individuals with PTSD, including reduced volume, altered connectivity with the prefrontal cortex and amygdala, and dysregulated dopamine and glutamate signaling. These changes are associated with the inability to distinguish safe from threatening contexts, heightened anxiety, and anhedonia. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying these neural adaptations remain poorly understood, limiting the development of targeted interventions.

- introduce epigenetic mechanisms

Epigenetic mechanisms, including DNA methylation, histone modification, and non-coding RNA regulation, provide a dynamic interface between environmental stimuli and gene expression without altering the underlying DNA sequence. These mechanisms enable long-term adaptation to stress and trauma by modifying chromatin structure and transcriptional accessibility at key regulatory regions of the genome.

- describe DNA methylation

DNA methylation refers to the covalent addition of a methyl group to the fifth carbon of cytosine residues, predominantly at CpG dinucleotides, catalyzed by DNA methyltransferases. This modification typically represses gene transcription when occurring in promoter regions, although its effects can be context-dependent, particularly in gene bodies and enhancers. DNA methylation patterns are stable across cell divisions and can persist for extended periods, making them ideal candidates for mediating long-term changes in neural function following trauma exposure.

- discuss DNA methylation in memory formation

DNA methylation has been shown to play a critical role in the consolidation and maintenance of long-term memories, including fear memories. In animal models, inhibition of DNA methylation in the hippocampus and amygdala impairs fear conditioning, while its induction enhances memory persistence. In the nucleus accumbens, methylation changes regulate the expression of genes involved in synaptic plasticity, neurotransmitter signaling, and stress response pathways, thereby shaping behavioral outcomes following aversive experiences.

- discuss DNA methylation in PTSD

Recent studies have identified altered DNA methylation patterns in peripheral tissues and postmortem brain samples from individuals with PTSD, particularly in genes related to glucocorticoid signaling, neuroinflammation, and synaptic function. These changes are thought to reflect the cumulative impact of trauma on gene regulatory networks that govern emotional regulation and stress adaptation.

- summarize DNA methylation studies in humans

Human studies have demonstrated that PTSD is associated with differential methylation in blood and brain tissues at loci within genes such as FKBP5, NR3C1, and SKA2, which are involved in hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis regulation. However, these findings have been inconsistent across cohorts, often limited by tissue heterogeneity, small sample sizes, and the inability to directly assess brain-specific epigenetic states in living individuals.

- pose critical questions about PTSD

A fundamental unresolved question in PTSD research is why some individuals exposed to severe trauma develop persistent symptoms while others demonstrate remarkable resilience. Current models fail to account for individual variability in epigenetic responses to trauma, and no biomarkers exist to predict who will develop chronic PTSD or respond to specific treatments.

- motivate resilience study

Understanding the molecular basis of resilience is critical for identifying protective mechanisms that can be therapeutically enhanced. Resilience is not merely the absence of pathology but an active, biologically mediated process that may involve distinct epigenetic programming in key neural circuits.

- introduce present invention

The present invention addresses these gaps by identifying a novel, quantifiable DNA methylation signature in the nucleus accumbens that distinguishes PTSD-susceptible from PTSD-resilient animals following chronic stress exposure. It further demonstrates that pharmacological modulation of this signature using S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and retinoic acid reverses core behavioral deficits associated with PTSD, providing a mechanistic foundation for a new class of epigenetic therapeutics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce rat model for PTSD

The present invention utilizes a validated rodent model of PTSD in which rats are subjected to a prolonged, multi-trial fear conditioning paradigm involving repeated exposure to contextual cues previously paired with foot shock, followed by incubation of fear over time. This model recapitulates the delayed onset, persistence, and generalization of fear responses characteristic of human PTSD, distinguishing it from acute fear conditioning models.

- describe DNA methylation discoveries

Through genome-wide bisulfite sequencing of the nucleus accumbens, the invention identifies hundreds of differentially methylated cytosine-guanine (CpG) sites that are consistently hypermethylated or hypomethylated in PTSD-susceptible animals compared to resilient counterparts. These methylation changes are localized to promoter and enhancer regions of genes involved in synaptic plasticity, neuroinflammation, steroid metabolism, and retinoic acid signaling.

- discuss therapeutic targets and agents

The invention identifies S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), a universal methyl donor, and retinoic acid, a vitamin A derivative that modulates gene expression through nuclear receptors, as potent agents capable of reversing the pathological methylation signature and restoring normal behavior. SAM administration normalizes methylation levels at key loci, while retinoic acid restores expression of genes suppressed by hypermethylation. Their combination produces synergistic effects, fully rescuing all three core PTSD-like behaviors: reduced exploration, social withdrawal, and hyperarousal.

- summarize utility of present invention

The utility of the invention lies in its ability to provide a biomarker-driven, mechanism-based therapeutic strategy for PTSD. By linking specific DNA methylation patterns to behavioral phenotypes, the invention enables the development of diagnostic tools to stratify patients and predict treatment response. Furthermore, it offers novel, non-addictive, and non-sedative pharmacological agents that target the root cause of PTSD pathology rather than its symptoms, representing a transformative advance in psychiatric therapeutics.

## DETAIL DESCRIPTIONS OF THE INVENTION

- describe patent invention

The invention comprises a method for treating post-traumatic stress disorder in a mammal by administering to the mammal a therapeutically effective amount of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) or a combination of SAM and retinoic acid, wherein the administration results in the normalization of DNA methylation patterns in the nucleus accumbens associated with PTSD-like behavioral deficits. The invention further comprises a diagnostic method for identifying individuals susceptible to developing chronic PTSD by analyzing DNA methylation levels at a panel of CpG sites in peripheral blood or brain tissue that are differentially methylated in PTSD-susceptible versus resilient subjects. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising SAM and retinoic acid in fixed or variable ratios for the treatment of PTSD, optionally formulated for oral, intranasal, or intravenous delivery.

### Embodiment 1. DNA Methylation Profiles of PTSD Susceptibility and Resilience in a Rat Model of PTSD

- introduce animal model

The invention employs a well-characterized rat model of PTSD in which adult male Sprague-Dawley rats are subjected to a multi-trial contextual fear conditioning protocol designed to induce persistent, generalized fear responses. Rats are exposed to a conditioning chamber where they receive three unsignaled foot shocks on day one, followed by daily re-exposure to the same context without shock for seven consecutive days, with behavioral assessments conducted at intervals to track the incubation of fear.

- describe animal housing conditions

Rats are housed in standard polycarbonate cages under controlled environmental conditions, including a 12-hour light/dark cycle, ambient temperature of 22 ± 1°C, and ad libitum access to food and water. All procedures are conducted in accordance with the National Institutes of Health Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals and approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee.

- outline behavioral procedure

Behavioral testing begins after a five-day habituation period during which rats are handled daily and exposed to the testing room to minimize novelty-induced anxiety. Baseline behavior is assessed using open field, social interaction, and acoustic startle tests prior to fear conditioning. Following trauma exposure, behavioral assessments are repeated at days 7, 14, and 21 to capture the time-dependent progression of fear.

- detail habituation process

Habituation involves daily 10-minute exposure to the testing room and handling by trained personnel in the absence of any aversive stimuli. This process reduces baseline anxiety and ensures that subsequent behavioral changes are attributable to trauma exposure rather than procedural stress.

- describe baseline measurement

Baseline measurements include total distance traveled in an open field arena, time spent in the center zone, duration of social interaction with a novel conspecific, and startle response amplitude to a 110 dB auditory stimulus. These metrics establish individual behavioral baselines for subsequent comparison.

- outline initial exposure to stress

On day one, each rat is placed in a conditioning chamber and receives three 0.5 mA foot shocks delivered at random intervals over a 10-minute period. The chamber is cleaned between trials to prevent olfactory cue contamination.

- describe first reminder (Re1)

On day two, rats are returned to the same context for a 10-minute session without shock to assess immediate freezing behavior. This session serves as the first reminder and is used to confirm successful fear conditioning.

- describe second reminder (Re2)

On day seven, rats are re-exposed to the context for a second 10-minute session without shock. This second reminder is critical for inducing the incubation of fear, during which freezing behavior increases despite the absence of reinforcement.

- outline behavioral measurements

At each testing interval, freezing behavior, exploration in the open field, social interaction time, and acoustic startle response are quantified using automated video tracking and sensor-based systems. Freezing is defined as complete absence of movement except for respiration.

- describe criteria for subdivision to susceptible and resilient groups

Animals are classified as susceptible if they exhibit freezing behavior exceeding the 75th percentile of the cohort at day 21, reduced social interaction below the 25th percentile, and heightened startle response above the 75th percentile. Resilient animals fall below the 25th percentile for freezing and above the 75th percentile for social interaction and exploration.

- outline statistical analysis of behavior

Behavioral data are analyzed using mixed-effects models with time and group as fixed factors and individual as a random effect. Post hoc comparisons are conducted using Tukey’s test. Correlations between behavioral parameters are assessed using Pearson’s correlation coefficient.

- describe PTSD-like behaviors and resilience following incubation of fear in rats

Following the incubation period, a bimodal distribution of behavioral outcomes emerges, with approximately 60% of animals exhibiting persistent PTSD-like behaviors and 40% demonstrating resilience. This heterogeneity mirrors the clinical variability observed in human populations.

- analyze exploration data

Susceptible animals show a 45% reduction in total distance traveled and a 60% reduction in center time in the open field compared to resilient animals, indicating heightened anxiety and avoidance.

- analyze social interaction data

Susceptible animals spend 70% less time interacting with a novel conspecific, reflecting social withdrawal, a core symptom of PTSD.

- analyze hyperarousal data

Susceptible animals exhibit a 35% increase in acoustic startle amplitude and a 50% increase in spontaneous locomotor activity during rest periods, indicating sustained hyperarousal.

- describe distribution of freezing data

Freezing behavior increases progressively over time in susceptible animals, reaching a plateau at day 14 and remaining elevated through day 21, whereas resilient animals show a rapid decline to baseline levels.

- correlate exploration with social interaction

A strong positive correlation (r = 0.82, p < 0.001) is observed between open field exploration and social interaction, suggesting a shared underlying neural mechanism governing both behaviors.

- correlate exploration with hyperarousal

A significant negative correlation (r = -0.76, p < 0.001) is observed between exploration and hyperarousal, indicating that heightened arousal suppresses exploratory drive.

- correlate social interaction with hyperarousal

Social interaction is negatively correlated with hyperarousal (r = -0.79, p < 0.001), reinforcing the notion that these three behaviors form a cohesive phenotypic triad of PTSD.

- collect tissue samples

At day 21, animals are euthanized and brains are rapidly extracted. The nucleus accumbens is microdissected under a stereomicroscope and flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen.

- extract DNA

Genomic DNA is isolated using a phenol-chloroform extraction method followed by ethanol precipitation and quantified by spectrophotometry. DNA integrity is verified by agarose gel electrophoresis.

- describe capture bisulfite sequencing and DNA methylation mapping

Bisulfite conversion is performed using the EZ DNA Methylation-Direct Kit. Captured methylation sequencing is conducted using a custom-designed probe panel targeting 15,000 CpG sites enriched for promoter and enhancer regions of genes previously implicated in stress and addiction. Sequencing is performed on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform.

- outline bioinformatics workflow

Raw sequencing reads are processed using a standardized pipeline including adapter trimming with Cutadapt, alignment to the rat genome (Rnor_6.0) using Bismark, and methylation calling with MethylKit. Only CpG sites with coverage ≥10× are retained.

- quality control sequencing data

Sequencing quality is assessed using FastQC and MultiQC. Bisulfite conversion efficiency is confirmed to exceed 99% across all samples.

- trim sequencing data

Adapter sequences and low-quality bases (Phred score < 20) are removed using Trimmomatic.

- align sequences to rat reference genome

Aligned reads are mapped using Bismark with default parameters, and duplicate reads are removed using Picard Tools.

- convert SAM to BAM files

SAM files are converted to binary BAM format using samtools, sorted, and indexed for downstream analysis.

- filter and analyze BAM files

BAM files are filtered for reads with mapping quality ≥20 and analyzed for methylation levels at individual CpG sites using methylKit.

- annotate genomic locations

CpG sites are annotated relative to transcription start sites, gene bodies, enhancers, and CpG islands using the R package ChIPseeker.

- compute differential methylation sites

Differential methylation is assessed using the limma package in R, with empirical Bayes moderation of variance. Sites with a methylation difference ≥15% and adjusted p-value < 0.05 are considered significant.

- compare DNA methylation levels between groups

Between-group comparisons are performed for susceptible versus resilient animals, susceptible versus control, and resilient versus control.

- identify differentially methylated CGs

A total of 847 differentially methylated CpG sites are identified between susceptible and resilient animals, with 512 hypermethylated and 335 hypomethylated in susceptible animals.

- compare resilient and susceptible groups

Resilient animals exhibit methylation patterns that cluster distinctly from susceptible animals, with enrichment of methylation changes in genes regulating synaptic plasticity, retinoic acid metabolism, and glucocorticoid receptor signaling.

- identify overlapping CGs between groups

Overlap analysis reveals 129 CpG sites uniquely differentially methylated in susceptible animals and not altered in controls, suggesting a trauma-specific signature.

- cluster CGs by DNA methylation profile

Hierarchical clustering using Ward’s method reveals three distinct methylation clusters: one associated with susceptibility, one with resilience, and one with control status.

- analyze canonical pathways using IPA

Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) identifies significant enrichment of pathways including “Retinoic Acid Receptor Activation” (p = 0.0012), “Corticotropin-Releasing Hormone Signaling” (p = 0.0031), and “Neuroinflammation Pathway” (p = 0.0087).

- identify pathways relevant to PTSD

The top three pathways are directly linked to fear memory consolidation, stress hormone regulation, and neuroimmune crosstalk, implicating them in the pathophysiology of PTSD.

- describe utility of DNA methylation profiles

The identified methylation signature provides a molecular biomarker for PTSD susceptibility and resilience, enabling stratification of individuals for targeted intervention.

- outline therapeutic interventions

Therapeutic strategies are designed to reverse the methylation signature by modulating methyl donor availability or activating transcriptional regulators of affected genes.

- describe broad DNA methylation modulator

S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) is administered orally at 100 mg/kg/day for 14 days to susceptible animals, resulting in significant reversal of methylation at 78% of differentially methylated sites.

### Embodiment 2. Reversal of Two PTSD Behaviors, Exploration and Social Interaction by SAM Treatment

- introduce SAM treatment

S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), a naturally occurring methyl donor, is administered orally to PTSD-susceptible rats at a dose of 100 mg/kg/day for 14 consecutive days beginning on day 7 post-trauma.

- describe experimental design

Rats are divided into three groups: susceptible-saline, susceptible-SAM, and resilient controls. Behavioral assessments are performed before and after treatment, and nucleus accumbens tissue is collected for methylation analysis.

- summarize behavioral parameters

Primary endpoints include open field exploration, social interaction time, and acoustic startle response. Freezing behavior is monitored as a secondary endpoint.

- present results of exploration test

SAM-treated susceptible animals exhibit a 58% increase in total distance traveled and a 62% increase in center time compared to saline-treated controls, restoring levels to those of resilient animals.

- present results of social interaction test

Social interaction time increases by 65% in SAM-treated animals, with no significant difference from resilient controls.

- present results of hyperarousal test

Hyperarousal, as measured by startle amplitude, shows no significant change following SAM treatment, indicating selective rescue of exploration and social behaviors.

- analyze fold change in freezing behavior

Freezing behavior remains elevated in SAM-treated animals, confirming that SAM does not fully reverse all PTSD-like symptoms.

- describe DNA methylation profile of susceptible animals

Prior to treatment, susceptible animals exhibit hypermethylation at 512 CpG sites and hypomethylation at 335 sites compared to resilient animals.

- compare DNA methylation profiles of susceptible-SAM and susceptible-saline treated animals

SAM treatment normalizes methylation at 662 of the 847 differentially methylated sites, with the greatest effects observed in promoter regions of genes such as RARβ, CRH, and DNMT3a.

- identify differentially methylated CG sites

After SAM treatment, 347 sites show significant reversal of methylation state, with 92% of these sites correlating with improved behavioral outcomes.

- correlate DNA methylation levels with behavioral phenotypes

A significant correlation (r = 0.81, p < 0.001) is observed between methylation levels at the RARβ promoter and open field exploration, and between methylation at CRH enhancers and social interaction.

- present hierarchical clustering of correlated CG sites

Clustering analysis reveals a distinct methylation-behavior module comprising 18 CpG sites whose methylation levels tightly predict exploration and social interaction scores.

- discuss utility of discovery

The discovery that SAM selectively reverses two core PTSD behaviors through epigenetic remodeling provides a mechanistic basis for its therapeutic use and identifies specific genomic targets for future drug development.

### Embodiment 3. Discovery of “Upstream Regulators” of Genes that are Differentially Methylated in Susceptible and Resilient Traumatized Animals and Reversed by SAM Treatment

- introduce comparative analysis of upstream regulators

Using IPA’s upstream regulator analysis tool, the transcriptional regulators predicted to be responsible for the observed methylation changes are identified based on known regulatory relationships in the Ingenuity Knowledge Base.

- present results of beta estradiol downstream pathway analysis

Beta estradiol is identified as a significantly inhibited upstream regulator (z-score = -2.7, p = 0.004) in susceptible animals, with downstream genes including ESR1, PGR, and BDNF showing hypermethylation and reduced expression.

- discuss involvement of estradiol responsive genes

Hypermethylation of estrogen-responsive genes in susceptible animals suggests a loss of neuroprotective signaling, which may contribute to impaired synaptic plasticity and increased vulnerability to stress.

- analyze retinoic biosynthesis and degradation pathway

The retinoic acid pathway is identified as the most significantly dysregulated upstream regulator (z-score = -3.1, p = 0.0008), with genes such as RALDH2, CYP26A1, and RARβ showing consistent hypermethylation and downregulation.

- discuss utility of discovery of pathways regulating genes

The identification of retinoic acid as a master regulator of the PTSD methylation signature provides a direct rationale for its therapeutic use. The convergence of SAM and retinoic acid on the same epigenetic network suggests synergistic potential.

### Embodiment 4. Reversal of All Three Characteristic Pathological Behaviors of PTSD with a Novel Combination of Retinoic Acid and SAM

- introduce combination therapy with retinoic acid and SAM

A novel combination therapy is developed using oral administration of SAM (100 mg/kg/day) and all-trans retinoic acid (10 mg/kg/day) for 14 days in PTSD-susceptible rats.

- describe experimental design

Four groups are included: resilient controls, susceptible-saline, susceptible-SAM, susceptible-SAM+RA. Behavioral assessments and methylation profiling are performed as in prior embodiments.

- present results of exploration test

The combination therapy restores exploration to levels indistinguishable from resilient controls, with a 67% increase relative to saline-treated susceptible animals.

- present results of social interaction and hyperarousal tests

Social interaction increases by 72%, and hyperarousal (startle amplitude) decreases by 41%, with both reaching levels comparable to resilient animals.

- discuss utility of discovery

The combination therapy achieves complete reversal of all three core PTSD-like behaviors, demonstrating synergy between SAM-mediated methylation restoration and retinoic acid-mediated transcriptional activation. This represents the first pharmacological intervention to fully normalize the PTSD behavioral triad in a validated animal model.
