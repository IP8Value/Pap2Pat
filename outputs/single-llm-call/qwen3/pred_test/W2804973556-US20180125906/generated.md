# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

- relate to treating ischemic conditions

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for the treatment of ischemic conditions, particularly those involving myocardial tissue. More specifically, the invention concerns the use of a purified carbohydrate composition derived from Panax ginseng to mitigate cellular injury resulting from ischemia and subsequent reperfusion. Ischemic conditions, including but not limited to myocardial infarction, angina, and stroke, are characterized by inadequate oxygen and nutrient supply to tissues, followed by a paradoxical exacerbation of damage upon restoration of blood flow. The invention provides a novel therapeutic approach that targets the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying ischemia-reperfusion injury, offering a safe, naturally derived intervention with minimal adverse effects. The compositions and methods described herein are particularly suited for use in cardiovascular medicine, where the prevention of cardiomyocyte death and preservation of mitochondrial integrity are critical to improving patient outcomes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce ischemic heart disease

Ischemic heart disease represents the leading cause of mortality worldwide, accounting for millions of deaths annually. It arises from the narrowing or occlusion of coronary arteries, which restricts the delivery of oxygen and essential nutrients to cardiac muscle. The resulting hypoxia triggers a cascade of metabolic disturbances, including ATP depletion, acidosis, and calcium overload, which compromise cellular function and viability. While reperfusion is necessary to salvage ischemic tissue, the abrupt reintroduction of oxygen initiates a secondary wave of injury known as ischemia-reperfusion injury, characterized by oxidative stress, inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and programmed cell death. This dual-phase pathology significantly limits the efficacy of current therapeutic interventions and contributes to long-term cardiac remodeling and heart failure.

- describe current treatment options

Current clinical strategies for managing ischemic heart disease include pharmacological agents such as aspirin, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, and statins, as well as invasive procedures like percutaneous coronary intervention and coronary artery bypass grafting. These approaches aim to restore perfusion, reduce myocardial workload, and prevent thrombus formation. However, they do not directly address the molecular mechanisms of reperfusion injury, leaving cardiomyocytes vulnerable to oxidative damage and apoptotic signaling during the critical post-ischemic period. Despite advances in revascularization techniques, a substantial proportion of patients continue to experience adverse outcomes due to the inability of existing therapies to fully protect the myocardium from reperfusion-induced cell death.

- limitations of current treatments

The limitations of current treatments are multifaceted. Pharmacological agents often exert systemic effects that may lead to hypotension, bradycardia, or renal impairment, particularly in elderly or comorbid populations. Invasive procedures carry inherent risks of bleeding, infection, and procedural failure. Moreover, no approved drug specifically targets the reperfusion phase to prevent mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening, cytochrome c release, or caspase activation. The absence of a targeted, safe, and effective therapy for ischemia-reperfusion injury represents a significant unmet medical need, particularly in settings where rapid reperfusion is achieved but long-term functional recovery remains suboptimal.

- introduce traditional Chinese medicine

Traditional Chinese medicine has long been employed in the management of cardiovascular ailments, with Panax ginseng being one of the most extensively used herbal remedies. For over two millennia, ginseng has been valued for its adaptogenic, anti-inflammatory, and circulatory-enhancing properties. Modern pharmacological investigations have identified ginsenosides as the primary bioactive constituents responsible for many of its cardiovascular effects. However, the water-soluble fraction of ginseng, historically regarded as an impurity in industrial extraction processes, has been largely overlooked despite mounting evidence suggesting its therapeutic potential. Recent studies on other medicinal plants have demonstrated that polysaccharide-rich fractions can exert potent cardioprotective effects, prompting a reevaluation of ginseng’s non-ginsenoside components.

- motivate need for new treatment options

There is a compelling need for novel therapeutic agents that can be administered prophylactically or adjunctively to standard reperfusion protocols to limit myocardial damage without introducing additional systemic toxicity. Natural compounds derived from traditional medicines offer a promising avenue due to their multi-targeted mechanisms, low toxicity profiles, and compatibility with long-term use. The discovery of a highly purified, bioactive carbohydrate composition from Panax ginseng that specifically protects cardiomyocytes against hypoxia-reoxygenation injury represents a significant advancement in this field. This invention fulfills the urgent demand for a safe, effective, and mechanistically defined intervention that addresses the root causes of reperfusion injury at the cellular and mitochondrial levels.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce method of treating ischemic conditions

The present invention provides a method of treating ischemic conditions by administering to a subject in need a therapeutically effective amount of a purified carbohydrate composition derived from Panax ginseng. This composition is capable of mitigating cellular injury induced by ischemia and subsequent reperfusion, particularly in cardiac tissue. The method involves the isolation and purification of a specific acidic polysaccharide fraction from ginseng extract, which, when administered prior to or during reperfusion, preserves mitochondrial integrity, suppresses oxidative stress, inhibits apoptotic signaling, and enhances cellular viability. The invention encompasses both prophylactic and therapeutic applications, making it suitable for use in acute clinical settings such as myocardial infarction, cardiac surgery, or percutaneous coronary intervention.

- describe carbohydrate composition

The carbohydrate composition of the invention is a purified, water-soluble fraction obtained from Panax ginseng that consists predominantly of acidic polysaccharides with a molecular weight of approximately 3.8 × 10⁴ Da. It contains a high proportion of glucose and galactose, with minor constituents including galacturonic acid, arabinose, and glucuronic acid. The composition is characterized by a uronic acid content of at least 15% by weight and a protein content of less than 10% by weight, ensuring high purity and biological specificity. This fraction, designated herein as AP1, exhibits no cytotoxicity at therapeutic concentrations and demonstrates a dose-dependent protective effect against hypoxia-reoxygenation injury in cardiomyocytes.

- extraction of carbohydrate composition

The carbohydrate composition is extracted from dried and cut Panax ginseng root material through a multi-step process involving solvent extraction, ethanol precipitation, and chromatographic purification. The process begins with the contact of ginseng material with an aqueous-alcoholic solvent under controlled temperature and agitation conditions to yield a crude extract. This extract is then subjected to sequential ethanol precipitation to enrich for high-molecular-weight carbohydrates. The resulting precipitate is further purified by anion-exchange chromatography to isolate the active acidic polysaccharide fraction, followed by concentration and lyophilization to obtain the final composition.

- isolate carbohydrate composition

The isolation of the carbohydrate composition is achieved through a series of physical and chemical separation techniques designed to remove non-carbohydrate contaminants such as proteins, ginsenosides, and low-molecular-weight sugars. The crude extract is fractionated using high-speed counter-current chromatography to separate components based on partition coefficients. Bioactive fractions are then subjected to stepwise ethanol precipitation, with the 70% ethanol precipitate demonstrating the highest cardioprotective activity. This precipitate is subsequently loaded onto a Toyopearl DEAE 650 M anion-exchange column and eluted with increasing concentrations of sodium chloride. The fraction eluted with 0.5 M NaCl, designated AP1, is collected, dialyzed against distilled water, and lyophilized to yield a highly purified carbohydrate composition.

- describe crude Panax ginseng extract

The crude Panax ginseng extract used as the starting material is prepared by macerating dried ginseng root in a mixture of water and ethanol, followed by filtration and concentration under reduced pressure. This extract contains a complex mixture of ginsenosides, polysaccharides, organic acids, and trace proteins. While the ginsenoside content is typically quantified and standardized in commercial preparations, the present invention demonstrates that the polysaccharide-rich fraction, often discarded as an impurity, possesses superior cardioprotective activity compared to the whole extract.

- fractionate crude extract

The crude extract is fractionated using high-speed counter-current chromatography with a two-phase solvent system composed of butanol and water, followed by ethyl acetate and water. This technique enables the separation of components based on differential solubility in the two immiscible phases, allowing for the isolation of distinct chemical groups. The fractions collected during elution are analyzed for biological activity using in vitro models of hypoxia-reoxygenation injury in cardiomyocytes. The most active fractions are pooled and further processed to isolate the carbohydrate composition.

- add water and aliphatic alcohol

To enrich for polysaccharides, the crude extract is diluted with water and mixed with an aliphatic alcohol, preferably ethanol, to achieve a final concentration of 90%, 80%, and 70% sequentially. This step induces the precipitation of high-molecular-weight carbohydrates while leaving smaller molecules and non-polar compounds in solution. The precipitates are collected by centrifugation and lyophilized, with the 70% ethanol precipitate selected for further purification due to its highest biological activity.

- subject to anion-exchange chromatography

The 70% ethanol precipitate is dissolved in water and loaded onto an anion-exchange chromatography column packed with DEAE-functionalized resin. The column is washed with distilled water to remove neutral polysaccharides, followed by stepwise elution with increasing concentrations of sodium chloride. The fraction eluted with 0.5 M NaCl, which contains the acidic polysaccharide AP1, is collected and subjected to dialysis to remove salts and small molecules.

- concentrate eluate

The eluate containing the purified carbohydrate composition is concentrated under reduced pressure at a temperature not exceeding 40°C to prevent degradation. The concentrated solution is then freeze-dried to yield a stable, powdered form of the carbohydrate composition suitable for pharmaceutical formulation. The resulting material is characterized for purity, molecular weight, monosaccharide composition, and biological activity.

- describe method of protecting cells

The method of protecting cells involves contacting cardiomyocytes or other ischemia-sensitive cells with the purified carbohydrate composition prior to or during exposure to hypoxic conditions followed by reoxygenation. The composition is administered at concentrations ranging from 12.5 μg/mL to 200 μg/mL, with optimal protection observed at 200 μg/mL. The treatment is maintained throughout the hypoxic and reoxygenation phases, resulting in significant preservation of mitochondrial membrane potential, reduction in reactive oxygen species, suppression of caspase activation, and inhibition of apoptosis.

- describe pharmaceutical formulation

The carbohydrate composition may be formulated into a pharmaceutical composition suitable for parenteral, oral, or intracoronary administration. The formulation includes a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent, such as sterile water, saline, dextrose, or hydroxypropyl methylcellulose. The composition may be presented as a lyophilized powder for reconstitution, a solution, a suspension, or a controlled-release formulation. The formulation is sterile, pyrogen-free, and stable under standard storage conditions.

- describe use of carbohydrate composition

The carbohydrate composition is used for the preparation of a medicament for the treatment of ischemic conditions, particularly those involving myocardial tissue. It may be administered prophylactically to patients at risk of ischemia-reperfusion injury, such as those undergoing cardiac surgery, percutaneous coronary intervention, or thrombolytic therapy. It may also be administered therapeutically during or immediately after reperfusion to limit ongoing cellular damage.

- describe benefits of carbohydrate composition

The carbohydrate composition offers multiple benefits over existing therapies. It is derived from a natural source with a long history of safe use, exhibits no cytotoxicity at therapeutic doses, and targets multiple pathways involved in ischemia-reperfusion injury, including oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and apoptotic signaling. Unlike synthetic drugs, it does not interfere with normal physiological functions and can be administered without significant systemic side effects. Its multi-targeted mechanism enhances its efficacy and reduces the likelihood of resistance or compensatory pathways.

- summarize invention

In summary, the invention provides a novel, purified carbohydrate composition derived from Panax ginseng that effectively protects cardiomyocytes from ischemia-reperfusion injury by modulating mitochondrial function, suppressing oxidative stress, and inhibiting apoptosis. The composition is obtained through a reproducible extraction and purification process and may be formulated into a safe and effective pharmaceutical product for clinical use in ischemic cardiovascular conditions.

The method of preventing ischemia-reperfusion injury involves the administration of the carbohydrate composition to a subject prior to the anticipated onset of ischemia or during the reperfusion phase. This prophylactic or peri-reperfusion intervention significantly reduces infarct size, preserves cardiac contractility, and improves survival in experimental models.

- define "ischemic condition"

An ischemic condition is defined as a pathological state in which the oxygen supply to a tissue or organ is insufficient to meet its metabolic requirements, leading to cellular dysfunction and, if prolonged, irreversible damage. Common examples include myocardial infarction, transient ischemic attack, acute limb ischemia, and renal ischemia. The condition may be caused by thrombosis, embolism, vasoconstriction, or mechanical obstruction of blood vessels.

- specify ischemic condition affecting the heart

The ischemic condition affecting the heart is specifically myocardial ischemia, which results from the occlusion or narrowing of one or more coronary arteries. This leads to reduced perfusion of the myocardium, causing energy depletion, acidosis, and accumulation of metabolic waste products. Upon reperfusion, the reintroduction of oxygen triggers a cascade of oxidative stress and inflammatory responses that exacerbate tissue injury, resulting in cardiomyocyte death and impaired cardiac function.

- define "ischemia-reperfusion injury"

Ischemia-reperfusion injury is defined as the paradoxical exacerbation of tissue damage that occurs when blood flow is restored to an ischemic organ. This injury is mediated by the generation of reactive oxygen species, calcium overload, mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening, activation of caspase-dependent apoptosis, and recruitment of inflammatory cells. The injury is distinct from the initial ischemic insult and represents a major contributor to poor outcomes in patients undergoing revascularization.

- describe cellular damage after reperfusion

After reperfusion, cardiomyocytes exhibit marked structural and functional alterations, including loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, swelling of organelles, disruption of sarcomeric architecture, and release of intracellular enzymes such as lactate dehydrogenase and creatine kinase. These changes are accompanied by increased production of reactive oxygen species, activation of stress kinases such as JNK and p38 MAPK, and induction of pro-apoptotic proteins including Bax and caspase-3. The cumulative effect is widespread cell death, fibrosis, and progressive ventricular remodeling.

- introduce carbohydrate composition extracted from Panax ginseng

The carbohydrate composition of the invention is extracted from the root of Panax ginseng C.A. Meyer through a series of physical and chromatographic purification steps. Unlike conventional ginseng extracts that focus on ginsenosides, this composition is enriched in acidic polysaccharides and contains minimal levels of ginsenosides and proteins. It is distinguished by its unique molecular weight profile, monosaccharide composition, and high uronic acid content, which collectively confer its potent cardioprotective activity.

The subject to whom the carbohydrate composition is administered may be a human or an animal, including but not limited to mice, rats, rabbits, dogs, or non-human primates. The composition is suitable for use in preclinical models of ischemia-reperfusion injury as well as in clinical settings for the treatment of acute myocardial infarction, cardiac surgery, or percutaneous coronary intervention.

- define "carbohydrate composition"

The carbohydrate composition is defined as a purified, water-soluble fraction derived from Panax ginseng that consists of polysaccharides with a molecular weight range of 10⁴ to 10⁶ Da, a uronic acid content of at least 15% by weight, and a protein content of less than 10% by weight. It is substantially free of ginsenosides, lipids, and low-molecular-weight sugars, and exhibits a characteristic monosaccharide profile dominated by glucose and galactose.

- describe carbohydrate portion

The carbohydrate portion of the composition is composed primarily of acidic heteropolysaccharides, with glucose accounting for approximately 76% of the total monosaccharide content, galactose for 13%, and galacturonic acid for 6%. Other minor components include arabinose, glucuronic acid, and xylose. The polysaccharide backbone is likely composed of β-(1→3)- and β-(1→6)-linked glucopyranosyl units with side chains containing galacturonic acid residues, contributing to its anionic character and biological activity.

- define "carbohydrates"

Carbohydrates, as used herein, refer to polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or substances that yield such compounds upon hydrolysis. In the context of this invention, carbohydrates encompass monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides, including both neutral and acidic forms, provided that they are derived from Panax ginseng and exhibit the biological activity described herein.

- describe polysaccharides

Polysaccharides are long-chain polymers composed of repeating monosaccharide units linked by glycosidic bonds. In the present invention, the polysaccharides are water-soluble, high-molecular-weight structures that are resistant to enzymatic degradation in the gastrointestinal tract and are capable of interacting with cell surface receptors to modulate intracellular signaling pathways.

- specify monosaccharides

The monosaccharides present in the carbohydrate composition include glucose, galactose, galacturonic acid, arabinose, glucuronic acid, and trace amounts of xylose and mannose. Glucose and galactose are the predominant components, accounting for over 89% of the total monosaccharide content. The presence of uronic acids such as galacturonic and glucuronic acid imparts a negative charge to the polysaccharide, facilitating its interaction with positively charged domains on cell membranes and signaling proteins.

- describe acidic polysaccharides

Acidic polysaccharides are defined as polysaccharides containing one or more uronic acid residues, which confer a negative charge to the molecule. In the present invention, the acidic polysaccharides are the primary bioactive component of the carbohydrate composition and are responsible for its ability to activate the RISK pathway, inhibit mitochondrial permeability transition, and suppress apoptosis. The presence of uronic acid is essential for the observed cardioprotective effects.

- specify preferred carbohydrate composition

The preferred carbohydrate composition is the fraction designated AP1, which is obtained by anion-exchange chromatography of the 70% ethanol precipitate of the crude ginseng extract. AP1 has an apparent molecular weight of approximately 3.8 × 10⁴ Da, a uronic acid content of 17.93% by weight, a protein content of 9.85% by weight, and a monosaccharide composition dominated by glucose (76.31%), galactose (12.70%), and galacturonic acid (5.64%).

- describe molecular weight of polysaccharide

The polysaccharide component of the carbohydrate composition has an apparent molecular weight of between 1 × 10⁴ and 1 × 10⁶ Da, as determined by high-performance gel permeation chromatography. The preferred molecular weight range for optimal biological activity is between 2 × 10⁴ and 5 × 10⁴ Da, with the most effective fraction exhibiting a molecular weight of approximately 3.8 × 10⁴ Da.

- specify preferred monosaccharides

The preferred monosaccharides are glucose and galactose, which together constitute at least 85% of the total monosaccharide content. The presence of galacturonic acid at a minimum of 5% by weight is essential for the acidic character and biological activity of the composition.

The carbohydrate portion constitutes at least 90% of the total weight of the purified composition, as determined by the phenol-sulfuric acid method. - specify uronic acid content

The uronic acid content of the carbohydrate composition is at least 15% by weight, with a preferred range of 16% to 20% by weight. The uronic acid content is determined by the m-hydroxydiphenyl method, which quantifies the carboxyl groups present in galacturonic and glucuronic acid residues.

- describe method of determining uronic acid content

The uronic acid content is determined by the m-hydroxydiphenyl method, in which the carbohydrate composition is reacted with m-hydroxydiphenyl in concentrated sulfuric acid to form a colored complex measurable at 520 nm. A standard curve is generated using galacturonic acid as a reference, and the concentration of uronic acid in the sample is calculated based on absorbance readings.

The protein portion of the carbohydrate composition is derived from trace contaminants present in the crude ginseng extract and is minimized through purification steps. The protein portion content is less than 10% by weight, with a preferred content of less than 5% by weight, as determined by the Bradford assay using bovine serum albumin as a standard.

- describe method of determining protein content

The protein content is determined using the Bradford assay, in which the carbohydrate composition is mixed with Coomassie Brilliant Blue G-250 dye, and the absorbance is measured at 595 nm. The concentration of protein is calculated by comparison to a standard curve prepared with known concentrations of bovine serum albumin.

- detail high-speed counter-current chromatography

High-speed counter-current chromatography is a liquid-liquid partition chromatography technique that separates compounds based on their differential solubility in two immiscible phases. It is performed using a TBE-1000A system with a 1000 mL coil, rotating at 800 rpm, and is ideal for the separation of complex natural product mixtures without irreversible adsorption.

The column is eluted with a linear gradient of sodium chloride to separate neutral and acidic polysaccharides.

Concentrating the eluate involves reducing the volume of the collected fraction under reduced pressure at a temperature not exceeding 40°C to prevent degradation of the polysaccharide structure, followed by lyophilization to obtain a stable, dry powder.

## EXAMPLES

- list reagents and materials

Reagents and materials include Panax ginseng root, ethanol, water, methanol, chloroform, ethyl acetate, sodium chloride, ammonium acetate, TFA, phenol, sulfuric acid, Bradford reagent, DCFH-DA, mitoSOX, rhodamine 123, ATP assay kit, Griess reagent, caspase-Glo kits, LY294002, U0126, L-NAME, tamoxifen, mifepristone, and cell culture media.

### Example 1

- extract Panax ginseng

Dried Panax ginseng root (500 g) was cut into small pieces and extracted with 5 L of a 1:3 water-ethanol mixture at 70°C for 4 hours. The extract was filtered and concentrated under reduced pressure to yield 80 g of crude extract.

- prepare standardized ginseng extract

The crude extract was freeze-dried and standardized to contain 10% ginsenosides by HPLC analysis. The remaining polysaccharide-rich fraction was retained for further purification.

- establish chemical fingerprints

Chemical fingerprints of the crude extract were established using HPLC-UV and HPLC-MS to identify major ginsenosides and polysaccharide markers.

- identify and quantify ginsenosides

Ginsenosides Rb1, Rg1, Re, Rc, and Rd were quantified using a C18 column and UV detection at 203 nm. Total ginsenoside content was determined to be 12.4% by weight.

- fractionate crude extract using HSCCC

The crude extract (4.12 g) was dissolved in 60 mL of butanol-water (1:1) and subjected to high-speed counter-current chromatography using a TBE-1000A system. The mobile phase was changed from butanol-water (1:1) to ethyl acetate-water (1:1) after 100 minutes. Forty-eight fractions were collected and analyzed by TLC.

- isolate carbohydrate composition

Bioactive fractions (fractions 2 and 3) were pooled and subjected to stepwise ethanol precipitation at 90%, 80%, and 70% concentrations. The 70% ethanol precipitate was selected for further purification.

- perform stepwise ethanol precipitation

The pooled bioactive fractions were dissolved in water and ethanol was added to achieve final concentrations of 90%, 80%, and 70%. Precipitates were collected by centrifugation at 4000 rpm for 15 minutes and lyophilized.

- subject precipitates to anion-exchange chromatography

The 70% ethanol precipitate (0.74 g) was dissolved in water and loaded onto a Toyopearl DEAE 650 M column. The column was washed with water, then eluted with 0.5 M, 1.0 M, 2.0 M NaCl, and 0.2 M NaOH. The 0.5 M NaCl eluate was collected.

- elute and collect fractions

The eluate from 0.5 M NaCl was collected in 10 mL fractions, dialyzed against distilled water using a 1000 Da cutoff membrane for 24 hours, and lyophilized to yield the purified carbohydrate composition (AP1).

- isolate carbohydrate composition without HSCCC

In an alternative embodiment, the crude extract was directly subjected to ethanol precipitation and anion-exchange chromatography without prior HSCCC fractionation, yielding a composition with comparable biological activity.

- purify and obtain final carbohydrate composition

The final carbohydrate composition was analyzed for molecular weight, monosaccharide composition, uronic acid content, and protein content, and was confirmed to be AP1 with a molecular weight of 3.8 × 10⁴ Da and a uronic acid content of 17.93%.

### Example 2

- introduce chemical characterization

The purified carbohydrate composition was subjected to comprehensive chemical characterization using high-performance gel permeation chromatography, monosaccharide analysis, and protein quantification.

- describe HPGPC analysis

High-performance gel permeation chromatography was performed using a TSKgel G3000PWXL column with water as the mobile phase. The molecular weight of AP1 was determined to be 3.8 × 10⁴ Da by comparison with dextran standards.

- detail monosaccharide composition analysis

Monosaccharide composition was analyzed by acid hydrolysis followed by derivatization with PMP and HPLC analysis. Glucose (76.31%), galactose (12.70%), galacturonic acid (5.64%), arabinose (3.72%), and glucuronic acid (1.64%) were identified.

- outline chemical analysis

Total sugar content was determined by the phenol-sulfuric acid method (90.28%), uronic acid content by the m-hydroxydiphenyl method (17.93%), and protein content by the Bradford assay (9.85%).

- introduce pharmacological characterization

Pharmacological characterization was performed using H9c2 cardiomyocytes subjected to hypoxia-reoxygenation injury in the presence or absence of AP1.

- describe cell culture conditions

H9c2 cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin at 37°C in a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. Cells were passaged weekly and used between passages 3 and 4.

- outline statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test. P-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All experiments were repeated at least three times.

- detail cytotoxicity test

Cytotoxicity of AP1 was assessed by treating H9c2 cells with concentrations ranging from 6.25 to 800 μg/mL for 24 hours under normoxic conditions. No significant cytotoxicity was observed.

- describe hypoxia/reoxygenation conditions

Hypoxia was induced by placing cells in a sealed chamber flushed with 95% N₂ and 5% CO₂ for 3 hours at 37°C. Reoxygenation was achieved by returning cells to normoxic conditions for 4 hours.

- outline drug treatment

Cells were pretreated with AP1 (12.5–800 μg/mL) for 1 hour prior to hypoxia and maintained in the presence of AP1 throughout reoxygenation. Positive controls included DZ (100 μM).

- detail pharmaceutical blockages

Cells were pretreated with inhibitors of PI3K (LY294002), ERK1/2 (U0126), eNOS (L-NAME), ER (tamoxifen), GR (mifepristone), or Akt (Akt inhibitor IV) for 1 hour prior to AP1 treatment.

- describe assay for cell viability

Cell viability was assessed using the mitochondrial viability stain assay, with fluorescence measured at 550 nm excitation and 590 nm emission.

- outline Western blot analysis

Total and phosphorylated proteins were detected using specific antibodies against p-Akt, Akt, p-eNOS, eNOS, Bcl-2, Bax, cytochrome c, and β-actin. Bands were visualized using an Odyssey CLx Imaging System.

- detail measurement of nitric oxide production

Nitric oxide production was measured using the Griess reaction, with absorbance read at 540 nm after reaction with Griess reagent.

- describe flow cytometric analysis of cardiomyocyte apoptosis

Apoptosis was quantified using an Annexin V-FITC/PI kit and analyzed by flow cytometry. Cells were classified as viable, early apoptotic, late apoptotic, or necrotic.

- summarize experimental design

The experimental design included six groups: normoxia, hypoxia/reoxygenation, hypoxia/reoxygenation + AP1, hypoxia/reoxygenation + DZ, hypoxia/reoxygenation + AP1 + inhibitor, and inhibitor alone.

- conclude pharmacological characterization

AP1 significantly improved cell viability, reduced apoptosis, suppressed ROS, preserved mitochondrial function, and activated the RISK pathway. These effects were abolished by inhibitors of PI3K, Akt, ERK1/2, eNOS, GR, or ER.
