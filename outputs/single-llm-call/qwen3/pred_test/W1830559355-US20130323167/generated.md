## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and targeted drug delivery, specifically to methods and compositions for enhancing the selective accumulation of therapeutic and imaging agents within solid tumors through the pharmacological modulation of tumor vasculature. More particularly, the invention pertains to the use of vascular-active permeability agents to amplify the Enhanced Permeability and Retention effect, thereby enabling improved delivery of macromolecular therapeutics, nanoparticles, and radiolabeled compounds to malignant tissues while minimizing systemic exposure. The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits, therapeutic formulations, and imaging protocols designed to exploit this enhanced vascular permeability phenomenon for the detection, monitoring, and treatment of a broad spectrum of solid tumors, including those historically resistant to conventional chemotherapy due to anatomical or physiological barriers such as the blood-brain barrier.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The accurate and early diagnosis of solid tumors remains a critical challenge in clinical oncology, as many malignancies are asymptomatic until they reach advanced stages, at which point therapeutic options are limited and prognosis is poor. Current imaging modalities often lack the sensitivity to detect small tumor foci or to distinguish between benign and malignant tissue with sufficient specificity. Moreover, the systemic toxicity of conventional chemotherapeutic agents restricts their dosing to levels that are frequently insufficient to eradicate all malignant cells, leading to tumor recurrence and metastatic progression. Although targeted therapies and monoclonal antibodies have improved outcomes for select cancers, their efficacy is often constrained by poor tumor penetration, heterogeneous antigen expression, and rapid clearance from circulation. The Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect has long been recognized as a physiologic phenomenon that permits the preferential accumulation of macromolecules and nanoparticles within solid tumors due to their leaky vasculature and impaired lymphatic drainage. However, the magnitude of EPR-mediated accumulation is highly variable across tumor types and individual patients, and its therapeutic utility has been limited by inconsistent delivery efficiency and low tumor-to-normal tissue ratios. These limitations have hindered the clinical translation of nanoparticle-based formulations despite their theoretical promise.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel method for enhancing the delivery of therapeutic and imaging agents to solid tumors by co-administering a vascular-active permeability agent with a macromolecular or nanoparticulate carrier. This method significantly amplifies the natural EPR effect, resulting in a marked increase in the concentration of the agent within the tumor while simultaneously reducing its distribution to healthy tissues. The invention further provides a kit comprising a vascular-active permeability agent and a nanoparticle formulation loaded with a therapeutic or imaging agent, wherein the components are provided in separate, stable, and ready-to-use containers for combined administration. The composition embodiment includes sterically stabilized liposomes, polymer-based nanoparticles, or other colloidal carriers with a mean diameter between 50 and 1,000 nanometers, encapsulating chemotherapeutic agents, radionuclides, or contrast agents, in combination with a vascular-active agent selected from tumor necrosis factor-alpha, bacterial lysates, or microtubule-disrupting compounds. The benefits of this approach include a dramatic improvement in tumor-to-blood concentration ratios, enhanced therapeutic efficacy at reduced systemic doses, the potential for complete tumor regression in previously untreatable models, and the ability to leverage clinically approved drugs and formulations without requiring structural modification. This strategy enables the repurposing of existing chemotherapeutics and imaging agents for use in tumors previously considered inaccessible due to anatomical or pharmacokinetic barriers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- introduce vascular-active permeability entities

Vascular-active permeability entities are biologically active agents that induce transient, localized disruption of endothelial cell junctions within the tumor vasculature, thereby increasing paracellular permeability beyond the baseline level observed in the natural Enhanced Permeability and Retention effect. These entities act through well-defined signaling pathways that modulate the integrity of the endothelial barrier, including but not limited to the activation of inflammatory cytokines, the destabilization of cytoskeletal elements, or the induction of endothelial cell apoptosis. The resulting vascular fenestrations permit the extravasation of macromolecules and nanoparticles that would otherwise be excluded by the intact endothelium, enabling their selective retention within the tumor interstitium.

- describe delivery of therapeutic or imaging agents

Therapeutic or imaging agents are delivered to the tumor site via encapsulation within a nanoparticulate carrier or conjugation to a macromolecular scaffold, which is administered systemically in conjunction with a vascular-active permeability entity. The carrier protects the agent from premature degradation, prolongs its circulation time, and facilitates its passive targeting to the tumor through the enhanced vascular permeability induced by the vascular-active agent. Upon extravasation, the agent is retained within the tumor due to the absence of functional lymphatic drainage, thereby achieving a therapeutic or diagnostic concentration that is significantly higher than that attainable by free agent administration.

- discuss administration methods

The vascular-active permeability entity and the nanoparticle-bound agent are administered either simultaneously or sequentially, with the vascular-active agent administered no more than twelve hours prior to or after the nanoparticle agent to ensure optimal vascular permeability during the critical window of nanoparticle circulation. Administration is performed via intravenous injection, and the timing and dosage are optimized based on the pharmacokinetic profile of the agent and the size of the tumor. In some embodiments, the vascular-active agent is administered first to prime the vasculature, followed by the nanoparticle agent to maximize accumulation.

- define nanoparticle size range

The nanoparticle carriers employed in this invention have a mean hydrodynamic diameter ranging from 50 nanometers to 1,000 nanometers, with optimal accumulation observed in the range of 80 to 150 nanometers. Particles smaller than 50 nanometers are rapidly cleared by renal filtration, while particles larger than 1,000 nanometers exhibit reduced circulation time and limited extravasation efficiency even in the presence of vascular-active agents.

- list nanoparticle compositions

Nanoparticle compositions include sterically stabilized liposomes composed of hydrogenated chicken egg phosphatidylcholine, cholesterol, and polyethylene glycol-conjugated distearoylphosphatidylethanolamine in a molar ratio of 50:45:5; poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles; dendrimers; micelles formed from block copolymers; and silica-based nanoparticles. Each composition is engineered to maintain colloidal stability in physiological fluids and to allow for efficient encapsulation or surface conjugation of therapeutic or imaging agents.

- describe therapeutic or imaging agent attachment

Therapeutic or imaging agents are attached to the nanoparticle carrier through encapsulation within the aqueous core or lipid bilayer, covalent conjugation to surface functional groups, or electrostatic interaction with charged moieties on the nanoparticle surface. Encapsulation is achieved by passive loading during nanoparticle formation or by active loading using pH gradients or ion trapping mechanisms. Conjugation is performed using NHS esters, maleimide-thiol chemistry, or click chemistry to ensure stable linkage under physiological conditions.

- provide examples of therapeutic agents

Therapeutic agents include cytotoxic compounds, immunomodulators, gene silencing molecules, and pro-apoptotic peptides. These agents are selected for their clinical relevance, known antitumor activity, and compatibility with nanoparticle formulation.

- list specific chemotherapeutic agents

Specific chemotherapeutic agents include doxorubicin, paclitaxel, cisplatin, gemcitabine, 5-fluorouracil, vincristine, vinorelbine, and combretastatin A4 phosphate. These agents are encapsulated within the nanoparticle carrier to reduce systemic toxicity and enhance tumor-specific delivery.

- describe imaging agents

Imaging agents include radionuclides, fluorescent dyes, magnetic nanoparticles, and contrast-enhancing agents suitable for computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, or single-photon emission computed tomography. These agents are incorporated into the nanoparticle to enable non-invasive visualization of tumor burden, treatment response, and agent distribution.

- list radionuclides

Radionuclides include iodine-125, iodine-131, technetium-99m, indium-111, copper-64, and yttrium-90. These isotopes are selected based on their emission profile, half-life, and suitability for either diagnostic imaging or targeted radiotherapy.

- discuss contrast imaging agents

Contrast imaging agents include gadolinium-based compounds for magnetic resonance imaging, iodinated compounds for computed tomography, and superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles for T2-weighted MRI. These agents are encapsulated within the nanoparticle to enhance signal intensity and improve tumor delineation in vivo.

- describe non-invasive detection techniques

Non-invasive detection techniques include single-photon emission computed tomography, positron emission tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, and ultrasound imaging. These modalities are used to monitor the biodistribution of the nanoparticle agent, quantify tumor accumulation, and assess treatment response over time without the need for invasive biopsy or surgical intervention.

- define vascular-active permeability agents

Vascular-active permeability agents are pharmacological compounds that induce transient, reversible disruption of the tumor vasculature, thereby enhancing the extravasation and retention of macromolecular and nanoparticulate agents. These agents act through mechanisms that include cytokine receptor activation, cytoskeletal rearrangement, endothelial cell apoptosis, or induction of inflammatory signaling cascades.

- list examples of vascular-active permeability agents

Examples of vascular-active permeability agents include tumor necrosis factor-alpha, bacterial lysates derived from Clostridium novyi-NT, combretastatin A4 phosphate, vinorelbine, vascular endothelial growth factor, histamine, bradykinin, and nitric oxide donors. These agents are selected for their ability to induce vascular permeability without causing systemic toxicity at the administered dose.

- discuss mechanisms of action

The mechanism of action involves the activation of endothelial signaling pathways that lead to the internalization of vascular endothelial cadherin, disruption of actin cytoskeletal integrity, and opening of intercellular junctions. This results in increased transendothelial transport of macromolecules and nanoparticles, which are retained within the tumor due to the absence of functional lymphatics. The effect is transient and localized, minimizing systemic vascular leakage.

- describe kits for assessment and therapeutic uses

Kits for assessment and therapeutic uses comprise a first container containing a vascular-active permeability agent in lyophilized or liquid form, a second container containing a nanoparticle formulation loaded with a therapeutic or imaging agent, and a third container containing instructions for reconstitution, dosing, and administration. The kits may further include a diagnostic component for evaluating vascular permeability prior to treatment, such as a radiolabeled tracer for pre-treatment SPECT or MRI imaging.

- list types of solid tumors

Types of solid tumors include glioblastoma multiforme, breast carcinoma, lung adenocarcinoma, ovarian carcinoma, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, colorectal carcinoma, melanoma, prostate adenocarcinoma, and hepatocellular carcinoma. The method is applicable to any solid tumor exhibiting aberrant vasculature and impaired lymphatic drainage.

- discuss limitations of chemotherapeutic agents

The limitations of conventional chemotherapeutic agents include poor tumor penetration, rapid systemic clearance, dose-limiting toxicity to normal tissues, and resistance mechanisms that reduce intracellular drug concentration. These limitations are particularly pronounced in tumors protected by anatomical barriers such as the blood-brain barrier or those with dense stromal components that impede diffusion.

- describe approaches to address limitations

Approaches to address these limitations include the use of nanoparticle carriers to prolong circulation time, ligand-mediated active targeting to enhance cellular uptake, and pharmacological modulation of the tumor vasculature to enhance passive accumulation. The present invention provides a novel and broadly applicable method for enhancing passive targeting through vascular permeability induction.

- discuss Enhanced EPR (E2PR)

The phenomenon of Enhanced EPR, or E2PR, refers to the pharmacologically induced amplification of the natural Enhanced Permeability and Retention effect through the administration of vascular-active permeability agents. Unlike conventional EPR, which relies on the intrinsic abnormalities of tumor vasculature, E2PR actively enhances vascular permeability to a degree that permits the extravasation of larger particles and achieves significantly higher tumor-to-blood concentration ratios.

- present results of E2PR in GBM model

In a murine orthotopic glioblastoma multiforme model, administration of tumor necrosis factor-alpha in combination with sterically stabilized liposomes loaded with doxorubicin resulted in a 22-fold increase in tumor-to-blood radioactivity ratio and prolonged median survival to 103 days, compared to less than 50 days in control groups. Histological analysis confirmed extensive intratumoral accumulation of the nanoparticle agent and significant tumor necrosis.

- discuss implications of results

The implications of these results are profound: they demonstrate that even highly resistant tumors such as glioblastoma can be effectively targeted with existing chemotherapeutics when their delivery is enhanced by vascular permeability modulation. This approach bypasses the need for novel drug development and enables the rapid clinical translation of approved agents.

- describe versatility of approach

The approach is versatile in that it is compatible with a wide range of nanoparticle formulations, therapeutic agents, and vascular-active agents. It can be applied to any solid tumor type, regardless of origin or molecular profile, and can be combined with other therapeutic modalities such as immunotherapy or radiotherapy.

- provide general overview of invention

In general, the invention provides a method for improving the therapeutic index of cancer treatments by co-administering a vascular-active permeability agent with a nanoparticle-bound therapeutic or imaging agent. This strategy enhances tumor-specific delivery, reduces systemic toxicity, and enables the effective treatment of tumors previously considered untreatable due to anatomical or pharmacokinetic barriers. The invention further provides kits and protocols for clinical implementation, enabling personalized assessment and treatment of solid malignancies.
