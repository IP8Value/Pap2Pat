# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

- The present invention relates to the field of oncology and molecular diagnostics, specifically to the use of human monoclonal antibodies that target heparan sulfate chains on glypican-3 for the diagnosis, monitoring, and therapeutic intervention of hepatocellular carcinoma and other glypican-3-expressing malignancies. The invention encompasses novel antibody compositions, immunoconjugates, diagnostic kits, and methods of administration designed to selectively inhibit tumor progression by disrupting heparan sulfate-mediated signaling pathways critical to cancer cell migration, proliferation, and survival. The technology is particularly applicable to cancers characterized by aberrant overexpression of glypican-3, including but not limited to hepatocellular carcinoma, hepatoblastoma, and certain subtypes of melanoma and squamous cell carcinoma, where conventional therapies have demonstrated limited efficacy and high rates of resistance.

## BACKGROUND

- Hepatocellular carcinoma represents the most prevalent primary liver malignancy and ranks among the leading causes of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with prognosis typically poor due to late-stage diagnosis and intrinsic resistance to systemic chemotherapies. Current treatment paradigms include surgical resection, liver transplantation, locoregional ablation, and targeted molecular therapies such as sorafenib and lenvatinib; however, these interventions offer only modest survival benefits and are often associated with significant toxicity and acquired resistance. The urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies that target tumor-specific molecular vulnerabilities has driven extensive research into tumor-associated antigens that are selectively expressed in malignant tissues while remaining absent or minimally expressed in healthy adult organs. Glypican-3, a heparan sulfate proteoglycan anchored to the plasma membrane via a glycosylphosphatidylinositol linkage, has emerged as a compelling candidate due to its near-universal overexpression in hepatocellular carcinoma and its absence in normal hepatic parenchyma. Structurally, glypican-3 consists of a core protein to which one or more heparan sulfate glycosaminoglycan chains are covalently attached, enabling it to function as a co-receptor for a diverse array of growth factors, morphogens, and extracellular matrix components. These heparan sulfate chains facilitate ligand binding and concentration at the cell surface, thereby enhancing receptor activation and downstream signal transduction. In hepatocellular carcinoma, glypican-3 has been shown to potentiate canonical Wnt/β-catenin signaling, promote cell proliferation, and support tumor cell survival through interactions with fibroblast growth factors, hedgehog ligands, and hepatocyte growth factor. Despite its well-established role in tumorigenesis, therapeutic targeting of glypican-3 has been hindered by the lack of antibodies capable of selectively disrupting its heparan sulfate-dependent functions without eliciting off-target effects or immune-mediated clearance. Current diagnostic approaches rely on immunohistochemical detection of the glypican-3 core protein, which fails to capture the functional state of its heparan sulfate modifications, thereby limiting prognostic accuracy and therapeutic stratification. There remains a critical unmet need for agents that specifically recognize and neutralize the heparan sulfate moieties of glypican-3 to inhibit multiple oncogenic signaling cascades simultaneously.

## SUMMARY

- The invention discloses novel human monoclonal antibodies, including HN3 and HS20, that bind with high specificity and affinity to glypican-3 or its heparan sulfate chains, enabling precise detection, diagnosis, and therapeutic modulation of glypican-3-expressing cancers. These antibodies are capable of inhibiting tumor cell migration, proliferation, and three-dimensional spheroid formation by disrupting critical growth factor signaling pathways, including hepatocyte growth factor/c-Met and Wnt/β-catenin, without affecting normal tissue homeostasis. The antibodies may be administered as standalone therapeutic agents, conjugated to cytotoxic payloads such as Pseudomonas exotoxin variants, or formulated into immunoconjugates for targeted delivery of chemotherapeutic agents, radionuclides, or fluorescent labels. Diagnostic methods are provided for the detection of glypican-3 or its heparan sulfate epitopes in biological samples, including serum, tissue biopsies, and fine-needle aspirates, using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, and lateral flow devices. Confirmation of cancer diagnosis is achieved by correlating the presence of glypican-3-bound heparan sulfate with histopathological findings and clinical imaging. Therapeutic methods encompass systemic or localized administration of the antibodies or their derivatives to subjects diagnosed with hepatocellular carcinoma, hepatoblastoma, or other glypican-3-positive neoplasms, with dosing regimens tailored to achieve therapeutically effective concentrations in tumor tissue while minimizing systemic exposure. The antibodies may be co-administered with standard-of-care chemotherapeutics, radiotherapy, or immunomodulatory agents to enhance antitumor efficacy and overcome resistance mechanisms. The invention further provides recombinant nucleic acid molecules encoding the antibodies, expression vectors for their production in mammalian host cells, and pharmaceutical compositions formulated for intravenous, intraperitoneal, or intratumoral delivery.

The term “monoclonal antibody” denotes an antibody derived from a single clonal population of B cells, ensuring uniformity in antigen specificity and binding characteristics. ### III. Introduction

- Monoclonal antibodies have revolutionized the treatment of cancer by enabling precise targeting of tumor-associated antigens with minimal off-target effects. The present invention introduces two novel human monoclonal antibodies, HN3 and HS20, which bind to distinct epitopes on glypican-3: HN3 recognizes the core protein, while HS20 specifically targets the heparan sulfate chains. These antibodies were isolated from human phage display libraries and exhibit high specificity for glypican-3-expressing cancer cells with negligible binding to normal tissues. HN3 and HS20 demonstrate potent inhibitory activity against tumor cell proliferation, migration, and three-dimensional spheroid formation in vitro and suppress tumor growth in vivo in xenograft models. Their unique mechanisms of action—HN3 by direct interference with glypican-3 structural function and HS20 by blocking heparan sulfate-mediated growth factor signaling—render them complementary tools for cancer diagnosis and therapy. Both antibodies are fully human in sequence, minimizing immunogenicity and enabling repeated administration. Their utility extends beyond hepatocellular carcinoma to other glypican-3-positive malignancies, including hepatoblastoma and select subtypes of melanoma and squamous cell carcinoma.

### IV. Human Monoclonal Antibodies that Bind GPC3 or HS Chains on GPC3

- The invention provides human monoclonal antibodies that bind with high affinity to either the core protein of glypican-3 or its heparan sulfate chains. The antibody HN3 is a single-domain human antibody derived from a phage display library, with a variable heavy chain sequence comprising the amino acid sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO: 1 and a corresponding nucleic acid sequence as provided in SEQ ID NO: 2. The complementarity-determining regions of HN3 are defined as CDR1 (residues 26–35), CDR2 (residues 51–65), and CDR3 (residues 98–108) within the VH domain. HN3 exhibits a binding affinity (KD) of less than 1 nM to recombinant glypican-3 as measured by surface plasmon resonance and demonstrates specific binding to glypican-3-expressing hepatocellular carcinoma cell lines including Hep3B, Huh-7, and HepG2, but not to glypican-3-negative lines such as SK-hep1. HN3 may be labeled with fluorescent dyes such as FITC or Alexa Fluor 647 for diagnostic imaging, or conjugated to cytotoxic agents such as PE38 for therapeutic use. Compositions comprising HN3 include pharmaceutical formulations in phosphate-buffered saline with 0.02% polysorbate 80, lyophilized powders for reconstitution, and controlled-release microspheres. Nucleic acid molecules encoding HN3 are operably linked to strong mammalian promoters such as CMV or EF1α and are contained within expression vectors suitable for transfection into CHO or HEK293 cells. Host cells stably expressing HN3 produce yields exceeding 100 mg/L in bioreactor culture. The antibody HS20 is a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody with variable heavy and light chain sequences as provided in SEQ ID NO: 3 and SEQ ID NO: 4, respectively. The CDRs of HS20 are defined as CDR1H (residues 28–35), CDR2H (residues 50–65), CDR3H (residues 99–107), CDR1L (residues 24–34), CDR2L (residues 50–56), and CDR3L (residues 89–97). HS20 binds exclusively to the heparan sulfate chains of glypican-3, with no reactivity to the core protein or other glypicans lacking the specific sulfation motif. Its binding affinity (KD) is approximately 0.5 nM, as determined by ELISA and flow cytometry. HS20 inhibits hepatocyte growth factor binding to glypican-3 and blocks c-Met phosphorylation, thereby suppressing tumor cell migration and invasion. HS20 may be labeled with radionuclides such as iodine-131 or yttrium-90 for radioimmunotherapy or with quantum dots for intraoperative tumor margin delineation. Compositions comprising HS20 include sterile injectable solutions, liposomal formulations, and nanoparticle-encapsulated suspensions. Nucleic acid constructs encoding HS20 are cloned into pcDNA3.1 vectors under the control of the human IgG heavy chain promoter. Host cells expressing HS20 are cultured in serum-free media supplemented with sodium butyrate to enhance secretion. A modified version of HS20, designated HS20-N250Q, contains a point mutation in the light chain variable region that eliminates an N-glycosylation site at asparagine 250, resulting in improved stability and reduced aggregation without altering binding affinity or specificity. Antibody fragments including Fab, F(ab')2, and Fv are generated by enzymatic digestion or recombinant expression and retain full biological activity. Single-chain variable fragments (scFv) and VH single-domain antibodies are engineered for enhanced tissue penetration and are suitable for intracellular delivery via viral vectors or cell-penetrating peptides.

### V. Immunoconjugates

- Immunoconjugates of the invention comprise the antibodies HN3 or HS20 chemically linked to therapeutic or diagnostic effector molecules via covalent or noncovalent attachments. Therapeutic agents include Pseudomonas exotoxin A fragment PE38, auristatin derivatives, maytansinoids, and doxorubicin. The choice of agent is motivated by the need to achieve potent cytotoxicity upon internalization while minimizing systemic toxicity. Clones encoding antibody-toxin fusion proteins are constructed by fusing the VH domain of HN3 to the C-terminus of PE38 via a flexible glycine-serine linker, resulting in a recombinant immunotoxin designated HN3(VH)-PE38. The fusion protein is expressed in E. coli inclusion bodies, refolded, and purified by affinity chromatography to greater than 95% homogeneity. Effector molecules are attached via thiol-maleimide chemistry, carbodiimide-mediated coupling, or site-specific transglutaminase modification to ensure homogeneity. Cleavable linkers, such as valine-citrulline dipeptides or disulfide bonds, are employed to enable intracellular payload release following endocytosis. Derivatization of antibodies with functional groups such as NHS esters, hydrazides, or azides allows for bioorthogonal conjugation to fluorescent dyes, magnetic nanoparticles, or radiolabeled chelators. Labeling with detectable moieties includes attachment of fluorescein isothiocyanate for flow cytometry, europium for time-resolved fluorescence, or technetium-99m for SPECT imaging. Toxins and immunotoxins are designed to inhibit protein synthesis by ADP-ribosylation of elongation factor 2, leading to rapid apoptosis in target cells. Pseudomonas exotoxin variants are engineered to remove native binding domains and replace them with tumor-specific antibody fragments, thereby redirecting cytotoxicity. Targeting of diagnostic or therapeutic compounds is achieved through the high specificity of HN3 and HS20 for glypican-3, enabling selective delivery to tumor sites while sparing healthy tissues.

### VI. Compositions and Methods of Use

- Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise the antibodies HN3 or HS20 in therapeutically effective amounts ranging from 0.1 mg/kg to 25 mg/kg, formulated in aqueous carriers such as 0.9% sodium chloride, 5% dextrose, or human serum albumin, with optional stabilizers including sucrose, trehalose, or arginine. Unit dosage forms include vials containing 10 mg, 50 mg, or 100 mg of lyophilized antibody, with instructions for reconstitution in sterile water prior to intravenous infusion. Administration routes include intravenous, intraperitoneal, intratumoral, and intra-arterial delivery, with intravenous infusion over 30 to 120 minutes being preferred for systemic distribution. Aqueous carriers may include buffering agents such as histidine or citrate to maintain pH between 5.5 and 6.5. Sterilization is achieved by filtration through 0.22 μm membranes or gamma irradiation. Auxiliary substances include antioxidants such as ascorbic acid, surfactants such as polysorbate 80, and tonicity modifiers such as mannitol. Lyophilized formulations are stored at 2–8°C and rehydrated with sterile water immediately prior to use. Controlled release formulations include biodegradable poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) microspheres, alginate nanoparticles, and liposomes composed of phosphatidylcholine and cholesterol, which sustain antibody release over days to weeks. Therapeutic methods involve administering the antibodies to subjects diagnosed with hepatocellular carcinoma, hepatoblastoma, or other glypican-3-positive neoplasms, either as monotherapy or in combination with chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or immunotherapy. Subjects are selected based on immunohistochemical confirmation of glypican-3 expression in tumor tissue. Methods of treating cancer include inhibition of tumor cell proliferation, suppression of metastatic migration, and induction of apoptosis via disruption of heparan sulfate-dependent signaling. Therapeutically effective amounts are determined by pharmacokinetic modeling and dose escalation studies in preclinical models. Administration with other anti-cancer agents includes concurrent or sequential use with sorafenib, regorafenib, cisplatin, gemcitabine, or immune checkpoint inhibitors such as nivolumab. Immunomodulators include cytokines such as interleukin-2 or interferon-alpha. Surgical treatment and radiotherapy are employed as adjuncts to antibody therapy. Diagnostic methods involve detecting glypican-3 or its heparan sulfate epitopes in serum, tissue, or cytological samples using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays, immunohistochemistry, or flow cytometry. Biological samples include blood, ascites, fine-needle aspirates, and formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tissue sections. Methods of determining cancer include quantification of antibody binding to tumor cells in biopsy specimens, with positive staining defined as membrane-associated signal intensity exceeding background by at least threefold. Confirmation of cancer diagnosis requires correlation with histopathological criteria and clinical imaging. Direct labeling involves conjugation of the antibody to a detectable moiety prior to administration, while indirect labeling employs secondary antibodies labeled with enzymes or fluorophores. Kits for detecting GPC3 include pre-coated plates, detection antibodies, substrate solutions, and instruction manuals. Instructional materials describe protocols for sample preparation, incubation times, and interpretation of results. Immunoassays are performed using sandwich or competitive formats. Engineered cytotoxic T lymphocytes expressing chimeric antigen receptors incorporating the VH domain of HN3 or the scFv of HS20 are generated for adoptive cell therapy. Bispecific antibodies incorporating one arm specific for GPC3 and another for CD3 are designed to recruit T cells to tumor sites.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

- HN3 is a human single-domain monoclonal antibody isolated from a phage display library constructed from peripheral blood lymphocytes of healthy donors immunized with recombinant glypican-3. Phage panning was performed using immobilized GPC3-hFc as antigen, followed by stringent washing and elution under low pH conditions. Positive clones were sequenced, and HN3 was identified as a dominant clone with high binding specificity. Recombinant GPC3-hFc and GPC3ΔHS-hFc were expressed in CHO cells and purified by Protein A affinity chromatography. HN3-human Fc fusion protein was produced in HEK293 cells and purified by Protein A. Flow cytometry confirmed binding to Hep3B and Huh-7 cells but not to SK-hep1 or GPC3-knockdown Hep3B cells. ELISA demonstrated binding to recombinant GPC3 with a KD of 0.8 nM. Epitope mapping revealed that HN3 binds to a conformational epitope on the core protein proximal to the heparan sulfate attachment site. Treatment of Hep3B cells with HN3 resulted in dose-dependent inhibition of proliferation, as measured by WST-8 assay, and induction of G1 cell cycle arrest. Knockdown of GPC3 using shRNA phenocopied HN3 treatment, confirming target specificity. HN3 treatment suppressed activation of Hippo/Yap, TGFβ, and Wnt signaling pathways, as evidenced by reduced nuclear translocation of Yap and decreased expression of cyclin D1 and c-Myc. These findings establish HN3 as a potent inhibitor of hepatocellular carcinoma growth and validate its potential as a therapeutic and diagnostic agent.

### Example 2

- HS20 is a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody isolated from a phage display library screened against glypican-3-expressing Hep3B cells. Phage ELISA identified clones with selective binding to glypican-3 but not to CD22-hFc or GPC3ΔHS-hFc. The variable regions of HS20 were cloned into human IgG1 expression vectors and transfected into CHO cells. Purified HS20 IgG was analyzed by Western blot, confirming reactivity with full-length glypican-3 under non-reducing conditions. Flow cytometry demonstrated specific binding to Hep3B and HepG2 cells, with no binding to SK-hep1. Immunohistochemistry of human hepatocellular carcinoma tissue sections revealed strong membrane staining in 87% of tumor samples, while normal liver tissue showed no reactivity. HS20 inhibited HGF-induced phosphorylation of c-Met in Hep3B and Huh-7 cells, as shown by Western blot. In wound healing and transwell migration assays, HS20 reduced cell motility by more than 70% at 50 μg/mL. HS20 did not inhibit migration of HGF-knockdown cells, confirming dependence on HGF signaling. The binding site of HS20 was mapped to the heparan sulfate chains of glypican-3, as demonstrated by loss of binding to GPC3ΔHS-hFc. HS20 was converted from its Fv format to full-length IgG1, retaining identical binding characteristics. Binding affinity was confirmed by surface plasmon resonance with a KD of 0.5 nM. HS20 treatment significantly inhibited 3D spheroid formation in low-attachment plates, with spheroid volume reduced by 80% compared to control IgG. These results demonstrate that HS20 selectively targets the functional heparan sulfate epitope of glypican-3 and inhibits tumor cell migration and invasion.

### Mutation of HS20 to Remove an N-Glycosylation Site does not Alter Binding Affinity and Specificity

- An N-glycosylation site at asparagine 250 in the VL domain of HS20 was identified by sequence analysis. A point mutation (N250Q) was introduced to eliminate this glycosylation site. The mutant HS20-N250Q was expressed and purified under identical conditions as wild-type HS20. Binding affinity to glypican-3 was unchanged, as confirmed by ELISA and flow cytometry. No differences in epitope recognition were observed in immunohistochemistry of tumor tissue sections. Thermal stability assays demonstrated improved resistance to aggregation in the N250Q variant. These findings indicate that removal of the N-glycosylation site enhances the biophysical properties of HS20 without compromising its biological function, thereby improving manufacturability and shelf-life.

### HS20 Inhibits HCC Tumor Growth In Vivo

- Hep3B cells were subcutaneously injected into nude mice to establish xenograft tumors. Once tumors reached 100 mm³, mice were treated with intravenous injections of 25 mg/kg HS20 or control IgG twice weekly for three weeks. Tumor volume was measured twice weekly. HS20-treated mice exhibited a 68% reduction in tumor volume compared to controls (p < 0.001). Histological analysis revealed decreased proliferation (Ki-67 staining), increased apoptosis (TUNEL staining), and reduced c-Met phosphorylation in HS20-treated tumors. No signs of systemic toxicity or weight loss were observed. These results confirm the in vivo efficacy of HS20 in suppressing hepatocellular carcinoma growth.

### The HN3 Human Antibody Inhibits HCC Tumor Growth In Vivo

- Hep3B xenografts were established in nude mice as described above. Mice received intravenous injections of 20 mg/kg HN3 or control IgG twice weekly. HN3 treatment resulted in a 72% reduction in tumor volume after 21 days (p < 0.001). Immunohistochemistry showed reduced Yap nuclear localization and downregulation of Wnt target genes. No adverse effects on liver or kidney function were detected. These findings demonstrate that HN3 is a potent in vivo therapeutic agent against hepatocellular carcinoma.

### Example 6

- An immunotoxin, HN3(VH)-PE38, was constructed by fusing the VH domain of HN3 to the catalytic domain of Pseudomonas exotoxin A (PE38) via a (G4S)3 linker. The fusion protein was expressed in E. coli, refolded, and purified by nickel affinity chromatography. Purity exceeded 95% as confirmed by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry. The immunotoxin demonstrated no aggregation under physiological conditions. In vitro cytotoxicity assays against Hep3B, Huh-7, and HepG2 cells showed IC50 values of 0.2–0.5 ng/mL, while non-expressing cells were unaffected. In a Hep3B xenograft model, daily intravenous administration of 0.5 mg/kg HN3(VH)-PE38 for 14 days resulted in complete tumor regression in 60% of mice and significant growth delay in the remainder. No weight loss or hepatotoxicity was observed. These results establish HN3(VH)-PE38 as a highly potent and selective therapeutic candidate for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma.
