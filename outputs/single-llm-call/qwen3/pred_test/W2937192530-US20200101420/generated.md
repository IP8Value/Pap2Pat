# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- define field of invention  
The present invention resides in the field of solar-driven desalination and water purification systems, specifically involving the design and implementation of a novel solar membrane distillation apparatus that integrates a photothermal, superhydrophobic graphitic foam membrane with integrated micro- and nano-scale surface modifications to enhance water evaporation efficiency, minimize thermal losses, and prevent salt fouling during continuous operation under natural solar irradiation. The invention is particularly directed to the structural and compositional optimization of porous carbon-based membranes for use in low-cost, scalable, and energy-efficient desalination units suitable for deployment in arid, off-grid, and coastal regions where access to potable water is limited and conventional desalination technologies are economically or logistically impractical.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- motivate solar desalination  
The global demand for clean drinking water continues to rise in tandem with population growth, climate-induced water scarcity, and the degradation of freshwater sources. Solar desalination presents a sustainable and renewable solution to this challenge, leveraging abundant sunlight as the primary energy source to drive the separation of salt from seawater or brackish water without reliance on grid electricity or fossil fuels. Unlike conventional thermal or reverse osmosis methods, solar desalination systems offer the potential for decentralized operation, minimal maintenance, and reduced environmental impact, making them uniquely suited for remote communities and disaster relief scenarios.

- describe limitations of direct approach  
Direct solar evaporation methods, while simple in design, suffer from low efficiency due to excessive heat loss to the ambient environment, poor light absorption by water surfaces, and rapid salt crystallization on the evaporative interface, which inhibits sustained water vapor production. These systems typically rely on shallow water pools exposed to sunlight, resulting in slow evaporation rates and frequent cleaning cycles, rendering them unsuitable for continuous, high-yield operation.

- introduce water evaporation using porous carbon nanomaterials  
Recent advances have demonstrated that porous carbon nanomaterials, particularly graphitic foams, possess exceptional light-absorbing properties across the solar spectrum, enabling efficient conversion of incident solar radiation into localized thermal energy. When engineered with appropriate surface architecture, these materials can serve as photothermal platforms that concentrate heat at the air–water interface, thereby accelerating evaporation without requiring bulk heating of the entire water volume.

- describe benefits of nano-structures  
The nanostructured surface of graphitic foams enhances light trapping through multiple internal reflections and reduces reflectance to less than 5% across the visible and near-infrared spectrum. Their high thermal conductivity ensures rapid heat distribution, while their open-pore morphology facilitates capillary-driven water transport from the bulk reservoir to the evaporation surface, maintaining a continuous supply of feedwater without external pumping.

- discuss limitations of solar evaporation systems  
Despite these advantages, existing solar evaporation systems remain vulnerable to salt accumulation at the vapor–liquid interface, which progressively blocks pore access, reduces evaporation rates, and ultimately causes system failure. Additionally, the lack of effective vapor condensation and collection mechanisms results in significant energy loss and low overall water recovery efficiency.

- introduce membrane distillation  
Membrane distillation offers a promising alternative by physically separating the evaporation and condensation processes via a porous, hydrophobic membrane that permits vapor transport while rejecting liquid water and dissolved salts. This configuration inherently resists salt fouling and enables higher purity permeate production.

- describe limitations of membrane distillation  
However, conventional membrane distillation systems require external heating sources and suffer from low vapor flux due to poor thermal conductivity of polymeric membranes, high thermal resistance, and susceptibility to wetting under prolonged exposure to saline feed. Furthermore, the cost and scalability of existing membrane materials have hindered widespread adoption in resource-limited settings.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce solar membrane distillation apparatus  
The present invention introduces a solar membrane distillation apparatus that integrates a photothermal graphitic foam membrane with a superhydrophobic surface, a pre-heating chamber, a vapor chamber, and a condenser to achieve high-efficiency, salt-resistant, and self-sustaining desalination under natural solar irradiation without the need for external power or mechanical pumps.

- describe solar distillation membrane  
The solar distillation membrane comprises a three-dimensional graphitic foam structure with controlled pore architecture, engineered to absorb over 95% of incident solar radiation and convert it into localized heat at the vapor–liquid interface, thereby driving efficient water evaporation while minimizing thermal conduction losses to the underlying water reservoir.

- describe water chamber  
The water chamber is positioned beneath the distillation membrane and contains the saline feedwater, which is drawn upward through capillary action into the porous structure of the membrane. The chamber is constructed from thermally insulating materials to reduce heat loss downward and is optionally lined with a reflective layer to redirect unabsorbed light back toward the membrane.

- describe vapor chamber  
The vapor chamber is an enclosed space located above the distillation membrane, designed to collect and channel water vapor generated at the membrane’s upper surface. Its walls are thermally insulated and sealed to prevent vapor escape, ensuring that the vapor remains in close proximity to the condenser surface for efficient recovery.

- describe condenser  
The condenser is positioned adjacent to the vapor chamber and is maintained at a temperature lower than the vapor’s dew point through passive heat dissipation to the ambient environment or via a heat exchange conduit connected to incoming feedwater. It is constructed from a material with high thermal conductivity and a hydrophilic inner surface to promote rapid condensation and droplet coalescence.

- describe superhydrophobic membrane surface  
The upper surface of the distillation membrane is rendered superhydrophobic through the application of a fluorosilane-based coating combined with micro- and nano-scale surface texturing, which prevents liquid water from penetrating the pores while allowing water vapor to pass freely, thereby eliminating salt fouling and ensuring long-term operational stability.

- describe tubular distillation membrane  
In an alternative embodiment, the distillation membrane is configured in a tubular geometry, enabling increased surface area per unit footprint and facilitating integration into modular, scalable systems. The tubular design also enhances thermal gradients along the length of the membrane, improving vapor transport dynamics and condensation efficiency.

- describe pre-heating chamber  
The apparatus includes a pre-heating chamber that utilizes waste heat from the condenser to elevate the temperature of incoming feedwater prior to its entry into the water chamber, thereby reducing the thermal load on the photothermal membrane and increasing overall energy efficiency.

- describe micro/nano particles and hydrophobic composition  
The membrane surface is modified with a composite layer of graphite micro-particles and nano-scale graphene platelets, embedded within a phenolic resin matrix, followed by a thin, covalently bonded fluorosilane coating. This dual-layer architecture enhances both hydrophobicity and mechanical durability, enabling resistance to abrasion and chemical degradation under prolonged solar exposure.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- describe membrane distillation apparatus  
The membrane distillation apparatus consists of a vertically stacked assembly comprising a water chamber at the base, a photothermal graphitic foam membrane positioned horizontally above it, a vapor chamber enclosing the upper surface of the membrane, and a condenser mounted on the outer wall of the vapor chamber. The entire system is enclosed within a housing that includes a light-transmitting top wall, typically fabricated from tempered glass or high-transmittance polymer, to maximize solar irradiation while minimizing convective heat loss.

- introduce solar distillation membrane  
The solar distillation membrane is composed of a rigid, open-cell graphitic foam with a thickness ranging from 2 to 10 millimeters, selected for optimal balance between light absorption, thermal conductivity, and capillary water transport. The foam is derived from heat-treated pitch-based carbon and reinforced with 5 to 10 weight percent ground graphene platelets to enhance structural integrity and photothermal performance.

- describe graphitic foam structure  
The graphitic foam exhibits a hierarchical pore structure, with macropores of 100 to 500 micrometers facilitating bulk water transport and mesopores of 10 to 50 micrometers enabling localized vapor generation. The foam’s bulk porosity exceeds 90%, ensuring minimal resistance to water flow and maximal surface area for solar absorption.

- introduce hydrophobic composition  
The hydrophobic composition consists of a mixture of heptadecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrodecyl trichlorosilane and phenolic resin, applied in a two-step process to simultaneously functionalize the surface chemistry and reinforce the mechanical substrate.

- describe application of hydrophobic composition  
The hydrophobic composition is applied via immersion of the dried graphitic foam in a 0.1 weight percent fluorosilane solution in hexane for a minimum of 12 hours, followed by thermal curing at 100°C for one hour to promote covalent bonding of silane molecules to surface hydroxyl and carbonyl groups.

- describe superhydrophobic surface  
The resulting surface exhibits a water contact angle exceeding 150 degrees and a sliding angle below 5 degrees, characteristic of a superhydrophobic state. This property is maintained even after prolonged exposure to saline solutions and repeated thermal cycling.

- introduce housing and light transmitting wall  
The housing is constructed from low-thermal-conductivity polymers or composites, with a transparent top wall that transmits at least 85% of solar radiation in the 300 to 2500 nm range. The wall is optionally coated with an anti-reflective layer to further enhance light coupling.

- describe water chamber  
The water chamber is a shallow reservoir with a depth of 5 to 20 millimeters, lined with a mirrored surface to reflect unabsorbed light upward. It is connected to a feedwater inlet and a salt discharge outlet, allowing for continuous or batch operation.

- describe vapor chamber  
The vapor chamber is sealed along its lateral and bottom edges, with a single outlet leading to the condenser. Its internal volume is optimized to maintain a saturated vapor environment, promoting condensation on the cooler condenser surface.

- introduce condenser  
The condenser is a finned or corrugated metal surface, typically aluminum or copper, oriented perpendicular to the direction of vapor flow. It is thermally coupled to the feedwater inlet via a heat exchange conduit, enabling pre-heating of incoming water using waste condensation heat.

- describe distillation membrane usage  
The distillation membrane operates passively under solar irradiation, drawing water upward through capillary forces, converting solar energy into localized heat, and releasing vapor into the vapor chamber without the need for external pressure differentials or pumps.

- describe hydrophobic composition application methods  
Alternative application methods include spray coating, dip-coating, and vapor-phase deposition, each tailored to the geometry of the membrane. Spray coating is preferred for tubular or curved surfaces, while immersion is optimal for planar configurations.

- describe nanotexture of graphitic foam  
The surface of the graphitic foam is naturally textured at the nanoscale due to the irregular arrangement of carbon layers, which is further enhanced by controlled thermal etching to create micro-cavities that trap air and amplify hydrophobicity.

- describe planar distillation membrane  
The planar configuration is ideal for flat-panel installations and is mounted horizontally within the housing, maximizing exposure to direct sunlight.

- describe tubular distillation membrane  
The tubular configuration is arranged in a helical or straight geometry within a cylindrical housing, enabling multi-stage evaporation and improved condensation efficiency through extended vapor travel paths.

- describe vessel-shaped distillation membrane  
In a third embodiment, the membrane is shaped as an open vessel with upward-curving walls, allowing for natural convection of vapor and enhanced condensation along the inner surfaces.

- introduce condenser construction  
The condenser is constructed from a metal substrate coated with a thin layer of hydrophilic polymer, such as polyvinyl alcohol, to promote rapid droplet formation and gravity-driven drainage into a collection reservoir.

- describe feed water heat exchange conduit  
The heat exchange conduit is a coiled metal pipe that runs from the condenser outlet to the feedwater inlet, transferring thermal energy from the condensed vapor to the incoming saline water, thereby reducing the energy required for evaporation.

- introduce pre-heating chamber  
The pre-heating chamber is an insulated compartment through which the feedwater passes prior to entering the water chamber, raised to a temperature of 30 to 45°C by latent heat recovered from the condensation process.

- describe solar water heater  
In an optional configuration, the pre-heating chamber is supplemented with a secondary solar water heater consisting of a blackened absorber plate beneath a transparent cover, which further elevates the feedwater temperature before it reaches the main distillation unit.

- describe metal pipe pre-heater  
The metal pipe pre-heater is fabricated from copper or aluminum tubing, arranged in a serpentine pattern within the pre-heating chamber, maximizing surface contact with warm vapor and minimizing thermal resistance.

- introduce nano/micro particles  
The surface of the graphitic foam is coated with a composite layer of graphite micro-particles (20–50 micrometers) and graphene nanoplatelets (50–200 nanometers), dispersed in a phenolic resin binder to form a mechanically robust, light-absorbing skin.

- describe particle size range  
The micro-particles provide structural reinforcement and thermal conductivity, while the nano-particles enhance surface roughness and light trapping, collectively contributing to the superhydrophobic effect.

- describe particle concentration control  
The concentration of particles is maintained between 10 and 30 weight percent relative to the resin matrix, optimized to balance hydrophobicity, porosity, and mechanical stability.

- describe pore size of graphitic foam  
The average pore size of the graphitic foam ranges from 100 to 400 micrometers, selected to permit capillary rise of water while limiting salt transport to the evaporation surface.

- describe bulk porosity of graphitic foam  
The bulk porosity exceeds 92%, ensuring minimal hydraulic resistance and rapid water replenishment to the evaporation zone.

- describe surface porosity control  
Surface porosity is controlled by selective laser ablation or chemical etching to create a micro-porous skin layer with pore sizes below 10 micrometers, enhancing vapor selectivity and reducing liquid intrusion.

- introduce hydrophobic coating materials  
The hydrophobic coating is composed of fluorinated silanes, including heptadecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrodecyl trichlorosilane, chosen for their chemical inertness, thermal stability, and strong covalent bonding to carbon surfaces.

- describe hydrophobic coating thickness  
The coating thickness is maintained between 50 and 200 nanometers, sufficient to impart superhydrophobicity without blocking pore openings or impeding vapor diffusion.

- describe apparatus operation  
The apparatus operates by exposing the light-transmitting wall to sunlight, which is absorbed by the graphitic foam membrane, heating the water at the interface and generating vapor that diffuses into the vapor chamber. The vapor contacts the cooler condenser surface, condenses into liquid water, and drains into a collection reservoir.

- describe preheater operation  
The pre-heating chamber receives the condensed water, which transfers its latent heat to the incoming saline feedwater, elevating its temperature and reducing the energy burden on the photothermal membrane.

- describe condenser operation  
The condenser is maintained at a temperature below the dew point of the vapor through passive heat dissipation to ambient air, with optional enhancement via radiative cooling surfaces or forced convection.

- describe treated water exhaust  
The treated water is collected in a separate reservoir and may be further purified through filtration or UV treatment if required for potable use.

- describe air evacuation from distillation chamber  
Air within the vapor chamber is evacuated through a one-way valve during initial operation, ensuring that the internal environment remains saturated with water vapor and free of non-condensable gases.

- describe vapor-driven air evacuation  
As vapor is generated, it displaces residual air through the condenser outlet, creating a self-sustaining purge mechanism that maintains optimal vapor pressure without external pumps.

- describe method of solar distillation  
The method of solar distillation comprises the steps of: (a) introducing saline water into the water chamber; (b) exposing the photothermal membrane to solar irradiation; (c) enabling capillary-driven water transport to the membrane surface; (d) converting solar energy into localized heat to generate water vapor; (e) directing vapor into the vapor chamber; (f) condensing vapor on a cooled surface; (g) recovering purified water; and (h) pre-heating incoming feedwater using waste heat from condensation.
