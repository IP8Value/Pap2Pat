# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

- The present invention relates to isolated antibodies that specifically bind to human platelet-derived growth factor C (PDGF-C), particularly those that recognize epitopes within the conserved growth factor domain of PDGF-CC, and which are capable of neutralizing PDGF-CC-mediated signaling through the PDGFRα receptor. These antibodies are distinct from previously described reagents in their high specificity for human PDGF-CC, absence of cross-reactivity with other PDGF family members including PDGF-DD, PDGF-AA, and PDGF-BB, and their ability to inhibit both autocrine and paracrine activation pathways critical to tumor progression and blood-brain barrier disruption. The antibodies are produced through hybridoma technology and further engineered into chimeric and humanized forms for therapeutic and diagnostic applications. They are substantially free from contaminating immunoglobulins and are formulated for use in biological systems without inducing nonspecific immune activation or aggregation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- Platelet-derived growth factors (PDGFs) are a family of dimeric signaling proteins that regulate cell proliferation, migration, survival, and tissue remodeling through activation of PDGF receptor tyrosine kinases. Among these, PDGF-C is a unique member requiring proteolytic cleavage of its N-terminal CUB domain to release the biologically active growth factor domain (GFD), a process mediated in part by tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) in the central nervous system and by other proteases in peripheral tissues. Once activated, PDGF-CC binds and induces dimerization of PDGFRα homodimers, triggering downstream signaling cascades that promote fibroblast recruitment, angiogenesis, and stromal support in pathological contexts. Dysregulated PDGF-CC expression has been implicated in a broad spectrum of malignancies including epithelial carcinomas of the breast, colon, pancreas, prostate, and kidney, as well as in fibrotic diseases of the liver, lung, and kidney. In addition to its role in tumor biology, PDGF-CC has been identified as a key mediator of blood-brain barrier integrity, where its overexpression leads to increased vascular permeability, edema, and neuroinflammation in conditions such as stroke, traumatic brain injury, multiple sclerosis, and diabetic retinopathy. Despite its clear pathophysiological relevance, no clinically approved therapeutic antibodies targeting PDGF-CC have been developed to date. Existing small molecule inhibitors of PDGFRs lack specificity and exhibit off-target effects due to inhibition of other receptor tyrosine kinases. There remains a critical unmet need for highly specific, high-affinity monoclonal antibodies capable of selectively neutralizing PDGF-CC without interfering with other PDGF ligands or physiological functions of the PDGFR system.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- The invention provides novel isolated monoclonal antibodies that bind with high specificity and affinity to human PDGF-C, particularly to a defined epitope within the core active domain of the PDGF-CC growth factor fragment. These antibodies include murine, chimeric, and humanized variants designated 6B3, ch6B3, 11F5, 12F5, and 19C7, each exhibiting complete absence of cross-reactivity with PDGF-DD, PDGF-AA, or PDGF-BB. The antibodies are characterized by specific complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) in their variable light and heavy chains, which confer neutralizing activity against PDGF-CC-induced PDGFRα phosphorylation and subsequent cellular proliferation. The heavy and light chain CDR sequences of 6B3, ch6B3, 11F5, 12F5, and 19C7 are disclosed in amino acid and nucleic acid form, enabling recombinant production and further engineering. The invention further encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding these antibody chains, expression vectors containing such sequences under control of appropriate promoters, and host cells—bacterial, yeast, insect, or mammalian—used for recombinant expression of the antibodies. Therapeutic methods are described for modulating PDGF-CC activity in vivo, including inhibition of autocrine or paracrine signaling in tumor microenvironments, reduction of tumor angiogenesis and stromal support, suppression of tumor growth and metastasis, and modulation of blood-brain barrier permeability in neurological disorders. The antibodies are shown to inhibit PDGF-CC-mediated opening of the blood-brain barrier induced by exogenous ligand or pathological insult, thereby reducing vascular leakage and edema. Diagnostic assays utilizing these antibodies for detection of PDGF-C in tissue samples, serum, or cerebrospinal fluid are disclosed, as well as drug screening platforms for identifying compounds that interfere with PDGF-CC/antibody binding. The antibodies serve as molecular tools for research, imaging, and targeted delivery. A diagnostic kit is described comprising a target-specific anti-PDGF-C antibody, a labeled secondary detection reagent, buffer components, and instructions for use. The invention further includes non-human transgenic animals expressing human PDGF-C in place of the endogenous murine ortholog, wherein the transgene exhibits at least 85% sequence identity to human PDGF-C as determined by the Needleman-Wunsch algorithm with gap penalties of 10 and extension of 0.5. Substantially homologous variants are defined as polypeptides or nucleic acids retaining at least 80% identity to the disclosed sequences while maintaining the ability to bind the antibody or mediate biological activity. The term “isolated antibody” refers to an antibody that is substantially free from other cellular components and is produced by recombinant or hybridoma methods. The term “subject” or “patient” refers to a human or non-human mammal requiring therapeutic or diagnostic intervention. “Treating” or “treatment” encompasses prophylactic, therapeutic, and palliative measures aimed at reducing disease burden, slowing progression, or alleviating symptoms associated with PDGF-C dysregulation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

- PDGF-C has been identified as a molecular hallmark of epithelial carcinomas and other malignancies characterized by aberrant stromal activation and angiogenesis. Its expression is markedly elevated in tumor tissues compared to adjacent normal parenchyma, particularly in breast, colon, pancreatic, renal, and prostate carcinomas, where it is localized to both tumor cells and associated vasculature. The invention demonstrates that PDGF-C expression correlates with increased microvessel density, cancer-associated fibroblast infiltration, and poor clinical prognosis, establishing it as a viable therapeutic target. The isolated antibodies of the invention bind with high affinity to a conformational epitope within the PDGF-CC core domain, specifically within residues 145–165 of the mature growth factor sequence, as confirmed by biosensor analysis and peptide competition assays. These antibodies are bivalent, comprising two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, each chain containing variable and constant domains. The variable domains include framework regions and three complementarity-determining regions per chain, which together form the antigen-binding site. The antibodies belong to the IgG isotype, particularly IgG1, which confers strong effector functions including complement-dependent cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, although isotypes with reduced effector function such as IgG2, IgG4, or IgA may be employed for applications requiring minimal immune activation. The Fc region mediates binding to Fc receptors on immune cells, while the Fv region, composed of the variable heavy and light chains, is responsible for specific recognition of PDGF-C. The amino acid sequences of the variable regions of 6B3, ch6B3, 11F5, 12F5, and 19C7 are disclosed, as are the corresponding nucleic acid sequences encoding these regions. Conservative modifications, including substitutions within families of amino acids such as hydrophobic, aromatic, basic, or acidic residues, are encompassed provided they do not impair binding or neutralizing activity. Antibodies may be modified by conjugation to polymers such as polyethylene glycol to extend serum half-life, encapsulated in microcapsules for controlled release, or fused to other functional domains such as toxins or radionuclide chelators. The nucleic acids encoding the antibody chains are substantially free from other genomic or cDNA sequences and are subcloned into expression vectors containing strong promoters such as CMV, SV40, or EF1α, and transcriptional enhancers suitable for mammalian, insect, or bacterial expression systems. Recombinant vectors may be co-transfected with selectable markers such as glutamine synthetase or dihydrofolate reductase to enable stable cell line generation. Alternative antibody formats including single-chain variable fragments (scFv), Fab fragments, nanobodies, and bispecific constructs are also described. Codon optimization for expression in human, CHO, or other host cells is employed to enhance yield. RNA editing, site-directed mutagenesis, and affinity maturation techniques such as error-prone PCR or phage display are used to generate variants with improved binding kinetics, thermal stability, or resistance to proteolysis. The antibodies may be humanized by grafting the CDRs of murine antibodies onto human framework regions, followed by back-mutation of key framework residues to restore affinity. The resulting humanized antibodies exhibit reduced immunogenicity while retaining full neutralizing capacity. The invention further encompasses methods for producing recombinant antibodies in transgenic animals, cell culture supernatants, or bioreactor systems, and for purifying them using protein A/G affinity chromatography, ion-exchange, or size-exclusion methods.

### Treatment Methods and Pharmaceutical Formulations

- The isolated antibodies of the invention are administered to subjects suffering from diseases characterized by aberrant PDGF-C expression, including but not limited to epithelial carcinomas, gliomas, fibrotic disorders, stroke, traumatic brain injury, multiple sclerosis, diabetic retinopathy, and cerebral edema. Administration may be systemic via intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular routes, or localized via intrathecal, intranasal, lumbar, cisterna magna, or cerebral ventricular delivery, particularly for CNS indications. Infusion pumps may be employed for continuous delivery. The antibodies may be administered alone or in combination with chemotherapeutic agents, radiation therapy, or thrombolytic agents such as tissue plasminogen activator (tPA), where PDGF-CC inhibition enhances therapeutic efficacy and reduces hemorrhagic complications. Prophylactic administration is contemplated for high-risk individuals, such as those with preneoplastic lesions or genetic predispositions to PDGF-C-driven malignancies. Pharmaceutical compositions are formulated as sterile, pyrogen-free solutions containing appropriate diluents such as phosphate-buffered saline, carriers such as human serum albumin, excipients including sugars and polyols, bacteriostats like benzyl alcohol, cryoprotectants such as sucrose, salts including sodium chloride, divalent cations such as calcium or magnesium, and non-ionic surfactants of the Pluronic series to prevent aggregation. Ionic binding, gel encapsulation, physical trapping in liposomes, iontophoresis, or soaking in sustained-release matrices may be employed for localized delivery. Dosage regimens are determined based on body weight, disease severity, route of administration, and pharmacokinetic profile, with effective ranges typically between 0.1 mg/kg and 20 mg/kg, administered weekly or biweekly. Depot injections or implants may be used for prolonged release over weeks to months.

### PDGF-C/PDGF-CC Detection/Diagnosis Assays

- Immunoassays for the detection of PDGF-C and PDGF-CC in biological fluids or tissue sections are provided, including two-site sandwich assays, single-site immunoassays, forward and reverse sandwich formats, and simultaneous detection systems. These assays utilize the isolated antibodies as capture or detection reagents, with binding detected via enzyme-linked reporters such as horseradish peroxidase or alkaline phosphatase, fluorescent compounds such as fluorescein or rhodamine, chemiluminescent tags, or radioactive isotopes. Immunofluorescence and enzyme immunoassay techniques are employed for tissue localization, with controls including isotype-matched antibodies, antigen pre-absorption, and omission of primary antibody. The assays are suitable for use in clinical diagnostics, prognostic stratification, and monitoring of therapeutic response. A single-site immunoassay format is described wherein the antibody is immobilized on a solid support and used to capture PDGF-C from serum or cerebrospinal fluid, followed by detection with a labeled secondary antibody or direct labeling of the primary antibody.

### Kits

- Diagnostic kits are provided comprising a container holding an isolated anti-PDGF-C antibody, a labeled secondary antibody or detection reagent, buffer solutions, positive and negative control samples, and written instructions for performing the assay. The kit may include software for data analysis, calibration curves, or interpretation algorithms. The secondary antibody may be conjugated to an enzyme, fluorophore, or nanoparticle. The container is sealed, sterile, and labeled with expiration date, storage conditions, and batch number. The kits are suitable for use in clinical laboratories, research settings, or point-of-care diagnostics.

### Drug Screening Assay

- A drug screening assay is described wherein test compounds are evaluated for their ability to inhibit the binding of the isolated anti-PDGF-C antibody to immobilized PDGF-CC antigen. Binding is measured using surface plasmon resonance, ELISA, or fluorescence polarization. Compounds that disrupt antibody-antigen interaction are identified as potential inhibitors of PDGF-CC signaling and are further evaluated for biological activity in cell-based assays. This platform enables high-throughput screening of small molecules, peptides, or nucleic acid aptamers for therapeutic development.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Generation of Monoclonal Antibodies to Human PDGF-CC

- Recombinant human PDGF-CC core domain was produced and purified from mammalian expression systems. Mice were immunized with the antigen, and splenocytes were fused with myeloma cells to generate hybridomas. Supernatants from 16 clones were screened by ELISA and immunoblot for reactivity with human PDGF-CC. Seven stable clones—6B3, 9A5, 11F5, 19A1, 19B1, 19C7, and 19D1—were selected for further characterization. Purified IgGs from these clones were tested for binding specificity, and clones 6B3, 11F5, and 19C7 demonstrated high affinity and neutralizing activity. Isotypes were determined as IgG1 for 6B3 and 19C7, and IgG2a for 11F5.

### Example 2—Amino Acid Sequence of Antibodies

- The variable heavy and light chain sequences of 6B3, 11F5, and 19C7 were determined by Edman degradation and cDNA sequencing. The CDRs of 6B3 and 19C7 were found to be identical, explaining their cross-competition in binding assays. The sequences of the heavy and light chains of ch6B3 were confirmed to differ by a single conservative substitution in the heavy chain framework region.

### Example 3—Generation of Chimeric Anti-PDGF-CC Antibody 6B3 (ch6B3)

- Murine variable regions of 6B3 were cloned into human IgG1 constant region expression vectors. The resulting chimeric construct, ch6B3, was expressed in CHO cells, purified, and validated for binding to human PDGF-CC by ELISA and biosensor analysis. Neutralization of PDGFRα phosphorylation was retained, confirming functional equivalence to the murine parent.

### Example 4—Identification of the 6B3 Binding Epitope on PDGF-C

- Computational modeling predicted four potential epitope regions on PDGF-CC. Synthetic peptides corresponding to these regions were immobilized on biosensor chips. Only peptide 3, corresponding to residues 145–165, inhibited 6B3 binding to full-length PDGF-CC. Direct binding of 6B3 to immobilized peptide 3 confirmed this region as the core epitope. Overlapping peptides refined the minimal binding sequence to residues 148–162.

### Example 5—Biosensor Confirmation of Human PDGF-CC Specificity

- Binding kinetics of 6B3, 11F5, 19C7, and ch6B3 to PDGF-CC and PDGF-DD were measured. All antibodies bound PDGF-CC with K_D values between 0.1 and 0.8 nM, while showing no detectable binding to PDGF-DD. Competitive binding studies revealed two distinct epitope groups: 6B3/19C7 and 11F5. Binding was unaffected by reduction and alkylation of PDGF-CC, indicating a conformational epitope independent of disulfide bonds.

### Example 6—PDGF-Ligand Induced Receptor Activation and Cell Proliferation

- PAE cells expressing PDGFRα were stimulated with PDGF-CC in the presence or absence of 6B3. Pre-incubation with 6B3 completely abolished PDGFRα tyrosine phosphorylation. Cell proliferation assays confirmed dose-dependent inhibition of mitogenesis by 6B3, with IC50 values below 1 nM.

### Example 7—Immunohistochemistry—Tissue Screens

- Immunohistochemistry using 6B3 on 17 normal and 16 tumor tissue samples revealed strong cytoplasmic and membranous staining in tumor cells and vasculature across breast, colon, pancreas, kidney, and prostate cancers. Control tissues showed weak or moderate staining. Modified H-scores demonstrated statistically significant increases in PDGF-CC expression in all tumor types compared to matched normal tissues (p < 0.05). Staining was abolished by pre-incubation with excess PDGF-CC but not VEGF-B, confirming specificity.

### Example 8—In Vivo Therapy Studies

- Human colon carcinoma (Colo205) and breast carcinoma (MDA-MB-231) xenografts were established in immunodeficient mice. Treatment with 6B3 or 12F5 (10 mg/kg, biweekly) significantly reduced tumor volume by 50–70% compared to PBS controls. Tumor vasculature density and proliferation markers (Ki67) were markedly reduced. Histological analysis confirmed decreased microvessel density and increased apoptosis in treated tumors.

### Example 9—Modulation of PDGF-CC Mediated Opening of the Blood-Brain Barrier by Anti-PDGF-C Antibody 6B3

- In a mouse model of blood-retinal barrier disruption, intraocular injection of PDGF-CC induced significant extravasation of fluorescent dextran. Intraperitoneal pretreatment with ch6B3 completely prevented this leakage. Quantification of fluorescence intensity showed a 90% reduction in tracer extravasation compared to isotype control. Similar results were observed in a murine stroke model, where ch6B3 administration reduced infarct volume and hemorrhage score. Prophylactic and therapeutic administration both conferred significant neuroprotection. Humanized variants of 6B3 were generated and shown to retain binding affinity and neutralizing activity in vitro.
