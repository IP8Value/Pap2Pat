# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) is a small, non-enveloped, single-stranded circular DNA virus belonging to the genus Circovirus within the family Circoviridae. It is recognized as one of the smallest autonomously replicating DNA viruses known to infect mammals. The viral genome is approximately 2 kilobases in length and encodes a single major capsid protein (CP), which self-assembles into an icosahedral capsid approximately 17 nanometers in diameter. PCV-2 was first isolated in 1991 from Canadian piglets exhibiting postweaning multisystemic wasting syndrome (PMWS), a condition that has since become the leading cause of economic loss in the global swine industry. Beyond PMWS, PCV-2 infection has been associated with a range of other clinical manifestations collectively termed porcine circovirus-associated diseases (PCAD), including porcine dermatitis and nephropathy syndrome (PDNS), reproductive failure, and enteric and respiratory disorders. Transmission occurs primarily via the oral-nasal route, although all bodily secretions and excretions may serve as vectors for viral spread. More recently, a novel strain designated PCV-3 has emerged, linked to similar syndromes, though its full pathogenic profile remains under investigation.

Given the substantial economic impact of PCV-2 and related diseases, vaccination has become the cornerstone of disease control in commercial swine operations worldwide. Currently, five commercial vaccines are available: two whole-killed virus vaccines and three subunit vaccines, all of which are based on the ORF2-encoded capsid protein. This protein harbors the primary neutralizing epitopes capable of eliciting robust and durable humoral immunity. Recombinant expression of the CP in heterologous systems such as insect cells has demonstrated its capacity to self-assemble into virus-like particles (VLPs) that are morphologically indistinguishable from native virions. VLP-based vaccines offer significant advantages over traditional platforms: they mimic the native conformation of the virus without containing infectious genetic material, thereby combining the immunogenicity of live-attenuated or whole-inactivated vaccines with the safety of subunit approaches. Despite the success of VLP-based human vaccines—such as those for hepatitis B and human papillomavirus—no veterinary VLP vaccines have yet reached commercialization, highlighting an unmet need in animal health.

Existing recombinant production platforms for PCV-2 CP—including bacterial, yeast, and insect cell systems—have proven effective in generating immunogenic VLPs. However, these systems are often constrained by high production costs, complex downstream purification requirements, and biosafety concerns related to potential contamination with animal pathogens. Moreover, many current purification strategies rely on affinity tags and multi-step chromatographic processes that increase both complexity and expense, limiting scalability and accessibility, particularly in resource-limited settings. These limitations underscore the necessity for alternative, cost-effective, and scalable production platforms that maintain high product quality and immunogenicity.

Plant-based transient expression systems present a compelling solution to these challenges. Plants are eukaryotic organisms capable of performing essential post-translational modifications, and they pose negligible risk of harboring human or animal pathogens. Transient expression in plants—typically mediated by Agrobacterium tumefaciens infiltration of Nicotiana benthamiana leaves—enables rapid, high-level protein production within days, using only basic greenhouse infrastructure and standard molecular biology tools. This system operates under Biosafety Level 1 (BSL-1) conditions, making it widely accessible to research institutions and vaccine developers globally. Furthermore, plant biomass can be scaled linearly from laboratory to industrial levels without the need for expensive bioreactors or sterile fermentation media.

One particularly promising application of plant-based systems is the production of pseudovirions (PsVs)—VLPs that encapsidate non-viral nucleic acid payloads, such as reporter genes. Unlike conventional VLPs, PsVs can be engineered to deliver functional genetic material into target mammalian cells, enabling their use not only as vaccines but also as tools for neutralization assays, gene delivery, and diagnostic reagent development. The ability to produce PCV-2 PsVs in plants would represent a significant advance, offering a safe, scalable, and versatile platform for next-generation veterinary vaccines and serological diagnostics. The present invention addresses this need by providing methods for the efficient production, purification, and functional validation of PCV-2 pseudovirions in plant cells, along with their application in neutralization assays and pharmaceutical compositions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for producing porcine circovirus (PCV) pseudovirions (PsVs) in plant cells, comprising the expression of a PCV capsid polypeptide in a plant host, wherein said polypeptide self-assembles into virus-like particles that encapsidate a non-native single-stranded DNA molecule to form functional pseudovirions. In a preferred embodiment, the PCV capsid polypeptide is derived from PCV-2 and is encoded by a codon-optimized nucleotide sequence operably linked to a plant-compatible promoter within an expression vector. The method further involves introducing into the same plant cell a replicating vector that directs the synthesis of a single-stranded DNA amplicon containing a heterologous polynucleotide sequence, such as a reporter gene. This amplicon is replicated via a rolling-circle mechanism mediated by a viral replication regulatory protein, such as Rep/RepA from a geminivirus, and is subsequently encapsidated by the co-expressed PCV capsid polypeptide to yield infectious pseudovirions capable of delivering the heterologous sequence into mammalian cells.

The invention also encompasses a neutralization assay for detecting neutralizing antibodies against PCV in a biological sample, comprising the steps of combining the biological sample with PCV PsVs produced according to the disclosed method, contacting the resulting mixture with susceptible mammalian cells, and assaying for expression of a reporter polypeptide encoded by the heterologous sequence within the PsV genome. Reduction in reporter signal relative to a control indicates the presence of neutralizing antibodies in the sample, thereby enabling serological diagnosis and vaccine efficacy evaluation.

Additionally, the invention includes the PCV PsV per se, which comprises a PCV capsid formed from PCV capsid polypeptides and encapsidating a single-stranded DNA molecule that is non-native to PCV and contains at least one heterologous polynucleotide sequence encoding a reporter polypeptide or an antigenic determinant. The composition of the PsV is defined by its structural integrity, buoyant density (approximately 1.56 g/cm³ in CsCl gradients), particle size (12–24 nm in diameter), and functional capacity to transduce mammalian cells.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising the PCV PsV in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, adjuvant, or excipient, suitable for administration to swine for the prevention or treatment of PCV-associated diseases. In specific embodiments, the pharmaceutical composition is formulated as a vaccine that elicits protective humoral and cellular immune responses against PCV-2.

In another aspect, the invention discloses a method for producing PCV PsVs in planta, wherein a first polynucleotide encoding the PCV capsid polypeptide and a second polynucleotide encoding a replicating cassette—comprising a geminiviral origin of replication, a gene encoding a replication regulatory protein (e.g., Rep/RepA), and a heterologous gene of interest—are co-delivered into plant cells via Agrobacterium-mediated transient expression. The replication regulatory protein initiates rolling-circle replication of the amplicon, releasing single-stranded DNA that is packaged into capsids formed by the PCV polypeptide. The resulting PsVs are purified by density gradient centrifugation and retain biological activity upon delivery to mammalian cells.

The invention also contemplates embodiments wherein the heterologous polypeptide is a reporter such as yellow fluorescent protein (YFP), enabling visual or quantitative assessment of PsV infectivity. Alternatively, the heterologous sequence may encode an immunomodulatory protein, a multivalent antigen, or a therapeutic peptide, expanding the utility of the PsV platform beyond diagnostics and vaccination.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the production of porcine circovirus pseudovirions (PsVs) in plant cells and their use in diagnostics, vaccination, and functional assays. Reference is made to the accompanying drawings, which form part of this specification, though the scope of the invention is not limited to the illustrated embodiments. As used herein, the singular forms “a,” “an,” and “the” include plural referents unless the context clearly dictates otherwise. The terminology employed is intended to describe particular embodiments and should not be construed as limiting the invention.

Porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) is a pathogen of significant veterinary importance, causing a spectrum of diseases collectively known as PCAD. Immune responses against PCV-2 are primarily directed toward the capsid (CP) protein, which forms the viral shell and contains key neutralizing epitopes. Traditional vaccine approaches rely on recombinant CP expressed in microbial or insect systems, but these face limitations in cost, scalability, and safety. The present invention overcomes these by leveraging plant-based transient expression to produce PCV PsVs—non-infectious particles that mimic native virions but carry engineered genetic payloads.

Unlike prior art methods that produce empty VLPs or require in vitro assembly, the disclosed approach enables in planta formation of PsVs through coordinated expression of the CP and a replicating DNA amplicon. This is achieved using a geminivirus-derived replicating vector system, such as one based on bean yellow dwarf virus (BeYDV), which supports high-level amplification of single-stranded DNA in plant nuclei. The term “circovirus” refers to viruses in the family Circoviridae, characterized by small circular ssDNA genomes and icosahedral capsids. “Virus-like particles” (VLPs) are non-infectious structures that resemble native virions but lack viral genomic material. “Pseudovirions” or “PsVs” are a subclass of VLPs that encapsidate non-viral nucleic acids, conferring functionality such as gene delivery.

The method begins with the introduction into plant cells—preferably Nicotiana benthamiana—of two genetic elements: (1) a first polynucleotide encoding the PCV capsid polypeptide under control of a strong plant promoter (e.g., Cauliflower Mosaic Virus 35S promoter in the pEAQ-HT vector), and (2) a second polynucleotide comprising a replicating cassette. This cassette includes a geminiviral origin of replication, a gene encoding a replication initiation protein (Rep/RepA), and a heterologous sequence flanked by replication termination signals. Upon expression, Rep/RepA nicks the origin and initiates rolling-circle replication, generating concatemeric ssDNA that is cleaved and ligated to release unit-length amplicons. These ssDNA molecules are then encapsidated by self-assembling CP subunits to form PsVs.

The heterologous polynucleotide may encode any protein of interest, but in preferred embodiments, it is a reporter such as YFP, luciferase, or β-galactosidase, enabling straightforward detection of PsV-mediated transduction in mammalian cells. The amplicon may be derived from synthetic constructs, viral genomes, or cDNA libraries, provided it lacks sequences that interfere with replication or packaging.

Following agroinfiltration and incubation (typically 3–7 days), plant tissue is homogenized, and crude extracts are clarified by centrifugation and filtration. PsVs are purified via sucrose or cesium chloride density gradient centrifugation, yielding particles with buoyant densities around 1.56 g/cm³ and diameters of 12–24 nm, as confirmed by transmission electron microscopy. The purified PsVs are stable, non-toxic, and capable of delivering their genetic cargo to permissive mammalian cells, where reporter expression confirms successful transduction.

This system contrasts with indirect methods that require separate production and in vitro reassembly of capsids and nucleic acids. By enabling co-expression and co-assembly in planta, the invention streamlines production and enhances particle authenticity. The inclusion of Rep/RepA ensures high-yield amplicon synthesis, while plant-specific codon optimization of the CP gene maximizes expression.

The resulting PsVs are integral to a neutralization assay: when mixed with serum containing anti-PCV antibodies, neutralization occurs, reducing reporter signal in subsequently infected cells. This assay provides a sensitive, quantitative measure of neutralizing antibody titers, useful for vaccine evaluation and serosurveillance.

Plant-based production offers numerous benefits: low cost, scalability, absence of animal pathogens, and compatibility with existing agricultural infrastructure. The method requires no affinity tags or chromatography, relying instead on simple centrifugation for purification. Yields of up to 6.5 mg CP per kg leaf tissue have been achieved, comparable to microbial systems.

### Example 1

To implement the invention, a PCV-2 genome-like plant expression vector was designed by codon-optimizing the ORF2 gene from a Lithuanian isolate (GenBank KJ128269) for Nicotiana benthamiana, increasing GC content, and removing cryptic splice sites and restriction motifs. The synthetic gene was cloned into the pEAQ-HT vector between AgeI and XhoI sites. Separately, a replicating module was constructed containing the BeYDV origin of replication, the Rep/RepA gene, and a YFP reporter gene flanked by hairpin terminators. This entire module was inserted into a compatible plant expression backbone. Both constructs were transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404 via electroporation, and transformants were screened by PCR using vector-specific primers.

Agrobacterium cultures were grown in LB broth with appropriate antibiotics, induced with acetosyringone, and adjusted to OD600 = 1.0. Six- to eight-week-old N. benthamiana plants were vacuum-infiltrated with the bacterial suspension. At 4 days post-infiltration, leaves were harvested, homogenized in DB150 buffer with protease inhibitors, and clarified by centrifugation at 8000 × g. The supernatant was filtered through Miracloth and layered onto a sucrose step gradient (45%/65%). After ultracentrifugation at 120,000 × g for 4 hours, the pellet was resuspended in PBS. Fractions and pellets were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and immunoblotting using anti-PCV-2 serum, revealing a 27 kDa CP band and a 54 kDa dimer.

For further purification, the pellet was subjected to CsCl density gradient centrifugation, yielding a visible band at ~1.56 g/cm³. Total DNA was isolated from plant tissue and analyzed by rolling circle amplification followed by PstI digestion and gel electrophoresis, confirming amplicon formation. Quantitative PCR demonstrated high copy numbers of the YFP-containing amplicon.

Mammalian cells (e.g., PK-15) were maintained in standard culture conditions. To test PsV functionality, cells were exposed to purified plant extracts. Fluorescence microscopy revealed YFP expression, confirming successful delivery and expression. SDS-PAGE and immunoblotting verified CP identity, while transmission electron microscopy showed 16 nm particles consistent with PCV-2 VLPs. Co-infiltration of plants with PCV-2 CP and the replicating YFP module yielded PsVs that were purified and shown to transduce mammalian cells efficiently, validating the platform.

### Discussion

The invention leverages a geminivirus-based replicating vector to drive high-level synthesis of ssDNA amplicons in plant nuclei via rolling-circle replication. This mechanism ensures abundant template for encapsidation. The successful cloning of YFP into the expression cassette and its functional delivery to mammalian cells demonstrates the versatility of the system. Co-infiltration with PCV-2 CP results in self-assembly of capsids around the amplicon, forming PsVs that are structurally authentic and biologically active. Electron microscopy confirms particle formation, while neutralization assays validate their utility in serological testing. This plant-based PsV platform represents a scalable, safe, and cost-effective alternative to conventional vaccine and diagnostic reagent production systems.
