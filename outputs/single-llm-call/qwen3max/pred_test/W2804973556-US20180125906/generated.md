# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutics for ischemic conditions, particularly those affecting the cardiovascular system. More specifically, the invention provides a novel method and composition for treating ischemic heart disease and preventing or mitigating ischemia-reperfusion injury through the administration of a defined carbohydrate composition extracted from Panax ginseng C.A. Meyer. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations comprising this carbohydrate composition, methods for its preparation, and its use in protecting cardiomyocytes and other cells from hypoxia and reoxygenation-induced damage. The disclosed carbohydrate composition represents a previously underappreciated bioactive fraction of ginseng that demonstrates significant cardioprotective effects by modulating key cellular signaling pathways and preserving mitochondrial function during ischemic stress.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ischemic heart disease (IHD) remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, responsible for approximately 7.4 million deaths annually as reported by the World Health Organization in 2014. IHD arises primarily from the occlusion of coronary arteries, which deprives myocardial tissue of essential oxygen and nutrients, ultimately leading to cardiomyocyte death and impaired cardiac function. While timely reperfusion through interventions such as percutaneous coronary intervention or thrombolytic therapy can salvage ischemic myocardium and reduce short-term mortality, the reintroduction of blood flow paradoxically induces additional injury known as ischemia-reperfusion (I/R) injury. This phenomenon involves a cascade of pathological events including oxidative stress, calcium overload, inflammation, and activation of cell death pathways, which collectively contribute to adverse cardiac remodeling and long-term morbidity.

Current treatment options for IHD include antiplatelet agents, beta-blockers, angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, statins, and revascularization procedures. Despite these advances, a significant proportion of patients continue to experience progressive heart failure or recurrent ischemic events, underscoring the limitations of existing therapeutic strategies. Moreover, pharmacological agents aimed at directly mitigating I/R injury have largely failed in clinical trials due to issues related to efficacy, safety, or narrow therapeutic windows. Consequently, there remains an urgent unmet medical need for novel, safe, and effective interventions that can protect the myocardium during both the ischemic phase and subsequent reperfusion.

Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) offers a rich source of potential therapeutic agents with centuries of empirical use in managing cardiovascular disorders. Within the TCM framework, food and medicine are considered to share common origins, with many botanicals serving dual roles as both dietary supplements and medicinal remedies. Ginseng (Panax ginseng C.A. Meyer), one of the most revered herbs in TCM, has been used for over two millennia to enhance vitality and treat various ailments, including cardiovascular conditions. Modern research has identified ginsenosides—triterpenoid saponins—as the primary bioactive constituents responsible for many of ginseng’s pharmacological effects. However, in contemporary industrial processing of ginseng extracts, polysaccharides are often discarded as impurities to reduce dosage volume, despite growing evidence that they possess immunomodulatory, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory properties. Notably, polysaccharides from other medicinal plants such as Aralia elata, Ophiopogon japonicus, and Salvia miltiorrhiza have demonstrated cardioprotective effects, yet the potential of ginseng-derived polysaccharides in this context has remained unexplored until now. This gap in knowledge motivates the development of new treatment options based on the systematic isolation and characterization of bioactive carbohydrate compositions from ginseng, offering a promising avenue for addressing the persistent challenge of I/R injury.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for treating ischemic conditions, particularly ischemic heart disease, and for preventing or reducing ischemia-reperfusion injury through the administration of a specific carbohydrate composition isolated from Panax ginseng. This carbohydrate composition is obtained through a multi-step extraction and purification process involving the preparation of a crude Panax ginseng extract, followed by fractionation using high-speed counter-current chromatography (HSCCC), stepwise ethanol precipitation, and anion-exchange chromatography. In a preferred embodiment, the crude extract is prepared by contacting dried and cut Panax ginseng plant material with an extraction solvent, typically aqueous ethanol, under conditions of stirring, heating, or sonication, followed by isolation of the extract via filtration or centrifugation. The crude extract is then fractionated, and a carbohydrate-enriched precipitate is obtained by adding water and an aliphatic alcohol such as ethanol to achieve a final concentration of 70% alcohol. This precipitate is subjected to anion-exchange chromatography using a resin such as Toyopearl DEAE 650 M, eluted with increasing concentrations of sodium chloride and sodium hydroxide, and the resulting eluate is concentrated and lyophilized to yield the active carbohydrate composition, designated herein as AP1.

The invention further provides a method for protecting cells, particularly cardiomyocytes, against hypoxia and reoxygenation-induced cell death by contacting the cells with an effective amount of the carbohydrate composition. This protective effect is mediated through multiple mechanisms, including the reduction of oxidative stress, inhibition of mitochondrial dysfunction, suppression of apoptosis, and activation of pro-survival signaling pathways. The carbohydrate composition is formulated into a pharmaceutical formulation suitable for systemic administration, comprising the active ingredient in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients such as fillers, binders, disintegrants, and stabilizers. The formulation may be presented in various dosage forms including tablets, capsules, injectables, or oral solutions.

The use of the carbohydrate composition as a medicament for the treatment of ischemic conditions, or for the preparation of a medicament for such use, is also encompassed by the invention. Additionally, the composition may be employed specifically for protecting cells against hypoxia and reoxygenation-induced cell death in vitro or in vivo. The benefits of the carbohydrate composition include its natural origin, low cytotoxicity, potent cardioprotective activity at low concentrations, and multimodal mechanism of action targeting both oxidative stress and mitochondrial integrity. In summary, the invention discloses a novel, efficacious, and safe therapeutic agent derived from a traditionally used herb, offering a significant advancement in the management of ischemic heart disease and associated reperfusion injury.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

For the purposes of this invention, the term “comprising” is to be interpreted as open-ended, allowing for the inclusion of additional elements or steps not explicitly recited. The phrase “essentially consisting of” limits the scope to the specified materials or steps and those that do not materially affect the basic and novel characteristics of the claimed subject matter. The expression “consisting of” excludes any element, step, or ingredient not specified in the claim. The invention provides a method for treating an ischemic condition in a subject, wherein the subject is a human or non-human animal, preferably a mammal, and more preferably a human patient suffering from or at risk of ischemic heart disease. The term “ischemic condition” refers to a pathological state characterized by inadequate blood supply to an organ or tissue, resulting in oxygen and nutrient deprivation; in a preferred embodiment, the ischemic condition affects the heart, manifesting as myocardial infarction, angina pectoris, or other forms of coronary artery disease. The invention also encompasses a method for preventing or reducing ischemia-reperfusion injury, which is defined as the tissue damage that occurs when blood flow is restored to previously ischemic tissue, leading to oxidative stress, inflammation, and cell death.

The active agent of the invention is a carbohydrate composition extracted from Panax ginseng C.A. Meyer. As used herein, “carbohydrate composition” refers to a complex mixture predominantly composed of carbohydrates, optionally containing minor amounts of protein and other biomolecules. The term “carbohydrates” includes monosaccharides, oligosaccharides, and polysaccharides. Polysaccharides are high-molecular-weight polymers of monosaccharide units linked by glycosidic bonds. In a preferred embodiment, the carbohydrate composition is an acidic heteroglycan comprising glucose (Glc), galactose (Gal), galacturonic acid (Gal A), arabinose (Ara), and glucuronic acid (Glc A). The molecular weight of the polysaccharide component is approximately 38,000 Daltons, as determined by high-performance gel permeation chromatography (HPGPC). The carbohydrate portion constitutes at least 90% by weight of the composition, with uronic acid content of about 17.93% and protein content of about 9.85%, as measured by the m-hydroxydiphenyl method and Bradford assay, respectively.

The carbohydrate composition is prepared by a method comprising: (i) providing a crude Panax ginseng extract by drying and cutting ginseng roots, contacting the plant material with an extraction solvent such as 70% aqueous ethanol, subjecting the mixture to stirring, heating, or sonication for several hours, and isolating the extract by filtration or centrifugation; and (ii) isolating the carbohydrate composition from the crude extract by fractionating the extract via HSCCC, performing stepwise ethanol precipitation to obtain a 70% ethanol precipitate, subjecting this precipitate to anion-exchange chromatography on a DEAE resin, eluting with a gradient of NaCl and NaOH, and concentrating the active eluate. The resulting composition is administered in an effective amount, typically ranging from 10 to 500 mg per day for an adult human, formulated with excipients such as microcrystalline cellulose, lactose, magnesium stearate, and hydroxypropyl methylcellulose into tablets, capsules, or injectable solutions. The pharmaceutical formulation is manufactured by standard techniques including blending, granulation, compression, or lyophilization. The composition is used as a medicament for protecting cardiomyocytes against hypoxia/reoxygenation injury by contacting cells with 12.5 to 200 μg/mL of the composition for at least one hour prior to and during hypoxic insult, thereby preserving mitochondrial membrane potential, reducing reactive oxygen species, inhibiting caspase activation, and enhancing cell viability through modulation of the RISK (Reperfusion Injury Salvage Kinase) pathway involving PI3K/Akt, ERK1/2, eNOS, and nitric oxide production.

## EXAMPLES

Standard laboratory reagents and analytical-grade solvents were used throughout. Deionized water was prepared using a Millipore MILLI Q-Plus system. Ethanol, methanol, chloroform, and other organic solvents were of ACS grade. Cell culture media and sera were purchased from Gibco. All chromatographic resins and analytical standards were obtained from Sigma-Aldrich unless otherwise specified.

### Example 1

Dried roots of Panax ginseng C.A. Meyer, certified to meet the Hong Kong Standard of Chinese Materia Medica, were cut into small pieces and extracted with 70% aqueous ethanol under reflux for 3 hours. The extract was filtered, concentrated under reduced pressure, and lyophilized to yield a crude ginseng extract (RSE). The RSE was standardized by HPLC fingerprinting and quantified for major ginsenosides. For bioassay-guided fractionation, 4.12 g of RSE was dissolved in a biphasic solvent system (butanol-water, 1:1) and separated by high-speed counter-current chromatography (HSCCC) using a TBE-1000A instrument. Fractions were collected every 5 minutes and combined based on TLC profiles into nine pooled fractions. These were tested for cardioprotective activity in H9c2 cardiomyocytes subjected to hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R). Fractions 2 and 3 showed the highest activity and were combined (0.74 g). This mixture was dissolved in water and subjected to stepwise ethanol precipitation at 90%, 80%, and 70% ethanol. The 70% ethanol precipitate exhibited the strongest activity and was further purified by anion-exchange chromatography on Toyopearl DEAE 650 M, eluted sequentially with water, 0.5 M NaCl, 1.0 M NaCl, 2.0 M NaCl, and 0.2 M NaOH, yielding five fractions: NP, AP1, AP2, AP3, and AP4. AP1, the most active fraction, was dialyzed, lyophilized, and used for subsequent studies. In an alternative embodiment, the HSCCC step was omitted, and the crude extract was directly subjected to ethanol precipitation and anion-exchange chromatography, though with lower purity and yield.

### Example 2

The chemical characterization of AP1 revealed a total sugar content of 90.28% by phenol-sulfuric acid assay, uronic acid content of 17.93% by m-hydroxydiphenyl method, and protein content of 9.85% by Bradford assay. HPGPC analysis estimated an apparent molecular weight of 38,000 Da. Monosaccharide composition by HPLC-PMP derivatization showed Glc (76.31%), Gal (12.70%), Gal A (5.64%), Ara (3.72%), and Glc A (1.64%). Pharmacological characterization was performed in H9c2 rat cardiomyoblasts cultured in DMEM with 10% FBS. Cells were pretreated with AP1 (6.25–800 μg/mL) for 1 hour, then subjected to 3 hours of hypoxia (95% N2/5% CO2) followed by 4 hours of reoxygenation. Cytotoxicity was assessed by mitochondrial viability stain (MVS) assay; AP1 showed no toxicity up to 800 μg/mL. AP1 (200 μg/mL) significantly improved cell viability, reduced LDH and CK release, and decreased apoptosis as measured by Annexin V/PI staining and caspase-3/7 and -9 activities. Mechanistically, AP1 suppressed intracellular and mitochondrial ROS, reduced MDA levels, enhanced SOD activity, inhibited JNK/p38 phosphorylation, preserved mitochondrial membrane potential, increased ATP levels, and enhanced oxygen consumption rate. Western blotting showed AP1 activated the RISK pathway by upregulating GR and ER expression, enhancing phosphorylation of PI3K, Akt, ERK1/2, and eNOS, while suppressing iNOS. The protective effect was abolished by inhibitors of GR (mifepristone), ER (tamoxifen), PI3K (LY294002), Akt (Akt inhibitor IV), ERK1/2 (U0126), and eNOS (L-NAME), confirming pathway dependence. Nitric oxide production, measured by Griess assay, was increased by AP1 in a RISK-dependent manner. These results demonstrate that AP1 exerts potent cardioprotection via receptor-mediated activation of pro-survival kinases and preservation of mitochondrial bioenergetics.
