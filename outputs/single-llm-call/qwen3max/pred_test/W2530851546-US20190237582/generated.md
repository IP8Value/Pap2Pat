# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to magnesium zinc oxide based high voltage thin film transistors. More particularly, the invention pertains to high voltage thin film transistors (HVTFTs) fabricated on transparent glass substrates using a magnesium-doped zinc oxide (MZO) channel layer, optionally incorporating a modulation-doped transition layer to enhance interfacial properties, thermal stability, and high voltage blocking capability. These devices are specifically engineered for integration into building-integrated photovoltaic (BIPV) systems, including photovoltaic smart-on-glass (PV-SOG) architectures, where transparency, reliability under high bias conditions, and compatibility with large-area fabrication processes are essential. The invention further encompasses novel structural designs, such as ring-shaped gate geometries, and optimized material compositions that collectively enable stable operation at drain voltages exceeding 200 volts and blocking voltages surpassing 600 volts, thereby addressing critical limitations of conventional thin-film transistor technologies in high-voltage power electronics applications.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Solar energy represents one of the most promising sustainable energy sources due to its abundance, renewability, and minimal environmental impact during operation. Conventional photovoltaic (PV) systems typically employ centralized inverters to convert direct current (DC) generated by solar panels into alternating current (AC) suitable for grid integration or local consumption. However, these systems suffer from several limitations, including significant power losses due to mismatch among panels, reduced efficiency under partial shading, and complex wiring requirements. To overcome these drawbacks, micro-inverter technology has emerged, wherein each solar panel is equipped with its own dedicated inverter, enabling independent maximum power point tracking and enhanced system reliability. Despite these advantages, conventional micro-inverters are often bulky, expensive, and incompatible with emerging architectural trends that demand seamless integration of energy generation into building materials.

Building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV) offer a compelling solution by embedding PV functionality directly into structural elements such as windows, facades, and roofs. A particularly attractive BIPV configuration involves transparent solar PV systems integrated onto glass substrates—commonly referred to as photovoltaic smart-on-glass (PV-SOG)—which maintain optical clarity while generating electricity. Such systems require not only transparent active layers but also transparent or minimally obstructive power electronics, including high-voltage switching components capable of operating reliably under elevated electrical stresses.

Conventional wide-bandgap semiconductor transistors based on silicon carbide (SiC) or gallium nitride (GaN) exhibit excellent high-voltage performance but are prohibitively expensive, difficult to fabricate on large-area glass substrates, and inherently opaque, rendering them unsuitable for transparent BIPV applications. Alternative thin-film transistor (TFT) technologies have been explored, including those based on amorphous silicon (a-Si) and polycrystalline silicon (poly-Si). However, a-Si TFTs suffer from low carrier mobility and poor stability under high-field stress, while poly-Si TFTs require high-temperature processing incompatible with standard soda-lime glass. Organic TFTs offer mechanical flexibility and low-temperature processing but lack the necessary breakdown voltage and thermal stability for power conversion applications. Indium gallium zinc oxide (IGZO) TFTs provide improved mobility and transparency but still exhibit limited blocking voltage (<200 V) and degrade under prolonged high-bias operation, especially at elevated temperatures encountered in outdoor PV environments.

Magnesium zinc oxide (MZO), a ternary alloy of ZnO and MgO, presents a promising channel material due to its tunable bandgap, high transparency, and potential for low-temperature deposition. However, prior art MZO-based TFTs have failed to achieve the combination of high blocking voltage, thermal stability, low off-current, and manufacturability on glass required for practical PV-SOG inverters. Critical challenges include interfacial diffusion between the channel and gate dielectric, high trap densities leading to threshold voltage instability, and electric field crowding in conventional rectangular device geometries. Thus, there remains a pressing need for an HVTFT architecture that overcomes these limitations through synergistic innovations in material composition, interface engineering, and device geometry—all compatible with scalable, low-cost fabrication on transparent substrates.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a magnesium zinc oxide high voltage thin film transistor (MZO-HVTFT) specifically designed for integration into transparent photovoltaic systems on glass. The MZO-HVTFT comprises a bottom-gate inverted-staggered structure fabricated on a transparent substrate, featuring a channel layer composed of MgₓZn₁₋ₓO (where x is approximately 0.03), which enhances thermal stability and reduces bulk trap density compared to pure ZnO. A key innovation is the incorporation of a passivation layer, typically a photoresist film, applied over the channel to mitigate ambient-induced electrical drift during operation. The Mg composition in the MZO channel is precisely tailored—not only to widen the bandgap slightly but more importantly to strengthen metal-oxygen bonding, thereby suppressing oxygen vacancy formation and improving operational stability under thermal stress.

To address electric field non-uniformity inherent in conventional planar TFTs, the invention employs a circular-symmetric, ring-shaped gate design. This geometry eliminates sharp corners that cause field crowding, resulting in a significantly more uniform lateral electric field distribution from drain to source and enabling higher blocking voltages. Furthermore, the device incorporates carefully engineered offset distances between the gate and both the source and drain electrodes; these offsets—typically 3 μm to the source and 5–20 μm to the drain depending on the target blocking voltage—help distribute the high electric field away from the channel edges, reducing leakage and preventing premature breakdown.

An essential aspect of the invention is the interface modification achieved through a modulation-doped transition layer (MZO-TL) inserted between the SiO₂ gate dielectric and the MZO channel in the preferred embodiment (termed m-MZO-HVTFT). This ultra-thin (~10 nm) layer features a graded Mg composition, decreasing continuously from y = 1 (pure MgO) adjacent to SiO₂ to y = 0.03 (matching the channel) at the interface with the MZO layer. This gradient serves dual purposes: it acts as a diffusion barrier to prevent intermixing of Zn and Si across the interface, and it modulates the band alignment to reduce interface trap states. The thickness of this transition layer is optimized to balance diffusion suppression against parasitic capacitance effects, with 10 nm found to provide maximal performance enhancement without compromising switching speed.

The substrate may be selected from commercially available transparent glasses, including soda-lime or borosilicate variants, with thicknesses ranging from 0.3 to 1.0 mm, enabling compatibility with standard architectural glazing. Application articles encompass PV-SOG modules, self-powered smart windows with integrated power management, high-voltage drivers for digital radiology detectors, and portable electronic systems requiring robust, transparent power switches. The method of manufacturing involves low-temperature processes (<450°C), including sputtering for gate metallization, plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) for SiO₂ dielectric formation, and metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) for precise MZO and MZO-TL growth, ensuring scalability and compatibility with roll-to-roll or large-area batch processing.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a magnesium zinc oxide high voltage thin film transistor (MZO-HVTFT) fabricated directly on glass, offering unprecedented performance for transparent power electronics. The primary advantage of the MZO-HVTFT lies in its ability to combine high transparency, thermal stability up to 367 K, blocking voltages exceeding 600 V, and on/off current ratios greater than 10⁷—all achieved through synergistic material, interface, and geometric innovations. The scope of the invention includes not only the device structure but also its method of manufacture and integration into functional systems such as building-integrated photovoltaics. As used herein, the articles “a” and “an” are intended to mean one or more unless the context clearly indicates otherwise. The term “about” signifies a range of ±10% of the stated value unless otherwise specified by experimental tolerance.

The MZO-HVTFT adopts a bottom-gate inverted-staggered architecture. The dielectric insulating layer is preferably silicon dioxide (SiO₂), deposited via PECVD to a thickness of approximately 200 nm, though alternative high-k dielectrics such as Al₂O₃ or HfO₂ may be used in modified embodiments. The channel layer consists of Mg₀.₀₃Zn₀.₉₇O with a nominal thickness of 50 nm, deposited by MOCVD using diethylzinc and bis(methylcyclopentadienyl)magnesium precursors. The incorporation of magnesium into the ZnO lattice strengthens the metal-oxygen bonds, reducing oxygen vacancy concentration and thereby lowering bulk trap density, which directly improves subthreshold swing and thermal stability.

In the advanced m-MZO-HVTFT variant, a modulation-doped transition layer (MZO-TL) of MgyZn₁₋yO, 10 nm thick, is interposed between the SiO₂ and the MZO channel. The Mg fraction y decreases linearly from 1.0 at the SiO₂ interface to 0.03 at the channel interface. This graded layer functions as a diffusion barrier, as confirmed by TEM/EDS and XPS analyses showing suppressed Zn out-diffusion and Si in-diffusion. Electrodes comprise a Cr gate (50 nm), and Ti/Au source/drain stacks (100/50 nm), patterned via lift-off or etching. The ring-shaped gate design—circular or polygonal with rounded corners—ensures uniform electric field distribution, eliminating hot spots that trigger early breakdown in rectangular designs. Offset distances are critical: gate-to-source is fixed at 3 μm to maintain drive capability, while gate-to-drain is varied (5–20 μm) to trade off blocking voltage against on-current.

Substrates are transparent glasses, including Corning Eagle XG or standard soda-lime, with thicknesses of 0.4 mm typical. Applications span PV-SOG inverters, where the HVTFT converts DC from transparent solar cells to AC; self-powered smart glass that harvests light and controls tinting autonomously; high-voltage switches in medical imaging; and ruggedized portable electronics. The manufacturing method involves sequential deposition and patterning: sputter Cr gate, PECVD SiO₂, MOCVD MZO (with or without MZO-TL), e-beam evaporate Ti/Au, and coat photoresist passivation. All steps occur below 450°C, preserving substrate integrity.

### Material Preparation and Device Fabrication Process

The HVTFT fabrication begins with cleaning a 0.4 mm thick commercial glass substrate. A 50 nm chromium layer is sputtered and patterned via dry etching to form the bottom gate electrode. A 200 nm SiO₂ gate dielectric is then deposited by PECVD at 300°C. The channel is grown by MOCVD at 400°C using diethylzinc and bis(methylcyclopentadienyl)magnesium to form either pure ZnO, MZO (Mg₀.₀₃Zn₀.₉₇O), or m-MZO with a 10 nm graded MgyZn₁₋yO transition layer. Source and drain contacts are defined by electron-beam evaporation of 100 nm Ti and 50 nm Au, followed by lift-off. A photoresist passivation layer is spin-coated to protect the channel. Devices are fabricated with channel lengths of 10, 15, or 25 μm and corresponding gate-to-drain offsets of 5, 10, or 20 μm, while gate-to-source offset remains 3 μm. The final device structure thus integrates optimized materials, geometry, and interfaces in a manufacturable process flow.

### Device Testing Conditions

Electrical characterization employs two systems. Low-bias transfer/output curves use an HP-4156C semiconductor parameter analyzer with an HP-41501B pulse generator, extending voltage compliance to 200 V and current resolution to 1 fA. High-voltage blocking tests utilize a Tektronix 370 curve tracer with a probe station, limited to 1 μA resolution but capable of >600 V sweeps. To prevent arcing, devices are immersed in Fluorinert FC-40 during high-voltage testing. Temperature-dependent measurements use an Agilent 1500B thermal chuck in a light-tight probe station, spanning 294 K to 367 K. All tests ensure reliable assessment of high-field performance under realistic operating conditions.

### Material Characterizations

Material analysis employs cross-sectional TEM on FIB-prepared samples using a JEOL 2100F microscope to examine interface abruptness. EDS mapping identifies elemental interdiffusion across the channel/dielectric interface. XPS depth profiling, performed with in-situ Ar⁺ sputtering, quantifies atomic concentrations of Si, Zn, and Mg as a function of depth, revealing diffusion barriers’ efficacy. These techniques collectively validate the suppression of Zn/Si intermixing in m-MZO devices and confirm the graded composition of the MZO-TL.

### Device Design

The ring structure replaces conventional rectangles to eliminate field crowding. Simulations show peak electric fields in ring devices are ~50% lower than in rectangular counterparts. The circular symmetry ensures uniform lateral field distribution along the entire channel perimeter, delaying avalanche breakdown. Rectangular designs concentrate fields at corners, initiating leakage at lower voltages. Thus, the ring geometry is fundamental to achieving >600 V blocking capability.

### Transfer Characteristics and Thermal Stability

MZO-HVTFTs exhibit superior transfer characteristics versus ZnO: steeper subthreshold swing (SS ≈ 0.8 V/dec vs. 1.2 V/dec) and higher on-current. Thermal stability is markedly improved: ΔVth = −6 V for MZO vs. −10.5 V for ZnO when heated from 294 K to 367 K. Activation energy analysis reveals bulk trap density reduction in MZO due to stronger Mg–O bonds. The m-MZO variant further enhances performance: SS ≈ 0.5 V/dec, on-current increased tenfold, and activation energy slope of 0.246 eV/V indicating 40% lower total trap density than MZO. TEM and XPS confirm the MZO-TL creates an abrupt, diffusion-free interface, directly linking interface engineering to electrical improvements.

### High Voltage Blocking Capability

m-MZO-HVTFTs demonstrate exceptional blocking: 609 V at L = 25 μm with on/off ratio >10⁸. Drain leakage remains <1 pA up to 200 V. Longer channels increase blocking voltage but reduce on-current, establishing a design trade-off. Output characteristics show good saturation at low VGS, with a kink effect at high VGS attributed to self-heating. All devices maintain stable VOFF and off-current up to their maximum operating drain bias (70–200 V). The MZO-TL’s role is critical: by blocking Zn²⁺ diffusion into SiO₂, it prevents positive fixed charges that would otherwise increase field crowding. SILVACO simulations confirm reduced peak electric field (1,410 kV/cm vs. 1,520 kV/cm) in m-MZO devices, directly enabling higher breakdown voltage.

### Statistical Data of Electrical Performance

Statistical analysis across multiple m-MZO-HVTFTs confirms reproducibility: threshold voltage variation < ±1.5 V, on/off ratio consistently >10⁷, and blocking voltage within ±15 V of nominal values for a given channel length. Yield exceeds 90% on 4-inch glass substrates, demonstrating manufacturability.

### Simulation of Electrical Field Distribution

SILVACO simulations compare ring and rectangular geometries: rings exhibit uniform field distribution, while rectangles show severe crowding at corners (>2× average field). In MZO vs. m-MZO, the latter shows lower peak fields due to reduced interface charge. Gate connection topology also influences field: direct radial connections minimize parasitic resistance and field distortion. Path 1 (under gate overlap) and Path 2 (through offset region) analyses reveal that field in Path 2 dominates breakdown; optimizing offset length spreads this field. Defined parameters include Vth (gate voltage at ID = 1 nA·W/L), VOFF (voltage where ID drops to 1 pA), SS (dVGS/d(log ID)), Ion (ID at VGS = VDS = 10 V), Ion/Ioff ratio, and blocking voltage (VDS where ID surges >1 nA). These metrics collectively validate the invention’s superiority for high-voltage transparent electronics.
