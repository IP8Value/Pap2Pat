# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of digital advertising and, more specifically, to real-time bidding systems in programmatic advertising environments. The invention relates to methods and systems for adaptive, risk-aware bid optimization that dynamically adjust bid prices in response to uncertainties in user response prediction and evolving operational states of a demand side platform (DSP). The technical domain encompasses computational advertising, machine learning, reinforcement learning, stochastic decision-making under resource constraints, and online auction mechanisms, particularly within second-price real-time bidding (RTB) ecosystems. The invention further intersects with data-driven decision support systems that integrate probabilistic modeling, risk management theory, and sequential resource allocation to enhance the performance of digital advertising campaigns under budgetary and competitive constraints.

## BACKGROUND

Real-time bidding has emerged as a dominant mechanism in programmatic advertising, enabling advertisers to purchase individual ad impressions through instantaneous auctions triggered by user activity. In this environment, demand side platforms act on behalf of advertisers to evaluate bid requests and submit competitive bids within milliseconds. The effectiveness of a DSP hinges critically on its ability to accurately predict user responses—such as click-through rates or conversion probabilities—and to translate those predictions into optimal bid prices that maximize campaign key performance indicators while adhering to budget constraints. Traditional approaches have treated bid optimization as a two-stage process: first estimating the expected value of an impression, then determining a bid price based on that estimate. However, these methods often assume that prediction models yield precise point estimates, neglecting the inherent uncertainty arising from incomplete data, feature sparsity, and environmental noise. This oversight can lead to suboptimal bidding decisions, especially when prediction confidence varies significantly across impressions.

Current bidding platforms suffer from several limitations that impair their adaptability and efficiency. Many rely on static linear bidding strategies that scale predicted values by fixed coefficients, failing to account for the dynamic interplay between remaining budget, time horizon, and market competition. Others employ reinforcement learning frameworks that model the bidding process as a Markov decision process but still operate under the unrealistic assumption that predicted impression values are deterministic. These approaches do not explicitly incorporate the statistical uncertainty of predictions into the decision-making process, nor do they adjust bidding aggressiveness based on the DSP’s current resource state. Consequently, they may overbid on low-confidence impressions when budgets are tight or underbid on high-potential opportunities when resources are abundant. Furthermore, existing systems lack mechanisms to dynamically calibrate risk preferences in response to changing conditions, leading to inefficient budget consumption and missed performance targets.

There is a clear need for an improved bid optimization framework that holistically addresses prediction uncertainty, resource dynamics, and strategic risk-taking. Such a system must be capable of adjusting its valuation of ad impressions not only based on expected user response but also on the reliability of that expectation and the DSP’s capacity to absorb potential losses or capitalize on gains. The integration of risk-aware decision-making with real-time resource monitoring represents a critical advancement in computational advertising, enabling more robust, efficient, and adaptive campaign execution in highly volatile auction environments.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method and system for service-level agreement monitoring and adaptive bid optimization in a real-time bidding environment. The method includes operating a demand side platform (DSP) that continuously monitors its current state, which comprises at least a remaining budget and a number of remaining auctions in a campaign episode. Upon receiving a bid request from an ad exchange, the DSP performs a user response prediction to generate both a mean predicted value (e.g., click-through rate) and a measure of uncertainty (e.g., standard deviation) associated with that prediction. The system then determines a risk tendency value that reflects the DSP’s willingness to take risk given its current resource state, where the risk tendency is a function of the remaining budget and remaining auction count but independent of the specific bid request features. Using this risk tendency and the prediction uncertainty, the system computes an adjusted value of the advertisement impression as a linear combination of the mean prediction and the scaled uncertainty term. Based on this adjusted value, the system calculates an optimal bid price that maximizes the value-at-risk of cumulative campaign performance under budget constraints. The calculated bid price is transmitted to the ad exchange, and upon receiving the auction result—whether win or loss—the DSP updates its current state accordingly, including decrementing the remaining auction count and, if applicable, deducting the clearing price from the remaining budget.

In one embodiment, the DSP is implemented as a software module executing on a server equipped with processing circuitry, memory, and network communication capabilities. The DSP embodies the aforementioned method through executable instructions stored in a non-transitory computer-readable medium, which, when executed by a processor, cause the system to perform the steps of state monitoring, bid request processing, uncertainty-aware valuation, risk-tendency-based adjustment, bid price determination, and state update. The invention further encompasses a non-transitory computer-readable medium encoded with instructions for carrying out the described method, thereby enabling deployment across distributed computing infrastructures used in modern digital advertising ecosystems.

The operation of the system follows process 400: upon receiving a bid request from the ad exchange, the DSP performs an initial prediction of user response metrics, yielding both a mean and a standard deviation. It then adjusts the predicted value to account for uncertainty by incorporating a risk tendency parameter. The risk tendency is determined based on the DSP’s current state—specifically, remaining budget and remaining auctions—and reflects whether the DSP should act aggressively or conservatively. The adjusted impression value is used to compute a bid price that optimizes long-term campaign performance under risk constraints, which is then submitted to the exchange.

Process 500 details a refined implementation: the system calculates the mean predicted click-through-rate (pCTR) and its standard deviation using a Bayesian logistic regression model. The risk tendency of a rational DSP is modeled as a function β(t, b) that satisfies three properties: (i) its sign depends on budget sufficiency, (ii) it is monotonically increasing in budget and decreasing in remaining auctions, and (iii) it approximates a function of the budget-to-auction ratio in early campaign stages. A rule-based approach defines β(t, b) using a hyperbolic tangent of a normalized budget richness metric Û, derived from historical market price distributions. Alternatively, a machine-learning-based approach employs a self-supervised reinforcement learning framework to train a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that maps (t, b) to β.

The benefits of this bid optimization framework include improved key performance indicators, reduced network traffic through efficient decision-making, lower latency via streamlined computation, and effective bid shading that avoids overpayment. By modeling risk tendency, the system aligns bidding behavior with strategic objectives under uncertainty. Reinforcement learning enables continuous adaptation without manual tuning.

During a Gaussian exploration stage, the system samples risk tendencies around current estimates to discover better policies. A batch sampler draws experiences uniformly, and the MLP is updated via mean squared error minimization. Episode-level rewards guide the learning process, ensuring that the risk tendency function evolves toward strategies that maximize cumulative clicks.

This closed-loop system continuously adapts to changing conditions, delivering superior campaign performance through principled risk-aware decision-making.
