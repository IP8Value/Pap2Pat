# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD AND INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of molecular pharmacology and signal transduction, specifically relating to the regulation of Src family kinases through interaction with membrane-bound ion transporters, particularly the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. The invention provides novel compositions and methods for modulating Src kinase activity by targeting specific protein–protein interaction domains within the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit. These compositions include peptides derived from defined structural motifs of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, such as the ND1 domain, which have been demonstrated to selectively inhibit or modulate Src kinase activity in a context-dependent manner. The industrial applicability of the invention spans multiple therapeutic areas, including oncology, cardiology, and inflammatory diseases, where aberrant Src signaling contributes to pathological processes. In particular, the invention enables the development of pathway-selective Src inhibitors that avoid the systemic toxicity associated with conventional pan-Src inhibitors by specifically disrupting the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling complex without globally suppressing all Src functions. This targeted approach is especially valuable in conditions such as cardiac hypertrophy, where cardiotonic steroids like ouabain trigger maladaptive signaling through this complex, and in cancers driven by hyperactive Src signaling localized to membrane microdomains. Furthermore, the invention facilitates the creation of diagnostic tools and high-throughput screening assays to identify small molecules that mimic or antagonize the effects of these peptides, thereby accelerating drug discovery efforts aimed at fine-tuning Src activity in disease-relevant contexts.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cardiotonic steroids, historically known for their ability to inhibit the ion-pumping function of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, have recently emerged as important signaling molecules that activate intracellular kinase cascades independent of their effects on ion homeostasis. Among these, ouabain and marinobufagenin are endogenous mammalian cardiotonic steroids that bind to the extracellular domain of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and initiate signal transduction events leading to cell growth, proliferation, and hypertrophy. Central to this signaling mechanism is the Src family of non-receptor tyrosine kinases, which includes Src, Yes, Fyn, and Lyn, among others. These kinases regulate diverse cellular processes, including cell adhesion, migration, survival, and differentiation, and their dysregulation is implicated in numerous cancers and cardiovascular disorders. The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, traditionally viewed solely as an ion pump maintaining electrochemical gradients across the plasma membrane, has been redefined as a scaffold protein that forms a functional signaling complex with Src. In the basal state, the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase binds to and inhibits Src kinase activity through direct protein–protein interactions. Upon binding of cardiotonic steroids such as ouabain, a conformational change in the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase releases Src from this inhibitory interaction, leading to its autophosphorylation at tyrosine 418 and subsequent activation of downstream effectors like ERK1/2. Despite the therapeutic potential of modulating this pathway, current Src inhibitors—such as PP2—are ATP-competitive compounds that lack selectivity for specific Src pools or signaling contexts, resulting in off-target effects and limited clinical utility. Moreover, while cardiotonic steroids themselves can act as agonists of this pathway, their use is constrained by narrow therapeutic windows due to their potent ion-pumping inhibition, which can cause arrhythmias and cytotoxicity. Consequently, there exists a critical need for novel agents that can selectively modulate the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling axis without affecting global Src activity or ion transport. This necessitates the identification of precise molecular interfaces governing this interaction and the development of peptide-based or small-molecule mimetics that can either stabilize or disrupt the complex in a controlled manner. Additionally, robust assays capable of measuring the formation and dissociation of this complex in live cells are essential for screening and optimizing such therapeutics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for regulating Src kinase activity by targeting its interaction with the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit through specific protein domains. More particularly, the invention identifies the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase as a receptor for ouabain-provoked signal transduction, wherein the binding of ouabain induces conformational changes that release Src from inhibitory constraints. The invention further discloses specific interacting sites between the kinase domain and SH2 domain of Src and the cytoplasmic domains of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit, particularly within the large central loop designated CD3. Based on this mapping, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising peptides derived from these interaction domains, which function as either Src inhibitors or activators depending on their mode of action. In one embodiment, the invention provides a Src inhibitor in the form of a peptide derived from the ND1 subdomain of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit, referred to herein as NaKtide, which binds to Src and suppresses its kinase activity. In another embodiment, the invention provides a therapeutic composition comprising a cell-permeable derivative of NaKtide, such as HIV-Tat-conjugated NaKtide (pNaKtide), which effectively antagonizes ouabain-induced signaling in live cells. The invention also encompasses methods for developing small molecules that mimic the structure and function of these peptides to modulate the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex. Additionally, the invention provides a signal transducer comprising the functional domain of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase that mediates Src interaction, as well as isolated compositions containing the ND1 peptide or peptides derived from the Src kinase domain that modulate this interaction. The invention further includes peptides designed to either activate or inhibit the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase-mediated Src signaling pathway, offering dual functionality for research and therapeutic applications. Critically, the invention enables the development of isoform-specific Src inhibitors and/or activators that target only the pool of Src associated with Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, thereby minimizing off-target effects. Finally, the invention provides a rapid screen assay based on fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) or bioluminescence resonance energy transfer (BRET) to quantify the interaction between Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and Src in real time, facilitating high-throughput drug discovery.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

Src and Src family kinases (SFKs) are pivotal regulators of cellular signaling networks, and their dysregulation is a hallmark of many human diseases, particularly cancer. Consequently, significant efforts have been directed toward developing Src inhibitors; however, most existing compounds are broad-spectrum kinase inhibitors that lack spatial and contextual specificity. The present invention addresses this limitation by leveraging the natural regulatory relationship between Src and the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. Specifically, the invention discloses that the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit functions as an endogenous inhibitor of Src under basal conditions by directly binding to its kinase and SH2 domains. This interaction maintains Src in a closed, inactive conformation. Peptide-based inhibitors derived from the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, such as those encompassing the ND1 domain (residues 379–435 of the pig kidney α1 subunit), recapitulate this inhibitory function by competitively binding to Src and preventing its activation. Conversely, peptides derived from the Src kinase domain can be used to disrupt the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src complex, thereby acting as activators in specific experimental contexts. The functional domains responsible for this interaction have been precisely mapped: the CD3 region (residues 350–785) of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit contains the primary Src-binding site, which is further refined to the ND1 segment within the N-domain of CD3. A Src-inhibitory peptide corresponding to ND1, termed NaKtide, has been shown to potently inhibit Src with an IC₅₀ of approximately 70 nM in vitro and exhibits selectivity over other kinases such as Lyn and PKC. When conjugated to cell-penetrating peptides like HIV-Tat, NaKtide (as pNaKtide) becomes membrane-permeable and localizes predominantly to the plasma membrane, where it effectively blocks ouabain-induced Src activation and downstream ERK1/2 signaling. Similarly, Src-activating peptides can be designed to interfere with the inhibitory interface, though such applications are primarily research-oriented. The advantages of peptide inhibitors and activators include their high specificity for the target protein–protein interaction, reduced off-target kinase effects, and modular design that allows for optimization of stability, permeability, and affinity. Looking forward, these peptides serve as templates for the rational design of non-peptidic small molecules that mimic their structural and functional properties, enabling oral bioavailability and improved pharmacokinetics. Furthermore, the invention supports the development of rapid screening assays using FRET/BRET technologies to identify compounds that modulate the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src interaction, thereby accelerating therapeutic discovery.

### Binding of Src to Na+/K+-ATPase Forms a Functional Signaling Complex

The interaction between Src and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase constitutes a fundamental signaling module that transduces extracellular stimuli into intracellular responses. Experimental validation of this complex employed a comprehensive suite of biochemical and biophysical techniques. Antibodies against Src (B12), ERK1/2, phospho-ERK1/2, His-tag, and α1 subunit (α6F) were sourced from Santa Cruz Biotechnology, GE Healthcare, and the Developmental Studies Hybridoma Bank. Plasmids encoding GST-fusion proteins (e.g., GST-CD3, GST-ND1) and His-tagged Src were constructed using standard molecular cloning methods and verified by sequencing. Cell models included LLC-PK1 (pig kidney epithelial), SYF (Src/Yes/Fyn triple knockout mouse fibroblasts), and SYF+Src (rescued line), cultured under standard conditions and transiently transfected using Lipofectamine 2000. Proteins were expressed in E. coli and purified via glutathione or ProBond affinity chromatography. Immunoprecipitation using anti-α1 antibody followed by Western blot confirmed co-association of Src with Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase. GST pulldown assays demonstrated direct, concentration-dependent binding of purified Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase to GST-Src, even in the absence of detergent, indicating a robust interaction. Domain mapping revealed that Src binds to the kinase domain, SH3-SH2 linker, and SH2 domain of itself when presented as GST fusions, while the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit binds via its N-terminus (NT), second cytosolic loop (CD2), and large central loop (CD3), with strongest interaction observed for CD3. FRET analysis by acceptor photobleaching in fixed and live cells, along with BRET in live cells expressing Src-Rluc and GFP-α1, confirmed close proximity (<10 nm) consistent with direct binding. Colocalization studies via confocal microscopy further supported plasma membrane co-distribution. Functional assays showed that Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase suppresses Src autophosphorylation at Y418, and ouabain reverses this inhibition, triggering Src activation and global tyrosine phosphorylation—an effect abolished by Src inhibitor PP2. Ouabain also disrupted FRET/BRET signals, confirming complex dissociation. These findings establish Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase as a physiological Src inhibitor and a receptor for cardiotonic steroids, with profound implications for understanding membrane transporter signaling and developing targeted therapeutics.

### Functional Characterization of Src-Interacting Na+/K+-ATPase Using RNA Interference Assay

To validate the physiological relevance of the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src interaction, RNA interference was employed to deplete the α1 subunit in LLC-PK1 cells, generating the knockdown line PY-17. Stable rescue was achieved by expressing siRNA-resistant rat α1 cDNA, including a pumping-null mutant (D371E). Knockdown efficiency was confirmed by Western blot using multiple α1 antibodies. Basal Src activity, measured by pY418 levels, was elevated in PY-17 cells, consistent with loss of inhibitory constraint, and restored to normal upon rescue with wild-type rat α1. Notably, the D371E mutant, though catalytically inactive, fully rescued basal Src regulation, proving that ion transport is dispensable for Src modulation. FRET analysis in rescued cells confirmed re-establishment of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src proximity. Ouabain-induced activation of Src and ERK1/2 was abolished in PY-17 cells but restored in rescued lines, demonstrating dependence on α1 expression. In contrast, EGF-induced ERK activation remained intact, highlighting pathway specificity. FAK, a known Src substrate, showed reduced tyrosine phosphorylation in PY-17 cells, reversible by Src inhibitor PP2. Confocal imaging of co-cultured P-11 (control) and AAC-19 (rescued) cells confirmed proper membrane localization. Intracellular Na⁺ measurements verified loss of pump function in PY-17, yet Src dysregulation persisted independently. This assay system provides a powerful platform for dissecting structure–function relationships in the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src axis and confirms that a distinct “signaling pool” of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase regulates Src irrespective of ion transport.

### Example III

Binding assays confirmed direct interaction between the ND1 peptide (derived from Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 residues 379–435) and Src kinase. GST-ND1 fusion protein pulled down purified His-Src in vitro, and soluble ND1 peptide inhibited Src autophosphorylation in a dose-dependent manner, establishing ND1 as a minimal Src-interacting and inhibitory motif.

### Example IV

Reciprocal binding studies demonstrated that the Src kinase domain (KD1) interacts directly with the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase α1 subunit. GST-KD1 fusion protein captured purified Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase in pulldown assays, confirming that the kinase domain serves as a docking site for the transporter, thereby defining a bidirectional interaction interface.

### Example V

Functional validation confirmed that both ND1 and KD1 are necessary and sufficient for Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase-mediated regulation of Src. Disruption of either domain abrogated complex formation and Src inhibition, while exogenous addition of ND1 peptide restored inhibitory control in α1-depleted cells, proving their central role in the signaling mechanism.

### Example VI

The ND1-derived peptide NaKtide significantly inhibited the growth of prostate cancer cells in vitro. Treatment with pNaKtide reduced cell proliferation and induced apoptosis in a dose-dependent manner, demonstrating therapeutic potential in oncology by selectively targeting the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase/Src signaling axis prevalent in many carcinomas.

### Example VII

Peptide P3, corresponding to residues within ND1, was identified as a potent Src inhibitor. In vitro kinase assays showed P3 suppressed Src activity with high potency (IC₅₀ ~70 nM). Conjugation to HIV-Tat yielded pNaKtide, which exhibited enhanced cell membrane permeability, as confirmed by FITC labeling and confocal microscopy, enabling effective intracellular delivery and functional antagonism of ouabain signaling.
