# DESCRIPTION

The present invention introduces a novel therapeutic approach for enhancing cognitive performance and mitigating the detrimental effects of sleep deprivation through the use of AMPA receptor potentiators. Specifically, the invention provides methods and pharmaceutical compositions that utilize positive allosteric modulators of α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptors to improve working memory, attention, and task accuracy in both normally alert and sleep-deprived subjects. The invention is grounded in the discovery that administration of an AMPA receptor potentiator, such as CX717, significantly enhances performance in a delayed-match-to-sample (DMS) cognitive task in non-human primates under baseline conditions and fully reverses the cognitive impairments induced by 30–36 hours of continuous sleep deprivation. Furthermore, neuroimaging studies reveal that these behavioral improvements are accompanied by normalization of cerebral glucose metabolism in key brain regions—including the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, dorsal striatum, and medial temporal lobe—thereby establishing a direct neurobiological correlate for the observed cognitive enhancement. This invention represents a significant advancement over existing stimulant-based countermeasures, which often carry risks of addiction, anxiety, or sensory distortion, by offering a non-stimulant, cognition-specific intervention that targets glutamatergic synaptic efficacy without inducing generalized arousal. The methods described herein are particularly suited for individuals whose cognitive function is compromised due to voluntary or involuntary sleep loss, including shift workers, military personnel, caregivers, and patients with sleep-disrupting medical conditions.

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods of use for compounds and pharmaceutical compositions that modulate AMPA receptor activity. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of AMPA receptor potentiators—also known as ampakines—for the enhancement of cognitive performance in alert individuals and for the restoration of cognitive function impaired by acute or chronic sleep deprivation. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations containing such compounds, methods for their administration, and their application in the manufacture of medicaments intended to treat or prevent cognitive deficits associated with insufficient sleep.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Electrophysiology of Sleep and Sleep-Deprivation

Sleep is a complex neurophysiological state characterized by distinct electrophysiological signatures observable through electroencephalography (EEG). During non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep, particularly slow-wave sleep, EEG recordings exhibit high-amplitude, low-frequency delta waves (0.5–5 Hz), reflecting synchronized neuronal activity across cortical networks. In contrast, rapid eye movement (REM) sleep is marked by low-amplitude, mixed-frequency activity resembling wakefulness, with prominent beta band oscillations (18–25 Hz). Sleep deprivation disrupts this natural architecture, leading to increased delta power during wakeful periods—a phenomenon interpreted as evidence of “microsleep” episodes or heightened sleep pressure. Event-related potentials (ERPs), which reflect neural responses to specific stimuli, also show attenuation following sleep loss, indicating reduced sensory processing and attentional engagement. Nonlinear analyses of EEG during prolonged wakefulness reveal increased signal complexity and instability, suggesting a breakdown in the regulatory mechanisms that maintain coherent brain dynamics. These electrophysiological alterations are not species-specific; similar patterns of delta intrusion, beta elevation, and ERP suppression have been documented in rodents and nonhuman primates subjected to extended wakefulness, validating cross-species models for studying the neural consequences of sleep loss.

### Neuroanatomical Substrates of Sleep Deprivation

Neuroimaging studies using positron emission tomography (PET) have demonstrated that sleep deprivation induces significant reorganization of functional brain activity. Glucose metabolism, a proxy for neuronal energy demand, decreases in subcortical structures such as the thalamus, basal ganglia, and cerebellum—regions critical for sensory gating, motor coordination, and automaticity. Concurrently, task-dependent increases in metabolic activity emerge in cortical areas involved in executive control, particularly the prefrontal cortex, suggesting compensatory recruitment to maintain performance. Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) studies corroborate these findings, showing that sleep-deprived individuals exhibit heightened activation in the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex during working memory tasks and in the thalamus during sustained attention paradigms. Notably, sleep deprivation often triggers the engagement of brain regions not typically activated during the same task under rested conditions, indicative of neural inefficiency or adaptive reallocation of resources. These metabolic and hemodynamic changes correlate strongly with behavioral outcomes: individuals who maintain performance despite sleep loss show greater prefrontal activation, whereas those who decline exhibit reduced thalamic and striatal metabolism. Mood disturbances accompanying sleep deprivation, including irritability and emotional lability, have been linked to dysregulation of dopaminergic signaling, particularly in mesolimbic pathways. Nonhuman primates serve as especially valuable models for these investigations due to their neuroanatomical and cognitive similarity to humans, enabling precise translation of findings across species.

### Role of Stress in Sleep Deprivation and Cognitive Performance

Chronic stress exerts profound effects on hippocampal structure and function, impairing long-term potentiation, reducing dendritic arborization, and suppressing neurogenesis—all of which contribute to deficits in hippocampal-dependent memory. Given that extended sleep deprivation activates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and elevates glucocorticoid levels, it is widely regarded as a form of physiological stress. This perspective helps explain why sleep loss disproportionately affects cognitive domains reliant on the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex, such as declarative memory, spatial navigation, and executive function. Animal models have been instrumental in isolating the cognitive effects of sleep deprivation from confounding variables like circadian disruption or environmental novelty, and nonhuman primates offer a critical bridge between rodent studies and human clinical applications due to their advanced cognitive repertoire and conserved stress response systems.

### Effects of Sleep-Deprivation on Memory

Memory consolidation—the process by which labile short-term memories are stabilized into long-term storage—is critically dependent on sleep, particularly slow-wave sleep, during which hippocampal neurons replay waking experiences to neocortical networks. Sleep deprivation disrupts this reactivation, leading to impaired retention in tasks ranging from learned avoidance in rodents to spatial navigation in water and radial mazes. Neurochemically, sleep loss alters monoaminergic tone: serotonin metabolism shifts toward catabolic pathways, norepinephrine levels fluctuate unpredictably, and prostaglandin synthesis increases, promoting inflammatory signaling that may further impair synaptic plasticity. The hippocampus, central to relational memory and spatial working memory, is especially vulnerable; human patients with medial temporal lobe lesions exhibit profound amnesia, and primate studies confirm that hippocampal damage impairs performance in delayed-response tasks. Single-unit recordings in behaving monkeys reveal that hippocampal neurons encode task-relevant variables such as sample identity and delay duration during working memory tasks, providing a cellular basis for the memory deficits observed after sleep deprivation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

As used herein, the term “subjects” or “patients” refers to human or non-human mammalian individuals, including but not limited to primates, who may benefit from cognitive enhancement or protection against sleep deprivation-induced impairment. “AMPA receptor modulators” are compounds that alter the function of AMPA-type glutamate receptors, with “potentiators” or “up-modulators” specifically enhancing receptor response to glutamate by slowing desensitization or deactivation. The term “treat” encompasses both therapeutic intervention in impaired states and prophylactic enhancement in normal conditions. “Acute” administration refers to single or short-term dosing, while “chronic” denotes repeated administration over days or weeks. “Concurrent administration” means co-delivery of two or more agents in a manner that their biological effects overlap temporally. An “effective” amount is one sufficient to produce a measurable improvement in cognitive performance or neurophysiological markers. “Prevention” includes mitigation or reversal of cognitive decline before or after onset.

The invention provides a method of using AMPA receptor potentiator compounds to enhance cognitive performance in alert subjects and to restore cognitive function impaired by sleep deprivation. A pharmaceutical composition comprising an effective amount of an AMPA receptor potentiator and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier is administered to a subject in need thereof. Such compositions may be used in the manufacture of a medicament for improving working memory, attention, and decision-making under conditions of sleep loss. AMPA receptor modulators are motivated by their ability to amplify endogenous glutamatergic signaling without inducing receptor overactivation or excitotoxicity. Exemplary AMPA receptor up-modulators include CX516, CX614, CX717, and related benzamide derivatives disclosed in U.S. Pat. Nos. 5,736,552; 5,939,429; and 6,218,408, as well as international publications WO 95/13273, WO 97/34874, and WO 00/23425. AMPA receptor potentiators are defined as compounds that bind allosterically to GluR1–GluR4 subunits to increase the amplitude and/or duration of ion channel currents evoked by glutamate.

The novel aspects of the invention are twofold: first, the use of non-stimulant AMPA potentiators avoids the adverse effects of traditional wake-promoting agents; second, the unprecedented demonstration that such compounds can not only enhance normal cognition but also fully reverse sleep deprivation-induced deficits. Beneficiaries include individuals with circadian rhythm disorders (e.g., shift workers), caretakers of newborns or critically ill patients, patients with insomnia or sleep apnea, and those who voluntarily extend wakefulness (e.g., students, military personnel).

Biological activity is confirmed via whole-cell patch-clamp recordings in acute hippocampal slices, where test compounds increase AMPA receptor-mediated currents. Field excitatory postsynaptic potentials (EPSPs) are recorded in CA1 stratum radiatum using a bipolar nichrome stimulating electrode to activate Schaffer-commissural fibers; potentiation is evidenced by increased EPSP amplitude. Although indirect measures like glutamate binding assays exist, electrophysiological methods are preferred for functional assessment.

### II. Non-Human Primate Model for Testing Effects of Sleep Deprivation on a Delayed-Match-to-Sample Task (DMTS)

The DMTS task requires monkeys to view a sample image on a video display, retain it across a variable delay (1–30 s), and then select it from an array of 2–6 images. Hand position is tracked via a fluorescent marker, with cursor movement mapped to arm displacement. Correct matches yield fluid reward. Hippocampal neural activity is recorded to correlate firing patterns with memory load. Animals are trained to stable baseline performance (>70% correct) before testing. Sleep deprivation entails 30–36 h of continuous wakefulness maintained by environmental enrichment and gentle stimulation. Test compounds are administered intravenously 10 min pre-task, and performance is compared across vehicle, drug, sleep-deprived, and sleep-deprived-plus-drug conditions.

### Salts of Compounds

Pharmaceutically acceptable salts include acid addition salts (e.g., hydrochloride, sulfate) and base-derived salts (e.g., sodium, potassium), provided they retain the parent compound’s activity and safety profile.

### Pharmaceutical Formulations

Active compounds are admixed with carriers to form unit-dose formulations suitable for oral (tablets, capsules), intravenous (solutions), buccal (films), topical (creams), parenteral (injectables), or transdermal (patches) administration. Isotonic preparations ensure compatibility with biological fluids. Aqueous suspensions may contain stabilizers; lyophilized powders are reconstituted before use. Emulsifying agents facilitate lipid-based delivery. Rectal suppositories, ointments, and liposomal encapsulations offer alternative routes.

### Dosage

Therapeutically effective doses range from 0.1–10 mg/kg for intravenous administration and 1–100 mg/kg for oral delivery, adjusted based on subject weight, route, and severity of impairment. In primates, 0.8 mg/kg IV of CX717 produced optimal cognitive enhancement without side effects.
