# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to agents for improving sperm motility, and more particularly to methods and compositions utilizing molecular hydrogen (H₂) for the enhancement of sperm motility in human males suffering from infertility due to asthenozoospermia or other motility-related sperm dysfunctions. The invention further encompasses the use of gaseous or liquid formulations containing dissolved hydrogen molecules for the treatment of fresh, experimentally damaged, or cryopreserved-thawed spermatozoa to restore or improve forward motility, thereby increasing the likelihood of successful fertilization in assisted reproductive technologies such as in vitro fertilization (IVF) and intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI). The technical field also includes diagnostic and therapeutic applications in reproductive medicine, specifically targeting mitochondrial function and oxidative stress mitigation in sperm cells through the administration of molecular hydrogen.

## BACKGROUND ART

Male infertility is a significant clinical condition affecting approximately half of all infertile couples worldwide, with sperm motility defects representing one of the most common underlying causes. Asthenozoospermia, defined by the World Health Organization as a condition wherein less than 32% of sperm exhibit progressive motility, severely impairs the ability of spermatozoa to traverse the female reproductive tract and achieve fertilization. Male infertility may arise from a variety of etiologies, including genetic abnormalities such as mutations in DNMT3B or MTHFR genes, mitochondrial DNA haplogroup variations, systemic diseases like polycystic kidney disease, and environmental or lifestyle factors that induce oxidative stress. Among the most severe forms of male infertility is azoospermia, characterized by the complete absence of sperm in the ejaculate. Azoospermia is broadly classified into obstructive and non-obstructive types; the latter results from impaired spermatogenesis and presents a greater therapeutic challenge due to the limited availability of viable spermatozoa.

In clinical practice, the hypo-osmotic swelling (HOS) test is frequently employed to assess sperm membrane integrity and viability, particularly in cases of immotile sperm where distinguishing live from dead cells is critical. Micro-insemination techniques, especially ICSI, have revolutionized the treatment of severe male factor infertility by enabling the direct injection of a single spermatozoon into an oocyte, bypassing natural barriers to fertilization. However, the success of ICSI remains contingent upon the selection of viable, preferably motile, sperm. Cryopreservation of sperm is routinely performed to preserve fertility potential prior to medical interventions such as chemotherapy or in anticipation of assisted reproductive procedures. Unfortunately, the freeze-thaw process induces significant cellular damage, including disruption of mitochondrial membranes, loss of motility, and increased susceptibility to oxidative stress, thereby reducing post-thaw sperm quality.

Sperm flagellar movement, which drives motility, is energetically demanding and relies heavily on adenosine triphosphate (ATP) generated primarily through oxidative phosphorylation in the mitochondria located in the sperm midpiece. While mitochondria are essential for ATP production, they concurrently serve as a major source of reactive oxygen species (ROS), particularly superoxide radicals produced during electron transport. Although low physiological levels of ROS act as signaling molecules necessary for capacitation and acrosome reaction, excessive ROS accumulation leads to oxidative damage of lipids, proteins, and DNA—especially detrimental in sperm due to their high polyunsaturated fatty acid content, minimal cytoplasmic volume, and lack of robust antioxidant defense systems. Antioxidants, both endogenous and exogenous, play a crucial role in neutralizing ROS and preserving sperm function. Prior research has explored the use of compounds such as zinc, coenzyme Q10, myoinositol, and xanthine derivatives to improve sperm parameters, with variable efficacy.

Notably, molecular hydrogen (H₂) has emerged in recent years as a selective antioxidant capable of scavenging cytotoxic hydroxyl radicals (•OH) without interfering with physiologically beneficial ROS such as nitric oxide (NO•) or superoxide. Due to its small molecular size and high diffusibility, H₂ readily penetrates cellular and subcellular membranes, including those of mitochondria and nuclei, offering protection against oxidative injury in diverse biological contexts. Previous studies have demonstrated H₂’s therapeutic potential in mitigating ischemia-reperfusion injury, radiation damage, and drug-induced organ toxicity. In the realm of reproductive biology, however, the application of H₂ to improve sperm motility—particularly in compromised or cryopreserved samples—has remained unexplored until the present invention.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the clinical challenge posed by immotile or severely hypomotile spermatozoa in infertile men, particularly those diagnosed with asthenozoospermia or exhibiting poor post-thaw motility following cryopreservation. Current therapeutic options for enhancing sperm motility are limited, often ineffective, and lack specificity for mitochondrial protection or oxidative stress mitigation. There exists a critical unmet need for a safe, rapid, and efficacious method to restore or improve sperm motility without compromising cellular integrity or fertilization potential, especially in the context of assisted reproductive technologies where even marginal improvements in motility can significantly influence clinical outcomes.

### Means to Solve the Object

The inventors conducted extensive research into the biological effects of molecular hydrogen on human spermatozoa and discovered that exposure to H₂—either in gaseous form or dissolved in aqueous media—significantly enhances forward motility in both experimentally damaged and cryopreserved-thawed sperm samples. This effect was observed within 30 minutes of treatment and was not replicated by inert gases such as nitrogen, indicating a specific biochemical action of H₂ rather than a mechanical or pressure-related artifact. The recovery of motility was particularly pronounced in sperm suspensions with higher baseline viability (>20% live cells), though even samples with very low viability exhibited signs of reactivated sluggish movement in select cases. Crucially, H₂ treatment was found to increase mitochondrial membrane potential, as evidenced by enhanced fluorescence of the potential-sensitive dye TMRM, suggesting that H₂ supports mitochondrial function and ATP synthesis, thereby fueling flagellar activity.

The invention provides a culture solution or washing medium saturated with molecular hydrogen, which may be prepared by bubbling a gas mixture containing H₂ (e.g., 50% H₂, 20% O₂, 5% CO₂, balance N₂) through a physiologically compatible liquid such as cleavage medium or saline. Alternatively, sperm may be directly exposed to a controlled atmosphere enriched with H₂. For cryopreserved sperm, thawed samples are washed in H₂-saturated media prior to use in IVF or ICSI. The findings demonstrate that H₂ treatment does not alter sperm swimming velocity, confirming that it does not induce hyperactivation but rather rescues basal motility. Moreover, the motility-enhancing effect persists for at least 2.5 hours after H₂ removal, and repeated treatments can further boost motility, offering practical flexibility in clinical workflows. These collective observations form the scientific foundation of the present invention.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention offers a novel, non-toxic, and rapidly acting therapeutic strategy to improve sperm motility in infertile men. By leveraging the unique antioxidant and mitochondrial-protective properties of molecular hydrogen, the invention enables the functional rescue of otherwise immotile or poorly motile spermatozoa, thereby expanding the pool of usable sperm for assisted reproduction. This is especially valuable in cases of severe asthenozoospermia or post-thaw sperm with compromised motility, where traditional methods fail. The invention enhances the success rates of ICSI by facilitating the identification and selection of viable, motile sperm, reduces the need for testicular sperm extraction in borderline cases, and provides a simple, cost-effective adjunct to existing laboratory protocols. Importantly, H₂ is biocompatible, leaves no toxic residues, and acts without altering pH or osmolarity, ensuring safety for both gametes and embryos.

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The agent for improving sperm motility according to the present invention comprises molecular hydrogen (H₂) in either gaseous or dissolved form. The composition of the agent may vary depending on the mode of delivery but consistently includes H₂ at a concentration sufficient to exert a measurable improvement in sperm forward motility. Sperm motility is measured using standardized methods, including visual assessment under a microscope or computer-assisted sperm analysis (CASA), wherein the percentage of sperm exhibiting progressive (forward) motility is quantified relative to total sperm count. The preferable method involves placing a 5–10 μL aliquot of sperm suspension in a counting chamber and analyzing movement over a defined period, typically 10 seconds, with triplicate measurements to ensure reliability.

Sperm is collected via masturbation following 3–5 days of ejaculatory abstinence, in accordance with WHO guidelines. The raw semen is processed to remove seminal plasma by centrifugation through a Percoll® density gradient, followed by resuspension in a suitable culture medium such as SAGE cleavage medium supplemented with 10% plasma protein fraction. For cryopreservation, sperm is mixed with a cryoprotectant like TEST-yolk buffer, slowly cooled over nitrogen vapor, and stored in liquid nitrogen. Thawing is performed by incubation at 37°C for 5 minutes, after which the sample is ready for H₂ treatment.

The origin of sperm may be from fertile donors or infertile patients undergoing IVF/ICSI, including those with diagnoses of oligozoospermia, asthenozoospermia, or necrozoospermia. Examples of the agent include a gas mixture containing 50% H₂, 20% O₂, 5% CO₂, and 25% N₂, or a liquid phase wherein H₂ is dissolved in physiological saline, culture medium, or buffer solutions such as HEPES-buffered saline. The concentration of hydrogen gas in the liquid phase is typically near saturation (approximately 0.8 mM at 1 atm and 25°C), achieved by bubbling the gas mixture through the liquid at a flow rate of 1 L/min for at least 5 minutes in a sealed chamber. Buffer solutions may contain standard electrolytes and pH stabilizers to maintain physiological conditions.

The manufacturing of the liquid agent involves equilibrating the base solution with the H₂-containing gas mixture in a hydrogen-impermeable container, such as glass or aluminum-laminated plastic, to prevent gas escape. Hydrogen-permeable materials like certain polymers are avoided for storage. Treatment methods include placing sperm suspensions in a sealed exposure chamber flushed with H₂-mixed gas for 30–60 minutes, or washing sperm pellets with H₂-saturated liquid medium for 5–30 minutes. Repeated treatments are feasible and beneficial, as motility gains can be re-established after decline, supporting multi-step clinical protocols.

### EXAMPLES

Human sperm was collected from patients undergoing IVF/ICSI at Yamashita Shonan Yume Clinic following informed consent and ethical approval. Sperm suspensions were prepared by density gradient centrifugation and resuspended in cleavage medium. For gaseous treatment, 100 μL aliquots were placed in a 5 L chamber and exposed to either H₂-mixed gas (50% H₂, 20% O₂, 5% CO₂, 25% N₂) or control N₂-mixed gas (75% N₂, 20% O₂, 5% CO₂) at 1 L/min for specified durations. Motility was assessed before and after treatment. In 35 severely damaged samples (stored >5 days), H₂ treatment for 40 minutes increased forward motility from 3.6% to 16.8%, while N₂ had no significant effect (7.7%). Time-course experiments showed 30-minute exposure was optimal.

For liquid treatment, cleavage medium was saturated with H₂ and mixed with CO₂-equilibrated medium to yield final H₂ concentrations of 50%, 75%, or 100%. Frozen-thawed sperm from 21 patients was washed in these media. In asthenozoospermic samples (<50% motility), 50% and 75% H₂ significantly improved motility, whereas 100% did not, suggesting an oxygen-dependent mechanism. Mitochondrial function was assessed by co-staining with MitoTracker Green and TMRM; H₂-treated sperm showed significantly higher TMRM fluorescence, indicating enhanced membrane potential.

Retention studies revealed that H₂-induced motility persisted for 2.5 hours post-treatment despite rapid H₂ diffusion out of solution. A second H₂ treatment after 24 hours restored motility to 67% of the initial peak. Comparative analysis confirmed H₂’s superiority over N₂, with no effect on swimming speed, ruling out hyperactivation. Overall, H₂ treatment proved effective across diverse sperm populations, particularly rescuing motility in viable but immotile cells, thereby demonstrating robust industrial applicability in reproductive medicine.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The sperm-motility agent of the present invention is directly applicable in clinical embryology laboratories and fertility clinics worldwide. It can be integrated into standard sperm preparation protocols for IVF and ICSI, especially for patients with asthenozoospermia or poor post-thaw sperm quality. The agent enhances the selection of viable sperm, improves fertilization rates, and potentially increases pregnancy outcomes without requiring complex equipment or regulatory hurdles, given H₂’s established safety profile. Commercial kits containing pre-saturated H₂ media or portable H₂-generating devices could be developed for widespread adoption.
