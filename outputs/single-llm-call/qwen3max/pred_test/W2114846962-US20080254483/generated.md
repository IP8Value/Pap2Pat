# DESCRIPTION

## 1. FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology, specifically to the diagnosis and clinical management of renal cell carcinoma (RCC). RCC is the most common form of kidney cancer in adults, accounting for approximately 90% of all malignant renal tumors. Despite advances in imaging technologies and surgical interventions, early detection of RCC remains a significant clinical challenge due to the asymptomatic nature of the disease in its initial stages. The invention provides novel methods and compositions for the detection, diagnosis, prognosis, and monitoring of RCC through the identification and quantification of a humoral biomarker—specifically, matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP-7), including its pro-form (pro-MMP-7)—in biological fluids such as serum. A humoral biomarker, as used herein, refers to a molecule detectable in bodily fluids—particularly blood or serum—that reflects the presence, progression, or response to treatment of a pathological condition such as cancer. Unlike tissue-based markers that require invasive biopsy procedures, humoral biomarkers offer a non-invasive, repeatable means of assessing disease status over time. The identification of MMP-7 as a humoral biomarker for RCC represents a significant advancement in the development of liquid biopsy approaches for this malignancy, enabling earlier intervention, improved risk stratification, and real-time monitoring of therapeutic efficacy.

## 2. BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Renal cell carcinoma often presents with nonspecific symptoms such as flank pain, hematuria, weight loss, or fatigue, which typically manifest only at advanced stages of the disease. Consequently, up to one-third of patients are diagnosed with metastatic disease, significantly reducing five-year survival rates. Current diagnostic methods for RCC rely heavily on imaging modalities such as computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), which, while effective for detecting anatomical abnormalities, lack molecular specificity and cannot reliably distinguish benign from malignant renal masses without histopathological confirmation. Moreover, these techniques are costly, expose patients to ionizing radiation (in the case of CT), and are unsuitable for frequent serial monitoring. Efforts to characterize RCC at the molecular level have led to the investigation of various potential biomarkers. Interleukin-6 (IL-6) and fibroblast growth factor (FGF) have been proposed as candidate markers due to their roles in inflammation and angiogenesis, respectively, but neither has demonstrated sufficient sensitivity or specificity for routine clinical use. Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) has been identified as a prognostic factor associated with poor outcomes in RCC, yet it is not specific to renal malignancy and is elevated in numerous other conditions. Similarly, reduced expression of E-cadherin—a cell adhesion molecule—has been correlated with tumor aggressiveness in RCC, but its assessment requires tissue sampling and immunohistochemical analysis, limiting its utility in longitudinal monitoring. International Patent Publication WO 02/082076 describes methods for detecting autoantibodies against tumor-associated antigens in various cancers, including RCC, but does not identify MMP-7 as a relevant target. Despite these efforts, there remains an unmet clinical need for a validated, non-invasive, humoral biomarker capable of aiding in the early detection, differential diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment monitoring of RCC. The absence of such a marker hinders the implementation of effective screening programs, particularly in high-risk populations, and limits the ability to dynamically assess tumor burden during therapy.

## 3. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP-7) as a novel humoral biomarker for renal cell carcinoma. MMP-7, also known as matrilysin-1, is a member of the matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) family of zinc-dependent endopeptidases involved in the degradation of extracellular matrix components, thereby facilitating processes such as tissue remodeling, wound healing, and, pathologically, tumor invasion and metastasis. Structurally, MMP-7 is synthesized as a zymogen (pro-MMP-7) containing a prodomain that maintains the enzyme in an inactive state; activation occurs through proteolytic cleavage of this prodomain, yielding the mature, catalytically active form. The invention discloses that both pro-MMP-7 and MMP-7 are overexpressed in RCC, but notably, only pro-MMP-7 is detectable in human serum, likely due to complex formation with serum proteins or rapid clearance of the active form. While prior art has documented elevated MMP-7 mRNA or protein levels in RCC tumor tissues and associated such overexpression with poor prognosis, the present invention is the first to demonstrate that circulating pro-MMP-7 in serum serves as a reliable, quantifiable humoral indicator of RCC presence. As used in the claims, the term “level” refers to the concentration of MMP-7 or pro-MMP-7, or autoantibodies thereto, measured in a biological sample using immunoassay techniques. The novelty of the invention lies in the unexpected discovery that serum pro-MMP-7 levels are significantly elevated in RCC patients compared to healthy individuals or those with non-malignant conditions, with high sensitivity (93%) and moderate specificity (75%). This finding enables the development of diagnostic methods wherein the detection or quantification of MMP-7 in a patient’s serum aids in confirming or ruling out RCC, particularly in ambiguous clinical scenarios. Furthermore, the invention encompasses methods for monitoring disease progression, assessing treatment response, and detecting recurrence by serial measurement of serum MMP-7 levels. These applications represent a paradigm shift from static tissue-based diagnostics to dynamic, non-invasive liquid biopsies for RCC management.

## MMP-7

Matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP-7) is a 19-kDa secreted protease encoded by the MMP7 gene located on chromosome 11q22.3. It is synthesized as a 29-kDa inactive precursor, pro-MMP-7, which includes an N-terminal propeptide domain that coordinates with the catalytic zinc ion to maintain latency. Activation of pro-MMP-7 occurs extracellularly through cleavage by serine proteases such as trypsin or plasmin, or autocatalytically under certain conditions, resulting in the removal of the prodomain and exposure of the active site. MMP-7 exhibits broad substrate specificity, degrading components of the basement membrane and extracellular matrix including fibronectin, laminin, elastin, entactin, and proteoglycans, as well as non-matrix proteins such as E-cadherin, Fas ligand, and pro-tumor necrosis factor-α, thereby influencing cell adhesion, apoptosis, and immune evasion. MMP-7 is constitutively expressed at low levels in normal epithelial cells of the gastrointestinal tract, kidney, and reproductive organs, where it participates in physiological tissue turnover. However, its expression is markedly upregulated in various epithelial cancers, including colorectal, gastric, pancreatic, and ovarian carcinomas, where it correlates with advanced stage, metastasis, and poor survival. In renal cell carcinoma, prior studies have reported increased MMP-7 mRNA and protein in tumor tissues, particularly in high-grade and metastatic lesions, suggesting a role in RCC pathogenesis. Nevertheless, reliance on mRNA overexpression analysis is limited by post-transcriptional regulation and the inability to reflect actual protein secretion into circulation. The inventors’ discovery—achieved through a proteomic approach combining two-dimensional electrophoresis of RCC cell line secretome with serological screening using patient sera—revealed that pro-MMP-7 is an immunogenic protein specifically recognized by IgG antibodies in RCC patient sera but not in healthy controls. This led to the development of sensitive immunoassays for detecting circulating pro-MMP-7. The invention thus establishes MMP-7, particularly its pro-form, as a humoral biomarker for RCC. Diagnostic applications include aiding in initial diagnosis, especially in patients with indeterminate renal masses, and differentiating RCC from benign renal conditions. Additionally, the method enables monitoring of treatment efficacy—for instance, a decline in serum MMP-7 following nephrectomy or targeted therapy may indicate favorable response—while rising levels may signal residual disease or recurrence. Alternative detection methods encompass enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA), homogeneous immunoassays based on time-resolved amplified cryptate emission (TRACE) technology, and Western blotting. The invention further contemplates the detection of autoantibodies against MMP-7 or pro-MMP-7 as complementary diagnostic indicators. Combination assays incorporating MMP-7 with other established or emerging RCC markers (e.g., VEGF, CAIX) may enhance overall diagnostic accuracy. Specific ligand assay techniques described herein utilize monoclonal antibodies—such as clones 01S and 29S—that recognize distinct epitopes on MMP-7, enabling sandwich immunoassay formats. The invention also provides kits comprising these antibodies, calibrators, and instructions for performing the diagnostic method, facilitating standardized clinical implementation.

## 4. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION AND EXAMPLES

Serum samples were collected from 30 patients diagnosed with renal cell carcinoma (stages I–IV), 40 control patients with benign or other malignant conditions, and 30 healthy donors, in accordance with institutional ethics guidelines and with informed consent. Blood was drawn into serum separator tubes, centrifuged within 30 minutes, and stored at −20°C until analysis. Immunoassays were developed to quantify MMP-7 in these samples. An enzyme-linked immunoassay (ELISA) was first established using monoclonal antibodies specific for human MMP-7. A standard curve was generated by serial dilution of recombinant human MMP-7, demonstrating a linear range from 0.5 to 40 μg/L with a correlation coefficient (r²) exceeding 0.99. Using this ELISA, MMP-7 levels were measured in all serum samples. RCC patients exhibited significantly elevated median MMP-7 concentrations (7.56 μg/L; range: 3.12–30.5 μg/L) compared to healthy controls (median: 2.13 μg/L; range: 0.17–3.5 μg/L), with a p-value <0.0001 by Mann-Whitney U test. Subsequently, a homogeneous immunoassay based on TRACE technology was developed to improve sensitivity and reduce assay time. This assay employed two monoclonal antibodies—clone 01S conjugated to europium cryptate and clone 29S labeled with AF647 fluorophore—in a sandwich format. A standard curve for the TRACE assay was similarly generated using CAL54 cell supernatant spiked with known amounts of recombinant MMP-7, calibrated against purified standards. Serum MMP-7 levels were then measured using this platform, confirming the same trend of significant elevation in RCC patients versus controls. Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism 4.0. Data distribution was assessed via Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, confirming non-normality and justifying nonparametric testing. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was conducted to evaluate diagnostic performance. The area under the curve (AUC) indicated strong discriminatory power, with a clinical sensitivity of 93% (95% CI: 78%–99%) at a specificity of 75% (95% CI: 59%–87%) for distinguishing RCC patients from non-RCC individuals. These results validate serum pro-MMP-7 as a robust humoral biomarker for RCC and support its integration into clinical diagnostic algorithms.
