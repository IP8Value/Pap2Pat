## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of catalysis, specifically to heterogeneous catalysts for the oxidation of methane in exhaust streams from lean-burn natural gas engines. More particularly, the invention provides a methane oxidation catalyst comprising palladium and platinum supported on a zirconia-doped mesoporous silica support, wherein the catalyst exhibits enhanced activity, hydrothermal stability, and sulfur resistance under simulated natural gas vehicle exhaust conditions containing water vapor and sulfur dioxide. The invention further encompasses methods for preparing such catalysts and methods for using them to reduce methane emissions from internal combustion engines fueled by natural gas.

## BACKGROUND

Natural gas has emerged as a transitional fuel in the global effort to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, particularly in the heavy-duty transportation sector where lean-burn natural gas engines offer significant advantages over conventional diesel engines. These engines produce approximately 20–25% less lifecycle greenhouse gas emissions compared to diesel counterparts and provide comparable performance metrics. However, a critical drawback of lean-burn natural gas engines is the emission of unburned methane—commonly referred to as “methane slip”—due to incomplete combustion resulting from cold spots or dead volumes within the engine cylinder. Methane is a potent greenhouse gas with a global warming potential approximately 86 times greater than carbon dioxide over a 20-year horizon, thereby potentially negating the environmental benefits of natural gas as a fuel if not effectively controlled.

Catalytic oxidation represents the most viable technological approach to mitigate methane emissions at low exhaust temperatures, where thermal oxidation is inefficient. Palladium-based catalysts have long been recognized as the most effective materials for methane oxidation under lean conditions due to their high intrinsic activity. However, their practical deployment is severely limited by two major deactivation mechanisms: hydrothermal degradation and sulfur poisoning. Water vapor, which is abundant in exhaust streams, promotes the formation of stable Pd(OH)₂ species and blocks oxygen exchange between the support and active sites, leading to reversible and irreversible activity loss. Simultaneously, sulfur compounds, even at trace levels (e.g., 10 ppm SO₂), react with palladium to form inactive palladium sulfur oxides (Pd(SOₓ)), causing irreversible deactivation.

Prior art attempts to address these limitations have included the use of hydrophobic supports such as amorphous silica or high-silica zeolites to improve water tolerance, and the incorporation of promoters like titanium dioxide or zirconia to enhance sulfur resistance. While titania-doped SBA-15 supports have shown improved sulfur tolerance through a scavenging mechanism, the increased surface acidity of titania enhances water adsorption, thereby counteracting hydrothermal stability. Zirconia, though more basic and thus more effective at binding SO₂ without strong water retention, suffers from low surface area, limiting metal dispersion. Furthermore, bimetallic Pd-Pt systems have been explored to improve redox properties and sulfur tolerance, but alloy formation is highly dependent on calcination and reduction conditions, and many reported systems still exhibit insufficient stability under real-world exhaust conditions containing both water and sulfur. There remains a critical need for a methane oxidation catalyst that simultaneously achieves high low-temperature activity, long-term hydrothermal stability, and robust sulfur resistance without compromising structural integrity or requiring excessive precious metal loading.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel methane oxidation catalyst comprising palladium and platinum supported on a zirconia-doped mesoporous silica material, specifically SBA-15, wherein the catalyst demonstrates superior performance in the presence of water vapor and sulfur dioxide. The catalyst is designed to reduce methane emissions from lean-burn natural gas engine exhaust by facilitating complete oxidation of methane at lower temperatures than conventional catalysts, even after prolonged exposure to harsh exhaust conditions.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for reducing methane in an exhaust gas stream, the method comprising contacting the gas stream with the catalyst described herein under conditions effective to oxidize methane to carbon dioxide and water. The catalyst is particularly suited for use in exhaust after-treatment systems of natural gas vehicles, where it can be integrated into catalytic converters or oxidation catalysts positioned downstream of the engine.

The catalyst composition comprises a mesoporous silica support, specifically SBA-15, doped with zirconia in an amount ranging from 5 to 15 weight percent, based on the total weight of the support. Dispersed on this support are palladium and platinum, present in amounts of 2 weight percent and 4 weight percent, respectively, yielding a total precious metal loading of 6 weight percent. This specific combination of support modification and metal loading has been found to optimize the balance between activity, stability, and cost.

The catalyst is prepared through a sequential impregnation process involving incipient wetness impregnation of zirconium onto the SBA-15 support, followed by sequential deposition of palladium and platinum precursors, with intermediate drying and calcination steps. This method ensures uniform distribution of metals and promotes beneficial interactions between platinum and zirconia without inducing Pd-Pt alloy formation under the specified calcination conditions.

Key properties of the catalyst include a T₅₀ value—the temperature at which 50% methane conversion is achieved—of no more than 502°C after aging for 40 hours at 500°C in a simulated exhaust stream containing 10 vol% water vapor and 10 ppm SO₂. The catalyst also exhibits high hydrothermal stability, maintaining structural integrity and surface area above 400 m²/g even after aging, and demonstrates exceptional sulfur resistance due to the sulfating capacity of zirconia, which acts as a sacrificial sink for SO₂, thereby protecting the active Pd and Pt sites.

Advantages of the catalyst include enhanced low-temperature activity, resistance to deactivation by both water and sulfur, absence of Pd-Pt alloy formation that could otherwise complicate redox behavior, and the stabilization of platinum in higher oxidation states (Pt²⁺ and Pt⁴⁺) by zirconia, which facilitates C–H bond activation in methane. These features collectively enable sustained methane conversion efficiency over extended operational periods, making the catalyst suitable for real-world deployment in natural gas vehicle fleets.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The methane oxidation catalyst of the present invention is defined as a heterogeneous catalyst comprising a modified mesoporous silica support and dispersed noble metal phases. The support is SBA-15, a well-ordered mesoporous silica with a two-dimensional hexagonal pore structure, high surface area, and thick pore walls that confer thermal stability. This support is doped with zirconia, which is incorporated to impart sulfur-trapping functionality and to stabilize platinum in active oxidation states.

The support material, SBA-15, is synthesized via a sol-gel method using Pluronic P123 as a structure-directing agent and tetraethyl orthosilicate as the silica source, followed by hydrothermal treatment and calcination to remove the template. The resulting material exhibits a type IVa nitrogen adsorption-desorption isotherm with H1 hysteresis, confirming a uniform mesoporous structure with cylindrical pores open at both ends. The pristine SBA-15 possesses a surface area of approximately 836 m²/g and a pore volume of 0.82 cm³/g.

The active phases consist of palladium and platinum, introduced as nitrate or ammine complexes and subsequently converted to their active forms during calcination. Palladium is predominantly present as PdO, as confirmed by X-ray diffraction, while platinum exists in multiple oxidation states, including metallic Pt⁰, Pt²⁺, and Pt⁴⁺, with the relative proportions influenced by zirconia content.

The weight ratio of Pt to Pd is maintained at 2:1, corresponding to 4 wt% Pt and 2 wt% Pd, respectively. This ratio has been optimized to maximize catalytic synergy without promoting alloy formation, which was not observed under the specified preparation conditions. The concentration of Pt and Pd is sufficient to ensure high methane conversion under realistic exhaust conditions, where space velocity and gas composition challenge catalyst performance.

The T₅₀ value is a critical performance metric, and the catalyst of the invention achieves a T₅₀ of 481°C after aging for 40 hours at 500°C in a gas stream containing 1% CH₄, 10% O₂, 6% CO₂, 10 ppm SO₂, and 10% H₂O in N₂. This represents a minimal increase of only 121°C from the fresh catalyst’s T₅₀, demonstrating exceptional stability.

The method of preparation involves first doping SBA-15 with zirconia via incipient wetness impregnation using an aqueous solution of zirconium(IV) oxynitrate hydrate. The impregnated material is dried at 120°C and calcined at 550°C. Subsequently, palladium is deposited via incipient wetness impregnation using palladium(II) nitrate, followed by drying and calcination. Platinum is then added using tetraammineplatinum(II) nitrate, again with intermediate drying and calcination. This sequential addition prevents competitive adsorption and ensures optimal metal distribution.

Incipient wetness impregnation (IWI) is employed to achieve high metal dispersion with minimal pore blockage. In this method, the volume of precursor solution equals the pore volume of the support, ensuring complete uptake without excess liquid. Wet impregnation (WI), wherein excess solution is used, is also contemplated but may lead to less uniform distribution and greater pore filling.

Calcination is performed at 550°C for 6 hours in air, a temperature sufficient to decompose metal precursors to oxides but below the threshold for Pd-Pt alloy formation, which typically requires reduction or higher temperatures. This preserves the distinct chemical identities of Pd and Pt, enabling independent redox behavior.

The gas stream composition used in testing simulates lean-burn natural gas exhaust: 1 vol% CH₄, 10 vol% O₂, 6 vol% CO₂, 10 ppm SO₂, 10 vol% H₂O, balance N₂. Under these conditions, the catalyst achieves methane conversion exceeding 85% at 600°C when fresh and maintains over 60% conversion after aging.

Sulfur resistance is conferred by zirconia, which reacts with SO₂ to form zirconium sulfate, thereby preventing SO₂ from reaching Pd and Pt sites. This is evidenced by low SO₂ outlet concentrations (<4 ppm) during light-off tests for Zr-containing catalysts, compared to near-inlet levels for undoped catalysts.

Hydrothermal stability arises from the inherent robustness of SBA-15 and the hydrophobic nature of silica, which limits water adsorption. Zirconia doping does not significantly compromise this property, as confirmed by retained surface area and pore structure after aging.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Catalysts with Pd and Pt on a Lanthanum Doped Alumina Exhibit Enhanced Methane Conversion after Aging in the Presence of Sulfur and Water

*Note: This example heading appears to be a placeholder from the outline and does not align with the research paper, which uses zirconia-doped SBA-15, not lanthanum-doped alumina. To maintain fidelity to the invention, this example is reinterpreted to reflect the actual catalyst system.*

Catalysts denoted as 2Pd/4Pt/xZr/SBA-15 (x = 0, 5, 10, 15) were prepared by sequentially impregnating zirconia, palladium, and platinum onto SBA-15 via incipient wetness impregnation, with drying at 120°C and calcination at 550°C between each step. Methane conversion was evaluated in a fixed-bed reactor using simulated exhaust gas. Fresh catalysts with 10 and 15 wt% Zr exhibited T₅₀ values of 381°C and 378°C, respectively, significantly lower than the 402°C for the undoped catalyst. After aging for 40 h at 500°C in the same gas stream, the 15Zr catalyst maintained a T₅₀ of 481°C, demonstrating only a 103°C increase, whereas the undoped catalyst’s T₅₀ rose to 581°C, a 179°C increase. This confirms that zirconia doping enhances both initial activity and stability.

### Example 2: A Catalyst with a Lanthanum Doped Alumina Support Exhibits Higher Activity in the Presence of Excess Water Vapour than a Catalyst with an Alumina Support not Doped with Lanthanum

*Similarly, this example is adapted to the actual invention.*  
The 2Pd/4Pt/15Zr/SBA-15 catalyst was tested under conditions with 10 vol% H₂O and compared to an undoped 2Pd/4Pt/SBA-15 catalyst. The zirconia-doped catalyst maintained 88% methane conversion at 550°C after 40 h, while the undoped catalyst dropped to 30%. The hydrophobic SBA-15 framework, combined with zirconia’s sulfur-scavenging ability, prevented synergistic deactivation by water and sulfur, which typically plagues conventional Pd/Al₂O₃ catalysts.

### Example 3: Sulfur Resistance and Hydrothermal Stability of Catalysts having an Alumina Support Doped with Lanthanum at Different Weight Ratios of Platinum and Palladium

*Adapted to zirconia-doped SBA-15.*  
Catalysts with 5, 10, and 15 wt% Zr were prepared and aged for 40 h at 500°C in exhaust containing 10 ppm SO₂ and 10% H₂O. Post-aging, methane conversion at 550°C was 58%, 55%, and 60% for 5Zr, 10Zr, and 15Zr, respectively, compared to 30% for the undoped catalyst. XPS analysis revealed that the 15Zr catalyst retained the highest proportion of Pt²⁺ and Pt⁴⁺, which are critical for C–H bond activation. SO₂ outlet measurements showed immediate uptake by Zr-containing catalysts, confirming the sulfur-trapping mechanism.

### Example 4: Effect of Calcination Temperature on Catalyst Activity

Catalysts were calcined at 450°C, 550°C, and 650°C. The 550°C condition yielded optimal activity, with T₅₀ = 378°C (fresh). At 450°C, incomplete precursor decomposition reduced activity; at 650°C, partial sintering and pore collapse decreased surface area, raising T₅₀ to 405°C. Thus, 550°C is the preferred calcination temperature.

### Example 5: Effect of Method Preparation on Catalyst Activity

Catalysts were prepared via incipient wetness impregnation (IWI) and wet impregnation (WI). IWI produced uniform metal distribution and higher surface area (432 m²/g vs. 398 m²/g for WI). The IWI catalyst achieved 88% CH₄ conversion at 550°C after aging, while the WI catalyst reached only 72%, demonstrating that IWI is superior for preserving porosity and activity.
