# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a complex neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction across multiple contexts, as well as restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities. The clinical presentation of ASD varies widely among individuals, encompassing a broad spectrum of symptom severity, cognitive ability, language development, and adaptive functioning. This heterogeneity has historically posed significant challenges to both diagnosis and treatment planning. Diagnosis of ASD currently relies primarily on behavioral observation and standardized psychometric assessments administered by trained clinicians, such as the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS) and the Autism Diagnostic Interview-Revised (ADI-R). These tools, while validated and widely used, are inherently subjective, time-intensive, and require specialized expertise, thereby limiting accessibility, particularly in resource-constrained settings or regions with shortages of qualified diagnosticians. Moreover, the absence of objective, biologically based diagnostic criteria contributes to delays in diagnosis—often not occurring until after age four—and introduces variability in diagnostic outcomes across different evaluators and clinical settings.

The etiology of ASD remains incompletely understood but is widely accepted to involve a complex interplay between genetic susceptibility and environmental influences. Numerous genetic variants, including single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), copy number variations (CNVs), and de novo mutations, have been associated with increased risk for ASD. However, no single genetic marker accounts for more than a small fraction of cases, underscoring the polygenic nature of the disorder. Environmental factors, such as prenatal exposure to certain medications (e.g., valproic acid), maternal immune activation, advanced parental age, and perinatal complications, have also been implicated as potential contributors to ASD risk. Despite extensive research into potential biochemical correlates—including investigations into neurotransmitter systems, oxidative stress markers, immune profiles, and metabolic pathways—no reliable, clinically validated biomarker for ASD has yet emerged. In particular, studies examining fatty acid metabolism, especially levels of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) like docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and arachidonic acid (AA) in blood or erythrocyte membranes, have yielded inconsistent results, often reporting statistically significant group differences that fail to translate into meaningful individual-level diagnostic utility due to substantial overlap between ASD and neurotypical populations. This gap between population-level statistical significance and individual-level classification performance highlights a critical limitation in current biomarker research paradigms and underscores the urgent need for robust, data-driven approaches capable of generating objective, quantifiable metrics for ASD status determination.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present disclosure provides a novel system and method for determining an autism state in a subject based on quantitative analysis of biological data, specifically metabolite concentrations derived from erythrocyte membrane fatty acid profiles. The system comprises a data processing architecture configured to receive a test data array comprising measured concentrations of a defined panel of fatty acids from a biological sample of the subject. This data array is structured as a multidimensional vector wherein each dimension corresponds to a specific metabolite concentration normalized to total fatty acid content. The system further includes a classifier and scoring engine that implements a machine learning algorithm—specifically Fisher Discriminant Analysis (FDA)—to project the input data array onto a discriminant axis optimized to maximize separation between pre-defined autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and neurotypical (NEU) reference classes. The scoring engine calculates a numerical score for the test data array based on its position along this discriminant axis relative to class templates generated from training datasets comprising confirmed ASD and NEU individuals. A border threshold, empirically determined from the distribution of scores in the reference populations, is applied to classify the subject’s autism state: scores exceeding the threshold are indicative of an ASD classification, while scores below the threshold correspond to a neurotypical classification. The metabolite panel utilized in the disclosed method includes, but is not limited to, arachidonic acid (AA), dihomo-γ-linoleic acid (DGLA), docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA), linoleic acid (LA), oleic acid, palmitic acid, stearic acid, and derived ratios such as DHA/AA, EPA/AA, and n-3/n-6. The machine learning framework employs kernel extensions and cross-validatory techniques to enhance generalizability and mitigate overfitting, ensuring robust performance across diverse populations. This approach shifts the paradigm from traditional hypothesis testing of group means to individual-level probabilistic classification, thereby providing a clinically actionable, objective metric for aiding in the diagnosis and stratification of individuals along the autism spectrum.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The system described herein is implemented as a computational platform comprising hardware and software components designed to process biological data and output a diagnostic classification. Central to the invention is the identification and utilization of specific biomarkers—namely, erythrocyte membrane fatty acid concentrations—as inputs for a multivariate classification algorithm. These biomarkers are selected based on their involvement in key metabolic pathways implicated in neurodevelopment, particularly the folate-dependent one-carbon metabolism (FOCM) and transsulfuration (TS) pathways, which intersect with lipid metabolism and redox regulation. Genetic mutations and altered gene expression in enzymes regulating these pathways, combined with environmental factors such as dietary intake (e.g., seafood consumption influencing n-3 PUFA levels), contribute to metabolic dysregulation hypothesized to underlie aspects of ASD pathophysiology. To model the complex, nonlinear relationships within this high-dimensional data space, the system employs latent variable techniques, including Fisher Discriminant Analysis (FDA), Kernel FDA (KFDA), Partial Least Squares (PLS), and Kernel PLS (KPLS). FDA seeks a linear projection that maximizes between-class scatter while minimizing within-class scatter, effectively separating ASD and NEU groups in a reduced-dimensional space. KFDA extends this capability to nonlinear decision boundaries using kernel functions. Similarly, PLS and KPLS are used for regression tasks, such as predicting adaptive behavior scores from metabolite profiles.

The system architecture includes a data acquisition interface for receiving test data arrays, a classifier module housing the trained FDA model, and a scoring engine that computes a discriminant score. Class templates are generated during a training phase using labeled datasets: one comprising individuals clinically diagnosed with ASD and another comprising age- and gender-matched neurotypical controls. Each data array is structured as a vector **x** = [x₁, x₂, ..., xₙ]ᵀ, where each xᵢ represents the normalized concentration of a specific fatty acid (e.g., AA, DHA) or ratio (e.g., n-3/n-6). The FDA calculation involves computing the within-cluster variation matrix **S_w** and between-cluster variation matrix **S_b** from the training data. The optimal projection vector **w** is obtained by solving the generalized eigenvalue problem **S_b w** = λ **S_w w**, and the discriminant score for a test sample **x** is given by s = **w**ᵀ**x**. The probability distribution function (PDF) of scores for each class is estimated using kernel density estimation (KDE) with a Gaussian kernel, yielding smooth, non-parametric density estimates that capture the full distributional characteristics beyond mere central tendency. The scoring engine compares the computed score against a border threshold—typically set at the point of equal likelihood between the two class PDFs or optimized via receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis—to assign a final classification. This method enables objective, reproducible determination of autism state based on quantifiable biochemical data, integrated within a scalable digital health platform.

### EXAMPLES

An exemplary study was conducted to validate the disclosed system using baseline data from the ASU Comprehensive Nutrition/Diet Treatment Study, involving 63 children diagnosed with ASD and 49 neurotypical (NEU) controls, matched for age and gender, with median ages of 9.7 and 10.0 years, respectively. Participants were excluded if they had consumed nutritional supplements or followed restrictive diets in the prior two months. Blood samples were collected via standard venipuncture, and plasma was separated by centrifugation within two hours of collection. Erythrocytes were washed, and fatty acids were extracted, derivatized to methyl esters, and quantified using gas chromatography with flame ionization detection by a CLIA-certified laboratory. All measurements were normalized to total fatty acid concentration. DNA was also extracted from whole blood for ancillary genetic analyses, though not used in the primary classifier. FDA scores were calculated for each participant using all 15 fatty acid variables listed in Table 2 of the research paper. The resulting score distributions showed partial separation between ASD and NEU groups, with a C-statistic of 0.76, indicating moderate but insufficient diagnostic accuracy for clinical deployment as a standalone test. Performance was further evaluated using a sibling (SIB) control subgroup, revealing even poorer separability (C-statistic ~0.60), highlighting the influence of shared familial factors. ROC curve analysis confirmed suboptimal sensitivity and specificity trade-offs.

To identify a minimal yet effective biomarker panel, variable combinations were systematically evaluated for classification separability using cross-validation. The maximum C-statistic across all bivariate combinations was 0.68, achieved with DGLA and stearic acid, still below the threshold for clinical utility. The final classifier, incorporating all variables, demonstrated a cross-validated confusion matrix with high false positive and false negative rates, reinforcing the conclusion that erythrocyte fatty acid profiles alone lack sufficient predictive power for ASD diagnosis. Additional analyses explored the relationship between metabolites and adaptive behavior using KPLS regression; however, cross-validated R² values remained low (<0.2), indicating poor predictability. These findings underscore the importance of FOCM/TS pathway dysfunction not being reliably captured by peripheral fatty acid measures alone. The system can be implemented on standard computer hardware comprising a processor, memory, storage medium, communication interface, display, and user input devices (e.g., keyboard, touchscreen). Software is encoded on a non-transitory computer-readable medium and executed to perform data ingestion, preprocessing, FDA scoring, and classification output via a graphical user interface. Network connectivity enables remote data upload and cloud-based processing. Definitions provided clarify that “data array” refers to a structured set of metabolite measurements, “classifier” denotes the machine learning model, and “score” is the continuous output of the discriminant function used for binary classification against a defined threshold.
