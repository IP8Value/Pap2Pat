# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Biofouling represents a persistent and economically burdensome challenge across numerous industrial sectors that rely on water-handling systems, including but not limited to cooling towers, heat exchangers, drinking water distribution networks, wastewater treatment facilities, and seawater desalination plants. Biofouling is broadly defined as the undesirable accumulation of biological material—particularly microorganisms and their secreted extracellular polymeric substances (EPS)—onto surfaces immersed in aqueous environments. In the context of membrane-based water treatment technologies, biofouling emerges as one of the most recalcitrant forms of fouling, distinct from mineral scaling, colloidal deposition, or organic adsorption. It arises through a complex sequence of microbial adhesion, proliferation, and biofilm maturation, processes that are heavily influenced by the physicochemical properties of the membrane surface, the composition of the feedwater, and operational parameters such as flow rate, pressure, and temperature. The formation of biofilms on membrane surfaces significantly impedes water permeation by obstructing pores and increasing hydraulic resistance, thereby necessitating elevated operating pressures, more frequent cleaning cycles, and ultimately premature membrane replacement—all of which contribute to increased energy consumption and operational expenditures.

Conventional polymer membranes employed in water purification—typically fabricated from hydrophobic polymers such as polysulfone (PSF) or polyethersulfone (PES)—are particularly susceptible to biofouling due to their inherent surface characteristics. These materials exhibit low surface energy, high hydrophobicity, and minimal electrostatic repulsion against negatively charged bacterial cells, all of which facilitate microbial attachment and subsequent biofilm development. While various mitigation strategies have been explored—including chemical pretreatment of feedwater with biocides like chlorine or ozone, physical cleaning protocols, and biological interventions such as quorum sensing inhibition or bacteriophage application—each approach suffers from significant drawbacks. Chlorination and ozonation, for instance, can generate toxic disinfection byproducts that permeate through membranes and compromise water safety, while biological controls remain largely unproven at industrial scale. Moreover, conventional cleaning methods often fail to eradicate resilient biofilms completely, and residual dead biomass may even serve as a nutrient source for regrowth. Surface modification techniques, including polymer blending with hydrophilic additives, have shown promise in enhancing antifouling performance, yet many existing solutions lack broad-spectrum efficacy, long-term stability, or compatibility with scalable manufacturing processes.

The limitations of current antifouling membranes underscore a critical need for innovative materials that intrinsically resist microbial colonization without relying on leachable biocides or complex post-fabrication treatments. Ideally, such membranes would combine enhanced hydrophilicity, tunable surface charge, and structural porosity to create a physical and chemical barrier against bacterial adhesion. Furthermore, the additive used to confer these properties should be non-toxic, naturally derived, cost-effective, and compatible with standard phase inversion fabrication methods. Addressing this unmet need requires a paradigm shift toward sustainable, bio-inspired membrane design that leverages natural compounds with inherent antifouling attributes. The present invention responds to this challenge by introducing a novel class of polymer membranes incorporating Arabic gum—a natural, amphiphilic polysaccharide—as a functional additive to impart robust, non-biocidal resistance to biofouling across diverse bacterial strains, thereby offering a durable and environmentally benign solution for next-generation water treatment systems.

## SUMMARY

The present disclosure introduces an advanced water treatment system centered around a novel polymer membrane engineered to resist biofouling through the strategic incorporation of Arabic gum (AG). This system comprises a filtration unit housing a membrane composed of a base polymer—such as polysulfone (PS) or polyethersulfone (PES)—blended with a controlled amount of AG, typically ranging from 0.1 to 7 weight percent relative to the total casting solution. The inclusion of AG fundamentally alters the membrane’s physicochemical landscape, enhancing hydrophilicity, increasing negative surface charge, and optimizing pore architecture to collectively impede bacterial adhesion and biofilm formation without exerting bactericidal effects that could promote resistance or environmental toxicity.

Key aspects of the disclosure include the composition of the membrane itself, the method of its fabrication via phase inversion, and its integration into a functional water treatment apparatus featuring a water tank with defined inlet and outlet ports, through which a contaminated water stream is directed across the membrane surface under controlled pressure. The membrane operates by permitting water permeation while rejecting particulates, organic matter, and crucially, resisting colonization by both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria, including clinically relevant strains such as *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. 

The benefits of this disclosure are manifold. First, the AG-modified membrane demonstrates significantly improved pure water flux—exceeding that of unmodified counterparts by over 100% in optimal formulations—due to increased porosity and interconnected macrovoid structures. Second, the membrane exhibits superior antifouling performance, as evidenced by scanning electron microscopy showing minimal bacterial adhesion even after prolonged exposure, attributable to the synergistic effects of enhanced surface hydrophilicity (reduced contact angle by up to 45%) and amplified negative zeta potential (increased by over 100% at neutral pH). Third, because the antifouling mechanism is anti-adhesive rather than biocidal, the risk of microbial resistance development is mitigated, and no harmful leachates are introduced into the treated water. Finally, the use of Arabic gum—a renewable, food-grade, and biocompatible biopolymer—aligns with principles of green chemistry and sustainable engineering.

In overview, the disclosure provides a comprehensive solution to the longstanding problem of membrane biofouling by re-engineering the membrane matrix at the molecular level using a natural additive. The resulting system offers enhanced durability, reduced maintenance frequency, lower energy consumption, and improved water quality, making it suitable for applications ranging from municipal drinking water production to industrial wastewater reuse and point-of-use filtration devices.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The water treatment system of the present invention comprises a housing containing a filtration chamber in fluid communication with a water tank equipped with an inlet for receiving untreated water and an outlet for discharging permeate. Within the filtration chamber resides a polymer membrane specifically designed to treat a water stream by removing contaminants while resisting biofouling. Membrane fouling, particularly biofouling—the accumulation of microorganisms and their extracellular matrices on membrane surfaces—remains a primary cause of performance decline in pressure-driven membrane processes. Biofouling reduces permeability, increases transmembrane pressure, and necessitates frequent chemical or mechanical cleaning, all of which elevate operational costs and shorten membrane lifespan. The deleterious effects of biofouling are exacerbated by the hydrophobic nature and neutral-to-slightly-negative charge of conventional polymer membranes, which provide favorable conditions for bacterial adhesion.

To address this, the invention introduces an antimicrobial membrane that does not kill bacteria but instead prevents their initial attachment. Central to this innovation is the incorporation of Arabic gum (AG), a natural branched polysaccharide exuded by *Acacia* species, known for its amphiphilic character due to the presence of both neutral sugars (e.g., arabinose, galactose) and acidic residues (e.g., glucuronic acid). The polymer membrane is composed primarily of a sulfonated aromatic polymer such as polysulfone (PS) or polyethersulfone (PES), blended with AG at concentrations between 0.1 and 7 wt.% in the casting dope. This composition is achieved by dissolving the base polymer in a polar aprotic solvent such as N,N-dimethylacetamide (DMAc), followed by dispersion of AG under sonication and stirring to ensure homogeneity, degassing to remove entrapped air, and subsequent casting via phase inversion.

The membrane fabrication process involves spreading the dope solution onto a glass substrate using a precision casting knife set to a gap height of approximately 200 µm, followed by immediate immersion into a coagulation bath of deionized water. This induces instantaneous liquid-liquid demixing, precipitating a porous asymmetric structure characterized by a dense selective skin layer atop a highly porous substructure with finger-like macrovoids. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) reveals that AG incorporation increases surface porosity and enlarges macrovoid dimensions, effects attributed to AG’s hydrophilicity accelerating solvent-nonsolvent exchange during phase inversion.

When deployed in the water treatment system, the membrane treats a flowing water stream by allowing water molecules to permeate while rejecting suspended solids, organic macromolecules, and microorganisms. Critically, the membrane’s surface properties—enhanced hydrophilicity (contact angles as low as 45° less than neat PS) and heightened negative charge (zeta potential at pH 7 increased by over 100%)—create an energetic barrier to bacterial adhesion. Experimental testing against *S. aureus* (Gram-positive) and *E. coli*, *K. pneumoniae*, and *P. aeruginosa* (Gram-negative) demonstrated near-complete suppression of colonization on AG-containing membranes, even after 24-hour incubation, whereas control membranes were densely covered in intact, viable cells. Notably, SEM imaging confirmed that bacterial morphology remained undamaged, indicating an anti-adhesive rather than bactericidal mechanism.

The advantages of this membrane are rooted in its dual functionality: improved hydraulic performance and intrinsic biofouling resistance. Porosity increases by up to 24%, average pore size expands from 24 nm (neat PS) to 39 nm (3 wt.% AG), and pure water flux more than doubles—all contributing to energy-efficient operation. Simultaneously, the hydrated surface layer formed by hydrophilic AG chains and the electrostatic repulsion from dissociated carboxyl groups prevent microbial attachment. This approach avoids the pitfalls of biocidal additives, offering a sustainable, long-lasting solution applicable to microfiltration, ultrafiltration, and potentially nanofiltration contexts. Additional features may include modular housing designs, backflush capabilities, or integration with monitoring sensors. The scope of the disclosure encompasses all embodiments wherein a natural polysaccharide like AG is blended into a sulfonated polymer matrix to confer anti-biofouling properties via surface modification, concluding that this invention represents a significant advancement in durable, high-performance water purification technology.
