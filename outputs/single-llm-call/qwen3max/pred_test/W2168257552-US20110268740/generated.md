# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of immunology and therapeutic antibodies, specifically to fully human monoclonal antibodies that bind with high affinity to the extracellular domain of the matrix protein 2 (M2e) of Influenza A virus. These antibodies are derived from naturally occurring human B cells and represent a novel class of immunotherapeutic agents suitable for both prophylactic and therapeutic applications against influenza infection. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies, methods for their production, and their use in passive immunization strategies to prevent or treat Influenza A virus infections in humans. Given the global burden of seasonal and pandemic influenza, the development of broadly protective therapeutics that target conserved viral epitopes is of critical medical importance. The antibodies described herein fulfill this unmet need by targeting a highly conserved region of the M2 protein, which remains stable across diverse Influenza A strains, including those with pandemic potential such as H5N1. Unlike conventional vaccines that require annual reformulation due to antigenic drift and shift in hemagglutinin and neuraminidase, the M2e-targeting antibodies of the present invention offer a strain-independent approach to influenza control. This field bridges virology, molecular biology, antibody engineering, and clinical immunology, and the invention provides a robust platform for isolating natural human antibodies with therapeutic relevance using advanced mammalian cell display technologies.

## RELATED ART

Influenza A virus remains a significant public health threat, causing an estimated 250,000 to 500,000 deaths annually worldwide. Current influenza vaccines primarily elicit neutralizing antibodies against the surface glycoproteins hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA), which are subject to frequent antigenic drift and occasional antigenic shift due to the segmented nature of the viral genome. These genetic changes necessitate annual reformulation of seasonal vaccines and limit their effectiveness against emerging pandemic strains. Consequently, vaccine efficacy varies significantly from year to year and is often suboptimal in vulnerable populations such as the elderly and immunocompromised individuals. Passive immunization with monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) offers a promising alternative, providing immediate immunity without relying on the host’s immune response. However, HA-targeted mAbs face the same limitations as vaccines due to antigenic variability. The matrix protein 2 (M2) of Influenza A virus presents an attractive alternative target because its 23-amino acid extracellular domain (M2e) is remarkably conserved across human influenza isolates over the past century. M2 functions as a proton channel essential for viral uncoating and maturation, and although it is sparsely expressed on virions, it is abundantly present on infected cells. M2e-specific antibodies do not neutralize free virus but instead mediate protection through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) by eliminating infected cells. Several murine and humanized M2e-specific mAbs have demonstrated prophylactic efficacy in animal models, but none have been derived from natural human B cell repertoires. Fully human antibodies generated from transchromosomic mice or phage display libraries may carry residual immunogenicity or lack the refined specificity achieved through natural affinity maturation. Moreover, prior art has not demonstrated therapeutic efficacy—i.e., treatment after infection—with a single dose of a naturally derived human M2e antibody. Thus, there exists a compelling need for naturally occurring, fully human monoclonal antibodies that bind M2e with high affinity, recognize diverse Influenza A strains, and exhibit both prophylactic and therapeutic activity in vivo, particularly in a clinically relevant single-dose regimen.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides fully human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the extracellular domain of the Influenza A virus M2 protein (M2e) with high affinity, isolated through a library-based screening approach using Sindbis-mediated mammalian cell display. From peripheral blood mononuclear cells of a human donor with high anti-M2e titers, 334 M2e-specific B cells were sorted, and their immunoglobulin variable regions were used to construct scFv libraries displayed on the surface of BHK cells. Screening of these libraries led to the identification and isolation of multiple scFv clones, including D005, E040, and F052, which were subsequently engineered into fully human IgG1 antibodies. These antibodies exhibit the advantages of natural human origin, including minimal immunogenicity, low off-target reactivity, and high affinity resulting from in vivo affinity maturation. The complementarity determining regions (CDRs) of both the light chain variable regions (LCVRs) and heavy chain variable regions (HCVRs) have been precisely defined, with LCVRs belonging to the kappa light chain type and HCVRs exhibiting specific CDR3 motifs. The invention encompasses a vast array of clonally related antibodies sharing core CDR sequences, with numerous combinations possible due to the diversity in framework regions. The lead antibody, D005, binds M2e with a dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 4–5 nM and recognizes M2e from diverse Influenza A strains, including H1N1 (PR/8/34) and H5N1 (A/VN/1203/2004). Epitope mapping confirms binding to the conserved N-terminal region of M2e, and the antibody effectively binds both cell-associated M2 and M2 present on virus particles. In a mouse model of lethal Influenza A infection, a single intraperitoneal administration of D005 conferred complete protection when given prophylactically and significant therapeutic benefit when administered up to three days post-infection. The invention thus introduces a monoclonal antibody comprising a specific LCVR and HCVR that form an antigen-binding site capable of high-affinity M2e recognition. Additionally, the invention includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising these antibodies in pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, suitable for systemic administration in humans for the prevention or treatment of Influenza A virus infection.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

An antibody, as used herein, refers to an immunoglobulin molecule capable of specifically binding an antigen via its variable regions. Antibody structure comprises two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, each containing constant and variable domains. The primary classes of antibodies include IgG, IgM, IgA, IgD, and IgE, with IgG being the most abundant in serum and preferred for therapeutic applications. “Recognizing” denotes specific binding to an antigen with measurable affinity. The Fv fragment consists of the variable domains of one heavy and one light chain, forming the minimal antigen-binding unit. A single-chain antibody (scFv) links the VH and VL domains via a flexible peptide linker, typically (Gly4Ser)3, enabling expression as a single polypeptide while retaining antigen specificity. A diabody is a bivalent dimeric scFv formed by shortening the linker to promote intermolecular pairing. A human antibody is one wherein the amino acid sequence corresponds to naturally occurring human immunoglobulins, as opposed to chimeric or humanized variants. A fully human antibody contains only human-derived sequences in both variable and constant regions, offering advantages of reduced immunogenicity and compatibility with human immune effector functions. The human antibody comprises variable regions that determine antigen specificity and constant regions that mediate biological effector functions. Monoclonal antibodies are derived from a single B cell clone and exhibit uniform binding properties. The constant region of the heavy chain defines the antibody class; for IgG1, it includes CH1, hinge, CH2, and CH3 domains. The light chain constant region is either kappa (κ) or lambda (λ); the antibodies of the present invention predominantly utilize κ chains. The variable region, located at the N-terminus of both chains, contains framework regions and three complementarity determining regions (CDRs) that form the antigen-binding site. Human variable regions are encoded by V, (D for heavy chain), and J gene segments rearranged during B cell development. The CDRs, particularly CDR3, are the most diverse and directly contact the antigen. The antigen in this context is the extracellular domain of Influenza A virus M2 protein (M2e), a 23-residue peptide highly conserved among human strains. The M2e antigen may be presented as a conjugate (e.g., fused to RNase A or Qβ virus-like particle), as a fusion protein, or in its native context on virus particles or infected cells. “Specifically binding” means the antibody binds M2e with at least 10-fold higher affinity than to unrelated antigens. Affinity is quantified by dissociation constant (Kd), determined by Friguet ELISA, standard ELISA, or Biacore analysis, with EC50 values derived from binding curves. An “effective amount” is the quantity sufficient to achieve a therapeutic or prophylactic effect in a human. Tags such as haemagglutinin (HA), myc, poly-histidine, FLAG, or streptavidin-binding peptide may be incorporated for purification or detection, though the therapeutic antibodies of the invention are typically tag-free. The library-based screening method involved isolating M2e-specific B cells, constructing scFv libraries, and selecting binders via FACS. The monoclonal antibodies are fully human, with defined LCVR and HCVR sequences. Clonally related antibodies share identical CDRs but may differ in framework regions. Preferred embodiments include LCVRs with SEQ ID NO:1–10 and HCVRs with SEQ ID NO:11–20, with D005 comprising LCVR SEQ ID NO:1 and HCVR SEQ ID NO:11. The antigen-binding site is formed by the paired CDRs of these regions. Sequence similarities define groups of LCVRs and HCVRs, with the most abundant combinations reflecting natural pairing. Cloning artifacts were removed by sequence validation. The lead antibody, D005, exhibits high affinity (Kd ~4–5 nM) and cross-reactivity with M2e variants. It binds cell-surface M2 and mediates protection in mice via ADCC. The nucleic acid molecules encoding these variable regions are provided, along with expression vectors (e.g., EBV-based episomal vectors) and host cells (e.g., HEK-293T) for recombinant production. Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the antibody in a carrier suitable for intravenous or intramuscular administration. Methods of passive immunization, treatment, and prophylaxis are disclosed, as is the use of the antibody in medicament manufacture. The invention also enables detection of M2 protein in diagnostic assays. Administration is typically systemic, with single-dose regimens showing efficacy. The antibodies are formatted as IgG1 with κ light chains and γ1 heavy chains, ensuring optimal half-life and effector function engagement. Binding to M2e is specific, with epitope mapping confirming recognition of the conserved N-terminal epitope SLLTEVETPTRSE. The minimal epitope and variant recognition are characterized. The most preferred embodiments include the fully human IgG1-D005 with the specified LCVR and HCVR, produced recombinantly and formulated for clinical use. Examples demonstrate isolation, expression, binding analysis, and in vivo efficacy, confirming the therapeutic potential of these natural human antibodies against Influenza A.
