## BACKGROUND

The localization and tracking of acoustic sources in underwater environments present significant challenges due to the complex nature of acoustic wave propagation in such media. In shallow-water environments, multipath propagation—where sound waves reflect off the surface and seabed—creates intricate interference patterns that complicate the interpretation of received signals. While these multipath effects introduce difficulties in source localization, they also provide the necessary spatial diversity that enables estimation of source positions in range, depth, and azimuth. Traditional passive sonar systems rely on arrays of hydrophones to capture these acoustic signals and infer the location of emitting sources. However, the nonlinear relationship between source position and the measured acoustic field, combined with environmental uncertainties and model inaccuracies, renders accurate and robust source tracking a persistent challenge in underwater acoustics. These limitations have motivated the development of advanced signal processing techniques capable of overcoming the inherent ambiguities and computational burdens associated with conventional approaches.

The present invention introduces a sparsity-driven framework for the broadband localization and tracking of acoustic sources using passive sonar. This framework is predicated on the observation that, at any given time, only a small number of locations within a discretized search space correspond to actual acoustic sources, while the remainder are inactive. This inherent sparsity is exploited to formulate a computationally efficient and robust estimation problem. Central to this approach is the concept of a source location map (SLM), which represents the spatial distribution of acoustic activity across a predefined grid of tentative source locations. Each SLM assigns a value to every grid point, with nonzero values indicating the presence of a source and zero values denoting absence.

The invention employs an iterative solver based on the proximal gradient (PG) method to estimate the SLMs from measured acoustic data. This solver is specifically designed to handle the high-dimensional, structured sparsity of the underlying regression problem while maintaining computational tractability. The motivation for tracking acoustic sources stems from critical applications in maritime surveillance, environmental monitoring, and naval operations, where real-time knowledge of source trajectories is essential for situational awareness and decision-making.

Underwater source localization is particularly challenging due to the dynamic and uncertain nature of the acoustic propagation environment. Traditional matched-field processing (MFP) techniques attempt to address this by comparing measured acoustic fields with model-predicted replicas generated for a grid of candidate source locations. However, MFP suffers from several limitations, including sensitivity to model mismatch, poor performance in multi-source scenarios, and the generation of ambiguous ambiguity surfaces that obscure true source locations. Matched-field tracking (MFT) extends MFP to the temporal domain by constructing sequences of ambiguity surfaces and linking peaks across time to form tracks. Yet, MFT is computationally prohibitive for multiple sources due to the combinatorial explosion of possible track hypotheses and is inherently batch-oriented, making it unsuitable for real-time implementation.

Bayesian approaches offer a principled framework for joint tracking and parameter estimation but often rely on Markov Chain Monte Carlo methods that are too computationally intensive for online use. Sparsity-driven Kalman-filter methods leverage the sparse structure of the source distribution but become impractical when applied to fine-grained grids due to the high dimensionality of the state vector. Relevance vector machine-based Bayesian trackers also face scalability issues owing to the need to maintain large covariance matrices.

To overcome these limitations, the present invention introduces a novel sparsity-driven framework that integrates temporal coherence, group sparsity across frequency bands, and resilience to model mismatch. The system architecture, referred to herein as system 10, includes an acoustic sensor array 50 comprising a plurality of hydrophones arranged to capture spatially distributed acoustic pressure measurements. These time-series data are transformed into the frequency domain using a short-time Fourier transform (STFT) to yield a set of Fourier coefficient vectors for each frequency of interest.

An acoustic propagation model, such as the KRAKEN normal-mode model, is used to compute replicas—model-predicted frequency-domain responses—for each grid point in the search space. The spectral passive-acoustic tracking problem is then formulated as the estimation of a regression coefficient matrix S(t), whose rows correspond to grid locations and whose columns correspond to frequencies. The entries of S(t) represent the complex amplitudes of the acoustic sources at each location and frequency.

The estimation of S(t) is performed by solving a regularized optimization problem that enforces both group sparsity—encouraging entire rows of S(t) to be zero except for those corresponding to active sources—and temporal smoothness—biasing the current estimate toward the previous SLM. This leads to the formulation of a real-valued convex optimization problem after decomposing complex variables into their real and imaginary parts. The resulting problem is solved using a proximal gradient algorithm that exploits the problem’s structure to enable parallel updates and closed-form proximal operations.

The PG algorithm iteratively refines the estimate of S(t) by alternating between a gradient-descent step on the smooth part of the cost function and a proximal step that enforces sparsity. A majorizer H is constructed to upper-bound the smooth cost, ensuring convergence. The algorithm terminates when successive iterates differ by less than a predefined threshold. An accelerated variant of the PG algorithm may also be employed to achieve faster convergence.

In operation, the system collects time-series acoustic data, computes Fourier coefficients, generates replicas using the acoustic model, and solves the optimization problem to produce an SLM. The SLM is then used to identify source locations by detecting peaks in the map. The method is implemented as a series of software modules executable on a computing device, with data and intermediate results stored on non-transitory storage media. Performance evaluations using the SWellEX-3 dataset demonstrate the method’s ability to track single and multiple sources, even in the presence of model mismatch, and show superior computational efficiency compared to MFT.

The operational environment includes a known bathymetry profile and a moving acoustic source, with the hydrophone array deployed at a fixed depth. The grid of tentative locations spans the expected range and depth of the source. The method successfully reconstructs the source trajectory, with performance degrading only when severe environmental mismatch occurs.
