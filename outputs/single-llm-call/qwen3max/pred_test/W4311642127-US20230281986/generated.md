# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of computer vision and artificial intelligence, and more specifically to systems and methods for video anomaly detection. In particular, the invention provides a novel framework for zero-shot cross-domain video anomaly detection (zxVAD) that enables the identification of anomalous events in video streams captured in domains or environments for which no training data is available. The disclosed system operates without requiring any labeled abnormal examples, pre-trained object detectors, or adaptation procedures involving target domain data, thereby addressing critical limitations in existing unsupervised and cross-domain video anomaly detection approaches. The invention is particularly suited for real-world surveillance, security monitoring, industrial safety, and smart infrastructure applications where access to target-specific training data is impractical, restricted due to privacy or intellectual property concerns, or simply unavailable at deployment time.

## BACKGROUND

Unsupervised video anomaly detection (VAD) has emerged as a preferred paradigm in practical surveillance systems because it eliminates the need for labor-intensive annotation of rare abnormal events. Traditional unsupervised VAD methods train exclusively on videos depicting normal activities and subsequently flag deviations from learned normal patterns as anomalies during inference. While effective within the same domain, these methods suffer from poor generalization when applied to videos captured under different environmental conditions, camera viewpoints, lighting, or scene semantics—a scenario commonly referred to as cross-domain video anomaly detection (xVAD). Recent xVAD approaches have attempted to bridge this domain gap by either utilizing paired source and target domain training data or leveraging strong priors such as pre-trained object detectors (e.g., YOLOv3) to extract foreground objects for focused anomaly analysis. However, both strategies impose significant practical constraints: collecting even unlabeled target domain data may be infeasible for end-users seeking out-of-the-box solutions, and reliance on external object detectors introduces computational overhead, licensing dependencies, and potential failure modes due to detector inaccuracies or domain shifts affecting detector performance.

These limitations highlight a critical unmet need in the field: a truly zero-shot xVAD system that requires no target domain data—neither for training nor fine-tuning—and operates without external supervision or strong priors. Such a system must generalize across unseen domains while maintaining high detection accuracy and computational efficiency. Prior art fails to address this stringent yet realistic operational requirement, often assuming some form of target-domain exposure or auxiliary model components that compromise deployability. Consequently, there exists a pressing need for an improved xVAD framework that achieves robust cross-domain anomaly detection with minimal assumptions, reduced computational footprint, and complete independence from target-specific information or third-party models.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a zero-shot cross-domain video anomaly detection system designed to detect anomalous events in a target domain without any access to target domain training data or prior knowledge of anomaly types. The system begins by collecting video sequences from a source domain containing only normal events. From these sequences, images of foreground objects are extracted using a novel, training-free mechanism based on randomly initialized convolutional neural networks. A first neural network, configured as a future-frame prediction generator, is trained to reconstruct upcoming video frames based on historical context. Concurrently, a second neural network, termed the normalcy classifier module, is trained to distinguish between predicted normal frames and synthetically generated pseudo-abnormal frames. This pseudo-abnormality is created through an object-aware anomaly synthesis module that pastes localized foreground objects—extracted from either source-domain or task-irrelevant videos—onto normal frames at random locations and scales. The joint training of the generator and normalcy classifier is guided by a suite of novel loss functions, including normalcy loss, relative normalcy loss, attention affirmation loss, and relative attention affirmation loss, which collectively enforce a “relative normalcy” learning strategy. During inference, the trained system processes video from the target domain and performs anomaly detection by evaluating reconstruction errors and classifier outputs, thereby enabling accurate, efficient, and truly zero-shot cross-domain anomaly detection.

### System Overview

The training system of the present invention comprises several interconnected components orchestrated to achieve zero-shot cross-domain video anomaly detection. These include a memory storage unit, one or more processors, and specialized software modules executing on the processor(s). The memory stores source domain video data consisting exclusively of normal events, along with optional task-irrelevant (TI) video datasets sourced from non-VAD tasks such as action recognition. The processor executes instructions to implement a foreground object extraction pipeline that leverages a randomly initialized convolutional neural network (CNN)—never trained on any dataset—to localize salient regions in input frames via channel-wise summation and thresholding, producing binary masks indicative of foreground objects.

A first neural network, designated as the future frame prediction module, is implemented as a U-Net-based generator augmented with a memory module at its bottleneck. This generator receives a sequence of T consecutive video frames and predicts the (T+1)-th frame. Its training is governed by a composite loss combining reconstruction loss (mean squared error and structural similarity index), gradient loss, adversarial loss from a PatchGAN discriminator, and sparsity regularization on memory addressing vectors. A second neural network, the normalcy classifier module, operates in parallel to classify frames as normal or abnormal. Critically, this classifier is trained not on real anomalies—which are absent—but on pseudo-abnormal frames synthesized by the object-aware anomaly synthesis module. This module takes foreground masks extracted from either source-domain or TI video frames and overlays them onto normal VAD frames using a randomized cut-and-paste operation, thereby generating diverse synthetic anomalies without requiring object annotations or detector models.

The training process employs four novel loss functions to guide the normalcy classifier. The normalcy loss enforces correct binary classification of predicted normal versus pseudo-abnormal frames. The relative normalcy loss introduces a comparative learning objective, maximizing the margin between the classifier’s confidence in normal frames versus pseudo-abnormal ones. The attention affirmation loss ensures that the classifier’s decision is spatially aligned with ground-truth anomaly locations derived from the synthesis process, by comparing attention maps (obtained via SCDA on classifier feature maps) against known mask positions. Finally, the relative attention affirmation loss extends this concept using metric learning principles—specifically ArcFace loss—to enforce greater similarity between attention maps of normal and augmented-normal frames than between normal and pseudo-abnormal frames, thereby enhancing intra-class compactness and inter-class separation.

Joint end-to-end training of the generator and normalcy classifier optimizes all parameters simultaneously, with the untrained CNN for object extraction remaining fixed throughout. At inference time, the system processes target domain video by feeding frame sequences into the trained generator; anomalies are detected based on elevated reconstruction errors and low normalcy scores from the classifier.
