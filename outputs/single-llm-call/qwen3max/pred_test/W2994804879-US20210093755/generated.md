# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of endoscopic biliary interventions, and more particularly to a medical device and associated method for facilitating nasobiliary drainage following endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP). Specifically, the invention provides an improved apparatus and technique for repositioning an endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) catheter from the oral cavity to the nasal passage without requiring direct manual manipulation within the patient’s mouth. The invention is especially suited for use in sedated patients undergoing ERCP procedures where conventional repositioning methods pose risks of injury to both the patient and the operator, and where procedural efficiency is critical to clinical workflow and patient safety.

## BACKGROUND ART

Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) is a well-established interventional endoscopic procedure used for the diagnosis and treatment of biliary and pancreatic disorders, including cholangitis, biliary obstruction, and bile leaks. In many such cases, endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) is performed to provide external biliary decompression, facilitate bile sampling for cytological or microbiological analysis, and reduce serum bilirubin levels more effectively than internal stenting alone. A critical step in completing ENBD involves repositioning the distal end of the drainage catheter from the oral cavity—where it exits following ERCP—through the nasopharynx and out through the nostril, thereby establishing a secure external drainage route.

The conventional repositioning technique requires the removal of the endoscopic mouthpiece after scope withdrawal, followed by manual insertion of a plastic nasal tube through one nostril into the pharynx. The operator must then reach into the patient’s mouth—often while the patient is still sedated or semi-conscious—to grasp the emerging tip of the nasal tube using fingers or forceps. Once retrieved, the tube is connected to the ENBD catheter, and the assembly is pulled through the nasal passage. This method suffers from several significant limitations. First, the mouthpiece must be removed before repositioning, which often necessitates waiting for the patient to regain sufficient consciousness to open their mouth voluntarily—a delay that prolongs procedural time and increases resource utilization. Second, manual retrieval of the tube tip carries a high risk of oral trauma, including mucosal lacerations, tooth damage, or gagging-induced vomiting, particularly in patients with anatomical challenges such as a short neck, limited jaw opening, or a high Mallampati score indicating poor oropharyngeal visibility.

Moreover, the operator is frequently exposed to injury, most commonly finger bites from uncooperative or reflexively clenching patients during deep sedation. Use of forceps may mitigate direct contact but introduces additional risks of iatrogenic pharyngeal or dental injury due to blind grasping in poorly visualized anatomy. Prior attempts to improve this process, such as forming a wire loop from a guidewire to capture the nasal tube, have proven unreliable due to slippage, inconsistent loop geometry, and dependence on real-time fluoroscopic or endoscopic guidance, which adds complexity and radiation exposure. These shortcomings underscore a clear and persistent need for a safe, efficient, and universally applicable solution that eliminates manual intraoral manipulation, reduces procedural time, minimizes complications, and enables successful ENBD completion even in anatomically challenging or deeply sedated patients.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The present invention addresses the technical problem of inefficiency, safety hazards, and procedural unpredictability inherent in conventional ENBD catheter repositioning methods. Specifically, existing techniques require direct manual access to the patient’s oral cavity, leading to prolonged procedure times, risk of oral and dental injury, potential harm to the operator, and failure in patients with unfavorable oropharyngeal anatomy. The invention seeks to eliminate these drawbacks by providing a device and method that enable reliable, rapid, and non-invasive transnasal repositioning of the ENBD catheter without intraoral manipulation.

### Technical Solution

The invention provides a novel medical device comprising three primary components: a ring-type module, a nasal tube, and an adjuster. The ring-type module includes a curved ring part configured to fit comfortably within the oropharynx and a rigid ring handle extending externally for operator control. The nasal tube is a flexible, biocompatible tube marked with multistep depth indicators and designed for insertion through the nostril into the nasopharynx. The adjuster, integrated into the system, allows for precise control over the engagement between the ring part and the nasal tube. In use, the ENBD catheter is first withdrawn orally with the endoscope. The ring-type module is then inserted through the mouth into the oropharynx, while the nasal tube is simultaneously advanced through the nostril until its distal end enters the oropharyngeal space. The ring part captures the nasal tube, and the operator pulls the ring handle, drawing the nasal tube through the oropharynx and out the mouth. The nasal tube is then connected to the ENBD catheter, and the entire assembly is pulled through the nasal passage to complete drainage setup—all without the need to remove the mouthpiece or insert fingers into the patient’s mouth.

### Advantageous Effects

The invention offers multiple advantageous effects over prior art. It significantly enhances procedural safety by eliminating direct contact with the patient’s oral cavity, thereby preventing mucosal injury, tooth damage, and operator finger injuries. The method is highly efficient, reducing ENBD repositioning time by approximately 77% compared to conventional techniques, as demonstrated in clinical studies. The device is adjustable via multistep markers on the nasal tube, allowing customization based on patient anatomy such as neck length or Mallampati class, ensuring consistent success across diverse populations. Furthermore, the design is economically efficient, constructed from reusable or low-cost disposable materials, and requires no additional imaging or specialized equipment, making it suitable for widespread adoption in both high- and low-resource settings.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The invention is embodied in a medical device specifically engineered for ENBD catheter repositioning. The core component is the ring-type module, which consists of a ring part and a ring handle. The ring part is formed as a smooth, curved loop made of medical-grade stainless steel or nitinol, with a diameter optimized to encircle the nasal tube without causing tissue trauma. The ring handle is a straight, rigid bar attached perpendicularly to the ring part, enabling precise manipulation and withdrawal. An adjuster mechanism, such as a sliding groove with a fixing means (e.g., a locking screw or snap-fit), allows fine-tuning of the ring’s orientation relative to the handle for optimal alignment during insertion.

The nasal tube is a flexible polymer tube, typically 120–150 cm in length, with an outer diameter compatible with standard ENBD catheters. It features a multistep marking system: a first marker indicates minimal safe insertion depth, a second marker corresponds to average adult anatomy, and a third marker accommodates larger patients or those with elongated nasopharyngeal pathways. These markers guide consistent, anatomy-adapted placement without guesswork.

In practice, after ERCP and oral withdrawal of the ENBD catheter, the ring-type module is introduced orally into the oropharynx while the patient remains sedated and the mouthpiece stays in place. Concurrently, the nasal tube is inserted through the selected nostril up to the appropriate multistep mark. The ring part naturally captures the distal end of the nasal tube as both reside in the oropharynx. The operator then withdraws the ring handle, pulling the nasal tube out through the mouth. The externalized end of the nasal tube is connected to the ENBD catheter using a standard Luer-lock or friction-fit coupling. Finally, the nasal tube is gently pulled through the nasal passage, dragging the ENBD catheter into position, after which the nasal tube is separated and discarded.

Clinical evaluation involving 145 patients demonstrated a mean repositioning time of 44 seconds with the inventive device versus 194 seconds conventionally (p<0.001). The success rate was 97.3%, with failures only in cases of excessive J-loop insertion depth—easily corrected by technique refinement. Critically, zero incidents of oral cavity damage, tooth injury, pharyngeal bleeding, or operator injury were recorded. The device proved equally effective across all Mallampati classes, confirming its utility in anatomically challenging patients. Moreover, it enabled immediate post-ERCP repositioning without waiting for patient arousal, streamlining workflow. The device is operable by physicians of varying skill levels, and its material composition ensures durability, sterilizability, and biocompatibility. The scope of the invention encompasses not only the specific embodiment described but also variants incorporating alternative ring geometries, handle ergonomics, or marker configurations, provided they achieve the core function of non-manual, efficient, and safe ENBD catheter repositioning.
