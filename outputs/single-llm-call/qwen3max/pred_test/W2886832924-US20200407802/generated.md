## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods and systems for measuring genomic DNA methylation loss, particularly replication-associated DNA methylation loss occurring in partially methylated domains (PMDs) of the genome. More specifically, the invention provides a novel molecular signature—termed the Solo-WCGW motif—that enables precise quantification of cumulative mitotic cell divisions through the analysis of DNA methylation patterns at specific CpG dinucleotides. The invention further encompasses diagnostic, prognostic, and research tools based on this signature for assessing biological age, tissue turnover rates, cancer progression, and environmental impacts on cellular proliferation.

## BACKGROUND

DNA methylation, a key epigenetic modification involving the addition of a methyl group to the cytosine residue in CpG dinucleotides, plays a critical role in regulating gene expression, maintaining genomic stability, and suppressing transposable elements. A well-documented phenomenon in both normal development and disease is the progressive loss of DNA methylation across large genomic regions known as partially methylated domains (PMDs). These domains, often spanning hundreds of kilobases to megabases, are characterized by reduced average methylation levels compared to highly methylated domains (HMDs). While PMD hypomethylation has been observed in numerous cancers and aging tissues, the underlying mechanisms and biological significance have remained incompletely understood.

Genomic studies have demonstrated that PMDs are enriched in late-replicating regions, lamina-associated domains (LADs), and heterochromatic compartments identified by Hi-C chromatin conformation capture. These observations suggest a link between nuclear architecture, replication timing, and DNA methylation maintenance. However, conflicting evidence exists regarding the extent and consistency of PMD hypomethylation across different cell types and developmental stages. Some reports indicate that PMDs are largely absent in pluripotent stem cells and early embryos, while others describe their presence in differentiated somatic tissues. Moreover, the relationship between PMD hypomethylation and chronological age versus mitotic history has been unclear, with prior studies yielding inconsistent conclusions about whether methylation loss reflects time per se or the cumulative number of cell divisions.

Previous attempts to define PMDs relied on whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS) data analyzed using hidden Markov models (HMMs) or other segmentation algorithms applied to all CpG sites. These approaches often suffered from low signal-to-noise ratios in normal tissues or weakly hypomethylated tumors, limiting their sensitivity and reproducibility. Furthermore, existing “epigenetic clocks” based on methylation at specific CpG sites—such as the Hannum clock, Horvath’s DNAm Age, or PhenoAge—primarily correlate with chronological age and are influenced by transcriptional regulation rather than directly measuring replicative history. There remains a critical need for a robust, sequence-informed method to quantify replication-associated DNA methylation loss that is independent of confounding factors such to transcription, CpG island status, or technical noise.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel and highly sensitive method for measuring replication-associated DNA methylation loss by focusing on a specific subset of CpG dinucleotides defined by the Solo-WCGW sequence motif. Through comprehensive whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (WGBS) experiments across thousands of human and mouse samples—including tumors, normal tissues, developmental stages, and primary cell types—the inventors identified that CpGs occurring in isolation (i.e., with no neighboring CpGs within ±35 base pairs) and flanked by adenine or thymine residues on both sides (WCGW, where W = A/T) are exquisitely prone to hypomethylation in a manner that directly reflects the cumulative number of cell divisions.

The invention establishes that PMD hypomethylation is not merely a stochastic artifact but a deterministic process driven by mitotic turnover. By analyzing the methylation status of Solo-WCGW CpGs, one can determine the degree of PMD hypomethylation, which serves as a quantitative readout of the total mitotic history of a cell or tissue. This metric correlates strongly with donor age in diverse hematopoietic lineages, accelerates upon environmental stressors such as UV exposure in skin, and emerges progressively during embryonic development. Critically, the invention demonstrates that PMD hypomethylation is nearly universal in cancer and varies systematically across tumor types in accordance with their proliferative activity and somatic mutation burden.

The method involves obtaining a test cell or tissue sample, extracting genomic DNA, and generating methylation data for CpG dinucleotides—preferably via WGBS or high-density microarray platforms such as HM450. From this dataset, only CpG sites matching the Solo-WCGW motif are retained, excluding those located within CpG islands, H3K36me3-marked regions, or non-intergenic contexts when appropriate. The mean methylation value of these selected CpGs within defined PMD regions is then calculated to yield a measure of replication-associated DNA methylation loss. This value reflects the cumulative number of effective cell divisions the sample has undergone since its origin in the zygote.

The invention further defines PMDs using objective criteria such as standard deviation (SD) of Solo-WCGW methylation across multiple samples, late replication timing, nuclear lamina localization, or Hi-C-defined heterochromatic compartment B. It enables identification of common PMDs shared across cell types, as well as cell-type-specific PMDs that reflect lineage-specific replicative dynamics. The number of Solo-WCGW motifs analyzed may be varied to optimize sensitivity, and the x value in the general n(x)WCpGWn(x) motif may be adjusted to refine specificity. The resulting metric is distinct from traditional epigenetic clocks and provides a direct window into the mitotic clock governing cellular aging and oncogenesis.
