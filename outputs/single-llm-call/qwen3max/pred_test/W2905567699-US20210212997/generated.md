# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention resides in the field of oncology and immunotherapy, specifically relating to methods and compositions for treating cancer by modulating integrin CD11b signaling in myeloid cells. The invention further encompasses diagnostic and therapeutic strategies based on the regulation of macrophage polarization through CD11b activation or inhibition of microRNA Let7a, thereby altering the tumor immune microenvironment and vascular architecture to suppress tumor growth and enhance responsiveness to conventional chemotherapeutic agents.

### Background Information

Myeloid cells, including macrophages, monocytes, and neutrophils, are critical components of both innate and adaptive immune responses and play pivotal roles in acute and chronic inflammation. In the context of cancer, these cells exhibit remarkable plasticity, capable of adopting either pro-inflammatory or immune suppressive phenotypes that profoundly influence tumor progression. Pro-inflammatory myeloid cells contribute to anti-tumor immunity by stimulating cytotoxic T cell activity and producing reactive oxygen and nitrogen species that can eliminate malignant cells. Conversely, immune suppressive myeloid cells, particularly tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), promote tumor immune evasion, angiogenesis, metastasis, and resistance to therapy through the secretion of cytokines such as IL-10, TGF-β, and IL-6.

Macrophage polarization—the process by which macrophages adopt distinct functional phenotypes in response to environmental cues—is a central mechanism governing their role in tumor biology. Classically activated (M1) macrophages, induced by interferon-gamma (IFNγ) and lipopolysaccharide (LPS), exhibit anti-tumor properties through the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-α, IL-12, and nitric oxide. Alternatively activated (M2) macrophages, driven by IL-4, IL-13, or TGF-β, support tissue repair, angiogenesis, and immune suppression, thereby facilitating tumor growth. The balance between these phenotypes within the tumor microenvironment is a key determinant of clinical outcomes.

Colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R) inhibitors have emerged as promising therapeutic agents aimed at depleting or reprogramming TAMs. By blocking CSF1R signaling, these inhibitors reduce the recruitment and survival of pro-tumoral macrophages, thereby enhancing anti-tumor immunity. However, clinical efficacy has been limited, underscoring the need for complementary strategies that target alternative pathways regulating myeloid cell function.

Integrin CD11b (also known as αM or ITGAM), which pairs with CD18 (β2) to form the Mac-1 (αMβ2) heterodimer, is a myeloid-specific adhesion receptor that binds ligands such as ICAM-1, fibrinogen, and complement component iC3b. While traditionally associated with leukocyte adhesion, migration, and phagocytosis during inflammation, recent evidence suggests that CD11b also transduces intracellular signals that modulate immune cell activation. Unlike other integrins such as α4β1, which mediates myeloid cell trafficking into tumors, CD11b does not appear essential for cellular infiltration but instead regulates functional polarization once cells are within the tumor niche.

Despite advances in understanding myeloid cell biology, there remains a critical unmet need for novel cancer therapeutics that effectively reprogram the tumor microenvironment to overcome immune suppression and vascular abnormalities. Current approaches often fail to address the dual challenges of immune evasion and dysfunctional tumor vasculature, which together limit drug delivery and T cell infiltration. Thus, there is a pressing need for agents that simultaneously enhance anti-tumor immunity and normalize tumor blood vessels—objectives that the present invention fulfills through targeted modulation of CD11b signaling and downstream microRNA networks.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for treating cancer by activating integrin CD11b signaling in myeloid cells, thereby promoting a pro-inflammatory macrophage phenotype and suppressing tumor growth. CD11b signaling exerts potent anti-tumor effects by inhibiting immune suppressive gene expression, modulating neovascularization, and enhancing CD8+ T cell recruitment to tumors. A key mechanism involves the upregulation of microRNA Let7a, which suppresses IL-6 expression and downstream STAT3 activation, thereby breaking a critical axis of immune suppression in the tumor microenvironment.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method of treating cancer in a subject comprising administering an agonist of CD11b expression or activity. Such agonists include small molecule activators such as Leukadherin 1 (LA1), which stabilizes CD11b in its high-affinity conformation, thereby enhancing its signaling capacity. The method is applicable to a broad spectrum of cancers, including but not limited to lung carcinoma (e.g., Lewis Lung Carcinoma), melanoma (e.g., B16), breast cancer (e.g., PyMT-driven mammary tumors, CL66, MDA-MB-231), and other solid tumors characterized by abundant myeloid infiltration.

The invention further encompasses combination therapy, wherein a CD11b agonist is administered concurrently or sequentially with a chemotherapeutic agent such as gemcitabine or taxol. This approach leverages the ability of CD11b activation to normalize tumor vasculature—by reducing PDGF-BB/VEGF-A ratios—and thereby improve drug perfusion and efficacy. In particular, while CD11b loss increases pericyte coverage and vessel maturity (which can paradoxically enhance chemotherapy delivery), CD11b *activation* repolarizes macrophages toward an anti-tumor state, creating a synergistic effect when combined with cytotoxic agents.

Additionally, the invention provides a method of treating cancer by administering an agent that enhances Let7a microRNA activity or expression. Since Let7a directly targets IL-6 and c-Myc—both drivers of immune suppressive macrophage polarization—restoration of Let7a levels reverses the M2-like phenotype and restores anti-tumor immunity. Conversely, in contexts where enhanced vascular normalization is desired to improve chemotherapy access, transient inhibition of Let7a using anti-miR oligonucleotides may be employed, though this is balanced against its pro-tumor immune effects.

A transgenic non-human mammal is provided, specifically a constitutively activated CD11b knockin mouse harboring an I332G mutation in the Itgam gene. This model exhibits reduced tumor growth and enhanced anti-tumor immunity, serving as a valuable tool for preclinical validation of CD11b-targeted therapies.

The invention also includes methods of enhancing anti-tumor immunity by promoting CD11b-mediated signaling in myeloid cells, leading to increased CD8+ T cell infiltration and decreased regulatory T cell accumulation. Furthermore, it provides a method of rendering cancer cells susceptible to therapeutic regimens by modulating the tumor microenvironment via CD11b activation, thereby overcoming stromal barriers to treatment.

Finally, the invention enables screening for therapeutic agents that modulate CD11b signaling or Let7a expression by assessing changes in macrophage polarization, cytokine profiles, or tumor growth in vitro or in vivo. Such screens facilitate the identification of novel agonists, antagonists, or modulators of this pathway for cancer therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the discovery that integrin CD11b signaling in myeloid cells, particularly macrophages, plays a pivotal role in restraining tumor progression by promoting a pro-inflammatory transcriptional program and inhibiting immune suppressive polarization. Unlike its role in acute inflammation, CD11b is not required for myeloid cell trafficking into tumors but instead functions as a molecular switch that determines macrophage phenotype within the tumor microenvironment. Activation of CD11b—whether through genetic means (e.g., I332G knockin) or pharmacological agonists such as Leukadherin 1 (LA1)—suppresses the expression of immune suppressive factors (e.g., Arg1, Il10, Tgfb, Il6, Pdgfb) and enhances pro-inflammatory mediators (e.g., Nos2, Tnfa, Ifng, Il12b), thereby shifting the balance toward anti-tumor immunity.

This shift is mediated in part through the upregulation of microRNA Let7a, which directly targets Il6 mRNA and suppresses its translation. IL-6, in turn, drives STAT3 phosphorylation and the expression of downstream immune suppressive genes. In CD11b-deficient (Itgam−/−) macrophages, Let7a expression is markedly reduced, leading to elevated IL-6, constitutive STAT3 activation, and enhanced M2-like polarization. Restoring Let7a—via pre-miR transfection or CD11b agonism—reverses this phenotype, demonstrating a linear CD11b–Let7a–IL-6–STAT3 axis that governs macrophage function in cancer.

Moreover, CD11b signaling modulates tumor neovascularization by controlling the PDGF-BB/VEGF-A ratio. Itgam−/− macrophages overexpress PDGF-BB, leading to increased pericyte coverage, vessel maturation, and reduced vascular leakiness. While this "normalization" improves tumor perfusion and may enhance chemotherapy delivery, it also supports tumor growth in the absence of immune intervention. CD11b activation, by contrast, reduces PDGF-BB and promotes a more chaotic, immature vasculature—but crucially, this occurs in the context of a reprogrammed immune microenvironment that dominates to suppress tumor progression.

The invention utilizes isolated cells, including bone marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs) from wild-type or genetically modified mice, or human macrophages differentiated from peripheral blood monocytes. The amino acid sequence of CD11b is well-known (UniProt P11215), and the I332G mutation substitutes isoleucine at position 332 with glycine in the β-propeller domain, locking the integrin in an active conformation.

Macrophages express multiple integrins, including VCAM receptor α4β1, which mediates trafficking into tumors, and CD11b (αMβ2), which regulates post-infiltration function. Itgam−/− macrophages show enhanced immune suppressive gene expression even under pro-inflammatory stimulation. F4/80+ TAMs from Itgam−/− mice similarly exhibit M2-skewing. siRNA knockdown of CD11b recapitulates this phenotype, confirming its cell-intrinsic role. Inhibitory CD11b antibodies block adhesion to ICAM-1 and induce immune suppression, whereas adhesion to ICAM-1 (but not VCAM-1 or suspension) promotes pro-inflammatory transcription.

CD11b ligation enhances NFκB activation, as evidenced by increased Ser536 phosphorylation of p65. Adoptive transfer of Itgam−/− macrophages accelerates tumor growth in both WT and Itgam−/− hosts, confirming their pro-tumoral function. Tumors in Itgam−/− mice grow faster across multiple models (LLC, B16, PyMT) and show reduced CD8+ T cells and increased Tregs. In contrast, I332G knockin mice—with constitutively active CD11b—exhibit suppressed tumor growth.

Immune suppressive cytokines (IL-4, TGF-β, tumor-conditioned medium) downregulate CD11b expression, creating a feedback loop that reinforces M2 polarization. CD11b loss increases PDGF-BB, leading to pericyte-coated, normalized vessels with reduced leakiness. Imatinib (a PDGFR inhibitor) reverses the enhanced tumor growth in Itgam−/− mice, confirming PDGF-BB’s role.

Mechanistically, CD11b suppresses c-Myc, which represses Let7 transcription. Let7a, in turn, inhibits IL-6 and c-Myc, forming a double-negative feedback loop. LA1, a small molecule CD11b agonist, stimulates Let7a, inhibits IL-6/PDGF-BB, repolarizes TAMs, recruits CD8+ T cells, and suppresses tumor growth in murine and human xenograft models. LA1’s effects are CD11b-dependent, as they are absent in Itgam−/− mice.

Anti-miR Let7a delivery mimics CD11b loss, promoting tumor growth but enhancing chemotherapy response due to vascular normalization. Thus, the invention provides context-dependent strategies: CD11b agonists for immune activation, or Let7a inhibition for chemo-sensitization.

The composition of the invention comprises a CD11b agonist (e.g., LA1) or a Let7a mimic, formulated for systemic administration. These agents may be used alone or in combination with chemotherapy, immunotherapy, or radiation.

### EXAMPLE 1

To exemplify the invention, murine Lewis Lung Carcinoma (LLC), B16 melanoma, CL66 breast cancer, and human MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cell lines were obtained from ATCC and authenticated by sequencing and mycoplasma testing. C57BL/6 wild-type and Itgam−/− mice were sourced from Jackson Laboratories. A constitutively activated CD11b knockin mouse strain (ITGAM I332G) was generated by homologous recombination in embryonic stem cells, introducing a glycine substitution at isoleucine 332 in exon 9 of the Itgam gene. Correctly targeted clones were identified by PCR, Southern blot, and sequencing, then injected into blastocysts to generate chimeras. Heterozygous mice were bred to homozygosity and backcrossed to C57BL/6 for six generations. Mice were housed under pathogen-free conditions with ad libitum access to food and water.

For tumor studies, 5×10⁵ LLC cells were injected subcutaneously into syngeneic mice. Tumor volume was measured biweekly using calipers and calculated as (length² × width)/2. Mice were randomized into cohorts based on initial tumor size and treated with vehicle, LA1 (2–6 mg/kg i.p. daily), gemcitabine, taxol, or combinations. At endpoint, tumors were harvested for histology, RNA analysis, or flow cytometry.

Tissues were cryopreserved in OCT or fixed in formalin. Immunohistochemistry was performed on 5-μm sections using antibodies against CD31, desmin, SMA, F4/80, CD4, and CD8. Fluorescent images were acquired on a Nikon Eclipse TE2000-U microscope and quantified using Metamorph software. Flow cytometry was conducted on single-cell suspensions from digested tumors, stained with viability dye, Fc block, and fluorophore-conjugated antibodies against CD45, CD11b, F4/80, Gr1, CD3, CD4, and CD8. Data were analyzed using FlowJo.

Human macrophages were differentiated from apheresis-derived monocytes cultured in RPMI with 20% serum and 50 ng/mL M-CSF for 6 days. Murine BMDMs were generated similarly from bone marrow flushed from femurs and tibias. Macrophages were polarized with IFNγ/LPS (M1) or IL-4 (M2) for 24–48 hours. Adhesion assays were performed by plating macrophages on ICAM-1, VCAM-1, or BSA-coated plates, with or without anti-CD11b antibodies.

Lentiviral transduction of BMDMs was used for stable gene expression, with viral titer assessed by GFP positivity. Gene expression was analyzed by qPCR using Qiagen QuantiTect primers, normalized to Gapdh. Immunoblotting was performed with antibodies against phospho-NFκB, total NFκB, c-Myc, and actin. ELISA measured secreted cytokines in supernatants.

In adoptive transfer experiments, F4/80+ TAMs or BMDMs were isolated by FACS, treated with LA1 or vehicle, and co-injected with tumor cells. siRNA knockdown of Itgam or Il6 was achieved via nucleofection. MicroRNA modulation used pre-miR or anti-miR Let7a delivered by siPORT or RGD-targeted nanoparticles.

Statistical analysis employed Student’s t-test, ANOVA with Tukey post-hoc, or non-parametric equivalents as appropriate, with p<0.05 considered significant. All experiments were blinded and randomized.

The invention builds upon prior art showing that PI3Kγ, CSF-1R, and integrin α4β1 regulate myeloid cell function in tumors. However, unlike these targets, CD11b uniquely controls macrophage polarization without affecting trafficking, offering a more precise therapeutic window. Previous studies on Mac-1 in neutrophil apoptosis, NFκB in inflammation, and PDGF-B in pericyte recruitment inform the current findings, but the link between CD11b, Let7a, and tumor immunity is novel.
