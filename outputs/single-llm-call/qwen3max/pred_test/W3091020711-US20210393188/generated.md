# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE DISVENTION

The present disclosure relates generally to health monitors, and more particularly to wearable or implantable systems and methods for the continuous, non-invasive monitoring of cardiovascular function through the analysis of time-series physiological data. The invention provides novel computational techniques for assessing baroreflex functionality by analyzing spontaneous fluctuations in arterial blood pressure and heart rate, enabling the identification of intermittent on and off states of baroreflex-mediated control. These techniques facilitate the diagnosis, classification, prediction, and management of hypertension and other autonomic dysfunctions without requiring external perturbations or pharmacological interventions. The disclosed systems integrate sensor hardware with advanced signal-processing algorithms to deliver real-time diagnostic insight into cardiovascular regulatory mechanisms, supporting personalized therapeutic strategies and improved models of chronic disease care.

## BACKGROUND

Hypertension, or chronically elevated arterial blood pressure (BP), is a leading risk factor for cardiovascular morbidity and mortality worldwide. Despite its prevalence, hypertension often remains undetected due to its asymptomatic nature during early stages, earning it the moniker “the silent killer.” Traditional clinical approaches rely on sporadic BP measurements taken during infrequent office visits, which fail to capture the dynamic, time-varying nature of BP regulation and may miss critical patterns indicative of underlying pathophysiology. This limitation is particularly problematic in cases of labile or masked hypertension, where BP fluctuates significantly outside clinical settings.

Wearable devices have emerged as promising tools for continuous physiological monitoring, offering the potential to transform hypertension from a condition diagnosed episodically into one managed proactively through longitudinal data. However, current wearable technologies predominantly focus on measuring raw BP values or heart rate without providing deeper insight into the regulatory mechanisms governing these parameters. Conventional BP measurement techniques—whether oscillometric, auscultatory, or invasive—are inherently limited by their reliance on discrete snapshots rather than continuous observation, thereby obscuring transient autonomic events that may precede sustained hypertension.

Non-continuous monitoring further suffers from an inability to assess the functional integrity of key homeostatic systems such as the arterial baroreflex, a rapid neural feedback loop that buffers acute changes in BP by modulating heart rate and vascular tone. While baroreflex sensitivity has long been studied using perturbative methods like the Valsalva maneuver or pharmacological challenges, these approaches are impractical for routine use and do not reflect baseline autonomic tone under natural conditions. Moreover, existing metrics such as baroreflex effectiveness index (BEI) are constrained to very short time scales (a few heartbeats) and are highly sensitive to respiratory artifacts, limiting their clinical utility.

These shortcomings highlight an urgent need for new healthcare delivery models that move beyond static vital sign recording toward dynamic, systems-level diagnostics. There is a corresponding need for computational frameworks capable of extracting meaningful physiological insight from continuous, unperturbed time-series data—particularly those that can identify subtle but clinically significant disruptions in autonomic control before they manifest as overt disease. Such innovations would enable earlier intervention, more precise phenotyping of hypertensive subtypes, and better prediction of treatment response, ultimately improving outcomes in cardiovascular medicine.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces novel techniques for identifying cardiovascular pathologies through the computational analysis of spontaneous, unperturbed time-series data of arterial blood pressure and heart rate. Specifically, the invention provides methods for diagnosing hypertension by quantifying the intermittent engagement and disengagement of baroreflex-mediated control of heart rate—a phenomenon herein referred to as baroreflex intermittency. The invention further enables the prediction of the onset of hypertensive disease by tracking age-related declines in baroreflex “on fraction,” defined as the proportion of time during which baroreflex coupling between blood pressure and heart rate is functionally active.

In addition, the invention supports the prediction of optimal treatments by correlating baroreflex dynamics with responsiveness to neuromodulatory therapies such as carotid baroreceptor activation. Central to the invention is a computational framework that analyzes continuous arterial pressure waveforms to extract systolic, diastolic, and mean arterial pressures along with pulse intervals, then applies a linear filter-based mathematical model of the baroreflex arc to simulate expected heart rate responses. By comparing observed heart rate dynamics to model predictions, the system identifies periods when the baroreflex is operating in an “on” state (physiologically coupled) versus an “off” state (decoupled).

The invention determines an indicator of cardiovascular disease—specifically, the on fraction—which has been shown to correlate strongly with mean arterial pressure in certain genetic models of hypertension. This metric is derived by comparing measured blood pressure and heart rate data against the mathematical model and computing the fraction of time during which the observed dynamics align with baroreflex-mediated control. Furthermore, the invention calculates the likelihood of developing hypertension by analyzing trends in on fraction over time, particularly in relation to aging and genetic predisposition. These capabilities collectively enable a new paradigm of precision cardiovascular diagnostics based on autonomic functional phenotyping rather than isolated pressure readings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention encompasses devices and methods for measuring blood flow and autonomic cardiovascular regulation through continuous, non-invasive monitoring. Central to the system are wearable sensor devices configured to capture high-fidelity arterial pressure waveforms and interbeat intervals over extended periods, typically during normal daily activities or sleep cycles. These sensors may include photoplethysmographic (PPG), impedance plethysmographic, or miniaturized tonometric transducers integrated into wristbands, chest straps, or epidermal patches.

Physiological control of arterial BP is a multifactorial systems-level phenomenon involving mechanical, neural, and endocrine components. Among these, the arterial baroreflex serves as a primary short-term regulator, detecting stretch in the carotid sinus and aortic arch and modulating parasympathetic and sympathetic outflow to adjust heart rate and vascular resistance. Dysfunction of autonomic systems—particularly reduced baroreflex engagement—has been implicated in the pathogenesis of primary hypertension, though traditional assessment methods fail to capture its dynamic, intermittent nature under baseline conditions.

The invention addresses this gap through methods for analyzing time-course arterial pressure data to detect baroreflex intermittency. This methodology was first validated in rodent models, including spontaneously hypertensive rats (SHR) and Wistar-Kyoto (WKY) controls, where telemetry data revealed that baroreflex control of heart rate switches stochastically between prolonged on and off states lasting up to several minutes. The same analytical framework was successfully applied to human subjects using wearable monitors, demonstrating translatability across species.

A key embodiment is a wearable monitor for measuring physiological data, comprising a sensor assembly, signal-processing device, controller, database, program memory, processor, RAM, and I/O circuitry. The sensor assembly interfaces with the subject’s skin to acquire arterial pressure or surrogate waveforms. The signal-processing device filters and digitizes raw signals, extracting beat-to-beat systolic, diastolic, mean arterial pressure, and RR intervals. The controller executes software routines stored in program memory to implement the baroreflex intermittency algorithm, while the processor performs real-time computations using data stored temporarily in RAM.

The I/O circuit facilitates communication between the wearable sensor and external devices, enabling data upload to cloud-based medical treatment networks for remote clinical review. The system operates under a real-time operating system managing subroutines for data acquisition, noise filtering, model fitting, state classification, and diagnostic output. Configuration and operational data—including calibration parameters, subject identifiers, and analysis thresholds—are stored in non-volatile memory.

Input/output hardware supports user interaction via displays, haptic alerts, or wireless connectivity to smartphones and electronic health records. Critically, the system employs non-perturbative techniques, requiring no external stimuli or subject compliance beyond wearing the device. The autonomic baroreflex monitoring system thus enables passive, continuous assessment of cardiovascular regulatory health.

Potential healthcare applications include early detection of prehypertension, stratification of hypertensive subtypes (e.g., neurogenic vs. volume-dependent), evaluation of medication efficacy through changes in on fraction, and monitoring of autonomic neuropathies in diabetes or Parkinson’s disease. The system also supports dynamic classification of hypertension severity based on baroreflex functionality rather than absolute pressure values alone.

## EXAMPLES

Arterial pressure waveform data were collected from multiple rat strains using implanted telemetry devices sampling at 500 Hz during the 12-hour dark cycle. Data from SHR and WKY rats were recorded at 7, 10, and 15 weeks of age; additional data came from Dahl salt-sensitive (SS), salt-resistant (SR), and Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats. Vessel ultrasound was performed to measure aortic and carotid elasticity, revealing higher carotid stiffness in SHR versus WKY.

Telemetry data underwent rigorous preprocessing: twelve 5-minute windows of artifact-free data were selected per animal per recording session. From each window, systolic, diastolic, and mean arterial pressures (MAP) were extracted, along with pulse intervals (RR = 1/HR). A linear filter-based model of the baroreflex arc was applied, assuming RR interval dynamics follow a first-order differential equation driven by MAP input: dRR/dt = (–RR + R₀ + α·MAP)/τ, where α is baroreflex gain, τ is time constant, and R₀ is baseline RR.

Initial parameter estimates for α and τ were obtained by minimizing a cost function quantifying mismatch between model-predicted and observed RR variability. Specifically, local slopes of RR(t) over 10-second windows were computed for both data (μd) and model output (μm); the cost J = (σm – σd)² was minimized via optimization, yielding strain- and age-specific parameters. R₀ was then calculated as R₀ = ⟨RR⟩ – α⟨MAP⟩.

Model fits were compared to data to identify baroreflex on/off states. For each heartbeat, μm and μd were plotted; points within a hyperbolic boundary defined by |μm – μd| < r(1 + α|μm + μd|) were classified as “on,” others as “off.” A smoothing procedure applied a 10-second moving average iteratively to eliminate spurious transitions, yielding stable state assignments.

The on fraction was computed as total on-time divided by recording duration. Analysis revealed that on fraction decreased with age and MAP in SHR/WKY but not in SS/SR/SD strains. Probability density distributions of MAP during on versus off states showed higher and more variable pressure during off states exclusively in SHR/WKY. Time-course analysis of the first 20 seconds of each state demonstrated rising MAP during off states and falling MAP during on states in SHR/WKY—trends absent in other strains.

Baroreflex ablation via sinoaortic denervation (SAD) in WKY rats caused a transient ~9 mmHg MAP increase, while SHR showed no change—supporting the hypothesis that greater baroreflex engagement protects WKY from hypertension. These findings validate on fraction as a causal indicator of hypertensive risk in susceptible genotypes.

Translating to humans, the method involves sensing BP via wearable PPG, extracting time-series data, performing identical computational analysis, and outputting on fraction as a diagnostic metric. This enables identification of autonomic dysfunction etiologies, prediction of disease progression, and personalization of therapy—all implemented via distributed hardware modules communicating across medical networks, with operations scalable across geographic and processor boundaries.
