# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of regenerative medicine and cardiovascular therapeutics, specifically relating to methods and compositions for inducing the maturation of cardiomyocytes derived from pluripotent stem cells. More particularly, the invention provides a three-dimensional (3D) cardiac extracellular matrix (ECM) scaffold, optionally in combination with endothelial cells or stromal cells, that promotes structural, functional, and molecular maturation of immature cardiomyocytes toward an adult-like phenotype. The invention further encompasses methods for generating mature cardiomyocytes in vitro for use in cell-based therapies, drug screening platforms, disease modeling, and diagnostic applications related to cardiovascular disorders.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with myocardial infarction representing one of its most devastating clinical manifestations. Myocardial infarction results in extensive loss of cardiomyocytes—cells with minimal regenerative capacity in the adult human heart—leading to irreversible damage, fibrotic scarring, impaired contractility, arrhythmias, and ultimately heart failure. Despite advances in medical and interventional cardiology, current therapeutic strategies are largely palliative and do not restore lost myocardium. Consequently, there exists an urgent unmet need for approaches capable of replacing or regenerating functional cardiac tissue.

Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) have emerged as a promising autologous cell source for generating patient-specific cardiomyocytes for transplantation, disease modeling, and pharmacological testing. However, a significant limitation of existing differentiation protocols is that the resulting cardiomyocytes exhibit an immature, fetal-like phenotype characterized by disorganized sarcomeres, spontaneous automaticity, inefficient calcium handling, altered electrophysiological properties, and reduced contractile force. These immature features compromise their therapeutic efficacy, increase arrhythmic risk upon engraftment, and limit their predictive value in preclinical drug screening.

Current strategies to enhance cardiomyocyte maturation include long-term culture, mechanical stimulation, electrical pacing, hormonal treatment (e.g., triiodothyronine), microRNA modulation, and co-culture with non-cardiomyocyte cell types. While these approaches yield partial improvements, they often fail to recapitulate the complex biochemical, biomechanical, and architectural cues present in the native adult cardiac microenvironment. Notably, many studies rely on two-dimensional (2D) culture systems that lack the spatial organization, mechanical properties, and compositional complexity of the in vivo extracellular matrix. Furthermore, immature cardiomyocytes derived under such conditions remain functionally and molecularly distinct from adult human cardiomyocytes, underscoring the inadequacy of existing methodologies.

There is therefore a critical need for a robust, physiologically relevant platform that drives comprehensive maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes. Such a platform must integrate key elements of the native cardiac niche—including appropriate ECM composition, 3D architecture, mechanical stiffness, and supportive non-myocyte cell populations—to induce structural alignment, metabolic shift, electrophysiological stability, and adult-like calcium handling. The present invention addresses this need by leveraging decellularized 3D cardiac ECM scaffolds derived from adult tissue, which provide a biomimetic environment that significantly enhances cardiomyocyte maturation beyond what is achievable with conventional 2D substrates or synthetic matrices.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention provides a method for inducing maturation of immature cardiomyocytes by culturing them within a three-dimensional (3D) cardiac extracellular matrix (ECM) scaffold. This scaffold is prepared from decellularized adult cardiac tissue and retains the native biochemical composition, fiber architecture, and mechanical properties essential for promoting cardiomyocyte maturation. In specific embodiments, the method further includes co-culturing the cardiomyocytes with endothelial cells or stromal cells, which secrete paracrine factors that synergistically enhance maturation.

The 3D cardiac ECM scaffold is fabricated by decellularizing adult mammalian (e.g., bovine or porcine) heart tissue using detergent-based protocols, followed by lyophilization, milling into powder, and enzymatic digestion with pepsin to form a hydrogel that polymerizes at physiological temperatures. When seeded with immature cardiomyocytes—such as those derived from human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)—this scaffold supports their structural organization, upregulates expression of maturation markers (including MYL2, CaV1.2, connexin 43, SERCA2a, junctin, and Kir2.1), and improves functional characteristics such as calcium transient kinetics and drug responsiveness.

Maturation may be assessed in vitro through molecular, structural, and functional assays, including quantitative RT-PCR for maturation-associated genes, immunocytochemistry for sarcomeric proteins, calcium imaging using genetically encoded indicators (e.g., GCaMP6f), and pharmacological challenge with β-adrenergic agonists or ryanodine receptor modulators. Mature cardiomyocytes harvested from the 3D ECM scaffold exhibit reduced automaticity, enhanced contractile synchrony, and adult-like electrophysiological profiles, making them suitable for therapeutic transplantation, high-fidelity drug screening, and disease modeling.

The invention also encompasses methods for treating cardiovascular disease in a mammal by administering a composition comprising mature cardiomyocytes generated via the disclosed method. Additionally, the mature cardiomyocytes serve as a superior platform for diagnosing, prognosing, and screening potential therapeutics for cardiovascular conditions due to their physiological relevance. The 3D cardiac ECM scaffold itself may be formulated as part of a pharmaceutical composition, optionally combined with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients and delivered via intramyocardial injection or engineered tissue patches.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present disclosure incorporates by reference all publications, patents, and patent applications cited herein. As used herein, the term “immature cardiomyocyte” refers to a cardiomyocyte exhibiting fetal-like characteristics, including but not limited to spontaneous beating, disorganized sarcomeres, low expression of adult isoforms of contractile and ion channel proteins, and immature calcium handling. The “cardiac microenvironment” denotes the native niche in which cardiomyocytes reside in vivo, comprising extracellular matrix, neighboring endothelial cells, fibroblasts, immune cells, soluble factors, mechanical forces, and electrical signals. “Seeding” refers to the process of introducing cells into or onto a scaffold or substrate for culture.

The method of making mature cardiomyocytes comprises providing immature cardiomyocytes—preferably derived from human iPSCs—and seeding them into a 3D cardiac ECM scaffold. The scaffold is prepared by decellularizing adult cardiac tissue (e.g., bovine or porcine ventricle) using sequential washes with water, PBS, sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS, 0.5–1%), and Triton X-100, followed by extensive rinsing, lyophilization, mechanical grinding, and pepsin digestion in dilute HCl to yield a solubilized ECM solution that forms a hydrogel at 37°C. Critical parameters of the cardiac microenvironment replicated in this scaffold include collagen and elastin fiber architecture, matrix stiffness (adult ECM hydrogels exhibit storage moduli ~67 Pa versus ~7 Pa for fetal ECM), and retention of key proteins such as collagen I/III, fibronectin, laminin, periostin, fibrillin, and fibulin.

Maturation is demonstrated by increased expression of maturation marker proteins, including myosin light chain 2 ventricular isoform (MYL2), L-type calcium channel subunit CaV1.2, gap junction protein connexin 43 (Cx43), sarcoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺-ATPase (SERCA2a), junctin, triadin, and inward rectifier potassium channel Kir2.1. Functional maturation is evidenced by improved calcium transient amplitude, faster upstroke and decay kinetics, reduced spontaneous beat rate, and appropriate responses to pharmacological agents such as isoproterenol (increased chronotropy) and caffeine (enhanced calcium release).

The composition comprising the 3D ECM scaffold and mature cardiomyocytes may be used in a method of treating cardiovascular disease in a mammal by implanting the construct into ischemic myocardium to restore contractile function. Alternatively, the mature cardiomyocytes serve as a drug screening platform wherein test compounds are evaluated for efficacy, toxicity, or pro-arrhythmic potential based on changes in calcium dynamics, electrophysiology, or gene expression.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention include the 3D ECM scaffold alone or with embedded cardiomyocytes, formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients such as saline, hyaluronic acid, or biocompatible polymers. Administration routes include direct intramyocardial injection, epicardial patch placement, or catheter-based delivery. A therapeutically effective amount is that sufficient to improve ejection fraction, reduce scar size, or enhance electrical conduction in the recipient. Typical dosages range from 1×10⁶ to 1×10⁹ cells per administration, depending on disease severity.

Examples of the present disclosure include protocols for iPSC maintenance on Matrigel in mTeSR1 medium, cardiac differentiation using CHIR99021 and IWP2 in RPMI/B27, decellularization of bovine hearts, ECM hydrogel preparation, multiphoton imaging of collagen (via SHG) and elastin (via autofluorescence), rheological measurement of gel stiffness, qRT-PCR for maturation genes, calcium imaging with GCaMP6f, and pharmacological testing. Statistical analyses employ ANOVA and Student’s t-tests to confirm significance (p<0.05). Results demonstrate that 3D adult cardiac ECM—not fetal ECM or 2D cultures—robustly induces structural and functional maturation, with endothelial cell co-culture further enhancing outcomes via endothelin-1 and Notch signaling. Variations include use of human-derived ECM, alternative decellularization agents, or incorporation of mechanical stretch, all within the scope of the invention.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope. Human WTC-11 GCaMP iPSCs were maintained on Growth Factor-Reduced Matrigel in mTeSR1 medium and differentiated into cardiomyocytes using a monolayer protocol involving CHIR99021 (Day 0) and IWP2 (Day 3) in RPMI/B27 medium, yielding spontaneously beating cells by Day 12. Bovine adult and fetal hearts were decellularized by sequential incubation in ddH₂O, PBS, SDS (1% for adult, 0.5% for fetal), and Triton X-100, followed by extensive washing, lyophilization, and grinding into powder. ECM powder was digested with pepsin (2 mg/mL in 0.01 M HCl) for 48–72 hours to form injectable hydrogels.

Multiphoton imaging at the Laboratory for Fluorescence Dynamics utilized a Ti:Sapphire laser (740 nm for SHG, 860 nm for autofluorescence) with bandpass filters to visualize collagen and elastin fibers in z-stacks; image analysis via ctFIRE MATLAB quantified fiber width and density, revealing greater organization and intensity in adult ECM. Rheology showed adult ECM hydrogels were ~10-fold stiffer than fetal (67.5 vs. 7.2 Pa). iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes were seeded as single cells or aggregates into 2D-coated wells or 3D hydrogels and cultured for 7–21 days.

Quantitative RT-PCR confirmed upregulation of maturation genes (JCN, SERCA2a, MYL2, Kir2.1) in 3D adult ECM versus 2D or fetal ECM. Calcium imaging using GCaMP6f revealed that aggregated cardiomyocytes in 3D adult ECM exhibited higher transient amplitude, faster upstroke/downstroke, and shorter decay times compared to 2D controls. Single-cell 3D cultures showed opposite trends, highlighting the importance of cell-cell coupling. Pharmacological tests demonstrated that 3D-cultured cardiomyocytes responded appropriately to 1 µM isoproterenol (increased beat rate) and 10 mM caffeine (steeper calcium upstroke), confirming functional maturity.

Endothelial cell co-culture or conditioned medium further enhanced maturation marker expression, an effect blocked by inhibitors of endothelin and Notch signaling, implicating these pathways in paracrine-mediated maturation. NanoLC-MS/MS identified key ECM proteins, with mimecan and versican unique to fetal ECM. Flow cytometry and immunocytochemistry confirmed >50% cTNT⁺ cardiomyocytes after differentiation. Statistical analysis used Split-Unit ANOVA (qRT-PCR) and Student’s t-test (calcium data), with significance defined as *p<0.05, **p<0.01, ***p<0.001.

These results collectively demonstrate that 3D architecture, adult ECM composition, mechanical stiffness, and endothelial crosstalk are critical for cardiomyocyte maturation. The invention thus provides a transformative platform for generating clinically relevant cardiomyocytes, overcoming the limitations of prior art 2D systems and immature cell phenotypes. Alternative embodiments include use of porcine ECM, inclusion of stromal cells, dynamic culture with perfusion, or combination with electrical pacing—all of which fall within the spirit and scope of the claimed invention.
