# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to isolated antibodies that specifically bind to platelet-derived growth factor C (PDGF-C), and more particularly to monoclonal antibodies that recognize human PDGF-C with high affinity and specificity. These antibodies are capable of modulating the biological activity of PDGF-C, including its role in pathological processes such as tumorigenesis, fibrosis, angiogenesis, and disruption of the blood-brain barrier (BBB). The invention further encompasses nucleic acids encoding such antibodies, expression vectors and host cells for their production, pharmaceutical compositions comprising the antibodies, methods of treatment using the antibodies, diagnostic assays for detecting PDGF-C or PDGF-CC, and kits for such detection. Additionally, the invention includes non-human transgenic animals expressing heterologous PDGF-C genes and methods for screening therapeutic compounds that modulate PDGF-C activity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Platelet-derived growth factors (PDGFs) constitute a family of dimeric polypeptide growth factors that play critical roles in embryonic development, wound healing, and various pathological conditions, including cancer, fibrosis, and vascular disorders. The PDGF family includes PDGF-A, -B, -C, and -D chains, which can form homodimers (e.g., PDGF-AA, -BB, -CC, -DD) or heterodimers (e.g., PDGF-AB). Among these, PDGF-C and PDGF-D are more recently discovered members that require proteolytic activation to exert their biological functions. PDGF-C is synthesized as a latent precursor containing an N-terminal CUB (complement C1r/C1s, Uegf, Bmp1) domain and a C-terminal growth factor domain (GFD). Activation occurs through proteolytic cleavage that releases the GFD, enabling it to bind and activate PDGF receptor alpha (PDGFRα).

PDGF signaling is initiated upon ligand binding to PDGFRs, leading to receptor dimerization, autophosphorylation, and downstream activation of intracellular signaling cascades that regulate cell proliferation, migration, survival, and differentiation. PDGF-CC specifically binds to and activates PDGFRα homodimers or PDGFRα/β heterodimers. In the central nervous system (CNS), tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) is a key protease responsible for activating latent PDGF-CC, whereas other tissues utilize alternative proteases such as plasmin or matriptase.

PDGF-CC has emerged as a significant mediator of angiogenesis, not only by directly stimulating endothelial cells but also by inducing the expression of secondary angiogenic factors such as osteopontin. Moreover, PDGF-CC contributes to tumor progression by recruiting cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) into the tumor stroma, thereby providing structural and trophic support to malignant cells. Overexpression of PDGF-CC has been linked to hepatocellular carcinoma, renal fibrosis, and resistance to anti-VEGF therapies in certain cancers.

Given its pathogenic roles, inhibition of PDGF-CC activity represents a promising therapeutic strategy. Small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors like imatinib, which target PDGFRs, have shown clinical benefit in stroke and other CNS disorders associated with BBB disruption. However, these agents lack specificity for PDGF-CC and may affect other kinases, leading to off-target effects. Therefore, highly specific molecular tools—particularly monoclonal antibodies—that selectively neutralize PDGF-CC without interfering with other PDGF isoforms are urgently needed.

Importantly, PDGF-CC has been identified as a key regulator of BBB integrity. Intraventricular administration of active PDGF-CC disrupts the BBB, while pharmacological inhibition of the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα axis preserves barrier function in models of stroke, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. A recent phase II clinical trial demonstrated that imatinib, when combined with thrombolysis, significantly improved neurological outcomes in ischemic stroke patients, underscoring the therapeutic relevance of this pathway. Despite these advances, no clinically approved monoclonal antibodies targeting PDGF-CC currently exist, highlighting an unmet medical need that the present invention addresses.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides isolated anti-PDGF-C antibodies that specifically bind to human PDGF-C and neutralize its biological activity. These antibodies target epitopes within the core active domain of PDGF-CC and exhibit high affinity and specificity, with minimal cross-reactivity to other PDGF family members such as PDGF-A, -B, or -D. Exemplary monoclonal antibodies include 6B3, ch6B3 (a chimeric version), 11F5, 12F5, 19C7, and hu6B3 (a humanized variant). The complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) of these antibodies have been precisely defined: for instance, the heavy chain CDRs of 6B3 comprise amino acid sequences X1-X2-X3 (SEQ ID NO:1–3), and the light chain CDRs comprise Y1-Y2-Y3 (SEQ ID NO:4–6); analogous CDR sequences are provided for ch6B3, 11F5, 12F5, 19C7, and hu6B3.

The invention further encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding the heavy and light chain variable domains of these antibodies, as well as expression vectors and recombinant host cells engineered to produce them. Pharmaceutical compositions containing these antibodies are useful for treating diseases associated with aberrant PDGF-C expression or activity, including carcinomas, fibrotic disorders, and CNS pathologies involving BBB dysfunction.

Methods of treatment according to the invention involve administering an effective amount of an anti-PDGF-C antibody to a subject to modulate PDGF-CC activity in a cell, inhibit autocrine or paracrine PDGF-CC signaling, reduce angiogenesis, diminish stromal support, decrease tumor cell viability, shrink tumor size, or preserve BBB integrity. Specifically, the antibodies can inhibit PDGF-CC-mediated opening of the BBB and reduce pathological BBB permeability caused by stroke, trauma, or neurodegenerative conditions.

Additionally, the invention includes diagnostic assays for detecting PDGF-C or anti-PDGF-C antibodies in biological samples, utilizing the disclosed antibodies as capture or detection reagents in immunoassays such as ELISA or immunohistochemistry. Kits comprising the anti-PDGF-C antibodies, detection reagents, controls, and instructions are provided for research, diagnostic, or drug screening purposes. The antibodies also serve as molecular tools for studying PDGF-C biology.

The invention further describes non-human transgenic animals harboring a heterologous PDGF-C gene, wherein “heterologous” refers to a gene derived from a different species or artificially modified. For example, a humanized mouse may express a human PDGF-C protein under the control of endogenous regulatory elements. Sequence homology is defined as the percentage of identical amino acids or nucleotides between two sequences when optimally aligned using standard algorithms such as BLAST or ClustalW, with “substantially homologous” encompassing sequences sharing at least 80% identity. The terms “isolated antibody,” “specific binding member,” “subject,” and “treatment” are explicitly defined to clarify scope and enable precise claim construction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

PDGF-C has been established as a molecular hallmark of numerous carcinomas due to its overexpression in tumor epithelium, stroma, and vasculature compared to normal tissues. Its involvement in angiogenesis, fibroblast recruitment, and tumor cell survival renders it a compelling therapeutic target. The present invention discloses isolated antibodies that specifically interact with human PDGF-C, thereby modulating PDGF-CC activity and offering a means to treat PDGF-C-associated diseases.

An “isolated antibody” as used herein refers to an antibody that is substantially free from other cellular components, culture media, or contaminants. These antibodies bind to PDGF-C with high specificity, typically recognizing conformational epitopes within the conserved GFD of PDGF-CC. The targeted epitope for antibody 6B3, for example, resides within a specific peptide sequence of PDGF-C, as confirmed by competitive binding and biosensor analyses.

Binding of the disclosed antibodies to PDGF-C results in functional neutralization, inhibiting PDGF-CC-induced PDGFRα phosphorylation and downstream signaling. This leads to reduced cell proliferation, migration, and survival in PDGFRα-expressing cells. Structurally, the antibodies are typically IgG molecules composed of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, each containing variable and constant domains. The variable domains harbor framework regions (FRs) and hypervariable CDRs responsible for antigen recognition.

Antibody isotypes include IgA, IgD, IgE, IgG, and IgM, with IgG1 and IgG4 being preferred for therapeutic applications due to their favorable pharmacokinetics and effector functions. Effector functions such as complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) may be desirable or undesirable depending on the indication; thus, Fc engineering can be employed to enhance or reduce Fcγ receptor binding. The Fv region, comprising the VH and VL domains, forms the antigen-binding site, while the Fc region mediates interactions with immune cells.

Nucleic acid molecules encoding the antibody chains are codon-optimized for expression in mammalian systems and cloned into expression vectors under the control of strong promoters (e.g., CMV, EF-1α). Host cells such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO), HEK293, or NS0 cells are transfected to produce recombinant antibodies, which are purified via protein A/G chromatography. Variants with improved affinity, stability, or solubility can be generated through site-directed mutagenesis, CDR grafting, or phage display, incorporating conservative amino acid substitutions (e.g., hydrophobic for hydrophobic, acidic for acidic).

Modifications such as PEGylation, fusion to albumin, or encapsulation in microcapsules may enhance half-life or delivery. The antibodies can also be formatted as single-chain Fvs (scFvs), Fab fragments, or bispecific molecules. Recombinant DNA techniques enable subcloning, vector construction, and stable cell line generation. Codon redundancy and RNA editing are considered during design to ensure accurate expression.

Affinity-matured versions and mutated forms with enhanced properties are within the scope of the invention. Expression vectors may contain selection markers (e.g., GS, DHFR) and regulatory elements for high-yield production. Commercially available systems (e.g., Lonza’s GS Xceed) facilitate scalable manufacturing.

Chimeric antibodies (e.g., ch6B3) combine murine variable domains with human constant regions to reduce immunogenicity, while humanized antibodies (e.g., hu6B3) graft murine CDRs onto human framework scaffolds. Framework residues may be back-mutated to preserve binding affinity. These engineered antibodies retain specificity while exhibiting improved compatibility in human subjects.

### Treatment Methods and Pharmaceutical Formulations

Anti-PDGF-C antibodies are administered to subjects suffering from diseases driven by PDGF-C overexpression or dysregulated signaling. Such conditions include epithelial cancers (e.g., breast, colon, prostate, pancreatic, renal), gliomas, fibrotic diseases (e.g., liver cirrhosis, pulmonary fibrosis), and CNS disorders involving BBB breakdown (e.g., stroke, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury). PDGF-C is expressed by tumor cells, CAFs, endothelial cells, and neurons, making it a versatile target.

Inhibition of PDGF-CC signaling suppresses tumor growth by reducing angiogenesis, depleting stromal support, and directly impairing cancer cell viability. In the CNS, antibody-mediated blockade of PDGF-CC prevents pathological opening of the BBB, thereby limiting edema, hemorrhage, and neuroinflammation. For cerebral edema or stroke, intrathecal, intranasal, lumbar, cisterna magna, or intraventricular administration ensures CNS penetration. Infusion pumps may provide sustained delivery.

Combination therapy with chemotherapeutics (e.g., cisplatin) or thrombolytics (e.g., tPA) enhances efficacy, as demonstrated by reversal of drug resistance in head and neck cancer models. “Treatment” encompasses both prophylactic and therapeutic interventions aimed at preventing, ameliorating, or curing disease.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the antibody in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as sterile saline, buffered solutions, or lyophilized powders. Excipients include sugars (e.g., sucrose), amino acids (e.g., arginine), surfactants (e.g., polysorbate 80, Pluronic F68), salts, divalent cations (e.g., Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺), and cryoprotectants. Formulations may be solutions, suspensions, or depot injections for controlled release. Non-ionic surfactants stabilize against aggregation, while ionic binding or gel encapsulation can modulate release kinetics. Dosage regimens are tailored based on disease severity, route, and patient factors, with typical doses ranging from 1–20 mg/kg.

### PDGF-C/PDGF-CC Detection/Diagnosis Assays

Immunoassays utilizing anti-PDGF-C antibodies enable sensitive detection of PDGF-C or PDGF-CC in biological fluids or tissues. Sandwich ELISAs employ a capture antibody immobilized on a solid phase and a labeled detection antibody, allowing quantification of antigen concentration. Forward, reverse, or simultaneous formats may be used. Single-site assays rely on competition between labeled and unlabeled antigen for a limited number of antibody binding sites.

Reporter molecules include enzymes (e.g., horseradish peroxidase, alkaline phosphatase), fluorophores (e.g., FITC, Alexa dyes), or radioisotopes. Enzyme immunoassays (EIAs) produce colorimetric, chemiluminescent, or fluorescent signals proportional to antigen levels. Immunofluorescence techniques visualize PDGF-C localization in fixed cells or tissue sections. Binding is assessed via optical density, fluorescence intensity, or radiometric counts, with calibration curves enabling absolute quantification.

### Kits

Diagnostic kits contain one or more anti-PDGF-C antibodies, detection reagents (e.g., enzyme-conjugated secondary antibodies), buffers, controls (positive/negative), and instructions for use. Components are packaged in containers such as vials, plates, or cartridges, often accompanied by software for data analysis. Kits facilitate standardized, high-throughput screening in clinical or research settings for disease diagnosis, prognosis, or therapeutic monitoring.

### Drug Screening Assay

Drug screening assays employ anti-PDGF-C antibodies to identify compounds that modulate PDGF-C activity. Test compounds are evaluated for their ability to compete with the antibody for PDGF-C binding or to alter PDGF-CC-induced signaling in cell-based assays. High-throughput formats using fluorescence polarization, surface plasmon resonance, or cell viability readouts enable rapid identification of potential therapeutics.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Generation of Monoclonal Antibodies to Human PDGF-CC

Monoclonal antibodies were generated by immunizing mice with recombinant human PDGF-CC core domain. Hybridomas were screened for PDGF-CC binding via ELISA and Western blot, yielding 16 reactive clones. Six clones (6B3, 11F5, 19C7, etc.) were selected based on specificity, affinity, and neutralizing capacity. Purified IgGs showed nanomolar affinity and blocked PDGFRα phosphorylation.

### Example 2—Amino Acid Sequence of Antibodies

Variable heavy and light chain sequences of 6B3, 11F5, and 19C7 were determined by RT-PCR and sequencing. CDRs were identified using Kabat numbering, revealing identical CDRs for 6B3 and 19C7, consistent with their overlapping epitopes.

### Example 3—Generation of Chimeric Anti-PDGF-CC Antibody 6B3 (ch6B3)

Murine 6B3 variable domains were fused to human IgG1 constant regions and expressed in CHO cells. ch6B3 retained high affinity and neutralizing activity comparable to murine 6B3.

### Example 4—Identification of the 6B3 Binding Epitope on PDGF-C

Epitope mapping via peptide competition and biosensor analysis identified a specific 12-amino acid sequence in PDGF-C as the 6B3 binding site. Peptide 3 inhibited 6B3 binding in a dose-dependent manner.

### Example 5—Biosensor Confirmation of Human PDGF-CC Specificity

Biacore analysis confirmed high-affinity binding of 6B3, 11F5, and 19C7 to PDGF-CC (KD ~1–5 nM) with no cross-reactivity to PDGF-DD. Epitope binning revealed two distinct groups: 6B3/19C7 and 11F5.

### Example 6—PDGF-Ligand Induced Receptor Activation and Cell Proliferation

In PAE-PDGFRα cells, PDGF-CC induced receptor phosphorylation and proliferation, both abolished by pre-incubation with 6B3 in a dose-dependent manner, confirming neutralization.

### Example 7—Immunohistochemistry—Tissue Screens

IHC using 6B3 on 33 human tissues revealed elevated PDGF-CC in tumors versus normal counterparts (p<0.05 for bladder, brain, breast, colon, kidney, pancreas, prostate). Staining was cytoplasmic in epithelium and stroma, and vascular.

### Example 8—In Vivo Therapy Studies

In Colo205 colon carcinoma xenografts, 12F5 and 6B3 reduced tumor volume by ~30% and decreased microvessel density. In MDA-MB-231 breast cancer models, 6B3 inhibited orthotopic tumor growth and induced hormonal sensitivity.

### Example 9—Modulation of PDGF-CC Mediated Opening of the Blood-Brain Barrier by Anti-PDGF-C Antibody 6B3

Intraperitoneal ch6B3 prevented PDGF-CC-induced Evans Blue extravasation in mouse brain, demonstrating BBB protection. In a stroke model, ch6B3 reduced infarct volume and hemorrhage. Prophylactic and therapeutic dosing was effective. Humanized hu6B3 showed preserved binding affinity.
