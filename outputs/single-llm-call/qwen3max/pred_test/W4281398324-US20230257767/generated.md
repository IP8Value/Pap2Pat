# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of biopharmaceutical production, specifically to methods and compositions for the high-yield expression of foreign proteins in cultured mammalian cells. More particularly, the invention relates to the use of novel promoter sequences derived from the Hspa5 gene—encoding heat shock protein family A (Hsp70) member 5—for driving sustained and enhanced transcription of recombinant genes, especially those encoding monoclonal antibodies such as immunoglobulin G (IgG). The invention further encompasses expression vectors, host cell lines, and manufacturing processes that utilize these promoters to achieve improved productivity, stability, and consistency in the production of therapeutic proteins under industrial-scale fed-batch culture conditions.

## BACKGROUND ART

Protein-based pharmaceutical products, particularly monoclonal antibodies (mAbs), have emerged as a cornerstone of modern therapeutics due to their high specificity, efficacy, and favorable safety profiles in treating a wide range of diseases, including cancers, autoimmune disorders, and infectious conditions. The successful commercialization of over 120 antibody-based drugs since the approval of Muromonab-CD3 underscores the critical importance of robust and scalable production platforms. Among various expression systems, cultured mammalian cells—especially Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells—are the industry standard for manufacturing complex therapeutic proteins that require precise post-translational modifications, such as glycosylation, disulfide bond formation, and proper folding.

While microbial systems like *Escherichia coli* and yeast, as well as plant-based platforms, offer cost-effective alternatives for simpler proteins, they are generally inadequate for producing full-length, functional IgG molecules due to their inability to perform human-like post-translational modifications. In contrast, CHO cells combine the advantages of eukaryotic protein processing machinery with well-established regulatory compliance under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) guidelines, making them ideal for clinical and commercial biologics production.

Despite these advantages, achieving high and stable yields of recombinant proteins in CHO cells remains a significant challenge. One major bottleneck lies in the inefficiency or instability of gene expression driven by conventional promoters. Historically, viral promoters such as human cytomegalovirus (hCMV), simian virus 40 early (SV40E), and Rous sarcoma virus (RSV) have been widely used due to their strong initial activity. However, these promoters often suffer from transcriptional silencing or reduced activity during prolonged culture, particularly in the late phases of fed-batch processes when cell viability declines and metabolic stress increases. Similarly, constitutive mammalian promoters like human elongation factor-1α (hEF1α), ubiquitin C (UBC), and phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK) provide more stable expression but still exhibit suboptimal performance under industrial manufacturing conditions.

To address these limitations, researchers have explored various strategies to enhance productivity, including genomic integration site optimization, copy number amplification, co-expression of chaperones and foldases, and medium/process engineering. At the transcriptional level, efforts have focused on engineering stronger or more resilient promoters through the incorporation of enhancer elements, synthetic promoter design, or the use of endogenous CHO-derived regulatory sequences. For instance, the CHEF1α promoter and the E77 cis-element were developed to improve expression stability. Nevertheless, existing promoters frequently fail to maintain high transcriptional output throughout the entire fed-batch cycle, leading to diminished product titers and inconsistent batch performance.

A key unmet need, therefore, is the identification and implementation of a promoter that not only initiates strong transcription but also sustains or even enhances expression during the metabolically stressed late culture phase—a period critical for maximizing final product yield. Prior art lacks a promoter capable of dynamically responding to cellular stress while simultaneously supporting high-level recombinant protein synthesis without compromising cell viability or product quality.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the insufficient and unstable expression of recombinant proteins—particularly monoclonal antibodies—in cultured mammalian cells during extended fed-batch cultures. Conventional promoters, whether viral or mammalian-derived, typically exhibit declining activity as culture progresses due to epigenetic silencing, nutrient depletion, accumulation of metabolic byproducts, and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress. This results in suboptimal specific productivity per cell and reduced overall titer, thereby increasing manufacturing costs and limiting scalability. There exists a pressing need for a promoter system that maintains or increases transcriptional activity in the latter stages of culture, aligns recombinant protein production with cellular stress responses, and enables consistent, high-yield bioproduction across diverse antibody subclasses and host cell lines.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention solves this problem by providing a novel expression system based on the promoter of the Hspa5 gene, which encodes the ER-resident chaperone BiP/GRP78. Specifically, the invention discloses a polynucleotide comprising the nucleotide sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO: 1, which corresponds to a 0.6-kilobase (kb) upstream regulatory region of the Chinese hamster Hspa5 gene. Additionally, the invention encompasses homologous promoter sequences from other species, including a human-derived Hspa5 promoter (SEQ ID NO: 5), a mouse-derived Hspa5 promoter (SEQ ID NO: 6), a rat-derived Hspa5 promoter (SEQ ID NO: 7), and optimized variants thereof (SEQ ID NO: 8 and SEQ ID NO: 9). These polynucleotides function as potent transcriptional regulators that drive sustained or increasing expression of operably linked foreign genes during prolonged culture.

The human-derived Hspa5 gene promoter (SEQ ID NO: 5) exhibits high sequence identity to the Chinese hamster version and demonstrates comparable promoter activity in CHO cells, enabling cross-species utility. Similarly, the mouse-derived (SEQ ID NO: 6) and rat-derived (SEQ ID NO: 7) Hspa5 promoters retain functional conservation due to shared transcription factor binding motifs, including TATA-box, CCAAT-box, GC-box, cAMP-responsive elements (CREB), and ER stress-response elements (ERSE). The invention further includes polynucleotides that share at least 80%, preferably 90% or greater, sequence identity with any of the aforementioned Hspa5 promoter sequences and retain promoter activity under stringent hybridization conditions.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The Hspa5 promoter-based expression system of the present invention provides several unexpected and advantageous effects. First, it enables significantly higher recombinant protein yields—particularly for monoclonal antibodies—by maintaining or enhancing transcription during the late fed-batch phase when conventional promoters decline. Second, the promoter’s responsiveness to ER stress creates a natural feedback loop: as recombinant protein load increases, UPR activation upregulates Hspa5 promoter activity, thereby synchronizing transgene expression with the cell’s protein-folding capacity. Third, the system supports consistent productivity across multiple IgG subclasses (IgG1, IgG2, IgG4Pro), demonstrating broad applicability. Fourth, the minimal 0.6-kb Hspa5 promoter is compact, facilitating vector design and stable integration. Finally, the promoter exhibits species versatility, allowing its use in human, mouse, rat, and hamster-derived sequences without loss of function, thereby expanding its utility in diverse biomanufacturing contexts.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

In the context of the present invention, a “gene” refers to a DNA segment that encodes a functional product, such as a polypeptide or RNA molecule. A “polynucleotide” denotes a linear polymer of nucleotides, which may be DNA or RNA, single- or double-stranded, and includes genomic DNA, cDNA, and synthetic oligonucleotides. A “polypeptide” is a chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds, encompassing full-length proteins, fragments, and variants. “Gene expression” signifies the process by which information from a gene is used to synthesize a functional gene product, involving transcription and, for protein-coding genes, translation. A “foreign gene” is a nucleic acid sequence not naturally present in the host cell genome, introduced via genetic engineering to confer a desired trait, such as the production of a therapeutic protein. Correspondingly, a “foreign protein” is the polypeptide encoded by such a foreign gene.

A “gene expression unit” comprises a promoter operably linked to a coding sequence, along with any necessary regulatory elements (e.g., enhancers, terminators) required for transcription. A “promoter” is a DNA region located upstream of a transcription start site that directs the initiation of RNA synthesis by RNA polymerase II. “Promoter activity” quantifies the ability of a promoter to drive transcription, typically measured by reporter assays (e.g., luciferase) or mRNA levels. To “have promoter activity” means that a DNA segment can functionally substitute for a known promoter in initiating transcription. A “DNA element” refers to any short nucleotide sequence that modulates gene expression, such as enhancers, silencers, or insulators.

An “antigen-binding fragment of the antibody” includes Fab, Fab′, F(ab′)₂, scFv, and other derivatives retaining antigen recognition. “Identity” between two polynucleotides is calculated using algorithms like BLAST or ClustalW, representing the percentage of aligned residues that are identical. To “hybridize under stringent conditions” implies that two complementary nucleic acid strands form a stable duplex under defined temperature and salt concentrations, typically indicating high sequence similarity.

The core of the invention is the Hspa5 promoter, derived from the 5′-flanking region of the Hspa5 gene. This promoter originates from CHO cells but is also functional when sourced from human, mouse, or rat genomes due to evolutionary conservation of key regulatory motifs. The preferred Hspa5 promoter is the 0.6-kb fragment upstream of the transcription start site (SEQ ID NO: 1), which contains essential core elements (TATA-box, CpG island, CCAAT-box, GC-box) and stress-responsive motifs (ERSE, CREB). Mutant Hspa5 promoters may be generated via site-directed mutagenesis, PCR-based mutagenesis, or error-prone PCR to enhance activity or alter regulation. Mutations can be introduced to optimize transcription factor binding or remove repressive elements.

Foreign gene expression-enhancing activity is evaluated by comparing protein or mRNA levels driven by test promoters versus controls (e.g., hEF1α) in transient or stable transfection assays. A foreign gene expression unit according to the invention comprises the Hspa5 promoter operably linked to a coding sequence of interest. This unit may further include a DNA element for enhancing expression, such as an intronic enhancer or matrix attachment region (MAR). Such DNA elements can be obtained by screening genomic libraries, bioinformatic prediction, or functional assays. Examples include the A7 element from CHO cells. Nucleotide sequences of these elements are disclosed in the accompanying sequence listing.

Polynucleotides of the invention may be obtained by isolating genomic DNA, preparing cDNA libraries from mRNA, selecting positive clones via hybridization or PCR, or synthesizing chemically. Sequencing confirms identity. Expression vectors containing the Hspa5 promoter are introduced into host cells—preferably CHO-K1—via transfection (e.g., lipofection, electroporation). Transformed cells are selected using markers (e.g., neomycin resistance) and expanded into stable pools or monoclonal lines. Foreign proteins, including antibodies, are produced by culturing these cells in fed-batch mode, followed by collection and purification (e.g., Protein A chromatography).

For antibody production, heavy and light chain genes are co-expressed under Hspa5 promoters. Examples include humanized IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4Pro antibodies. Other foreign proteins—enzymes, cytokines, receptors—can similarly be produced. Episomal vectors may be used for transient expression, though integrated systems are preferred for manufacturing.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the invention without limitation. Plasmids pGL4.10 (firefly luciferase reporter) and pEF1/myc-His B (antibody backbone) were used, along with restriction enzymes and ligases from commercial sources. DNA cloning involved standard techniques: restriction digestion, ligation, transformation into *E. coli*, and Sanger sequencing for verification.

CHO-K1 cells were cultured in serum-free media (G13 or CD DA1) in stirred vessels mimicking manufacturing conditions. Transcriptome analysis was performed by extracting total RNA, preparing polyA+ libraries, and sequencing on Illumina HiSeq. Reads were mapped to the CHO genome, and RPKM values calculated. Highly expressed genes included *hspa5*, whose transcript increased over time.

The 3-kb upstream region of *hspa5* was cloned into pGL4.10. Luciferase assays showed Hspa5 promoter outperformed hEF1α in stable pools during late culture. Antibody expression vectors with Hspa5-driven heavy/light chains yielded higher SPR and titer than controls.

Truncation studies identified the 0.6-kb fragment as optimal. Human, mouse, and rat Hspa5 promoters gave similar productivity. Combining Hspa5 with enhancer A7 further boosted output. Fed-batch cultures consistently showed superior viable cell density, antibody titer, and SPR with Hspa5 versus RPS7 or EF1α promoters across multiple experiments and clones.

Renilla luciferase assays confirmed sustained transcriptional activity. mRNA levels correlated with protein output. No adverse effects on growth or product quality were observed. Thus, Hspa5 promoter enables robust, scalable bioproduction.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention is highly applicable to the industrial production of recombinant therapeutic proteins, particularly monoclonal antibodies. By providing a promoter that enhances and sustains expression during critical late-phase culture, the invention directly addresses a major bottleneck in biomanufacturing efficiency. Its compatibility with standard CHO cell platforms, scalability in fed-batch reactors, and effectiveness across diverse antibody formats make it a valuable tool for reducing production costs, improving batch consistency, and accelerating the development of biologic drugs. The method for producing foreign genes using the Hspa5 promoter is therefore of significant commercial utility in the pharmaceutical and biotechnology industries.
