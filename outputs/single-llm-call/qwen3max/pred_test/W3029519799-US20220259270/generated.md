# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL DOMAIN

The present invention pertains to the field of industrial biotechnology, specifically to the development and application of genetically engineered microbial strains for the high-yield production of cellulolytic and hemicellulolytic enzymes. More particularly, the invention relates to a novel strain of the filamentous fungus *Trichoderma reesei*, which has been rationally engineered through targeted genetic modifications to enhance its capacity for secreting industrially relevant levels of cellulases and hemicellulases. The invention further encompasses optimized fermentation processes that utilize low-cost, renewable agroindustrial by-products—such as sugarcane molasses—as carbon sources for enzyme production. This technological platform addresses critical limitations in current bio-based manufacturing systems, including high production costs, dependence on expensive inducing sugars, and insufficient enzymatic activities—particularly β-glucosidase—that hinder the efficient saccharification of lignocellulosic biomass. The disclosed strain and associated bioprocess are designed to support the economic viability of second-generation biofuel production and other biorefinery applications by enabling the cost-effective, large-scale generation of high-performance enzyme cocktails from non-food biomass feedstocks.

## PRIOR ART

Cellulases and hemicellulases are complex enzyme systems that catalyze the hydrolysis of cellulose and hemicellulose—the primary polysaccharides in plant cell walls—into fermentable monosaccharides such as glucose and xylose. These enzymes are indispensable for the conversion of lignocellulosic biomass into biofuels, biochemicals, and biomaterials, forming a cornerstone of the emerging circular bioeconomy. Despite their importance, the commercial deployment of these enzymes has been constrained by high production costs, suboptimal catalytic efficiency, and the need for complex induction strategies during microbial fermentation. Historically, *Trichoderma reesei* has emerged as the dominant industrial host for cellulase production due to its exceptional natural ability to secrete large quantities of extracellular proteins, particularly cellobiohydrolases and endoglucanases. However, native *T. reesei* strains suffer from several deficiencies, including low β-glucosidase activity, strong carbon catabolite repression (CCR) by glucose, and an inability to utilize certain inexpensive carbon sources like sucrose, which is abundant in agroindustrial residues such as sugarcane molasses.

Efforts to overcome these limitations have motivated extensive research into strain improvement strategies. Classical approaches relied on random mutagenesis and screening, yielding hyperproducing derivatives such as the widely used RUT-C30 strain, which carries a truncated *cre1* gene that partially alleviates CCR. While effective, such methods are labor-intensive and yield poorly characterized genetic backgrounds. In recent decades, rational metabolic engineering has gained prominence, targeting key regulatory genes. For instance, the transcription factor XYR1 is recognized as the master activator of cellulase and hemicellulase gene expression in *T. reesei*. Constitutive overexpression of *xyr1* has been shown to enhance enzyme titers and reduce dependence on inducing sugars. Further improvements were achieved by introducing point mutations (e.g., V821F) in *xyr1*, rendering the strain “glucose-blind” and enabling enzyme production even in repressive carbon environments. Concurrently, deletion or silencing of repressor genes such as *ace1*—a known negative regulator of cellulase expression—has synergistically boosted enzyme yields. Additionally, the removal of extracellular proteases like SLP1 and PEP1 has been demonstrated to increase protein stability and secretion by reducing proteolytic degradation in the culture medium.

Despite these advances, prior art strains remain limited in industrial applicability. Most high-performing strains are proprietary, with restricted access and undisclosed genetic modifications, hindering reproducibility and academic validation. Publicly available strains like RUT-C30, while well-characterized, produce enzyme titers far below industrial benchmarks (typically <20 g L⁻¹), whereas commercial processes reportedly achieve >100 g L⁻¹. Moreover, existing engineered strains often still require costly inducing sugars (e.g., lactose or cellulose) and complex nitrogen sources, undermining process economics. The inability to utilize sucrose—a major component of sugarcane molasses—further restricts the use of low-cost feedstocks. Although some studies have introduced heterologous invertase genes to enable sucrose metabolism, these modifications have rarely been combined with other productivity-enhancing edits in a single strain. Furthermore, traditional genetic engineering in *T. reesei* has been hampered by low transformation efficiency and a scarcity of selectable markers, limiting the number of concurrent modifications. While CRISPR/Cas9 technology has recently alleviated these constraints, systematic combinatorial engineering of multiple traits—enhanced transcription, protease deficiency, β-glucosidase supplementation, and sucrose utilization—remains underexplored in the public domain. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for a genetically defined, publicly accessible *Trichoderma* strain capable of high-titer enzyme production on inexpensive, non-inducing, sucrose-rich media, thereby bridging the gap between academic innovation and industrial feasibility.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a novel, genetically engineered strain of *Trichoderma reesei*, designated herein as Br_TrR03, which exhibits significantly enhanced capabilities for the production of cellulolytic and hemicellulolytic enzymes. This strain is derived from the well-characterized RUT-C30 background and incorporates six specific, rationally selected genetic modifications designed to synergistically improve enzyme titer, specific activity, substrate range, and process compatibility. The Br_TrR03 strain is characterized by: (1) the insertion of a heterologous β-glucosidase-encoding gene (*cel3a*) from *Talaromyces emersonii* under the control of the *xyn1* promoter at the *slp1* locus, thereby supplementing the deficient β-glucosidase activity of native *T. reesei* secretomes; (2) the deletion of the *slp1* gene encoding a subtilisin-like protease, reducing extracellular proteolytic activity and enhancing protein stability; (3) the replacement of the *ace1* repressor gene with a constitutively expressed, mutated *xyr1* allele (V821F) driven by the *pdc1* promoter, resulting in derepressed, high-level transcription of cellulase and hemicellulase genes even in the presence of glucose; (4) the deletion of the *pep1* gene encoding a major aspartic protease, further minimizing proteolytic degradation; and (5) the insertion of the *suc1* invertase gene from *Aspergillus niger*, including its native regulatory sequences, at the *pep1* locus, enabling the strain to hydrolyze and utilize sucrose as a sole carbon source. Collectively, these modifications confer upon Br_TrR03 the ability to achieve extracellular protein titers of up to 80.6 g L⁻¹ in fed-batch bioreactor fermentations using a low-cost, molasses-based medium, representing the highest experimentally validated titer reported for any publicly available *T. reesei* strain. The invention further includes optimized fermentation processes employing sugarcane molasses and molasses-grown yeast as economical carbon and nitrogen sources, respectively, thereby enabling industrially scalable, cost-effective enzyme production without reliance on expensive inducers or purified nutrients.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

The present invention employs advanced genetic engineering techniques to construct superior *Trichoderma reesei* strains for industrial enzyme production. Gene deletion techniques were utilized to remove genomic segments encoding undesirable functions, such as proteolytic activity or transcriptional repression. Specifically, homologous recombination was employed to replace the open reading frames of target genes (*slp1*, *ace1*, *pep1*) with engineered cassettes, effectively ablating gene function. Gene disruption techniques, including the insertion of exogenous DNA sequences into coding regions, were also applied to ensure complete loss of function, particularly when precise deletions might be prone to reversion or incomplete knockout. Conversely, gene overexpression techniques were implemented to amplify beneficial traits; for instance, the *xyr1-V821F* allele was placed under the control of the strong, constitutive *pdc1* promoter to drive persistent, high-level expression independent of environmental cues. These strategies were motivated by the need to overcome inherent limitations of wild-type and classical mutant strains, such as carbon catabolite repression, protease-mediated enzyme degradation, and insufficient β-glucosidase activity, which collectively impede industrial-scale productivity.

The foundational strain for engineering was *Trichoderma reesei* RutC30, a derivative of the QM6a isolate that carries a naturally occurring 85-kb chromosomal deletion and a truncated *cre1* gene, conferring partial resistance to glucose-mediated repression. Starting from this platform, specific genetic modifications were introduced iteratively. The *xyr1* gene encodes the primary transcriptional activator of cellulase and hemicellulase genes in *T. reesei*. Its constitutive expression, particularly with the V821F mutation in a conserved regulatory domain, renders the strain insensitive to glucose repression and dramatically elevates enzyme transcription. The *ace1* gene functions as a repressor of cellulase expression; its deletion removes this inhibitory influence, synergizing with *xyr1* overexpression to maximize transcriptional output. The *slp1* gene encodes a subtilisin-like serine protease that contributes significantly to extracellular proteolytic activity; its deletion enhances the stability of secreted enzymes. Similarly, the *pep1* gene encodes the predominant aspartic protease in the *T. reesei* secretome, and its removal further reduces proteolysis, improving net protein accumulation. The *cel3a* gene from *Talaromyces emersonii* encodes a highly active β-glucosidase, which was expressed under the *xyn1* promoter to compensate for the native deficiency in *T. reesei* and prevent cellobiose accumulation during biomass hydrolysis. Additional gene modifications included the integration of the *Aspergillus niger suc1* gene, which encodes an extracellular invertase, enabling the hydrolysis of sucrose into glucose and fructose for metabolic utilization.

Cultivation of the engineered *Trichoderma* strains was performed under controlled conditions to maximize enzyme yield. Shake flask experiments utilized defined media with specific carbon sources (lactose, glucose, or sucrose) to assess basic phenotypic traits. For industrial-scale production, fed-batch bioreactor fermentations were employed, with careful regulation of pH, dissolved oxygen, temperature, and nutrient feed rates. Critically, the invention discloses a molasses-based medium comprising sugarcane molasses as the primary carbon source and molasses-grown whole yeast cells as an organic nitrogen supplement, eliminating the need for expensive components like yeast extract or peptone. This cultivation strategy not only reduces raw material costs but also aligns with circular economy principles by valorizing agroindustrial waste streams.

### Examples

The CTBE_R2 strain, corresponding to the intermediate engineered strain Br_TrR02, was constructed by introducing three genetic modifications into RUT-C30: insertion of *cel3a* at the *slp1* locus (deleting *slp1*), and replacement of *ace1* with the *pdc1*-driven *xyr1-V821F* cassette. This strain exhibited a threefold increase in extracellular protein titer compared to its predecessor and displayed high enzyme production even in glucose-containing media, confirming relief from carbon catabolite repression. However, it remained unable to grow on sucrose due to the absence of invertase activity.

The CTBE_R4 strain, corresponding to the final engineered strain Br_TrR03, was generated by adding a third round of modification to Br_TrR02: insertion of the *A. niger suc1* gene at the *pep1* locus, thereby deleting *pep1* and conferring sucrose utilization capability. This strain retained the high productivity of Br_TrR02 while gaining the ability to efficiently produce enzymes on sucrose and sucrose-rich molasses. In bioreactor fermentations using a molasses-based medium supplemented with molasses-grown yeast, CTBE_R4 achieved an extracellular protein titer of 80.6 g L⁻¹ over 336 hours, with a production rate of 0.24 g L⁻¹ h⁻¹—the highest reported for any publicly available *T. reesei* strain. Enzyme cocktails produced by CTBE_R4 displayed markedly enhanced specific activities, including a 72-fold increase in β-glucosidase and a 42-fold increase in xylanase compared to RUT-C30, and demonstrated saccharification efficiency on pretreated sugarcane straw equivalent to that of the commercial CTec2 preparation. In direct comparison, CTBE_R2 cultivated on acid-inverted molasses reached only 59.2 g L⁻¹ under optimized conditions, underscoring the critical contribution of the *suc1* integration and *pep1* deletion to achieving maximal productivity on non-inverted, cost-effective feedstocks.
