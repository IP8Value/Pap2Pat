# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNOLOGY FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of molecular oncology and precision medicine, specifically relating to the identification and therapeutic targeting of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) that modulate the response of malignant glioma cells to ionizing radiation. More particularly, the invention provides methods for diagnosing and treating malignant gliomas—including glioblastoma (GBM) in adults and diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG) in pediatric patients—through the selective knockdown of a primate-conserved lncRNA designated lncGRS-1 (CTC-338M12.4). The invention further encompasses novel three-dimensional human brain organoid models that recapitulate the cellular architecture of the mature human brain and serve as platforms for evaluating both the efficacy and neurotoxicity of candidate therapeutics, including antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs), in combination with radiotherapy. Additionally, the invention includes compositions comprising nucleic acid agents capable of specifically inhibiting lncGRS-1 expression, as well as diagnostic systems for detecting lncGRS-1 levels in biological samples to aid in patient stratification and treatment monitoring.

## BACKGROUND

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) constitute a diverse class of RNA transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides in length that lack protein-coding potential yet play critical regulatory roles in gene expression, chromatin remodeling, and cellular differentiation. Unlike protein-coding genes, lncRNAs often exhibit highly cell type-specific expression patterns, rendering them attractive candidates for targeted cancer therapy with potentially reduced off-target effects. Despite their abundance in the human genome—with estimates exceeding tens of thousands of distinct loci—the functional significance of the vast majority of lncRNAs remains uncharacterized, particularly in the context of therapeutic intervention.

Malignant glioma represents one of the most aggressive and lethal forms of primary brain cancer, with dismal prognoses across both adult and pediatric populations. In adults, glioblastoma (GBM) is the most common and virulent subtype, characterized by rapid growth, diffuse infiltration into surrounding brain parenchyma, and resistance to conventional therapies. Standard treatment involves maximal safe surgical resection followed by concurrent radiotherapy and temozolomide chemotherapy; however, median survival remains approximately 14 months due to inevitable tumor recurrence. In children, diffuse intrinsic pontine glioma (DIPG) arises in the brainstem and is largely inoperable, with radiotherapy serving as the only effective palliative modality. Despite this, median survival is only 9–10 months, underscoring the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies that enhance the efficacy of existing treatments without exacerbating neurotoxicity.

Current approaches to identifying therapeutic targets in glioma have been limited by reliance on low-throughput, hypothesis-driven methods or screens focused predominantly on protein-coding genes. Genome-wide functional screens using CRISPR-based technologies have revolutionized target discovery but have rarely been integrated with clinically relevant treatment modalities such as fractionated radiotherapy. Moreover, traditional preclinical models—including two-dimensional cell cultures and murine xenografts—fail to adequately recapitulate the complexity of the human brain microenvironment or account for species-specific genomic elements. Notably, many human lncRNAs, including the target described herein, lack orthologs in rodents, thereby precluding meaningful toxicity assessment in standard animal models.

Organoids—three-dimensional, self-organizing structures derived from pluripotent stem cells—have emerged as powerful tools for modeling human development and disease. While embryonic brain organoids have been used to study early gliomagenesis, they predominantly consist of proliferative neural progenitors and lack the mature glial and neuronal populations characteristic of the adult human brain. Consequently, these models are suboptimal for evaluating the therapeutic index of agents intended for use in postnatal patients, particularly when assessing potential toxicity to differentiated neural cells during radiotherapy. There exists a critical gap in the art for organoid systems that mirror the cellular composition and physiological state of the mature human brain to enable simultaneous evaluation of anti-tumor efficacy and normal tissue toxicity in a human-relevant context.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a comprehensive platform for the identification, validation, and therapeutic exploitation of lncRNAs that function as radiation sensitizers in malignant glioma. Central to this platform is a genome-scale CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) screen designed to identify lncRNA loci whose transcriptional repression enhances the anti-proliferative effects of clinically relevant fractionated radiotherapy in glioma cells. Using this approach, the inventors identified 467 lncRNA hits that modify glioma cell growth under irradiation, of which 33 exhibited true radiosensitizing activity—defined by a greater phenotypic effect in the presence versus absence of radiation. Among these, nine lncRNAs were prioritized based on their expression in both adult GBM and pediatric DIPG patient-derived cultures and designated as lncRNA Glioma Radiation Sensitizers (lncGRS-1 through lncGRS-9).

Particularly, lncGRS-1 (CTC-338M12.4), located on chromosome 5q35.3, was validated as a potent and glioma-selective therapeutic target. Knockdown of lncGRS-1 via CRISPRi or antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) significantly inhibited the proliferation of multiple patient-derived glioma cell lines—including SF10360 (GBM) and SF8628 (DIPG)—while sparing normal human astrocytes and other non-glioma cell types. This glioma-specific dependency was further confirmed through transcriptomic analyses revealing broad activation of p53 signaling and cell cycle arrest pathways in tumor cells upon lncGRS-1 depletion, with minimal gene expression changes observed in normal astrocytes.

To address the limitations of rodent models for evaluating human-specific lncRNA targets, the inventors developed a novel three-dimensional human brain organoid model termed “mature brain organoids” (MBOs). These MBOs are assembled from induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived mature astrocytes (iAstrocytes) and cortical neurons (i3Neurons), thereby recapitulating key features of the postnatal human brain. When co-cultured with fluorescently labeled glioma cells, MBOs support invasive tumor growth and enable real-time quantification of therapeutic response. Critically, ASO-mediated knockdown of lncGRS-1 in MBO-glioma co-cultures significantly reduced tumor burden and synergized with fractionated radiotherapy, without compromising organoid viability or structure.

The invention thus provides multiple interrelated embodiments: (1) a method for identifying radiotherapy sensitizers via CRISPRi screening in glioma cells exposed to fractionated radiation; (2) diagnostic methods for detecting lncGRS expression in biological samples using nucleic acid probes complementary to lncGRS transcripts; (3) therapeutic methods involving administration of agents—such as ASOs, siRNAs, or CRISPRi constructs—that knock down lncGRS-1 expression, preferably in combination with radiotherapy; (4) pharmaceutical compositions comprising such agents in pharmaceutically acceptable carriers; and (5) a human brain organoid model for screening candidate radiosensitizers and assessing neurotoxicity. The invention establishes a generalizable framework for mining the non-coding genome for context-dependent therapeutic vulnerabilities and accelerating their translation into clinical practice.
