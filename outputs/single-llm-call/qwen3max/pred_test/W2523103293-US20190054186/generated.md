## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to magnetic nanoparticles and, more specifically, to polymer-coated magnetic nanoparticle complexes designed for biomedical applications including cancer therapy, diagnostic imaging, and targeted drug delivery. The invention further pertains to methods of using such complexes under the influence of low or super low frequency alternating current (AC) magnetic fields to selectively disrupt the cytoskeletal architecture of cancerous cells without causing significant damage to non-cancerous cells, thereby enabling a novel, non-thermal, mechano-responsive therapeutic approach.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Magnetic hyperthermia has emerged as a promising modality in oncology, leveraging the ability of magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) to convert electromagnetic energy into heat under the influence of high-frequency AC magnetic fields. This localized heating can induce thermal ablation of tumor tissue while sparing surrounding healthy structures. However, clinical translation of magnetic hyperthermia has been hindered by several limitations, including the inability to achieve sufficient temperature elevation in deep-seated tumors without exceeding safety thresholds for systemic heating, the requirement for high-frequency fields that are restricted by regulatory limits due to eddy current induction in biological tissues, and the non-specific nature of thermal effects, which may damage both malignant and benign cells.

In response to these challenges, researchers have explored alternative mechanisms of action beyond bulk heating, leading to the concept of "surface heating" or "mechanical actuation," wherein MNPs generate localized mechanical forces rather than thermal energy. Early studies demonstrated that MNPs subjected to rotating or oscillating magnetic fields could exert torque on cellular structures, particularly when internalized into organelles such as lysosomes. These forces were hypothesized to induce lysosomal membrane permeabilization (LMP), triggering caspase-dependent apoptosis. While promising, such approaches often required large nanoparticles (e.g., >100 nm) and high-field gradients not compatible with deep-tissue penetration or clinical safety standards.

Moreover, prior art systems suffered from poor colloidal stability, limited cellular uptake, and insufficient targeting specificity. Many reported formulations relied on simple surfactant coatings that failed to provide long-term dispersion stability in physiological media or enhance intracellular internalization. Additionally, the absence of a clear mechanistic understanding of how sub-thermal magnetic actuation leads to selective cytotoxicity has impeded rational design and optimization of next-generation nanotherapeutics.

A critical shortcoming in the existing literature is the lack of a system that combines high biocompatibility, efficient cellular internalization, precise intracellular trafficking, and a well-defined, non-thermal mechanism of action that discriminates between cancerous and non-cancerous cells based on intrinsic biomechanical differences. There remains a pressing need for a magnetic nanoparticle platform that operates under clinically permissible, low-frequency AC magnetic fields and exploits the inherent mechanical vulnerability of cancer cells to achieve selective therapeutic outcomes without relying on thermal effects or lysosomal rupture.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a magnetic nanoparticle polymer complex (MNPC) comprising a core of superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles (SMNPs) coated with an amphiphilic block copolymer, wherein the block copolymer comprises a hydrophilic poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO) block, a hydrophobic poly(propylene oxide) (PPO) block, and a polyanionic block such as poly(acrylic acid) (PAA). In a preferred embodiment, the block copolymer is a pentablock structure of the form PAA-b-P85-b-PAA, where P85 denotes Pluronic® P85, a triblock copolymer of PEO₂₆-PPO₃₉-PEO₂₆. The MNPCs are remotely actuated by low or super low frequency AC magnetic fields (e.g., 30–3000 Hz, preferably 50 Hz) at field strengths of 10–100 kA/m, generating mechanical forces that selectively disrupt the actin cytoskeleton of cancerous cells without causing lysosomal membrane permeabilization or significant heating.

The invention further encompasses a method of treating cancer by administering MNPCs to a subject and exposing the tumor site to a pulsed or continuous low-frequency AC magnetic field, resulting in cytoskeletal damage and selective death of cancer cells while sparing healthy tissue. The MNPCs may also be loaded with therapeutic agents such as paclitaxel (PTX) for combination therapy, or functionalized with targeting moieties to enhance tumor accumulation. Additionally, the high iron content and magnetic properties of the MNPCs enable their use as contrast agents in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), providing a theranostic platform for simultaneous diagnosis and treatment.

The hydrophobic modification of the MNP surface via the PPO block promotes micelle formation and enhances cellular uptake, particularly in cancer cells. The polyanionic PAA block facilitates stable anchoring to the MNP surface through carboxylate coordination and provides sites for conjugation of drugs or imaging agents. The resulting MNPCs exhibit hydrodynamic diameters of 30–70 nm, negative zeta potentials of −35 to −50 mV, and excellent colloidal stability in physiological media. Upon internalization, MNPCs accumulate in lysosomes, and under AC magnetic field exposure, they form dynamic aggregates that transmit mechanical stress to the underlying cytoskeleton, exploiting the reduced mechanical stiffness of cancer cells to achieve selective cytotoxicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is described herein with reference to the accompanying drawings, which illustrate exemplary embodiments but do not limit the scope of the claims. As used herein, the term “magnetic nanoparticle polymer complex” (MNPC) refers to a nanostructure comprising one or more superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticles (e.g., Fe₃O₄ or γ-Fe₂O₃) coated with a block copolymer, wherein the copolymer is covalently or coordinatively bound to the nanoparticle surface and self-assembles into a micellar or flower-like architecture. The term “block copolymer” denotes a polymer composed of two or more chemically distinct homopolymer subunits linked by covalent bonds; “amphiphilic block copolymer” refers to a block copolymer containing both hydrophilic and hydrophobic segments, such as Pluronic® triblock copolymers (PEO-PPO-PEO).

The term “cancer” includes any malignant neoplasm, with “breast cancer” encompassing ductal carcinoma, lobular carcinoma, and triple-negative subtypes (e.g., MDA-MB-231). The phrase “therapeutically effective amount” means a quantity of MNPC sufficient to elicit a desired biological response, such as reduction in tumor volume or induction of cancer cell death, without undue toxicity. “Treating” or “treatment” includes prophylactic, palliative, and curative interventions.

The MNPCs of the invention comprise SMNPs with diameters of 5–15 nm, preferably 7–8 nm, synthesized by thermal decomposition of iron precursors (e.g., Fe(acac)₃) in organic solvents. These are coated with amphiphilic block copolymers having a hydrophilic-lipophilic balance (HLB) value between 10 and 20, calculated according to Griffin’s method. Preferred copolymers include PAA-b-P85-b-PAA, wherein the PPO block has a molecular mass of 1500–2500 Da, and the PEO blocks each have a mass of 1000–1200 Da. The PAA block, typically 5–20 kDa, provides anchoring via carboxylate groups and may be conjugated to drugs or dyes.

Coating is achieved by ligand exchange in aqueous or organic media, yielding MNPCs with hydrodynamic diameters of 30–70 nm and polydispersity index <0.2. The complexes may be further modified with hydrophobic moieties (e.g., oleic acid, dopamine) or surfactants (e.g., cationic CTAB, anionic SDS) to tune surface properties. Therapeutic agents—such as paclitaxel, doxorubicin, or siRNA—are incorporated into the hydrophobic core or conjugated to the polymer backbone, enhancing solubility and enabling controlled release.

Upon administration, MNPCs are internalized via endocytosis, accumulating in lysosomes. When exposed to a 50 Hz, 50 kA/m AC magnetic field in a pulsed regime (10 min on, 5 min off), the MNPCs aggregate within lysosomes, forming dynamic clusters that exert cyclic mechanical stress on actin filaments. Cancer cells, being mechanically softer (Young’s modulus <1 kPa) than non-cancerous cells (>2 kPa), undergo cytoskeletal collapse and apoptosis, while healthy cells remain intact. This selectivity is independent of uptake differences and is enhanced by cytoskeletal destabilizers like cytochalasin D.

Pharmaceutical formulations include aqueous suspensions for intravenous injection, lyophilized powders for reconstitution, or depot systems for sustained release. Kits comprising MNPCs, magnetic field applicators, and instructions for use are provided. The invention is applicable to human and veterinary medicine, with subjects including mammals diagnosed with solid tumors.

### Experimental Methods

Human breast cancer cell lines MDA-MB-231 (triple-negative), BT474 (ductal carcinoma), and non-tumorigenic MCF10A were cultured in DMEM or DMEM/F12 supplemented with 10% FBS, antibiotics, insulin, and EGF. MNPCs were synthesized by coating SMNPs with PAA-b-P85-b-PAA via ligand exchange. Hydrodynamic diameter (30–70 nm) and zeta potential (−35 to −50 mV) were measured by DLS; iron content was quantified by ICP-MS, and polymer content by TGA. Alexa Fluor 647 labeling enabled tracking via fluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry.

Cytotoxicity was assessed by MTT assay after 24 h MNPC exposure (0.005–0.5 mg/mL) followed by AC magnetic field exposure (50 Hz, 50 or 100 kA/m, continuous or pulsed). Intracellular uptake was quantified by ICP-MS after cell lysis. Confocal microscopy with Lysotracker Green and phalloidin-based actin stains revealed lysosomal localization and cytoskeletal disruption. TEM confirmed lysosomal accumulation of MNPC clusters. Acridine orange assays ruled out LMP, and controls excluded heating effects. Statistical analysis used ANOVA with post-hoc correction.

### Example 2

Block copolymers PAA-PEG and PAA-P85 were synthesized and used to coat SMNPs. PAA-P85-SMNPs showed enhanced uptake in all cell lines, particularly in BT474 and MCF10A, as confirmed by flow cytometry and confocal imaging. Physicochemical characterization confirmed stable, monodisperse complexes with ~60 wt% polymer content and minimal cytotoxicity in the absence of magnetic fields.

### Example 3

Confocal microscopy demonstrated that at low doses (0.05 mg/mL), PAA-P85-SMNPs colocalized with lysosomes (>80% overlap), while at high doses (0.5 mg/mL), they dispersed into the cytoplasm (~30% colocalization). TEM images corroborated dense lysosomal accumulation.

### Example 4

Exposure to 50 Hz, 50 kA/m pulsed AC field induced significant death in MDA-MB-231 and BT474 cells (viability <50%) but not in MCF10A, despite similar uptake. Continuous fields were less effective. Acridine orange staining and Lysotracker imaging confirmed no LMP. Actin staining revealed cytoskeletal disruption only in cancer cells. Pretreatment with cytochalasin D sensitized MCF10A to field-induced death, confirming mechanical vulnerability as the key determinant.

### Example 5

PTX-loaded MNPCs were prepared using dopamine-conjugated poly(2-oxazoline) copolymers. Six formulations varied in PTX:polymer:MNP ratios. DLS, NTA, and HPLC confirmed particle size (~100 nm), zeta potential (−20 mV), and drug loading (10–15%). SQUID-VSM showed saturation magnetization of 60–70 A·m²/kg. In vitro, PTX-MNPCs + pulsed field reduced viability more than either alone, demonstrating synergistic efficacy.

### Example 6

PTX-MNPCs were cross-linked with BS3 and purified by gel filtration. Dialysis showed sustained PTX release over 72 h. Magnetic field exposure enhanced cytotoxicity in MDA-MB-231 cells, confirming mechano-chemotherapeutic synergy.

### Example 7

Fe₃O₄ MNPs were synthesized by thermal decomposition of Fe(acac)₃ in benzyl alcohol, coated with oleic acid, then displaced with bisphosphonate ligands. MNPCs formed in aqueous media via turbulent mixing exhibited high stability and uniform size.

### Example 8

AB or ABA polyelectrolyte block copolymers (e.g., PAA-PEO) were modified with catechol anchor groups and grafted onto magnetite. Binding was stable even in high salt, and complexes formed polyion networks with multivalent ions, enhancing structural integrity for in vivo applications.
