# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and diagnostic medicine, specifically to engineered DNA polymerases with enhanced selectivity for use in nucleic acid amplification-based detection methods. DNA polymerases are essential enzymes that catalyze the template-directed synthesis of DNA from deoxyribonucleotide triphosphates, and they serve as the cornerstone of numerous molecular biology techniques including the polymerase chain reaction (PCR), real-time PCR, allele-specific amplification (ASA), methylation-specific PCR (MSP), and other isothermal or thermocycling amplification assays. These applications rely critically on the fidelity and discrimination capacity of the DNA polymerase employed, particularly its ability to distinguish between perfectly matched primer-template duplexes and those containing mismatches at or near the 3′-terminus of the primer. The invention provides novel mutant DNA polymerases derived from thermostable polymerases such as KlenTaq—a truncated form of Thermus aquaticus (Taq) DNA polymerase—that exhibit significantly improved mismatch extension selectivity without compromising enzymatic activity or stability. Such engineered polymerases enable more accurate, sensitive, and robust detection of single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), epigenetic modifications such as DNA methylation, and other sequence variations relevant to personalized medicine, cancer diagnostics, pharmacogenomics, transfusion medicine, and forensic analysis.

## BACKGROUND OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

Personalized medicine aims to tailor medical treatment to the individual genetic profile of each patient, thereby optimizing therapeutic efficacy and minimizing adverse effects. A central pillar of this approach is the identification and interpretation of genetic variations, particularly single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), which represent the most common form of genetic diversity among individuals of the same species. SNPs can occur in coding or non-coding regions of the genome and may influence protein structure, gene expression, drug metabolism, disease susceptibility, and response to environmental factors. Accurate and efficient SNP genotyping is therefore critical for clinical diagnostics, prognostics, and therapeutic decision-making. Among the various methods available for SNP detection, allele-specific amplification (ASA) stands out due to its simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and compatibility with real-time PCR platforms. ASA exploits the inherent inability of many DNA polymerases to efficiently extend primers that are mismatched at their 3′-terminal nucleotide when hybridized to a template containing a variant allele. However, wild-type DNA polymerases often lack sufficient discrimination, leading to false-positive amplification and reduced assay reliability.

In parallel, epigenetic alterations—particularly DNA methylation at CpG dinucleotides—have emerged as crucial biomarkers in oncology and other disease areas. Methylation-specific PCR (MSP) is a widely adopted technique for assessing the methylation status of specific genomic loci following bisulfite conversion, which differentially modifies unmethylated cytosines to uracil while leaving 5-methylcytosine intact. MSP relies on primers designed to anneal specifically to either methylated or unmethylated sequences, and its success similarly depends on the polymerase’s ability to discriminate against mismatches introduced by bisulfite treatment. Despite advances in primer design and reaction optimization, the performance of both ASA and MSP remains fundamentally limited by the intrinsic selectivity of the DNA polymerase used.

Numerous studies have sought to enhance polymerase fidelity through protein engineering. Known high-fidelity polymerases such as Pfu (from Pyrococcus furiosus) possess proofreading 3′→5′ exonuclease activity but are often less suitable for ASA due to their processivity and kinetics. In contrast, Taq and its derivative KlenTaq lack exonuclease activity but offer robust amplification under standard PCR conditions. Prior art, including international patent applications WO 2005/074350, US2012/0258501, WO 2011/157435, and German patent DE 10 2006 025 153, has described mutations in conserved motifs (e.g., motif C) of family A polymerases to improve selectivity, typically by substituting polar residues like glutamine or histidine with hydrophobic amino acids such as leucine. These efforts demonstrated that altering residues involved in primer-template interaction could modulate mismatch extension efficiency.

However, a persistent technical problem remained: existing mutants either exhibited insufficient selectivity for demanding diagnostic applications, suffered from reduced catalytic activity, or displayed instability during storage or in complex biological matrices such as whole blood. The present invention addresses this gap by focusing on basic amino acid residues—specifically arginines and lysines—that contact the phosphate backbone of the primer strand in the closed conformation of KlenTaq. Through systematic saturation mutagenesis and high-throughput screening, the inventors discovered that substitution of these basic residues, particularly with hydrophobic or polar uncharged amino acids, yields mutants with dramatically enhanced mismatch discrimination while maintaining high activity, thermostability, and compatibility with crude samples. Notably, the R660V mutant of KlenTaq exhibits superior performance in ASA, multiplex SNP detection, direct amplification from whole blood, HLA typing, and MSP for cancer biomarker detection such as Septin 9 methylation. This mutant enables reliable distinction between homozygous and heterozygous genotypes, reduces false positives in HLA-SSP assays, and outperforms commercial polymerases in both SNP and methylation detection. The invention thus provides a versatile, high-selectivity DNA polymerase platform for in vitro diagnostic applications in genetic and epigenetic disease profiling.
