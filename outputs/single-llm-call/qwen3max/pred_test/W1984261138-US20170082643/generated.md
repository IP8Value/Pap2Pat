# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of clinical diagnostics, particularly in the context of metabolic disorders. More specifically, the invention provides methods for assessing insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes mellitus through the measurement of circulating levels of 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA), a metabolite generated by the enzymatic activity of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT). The invention further encompasses diagnostic and therapeutic applications based on MNA levels, including the identification of patients who are likely to benefit from treatment with NNMT inhibitors, monitoring of therapeutic response, and dose adjustment of such inhibitors. These methods offer a novel approach to personalized medicine in the management of insulin resistance and related metabolic conditions, enabling clinicians to make informed decisions regarding patient stratification, treatment initiation, and therapeutic optimization.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Insulin resistance is a pathological condition characterized by diminished responsiveness of target tissues—particularly skeletal muscle, liver, and adipose tissue—to the actions of insulin. It represents a central defect in the pathogenesis of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D), metabolic syndrome, and cardiovascular disease. Among the various risk factors associated with insulin resistance, increased abdominal adiposity—especially expansion of visceral white adipose tissue (WAT)—has been consistently identified as a major contributor. Visceral adiposity promotes systemic inflammation, dysregulated adipokine secretion, and elevated free fatty acid flux, all of which impair insulin signaling pathways. Despite extensive research, the molecular mechanisms linking adipose tissue dysfunction to systemic insulin resistance remain incompletely understood, highlighting the need for novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets.

Nicotinamide, the amide form of vitamin B3, serves as a precursor for the biosynthesis of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), a coenzyme essential for redox reactions, energy metabolism, and regulation of sirtuin activity. However, prolonged high-dose administration of nicotinamide has been shown to induce insulin resistance in individuals with normal or impaired glucose tolerance, suggesting a paradoxical role for this vitamin in glucose homeostasis. The underlying mechanism appears to involve the metabolism of nicotinamide by nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT), an enzyme that catalyzes the transfer of a methyl group from S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) to nicotinamide, yielding 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA) and S-adenosylhomocysteine (SAH). Elevated NNMT activity may deplete cellular SAM pools, reduce methylation potential, and alter epigenetic regulation, thereby contributing to metabolic dysfunction.

NNMT is expressed in multiple tissues, with highest levels in the liver, but also significantly in adipose tissue, kidney, and muscle. In murine models, high-fat diet feeding selectively increases NNMT activity in white adipose tissue without corresponding changes in the liver. Moreover, genetic or pharmacological suppression of NNMT protects against diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance. Human studies have reported elevated urinary excretion of MNA and its metabolites in individuals with T2D, but the relationship between plasma MNA concentrations, adipose tissue NNMT expression, and insulin resistance in humans has remained unclear.

Prior art lacks reliable, non-invasive methods for identifying individuals who would benefit from NNMT-targeted therapies. Existing diagnostic tools for insulin resistance—such as the euglycemic-hyperinsulinemic clamp, HOMA-IR index, or oral glucose tolerance test—are either invasive, labor-intensive, or indirect. Furthermore, no validated circulating biomarker currently exists that directly reflects adipose tissue NNMT activity or predicts therapeutic response to NNMT inhibition. This gap in clinical practice limits the ability to implement precision medicine approaches for metabolic disease.

### Technical Problems Underlying the Present Invention

A critical deficiency in current clinical practice is the absence of a robust, quantifiable biomarker that correlates with adipose tissue NNMT expression and the degree of insulin resistance in human patients. Without such a biomarker, it is not possible to identify which patients exhibit elevated NNMT-driven metabolic dysfunction and would therefore derive therapeutic benefit from NNMT inhibition. Additionally, there is no established method to monitor real-time changes in NNMT pathway activity during treatment, making dose titration and assessment of therapeutic efficacy largely empirical.

Existing diagnostic strategies fail to capture the dynamic interplay between adipose tissue metabolism and systemic insulin sensitivity. While BMI and waist circumference provide crude estimates of adiposity, they do not reflect molecular alterations within adipose depots that drive insulin resistance. Similarly, fasting glucose and insulin levels offer only indirect proxies for insulin action and are influenced by numerous confounding variables, including beta-cell function and hepatic glucose output.

The prior art also does not disclose a causal or correlative link between circulating MNA levels and adipose tissue NNMT expression specifically in insulin-resistant or diabetic individuals. Earlier studies either lacked tissue-specific NNMT measurements or failed to stratify participants by glucose tolerance status, leading to inconclusive or contradictory findings. Consequently, the potential utility of MNA as a companion diagnostic for NNMT-targeted therapeutics has remained unrealized.

The present invention addresses these limitations by establishing, for the first time, that plasma MNA concentration serves as a functional readout of adipose tissue NNMT activity in individuals with insulin resistance or T2D. The invention introduces a novel diagnostic paradigm wherein MNA levels are used not only to assess disease severity but also to guide therapeutic decision-making. By leveraging this biomarker, clinicians can objectively determine patient eligibility for NNMT inhibitor therapy, evaluate early treatment response, and adjust dosing regimens to optimize metabolic outcomes—thereby overcoming the significant technical and clinical shortcomings of existing approaches.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for determining whether a patient suffering from or at risk of insulin resistance will benefit from treatment with an inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT). This method comprises determining the level of 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA) in a blood sample obtained from the patient, comparing the determined MNA level with a reference value, and indicating that the patient will benefit from NNMT inhibitor treatment if the MNA level is elevated relative to the reference value. In specific embodiments, the reference value is derived from healthy subjects with normal glucose tolerance, and a patient is deemed likely to benefit if their MNA level exceeds the reference by at least 20% or is greater than or equal to 100 nmol/L.

The invention further provides a method for determining whether a patient is responding to treatment with an NNMT inhibitor. This method involves obtaining a blood sample from the patient after initiation of therapy, measuring the MNA level therein, comparing it with a pre-treatment MNA level obtained prior to drug administration, and indicating a positive treatment response if the post-treatment MNA level is decreased relative to the pre-treatment level. In preferred embodiments, a decrease of at least 10% is considered indicative of therapeutic efficacy.

Additionally, the invention encompasses a method for adjusting the dose of a therapeutic NNMT inhibitor administered to a patient. This method includes measuring the MNA level in a blood sample taken during treatment, comparing it with the patient’s pre-treatment MNA level, and increasing, decreasing, or maintaining the current dose based on the magnitude of MNA reduction. For instance, insufficient MNA lowering may prompt a dose escalation, whereas excessive reduction or adverse effects may warrant dose reduction.

The invention also includes a method for determining the degree of insulin resistance in a patient, comprising measuring plasma MNA concentration and correlating it with established metrics of insulin sensitivity such as glucose infusion rate (GIR) during a euglycemic-hyperinsulinemic clamp. Higher MNA levels are associated with greater insulin resistance, particularly in individuals with impaired glucose tolerance or T2D.

It is expressly disclaimed that the invention encompasses methods of treatment per se or the use of MNA as a therapeutic agent. The scope of the invention is limited to diagnostic, prognostic, and dose-monitoring methods based on MNA quantification in the context of NNMT pathway activity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the term “comprise” is to be interpreted as “include” and is not intended to exclude additional elements or steps. The term “about” means within ±10% of a stated numerical value unless otherwise specified. “Body mass index (BMI)” is defined as body weight in kilograms divided by the square of height in meters (kg/m²). An “inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT)” refers to any compound—small molecule, peptide, nucleic acid, or antibody—that reduces NNMT enzymatic activity or expression. A “level of 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA)” denotes the concentration of MNA in a biological sample, typically plasma or serum, measured in nanomoles per liter (nmol/L).

A “small molecule” is an organic compound with a molecular weight less than 900 Daltons that is capable of modulating biological activity. A “peptide” is a short chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds, typically fewer than 50 residues. A “nucleic acid” includes DNA, RNA, antisense oligonucleotides, siRNA, and other polynucleotides capable of regulating gene expression. An “antibody” encompasses monoclonal, polyclonal, chimeric, humanized, or fragment antibodies that specifically bind NNMT or MNA.

A “reference value” is a predetermined MNA concentration derived from a population of healthy subjects with normal glucose tolerance, used as a benchmark for comparison. A “pre-treatment value” is the MNA level measured in a patient before administration of an NNMT inhibitor. “Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D)” is diagnosed according to ADA criteria based on fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, 2-hour OGTT glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L, or HbA1c ≥6.5%. “Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT)” is defined by 2-hour OGTT glucose between 7.8 and 11.0 mmol/L, while “normal glucose tolerance (NGT)” indicates values below these thresholds.

“White adipose tissue (WAT)” refers to fat tissue specialized for energy storage, including both subcutaneous and visceral depots. “Visceral WAT” is intra-abdominal fat surrounding internal organs, and “omental WAT” is a specific visceral depot located in the greater omentum. “Lack of decrease of MNA level” signifies a reduction of less than 10% from baseline following treatment. A “patient” is a human individual diagnosed with or at risk of insulin resistance, IGT, or T2D. A “healthy subject” is an individual without metabolic disease, confirmed by normal glucose tolerance and absence of inflammatory or endocrine disorders. A “therapeutically effective amount” is the dose of an NNMT inhibitor sufficient to produce a clinically meaningful reduction in MNA levels and improvement in insulin sensitivity.

### EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

In a first aspect, the invention provides a method for determining whether a patient will benefit from treatment with an NNMT inhibitor. The method begins by obtaining a blood sample from the patient under fasting conditions. The level of MNA in the plasma is then quantified using liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) or an equivalent validated assay. This measured MNA level is compared to a reference value established from a cohort of healthy subjects with NGT, matched for age and sex. If the patient’s MNA level is found to be increased relative to the reference—specifically, if it exceeds the reference by at least 20%—the patient is indicated as likely to benefit from NNMT inhibitor therapy. In another embodiment, the reference level is set at an absolute threshold of 100 nmol/L; patients with MNA levels at or above this value are selected for treatment.

In a second aspect, the invention enables assessment of treatment response. After initiating therapy with an NNMT inhibitor, a subsequent blood sample is collected from the patient, and MNA concentration is again measured. This post-treatment level is compared to the patient’s own pre-treatment MNA level. A decrease in MNA concentration indicates that the drug is effectively inhibiting NNMT activity. In a preferred embodiment, a reduction of at least 10% from baseline is considered a positive response, supporting continuation of the current regimen.

In a third aspect, the invention facilitates dose adjustment. During ongoing treatment, periodic MNA measurements are performed. If the MNA level remains above a target range (e.g., fails to drop below 80% of baseline), the dose of the NNMT inhibitor may be increased. Conversely, if MNA falls excessively or if adverse effects emerge, the dose may be reduced. This feedback loop ensures that therapy is tailored to individual pharmacodynamic responses, optimizing both efficacy and safety.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the invention without limiting its scope. All molecular weights are expressed in Daltons, and temperatures in degrees Celsius unless otherwise noted. Study participants were selected from three independent cohorts totaling 314 individuals. Inclusion criteria required absence of acute or chronic inflammation (leucocyte count ≤8,000 × 10⁹/L, CRP ≤952 nmol/L), negative GAD antibodies, blood pressure <140/90 mmHg, no cardiovascular or thyroid disease, and no substance abuse or pregnancy. Exclusion criteria included evidence of infection, autoimmune diabetes, or unstable weight (>10% fluctuation in 3 months). All protocols were approved by the University of Leipzig Ethics Committee (Reg. nos. 031-2006, 017-12-23012012), and written informed consent was obtained.

The exercise intervention involved 60 participants stratified into NGT, IGT, and T2D groups (n=20 each) based on 75g OGTT. Participants underwent three weekly 60-minute supervised sessions of endurance training for 12 weeks. Maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂max) was assessed via incremental cycle ergometry, and submaximal heart rate was calculated at 60% of VO₂max. Blood samples were drawn after overnight fast between 08:00–10:00. Body fat was measured by DEXA; insulin sensitivity by HOMA-IR or euglycemic-hyperinsulinemic clamp (GIR <50 μmol/kg/min defined insulin resistance). Plasma insulin was assayed by IMMULITE; lipids by standard enzymatic methods.

Adipose tissue biopsies were analyzed for NNMT mRNA via quantitative real-time RT-PCR using TaqMan assays on an ABI PRISM 7000. Plasma MNA was quantified by LC-MS/MS with electrospray ionization. Sample preparation involved protein precipitation with acetonitrile, centrifugation, and injection of supernatant. Statistical analysis used Student’s t-test (two-sided, p<0.05 significant) and Spearman’s correlation.

Results showed that in 199 surgical patients, NNMT expression was ~2-fold higher in omental and subcutaneous WAT of T2D vs non-diabetic individuals (p<0.01). Plasma MNA correlated strongly with WAT NNMT only in T2D (r=0.6, p<0.001), not in NGT. In insulin-resistant individuals (GIR<50), omental NNMT was 3.5-fold elevated (p<0.001), and MNA correlated with GIR (r=−0.44, p=0.01). After 12-week exercise, NNMT expression decreased by 21% in IGT and 16% in T2D (p<0.001), with strong pre- and post-exercise correlations between NNMT and GIR (r=0.78 and 0.70, respectively). In bariatric surgery patients (n=55), sleeve gastrectomy reduced WAT NNMT by 41–50% and plasma MNA by 24% (p=0.001) over 12 months.

These data confirm that MNA is a dynamic biomarker of adipose NNMT activity in insulin-resistant states and validate its utility for patient selection, response monitoring, and dose guidance in NNMT inhibitor therapy. Experimental deviations due to assay variability or biological heterogeneity are accounted for by using log-transformed data and robust statistical thresholds.
