# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to a method and a kit for evaluating the prognosis in a patient diagnosed with breast cancer, particularly luminal breast cancer that is hormone receptor (HR) positive and human epidermal growth factor receptor type 2 (HER2) negative. More specifically, the invention provides a prognostic evaluation based on the detection and quantification of pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) in tumor tissue samples obtained from such patients. The method and kit are designed to assist clinicians in stratifying patients into risk categories for disease recurrence, thereby enabling more personalized decisions regarding adjuvant therapy, including the potential omission or intensification of treatment regimens.

### 2. The Prior Art

Current approaches for evaluating prognosis in patients with HR+/HER2− breast cancer rely heavily on clinicopathological parameters such as tumor size, nodal status, histological grade, and patient age. Tools such as the Nottingham Prognostic Index (NPI) and the Clinical Treatment Score post-5 years (CTS5) integrate these variables to estimate the risk of late recurrence, particularly beyond the first five years of endocrine therapy. While these tools have demonstrated clinical utility, they exhibit limitations in accurately predicting outcomes for individual patients, especially among younger, premenopausal women who represent a growing proportion of breast cancer cases in East Asia. Furthermore, multigene expression assays—such as Oncotype DX, MammaPrint, EndoPredict, Prosigna, and Breast Cancer Index—offer refined risk stratification but are often cost-prohibitive, require specialized infrastructure, and may not be readily accessible in all healthcare settings. Importantly, these genomic assays do not directly capture metabolic alterations that are increasingly recognized as central drivers of tumor progression and therapeutic resistance. Despite advances in molecular diagnostics, there remains a significant unmet need for a simple, cost-effective, and clinically implementable prognostic tool that incorporates biologically relevant markers reflective of the underlying metabolic reprogramming in breast cancer cells. The present invention addresses this gap by introducing PKM2 and O-GlcNAc—two key regulators of cancer metabolism—as novel immunohistochemical biomarkers that, when assessed in combination, significantly enhance the accuracy of recurrence risk prediction in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for evaluating the prognosis in a patient diagnosed with breast cancer, wherein the breast cancer is luminal, hormone receptor (HR) positive, and human epidermal growth factor receptor type 2 (HER2) negative. The method comprises obtaining a formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tumor tissue sample from the patient; performing immunohistochemical staining on the sample to detect the presence and abundance of pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc); quantifying the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc using a Q score derived from the product of staining intensity and the percentage of immunopositive cells; and classifying the patient as having a high risk of recurrence if both PKM2 and O-GlcNAc are expressed at high levels, as defined by a Q score exceeding the mean value observed in a reference population. The invention further encompasses a kit for carrying out this method, which includes primary antibodies specific for PKM2 and O-GlcNAc, detection reagents compatible with standard immunohistochemistry protocols, and instructions for use. The method and kit offer significant advantages over existing prognostic tools by integrating metabolic biomarkers that are mechanistically linked to tumor aggressiveness and endocrine resistance, providing enhanced discriminatory power for disease-free survival, particularly in long-term follow-up. The combined assessment of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc yields a synergistic prognostic signal that is independent of—and complementary to—conventional clinical parameters, thereby enabling more precise risk stratification and informed clinical decision-making regarding adjuvant therapy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Definition

In the context of the present invention, “data representation” refers to the numerical or categorical output generated from the immunohistochemical analysis of tumor tissue, specifically the Q score, which serves as a quantitative measure of biomarker expression. “High expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc” are defined as Q scores greater than the arithmetic mean of the Q scores obtained from a cohort of HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients with known long-term clinical outcomes. The Q score is calculated by multiplying the staining intensity score (ranging from 0 to 3, where 0 = negative, 1 = weak, 2 = moderate, 3 = strong) by the percentage of immunopositive tumor cells (expressed as a whole number from 0 to 100), resulting in a value between 0 and 300. The “clinical treatment score post-5 years (CTS5)” is a validated prognostic index that integrates tumor size, tumor grade, patient age, and the number of positive lymph nodes to estimate the risk of distant recurrence between 5 and 10 years after diagnosis in patients receiving endocrine therapy. It is used herein as a benchmark for comparing the prognostic performance of the PKM2-O-GlcNAc biomarker panel.

### High Expression of Pyruvate Kinase Isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) is Associated With Poor Clinical Outcome in Luminal Breast Cancer

The association between PKM2 expression and clinical outcome in luminal breast cancer was initially established through bioinformatic analysis of the METABRIC dataset, a large publicly available cohort of breast cancer patients with annotated molecular subtypes and long-term survival data. The study protocol for subsequent retrospective validation was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Chang Gung Memorial Hospital (IRB#201700716A3), and informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of the study. Patient selection focused on individuals diagnosed with HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer between 2005 and 2013, with complete pathological records and available formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tumor blocks. From an initial pool of 3166 patients, a final cohort of 162 subjects—comprising 112 non-recurrent and 50 recurrent cases with a minimum 10-year follow-up—was enrolled. PKM2 expression was analyzed by immunohistochemistry using a validated anti-PKM2 monoclonal antibody (D78A4, Cell Signaling Technology) at a dilution of 1:100. Staining was evaluated independently by two board-certified pathologists blinded to clinical outcomes. Survival analysis was performed using the Kaplan-Meier method, with statistical significance assessed by the log-rank test. Results demonstrated that patients with high PKM2 expression (top quartile in METABRIC; Q > mean in the validation cohort) exhibited significantly worse 10-year overall survival (p = 0.005) and relapse-free survival (p = 0.045) compared to those with low expression, confirming PKM2 as a robust prognostic marker in luminal tumors.

### High Expression of O-Linked β-N-Acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) and PKM2 Can Predict Poor Prognosis of HR+/HER2- Breast Cancer

Immunohistochemical assessment of O-GlcNAc and PKM2 was conducted on consecutive serial sections of tumor tissue from the 162-patient retrospective cohort. Antigen retrieval was performed using citrate buffer (pH 6.0) under autoclave conditions, followed by staining with anti-O-GlcNAc (1:50, Biolegend) and anti-PKM2 (1:100) antibodies using the Novolink Polymer Detection System. Patient characteristics included median age of 51–53 years, with balanced representation of luminal A and luminal B subtypes based on Ki-67 index and histological grade. Expression levels of both biomarkers were quantified using the Q score system. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that high expression of either O-GlcNAc (p = 0.038) or PKM2 (p = 0.032) was significantly associated with reduced disease-free survival (DFS). Critically, when patients were stratified by combined double-marker status, those exhibiting high expression of both O-GlcNAc and PKM2 (O-GlcNAc^High^/PKM2^High^) showed markedly worse DFS compared to those with low expression of both markers (O-GlcNAc^Low^/PKM2^Low^), with a highly significant p-value of 0.004. This synergistic effect was visually evident in DFS curve diagrams, where the double-high group diverged early and consistently from the double-low group. Subgroup analysis confirmed that the prognostic power of the double-marker signature was preserved within both luminal A and luminal B subtypes, and was strongly associated with documented recurrence events. These findings establish the combined O-GlcNAc/PKM2 IHC profile as a superior predictor of poor prognosis in HR+/HER2− breast cancer.

### Logistic Regression and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve Analysis

To evaluate the incremental prognostic value of O-GlcNAc and PKM2 beyond standard clinical parameters, logistic regression and ROC curve analyses were performed. Logistic regression models incorporating PKM2, O-GlcNAc, and clinical variables (invasive tumor size, T stage, or CTS5) were constructed to predict 10-year DFS status. All three components—PKM2, O-GlcNAc, and each clinical parameter—significantly contributed to model performance in triple-marker configurations. ROC curve analysis was then used to compare the diagnostic accuracy of different models, with the area under the curve (AUC) serving as the primary metric. The model combining T stage, O-GlcNAc, and PKM2 achieved the highest AUC of 0.722, outperforming models based on CTS5-O-GlcNAc-PKM2 (AUC = 0.712) and invasive tumor size-O-GlcNAc-PKM2 (AUC = 0.699). Single-marker models (PKM2, O-GlcNAc, or CTS5 alone) and the dual-marker O-GlcNAc-PKM2 model showed lower AUCs, underscoring the necessity of integrating clinical staging with metabolic biomarkers. These results demonstrate that the inclusion of O-GlcNAc and PKM2 expression data significantly enhances the predictive power of conventional staging systems, providing a more comprehensive and accurate assessment of recurrence risk. The combined PKM2-O-GlcNAc-CTS5 model, while slightly less performant than the T stage-based model, still offers a clinically valuable tool, particularly in settings where detailed T staging is less reliable. Overall, the statistical analyses confirm that the metabolic signature captured by O-GlcNAc and PKM2 provides independent and complementary information that refines prognostic stratification in HR+/HER2− luminal breast cancer.
