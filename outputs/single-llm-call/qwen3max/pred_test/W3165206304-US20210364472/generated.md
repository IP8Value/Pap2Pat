# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates generally to the field of non-destructive testing (NDT) of composite materials, and more particularly to systems and methods for high-resolution detection, characterization, and quantification of foreign object inclusions, bond line anomalies, impact damage, wrinkles, porosity, degree of cure, and layer orientation within fiber-reinforced polymer composites using ultrasonic inspection techniques. The invention encompasses novel signal processing algorithms, data visualization methodologies, and hardware configurations that enable accurate sizing and spatial mapping of sub-millimeter defects in woven carbon fiber laminates through pulse-echo ultrasonic scanning, thereby addressing critical limitations in existing NDT approaches for advanced composite structures used in aerospace, automotive, and other high-performance industries.

### 2. Description of Related Art

Non-destructive testing (NDT) is a critical discipline in modern engineering, enabling the evaluation of material integrity, structural soundness, and manufacturing quality without causing damage to the inspected component. Traditional NDT methods include visual inspection, liquid penetrant testing, magnetic particle testing, radiographic testing, eddy current testing, and ultrasonic testing. Among these, ultrasonic testing has emerged as a dominant technique for inspecting composite materials due to its safety, portability, cost-effectiveness, and ability to provide depth-resolved information about internal features. U.S. Pat. No. 9,207,639 discloses methods for visualizing A-scan ultrasonic data in three-dimensional space, enhancing operator interpretation of subsurface features. Similarly, U.S. Pat. No. 8,265,886 describes a system for non-destructive testing of pipes that extracts defect information from ultrasonic signals, while U.S. Pat. No. 9,121,817 introduces an ultrasonic testing device with an adjustable water column height to maintain consistent acoustic coupling. U.S. Pat. No. 10,302,600 details an inspection device featuring a nozzle portion and transducer arrangement optimized for surface scanning.

Further advancements include U.S. Pat. No. 4,215,583, which employs ultrasonic complex impedance plane analysis for bond testing, representing impedance variations to assess adhesive integrity. U.S. Pat. No. 4,184,373 provides means for transmitting ultrasonic wave energy to evaluate bond quality, and U.S. Pat. No. 8,347,723 describes a sonic resonator system with a calibration method to ensure measurement accuracy. US Patent Publication No. 2019/0293610 addresses the detection of “kiss bonds”—imperfect adhesive interfaces lacking mechanical strength—through specialized ultrasonic signal analysis. U.S. Pat. No. 7,574,915 simplifies impedance plane bondtesting by integrating an ultrasonic transducer with an electronic processing unit. Additional relevant patents include U.S. Pat. No. 8,234,924 for damage location in composites, U.S. Pat. No. 7,017,422 for bond strength testing, and US Patent Publication No. 2014/0216158 describing air-coupled ultrasonic contactless methods.

Patents such as 10,444,195; 2019/0187107; 2018/0120268; 10,605,781; 10,161,910; 7,895,895; 8,522,615; 7,010,980; 6,959,602; 9,297,789; 6,945,111; 10,697,941; 10,345,272; 5,408,882; 10,761,067; 10,953,608; and 7,975,549 collectively address various aspects of ultrasonic inspection, including phased arrays, immersion systems, signal processing, and defect classification. Despite these innovations, significant challenges remain in the reliable detection and precise quantification of small-scale defects—particularly in woven composites where structural noise from fiber undulation complicates signal interpretation.

Ultrasonic testing remains central to NDT, especially for layered materials like carbon fiber reinforced polymers (CFRPs). Composite materials, widely adopted in aerospace for their high strength-to-weight ratio, are prone to manufacturing defects such as delaminations, voids, inclusions, and incorrect layup. Porosity measurement, cure monitoring, adhesive inspection, and laminate characterization are essential for ensuring performance and safety. Automated calibration, inspection of curved composite structures, structural health monitoring, interface guided waves, and workpiece inspection represent active research areas. However, conventional systems often fail to deliver sufficient resolution for sub-millimeter foreign objects or accurate bond line thickness assessment. Existing approaches suffer from limited through-thickness resolution, inability to distinguish between defect types (e.g., air pockets vs. solid inclusions), and poor performance on woven architectures due to inherent signal scattering. Thus, there exists a pressing need for an integrated NDT system capable of high-fidelity defect sizing, real-time visualization, and multi-parameter material characterization—capabilities not fully realized in the prior art.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

Non-destructive testing is essential for ensuring the reliability of composite components, yet current systems lack the precision needed for modern manufacturing tolerances. The present invention addresses this gap by introducing real-time visualization and high-resolution defect quantification through advanced ultrasonic signal processing. In one embodiment, the system comprises a spherically focused ultrasonic transducer mounted in a portable housing assembly, coupled with a processor configured to generate enhanced C-scan images via Gaussian smoothing and Fourier-based interpolation, followed by edge detection using the magnitude of the gradient (MGT) to precisely delineate foreign object boundaries. An alternative embodiment integrates a material database containing reference A-scan signatures for known defect types, enabling automated classification through pattern matching. Another alternative embodiment employs an artificial intelligence module trained on labeled ultrasonic datasets to identify and characterize multiple defect classes—including kiss bonds, impact damage, and wrinkles—simultaneously.

The system is specifically designed to detect foreign objects with characteristic dimensions as small as 1.59 mm (1/16 inch), distinguishing them from benign features such as air pockets or resin-rich zones based on amplitude gradients, frequency content, and spatial coherence. Foreign object characteristics—including diameter, area, depth, and edge irregularity—are quantified with an average error of 0.11 mm in equivalent diameter, significantly outperforming conventional 6 dB drop methods and prior art algorithms. The invention further enables the visualization of bond line thickness variations, wrinkle geometry in both unidirectional and woven laminates, porosity distribution, and degree of cure through tailored signal analysis pipelines, all accessible via an intuitive graphical user interface that supports interactive exploration and reporting.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Non-destructive testing systems for composite materials must balance resolution, speed, and interpretability. The present invention provides a comprehensive solution through a modular architecture centered on a high-frequency ultrasonic transducer housed in a precision-engineered assembly. This system is designed for pulse-echo operation, requiring access to only one side of the test article—a critical advantage for in-service inspection. The transducer housing assembly includes a central housing made of corrosion-resistant aluminum, an elongate member of stainless steel for rigidity, and a lens housing that secures a spherically focused 7.5 MHz transducer. A fluid connector attaches to a water supply, enabling bubbler-style coupling without full immersion, while a membrane at the distal end maintains consistent acoustic contact. Sealing elements prevent leakage, and a mounting bracket allows attachment to robotic arms, translation stages, or manual handles.

During operation, the transducer emits short ultrasonic pulses into the composite. Reflected signals (A-scans) are captured at each scan point across a raster grid. The processor first performs time-shift correction by fitting a polynomial surface to front-wall echo times, compensating for minor surface topography. Next, it generates an average A-scan to identify ply interfaces and defines a gate window covering three-quarters of the inter-ply distance. Within this gate, a modified Maximum Gated Amplitude (MGA) method extracts peak amplitudes—without absolute value conversion—to preserve phase information critical for edge definition. The resulting 2D amplitude map undergoes Gaussian smoothing with σ = 5/3 of the step size to suppress noise, followed by 5× Fourier interpolation in both lateral dimensions to enhance apparent resolution.

Foreign object detection proceeds by computing the magnitude of the gradient (G) of the interpolated image using fourth-order central differences. The MGT algorithm then identifies boundary points as locations of maximum gradient magnitude along radial projections from an interior seed point. This shape-agnostic approach yields closed contours whose enclosed area is converted to an effective diameter. Validation against optically measured PTFE inclusions shows average diameter errors of 0.11 mm—superior to literature values ranging from 0.175 mm to 2.1 mm. The system distinguishes foreign objects from air pockets by analyzing amplitude histograms, frequency shifts, and Laplace transform features stored in a material database.

Alternative embodiments include: (1) a database-driven mode where characteristic A-scan “fingerprints” for Teflon, Mylar, paper, etc., are matched against incoming signals; (2) an AI-enhanced mode using neural networks trained on thousands of labeled scans to classify defects and predict mechanical impact via finite element analysis (FEA) integration; and (3) a bond line thickness module that constructs 3D surfaces from time-of-flight measurements between adherend echoes, color-coded for deviation from nominal thickness. For impact damage, the system reconciles C-scans from multiple gates and frequencies to generate 3D damage profiles aligned to centroids, enabling accurate von Mises stress prediction. Wrinkle detection uses B-scan series with automatic tracing and 3D rendering, while porosity and cure state are inferred from amplitude attenuation and velocity changes correlated with DSC-derived polymer models.

The system supports curved parts via dynamic focusing and offset elements, and operates in manual, robotic, or stage-mounted modes. Data input screens allow entry of material parameters (density, sound speed), while output modules display A-, B-, C-, and 3D scans with editing tools for region-of-interest analysis. Full waveform retrieval enables post-processing flexibility, and preliminary scans guide optimal gate placement. Through these innovations, the invention overcomes longstanding limitations in composite NDT, delivering unprecedented accuracy in defect sizing, material characterization, and structural health assessment.
