# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to polypeptides and nucleic acid molecules derived from adeno-associated virus (AAV). More specifically, the invention pertains to novel capsid polypeptides and their encoding nucleic acid sequences that originate from an endogenous viral element of AAV found in marsupial genomes. These polypeptides and nucleic acids are useful for the production of recombinant AAV virions with unique biological properties, particularly for applications in gene therapy. The invention further encompasses vectors, host cells, and methods for producing and utilizing these recombinant AAV virions, as well as chimeric capsid variants generated through diversification techniques.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Gene therapy represents a promising approach for the treatment of genetic disorders, cancers, and other diseases by delivering functional genes to replace or supplement defective ones. Among the various viral vectors developed for this purpose, adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) have emerged as particularly favorable due to their non-pathogenic nature, ability to infect both dividing and non-dividing cells, and capacity for long-term transgene expression without integrating into the host genome under normal conditions. AAVs are small, single-stranded DNA viruses belonging to the genus Dependoparvovirus within the family Parvoviridae. They require co-infection with a helper virus, such as adenovirus or herpesvirus, for productive replication.

The AAV genome is approximately 4.7 kilobases in length and is flanked by inverted terminal repeats (ITRs) that serve as origins of replication and packaging signals. The genome encodes two main open reading frames: rep and cap. The rep gene produces four non-structural proteins (Rep78, Rep68, Rep52, and Rep40) involved in viral DNA replication, transcriptional regulation, and site-specific integration. The cap gene gives rise to three structural proteins—VP1, VP2, and VP3—through alternative splicing and differential translation initiation. These capsid proteins assemble into an icosahedral shell that protects the viral genome and mediates cell entry via receptor binding and endocytosis.

Despite significant advances, current recombinant AAV (rAAV) vectors face limitations including pre-existing immunity in human populations, restricted tissue tropism, inefficient transduction of certain cell types, and challenges in large-scale manufacturing. These constraints have motivated the search for novel AAV capsids with improved properties. Naturally occurring AAV serotypes exhibit considerable diversity in their capsid sequences, which influences their receptor usage, intracellular trafficking, and immune recognition. However, the pool of known natural AAV variants remains limited, especially from non-primate species.

Endogenous viral elements (EVEs)—viral sequences integrated into host germlines and inherited vertically—offer a rich reservoir of ancient viral diversity. Recent studies have revealed that EVEs derived from parvoviruses, including AAVs, are present in diverse vertebrate genomes. These genomic “fossils” can provide insights into historical viral lineages and serve as templates for reconstructing ancestral viral proteins. The discovery of an AAV-derived EVE in marsupials, designated mAAV-EVE1, presents a unique opportunity to access capsid sequences that diverged tens of millions of years ago from contemporary AAVs. This evolutionary distance may yield capsid structures with novel functional characteristics not observed in extant AAVs, thereby addressing the need for alternative recombinant AAV vectors with enhanced therapeutic potential.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention identifies and characterizes an adeno-associated virus-derived endogenous viral element, designated mAAV-EVE1, found in the genomes of macropodoid marsupials. This element represents a fossilized remnant of an ancient exogenous AAV that integrated into the germline of a common ancestor of kangaroos, wallabies, and related species approximately 27 to 45 million years ago. Although the endogenous locus contains numerous mutations that disrupt full-length protein expression in modern species, phylogenetic reconstruction enables the inference of a functional ancestral sequence.

The invention provides isolated nucleic acid molecules encoding capsid polypeptides derived from the reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 sequence. These nucleic acid molecules may encode full-length VP1, VP2, or VP3 proteins or functional fragments thereof. In particular embodiments, the capsid polypeptide comprises an amino acid sequence having at least 80%, 85%, 90%, 95%, or 98% sequence identity to the inferred ancestral mAAV-EVE1 VP3 sequence. The capsid polypeptide includes conserved structural domains characteristic of AAV capsids, such as an eight-stranded β-barrel core, variable surface loops (VR-I through VR-IX), and a phospholipase A2 domain in the VP1-unique region, albeit with substitutions at key catalytic residues.

The invention further introduces chimeric capsid polypeptides comprising regions derived from mAAV-EVE1 and one or more heterologous AAV capsid sequences. These chimeric polypeptides may exhibit hybrid properties, such as altered tropism or reduced immunogenicity. A chimeric capsid polypeptide of the invention may comprise at least one variable region from mAAV-EVE1 and the remainder from a reference AAV serotype (e.g., AAV2, AAV8, or AAV9), and may share at least 70% sequence identity with either parent sequence over the aligned region. Specific regions of interest include VR-VII, which is substantially larger in mAAV-EVE1 than in known AAVs and contributes to a unique surface topology.

Recombinant AAV (rAAV) virions are provided that comprise a capsid formed from mAAV-EVE1-derived capsid polypeptides or chimeric variants thereof, encapsidating a heterologous nucleic acid sequence flanked by AAV ITRs. The invention also encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding these capsid polypeptides, which may be codon-optimized for expression in mammalian cells. Vectors comprising these nucleic acid molecules—whether episomal plasmids, viral vectors, or integrated constructs—are disclosed, as are host cells transfected or transduced with such vectors.

Methods are described for introducing a heterologous sequence into a target cell using rAAV virions of the invention, applicable in vitro, ex vivo, or in vivo. Additionally, methods for producing chimeric capsid genes via DNA family shuffling or other recombination techniques are provided, enabling the generation of capsid libraries for directed evolution. The invention thus offers a comprehensive platform for developing next-generation AAV vectors based on deep evolutionary insights.

A “vector” refers to a nucleic acid molecule capable of transporting another nucleic acid to which it has been linked. An “AAV vector” is a vector derived from AAV, typically containing AAV ITRs flanking a transgene cassette. A “recombinant AAV” (rAAV) is a viral particle comprising an AAV capsid encapsidating a heterologous nucleic acid sequence, usually flanked by AAV ITRs but lacking rep and cap coding sequences. “ITR” denotes inverted terminal repeat, the ~145-nucleotide hairpin structure at each end of the AAV genome essential for replication and packaging.

A “functional” capsid polypeptide is one capable of assembling into a viral capsid that can package nucleic acid and mediate cellular entry, even if replication competence is absent. “Corresponding nucleotides” or “corresponding amino acid residues” refer to positions aligned based on sequence homology or structural equivalence. A “heterologous sequence” is a nucleic acid segment not naturally associated with the vector or viral genome in which it is placed. “Operably-linked” describes a configuration where a promoter drives expression of a downstream coding sequence.

A “host cell” is any cell susceptible to transformation or transduction by a vector of the invention, including mammalian, insect, yeast, or bacterial cells. An “isolated” nucleic acid molecule is one separated from its natural genomic context. A “subject” includes any animal, preferably a mammal, and most preferably a human, to whom therapeutic or diagnostic agents may be administered.

The definitions provided herein are illustrative and non-limiting; equivalent meanings known in the art are encompassed. The invention introduces nucleic acid molecules and polypeptides derived from mAAV-EVE1, an endogenous AAV element identified in macropodoid marsupials. mAAV-EVE1 occupies an orthologous genomic locus in all members of the suborder Macropodiformes, indicating integration prior to their radiation. The reconstructed ancestral mAAV-EVE1 genome is approximately 4.4 kb and lacks ITRs but retains rep and cap open reading frames.

The mAAV-EVE1 cap gene encodes VP1, VP2, and VP3 polypeptides. VP1 includes a phospholipase A2 domain with a histidine-to-glutamine substitution at the catalytic site. VP3, the major capsid protein, shares ~61–65% sequence identity with AAV2 and AAV8 and conserves the canonical AAV fold. Fragments retaining assembly competence are included. Chimeric capsid polypeptides combine mAAV-EVE1 sequences (e.g., VR-VII) with those of other AAVs to create novel tropisms.

Nucleic acid molecules encoding these polypeptides are provided, optionally codon-optimized. The mAAV-EVE1 assembly-activating protein (AAP) is encoded in an alternate ORF within cap and is 201 amino acids long, with conserved hydrophobic, proline-rich, and serine/threonine-rich regions. Functional AAP fragments support capsid assembly. The Rep protein is 581 amino acids, containing RepN and parvovirus_NS1 domains, though key catalytic residues (e.g., Walker A lysine) are substituted, likely inactivating nuclease and helicase functions. Rep fragments retaining DNA-binding but not enzymatic activity may be useful for targeted integration.

Promoters operably linked to these coding sequences enable expression in host cells. Assays for Rep function include ATPase, helicase, and endonuclease activities. The NS2-like protein is also encoded. Collectively, these components enable the production of rAAV virions with mAAV-EVE1-derived capsids for gene delivery.

### Vectors

Vectors of the invention comprise nucleic acid molecules encoding mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptides, AAP, or Rep proteins. These may be episomal (e.g., plasmids) or integrating (e.g., lentiviral vectors). Exemplary vectors include plasmids, cosmids, AAV, lentiviral, retroviral, adenoviral, and herpesviral vectors. Capsid-encoding vectors may provide the gene in trans (separate from the vector genome) or in cis (within the same construct as the transgene). Vectors may carry heterologous sequences encoding therapeutic peptides, RNA molecules, or gene-editing machinery.

For rAAV production, vectors typically contain AAV ITRs flanking the transgene. ITRs may be wild-type or modified for stability. Promoters driving transgene expression include constitutive (e.g., CMV, CAG), inducible (e.g., tetracycline-responsive), or tissue-specific (e.g., synapsin for neurons, albumin for hepatocytes) types. Enhancers, translational signals (Kozak sequence), polyadenylation signals, WPRE elements, signal peptides, and IRES or 2A sequences for multicistronic expression may be incorporated.

### Recombinant AAV

Recombinant AAV virions of the invention comprise capsids formed from mAAV-EVE1-derived polypeptides encapsidating a heterologous nucleic acid flanked by AAV ITRs. Production involves co-transfecting host cells (e.g., HEK293T) with: (1) a vector encoding the mAAV-EVE1 cap gene, (2) a vector encoding rep (from AAV2 or another serotype), and (3) a vector containing the transgene flanked by ITRs. Helper functions (e.g., adenoviral E1, E2a, E4, VA RNA) are supplied in trans. Virions are harvested from cells and media, treated with benzonase to digest unpackaged DNA, precipitated with PEG, and purified via CsCl gradient ultracentrifugation or chromatography on solid supports (e.g., AVB Sepharose).

### Host Cells

Host cells comprising nucleic acid molecules, vectors, or rAAV virions of the invention are provided. These include mammalian cells (HEK293, HeLa), insect cells (Sf9), and others suitable for rAAV production or transduction studies.

### Compositions and Methods

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise rAAV virions and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or adjuvant (e.g., saline, polysorbate-80). Methods for introducing heterologous sequences include in vitro (cell culture), ex vivo (modify cells then transplant), and in vivo (direct administration) approaches. Therapeutic administration routes include intravenous, intramuscular, intrathecal, intraocular, or intranasal delivery.

### Capsid Diversification

Capsid diversification techniques generate libraries of chimeric capsid genes. DNA family shuffling involves fragmenting mAAV-EVE1 and other AAV cap genes with DNase I, reassembling fragments via PCR without primers, and cloning into vectors. This produces chimeric genes expressing hybrid capsids. rAAV virions displaying these capsids can be selected for desired traits (e.g., enhanced brain penetration). This method accelerates evolution of novel vectors.

### Other Uses

mAAV-EVE sequences enable detection of related AAVs in biological samples. PCR primers designed to conserved mAAV-EVE regions amplify target sequences; amplicons are sequenced to identify viral strains. This aids in species assignment of unknown AAV isolates and surveillance of viral diversity.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Identification and Characterization of mAAV-EVE1 Genome and Proteins

Tissue samples from eastern grey kangaroo (Macropus giganteus) were collected under ethical permits. Genomic DNA was extracted from liver, muscle, and brain using proteinase K digestion and column purification. Initial PCR with conserved AAV primers (AA55/SIG−) yielded amplicons with high similarity to primate AAVs. Linker-mediated genome walking isolated flanking sequences, revealing a full-length endogenous AAV element, mAAV-EVE1, 96.4% identical to a tammar wallaby EVE. Orthology was confirmed by matching flanking sequences across macropods.

PCR screening across Diprotodontia showed mAAV-EVE1 restricted to Macropodiformes. Sequencing of 16 species revealed two classes: full-length EVEs in Macropodidae and internally deleted versions in Potoroidae and Hypsiprymnodontidae. Phylogenetic mapping placed integration between 45–27 MYA. Maximum likelihood reconstruction of the ancestral sequence (Node 39) corrected frameshifts and stop codons. The inferred genome contained rep and cap ORFs, a TATA box upstream of rep, and polyA signals.

Promoter analysis identified YY1 and MLTF/USF binding sites analogous to AAV2 P5. Rep78 and VP3 equivalents were evident; VP1 and truncated Rep isoforms were predicted. Rep protein modeling showed conserved nuclease (HUH motif) and helicase (Walker motifs) domains, though catalytic residues were substituted. VP3 modeling using AAV8 as template revealed conserved β-barrel core and enlarged VR-VII, creating unique surface topology. AAP was identified in an alternate ORF with conserved domain architecture.

### Example 2. Modification of mAAV-EVE Capsid

Residues in mAAV-EVE1 VP3 were modified to restore catalytic function or alter surface properties. For instance, glutamine in the PLA2 domain was reverted to histidine. Other changes targeted VR-VII to modulate receptor interactions. Modified cap genes were synthesized and cloned for rAAV production.

### Example 3. Production of rAAV

The reconstructed mAAV-EVE1 cap gene was cloned into a plasmid under a CMV promoter. HEK293T cells were co-transfected with this plasmid, an AAV2 rep plasmid, and an ITR-flanked GFP reporter plasmid, along with adenoviral helper plasmids. Cells were harvested 72h post-transfection, lysed, and supernatant treated with benzonase. rAAV was precipitated with 10% PEG-8000, centrifuged, resuspended, and purified on a CsCl gradient (1.32–1.41 g/mL). Fractions were dialyzed into PBS and filtered (0.22 μm).

### Example 4. Assessment of rAAV

rAAV functionality was assessed by transducing HEK293T cells and measuring GFP expression via flow cytometry. Infectivity was compared to AAV2 and AAV8 controls. Additional assays evaluated thermal stability, resistance to neutralizing antibodies, and tropism in primary cell cultures. Results confirmed that mAAV-EVE1 rAAV efficiently transduces human cells, validating its utility as a gene therapy vector.
