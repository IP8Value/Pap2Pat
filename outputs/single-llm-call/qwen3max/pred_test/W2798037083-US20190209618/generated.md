# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of hematopoietic stem cells, specifically human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs). More particularly, the invention relates to methods and compositions for enhancing the self-renewal and ex vivo expansion of HSPCs through modulation of molecular pathways involving Musashi-2 (MSI2), cytochrome P450 1B1 (CYP1B1), and the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) signaling axis. The invention further encompasses the use of CYP1B1 inhibitors alone or in combination with MSI2 activators to promote HSPC maintenance, expansion, and therapeutic utility in transplantation settings. This field intersects regenerative medicine, hematology, oncology, and molecular biology, with direct applications in treating hematopoietic disorders, malignancies, immunodeficiencies, and conditions requiring hematopoietic reconstitution following myeloablative therapies such as chemotherapy or radiation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Umbilical cord blood (CB) has emerged as a valuable source of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) for allogeneic transplantation due to its ready availability, lower risk of graft-versus-host disease, and less stringent HLA matching requirements compared to bone marrow or mobilized peripheral blood. However, a significant limitation of CB-derived HSPCs is the low absolute number of transplantable cells per unit, which often results in delayed engraftment, poor immune reconstitution, and higher rates of graft failure, particularly in adult recipients. This constraint has spurred extensive research into strategies for ex vivo expansion of HSPCs without compromising their long-term repopulating capacity or inducing malignant transformation. Despite advances in cytokine-based culture systems and small molecule modulators, achieving clinically meaningful expansion of functional, long-term repopulating HSCs remains a major challenge. A critical barrier to progress is the incomplete understanding of the molecular pathways that govern HSPC self-renewal versus differentiation. While key regulators such as HOXB4, Wnt/β-catenin, and Notch have been explored, their manipulation often leads to lineage skewing, exhaustion, or leukemogenesis. Thus, there exists an urgent need to identify novel, physiologically relevant molecular targets and signaling nodes that can be safely and effectively modulated to enhance HSPC expansion while preserving stemness and genomic integrity.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is motivated by the discovery that overexpression of Musashi-2 (MSI2), an RNA-binding protein highly expressed in primitive human HSPCs, confers potent pro-self-renewal phenotypes, including enhanced clonogenic potential, increased competitive repopulation capacity in vivo, and resistance to differentiation cues. Mechanistically, MSI2 was found to post-transcriptionally repress Cytochrome P450 1B1 (CYP1B1), a downstream effector of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) signaling pathway, which normally promotes HSPC differentiation and limits self-renewal. Inhibition of CYP1B1 activity, either genetically or pharmacologically, mimics the effects of MSI2 overexpression, leading to significant expansion of CD34+ HSPCs. The invention provides a method of increasing the self-renewal and expansion of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs), wherein said HSPCs are defined as CD34+ cells. These HSPCs may be sourced from umbilical cord blood, bone marrow, mobilized peripheral blood, or other hematopoietic tissues. The method may be performed in vivo, ex vivo, or in vitro. Central to the invention is the use of CYP1B1 inhibitors, which include but are not limited to stilbenoids (e.g., resveratrol analogs), flavonoids (e.g., chrysin, apigenin), coumarins, alkaloids, synthetic small molecules, natural products, monoclonal antibodies, antisense oligonucleotides, siRNA, aptamers, and peptibodies. The IC50 of the CYP1B1 inhibitor is preferably in the nanomolar to low micromolar range to ensure specificity and minimize off-target effects. Contacting CD34+ HSPCs with a CYP1B1 inhibitor results in increased frequency and total numbers of primitive HSPCs, enhanced colony-forming unit (CFU) output, and improved engraftment potential in xenotransplantation models. Additionally, the invention contemplates the use of an MSI2 activator—such as an MSI2-encoding mRNA, DNA vector, recombinant MSI2 protein, or functional fragment thereof, optionally conjugated to a protein transfection reagent—to directly elevate MSI2 levels. In a preferred embodiment, HSPCs are contacted with both a CYP1B1 inhibitor and an MSI2 activator to achieve synergistic expansion. The resulting expanded HSPCs are suitable for administration to subjects suffering from diseases amenable to HSPC transplantation, including hematopoietic disorders (e.g., aplastic anemia), malignancies (e.g., leukemia, lymphoma), autoimmune diseases (e.g., multiple sclerosis), and primary or acquired immunodeficiencies. The invention also encompasses a pharmaceutical composition comprising a CYP1B1 inhibitor and an MSI2 activator in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, formulated for ex vivo cell processing or direct in vivo delivery.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

A hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) is defined as a multipotent cell capable of long-term self-renewal and the ability to differentiate into all mature blood cell lineages, including myeloid and lymphoid cells. A hematopoietic progenitor cell (HPC) is a more committed descendant of the HSC that retains proliferative capacity but exhibits limited or no long-term self-renewal potential and is restricted to one or a few lineages. Differentiation refers to the process by which HSCs and HPCs undergo maturation into functionally specialized blood cells such as erythrocytes, granulocytes, monocytes, lymphocytes, and platelets. Culturing denotes the maintenance and propagation of cells under controlled in vitro conditions using appropriate media, growth factors, and physical parameters. A hematopoietic lineage encompasses any of the developmental pathways leading to a specific type of mature blood cell.

### Methods of Expanding HSPCs

The invention provides methods to expand HSPCs by inhibiting CYP1B1 to increase self-renewal and expansion. Alternatively or additionally, MSI2 expression or activity may be increased to achieve the same effect. The goal is to expand HSPCs to increase their frequency and/or absolute number in a given population. Prior to or after expansion, the cell population may be tested for biomarkers such as CD34, CD38, CD90, CD45RA, and lineage markers (Lin) to assess purity and potency. HSPCs may be separated from heterogeneous cell populations using positive selection (e.g., anti-CD34 magnetic beads) or negative selection (e.g., depletion of lineage-positive cells). Following expansion, hematopoietic stem cells (e.g., CD34+CD38−CD90+CD45RA−) or hematopoietic progenitor cells (e.g., CD34+CD38+) may be isolated from the expanded HSPC pool using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) or magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS). Expansion may occur in vivo (within a subject), ex vivo (outside the body followed by reinfusion), or in vitro (in culture vessels). HSPCs may be derived from a single subject (autologous) or multiple donors (allogeneic). Expanded HSPCs are intended for transplantation into recipients. CYP1B1 activity and/or expression may be inhibited using various modalities: small molecule inhibitors (e.g., stilbenoids like piceatannol; flavonoids like luteolin; coumarins like scopoletin; alkaloids like berberine); synthetic compounds; natural products; neutralizing antibodies; antisense nucleic acids; siRNA; aptamers; or peptibodies. MSI2 activators include nucleic acid molecules encoding full-length or functional fragments of MSI2 mRNA or DNA, or the MSI2 protein itself, which may be delivered via viral vectors, electroporation, or protein transfection reagents. In a combinatorial approach, HSPCs are contacted with both a CYP1B1 inhibitor and an MSI2 activator to maximize expansion. Additional hematopoiesis-inducing agents such as stem cell factor (SCF), thrombopoietin (TPO), FLT3 ligand, or UM171 may be included in the culture medium. The combined use of CYP1B1 inhibition and MSI2 activation robustly increases HSPC self-renewal and expansion beyond either intervention alone.

### Expanded HSPCs for Therapeutic Use

Expanded HSPCs produced by the methods described herein are suitable for transplantation or other therapeutic applications. They may be autologous (from the patient) or allogeneic (from a donor). CYP1B1 inhibitors and/or MSI2 activators may also be administered in vivo to stimulate endogenous HSPC expansion. Subjects eligible for treatment include those with hematopoietic disorders (e.g., Fanconi anemia), hematologic malignancies (e.g., acute myeloid leukemia post-chemotherapy), autoimmune diseases (e.g., systemic lupus erythematosus), or immunodeficiencies (e.g., severe combined immunodeficiency). The therapy is particularly beneficial for patients who have undergone myeloablative chemotherapy and/or radiation and require rapid hematopoietic reconstitution. A therapeutically effective amount of expanded HSPCs—typically defined as sufficient to achieve durable multilineage engraftment—is administered via intravenous infusion.

### Compositions

The invention includes compositions comprising a CYP1B1 inhibitor, alone or in combination with an MSI2 activator, formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. Cell culture embodiments contain basal media (e.g., StemSpan™), cytokines (e.g., SCF, TPO, FLT3L), and the active agents. Pharmaceutical compositions for in vivo use may be sterile, isotonic, and compatible with parenteral administration, including liposomal or nanoparticle formulations to enhance bioavailability and target specificity.

### Methods of Culturing Leukemic Cells

Leukemic cell cultures may be produced by treating leukemic stem/progenitor cells with a CYP1B1 inhibitor or MSI2 activator to study drug resistance or differentiation blockade. These cultures serve as platforms for high-throughput screening of compounds that modulate HSPC or leukemic stem cell behavior. Terms such as “cell growth” refer to increases in cell number or biomass, while “cell death” encompasses apoptosis, necrosis, or differentiation-induced loss of proliferative potential.

### Example 1: Inhibition of Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase Causes Expansion of Hematopoietic Stem and Progenitor Cells

Human CD34+ HSPCs were isolated from umbilical cord blood units obtained with ethical approval. Cells were cultured in serum-free medium supplemented with cytokines. Lentiviral vectors expressing MSI2 or control sequences were produced by co-transfecting HEK293T cells with packaging plasmids. shRNAs targeting MSI2 or CYP1B1 were cloned into lentiviral backbones and validated by qRT-PCR. Transduced cells were subjected to clonogenic assays (CFU-GEMM, BFU-E, CFU-GM) and secondary replating to assess self-renewal. Flow cytometry was used to quantify CD34+ populations and cell cycle status (Ki67/DAPI). RNA sequencing identified differentially expressed genes, with gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) revealing downregulation of AHR signaling upon MSI2 overexpression. Chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (CLIP-seq) mapped MSI2-bound transcripts, confirming CYP1B1 as a direct target. Xenotransplantation into NSG mice demonstrated enhanced human chimerism and multilineage engraftment from MSI2-overexpressing or CYP1B1-inhibited HSPCs. Limiting dilution analysis (LDA) quantified functional HSC frequency, showing significant increases with treatment. Statistical analyses confirmed significance (p<0.05).

### Results & Discussion

MSI2 was found to post-transcriptionally regulate human HSPC self-renewal. It is highly expressed in primitive cord blood HSPCs. Lentiviral overexpression of MSI2 increased primary and secondary CFU output, maintained CD34+ cell numbers during culture, reduced apoptosis, and enhanced short-term repopulating cell (STRC) activity. LDA revealed a 4–6-fold increase in functional HSC frequency. Conversely, MSI2 knockdown impaired clonogenicity and accelerated differentiation. RNA-seq showed repression of AHR pathway genes, particularly CYP1B1. Ex vivo expansion with MSI2 or CYP1B1 inhibitors yielded HSPCs with superior long-term engraftment. Mechanistically, MSI2 binds CYP1B1 mRNA, reducing its translation without affecting transcript levels, thereby attenuating AHR signaling—a known suppressor of HSC self-renewal.

### Example 2: CYP1B1 Knockdown Increases the Number of CD34 Marked HSPCs

shRNA lentiviruses targeting CYP1B1 were transduced into CD34+Lin− cord blood cells. Efficient knockdown (>70%) was confirmed by qRT-PCR. Treated cells exhibited a 2.5-fold increase in CD34+ cell frequency after 7 days in culture compared to control shRNA, demonstrating that CYP1B1 suppression alone is sufficient to expand primitive HSPCs.

### Example 3: High Throughput Screen to Test CYP Family Inhibitors

A library of CYP family inhibitors was screened on cultured CD34+ HSPCs. Compounds were assessed for their ability to maintain CD34 expression and CFU potential. Select CYP1B1-specific inhibitors (e.g., TMS, alpha-naphthoflavone) significantly enhanced HSPC expansion, whereas non-selective or CYP1A1-preferring inhibitors showed minimal or detrimental effects, confirming the specificity of CYP1B1 as a therapeutic target.

### Example 3: Combinatorial HSPC Promoting Effects of SR1 with TMS

SR1 (an AHR antagonist) and TMS (a CYP1B1 inhibitor) were tested alone and in combination. While each agent moderately expanded HSPCs, their combination yielded synergistic increases in CD34+ cell numbers and CFU output, exceeding additive effects, thereby validating dual targeting of the AHR-CYP1B1 axis as a superior strategy for HSPC expansion.
