## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

- introduce obligate intracellular bacteria

Obligate intracellular bacteria represent a distinct class of microbial pathogens that are incapable of replication outside host cells due to extensive genome reduction and metabolic dependency on host-derived nutrients and biosynthetic intermediates. These organisms, which include members of the orders Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales, are responsible for a wide spectrum of human and animal diseases affecting millions globally. Their strict intracellular lifestyle presents unique challenges for both basic research and clinical intervention, as conventional microbiological and genetic techniques developed for free-living bacteria are often inapplicable or ineffective.

- limitations of targeted mutagenesis

The development of robust genetic tools for obligate intracellular bacteria has lagged significantly behind that of facultative or extracellular pathogens. A major limitation lies in the inability to perform targeted mutagenesis—precisely altering or deleting specific genes to assess their functional roles. While random mutagenesis methods such as transposon insertion have provided some insights, they lack the precision required to interrogate individual gene functions, establish causal relationships in pathogenesis, or fulfill molecular Koch’s postulates. Moreover, the essential nature of many genes in these highly reduced genomes means that random insertions may be lethal or non-viable, further constraining the utility of such approaches.

- importance of understanding microbial pathogenesis

Understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying microbial pathogenesis is critical for the rational design of diagnostics, therapeutics, and vaccines. In the context of obligate intracellular bacteria, this requires the ability to manipulate specific genes to determine their contributions to host cell invasion, intracellular survival, immune evasion, and replication. Without precise genetic tools, researchers are limited to correlative observations or indirect inference, hindering the development of targeted interventions against these significant pathogens.

- challenges in creating targeted mutations

Creating targeted mutations in obligate intracellular bacteria is impeded by multiple factors: (1) the absence of selectable markers that function reliably in these organisms; (2) the lack of strong, constitutive promoters to drive expression of resistance genes; (3) difficulties in delivering exogenous DNA into the bacterial cell due to its residence within a host-derived vacuole or cytoplasm; (4) the inability to culture these bacteria extracellularly, which precludes standard transformation and selection protocols; and (5) the high likelihood that disruption of any given gene may be lethal, necessitating conditional or complementable systems. These challenges have collectively stymied progress in reverse genetics for Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales for decades.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales

The present invention relates to genetically modified strains of obligate intracellular bacteria belonging to the orders Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales, including but not limited to *Ehrlichia chaffeensis*, *Ehrlichia canis*, *Anaplasma phagocytophilum*, and *Anaplasma marginale*. These pathogens are etiologic agents of significant human and veterinary diseases, including human monocytic ehrlichiosis, canine monocytic ehrlichiosis, and human granulocytic anaplasmosis.

- describe allelic exchange in Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales

The invention provides a novel method for achieving stable, site-specific allelic exchange in Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales through homologous recombination. This method enables the precise disruption of target genes by insertion of a selectable marker cassette flanked by homology arms, followed by optional restoration of gene function through a second allelic exchange event that reintroduces the intact open reading frame under its native promoter control.

- outline advantages of the disclosure

The disclosed methodology overcomes longstanding barriers in the genetic manipulation of obligate intracellular bacteria. It allows for the creation of isogenic mutant strains with defined genetic lesions, enabling rigorous functional studies of gene products, validation of virulence factors, and development of attenuated vaccine candidates. The system is reproducible, stable over long-term culture, and does not require proprietary algorithms or specialized equipment beyond standard molecular biology and cell culture facilities.

- describe attenuated forms of bacteria

Attenuated bacterial strains generated by the disclosed methods exhibit reduced virulence while maintaining immunogenicity. These strains are produced by targeted disruption of genes essential for full pathogenicity but not for in vitro growth, such as those encoding antiporter proteins, type IV secretion system components, or metabolic enzymes. The attenuation is genetically stable and does not revert to wild-type virulence under standard culture conditions.

- outline immunogenic compositions

The invention encompasses immunogenic compositions comprising the attenuated bacterial strains described herein. These compositions are capable of eliciting protective immune responses in mammalian hosts against challenge with wild-type pathogenic strains. The immunogenic compositions may be formulated as live-attenuated vaccines, optionally combined with adjuvants, carriers, or other immunomodulatory agents to enhance efficacy.

- describe prophylactic and therapeutic uses

The immunogenic compositions of the invention are useful for both prophylactic and therapeutic applications. Prophylactically, they may be administered to at-risk individuals or animals to prevent infection or disease caused by Rickettsiales or Chlamydiales pathogens. Therapeutically, they may be used to treat active infections by boosting host immunity and accelerating pathogen clearance, particularly in cases of antibiotic resistance or treatment failure.

- define sequence identity

As used herein, “sequence identity” refers to the percentage of nucleotide or amino acid residues in a candidate sequence that are identical to the corresponding residues in a reference sequence, after aligning the sequences and introducing gaps, if necessary, to achieve maximum percent identity. Sequence identity is determined using standard algorithms known in the art.

- explain determination of sequence identity

Sequence identity is determined by comparing two optimally aligned sequences over a defined comparison window, wherein the portion of the candidate sequence that aligns with the reference sequence may include additions or deletions (i.e., gaps) relative to the reference sequence. The percentage of identical positions is then calculated as the number of identical positions divided by the total number of positions in the comparison window, multiplied by 100.

- describe methods to determine sequence identity

Methods for determining sequence identity include manual alignment, dot matrix analysis, and computational algorithms such as BLAST, FASTA, or Needleman-Wunsch global alignment. These methods utilize scoring matrices (e.g., BLOSUM62 for proteins) and gap penalties to optimize alignment accuracy and biological relevance.

- outline preferred methods to determine sequence identity

The preferred method for determining sequence identity is the use of the BLASTN algorithm (for nucleotide sequences) or BLASTP algorithm (for protein sequences) as implemented by the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI), with default parameters unless otherwise specified. For high-precision comparisons, the EMBOSS Needle tool implementing the Needleman-Wunsch algorithm is also preferred.

- define sequence homology

“Sequence homology,” as used herein, refers to shared evolutionary ancestry between two sequences, inferred from significant similarity in primary structure. Homologous sequences may exhibit varying degrees of sequence identity but retain conserved structural or functional domains indicative of common descent.

- explain determination of sequence homology

Sequence homology is determined by statistical evaluation of sequence similarity, typically using E-values or p-values derived from alignment scores. A low E-value (e.g., <10⁻⁵) indicates that the observed similarity is unlikely to have occurred by chance, supporting a conclusion of homology.

- describe conservative substitutions

Conservative substitutions refer to amino acid replacements that preserve the physicochemical properties of the side chain, such as charge, hydrophobicity, size, or hydrogen-bonding capacity. Examples include substitution of leucine for isoleucine, aspartate for glutamate, or serine for threonine.

- outline characteristics of conservative substitutions

Conservative substitutions generally do not disrupt protein folding, stability, or function. They are often tolerated in functional domains and are commonly observed in orthologous proteins across species. In the context of the invention, polypeptides exhibiting conservative substitutions relative to a reference sequence are considered functionally equivalent if they retain the biological activity of the native protein.

- describe immunogenic composition

An immunogenic composition, as used herein, is a preparation containing one or more antigens capable of inducing an adaptive immune response in a recipient host. The composition may include whole attenuated bacteria, bacterial lysates, recombinant proteins, or nucleic acid constructs encoding immunogenic epitopes, formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients.

- outline components of immunogenic composition

The immunogenic composition comprises at least one attenuated strain of a Rickettsiales or Chlamydiales bacterium, wherein the strain contains a stable targeted mutation that disrupts a gene involved in virulence or intracellular survival. The composition may further include adjuvants, stabilizers, preservatives, buffers, and delivery vehicles to enhance immunogenicity, shelf life, and route-specific administration.

- describe adjuvants

Adjuvants are substances that enhance, modulate, or prolong the immune response to an antigen. They act by stimulating innate immune receptors, promoting antigen uptake and presentation, or forming depots for sustained antigen release. Adjuvants are critical for improving the efficacy of subunit or live-attenuated vaccines, particularly in immunocompromised or elderly populations.

- outline types of adjuvants

Types of adjuvants include aluminum salts (e.g., alum), oil-in-water emulsions (e.g., MF59), saponin-based formulations (e.g., QS-21), Toll-like receptor agonists (e.g., CpG oligonucleotides), and bacterial toxin derivatives (e.g., monophosphoryl lipid A). The choice of adjuvant depends on the desired immune profile (Th1 vs. Th2 bias), route of administration, and target population.

- describe emulsions

Emulsions are colloidal dispersions of two immiscible liquids, typically oil and water, stabilized by surfactants or emulsifiers. In vaccine formulations, oil-in-water emulsions serve as delivery systems that enhance antigen persistence and recruit immune cells to the injection site, thereby amplifying the immune response.

- outline components of emulsions

Components of vaccine emulsions include metabolizable oils (e.g., squalene), surfactants (e.g., Tween 80, Span 85), and aqueous phases containing buffer salts and antigens. The droplet size, zeta potential, and viscosity are carefully controlled to ensure stability, sterility, and biocompatibility.

- describe polymers

Polymers used in vaccine formulations include natural and synthetic macromolecules that can encapsulate antigens, provide controlled release, or act as immunostimulants. Examples include chitosan, poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA), and polyethyleneimine (PEI).

- outline characteristics of polymers

Desirable characteristics of vaccine polymers include biodegradability, non-toxicity, mucoadhesiveness, and the ability to protect antigens from degradation. Polymers may also be functionalized with targeting ligands or immune potentiators to enhance delivery to antigen-presenting cells.

- describe carbomer

Carbomer is a high-molecular-weight polymer of acrylic acid cross-linked with allyl sucrose or pentaerythritol. It is widely used in pharmaceutical formulations as a thickening agent, emulsifier, and sustained-release matrix due to its ability to form clear gels at low concentrations.

- outline characteristics of carbomer

Carbomer exhibits pseudoplastic rheology, pH-dependent swelling, and mucoadhesive properties. It is compatible with a wide range of active ingredients and enhances mucosal retention, making it suitable for intranasal or topical vaccine delivery.

- describe RIBI adjuvant system

The RIBI adjuvant system is a formulation containing monophosphoryl lipid A (MPL), trehalose dimycolate (TDM), and cell wall skeleton (CWS) in an oil-in-water emulsion. It stimulates both humoral and cell-mediated immunity through activation of TLR4 and NOD2 pathways.

- outline other adjuvants

Other adjuvants include cytokines (e.g., IL-12, GM-CSF), virosomes, liposomes, ISCOMs (immune-stimulating complexes), and nanoparticle platforms. These can be tailored to elicit specific immune effector mechanisms, such as cytotoxic T lymphocyte responses or mucosal IgA production.

- describe pharmaceutical-acceptable carriers

Pharmaceutical-acceptable carriers are inert excipients that facilitate the formulation, storage, and delivery of active pharmaceutical ingredients. They include solvents, diluents, stabilizers, and bulking agents that meet safety and regulatory standards for human use.

- outline characteristics of pharmaceutical-acceptable carriers

Characteristics include non-toxicity, non-immunogenicity, chemical stability, compatibility with the active ingredient, and compliance with pharmacopeial specifications (e.g., USP, Ph. Eur.). Carriers must not interfere with the biological activity or immunogenicity of the vaccine antigen.

- describe veterinary-acceptable carriers

Veterinary-acceptable carriers are analogous to pharmaceutical carriers but formulated for use in animals. They may include species-specific excipients and are subject to regulatory oversight by agencies such as the USDA or EMA Veterinary Medicines Division.

- outline characteristics of veterinary-acceptable carriers

These carriers must be safe for the target species, environmentally benign, cost-effective, and compatible with large-scale production and field administration (e.g., oral baits, pour-ons, or injectables for livestock).

- describe administration of immunogenic composition

The immunogenic composition may be administered via parenteral routes (intramuscular, subcutaneous, intradermal), mucosal routes (intranasal, oral), or other clinically appropriate methods. The choice of route depends on the pathogen’s natural portal of entry and the desired immune compartment (systemic vs. mucosal).

- outline dosage ranges

Dosage ranges vary depending on the bacterial strain, host species, and formulation. Typical doses range from 10³ to 10⁷ colony-forming units (CFU) per administration for live-attenuated vaccines, with booster doses administered 2–4 weeks apart to achieve durable immunity.

- describe combination vaccines

Combination vaccines contain antigens from multiple pathogens or multiple strains of the same pathogen in a single formulation. They reduce the number of injections required and improve compliance, particularly in veterinary settings where co-infections are common (e.g., *E. canis* and *A. platys* in dogs).

- outline further pathogens

Further pathogens that may be included in combination vaccines encompass other vector-borne agents such as *Babesia*, *Borrelia*, *Leishmania*, and *Dirofilaria*, as well as viral pathogens like canine distemper virus or feline leukemia virus, depending on the target species and epidemiological context.

- describe immunomodulatory agents

Immunomodulatory agents are compounds that enhance, suppress, or redirect immune responses. In vaccine contexts, they are used to skew responses toward protective phenotypes (e.g., Th1 for intracellular pathogens) or overcome immunosenescence.

- outline characteristics of immunomodulatory agents

Effective immunomodulators are potent at low doses, have defined mechanisms of action, and exhibit favorable safety profiles. Examples include TLR agonists, STING activators, and checkpoint inhibitors repurposed as vaccine adjuvants.

- describe antibiotics

Antibiotics may be included in vaccine formulations as preservatives to prevent microbial contamination during multi-dose use. Common examples include gentamicin, neomycin, and polymyxin B at concentrations below therapeutic levels.

- outline concentrations of adjuvants and additives

Adjuvant concentrations are optimized empirically but typically range from 0.01% to 5% (w/v) for emulsions, 1–100 µg/dose for TLR agonists, and 0.1–1 mg/dose for aluminum salts. Additives such as EDTA or polysorbate 80 are used at 0.001–0.1% to stabilize the formulation.

- introduce immunogenic composition administration

Administration of the immunogenic composition is performed under aseptic conditions using sterile devices. Pre-vaccination screening for contraindications (e.g., pregnancy, immunosuppression) is recommended in accordance with regulatory guidelines.

- describe immunogenic composition with immune stimulant

The immunogenic composition may be co-formulated with an immune stimulant such as a cytokine or danger signal to amplify dendritic cell maturation and T-cell priming. This is particularly beneficial for pathogens that naturally suppress host immunity.

- provide methods for generating stable targeted mutations

The invention provides methods for generating stable targeted mutations in Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales by electroporating linear DNA fragments containing homology arms flanking a selectable marker into host cell-free bacteria, followed by antibiotic selection in permissive host cell lines such as ISE6 tick cells or DH82 macrophages.

- describe Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales mutated using disclosed methods

Mutated strains include *E. chaffeensis* with disruptions in *Ech_0379* or *Ech_0660*, *E. canis* with inactivation of *Ecaj_0381*, and *A. phagocytophilum* with mutations in *APH_0634*. These mutations confer stable attenuation without loss of in vitro viability.

- describe immunogenic compositions against Rickettsiale and Chlamydiale pathogens

Immunogenic compositions are effective against a broad range of pathogens within these orders, including emerging species identified through genomic surveillance. Cross-protection may occur due to conserved antigens among related strains.

- describe administration of immunogenic compositions

Administration is typically performed in two doses, with the first priming the immune system and the second boosting memory responses. Protection is evaluated by challenge studies or seroconversion assays.

- describe stable targeted mutation disrupting gene function

The stable targeted mutation involves insertion of a *tuf-aadA* cassette into the coding sequence, abolishing transcription and translation of the target gene. The mutation is maintained for >6 months in continuous culture without antibiotic pressure.

- describe restoring function of disrupted gene

Gene function is restored by a second allelic exchange event that replaces the *aadA* cassette with the intact open reading frame plus a reporter gene (e.g., *mCherry*) and a second selectable marker (e.g., gentamicin resistance), allowing real-time tracking and selection.

- provide example of stable targeted mutations in E. chaffeensis

In *E. chaffeensis*, stable mutations were created in *Ech_0379* (encoding an antiporter) and *Ech_0230*, with the *Ech_0379* mutant showing loss of Na⁺/H⁺ antiporter activity in complementation assays using *E. coli* EP432.

- describe method of targeted gene disruption or mutation

The method comprises: (1) designing homology arms (~1 kb each) flanking the target site; (2) cloning them into a plasmid with a *tuf*-driven *aadA* cassette; (3) generating linear DNA by PCR; (4) electroporating into purified dense-core bacteria; (5) selecting transformants in ISE6 cells with spectinomycin/streptomycin.

- describe method for eliciting immune response

The attenuated strain is administered to a host, where it undergoes limited replication, presenting antigens via MHC class I and II pathways to activate CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells, as well as B cells for antibody production.

- describe method for reducing incidence and/or severity of clinical signs

Vaccinated hosts exhibit reduced fever, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, and hepatic enzyme elevation upon challenge, indicating mitigation of systemic inflammation and organ damage.

- describe immunogenic composition including modified live species

The composition includes live bacteria that are replication-competent but avirulent due to defined genetic lesions, offering superior immunogenicity compared to killed or subunit vaccines.

- describe E. chaffeensis, E. canis, A. phagocyophilum, or A. marginale with targeted mutagenesis

Each species is modified by disrupting orthologs of virulence-associated genes, such as those involved in ion transport (*Ech_0379*), secretion systems, or stress response.

- describe mutation inactivating bacteria and interfering with replication

While most mutations described do not abolish replication in vitro, essential gene disruptions may be engineered conditionally (e.g., using inducible promoters) to create replication-deficient strains for safer vaccine platforms.

- describe insertion or deletion in Ech_0379 or Ech_0660 gene

Insertion of the *tuf-aadA* cassette at nucleotide position 374,200 of GenBank CP000236.1 disrupts *Ech_0379*. Similarly, *Ech_0660* is disrupted by insertion within its coding sequence.

- describe sequence identity to Ech_0660 (SEQ ID No. 35)

Polypeptides sharing at least 85% sequence identity with SEQ ID No. 35 over its full length are considered functional homologs and may be targeted for mutagenesis in related species.

- describe E. canis with targeted mutagenesis

*E. canis* strain Jake was modified by disrupting *Ecaj_0381*, an ortholog of *Ech_0379*, using homologous recombination with 1-kb flanking arms and *tuf-aadA* selection.

- describe mutation inactivating Ecaj_0381 gene

The mutation introduces a premature stop codon and removes critical transmembrane domains, abolishing antiporter function as confirmed by *E. coli* complementation.

- describe sequence identity to SEQ ID No. 54

Nucleotide sequences exhibiting ≥90% identity to SEQ ID No. 54 over a 500-bp window are deemed orthologous and amenable to the same mutagenesis strategy.

- describe A. phagocytophilum with targeted mutagenesis

*A. phagocytophilum* HZ strain was mutated in *APH_0634*, a gene encoding a putative outer membrane protein, using analogous methods.

- describe mutation inactivating APH_0634 gene

The insertion disrupts the signal peptide and beta-barrel domain, preventing proper localization and function.

- describe sequence identity to SEQ ID No. 55

Sequences with ≥88% identity to SEQ ID No. 55 are considered equivalent targets for vaccine development.

- describe immunogenic composition or vaccine including any member of Rickettsiales or Chlamydiales

The platform is broadly applicable across these orders due to conserved genetic machinery for homologous recombination and shared promoter elements like *tuf*.

- describe homolog of E. chaffeensis ECH_0660 gene with targeted mutagenesis

Homologs in *Rickettsia prowazekii* (GenBank CP000107.1), *Orientia tsutsugamushi* (CR767821.1), *Anaplasma marginale* (CP000235.1), *Ehrlichia ruminantium* (CP000030.1), and *Wolbachia* (CP006917.1) share significant synteny and can be mutated using adapted protocols.

- describe sequence homology to GenBank # CP000107.1

Proteins encoded by CP000107.1 exhibit 45% amino acid identity over 300 residues with ECH_0660, indicating functional conservation.

- describe sequence homology to GenBank # CR767821.1

CR767821.1 encodes a homolog with 42% identity, suggesting a common ancestral gene involved in intracellular adaptation.

- describe sequence homology to GenBank # CP000235.1

CP000235.1 shows 51% identity, supporting cross-species vaccine strategies targeting this conserved pathway.

- describe sequence homology to GenBank # CP000030.1

CP000030.1 shares 48% identity, reinforcing the essential nature of this gene family in Anaplasmataceae.

- describe sequence homology to GenBank # CP006917.1

CP006917.1, from an endosymbiont, retains 39% identity, highlighting deep evolutionary conservation.

- describe administration routes for immunogenic composition

Preferred routes include subcutaneous injection for dogs and intramuscular for humans, though intranasal delivery is feasible for mucosal immunity.

- describe age range for administering immunogenic composition

The composition is suitable for administration to individuals aged 6 weeks and older, with pediatric and geriatric dosing adjusted based on body weight and immune status.
