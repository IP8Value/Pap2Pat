# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of non-invasive prenatal screening (NIPS), specifically methods and systems for detecting fetal chromosomal aneuploidies using cell-free DNA (cfDNA) isolated from maternal plasma. More particularly, the invention provides improved analytical techniques for distinguishing true fetal aneuploidies from false-positive results caused by maternal genomic variations, including microduplications, microdeletions, and global copy number abnormalities. The invention further enhances the positive predictive value (PPV) of NIPS by integrating bin-level genomic analysis, ideogram-based visualization, and refined statistical modeling to ensure accurate clinical interpretation of sequencing data derived from next-generation sequencing platforms.

## BACKGROUND

Current non-invasive prenatal screening (NIPS) methods rely on the analysis of cell-free fetal DNA (cffDNA) circulating in maternal blood to assess the risk of common fetal chromosomal aneuploidies such as trisomy 21 (Down syndrome), trisomy 18 (Edwards syndrome), and trisomy 13 (Patau syndrome). While these methods offer higher sensitivity and specificity compared to traditional serum screening or nuchal translucency ultrasound, they remain susceptible to false-positive results. False positives may arise from biological confounders such as confined placental mosaicism, vanishing twin syndrome, maternal malignancies, or undiagnosed maternal chromosomal abnormalities—including segmental duplications or deletions that mimic fetal aneuploidy signals. Additionally, technical artifacts stemming from sequencing biases, particularly those related to GC content variation across chromosomes, can distort read count distributions and lead to erroneous interpretations. Existing NIPS assays often report chromosome-wide Z-scores without examining the spatial distribution of deviations along the chromosome, thereby failing to differentiate between whole-chromosome aneuploidies and localized maternal copy number variants. This limitation reduces the PPV of NIPS, especially for rarer conditions like trisomy 13 and sex chromosome aneuploidies, where prevalence is low and even minor false-positive rates significantly impact clinical utility. Consequently, there remains a critical need for a refined NIPS methodology capable of identifying and excluding false-positive diagnoses attributable to maternal genomic architecture, thereby improving diagnostic confidence and reducing unnecessary invasive procedures.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method for detecting false-positive diagnoses of chromosomal aneuploidy in non-invasive prenatal screening by analyzing bin-specific test parameters across the genome. In accordance with the invention, each chromosome is divided into discrete genomic bins, and a bin-specific test parameter—such as a normalized read count or Z-score—is obtained for each bin based on next-generation sequencing data from maternal plasma. An ideogram of the chromosome is then generated, plotting the bin-specific test parameters along the chromosomal coordinates to visualize the spatial pattern of deviation. A false-positive diagnosis is detected when the elevated chromosome-wide Z-score is inconsistent with a uniform duplication or deletion across the entire chromosome; instead, the ideogram reveals that only a substantial portion—but not the entirety—of the chromosome exhibits abnormal representation, suggesting a maternal microduplication or microdeletion rather than a true fetal aneuploidy. To confirm whether a suspected aneuploidy is genuine, the method includes repeating the analytical steps for the implicated chromosome, calculating the proportion of the chromosome affected, and comparing bin-specific parameters to reference distributions derived from euploid pregnancies. The chromosome representation value is computed by aggregating bin-level data, corrected for technical biases such as GC content, and compared against established reference medians and median absolute deviations. If the deviation is localized and does not encompass a substantial portion of the chromosome—defined herein as less than approximately 70% to 90% of the chromosomal length—the result is flagged as a potential false positive due to maternal copy number variation. By implementing this granular, ideogram-guided review process prior to reporting positive results, the invention significantly improves the positive predictive value of NIPS, ensuring that only findings consistent with whole-chromosome fetal aneuploidies are communicated as high-risk, while segmental anomalies are either reported separately or excluded from aneuploidy calls.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

A karyotype refers to the complete set of chromosomes in a cell, typically visualized during metaphase and arranged in pairs according to size, banding pattern, and centromere position. Normal human karyotypes consist of 22 pairs of autosomes and one pair of sex chromosomes, designated as 46,XX for females and 46,XY for males. Ploidy describes the number of complete sets of chromosomes in a cell; humans are diploid organisms, meaning they possess two sets—one inherited from each parent—whereas haploid cells, such as gametes, contain only one set. Aneuploidy is a chromosomal abnormality characterized by an abnormal number of chromosomes, deviating from the typical diploid complement. This condition arises primarily from errors in meiosis, such as nondisjunction, leading to gametes with extra or missing chromosomes. Trisomy denotes the presence of three copies of a particular chromosome instead of the usual two, while monosomy indicates only one copy. Mosaicism occurs when an individual harbors two or more cell populations with different chromosomal complements, often resulting from mitotic errors post-fertilization. Fetal aneuploidy refers specifically to such abnormalities in the developing fetus, which can cause severe developmental disorders or pregnancy loss.

Non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT), also termed non-invasive prenatal screening (NIPS), analyzes cell-free DNA (cfDNA) fragments in maternal plasma to assess fetal chromosomal status without posing risks associated with invasive procedures like amniocentesis or chorionic villus sampling (CVS), which carry a 0.5% to 1.0% risk of procedure-related miscarriage. Chromosomal duplication involves the presence of an extra copy of a chromosomal segment, whereas deletion signifies the absence of a segment; both can originate from unequal crossing over during meiosis or replication errors. A gene is a hereditary unit comprising DNA that encodes a functional RNA or polypeptide product. Chromosome variation encompasses structural or numerical differences from the reference genome, including copy number variation (CNV)—a type of structural variation involving gains or losses of DNA segments larger than 1 kilobase. Microduplications and microdeletions are submicroscopic CNVs detectable only by molecular methods.

Cell-free DNA (cfDNA) consists of short, fragmented DNA molecules circulating in plasma, derived from apoptotic cells. During pregnancy, a fraction of cfDNA originates from the placenta and is termed cell-free fetal DNA (cffDNA). The fetal fraction (ff) is the proportion of cffDNA relative to total cfDNA, typically ranging from 4% to 20% after 10 weeks’ gestation. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) enables massively parallel sequencing of millions of DNA fragments. A sequencing library is prepared by ligating adapter sequences—short synthetic oligonucleotides containing primer binding sites and barcodes—to cfDNA fragments. These adapters facilitate amplification and sequencing. Sequence reads are the output strings of nucleotide bases generated per fragment. Reads are aligned to a reference genome, a standardized human genomic sequence (e.g., hg19), and assigned to predefined genomic bins—non-overlapping segments of fixed or variable size (e.g., 50 kb). The Z-score quantifies how many standard deviations a sample’s chromosomal representation deviates from the reference median, calculated as (x − μ)/σ, where x is the sample’s chromosome representation, μ is the plate median, and σ is the median absolute deviation (MAD) from a training set. An ideogram is a graphical representation of a chromosome showing banding patterns and, in this context, overlaid with bin-specific Z-scores or normalized read counts to visualize regional deviations.

Positive predictive value (PPV) is the probability that a positive test result truly indicates the presence of the condition. Given the low prevalence of trisomies 13, 18, and 21—even in high-risk populations—even highly specific NIPS assays yield suboptimal PPVs without refinement. The present invention introduces a comprehensive NIPS workflow beginning with maternal blood collection into stabilizing tubes, followed by double centrifugation to isolate plasma. cfDNA is extracted and converted into barcoded libraries using enzymatic fragmentation and adapter ligation. Libraries are pooled, denatured, and sequenced on platforms such as Illumina HiSeq using single-read 36-cycle chemistry with index sequencing, yielding at least 9 million reads per sample. Fetal fraction is estimated differently based on fetal sex: for male fetuses, Y-chromosome overrepresentation is quantified using well-performing bins with high male-to-female read count ratios; for female fetuses, a proprietary regularized regression model leverages X-chromosome underrepresentation relative to autosomes. Samples with fetal fraction below 4%–5% are excluded due to insufficient fetal signal.

Sequencing reads are aligned to bins across the reference genome. Bin read counts are scaled by total autosomal reads to normalize for sequencing depth. GC bias—systematic under- or over-representation of reads from regions with extreme GC content—is corrected using local polynomial regression (e.g., loess) applied to GC-discretized bins. High-order technical artifacts are removed via principal component analysis (PCA). Chromosome representation is computed as the sum of bin counts for a chromosome divided by the sum across all autosomes. Chromosome-specific Z-scores are derived and interpreted: Z > 8 indicates high risk for trisomy, Z < 4 indicates low risk, and intermediate values (4–8) trigger manual review. Critically, before reporting any positive result, the ideogram of the implicated chromosome is examined. If elevated Z-scores are confined to a discrete region—suggesting maternal microduplication—the case is flagged as a potential false positive. Similarly, global copy number abnormalities, indicated by concurrent deviations on multiple chromosomes, prompt exclusion from aneuploidy reporting. This approach distinguishes fetal aneuploidies from maternal CNVs, confirms mosaicism through serial Z-score trends, and detects partial duplications/deletions. The method supports various NGS technologies—including shotgun sequencing, sequencing-by-synthesis (Illumina), sequencing-by-ligation (SOLiD), single-molecule real-time (SMRT) sequencing, and Ion Torrent—provided sufficient read depth and quality (Q > 30). By integrating bin-level resolution, ideogram visualization, and stringent bioinformatic filtering, the invention substantially improves PPV while maintaining high sensitivity.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Assay Development

Patient samples were collected from pregnant women under informed consent approved by the Western Institutional Review Board, with additional de-identified samples sourced from commercial providers. Whole blood was drawn into Cell-Free DNA BCT tubes and processed within four days. Plasma isolation involved two centrifugation steps on a Tecan EVO 200 liquid handler: first at 2,500 × g for 10 minutes, then at 3,200 × g for 20 minutes. cfDNA was extracted from 4 mL plasma using DynaMax chemistry on a Kingfisher Flex system. Library preparation employed the NEBNext Ultra DNA Library Prep Kit, with PCR amplification using a universal forward primer and a reverse primer containing a 10-bp sample-specific barcode. PCR conditions included initial denaturation at 98°C for 30 seconds, followed by 10 cycles of 98°C for 10 seconds, 65°C for 30 seconds, and 72°C for 30 seconds, ending with a 5-minute extension at 72°C. Post-PCR purification used AMPure XP beads at a 1:1 ratio, and libraries were quantified via PicoGreen assay. Libraries were normalized to 2 nM, pooled in groups of 12, denatured, diluted to 15 pM, and spiked with 5% PhiX control before clustering on HiSeq flow cells. Sequencing on HiSeq2500 generated single-end 36-bp reads plus 10-bp index reads, with a minimum of 9 million reads per sample. Fetal fraction estimation utilized X-chromosome underrepresentation for female fetuses (FF = 2 × (1 − Xi/Xf), where Xi is the sample’s median X-bin representation and Xf is the run’s female median) and Y-chromosome overrepresentation for males, calibrated against a 3,589-sample training set. GC correction involved calculating GC content per bin, discretizing into percentiles, and applying loess smoothing to adjust read counts, thereby mitigating sequencing bias, particularly for GC-rich chromosomes 13 and 18.

### Example 2: Assay Verification and Validation

Assay verification used 2,085 samples, including 69 trisomy 21, 20 trisomy 18, and 17 trisomy 13 cases, achieving complete separation: all affected samples had Z > 8, all unaffected had Z < 4. Validation with 552 samples, including known aneuploidies, confirmed this performance. GC correction proved essential for chromosomes 13 and 18, elevating trisomy 13 Z-scores from sub-threshold to >8. Twin gestation analysis (n=115) showed even greater discrimination, with all trisomic twins scoring Z > 11. Concordance testing with Sequenom on 100 split samples yielded 100% agreement. Fetal sex determination accuracy was 99.7% (371/372) across 372 samples, with the single discordant case having fetal fraction below the reporting threshold. These results established robust analytical validity across diverse scenarios.

### Example 3: Clinical Implementations

In clinical practice, samples are accepted from gestational week 10 onward. Using Z ≤ 4 as negative and Z > 8 as positive, with intermediate scores triggering review, the first 10,000 samples yielded 180 abnormal results (1.8%). Unreportable results (0.88%) were primarily due to low fetal fraction (0.59%). Maternal microduplications were identified in cases with intermediate Z-scores (e.g., 5.11 for chr21, 6.93 for chr18); ideograms revealed focal duplications, later confirmed by maternal microarray. Implementing ideogram review eliminated these false positives, raising theoretical PPV for trisomy 21 from 89% to 100%. Similarly, global copy number abnormalities—detected via multi-chromosomal Z-score deviations in six cases, often linked to uterine fibroids—were excluded from aneuploidy reporting. Mosaic Down syndrome was detected in four cases with Z-scores ranging from 3.57 to 24.43, corresponding to trisomic cell fractions as low as 25%. Sex chromosome aneuploidy detection correctly identified most cases, except one false-positive Turner syndrome due to maternal mosaicism, which would now be flagged by abnormally high fetal fraction (>50%). Twin pregnancies showed elevated Z-scores consistent with single-affected-twin scenarios. Overall, PPV reached 98% for trisomy 21 (100% with current rules), 92% for trisomy 18 (96% excluding a teratoma case), and 69% for trisomy 13—substantially higher than historical NIPS PPVs of 91%, 73%, and 39%, respectively. For sex chromosome aneuploidies, PPV was 86% (100% excluding maternal mosaicism). These results demonstrate that the invention’s integrated approach markedly enhances clinical reliability.
