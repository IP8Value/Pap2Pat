# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel vaccine consortium comprising a bivalent formulation of isolated outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) derived from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi and *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A. This vaccine consortium is designed for oral administration and is intended to elicit robust mucosal and systemic immune responses against both typhoidal and paratyphoidal salmonellae, thereby providing broad-spectrum protection against enteric fever. The invention further encompasses methods for the preparation, characterization, and immunological evaluation of this OMV-based bivalent vaccine, as well as its use in prophylactic immunization against invasive febrile illnesses caused by *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A.

## BACKGROUND AND PRIOR ART OF THE INVENTION

Enteric fever remains a significant global public health burden, particularly in developing regions where sanitation and clean water access are limited. Caused primarily by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A, this invasive disease affects millions annually, with recent epidemiological trends indicating a rising incidence of paratyphoid fever relative to typhoid fever in endemic areas such as South and Southeast Asia. Current licensed vaccines offer protection only against *S*. Typhi and include a live-attenuated oral strain (Ty21a), a parenteral Vi-polysaccharide vaccine, and a newer Vi-tetanus toxoid conjugate vaccine. However, none of these formulations confer reliable cross-protection against *S*. Paratyphi A due to the absence of the Vi capsule in paratyphoidal strains, highlighting a critical gap in available preventive measures.

Outer membrane vesicles (OMVs), naturally secreted by Gram-negative bacteria during growth, have emerged as promising next-generation vaccine platforms. These nano-sized structures encapsulate a diverse array of pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs)—including lipopolysaccharide (LPS), outer membrane proteins, periplasmic components, and nucleic acids—that collectively stimulate potent innate and adaptive immune responses without requiring external adjuvants. The success of the licensed OMV-based vaccine against *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup B (MeNZB), which demonstrated approximately 80% efficacy in epidemic settings, underscores the translational potential of this approach. Despite these advances, no OMV-based vaccine has been developed or approved for enteric fever, and existing strategies fail to address the dual threat posed by both *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A.

Moreover, conventional vaccine development for enteric pathogens often relies on heat-killed whole-cell preparations or purified polysaccharides, which suffer from suboptimal immunogenicity, short-lived immunity, and an inability to engage cell-mediated immune mechanisms essential for combating intracellular pathogens like *Salmonella*. There exists a pressing need for a cost-effective, thermostable, and broadly protective vaccine that can be administered orally to induce mucosal immunity at the primary site of infection—the intestinal epithelium—while simultaneously generating durable systemic protection. The present invention addresses this unmet medical need through a rationally designed bivalent OMV consortium that leverages the natural immunostimulatory properties of bacterial vesicles to confer comprehensive defense against both major causative agents of enteric fever.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide a novel, bivalent vaccine formulation comprising isolated outer membrane vesicles derived from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi and *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A, formulated in a 1:1 ratio, for the prevention of enteric fever caused by either or both pathogens. This formulation is specifically engineered to elicit strong humoral and cell-mediated immune responses following oral administration, thereby blocking initial bacterial invasion at the intestinal mucosa and controlling systemic dissemination post-infection.

Additional objectives include: (i) to develop a scalable and reproducible method for the isolation and purification of OMVs from clinically relevant strains of *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A under defined culture conditions; (ii) to demonstrate that the bivalent OMV formulation exhibits superior immunogenicity compared to traditional heat-killed whole-cell vaccines; (iii) to confirm the presence of key protective antigens—including LPS, Vi-polysaccharide (in *S*. Typhi-derived OMVs), and immunodominant outer membrane proteins—in the OMV cargo; (iv) to establish protective efficacy against lethal and sub-lethal challenges with heterologous virulent strains in a validated murine model of systemic salmonellosis; (v) to elucidate the mechanisms of protection, including antibody-mediated agglutination, inhibition of bacterial motility and mucin penetration, opsonophagocytosis, and Th1/Th17-polarized cellular immunity; and (vi) to provide a foundation for future clinical translation by demonstrating safety, stability, and functional immunogenicity in preclinical studies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Introduction of Novel Formulation of Isolated Outer Membrane Vesicles

The present invention discloses a bivalent vaccine composition comprising a substantially pure preparation of outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) isolated from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhi strain C-6953 and *Salmonella enterica* serovar Paratyphi A strain C-6915, combined in an equimolar or equal protein-mass ratio (1:1). These OMVs are naturally secreted during the exponential phase of bacterial growth in standard laboratory media and are harvested, purified, and formulated for oral delivery without chemical detoxification or genetic modification. The resulting formulation retains native conformational epitopes and endogenous adjuvant properties conferred by LPS and membrane-associated proteins, enabling robust activation of both mucosal and systemic immunity upon administration.

### Composition of Formulation

Each dose of the bivalent OMV vaccine contains approximately 25 μg of total protein, equally divided between *S*. Typhi-derived and *S*. Paratyphi A-derived OMVs, suspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4). Biochemical analyses confirm that each OMV preparation contains 400–440 μg/mL of protein and approximately 250 μg/mL of O-antigen polysaccharide. Critically, OMVs derived from *S*. Typhi additionally incorporate the Vi capsular polysaccharide—a key virulence factor and protective antigen—into their membrane architecture, thereby presenting this T-independent antigen in a particulate, membrane-anchored context that facilitates T-cell-dependent immune responses via associated protein carriers. Proteomic profiling reveals the presence of numerous immunogenic outer membrane proteins (e.g., OmpC, OmpF, OmpS1, OmpS2), cytosolic chaperones, and virulence-associated factors, all of which contribute to the vaccine’s multifaceted immunogenicity.

### Immunogenicity and Protective Efficacy

Oral immunization of BALB/c mice with three doses of the bivalent OMV formulation induces high titers of antigen-specific serum IgG (including subclasses IgG1, IgG2a, and IgG3), IgA, and secretory IgA in stool, with peak responses observed between days 28 and 35 post-primary immunization and sustained detectable levels up to day 90. Notably, the IgG3:IgG1 ratio exceeds unity, indicative of a Th1-biased response essential for intracellular pathogen clearance. Concurrently, significant upregulation of CD4+, CD8+, and CD19+ lymphocyte populations in the spleen confirms activation of adaptive immunity. Ex vivo re-stimulation of splenocytes and bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) with bivalent OMVs triggers robust secretion of IFN-γ, IL-17, IL-6, IL-12p70, and IL-23—cytokines central to Th1 and Th17 differentiation and effector function. In challenge studies, immunized mice exhibit 80–90% survival following intraperitoneal inoculation with >LD₅₀ doses of heterologous *S*. Typhi or *S*. Paratyphi A, compared to 0% survival in non-immunized controls, with markedly reduced bacterial loads in spleen and liver (10¹–10² CFU/g vs. 10⁵–10⁸ CFU/g).

### Comparison with Mutated Strains

Unlike live-attenuated vaccines such as Ty21a—which rely on defined mutations (e.g., *galE*) that may compromise immunogenicity or revert to virulence—the present OMV formulation is acellular and non-replicating, eliminating risks of reversion or persistent infection. Moreover, OMVs capture a broader antigenic repertoire than single-gene deletion mutants, including conformationally intact surface structures absent in genetically modified strains grown under non-native conditions.

### Advantages Over Conventional Methods

The bivalent OMV vaccine surpasses conventional heat-killed whole-cell vaccines in immunogenicity, inducing significantly higher antibody titers and more balanced Th1/Th17 responses. It also avoids the limitations of Vi-polysaccharide vaccines, which fail to protect against *S*. Paratyphi A and elicit only transient, T-independent immunity. By delivering Vi-polysaccharide in association with immunostimulatory OMV proteins, the invention converts this T-independent antigen into a T-dependent one, enabling class switching, affinity maturation, and long-term memory.

### Preparation of OMVs

OMVs are prepared by culturing *S*. Typhi C-6953 and *S*. Paratyphi A C-6915 in Tryptic Soy Broth at 37°C with shaking until mid-exponential phase. Culture supernatants are clarified by centrifugation (8,000 rpm, 40 min, 4°C), filtered through 0.22 μm membranes, and subjected to ultracentrifugation (140,000 × g, 4 h, 4°C). The resulting OMV pellets are resuspended in PBS, quantified by Lowry assay, and stored at −80°C. Endotoxin content and sterility are verified prior to use.

### Significance of Vi-Polysaccharide in Vaccine Constituents

The inclusion of Vi-polysaccharide within *S*. Typhi-derived OMVs is a critical feature of the invention. Unlike free Vi-polysaccharide, which poorly engages T-cell help, its presentation on OMVs enables co-delivery with protein antigens that activate CD4⁺ T cells, thereby promoting high-affinity, long-lasting anti-Vi antibodies and cross-reactive cellular immunity—key attributes absent in current Vi-based vaccines.

### Bacterial Strains and Culture Conditions

*S*. Typhi strain C-6953 and *S*. Paratyphi A strain C-6915, along with challenge strains *S*. Typhi C-6.946 and *S*. Paratyphi A BCR 148, are maintained in 20% glycerol/BHI broth at −80°C. Prior to OMV production or challenge, strains are revived on Tryptic Soy Agar and confirmed by PCR (targeting *prtA*, *fliC-d/j*, *parat*) and serotyping per White-Kauffmann-Le Minor scheme. Cultures are grown in Tryptic Soy Broth at 37°C under aerobic shaking (100 rpm) to OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.8.

### Preparation of OMVs

As detailed above, OMVs are isolated from exponential-phase cultures via sequential centrifugation, filtration, and ultracentrifugation. Protein concentration is standardized to 400–440 μg/mL using Lowry assay, and O-antigen content is quantified by Dubois method.

### Characterization of Isolated OMVs

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) of negatively stained samples reveals spherical vesicles 130–300 nm in diameter, with *S*. Typhi OMVs slightly larger than *S*. Paratyphi A counterparts. SDS-PAGE shows distinct protein profiles (40–62 kDa for *S*. Typhi; 33–73 kDa for *S*. Paratyphi A), differing from purified outer membrane proteins, confirming enrichment of non-OMP cargo. MALDI-TOF/TOF identifies over 100 hypothetical proteins in *S*. Typhi OMVs and 42 in *S*. Paratyphi A OMVs, including cytosolic, inner membrane, and virulence factors.

### Negative Staining of OMVs and OMV-Secreting Bacteria

OMV suspensions (5 μL) are adsorbed onto carbon-coated copper grids, washed with Tris-HCl buffer, and stained with 2% uranyl acetate. Similarly, log-phase bacteria are processed identically. Samples are imaged using a Tecnai 12 transmission electron microscope at 80 kV, confirming vesicle budding from bacterial surfaces and homogeneous vesicle morphology.

### Animals

Seven-week-old female BALB/c mice are housed under specific pathogen-free conditions at 25°C and 75% humidity, with sterile food and water ad libitum. All procedures comply with CPCSEA guidelines and are approved by the Institutional Animal Ethical Committee (Project No. PRO/117/June 2015–June 2018).

### Collection of Serum and Stool

Blood is collected from the tail vein on days 0, 14, 21, 28, 35, 78, and 90 post-immunization, allowed to clot, and centrifuged (1,000 rpm, 10 min, 4°C) to obtain serum. Fresh stools are homogenized in PBS containing protease inhibitors, centrifuged (10,000 × g, 10 min), and supernatants stored at −20°C for IgA analysis.

### SDS-PAGE and Immunoblot

OMVs, outer membrane proteins, LPS, and whole-cell lysates are resolved on 12% SDS-PAGE gels under reducing conditions and stained with Coomassie or silver. For immunoblotting, proteins are transferred to nitrocellulose membranes and probed with sera from immunized mice or convalescent animals, followed by alkaline phosphatase- or HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies and colorimetric or chemiluminescent detection.

### Dot Blot Assay

Purified LPS from both serovars is spotted directly onto nitrocellulose membranes at varying concentrations, blocked, and incubated with anti-OMV sera and HRP-conjugated anti-mouse IgG. Signal development via chemiluminescence confirms recognition of LPS by vaccine-induced antibodies.

### ELISA

Microtiter wells are coated with 5 μg/well of OMVs or Vi-polysaccharide, blocked with BSA, and incubated with serially diluted serum or stool supernatants. HRP-conjugated anti-mouse IgG, IgG1, IgG2a, IgG3, IgA, or IgM is added, followed by OPD substrate. Reactions are stopped with sulfuric acid, and absorbance at 492 nm is measured to quantify antigen-specific immunoglobulins.

### Ex Vivo Studies on Isolated Dendritic Cells

Bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) from naïve mice are matured in GM-CSF-supplemented RPMI and stimulated with 10 μg/mL bivalent OMVs for 24 h. Cytokines (IL-6, IL-12p70, IL-23) in supernatants are quantified by ELISA. In co-culture experiments, BMDCs are mixed with CD4⁺ T cells from immunized mice at 1:4 ratio, stimulated with OMVs, and assayed for IFN-γ, IL-17, and IL-6. Splenocytes from immunized mice are similarly re-stimulated ex vivo to assess cytokine recall responses. Motility assays involve mixing heat-inactivated sera (1:400) with soft agar; log-phase bacteria are stabbed centrally, and motility halos are measured after 24 h incubation. Immunized sera significantly reduce motility versus controls. In protective efficacy studies, mice immunized orally with bivalent OMVs show 80–90% survival after lethal intraperitoneal challenge, minimal weight loss, and 3–5 log reductions in spleen/liver colonization. Challenge with heterologous strains confirms cross-serovar protection. Statistical analysis employs non-parametric tests (GraphPad Prism 5); p < 0.05 denotes significance.

## The Non-Limiting Advantages are Given Below:

The bivalent OMV consortium offers multiple advantages: it is acellular, eliminating biosafety concerns; orally deliverable, enabling needle-free vaccination; thermostable and cost-effective to produce; and capable of inducing dual mucosal-systemic immunity. It demonstrates superior immunogenicity over heat-killed vaccines, generates Vi-polysaccharide-specific IgG via T-dependent mechanisms, and provides robust protection against both *S*. Typhi and *S*. Paratyphi A in vivo. Functional assays confirm that vaccine-induced antibodies mediate bacterial agglutination, inhibit motility and mucin penetration, and enhance macrophage opsonophagocytosis—collectively establishing a multi-layered defense against enteric fever.
