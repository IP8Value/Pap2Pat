# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The development of ribozyme-based technologies represents a significant advancement in the field of molecular biology and gene regulation. Historically, gene expression control systems have predominantly relied on transcriptional regulation mechanisms, such as inducible promoters or repressor-operator systems. While effective, these approaches often suffer from limitations including slow response times, leaky basal expression, and complex regulatory components that can interfere with normal cellular processes. The discovery of RNA interference further expanded the toolkit for post-transcriptional gene silencing but introduced additional complexities related to off-target effects and delivery challenges. In this context, the exploration of ribozymes—catalytic RNA molecules capable of site-specific self-cleavage—offers a novel paradigm for precise and rapid control of gene expression at the RNA level. Ribozymes function through intrinsic catalytic activity without requiring protein cofactors, making them ideal candidates for minimalistic gene regulation systems. However, natural ribozymes often exhibit suboptimal activity in mammalian cellular environments due to structural instability, inefficient folding, or susceptibility to nucleases. Therefore, there exists a critical need to engineer modified ribozyme variants that maintain robust self-cleavage activity within mammalian cells while remaining responsive to exogenous modulators. This necessity has motivated extensive research into optimizing ribozyme sequences, particularly those derived from parasitic organisms like *Schistosoma mansoni*, which possess unique structural features amenable to engineering. The successful development of such ribozyme platforms would enable not only fundamental studies of gene function but also therapeutic applications where tight temporal and spatial control over transgene expression is essential.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a ribozyme-based gene regulation system that enables precise control over nucleic acid product expression through modulation of RNA self-cleavage activity. Central to this system is the use of self-cleaving ribozymes, which are RNA motifs capable of catalyzing their own cleavage at specific sites within an mRNA transcript. A self-cleaving ribozyme, as defined herein, refers to any RNA sequence that folds into a catalytically active conformation permitting intramolecular phosphodiester bond hydrolysis, thereby resulting in fragmentation of the host mRNA and subsequent degradation by cellular nucleases. The invention specifically focuses on modified schistosome RNA mutant motifs derived from the hammerhead ribozyme family originally identified in *Schistosoma mansoni*. These schistosome RNA mutant motifs have been engineered to enhance stability and catalytic efficiency in mammalian cellular environments. A key innovation involves the modification of loop III within the ribozyme structure, which significantly influences cleaving activity. Loop III modifications include alterations in nucleotide length and composition, with exemplary sequences comprising four-, six-, or eight-nucleotide loops that optimize ribozyme performance under physiological conditions. Additional structural elements, such as stem extensions and stabilizing hairpins, may be incorporated to further refine ribozyme behavior. The invention further encompasses the grafting of aptamer sequences onto the ribozyme scaffold, enabling ligand-dependent regulation of cleavage activity. When an effector molecule binds to the grafted aptamer, it induces conformational changes that either inhibit or enhance ribozyme self-cleavage, thereby providing a mechanism for external control over gene expression. DNA constructs encoding these engineered ribozymes are designed with appropriate promoters and nucleic acid products, allowing transcription of chimeric mRNAs containing the self-cleaving motif. Upon transcription, spontaneous cleavage occurs unless modulated by specific effectors, thus regulating the availability of intact mRNA for translation. Specific embodiments include DNA constructs featuring aptamer-grafted self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motifs positioned upstream or downstream of reporter genes or therapeutic transgenes. The system supports both single and multiple ribozyme arrangements, with tandem motifs enhancing regulatory stringency. Host cells, including packaging cell lines for viral vector production, can be engineered to stably express these constructs, facilitating both research and clinical applications. Methods for modulating expression involve administration of small molecule effectors, antisense oligonucleotides, or other inhibitors that interfere with ribozyme activity. Ex vivo and in vivo approaches enable controlled gene expression in diverse biological contexts, including endogenous gene regulation through targeted integration strategies. Screening methodologies allow identification of novel ribozyme inhibitors and aptamer-binding effectors, supporting continuous optimization of the system. Transgenic nonhuman animals carrying these regulatory constructs provide valuable models for studying gene function and therapeutic interventions. Kits containing essential components for implementing the technology streamline experimental workflows. Diagnostic methods for determining inhibitor levels in biological samples extend the utility of the platform beyond basic research into clinical monitoring applications. Ultimately, the invention provides a versatile, tunable, and responsive gene regulation system applicable across biomedical research, drug discovery, and gene therapy domains.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Self-Cleaving RNA Mutant Motifs

The invention centers on self-cleaving RNA mutant motifs, which are engineered variants of naturally occurring ribozymes optimized for function in mammalian systems. These motifs serve as molecular switches that regulate gene expression through controlled mRNA fragmentation. Structurally, they derive from hammerhead ribozymes characterized by three base-paired stems radiating from a conserved catalytic core. Standard nomenclature assigns positions 1–15 to the core nucleotides, with stems I–III forming helical domains that stabilize the active conformation. Modifications to these ribozymes include substitutions at critical positions such as 5, 7, and 14 within the conserved core, as well as alterations to loop structures connecting the stems. Particularly impactful are modifications to loop III, which can be engineered as four-, six-, or eight-nucleotide sequences to fine-tune cleavage kinetics. For instance, a self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif featuring a four-nucleotide loop III combined with a G→A substitution at position 7 exhibits enhanced activity under low magnesium conditions typical of mammalian cytoplasm. Additional variants incorporate stem extensions or terminal hairpins to improve thermodynamic stability. Aptamer sequences can be grafted onto peripheral regions without disrupting catalytic function, creating ligand-responsive ribozymes. Substitutions refer to nucleotide replacements that preserve secondary structure while altering chemical properties, and a self-cleaving schistosome RNA mutant motif is defined as any derivative of the native *S. mansoni* Sm1 ribozyme containing one or more such modifications that enhance performance in eukaryotic cells.

### Expression Vectors and Cell Lines

Expression vectors implementing the invention contain a promoter operably linked to a nucleic acid sequence encoding both a self-cleaving ribozyme and a nucleic acid product (e.g., reporter or therapeutic gene). Essential transcription elements include enhancers, TATA boxes, and polyadenylation signals compatible with mammalian expression machinery. Upon transfection into host cells, these constructs produce chimeric mRNAs wherein the ribozyme domain mediates self-cleavage, preventing translation of the downstream product unless inhibited. Viral vectors, including lentiviral and adeno-associated virus (AAV) backbones, similarly incorporate these regulatory cassettes for efficient delivery. Manufacturing employs standard recombinant DNA techniques or chemical synthesis, with kits providing pre-engineered ribozyme modules and cloning sites for user-defined inserts. Optional inclusion of ribozyme inhibitors allows immediate assessment of regulatory dynamics. Methods for modulating expression involve introducing these DNA constructs into cells via transfection or viral transduction, followed by administration of effectors that alter ribozyme conformation. Structural elements like internal ribosome entry sites (IRES) or stability-enhancing untranslated regions (UTRs) may be added to optimize expression profiles. Aptamer-effector interactions provide reversible control, with binding inducing allosteric changes that suppress cleavage activity.

### Methods of Delivering Nucleic Acids

Nucleic acid delivery encompasses multiple established techniques tailored to experimental or therapeutic needs. Transformation and transfection utilize calcium phosphate precipitation, electroporation, or lipid-based reagents to introduce plasmid DNA into cultured cells. Direct uptake methods employ naked DNA or peptide-conjugated nucleic acids for passive cellular entry. Projectile bombardment delivers genetic material coated onto microcarriers using gene guns, particularly useful for plant or tissue explant work. Liposomal formulations encapsulate DNA/RNA for enhanced membrane penetration and endosomal escape. Viral vectors, especially those pseudotyped with VSV-G envelope proteins, achieve high-efficiency transduction across diverse cell types by targeting ubiquitous phospholipid receptors. Recombinant viral genomes are constructed by inserting the regulatory cassette into transfer plasmids, then co-transfecting packaging cells expressing necessary viral proteins to generate infectious particles.

### Methods of Identifying Ribozyme Regulators

Screening methodologies identify agents that modulate ribozyme activity through direct binding or indirect mechanisms. Primary assays use reporter constructs where luciferase or LacZ expression depends on ribozyme inhibition. Cells transfected with these constructs are exposed to compound libraries, with increased luminescence or colorimetric signal indicating potential inhibitors. Aptamer-containing variants enable parallel screening for effector molecules that bind grafted domains and alter cleavage rates. Randomized ribozyme libraries facilitate discovery of sequence-specific regulators. Combinatorial compound collections, including FDA-approved drugs and natural product extracts, undergo high-throughput evaluation using automated plate readers. Hits are validated through dose-response curves and counter-screens against inactive ribozyme controls. Antisense oligonucleotides, particularly morpholino derivatives, serve as positive controls by sterically blocking catalytic cores. Modified oligonucleotides with phosphorothioate linkages or 2'-O-methyl sugars enhance nuclease resistance. Specific inhibitors like toyocamycin and 5-fluorouridine are characterized for potency (EC50) and maximal induction (Foldmax). Diagnostic applications extend to measuring intracellular inhibitor levels by correlating reporter activity with compound concentration in biological samples.

### Methods of Treatment and Administration

Therapeutic implementations involve engineering patient-derived or allogeneic cells with ribozyme-regulated transgenes for ex vivo manipulation prior to reinfusion. Suitable cell types include hematopoietic stem cells, T lymphocytes, and mesenchymal stromal cells sourced from bone marrow, peripheral blood, or adipose tissue. Homologous recombination or site-specific integrases (Cre/loxP, FLP/FRT) enable precise genomic insertion of regulatory cassettes. Transgenic animals generated through embryonic stem cell modification or direct zygote injection serve as disease models. In vivo treatment administers DNA constructs via systemic or localized routes (intravenous, intratumoral), often using viral vectors for sustained expression. Effector molecules like toyocamycin-FUR cocktails are delivered orally, intraperitoneally, or via implantable pellets for controlled release. Dosage regimens balance induction efficacy against cytotoxicity, informed by pharmacokinetic studies. Pharmaceutical compositions formulate nucleic acids with carriers like liposomes or polymeric nanoparticles, incorporating modified nucleotides (e.g., 2'-fluoro) to enhance stability.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

Pharmaceutical preparations contain ribozyme-regulated nucleic acids formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers suitable for intended administration routes. Parenteral formulations use isotonic saline with preservatives; oral preparations employ enteric coatings to protect against gastric degradation. Liposomal encapsulation improves biodistribution and reduces immunogenicity. Non-viral vectors like minicircle DNA offer prolonged expression with reduced size constraints. Topical applications utilize transdermal patches with permeation enhancers (e.g., oleic acid) for localized delivery. All compositions undergo rigorous quality control for sterility, endotoxin levels, and potency before clinical use.

### Exemplification

Experimental validation demonstrates the system's functionality across multiple parameters. Transient transfections with candidate ribozymes (Sm1, Pst-3, 5ml) identify N79—a modified Sm1 variant—as optimal for mammalian use. LacZ reporter assays quantify β-galactosidase levels to define cleavage efficiency, revealing >95% suppression by functional ribozymes versus baseline in mutants. Structural analyses confirm hammerhead architecture with optimized stem-loop configurations. Positional studies show consistent activity whether placed 5' or 3' to coding sequences. Tandem ribozyme arrays enhance repression stringency. Antibiotic screens identify toyocamycin (365-fold induction) and FUR (110-fold) as lead inhibitors, with synergistic effects yielding >600-fold induction when combined. Stable HEK79 cell lines enable high-throughput screening, leading to discovery of 15 inhibitors across adenosine/uridine analog classes. Northern blots verify mRNA stabilization correlates with protein induction. In vivo AAV delivery to nude mice demonstrates spatially controlled luciferase expression, with cocktail administration producing 13–37-fold induction. These results establish the platform's superiority over transcription-based systems in speed, reversibility, and dynamic range.

### Methods:

Standard protocols govern all experimental procedures. Transient transfections use Fugene6 reagent according to manufacturer specifications. Reporter assays employ ONPG substrate for β-galactosidase quantification or luciferin addition for luminescence measurement. Ribozyme activity is assessed through in vitro transcription with [α-32P]UTP labeling, followed by denaturing PAGE and PhosphorImager analysis to calculate cleavage percentages. Bioluminescent imaging uses IVIS Spectrum systems with Living Image software for photon quantification. Adenosine and derivatives are sourced from commercial suppliers (Sigma, Berry & Associates) and prepared as DMSO or PBS stocks at defined concentrations for dose-response studies.
