# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and, more specifically, to the treatment of glioma, a primary malignant brain tumor. In particular, the invention provides a novel therapeutic approach for the management of glioblastoma multiforme through the inhibition of nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase), also known as Pre-B-cell colony-enhancing factor 1 (PBEF1) or visfatin. The invention encompasses methods for identifying small-molecule inhibitors of NMPRTase via structure-based virtual screening, as well as the use of a newly identified lead compound—3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro—as a potent inhibitor of both NMPRTase enzymatic activity and glioblastoma cell proliferation. This compound represents a non-FK866 scaffold with demonstrated efficacy in selectively targeting NMPRTase-overexpressing glioma cells, thereby offering a promising strategy for the development of targeted glioma therapeutics.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION:

Gliomas are the most common primary malignant tumors of the central nervous system in adults, accounting for approximately 80% of all such neoplasms. Among gliomas, glioblastoma multiforme (GBM), classified as a grade IV astrocytoma by the World Health Organization, is the most aggressive and lethal form, characterized by rapid growth, diffuse infiltration into surrounding brain tissue, resistance to conventional therapies, and a dismal prognosis with a median survival of less than one year despite maximal surgical resection, radiation, and chemotherapy. The molecular pathogenesis of GBM involves multiple genetic and epigenetic alterations, including amplification of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), loss of function of the tumor suppressor PTEN, dysregulation of the RB1 pathway, and overexpression of platelet-derived growth factor receptor alpha (PDGFRA) and cyclin-dependent kinase 4 (CDK4). These aberrations converge on pathways that promote uncontrolled proliferation, evasion of apoptosis, and metabolic reprogramming.

A critical aspect of cancer cell metabolism is the heightened demand for nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), a coenzyme essential not only for redox reactions but also as a substrate for enzymes involved in DNA repair (e.g., PARPs), gene silencing (e.g., sirtuins), and calcium signaling (e.g., CD38). Cancer cells, including gliomas, exhibit upregulated NAD+ biosynthesis, particularly through the salvage pathway, which recycles nicotinamide back into NAD+. The rate-limiting enzyme in this pathway is NMPRTase, which catalyzes the conversion of nicotinamide and phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate (PRPP) into nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN), a direct precursor of NAD+. Microarray and protein expression analyses have revealed that NMPRTase is significantly overexpressed in human gliomas, with expression levels correlating positively with tumor grade—Grade IV (GBM) exhibiting the highest upregulation compared to lower-grade gliomas and normal glial cells. This dependency on NMPRTase-mediated NAD+ production renders glioma cells vulnerable to its pharmacological inhibition.

FK866 (also known as APO866 or daporinad) is a well-characterized, highly potent small-molecule inhibitor of human NMPRTase. It binds competitively at the active site, leading to profound NAD+ depletion, energy crisis, and selective apoptosis in tumor cells while sparing normal cells. Despite its promise in preclinical models, FK866 remains the only clinically advanced NMPRTase inhibitor, and its development has faced challenges related to pharmacokinetics and dose-limiting toxicities. Moreover, reliance on a single chemical scaffold limits opportunities for optimization. Therefore, there exists a compelling need for novel, structurally distinct NMPRTase inhibitors with improved therapeutic profiles.

Recent advances in structural biology have provided high-resolution crystal structures of human NMPRTase in its apo form, bound to its product NMN (PDB: 2GVG), and complexed with FK866 (PDB: 2GVJ). These structures reveal a homodimeric enzyme with two symmetric active sites located at the subunit interface, each featuring a deep, tunnel-like binding pocket capable of accommodating both the natural substrate/product and larger synthetic inhibitors like FK866. Critically, the binding modes of NMN and FK866 partially overlap but extend into adjacent regions, suggesting opportunities for designing inhibitors that exploit unique sub-pockets. Furthermore, sequence and structural comparisons with related enzymes in the NAD+ biosynthetic pathway—such as nicotinate phosphoribosyltransferase (NAPRT) and quinolinate phosphoribosyltransferase (QPRT)—demonstrate significant divergence in the active site architecture, supporting the feasibility of developing NMPRTase-selective inhibitors.

To address the urgent need for new glioma therapeutics, the inventors employed a structure-based drug discovery approach centered on virtual screening. Leveraging the atomic-level detail of the NMPRTase–FK866 complex, a large library of drug-like compounds was computationally docked into the enzyme’s active site using a high-performance parallel implementation of the AutoDock algorithm. This approach enabled the rapid evaluation of over 13,000 compounds, leading to the identification of a lead molecule—3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro—that demonstrates potent inhibition of both NMPRTase enzymatic activity and the proliferation of NMPRTase-overexpressing glioblastoma cells, thus validating the utility of this rational design strategy.

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION

It is an object of the present invention to provide a method for the treatment of glioma, particularly glioblastoma multiforme, by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a novel small-molecule inhibitor of nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase). It is a further object to provide a composition comprising said inhibitor for use in the therapy of NMPRTase-overexpressing cancers. Additionally, it is an object to disclose a structure-based method for identifying such inhibitors through virtual screening of ligand libraries against the three-dimensional structure of human NMPRTase.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for identifying compounds useful as therapeutic agents for glioblastoma multiforme, comprising the steps of performing virtual screening of a ligand library against the crystal structure of human NMPRTase, selecting candidate compounds based on binding energy, cluster size, and conservation of key interactions observed in native ligand complexes, and experimentally validating the inhibitory activity of selected compounds against NMPRTase enzymatic function and glioma cell proliferation. Through this approach, the inventors have identified a lead compound, designated herein as Compound 5 and chemically named 3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro, which exhibits potent inhibition of NMPRTase and selective cytotoxicity against glioblastoma cells overexpressing PBEF1/NMPRTase. The invention further encompasses a method of treating glioblastoma multiforme in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering a pharmaceutical composition containing an effective amount of said compound. In summary, the invention discloses a novel therapeutic agent, a method for its identification, and its application in the targeted therapy of glioma based on metabolic vulnerability conferred by NMPRTase overexpression.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention is directed to a method of identifying a compound useful as a glioblastoma therapeutic agent, wherein said method comprises conducting a structure-based virtual screening campaign against the three-dimensional coordinates of human nicotinamide phosphoribosyltransferase (NMPRTase). The virtual screening involves the computational docking of a diverse library of drug-like small molecules into the active site of NMPRTase, utilizing structural data derived from X-ray crystallography of the enzyme in complex with either its reaction product NMN (PDB ID: 2GVG) or the inhibitor FK866 (PDB ID: 2GVJ). The ligand library employed consists of 13,214 compounds selected from the Maybridge HitFinder™ collection based on adherence to Lipinski’s rule of five, ensuring favorable pharmacokinetic properties. Each ligand is prepared by generating low-energy three-dimensional conformers, adding hydrogen atoms, assigning appropriate protonation states at physiological pH, and optimizing geometry using the OPLS_2005 force field.

The NMPRTase protein structure is prepared by removing crystallographic water molecules, excising co-crystallized ligands and phosphate ions, adding polar hydrogens, and assigning Kollman united-atom charges. A rigid-receptor docking protocol is implemented using a grid box of dimensions 86 × 60 × 50 points with 0.375 Å spacing, encompassing the entire ligand-binding tunnel observed in both NMN- and FK866-bound structures. Docking simulations are performed using a parallelized version of AutoDock, with genetic algorithm parameters set to ensure thorough conformational sampling. Resulting poses are clustered based on root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) thresholds of 1.0 Å, and compounds are shortlisted if they exhibit at least one docked pose with a predicted binding energy lower than that of FK866 (used as a benchmark) and a cluster size exceeding a predefined cutoff derived from control docking experiments.

Experimental validation of shortlisted compounds includes assessment of NMPRTase inhibitory activity using a radiometric assay that measures the conversion of [14C]-nicotinamide to [14C]-NAD+ in cytoplasmic extracts from glioblastoma cell lines, as well as evaluation of anti-proliferative effects via MTT assays in NMPRTase-overexpressing (e.g., U87) versus NMPRTase-low (e.g., U251) glioma cells. Through this integrated computational-experimental pipeline, the compound 3-amino-2-benzyl-7-nitro-4-(2-quinolyl)-1,2-dihydro was identified as a potent dual-function agent that inhibits both NMPRTase enzymatic activity and glioblastoma cell growth with micromolar potency.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising said compound, optionally in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient, for use in the treatment of glioma. As used herein, “glioma” refers to any primary brain tumor originating from glial cells, including astrocytomas, oligodendrogliomas, and glioblastomas, with particular emphasis on glioblastoma multiforme (WHO Grade IV). The rationale for targeting NMPRTase stems from its documented overexpression in glioma tissues relative to normal brain, with expression levels increasing with tumor grade, and the consequent metabolic dependence of these tumors on the NAD+ salvage pathway. Structural studies confirm that the active site of NMPRTase is amenable to inhibition by small molecules, and the disclosed compound binds within the same tunnel-like pocket as FK866, forming critical hydrogen bonds and hydrophobic interactions with conserved residues such as Y18, F193, Y188, and R349. Unlike FK866, however, the disclosed compound belongs to a distinct chemical scaffold, offering new avenues for medicinal chemistry optimization and reduced risk of cross-resistance.

### EXAMPLE 1

The methods and reagents employed in the present invention include standard molecular biology, biochemical, and computational techniques. Virtual screening was initiated by selecting the Maybridge HitFinder™ ligand library, which contains 14,400 structurally diverse, drug-like compounds. Ligands were prepared using Schrödinger’s LigPrep module to generate energetically minimized 3D structures, add hydrogens, remove salts, and enumerate tautomers and ionization states at pH 7 ± 2. Compounds failing basic drug-likeness filters (e.g., molecular weight < 200, excessive H-bond donors/acceptors) were excluded, yielding 13,214 candidates. The NMPRTase protein structure (PDB: 2GVJ) was processed by removing water, ligands, and phosphate groups, followed by addition of polar hydrogens and Kollman charges. A grid box spanning the entire active site tunnel (86 × 60 × 50 points, 0.375 Å spacing) was constructed using AutoGrid. Docking parameter files specified a population size of 150, 2.5 million energy evaluations, 500 generations, and 100 independent runs per ligand. Simulations were executed on an IBM BlueGene supercomputer using a parallel AutoDock implementation. Post-docking, poses were clustered by RMSD (1.0 Å cutoff), and top hits were selected based on binding energy and cluster size relative to FK866 controls. Energy minimization of top poses was performed using CNS with a 6 Å radius around the ligand. Cell lines used included U87, U251, and others, cultured in DMEM with 10% FBS. RNA isolation utilized TRI reagent, and RT-qPCR was performed using PBEF1-specific primers with GARS, AGPAT1, ATP5G1, and RPL35A as internal controls; fold changes were calculated via the ΔΔCT method.

### EXAMPLE 2

Western blot analysis was conducted to assess PBEF1/NMPRTase protein expression across glioma cell lines. Cells were lysed in RIPA buffer, and equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE, transferred to PVDF membranes, and probed with a rabbit polyclonal antibody raised against GST-PBEF1. An anti-tubulin antibody served as a loading control. Enhanced chemiluminescence was used for detection, confirming that U87 and U138 cells exhibit high NMPRTase levels, while U251 shows minimal expression.

### EXAMPLE 3

The NMPRTase assay measured enzymatic conversion of [14C]-nicotinamide to [14C]-NAD+. Cytoplasmic extracts were prepared from U87 cells by hypotonic lysis, freeze-thaw cycles, and centrifugation, followed by protamine sulfate precipitation to remove nucleic acids. The reaction mixture contained 5 mM MgCl2, 2 mM ATP, 0.5 mM PRPP, 0.1 mM [14C]-nicotinamide, and 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 8.8). Reactions were initiated by adding enzyme extract, incubated at 37°C for 1 hour, stopped by heating, and products separated by thin-layer chromatography on cellulose plates using 1 M ammonium sulfate:ethanol (3:7). Radiolabeled NAD+ was quantified using a phosphorimager and Alpha Innotec software.

### EXAMPLE 4

The MTT assay assessed cell viability after compound treatment. U87 or U251 cells (1.5 × 10³/well) were seeded in 96-well plates, treated with test compounds for 48 hours, then incubated with MTT (5 mg/mL) for 3 hours. Formazan crystals were dissolved in DMSO, and absorbance was read at 550 nm. IC50 values were calculated from dose-response curves, revealing that Compound 5 inhibited U87 growth with an IC50 of 325 μM but had no effect on U251 cells.

### EXAMPLE 5

Analysis of the NMPRTase binding site was based on crystal structures 2GVG and 2GVJ. The active site forms a tunnel at the dimer interface, with NMN binding near the catalytic center and FK866 extending deeper. Key interactions include π-stacking between ligand aromatics and F193/Y18. Sequence alignment with NAPRT and QPRT showed low active-site homology, supporting selectivity. PocketMatch analysis confirmed structural divergence, enabling design of NMPRTase-specific inhibitors.

### EXAMPLE 6

Control compounds NMN and FK866 were docked to validate the protocol; their poses reproduced crystallographic conformations with RMSD < 1.0 Å. Top-ranking poses of test compounds were analyzed for ligand-protein contacts using LPC software. Compounds were prioritized if they formed hydrogen bonds with residues involved in NMN/FK866 binding (e.g., Y188, S241, R349) and exhibited large clusters of low-energy poses. Compound 5 emerged as the top candidate due to strong interactions in both NMN- and FK866-like binding modes.

### Inhibition of NMPRTase Activity by Selected Lead Compounds

NMPRTase enzyme was prepared from U87 cell cytoplasmic extracts as described. Inhibition assays showed that Compound 5 reduced [14C]-NAD+ formation by >80% at 100 μM, comparable to FK866. Compounds 1–6 were tested; only Compounds 4 and 5 showed significant enzyme inhibition, with Compound 5 being the most potent.

### EXAMPLE 8

Inhibition of cell growth was tested in U87 (NMPRTase-high) and U251 (NMPRTase-low) cells. Compound 5 inhibited U87 proliferation with an IC50 of 325 μM but showed no activity against U251, confirming on-target NMPRTase dependence. Adriamycin, used as a positive control, inhibited both lines, validating assay sensitivity.
