# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of peptidomimetic foldamers, specifically peptide-amino urea hybrid foldamer compounds designed to mimic biologically active α-helical peptides while exhibiting enhanced pharmacological properties. These hybrid molecules combine segments of natural or modified amino acid sequences with oligourea-based foldamer motifs that adopt stable helical conformations structurally analogous to native peptide α-helices. The invention is particularly directed toward the development of therapeutic agents for metabolic disorders, including type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), and related conditions, through the rational design of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) analogues incorporating oligourea residues. By replacing selected tetrapeptide segments of GLP-1 with triads of amino urea residues, the resulting hybrids retain receptor binding affinity and agonistic activity while demonstrating improved resistance to enzymatic degradation, prolonged plasma half-life, and extended in vivo efficacy compared to native peptide counterparts.

## BACKGROUND

Protein–protein interactions (PPIs) represent a critical class of molecular targets in modern drug discovery, governing numerous physiological and pathological processes. Peptides have emerged as valuable tools for modulating PPIs due to their ability to engage large, shallow interaction surfaces with high specificity. However, native peptides suffer from significant pharmaceutical limitations, including poor membrane permeability, low oral bioavailability, rapid proteolytic degradation, and short in vivo half-lives, which collectively hinder their clinical utility. To overcome these drawbacks, extensive research has focused on peptidomimetic strategies that preserve biological activity while enhancing stability and pharmacokinetic profiles. Among these approaches, foldamers—oligomeric architectures that adopt well-defined secondary structures—have garnered considerable interest as promising scaffolds for mimicking protein structural motifs. Oligoureas, in particular, constitute a compelling class of peptidomimetic foldamers owing to their capacity to form stable helical conformations that closely resemble peptide α-helices in spatial geometry and side-chain projection. Unlike peptides, the oligourea backbone is inherently resistant to proteolytic cleavage, offering a strategic advantage for improving metabolic stability. Moreover, oligoureas are compatible with solid-phase synthesis techniques, enabling modular construction and sequence diversification. Despite these favorable attributes, the integration of oligourea segments into bioactive peptides to generate functional hybrids remained largely unexplored prior to the present invention, especially in the context of in vivo therapeutic applications. The need persists for novel peptidomimetic compounds that effectively mimic α-helical domains of therapeutic peptides while overcoming the inherent instability and short duration of action that limit their clinical translation.

## SUMMARY

The invention provides peptide-amino urea hybrid foldamers comprising a peptide segment covalently linked to an oligourea segment, wherein the oligourea portion consists of at least three consecutive amino urea residues that adopt a helical conformation structurally analogous to an α-helix. These hybrids are designed by replacing four consecutive α-amino acid residues in a native α-helical peptide with three amino urea residues, thereby preserving the overall helical topology and key side-chain orientations necessary for biological activity. Central to the invention is the Xu residue, a non-natural amino urea monomer bearing a side chain at the β-position relative to the urea nitrogen, which enables precise mimicry of native amino acid side-chain projections. Substitutions of the Xu residue include Xuα variants, wherein the side chain is attached at the α-carbon adjacent to the urea carbonyl, allowing further fine-tuning of spatial orientation. Formula I:  
Peptide-[Xu(R¹)-Xu(R²)-Xu(R³)]ₙ-Peptide  

wherein R¹, R², and R³ independently represent hydrogen, alkyl, aryl, heteroaryl, or any natural or non-natural amino acid side chain, and n is an integer from 1 to 5. A preferred embodiment is described by Formula II:

Formula II:  
Peptide-[Xu(Rᵃ)-Xu(Rᵇ)-Xu(Rᶜ)]-[Xu(Rᵈ)-Xu(Rᵉ)-Xu(Rᶠ)]-Peptide  

wherein Rᵃ through Rᶠ are selected to replicate critical side chains from the replaced tetrapeptide segments of the parental peptide. Exemplary compounds include GLP-1 peptide-oligourea hybrids designated as SEQ ID NO: 2–24, which incorporate oligourea triads at positions 9–12, 11–14, 14–17, 15–18, 18–21, 22–25, 28–31, or combinations thereof within the GLP-1 sequence. These hybrids exhibit potent agonistic activity at the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R), with EC₅₀ values ranging from sub-nanomolar to low nanomolar concentrations. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising an effective amount of a peptide-amino urea hybrid compound in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. Such compositions are useful in methods of treating metabolic disorders, particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus, by administering a therapeutically effective dose to a subject in need thereof. Additional chemical modifications, such as fatty acid acylation (e.g., C18 diacid attachment via PEG spacers), may be incorporated to enhance albumin binding and prolong systemic exposure. The compounds may also be formulated as pharmaceutically acceptable acid or base addition salts, prodrugs, or protected derivatives without loss of biological activity. Critically, the oligourea segments confer resistance to dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) and neutral endopeptidase 24.11 (NEP 24.11), two major proteases responsible for GLP-1 inactivation, thereby extending plasma half-life and duration of action in vivo.

Oligoureas are peptidomimetic foldamers characterized by a backbone composed of repeating N,N'-disubstituted urea units that adopt a stable 2.5-helix conformation with 10 atoms per turn, closely mimicking the pitch and side-chain display of peptide α-helices. Peptide-amino urea hybrid foldamer compounds of the invention comprise a native or modified peptide segment fused to one or more oligourea segments, wherein the fusion preserves the overall secondary structure and biological function of the parental peptide. The parental peptide—such as GLP-1—typically contains an α-helical domain essential for receptor binding; substitution of four consecutive α-amino acids with three amino urea residues maintains helical continuity while imparting superior protease resistance and extended half-life. Peptide-oligourea hybrid compounds are defined as molecules wherein at least one tetrapeptide segment is replaced by a triad of non-peptide amino urea residues, which may bear proteinaceous side chains identical or analogous to those of the replaced amino acids. This substitution strategy leverages the structural compatibility between oligourea helices and peptide α-helices, as demonstrated by crystallographic overlays showing minimal deviation in Cα-to-Cα distances and side-chain vector orientations. The resulting hybrids exhibit enhanced resistance to DPP-4 and NEP 24.11, key enzymes that rapidly degrade native GLP-1, leading to prolonged receptor activation and improved pharmacodynamics. Biological activity is retained or even enhanced in certain embodiments, as evidenced by EC₅₀ values in cAMP production assays comparable to or better than native GLP-1 analogues.
