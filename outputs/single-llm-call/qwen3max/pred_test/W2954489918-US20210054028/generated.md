# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of bioinorganic chemistry and microbial physiology, particularly to a novel class of peptide siderophores produced by Gram-negative bacteria. More specifically, the invention concerns a cyclic peptide siderophore containing N-nitroso-hydroxylamine functional groups that function as high-affinity iron(III) chelators. The invention further encompasses bacterial cells capable of producing such peptide siderophores, including but not limited to strains of *Burkholderia graminis*, which have been isolated from the rhizosphere of grasses and other monocotyledonous plants. These bacterial cells synthesize and secrete the peptide siderophore under iron-limiting conditions as part of a sophisticated iron acquisition system that supports both bacterial survival and plant growth promotion. The peptide siderophore described herein represents a structurally and functionally distinct class of microbial iron chelators, differing fundamentally from classical hydroxamate, catecholate, or salicylate-type siderophores in both chemical architecture and biological activity. The invention thus provides new tools for understanding microbial iron metabolism, plant-microbe interactions, and potential applications in sustainable agriculture through enhanced iron bioavailability.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Iron is an essential micronutrient required for fundamental life processes across all domains of life, including electron transport, DNA synthesis, and enzymatic catalysis. Despite its abundance in the Earth’s crust, iron exists predominantly in the oxidized ferric (Fe³⁺) state under aerobic and neutral pH conditions, rendering it highly insoluble and biologically inaccessible. Microorganisms have evolved specialized low-molecular-weight compounds known as siderophores to solubilize and acquire ferric iron from the environment. Traditional siderophores typically employ hydroxamate, catecholate, or α-hydroxycarboxylate moieties as iron-coordinating ligands, forming stable octahedral complexes with Fe³⁺. However, these conventional siderophores exhibit limitations in efficiency under certain environmental conditions, such as alkaline soils or oxidative stress, where their stability or bioavailability may be compromised. Moreover, many synthetic iron chelators used in agriculture suffer from poor biodegradability, environmental persistence, or limited uptake by plants. There remains a significant unmet need for novel, naturally derived iron-chelating systems that combine high affinity for Fe³⁺ with biocompatibility, biodegradability, and additional beneficial biological functions. The discovery of a new structural class of siderophores featuring N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands addresses this need by offering a unique mechanism of iron coordination coupled with unexpected signaling properties, thereby expanding the repertoire of strategies for iron mobilization in biological and agricultural contexts.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of efficient, environmentally benign, and multifunctional iron-chelating agents that can enhance iron bioavailability in both microbial and plant systems under diverse environmental conditions. The solution provided herein is a novel cyclic peptide siderophore, designated gramibactin, which incorporates two N-nitroso-hydroxylamine functional groups as iron-coordinating ligands. Gramibactin is produced by specific rhizobacteria, notably *Burkholderia graminis*, and exhibits dual functionality: it acts as a high-affinity Fe³⁺ chelator and serves as a nitric oxide (NO) donor upon iron binding or reduction. Positron emission tomography–computed tomography (PET-CT) tracer studies using ⁶⁸Ga-labeled gramibactin demonstrated efficient uptake in plant roots, while supplementation experiments confirmed its ability to rescue iron-deficient plants by restoring chlorophyll synthesis. In vitro assays and in vivo fluorescence imaging further validated its role in iron delivery and NO release within plant tissues. The ecological and agricultural implications are substantial, as gramibactin-producing bacteria can promote plant growth in iron-poor soils without synthetic fertilizers. The chelating properties of N-nitroso-hydroxylamines enable formation of exceptionally stable Fe³⁺ complexes, surpassing many hydroxamate siderophores in affinity. A preferred embodiment of the peptide siderophore comprises a cyclic hexapeptide backbone with two modified ornithine-derived residues (Gra1 and Gra2) bearing N-nitroso-hydroxylamine side chains. In one embodiment, the peptide has the sequence X1-X2-X3-Gra1-X4-Gra2, wherein X1 is Asp, X2 and X3 are Thr, X4 is Gly, and Gra1/Gra2 are N-nitroso-hydroxylamine-functionalized graminoic acid residues. Another embodiment features the sequence Asp-Thr1-Thr2-Gra1-Gly-Gra2, wherein the side chain hydroxyl of Thr1 and the C-terminus of Gra2 participate in macrocyclization. Additional embodiments include the core motif X2-X3-Gra1-X4-Gra2 and linear precursors. The N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligand is defined by the –N(OH)–N=O moiety, which coordinates Fe³⁺ in a bidentate fashion. The invention further includes cyclic peptide siderophores according to Formula I and Formula II, which specify stereochemistry, ring size, and substituent positions. Also encompassed are bacterial cells genetically capable of synthesizing gramibactin via a nonribosomal peptide synthetase (NRPS) pathway.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Iron plays a central role in cellular respiration, photosynthesis, and redox homeostasis, making its acquisition critical for all aerobic organisms. Siderophores are secondary metabolites secreted by microbes to scavenge Fe³⁺ from the environment through high-affinity chelation, followed by receptor-mediated uptake of the iron-siderophore complex. Metal-ligand bonding in siderophores typically involves oxygen-donor atoms forming octahedral coordination spheres around Fe³⁺. Siderophores can be produced either by culturing siderophore-producing microorganisms under iron-depleted conditions or by total chemical synthesis. Peptide siderophores represent a subclass wherein the iron-chelating scaffold is embedded within a peptide backbone, often cyclized for stability.

A nitroso group is defined as –N=O; a hydroxylamine group as –N(OH)–R; and an N-nitroso-hydroxylamine as –N(OH)–N=O, wherein the nitrogen adjacent to the hydroxyl is bonded to the peptide backbone. A hydroxy carboxylic acid contains both –OH and –COOH on adjacent carbons. Hydroxamates (–C(O)N(OH)R), catecholates (ortho-dihydroxybenzenes), and salicylates (2-hydroxybenzoates) are classical siderophore ligands. Modifications to the peptide siderophore include substitutions at X1–X4 with conservative amino acids (e.g., Ser for Thr, Glu for Asp), alkyl (C₁–C₆ saturated hydrocarbon), alkoxy (–OR, R = alkyl), carboxyl (–COOH), or hydroxyl (–OH) groups. The siderophore functions to solubilize Fe³⁺, deliver iron to plants, and release NO, modulating iron homeostasis. The invention includes methods for promoting plant growth by applying gramibactin or gramibactin-producing bacteria to seeds, roots, or soil. Compositions comprise purified gramibactin, bacterial inoculants, or synthetic analogs in formulations such as aqueous suspensions, granules, or seed coatings for agricultural use.

## Methods of Administration

The methods of application involve delivering the peptide siderophore or siderophore-producing bacterial cells to plants or their growing environment. Treatment of plants and plant parts includes foliar sprays, root drenches, or seed priming with gramibactin solutions or bacterial suspensions. Soil treatment entails incorporation of gramibactin-producing rhizobacteria into the rhizosphere to establish symbiotic relationships. Plant-bacterial interactions are leveraged through the natural colonization of root surfaces by plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR), such as *Burkholderia graminis*, which produce gramibactin to enhance iron availability. These PGPR improve plant fitness under iron-limiting conditions by secreting gramibactin, which chelates soil Fe³⁺, facilitates its uptake via plant transporters, and releases NO to modulate iron-regulatory pathways, thereby increasing chlorophyll content and biomass.

## EXAMPLES

Rhizosphere bacteria, particularly *Burkholderia graminis* strain DSM 11363, were found to produce a novel siderophore under iron starvation. Siderophore biosynthesis was linked to a nonribosomal peptide synthetase (NRPS) gene cluster identified in the bacterial genome. Bioinformatic analysis predicted a hexapeptide structure with unusual modifications. Siderophore production was detected using chrome azurol S (CAS) agar assays, showing strong iron-chelating activity. Gene knockout of the NRPS cluster abolished siderophore production, confirming its responsibility. Structural elucidation via NMR and mass spectrometry revealed a cyclic hexapeptide with two N-nitroso-hydroxylamine groups. Absolute configuration was determined by Marfey’s analysis and isotopic labeling. The N-nitroso-hydroxylamine functionality was confirmed by characteristic UV and NMR shifts. Metal complexation studies showed high affinity for Fe³⁺ and Ga³⁺. Acid/base titrations indicated pKa values consistent with hydroxylamine protonation. The Fe³⁺-gramibactin complex formation constant exceeded 10³⁰, surpassing enterobactin. PET/CT with ⁶⁸Ga-gramibactin showed rapid root uptake in maize. Ga-complex stability was confirmed in planta. Iron-deficient barley treated with gramibactin showed restored chlorophyll levels comparable to Fe-EDTA. Nitric oxide release was detected via Griess assay and DAF-FM fluorescence in vitro and in planta, localized to root epidermal cells. Gramibactin modulates plant iron homeostasis genes (e.g., *IRT1*, *FRO2*). Ecologically, gramibactin enhances bacterial fitness and plant health in iron-poor soils. Agricultural applications include biofertilizers for calcareous soils. The bifunctionality as siderophore and NO donor is unprecedented. The N-nitroso ligands represent a new paradigm in microbial iron acquisition.

## Materials and Methods

### Bacterial Strains and General Culture Conditions

*B. graminis* DSM 11363 was obtained from the German Collection of Microorganisms. Cultures were grown in MM9 medium at 30°C with shaking. Knockout strains were generated via homologous recombination targeting the NRPS cluster. Bacterial cells were transformed using electroporation.

### MM9 Media Preparation

MM9 medium was prepared with Na₂HPO₄, KH₂PO₄, NaCl, NH₄Cl, MgSO₄, CaCl₂, glucose, and trace metals, adjusted to pH 7.0.

### CAS Agar Preparation

CAS agar contained chrome azurol S, FeCl₃, and hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide in LB agar; orange halos indicated siderophore production.

### Siderophore Production in Liquid Media

Bacteria were cultured in iron-depleted MM9 for 72 h; supernatants were extracted with ethyl acetate.

### General Analytical Procedures

¹H/¹³C NMR spectra were recorded at 600 MHz. LC-MS used C18 columns with ESI detection.

### Isolation of Gramibactin

Crude extract was separated by HPLC on a C18 column with acetonitrile/water gradients; pure gramibactin was lyophilized.

### Structure Elucidation of Gramibactin

Acid hydrolysis followed by Marfey’s analysis confirmed L-Thr, L-Asp, and D-Gra. NMR and HRMS established the cyclic structure.

### Griess Test

Nitrite accumulation from NO release was quantified by absorbance at 540 nm after sulfanilamide/NEDD reaction.

### ¹⁵N Labeling of Gramibactin

(NH₄)₂SO₄ in MM9 was replaced with (¹⁵NH₄)₂SO₄; labeled gramibactin showed +2 Da shift in MS, confirming two nitrogen atoms from ammonium in the N-nitroso groups. Synthesis of N²-benzyloxycarbonyl-N⁶-benzylidene-L-ornithine: KOH in MeOH, addition of Cbz-L-Orn, then benzaldehyde with molecular sieves; filtered, concentrated, lyophilized. NMR confirmed imine formation. Nitrone 3 synthesis: m-CPBA oxidation of imine 2 in MeOH; workup with ethyl acetate, TFA deprotection, recrystallization. N²-benzyloxycarbonyl-L-graminine (4): nitrone 3 in HCl/TFA/hexane, heated, extracted, purified by HPLC. Incorporation of L-threonine-2,3-d₂ into gramibactin confirmed Thr origin via MS.
