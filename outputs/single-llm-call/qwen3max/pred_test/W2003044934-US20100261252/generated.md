# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to recombinant production of IgA proteases, particularly bacterial IgA1 proteases that specifically cleave human immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1). More specifically, the invention provides improved methods for the high-yield expression, isolation, and purification of soluble and enzymatically active recombinant IgA1 protease polypeptides in non-pathogenic bacterial host cells, such as Escherichia coli. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the recombinantly produced IgA1 proteases and methods for their use in the treatment of IgA deposition disorders, including IgA nephropathy.

## BACKGROUND

Immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1) proteases are bacterial enzymes that specifically cleave the hinge region of human IgA1, a subclass of the predominant antibody class found at mucosal surfaces. These proteases are produced by a variety of pathogenic bacteria, including Neisseria meningitidis, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Haemophilus influenzae, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and others, as a mechanism to evade host immune defenses. The function of IgA1 proteases is to disrupt the effector functions of IgA1 antibodies, thereby facilitating bacterial colonization and persistence within the host. Structurally, serine-type IgA1 proteases are synthesized as precursor proteins containing an N-terminal signal peptide, a central protease domain, and C-terminal α-protein and β-core domains that mediate secretion across the outer membrane.

IgA deposition disorders, such as IgA nephropathy (IgAN), are characterized by the pathological accumulation of IgA1-containing immune complexes in tissues, particularly in the glomerular mesangium of the kidney. This deposition triggers inflammation, leading to progressive renal damage and, in severe cases, end-stage renal disease. Given the specificity of IgA1 proteases for human IgA1, these enzymes have been explored as potential therapeutic agents to degrade pathogenic IgA1 deposits in affected tissues. However, the clinical utility of IgA1 proteases has been severely limited by the inability to produce sufficient quantities of pure, active enzyme.

Previous attempts at recombinant production of IgA1 proteases have met with limited success. Expression of full-length IgA1 protease precursors in heterologous hosts, such as E. coli, often results in low yields, poor solubility, inclusion body formation, or inactive protein due to improper folding or lack of necessary post-translational processing. Moreover, native production from pathogenic bacteria like H. influenzae is impractical for large-scale manufacturing due to biosafety concerns, slow growth requirements (e.g., hemin supplementation for H. influenzae), and inherently low expression levels. For instance, yields of purified IgA1 protease from natural sources have been reported as low as 0.03 mg/L or less, which is insufficient for therapeutic applications.

IgA nephropathy, the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide, lacks a specific and effective therapy. Current management strategies focus on controlling blood pressure and reducing proteinuria, but do not address the underlying pathogenic IgA1 deposits. While non-specific proteases like chymopapain and subtilisin have shown some efficacy in animal models, their lack of specificity poses significant safety risks due to off-target proteolysis. In contrast, the isolated H. influenzae IgA1 protease has demonstrated the ability to reduce glomerular IgA1 immune complex deposition in murine models, highlighting its therapeutic potential. Nevertheless, the absence of a robust, scalable production method has hindered its clinical development.

In summary, there exists a critical need for improved methods to produce high yields of soluble, active, and pure recombinant IgA1 protease in a safe and scalable manner. Such methods would enable the development of effective therapies for IgA deposition disorders and facilitate further research into the biological and therapeutic roles of these unique enzymes.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces novel methods for producing recombinant IgA proteases, particularly IgA1 proteases, by expressing truncated polypeptides that lack the α-protein domain and β-core domain typically found in the full-length precursor. These methods involve the expression of IgA protease polypeptides comprising only the catalytically active protease domain, optionally fused to a signal peptide, in suitable host cells using appropriate expression vectors. The invention provides for the production of soluble and active IgA protease in high yield, overcoming the limitations of previous approaches.

In one embodiment, host cells, such as bacterial cells (e.g., E. coli strains C41(DE3) or C43(DE3)), are transformed with an expression vector encoding an IgA protease polypeptide lacking the α-protein and β-core domains. The host cells are cultured under conditions that promote expression, such as induction with isopropyl β-D-1-thiogalactopyranoside (IPTG) at a concentration of about 0.2 to 1.0 mM, preferably about 0.4 mM, at a temperature of about 12°C to 30°C, preferably about 20°C, for a period of about 3 to 24 hours. In some cases, the expressed IgA protease may form inclusion bodies, which are then isolated, solubilized using chaotropic agents like urea or guanidine hydrochloride, and subsequently refolded into their active conformation using optimized refolding buffers.

The invention further provides compositions comprising host cells engineered to produce the recombinant IgA protease, wherein the wet weight of the host cells can be scaled to industrial fermentation volumes, such as 100 liters or more of culture media. The productivity level of soluble protease achieved by the present methods is significantly enhanced, yielding at least 20-40 mg/L of purified active protein, representing a greater than 1000-fold increase over prior art methods. The ratio of soluble to total IgA protease produced is also markedly improved, facilitating efficient downstream purification.

The recombinant IgA proteases produced by the disclosed methods exhibit high purity, typically greater than 95%, and retain full enzymatic activity, as demonstrated by their ability to specifically cleave human IgA1. The polypeptides may share substantial identity (e.g., at least 90%, 95%, or 99%) with reference sequences such as SEQ ID NO:1 (corresponding to the H. influenzae IgA1 protease domain). The invention also encompasses sterile pharmaceutical compositions comprising the purified IgA protease, formulated for administration to subjects suffering from IgA deposition disorders, such as IgA nephropathy, for the purpose of degrading pathogenic IgA1 deposits and alleviating disease symptoms.

### IgA Protease Polypeptides

IgA proteases are bacterial enzymes that specifically target human IgA1. Serine-type IgA proteases, such as those from Neisseria and Haemophilus species, are synthesized as precursors with a signal peptide, protease domain, α-protein domain, and β-core domain. The β-core domain facilitates outer membrane translocation, and autocatalytic cleavage releases the mature protease. Type 1 serine proteases cleave between Pro-Ser, while type 2 cleave between Pro-Thr in the IgA1 hinge. Bacterial strains expressing IgA proteases include N. meningitidis (GenBank X59800), N. gonorrhoeae, H. influenzae, and streptococcal species. These are IgA-specific serine endopeptidases. Previous production methods from natural sources yielded low amounts due to pathogenicity and slow growth. Cloning full-length genes into E. coli often resulted in insoluble or inactive protein. The present invention overcomes this by expressing only the protease domain, lacking the α and β domains, which eliminates the need for complex secretion and processing. This truncated polypeptide is expressed in E. coli, such as C41(DE3), using vectors like pET21a with a T7 promoter or pColdIV with a cold-shock promoter. Expression is induced with IPTG at low temperature (e.g., 20°C) to enhance solubility. The polypeptide may include a signal peptide for periplasmic targeting or be cytoplasmic. Tags like His-tag facilitate purification. Host cells include various E. coli strains, with C41(DE3) showing superior productivity. Large-scale fermentation in 100 L+ volumes yields high titers. Productivity exceeds 100 mg/L of soluble protein, with purification yields of 20-40 mg/L of active enzyme. Solubility is increased by avoiding aggregation-prone domains. Chaperone co-expression or fusion partners may further aid folding. Secretion is not required, simplifying production. Cell lysis under mild conditions preserves activity. When inclusion bodies form, they are solubilized with urea and refolded in buffers like Tris, CHES, MES, or PBS, often with arginine to suppress aggregation. The final product is a soluble, active IgA protease with high specific activity, suitable for therapeutic use.

### Derivatives of IgA Protease Polypeptides

Polypeptide derivatives of IgA proteases include chemically modified forms designed to enhance stability, half-life, or reduce immunogenicity. A key derivative is PEGylated IgA protease, where polyethylene glycol (PEG) chains are covalently attached to lysine residues or the N-terminus. PEGylation is motivated by the need to prolong serum half-life and reduce clearance. Methods involve reacting the purified IgA protease with activated PEG reagents (e.g., mPEG-succinimidyl succinate) under controlled pH (7.0-8.5) and temperature (4-25°C) for 1-24 hours. The PEG group may have a molecular weight of 5-40 kDa, linear or branched. Reaction conditions are optimized to achieve site-specific or random modification while preserving enzymatic activity. The resulting PEGylated polypeptides are characterized by size-exclusion chromatography, mass spectrometry, and activity assays to confirm conjugation and functionality.

### IgA Deposition Disorders

IgA deposition disorders are a group of conditions characterized by abnormal accumulation of IgA1-containing immune complexes in tissues. IgA nephropathy (IgAN) is the prototypical disorder, featuring mesangial IgA1 deposits in the kidney, leading to hematuria, proteinuria, and progressive renal failure. Dermatitis herpetiformis involves IgA deposits in the skin, causing blistering rash. Henoch-Schoenlein purpura is a systemic vasculitis with IgA deposits in skin, joints, gut, and kidneys. Celiac disease may show IgA deposits in the gut. Liver diseases like alcoholic cirrhosis can also feature IgA deposits. These disorders share a common pathogenic mechanism involving galactose-deficient IgA1, which forms immune complexes that deposit in tissues. Treatment with IgA protease is motivated by its ability to specifically cleave and dissolve these pathogenic IgA1 deposits, thereby halting the inflammatory cascade and preventing organ damage.

### Representative Embodiments of the Disclosure

A representative method for producing serine-type IgA protease involves transforming E. coli C41(DE3) with a pET21a vector encoding the H. influenzae IgA1 protease domain (SEQ ID NO:1) with a C-terminal His-tag. Cells are grown in 100 L of culture media at 37°C to OD600 0.6, then induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at 20°C for 24 hours. Cells are harvested, lysed, and inclusion bodies isolated by centrifugation. Inclusion bodies are washed with detergent, solubilized in 8 M urea, and refolded by slow dilution into Tris buffer (pH 8.0) containing 0.5 M arginine at 10°C. The refolded protein is purified by nickel-affinity chromatography, anion-exchange (Q Sepharose), and size-exclusion chromatography (S300), yielding >95% pure, active IgA1 protease at 20-40 mg/L. The ratio of soluble to total IgA protease is >20%, a significant fold-increase over prior methods. The final product is formulated in sterile PBS for intravenous administration to treat IgA nephropathy.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The challenge of producing soluble IgA1 protease was addressed by generating expression constructs encoding truncated H. influenzae IgA1 protease (GenBank X59800). Full-length and domain-deleted variants were cloned into pET21a (T7 promoter) and pColdIV (cold-shock promoter) vectors, creating pET-S-IGAN (with signal peptide), pET-IGAN (protease domain only), pCold-S-IGAN, and pCold-IGAN. These were transformed into various E. coli strains, including BL21(DE3), C41(DE3), and Origami B(DE3). Protein expression was induced with 0.2-1.0 mM IPTG at 12-30°C for 3-24 hours. Cell lysates were analyzed by Western blot using anti-His antibodies, confirming expression of ~100 kDa bands. Activity was assessed by incubating lysates with human IgA1 and detecting cleavage via anti-IgA Western blot. ELISA quantified soluble expression levels. Among all conditions, pET-IGAN in C41(DE3) induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at 20°C for 24 hours yielded the highest levels of soluble, active IgA1 protease, demonstrating that truncation of the α and β domains and use of the C41 strain are critical for success.

### Example 2

Direct production of soluble IgA1 protease was achieved using E. coli C41(DE3) harboring pET-IGAN. Fermentation was performed in a 10 L bioreactor at 37°C, inducing with 0.4 mM IPTG at OD600 0.6 and shifting to 20°C for 24 hours. Cells were harvested by centrifugation, resuspended in TBS, and lysed by high-pressure homogenization. The lysate was clarified by centrifugation and filtration. Soluble IgA1 protease was purified sequentially: first by nickel-affinity chromatography (elution with imidazole gradient), then by anion-exchange chromatography on Q Sepharose (collecting flow-through), and finally by size-exclusion chromatography on S300. This process yielded >95% pure, active enzyme at 35 mg/L, confirming the scalability and efficiency of the method.

### Example 3

To maximize yield, IgA1 protease was expressed as inclusion bodies in E. coli C41(DE3) with pET-IGAN. After induction, cells were lysed, and inclusion bodies were isolated by centrifugation, washed with Triton X-100, and solubilized in 8 M urea. Refolding was optimized by screening buffers: Tris, CHES, MES, and PBS, all with 0.5 M arginine. Refolding at 10°C by slow addition of solubilized protein to refolding buffer yielded the highest activity. The refolded protein was purified by nickel column, dialyzed, and further purified by size-exclusion chromatography. Activity was assayed using an Experion system, measuring IgA1 cleavage rate. The refolded protease showed specific activity of 46 ng/μL/min/ng, surpassing the native H. influenzae enzyme (27 ng/μL/min/ng). From 1 L culture, ~1-2 g of inclusion bodies were obtained, yielding 1-2 g/L of soluble, active protease after refolding, demonstrating industrial-scale feasibility.

### Example 4

A large-scale process was developed for GMP production. E. coli C41(DE3) with pET-IGAN was fermented in 100 L, induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at 20°C for 24 hours. Cells were harvested, stored at -80°C, then lysed by homogenization. Inclusion bodies were isolated by centrifugation, washed with 2% Triton X-100, and solubilized in 8 M urea. The solubilized protein was adjusted to 0.1 mg/mL and refolded by slow addition into Tris buffer with arginine. The refolded solution was ultrafiltrated/diafiltrated, loaded onto a nickel column, washed, and eluted. The eluate was diluted and loaded onto Q Sepharose; the flow-through was concentrated and purified on S300. Automation using an AKTAcrossflow system streamlined washing and refolding. Yields were 100-fold higher than previous methods, enabling therapeutic applications. The high yield is attributed to the truncated construct, optimized host strain, and efficient refolding/purification, providing a robust platform for IgA1 protease therapeutics.
