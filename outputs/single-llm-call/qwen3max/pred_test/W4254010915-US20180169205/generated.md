# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and vaccine development, specifically to novel vaccine platforms capable of eliciting T cell-independent (TI) humoral immune responses. More particularly, the invention provides engineered hybrid flagellin proteins that function as polymeric scaffolds for the presentation of heterologous antigens in a repetitive, high-density array suitable for robust B cell receptor (BCR) crosslinking and activation without the need for cognate T cell help. The invention further encompasses compositions, methods of manufacture, and therapeutic uses of such hybrid flagellin-based vaccines, especially in contexts where conventional T cell-dependent (TD) immune responses are compromised or undesirable.

### Related Art

The adaptive immune response is traditionally understood to rely on coordinated interactions between antigen-specific T helper cells and B lymphocytes, leading to B cell activation, isotype switching, affinity maturation, and the generation of long-lived plasma cells and memory B cells. This T cell-dependent (TD) pathway is essential for high-affinity, class-switched antibody responses against most protein antigens. However, in clinical settings characterized by T cell deficiency—such as human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)/acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), congenital immunodeficiencies like DiGeorge syndrome, chemotherapy-induced lymphopenia, or age-related immunosenescence—the efficacy of TD vaccines is severely diminished.

Fortunately, a subset of B lymphocytes known as “innate-like” B cells, including marginal zone (MZ) B cells in the spleen and B-1 B cells in the peritoneal and pleural cavities, can mount rapid antibody responses independent of T cell help. These T cell-independent (TI) responses are triggered when BCRs recognize highly repetitive or polymeric epitopes that induce spatial clustering of surface immunoglobulins. It has been established that effective TI activation requires the crosslinking of at least 10–20 BCRs, which necessitates antigens with repetitive structures spaced approximately 5–10 nanometers apart and extending over a physical length of at least 500 nanometers to overcome the natural spacing of BCRs (~35 nm) on resting B cells.

Bacterial capsular polysaccharides have long served as prototypical TI antigens and form the basis of several licensed vaccines (e.g., pneumococcal and meningococcal polysaccharide vaccines). However, these vaccines often suffer from limited immunogenicity in infants and fail to induce immunological memory. Moreover, many protein-based vaccine candidates cannot naturally adopt the polymeric architecture required for efficient BCR clustering.

Bacterial flagellin, the structural protein of the flagellar filament, presents a promising scaffold for TI vaccine design. Flagellin from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium (FliC) self-assembles into helical filaments up to 15 micrometers in length, far exceeding the 500-nm threshold for TI activation. FliC comprises four structural domains (D0, D1, D2, D3), with D0 and D1 forming the inner core responsible for polymerization and containing binding sites for innate immune receptors Toll-like receptor 5 (TLR5) and NOD-like receptor CARD domain-containing protein 4 (NLRC4). The D3 domain, located on the solvent-exposed outer surface of the filament, is dispensable for filament stability and exhibits high conformational flexibility. Structural studies indicate that D3 domains are spaced approximately 5 nm apart along the filament axis—ideal for BCR crosslinking.

Despite its polymeric nature, prior studies suggested that flagellin-induced antibody responses were T cell-dependent, though these analyses omitted key TI-associated isotypes such as IgM and IgG3. Furthermore, while flagellin has been explored as a molecular adjuvant due to its TLR5 agonism, its potential as a structural scaffold for displaying foreign antigens in a TI context remained unexplored. Notably, bacterial flagellin itself—particularly from commensal organisms—can act as a natural TI antigen, suggesting untapped utility in vaccine engineering.

One candidate for such engineering is the envelope (E) glycoprotein of Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2), a major target of neutralizing antibodies. DENV E mediates viral entry and is organized as dimers on the virion surface. However, recombinant subunit DENV vaccines have shown limited efficacy, partly due to poor induction of balanced immunity across all four serotypes and risks of antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) from subneutralizing cross-reactive antibodies. A vaccine platform that presents DENV E in a highly ordered, polymeric array could potentially overcome these limitations by driving potent, TI-mediated neutralizing responses without requiring T cell help.

Thus, there exists a critical need for a versatile, self-assembling protein scaffold that can display conformationally intact, heterologous antigens in a repetitive, high-avidity format capable of inducing robust TI antibody responses—particularly in immunocompromised populations. The present invention fulfills this need through the rational design of hybrid flagellin proteins wherein the native D3 domain is replaced with a full-length viral antigen, enabling the creation of immunogenic filaments that retain both structural integrity and antigenic fidelity.

## SUMMARY

The present invention is motivated by the urgent need for vaccine platforms that can elicit protective antibody responses in individuals with impaired T cell function. Conventional vaccines relying on T cell help are often ineffective in such populations, leaving them vulnerable to infectious diseases. The invention addresses this gap by providing a method for inducing a T cell-independent (TI) humoral immune response through the administration of engineered hybrid flagellin proteins that self-assemble into polymeric filaments displaying repetitive arrays of heterologous antigens.

In one aspect, the invention provides a hybrid flagellin protein comprising a modified FliC backbone wherein the native D3 domain (residues 185–285 of *Salmonella* FliC) is deleted and replaced with a heterologous antigenic polypeptide, such as the envelope (E) glycoprotein of Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2 E). Flexible glycine-serine (GS) linkers flank the inserted antigen to preserve its conformational independence and ensure proper folding. The resulting hybrid protein retains the ability to polymerize into filaments under physiological conditions, presenting the foreign antigen in a highly repetitive, surface-exposed configuration ideal for BCR crosslinking.

The method of the invention involves administering to a subject an effective amount of the hybrid flagellin protein, either in monomeric or, preferably, polymeric form. The polymeric filaments are formed by incubating the purified hybrid protein in high-molarity salts such as ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate, conditions known to promote FliC polymerization. Upon administration—via routes including intranasal (IN), intraperitoneal (IP), or other mucosal or systemic pathways—the filaments engage antigen-specific B cells, triggering TI activation, proliferation, and differentiation into antibody-secreting plasma cells.

In another aspect, the invention encompasses a vaccine composition comprising the hybrid flagellin protein in association with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition may be formulated with or without additional adjuvants, as the flagellin scaffold itself provides intrinsic adjuvant activity via TLR5 and NLRC4 signaling. The vaccine is particularly suited for use in T cell-deficient subjects but is also effective in immunocompetent individuals, where it can elicit mixed TI/TD responses.

Further, the invention includes a nucleic acid molecule encoding the hybrid flagellin protein, which may be used for recombinant expression in eukaryotic (e.g., baculovirus/insect cell) or prokaryotic systems. The nucleic acid construct typically includes sequences encoding the N-terminal (residues 1–184) and C-terminal (residues 285–494) portions of FliC, flanking the inserted antigen and optionally a C-terminal purification tag (e.g., His6).

Collectively, the invention provides a modular, scalable platform for developing TI vaccines against a wide range of pathogens by substituting the D3 domain of FliC with antigens from viruses, bacteria, parasites, or tumor-associated molecules. The approach is exemplified herein with DENV2 E, but is broadly applicable to any conformationally stable polypeptide domain amenable to surface display.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a hybrid protein composition based on bacterial flagellin, engineered to serve as a self-assembling, polymeric vaccine scaffold. The core component is a modified FliC protein derived from *Salmonella enterica* serovar Typhimurium, in which the native D3 domain is replaced with a heterologous antigenic polypeptide. This design leverages the natural polymerization capacity of FliC while converting its surface into a dense array of foreign epitopes.

The heterologous antigen may be any immunogenic polypeptide capable of eliciting a protective antibody response. Examples include viral envelope proteins (e.g., influenza hemagglutinin, HIV gp120, SARS-CoV-2 spike), bacterial surface adhesins, parasitic antigens (e.g., *Plasmodium* circumsporozoite protein), or tumor-associated antigens (e.g., HER2/neu extracellular domain). In a preferred embodiment, the antigen is the full-length ectodomain of Dengue virus serotype 2 envelope glycoprotein (DENV2 E), which mediates viral attachment and fusion and is a primary target of neutralizing antibodies.

The pathogen or infectious agent targeted by the vaccine may be any microorganism against which a humoral immune response is protective. This includes enveloped and non-enveloped viruses, Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria, fungi, and protozoa. The invention is particularly advantageous for pathogens that exhibit antigenic variation or require high-avidity antibodies for neutralization, as the polymeric display enhances BCR engagement and may favor the selection of broadly reactive clones.

The FliC flagellin protein used as the scaffold is well-characterized structurally and functionally. It consists of 494 amino acids organized into four domains: D0 and D1 form the conserved, helical inner core responsible for filament assembly and innate immune recognition; D2 is a hypervariable region involved in serotype specificity; and D3 is a solvent-exposed, globular domain that is non-essential for polymerization. Deletion of D3 (residues 185–285) creates a permissive site for antigen insertion without disrupting filament formation.

Multiple hybrid flagellin proteins may be co-expressed and co-polymerized to create multivalent or mosaic filaments. For example, four distinct hFliC proteins, each displaying the E protein from one of the four Dengue serotypes, could be mixed in stoichiometric ratios to produce a tetravalent filament that induces balanced, broadly neutralizing antibodies—addressing a key challenge in dengue vaccine development.

Administration of the hybrid protein is performed in a therapeutically effective amount to a subject in need of immunization. Subjects include humans and non-human animals, particularly those at risk for infection or exhibiting T cell deficiencies. The protein may be administered as a monomer, but superior immunogenicity is achieved in its polymeric filament form, which mimics the natural repetitive architecture of pathogens.

The immune response elicited includes the production of antigen-specific antibodies, predominantly of the IgM, IgG3, and IgA isotypes—hallmarks of TI activation. In immunocompetent hosts, additional isotypes (e.g., IgG1, IgG2a) may also be induced via concurrent TD pathways. Critically, the vaccine induces functional antibodies capable of neutralizing pathogens or opsonizing microbes, as demonstrated by in vitro and in vivo assays.

The vaccine composition comprises the hybrid flagellin protein in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), saline, or buffered aqueous solutions. The composition is sterile, pyrogen-free, and suitable for parenteral, mucosal, or transdermal delivery. No external adjuvant is required, though optional adjuvants (e.g., aluminum salts, saponins, TLR agonists) may be included to modulate the response.

Administration routes include intranasal (IN), intraperitoneal (IP), subcutaneous (SC), intramuscular (IM), oral, or intradermal injection. IN delivery favors mucosal IgA responses, while IP or SC routes enhance systemic IgM and IgG. The choice of route depends on the desired immune compartment and pathogen tropism.

The pharmaceutically acceptable carrier ensures protein stability, solubility, and biocompatibility. It may include buffers (e.g., Tris, HEPES), stabilizers (e.g., sucrose, trehalose), surfactants (e.g., polysorbate 80), and preservatives. Lyophilized formulations are also contemplated for long-term storage.

Nucleic acid molecules encoding the hybrid protein are prepared by standard recombinant DNA techniques. The gene is assembled by overlapping PCR or Gibson assembly, inserting the antigen sequence between FliC fragments lacking D3, with GS linkers and a C-terminal His-tag. The construct is cloned into an expression vector (e.g., pENTR, pFastBac) and expressed in host cells such as SF9 insect cells using the baculovirus system, which supports proper folding and post-translational modifications.

### Example 1

The hFliC protein was designed by replacing the D3 domain of native FliC (residues 185–285) with the full-length ectodomain of Dengue virus serotype 2 envelope glycoprotein (DENV2 E), flanked by flexible GGGSGGGS linkers at both termini to ensure conformational independence. A C-terminal His6-tag was added for purification. The gene construct was assembled by first PCR-amplifying the N-terminal FliC fragment (residues 1–184) with a 5′ BamHI site and a 3′ KpnI/EcoRI site, and the C-terminal FliC fragment (residues 285–494 plus His-tag) with a 5′ KpnI/EcoRI site and a 3′ XhoI site. The DENV2 E sequence was PCR-amplified from a mouse-adapted strain and ligated into the KpnI/EcoRI sites between the FliC fragments. The final construct was verified by sequencing and cloned into the pENTR plasmid.

Protein production utilized the baculovirus expression system. The pENTR construct was recombined into a baculovirus transfer vector and transfected into SF21 insect cells. High-titer viral stocks were generated and used to infect SF9 cells cultured in serum-free media. The hFliC protein, retaining the native FliC secretion signal, was secreted into the supernatant and harvested after 72 hours. Protein purification involved ammonium sulfate precipitation, dialysis, and immobilized metal affinity chromatography (IMAC) using cobalt resin to capture the His-tagged protein. Eluted protein was dialyzed again to remove imidazole and concentrated.

Western blot analysis using an anti-His primary antibody confirmed the presence of a band at the expected molecular weight (~85 kDa), though a minor doublet suggested partial N-terminal proteolysis. Coomassie staining indicated ~15% purity, with degradation products visible. Circular dichroism (CD) spectroscopy revealed that hFliC exhibited a hybrid secondary structure: the FliC portion contributed α-helical content, while DENV2 E contributed β-sheet character, consistent with their native folds.

Negative stain transmission electron microscopy (TEM) showed that hFliC polymerized into filaments in 1.5 M ammonium sulfate, with a diameter of ~35 nm—significantly larger than wild-type FliC filaments (~20 nm)—consistent with computational modeling that placed DENV2 E on the filament surface. ELISA using a DENV2-specific monoclonal IgA antibody confirmed that the inserted E protein retained antigenicity, as hFliC bound the antibody while wild-type FliC did not.

A recombinant DENV2-specific IgA was produced by cloning the variable regions from the 4G2 hybridoma into expression vectors containing IgA constant regions and a kappa light chain. Stable CHO cell lines were generated, and IgA was purified from culture supernatants using protein A/G chromatography. Immunogold labeling of hFliC filaments with DENV2-specific IgG followed by gold-conjugated secondary antibodies confirmed that the E protein was displayed on the filament exterior.

In vitro, hFliC filaments induced BCR clustering in 4G2 B cell hybridoma cells, as visualized by confocal microscopy showing reorganization of surface Ig around the cell periphery upon stimulation. For in vivo studies, C57BL/6J mice were immunized with either monomeric or polymeric hFliC via IP or IN routes, followed by three IN boosts. Serum antibody titers were measured by ELISA using recombinant DENV2 E as capture antigen. Statistical analysis employed Student’s t-tests with significance set at p<0.05. Mice were maintained under SPF conditions in accordance with IACUC guidelines. Protein samples were prepared in PBS, and intranasal injections delivered 10 µg per nostril, while IP injections used 20 µg in 200 µl. Humane sacrifice was performed by anesthesia followed by cervical dislocation. Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism.

### Example 2

The FliC D3 domain was validated as an optimal site for insertion of independent conformational domains. Native FliC was engineered by precisely deleting residues 185–285 and replacing them with the DENV2 E ectodomain, yielding the hybrid flagellin protein hFliC (FIG. 1A). The protein was expressed in SF9 insect cells using the baculovirus system and purified via His-tag affinity chromatography (FIG. 1B). Coomassie staining and Western blot confirmed the expected molecular weight (~85 kDa) and identity, though purity was moderate due to proteolytic fragments.

Circular dichroism analysis (FIG. 1C) demonstrated that hFliC retained significant secondary structure from both parental proteins: α-helical content from FliC and β-sheet from DENV2 E, indicating proper folding. The hybrid protein was induced to polymerize in high-salt conditions (1.5 M ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate), forming filaments that were analyzed by negative stain TEM (FIG. 2A). Filament diameter increased to ~35 nm versus ~20 nm for wild-type FliC, matching predictions from molecular modeling of hFliC filaments.

Binding ELISA (FIG. 2B) using a DENV2-specific monoclonal IgA confirmed that the E domain in hFliC was antigenically intact and accessible. Immunogold labeling with DENV2-specific IgG (from 4G2 hybridoma) followed by 10-nm gold particles showed specific binding to the outer surface of hFliC filaments, but not wild-type FliC (FIG. 2C), proving surface exposure of the inserted antigen.

In wild-type C57BL/6J mice (FIG. 3), immunization with polymeric hFliC induced robust humoral responses: IN priming favored IgA, while IP priming enhanced IgM and IgG (total and subclasses IgG1, IgG2a, IgG2b). Monomeric hFliC was significantly less immunogenic. In vitro, hFliC filaments crosslinked BCRs on 4G2 hybridoma cells, as shown by confocal imaging of surface Ig redistribution (FIG. 4).

Critically, in a T cell-restricted model (6.5-TCR transgenic mice on B10.D2 background), polymeric hFliC administered IP induced significant IgM and IgG3 responses—classic TI isotypes—despite the absence of cognate T cell help (FIG. 5). Control B10.D2 mice mounted broader responses, confirming the TI nature of the hFliC-induced immunity in transgenic animals.

Additional characterization (FIGS. 6–8) confirmed the production of recombinant FliC and DENV2 E controls, detailed filament morphology by TEM, and successful generation of recombinant DENV2-specific IgA. The hFliC filaments formed reliably in ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate, exhibited correct morphology, and displayed DENV2 E epitopes on their surface, as verified by antibody binding.

Immunogenicity studies comparing monomeric and polymeric hFliC in wild-type mice confirmed the superiority of the polymeric form for inducing IgG, IgM, and IgA. In the 6.5-TCR model, only polymeric hFliC induced measurable IgM and IgG3, underscoring its TI capacity. The data support hFliC filaments as a potent vaccine framework, particularly advantageous for recruiting B-1 B cells—abundant in the peritoneum—and overcoming limitations of current dengue vaccines, such as imbalanced serotype responses in live attenuated tetravalent formulations or weak immunogenicity of subunit vaccines. This platform offers a path toward a universal dengue vaccine by co-displaying all four serotype E proteins on mixed filaments.

## CONCLUSIONS

The present invention demonstrates that hybrid protein filaments, engineered by replacing the D3 domain of bacterial flagellin with a heterologous antigen such as Dengue virus envelope protein, constitute a novel and effective vaccine scaffold. These filaments self-assemble into polymeric structures that present antigens in a repetitive, high-density array capable of inducing robust T cell-independent humoral immune responses. The platform is particularly valuable for immunizing individuals with compromised T cell function and offers a modular strategy for developing vaccines against diverse pathogens.
