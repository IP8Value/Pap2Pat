# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global prevalence of obesity and its associated metabolic complications has reached epidemic proportions, with over 70% of adults in the United States classified as overweight or obese. This dramatic increase in adiposity has been paralleled by a surge in type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), a condition in which more than 85% of patients are also obese. The pathophysiological link between obesity and T2DM is primarily mediated through insulin resistance—a state in which insulin-responsive tissues, including skeletal muscle, liver, and adipose tissue, fail to respond adequately to circulating insulin. Central to this dysfunction is the limited capacity of white adipose tissue to safely store excess energy in the form of triglycerides. When adipocytes reach their lipid storage threshold, they undergo hypertrophy, a process that compromises their metabolic flexibility and promotes systemic lipotoxicity through ectopic fat deposition in non-adipose organs such as the liver, pancreas, and skeletal muscle. This overflow of lipids into metabolically sensitive tissues directly impairs insulin signaling and contributes to the development of insulin resistance. In contrast, a subset of individuals—termed “metabolically healthy obese”—exhibit preserved insulin sensitivity due to their ability to expand adipose tissue through hyperplasia, the generation of new, smaller adipocytes from precursor cells. These newly formed adipocytes retain high insulin sensitivity and efficient lipid-handling capacity, thereby mitigating the adverse metabolic consequences of caloric excess. Consequently, therapeutic strategies that promote adipocyte hyperplasia rather than hypertrophy represent a promising avenue for treating insulin resistance and related metabolic disorders. However, the molecular mechanisms governing adipocyte proliferation, particularly in mature, differentiated adipocytes, remain poorly understood. While preadipocyte differentiation is a well-characterized process involving mitotic clonal expansion and transcriptional activation of adipogenic genes, recent evidence suggests that fully differentiated adipocytes may retain the capacity to re-enter the cell cycle under certain conditions. This potential plasticity challenges the long-held view that mature adipocytes are terminally differentiated and post-mitotic. Identifying regulators of this proliferative capacity could unlock novel therapeutic targets for metabolic disease. Among candidate regulators is the monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1), a proton-coupled membrane transporter responsible for the bidirectional flux of lactate, pyruvate, and other monocarboxylates. MCT1 is highly expressed in proliferating cells and is transcriptionally upregulated by the oncogene c-Myc. In adipose tissue, MCT1 expression increases during differentiation and is implicated in lactate shuttling, redox homeostasis, and mitochondrial biogenesis. Given that lactate metabolism is tightly linked to cellular redox state and that redox signaling influences cell cycle progression, MCT1 emerges as a plausible modulator of adipocyte proliferation. Despite these associations, the functional role of MCT1 in regulating adipocyte cell cycle dynamics and its potential as a target for inducing hyperplasia have not been previously explored. Thus, there exists a critical need to elucidate the role of MCT1 in adipocyte biology and to determine whether its inhibition can promote adipocyte proliferation, enhance insulin sensitivity, and improve metabolic health in the context of obesity and insulin resistance.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a method for treating insulin resistance and related metabolic disorders by administering a monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) inhibitor to a subject in need thereof. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that pharmacological or genetic inhibition of MCT1 in mature, differentiated adipocytes induces their re-entry into the cell cycle, leading to adipocyte proliferation and hyperplasia. This proliferative response is accompanied by a reduction in intracellular lipid content, enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake, and increased triglyceride storage capacity upon subsequent insulin stimulation. The invention further demonstrates that MCT1 inhibition does not cause adipocyte de-differentiation or cytotoxicity but instead promotes a transcriptional program characterized by the upregulation of cell cycle-promoting genes, including CDK1, PLK1, TOP2A, and Cyclin B. Critically, the enhancement of insulin sensitivity following MCT1 inhibition is dependent on CDK1 activity, as co-administration of a CDK1 inhibitor abrogates the improvement in glucose uptake. The invention encompasses the use of specific MCT1 inhibitors, including but not limited to AZD3965, AR-C155858, α-cyano-4-hydroxycinnamic acid (α-CCA), and radiolabeled derivatives such as [F18]FACH, for the treatment of insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes mellitus, obesity, metabolic syndrome, and other conditions associated with impaired adipose tissue expandability. The method may be applied therapeutically to improve metabolic health in subjects exhibiting adipocyte hypertrophy or prophylactically in at-risk individuals to prevent the onset of insulin resistance. By promoting adipocyte hyperplasia, the invention offers a novel mechanism to redistribute lipid burden across a greater number of smaller, insulin-sensitive adipocytes, thereby reducing ectopic fat deposition and systemic metabolic dysfunction.

### EXAMPLES

Obesity and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) represent a global health crisis, with epidemiological data confirming that obesity is the leading risk factor for T2DM. The molecular basis of this association lies in adipose tissue dysfunction, where the inability to expand via hyperplasia forces lipid storage into existing adipocytes, causing hypertrophy. Hypertrophic adipocytes exhibit impaired insulin signaling, reduced glucose uptake, and increased inflammation, contributing to systemic insulin resistance. In contrast, hyperplasia—mediated by the recruitment and differentiation of preadipocytes or the proliferation of mature adipocytes—maintains metabolic health. Paracrine growth factors regulate preadipocyte proliferation, but recent evidence suggests mature adipocytes may also re-enter the cell cycle. This process involves de-differentiation, proliferation, and re-differentiation, or direct mitotic division. Monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) is a key lactate transporter upregulated during adipocyte differentiation and in proliferating cells. Lactate metabolism influences redox homeostasis via the NAD+/NADH ratio, which in turn regulates cell cycle progression and mitochondrial biogenesis. We hypothesized that inhibiting MCT1 would alter lactate flux, induce redox stress, and trigger adipocyte proliferation. To test this, we used the 3T3-L1 murine adipocyte model. Differentiation of 3T3-L1 preadipocytes in the presence of the MCT1 inhibitor AZD3965 (1 µM) significantly reduced lipid accumulation, as shown by Oil Red O staining. Similarly, MCT1 knockdown via shRNA (3T3-L1-shMCT1) impaired differentiation. Adipogenic gene expression—PPARγ, FABP4, and HSL—was suppressed at both mRNA and protein levels, confirming that MCT1 activity is required for full differentiation. In mature adipocytes, AZD3965 treatment reduced lipid content without inducing cytotoxicity, as confirmed by viability assays. This reduction was attributed to increased lipolysis (elevated intracellular glycerol) and modestly reduced NADP+/NADPH bioavailability, rather than de-differentiation, as adipokine secretion remained unchanged and PPARγ protein levels were stable. Transcriptomic analysis via RNA-seq revealed 277 differentially expressed genes after 24 h of AZD3965 treatment, with pathway analysis (IPA) highlighting upregulation of cell cycle and mitosis genes (CDK1, PLK1, TOP2A) and downregulation of cell cycle inhibitors (DUSP1). RT-qPCR validated these changes. CDK1, a master regulator of mitosis, showed decreased inhibitory phosphorylation at Tyr15, indicating activation. Proliferation assays demonstrated increased cell numbers in mature adipocytes—but not preadipocytes—following AZD3965 treatment. Confocal microscopy confirmed Ki67 expression in lipid-laden cells, proving mature adipocytes re-enter the cell cycle. Functionally, MCT1 inhibition enhanced insulin-stimulated glucose uptake in a CDK1-dependent manner, as co-treatment with RO-3306 abolished this effect. 3T3-L1-shMCT1 cells exhibited blunted insulin responsiveness, suggesting chronic MCT1 loss impairs adipocyte maturation, whereas acute inhibition enhances function. Finally, AZD3965-pretreated adipocytes showed increased triglyceride storage capacity upon insulin stimulation, supporting hyperplasia. Experimental techniques included standard cell culture, lentiviral shRNA knockdown, Oil Red O staining, RT-qPCR with validated primers, immunoblotting with RIPA or HES lysis buffers, and specialized assays for lactate, pyruvate, NAD+/NADH, NADP+/NADPH, glycerol, adipokines, and glucose uptake. RNA-seq was performed on Illumina NovaSeq, with IPA for pathway analysis. Confocal imaging used Ki67/BODIPY/DAPI co-staining. All protocols followed established methods, with statistical significance assessed by t-test or ANOVA (p < 0.05). These findings collectively demonstrate that MCT1 inhibition induces adipocyte hyperplasia, offering a novel therapeutic strategy for insulin resistance.
