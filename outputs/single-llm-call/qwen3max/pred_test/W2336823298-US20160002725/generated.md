# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology, specifically to methods and compositions for the prediction, diagnosis, and treatment of Alzheimer’s disease (AD). More particularly, the invention provides novel uses of Transient Receptor Potential Canonical 6 (TRPC6) gene products—both nucleic acids and proteins—as biomarkers for early detection of AD and as therapeutic agents capable of modulating amyloid-beta (Aβ) production. The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits, pharmaceutical compositions, and screening platforms based on TRPC6 expression or activity, offering a targeted approach to AD intervention that avoids the adverse effects associated with broad-spectrum γ-secretase inhibitors.

## BACKGROUND

Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder and the most common cause of dementia in the elderly. It is characterized clinically by memory loss, cognitive decline, behavioral disturbances, and eventual loss of independent function. Pathologically, AD is defined by the accumulation of extracellular amyloid plaques composed primarily of amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles formed by hyperphosphorylated tau protein. These pathological hallmarks lead to synaptic dysfunction, neuronal loss, and brain atrophy, particularly in the hippocampus and cerebral cortex.

The clinical manifestations of AD typically begin insidiously with mild forgetfulness and gradually progress to severe impairment in language, judgment, visuospatial skills, and activities of daily living. Early symptoms often go unrecognized or are misattributed to normal aging, delaying diagnosis until significant neurodegeneration has already occurred. This late-stage detection severely limits the potential efficacy of therapeutic interventions, which are most likely to succeed when administered during the preclinical or early symptomatic phases of the disease.

The dominant mechanistic framework for understanding AD pathogenesis is the Amyloid Hypothesis, which posits that the accumulation of Aβ peptides—particularly the longer, more aggregation-prone Aβ42—is the primary initiating event in a cascade that ultimately leads to neurodegeneration and dementia. According to this hypothesis, Aβ is generated through the sequential proteolytic cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein (APP) by β-secretase (BACE1) and then γ-secretase. The γ-secretase complex, composed of presenilin (PS1 or PS2), nicastrin, PEN-2, and APH-1, cleaves the C99 fragment of APP to release Aβ peptides of varying lengths.

Despite decades of research, current diagnostic methods for AD remain suboptimal. Definitive diagnosis still requires postmortem neuropathological examination. In living patients, diagnosis relies on clinical evaluation, neuropsychological testing (e.g., Mini-Mental State Examination, MMSE), structural and functional neuroimaging (MRI, PET), and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analysis for Aβ42 and tau levels. However, CSF collection is invasive, imaging is expensive and not widely accessible, and cognitive tests lack specificity, especially in early stages. There is an urgent need for a reliable, non-invasive, and cost-effective biomarker that can predict or diagnose AD before significant cognitive decline occurs.

This need is further underscored by the repeated failures of clinical trials targeting Aβ. Numerous therapeutic strategies, including γ-secretase inhibitors, have been developed to reduce Aβ production. However, these compounds often produce severe side effects because γ-secretase also cleaves other essential substrates, most notably Notch, which is critical for cell differentiation, immune function, and gastrointestinal homeostasis. Inhibition of Notch signaling leads to toxicities such as goblet cell metaplasia and impaired lymphocyte development, which halted the development of many γ-secretase inhibitors. Thus, there exists a critical unmet need for agents that can selectively reduce Aβ production without disrupting the processing of other γ-secretase substrates.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel biomarker for the prediction or diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease based on the expression level of TRPC6 mRNA in peripheral blood cells. In one aspect, the invention offers a method for aiding in the diagnosis or risk assessment of AD by detecting and quantifying TRPC6 mRNA in a biological sample from a subject, preferably peripheral blood mononuclear cells, and comparing the measured level to a reference value derived from healthy controls. Reduced TRPC6 mRNA expression is indicative of increased AD risk or presence of the disease.

In another aspect, the invention provides a therapeutic agent for the treatment or prevention of Alzheimer’s disease comprising TRPC6 protein, active fragments thereof, or derivatives that retain the ability to interact with APP and inhibit its cleavage by γ-secretase. Specifically, the invention identifies a transmembrane domain (TM2, residues 487–507 of human TRPC6) as the core functional region responsible for Aβ reduction. A cell-penetrating fusion peptide, TAT-TM2, is disclosed as a potent and selective inhibitor of Aβ production.

The invention further encompasses a diagnostic kit for AD prediction or diagnosis. The kit includes reagents necessary for extracting total RNA from peripheral blood cells, primers specific for reverse transcription of TRPC6 mRNA, a reverse transcriptase enzyme, a DNA polymerase for PCR amplification, and instructions for performing real-time fluorogenic quantitative PCR (qPCR) to determine TRPC6 mRNA levels. Optionally, the kit may include a nucleic acid chip or microarray containing probes for TRPC6 and control genes.

Additionally, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a TRPC6 polypeptide, an active fragment (such as TM2 or TAT-TM2), or a TRPC6 agonist, formulated with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. This composition is intended for administration to a subject to reduce Aβ levels, prevent amyloid plaque formation, and ameliorate cognitive deficits associated with AD. The detection method involves isolating mRNA from peripheral blood cells, converting it to cDNA, and quantifying TRPC6 transcript levels via qPCR, with results interpreted relative to a normal control cohort.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is founded on the discovery that TRPC6 mRNA levels in peripheral blood cells are significantly reduced in individuals with Alzheimer’s disease, establishing TRPC6 as a peripheral biomarker for AD. This correlation enables non-invasive early diagnosis, as blood sampling is routine and scalable. TRPC6 is a member of the transient receptor potential canonical (TRPC) family of non-selective cation channels, but surprisingly, its role in modulating Aβ production is independent of its ion channel function. Instead, TRPC6 acts as a selective endogenous modulator of γ-secretase activity by directly interacting with the C99 fragment of APP, thereby preventing its cleavage into Aβ peptides.

Structurally, TRPC6 is a multi-pass transmembrane protein with six transmembrane domains (S1–S6), intracellular N- and C-termini, and conserved motifs characteristic of the TRP channel superfamily, including N-terminal anchor motifs involved in membrane localization. Despite this canonical architecture, the invention reveals that the second transmembrane domain (TM2, spanning approximately amino acids 487–507 in human TRPC6) is both necessary and sufficient for Aβ reduction. Mutations within this domain abolish TRPC6’s ability to bind APP and suppress Aβ generation, without altering subcellular localization.

The invention defines “TRPC6 fragments, derivatives, and analogs” as any polypeptide comprising at least the TM2 domain (residues 437–507 or, more preferably, 487–507) that retains the capacity to interact with APP and inhibit γ-secretase-mediated cleavage. The preferred polypeptide of the invention is the 21-amino acid TM2 peptide fused to the HIV TAT protein transduction domain (TAT-TM2), which facilitates cellular uptake and demonstrates potent Aβ-lowering effects in vitro and in vivo.

The apolipoprotein E (APOE) gene, particularly the ε4 allele (APOE4), is the strongest genetic risk factor for sporadic AD. The invention further discloses that APOE genotype influences TRPC6 expression: carriers of the protective APOE3 allele exhibit higher TRPC6 mRNA levels in peripheral blood cells and neuronal cultures compared to APOE4 carriers. This finding links a major genetic risk factor to a modifiable molecular pathway, suggesting that TRPC6 upregulation may be a therapeutic strategy especially beneficial for APOE4-positive individuals.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise a TRPC6-derived polypeptide (e.g., TAT-TM2) or a small-molecule TRPC6 agonist, formulated for systemic or central nervous system delivery. Administration may be intravenous, intraperitoneal, or intranasal, using carriers that enhance blood-brain barrier penetration. The compositions are designed to elevate functional TRPC6 activity in the brain, thereby reducing Aβ burden and slowing disease progression.

A medication screening method is also provided, wherein candidate compounds are tested for their ability to upregulate TRPC6 mRNA or protein expression in neuronal or blood cell lines. Hits are further validated for their capacity to reduce Aβ levels in cellular models (e.g., HEK293APP cells) or primary neurons, and for selectivity—ensuring no inhibition of Notch cleavage.

Key advantages of the invention include: (1) provision of an objective, quantifiable, and non-invasive biomarker for early AD diagnosis; (2) extension of the diagnostic window to pre-symptomatic stages; (3) identification of a specific molecular target (TRPC6 TM2 domain) for rational drug design; and (4) a mechanism that avoids the toxicities of pan-γ-secretase inhibitors by selectively targeting APP processing.

Methodologically, total RNA is extracted from peripheral blood cells using standard kits (e.g., TRIzol or column-based purification). Reverse transcription is performed with oligo(dT) or random primers and a reverse transcriptase enzyme. Real-time qPCR is conducted using TRPC6-specific primers and a fluorescent probe (e.g., TaqMan), with normalization to housekeeping genes (e.g., GAPDH, β-actin). Protein extraction from cells or brain tissues employs RIPA buffer, followed by Western blotting with anti-TRPC6 and anti-Aβ antibodies.

Transgenic mouse models were instrumental in validating the invention. APP/PS1 mice, which overexpress mutant human APP and presenilin-1, develop robust amyloid pathology. Crossing these with TRPC6-overexpressing mice (driven by a forebrain-specific promoter) resulted in significant reductions in amyloid plaque load, soluble and insoluble Aβ levels, and rescue of synaptic and cognitive deficits, as assessed by Golgi staining (spine density) and Morris water maze performance.

Immunohistochemistry confirmed reduced plaque deposition in APP/PS1/TRPC6 mice using antibodies 6E10 and MOAB-2, as well as thioflavin S staining. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments demonstrated direct physical interaction between TRPC6 and APP/C99, but not with PS1, Notch, or APLP2, confirming substrate selectivity. Luciferase assays and stable cell lines (HEK293APP, COS7-C99) were used to quantify secretase activities and Aβ production.

Example 1 demonstrates that TRPC6 mRNA levels are significantly lower in AD patients’ blood compared to controls, with ROC curve analysis showing high diagnostic accuracy. TRPC5 levels showed no difference, underscoring specificity. TRPC6 levels also correlated positively with MMSE scores and were reduced even in early-stage AD.

Example 2 establishes the mechanistic link: TRPC6 mRNA levels inversely correlate with Aβ in both cultured neurons and APP/PS1 mouse brains, supporting its role as a predictive biomarker.

Example 3 shows that TRPC6 overexpression reduces Aβ in HEK293APP cells and APP/PS1 mice, while TRPC6 knockdown increases Aβ in neurons, confirming its regulatory function.

Example 4 reveals that TRPC6 specifically inhibits γ-secretase cleavage of C99 (reducing AICD and Aβ) but not of Notch or cadherins, highlighting its selectivity.

Example 5 maps the active domain to residues 437–507, with 487–507 identified as the core inhibitory sequence.

Example 6 validates TAT-TM2 as a therapeutic peptide, showing Aβ reduction across cellular and animal models.

Example 7 identifies compound C as a TRPC6 expression enhancer, illustrating the screening platform.

Example 8 links APOE3 to higher TRPC6 expression, providing a genetic context for biomarker interpretation.

Example 9 confirms that TAT-TM2, like full-length TRPC6, selectively inhibits APP but not Notch cleavage.

In summary, the present invention establishes TRPC6 as a dual-purpose molecule: a peripheral mRNA biomarker for early AD diagnosis and a source of selective γ-secretase modulators for therapy, thereby addressing critical gaps in AD management.
