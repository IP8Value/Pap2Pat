## BACKGROUND

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention resides in the field of biotechnology and therapeutic medicine, specifically relating to the development and use of monoclonal antibodies that selectively target connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels without affecting gap junction communication. The invention further encompasses methods of treating traumatic spinal cord injury, neurodegenerative diseases, cancer, osteoporosis, inflammatory conditions, and osteoarthritis through the administration of such antibodies or pharmaceutical compositions containing them. The invention also includes diagnostic kits, expression vectors encoding the antibody components, and in vitro and in vivo methods for assessing the functional impact of these antibodies on hemichannel activity and disease progression.

### 2. Description of Related Art

Traumatic spinal cord injury (SCI) is a devastating neurological condition affecting tens of thousands of individuals annually worldwide. Following the initial mechanical trauma, a cascade of secondary pathological processes—including inflammation, excitotoxicity, oxidative stress, and glial scar formation—amplifies tissue damage and impedes functional recovery. Current therapeutic strategies remain limited in efficacy, with no widely approved pharmacological intervention capable of significantly halting secondary injury or promoting meaningful neurological restoration. Standard care often involves surgical decompression, corticosteroids (e.g., methylprednisolone, though controversial), and rehabilitation, but these approaches do not directly address the molecular mechanisms driving secondary degeneration.

Connexin proteins, particularly Cx43, are abundantly expressed in astrocytes of the central nervous system and form two distinct structures: gap junctions that facilitate direct intercellular communication between adjacent cells, and hemichannels that open to the extracellular space under pathological conditions. While gap junctions play essential roles in homeostasis and metabolic coupling, unregulated hemichannel opening during injury permits the efflux of ATP, glutamate, and other cytotoxic molecules, exacerbating inflammation and neuronal death. Prior research has implicated Cx43 in SCI pathology, but existing tools—such as antisense oligonucleotides, small-molecule inhibitors like carbenoxolone, or mimetic peptides—lack specificity, often disrupting both gap junctions and hemichannels or exhibiting off-target effects. This lack of selectivity limits therapeutic utility and may interfere with beneficial gap junction functions.

There exists a critical need for targeted therapies that selectively inhibit Cx43 hemichannels while preserving gap junction integrity. Such an approach could mitigate secondary injury without compromising intercellular communication vital for tissue repair. The development of a monoclonal antibody capable of discriminating between hemichannels and gap junctions represents a novel and highly specific strategy to address this unmet medical need, with potential applications extending beyond SCI to other conditions involving dysregulated Cx43 hemichannel activity, including cancer metastasis, osteoporosis, neurodegenerative disorders, and chronic inflammatory diseases.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method of treating cancer in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of an isolated antibody that specifically binds to a connexin hemichannel polypeptide, wherein the antibody inhibits hemichannel opening without substantially inhibiting gap junction function. The invention further provides a method of treating osteoporosis, comprising administering the same antibody to promote bone integrity and reduce osteoclast-mediated resorption. In one embodiment, the antibody is administered systemically or locally, and may be formulated as part of a pharmaceutical composition comprising a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.

The antibody may be administered via intravenous, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, intrathecal, or intracerebroventricular routes, depending on the target tissue and disease indication. The antibody composition may include a humanized monoclonal antibody comprising specific heavy chain variable (VH) and light chain variable (VL) regions, with defined complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) that confer high-affinity binding to the extracellular domain of Cx43. In certain embodiments, the VH and VL amino acid sequences are those derived from the MHC1 hybridoma, as disclosed herein. The treatment may be combined with additional anticancer therapies, such as chemotherapy, radiotherapy, immunotherapy, or surgery, to enhance therapeutic outcomes.

The invention further introduces a method of treating neurodegenerative diseases, including but not limited to amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, and multiple sclerosis, by administering the anti-Cx43 hemichannel antibody to reduce neuroinflammation and neuronal loss. Similarly, the antibody may be used to treat acute neurological injuries, such as traumatic brain injury or stroke, where Cx43 hemichannel activation contributes to secondary damage. The antibody composition may be humanized to reduce immunogenicity in human patients, and its VH and VL sequences are optimized for stability and binding affinity.

Additionally, the invention provides a method of treating inflammatory diseases, such as rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, or systemic lupus erythematosus, by inhibiting Cx43-mediated release of proinflammatory mediators. The pharmaceutical composition may be formulated for sustained release or targeted delivery to inflamed tissues. The invention also encompasses a method of promoting wound healing, particularly in chronic or non-healing wounds, by modulating hemichannel activity to reduce inflammation and support tissue regeneration.

The isolated antibody of the invention specifically binds to connexin hemichannel polypeptides, particularly Cx43, and does not cross-react with pannexin channels or other unrelated proteins. Nucleic acid molecules encoding the antibody heavy and light chains are provided, enabling recombinant production. The antibody binds to epitopes within the extracellular loops of Cx43, particularly the second extracellular domain (E2), and may be used in fragment forms such as Fab, scFv, or diabodies. The invention further includes in vitro methods for determining the effect of the antibody on hemichannel opening, using dye uptake assays, and in vivo methods for evaluating therapeutic efficacy in animal models of disease. The term “providing” as used herein includes manufacturing, formulating, and delivering the antibody for therapeutic or diagnostic use.

## DESCRIPTION

Connexin proteins are a family of transmembrane proteins that assemble into hexameric structures known as connexons or hemichannels. When two hemichannels from adjacent cells dock, they form gap junction channels that permit the direct exchange of ions, metabolites, and signaling molecules up to approximately 1 kDa in size. Connexin 43 (Cx43) is the predominant isoform in astrocytes of the central nervous system and plays dual roles in physiological and pathological contexts. Under normal conditions, Cx43 gap junctions support metabolic coupling and spatial buffering of potassium and neurotransmitters. However, during pathological states such as traumatic injury, ischemia, or inflammation, undocked Cx43 hemichannels can open aberrantly, allowing the uncontrolled release of ATP, glutamate, NAD+, and prostaglandins, which contribute to cytotoxicity, inflammation, and cell death.

In the context of spinal cord injury, Cx43 expression is markedly upregulated in reactive astrocytes surrounding the lesion site. This upregulation correlates with the expansion of secondary injury and the formation of a glial scar, which acts as a physical and chemical barrier to axonal regeneration. While the glial scar may initially contain damage, its persistence impedes recovery. The bone remodeling process, governed by the balance between osteoblast-mediated bone formation and osteoclast-driven resorption, is also influenced by Cx43 hemichannel activity in osteocytes, which sense mechanical load and regulate osteoclast differentiation via RANKL signaling. Bisphosphonates like alendronate are commonly used to treat osteoporosis by inhibiting osteoclast activity, but they suffer from limitations including gastrointestinal side effects, poor bioavailability, and risk of atypical femoral fractures with long-term use. These shortcomings highlight the need for alternative mechanisms to modulate bone metabolism, such as targeting Cx43 hemichannels in osteocytes.

### I. ANTIBODIES

An antibody is a glycoprotein comprising at least two heavy (H) chains and two light (L) chains interconnected by disulfide bonds. Each heavy and light chain contains a variable region responsible for antigen recognition and a constant region that determines effector functions. The variable regions of the heavy (VH) and light (VL) chains each contain three complementarity-determining regions (CDRs)—CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3—that form the antigen-binding site. The CDRs are embedded within framework regions that provide structural support. Variant CDRs may be generated through site-directed mutagenesis to improve affinity, specificity, or stability. Amino acid substitutions, insertions, or deletions within the CDRs or framework regions can yield functionally equivalent antibodies.

Antibody fragments include Fab (antigen-binding fragment), which consists of one light chain and the VH and CH1 domains of the heavy chain; F(ab′)2, which contains two Fab fragments linked by disulfide bonds; Fv, the minimal antigen-binding unit comprising only VH and VL; and single-chain Fv (scFv), where VH and VL are connected by a flexible peptide linker. Other formats include diabodies, minibodies, and dAb (domain antibody) fragments. Antibody-like binding peptidomimetics may also be engineered to mimic CDR loops.

Polyclonal antibodies are produced by immunizing an animal (e.g., rabbit, goat) with an antigen and collecting serum. Monoclonal antibodies are generated via hybridoma technology, wherein B cells from an immunized mouse are fused with immortal myeloma cells to produce stable clones secreting a single antibody species. Alternatively, plasma B cells can be isolated from human donors, and RNA is extracted for cDNA synthesis. The VH and VL genes are amplified by PCR and cloned into phage display vectors to construct Fab libraries. Cx43-specific binders are selected through panning against Cx43 extracellular domain peptides. Full-length IgG is then expressed in mammalian cells (e.g., HEK293F) and purified via protein A chromatography. Chimeric antibodies combine murine variable regions with human constant regions, while humanized antibodies graft only the CDRs onto human frameworks. Fully human antibodies can be derived from transgenic mice or phage display libraries. Conjugates of the antibody with payloads (e.g., toxins, radionuclides) may be prepared for targeted delivery.

### II. TREATMENT OF DISEASES

“Treatment” or “treating” refers to the administration of a therapy to ameliorate symptoms, delay progression, or achieve a therapeutic benefit in a subject or patient. A “therapeutically effective dosage” is the amount sufficient to produce a desired clinical response without undue toxicity. Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the antibody and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, which may include buffers, isotonic agents (e.g., sodium chloride, mannitol), stabilizers, and preservatives. Formulations may be solutions, microemulsions, liposomes, or sterile powders reconstituted prior to use. Dosage regimens are tailored based on disease severity, route of administration, and patient response, typically ranging from 0.1 to 100 mg/kg. Combination therapy may include chemotherapeutic agents (e.g., alkylating agents like cyclophosphamide, antimetabolites like methotrexate), radiotherapy, immunotherapy (e.g., checkpoint inhibitors, CAR-T cells), or surgery. Administration may be repeated in cycles, with monitoring for toxicity. Local application (e.g., intrathecal injection for SCI) enhances target engagement while minimizing systemic exposure.

### IV. EXAMPLES

Anti-Cx43 monoclonal antibody MHC1 was generated by immunizing mice with a Cx43 extracellular domain peptide, followed by hybridoma screening and cloning of VH/VL genes. The CDR sequences were identified and paired to produce a chimeric antibody with human constant regions. MHC1 localized to Cx43 on the cell surface and inhibited hemichannel opening in HeLa-Cx43 cells and primary astrocytes, as shown by reduced ethidium bromide uptake, without affecting gap junction dye transfer. In mouse models of partial SCI, a single intraperitoneal injection of MHC1 (25 mg/kg) within 30 minutes post-injury significantly improved hind limb function (Basso Mouse Scale), reduced glial scarring (GFAP staining), preserved neurons (MAP2+), and decreased lesion size. Similar benefits were observed in osteolytic tumor models, where MHC1 blocked tumor growth in bone. In osteoarthritis, Cx43 expression was elevated in chondrocytes, suggesting therapeutic potential. These results demonstrate that selective inhibition of Cx43 hemichannels represents a promising strategy for multiple diseases.
