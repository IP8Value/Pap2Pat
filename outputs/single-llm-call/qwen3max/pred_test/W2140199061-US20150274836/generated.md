# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to mesothelin, a cell surface glycoprotein overexpressed in a variety of human epithelial cancers. More particularly, the invention provides novel fully human monoclonal antibodies—designated SD1 and SD2—that recognize distinct epitopes on mesothelin, with SD1 exhibiting potent complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC). These antibodies, along with their antigen-binding fragments, immunoconjugates, fusion proteins, nucleic acids encoding them, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising them, are useful for the diagnosis, detection, and therapeutic treatment of mesothelin-positive malignancies, including mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, pancreatic cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, lung adenocarcinoma, and triple-negative breast cancer.

## BACKGROUND

Mesothelin has emerged as a compelling therapeutic target due to its restricted expression in normal tissues and high overexpression in multiple aggressive solid tumors. The mesothelin (MSLN) gene encodes a 71 kDa precursor protein that undergoes proteolytic cleavage to yield a 31 kDa N-terminal fragment (megakaryocyte-potentiating factor) and a 40 kDa C-terminal membrane-anchored mature mesothelin. This mature form is tethered to the plasma membrane via a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor and is abundantly expressed on the surface of malignant mesotheliomas, ovarian carcinomas, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinomas, cholangiocarcinomas, lung adenocarcinomas, and certain subtypes of breast cancer. Its limited normal tissue distribution—primarily confined to mesothelial cells lining the pleura, peritoneum, and pericardium—renders it an attractive candidate for targeted cancer therapy with potentially reduced off-target toxicity.

Several anti-mesothelin monoclonal antibodies have been developed and advanced into clinical evaluation. Notably, SS1P is an immunotoxin comprising a murine anti-mesothelin Fv fragment fused to a truncated Pseudomonas exotoxin A, designed to deliver a cytotoxic payload directly to tumor cells. MORAb-009 (amatuximab) is a chimeric IgG1 antibody derived from the same murine SS1 Fv but engineered with a human constant region to enhance effector functions. MORAb-009 has demonstrated clinical activity primarily through ADCC, wherein immune effector cells such as natural killer (NK) cells recognize the antibody-coated tumor cells via Fcγ receptors and induce lysis. However, despite these advances, existing anti-mesothelin antibodies exhibit significant limitations. Critically, none have demonstrated robust complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), a potent immune-mediated mechanism that can significantly enhance antitumor efficacy.

CDC is initiated when the C1q component of the complement system binds to the Fc regions of antibodies clustered on a target cell surface, triggering a proteolytic cascade that culminates in the formation of the membrane attack complex (MAC), leading to osmotic lysis of the target cell. The efficacy of CDC is highly dependent on the spatial orientation and proximity of the antibody binding site relative to the cell membrane. Antibodies that bind epitopes closer to the membrane, such as ofatumumab (an anti-CD20 antibody), demonstrate superior CDC compared to those binding more distal epitopes like rituximab. Nearly all known anti-mesothelin antibodies—including SS1P, MORAb-009, HN1, and m912—recognize epitopes within Region I (residues 296–390) of mesothelin, which is located at the N-terminal extremity of the mature protein and presumed to be distal from the plasma membrane. Consequently, these antibodies fail to efficiently recruit C1q and activate the complement cascade. This gap in functionality underscores a critical unmet need: the development of anti-mesothelin antibodies that bind membrane-proximal epitopes to harness the full spectrum of immune effector mechanisms, including CDC, thereby potentially improving therapeutic outcomes in patients with mesothelin-expressing cancers.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides two novel fully human monoclonal antibodies, designated SD1 and SD2, which bind to non-overlapping epitopes on human mesothelin. The SD1 antibody specifically recognizes a conformational epitope within the C-terminal domain of mesothelin (Region III, residues 487–598), proximal to the GPI anchor and the cell membrane. In contrast, SD2 binds to an epitope within the N-terminal domain (Region I) but exhibits distinct specificity from known antibodies such as SS1 or HN1. The SD1 antibody, when formatted as an Fc fusion protein (SD1-hFc), demonstrates exceptional functional properties: it binds native mesothelin on the surface of cancer cells with high affinity and specificity, mediates potent complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) by efficiently recruiting C1q, and induces strong antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) via engagement of NK cells. These dual effector functions result in significant inhibition of tumor growth in preclinical xenograft models.

In one aspect, the invention encompasses isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind human mesothelin, wherein the antibody comprises a heavy chain variable region (VH) having the complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) of SD1 or SD2. In specific embodiments, the antibody is a single-domain VH antibody derived from SD1. The invention further includes nucleic acid molecules encoding the heavy chain variable domains of SD1 or SD2, vectors comprising such nucleic acids, and host cells transformed therewith for recombinant production. Additionally, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the disclosed antibodies or antigen-binding fragments thereof, formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers for systemic or localized administration.

The invention also features immunoconjugates wherein the anti-mesothelin antibody is linked to a therapeutic agent, such as a cytotoxic drug, radioisotope, or protein toxin (e.g., Pseudomonas exotoxin), enabling targeted delivery of the payload to mesothelin-positive tumor cells. Fusion proteins comprising the antigen-binding domain of SD1 or SD2 fused to heterologous polypeptides, including cytokines, enzymes, or other immune modulators, are also encompassed. The disclosed antibodies and compositions find utility in a range of applications: as therapeutic agents for treating mesothelin-expressing cancers by inhibiting tumor growth or metastasis; as diagnostic tools for detecting mesothelin expression in biological samples to aid in cancer diagnosis, prognosis, or patient stratification; and as components of kits for in vitro or in vivo imaging and detection.

### II. Terms and Methods

As used herein, “antibody” refers to an immunoglobulin molecule capable of specifically binding an antigen. An “immunoglobulin molecule” consists of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains interconnected by disulfide bonds. Each heavy and light chain contains a variable region at its N-terminus, which determines antigen specificity, and a constant region. The “variable regions” of heavy (VH) and light (VL) chains each contain three hypervariable loops known as “complementarity determining regions” (CDRs), which directly contact the antigen, flanked by four relatively conserved “framework regions” (FRs). “Specificity determining residues” include the CDRs and adjacent framework residues that influence binding affinity and specificity. A “monoclonal antibody” is an antibody produced by a single clone of B cells or hybridoma, exhibiting uniform binding specificity. Monoclonal antibodies may be produced by traditional hybridoma technology or recombinant methods, including phage display. A “chimeric antibody” comprises variable regions from one species (e.g., mouse) and constant regions from another (e.g., human). A “human antibody” is an antibody wherein both variable and constant regions are of human origin. A “humanized immunoglobulin” is derived from a non-human antibody but engineered to contain predominantly human sequences, typically retaining only the CDRs from the donor antibody. “Binding affinity” refers to the strength of the interaction between an antibody and its antigen, commonly quantified by the dissociation equilibrium constant (KD), measurable by techniques such as surface plasmon resonance or ELISA.

“Breast cancer” encompasses malignant neoplasms of breast tissue, including “triple-negative breast cancer,” which lacks expression of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2/neu. A “chemotherapeutic agent” is a chemical compound used to treat cancer, including alkylating agents, antimetabolites, natural products, and hormones. “Cholangiocarcinoma” is a carcinoma of the bile ducts. A “conservative variant” of an amino acid sequence involves substitutions with residues of similar physicochemical properties (e.g., leucine to isoleucine). “Complementarity determining region” (CDR) is as defined above. “Contacting” means bringing into physical proximity, as in mixing an antibody with a sample. “Cytotoxicity” refers to the quality of being toxic to cells. A “degenerate variant” of a nucleic acid sequence encodes the same amino acid sequence due to codon redundancy. “Diagnostic” pertains to identifying or confirming a disease state. “Sensitivity” is the proportion of true positives correctly identified, while “specificity” is the proportion of true negatives correctly identified. An “effector molecule” is a moiety that confers a biological effect, such as a toxin or radionuclide. “Therapeutic agents” treat disease, while “diagnostic agents” facilitate detection. An “epitope” is the specific part of an antigen recognized by an antibody. “Host cells” are cells used to produce recombinant proteins. A “hybridoma” is a fused cell line producing monoclonal antibodies. An “immune response” is the reaction of the immune system to an antigen. An “immunoconjugate” is an antibody linked to an effector molecule. A “label” is a detectable moiety, including fluorescent dyes, enzymes, or radioisotopes. A “linker” is a chemical moiety connecting an antibody to an effector molecule, which may be cleavable or non-cleavable. “Lung cancer” includes non-small cell and small cell types. A “mammal” includes humans and non-human mammals. “Mesothelin” is the 40 kDa GPI-anchored protein encoded by the MSLN gene. “Mesothelioma” is a malignancy of mesothelial cells. “MORAb-009” is a chimeric anti-mesothelin antibody. “Neoplasia” is abnormal tissue growth, including benign and malignant tumors. “Operably linked” describes nucleic acid sequences positioned to allow functional expression. “Ovarian cancer,” “pancreatic cancer,” “prostate cancer,” and “stomach cancer” refer to malignancies of the respective organs. A “pharmaceutical agent” is a composition suitable for administration to a subject, formulated with “pharmaceutically acceptable carriers.” “Preventing, treating or ameliorating a disease” includes reducing severity, progression, or symptoms. “Purified” indicates substantial freedom from contaminants. “Recombinant” denotes molecules produced by genetic engineering. “Recombinant toxins” are engineered cytotoxic proteins. A “sample” is any biological material. “Sequence identity” is the percentage of identical residues in aligned sequences, assessed by algorithms like BLAST. “Squamous cell carcinoma” is a cancer of squamous epithelium. “SS1P” is an anti-mesothelin immunotoxin. A “subject” is a human or animal recipient. “Synthetic” denotes chemically or recombinantly produced. A “therapeutically effective amount” is a dose sufficient to achieve a desired effect. A “toxin” is a poisonous substance. A “vector” is a nucleic acid vehicle for gene delivery. Singular terms include plurals unless context dictates otherwise.
