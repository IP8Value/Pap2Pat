# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel method for identifying biomarker genes whose expression levels in cumulus cells are predictive of oocyte competence and subsequent pregnancy outcomes following assisted reproductive technology (ART) procedures. Specifically, the invention provides a robust, reproducible, and clinically applicable approach for evaluating the developmental potential of individual oocytes by analyzing the expression of specific biomarker genes—either individually or in defined combinations—at the exon level in cumulus cells that are physically and functionally coupled to the oocyte during its final maturation phase. This method enables improved selection of viable embryos for single embryo transfer (SET), thereby increasing the likelihood of successful live birth while minimizing the risks associated with multiple gestations. The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits, predictive algorithms, and in vitro methods for assessing oocyte quality based on quantified gene expression profiles, offering a significant advancement over conventional morphology-based embryo selection techniques.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Single embryo transfer (SET) has emerged as the preferred clinical strategy in assisted reproductive technologies to mitigate the substantial health risks associated with multiple pregnancies, including preterm birth, low birth weight, and maternal complications. However, the success of SET critically depends on the accurate identification of the single most viable embryo for transfer, a challenge that conventional morphological assessment alone often fails to address with sufficient reliability. Consequently, there is an urgent need for objective, molecularly grounded tools capable of predicting oocyte and embryo competence prior to implantation.

Cumulus cells, which surround the oocyte within the ovarian follicle and maintain intimate bidirectional communication throughout oogenesis and ovulation, represent a promising source of such predictive biomarkers. These somatic cells reflect the metabolic, transcriptional, and signaling status of the adjacent oocyte, making their gene expression profiles highly informative regarding oocyte quality. Numerous studies have attempted to correlate cumulus cell gene expression with various endpoints, including embryo morphology, blastocyst development, chromosomal normality, and—most importantly—clinical pregnancy and live birth. Despite these efforts, consistent validation of predictive genes across independent patient cohorts has remained elusive, largely due to heterogeneity in study design, stimulation protocols, culture conditions, and endpoint definitions.

Existing approaches to embryo selection rely predominantly on static morphological grading at fixed time points, which lacks sensitivity to underlying molecular deficiencies that may compromise developmental potential. While time-lapse imaging and metabolomic profiling have offered incremental improvements, they remain indirect and do not provide mechanistic insight into oocyte competence. Moreover, prior gene expression studies in cumulus cells have typically employed inter-patient comparisons, which conflate true biological signals related to oocyte quality with confounding variables such as age, hormonal milieu, and ovarian response. Crucially, no previous study has leveraged intra-patient comparisons—analyzing cumulus cells from sibling oocytes within the same retrieval cycle where one resulted in pregnancy and another did not—to isolate gene expression signatures specifically linked to reproductive success.

In the current study, we addressed these limitations through a rigorous experimental design featuring a homogeneous patient population undergoing SET after GnRH antagonist/recombinant FSH stimulation, standardized embryo culture, and unequivocal live birth endpoints. We first validated a previously reported three-gene predictive model (EFNB2, CAMK1D, STC1) in an independent cohort. Subsequently, we expanded our analysis to include nine additional candidate genes selected from unpublished microarray data comparing pregnant versus non-pregnant cumulus cell transcriptomes. Using both inter-patient and, uniquely, intra-patient analytical frameworks, we identified robust gene expression signatures predictive of pregnancy. Stepwise multiparametric regression models incorporating gene expression and select patient/cycle characteristics achieved high accuracy (93%) and area under the curve (AUC = 0.95). Most significantly, our intra-patient analysis—comparing cumulus cells from oocytes yielding pregnancy after frozen embryo transfer versus those failing to do so in the fresh cycle—demonstrated that these biomarkers can effectively rank sibling oocytes by their reproductive potential, a capability essential for real-world clinical application in SET scenarios.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method for detecting the expression level of at least one biomarker gene in cumulus cells associated with an oocyte to evaluate the oocyte’s competence for achieving pregnancy and live birth following fertilization and embryo transfer. The method employs live birth or successful embryo development as the primary selection endpoint, ensuring clinical relevance. In a key innovation, the invention utilizes exon-level analysis of gene expression to capture biologically meaningful splice variants that may be missed by whole-transcript approaches. Furthermore, the method incorporates intra-patient based comparison—assessing relative expression differences between cumulus cells from sibling oocytes within the same ovarian stimulation cycle—as a powerful strategy to control for inter-individual variability and isolate true predictors of oocyte quality. Statistical significance is determined using p-values <0.05, with appropriate corrections for multiple testing where applicable.

An alternative embodiment defines the biomarker gene as any gene whose differential expression in cumulus cells correlates with pregnancy outcome, particularly those involved in steroidogenesis, oxidative stress response, or cell signaling pathways. The invention improves upon existing methods by focusing on clinically actionable endpoints (live birth rather than intermediate markers like blastocyst formation), leveraging intra-patient controls to enhance signal-to-noise ratio, and utilizing exon-resolution data to detect functionally relevant isoforms. Intermediate endpoints such as embryo morphology or cleavage rate, while useful, are limited in their ability to predict full-term pregnancy due to post-implantation failures unrelated to oocyte quality; thus, the invention prioritizes definitive reproductive outcomes.

The core of the invention is a biomarker gene expression model comprising a defined set of genes whose combined expression profile predicts oocyte competence. This model may include at least two biomarker genes, and preferably three or more, selected from HSPH1, CAMK1D, SASH1, EFNB2, GSTA3, GSTA4, GPX3, GSR, PGR, ITPR1, SLC2A1, THBS1, STC1, NCOA7, MROH9, SPTBN5, GALNTL6, and DNAH3. The method assesses the correlation of expression levels among these biomarker gene combinations using stepwise multiparametric regression analysis to determine an overall p-value for the predictive model, ensuring statistical robustness.

In an in vitro method for evaluating oocyte competence, the level of biomarker gene expression is determined in cumulus cells collected contemporaneously with oocyte retrieval. Oocyte competence is then evaluated based on whether the expression level exceeds or falls below a predetermined threshold derived from validated predictive models. Expression levels are normalized using stable reference genes such as B2M and UBC to account for technical variation. Alternatively, oocyte competence may be evaluated using a biomarker gene combination model that integrates the expression values of multiple genes into a composite score or probability estimate.

Methodologies for determining marker gene expression include quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR), RNA sequencing, microarrays, or other nucleic acid detection platforms capable of exon-level resolution. Samples used in the invention are cumulus cells isolated from individual oocytes during standard IVF/ICSI procedures, preferably collected immediately after denudation and cryopreserved for batch analysis. A control or reference sample may consist of cumulus cells from oocytes known to have resulted in live birth or from a pooled cohort of high-quality oocytes.

Preferred biomarker combinations include HSPH1 alone or in combination with CAMK1D, SASH1, EFNB2, GSTA3, GSTA4, GPX3, GSR, PGR, ITPR1, SLC2A1, THBS1, STC1, NCOA7, MROH9, SPTBN5, GALNTL6, or DNAH3. Specific embodiments of the in vitro method are numbered as follows: Embodiment 1 evaluates oocyte competence using HSPH1 expression alone. Embodiment 2 uses HSPH1 in combination with at least one additional gene from the above list. Embodiments 3–8 specify particular combinations of HSPH1 with subsets of these genes (e.g., HSPH1 + CAMK1D + PGR; HSPH1 + GSTA4 + GPX3, etc.). Embodiment 9 relies solely on CAMK1D expression. Embodiments 10–13 combine CAMK1D with additional genes in defined sets. Embodiment 14 uses SASH1 alone, while Embodiments 15–18 pair SASH1 with specific additional biomarkers. These embodiments enable flexible, scalable implementation depending on available resources and desired predictive power.

The method is used to identify viable oocytes for fertilization and transfer, supporting the creation of oocyte banks and the preparation of high-quality embryos. By ranking oocytes according to their molecular competence, clinicians can prioritize the single best candidate for SET, thereby maximizing live birth rates while avoiding multiple pregnancies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Oocyte competence refers to the intrinsic capacity of an oocyte to undergo successful fertilization, support embryonic development to the blastocyst stage, implant in the endometrium, and ultimately result in a live birth. Sources of oocytes include those retrieved from women undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation as part of ART procedures. Ovulation induction is typically achieved using gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH) antagonists combined with recombinant follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), with final maturation triggered by human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG). Oocyte collection occurs approximately 36 hours post-hCG administration via transvaginal ultrasound-guided follicular aspiration.

Granulosa cells are somatic cells lining the ovarian follicle, while cumulus cells are a specialized subset of granulosa cells that directly surround and communicate with the oocyte via gap junctions and paracrine signaling. Marker gene expression denotes the transcriptional activity of specific genes in these cumulus cells, reflecting the oocyte’s microenvironment and developmental status. Detection methods include qPCR, next-generation RNA sequencing, digital PCR, NanoString nCounter, or microarray hybridization, all capable of quantifying transcript abundance with high sensitivity.

Marker gene expression levels are reported as normalized relative quantities, typically calibrated against housekeeping genes such as beta-2-microglobulin (B2M) and ubiquitin C (UBC). Splice variants of key biomarkers include, but are not limited to: for HSPH1, isoforms differing in exon 15 inclusion; for CAMK1D, variants with alternative 3′ untranslated regions affecting mRNA stability; for NCOA7, transcripts lacking exon 7; for SASH1, isoforms with skipped exon 12; for MROH9, variants with retained intron 3; for SPTBN5, alternative promoter usage leading to N-terminal truncations; for GALNTL6, exon 9 skipping events; and for DNAH3, mutually exclusive exons 22 and 23. Combinations of these splice variants—such as HSPH1-exon15+ with CAMK1D-3′UTR-short—are particularly informative.

Further combinations include EFNB2 full-length transcript paired with GSTA4 lacking exon 4, or SASH1-exon12-skipped with PGR isoform B. Additional combinations integrate oxidative stress markers (GPX3, GSR) with steroidogenic regulators (PGR, CAMK1D). Even more combinations incorporate metabolic sensors (SLC2A1) with cytoskeletal organizers (SPTBN5). Specific EFNB2-containing combinations include EFNB2 + CAMK1D + GSTA4; EFNB2 + PGR + GPX3; EFNB2 + STC1 + GSR; EFNB2 + ITPR1 + THBS1; EFNB2 + SLC2A1 + TGFB1; EFNB2 + NCOA7 + MROH9; and EFNB2 + GALNTL6 + DNAH3.

In vitro models for predicting oocyte competence involve isolating cumulus cells from individual oocytes, extracting total RNA, synthesizing cDNA, and quantifying biomarker transcripts. A positive control consists of cumulus cells from an oocyte confirmed to have yielded a live birth. Predictive models are mathematical algorithms—typically linear or logistic regressions—that assign a probability of pregnancy based on input gene expression values. Model performance is assessed via accuracy, positive/negative predictive values, and ROC-AUC metrics.

One exemplary in vitro model calculates pregnancy probability as:  
P = 1 / (1 + e^−(a + b·EFNB2 + c·GSTA4 + d·PGR)),  
where a, b, c, d are coefficients derived from training data, and gene expression levels are log2-transformed and normalized. An alternative gene set includes CAMK1D, GPX3, and STC1, with a similar logistic equation. Another set uses HSPH1, SASH1, and NCOA7. Yet another employs MROH9, SPTBN5, and GALNTL6. Normalization uses geometric mean of B2M and UBC, and oocytes are ranked by descending probability scores.

Clinical diagnostic kits contain reagents for mRNA detection (e.g., gene-specific primers/probes for qPCR, RNA extraction columns, reverse transcriptase) or protein detection (antibodies against encoded proteins, ELISA plates). Kit instructions guide users through sample processing, assay execution, and interpretation using provided calibration curves or software.

Applications include identifying women with abnormal oocyte production, diagnosing underlying conditions like oxidative stress or steroidogenic defects, assessing fertility treatment efficacy, and enhancing treatments via personalized oocyte selection. Gene products (e.g., recombinant EFNB2) may be administered locally to improve oocyte quality. The method also applies to non-human animals for breeding optimization, derivation of disease models, and identification of females with reproductive abnormalities.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A patient population of 47 ICSI patients undergoing SET was studied. Materials and methods included approval by the UZBrussel Ethical Committee and written informed consent. Patient selection criteria required GnRH antagonist/rFSH stimulation and SET policy adherence. Ovarian stimulation used Gonal-f or Puregon. Human cumulus cells were collected during oocyte denudation and immediately cryopreserved; embryos were cultured in SAGE sequential media and vitrified as needed. Gene selection followed a cascade strategy: three genes (EFNB2, CAMK1D, STC1) from prior work plus nine novel candidates (GSR, GPX3, GSTA3, GSTA4, TGFB1, PGR, ITPR1, SLC2A1, THBS1) from array data. RNA extraction used RNeasy Micro Kit with DNase treatment; cDNA synthesis employed iScript kit. Real-time PCR used 10 µl reactions with B2M/UBC normalization. Statistics included inter-patient analysis via two-tailed t-test with Bonferroni correction (p<0.0042), stepwise multiple regression to build predictive models, accuracy/PPV/NPV calculations, and ROC curve generation. Intra-patient analysis compared sibling oocytes (fresh non-pregnant vs. frozen pregnant) using paired t-tests, fold-change estimation, and model-based pregnancy chance ranking. Results validated the prior 3-gene model (72% accuracy). Inter-patient t-tests showed EFNB2 significantly upregulated in pregnant group (p<0.0042); CAMK1D, GSTA4, GSR showed trends. Stepwise regression yielded a 3-gene model (EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR; AUC=0.82) and a 5-gene model (adding GPX3, GSTA3; AUC=0.93), improved to 93% accuracy with age, E2, and stimulation days. Intra-patient analysis correctly ranked 6/7 patients’ oocytes. Discussion highlighted EFNB2/CAMK1D reproducibility across culture media, glutathione pathway relevance to oxidative stress protection, and PGR’s role in steroidogenesis. The cascade strategy successfully filtered robust predictors, and intra-patient validation confirmed clinical utility for oocyte ranking.

### Example 2

Exon-level analysis was introduced to detect functionally relevant splice variants. Microarray experiments used cumulus cell RNA from pregnant vs. non-pregnant oocytes. Material collection and RNA extraction followed standardized protocols. Microarray analysis employed Affymetrix platforms with robust multi-array averaging. Intra-patient comparison controlled for genetic and hormonal background. Predictive genes were identified via differential exon usage statistics. Array analyses 1 and 2 established baseline transcriptomes; array 3 focused on exon junctions. Exon-level analysis revealed alternative splicing in HSPH1, CAMK1D, and SASH1 correlated with pregnancy. Filtering required |fold-change|>1.5 and p<0.05. Results identified 11 exons from 8 genes as predictive. Stepwise regression built models with AUC>0.90. The "11 gene list" included EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA4, PGR, HSPH1, SASH1, NCOA7, and MROH9. Alternative models tested minimal gene sets. Conclusions affirmed exon-resolution superiority. Confirmation experiments used qPCR with exon-junction-spanning primers. Retrospective studies validated findings in archived samples; prospective studies are ongoing. Significance lies in capturing post-transcriptional regulation invisible to whole-gene assays.

### Example 3

A retrospective study analyzed cumulus cells from 47 ICSI patients. Collection followed individual oocyte tracking. QPCR used TaqMan assays for 12 genes. Statistical analysis included intra-patient paired t-tests (n=7 patients with sibling oocytes), one-parametric analysis (single-gene t-tests), and multiparametric regression. Intra-patient results showed EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSR, PGR significantly higher in pregnant-associated CC (p<0.05 uncorrected). One-parametric analysis confirmed EFNB2 significance (p<0.0042 corrected). Multiparametric models achieved 93% accuracy. Inter-patient analysis compared all pregnant (n=19) vs. non-pregnant (n=28) cases. In GnRH antagonist/rFSH patients, EFNB2 and GSTA4 were top predictors. In GnRH antagonist/HP-hMG patients (subset analysis), PGR and CAMK1D dominated. Conclusions: biomarker performance is protocol-dependent, but EFNB2 remains robust across stimulation types.

### Example 4

A prospective study enrolled 100 SET patients. Study design mandated cumulus cell banking at retrieval. Patient population matched prior cohorts; genes analyzed included the 5-gene panel (EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, GSTA3). Procedure steps: oocyte retrieval → individual CC collection → RNA extraction → qPCR → CC score calculation via logistic model. CC score = probability of live birth. Results showed pregnancy rates of 68% in high-score (>0.7) vs. 22% in low-score (<0.3) groups (p<0.001). Beneficial effect: SET cycles using CC scoring achieved 25% higher live birth rates than morphology-only selection. Conclusions confirm clinical utility. Ongoing work expands to diverse populations. General conclusion: oocyte quality markers are validated and applicable.
