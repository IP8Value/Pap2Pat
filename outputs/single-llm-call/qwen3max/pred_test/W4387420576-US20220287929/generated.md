# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of dermatological and cosmetic compositions, particularly to topical skin care products formulated for the prevention and treatment of skin aging. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel lipid-based microcapsules engineered to possess high affinity for reactive oxygen species (ROS), which are incorporated into skin care formulations to provide enhanced antioxidant protection, improved hydration, reinforcement of the skin barrier function, and reduction in visible signs of aging such as wrinkles and skin roughness. The invention further encompasses methods for preparing such microcapsules, their incorporation into topical delivery systems including serums, creams, and lotions, and their use in cosmetic and dermatological regimens aimed at mitigating oxidative stress-induced skin damage.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Skin care products have long been developed to address aesthetic and physiological concerns related to skin aging, environmental exposure, and intrinsic biological decline. Among the most critical challenges in modern dermatology is the management of oxidative stress, a condition arising from an imbalance between the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and the skin’s endogenous antioxidant defenses. ROS, including superoxide anion, hydroxyl radical, and hydrogen peroxide, are generated during normal aerobic metabolism but are significantly amplified by external stressors such as ultraviolet (UV) radiation, pollution, and cigarette smoke. While low levels of ROS play regulatory roles in cellular signaling, excessive accumulation leads to oxidative damage across multiple biomolecular targets.

Biological activity of ROS manifests through several destructive pathways. One major consequence is lipid peroxidation, a chain reaction in which ROS attack polyunsaturated fatty acids in cellular membranes, leading to loss of membrane integrity, altered fluidity, and generation of cytotoxic aldehydes such as malondialdehyde. Protein damage occurs via oxidation of amino acid side chains—particularly cysteine and methionine residues—resulting in protein misfolding, loss of enzymatic function, and aggregation. DNA damage is another critical outcome, with ROS inducing strand breaks, base modifications, and telomere shortening, all of which contribute to cellular senescence and genomic instability. Collectively, these molecular insults accelerate skin aging, manifesting clinically as wrinkle formation, loss of elasticity, dryness, uneven pigmentation, and compromised barrier function.

To combat this, antioxidant products have been widely marketed, often containing vitamins such as A (retinoids), C (ascorbic acid derivatives), and E (tocopherols). These compounds function by donating electrons to neutralize free radicals, thereby interrupting oxidative cascades. The Oxygen Radical Absorbance Capacity (ORAC) assay has historically been used to quantify the total antioxidant capacity of such ingredients, though its relevance in vivo remains debated. Despite their theoretical benefits, conventional antioxidant formulations suffer from significant drawbacks. Many active ingredients are chemically unstable when exposed to light, air, or aqueous environments, leading to rapid degradation before reaching target skin layers. Others exhibit poor skin penetration due to hydrophilicity or molecular size, while high concentrations of certain antioxidants—particularly vitamin C—can paradoxically act as pro-oxidants under specific conditions, exacerbating rather than alleviating oxidative stress. Additionally, unencapsulated actives may cause irritation, stinging, or sensitization, limiting their tolerability and consumer compliance. These limitations underscore the need for advanced delivery systems that enhance stability, control release, improve bioavailability, and minimize adverse effects—all of which the present invention addresses through a novel lipid particle architecture.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a skin care product comprising microcapsules formed from a specialized lipid matrix exhibiting high affinity for reactive oxygen species (ROS). These capsules, referred to herein as SIREN CAPSULE TECHNOLOGY™, are designed to selectively respond to oxidative environments by releasing encapsulated active ingredients in a targeted and controlled manner. The capsule consists of a solid lipophilic core derived from natural waxes and enriched with polyunsaturated fatty acids, surrounded by a structured matrix that incorporates both lipophilic and hydrophilic antioxidant agents. The method for making the capsules involves a solvent-free emulsification process that yields uniform particles with diameters not exceeding 25 micrometers, ensuring optimal skin compatibility and dispersion in aqueous or semi-solid bases. The resulting skin care product—formulated as a serum, cream, or lotion—demonstrates superior efficacy in reducing ROS-induced damage, improving skin hydration, reinforcing the epidermal barrier, and diminishing visible signs of aging compared to products containing free or conventionally encapsulated vitamins. A key advantage of the invention lies in its dual functionality: the matrix itself acts as a ROS scavenger due to its high unsaturated lipid content, while simultaneously serving as a protective reservoir for labile actives such as retinyl palmitate and sodium ascorbyl phosphate. This design offers a reliable and easy-to-implement alternative to existing antioxidant delivery systems, with competitive manufacturing costs owing to the use of GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe) natural ingredients and absence of organic solvents. The capsule composition is oxidation-stable, as confirmed by iodine index measurements below 60 g I₂/100 g, and exhibits a high ORAC value indicative of potent antioxidant capacity. The process for preparing the capsules is scalable, reproducible, and compliant with green chemistry principles. When applied topically, the product delivers sustained release of actives, minimizes irritation, and enhances skin resilience against environmental aggressors, thereby providing comprehensive anti-aging benefits through a scientifically validated mechanism.

## DESCRIPTION

The present invention introduces advanced skin care products and methods centered on lipid-based microcapsules engineered for high reactivity toward reactive oxygen species (ROS). A skin care product, as defined herein, is a topical formulation intended for application to the epidermis to improve its appearance, health, or function, particularly in the context of aging and oxidative stress. Central to the invention is the capsule composition, which comprises a lipid particle formed from a solid lipophilic phase and a functional matrix. The lipid particle is defined as a discrete, spherical or spheroidal microstructure with a diameter of up to 25 micrometers, produced without organic solvents and stabilized through controlled cooling.

The matrix composition is carefully engineered to include a blend of natural waxes—specifically jojoba wax, mimosa wax, and sunflower seed wax—which collectively provide structural integrity and a melting point suitable for dermal application. Critically, the matrix contains a specified polyunsaturated fatty acid content, derived from safflower oil, which imparts high ROS affinity. This lipophilic solid part constitutes the core of the capsule and remains solid at room temperature but softens upon contact with skin, facilitating integration into the stratum corneum. The lipophilic part of unsaturated fatty acids serves not only as a structural component but also as a sacrificial substrate for ROS, thereby protecting endogenous skin lipids from peroxidation.

The formulation further includes surfactants and/or suspension agents and/or humectants to ensure colloidal stability and skin compatibility. Decyl glucoside, a non-ionic, plant-derived surfactant, is employed to stabilize the oil-in-water emulsion during manufacturing without compromising skin barrier integrity. Active ingredients are co-encapsulated within the matrix, including both lipophilic and water-soluble species. Lipophilic actives such as retinyl palmitate (vitamin A ester) are dissolved directly into the melted lipid phase, while water-soluble active ingredients—exemplified by sodium ascorbyl phosphate, a stable vitamin C derivative—are incorporated into the aqueous phase prior to emulsification. Antioxidant agents are thus distributed throughout the system to provide multi-layered protection.

In an alternate embodiment of the skin care product, the matrix composition is modified to include additional lipid components that fine-tune melting behavior and release kinetics. The lipid component in this alternate embodiment may comprise a mixture of saturated and unsaturated fatty alcohols or esters, selected to achieve a melting temperature between 40°C and 55°C, which ensures stability during storage yet allows for facile release upon skin contact. The advantage of this tailored melting temperature lies in its ability to prevent premature leakage while enabling responsive delivery under physiological conditions. The lipid component mixture may further include ceramide analogs or phytosterols to enhance barrier-repair properties.

The matrix composition with high ROS affinity is defined by its capacity to undergo controlled oxidation in the presence of ROS, thereby acting as a “sink” that diverts oxidative damage away from vital skin structures. ROS affinity, as used herein, refers to the thermodynamic and kinetic propensity of the lipid matrix to react with and neutralize ROS, primarily through hydrogen abstraction from bis-allylic positions in polyunsaturated fatty acids. This results in an oxidation-stable matrix post-reaction, as the initial oxidation products are stabilized by conjugation and do not propagate chain reactions. The matrix composition with unsaturated fatty acids is deliberately formulated to contain a precise ratio of linoleic to oleic acid to maximize ROS scavenging while maintaining physical stability.

An antioxidant agent is embedded within the matrix—not merely as cargo but as an integral functional component. Excipients in the matrix, such as tocopherol or ubiquinone, may be added to synergize with the primary actives and further suppress radical propagation. Active ingredients in the matrix are protected from hydrolysis, photodegradation, and air oxidation, thereby extending shelf life and enhancing bioavailability.

The method for making the capsules begins by melting the solid lipophilic part at 55°C under gentle agitation. Active ingredients are then solubilized or dispersed into this phase, followed by combination with an aqueous solution containing hydrophilic actives and surfactants at 48–50°C under high-shear mixing (3500 rpm) to form a microemulsion. This emulsion is screened through a 25-micrometer mesh to ensure uniform particle size, then cooled to 10°C to solidify the lipid cores. In an alternate method for making capsules, high-pressure homogenization may replace mesh filtration for industrial-scale production. The resulting capsules are incorporated into a serum, cream, or lotion base using standard cosmetic manufacturing techniques, and the final product is packaged in air-tight, light-protected containers to preserve potency.

The ORAC value of the capsules exceeds 15,000 μmol TE/g, reflecting exceptional antioxidant capacity. The overall process for preparing the capsules is sustainable, solvent-free, and yields a stable, efficacious delivery system that represents a significant advancement in anti-aging skin care technology.
