# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention resides in the technical field of immunology and biopharmaceutical therapeutics, specifically relating to the treatment of IgE-mediated allergic disorders. More particularly, the invention concerns novel compositions and methods for anti-IgE therapy that overcome limitations associated with existing anti-IgE agents such as omalizumab. The invention provides engineered IgE variants that are resistant to neutralization by omalizumab yet retain binding affinity for IgE receptors FcεRI and CD23. These omalizumab-resistant IgE variants enable a new therapeutic paradigm—receptor occupancy without allergen reactivity—by facilitating the exchange of endogenous, allergen-specific IgE on effector cells with non-pathogenic, engineered IgE fragments during concurrent anti-IgE therapy. The technical field further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations, combination therapies, diagnostic kits, and methods of administration designed to modulate IgE-dependent immune responses in subjects suffering from allergic asthma, atopic dermatitis, chronic urticaria, and other IgE-mediated conditions.

## BACKGROUND

Allergic diseases represent a significant global health burden, affecting hundreds of millions of individuals worldwide. These conditions arise from inappropriate immune responses to otherwise harmless environmental antigens, commonly referred to as allergens. Central to the pathogenesis of allergic disorders is immunoglobulin E (IgE), a class of antibody that mediates immediate hypersensitivity reactions through its high-affinity interaction with the FcεRI receptor expressed on mast cells and basophils. Upon allergen exposure, cross-linking of receptor-bound IgE triggers degranulation of these effector cells, resulting in the release of histamine, leukotrienes, and other inflammatory mediators responsible for clinical symptoms ranging from rhinorrhea and pruritus to life-threatening anaphylaxis.

IgE antibodies are heterotetrameric glycoproteins composed of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains. The heavy chains contain one variable domain (VH) and four constant domains (Cε1–Cε4). The Cε3 and Cε4 domains form the Fc region responsible for receptor binding, while the Cε2 domain serves as a flexible hinge. IgE exists in both free serum form and cell-bound form, with the latter stabilized through interactions with either the high-affinity FcεRI receptor on mast cells and basophils or the low-aff affinity CD23 receptor on B cells and other antigen-presenting cells. CD23 plays a critical role in IgE homeostasis by regulating IgE synthesis through feedback mechanisms.

FcεRI receptors are multimeric complexes consisting of an α-chain that binds IgE, a β-chain that amplifies signaling, and two disulfide-linked γ-chains that mediate intracellular signal transduction. The interaction between IgE and FcεRI is among the highest affinity receptor-ligand pairs in immunology, with dissociation constants in the picomolar range, which contributes to the long half-life of cell-bound IgE. This stable association allows sensitized effector cells to remain primed for allergen recognition over extended periods.

IgE-mediated antigen presentation represents another facet of allergic inflammation, wherein B cells internalize allergen-IgE complexes via CD23 and present processed peptides to T helper cells, thereby perpetuating the allergic response. This mechanism underscores the dual role of IgE not only as an effector molecule but also as a modulator of adaptive immunity.

Omalizumab, a humanized monoclonal antibody, was the first clinically approved anti-IgE therapeutic. It functions by binding free IgE at an epitope distinct from the FcεRI-binding site, thereby preventing IgE from engaging its high-affinity receptor. By sequestering circulating IgE, omalizumab indirectly downregulates FcεRI expression on effector cells due to receptor internalization in the absence of ligand stabilization. This leads to reduced cellular sensitivity to allergen challenge and amelioration of clinical symptoms in patients with moderate-to-severe allergic asthma and chronic idiopathic urticaria.

The mechanism of omalizumab involves selective binding to the Cε3 domain of free IgE, forming small, non-crosslinking immune complexes that are cleared without triggering effector cell activation. Importantly, omalizumab does not bind to receptor-bound IgE, thus avoiding anaphylactic reactions. Structural studies have revealed that omalizumab approaches the IgE Cε3 domain perpendicularly and sterically blocks access to both FcεRI and CD23 binding sites, primarily through physical overlap rather than direct competition for shared residues.

Despite its clinical utility, omalizumab exhibits several limitations. Its efficacy is restricted to patients with elevated serum IgE levels within a defined range, necessitating dose titration based on body weight and baseline IgE concentration. Moreover, omalizumab requires frequent subcutaneous injections, often every two to four weeks, which impacts patient compliance. Therapeutically, omalizumab depletes both pathogenic allergen-specific IgE and non-pathogenic polyclonal IgE, potentially disrupting IgE-dependent homeostatic pathways. Notably, clinical observations indicate that basophil sensitivity increases on a per-IgE basis during omalizumab therapy, possibly due to compensatory signaling adaptations. Additionally, omalizumab shows minimal activity against preformed IgE-FcεRI complexes on tissue-resident mast cells, leading to slow resolution of symptoms in certain compartments.

In response to these limitations, alternative inhibitory scaffolds such as Designed Ankyrin Repeat Proteins (DARPins) have been developed. DARPins like E2_79 and bi53_79 can disrupt preformed IgE-FcεRI complexes by binding IgE with high affinity and accelerating complex dissociation, offering potential advantages in acute settings. However, these agents remain investigational and do not address the fundamental issue of IgE depletion-induced homeostatic dysregulation.

There remains a compelling need for improved anti-IgE therapies that not only neutralize pathogenic IgE but also preserve or replace receptor occupancy to maintain immune homeostasis, enhance therapeutic kinetics, and broaden applicability across diverse patient populations and disease phenotypes.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel class of omalizumab-resistant IgE variants engineered to circumvent neutralization by omalizumab while retaining functional binding to IgE receptors FcεRI and CD23. These variants are characterized by specific amino acid substitutions, particularly at position 419 of the IgE heavy chain, where arginine is replaced by asparagine (R419N), thereby introducing a consensus sequence for N-linked glycosylation directly within the omalizumab epitope. This glycosylation sterically occludes omalizumab binding without significantly impairing receptor engagement, yielding a functionally active yet therapeutically inert IgE fragment.

A key aspect of the invention is a composition comprising an omalizumab-resistant IgE variant, such as IgE-R419N-Fc3-4, which includes at least the Cε3 and Cε4 domains of human IgE. This composition may be formulated as a standalone therapeutic agent or combined with additional pharmacologically active agents, including but not limited to omalizumab, corticosteroids, leukotriene modifiers, or biologics targeting interleukins such as IL-4, IL-5, or IL-13. The inclusion of omalizumab in such combinations enables a dual-action strategy: omalizumab neutralizes endogenous IgE, while the resistant variant occupies vacant FcεRI and CD23 receptors, preventing upregulation of receptor expression and maintaining immune quiescence.

The invention further provides a method of anti-IgE therapy for treating IgE-mediated disorders in a subject, comprising administering to the subject an effective amount of an omalizumab-resistant IgE variant. In preferred embodiments, this administration occurs concurrently with or sequentially following administration of omalizumab, thereby facilitating the exchange of native, allergen-reactive IgE on effector cells with the non-reactive, engineered variant. This exchange maintains receptor occupancy, suppresses basophil and mast cell hyperreactivity, and stabilizes CD23 expression on B cells, thereby inhibiting the positive feedback loop that drives IgE synthesis.

Therapeutic effects achieved by this approach include reduced basophil activation upon allergen challenge, decreased release of inflammatory mediators, attenuation of late-phase allergic responses, and potential modulation of IgE production through CD23-dependent mechanisms. The omalizumab-resistant IgE variant acts synergistically with omalizumab, as demonstrated by enhanced suppression of cellular activation even at subtherapeutic concentrations of each agent.

Multiple dosing regimens are encompassed within the invention, including single-dose, intermittent, and sustained-release protocols. Administration timing may be optimized based on disease severity, baseline IgE levels, and pharmacokinetic profiles, with initial loading doses followed by maintenance infusions or injections. Routes of administration include subcutaneous, intravenous, intramuscular, and inhalational delivery, tailored to target tissue compartments such as the lung, skin, or systemic circulation.

Combination therapy is a central feature of the invention, wherein the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant is co-administered with omalizumab to achieve simultaneous IgE neutralization and receptor reoccupation. This strategy addresses the paradoxical increase in cellular sensitivity observed during conventional anti-IgE monotherapy and offers a more comprehensive immunomodulatory effect.

The invention also includes a kit for implementing the described therapeutic method. The kit comprises a container holding a pharmaceutical composition of the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant, optionally a second container with omalizumab or another adjunctive agent, and a package insert providing instructions for use, dosage guidelines, and indications for IgE-mediated disorders. The kit may further include devices for reconstitution, dilution, or injection, ensuring ease of clinical deployment.

In summary, the invention provides an omalizumab-resistant IgE variant for use in the treatment of IgE-mediated allergic diseases, enabling a paradigm shift from IgE depletion to IgE replacement—a strategy that preserves receptor homeostasis while eliminating allergen reactivity.

### II. MODES OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The scope of the invention encompasses all embodiments falling within the claims, with preference given to materials and methods that maximize therapeutic efficacy, safety, and manufacturability. The core innovation is a novel anti-IgE therapy based on engineered IgE variants resistant to omalizumab neutralization.

This approach is motivated by the limitations of omalizumab monotherapy, particularly the loss of receptor occupancy and compensatory increases in cellular sensitivity. By administering an omalizumab-resistant IgE variant alongside omalizumab, endogenous IgE is displaced and replaced with a non-allergenic surrogate that maintains FcεRI and CD23 occupancy, thereby preserving homeostatic signaling while eliminating allergen reactivity.

Omalizumab-resistant IgE variants are designed through structure-guided mutagenesis. Based on crystallographic data of the IgE-omalizumab complex, residue Arg-419 in the Cε3 domain was identified as critical for omalizumab binding, forming salt bridges with complementarity-determining regions of the antibody. Substitution of Arg-419 with asparagine (R419N) introduces the NVT motif, creating a consensus site for N-linked glycosylation. This glycan insertion sterically blocks omalizumab access without disrupting the overall fold or receptor-binding interface.

Exemplary nucleic acid and protein sequences are provided as SEQ ID NO: 1 (encoding human IgE-Fc3-4) and SEQ ID NO: 2 (encoding IgE-R419N-Fc3-4). Additional sequences may be derived from NCBI database entries for human IgE (e.g., NP_001124378.1). Variants may be constructed via site-directed mutagenesis, PCR-based methods, or gene synthesis, followed by recombinant expression in mammalian (HEK293) or insect (High Five) cells.

Biologically active variants retain ≥50% binding affinity for FcεRIα and CD23 relative to wild-type IgE-Fc3-4, as measured by surface plasmon resonance (SPR). Optimal sequence alignment ensures accurate identification of equivalent positions across species or isoforms. Percent identity calculations account for conservative substitutions that preserve function.

A specific embodiment features the R419N substitution in the context of the IgE-Fc3-4 fragment (residues 330–500). This fragment lacks the Cε1 and Cε2 domains, minimizing risk of unintended effector functions while retaining receptor-binding capability.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the omalizumab-resistant IgE variant in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. Excipients include buffers (e.g., phosphate, citrate), stabilizers (e.g., sucrose, trehalose), antimicrobial agents (e.g., benzyl alcohol), antioxidants (e.g., methionine), and surfactants such as polysorbate 20, polysorbate 80, or poloxamer 188. Acids (e.g., hydrochloric, acetic) or bases (e.g., sodium hydroxide, tromethamine) adjust pH to 5.0–7.5.

The amount of active ingredient ranges from 0.1 mg to 100 mg per dose, depending on indication and route. Individual excipients are present in concentrations known to be safe and effective (e.g., 0.01–0.2% surfactant). Formulations may be liquid, lyophilized solid, or sustained-release depots. Solid compositions are reconstituted with sterile water, saline, or dextrose solutions.

Administration routes include subcutaneous (preferred for chronic therapy), intravenous (for rapid effect), or inhalation (for localized lung delivery). Concurrent therapy with omalizumab is performed with overlapping pharmacokinetic profiles to ensure continuous receptor coverage. Dosing regimens may involve weekly, biweekly, or monthly intervals, with initial loading doses followed by maintenance.

Kits include vials of the IgE variant, omalizumab, diluents, syringes, and a package insert detailing indications, contraindications, and administration protocols.

### III. EXPERIMENTAL

Example 1 demonstrates the structural and functional basis for omalizumab resistance. Motivated by clinical limitations of omalizumab, we solved the crystal structure of the IgE-G335C-Fc3-4:omalizumab-Fab complex at 2.5 Å resolution (PDB ID: 5HYS). Molecular replacement using known structures yielded a model revealing omalizumab’s perpendicular approach to the Cε3 domain, contacting residues below the FcεRIα-binding loops.

Prior epitope mapping aligned with the interface, which buries ~725 Å² and involves 23 IgE residues, including R419. Comparison with the DARPin inhibitor E2_79 showed overlapping binding sites but differing steric clashes with FcεRIα—omalizumab exhibited threefold greater atomic overlap, correlating with weaker disruptive activity.

We designed an IgE-Fc3-4 mutant, R419N, predicted to abolish omalizumab binding via glycosylation. SDS-PAGE and PNGaseF digestion confirmed addition of an N-glycan. SPR showed complete loss of omalizumab binding but preserved FcεRIα affinity.

In ex vivo basophil exchange experiments, blood from donors was stripped of surface IgE using the DARPin E2_79, then reloaded with JW8-IgE (trackable via mouse λ-light chain). Omalizumab alone removed JW8-IgE; co-administration with wild-type IgE-Fc3-4 failed to reload cells. In contrast, omalizumab + IgE-R419N-Fc3-4 efficiently exchanged JW8-IgE for the resistant variant, as confirmed by flow cytometry.

Similar results were seen on CD23+ B cells, with IgE-R419N-Fc3-4 stabilizing surface CD23 and preventing soluble CD23 release. Basophil activation tests showed synergistic inhibition: omalizumab (500 nM) + IgE-R419N-Fc3-4 (1 nM) nearly abolished NP-induced activation, surpassing either agent alone.

These data validate IgE exchange therapy as a viable strategy. Limitations include potential immunogenicity, though glycosylation may mask epitopes. Future work will explore full-length variants, alternative mutations, and in vivo efficacy.

Proteins were expressed in HEK293 or insect cells, purified via Ni-NTA and gel filtration. Crystallization used 41% PEG 400, and diffraction data were processed with HKL2000. Structures were refined in Phenix, validated with MolProbity. Volume overlaps were computed using CCP4 tools. Biotinylation enabled detection in flow cytometry. Functional assays used primary human basophils stimulated with NIP-BSA, with activation measured by CD63 upregulation.

In conclusion, the invention provides a structurally informed, functionally validated platform for next-generation anti-IgE therapy through engineered IgE variants that resist neutralization while maintaining receptor engagement, enabling a novel exchange-based treatment paradigm.
