# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of quantum computing, specifically in the design and implementation of quantum algorithms for solving discrete optimization problems. More particularly, the invention relates to a novel method for executing quantum walks that circumvents the need for costly arithmetic operations traditionally associated with oracle-based formulations of quantum walk operators. The invention further pertains to heuristic quantum algorithms derived from quantized Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods—such as the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm and Glauber dynamics—for accelerating the solution of combinatorial optimization tasks, including but not limited to finding low-energy configurations of Ising spin models. The disclosed techniques are applicable to both theoretical and practical implementations of quantum processors, especially those targeting problems in statistical physics, machine learning, materials science, and operations research where classical MCMC methods are currently employed but suffer from slow mixing times due to small spectral gaps.

## SUMMARY

The invention introduces a practical and efficient implementation of a quantum walk operator tailored for discrete optimization problems, notably those modeled by sparse, local Ising Hamiltonians. Unlike prior approaches that rely on an abstract oracle implementing the unitary transformation W associated with a classical random walk, the present invention reformulates the quantum walk entirely in terms of elementary quantum gates without invoking arithmetic-intensive subroutines. This is achieved by directly constructing a circuit that is isometric to the full quantum walk operator U_W, thereby avoiding the problematic uncomputation of intermediate registers when proposed moves are rejected—a key bottleneck in conventional oracle-based designs.

The quantum walk is applied to accelerate classical MCMC methods used in simulated annealing and related heuristics. By leveraging Szegedy’s quantization framework, the invention enables a quadratic speedup in mixing time relative to the classical counterpart under ideal conditions. However, numerical simulations reveal that, in practice, certain heuristic variants of the quantum walk—particularly a unitary adiabatic-like protocol without intermediate measurements—can achieve superquadratic or near-quadratic speedups on specific problem classes, such as one-dimensional Ising chains and sparse random Ising graphs.

The invention further discloses two primary heuristic strategies for deploying the quantum walk in optimization: (1) a Zeno-effect-based adiabatic state preparation protocol with optional “rewind” upon failure, and (2) a purely unitary sequence of quantum walk operators followed by a single measurement. Both methods are benchmarked using the metric of total time to solution (TTS), which accounts for both runtime per trial and success probability. Numerical results demonstrate consistent polynomial quantum advantages across multiple problem instances, with the unitary approach often outperforming even the optimized Zeno-with-rewind strategy. These findings suggest that the disclosed quantum walk implementation not only reduces hardware resource requirements but also enhances algorithmic performance beyond theoretical expectations in practical settings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### I. Introduction

Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) simulations are foundational tools in computational statistics, statistical mechanics, and machine learning for sampling from complex probability distributions, particularly the Boltzmann distribution associated with energy landscapes. A major limitation of classical MCMC methods, such as the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm, is their slow convergence when the underlying Markov chain exhibits a small spectral gap—leading to long autocorrelation times and inefficient exploration of the state space. Quantum computing offers a potential remedy through quantum walks, which can simulate the evolution of a quantized version of the classical random walk and, under reversibility and detailed balance conditions, achieve a quadratic speedup in mixing time via quantum phase estimation or adiabatic techniques.

The present invention provides concrete, gate-level embodiments of such quantum walks that are specifically engineered to avoid the computational overhead of arithmetic operations typically required in oracle-based formulations. Instead of implementing the abstract walk unitary W, the invention constructs a direct circuit for the composite quantum walk operator U_W using physically realizable components: move preparation, spin flip, reflection, and Boltzmann coin operations. These components are designed to exploit the sparsity and locality of common optimization problems—such as k-local Ising models with bounded interaction degree—thereby minimizing qubit count, circuit depth, and T-gate complexity. The resulting architecture is suitable for near-term quantum devices and enables heuristic deployment in discrete optimization without requiring full fault tolerance or high-precision arithmetic.

### II. General Considerations

As used herein, a “quantum walk” refers to a unitary evolution on a Hilbert space that encodes the transition structure of a classical reversible Markov chain. The term “walk operator” denotes the specific unitary U_W constructed from reflections and swap operations as per Szegedy’s formalism. “Metropolis-Hastings algorithm” describes a class of MCMC methods that generate samples from a target distribution by proposing transitions and accepting them with a probability that enforces detailed balance. “Glauber dynamics” is a specific instance of Metropolis-Hastings where acceptance probabilities are derived directly from conditional Boltzmann weights. “Spectral gap” refers to the difference between the largest and second-largest eigenvalues of the classical transition matrix, which governs mixing time; its quantum analog determines the resolution required in phase estimation.

The phrase “circuit implementation” encompasses any decomposition of a unitary operator into a sequence of elementary quantum gates (e.g., CNOT, Toffoli, single-qubit rotations) executable on a quantum processor. “Unary representation” means encoding an N-valued classical variable using N qubits, where exactly one qubit is in state |1⟩ and the rest in |0⟩. “Boltzmann coin” denotes a controlled rotation whose angle depends on the energy difference induced by a proposed move, ensuring compliance with detailed balance at the quantum level. All methods described assume access to a quantum computer capable of executing multi-controlled gates and synthesizing single-qubit rotations to sufficient precision.

### III. Example Embodiments

Quantum walks offer a natural framework for accelerating classical random walks used in optimization. Szegedy’s method quantizes a reversible classical walk W by defining a unitary operator U_W = ΛW(RW†ΛW)RW†, where Λ is the swap operator and R is a reflection about a reference subspace. The eigenvalues of U_W are e±iθ_k, where cosθ_k equals the k-th eigenvalue λ_k of W. The steady state |π⟩ of the classical walk corresponds to the eigenstate of U_W with eigenvalue 1 (i.e., θ=0). The spectral gap δ of U_W scales as √Δ, where Δ is the classical spectral gap, enabling quadratic speedup in state preparation via quantum phase estimation.

To prepare |π⟩ when no good initial guess exists, adiabatic state preparation is employed: a sequence of walks W_0,…,W_L=W is defined, each with coherent stationary states |π_j⟩, such that |⟨π_j|π_{j+1}⟩|² ≥ 1−1/L. Repeated projective measurements in the eigenbasis of each U_{W_j} yield |π_L⟩ with constant probability after L steps, at total cost ∑_j (1/δ_j)·C, where C is the cost per walk operator.

The classical Metropolis-Hastings algorithm proposes a move y from x with symmetric probability T_yx and accepts it with probability A_yx = min(1, π(y)/π(x)). For Boltzmann distributions π(x) ∝ e^{−βE(x)}, this becomes A_yx = min(1, e^{−β(E(y)−E(x))}). Glauber dynamics uses A_yx = 1/(1 + e^{β(E(y)−E(x))}).

The core innovation lies in the circuit for U_W. Rather than implementing W—which requires arithmetic to uncompute rejected moves—the invention constructs a direct isometry. The system uses three registers: System (n qubits), Move (N qubits in unary), and Coin (1 qubit). The walk operator is decomposed as U = R F B V, where V prepares a uniform superposition over moves, F conditionally flips spins based on the move and coin, B applies the Boltzmann coin rotation, and R reflects about |0⟩_M|0⟩_C.

Move preparation V uses √SWAP gates in a binary tree to generate 1/√N ∑_j |j⟩_M when N is a power of two; otherwise, padding ensures N' = 2^⌈log₂N⌉ with trivial moves, incurring <√2 slowdown. Spin flip F applies up to N·c Toffoli gates (c = max spins flipped per move), or uses parallel CNOT trees for depth reduction. Reflection R implements a multi-controlled NOT via binary-tree ancilla computation, achieving depth O(log N). The Boltzmann coin B applies N conditional rotations R_j, each depending on a constant-sized neighborhood N_j of spins affected by move j. Each R_j requires O(2^{|N_j|}) multi-controlled rotations, but quantum signal processing (QSP) may reduce cost by approximating the acceptance function via Fourier series.

Heuristically, the quantum walk is deployed via two protocols: (1) Zeno with rewind—sequential projections with backtracking on failure—and (2) unitary evolution—applying U_{W_j} sequentially without measurement. Both are evaluated via total time to solution (TTS = t · log(1−δ)/log(1−p(t))), minimized over walk duration t.

Numerical tests on 1D Ising chains (n=4–16) show TTS scaling as n^0.42 (unitary) and n^0.39 (Zeno), surpassing the expected n^0.5. On sparse random Ising graphs (n=4–14, 3.5n edges), scaling is n^0.75 (unitary) and n^0.92 (Zeno), still sub-quadratic but consistent. Cost analysis shows V costs O(log N) depth, F costs O(1) depth with parallelization, R costs O(log N), and B dominates with O(N·2^k·log(1/ε)) T-gates for k-body moves. Simplifications arise when couplings J_l are discrete or the graph is sparse.

Example circuits use unary move encoding, binary-tree V, parallel F via copied coin qubits, and QSP-enhanced B. Improved layouts minimize ancilla usage and maximize gate parallelism. The Zeno algorithm uses L interpolation steps in β, while the unitary method applies U_{W_j} directly. Both conclude with computational basis measurement to extract candidate solutions.

### V. Further Example Embodiments

An alternative quantum walk procedure begins by preparing the move register in superposition via V. The Metropolis-Hastings rotation is implemented by evaluating the acceptance probability A_xy = min(1, e^{−βΔE}) and rotating the coin qubit accordingly; Glauber dynamics uses a different functional form. To prepare the move register, a uniform distribution over N moves is created in unary encoding. The state of the left (system) register is copied onto a right register using CNOTs to enable energy difference computation. A bit is conditionally flipped in the system register based on the move and coin state.

The quantities A_xy are evaluated by computing the local energy change ΔE from a subset of spins, and the coin register is rotated by an angle θ = arccos(√A_xy) or equivalent. After the spin flip and reflection, the move register is uncomputed by reversing V, ensuring no residual entanglement. The full transformation implements the desired walk operator without intermediate measurement.

Variations include using non-uniform move distributions, adaptive β schedules, or hybrid classical-quantum feedback loops. The move set M may include multi-spin flips, cluster updates, or problem-specific proposals. The reflection operator may be replaced by approximate reflections to reduce depth. The Boltzmann coin may employ lookup tables or piecewise polynomial approximations to minimize rotation synthesis cost.

### VI. Concluding Remarks

The disclosed technology provides a complete, arithmetic-free implementation of quantum walks for discrete optimization, significantly reducing circuit complexity compared to oracle-based approaches. By directly constructing the walk operator from physically motivated components and leveraging problem sparsity, the invention enables practical deployment on near-term quantum hardware. Heuristic protocols—especially the unitary adiabatic variant—demonstrate robust polynomial speedups in numerical experiments, sometimes exceeding theoretical predictions. The framework is extensible to other MCMC methods and problem classes, offering a scalable pathway toward quantum advantage in combinatorial optimization.
