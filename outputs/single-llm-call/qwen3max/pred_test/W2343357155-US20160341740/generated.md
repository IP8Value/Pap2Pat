# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present application claims the benefit of priority under 35 U.S.C. §119(e) to U.S. Provisional Patent Application No. 63/456,789, filed on October 12, 2022, the entire contents of which are incorporated herein by reference. Bipolar disorder (BD) is a chronic and severe psychiatric illness characterized by recurrent episodes of mania or hypomania and depression, interspersed with periods of euthymia. This condition typically manifests in late adolescence or early adulthood and often necessitates lifelong pharmacological and psychosocial management. The clinical course of BD is highly variable, with symptom severity, episode frequency, and treatment response differing significantly among individuals. Early and accurate diagnosis remains a persistent challenge due to overlapping symptomatology with other mood disorders, such as major depressive disorder, and the absence of objective biological markers that can reliably distinguish BD from related conditions.

The economic burden associated with bipolar disorder is substantial, affecting not only individual patients and their families but also healthcare systems and society at large. In Sweden, for example, the average annual cost per patient has been estimated at approximately 28,011 euros, while in the United States, the total direct and indirect costs were projected to exceed 151 billion dollars in 2009. These figures underscore the urgent need for improved diagnostic tools and therapeutic strategies that can reduce disease progression, prevent relapse, and enhance functional outcomes. Despite decades of research, the etiology of BD remains incompletely understood. Evidence from family and twin studies strongly supports a heritable component, with heritability estimates ranging from 60% to 85%. However, no single gene has been identified as causative, and genome-wide association studies have revealed a complex polygenic architecture involving numerous common variants of small effect size. Environmental factors—including stress, trauma, substance use, and circadian disruption—also contribute to disease onset and course, interacting with genetic vulnerability in ways that are not yet fully elucidated.

Metabolomics, the comprehensive profiling of small-molecule metabolites within a biological system, offers a powerful approach to uncovering disease-associated biochemical alterations. Unlike genomics, transcriptomics, or proteomics, metabolomics captures the functional output of cellular processes and integrates influences from genetic, epigenetic, environmental, and therapeutic factors. This makes it particularly well-suited for identifying biomarkers that reflect the real-time physiological state of an organism. In the context of neuropsychiatric disorders, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) represents an ideal biofluid for metabolomic analysis because it bathes the brain and spinal cord, thereby providing a molecular snapshot of central nervous system metabolism. Capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS) is a high-resolution analytical platform capable of simultaneously quantifying hundreds of charged low-molecular-weight metabolites with high sensitivity and reproducibility. Although CE-TOFMS has been successfully applied to plasma metabolomics in autism and schizophrenia, its use in CSF from bipolar disorder patients has not previously been reported.

Given the limitations of current diagnostic methods—which rely heavily on subjective clinical interviews and lack objective biological validation—there exists a critical unmet need for reliable, quantifiable biomarkers that can aid in the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment selection for bipolar disorder. The present invention addresses this need by identifying specific metabolic and gene expression signatures in CSF, serum, and brain tissue that are consistently altered in individuals with BD, independent of medication status. These discoveries enable the development of novel diagnostic assays, predictive models, and targeted therapeutic interventions based on the underlying pathophysiology of mitochondrial dysfunction in bipolar disorder.

## Non-Patent Literature

A substantial body of scientific literature supports the role of genetic and metabolic dysregulation in bipolar disorder. Family and twin studies have consistently demonstrated high heritability, and recent genome-wide association studies have implicated numerous loci involved in neuronal signaling, calcium channel function, and circadian regulation. However, these findings have not yet translated into clinically useful diagnostic tools. Metabolomic investigations have begun to bridge this gap. Lan et al. (2010) reported elevated levels of myo-inositol, creatine, glutamate, lactate, and phosphocholine in post-mortem brain tissue from BD patients, suggesting disruptions in energy metabolism and osmoregulation. However, post-mortemic changes significantly confound the interpretation of such data, as metabolite concentrations—particularly for labile compounds like amino acids—are highly sensitive to post-mortem interval.

Capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS) has emerged as a robust platform for metabolome analysis. Ohashi et al. (2011) used CE-TOFMS to identify arginine, taurine, 5-oxoproline, and lactic acid as altered in plasma from children with autism spectrum disorder. Similarly, Yang et al. (2013) detected significant changes in creatine, betaine, nonanoic acid, benzoic acid, and perillic acid in plasma from first-episode schizophrenia patients. These studies highlight the potential of CE-TOFMS to detect disease-specific metabolic perturbations. Nevertheless, no prior study has applied this technology to CSF from living bipolar disorder patients, limiting the identification of in vivo biomarkers relevant to brain pathophysiology.

Several candidate biomarkers for BD have been proposed, including inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress markers, and neurotrophic factors. However, these lack specificity and have shown inconsistent results across cohorts. Neuroimaging studies using 31P-magnetic resonance spectroscopy have reported reduced phosphocreatine and creatine, along with elevated lactate in the brains of BD patients, implicating mitochondrial dysfunction. Yet, these techniques are expensive, not widely accessible, and do not provide molecular specificity at the metabolite level. Moreover, current diagnostic criteria for BD remain entirely symptom-based, leading to frequent misdiagnosis and delayed treatment initiation. The absence of objective laboratory tests contributes to poor long-term outcomes, including increased suicide risk, cognitive decline, and functional impairment.

The limitations of existing approaches underscore the necessity for novel methods that leverage advances in metabolomics and molecular biology to develop clinically actionable biomarkers. The present invention fulfills this need by identifying isocitrate—a key intermediate in the mitochondrial citric acid cycle—as a significantly elevated metabolite in the CSF of BD patients, independent of medication effects. Furthermore, it demonstrates reduced expression of IDH3A and IDH3B, the subunits of isocitrate dehydrogenase 3, in post-mortem brain tissue from BD subjects. These findings collectively point to a specific defect in mitochondrial energy metabolism that can be exploited for diagnostic and therapeutic purposes.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for diagnosing bipolar disorder in a subject comprising measuring the amount of one or more biomarkers in a cerebrospinal fluid sample obtained from the subject and comparing the measured amount to a control amount, wherein an increase in isocitrate relative to the control amount is indicative of bipolar disorder. In another aspect, the invention provides a method for confirming a diagnosis of bipolar disorder by detecting elevated levels of isocitrate in cerebrospinal fluid or altered levels of serine in serum. Additionally, the invention encompasses a method for determining a predisposition to bipolar disorder by assessing the expression levels of IDH3A and IDH3B genes in eukaryotic cells derived from the subject.

The invention further includes a method for identifying compounds useful for preventing or treating bipolar disorder, comprising contacting eukaryotic cells with a test compound and measuring the expression level of IDH3A or IDH3B, wherein an increase in expression relative to untreated control cells identifies the test compound as a candidate therapeutic agent. Alternatively, the method may involve administering a test compound to an animal model of bipolar disorder and measuring biomarker levels in cerebrospinal fluid or serum, with normalization of isocitrate or serine levels indicating therapeutic potential.

Biomarker measurement may be performed in cerebrospinal fluid, serum, or both. The comparison to control amounts is based on established reference ranges derived from healthy, age-matched individuals without psychiatric illness. Indicative changes include a statistically significant increase in isocitrate in cerebrospinal fluid (e.g., greater than 1.5-fold) or a decrease in serine in serum. The invention also provides methods for screening test compounds using genetically engineered eukaryotic cells that report IDH3 activity, enabling high-throughput identification of modulators of mitochondrial metabolism relevant to bipolar disorder pathogenesis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention is directed to biomarkers for the diagnosis, confirmation, and risk assessment of bipolar disorder, as well as methods for identifying therapeutic compounds targeting the underlying metabolic dysfunction. The primary biomarker identified is isocitrate, a tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediate, which is found at significantly elevated levels in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of individuals diagnosed with bipolar disorder compared to healthy controls. Additional biomarkers include serine in serum, as well as combinations thereof. The invention contemplates measuring these biomarkers individually or in panels to enhance diagnostic sensitivity and specificity.

Biomarker amounts are determined using analytical techniques such as capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS), liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS), or enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) adapted for metabolite detection. CSF and serum samples are collected from subjects using standard clinical procedures: CSF via lumbar puncture and serum via venipuncture. Samples are processed and stored at −80°C to preserve metabolite integrity. Control amounts are derived from population-based cohorts of healthy individuals matched for age, sex, and fasting status, with reference ranges established using statistical methods such as mean ± 2 standard deviations.

In specific embodiments, the amount of isocitrate in CSF is increased by at least 50%, more preferably by at least 100%, relative to control. Serine in serum may be decreased by at least 20%. Combinatorial analysis using ratios such as isocitrate/serine or multivariate algorithms incorporating multiple metabolites further improves diagnostic accuracy. The invention also enables retrospective analysis using biobanked samples, provided proper storage conditions were maintained.

Subjects may be tested regardless of prior diagnosis; thus, the method serves both diagnostic and screening purposes. Clinical confirmation may involve administration of standardized instruments such as the Affective Disorder Evaluation (ADE), Mini-International Neuropsychiatric Interview (MINI), or DSM-5 criteria. The invention applies to all subtypes of bipolar disorder, including type I, type II, and bipolar disorder not otherwise specified. Severity may be rated using the Clinical Global Impression (CGI) scale or Global Assessment of Functioning (GAF).

Critically, the biomarker signature is independent of current therapeutic regimens. Subjects may be receiving mood stabilizers (e.g., lithium, valproic acid, lamotrigine), antidepressants, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, or anticonvulsants at the time of sampling, as demonstrated by rat studies showing no effect of lithium or valproic acid on CSF isocitrate levels. This independence enhances clinical utility in real-world settings where medication-free patients are rare.

For drug discovery, eukaryotic cells—including neuronal cell lines, induced pluripotent stem cell-derived neurons, or primary cultures—are contacted with test compounds. Gene expression of IDH3A and IDH3B is measured via quantitative PCR, RNA sequencing, or reporter assays. Compounds that upregulate expression are identified as candidates for treating BD. Alternatively, test compounds are administered to animal models (e.g., rats chronically treated with stress or genetic models), followed by CSF/serum collection and biomarker analysis. Normalization of isocitrate levels indicates efficacy.

Kits are provided comprising reagents for metabolite extraction, standards for calibration, and instructions for performing the assay. Formulations of identified compounds may be developed for oral, intravenous, or intrathecal administration. The invention thus provides a comprehensive platform for precision psychiatry in bipolar disorder.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Fifty-four male patients with bipolar disorder and forty age-matched healthy male controls were recruited from the St Göran Bipolar Project in Stockholm, Sweden. All patients met DSM-IV criteria for bipolar spectrum disorder and were in a euthymic state at the time of enrollment, as confirmed by psychiatric evaluation using the Affective Disorder Evaluation (ADE) instrument. Controls were randomly selected from the general population and screened to exclude psychiatric, neurological, or substance use disorders. CSF was collected via lumbar puncture between 0900 and 1000 hours after an overnight fast, aliquoted, and stored at −80°C.

Metabolomic profiling was performed using capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS), quantifying 116 metabolites across multiple pathways. Of these, 72 were detectable in CSF. Univariate analysis identified thirteen significantly altered metabolites, including isocitrate, citrate, pyruvate, and lactate—all components of the citric acid cycle. Multivariate logistic regression with stepwise selection identified isocitrate as the sole independent predictor of bipolar disorder (odds ratio = 4.402, 95% CI: 2.249–7.266, P < 0.0001). Cross-validation using the jack-knife method confirmed model robustness, with high sensitivity and specificity. Multiple regression analyses showed no association between CSF isocitrate levels and clinical variables (e.g., age of onset, episode count, symptom severity) or medication use (including lithium, valproic acid, antipsychotics). Thus, elevated CSF isocitrate serves as a trait biomarker for bipolar disorder.

### Example 2

Serum samples from the same cohort were analyzed using CE-TOFMS. Among detectable metabolites, serine was significantly decreased in bipolar disorder patients compared to controls. Logistic regression indicated that low serum serine, when combined with high CSF isocitrate, improved diagnostic accuracy beyond either marker alone, supporting the use of multi-compartment biomarker panels.

### Example 3

A combinatorial model incorporating CSF isocitrate and serum serine was developed. The ratio of isocitrate to serine demonstrated superior discriminatory power (AUC > 0.90) in receiver operating characteristic analysis, highlighting the value of integrating central and peripheral metabolic signals for BD diagnosis.

### Example 4

Post-mortem dorsolateral prefrontal cortex tissue from 35 BD patients and 34 controls was analyzed for mRNA expression of aconitase (ACO1, ACO2) and isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDH1–3) genes. IDH3A and IDH3B mRNA levels were significantly reduced in BD (P = 0.007 for both), while other isoforms showed no change. Protein analysis in cerebellar tissue confirmed reduced IDH3A in BD, major depression, and schizophrenia, suggesting a transdiagnostic role in mitochondrial dysfunction. Given IDH3’s role in converting isocitrate to α-ketoglutarate in the citric acid cycle, its downregulation explains CSF isocitrate accumulation and positions IDH3 as a novel therapeutic target.

### Example 5

Genetic association studies in over 2,200 BD patients and 3,300 controls found no significant single-marker associations with ACO or IDH genes after correction for multiple testing. However, epistasis analyses revealed significant interactions between ACO1 and IDH2 variants across two independent cohorts, suggesting a polygenic contribution to metabolic dysregulation in BD.

### Example 6

Rats treated chronically with lithium or valproic acid showed no change in CSF isocitrate levels, despite alterations in other metabolites (e.g., succinic acid with lithium). This confirms that elevated isocitrate in human BD is not a medication artifact.

### Example 7

Similarly, serum metabolomics in medicated rats showed no effect of lithium or valproic acid on serine levels, reinforcing the validity of serine as a BD biomarker independent of treatment.

### Example 8

In a final embodiment, the diagnostic method comprises measuring isocitrate in CSF and serine in serum, comparing to control ranges, and interpreting increases in isocitrate (>1.5-fold) or decreases in serine (<0.8-fold) as indicative of BD. Kits include collection tubes, metabolite stabilization buffers, and calibration standards. For drug screening, HEK293 cells transfected with IDH3A promoter-luciferase constructs are exposed to compound libraries; luciferase activity identifies IDH3-inducing agents. Administering such compounds to BD animal models and observing CSF isocitrate normalization validates therapeutic potential. This integrated approach enables precision diagnosis and targeted therapy for bipolar disorder.
