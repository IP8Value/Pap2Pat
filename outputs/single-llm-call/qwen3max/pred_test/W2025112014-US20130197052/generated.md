# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of medicinal chemistry and gastroenterology, specifically to novel tryptamine-derived compounds useful for the prevention and treatment of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)-induced gastropathy. More particularly, the invention provides a new class of hybrid molecules comprising conjugated tryptamine and antioxidant moieties that exhibit potent gastroprotective effects through dual mechanisms of mitochondrial reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging and intramitochondrial iron chelation. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing these compounds and methods for their use in mitigating gastric mucosal injury, restoring mitochondrial function, and accelerating the healing of NSAID-induced gastric lesions.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are among the most widely used therapeutic agents globally for the management of pain, inflammation, and fever. Despite their clinical utility, chronic or high-dose NSAID use is frequently associated with significant gastrointestinal toxicity, particularly gastropathy characterized by mucosal erosion, ulceration, and hemorrhage. This adverse effect arises primarily from the inhibition of cyclooxygenase (COX) enzymes, which suppresses the synthesis of cytoprotective prostaglandins in the gastric mucosa. However, emerging evidence indicates that COX-independent mechanisms also play a critical role in NSAID-induced gastric injury, notably involving mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress.

NSAID treatment, particularly with agents such as indomethacin, leads to the uncoupling of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation and the generation of excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS), including superoxide anion (O₂•⁻). This mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS) triggers a cascade of pathological events, including lipid peroxidation, protein carbonylation, thiol depletion, and ultimately, activation of the intrinsic apoptotic pathway. A key contributor to this oxidative damage is the release of free iron from mitochondrial iron-sulfur clusters, which catalyzes the Fenton reaction to produce highly toxic hydroxyl radicals. Concurrently, serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5HT), an endogenous monoamine present in the gastrointestinal tract, has been shown to exert concentration-dependent toxicity; while it may participate in mucosal repair at physiological levels, elevated concentrations can exacerbate oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage.

Current gastroprotective strategies include the co-administration of proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) such as omeprazole or H₂ receptor blockers like ranitidine. Although these agents reduce gastric acid secretion and provide symptomatic relief, they do not address the underlying mitochondrial pathology induced by NSAIDs. Moreover, long-term use of PPIs is associated with adverse effects including increased risk of bacterial infections (e.g., *Clostridium difficile*), collagenous colitis, subacute cutaneous lupus erythematosus, acute interstitial nephritis, and myopathy. Additionally, PPIs have been shown to exacerbate NSAID-induced small intestinal injury through alterations in gut microbiota. Existing antioxidants and iron chelators, such as gallic acid, vitamin E, or desferrioxamine, demonstrate limited efficacy due to poor bioavailability, lack of mitochondrial targeting, or insufficient potency at clinically relevant doses. Thus, there remains a significant unmet medical need for novel therapeutic agents that directly target the mitochondrial origins of NSAID-induced gastropathy without the drawbacks of current gastroprotective regimens.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

It is an objective of the present invention to provide novel compounds of general formula 1, which are hybrid molecules derived from the conjugation of tryptamine derivatives with antioxidant moieties, particularly gallic acid, through amide or ester linkages. These compounds are designed to possess enhanced mitochondrial targeting capability, superior antioxidant activity, and intramitochondrial iron-chelating properties. A further objective is to provide a new chemical scaffold for the treatment of gastropathy, particularly that induced by NSAIDs, which operates through a mechanism distinct from acid suppression and instead addresses the root cause of mitochondrial oxidative stress and apoptosis. The invention aims to overcome the limitations of existing gastroprotective agents by offering a therapeutically effective, mitochondria-targeted approach with improved safety and efficacy profiles.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides compounds of general formula 1, wherein a tryptamine moiety is covalently linked to an antioxidant pharmacophore, preferably gallic acid, via an amide bond. Representative compounds include SEGA (3a), TRGA (3b), MEGA (3c), GASA (4d), and dimeric serotonin derivative 6c, among others. These compounds exhibit potent antioxidant properties, as demonstrated by their ability to reduce ferric ions in the FRAP assay and scavenge DPPH radicals in a concentration-dependent manner. Critically, the compounds display anti-apoptotic activity by inhibiting the mitochondrial pathway of cell death, preserving mitochondrial transmembrane potential, and maintaining dehydrogenase activity. A key feature of the invention is the capacity of these compounds, particularly SEGA (3a), to chelate intramitochondrial free iron, thereby preventing iron-mediated amplification of oxidative stress via the Fenton reaction.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of general formula 1 and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent. Also provided is a method for treating gastropathy in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a compound of the invention in an effective amount. The effective amount is typically in the range that achieves a gastroprotective effect, with SEGA (3a) exhibiting an ED₅₀ value of 6.9 mg·kg⁻¹ body weight in a rat model of indomethacin-induced gastric injury. Administration may be performed via various routes, including oral, intraperitoneal, or intravenous, with intraperitoneal administration demonstrating significant efficacy in preclinical models. The compounds of the invention not only prevent the onset of NSAID-induced gastric mucosal damage but also accelerate the healing of pre-existing lesions, offering both prophylactic and therapeutic utility.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The compounds of the present invention were synthesized through well-established organic chemistry methodologies, primarily utilizing carbodiimide-mediated coupling reactions. Tryptamine derivatives, including 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin), tryptamine, and 5-methoxytryptamine, were conjugated with antioxidant acids such as gallic acid, 4-hydroxycinnamic acid, or indole-3-acetic acid. The synthesis of SEGA (3a) was achieved by coupling serotonin with 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid using 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) and 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (HOBt) in N,N-dimethylformamide, followed by debenzylation via hydrogenolysis over palladium on carbon to yield the final trihydroxy compound. Similarly, TRGA (3b) and MEGA (3c) were synthesized by substituting serotonin with tryptamine or 5-methoxytryptamine, respectively, in the same coupling protocol. GASA (4d), an ester-linked hybrid, was prepared by reacting gallic acid with serotonin in the presence of EDC and 4-dimethylaminopyridine (DMAP). The 5-hydroxy derivative 5c and dimeric serotonin derivative 6c were synthesized through analogous procedures involving selective protection/deprotection and oxidative coupling steps, respectively.

Biological evaluation revealed that these tryptamine derivatives possess significant antioxidant activity. In vitro assays, including the Ferric Reducing Antioxidant Power (FRAP) and 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical scavenging tests, demonstrated that SEGA (3a) exhibits superior reducing capacity and free radical quenching ability compared to its ester-linked counterpart GASA (4d) and other analogs. The gastroprotective effect of these derivatives was assessed in a well-established rat model of indomethacin-induced gastric injury. Pretreatment with SEGA (3a) resulted in a dose-dependent reduction in gastric lesion index, with complete mucosal protection observed at 50 mg·kg⁻¹. Histological examination confirmed the preservation of normal gastric architecture, with minimal epithelial cell shedding and absence of hemorrhagic erosions. Furthermore, SEGA (3a) demonstrated a remarkable mitochondrial protective effect, as evidenced by the maintenance of mitochondrial respiratory control ratio, transmembrane potential, and dehydrogenase activity in gastric mucosal cells exposed to indomethacin.

### Biological Effects

The antioxidant property of the tryptamine derivatives was quantitatively assessed using the FRAP assay, which measures the ability of a compound to reduce Fe(III)-TPTZ to the blue-colored Fe(II)-TPTZ complex, with absorbance monitored at 595 nm. SEGA (3a) exhibited a significantly higher FRAP value than other synthesized compounds, indicating robust electron-donating capacity. In the DPPH assay, SEGA (3a) caused a concentration-dependent decrease in DPPH radical absorbance at 517 nm, confirming its potent free radical scavenging activity. Histological studies of gastric tissue sections stained with hematoxylin and eosin revealed that SEGA (3a) pretreatment effectively rescued the gastric mucosa from indomethacin-induced damage, preserving glandular structure and surface epithelium integrity. In contrast, serotonin alone showed minimal gastroprotective effect and, at higher concentrations, contributed to mucosal toxicity.

SEGA (3a) was particularly effective in protecting against mitochondrial oxidative stress. Using the fluorescent probe MitoSOX, a significant reduction in intramitochondrial superoxide levels was observed in gastric mucosal cells pretreated with SEGA (3a) prior to indomethacin exposure. Similarly, staining with Phen Green SK demonstrated that SEGA (3a) prevented the accumulation of chelatable intramitochondrial iron, a critical factor in ROS amplification. This dual action—scavenging ROS and sequestering free iron—enabled SEGA (3a) to prevent oxidative stress-induced apoptosis, as confirmed by reduced caspase-9 and caspase-3 activities, normalized Bcl-2/Bax expression ratios, and decreased TUNEL-positive cells in both in vitro and in vivo models. Moreover, SEGA (3a) restored mitochondrial dysfunction by preserving the respiratory control ratio, mitochondrial transmembrane potential (ΔΨm), and metabolic activity, thereby maintaining cellular energy homeostasis and viability.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A general EDC coupling procedure for the formation of esters or amides was employed as follows: to a solution of 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid (12 mmol) in N,N-dimethylformamide, 1-hydroxybenzotriazole (12 mmol), triethylamine (72 mmol), and serotonin hydrochloride (14.4 mmol) were added, followed by the addition of EDC hydrochloride (14.4 mmol) at 0 °C. The reaction mixture was stirred at room temperature overnight, monitored by thin-layer chromatography for completion. The reaction was quenched with ice-cold water, extracted with ethyl acetate, washed with brine, dried over sodium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by silica gel column chromatography to yield the desired amide.

### Example 2

The general procedure of debenzylation (hydrogenolysis) was carried out by dissolving the protected compound in a methanol/chloroform mixture (5:1, 10 mL) and adding 10% palladium on carbon (catalytic amount). The mixture was hydrogenated at 40 psi for 2 hours, filtered through Celite to remove the catalyst, and the filtrate was evaporated in vacuo to dryness to afford the deprotected product.

### Example 3

Benzyl 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoate (1a) was prepared by esterification of gallic acid with benzyl alcohol under standard conditions. Hydrolysis yielded 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid (1b). Coupling of 1b with serotonin using the EDC/HOBt method afforded 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)-N-(2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)benzamide, which was characterized by NMR and mass spectrometry. Subsequent hydrogenolysis produced 3,4,5-trihydroxy-N-(2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)benzamide (SEGA, 3a), confirmed by spectral data showing disappearance of benzylic protons and appearance of phenolic OH signals.

### Example 9

Compound 2g was synthesized by coupling 4-hydroxycinnamic acid with 5-methoxytryptamine, and its structure was confirmed by ¹H NMR showing characteristic vinyl protons and methoxy signals. Compound 3c (MEGA) was obtained from gallic acid and 5-methoxytryptamine, with NMR data consistent with the expected substitution pattern. Compounds 2h, 2i, 4b, 4c, 4d, 5a, 6a, 6b, and 6c were similarly prepared and characterized. NMR data for all compounds were fully consistent with assigned structures. FRAP and DPPH assays were performed as described, with FRAP values calculated relative to ferrous sulfate standards. In animal models, gastric damage was induced by oral indomethacin (48 mg·kg⁻¹), and test compounds were administered intraperitoneally 30 minutes prior. Ulcer index was scored based on lesion size, and histological sections were prepared from formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded stomach tissues stained with hematoxylin-eosin. Healing studies involved post-injury administration of SEGA (50 mg·kg⁻¹), with stomachs harvested at 2, 4, 8, and 24 h. Mitochondria were isolated from gastric mucosa, and oxidative stress markers—including protein carbonyls, lipid peroxidation (TBARS), and total thiols—were quantified. Caspase-9 and -3 activities were measured using colorimetric kits. In cell culture, mitochondrial transmembrane potential was assessed with JC-1 dye, dehydrogenase activity via MTT reduction, intramitochondrial superoxide with MitoSOX, and free iron with Phen Green SK, visualized by fluorescence microscopy.

## ADVANTAGES OF THE INVENTION

The present invention offers several significant advantages over existing therapies for NSAID-induced gastropathy. The compounds of general formula 1, particularly SEGA (3a), provide targeted protection at the mitochondrial level, addressing the primary pathophysiological mechanism of NSAID toxicity rather than merely suppressing acid secretion. By simultaneously scavenging reactive oxygen species and chelating intramitochondrial free iron, these compounds disrupt the vicious cycle of oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction that leads to mucosal apoptosis. This dual-action mechanism results in superior gastroprotective efficacy, as evidenced by a lower ED₅₀ compared to standard antioxidants and iron chelators. Unlike proton pump inhibitors, the compounds of the invention do not alter gastric pH, thereby avoiding the associated risks of bacterial overgrowth, enteric infections, and small intestinal injury. Furthermore, the hybrid design enhances the bioavailability and mitochondrial delivery of the antioxidant payload, overcoming the pharmacokinetic limitations of natural polyphenols like gallic acid. The ability of SEGA (3a) to not only prevent but also accelerate the healing of established gastric lesions represents a unique therapeutic benefit. Finally, the synthetic accessibility and structural tunability of this new scaffold allow for the development of optimized derivatives with improved potency, selectivity, and safety profiles for clinical translation.
