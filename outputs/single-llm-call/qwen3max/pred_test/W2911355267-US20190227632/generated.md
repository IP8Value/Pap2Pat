# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of haptic feedback devices, specifically wearable systems configured to apply controlled mechanical stimuli to human fingertips for the purpose of investigating, modulating, or augmenting sensorimotor perception. More particularly, the invention relates to a wearable fingertip device capable of independently and concurrently delivering compressive (normal) and shear (tangential) forces to the skin of the fingertip, thereby simulating tactile inputs associated with object manipulation without requiring active force generation by the user. The device is engineered to enable precise replication of cutaneous mechanical deformation patterns that occur during natural grasping tasks, making it suitable for applications in neuroscience research, neurorehabilitation, virtual reality, teleoperation, and prosthetic sensory feedback. The technical domain further encompasses human-machine interfaces wherein somatosensory signals are artificially induced to probe or influence central nervous system processing related to body representation, proprioception, and motor control.

## BACKGROUND

Current haptic devices designed to deliver tactile feedback to the fingertips suffer from significant limitations that restrict their utility in both research and practical applications. Many existing systems rely on vibrotactile actuators, which primarily stimulate Pacinian corpuscles and fail to replicate the sustained, directional mechanical deformations—such as skin indentation and lateral stretch—that occur during real object interaction. Other approaches employ pneumatic or electromagnetic actuators but lack the precision, bandwidth, or independence required to separately control normal and tangential force components at the fingertip interface. Furthermore, conventional haptic gloves or exoskeletons often constrain natural finger motion, introduce inertial artifacts, or require active user participation (e.g., gripping a handle), thereby confounding the isolation of purely tactile contributions to perception. In neuroscientific studies aiming to dissect the relative roles of tactile versus non-tactile (proprioceptive and efference copy) signals in spatial estimation tasks, such as judging the relative position of thumb and index finger, these limitations prevent clean experimental dissociation of sensory modalities. Additionally, many wearable haptic systems are bulky, non-modular, or incapable of delivering calibrated, repeatable stimuli across multiple trials—critical requirements for perceptual psychophysics. There remains a pressing need for a compact, lightweight, and highly controllable fingertip-worn device that can independently apply compressive and shear forces with high fidelity, minimal latency, and without obstructing natural hand posture or requiring voluntary motor output during stimulation.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel wearable fingertip device specifically engineered to address the aforementioned limitations by enabling precise, independent, and concurrent application of compressive and shear forces to the volar surface of a human fingertip. The device comprises an upper assembly and a lower assembly that together form a modular housing adapted to conform to the distal phalanx. Compressive force is generated through a rack-and-pinion mechanism driven by a miniature motor, translating vertical displacement into normal pressure against the fingertip pad via a contact panel integrated with a force sensor. Shear force is applied through a laterally movable shear plate actuated by a second motorized pinion engaging a linear track, producing controlled skin stretch in the anterior-posterior direction. A key innovation lies in the decoupling of these two force modalities: the compressive mechanism operates orthogonally to the shear mechanism, allowing simultaneous delivery of normal load and tangential traction without mechanical interference. The wearable fingertip device is designed to be minimally intrusive, permitting passive stimulation while the user’s hand remains relaxed, thereby isolating tactile afferent input from proprioceptive and motor efference signals. This configuration enables unprecedented experimental control in studies of sensorimotor integration, particularly those investigating how cutaneous cues contribute to the perception of digit position and inter-fingertip distance. The device offers several advantages over prior art, including high spatial and temporal resolution of force delivery, real-time closed-loop force regulation via embedded sensors, ergonomic fit for prolonged wear, and compatibility with motion capture systems.  

A first aspect of the invention pertains to the mechanical architecture enabling compressive force application. This aspect includes a compressive motor mounted within an electronics housing, driving a compressive pinion that engages dual compressive racks affixed to an upper platform. As the motor rotates, the pinion translates the racks vertically, thereby moving the upper platform—and the attached contact panel—toward or away from the fingertip support. An angular sensor monitors motor rotation to infer displacement, while a force sensor embedded in the contact panel provides direct measurement of applied normal load, enabling feedback-controlled force regulation.  

A second aspect of the invention concerns the shear force delivery subsystem. This aspect features a shear motor that drives a shear pinion engaged with a linear gear track on the underside of a shear plate. The shear plate is constrained to move only along a single axis within a central slot of the lower base, ensuring pure tangential motion. The shear plate interfaces directly with the fingertip skin or via an intermediate compliant layer, transmitting lateral force without inducing torque or off-axis loading. An angular sensor coupled to the shear motor allows precise tracking of shear displacement and inferred force magnitude.  

A third aspect of the invention integrates both force modalities into a unified, wearable form factor. This aspect includes a finger support structure comprising a fingertip cradle and proximal anchoring elements that stabilize the device on the digit without restricting blood flow or causing discomfort. The upper and lower assemblies are joined via alignment features that maintain structural integrity while permitting independent actuation paths. Electronics, including motor drivers, sensors, and a microcontroller, are housed in a compact compartment adjacent to the finger base, connected via flexible wiring to minimize motion artifacts. This integrated design ensures that compressive and shear stimuli can be delivered in any combination—simultaneously or sequentially—with millisecond-level synchronization, facilitating complex haptic protocols mimicking natural manipulation dynamics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The following detailed description sets forth specific embodiments of the invention, though it will be understood by those skilled in the art that numerous modifications and alternatives may be made without departing from the scope of the claims. The term “compressive force” as used herein refers to a mechanical load applied perpendicularly to the plane of the fingertip skin surface, simulating the normal component of grip force during object contact. The term “shear force” denotes a tangential load parallel to the skin surface, replicating the lateral friction or skin stretch experienced during sliding or torque application. Relative terms such as “upper,” “lower,” “front,” and “rear” are defined with respect to the anatomical orientation of the fingertip when the device is worn, with “upper” corresponding to the dorsal side and “lower” to the palmar support structure.  

The wearable fingertip device, generally designated as 10, consists of an upper assembly 12 and a lower assembly 14. The upper assembly 12 includes an upper platform 16 that houses the compressive force provision system. This system comprises dual compressive racks that pass through apertures in the upper platform 16 and engage a compressive pinion driven by a compressive motor 28. Rotation of the compressive motor 28 causes vertical translation of the compressive racks, thereby moving the upper platform 16 toward or away from the fingertip. An angular sensor 30 coupled to the compressive motor 28 provides rotational feedback for position control. The upper assembly 12 further includes a finger support 32 with a fingertip support 34 that cradles the distal phalanx, and an electronics housing 36 containing power and control circuitry.  

The lower assembly 14 features a lower base 18 with a central slot 52 that accommodates the shear plate 20. Front and rear sidewalls 54a, 54b define the longitudinal boundaries of the lower base 18, while front and rear retaining members 56a, 56b secure internal components. The shear plate 20 is positioned within the central slot 52 and constrained to linear motion along the anterior-posterior axis by guide rails or low-friction bearings. A shear motor, not shown in all figures but analogous to the compressive motor 28, drives a shear pinion that engages a rack on the shear plate 20, enabling controlled lateral displacement. An angular sensor monitors shear motor rotation for precise actuation.  

Compressive racks translate vertically to adjust the gap between the upper platform 16 and the shear plate 20, thereby modulating normal force on the fingertip placed between them. A contact panel mounted on the underside of the upper platform 16 incorporates a force sensor to measure actual compressive load, enabling closed-loop control. The shear plate 20 interfaces directly with the fingertip skin, transmitting tangential force during actuation. Both motors operate independently, allowing concurrent application of normal and shear forces.
