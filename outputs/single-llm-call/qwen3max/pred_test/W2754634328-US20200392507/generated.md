# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and methods for the treatment of fibrotic disorders, particularly hypertrophic scarring of the skin. More specifically, the invention provides novel compositions comprising small interfering RNA (siRNA) molecules targeting transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), formulated with a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) carrier to form stable nanoparticles suitable for intradermal or intrascar administration. The invention further encompasses methods for reducing the size of hypertrophic scars, down-regulating pro-fibrotic factors, and inducing apoptosis in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts through the localized delivery of these siRNA-loaded nanoparticles. The disclosed compositions and methods represent a synergistic therapeutic approach that simultaneously silences two key mediators of fibrosis—TGF-β1 and COX-2—thereby disrupting multiple interconnected pathways involved in excessive extracellular matrix deposition, fibroblast activation, and impaired resolution of wound healing.

## BACKGROUND

Hypertrophic scar reduction and management remain significant unmet clinical needs due to the complex pathophysiology of fibrosis and the limited efficacy of existing interventions. Hypertrophic scars arise from a dysregulated wound healing process characterized by prolonged inflammation, hypercellularity, excessive vascularization, and pathological accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins—primarily collagens—deposited by activated fibroblasts and myofibroblasts. Unlike normal scars, which mature and flatten over time, hypertrophic scars persist as raised, rigid, and often symptomatic lesions that can cause functional impairment and psychological distress. The underlying mechanism involves a failure to restore homeostasis between ECM synthesis and degradation, driven in part by sustained signaling through pro-fibrotic mediators.

Fibrosis, the pathological hallmark of hypertrophic scarring, is defined by the aberrant and persistent deposition of connective tissue components in response to injury. In dermal wounds, this process is orchestrated by fibroblasts, which proliferate, differentiate into contractile myofibroblasts expressing α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), and secrete excessive collagen types I and III. These cells are normally eliminated via apoptosis during the remodeling phase of wound healing; however, in hypertrophic scars, they exhibit resistance to apoptotic signals, leading to their pathological persistence. Central to this dysregulation is transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1), a master regulator of fibrogenesis that promotes fibroblast proliferation, myofibroblast differentiation, and collagen production while suppressing matrix metalloproteinases responsible for ECM turnover.

Current treatments for hypertrophic scars—including intralesional corticosteroids, surgical excision, laser therapy, pressure garments, silicone sheeting, and cryotherapy—offer only modest and inconsistent benefits, often requiring repeated interventions and carrying risks of recurrence or adverse effects. Emerging molecular therapies targeting TGF-β1 signaling (e.g., neutralizing antibodies, receptor kinase inhibitors) or other fibrotic mediators have shown promise in preclinical models but face challenges related to delivery, specificity, and systemic toxicity. Notably, RNA interference (RNAi)-based strategies using siRNA hold potential for precise gene silencing; however, the clinical translation of siRNA therapeutics has been hindered by poor cellular uptake, rapid degradation, and inefficient delivery to target tissues such as dense scar tissue.

This limitation underscores the critical need for new treatment modalities that not only target key fibrotic pathways with high specificity but also achieve effective local delivery without systemic exposure. The present invention addresses this need by combining dual-targeted siRNA molecules against TGF-β1 and COX-2 with an advanced nanoparticle delivery system based on a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer, enabling potent, localized, and sustained gene silencing within scar tissue. This approach leverages the synergistic interplay between TGF-β1 and COX-2 pathways—both of which are overexpressed in human hypertrophic scars—to disrupt fibrotic signaling at multiple nodes and promote the resolution of established scars through induction of fibroblast apoptosis.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel pharmaceutical composition comprising small interfering RNA (siRNA) molecules specifically designed to silence the expression of transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2), formulated in association with a histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) to form stable, biocompatible nanoparticles suitable for intradermal or intrascar administration. The siRNA molecules are double-stranded RNA oligonucleotides, typically 19 to 25 nucleotides in length, engineered to hybridize with complementary sequences in the messenger RNA (mRNA) transcripts of human TGF-β1 and COX-2 genes, thereby triggering sequence-specific degradation of the target mRNA via the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). In preferred embodiments, the siRNA duplexes are blunt-ended 25-mers targeting conserved regions of both human and murine TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNAs, enabling cross-species activity for preclinical validation and clinical translation.

The construction of these siRNA molecules follows standard synthetic protocols, wherein sense and antisense strands are chemically synthesized and annealed to form duplexes. To enhance stability, reduce immunogenicity, and improve pharmacokinetic properties, the siRNA molecules may be modified with chemical alterations such as 2′-O-methyl, 2′-fluoro, or phosphorothioate linkages at specific positions, particularly at the 3′ overhangs or terminal nucleotides, without compromising silencing efficacy. The length of the siRNA molecules is optimized to balance potency and specificity, with 21–25 nucleotide duplexes demonstrating superior target knockdown and reduced off-target effects compared to shorter or longer constructs.

The relative amounts of siRNA molecules and HKP copolymer are carefully controlled to achieve optimal nanoparticle formation and biological activity. In a preferred embodiment, the mass ratio of HKP to total siRNA payload is approximately 4:1 (w/w), corresponding to an N/P ratio that facilitates efficient electrostatic condensation of the negatively charged siRNA by the cationic HKP polymer. This ratio yields nanoparticles with an average hydrodynamic diameter of about 150 nanometers and a zeta potential of approximately +40 millivolts, characteristics that promote cellular uptake, endosomal escape, and sustained release of siRNA within target cells. The nanoparticles self-assemble spontaneously upon mixing aqueous solutions of HKP and siRNA under mild conditions, forming stable colloidal suspensions that can be lyophilized for long-term storage and reconstituted without loss of activity.

A method for identifying effective siRNA molecules is also provided, involving in silico design of candidate sequences followed by empirical screening in human cell lines—such as prostate adenocarcinoma PC3 cells or primary human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts—for silencing potency and cytotoxicity. Candidate siRNAs are transfected using standard reagents (e.g., Lipofectamine 2000), and target gene knockdown is quantified after 48 hours by quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). The most potent and well-tolerated sequences are selected for further development. Evaluation in animal wound models, particularly human hypertrophic scar tissue implanted subcutaneously in immunodeficient nude mice, confirms in vivo efficacy, biodistribution, and duration of action.

Preferred animal models include the human hypertrophic scar implant model and the human full-thickness skin graft model on nude mice, both of which recapitulate key features of human fibrotic pathology, including elevated TGF-β1 and COX-2 expression, myofibroblast infiltration, and excessive collagen deposition. The siRNA sequences are designed to target homologous regions in human, mouse, and porcine genes, ensuring translational relevance. The composition may contain multiple siRNA molecules—specifically, at least one siRNA targeting TGF-β1 and at least one targeting COX-2—administered simultaneously to achieve synergistic anti-fibrotic effects not attainable with single-gene silencing.

Pharmaceutical compositions for administering to mammals comprise the siRNA-HKP nanoparticles suspended in a pharmaceutically acceptable aqueous vehicle suitable for intradermal injection. The carrier, HKP, is a biodegradable polypeptide composed of repeating histidine and lysine residues arranged in branched architectures (e.g., H3K4b, featuring a lysine core with four branches of histidine-lysine repeats), which confers pH-dependent buffering capacity to facilitate endosomal escape and cytosolic delivery of siRNA. Nanoparticle formation occurs via electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions between the cationic HKP and anionic siRNA, yielding monodisperse particles stable for at least 12 months when lyophilized and stored at 4°C.

The invention further provides a method for down-regulating pro-fibrotic factors in tissue, comprising administering the siRNA-HKP composition to a site of fibrosis, such as a hypertrophic scar. This leads to simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2, resulting in reduced expression of downstream effectors including α-SMA, collagen I (Col1A1), and collagen III (Col3A1). Additionally, a method for activating apoptosis in fibroblasts and myofibroblasts is disclosed, wherein administration of the composition induces programmed cell death in these pathological cells, measurable by techniques such as TUNEL assay, Annexin V/propidium iodide staining, or electron microscopy showing characteristic apoptotic morphology.

A method for reducing the size of hypertrophic scars involves direct intrascar injection of the composition, typically at a dose of 20 μg siRNA per 50 μL per cm³ of scar tissue, administered in multiple aliquots across the lesion and repeated at 5-day intervals. Delivery is localized to minimize systemic exposure, and dosages are calibrated to achieve >40% knockdown of target genes with microgram-level siRNA doses—significantly lower than those required by competing oligonucleotide therapeutics. This approach effectively reduces scar volume, collagen content (as measured by hydroxyproline levels), and cellular density while promoting tissue remodeling and resolution of fibrosis.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) duplexes targeting human TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA sequences were designed as 25-nucleotide blunt-ended molecules based on conserved coding regions. Eight candidate siRNAs per target gene were initially screened in human PC3 cells transfected with Lipofectamine 2000. After 48 hours, total RNA was extracted and subjected to quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR) to assess target gene silencing. The most potent siRNAs—designated TGF-β1siRNA and COX-2siRNA—achieved greater than 70% knockdown of their respective mRNAs without significant cytotoxicity. Notably, cross-silencing was observed: COX-2siRNA reduced TGF-β1 expression by approximately 40%, and TGF-β1siRNA suppressed COX-2 expression by a similar margin, indicating regulatory crosstalk between the two pathways. When used in combination, the dual siRNA treatment produced additive or synergistic suppression of both targets in primary human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts, confirming the feasibility of simultaneous gene silencing.

### Example 2: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions Resulted in Activation of Apoptosis of Fibroblasts/Myofibroblasts

To investigate the cellular consequences of dual gene silencing, human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts were transfected with TGF-β1siRNA, COX-2siRNA, or the combination. Electron microscopy revealed that only the combination treatment induced classic apoptotic features, including chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, and plasma membrane blebbing. Flow cytometry using Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide staining demonstrated a marked increase in early and late apoptotic cells—approximately 35% of the population—following combination treatment, compared to less than 10% with either siRNA alone or control. Morphologically, treated fibroblasts exhibited reduced cell density and elongated, narrow shapes indicative of cytoskeletal collapse. Immunofluorescence staining confirmed a significant reduction in α-SMA protein expression, consistent with loss of myofibroblast phenotype. These findings establish that concurrent inhibition of TGF-β1 and COX-2 uniquely triggers fibroblast apoptosis, a key mechanism for resolving fibrotic tissue.

### Example 3. HKP Enhances siRNA Delivery Into Human Hypertrophic Scar

Biodegradable histidine-lysine polypeptides (HKP), specifically the H3K4b variant, were selected as a delivery vehicle due to their proven ability to form stable nanoparticles with siRNA. Mixing HKP and siRNA at a 4:1 (w/w) ratio in aqueous solution yielded nanoparticles with an average diameter of 150 nm and zeta potential of +40 mV, as confirmed by dynamic light scattering and scanning electron microscopy. These nanoparticles remained stable for over 12 months when lyophilized and reconstituted without aggregation. In a human hypertrophic scar implant model in nude mice, intrascar injection of Alexa Fluor-labeled siRNA packaged in HKP showed sustained presence in scar tissue for at least 48 hours, whereas naked siRNA dispersed rapidly and became undetectable by 24 hours. qRT-PCR analysis confirmed dose-dependent silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2, with maximal knockdown observed at day 5 post-injection, demonstrating that HKP significantly enhances siRNA retention, bioavailability, and functional delivery to fibrotic tissue.

### Example 4. HKP-Packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA Reduces Size of Human Hypertrophic Scar

Human hypertrophic scar tissues, confirmed to overexpress TGF-β1 and COX-2 relative to normal skin, were implanted subcutaneously into nude mice. Four weeks post-implantation, when fibrotic markers peaked, mice received intrascar injections of HKP-packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA (20 μg/50 μL/cm³) at five sites per scar, repeated every five days for three doses. By day 28 post-treatment, scar volume was reduced by approximately 45% compared to untreated controls. qRT-PCR of harvested tissue showed significant downregulation not only of TGF-β1 and COX-2 but also of α-SMA and Col1A1, confirming broad anti-fibrotic effects. Histological analysis revealed decreased cellularity and collagen density, supporting the therapeutic efficacy of dual-targeted siRNA delivered via HKP nanoparticles in reversing established human hypertrophic scars.

### Example 5. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Reduces Size of Human Skin Grafts

In a complementary model, full-thickness human skin grafts were placed on nude mice and allowed to heal, during which hypertrophic-like scarring developed. Treatment with HKP-packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA, initiated four weeks post-grafting using the same dosing regimen as in Example 4, resulted in a 40% reduction in graft thickness and area by day 28. Molecular analysis confirmed significant silencing of TGF-β1, COX-2, α-SMA, and Col1A1, mirroring results from the scar implant model. This demonstrates that the therapeutic approach is effective not only in pre-formed scars but also in preventing or mitigating scar formation during active wound healing, broadening its clinical applicability to both prophylactic and therapeutic settings.

### Example 6. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Demonstrates a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Mechanism of Action

To elucidate the mechanism underlying scar reduction, hydroxyproline content—a quantitative marker of total collagen—was measured in treated versus control tissues from both human scar implants and skin grafts. STP705 (the dual siRNA formulation) treatment led to a significant decrease in hydroxyproline levels, confirming reduced collagen deposition. Histological staining (H&E and Masson’s trichrome) showed thinner epidermal layers, reduced dermal thickness, and less densely packed collagen fibers in treated samples. Immunohistochemistry revealed diminished expression of pro-fibrotic markers including α-SMA, VEGF, and CD31, indicating suppression of myofibroblast activation, angiogenesis, and inflammation. These data collectively demonstrate that the composition exerts a multi-faceted anti-fibrotic effect beyond simple gene knockdown, fundamentally altering the scar microenvironment toward normalization.

### Example 7. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Induced Apoptotic Activities of the Fibroblast/Myofibroblasts In Vivo

Apoptosis of fibroblasts in vivo was assessed using the TUNEL (terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling) assay on tissue sections from STP705-treated human scar implants. Treated samples exhibited a marked increase in TUNEL-positive nuclei within the dermal compartment, particularly in areas rich in spindle-shaped fibroblasts. Quantitative image analysis confirmed a statistically significant elevation in apoptotic cell counts—approximately three-fold higher than in control tissues. This in vivo validation corroborates the in vitro findings and establishes fibroblast apoptosis as a central mechanism by which dual TGF-β1/COX-2 silencing resolves fibrosis, offering a previously unrecognized therapeutic axis for scar treatment.

### Example 8. HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Serves as a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Therapeutic Agent

Collectively, the data support the designation of HKP-packaged TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs (STP705) as a novel anti-fibrotic therapeutic agent. Its dual-target design exploits the interdependence of TGF-β1 and COX-2 signaling, achieving synergistic suppression of fibrogenesis at lower siRNA doses than monotherapies. The HKP delivery system enables efficient, localized, and sustained action with minimal systemic exposure. The induction of fibroblast apoptosis represents a paradigm-shifting mechanism—rather than merely inhibiting collagen production, the therapy actively eliminates the primary effector cells of fibrosis. This siRNA-based approach offers a clinically viable, potent, and mechanistically distinct strategy for the treatment of hypertrophic scars and potentially other fibrotic disorders.

## Discussion

The dermal wound healing process is a tightly regulated cascade involving inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling phases, orchestrated by a network of cytokines, growth factors, and structural cells. Fibroblasts are central to this process, responsible for synthesizing and organizing the extracellular matrix that restores tissue integrity. Under normal conditions, fibroblasts either revert to a quiescent state or undergo apoptosis once healing is complete. However, in pathological states such as hypertrophic scarring, persistent inflammatory signals—particularly TGF-β1—drive fibroblasts to differentiate into myofibroblasts, which resist apoptosis and deposit excessive collagen, leading to fibrosis.

Regulation of fibroblast proliferation, activation, and apoptosis is therefore a critical determinant of scar outcome. The present study demonstrates that simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 disrupts this pathological loop. Both TGF-β1 and COX-2 are significantly overexpressed in human hypertrophic scar tissue compared to normal skin, validating their selection as therapeutic targets. While TGF-β1 is a well-established driver of fibrosis, the role of COX-2 is more nuanced; although some in vitro studies suggest anti-fibrotic effects of its product PGE2, in vivo evidence from fetal wound healing—where low COX-2 correlates with scarless repair—supports a pro-fibrotic role in early wound responses. Our data confirm that COX-2 modulates TGF-β signaling, as COX-2 knockdown reduces phosphorylation of SMAD2/3, the canonical downstream effectors of TGF-β1.

The HKP (siRNA) nanoparticle formulation is pivotal to the success of this approach. Traditional siRNA delivery faces formidable barriers in skin, including enzymatic degradation, poor penetration, and inefficient cellular uptake. HKP nanoparticles overcome these by providing protection, enhancing cellular internalization via endocytosis, and facilitating endosomal escape through the proton sponge effect of histidine residues. This results in potent gene silencing at microgram doses—orders of magnitude lower than those used in clinical trials of other oligonucleotide agents like EXC001 or RXI109—suggesting a superior therapeutic index.

The therapeutic benefits of dual silencing are evident: only the combination, not individual siRNAs, induces fibroblast apoptosis both in vitro and in vivo. This synergy likely arises from concurrent disruption of survival signals mediated by both pathways. The resulting downregulation of α-SMA, collagens, and hydroxyproline, coupled with increased apoptosis, leads to significant scar size reduction in human tissue xenografts. The mechanism of action—activating apoptosis in pathological fibroblasts—represents a novel anti-fibrotic strategy, distinct from mere inhibition of matrix synthesis. Thus, the present invention proposes a clinically translatable, siRNA-based therapeutic approach that targets the root cellular cause of fibrosis, offering new hope for patients burdened by hypertrophic scars.

## Materials and Methods

### siRNA Sequence Design and Selection

Twenty-five-nucleotide blunt-ended siRNA duplexes targeting homologous regions of human, mouse, and porcine TGF-β1 or COX-2 mRNA were designed using bioinformatic tools to ensure cross-species reactivity and minimal off-target effects. Eight candidate siRNAs per gene were synthesized and screened in human PC3 cells. Cells were seeded in 6-well plates and transfected with siRNA-Lipofectamine 2000 complexes. After 48 hours, total RNA was isolated and analyzed by qRT-PCR. The siRNA sequences exhibiting the highest silencing efficiency (>70% knockdown) and lowest cytotoxicity were selected for subsequent experiments and designated as TGF-β1siRNA and COX-2siRNA.

### Growth Inhibition In Vitro

Primary human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 20% fetal bovine serum. For growth inhibition assays, cells were treated with HKP alone, HKP loaded with TGF-β1siRNA, COX-2siRNA, or the combination at a final concentration of 10 μg/mL HKP and 5 μg/mL siRNA. After 48 hours, cell viability and apoptosis were assessed using Annexin V-FITC/propidium iodide staining followed by flow cytometry (FACS). Cell cycle analysis was performed by ethanol fixation and propidium iodide staining. Results confirmed that only the dual siRNA combination significantly induced apoptosis without affecting cell cycle progression in non-target cells.

### Nanoparticle Preparation

Histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP), specifically the H3K4b variant, was used to formulate siRNA nanoparticles. HKP and siRNA were mixed in sterile water at a 4:1 (w/w) ratio, vortexed gently, and incubated at room temperature for 30 minutes to allow self-assembly. The resulting nanoparticles had an average size of 150 nm and zeta potential of +40 mV, as measured by dynamic light scattering. Formulations were either used immediately or lyophilized with cryoprotectants for long-term storage at 4°C, retaining full activity upon reconstitution.

### Mouse

Eight-week-old male nude mice (nu/nu Balb/c) were obtained from the Center for Experimental Animals in Shanghai, China. All procedures were approved by the Independent Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital and conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines for humane animal care. Mice were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions with ad libitum access to food and water.

### Human Hypertrophic Scar and Skin Tissue Implant Models

Human hypertrophic scar tissue was obtained from surgical excisions with informed consent, trimmed of subcutaneous fat, and cut into 2 cm² pieces. These were implanted subcutaneously on the dorsum of anesthetized nude mice and sutured to the deep fascia. Similarly, full-thickness human skin grafts (2 cm × 1.6 cm) from reduction mammoplasty specimens were grafted onto excised sites on mouse backs, secured with sutures, and covered with sterile dressings for two weeks. Both models were allowed to engraft for four weeks before initiating treatment.

### Therapeutic Evaluation with the Human Tissue Implant Models

Four weeks post-implantation, mice received intrascar or intragraft injections of HKP-packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA at 20 μg/50 μL/cm³, divided into five aliquots per lesion, administered every five days for three doses. Scar size was monitored by caliper measurements and imaging. At designated endpoints, tissues were harvested for qRT-PCR, Western blotting, histology, hydroxyproline assay, and TUNEL staining to evaluate molecular, structural, and cellular responses to treatment.

### Statistical Analysis

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) for in vitro experiments and mean ± standard error (SE) for in vivo studies. Statistical significance between two groups was determined by Student’s t-test. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 20.
