# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of molecular oncology and cellular neurobiology, particularly concerning the identification and functional characterization of secretory granules in glial astrocytes and their pathological relevance in brain cancers such as glioblastoma multiforme. More specifically, the invention relates to the discovery that astrocytes—traditionally not classified as classical secretory cells—possess bona fide secretory granules containing granin family proteins and inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptors (IP3Rs), thereby establishing these organelles as major IP3-sensitive intracellular calcium stores. This finding provides a novel mechanistic basis for understanding regulated exocytosis in astrocytes and offers new diagnostic and therapeutic avenues for cancers characterized by aberrant secretory granule biogenesis.

In the related art, glial cells, particularly astrocytes, have long been considered supportive elements in the central nervous system, primarily involved in maintaining ionic homeostasis, metabolic support, and structural integrity. However, accumulating evidence over the past two decades has redefined astrocytes as active participants in intercellular signaling through the regulated release of gliotransmitters such as glutamate, ATP, D-serine, neuropeptide Y (NPY), and atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP). These signaling molecules are stored in vesicular compartments and released in a calcium-dependent manner, implicating astrocytes in neuron-glia communication and synaptic modulation.

Astrocytes engage in cellular communication via two principal types of secretory vesicles: small translucent synaptic-like vesicles and large dense-core vesicles (LDCVs). While small vesicles are thought to store and release small molecules like glutamate and ATP, LDCVs have been implicated in the storage and regulated secretion of larger peptides and proteins, including NPY and ANP. Despite this functional distinction, the molecular identity and subcellular origin of LDCVs in astrocytes remained unclear until recently.

Exocytotic secretory vesicles in astrocytes operate under a Ca²⁺-dependent regulatory pathway, wherein increases in cytoplasmic Ca²⁺ concentration ([Ca²⁺]c) trigger vesicle fusion with the plasma membrane and subsequent release of cargo. This process is tightly coupled to intracellular Ca²⁺ mobilization, which is predominantly mediated by the phospholipase C (PLC)/inositol trisphosphate (IP3) signaling cascade. Upon receptor activation, PLC generates IP3, which binds to IP3 receptors (IP3Rs) on intracellular Ca²⁺ stores, leading to Ca²⁺ efflux into the cytosol.

Historically, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) was presumed to be the primary IP3-sensitive Ca²⁺ store in all cell types. However, recent studies in neuroendocrine cells have demonstrated that secretory granules—not the ER—constitute the dominant IP3-responsive Ca²⁺ reservoir, contributing over 70% of IP3-induced Ca²⁺ release. Secretory granules in such cells contain exceptionally high concentrations of Ca²⁺ (up to ~40 mM), far exceeding those in other organelles, and harbor the majority of cellular IP3R isoforms, particularly with enhanced sensitivity to IP3 compared to ER-localized channels.

Within astrocytes, secretory granules were not previously recognized due to the absence of definitive molecular markers. The granin family of proteins—including chromogranin A (CGA), chromogranin B (CGB), and secretogranin II (SgII)—are well-established constituents of secretory granules in neuroendocrine and endocrine cells. These acidic, soluble proteins serve dual roles as prohormone precursors and high-capacity, low-affinity Ca²⁺-binding proteins, enabling granules to sequester millimolar concentrations of Ca²⁺. Moreover, chromogranins directly interact with IP3Rs at the acidic intragranular pH (~5.5), potentiating channel activity and facilitating rapid Ca²⁺ release upon IP3 stimulation.

Critically, secretory granules function not only as storage depots for bioactive molecules but also as dynamic signaling organelles. Their enrichment in IP3Rs and Ca²⁺ renders them uniquely suited to act as primary sensors of IP3 signals, initiating localized Ca²⁺ microdomains that can trigger exocytosis even in the absence of extracellular Ca²⁺ influx. This mechanism has been validated in pancreatic β-cells, mast cells, and chromaffin cells, where granule-derived Ca²⁺ is sufficient to drive hormone or mediator release.

Patents and publications, including U.S. Patent Nos. 6,218,123 and 7,452,689, describe the use of granin proteins as biomarkers in neuroendocrine tumors, while scientific literature (e.g., *Journal of Cell Biology*, 2005; *Nature Neuroscience*, 2010) details Ca²⁺ dynamics in astrocytes. However, none disclose the presence of authentic secretory granules in astrocytes or their role as IP3-sensitive Ca²⁺ stores. The current invention bridges this gap by demonstrating, for the first time, that human astrocytes contain LDCVs that are molecularly and functionally equivalent to secretory granules of neuroendocrine cells, expressing CGB, SgII, and all three IP3R isoforms.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THIS INVENTION

The present invention introduces a paradigm-shifting approach to cancer treatment by targeting granulogenic factors—proteins essential for the biogenesis and maintenance of secretory granules—in cancers exhibiting aberrant secretory granule formation, particularly glioblastoma multiforme and other neuroendocrine-like brain tumors. The motivation for developing biomolecule-based therapeutics stems from the discovery that secretory granules in neoplastic astrocytes are not merely vestigial structures but active contributors to tumor pathophysiology through dysregulated Ca²⁺ signaling and gliotransmitter release.

Granulogenic factors, as defined herein, include chromogranin B (CGB), secretogranin II (SgII), and associated proteins that facilitate the assembly, acidification, cargo loading, or membrane trafficking of secretory granules. The objects of the invention are threefold: (1) to provide methods for screening agents that modulate the expression or function of granulogenic factors; (2) to develop pharmaceutical compositions that inhibit secretory granule formation or function in cancer cells; and (3) to establish diagnostic kits for identifying cancers characterized by elevated granulogenic factor expression.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for screening a candidate substance for anti-cancer activity by assessing its effect on granulogenic factor gene expression. The method comprises contacting a test cell—preferably a human glioblastoma cell line such as U87MG or primary astrocyte-derived tumor cells—with the test substance under conditions suitable for cell viability, followed by measurement of the expression level of at least one granulogenic factor gene, such as *CHGB* (encoding CGB) or *SCG2* (encoding SgII).

Expression levels may be quantified using any standard molecular biology technique. In a preferred embodiment, reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) is employed. Total RNA is extracted from treated and control cells using TRIzol or equivalent reagents, reverse-transcribed into cDNA using oligo(dT) or random primers, and amplified with gene-specific primers. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) using SYBR Green or TaqMan probes allows precise determination of transcript abundance relative to housekeeping genes such as *GAPDH* or *ACTB*.

Alternatively, immunoassay methods may be used to measure granulogenic factor protein levels. Radioimmunoassay (RIA) involves labeling purified CGB or SgII with a radioisotope (e.g., ¹²⁵I), incubating the labeled antigen with test samples and specific antibodies, and quantifying bound radioactivity. Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) is a non-radioactive alternative wherein capture antibodies immobilized on a microplate bind the target protein, which is then detected by enzyme-conjugated detection antibodies and colorimetric substrates. A capture-ELISA format enhances specificity by using two non-competing antibodies.

Signal detection in these assays may involve spectrophotometry, fluorometry, chemiluminescence, or electrochemical methods, depending on the label used. The invention further motivates the inhibition of granulogenic factors as a therapeutic strategy, as reducing secretory granule numbers disrupts IP3-mediated Ca²⁺ signaling, thereby impairing cancer cell proliferation, migration, and survival.

Preferable embodiments of the invention target cancers of neuroendocrine origin or those exhibiting neuroendocrine features, including glioblastoma multiforme, ganglioglioma, medulloblastoma, and pituitary adenomas. Pharmaceutical compositions may comprise antisense oligonucleotides designed to hybridize to mRNA transcripts of *CHGB* or *SCG2*, thereby inducing RNase H-mediated degradation or translational blockade. Such oligonucleotides may be chemically modified (e.g., phosphorothioate backbones, 2′-O-methyl or locked nucleic acid residues) to enhance stability and cellular uptake.

Small interfering RNA (siRNA) represents another preferred embodiment. siRNA molecules consist of a sense strand and an antisense strand, typically 19–25 nucleotides in length, with 2-nucleotide 3′ overhangs. The antisense strand is complementary to the target mRNA sequence. siRNAs may be delivered as duplexes or expressed in vivo from plasmid or viral vectors encoding short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) that are processed by Dicer into functional siRNAs. Self-complementary hairpin structures may also be engineered for direct Dicer processing.

The invention encompasses methods of synthesizing antisense oligonucleotides via solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry and preparing antisense RNA through in vitro transcription. siRNA-mediated gene silencing is achieved by introducing siRNA into target cells via transfection reagents, electroporation, or nanoparticle carriers, leading to sequence-specific mRNA degradation.

A method for preventing or treating cancer comprises administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a pharmaceutical composition comprising an inhibitor of a granulogenic factor. The composition includes pharmaceutically acceptable carriers such as saline, phosphate-buffered saline, liposomes, or polymeric nanoparticles, and may be administered intravenously, intratumorally, or via convection-enhanced delivery to the brain. Dosage ranges from 0.1 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg body weight, adjusted based on efficacy and toxicity.

Additionally, the invention provides a kit for identifying cancer in a biological sample. The kit includes primers or probes specific for *CHGB* or *SCG2* mRNA. Primers are typically 18–30 nucleotides long, designed to flank an exon-exon junction to avoid genomic DNA amplification. Probes may be labeled with fluorophores (e.g., FAM, HEX) or haptens (e.g., biotin, digoxigenin) and used in hybridization assays under stringent conditions (e.g., 65°C, 0.1× SSC). Nucleic acid samples may be derived from brain tissue, cerebrospinal fluid, or blood.

Antibody-based kits utilize monoclonal or polyclonal antibodies against CGB or SgII, prepared by immunizing animals with purified antigens and purifying IgG via protein A/G chromatography. Aptamers—single-stranded DNA or RNA molecules selected by SELEX—offer alternative binding agents. Immunoassays measure signal intensity proportional to target concentration, with kit components including buffers, controls, and detection reagents.

In summary, the invention features: (1) the identification of secretory granules in astrocytes as IP3-sensitive Ca²⁺ stores; (2) the correlation between granulogenic factor overexpression and brain cancer; (3) methods for screening and therapeutic inhibition of granulogenic factors; and (4) diagnostic tools for cancer detection based on granulogenic biomarkers.

### EXAMPLES

Antibodies against chromogranin B and secretogranin II were raised in rabbits by immunization with purified bovine proteins, followed by affinity purification on antigen-coupled columns. Specificity was confirmed by Western blotting and preadsorption controls. Human brain tissue samples, including normal temporal lobe and glioblastoma specimens, were obtained post-surgically with informed consent and IRB approval. Proteins were extracted using RIPA buffer supplemented with protease inhibitors.

Immunoblot analysis revealed elevated CGB and SgII in glioblastoma versus normal tissue. For electron microscopy, tissues were fixed in paraformaldehyde/glutaraldehyde, embedded in Epon, and sectioned. Immunogold labeling used 15-nm gold-conjugated secondary antibodies. Expression vectors encoding CGB or SgII were constructed in pcDNA3.1 and transfected into NIH3T3 or COS-7 cells using Lipofectamine. siRNA transfection into PC12 cells utilized DharmaFECT reagents, with efficiency monitored by fluorescently labeled siRNA.

Electron microscopy showed that CGB or SgII expression induced secretory granule formation in non-secretory cells, while siRNA knockdown in PC12 cells reduced granule numbers. Gold particle distribution analysis confirmed specific labeling of granules over mitochondria.

### Results

Brain tissue examination revealed 0–4 LDCVs per 6 µm² astrocyte area, predominantly in processes. Immunogold EM showed CGB and SgII exclusively in LDCVs, not mitochondria. Double labeling with GFAP confirmed astrocytic identity. Granules measured 300–400 nm, comparable to chromaffin cell granules.

Quantitative analysis (Table 1) showed 12.38 CGB-gold/µm² in granules vs. 0.37 in mitochondria; SgII showed 10.31 vs. 0.20. IP3R1, R2, and R3 localized to granule membranes (0.872, 0.586, 0.719 particles/µm, respectively), absent in mitochondria (background: ≤0.035). Immunoblots confirmed higher CGB/SgII in glioblastoma than normal tissue.

Ectopic CGB/SgII expression in NIH3T3/COS-7 induced granule formation; CGA was less effective. siRNA knockdown of CGA/CGB in PC12 reduced granule production.

### Discussion

The presence of CGB and SgII confirms LDCVs as secretory granules in astrocytes. Their increase in glioblastoma suggests a pathogenic role. Similar granule proliferation occurs in ganglioglioma neurons, with elevated CGA and NPY. Granins drive granule biogenesis via aggregation at low pH in the TGN.

Secretory granules serve as major IP3-sensitive Ca²⁺ stores due to high IP3R density and Ca²⁺ content. In astrocytes, this enables localized Ca²⁺ release for gliotransmitter exocytosis. Dysregulation in tumors may enhance Ca²⁺-dependent proliferation and invasion, as IP3R signaling promotes glioblastoma malignancy.

Thus, secretory granule number correlates with pathogenic state, offering a novel biomarker and therapeutic target. Inhibiting granulogenic factors disrupts this axis, providing a rational strategy for brain cancer treatment.
