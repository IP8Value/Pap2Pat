## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention resides in the technical field of catalysis and adsorption using microporous materials, specifically zeolites and zeolite-like frameworks, for energy-related applications. More particularly, the invention relates to the systematic discovery, selection, and application of mildly acidic or siliceous zeolitic materials exhibiting superior performance in hydrocarbon dewaxing via hydroisomerization and in the separation of ethanol from aqueous solutions. The invention further encompasses methods for high-throughput computational screening of candidate zeolite structures based on predictive modeling of adsorption thermodynamics and selectivity under industrially relevant conditions, thereby enabling the identification of optimal materials for complex separations and catalytic transformations involving large, flexible, or hydrogen-bonding molecules.

## BACKGROUND

Hydrocarbon feedstocks derived from crude oil remain the primary source for transportation fuels, lubricants, and chemical intermediates. Among the critical refining processes applied to these feedstocks is dewaxing, which aims to reduce the pour point of lubricant base oils and improve cold-flow properties of diesel fuels by removing or converting long-chain linear alkanes—commonly referred to as “waxes”—that solidify at low temperatures. Traditional solvent dewaxing is energy-intensive and inefficient, prompting the adoption of catalytic dewaxing techniques such as hydrotreating and hydrocracking. Hydrotreating removes heteroatoms like sulfur and nitrogen, while hydrocracking breaks down large hydrocarbons into smaller fragments. However, these processes often lead to excessive cracking, reducing yields of valuable high-molecular-weight products.

A more selective alternative is catalytic hydroisomerization, wherein linear alkanes are converted into slightly branched isomers that retain high viscosity indices and desirable lubricating properties while exhibiting lower pour points. This transformation requires catalysts that can promote isomerization without inducing significant cracking. Zeolites—microporous, crystalline aluminosilicates with well-defined pore architectures—have emerged as ideal candidates due to their shape-selective properties and tunable acidity. Their uniform sub-nanometer pores enable molecular discrimination based on size and branching, allowing preferential adsorption and reaction of linear alkanes while facilitating rapid desorption of mono-branched products before they undergo further degradation.

Despite decades of research, existing dewaxing technologies face limitations stemming from suboptimal zeolite catalysts. Many conventional zeolites either lack sufficient selectivity between linear and branched alkanes or exhibit poor activity across a broad range of carbon chain lengths (e.g., C18–C30). Moreover, experimental screening of zeolite candidates is prohibitively slow and resource-intensive, especially when considering the vast number of theoretically possible framework types, including those not yet synthesized. Prior art in zeolite catalysis has largely focused on known structures such as ZSM-22 (MTT), ZSM-23 (MTT), ferrierite (FER), and beta (BEA), which have demonstrated utility but leave room for significant performance improvements.

Zeolites possess unique properties including high thermal stability, well-defined pore geometries, and controllable Brønsted acid site density. These features make them suitable not only for catalysis but also for separations, such as the purification of bioethanol from fermentation broths. However, traditional approaches to zeolite selection rely heavily on trial-and-error experimentation or geometric heuristics that fail to capture the complex interplay of van der Waals interactions, confinement effects, and entropy in systems involving large, flexible hydrocarbons or polar, hydrogen-bonding mixtures like ethanol/water. Consequently, there exists a compelling need for a rational, predictive methodology to identify novel zeolite frameworks—both experimentally known and computationally predicted—that exhibit superior adsorption affinity, selectivity, and catalytic performance for these challenging industrial applications.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a class of mildly acidic or siliceous zeolites that demonstrate exceptional performance in two key energy-related processes: (1) the hydroisomerization of long-chain linear alkanes (C18–C30) for lubricant dewaxing, and (2) the selective adsorption of ethanol from dilute aqueous solutions for biofuel purification. These zeolites are characterized by specific pore architectures that optimize the balance between adsorption strength, shape selectivity, and resistance to co-adsorption of undesired species.

The benefits of the disclosed zeolites stem from their ability to strongly adsorb target molecules—linear alkanes or ethanol—while discriminating against structurally similar competitors—branched alkanes or water—through precise control of pore size, channel dimensionality, and surface topology. In hydroisomerization, this translates to high conversion of waxy alkanes into valuable isomers with minimal cracking. In ethanol/water separation, it enables high-purity ethanol recovery even from highly dilute feeds, potentially circumventing energy-intensive distillation.

The invention further provides a robust method for discovering such high-performance zeolites through a multi-stage computational screening workflow. This method leverages advanced molecular simulation techniques, accurate force fields, and massively parallel computing to evaluate hundreds of thousands of candidate structures from databases such as the International Zeolite Association Structure Commission (IZA-SC) and the Predicted Crystallography Open Database (PCOD). By focusing on fundamental thermodynamic quantities—such as Henry’s constants, adsorption enthalpies, loadings, and selectivities—the method identifies materials that excel under realistic process conditions without being tied to a specific reactor or separation configuration.

In summary, the invention enables the systematic identification and deployment of next-generation zeolitic materials for efficient hydrocarbon processing and biofuel purification, significantly accelerating the materials discovery pipeline and offering substantial economic and environmental advantages over conventional technologies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The hydrocarbon feedstock targeted by the present invention comprises long-chain normal alkanes typically found in vacuum gas oils or slack waxes, with carbon numbers ranging from 18 to 30. These linear alkanes impart high pour points to lubricant base oils, rendering them unsuitable for low-temperature applications. While branched isomers of similar molecular weight retain excellent viscosity indices, their synthesis requires selective catalytic transformation without excessive cracking—a challenge that conventional catalysts struggle to meet.

Zeolites serve as ideal catalysts for hydroisomerization due to their shape-selective micropores, which can accommodate linear alkanes while sterically hindering or disfavoring the adsorption of bulkier branched isomers. However, experimental evaluation of all potential zeolite frameworks is impractical given the enormous chemical and structural diversity of possible materials, including both synthesized and hypothetical structures. To overcome this bottleneck, the invention employs predictive modeling based on grand-canonical Monte Carlo (GCMC) simulations using the Transferable Potentials for Phase Equilibria (TraPPE) force field, which accurately captures dispersive and electrostatic interactions in complex sorbate-sorbent systems.

A high-throughput screening workflow was developed to efficiently evaluate over 330,000 zeolite structures from the IZA-SC and PCOD databases. The workflow begins with short simulations at infinite dilution and elevated temperature (573 K) to compute Henry’s constants and selectivities for an equimolar mixture of representative hydrocarbons: n-C18, n-C24, n-C30, 2-methylheptadecane, 4-methylheptadecane, and 2,2-dimethylhexadecane. Three key performance indicators are defined: (i) high affinity for linear alkanes (quantified by k_H,C18 or Q_C18), (ii) high selectivity for linear over branched alkanes (S_B), and (iii) low selectivity among different linear chain lengths (S_L), ensuring broad applicability across the feedstock distribution. A composite score P_HC = y_C18 × S_B / S_L is used to rank candidates.

Promising structures are pruned using a fast initial screen, retaining only those in the top percentiles across any performance metric. Surviving candidates undergo longer simulations at finite pressure (3 MPa) and representative reaction conditions to refine predictions. This two-tier approach balances computational cost with accuracy, capturing non-ideal mixture behavior and pressure-dependent effects that simple geometric rules miss.

The invention identifies both rediscovered known catalysts—such as ATO, MRE, and FER—and novel PCOD structures with dramatically enhanced performance. For instance, PCOD-8113534 exhibits S_B values exceeding 16,000, orders of magnitude higher than conventional zeolites, due to its narrow, smooth elliptical channels that strongly confine linear chains while excluding branched isomers. These materials are integrated into bi-functional catalyst systems comprising a mildly acidic zeolite component providing shape-selective isomerization sites and a hydrogenation/dehydrogenation metal component (e.g., Pt, Pd) dispersed on the zeolite or a support.

Operating conditions for the hydroisomerization process typically involve temperatures of 523–623 K, hydrogen pressures of 2–5 MPa, and liquid hourly space velocities tailored to the feed. The zeolite’s Brønsted acid functionality, derived from framework aluminum or introduced via post-synthetic modification, must be mild enough to avoid cracking but sufficient to catalyze skeletal rearrangement. The hydrogenation component ensures rapid (de)hydrogenation steps in the bifunctional mechanism, maintaining catalyst stability and selectivity.

Through this systematic discovery process, the invention not only validates known high-performing zeolites but also uncovers entirely new frameworks with superior properties, demonstrating the power of predictive modeling in accelerating materials innovation for complex industrial separations and reactions.

### Example

An exemplary application of the invention involves the identification of mildly acidic zeolites optimized for hydroisomerization of C18–C30 alkanes. The screening process begins with adsorption simulations using siliceous (defect-free, all-silica) forms of zeolite framework types to isolate the contribution of pore geometry from acid-site effects. Over 330,000 structures from the IZA-SC and PCOD databases are evaluated using a complex hydrocarbon feed mixture designed to mimic real refinery streams.

Performance is governed by three interrelated factors: (1) strong adsorption affinity for linear alkanes, (2) high selectivity against branched isomers, and (3) uniform response across different chain lengths. The first screening step employs short configurational-bias Monte Carlo (CBMC) simulations at infinite dilution (573 K) to compute Henry’s constants and selectivities rapidly. From the IZA-SC database, top performers include ATO, MRE, and MTT—frameworks previously patented for dewaxing—validating the methodology’s relevance.

The candidate pool is then expanded to include the PCOD database, which contains computationally predicted, thermodynamically feasible zeolite-like structures. Structures ranking in the top 64 (IZA-SC) or top 1,024 (PCOD) in any performance indicator are retained for longer simulations at 3 MPa and 573 K, reflecting industrial hydroisomerization conditions. This reveals critical pressure dependencies: while S_B remains relatively constant, S_L decreases significantly due to competitive adsorption effects, underscoring the necessity of multi-condition screening.

Analysis of top performers shows that k_H correlates weakly with adsorption enthalpy (ΔH_ads) due to enthalpy-entropy compensation in channels of optimal diameter (0.45–0.75 nm). More importantly, S_B is strongly linked to “pore bumpiness”—the difference between the maximum inscribed sphere diameter along a channel and the free diffusion diameter (Dd = Dd_incl − d_free). Smooth, narrow channels (d_free < 0.45 nm, Dd < 0.1 nm) favor linear alkanes, whereas bumpy, ultra-narrow pores (Dd > 0.3 nm) may invert selectivity.

Comparisons between IZA-SC and PCOD top structures reveal that PCOD materials achieve P_HC values up to two orders of magnitude higher, primarily due to extraordinary S_B. Visualization of adsorbed configurations in ATO (hexagonal channels), MTW (elliptical channels), and PCOD-8113534 (highly confined elliptical pores) confirms that optimal performance arises from precise molecular alignment and suppression of gauche defects in linear chains, while branched isomers are sterically excluded. These insights, inaccessible through geometric analysis alone, highlight the necessity of atomistic simulation in guiding synthesis toward truly transformative materials.

### Methods

The IZA-SC database comprises 402 unique zeolite structures, including idealized SiO₂ frameworks and experimentally determined structures refined to remove extra-framework species and assign full Si occupancy. The PCOD database contains 331,172 predicted zeolite-like frameworks generated by enumerating crystallographic parameters and selecting thermodynamically stable candidates after geometry optimization.

Simulations employ the TraPPE force field for hydrocarbons and the TraPPE-zeo variant for all-silica zeolites, validated against experimental adsorption data for alkanes, alcohols, and water in various frameworks. Water is modeled using TIP4P. Electrostatic interactions are handled via Ewald summation, and Lennard-Jones tail corrections are applied beyond a 1.4 nm cutoff. Zeolite frameworks are treated as rigid, while sorbates sample full conformational flexibility via angle bending and dihedral rotations.

Configurational-bias Monte Carlo (CBMC) simulations in the grand-canonical ensemble compute loadings as functions of chemical potential, derived from Gibbs ensemble or NPT simulations for bulk phases. For hydrocarbons, chemical potentials correspond to partial pressures at 3 MPa; for ethanol/water, they reflect solution concentrations (0.12–41 wt%). The coupled-decoupled CBMC algorithm enhances sampling of intramolecular degrees of freedom and enables identity-switch moves between hydrocarbon species, improving equilibration in multicomponent mixtures.

Energy grids are precomputed for each zeolite, tabulating interaction energies independent of sorbate identity, enabling rapid reuse across applications. High-throughput execution utilizes a two-level parallel hierarchy on supercomputers like Mira (Argonne National Laboratory), distributing thousands of structures across up to 131,072 cores, with each structure simulated on 4–256 cores. Simulation lengths range from 9×10⁴ MC steps (initial screen) to 1.6×10⁶ steps (final validation), ensuring statistical reliability.

### Exemplary Embodiments

Embodiment 1 comprises a catalyst system for hydroisomerization containing a zeolite with framework type ATO and a hydrogenation metal selected from Group VIII metals. Embodiment 2 utilizes a zeolite of framework type MRE combined with platinum. Embodiment 3 employs a zeolite selected from PCOD identifiers 8113534, 8113535, 8113536, 8113537, and 8113538, each exhibiting high S_B3 values. Embodiment 4 uses a zeolite from PCOD identifiers 8113539, 8113540, 8113541, 8113542, and 8113543 in combination with palladium.

Embodiment 5 defines a catalyst system comprising a zeolite with one-dimensional channel architecture and a Group VIII metal. Embodiment 6 specifies a zeolite with three-dimensional channels and platinum. Embodiment 7 includes a zeolite from the PCOD database with d_free between 0.45 and 0.75 nm and Dd < 0.1 nm. Embodiment 8 combines a zeolite of framework type FER with palladium.

Embodiment 9 optionally combines any zeolite of Embodiment 3 with a binder such as alumina. Embodiment 10 optionally combines any zeolite of Embodiment 4 with silica and specifies the hydrogenation component as platinum. One-dimensional channel systems include PCOD identifiers 8113534–8113550; two-dimensional include 8113551–8113570; three-dimensional include 8113571–8113600. Zeolite types for one-dimensional systems include ATO, MTW, TON; for two- and three-dimensional, include BEA, FAU, MFI.

Embodiments 11–21 define increasingly specific combinations: Embodiment 17 specifies a catalyst with PCOD-8113534 and Pt; Embodiment 20 uses PCOD-8113539 and Pd. Embodiments 24–25 specify zeolites with one- or three-dimensional channels and pore diameters of 0.45–0.75 nm. Embodiment 26 defines the hydrogenation component as a Group VIII metal; Embodiment 27 narrows this to Pt or Pd.

Embodiment 29 evaluates candidates based on adsorption affinity (k_H,C18 > 10⁻³ mol kg⁻¹ Pa⁻¹) and selectivity (S_B3 > 10). Embodiment 30 operates at 573 K and 3 MPa, defining SB3 = Q_linear/Q_branched and SL3 = (Q_C24 Q_C30)^½ / Q_C18. Embodiment 31 selects zeolites maximizing P = Q_nC18 × SB3 / SL3. Embodiment 32 restricts candidates to experimentally synthesized zeolites; Embodiment 33 includes predicted structures from PCOD.

The scope of the invention encompasses all such embodiments and their equivalents, enabling the design of superior catalysts and adsorbents for energy-efficient refining and biofuel production.
