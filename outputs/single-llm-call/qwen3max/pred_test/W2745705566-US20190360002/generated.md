# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of genetic engineering tools, particularly those based on CRISPR-associated (Cas) proteins derived from thermophilic microorganisms. More specifically, the invention provides novel Cas9 endonucleases exhibiting enhanced thermostability and activity over a broad temperature range, enabling precise genome editing, gene silencing, and nucleic acid detection in both thermophilic and mesophilic organisms. These tools expand the applicability of CRISPR-Cas technologies beyond the limitations imposed by conventional mesophilic Cas9 variants, such as Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 (SpCas9), which lose functionality at elevated temperatures. The invention encompasses isolated thermostable Cas9 polypeptides, nucleic acids encoding them, expression vectors, host cells engineered to express these components, and methods for modifying genetic material in prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells under conditions ranging from ambient to high temperatures. This advancement facilitates genetic manipulation in industrially relevant thermophiles and enables robust molecular biology applications requiring thermal stability.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

The CRISPR-Cas system functions as an adaptive immune mechanism in prokaryotes, providing sequence-specific defense against invading genetic elements such as bacteriophages and plasmids. This system is classified into two main classes and six types based on structural and functional characteristics, with Class 2 systems—particularly Type II involving Cas9—being widely adopted for genome engineering due to their simplicity, relying on a single effector nuclease guided by RNA. Cas9 from Streptococcus pyogenes (SpCas9) has become the predominant tool for targeted genome modification in eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells, leveraging its ability to generate double-strand breaks (DSBs) at specific genomic loci directed by a chimeric single-guide RNA (sgRNA). In eukaryotic cells, SpCas9 has enabled efficient gene knockout, knock-in, and transcriptional regulation through homology-directed repair (HDR) or non-homologous end joining (NHEJ). Similarly, in bacteria, Cas9-mediated DSBs serve as a powerful counter-selection mechanism when combined with homologous recombination templates, allowing precise genome edits without leaving antibiotic resistance markers.

Despite its versatility, SpCas9 exhibits significant limitations in thermophilic environments. Its enzymatic activity rapidly declines above 42°C, rendering it ineffective for obligate thermophiles that grow optimally between 50°C and 70°C. Although some facultative thermophiles have been engineered using temperature-shift strategies—performing homologous recombination at high temperatures followed by Cas9 counter-selection at permissive lower temperatures—these approaches are incompatible with strictly thermophilic organisms. Moreover, while CRISPR-Cas systems have been identified in certain thermophiles, prior art suggested that Type II systems were absent in thermophilic bacteria, limiting the exploration of native thermostable Cas9 orthologues. Targeting constructs for bacterial genome editing typically rely on homologous recombination flanked by sequences homologous to the target locus, with selection for successful recombination events often achieved through antibiotic resistance or metabolic complementation. However, without an efficient counter-selection mechanism like active Cas9, recombination frequencies remain low, especially in organisms with limited DNA repair machinery.

The establishment of CRISPR-Cas-mediated genome editing in diverse hosts has underscored the need for improved sequence-specific nucleic acid detection and manipulation tools that function reliably across a wide thermal spectrum. The limitations of SpCas9 in thermophilic contexts highlight a critical gap: the absence of a Cas9 variant naturally adapted to high-temperature environments. While bioinformatic analyses have revealed the presence of Cas9 homologues in thermophilic bacteria, including members of the Geobacillus genus, these had not been characterized or harnessed for genome engineering. Recent phylogenetic reassessments of Geobacillus species have clarified taxonomic relationships and facilitated the identification of conserved CRISPR-Cas loci. Notably, the discovery of thermostable Cas9 variants, such as the one described herein from Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, offers a solution to this challenge. These variants recognize distinct protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) sequences, which are essential for target discrimination and cleavage specificity. Optimizing PAM recognition is crucial for expanding targetable genomic sites and enhancing editing efficiency. Thus, there remains a compelling motivation to develop a Cas9-based editing platform specifically tailored for obligate thermophiles, capable of functioning robustly at temperatures where conventional Cas9 enzymes denature, thereby unlocking the biotechnological potential of these resilient microorganisms.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces ThermoCas9, a novel CRISPR-associated (Cas) protein derived from the thermophilic bacterium Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, which exhibits remarkable thermostability and catalytic activity across a broad temperature range from 20°C to 70°C. ThermoCas9 demonstrates robust in vitro nuclease activity, efficiently cleaving double-stranded DNA substrates when complexed with a suitable guide RNA. A key determinant of its thermostability is the structural integrity of the single-guide RNA (sgRNA), particularly the presence of three stem-loop structures in the tracrRNA scaffold; truncation of these hairpins significantly reduces cleavage efficiency at elevated temperatures, underscoring the importance of full-length sgRNA for optimal performance. The invention further provides methods for in vivo genome editing in both thermophilic and mesophilic hosts using ThermoCas9. In one embodiment, a method for modifying the genetic material of a eukaryotic cell comprises integrating a polynucleotide encoding ThermoCas9 into the host genome, followed by transformation with an expression vector comprising a guide RNA targeting a specific genomic locus, and co-delivery of a repair oligonucleotide to facilitate homology-directed repair. Alternatively, the cell may first be engineered to stably express ThermoCas9, after which it is transformed with an expression vector for the guide RNA and a repair oligonucleotide simultaneously.

In another aspect, the invention provides an alternative method wherein the polynucleotide encoding ThermoCas9 is integrated into the genome, and the cell is subsequently transformed with an expression vector comprising the guide RNA, with the repair oligonucleotide optionally delivered as a double-stranded DNA molecule designed to achieve deletion of a desired genomic locus. Promoter selection is critical for controlled expression: constitutive promoters ensure continuous ThermoCas9 production, while inducible promoters allow temporal regulation, minimizing off-target effects and cellular toxicity. Combinations of promoters may be employed to independently regulate ThermoCas9 and guide RNA expression. Transformation may involve delivery of expression plasmids and/or repair oligonucleotides via standard techniques such as electroporation or chemical competence. For prokaryotic hosts, the invention provides a method comprising transforming the organism with an expression vector containing a polynucleotide encoding ThermoCas9 under the control of a first promoter, a guide RNA sequence, and a homologous recombination (HR) fragment flanking the target locus. Inducible or constitutive promoters may drive ThermoCas9 expression, and the HR fragment facilitates precise genomic modifications, including insertions or deletions mediated by an insertion element.

The method is applicable to prokaryotic organisms cultured at temperatures ranging from 37°C to 65°C, with transformation and induction performed within this range to maintain enzyme activity. The invention also encompasses an isolated CRISPR-associated (Cas) protein or polypeptide, referred to as ThermoCas9, comprising specific amino acid motifs essential for nuclease function and thermostability. Representative peptide sequences include those corresponding to SEQ ID NOs: 1–12, which define conserved domains and structural features. ThermoCas9 contains characteristic RuvC and HNH nuclease motifs, and is defined as a Cas9 orthologue exhibiting at least 85% sequence identity to the amino acid sequence of SEQ ID NO: 1. It functions optimally between 30°C and 70°C, binding and cleaving target polynucleotides adjacent to a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM). Exemplary PAM sequences recognized by ThermoCas9 include 5′-NNNNCNRA-3′, with preferred variants featuring cytosine at the fifth position and adenine at the eighth position (e.g., 5′-CCCCCNAA-3′). The protein binds target nucleic acids within this temperature range and can also be used for marking or modifying nucleic acids when fused to functional moieties. Applications include in vivo genome editing, in vitro diagnostics, and transcriptional regulation. Expression of ThermoCas9 is achieved via nucleic acids encoding the protein, which may include conservative amino acid or nucleic acid substitutions preserving function. Transformed cells expressing ThermoCas9 and kits containing the protein, guide RNAs, and buffers are also provided.

## RNA Guides and Target Sequences

Cas proteins, including ThermoCas9, require association with targeting RNA molecules to recognize and bind specific nucleic acid sequences. The target nucleic acids may be double-stranded DNA, single-stranded DNA, or RNA, though ThermoCas9 primarily acts on double-stranded DNA. Guide RNAs (gRNAs), encompassing both natural crRNA:tracrRNA duplexes and engineered single-guide RNAs (sgRNAs), direct Cas proteins to complementary target sequences adjacent to a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM). The PAM is a short, sequence-specific motif essential for distinguishing self from non-self DNA and initiating cleavage. For ThermoCas9, exemplary PAM sequences include 5′-NNNNCNRA-3′, with optimal recognition occurring when the fifth position is cytosine and the eighth is adenine. The ribonucleoprotein complex consists of ThermoCas9 bound to a gRNA, which together scan the genome for matching protospacers flanked by compatible PAMs. This complex is used to bind, cleave, mark, or modify target nucleic acids in vitro or in vivo. Transformed non-human cells expressing this complex enable programmable genome engineering.

The RNA molecule typically ranges from 80 to 200 nucleotides in length, with preferred lengths between 150 and 190 nucleotides to accommodate full tracrRNA structure. The complementary portion (spacer) of the RNA, which base-pairs with the target DNA, is usually 18–25 nucleotides long, with 19–23 nucleotides being optimal for ThermoCas9. Specificity and affinity are high, with dissociation constants (Kd) in the low nanomolar range. CRISPR RNA (crRNA) molecules consist of a spacer flanked by repeat sequences, while in Type II systems like ThermoCas9’s, they pair with trans-activating crRNA (tracrRNA) to form the functional guide. Design of RNA guides relies on gene sequence information to ensure complementarity to the intended target. The SEED portion, located proximal to the PAM (typically positions 1–10 of the spacer), is critical for initial binding and mismatch sensitivity. Single-base changes within the SEED can abolish cleavage, enabling high-fidelity detection.

PAM design rules dictate that efficient targeting requires adherence to ThermoCas9’s PAM preferences. Unlike SpCas9, which recognizes 5′-NGG-3′, ThermoCas9 accommodates longer, more complex PAMs, expanding targetable sites in AT-rich genomes. PAM composition influences the operational temperature range: stringent PAM requirements (e.g., 5′-CVAA-3′) are necessary for activity below 30°C, while broader PAM tolerance occurs at higher temperatures. For broad-range applications, PAMs such as 5′-CCCCCNAA-3′ are preferred. The targeting RNA molecule includes a spacer of 19–23 nt linked to a tracrRNA scaffold with three stem-loops. The ribonucleoprotein complex comprises ThermoCas9 and this RNA, associating with the target strand opposite the PAM. Mesophilic SpCas9 fails above 44°C, but ThermoCas9 operates up to 70°C, with thermostability conferred by structural features including a compact REC lobe and stable sgRNA interactions. Its PAM preference for cytosine at position five and adenine at eight defines the invention’s targeting scope. Altering PAM sequences to match these preferences enhances binding and cleavage efficiency across temperatures, including at 30°C where only optimal PAMs support activity. The nucleoprotein complex thus enables precise, temperature-resilient genome manipulation.

### Binding, Cleavage, Marking and Modifying Temperatures

The Cas proteins of the invention, particularly ThermoCas9, exhibit an optimal temperature range of 37°C to 65°C for nuclease activity, with detectable function extending from 20°C to 70°C. This broad thermal profile confers a significant advantage in genetic engineering, enabling applications in both mesophilic and thermophilic hosts without enzyme denaturation. The thermostability of ThermoCas9 facilitates its use in molecular biology techniques requiring elevated temperatures, such as high-fidelity PCR-coupled editing or industrial bioprocesses. Beyond cleavage, ThermoCas9 can be repurposed for transcriptional control by fusing it to repressor domains, serving as a gene-silencing tool when rendered catalytically inactive (dThermoCas9). Nucleic acid binding occurs effectively between 20°C and 70°C, while cleavage is most efficient from 37°C to 65°C. Marking (e.g., via fluorescent tags) and modifying (e.g., base editing) activities follow similar thermal dependencies.

The PAM sequence critically modulates temperature-dependent activity. At lower temperatures (≤30°C), only targets with optimal PAMs (e.g., 5′-CVAA-3′) are cleaved, reducing off-target effects. Target RNA molecules are not primary substrates, as ThermoCas9 is a dsDNA nuclease. The temperature range for all activities—binding, cleaving, marking, modifying—is defined by the enzyme’s stability, which is enhanced by sgRNA association. TracrRNA and crRNA form the natural guide; synthetic linkers create chimeric sgRNAs. Full-length tracrRNA with three stem-loops is essential for thermostability—deletion of hairpins, especially the 3′ loop, drastically reduces cleavage at ≥60°C. Modified tracrRNAs retaining all loops support activity up to 70°C. Preferred sgRNAs contain three stem-loop structures; those with fewer show diminished performance. Spacer length also affects efficiency: 19–23 nt spacers yield highest cleavage, with 18 nt being suboptimal. Thus, maintaining full-length tracrRNA and optimal spacer length ensures maximal ThermoCas9 functionality across its operational temperature range.

### Functional Moieties

Functional moieties may be fused or linked to ThermoCas9 to expand its utility beyond nuclease activity. These moieties include fluorescent proteins (e.g., GFP), epitope tags, transcriptional activators/repressors, base editors, or recombinases. For example, fusion to a marker protein like GFP enables visualization of target genomic loci in live cells. Such fusions retain the RNA-guided targeting capability of ThermoCas9 while imparting new functionalities, facilitating applications in imaging, epigenetic modulation, or conditional gene activation.

### Nuclease Activity

The Cas ribonucleoprotein complex of ThermoCas9 exhibits sequence-specific nuclease activity between 20°C and 70°C, cleaving double-stranded DNA to generate blunt-ended double-strand breaks. This activity triggers endogenous cellular repair pathways—homologous recombination (HR) in the presence of a donor template or error-prone non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) in eukaryotes. In prokaryotes lacking NHEJ, DSBs are lethal without HR, enabling stringent counter-selection. Nuclease activity can be inactivated by mutating catalytic residues (e.g., D8A in RuvC, H582A in HNH), yielding dThermoCas9 for gene silencing. Fusing heterologous nucleases like FokI creates dimeric systems requiring paired guides for cleavage, enhancing specificity. Catalytically dead variants fused to marker proteins allow target visualization and transcriptional quantification. Nickase variants (single-domain mutants) facilitate HDR with reduced indel formation. These engineered forms support diverse applications, from metabolic studies to diagnostic assays detecting nucleic acid sequences via collateral cleavage.

## Expression Vectors

Expression vectors of the invention contain regulatory elements driving ThermoCas9 and guide RNA expression. Key components include promoters (constitutive or inducible), terminators, selectable markers (e.g., antibiotic resistance), and origins of replication. Inducible promoters (e.g., xylL in Bacillus, Pm in Pseudomonas) enable temporal control, minimizing toxicity. Vectors may deliver ThermoCas9 as protein, mRNA, or DNA, with guide RNAs expressed from Pol III promoters or as synthetic RNAs. Ribonucleoprotein complexes can be pre-assembled in vitro for direct delivery. Methods for modifying target nucleic acids involve transfecting, transforming, or transducing cells with these vectors. Delivery systems include electroporation, lipofection, or viral vectors like adeno-associated virus (AAV) for eukaryotes. Marker proteins (e.g., fluorescent reporters) facilitate screening of edited cells. Functional moieties fused to ThermoCas9 enable non-homologous end joining, homologous recombination, transcriptional modulation, or nucleic acid labeling, broadening the toolkit for genome engineering and diagnostics.

## Host Cells

Host cells for the invention include prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, excluding human cells. Preferred hosts are bacterial (e.g., Bacillus, Geobacillus, Pseudomonas), fungal (e.g., Aspergillus, Candida, Yarrowia), or thermophilic microbes. Thermophilic bacteria like Geobacillus thermodenitrificans, Bacillus smithii, and thermophilic bacilli or clostridia are ideal for high-temperature applications. Mesophilic hosts such as Pseudomonas putida, E. coli, or yeast (e.g., Hansenula) are also amenable. Transformed cells stably or transiently express ThermoCas9 and guide RNAs, enabling genome editing, silencing, or detection. The invention provides polynucleotides encoding ThermoCas9 (e.g., SEQ ID NO: 1) and corresponding amino acid sequences for expression in these hosts, facilitating metabolic engineering and functional genomics across diverse microbial platforms.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Example 1: Isolation of Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

Geobacillus thermodenitrificans strain T12 was isolated from compost samples and cultured under aerobic conditions at 65°C in nutrient-rich medium. Genomic DNA was extracted and sequenced, revealing a Type-IIC CRISPR-Cas locus encoding a Cas9 homologue, later designated ThermoCas9.

### Example 2: Defining the Essential Consensus Sequences for Cas9 in Geobacillus thermodenitrificans

Bioinformatic analysis using BLAST-P against NCBI databases identified Cas9 orthologues with high sequence identity (>87%) within Geobacillus species. Multiple sequence alignment and Neighbour-Joining phylogenetic tree construction confirmed evolutionary conservation. Protein domain homology modeling predicted a truncated REC lobe, consistent with smaller Cas9 variants.

### Example 3: Identifying Core Amino Acid Motifs which are Essential for the Function of CAS9 and Those which Confer Thermostability in Thermophilic Cas9 Nucleases

Protein sequence alignment of thermophilic and mesophilic Cas9 orthologues identified conserved catalytic residues (D8 in RuvC, H582 in HNH) and structural motifs associated with thermostability, including hydrophobic core packing and salt bridge networks. Architecture analysis revealed a compact conformation stabilized by sgRNA binding.

### Example 4: Determination of the PAM Sequence of G. thermodenitrificans gtCas9

CRISPR array prediction identified ten spacers. A tracrRNA-expressing module and chimeric sgRNA were designed with a T7 promoter and transcriptional terminator. Functional validation confirmed CRISPR system activity in vitro.

### Example 5: Target Generation with Randomized PAM

Spacers were amplified by PCR, and protospacers with randomized 7-nt PAM sequences were generated via primer annealing and extension, creating a library for PAM screening.

### Example 6: In Vitro Determination of PAM Sequences for gtCas9

The pRham:Cas9gt vector was constructed and expressed in E. cloni cells. Cell-free extracts containing gtCas9 were incubated with in vitro transcribed sgRNA and a PAM library. Cleavage products were analyzed by gel electrophoresis and deep sequencing, revealing a 5′-NNNNCNR-3′ PAM preference.

### Example 7: In Silico PAM Prediction for gtCas9

Genome mining of G. thermodenitrificans T12 identified CRISPR loci. Protospacer matches in phage databases showed flanking consensus sequences supporting cytosine at PAM position five and adenine at position eight.

### Example 8: Determination of 8 Nucleotide Long PAM Sequences for gtCas9

Synthetic PAM variants confirmed that an adenine at the eighth position enhances cleavage. Temperature assays established optimal activity between 37°C and 65°C, with strict PAM requirements below 30°C.

### Example 9: In Vivo Genome Editing of Bacillus smithii ET138 with gtCas9 and 8 Nucleotide Length PAM Sequences

Plasmids expressing gtCas9, sgRNA, and HR templates for pyrF deletion were transformed into B. smithii at 55°C. Colony PCR confirmed clean deletions, demonstrating in vivo activity. Constructs with PAMs like ACGGCCAA were functional, validating PAM recognition. ThermoCas9 outperformed SpCas9 at high temperatures, enabling efficient editing in thermophiles and mesophiles like P. putida. dThermoCas9 silenced ldhL, reducing lactate production and growth, proving utility in CRISPRi. Single-plasmid systems with β-glucosidase promoters enabled editing in Geobacillus thermoglucosidans and B. coagulans, confirming broad applicability across thermophiles and mesophiles.
