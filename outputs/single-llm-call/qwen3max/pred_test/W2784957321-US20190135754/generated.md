# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The construction of enantioenriched quaternary stereocenters represents one of the most formidable challenges in modern organic synthesis. Such structural motifs are prevalent in numerous biologically active natural products and pharmaceutical agents, yet their asymmetric synthesis often demands highly specialized methodologies due to the steric congestion and lack of prochiral faces inherent to tetrasubstituted carbon centers. Over the past several decades, significant efforts have been devoted to developing catalytic enantioselective strategies capable of forging these challenging architectures with high levels of stereocontrol. Among the most successful approaches is transition metal-catalyzed allylic alkylation, a transformation first reported by Tsuji in 1965 and subsequently refined into a powerful tool for stereoselective carbon–carbon bond formation. This methodology leverages the ability of transition metals to activate allylic electrophiles toward nucleophilic attack while simultaneously providing a chiral environment—typically through coordination of a chiral ligand—that enables enantioselective delivery of the nucleophile.

Despite extensive development, certain classes of prochiral nucleophiles remain underexplored in the context of enantioselective allylic alkylation. In particular, α-substituted lactones and lactams—cyclic esters and amides bearing a substituent at the α-position—have seen limited application in such transformations. These substrates are especially attractive because they generate products containing both a quaternary stereocenter and a synthetically versatile heterocyclic scaffold. Moreover, the α-acyl functionality (e.g., ester or amide) provides an additional handle for downstream derivatization, including ring-opening, reduction, or cross-coupling reactions. Lactone-derived products can be converted into acyclic quaternary stereocenters via hydrolysis or reduction, while lactam products serve as direct precursors to functionalized piperidine rings, which constitute the most common nitrogen-containing heterocycle in FDA-approved drugs. Nevertheless, prior art offers only a single example of transition metal-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation employing monocyclic α-acyl lactones or lactams, highlighting a critical gap in synthetic methodology.

Recently, Cossy and colleagues disclosed a palladium-catalyzed decarboxylative enantioselective allylic alkylation using enol carbonates derived from γ-butyrolactones. While this method delivers α-acyl quaternary butyrolactones in moderate to high enantioselectivity, it suffers from two major limitations: first, the requirement for preformed enol carbonates, which necessitates multi-step synthesis and limits substrate accessibility; and second, a narrow electrophile scope restricted primarily to activated allylic systems. These constraints significantly reduce the practical utility of the method for broad synthetic applications, particularly in medicinal chemistry where rapid access to diverse, complex scaffolds is essential.

In light of these shortcomings, there exists a compelling need for new reaction protocols that utilize readily available starting materials, operate under mild conditions, employ earth-abundant and cost-effective catalysts, and exhibit broad functional group tolerance. Ideally, such a method would enable direct coupling between simple α-acyl lactones or lactams and unactivated allylic alcohols—abundant, stable, and atom-economical electrophiles—without requiring prefunctionalization. Furthermore, the catalytic system should deliver high enantioselectivity across a wide range of nucleophiles and electrophiles, thereby enabling the modular construction of structurally diverse, enantioenriched building blocks for pharmaceutical discovery and development.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation process that addresses the aforementioned limitations in the art. Specifically, the invention enables the direct coupling of α-acyl lactones or lactams with allylic alcohols to afford compounds bearing enantioenriched quaternary stereocenters. ## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The present invention is directed to an enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones and lactams using nickel catalysis. This transformation enables the direct formation of quaternary stereocenters with high enantiomeric excess under mild, operationally simple conditions. The method employs unactivated allylic alcohols as electrophiles and avoids the need for prefunctionalized substrates, thereby enhancing synthetic efficiency and scalability.

### Transition Metal Catalysts

Transition metal catalysts, particularly nickel-based systems, are central to the invention. The catalyst is formed in situ by combining a Ni(0) source such as Ni(COD)₂ with a chiral ligand. Catalyst loading ranges from 0.01 to 10 mol%, preferably 5–10 mol%. Lower loadings may require extended reaction times. Specific embodiments use 10 mol% Ni(COD)₂ and 12 mol% ligand in Et₂O at –10 °C.

### Chiral Ligands

Chiral ligands induce asymmetry by creating a chiral environment around nickel. Bidentate phosphine ligands, especially bisphosphines like (R)-P-phos, are preferred. They complex to nickel, orienting substrates for enantioselective attack. Ligand loading is typically 1.0–2.0 equiv per Ni. Enantioenrichment is measured by ee, with values ranging from 78% to 91% for products of Formula (IX), (VI), (VII), (VIII), or (XI).

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Nickel-Catalyzed Enantioselective Allylic Alkylation of Lactones and Lactams with Unactivated Allylic Alcohols

The reaction of ethyl 2-oxotetrahydro-2H-pyran-3-carboxylate (1a) with allyl alcohol (2a) using Ni(COD)₂ (10 mol%) and (R)-P-phos (12 mol%) in Et₂O at –10 °C for 48 h afforded ethyl (R)-3-allyl-2-oxotetrahydro-2H-pyran-3-carboxylate (3aa) in 80% yield and 85% ee. Screening revealed bisphosphines superior to PHOX ligands. Substrate scope included lactones 1b–1c and lactams 4a–4b. Electrophiles encompassed aryl-, heteroaryl-, aliphatic-, and alkenyl-substituted allylic alcohols (3ac–3an, 5aa–5ab). Mechanistic studies indicated π-allyl nickel intermediates. Product derivatizations yielded diol 6 (88%), enone 7 (67%), spirocycle 8, piperidine 9, aldehyde 10 (75%), and deprotected lactam 11 (84%). Spectroscopic data (¹H/¹³C NMR, HRMS) confirmed structures. X-ray crystallography of 3af verified absolute stereochemistry.

### Example 2

Reaction of methyl 1-benzoyl-2-oxopiperidine-3-carboxylate with cinnamyl alcohol under standard conditions gave 5ba in 78% yield and 89% ee, confirmed by chiral HPLC.
