# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to systems and methodologies for providing medical treatment, particularly in the context of mechanical ventilation for patients requiring respiratory support. More specifically, the invention pertains to an advanced ventilator control system that implements a novel targeting scheme—referred to herein as mid-frequency ventilation (MFV)—which dynamically optimizes ventilator parameters to maximize alveolar ventilation while minimizing tidal volume and associated risks of ventilator-induced lung injury. The invention encompasses both hardware and software components configured to execute real-time physiological assessments, parameter calculations, and adaptive adjustments to ventilator settings based on patient-specific respiratory characteristics. This approach represents a significant departure from conventional ventilation strategies by prioritizing lung-protective mechanics over traditional objectives such as minimizing work of breathing or maintaining fixed tidal volumes. The disclosed system is designed for integration into modern intensive care environments, offering clinicians a reliable, automated alternative that adheres strictly to evidence-based physiological principles without reliance on subjective clinical judgment or variable human expertise.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Mechanical ventilators are life-sustaining medical devices used to support or replace spontaneous breathing in patients suffering from acute or chronic respiratory failure. These devices deliver controlled amounts of oxygenated gas into the lungs under positive pressure and facilitate carbon dioxide removal during expiration. Mechanical ventilation is commonly required in a wide range of critical conditions, including but not limited to acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), severe pneumonia, sepsis-induced respiratory compromise, postoperative recovery following major thoracic or abdominal surgery, neuromuscular disorders impairing diaphragmatic function, drug overdose leading to central respiratory depression, and exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) or status asthmaticus. In each of these scenarios, the primary goals of mechanical ventilation include ensuring adequate gas exchange, minimizing the work of breathing, preventing further lung injury, and supporting hemodynamic stability. Historically, ventilator settings such as tidal volume, respiratory rate, inspiratory-to-expiratory (I:E) ratio, positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP), and peak inspiratory pressure have been manually selected by clinicians based on clinical experience, institutional protocols, and real-time physiological feedback. However, this manual approach is inherently prone to inter-operator variability, cognitive bias, and suboptimal adherence to evolving best practices, particularly in complex or rapidly changing clinical situations. While recent advances have introduced computerized targeting schemes—such as Adaptive Support Ventilation (ASV)—that automate certain aspects of ventilator management, these systems remain limited in scope, primarily focusing on minimizing the theoretical work of breathing rather than explicitly optimizing for lung protection through precise control of alveolar ventilation and tidal volume. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for a next-generation ventilator control methodology that systematically prioritizes lung-protective ventilation across diverse pathophysiological states, thereby reducing the risk of volutrauma, atelectrauma, and other forms of ventilator-associated lung injury.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for controlling a mechanical ventilator that implements a novel ventilation strategy known as mid-frequency ventilation (MFV). This method comprises receiving patient-specific respiratory parameters—including alveolar minute ventilation requirement, dead space ratio, and inspiratory and expiratory time constants—and using these inputs within a mathematical model to compute optimal ventilator settings that maximize alveolar ventilation while minimizing tidal volume for a given inspiratory pressure above PEEP. The invention further discloses a mechanical ventilator system comprising an air source, a patient circuit, one or more sensors (including a return air sensor), a patient monitor, and a ventilator control unit operatively connected to execute the MFV algorithm. Additionally, the invention encompasses a non-transitory computer-readable medium storing instructions that, when executed by a processor, cause the ventilator to perform the steps of the MFV methodology. Central to the invention is the concept of mid-frequency mandatory ventilation, wherein the ventilator operates in a pressure-controlled continuous mandatory mode with dynamically adjusted frequency and duty cycle to achieve target alveolar ventilation with the lowest possible tidal volume, thereby promoting lung protection across a spectrum of respiratory pathologies, including normal lungs, restrictive disorders (e.g., ARDS, morbid obesity), and obstructive diseases (e.g., COPD, status asthmaticus).

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The ventilator system 10 of the present invention is a comprehensive, integrated platform designed to deliver lung-protective mechanical ventilation through real-time optimization of key ventilatory parameters. At its core, the system includes an air source 12 capable of delivering medical-grade gas mixtures at controlled pressures and flow rates. Connected to the air source 12 is a patient circuit 14, which consists of inspiratory and expiratory limbs configured to interface with the patient’s airway via an endotracheal tube or tracheostomy cannula. Integrated within the expiratory limb is a return air sensor 18, which measures exhaled gas flow, volume, and composition to provide critical feedback on ventilation efficacy and patient respiratory mechanics. A patient monitor 20 is communicatively linked to the system to display vital signs, blood gas estimates, and ventilator waveforms, enabling continuous clinical oversight. The ventilator control 22 serves as the central processing unit, executing the MFV algorithm and coordinating all subsystems.

Mid-frequency operation is achieved through a closed-loop control architecture that continuously evaluates and adjusts ventilator settings based on physiological targets. An optimization system is embedded within the control logic to iteratively refine parameters toward optimal performance. This system may incorporate expert systems—rule-based artificial intelligence modules trained on clinical guidelines and physiological models—to guide decision-making under uncertainty. The control system 50 interfaces with multiple hardware and software components, including a pressure and volume monitor interface 52 that receives real-time data from transducers measuring airway pressure and delivered tidal volume. A return air sensor interface 54 processes signals from the return air sensor 18, while a patient monitor interface 56 facilitates bidirectional communication with external monitoring devices.

A parameter calculation component 58 computes derived respiratory variables such as alveolar minute ventilation, dead space fraction, and time constants from raw sensor data. These values feed into the optimization system 60, which employs an adaptive rule-based optimization approach to determine ideal ventilator settings. The air source interface regulates gas delivery in response to control commands. The overall methodology 100 begins by initiating pressure-controlled ventilation with baseline settings. The system then computes the current minute alveolar ventilation and compares it to a predefined target range. If the computed value exceeds the target, the system decreases tidal volume by reducing inspiratory pressure and simultaneously increases the frequency of the respiratory cycle to maintain alveolar ventilation. The system recomputes minute alveolar ventilation and assesses whether the adjustment yielded the desired reduction. Through iterative testing, the system determines the optimal frequency that achieves target alveolar ventilation with minimal tidal volume and sets this value.

Next, the system optimizes the duty cycle—the ratio of inspiratory time to total respiratory cycle time. It first reduces the duty cycle and recomputes alveolar ventilation; if ventilation increases, the base duty cycle is restored. If not, the duty cycle is incrementally increased until alveolar ventilation improves, at which point the optimal duty cycle is identified and set. Finally, the system optimizes positive end-expiratory pressure (PEEP). It increases both PEEP and peak inspiratory pressure in tandem and monitors hemodynamic stability via integrated sensors or external monitors. If instability is detected, the optimization is aborted and baseline values are restored. If stable, the system then decreases PEEP and peak pressure to find the lowest effective level that maintains oxygenation and recruitment without compromising circulation. Upon confirming hemodynamic tolerance, the optimal PEEP is established and locked in, completing the initialization sequence of the MFV protocol.
