## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to brain-machine interfaces (BMIs), and more particularly to a novel decoder architecture for translating neural activity into kinematic outputs such as cursor position and velocity in real time. The invention specifically concerns a Big-Data Multiplicative Recurrent Neural Network (BD-MRNN) that enables robust, high-performance BMI control across diverse and variable neural recording conditions without requiring frequent recalibration or retraining during use.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Brain-machine interfaces hold significant promise for restoring motor function to individuals with paralysis or limb loss by decoding neural signals into actionable commands for prosthetic devices or computer cursors. However, a major obstacle to the clinical translation of BMIs is their susceptibility to performance degradation caused by changes in neural recording conditions over time. These changes may arise from electrode drift, signal attenuation, biological tissue responses, variations in user cognitive state, or environmental interference. Conventional decoders, such as the widely used Kalman filter-based approaches (e.g., FIT-KF), typically require daily recalibration using fresh training data collected immediately before each use session. This dependency on same-day calibration imposes a substantial burden on users and limits the practicality of long-term BMI deployment.

Moreover, even when trained on large datasets spanning multiple days, linear decoders like the Kalman filter fail to generalize effectively across heterogeneous recording conditions because they assume stationarity in the neural-to-kinematic mapping—a condition rarely met in real-world settings. Nonlinear adaptive decoders have been proposed to address this issue, but they often require periods of supervised or unsupervised learning during closed-loop operation, which can disrupt user control and introduce instability. There remains a critical need for a fixed, non-adaptive decoder that inherently accommodates neural variability through its architecture and training methodology, thereby maintaining high performance without interrupting user interaction or demanding frequent recalibration.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a brain-machine interface decoder based on a Big-Data Multiplicative Recurrent Neural Network (BD-MRNN) that overcomes the limitations of prior art by achieving both high decoding accuracy and exceptional robustness to neural recording condition changes. The BD-MRNN implements a nonlinear, multiplicative neural-to-kinematic mapping function that dynamically modulates its internal recurrent connectivity based on instantaneous neural input patterns. This architecture enables the decoder to implicitly select context-appropriate decoding strategies from a learned repertoire of neural-kinematic relationships.

A key advantage of the invention is its ability to leverage large repositories of historical neural and behavioral data—spanning months or even years—to train a single, unified decoder that performs reliably across a wide range of recording conditions, including those not explicitly encountered during training. By incorporating deliberate perturbations into the training data (e.g., simulating electrode dropout or global firing rate shifts), the BD-MRNN learns generalized representations that are resilient to unexpected signal degradations. In closed-loop experiments, the BD-MRNN consistently outperformed state-of-the-art Kalman filter decoders, both in ideal conditions and under severe challenges such as sudden loss of high-informative electrodes or use of stale training data separated by multi-month gaps. Critically, this robustness is achieved without sacrificing peak performance; indeed, the BD-MRNN slightly exceeds same-day Kalman filter performance under nominal conditions. The invention thus enables reliable, high-performance BMI control with minimal user burden, representing a significant advance toward clinically viable neuroprosthetic systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Introduction to Big-Data Multiplicative Recurrent Neural Network (BD-MRNN)

The core of the present invention is a Big-Data Multiplicative Recurrent Neural Network (BD-MRNN), a specialized artificial recurrent neural network designed to decode neural population activity into continuous kinematic variables such as hand or cursor position and velocity. Unlike conventional recurrent networks that treat external inputs as additive biases, the BD-MRNN employs a multiplicative interaction between input signals and the network’s internal recurrent dynamics. This design allows the network to adapt its functional connectivity in real time based on the statistical properties of the incoming neural data, effectively enabling it to switch between different decoding regimes depending on the current recording context. The BD-MRNN is trained offline on extensive datasets comprising neural recordings and corresponding kinematic measurements collected over many days, thereby learning a rich library of neural-to-kinematic mappings that span diverse physiological and technical conditions.

### MRNN Definition

The BD-MRNN is defined by a continuous-time dynamical system governed by an N-dimensional vector of activation variables **x**(t), where each component represents the internal state of a computational unit within the network. The corresponding firing rates are given by **r**(t) = tanh(**x**(t)), ensuring bounded, smooth output. The temporal evolution of **x**(t) is described by the differential equation:

d**x**/dt = (−**x** + **J**(**u**(t))**r** + **b**_x)/τ,

where τ is a time constant set to reflect physiological timescales (typically hundreds of milliseconds), **b**_x is a bias vector, and **J**(**u**(t)) is an input-dependent recurrent weight matrix. Crucially, **J**(**u**(t)) is not fixed but is instead parameterized by the instantaneous neural input **u**(t)—a vector of binned spike counts from E electrodes. This multiplicative coupling allows the input to directly reshape the network’s effective connectivity, enabling context-sensitive processing. To make this high-dimensional tensor tractable, **J**(**u**(t)) is factorized via three learnable matrices: **J**_xf (N×F), **J**_fu (F×E), and **J**_fx (F×N), such that **J**(**u**(t)) = **J**_xf diag(**J**_fu **u**(t)) **J**_fx, where F is a tunable factor controlling model complexity.

### MRNN Output Definition

The kinematic output **z**(t) of the BD-MRNN is derived from a linear readout of the network’s firing rates: **z**(t) = **W**_o **r**(t) + **b**_z, where **W**_o is an M×N output weight matrix and **b**_z is an output bias vector. In the preferred embodiment for two-dimensional cursor control, M=2, yielding separate outputs for horizontal (x) and vertical (y) components of either position or velocity. Two independent BD-MRNNs are trained—one to predict normalized hand position and another to predict hand velocity—whose outputs are subsequently blended during real-time operation to produce a stable, drift-corrected cursor trajectory.

### Network Construction for Cursor BMI Decoder

For implementation as a cursor BMI decoder, the BD-MRNN is constructed with network size N=100 for subjects implanted with two 96-electrode arrays (E=192) and N=50 for those with a single array (E=96). The factor dimension F is set equal to N to provide sufficient representational capacity. Initial weights for **J**_xf, **J**_fu, and **J**_fx are drawn from zero-mean Gaussian distributions with variances scaled inversely by input/output dimensions to ensure stable dynamics. Output weights **W**_o and biases **b**_x, **b**_z are initialized to zero. This configuration balances computational efficiency with the ability to model complex, nonlinear neural-kinematic relationships observed in motor cortical activity.

### MRNN Initialization

Prior to training, all network parameters are initialized according to the scheme described above to ensure numerical stability and facilitate convergence. Specifically, the elements of **J**_xf, **J**_fu, and **J**_fx are sampled independently from Gaussian distributions with zero mean and variances g_xf/F, g_fu/E, and g_fx/N, respectively, where g_xf = g_fu = g_fx = 1.0. The output matrix **W**_o and bias vectors **b**_x, **b**_z are set to zero matrices/vectors. This initialization strategy promotes balanced excitation and inhibition within the recurrent dynamics while allowing the readout layer to develop meaningful kinematic predictions during training.

### Concatenating Neural Trials for Seeding the MRNN During Training

To provide the BD-MRNN with sufficient temporal context for accurate decoding, individual experimental trials (each consisting of a point-to-point reach) are concatenated into longer synthetic training sequences. Specifically, five consecutive actual reaching trials are joined end-to-end to form one MRNN training trial. The first two actual trials in this sequence are used solely to initialize (“seed”) the hidden state **x**(t) of the network and are excluded from the error computation during training. The subsequent three actual trials constitute the learning segment, where the network’s predictions are compared against ground-truth kinematics to compute gradients. This approach ensures that the network begins each learning segment with a physiologically plausible internal state reflective of ongoing movement dynamics.

### Perturbing the Neural Input During Training

A critical innovation of the BD-MRNN is the intentional perturbation of neural inputs during training to enhance robustness against recording condition changes. For each training iteration, the binned spike counts **u**(t) are modified by stochastically adding or removing spikes according to two hierarchical Gaussian noise processes. A global modulation factor η_j ~ N(1, σ_trial²) scales all electrodes’ spike counts within a trial to simulate day-level variations (e.g., due to arousal or array micromotion). Additionally, an electrode-specific factor η_c ~ N(1, σ_electrode²) independently modulates each electrode’s activity to mimic localized signal changes (e.g., partial electrode failure). If the perturbed spike count falls outside valid bounds (negative or exceeding maximum possible spikes per bin), it is resampled until valid. Spikes are then added to or removed from random time bins within the trial to achieve the target count. This data augmentation exposes the network to a broad spectrum of plausible signal degradations, teaching it to maintain performance even under unfamiliar conditions.

### Using Many Days Training Data

The BD-MRNN is trained on expansive datasets aggregating neural and kinematic data from dozens to hundreds of recording sessions spanning many months. This “big-data” approach exploits the empirical observation that neural correlates of movement exhibit statistical similarities across days, even when separated by long intervals. During minibatch construction, trials are randomly sampled from all available training days to ensure uniform exposure to diverse recording conditions. The multiplicative architecture of the BD-MRNN is uniquely suited to integrate such heterogeneous data, as it can learn distinct decoding strategies for different input regimes without catastrophic interference. Offline evaluations confirm that decoding accuracy improves monotonically with the number of training days, demonstrating the model’s capacity to extract reusable structure from accumulated experience.

### Network Output

Two separate BD-MRNNs are trained in parallel: one to decode normalized hand position (x, y) and another to decode hand velocity (v_x, v_y). Velocity labels are derived from position data via central finite differences. During real-time BMI operation, these outputs are combined using a weighted blend: cursor displacement Δx(t) = β γ_p p_x(t) + (1−β) γ_v v_x(t), and similarly for Δy(t), where β=0.99 heavily favors velocity for responsiveness while retaining a small position component (γ_p, γ_v are scaling factors) to correct cumulative drift. This hybrid strategy yields smooth, accurate cursor trajectories that closely mimic natural hand movements.

### Training and Running the Networks

Training is performed offline using the Hessian-Free (HF) optimization algorithm, a second-order method well-suited for recurrent networks. Gradients are computed via backpropagation through time on the mean squared error between predicted and actual kinematics. Critical HF parameters include minibatch size (set to one-fifth of total trials), initial lambda (0.1), and max conjugate-gradient iterations (50). Training proceeds for 200 epochs, with network snapshots saved every 10 epochs; the snapshot with lowest validation error is selected for deployment. Once trained, the network is compiled into an embedded real-time system. At runtime, binned spike counts (25 ms bins) are fed into the BD-MRNN, which updates its hidden state via Euler integration (Δt=25 ms) and outputs blended position/velocity estimates. The cursor position is updated each cycle, and the hidden state and velocity are initialized to zero at session start. The model assumes that neural-kinematic relationships in new data resemble some subset of those in the training corpus—a premise validated by empirical subspace similarity analyses. Analogous to how deep networks trained on vast image datasets generalize across handwriting styles, the BD-MRNN leverages its extensive training to handle novel neural contexts without explicit adaptation.
