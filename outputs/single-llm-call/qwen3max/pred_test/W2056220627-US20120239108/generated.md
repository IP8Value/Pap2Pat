## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of implantable medical devices for neuromodulation and, more particularly, to energy-efficient electrical stimulation systems and methods that optimize both stimulus waveform parameters and compliance voltage regulation to reduce power consumption during neural tissue activation. The invention encompasses apparatuses, methods, and systems for delivering therapeutic electrical stimulation using non-rectangular waveforms in conjunction with dynamically adjustable compliance voltages to achieve significant reductions in energy expenditure compared to conventional fixed-compliance, rectangular-pulse stimulators.

## BACKGROUND

Electrical stimulation of excitable biological tissues, particularly neural structures, has become a well-established therapeutic modality for treating a wide range of neurological and physiological disorders. Implantable pulse generators (IPGs) are commonly employed to deliver controlled electrical stimuli to targeted neural populations in applications such as deep brain stimulation (DBS), spinal cord stimulation (SCS), peripheral nerve stimulation, and cochlear implants. These devices typically operate by generating biphasic current pulses through electrodes implanted adjacent to or within neural tissue, thereby inducing action potentials that modulate neural activity and alleviate symptoms associated with conditions like Parkinson’s disease, chronic pain, epilepsy, and hearing loss. Conventional IPGs utilize constant-current stimulation circuitry powered by internal batteries, which may be either primary (non-rechargeable) or secondary (rechargeable) cells sealed within a hermetic housing. A critical limitation of existing clinical neuromodulation systems is their relatively inefficient energy utilization, which directly constrains device longevity and necessitates frequent surgical interventions for battery replacement or recharging. Traditional stimulators employ fixed compliance voltages—typically set well above the minimum required to ensure reliable operation across a broad range of patient-specific impedances and stimulation thresholds. This conservative design choice results in substantial energy dissipation across the current-regulating transistor(s) during each stimulation pulse, as the excess voltage beyond what is needed to drive current through the electrode-tissue load is wasted as heat. Furthermore, standard clinical practice relies almost exclusively on rectangular current waveforms, despite theoretical and emerging experimental evidence suggesting that alternative waveform shapes could reduce the energy required to elicit neural activation. The combination of fixed high compliance voltages and suboptimal waveform geometries represents a significant untapped opportunity for improving the energy efficiency of implantable neurostimulation systems.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides an energy-efficient electrical stimulation system and method that significantly reduces power consumption during neural tissue activation by synergistically optimizing both the temporal shape of the stimulus waveform and the compliance voltage supplied to the stimulation circuitry. In one aspect, the invention employs non-rectangular current waveforms—particularly centered-triangular pulses comprising a symmetric ramp-up followed by a ramp-down phase—that require less energy to reach neural activation threshold compared to conventional rectangular pulses of equivalent pulse width. In another aspect, the invention implements a variable compliance voltage regulator that dynamically adjusts the supply voltage to the minimum level necessary to maintain constant-current output during each stimulation phase, thereby minimizing wasteful voltage drops across the current source transistor. The compliance voltage may be adjusted in real time based on feedback from the stimulation load or pre-programmed according to known tissue impedance and threshold characteristics. The system further incorporates intelligent selection of pulse width based on the target axon fiber diameter, recognizing that energy-optimal pulse durations vary inversely with fiber size. By integrating these three strategies—biophysically optimized waveform shape, dynamically minimized compliance voltage, and fiber-diameter-appropriate pulse width—the invention achieves substantial energy savings over traditional neurostimulation approaches, thereby extending battery life, reducing thermal load, and enabling smaller, longer-lasting implantable devices without compromising therapeutic efficacy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The energy-efficient stimulation apparatus of the present invention comprises an implantable pulse generator (IPG) configured to deliver therapeutic electrical stimuli to neural tissue using advanced waveform and power management techniques. Central to this apparatus is the use of non-rectangular waveforms, specifically centered-triangular current pulses, which have been experimentally demonstrated to reduce energy consumption by approximately 12% compared to the most efficient rectangular pulses when activating motor fibers in peripheral nerves. Unlike conventional rectangular pulses that maintain a constant amplitude throughout the pulse duration, the centered-triangular waveform features a linear increase in current to a peak value at the midpoint of the pulse, followed by a symmetric linear decrease back to baseline. This shape better matches the biophysical integration properties of neuronal membranes, resulting in lower total energy delivery for threshold activation.

A block diagram of the stimulation system, referred to herein as system 10, includes a power source, stimulation apparatus, controller, input interface, and feedback mechanisms. The power source typically consists of a rechargeable lithium-ion or primary lithium battery housed within a hermetically sealed titanium can, providing the primary energy reservoir for all device functions. The stimulation apparatus generates precisely controlled current pulses and includes a variable compliance regulator that dynamically sets the voltage headroom available to the current source circuitry. This regulator is a key innovation, as it eliminates the inefficiency inherent in fixed-compliance designs where excess voltage is continuously dissipated as heat regardless of actual load requirements.

The variable compliance regulator operates under the control of a microcontroller or application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) that processes stimulation parameters and load feedback. Pulse generation circuitry, which may include digital-to-analog converters (DACs), operational amplifiers, and switching transistors, shapes the output current according to programmed waveform templates. The controller receives input from an external programmer via telemetry and stores user-defined stimulation protocols, including waveform type, pulse width, amplitude, frequency, and electrode configuration. The output electrical signal is delivered through electrodes—typically platinum-iridium or iridium oxide contacts arranged in arrays or cuffs—configured in monopolar, bipolar, or multipolar arrangements depending on the clinical application.

An input interface allows clinicians or patients to program stimulation parameters via wireless communication, while a feedback loop monitors voltage and current at the output stage to dynamically adjust the compliance voltage in real time. Stimulus waveform parameters include pulse width (ranging from 10 to 1000 microseconds), peak current amplitude (0.1 to 20 milliamperes), interphase interval (e.g., 100 microseconds), and waveform shape (rectangular, triangular, Gaussian, or custom). The stimulus waveform is illustrated as having distinct cathodic and anodic phases separated by a brief interphase gap to ensure charge balancing and prevent tissue damage.

Pulse width is defined as the total duration of the cathodic phase for monophasic pulses or the sum of cathodic and anodic durations for biphasic pulses, though for non-rectangular waveforms, alternative definitions based on full-width at half-maximum may be employed. Examples of energy-efficient waveform shapes include centered-triangular, rising exponential, decaying exponential, and hybrid forms that combine linear and nonlinear segments. Mathematical expressions for output current waveforms include I(t) = (2I_peak / PW) * t for 0 ≤ t ≤ PW/2 and I(t) = 2I_peak - (2I_peak / PW) * t for PW/2 < t ≤ PW for a centered-triangular pulse.

Energy and charge requirements are calculated using E = ∫ V_load(t) * I_stim(t) dt and Q = ∫ I_stim(t) dt, respectively. Simulated transmembrane voltage responses demonstrate that non-rectangular waveforms achieve threshold depolarization with lower integral energy. Input interfaces enable setting of stimulation parameters via clinician programmers or patient remote controls, while pulse width determination may be automated based on fiber diameter estimates derived from clinical context (e.g., 200 µs for large SCS fibers vs. 600 µs for small DBS fibers).

Computer simulations of fiber populations show that energy minimization is diameter-dependent, with optimal pulse widths decreasing as fiber size increases. Programming sessions establish patient-specific parameters through threshold mapping, and calibration processes determine minimum compliance voltages for each electrode configuration. The variable compliance regulator is configured dynamically using feedback from current sense resistors, and control signals adjust the DC-DC switched-mode converter output to match real-time needs.

The compliance voltage is combined with the stimulus waveform such that the minimum potential required to maintain current regulation is applied only when necessary. Pulse-width modulated signals drive the DC-DC converter to regulate output efficiently. Internal circuitry benefits from optimized compliance, and the anodic phase may use a different compliance level than the cathodic phase. The regulator operates in adjustable mode (dynamic feedback) or fixed mode (pre-set voltage), with peak stimulation amplitudes often exceeding nominal battery voltage due to boost conversion.

Energy consumption in adjustable mode is significantly lower than in fixed mode, as demonstrated by plots showing charge versus pulse width and energy threshold curves. Dynamic compliance yields greater savings than static adjustment, with graphs illustrating reduced load and compliance power. Output waveforms under variable compliance show tighter voltage margins around the load requirement.

The IPG system is a self-contained unit featuring a control system (microprocessor/ASIC), transceiver (RF coil and ASIC for telemetry), output system (current sources and switches), pulse generator circuits, power system (battery, charger, regulators), and battery charging subsystem (inductive receiver and management IC). Feedback from the output system informs compliance adjustments, and information is transmitted bidirectionally via the transceiver. Rechargeable batteries are managed by dedicated charging control circuitry.

Alternative implementations may omit explicit compliance regulation by using dynamic voltage drivers that directly modulate output voltage based on current feedback. Current sense resistors provide real-time load monitoring, enabling precise control of voltage and current waveforms. Permutations of these components and methodologies yield numerous embodiments within the scope of the invention, including hardware, software, and computer-readable medium implementations that execute energy optimization algorithms.
