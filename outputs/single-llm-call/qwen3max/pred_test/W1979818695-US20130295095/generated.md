# DESCRIPTION

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cognitive decline, memory loss, and eventual neuronal death. Central to the pathogenesis of AD is the accumulation of amyloid-β (Aβ) peptides in the brain, which aggregate into various forms including monomers, oligomers, protofibrils, and mature fibrillar plaques. Among these, soluble Aβ oligomers have emerged as particularly toxic species that correlate strongly with synaptic dysfunction and cognitive impairment, often preceding the formation of extracellular amyloid plaques. The Aβ peptide is generated through the sequential cleavage of the amyloid precursor protein (APP) by β- and γ-secretases, resulting primarily in Aβ1-40 and Aβ1-42 isoforms. However, post-translational modifications and N-terminal truncations give rise to a heterogeneous mixture of Aβ variants, some of which exhibit enhanced aggregation propensity and neurotoxicity. Of particular relevance is the pyroglutamate-modified form of Aβ starting at position 3 (AβpE3), which arises from the cyclization of an N-terminal glutamate residue following enzymatic truncation. This modified peptide demonstrates increased resistance to degradation, heightened hydrophobicity, and a strong tendency to form stable oligomeric assemblies. The presence of AβpE3 has been consistently detected in the brains of AD patients, where it co-localizes with both intraneuronal inclusions and cerebral amyloid angiopathy, suggesting a critical role in disease progression. Recent evidence indicates that AβpE3 oligomers may serve as key seeding species that accelerate the aggregation of full-length Aβ peptides, thereby amplifying neurotoxic cascades. Given their unique structural and pathological properties, AβpE3 oligomers represent a compelling target for both diagnostic and therapeutic intervention in AD and related amyloidopathies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Alzheimer’s disease is the most common cause of dementia among the elderly, affecting millions worldwide. It is neuropathologically defined by the presence of extracellular amyloid plaques composed predominantly of aggregated Aβ peptides and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles consisting of hyperphosphorylated tau protein. While the precise mechanisms underlying AD remain incompletely understood, the amyloid cascade hypothesis posits that the accumulation of Aβ peptides initiates a sequence of events leading to synaptic dysfunction, neuroinflammation, oxidative stress, and ultimately neuronal loss. Within this framework, Aβ peptides—particularly the longer and more aggregation-prone Aβ1-42—are considered central players. However, not all Aβ species are equally pathogenic. N-terminally truncated and modified Aβ variants, such as those beginning with pyroglutamate at position 3 (AβpE3), have been increasingly recognized for their enhanced stability, resistance to proteolysis, and potent neurotoxicity. These peptides are formed through the action of aminopeptidases that remove the first two N-terminal amino acids of Aβ1-42, followed by glutaminyl cyclase (QC)-mediated cyclization of the exposed glutamate residue to pyroglutamate. AβpE3 was first identified in the core of amyloid plaques in AD brains and has since been shown to be abundant in both sporadic and familial AD cases. Its aggregation kinetics differ markedly from full-length Aβ: AβpE3 rapidly forms low-molecular-weight oligomers but exhibits reduced propensity to progress to mature fibrils, thereby accumulating in a highly toxic soluble oligomeric state. This behavior underscores its potential as a primary driver of early synaptic toxicity. Therapeutic strategies targeting QC inhibition have been explored to reduce AβpE3 formation, yet such approaches may lack specificity and fail to address pre-existing oligomeric pools. Transgenic mouse models overexpressing mutant human APP, such as APP/PS1KI and 5XFAD mice, recapitulate key features of AD pathology, including robust intraneuronal Aβ accumulation, plaque deposition, gliosis, and behavioral deficits. Notably, these models exhibit age-dependent increases in AβpE3, particularly within neurons, supporting the relevance of intraneuronal oligomers in disease progression. Current therapeutic modalities, including broad-spectrum anti-Aβ antibodies, often target multiple Aβ forms without discrimination, potentially limiting efficacy or triggering adverse effects such as amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA). Moreover, existing diagnostics struggle to differentiate between benign and pathogenic Aβ aggregates. Thus, there remains a critical unmet need for agents that specifically recognize and neutralize the most neurotoxic Aβ species—namely, oligomeric AβpE3—while enabling precise diagnosis and monitoring of disease activity.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses the technical problem of selectively targeting and detecting pathogenic Aβ oligomers, specifically those derived from N-terminally truncated and pyroglutamate-modified Aβ starting at position 3 (AβpE3). The solution lies in the development of novel binding molecules, particularly monoclonal antibodies, that specifically recognize oligomeric conformations of AβpE3 without cross-reacting with full-length Aβ1-42 or monomeric forms. These antibodies, exemplified by 9D5H6 and 8C4D2, enable neuropathological differentiation between healthy individuals and those suffering from Alzheimer’s disease or other amyloid-related disorders by selectively staining intraneuronal and vascular AβpE3 oligomers in human brain tissue. The invention provides new diagnostic tools capable of detecting AβpE3 oligomers in biological samples such as plasma, facilitating non-invasive diagnosis and monitoring. Furthermore, the antibodies serve as pharmaceutical agents that inhibit Aβ oligomerization, reduce plaque burden, and rescue behavioral deficits in animal models. In a key embodiment, passive immunization of 5XFAD mice with the 9D5 antibody resulted in significant reductions in AβpE3 levels, total Aβ plaque load in cortex and hippocampus, and normalization of anxiety-related behaviors in the elevated plus maze. This demonstrates that targeting oligomeric AβpE3 is not only diagnostically informative but also therapeutically efficacious. The invention thus identifies oligomeric AβpE3 as a key component in the pathogenesis of AD and provides methods for its detection, quantification, and neutralization. Compared to glutaminyl cyclase inhibitors, which prevent formation but do not eliminate existing oligomers, the disclosed antibodies directly engage and clear pathogenic aggregates. The binding molecules of the invention are capable of inhibiting Aβ-oligomerization in vitro and in vivo, and can be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions for the treatment or prevention of amyloid-related disorders. Diagnostic methods include determining the amount of Aβ oligomers in a biological sample from a subject suspected of having AD, comparing it to a reference amount, and diagnosing the disease based on deviation from normal levels. Similarly, the invention enables monitoring and prediction of treatment efficacy by tracking changes in oligomer levels over time. These advances represent a paradigm shift toward precision targeting of the most toxic Aβ species, offering improved diagnostic accuracy and therapeutic outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The antibodies of the present invention, particularly monoclonal antibodies 9D5H6 and 8C4D2, were generated by immunizing Balb/c mice with AβpE3-38 peptide and selecting hybridomas that produce antibodies specific for oligomeric AβpE3-42 but not Aβ1-42. Immunohistochemical analysis in APP/PS1KI and 5xFAD transgenic mice revealed abundant intraneuronal staining with these antibodies, which increased with age and was absent in young animals. In human post-mortem brain tissue from sporadic and familial AD cases, strong immunostaining was observed in neurons and cerebral blood vessels, consistent with cerebral amyloid angiopathy, whereas healthy control specimens showed negligible staining. This differential recognition enables neuropathological distinction between diseased and non-diseased states. The antibodies specifically recognize AβpE3-42 oligomers in SDS-Western blot and Dot Blot assays under both denaturing and native conditions, with no cross-reactivity to Aβ1-42, confirming epitope specificity. A sandwich ELISA using 9D5 as the capture antibody and biotinylated 2-48 as the detection antibody demonstrated significantly lower levels of AβpE3 oligomers in plasma from AD patients compared to healthy controls, suggesting sequestration of oligomers in the brain and supporting their use as peripheral biomarkers. Diagnostic methods involve obtaining a biological sample (e.g., plasma, cerebrospinal fluid, or tissue biopsy), determining the amount of Aβ oligomers using the disclosed antibodies, and comparing this amount to a reference value derived from healthy populations; a statistically significant deviation indicates disease presence, progression, or response to therapy. The binding molecules may be full-length antibodies (IgG1, IgG2a, IgG2b, etc.), fragments (Fab, scFv, F(ab')2), or non-antibody scaffolds such as peptidomimetics or small molecules, provided they specifically bind oligomeric AβpE3 and inhibit its aggregation. Antibody 9D5H6, produced by hybridoma PG3-38 9D5H6 deposited under international standards, comprises specific complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) within its variable heavy and light chains, encoded by nucleic acid sequences that can be expressed in prokaryotic or eukaryotic host cells (e.g., CHO, HEK293) using standard recombinant techniques. Pharmaceutical compositions include the binding molecule in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as saline, phosphate-buffered saline, or formulations containing stabilizers, preservatives, and excipients suitable for parenteral administration. Dosage regimens may involve intravenous, intraperitoneal, or subcutaneous delivery, with doses ranging from 0.1 to 100 mg/kg body weight, administered weekly or biweekly. The invention further encompasses kits containing the antibody, detection reagents, and instructions for use in diagnosis or research. Methods for identifying additional therapeutic agents include thioflavin T aggregation assays to measure inhibition of AβpE3 oligomerization, immunoisolation to assess binding affinity, and in vivo validation in transgenic mouse models. The term “amyloid-related disorder” includes Alzheimer’s disease, cerebral amyloid angiopathy, Down syndrome-associated AD, and other conditions involving AβpE3 deposition. Validation studies confirm that the reference amount is established using cohorts of age-matched healthy controls, and sample processing includes standardized protocols for storage, homogenization, and assay performance. The invention thus provides a comprehensive platform for the diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment of diseases driven by toxic AβpE3 oligomers.

### EXAMPLES

Monoclonal antibodies 9D5 and 8C4 were generated by immunizing Balb/c mice with synthetic AβpE3-38 peptide. Lymph node cells were fused with P3-X63-Ag8 myeloma cells, and hybridoma supernatants were screened by ELISA against AβpE3-38, AβpE3-42, and Aβ1-42. Clones showing reactivity with AβpE3 peptides but not Aβ1-42 were subcloned and expanded. For comparison, commercial antibodies such as 4G8, W0-2, G2-10, G2-11, NT78, and 2-48 were used. Synthetic Aβ peptides were monomerized in formic acid, evaporated, and resuspended in ammonia before use. Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) on a Superdex 75 column separated oligomeric species, which were analyzed by dot blot using W0-2 and 9D5 antibodies. Western blotting was performed on 4–12% gels with 1.5 µg peptide per lane, transferred to nitrocellulose, and probed with primary antibodies followed by chemiluminescent detection. Statistical analysis employed one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-hoc test or unpaired t-tests using GraphPad Prism. Immunohistochemistry on paraffin-embedded human and mouse brain sections involved antigen retrieval with citrate buffer and formic acid, followed by primary antibody incubation overnight and detection via ABC-DAB or fluorescence. Dot blot competition assays confirmed that 9D5 and 8C4 share an overlapping epitope. In 5XFAD mice, 9D5 immunostaining revealed age-dependent intraneuronal accumulation starting at 6 months, with strong co-localization with microglial marker Iba-1 but not astrocytic GFAP. A sandwich ELISA using 9D5 (capture) and biotinylated 2-48 (detection) measured plasma oligomer levels in AD patients (n=20) and healthy controls (n=20); AD patients showed a 46% reduction (p<0.05). Thioflavin T assays demonstrated that 9D5 inhibits higher-order aggregation of AβpE3-42 without affecting initial oligomer formation. In SH-SY5Y cells, 9D5 completely abolished AβpE3-42-induced toxicity. Passive immunization of 4.5-month-old 5XFAD mice with 250 µg 9D5 weekly for 6 weeks reduced AβpE3, Aβ40, Aβ42, and total plaque load in hippocampus and cortex, and normalized anxiety behavior. Brain lysates showed decreased AβpE3 in both TBS-soluble and SDS-soluble fractions. These results collectively validate AβpE3 oligomers as a therapeutically relevant target and 9D5 as a dual-purpose diagnostic and therapeutic agent.
