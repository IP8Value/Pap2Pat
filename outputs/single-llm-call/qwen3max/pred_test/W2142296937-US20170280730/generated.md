## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biological control of vector-borne diseases, particularly malaria and dengue fever. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of a novel bacterial strain, Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P), as a biocontrol agent for reducing or preventing transmission of pathogens such as Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus by mosquito vectors. The invention further encompasses compositions comprising Csp_P or its metabolites, methods for administering such compositions to mosquitoes, and strategies for deploying Csp_P in environmental settings to suppress mosquito populations and block pathogen transmission.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Vector-borne diseases, including malaria and dengue fever, represent major global public health challenges, causing hundreds of millions of infections and hundreds of thousands of deaths annually. These diseases are transmitted to humans through the bites of infected female mosquitoes—primarily Anopheles species for malaria and Aedes species for dengue. Current control strategies rely heavily on insecticide-treated bed nets, indoor residual spraying, and larval source reduction; however, the emergence of insecticide resistance in mosquito populations and limited efficacy of existing tools underscore the urgent need for novel, sustainable interventions.  

A growing body of evidence indicates that the gut microbiota of mosquitoes plays a critical role in modulating vector competence—the ability of a mosquito to acquire, support, and transmit a pathogen. Certain native or introduced bacterial species have been shown to inhibit the development of Plasmodium parasites or dengue virus within the mosquito midgut, either by directly producing anti-pathogen compounds or by stimulating the mosquito’s innate immune responses. Despite these advances, no microbial-based intervention has yet been successfully deployed at scale for disease control. A significant barrier has been the lack of bacterial strains that combine high colonization efficiency, potent anti-pathogen activity, entomopathogenic properties, environmental stability, and safety for non-target organisms. The present invention addresses this unmet need through the identification and application of a uniquely effective bacterial isolate, Csp_P.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) as a novel entomopathogenic agent with exceptional utility in controlling mosquito-borne disease transmission. Csp_P exhibits potent anti-pathogen properties against both Plasmodium falciparum, the causative agent of human malaria, and dengue virus (DENV), the etiological agent of dengue fever. The invention proposes the use of Csp_P in integrated disease control strategies aimed at reducing the burden of these devastating illnesses.  

In vivo studies demonstrate that Csp_P effectively colonizes the midguts of key mosquito vectors—Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti—and significantly reduces infection prevalence and intensity of both P. falciparum and DENV. The invention further proposes direct exposure of adult and larval mosquitoes to Csp_P as a means to achieve rapid mortality, thereby suppressing vector populations. Notably, Csp_P displays broad-spectrum anti-pathogen activity, inhibiting not only malaria parasites and dengue virus but also a wide range of other bacteria commonly found in the mosquito gut, suggesting a competitive advantage in natural environments.  

The invention encompasses compositions formulated to deliver Csp_P or its bioactive components to mosquitoes, including liquid suspensions, dried powders, baits, and trap-based systems. These compositions may be applied via sugar-feeding stations, breeding site treatments, or aerial or ground-based sprays. The invention thus provides practical, scalable methods for controlling disease transmission by simultaneously reducing mosquito survival and blocking pathogen development within surviving vectors.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The scope of the present invention encompasses the use of Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P), its derivatives, metabolites, and biofilm-associated components, for the control of mosquito populations and the interruption of pathogen transmission cycles. The invention includes methods of administration, formulation strategies, and deployment mechanisms designed to maximize contact between Csp_P and target mosquito vectors in both laboratory and field settings.  

Terminology used herein is defined to ensure clarity and consistency. For instance, “Csp_P” refers specifically to the Chromobacterium sp. isolate designated Panamam, originally recovered from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes in Panama. This bacterium is Gram-negative, facultatively anaerobic, and capable of robust biofilm formation. It exhibits remarkable colonization ability in the mosquito midgut, rapidly establishing high-density populations following ingestion via sugar or blood meals.  

Csp_P demonstrates strong entomopathogenic activity, causing significant mortality in both larval and adult stages of An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti. This lethality occurs regardless of the presence or absence of native gut microbiota, indicating intrinsic mosquitocidal properties. Critically, Csp_P also exerts in vivo anti-dengue and anti-Plasmodium effects: mosquitoes colonized with Csp_P show markedly reduced infection rates with DENV and P. falciparum compared to controls.  

In vitro assays confirm that Csp_P produces stable, heat-labile secondary metabolites—particularly when grown in biofilm—that directly inhibit Plasmodium asexual and sexual stages as well as dengue virus infectivity. These effects are independent of the mosquito host, demonstrating that Csp_P’s anti-pathogen activity is mediated by secreted or matrix-associated compounds. The biofilm form of Csp_P is especially potent, with 48-hour mature biofilms showing superior activity compared to planktonic cultures or immature biofilms.  

Moreover, Csp_P exhibits broad-spectrum antibacterial activity, inhibiting the growth of diverse bacterial isolates derived from mosquito midguts, including close relatives such as Chromobacterium violaceum. This competitive exclusion likely enhances Csp_P’s persistence and dominance in the gut environment.  

Potential applications of Csp_P include its incorporation into bait stations that attract sugar-feeding mosquitoes, treatment of larval breeding sites, and integration into existing vector control programs. Mosquito control products based on Csp_P may take the form of slow-release formulations, sprayable concentrates, or dry powders suitable for aerial dispersal. The dual functionality of Csp_P—as both a mosquitocidal agent and a transmission-blocking symbiont—makes it a uniquely valuable tool for public health interventions.

### II. Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P)

Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) is a novel bacterial isolate with distinctive biological properties relevant to vector control. Csp_P exhibits potent insecticidal activity against multiple life stages of disease-vector mosquitoes, making it a promising candidate for biocontrol. Molecular characterization based on 16S rDNA gene sequencing reveals 98% similarity to Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum, placing it within the Chromobacterium genus but distinguishing it as a unique strain.  

Methods of controlling insects using Csp_P involve exposing target mosquitoes to viable cells, biofilm, or cell-free preparations derived from Csp_P cultures. Compositions incorporating Csp_P may include liquid suspensions, dried biomass, or formulated baits designed for environmental deployment. Extracts prepared from Csp_P cultures—through solvent extraction, filtration, or lyophilization—retain insecticidal activity and can be used in place of live bacteria where regulatory or safety considerations apply. Extraction procedures typically involve growing Csp_P under biofilm-inducing conditions, harvesting the biomass or supernatant, and processing it to concentrate active principles.  

Effective insect control amounts vary depending on the delivery method and target species but generally range from 10⁶ to 10¹⁰ colony-forming units (CFU) per milliliter in liquid formulations or equivalent dry weight in solid preparations. These concentrations have been shown to induce significant mortality in both An. gambiae and Ae. aegypti within 1–3 days of exposure.

### III. Administration of Compositions Comprising Csp_P to Insects

Administration of Csp_P-based compositions to insects may be achieved through multiple routes. Insects may be directly exposed to a composition comprising Csp_P in pure or substantially pure form, either as a liquid, powder, or immobilized matrix. The composition may be linked to a food component naturally consumed by the insect, such as sugar solutions for adult mosquitoes. Oral introduction is a primary route, wherein the insect ingests the composition during feeding. Alternatively, the composition may be incorporated into the medium in which insect larvae develop, such as standing water in breeding sites.  

Bait systems may be designed to lure insects using attractants such as floral volatiles, carbon dioxide, or visual cues. Upon contact, the insect internalizes the composition by ingestion. In social or colonial insects, baits may be carried back to the nest or breeding site, enabling secondary exposure of conspecifics. The bait itself may serve as a food source for other insects, amplifying dissemination.  

Baits may be housed in traps or dispensers constructed from biodegradable or durable materials. Suitable housings include cylindrical containers, hanging stations, or ground-level reservoirs. Traps may be distinguished from passive housings by incorporating mechanical or adhesive capture mechanisms. For instance, the inside surfaces of a trap may be lined with a sticky substance to retain insects after bait consumption. Bait may be held in place using a trough or absorbent pad to prevent spillage.  

Areas infested with mosquitoes may be treated directly with Csp_P compositions via spray formulations, which can be applied manually or through aerial delivery systems such as drones or aircraft. Formulations may be oil-based, water-based, or designed for residual activity on surfaces. Combining formulation types can enhance coverage, persistence, and efficacy. The methods and compositions described herein are also applicable to the control of other insect pests beyond mosquitoes, broadly defined as any arthropod that transmits disease, damages crops, or poses a nuisance to humans or animals.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples provide detailed disclosure and description of the invention, illustrating specific embodiments without limiting the scope of the claims. Variations and modifications within the spirit of the invention are contemplated.

### Example 1: Chromobacterium Csp_P Reduces Malaria and Dengue Infection in Vector Mosquitoes

Mosquito-borne diseases such as malaria and dengue fever remain leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide. Recent research has highlighted the critical influence of gut microbiota on vector competence, with certain bacterial species capable of inhibiting pathogen development. This example demonstrates that Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P), isolated from wild Aedes aegypti, possesses exceptional anti-pathogen and entomopathogenic properties.  

Csp_P was introduced to antibiotic-treated Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti via sugar meals containing 10⁸ and 10⁶ CFU/ml, respectively. Midgut dissections at three days post-exposure revealed colonization prevalence of 80% and 97%, with average loads of 10⁴–10⁵ CFU/midgut. In non-antibiotic-treated mosquitoes, Csp_P caused rapid mortality, with >95% of An. gambiae dying within two days, though initial colonization was near-universal.  

Larval exposure to Csp_P resulted in near-complete mortality within 2–3 days. Adult mosquitoes fed Csp_P via blood meals also exhibited reduced survival. Crucially, surviving Csp_P-colonized mosquitoes showed significantly lower infection rates with Plasmodium falciparum and dengue virus. Oocyst counts in An. gambiae and viral titers in Ae. aegypti were drastically reduced compared to controls.  

In vitro assays confirmed direct anti-pathogen effects. Filtered supernatants and resuspended biofilms from 48-hour Csp_P cultures inhibited Plasmodium asexual stages, gametocytes, and ookinetes, as well as dengue virus infectivity in plaque assays. Heat-inactivated biofilm lost activity, indicating proteinaceous or thermolabile metabolites. Csp_P biofilm did not sequester viral particles, alter pH to inhibitory levels, or damage host cells, confirming a specific anti-pathogen mechanism.  

Csp_P also induced expression of immune genes such as cecropin and defensin in mosquito midguts and cultured cells, suggesting immune potentiation contributes to pathogen blocking. Furthermore, Csp_P inhibited the growth of diverse mosquito gut bacteria, including C. violaceum, indicating strong competitive fitness.  

These findings establish Csp_P as a multifunctional biocontrol agent with potential for deployment in bait stations, larviciding, or combination therapies. Ethical oversight was maintained throughout, with protocols approved by institutional animal care and biosafety committees.

### CONCLUSION

Insect-bacteria associations play pivotal roles in modulating vector competence. The present invention introduces Chromobacterium sp. Panamam (Csp_P) as a novel bacterial strain with exceptional properties for disease control. Csp_P demonstrates potent anti-pathogen activities against both Plasmodium and dengue virus, exerts strong entomopathogenic effects on mosquito larvae and adults, and outcompetes other gut bacteria. Its ability to form biofilms enhances the stability and potency of its anti-pathogen metabolites. The invention proposes practical applications of Csp_P in public health interventions, including baited traps, breeding site treatments, and sprayable formulations, offering a promising new strategy for reducing the global burden of mosquito-borne diseases.

### Example 2: Live Chromobacterium Csp_P is a General Mosquitocidal, and Malaria and Dengue Transmission-Blocking Agent

This example further validates Csp_P as a broad-spectrum mosquitocidal and transmission-blocking agent. Adult Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti were exposed to Csp_P via sugar meals at varying concentrations. At 10⁸ CFU/ml, Csp_P caused rapid mortality in An. gambiae, while Ae. aegypti required higher doses (10¹⁰ CFU/ml) for similar effects. Colonization of the mosquito gut was confirmed by plating and PCR-based detection, showing efficient midgut establishment within 24 hours.  

Csp_P demonstrated killing activity across multiple mosquito species, including laboratory and field-derived strains. When mosquitoes were fed infectious blood containing Plasmodium or dengue virus, prior colonization with Csp_P reduced pathogen infection by over 90%. Direct addition of Csp_P to infectious blood meals enhanced this blocking effect, likely due to higher bacterial loads.  

Comparative studies with other Chromobacterium strains revealed that Csp_P exhibits superior anti-pathogen and mosquitocidal activity. Notably, cell-free supernatants and extracts from Csp_P cultures retained significant mosquitocidal activity, indicating that secreted metabolites—not just live bacteria—are responsible for toxicity. These metabolites are stable under ambient conditions but lose activity upon heat treatment, consistent with protein or complex organic molecule identity.  

These results confirm that Csp_P functions as a general mosquitocidal agent and a potent blocker of malaria and dengue transmission, supporting its development as a next-generation biocontrol tool.
