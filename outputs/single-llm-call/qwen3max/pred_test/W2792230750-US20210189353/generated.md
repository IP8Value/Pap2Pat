## DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to an isolated thermostable Amadoriase enzyme exhibiting enhanced thermal stability relative to wild-type Amadoriase I derived from *Aspergillus fumigatus*. Specifically, the invention provides engineered variants of Amadoriase I containing one or more amino acid substitutions that introduce disulfide bonds, thereby conferring improved resistance to thermal denaturation without substantially compromising enzymatic activity. These thermostable variants retain the native catalytic function of cleaving Amadori products—glycated amino acids—into free amines, glucosone, and hydrogen peroxide, while demonstrating significantly increased half-life and functional integrity at elevated temperatures. The invention further encompasses polynucleotides encoding these thermostable proteins, recombinant expression vectors, host cells capable of producing them, methods of production, and their use in diagnostic, therapeutic, food processing, and cosmetic applications where enzyme stability under thermal stress is critical.

### STATE OF THE ART

Glycation is a non-enzymatic, spontaneous chemical reaction between reducing sugars and free amino groups of proteins, peptides, or amino acids, leading to the formation of reversible Schiff bases that rearrange into more stable Amadori products. Over time, these early glycation adducts can undergo further oxidation, dehydration, and cross-linking to form advanced glycation end-products (AGEs), which are implicated in numerous pathological conditions including diabetes complications, atherosclerosis, nephropathy, retinopathy, and neuropathy. Monitoring glycated biomarkers such as glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) is essential for long-term diabetes management, as HbA1c reflects average blood glucose levels over the preceding two to three months due to the 120-day lifespan of erythrocytes.

Current clinical methods for HbA1c measurement include high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), immunoassays, and enzymatic assays. Among these, enzymatic approaches using Fructosyl Peptide Oxidases (FPOX), also known as Amadoriases, offer advantages in terms of specificity, simplicity, and compatibility with point-of-care biosensors. In such assays, HbA1c is first proteolytically digested to release N-terminal fructosyl-valine, which is then oxidized by Amadoriase to produce hydrogen peroxide; the latter is quantified colorimetrically via horseradish peroxidase-coupled reactions. However, a major limitation of existing enzymatic biosensors is the thermal lability of wild-type Amadoriase enzymes, which degrade during storage, transport, or operation at ambient or elevated temperatures, thereby reducing shelf-life and reliability.

Amadoriase enzymes, particularly Amadoriase I from *Aspergillus fumigatus*, have been explored not only for diagnostics but also for potential therapeutic deglycation of early glycation adducts and for industrial applications such as mitigating acrylamide formation in processed foods. Acrylamide, a probable human carcinogen, arises during high-temperature cooking (e.g., frying, baking) via the Maillard reaction between asparagine and reducing sugars. Amadoriase could intercept this pathway by degrading fructosyl-asparagine before it dehydrates to acrylamide. Nevertheless, the utility of wild-type Amadoriase in such high-temperature food processes is severely limited by its low thermostability, typically losing activity above 55°C.

Although prior efforts have employed directed evolution to enhance Amadoriase stability—yielding modest T50 increases of ~5°C—these approaches are labor-intensive and require extensive screening infrastructure. Rational design strategies based on structural insights offer a more targeted alternative, yet conventional computational tools for predicting stabilizing mutations often yield false positives or fail to account for dynamic protein behavior. Thus, there remains a compelling need for Amadoriase variants with markedly improved thermostability that retain catalytic efficiency, enabling robust performance in diagnostics, therapeutics, and industrial biocatalysis under thermally challenging conditions.

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides an isolated thermostable Amadoriase protein comprising at least one amino acid substitution that introduces a disulfide bond, wherein said protein exhibits a T50 value at least 2°C higher than that of wild-type Amadoriase I (SEQ ID NO: 1). In preferred embodiments, the thermostable Amadoriase comprises substitutions S67C and P121C (corresponding to SEQ ID NO: 3), or D295C and K303C (corresponding to SEQ ID NO: 4), or both pairs in combination. These variants, designated SS03 and SS17 respectively, demonstrate T50 increases of approximately +3°C and +8°C relative to wild-type enzyme.

A derivative of the isolated thermostable Amadoriase protein is also claimed, wherein the derivative retains at least 50% of the enzymatic activity of the parent variant under standard assay conditions and includes fusion proteins, chemically modified forms, or fragments containing the catalytic domain and the engineered disulfide bond(s). Additionally, the invention encompasses a crystal or isomorph of the isolated thermostable Amadoriase protein, particularly those crystallizing in space group P212121 (SS03) or P21 (SS17), with resolutions of 2.15 Å and 2.85 Å, respectively.

The invention further includes a recombinant vector comprising a polynucleotide encoding the thermostable Amadoriase, operably linked to a promoter functional in a host cell, and a host cell—preferably *Escherichia coli* BL21(DE3)pLysS—transformed with said vector for recombinant expression. Applications of the thermostable Amadoriase span multiple industries: in the food sector, it serves to reduce acrylamide formation during thermal processing by degrading fructosyl-amino acids; as a diagnostic tool, it enables stable, long-shelf-life biosensors for HbA1c or glycated albumin detection; and in therapeutic contexts, it provides a stabilized scaffold for future engineering toward protein deglycation capabilities.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Amadoriase, also known as Fructosyl Amino Acid Oxidase (FAOX), is a flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD)-dependent oxidoreductase that catalyzes the oxidative deglycation of Amadori products—specifically, fructosyl-amino acids—yielding a free amino acid, 3-deoxyglucosone (glucosone), and hydrogen peroxide. The enzyme belongs to the glucose-methanol-choline (GMC) oxidoreductase superfamily and is naturally produced by fungi and bacteria. Among isoforms, Amadoriase I from *Aspergillus fumigatus* (wild-type sequence provided as SEQ ID NO: 1; corresponding gene as SEQ ID NO: 2) has been widely studied for its substrate specificity toward N-terminal fructosyl-valine and fructosyl-lysine.

To address the inherent thermal instability of wild-type Amadoriase I, the present invention introduces engineered variants featuring site-specific disulfide bonds designed to rigidify flexible regions of the protein structure. Using a high-throughput computational pipeline combining SSBOND software for disulfide bond prediction and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to assess thermal fluctuation, nineteen candidate double-cysteine mutants were modeled. Screening based on the slope (λ) of average root mean square fluctuation (avg-RMSF) versus temperature identified four promising variants: SS03 (S67C/P121C; SEQ ID NO: 3), SS07 (H106C/G150C), SS11 (K233C/P404C), and SS17 (D295C/K303C; SEQ ID NO: 4). Experimental validation confirmed that SS03 and SS17 exhibit significantly enhanced thermostability, with T50 values of 55.25°C and 60.62°C, respectively, compared to 52.40°C for wild-type.

The term “substitution of amino acid” as used herein refers to the replacement of a native amino acid residue with another, specifically cysteine, to enable covalent disulfide bond formation between spatially proximate residues in the folded protein. The SS03 variant introduces a disulfide bond between residues 67 and 121, located in surface loops distant from the active site, resulting in minimal structural perturbation (RMSD 0.268 Å vs. wild-type) and preserved catalytic efficiency (kcat/Km = 64.17 s⁻¹ mM⁻¹ vs. 41.68 for WT). The SS17 variant forms a disulfide between residues 295 and 303, inducing a local conformational shift near the mutation site and a distal displacement of loop 63–69, yet maintaining near-native kinetics (kcat/Km = 33.34 s⁻¹ mM⁻¹) and remarkable residual activity up to 95°C.

Polynucleotide sequences encoding these variants are provided as SEQ ID NO: 5 (SS03) and SEQ ID NO: 7 (SS17), derived from the wild-type gene (SEQ ID NO: 2) by site-directed mutagenesis. Recombinant expression vectors, such as pET-3a derivatives, contain these sequences under control of inducible promoters (e.g., T7/lac). Host cells, particularly *E. coli* BL21(DE3)pLysS, are transformed with these vectors and cultured in LB medium supplemented with ampicillin. Protein expression is induced with IPTG at 25°C overnight, followed by cell lysis, Ni-NTA affinity chromatography (exploiting an N-terminal His-tag), and size-exclusion polishing.

The purified thermostable Amadoriase variants are used to deglycate molecules bearing Amadori adducts. In the food industry, they are added to dough, potato products, or coffee precursors prior to thermal treatment to hydrolyze fructosyl-asparagine, thereby suppressing acrylamide generation. In medical diagnostics, they serve as the enzymatic core of HbA1c test kits: a blood sample is digested with protease to liberate fructosyl-valine, which is then deglycated by the thermostable Amadoriase; the resulting hydrogen peroxide is measured colorimetrically. As biosensors, these enzymes are immobilized on electrodes or test strips, where their thermal resilience ensures consistent performance during shipping and storage. Therapeutically, while current variants act only on small glycated amino acids, their enhanced stability provides a robust platform for future engineering aimed at expanding substrate scope to intact glycated proteins.

A diagnostic kit for measuring glycated haemoglobin comprises the thermostable Amadoriase, a protease (e.g., proteinase K), horseradish peroxidase, a chromogenic substrate (e.g., o-phenylenediamine), and appropriate buffers. The method involves digesting a hemolysate sample, incubating with the Amadoriase to deglycate valine, and quantifying H₂O₂ formation spectrophotometrically at 322 nm (via glucosone) or 450 nm (via oxidized chromogen).

Protein expression and purification follow established protocols: the Amadoriase I gene is cloned into pET-3a; double-cysteine mutations are introduced via QuikChange mutagenesis; constructs are verified by sequencing; *E. coli* cells are transformed and induced; and protein is purified by Ni-NTA and Superdex 200 chromatography. Enzyme activity is assayed by monitoring glucosone formation at 322 nm, steady-state kinetics are determined via Michaelis-Menten analysis, and thermal stability is assessed by measuring residual activity after 10-minute heat challenges across 25–100°C, with T50 derived from Boltzmann sigmoidal fits. Crystallography confirms disulfide bond formation and structural integrity, validating the rational design approach.
