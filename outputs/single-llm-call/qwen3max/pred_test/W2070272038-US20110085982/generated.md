## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel human triple-negative breast cancer cell line designated DKAT, which exhibits phenotypic plasticity through reversible epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET). This cell line provides a unique model system for studying the molecular mechanisms underlying tumor heterogeneity, metastasis, and therapeutic resistance in aggressive forms of triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line has been deposited with the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) under Accession No. PTA-10322 and is characterized by its ability to form solid tumors in immunocompromised non-human mammals, recapitulating key features of the original human disease including mixed epithelial and mesenchymal phenotypes within the same tumor mass.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer is a highly heterogeneous disease that manifests across multiple molecular subtypes, each with distinct clinical behaviors, prognoses, and responses to therapy. Gene expression profiling of patient-derived tumor samples has historically classified breast cancers into five major subgroups: luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched, normal-like, and basal-like. Among these, tumors exhibiting a basal-like gene signature are predominantly triple-negative, defined by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and amplification or overexpression of HER2/neu. Triple-negative breast cancers (TNBCs) disproportionately affect younger women, particularly those of African descent and carriers of BRCA1 mutations, and are associated with poor clinical outcomes due to their aggressive biology and limited treatment options.

A subset of TNBCs demonstrates high levels of invasiveness, metastatic potential, and resistance to conventional chemotherapy and radiation regimens. Recent studies have linked this aggressive behavior to the activation of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), a developmental program co-opted by cancer cells to acquire migratory and invasive properties. EMT is characterized by loss of epithelial markers such as E-cadherin, claudins, and occludins, along with gain of mesenchymal markers like vimentin and smooth muscle actin, accompanied by cytoskeletal reorganization and spindle-shaped morphology. The reverse process, mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET), involves reacquisition of epithelial traits and is thought to facilitate colonization at distant sites during metastasis.

Despite compelling evidence from in vitro models, direct observation of full EMT/MET cycles in human breast cancer remains elusive, as most metastases retain an epithelial phenotype similar to the primary tumor. This discrepancy has led to the hypothesis that EMT in human tumors may be transient, partial, and reversible—features collectively termed "epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity." Such plasticity may enable dynamic adaptation to microenvironmental cues, promoting both dissemination and colonization while evading immune surveillance and therapeutic intervention.

Concurrently, research has identified a population of cancer-initiating or stem-like cells within breast tumors, often marked by the CD44high/CD24low surface phenotype. These cells exhibit enhanced tumorigenicity, self-renewal capacity, and resistance to therapy. Notably, induction of EMT in mammary epithelial cells has been shown to confer stem-like properties, suggesting a mechanistic link between phenotypic plasticity and tumor aggressiveness.

However, current experimental models for studying TNBC are limited by their inability to faithfully recapitulate this dynamic interplay between epithelial and mesenchymal states. Many established cell lines either maintain a fixed epithelial or mesenchymal identity or fail to undergo reversible transitions in response to physiological stimuli. Furthermore, commonly used lines such as MDA-MB-231 (mesenchymal) or MCF-7 (epithelial) do not reflect the heterogeneity observed in patient tumors, where regions of both phenotypes may coexist. There exists a critical need for a biologically relevant, stable, and reproducible human TNBC cell line capable of modeling epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity to advance our understanding of disease progression and to develop more effective therapeutic strategies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel human triple-negative breast carcinoma cell line, designated DKAT, which was established from the malignant pleural effusion of a patient with rapidly progressive, treatment-resistant metastatic triple-negative breast cancer. The DKAT cell line has been deposited with the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC), 10801 University Boulevard, Manassas, VA 20110-2209, under Accession No. PTA-10322 on [date], and is publicly available pursuant to the Budapest Treaty.

The DKAT cell line exhibits a distinctive marker profile consistent with basal-like triple-negative breast cancer, including negativity for ER, PR, and HER2, and positivity for EGFR, cytokeratin 5 (CK5), cytokeratin 17 (CK17), p63, and E-cadherin. It also displays a high proportion of CD44high/CD24low cells, indicative of a stem-like phenotype. Genetically, DKAT harbors a missense mutation in TP53 at codon 273 (CGT > CAT), known to impair p53 function, but lacks mutations in PIK3CA exons 9 and 20.

Critically, the DKAT cell line demonstrates inherent phenotypic plasticity, undergoing reversible EMT and MET in response to changes in culture conditions. When cultured in Mammary Epithelial Growth Medium (MEGM), DKAT cells adopt an epithelial morphology with strong membrane-localized E-cadherin and low vimentin expression. Upon transfer to Stromal Cell Growth Medium (SCGM) containing insulin, basic fibroblast growth factor (bFGF), and 5% fetal bovine serum (FBS), the cells rapidly shift to a mesenchymal state, characterized by spindle morphology, cytoplasmic redistribution and eventual downregulation of E-cadherin, and robust upregulation of vimentin within hours. This transition is accompanied by increased migratory capacity in wound-healing assays. Remarkably, when mesenchymal DKAT cells are returned to MEGM, they revert to an epithelial phenotype over several passages, confirming the reversibility of the process.

Moreover, DKAT cells are highly tumorigenic in immunocompromised non-human mammals. As few as ten cells injected into the mammary fat pad of SCID mice consistently produce solid tumors that recapitulate the histological and immunophenotypic heterogeneity of the original human tumor, including regions expressing both epithelial (CK5, CK18, E-cadherin) and mesenchymal (vimentin, smooth muscle actin) markers. The invention thus further provides a non-human mammal, particularly an immune-deficient mouse such as a SCID, Rag1−/−, or Rag2−/− mouse, engrafted with the DKAT cell line, which develops a solid tumor suitable for preclinical studies.

The DKAT cell line enables identification of genes and proteins whose expression is modulated during invasion, metastasis, EMT, and MET. Through comparative transcriptomic and proteomic analyses of DKAT cells in epithelial versus mesenchymal states, the invention facilitates discovery of novel therapeutic targets and biomarkers. Additionally, the cell line serves as a platform for screening chemotherapeutic agents, biological agents, and candidate compounds for efficacy against aggressive, plastic TNBC. For example, modulation of Zeb1—a transcription factor upregulated during EMT in DKAT cells—was shown to be necessary for migration, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Triple-negative breast cancer represents a clinically challenging subtype due to its aggressive nature, lack of targeted therapies, and propensity for early metastasis. Existing cell line models often fail to capture the dynamic phenotypic shifts believed to underlie metastatic competence and therapeutic resistance. The DKAT cell line addresses this gap by providing a stable, well-characterized human TNBC model that intrinsically possesses the ability to reversibly transition between epithelial and mesenchymal states—a property termed epithelial-mesenchymal plasticity.

Unlike conventional TNBC lines that are locked in one phenotypic state, DKAT cells respond to microenvironmental cues mimicked by defined culture media. In serum-free, growth factor-supplemented MEGM, they maintain epithelial characteristics. In contrast, exposure to SCGM—which includes serum and stromal-derived factors—induces a rapid and coordinated shift toward a mesenchymal phenotype. This responsiveness is not due to selection of pre-existing subpopulations, as demonstrated by clonal derivation experiments: single-cell clones isolated from epithelial DKAT cultures undergo identical EMT upon SCGM exposure, and mesenchymal clones revert to epithelial states in MEGM.

The DKAT cell line was derived from a patient with a particularly aggressive clinical course: initial diagnosis of a 4 cm triple-negative, lymph node-negative tumor, followed by rapid recurrence within the radiation field at seven months, widespread metastasis by ten months, and death at twelve months despite multiple lines of chemotherapy. Histopathological analysis of the primary tumor revealed six distinct foci, five of which were epithelial, while one exhibited mixed epithelial-mesenchymal features. The chest wall recurrence showed reduced E-cadherin and increased vimentin, whereas bone marrow metastases reverted to an epithelial pattern—suggesting in vivo plasticity.

Cytogenetic analysis confirmed the human origin and genomic instability typical of advanced carcinomas, with a modal chromosome number of 56 and complex karyotypic abnormalities. The presence of a TP53 R273H mutation aligns with the high frequency of p53 dysfunction in basal-like TNBC. Despite lacking PIK3CA mutations, DKAT cells exhibit constitutive AKT phosphorylation at Ser473, likely due to low PTEN expression, a common feature in TNBC associated with poor prognosis.

The importance of the DKAT cell line lies in its fidelity to human disease biology. It enables real-time investigation of how tumor cells adapt to changing environments, how plasticity contributes to stemness and drug resistance, and how specific signaling nodes (e.g., Zeb1) regulate these processes. Its utility extends to drug screening, biomarker validation, and development of therapies aimed at locking cells in a less aggressive state or eliminating plastic subpopulations.

## DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the terms “a,” “an,” or “the” refer to one or more unless context clearly indicates otherwise. The term “and/or” means any one or more of the listed elements or combinations thereof. The term “about” refers to ±10% of the stated value.

“Nucleic acids” encompass DNA, RNA, cDNA, and synthetic analogs. An “isolated” nucleic acid or polypeptide is separated from its natural environment and is at least 60%, preferably 75%, and more preferably 90% pure. An “isolated nucleic acid” specifically refers to a nucleic acid molecule that is not immediately contiguous with both of the coding sequences with which it is immediately contiguous in the naturally occurring genome of the organism from which it is derived.

A “cell line” is a population of cells capable of continuous propagation in culture. A “continuous cell line” can be passaged indefinitely, while a “tumorigenic cell line” forms tumors when introduced into a suitable host. “Modulate” means to alter the level, activity, or function of a gene or protein, either up or down.

An “anticancer agent,” “chemotherapeutic agent,” or “anti-neoplastic agent” is any compound that inhibits tumor growth or kills cancer cells. Examples include alkylating agents (e.g., cyclophosphamide), antimetabolites (e.g., methotrexate, 5-fluorouracil), taxanes (e.g., docetaxel), vinca alkaloids (e.g., vinorelbine), platinum compounds, and targeted kinase inhibitors. A “biological agent” includes antibodies, cytokines, vaccines, and gene therapies. A “test compound” or “candidate agent” is any substance evaluated for biological activity.

An “effective amount” is the quantity sufficient to achieve a desired therapeutic or prophylactic effect. To “treat” or “treatment of” a disease means to ameliorate symptoms, reduce severity, or delay progression. To “prevent” or “prevention” means to reduce the likelihood or delay the onset of disease.

The “DKAT cell line” refers to the human triple-negative breast cancer cell line deposited as ATCC PTA-10322. It expresses basal markers (CK5, CK17, p63), luminal markers (CK18), and E-cadherin, is ER−/PR−/HER2−, and harbors a p53 R273H mutation. A “p53 mutant” denotes a cell with a dysfunctional p53 protein due to genetic alteration.

The cell line can be implanted subcutaneously or into the mammary fat pad of a “non-human animal,” preferably an “immune deficient non-human animal” such as SCID mice, Rag1−/− or Rag2−/− mice, or transgenic immune-deficient strains. These animals support engraftment of human cells without rejection. The resulting “solid tumor” recapitulates human disease heterogeneity.

Methods of producing solid tumors involve introducing DKAT cells into such mice, allowing tumor growth, and optionally harvesting and re-culturing cells. This system enables study of pathogenesis, evaluation of treatments, and identification of differentially expressed genes during tumor progression, metastasis, EMT, and MET.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The DKAT cell line was established from malignant pleural effusion of a 35-year-old Caucasian woman diagnosed with triple-negative breast cancer (ER−/PR−/HER2wt). The patient received six cycles of cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, and 5-fluorouracil but developed local recurrence at seven months and widespread metastases by ten months. Cells were isolated by centrifugation, resuspended in MEGM, and adapted to culture within 48 hours. The line has been maintained for over 70 passages with consistent doubling time (~24 hours).

### Example 2

Control cell lines included HMEC-hTERT (immortalized human mammary epithelial cells), MDA-MB-231 (mesenchymal TNBC), MCF-7 (luminal ER+), SUM-149, and SUM-190 (basal-like TNBC). These were maintained in standard media: MEGM for HMEC-hTERT; αMEM + 10% FBS for MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7; RPMI + 10% FBS for SUM lines.

### Example 3

Cytogenetic analysis via spectral karyotyping (SKY) and G-banding of 25 metaphase DKAT cells (passage 3) revealed a modal chromosome number of 56. Clonal populations included a stemline and sideline with double minutes. Chromosomal aberrations were classified per International System for Human Cytogenetic Nomenclature.

### Example 4

Immunocytochemistry on DKAT cells confirmed absence of ER/PR and diploid HER2 status. Positive staining was observed for EGFR, CK5, CK17, p63, and E-cadherin, consistent with basal-like TNBC.

### Example 5

Immunofluorescence utilized primary antibodies against CK5, CK14, CK17, CK18, E-cadherin, vimentin, smooth muscle actin, and claudin-1, followed by Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondaries. Cells were fixed in methanol or paraformaldehyde, counterstained with DAPI, and imaged via fluorescence microscopy.

### Example 6

Western blotting confirmed protein expression: high phospho-AKT (Ser473), low PTEN, E-cadherin, vimentin, CK5, CK17, claudin-1, N-cadherin, and Zeb1. Membranes were probed with specific antibodies and visualized using chemiluminescence.

### Example 7

TP53 exons 5–9 were sequenced from DKAT genomic DNA, revealing a heterozygous CGT→CAT mutation at codon 273 (R273H). PIK3CA exons 9 and 20 showed no mutations.

### Example 8

mRNA was extracted using RNeasy kits, converted to cDNA, labeled, and hybridized to Affymetrix HG-U133 Plus 2.0 arrays. Unsupervised clustering placed DKAT among basal-like cell lines, closest to SUM-149. Differential expression analysis identified downregulation of epithelial genes (KRT5, TP63, CLDN1) and upregulation of mesenchymal genes (VIM, CDH2, LOX, ALDH1A3) in SCGM.

### Example 9

Invasion was assessed via scratch assay: DKAT-SCGM cells closed wounds faster than DKAT-MEGM. For EMT induction, cells were switched to SCGM (insulin, bFGF, 5% FBS); MET was induced by returning to MEGM. Flow cytometry used APC-CD44 and PE-CD24 antibodies to confirm CD44high/CD24low phenotype. Mouse xenografts involved injecting 10–10⁴ DKAT cells ± Matrigel into mammary fat pads of SCID mice. Tumors were measured weekly, excised at endpoint, fixed, and analyzed histologically. Primary tumor analysis showed heterogeneous marker expression across foci. FISH confirmed HER2 diploidy. Protein expression by IHC revealed dynamic EMT/MET patterns in primary, recurrent, and metastatic lesions.
