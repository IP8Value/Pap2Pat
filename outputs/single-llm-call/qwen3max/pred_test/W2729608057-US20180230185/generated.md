## TECHNICAL FIELD

- introduce peptidomimetics for HER2-overexpressed cancers  

The present invention resides in the field of oncology and molecular therapeutics, specifically relating to novel peptidomimetic compounds designed to target human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-overexpressing cancers. More particularly, the invention provides cyclic peptidomimetics that selectively bind to domain IV of the HER2 extracellular domain, thereby inhibiting protein-protein interactions essential for HER2-mediated heterodimerization with other members of the EGFR family, including EGFR (HER1) and HER3. These peptidomimetics exhibit potent antiproliferative activity against HER2-positive tumor cell lines, suppress tumor growth in xenograft models, and demonstrate enhanced metabolic stability compared to linear peptide analogs. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising such peptidomimetics, methods for their synthesis, and their use in the treatment, prevention, or management of cancers characterized by HER2 overexpression or dysregulated EGFR signaling, including breast, lung, and ovarian carcinomas.

## BACKGROUND ART

- motivate EGFRs in human cancers  

Epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFRs), also known as the HER family of receptor tyrosine kinases, play a pivotal role in regulating cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, migration, and survival. Dysregulation of EGFR signaling—through overexpression, gene amplification, or activating mutations—is a well-established driver of tumorigenesis in multiple human malignancies, including breast, non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and ovarian cancer. Aberrant activation of these pathways leads to uncontrolled cell growth, resistance to apoptosis, and metastatic progression, contributing to poor clinical outcomes.

- describe EGFR family and signaling  

The HER family comprises four structurally related transmembrane receptors: HER1 (EGFR/ErbB1), HER2 (ErbB2), HER3 (ErbB3), and HER4 (ErbB4). Unlike other family members, HER2 has no known high-affinity ligand but serves as the preferred dimerization partner for ligand-bound EGFR, HER3, or HER4. Ligand binding induces conformational changes in the extracellular domain (ECD), promoting homo- or heterodimerization, which activates the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain. This triggers autophosphorylation of specific tyrosine residues, initiating downstream signaling cascades such as the RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK and PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathways, both critical for oncogenic transformation.

- limitations of existing cancer therapies  

Current therapeutic strategies targeting EGFR/HER2 include monoclonal antibodies (e.g., trastuzumab, pertuzumab) and small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs; e.g., lapatinib, erlotinib). While initially effective, these agents face significant limitations. Antibodies suffer from poor tissue penetration, high production costs, immunogenicity, and limited shelf stability. TKIs often induce acquired resistance, particularly through secondary mutations such as T790M in EGFR, rendering tumors refractory to first-generation inhibitors. Moreover, many existing agents lack specificity for particular dimer interfaces, leading to incomplete pathway inhibition or off-target effects.

- discuss HER2-overexpressed cancers  

HER2 overexpression occurs in approximately 15–20% of breast cancers and is associated with aggressive disease, increased recurrence, and reduced overall survival. It is also observed in subsets of gastric, ovarian, and NSCLC tumors. HER2-driven cancers rely heavily on HER2-containing heterodimers—especially HER2:HER3—for sustained oncogenic signaling, making disruption of these protein-protein interactions (PPIs) a compelling therapeutic strategy.

- summarize existing approaches to cancer therapy  

Existing PPI inhibitors include peptides derived from dimerization domains, but these are typically linear, proteolytically unstable, and exhibit poor pharmacokinetics. Efforts to stabilize them via cyclization, D-amino acid incorporation, or backbone modification have yielded mixed results, often compromising binding affinity or specificity.

- limitations of antibodies and tyrosine kinase inhibitors  

Trastuzumab binds HER2 domain IV and exerts effects through multiple mechanisms, including inhibition of ECD shedding and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), but does not directly block dimerization. Pertuzumab targets domain II and inhibits dimer formation, yet its efficacy is limited in certain resistant settings. TKIs inhibit kinase activity but do not prevent receptor dimerization at the membrane level, allowing residual signaling through alternative pathways.

- prior art on peptidomimetics for HER2 binding  

Prior studies have explored peptidomimetics mimicking key interaction motifs in EGFR dimers. However, none have successfully combined high-affinity, selective binding to HER2 domain IV with robust in vivo stability and dual inhibition of both HER2:EGFR and HER2:HER3 heterodimers.

- targeting HER2 domain IV and II  

Domain IV of HER2 contains critical residues involved in homotypic and heterotypic interactions. While domain II is targeted by pertuzumab, domain IV remains an underexploited site for small-molecule or peptidomimetic intervention, despite evidence suggesting its involvement in dimer stabilization.

- peptides targeting HER2  

Linear peptides based on sequences from HER2-interacting partners have shown modest activity but degrade rapidly in serum, limiting therapeutic utility.

- cyclic peptide incorporating D-Pro and L-Pro  

Cyclic structures incorporating proline residues can enforce β-turn conformations that mimic bioactive epitopes. The strategic use of D- and L-proline stereoisomers influences backbone geometry and side-chain orientation, potentially enhancing target engagement.

- unfilled need for improved treatments  

There remains a critical unmet need for therapeutics that combine the target specificity of biologics with the metabolic stability and oral bioavailability potential of small molecules, particularly agents capable of selectively disrupting pathogenic HER2 heterodimers without inducing widespread kinase inhibition or immune activation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

- introduce novel peptidomimetics for HER2-overexpressed cancers  

The present invention provides novel cyclic peptidomimetic compounds that specifically target HER2-overexpressing cancers by inhibiting essential protein-protein interactions within the EGFR family. These compounds represent a new class of dimerization inhibitors with optimized structural features conferring high potency, selectivity, and stability.

- describe peptidomimetics binding to HER2 protein  

The disclosed peptidomimetics bind with high affinity to the extracellular domain IV of HER2, as demonstrated by surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and competitive binding assays. This binding occurs at or near the dimerization interface, effectively blocking the association of HER2 with EGFR and HER3.

- inhibit protein-protein interactions and signaling  

By occupying the dimerization site, these compounds prevent heterodimer formation, leading to suppression of downstream kinase activation, reduced phosphorylation of HER2, and attenuation of proliferative and survival signals in cancer cells.

- advantages of peptidomimetics over antibodies and peptides  

Unlike monoclonal antibodies, the peptidomimetics are synthetically accessible, non-immunogenic, and exhibit superior tissue penetration. Compared to linear peptides, their cyclic architecture and incorporation of non-natural amino acids confer resistance to proteolytic degradation, as confirmed by in vitro serum stability studies showing no significant degradation over 48 hours.

- peptidomimetics bind to domain IV of HER2  

Binding studies using isolated HER2 domain IV confirm that the primary interaction site resides within this region, distinguishing the mechanism from pertuzumab (domain II) and aligning with structural insights from EGFR homodimer crystallography.

- antiproliferative activity and kinase domain phosphorylation  

The lead compound, designated Compound 18, exhibits nanomolar IC50 values in HER2-positive cell lines (e.g., 18 nM in Calu-3 lung cancer, 194 nM in SKBR-3 breast cancer) while showing minimal activity in HER2-negative lines (IC50 > 50 µM in MCF-7). Western blot analysis confirms dose-dependent inhibition of HER2 phosphorylation, linking target engagement to functional pathway suppression.

- tumor suppression in xenograft animal model  

In an orthotopic BT-474 breast cancer xenograft model, intratumoral administration of Compound 18 (4 mg/kg, twice weekly) significantly delayed tumor growth compared to vehicle control (p < 0.05), with concomitant reduction in HER2 phosphorylation and HER2:HER3 heterodimerization in excised tumors, as validated by PLA and immunoblotting.

- embodiments of peptidomimetics in consumable products  

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical formulations suitable for oral, parenteral, topical, or inhalation delivery, including tablets, capsules, injectables, and transdermal patches, all containing a therapeutically effective amount of the peptidomimetic alone or in combination with other anticancer agents.

- exemplary Compound 18 and its properties  

Compound 18 is a symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic featuring a central L-Pro-D-Pro motif flanked by repeated Arg-Anapa-Phe-Asp sequences, where Anapa denotes β-(2-naphthyl)-alanine. It adopts a stable β-hairpin conformation in solution, as evidenced by 2D NMR and molecular modeling. Its structure enables optimal spatial presentation of key pharmacophores—arginine for electrostatic interactions and naphthyl/phenyl groups for hydrophobic contacts—mimicking natural PPI interfaces. Compound 18 demonstrates low cytotoxicity in normal epithelial cells (IC50 = 40 µM in MCF-10A), indicating a favorable therapeutic window.
