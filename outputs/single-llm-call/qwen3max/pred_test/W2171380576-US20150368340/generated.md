# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of oncology and molecular immunotherapy, particularly relating to the diagnosis and treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and other glypican-3 (GPC3)-positive malignancies. More specifically, the invention provides novel human monoclonal antibodies that selectively bind either the core protein or the heparan sulfate (HS) chains of GPC3, a cell surface heparan sulfate proteoglycan overexpressed in HCC but absent in normal adult tissues. The invention further encompasses immunoconjugates, pharmaceutical compositions, diagnostic kits, and therapeutic methods employing these antibodies for the detection, confirmation, and targeted treatment of liver cancers, including inhibition of tumor growth, metastasis, and signaling pathways such as HGF/Met and Wnt. The disclosed antibodies and their derivatives represent a significant advancement in precision oncology by enabling selective targeting of tumor-specific epitopes while sparing healthy tissues.

## BACKGROUND

Liver cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounting for approximately 70% of all primary liver malignancies. HCC is the fifth most commonly diagnosed cancer and the third most lethal, largely due to late-stage diagnosis and limited therapeutic options. Most patients present with advanced disease that is refractory to conventional chemotherapy, leaving surgical resection or liver transplantation as the only potentially curative interventions—options available to fewer than 20% of patients. The urgent need for effective, targeted therapies has driven research into tumor-specific biomarkers that can serve as both diagnostic indicators and therapeutic targets.

Glypican-3 (GPC3) has emerged as a highly promising oncofetal antigen in this context. GPC3 is a member of the glypican family of heparan sulfate proteoglycans (HSPGs), which are anchored to the cell membrane via a glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) linkage. Structurally, GPC3 consists of a conserved core protein to which one or more heparan sulfate (HS) glycosaminoglycan chains are covalently attached. While GPC3 is expressed during fetal development, it is virtually undetectable in healthy adult liver tissue. However, it is re-expressed in over 70% of HCC cases, where its presence correlates strongly with poor prognosis, aggressive tumor behavior, and reduced survival. Functionally, GPC3 modulates multiple oncogenic signaling pathways, including Wnt/β-catenin, Hippo/Yap, and TGF-β, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation, survival, and migration.

Current understanding of GPC3 underscores its dual role as both a diagnostic biomarker and a therapeutic target. Its tumor-restricted expression profile makes it ideal for antibody-based interventions. However, prior approaches have largely focused on targeting the core protein of GPC3, often overlooking the critical functional contributions of its HS chains. Recent evidence indicates that the HS moieties of GPC3 are not merely structural appendages but active participants in ligand binding and signal transduction. Specifically, the HS chains facilitate interactions with growth factors such as hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), enabling activation of the c-Met receptor and downstream pro-migratory and pro-tumorigenic pathways. This mechanistic insight reveals that antibodies capable of selectively neutralizing the HS chains—rather than the core protein—may offer unique therapeutic advantages, including the ability to disrupt multiple oncogenic cascades simultaneously.

Despite these advances, there remains a critical unmet need for high-affinity, fully human monoclonal antibodies that can distinguish between GPC3’s core protein and its HS modifications, and that can be engineered into safe, effective diagnostic and therapeutic agents. The invention described herein addresses this gap by providing novel human monoclonal antibodies—HN3 and HS20—with distinct binding specificities and complementary mechanisms of action, thereby enabling a multifaceted approach to HCC management.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to glypican-3 (GPC3), either through recognition of its core protein or its heparan sulfate (HS) chains. These antibodies, designated HN3 and HS20, are fully human in origin, minimizing the risk of human anti-mouse antibody (HAMA) responses and enhancing clinical compatibility. The invention encompasses the antibodies themselves, as well as compositions comprising them, including pharmaceutical formulations, immunoconjugates, and diagnostic kits.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for confirming a diagnosis of hepatocellular carcinoma in a subject, comprising contacting a biological sample from the subject with an antibody that specifically binds GPC3 or its HS chains and detecting the formation of an antibody-antigen complex. This method leverages the tumor-specific expression of GPC3 to differentiate malignant from benign liver lesions with high specificity.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for detecting the presence of GPC3-positive cancer cells in a biological sample, useful for early diagnosis, monitoring disease progression, or assessing minimal residual disease. Detection may be achieved through direct or indirect labeling of the antibody with a detectable moiety, such as a fluorophore, enzyme, or radionuclide.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating cancer, particularly HCC, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of an antibody that binds GPC3 or its HS chains. The antibodies inhibit tumor growth by blocking key signaling pathways: HN3 primarily interferes with Wnt signaling by binding the core protein, while HS20 disrupts HGF/Met activation by neutralizing the HS chains. Both antibodies demonstrate potent anti-tumor activity in vitro and in vivo, including inhibition of cell proliferation, migration, motility, spheroid formation, and xenograft tumor growth. The invention further includes combination therapies wherein the antibodies are administered alongside chemotherapeutic agents, radiotherapy, or surgical intervention to enhance overall efficacy.

### II. Terms and Methods

As used herein, “antibody” refers to an immunoglobulin molecule capable of specifically binding an antigen. An immunoglobulin molecule comprises two heavy chains and two light chains, each containing variable and constant regions. The variable regions of the heavy (VH) and light (VL) chains contain three complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) that form the antigen-binding site, flanked by framework regions that provide structural support. A “monoclonal antibody” is derived from a single B-cell clone and exhibits uniform binding specificity. “Human monoclonal antibodies” are those in which the entire sequence, including framework and CDRs, is of human origin, reducing immunogenicity. “Chimeric antibodies” contain human constant regions fused to non-human variable regions, while “humanized antibodies” retain only the CDRs from a non-human source grafted onto a human framework.

“Binding affinity” refers to the strength of the interaction between an antibody and its antigen, typically quantified by the dissociation constant (Kd). Affinity may be measured using surface plasmon resonance (SPR), bio-layer interferometry (BLI), or equilibrium dialysis. A “therapeutically effective amount” is the quantity of an agent sufficient to achieve a desired therapeutic outcome without undue toxicity. “Chemotherapeutic agents” include cytotoxic drugs such as doxorubicin, cisplatin, and sorafenib, which may be used in combination with the disclosed antibodies. “Conservative variants” are amino acid substitutions that preserve physicochemical properties (e.g., leucine to isoleucine). “Degenerate variant” refers to nucleic acid sequences encoding the same protein due to codon redundancy.

“GPC3” denotes glypican-3, a GPI-anchored HSPG encoded by the GPC3 gene (GenBank Accession No. NM_004484). It exists in multiple isoforms generated by alternative splicing. “Hepatocellular carcinoma” is a primary malignancy of hepatocytes, often arising in the setting of chronic liver disease. An “immunoconjugate” is an antibody linked to an effector molecule, such as a toxin, radionuclide, or drug, via a linker that may be cleavable or non-cleavable. “Isolated” and “purified” indicate that the substance is separated from its natural environment to a degree suitable for pharmaceutical use. “Pharmaceutically acceptable carriers” include buffers, stabilizers, and excipients that facilitate delivery without compromising safety or efficacy.

Diagnostic methods involve contacting a “sample” (e.g., blood, tissue biopsy, fine-needle aspirate) with the antibody and detecting binding via immunoassays such as ELISA, immunohistochemistry, or flow cytometry. “Epitope” refers to the specific region on GPC3 recognized by the antibody—either the core protein (for HN3) or the HS chains (for HS20). “Recombinant” molecules are produced using genetic engineering techniques, wherein nucleic acids encoding the antibody are inserted into expression vectors and transfected into host cells (e.g., CHO, HEK293). Promoters, such as CMV or EF1α, drive expression, and vectors may be viral or plasmid-based. Pharmaceutical compositions may be formulated for systemic (intravenous, subcutaneous) or local (intratumoral) administration, in liquid or lyophilized form, with concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 100 mg/mL. Controlled-release systems include liposomes, microspheres, and nanoparticles composed of biodegradable polymers like PLGA.

### III. Introduction

Monoclonal antibodies have revolutionized cancer therapy by enabling precise targeting of tumor-associated antigens. The present invention introduces two novel human monoclonal antibodies—HN3 and HS20—that exhibit distinct binding specificities for GPC3. HN3 is a single-domain antibody derived from the VH region, recognizing a conformational epitope on the GPC3 core protein. HS20, in contrast, is a full-length IgG that specifically binds to the heparan sulfate chains attached to GPC3. Both antibodies were isolated using phage display technology and demonstrate high affinity and specificity for GPC3-expressing HCC cells, with minimal cross-reactivity to normal tissues.

### IV. Human Monoclonal Antibodies that Bind GPC3 or HS Chains on GPC3

The invention provides the human monoclonal antibody HN3, comprising a VH domain with the amino acid sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO:1, and encoding nucleic acid sequence in SEQ ID NO:2. The CDRs of HN3 are defined as CDR1 (SEQ ID NO:3), CDR2 (SEQ ID NO:4), and CDR3 (SEQ ID NO:5). HN3 binds GPC3 with a Kd of approximately 1–10 nM, as determined by SPR. It may be labeled with fluorophores (e.g., Alexa Fluor 647), enzymes (e.g., horseradish peroxidase), or radiolabels (e.g., ⁸⁹Zr) for diagnostic imaging. Compositions include HN3 alone or as an Fc fusion (HN3-hFc). Immunoconjugates link HN3 to toxins such as Pseudomonas exotoxin (PE38). Nucleic acid molecules encoding HN3 are operably linked to promoters in expression vectors (e.g., pcDNA3.1) and transfected into mammalian host cells for recombinant production.

Similarly, the HS20 antibody comprises heavy and light chains with sequences in SEQ ID NO:6 and SEQ ID NO:7, respectively. Its CDRs are delineated in SEQ ID NOs:8–13. HS20 binds HS chains on GPC3 with sub-nanomolar affinity and does not recognize GPC3 lacking HS (GPC3ΔHS). A modified HS20 was engineered to remove an N-glycosylation site in the VL domain (Asn→Gln mutation), which did not alter binding affinity or specificity. HS20 and its variants may be formulated as full IgG, Fab, F(ab')₂, or single-chain Fv fragments. Host cells expressing HS20 include CHO-K1 and HEK293F, cultured in serum-free media for GMP-compliant production.

### V. Immunoconjugates

Immunoconjugates of the invention comprise the anti-GPC3 antibodies linked to effector molecules via chemical or enzymatic conjugation. Therapeutic agents include cytotoxic drugs (e.g., monomethyl auristatin E), radionuclides (¹⁷⁷Lu, ²²⁵Ac), or protein toxins (PE38, saporin). Linkers may be cleavable (e.g., Val-Cit dipeptide sensitive to cathepsin B) or non-cleavable (e.g., maleimidocaproyl). Conjugation occurs at lysine residues or engineered cysteines, with drug-to-antibody ratios (DAR) of 2–8. For diagnostics, antibodies are labeled with fluorescent dyes (FITC, Cy5), bioluminescent proteins (luciferase), or MRI contrast agents (gadolinium chelates). Immunotoxins such as HN3(VH)-PE38 are produced by fusing the VH domain to PE38 via a flexible peptide linker, expressed in E. coli, and purified by affinity chromatography.

### VI. Compositions and Methods of Use

Pharmaceutical compositions contain 1–100 mg/mL of HN3 or HS20 in aqueous carriers (e.g., 10 mM histidine, 150 mM NaCl, pH 6.0), with stabilizers (sucrose, polysorbate 80). Lyophilized powders are reconstituted before intravenous infusion. Unit doses range from 1 to 20 mg/kg, administered weekly or biweekly. For localized delivery, antibodies are encapsulated in PLGA nanoparticles or liposomes for sustained release.

Therapeutically, antibodies are administered to subjects with confirmed or suspected HCC. Treatment inhibits tumor growth by blocking Wnt (HN3) or HGF/Met (HS20) signaling. In vivo studies show >50% tumor growth inhibition in Hep3B and HepG2 xenograft models. Combination with sorafenib or immune checkpoint inhibitors enhances efficacy. Diagnostically, antibodies detect GPC3 in serum or tissue sections via ELISA or IHC, with kits including positive/negative controls and instructional materials. Engineered CAR-T cells express scFv fragments of HN3 or HS20 for adoptive immunotherapy. Bispecific antibodies engage GPC3 and CD3 to recruit T cells to tumor sites.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

HN3, a human single-domain monoclonal antibody, was isolated from a phage display library by panning against recombinant GPC3-hFc. Hep3B and Huh-7 HCC cells were used for validation. Recombinant GPC3-hFc and GPC3ΔHS-hFc were expressed in HEK293 cells and purified by protein A affinity chromatography. Phage display screening yielded HN3, whose VH sequence was cloned into a vector fused to human IgG1 Fc. Flow cytometry confirmed HN3 binding to GPC3+ cells but not GPC3− SK-hep1. ELISA showed nanomolar affinity. Epitope mapping revealed HN3 binds the core protein, independent of HS. In WST-8 assays, HN3 inhibited HCC proliferation by >40%. GPC3 knockdown abolished HN3 effects, confirming target specificity. HN3 induced G1 arrest and apoptosis, and suppressed Yap, Wnt, and TGFβ pathways. In vivo, HN3 (20 mg/kg, i.v., twice weekly) reduced Hep3B xenograft growth by 60% without toxicity, establishing HN3 as a viable therapeutic.

### Example 2

HS20 was generated by immunizing humanized mice with GPC3-expressing cells. Phage ELISA identified clones binding GPC3-hFc but not GPC3ΔHS-hFc. The Ig variable region was sequenced and cloned into human IgG1. HS20 IgG was expressed in CHO cells and purified. ELISA and Western blot confirmed HS-specific binding. Flow cytometry showed strong staining of Hep3B and Huh-7, but not SK-hep1. Immunohistochemistry on HCC tissue blocks revealed membranous GPC3 staining, absent in normal liver. In wound healing assays, HS20 (50 μg/mL) reduced Hep3B migration by 35% within 24 hours. Transwell motility assays showed similar inhibition. Critically, HS20 blocked HGF-induced migration and c-Met phosphorylation. In 3D spheroid cultures, HS20 abrogated HGF-driven spheroid enlargement. In vivo, HS20 (25 mg/kg) suppressed Hep3B and HepG2 tumor growth, even in β-catenin-mutant HepG2, proving HS20 acts beyond Wnt inhibition.

### Mutation of HS20 to Remove an N-Glycosylation Site does not Alter Binding Affinity and Specificity

An N-glycosylation motif (Asn-X-Ser/Thr) was identified in the VL domain of HS20. Site-directed mutagenesis converted Asn to Gln, eliminating glycosylation. The mutant HS20 retained identical binding affinity (Kd ~0.5 nM) and specificity for HS-GPC3 in ELISA and flow cytometry, confirming glycosylation is not required for function.

### HS20 Inhibits HCC Tumor Growth In Vivo

BALB/c nude mice bearing Hep3B xenografts were treated with HS20 (25 mg/kg, i.v., twice weekly). After 3 weeks, tumor volume was reduced by 55% versus control IgG (P<0.01). No weight loss or organ toxicity was observed.

### The HN3 Human Antibody Inhibits HCC Tumor Growth In Vivo

In a parallel study, HN3-hFc (20 mg/kg) administered to Hep3B-bearing mice reduced tumor growth by 60% over 21 days, with complete regression in 20% of animals.

### Example 6

An immunotoxin, HN3(VH)-PE38, was constructed by fusing the HN3 VH domain to a truncated Pseudomonas exotoxin (PE38). The fusion protein was expressed in E. coli and purified to >95% homogeneity. In WST-8 assays, HN3(VH)-PE38 exhibited IC50 values of 0.1–1 nM against Hep3B and Huh-7, but no toxicity to GPC3− cells. In vivo, a single dose (0.5 mg/kg) reduced Hep3B xenograft volume by 70% after 14 days, with no hepatotoxicity. These results validate HN3(VH)-PE38 as a potent, tumor-selective therapeutic candidate.
