# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method and system for determining copy number variation in a genome sample derived from a single cell or trace amounts of nucleic acid. More specifically, the invention provides an integrated bioinformatics pipeline that enables accurate detection of copy number variations by correcting whole genome amplification-induced biases—particularly those arising from GC content heterogeneity—and by employing a binary segmentation algorithm combined with dynamic threshold determination to identify genomic breakpoints and classify copy number states with high sensitivity and specificity.

## BACKGROUND

Copy number variations (CNVs) are structural alterations in the genome involving gains or losses of DNA segments larger than 1 kilobase and are known to underlie numerous genetic disorders, developmental abnormalities, and cancers. Traditional methods for CNV detection, such as array comparative genomic hybridization (aCGH), have been widely applied but suffer from inherent limitations when used with minute biological samples, particularly single cells. The necessity of whole genome amplification (WGA) prior to analysis introduces significant technical artifacts, including non-uniform amplification across genomic regions due to sequence composition biases—most notably GC content. Regions with extremely low (<40%) or high (>48%) GC content are frequently under- or over-amplified, respectively, leading to false-positive or false-negative CNV calls. Although massively parallel sequencing (MPS) offers higher resolution and broader genomic coverage than microarray-based approaches, the application of standard CNV detection algorithms to WGA-amplified single-cell sequencing data remains problematic without explicit correction for amplification bias. Existing computational methods, such as SegSeq, rely on comparative genomic strategies that require matched control samples and fixed statistical thresholds, which are insufficient to account for the variable and sample-specific nature of WGA-induced noise. Consequently, there exists a critical need for a robust, self-contained analytical framework capable of accurately detecting CNVs from low-input, WGA-amplified genomic material without reliance on external controls or static filtering criteria, thereby enabling reliable clinical applications such as pre-implantation genetic diagnosis, non-invasive prenatal testing, and circulating tumor cell analysis.

## SUMMARY

The invention introduces a novel method for determining copy number variation in a genome sample. The method begins with sequencing a genome sample obtained from a single cell or trace nucleic acid source. The resulting sequencing reads are aligned to a reference genome sequence to establish their genomic coordinates. Subsequently, candidate breakpoints—genomic positions potentially marking transitions between copy number states—are identified through statistical analysis of read depth distribution. A detection window is then defined as the genomic interval bounded by two successive, statistically validated breakpoints. Within each detection window, a first parameter—representing normalized read depth—is computed to reflect local copy number status. Copy number variation is ultimately determined by comparing this first parameter against a dynamically established preset threshold. The invention further introduces a dedicated system configured to execute this method, comprising integrated hardware and software components for sample processing, sequencing, alignment, bias correction, breakpoint detection, and CNV classification. Additionally, the invention encompasses a non-transitory computer-readable medium encoded with instructions that, when executed by a processor, perform the steps of aligning sequencing results to a reference genome, determining read distribution, identifying breakpoints, defining detection windows, calculating the first parameter, and assessing the presence of copy number variation based on deviation from a threshold.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Copy number variation (CNV) refers to a type of structural genomic alteration wherein a segment of DNA, typically exceeding one kilobase in length, is present in a variable number of copies relative to a reference diploid genome. The present invention introduces a method for determining CNV in a genome sample, particularly when the sample originates from a single cell or contains only trace amounts of nucleic acid. The method commences with sequencing the genome sample, which is first extracted from a biological specimen such as peripheral blood, blastocyst trophectoderm, polar body, blastomere, or fetal nucleated red blood cell. In cases involving single cells, the isolated cell is lysed to release its genomic content, followed by whole genome amplification (WGA) using techniques such as degenerate oligonucleotide-primed PCR (DOP-PCR) to generate sufficient DNA for library construction. A sequencing library is then prepared, often incorporating unique molecular indexes to enhance multiplexing efficiency and reduce cross-contamination, and subjected to massively parallel sequencing—typically yielding single-end reads of 50 base pairs at low coverage (approximately 4% to 9.5% of the genome).

The raw sequencing data undergoes preprocessing to remove adapter sequences and low-quality reads. The cleaned reads are aligned to a standard human reference genome sequence (e.g., HG18/NCBI Build 36) using a high-throughput aligner such as SOAP2, allowing up to two mismatches and discarding non-uniquely mapped or PCR-duplicated reads. The aligned reads are distributed across the reference genome, and the total number of sequencing reads is calculated. Only uniquely aligned reads are retained for downstream analysis to ensure mapping accuracy. The reference genome is partitioned into non-overlapping primary windows of approximately 150 kilobases, selected to balance GC content homogeneity and statistical robustness given the low sequencing depth. For each primary window, the number of reads falling within it is recorded, and correlation analysis is performed between adjacent windows to assess continuity of coverage.

To detect potential breakpoints—genomic sites where copy number may change—a run test is applied to evaluate the statistical significance of differences in read depth between left and right flanking regions of each window. A p-value is computed for each site, and an initial set of candidate breakpoints is selected based on minimal p-values. A final p-value threshold is determined empirically from control samples, and iterative screening removes candidate breakpoints with p-values exceeding this threshold until all remaining breakpoints are statistically significant. The genomic region between two successive screened breakpoints is designated as a detection window.

Prior to breakpoint analysis, the raw read counts are corrected for WGA-induced GC bias. The relative number of reads (RRN) in each window is calculated as the ratio of observed reads to the genome-wide average. RRN is then adjusted using a GC-dependent weighting coefficient derived from the average RRN of windows sharing similar GC content (binned at 1% increments). This yields a corrected relative number of reads (CRN), which is further normalized to produce a normalized read count. GC content for each primary window is computed, and windows are grouped into GC regions to facilitate bias modeling. The mean RRN within each GC bin is used to compute the corrected RRN, effectively eliminating over- or under-representation due to amplification bias. A Z-score formula—defined as (CRN − mean)/SD, where mean and standard deviation (SD) are derived from GC-matched windows—is employed to standardize signals and eliminate residual amplification bias.

The first parameter, representing the average CRN within a detection window, is then compared to a dynamically determined preset threshold. This threshold is not fixed but is instead derived from the lower and upper quantiles (α = 0.05) of CRN distributions within windows of matching GC content, in accordance with the central limit theorem. If the first parameter falls below the lower threshold, a deletion is called; if above the upper threshold, a duplication is inferred. The boundary of statistical significance is thus contextually defined, improving detection accuracy. This approach solves the long-standing problem of analyzing single-cell or trace DNA samples by explicitly correcting for WGA bias, avoiding arbitrary thresholds, and enhancing detection efficiency. The use of unique indexes during library construction further improves multiplexing fidelity and analytical throughput.

The invention also provides a system for determining CNV, comprising a sequencing apparatus, a sample extraction module, a WGA module, a library preparation unit, and a bioinformatics analysis unit. The analysis unit includes a breakpoint determining unit that executes the binary segmentation algorithm: it calculates GC content, divides the genome into GC regions, computes mean RRN, determines corrected and normalized read counts, identifies detection windows, screens breakpoints via iterative p-value filtering, and classifies CNVs based on deviation from dynamic thresholds. Additionally, a non-transitory computer-readable medium is provided, storing executable instructions that, when run, cause a processor to align sequencing data, determine read distribution, identify breakpoints using run tests, define detection windows, compute the first parameter, and determine CNV presence by threshold comparison.

In a general embodiment, the method involves amplifying a whole genome sample from a single cell, sequencing the amplified product, aligning reads to a reference genome, calculating RRN, correcting for GC bias, normalizing data, determining candidate breakpoints, screening them via iterative p-value removal, establishing a final p-value cutoff, defining detection windows, and verifying CNV calls. In Example 1, two single cells from a healthy individual were subjected to WGA, sequenced to ~13 million reads each, and analyzed using the pipeline, successfully demonstrating >99.9% GC bias removal. In a comparative experiment without WGA, the method’s reliance on amplification correction was validated, as unamplified samples exhibited minimal GC bias, confirming that the correction strategy specifically targets WGA artifacts.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention possesses significant industrial applicability in the fields of clinical diagnostics, reproductive medicine, oncology, and genetic research. It enables reliable detection of pathogenic copy number variations from single cells or minute DNA samples, thereby supporting pre-implantation genetic diagnosis/screening (PGD/PGS) to prevent the implantation of embryos harboring deleterious genomic lesions. The method also facilitates non-invasive prenatal diagnosis using rare fetal cells and allows for the identification of circulating tumor cells in peripheral blood for early cancer detection. By providing a robust, bias-corrected, and threshold-adaptive analytical framework, the invention establishes a foundation for accurate genetic counseling and informed clinical decision-making. The scope of the invention encompasses all embodiments wherein genome samples—regardless of origin—are processed through the described sequencing, alignment, bias correction, breakpoint detection, and dynamic thresholding pipeline to yield clinically actionable CNV assessments.
