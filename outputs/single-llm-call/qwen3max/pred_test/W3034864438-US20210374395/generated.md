# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of machine-learned models for automated document processing, and more specifically to computer-implemented systems and methods that employ neural network architectures to extract structured information from form-like documents such as invoices, purchase orders, tax forms, insurance quotes, and receipts. The invention leverages representation learning to generate dense embeddings for candidate text portions and target field types, enabling robust extraction of relevant data even when presented with previously unseen document templates. By integrating spatial layout cues, textual context, and learned semantic representations, the disclosed system overcomes limitations inherent in traditional rule-based or template-matching approaches. The machine-learned models described herein are trained end-to-end to score candidate extractions based on their contextual and positional relationships within a document image, facilitating accurate assignment of extracted values to predefined fields in a target schema. This technological advancement significantly enhances the scalability, accuracy, and generalizability of document understanding systems deployed in enterprise workflows involving high-volume processing of heterogeneous form-like documents.

## BACKGROUND

Form-like documents—such as invoices, receipts, tax returns, and insurance applications—are ubiquitous in modern business operations. These documents typically follow semi-structured layouts where specific pieces of information, such as dates, amounts, identifiers, and addresses, are positioned in predictable but non-uniform ways across different issuers or templates. Despite their structural regularity within a given source, the visual and textual presentation of these documents varies widely across vendors, institutions, and jurisdictions, making automated extraction of structured data a persistent challenge. Historically, organizations have relied on manual data entry or brittle heuristic-based systems that require extensive configuration per template, resulting in high operational costs and error rates. Templatic documents differ fundamentally from natural language prose: they contain minimal narrative flow, rely heavily on spatial arrangement (e.g., tables, grids, alignment), and often embed critical semantic signals in proximity-based visual patterns rather than syntactic constructs. Consequently, conventional natural language processing techniques designed for sentence-level understanding perform poorly on such inputs.

Extracting information from these documents is further complicated by the fact that they are frequently received in non-machine-readable formats, such as scanned images or PDFs lacking semantic markup. While optical character recognition (OCR) can convert such documents into text, the resulting output often lacks explicit structural annotations, requiring downstream systems to infer relationships between text elements based solely on their content and relative positions. Current techniques for information extraction from form-like documents fall into several categories, each with significant limitations. Template registration methods require prior knowledge of document layouts and fail catastrophically on unseen templates. Rule-based systems using regular expressions or keyword matching lack robustness to linguistic variation and positional ambiguity. Wrapper induction approaches, effective on HTML-based web pages, cannot be applied to rasterized or unstructured document images. More recent deep learning models that combine computer vision and NLP—such as graph neural networks or grid-based representations—often demand large annotated datasets, are computationally intensive, or struggle to generalize beyond intra-template variations. Moreover, many existing solutions do not adequately model the interplay between a candidate value’s neighborhood context and its likelihood of belonging to a specific field, leading to misassignments when multiple candidates of the same type (e.g., multiple dates) appear in a single document. These challenges underscore the need for a flexible, learnable, and interpretable approach that can generalize across diverse document templates while leveraging both textual and spatial cues effectively.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel computer-implemented method for extracting structured information from form-like documents using a machine-learned scoring model grounded in representation learning. The method begins by receiving an image of a document, which may be a native digital file or a scanned image, and applying optical character recognition to extract text portions along with their positional metadata. A target schema—defining the expected fields (e.g., invoice_date, total_amount) and their associated data types (e.g., date, currency)—is then determined based on the document type, which may be identified through a preliminary classification step. For each field in the schema, the system generates candidate text portions by filtering all extracted text according to the field’s type using pre-trained detectors (e.g., selecting all date-like strings for a date field). Each candidate is then processed to extract features representing its content, absolute position, and the textual and positional characteristics of neighboring text elements within a defined spatial zone. These features are fed into a neural network model that generates a dense embedding for the candidate, independent of the specific field it is being evaluated for. Simultaneously, the model generates a separate embedding for the target field type. The system computes a similarity score—typically via cosine similarity—between the candidate embedding and the field embedding, which is then rescaled to produce a probability-like score in the range [0, 1]. This score reflects the likelihood that the candidate text portion corresponds to the true value for the given field. Finally, the highest-scoring candidate for each field is selected and assigned to that field, and the extracted structured data is transmitted to a central server or used to trigger automated business actions, such as invoice approval or expense reimbursement. This approach enables robust, template-agnostic extraction that generalizes to unseen document layouts while maintaining interpretability through meaningful learned representations.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention discloses a comprehensive system for extracting structured information from form-like documents, designed to operate robustly across both native digital documents and scanned images. The system is end-to-end trainable using machine learning models and is built upon three foundational principles derived from empirical observations of real-world form-like documents. First, each field in a target schema corresponds to a well-understood data type—such as date, currency, integer, email address, or physical address—enabling the use of type-specific candidate generators to narrow the search space. Second, each instance of a field value is typically associated with a key phrase (e.g., “Invoice Date” or “Total Due”) that exhibits a consistent visual relationship with the value, even if not adjacent in reading order. Third, these key phrases are drawn from a small, field-specific vocabulary, making the problem tractable with modest training data. The system architecture reflects these principles through a multi-stage pipeline comprising document ingestion, text extraction, candidate generation, and scoring/assignment.

Upon receiving a document image, the system first applies OCR to extract individual characters, words, and higher-level text blocks, each annotated with bounding box coordinates in the document’s 2D Cartesian plane. These coordinates are normalized by page dimensions to ensure resolution invariance. The document type is then identified—either via user input or an automated classifier—and mapped to a predefined target schema specifying the expected fields and their types. For each field, a candidate generation module uses type-specific detectors (e.g., regex patterns, sequence labeling models, or cloud-based entity recognition services) to identify all text spans matching the field’s type, producing a set of extraction candidates per field.

The core innovation lies in the scorer system, a neural network that evaluates each candidate independently. For a given candidate, the system defines a neighborhood zone—extending leftward across the page and upward by approximately 10% of the page height—and identifies all text portions whose bounding boxes overlap this zone by more than 50%. Features for the candidate include: (1) the normalized absolute position of the candidate’s centroid; (2) the text tokens of neighboring elements, mapped to a fixed vocabulary with special tokens for numbers, rare words, and padding; and (3) the relative positions of each neighbor, computed as the vector difference between the neighbor’s and candidate’s normalized coordinates. Notably, the candidate’s own text content is excluded from the input to prevent overfitting to superficial patterns in limited training data.

These features are embedded separately: neighbor text tokens via a word embedding table, relative positions via a nonlinear positional embedding (ReLU-activated layers with dropout), and candidate position via a linear layer. Field identity is also embedded via a dedicated embedding table. The initial neighbor embeddings are then refined using self-attention layers, allowing each neighbor’s representation to be influenced by all others in the neighborhood—this helps downweight irrelevant or obstructed neighbors. The self-attended neighbor embeddings are projected through a feedforward layer and max-pooled to produce a single neighborhood encoding, invariant to neighbor ordering. This is concatenated with the candidate position embedding and projected down to form a final candidate encoding.

The scoring model computes the cosine similarity between the candidate encoding and the field embedding, then rescales it linearly to [0, 1] to produce a prediction score. The model is trained using binary cross-entropy loss, where labels indicate whether a candidate matches the ground truth for its field and document. During inference, the highest-scoring candidate per field is selected and assigned. The extracted data can then be stored, transmitted to a second computing system, or used to automate downstream business processes—such as triggering payment workflows upon successful invoice parsing—thereby reducing manual effort and processing time.

The system’s design ensures robustness to template variation: because it learns to associate field types with contextual and positional patterns rather than fixed layouts, it generalizes to unseen document formats. Experimental results demonstrate superior performance over bag-of-words and MLP baselines, particularly in leveraging spatial relationships. Visualization of learned embeddings reveals coherent clustering of positive examples per field, confirming the model’s ability to capture meaningful semantic and structural cues. Variations of the embodiment may include alternative neighborhood definitions, different attention mechanisms, or integration with few-shot learning frameworks for rapid adaptation to new domains.
