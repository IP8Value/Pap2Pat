# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of molecular medicine and oncology, specifically in the therapeutic and diagnostic management of human papillomavirus (HPV)-associated neoplasias. More particularly, the invention relates to the use of Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF), a naturally occurring human cytokine, for the treatment of HPV-transformed epithelial cells by inhibiting transcription driven by the viral long control region (LCR), thereby downregulating expression of the E6 and E7 oncogenes essential for the maintenance of the malignant phenotype. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising LIF, methods for administering LIF to subjects diagnosed with or at risk of developing cervical dysplasia, cervical carcinoma, or other HPV-related pathologies, and diagnostic assays for monitoring LIF-mediated suppression of HPV oncogene expression. This technology represents a novel, non-surgical, biologically targeted intervention for HPV-associated diseases, addressing an unmet clinical need in women’s health and oncology therapeutics.

## BACKGROUND

Human papillomavirus (HPV) infection is a necessary causative agent in the development of cervical cancer, which remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide. Despite advances in screening through Pap smears and HPV DNA testing, and the availability of prophylactic vaccines, millions of women continue to be diagnosed annually with cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN) and invasive cervical carcinoma. Current treatment modalities for high-grade CIN and early-stage cervical cancer are predominantly surgical or ablative, including loop electrosurgical excision procedure (LEEP), cryotherapy, or conization, which carry risks of infertility, preterm labor, and psychological distress. For advanced or recurrent disease, cytotoxic chemotherapy combined with radiation offers limited survival benefit and significant toxicity, while no effective systemic therapy exists for persistent HPV infection underlying dysplasia. Although the viral oncoproteins E6 and E7—whose expression is governed by the HPV long control region (LCR)—are well-established drivers of cellular transformation and tumor progression, no approved pharmacologic agents directly target their expression. Prior attempts to modulate HPV transcription using cytokines such as interferons or TGF-β have yielded inconsistent clinical results, often due to paradoxical proliferative effects or loss of responsiveness in transformed cells. Thus, there exists a compelling medical need for a safe, effective, and mechanism-based therapeutic that selectively suppresses HPV oncogene expression without inducing widespread cytotoxicity. The discovery that Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) potently represses LCR-driven transcription and halts proliferation of HPV-transformed cells addresses this critical gap, offering a novel biologic approach to reverse the oncogenic state induced by persistent HPV infection.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods and compositions for the treatment of HPV-associated neoplasms through the administration of Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF). The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that LIF significantly inhibits transcription from the HPV long control region (LCR), resulting in reduced expression of the E6 and E7 oncogenes and consequent suppression of proliferation in HPV-transformed epithelial cells.

In a first embodiment, the invention provides a method of treating a subject diagnosed with cervical dysplasia, cervical carcinoma, or another HPV-associated pathology, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of LIF, wherein said LIF inhibits LCR-driven transcription of E6 and E7 viral oncogenes.

In a second embodiment, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising LIF in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, formulated for local or systemic delivery to cervical or other HPV-infected epithelial tissues.

In a third embodiment, the invention encompasses a method of inhibiting proliferation of HPV-transformed cells in a subject, comprising administering LIF to induce cell-cycle arrest without substantial induction of apoptosis, thereby promoting reversion toward a differentiated, non-malignant state.

In a fourth embodiment, the invention includes a method of enhancing the efficacy of conventional cytotoxic therapies, wherein LIF is co-administered with chemotherapy or radiation to sensitize HPV-positive tumor cells by downregulating E6/E7-mediated anti-apoptotic and pro-proliferative signals.

In a fifth embodiment, the invention provides a diagnostic method for monitoring therapeutic response in a subject undergoing LIF treatment, comprising measuring levels of E6 or E7 mRNA in cervical samples before and after administration, wherein a reduction indicates effective LCR suppression.

In a sixth embodiment, the invention relates to an in vitro assay for screening compounds that mimic or enhance LIF activity, utilizing HPV reporter cell lines stably expressing luciferase under the control of the HPV-16 LCR.

In a seventh embodiment, the invention includes a method of treating HPV-associated anal, oropharyngeal, laryngeal, or cutaneous neoplasms by topical, intralesional, or systemic administration of LIF.

In an eighth embodiment, the invention provides a kit comprising LIF, instructions for use in the treatment of HPV-associated dysplasia or cancer, and optionally, reagents for monitoring E6/E7 expression or STAT3 phosphorylation as a pharmacodynamic biomarker.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Introduction to Papillomavirus (PV) and its Relation to Cervical Cancer

Papillomaviruses (PVs) are small, non-enveloped, double-stranded DNA viruses that infect epithelial tissues of humans and other mammals. Human papillomaviruses (HPVs) are classified into more than 200 distinct types based on genomic sequence divergence, with approximately 40 types capable of infecting the anogenital tract. Persistent infection with high-risk HPV types, particularly HPV-16 and HPV-18, is the primary etiologic factor in the development of cervical cancer, which arises through a multistep process of viral integration, oncogene overexpression, and accumulation of host genomic alterations. The viral life cycle is tightly linked to epithelial differentiation, with early gene expression occurring in basal and parabasal cells and late gene expression restricted to terminally differentiated keratinocytes. In high-grade lesions and carcinomas, however, the normal regulation of viral transcription is disrupted, often due to integration of the HPV genome into the host chromosome, leading to constitutive overexpression of the E6 and E7 oncoproteins. These proteins inactivate key tumor suppressors—p53 and retinoblastoma protein (pRb), respectively—thereby disabling cell-cycle checkpoints, promoting genomic instability, and enabling immortalization and malignant transformation. The long control region (LCR), a non-coding regulatory segment upstream of the E6 gene, contains enhancer and promoter elements that govern viral transcription in response to host transcription factors and signaling pathways. Dysregulation of LCR activity is thus central to HPV oncogenesis, making it a rational target for therapeutic intervention.

### Classification of Papillomaviruses

Papillomaviruses are classified into genera (e.g., Alpha, Beta, Gamma, Mu, Nu) based on phylogenetic analysis of the L1 capsid gene, which encodes the major structural protein. The L1 open reading frame (ORF) is the most conserved region of the PV genome and serves as the basis for type designation: two PV types belong to the same species if their L1 nucleotide sequences share greater than 70% identity, and to the same genus if identity exceeds 60%. The alpha genus includes all mucosal HPVs, subdivided into high-risk (e.g., HPV-16, -18, -31, -33, -45) and low-risk (e.g., HPV-6, -11) types associated with cervical cancer and genital warts, respectively. Beta and gamma genera primarily comprise cutaneous HPVs linked to skin infections and epidermodysplasia verruciformis. The PV genome is approximately 8 kilobases in length and organized into early (E) and late (L) regions, with the LCR situated between the L1 and E6 genes. The LCR contains multiple transcription factor binding sites, including E2-binding sites that repress the E6/E7 promoter, SP1-binding sites that enhance basal transcription, and a TATA box that directs RNA polymerase II initiation. Phylogenetic trees constructed from L1 sequences reveal evolutionary relationships among PV types and correlate with tissue tropism and oncogenic potential.

### Papillomaviruses and Cancer

HPV infection is nearly universal in cervical cancer, with HPV-16 accounting for approximately 50% and HPV-18 for 20% of cases globally. Following initial infection, most HPV infections are cleared by the immune system within one to two years; however, persistent infection with high-risk types can lead to progressive cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN1 → CIN2/3) and ultimately invasive carcinoma over a period of 10–20 years. The integration of HPV DNA into the host genome disrupts the E2 gene, which normally represses the LCR, resulting in unchecked E6/E7 expression. E6 promotes p53 degradation via ubiquitin ligase E6AP, while E7 binds and inactivates pRb, releasing E2F transcription factors and driving S-phase entry. These actions collectively abrogate senescence and apoptosis, facilitate telomerase activation, and enable anchorage-independent growth. Moreover, E6 and E7 contribute to metastatic potential by modulating cell adhesion, motility, and immune evasion. Diagnosis of HPV-associated lesions relies on cytology (Pap smear), histopathology, and molecular detection of HPV DNA or RNA, with increasing emphasis on genotyping for high-risk types.

### Diagnosis and Treatment of HPV

Current diagnostic approaches include cytologic screening (Pap test), HPV DNA testing (e.g., Hybrid Capture II, PCR-based assays), and colposcopy with biopsy. Therapeutic strategies for low-grade dysplasia often involve observation, while high-grade lesions require excision or ablation. No approved antiviral agents exist to eradicate HPV infection. Therapeutic vaccines targeting E6/E7 have shown limited efficacy in clinical trials, and antiviral drugs such as cidofovir lack specificity. Immunomodulators like imiquimod are used for external genital warts but are ineffective for cervical disease. The absence of a pharmacologic option to reverse the oncogenic state underscores the novelty and utility of LIF-based therapy.

### Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) and its Utility in Treating HPV-Transformed Cells by Inhibiting LCR-Driven Transcription

LIF is a pleiotropic cytokine of the IL-6 family that signals through a heterodimeric receptor composed of LIFR and gp130, activating JAK-STAT, MAPK, and PI3K pathways. Contrary to its name, LIF exhibits context-dependent effects on cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. In HPV-transformed cervical cells, LIF was found to suppress LCR-driven transcription by approximately 60% in a dose- and time-dependent manner, as demonstrated using an HPV-16 LCR-Gaussia luciferase reporter system. This suppression correlated with significant reductions in endogenous E6 and E7 mRNA levels, confirming functional relevance. Critically, LIF treatment inhibited cell proliferation without inducing apoptosis, suggesting induction of cell-cycle arrest—a desirable outcome for reversing dysplasia. Unlike IL-6, which also represses LCR but stimulates proliferation, LIF uniquely couples transcriptional inhibition with growth suppression. Given its prior Phase I safety profile and low toxicity, LIF represents a promising candidate for clinical development in CIN and cervical cancer.

### Manipulating Proteins, DNA, and RNA

Standard molecular and cellular techniques are employed in the practice of the invention. These include cell culture of primary and immortalized lines (e.g., SiHa, CaSki), transfection with reporter plasmids, RNA isolation, quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) for E6/E7 quantification, Western blotting, flow cytometry for phospho-STAT3 detection, and protein purification via affinity chromatography (e.g., His-tag, GST-tag). Recombinant DNA methods such as PCR, restriction digestion, ligation, and cloning are used to construct expression vectors. Protein-protein interactions may be assessed by co-immunoprecipitation or yeast two-hybrid systems. Mass spectrometry (e.g., MALDI-TOF, LC-MS/MS) enables proteomic profiling, while microscopy techniques (confocal, electron) visualize subcellular localization. These conventional tools facilitate the characterization of LIF’s mechanism and the development of optimized formulations.

### Pharmaceutical Manufacturing

Pharmaceutical compositions of LIF are prepared using standard formulation principles. LIF may be formulated as a lyophilized powder or aqueous solution for parenteral (intravenous, subcutaneous, intralesional) or topical (vaginal gel, cream) administration. Excipients include buffers (e.g., phosphate, citrate), stabilizers (e.g., sucrose, albumin), and preservatives. Preformulation studies assess stability, solubility, and compatibility. Solid-state properties (polymorphs, hydrates) are characterized to ensure consistent bioavailability. Dosage forms are manufactured under GMP conditions, with quality control (QA/QC) ensuring sterility, potency, and purity. Pharmacokinetic studies guide dosing regimens to maintain therapeutic concentrations in cervical tissue.

### Experimental Procedures

Key experimental procedures include culturing SiHa and CaSki cells in DMEM with 10% FBS, generating stable HPV-16 LCR-luciferase reporter lines via G418 selection, performing MTT proliferation assays, conducting luciferase and qPCR measurements of E6/E7, and analyzing STAT3 phosphorylation by intracellular flow cytometry. Statistical analysis employs unpaired t-tests with significance at P<0.05.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

To evaluate LIF’s effect on HPV-16 LCR activity, SiHa cells were stably transfected with pLCRGLuc, containing the full HPV-16 LCR upstream of Gaussia luciferase. Treatment with 10 ng/mL LIF for 24 hours reduced luciferase activity by 60% compared to PBS control. qPCR confirmed dose-dependent suppression of E6 and E7 mRNA in CaSki cells after 1–3 days of LIF exposure, with maximal inhibition at 10 ng/mL. These results demonstrate that LIF directly represses viral oncogene transcription, providing a mechanistic basis for its anti-proliferative effects.

### Example 2

LIF signaling was assessed via STAT3 activation. SiHa cells treated with 10 ng/mL LIF showed transient phosphorylation of STAT3 at Tyr705 within 30 minutes, as detected by phosphospecific flow cytometry. Transcriptional activity was confirmed using a 4xM67-TK-Luc reporter, which exhibited 1.8-fold increased luminescence after 6 hours of LIF treatment. This indicates that LIF engages canonical JAK-STAT signaling in cervical cancer cells, though the downstream consequences of STAT3 activation in this context require further elucidation.

### Example 3

The anti-proliferative effect of LIF was quantified using MTT assays. CaSki cells treated with 10 ng/mL LIF in serum-free medium for 40 hours exhibited only 50% of the metabolic activity of untreated controls, whereas EGF stimulated proliferation. No significant apoptosis was observed, suggesting cytostatic rather than cytotoxic action. This growth inhibition aligns with reduced E6/E7 expression, supporting LIF’s potential to halt progression of HPV-driven neoplasia.
