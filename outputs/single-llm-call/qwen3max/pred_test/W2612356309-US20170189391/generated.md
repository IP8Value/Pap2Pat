# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of chelerythrine or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt or derivative thereof. The invention further encompasses methods of selectively inhibiting proliferation and inducing apoptosis in TNBC cells by targeting protein kinase C (PKC) isozymes, particularly PKN2 and PRKCA, which are overexpressed in TNBC relative to non-TNBC subtypes. Additionally, the invention includes combination therapies wherein chelerythrine is co-administered with conventional chemotherapeutic agents such as taxol to enhance anti-tumor efficacy. The disclosed methods exploit the differential expression and functional dependency of TNBC cells on specific PKC isozymes, thereby enabling a targeted therapeutic approach that spares non-TNBC and normal tissues, thus improving therapeutic selectivity and reducing systemic toxicity.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Breast cancer remains the most frequently diagnosed malignancy among women globally, with significant heterogeneity in clinical behavior, molecular profiles, and therapeutic responses. Based on the expression status of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), breast cancer is clinically stratified into distinct molecular subtypes: hormone receptor-positive (ER+ and/or PR+), HER2-positive, and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). TNBC, defined by the absence of ER, PR, and HER2 overexpression, accounts for approximately 10–15% of all breast cancer cases. Despite initial responsiveness to standard cytotoxic chemotherapy, TNBC is associated with aggressive tumor biology, early visceral metastases—particularly to the lungs and brain—and a high rate of disease recurrence within the first few years post-diagnosis. Consequently, patients with TNBC experience significantly poorer overall survival compared to those with other breast cancer subtypes.

Current systemic treatment for TNBC is limited to conventional chemotherapy, as hormonal therapies and HER2-targeted agents are ineffective due to the lack of their respective molecular targets. Although numerous investigational agents targeting pathways such as PARP, EGFR, PI3K, mTOR, CDK, HDAC, and angiogenesis have entered clinical trials, none have yet achieved regulatory approval for TNBC, underscoring the urgent unmet medical need for novel, effective, and selective therapeutic strategies. A major limitation of existing treatments lies in their non-specific cytotoxicity, which contributes to severe adverse effects and limits dose intensity. Moreover, the molecular complexity and heterogeneity of TNBC necessitate the identification of subtype-specific vulnerabilities that can be exploited for precision therapy. Protein kinase C (PKC), a family of serine/threonine kinases implicated in diverse cellular processes including proliferation, survival, migration, and stemness, has emerged as a candidate target due to its inverse correlation with ER expression and elevated activity in malignant breast tissue. However, despite preclinical evidence linking specific PKC isozymes to tumor aggressiveness and therapy resistance, no PKC-targeted agent has been successfully translated into clinical use for TNBC, largely due to a lack of understanding of isozyme-specific roles and the absence of selective inhibitors with favorable therapeutic indices.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for treating triple-negative breast cancer in a subject, comprising administering a therapeutically effective amount of chelerythrine or a pharmaceutically acceptable form thereof. In a preferred embodiment, the method exploits the selective sensitivity of TNBC cells to chelerythrine, a natural benzophenanthridine alkaloid and potent pan-PKC inhibitor, which induces dose- and time-dependent inhibition of proliferation and robust apoptosis specifically in TNBC cell lines while sparing non-TNBC counterparts. The invention is based on the discovery that TNBC cells exhibit heightened dependency on specific PKC isozymes—particularly PKN2 and, in certain contexts, PRKCA—for survival and growth. Chelerythrine’s anti-tumor activity is mediated through the functional inhibition of these overexpressed isozymes, leading to cell cycle perturbations and activation of intrinsic apoptotic pathways, as evidenced by chromatin condensation, nuclear fragmentation, cleavage of poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (cPARP), and annexin V positivity. Furthermore, the invention encompasses combination regimens wherein chelerythrine is administered concomitantly with standard chemotherapeutics such as taxol, resulting in additive or synergistic anti-proliferative effects against TNBC cells. This dual-modality approach enhances therapeutic efficacy and may overcome limitations of monotherapy, offering a promising strategy for the clinical management of TNBC.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

As used herein, the term “comprising” is intended to be open-ended and inclusive, encompassing not only the explicitly recited elements but also any additional components, steps, or features that do not materially alter the basic and novel characteristics of the claimed invention. The present invention centers on protein kinase C (PKC) as a critical therapeutic target for triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). PKC constitutes a family of at least twelve serine/threonine kinases involved in numerous pathological conditions, including cancer, diabetes, neurodegenerative disorders, and cardiovascular diseases. In breast cancer, an inverse relationship exists between estrogen receptor (ER) expression and both the activity and abundance of PKC, with PKC levels markedly elevated in malignant versus normal breast tissue. Notably, the alpha isoform, encoded by the PRKCA gene, is associated with antiestrogen resistance, tumor aggressiveness, and serves as a central signaling node in breast cancer stem cells—cell populations phenotypically aligned with TNBC. These findings collectively implicate PKC, and particularly PRKCA, as a rational target for TNBC therapy.

Chelerythrine, a naturally occurring benzophenanthridine alkaloid isolated from *Chelidonium majus*, functions as a highly specific ATP-competitive inhibitor of PKC by binding to its conserved catalytic domain. In vitro studies demonstrate that chelerythrine selectively inhibits the proliferation of TNBC cell lines—including MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and MDA-MB-468—with IC50 values ranging from 2.6 to 4.2 μM, whereas non-TNBC lines (MCF7, ZR-75-1, SK-BR-3, MDA-MB-453) exhibit resistance (IC50 >10 μM). This selectivity is time-dependent and confirmed by colony formation assays, wherein brief chelerythrine exposure abolishes clonogenic potential in TNBC cells but not in non-TNBC cells. Mechanistically, chelerythrine induces G0/G1 or G2/M arrest in a cell line-specific manner but predominantly triggers apoptosis in TNBC cells, as evidenced by Hoechst-stained nuclear fragmentation, cPARP detection via Western blotting, and flow cytometric quantification of annexin V-positive populations (up to 67.8% in MDA-MB-231). Apoptosis induction is time-dependent and absent in non-TNBC lines under identical conditions.

Gene expression profiling of 56 breast cancer cell lines from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) database revealed that, beyond PRKCA, the PKC-related kinases PKN1 and PKN2 are significantly overexpressed in TNBC. While PKN1 mRNA was elevated, protein levels were not consistently increased; in contrast, PKN2 was robustly overexpressed at both mRNA and protein levels across all tested TNBC lines. Functional validation via lentiviral shRNA-mediated knockdown confirmed that PKN2 is essential for TNBC cell proliferation in vitro and xenograft tumor growth in vivo, with no effect observed in non-TNBC models. Thus, PKN2 emerges as a key mediator of chelerythrine’s selective activity. Materials and methods employed include standard cell culture in RPMI-1640 with 10% FBS, SRB-based proliferation assays, crystal violet colony quantification, propidium iodide-based cell cycle analysis, annexin V/PI apoptosis detection, Hoechst 33258 morphological assessment, qRT-PCR using SYBR Green and EIF3S5 normalization, Western blotting with RIPA lysis and HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies, and dual-drug combination studies analyzed via Compusyn software for combination index (CI) calculation.

In vivo, chelerythrine (5 mg/kg, i.p., every 3–4 days) significantly suppresses MDA-MB-231 xenograft growth without affecting ZR-75-1 tumors. Moreover, combining subtherapeutic doses of chelerythrine (2 μM) and taxol (10 nM) yields additive or synergistic effects (CI 0.75–1.14) across all TNBC lines, enhancing cytotoxicity beyond either agent alone. These findings establish chelerythrine as a promising targeted agent for TNBC, acting through selective inhibition of overexpressed PKC isozymes—primarily PKN2 and secondarily PRKCA—to induce apoptosis and augment chemotherapy. The invention thus provides a foundation for clinical translation of PKC inhibition as a precision strategy against this recalcitrant breast cancer subtype.
