# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods for disrupting biological membranes. More specifically, the invention provides a novel small globular protein (SGP) and its functional derivatives that possess potent membrane-disrupting properties, leading to rapid cell lysis and anti-tumor activity. These proteins are engineered through de novo design principles and exhibit unique mechanisms of action distinct from conventional cytotoxic agents. The invention further encompasses therapeutic applications of SGP in the treatment of various neoplastic conditions, including solid tumors and hematological malignancies, by direct administration to tumor sites. Additionally, the invention includes formulations and delivery systems designed to enhance the selectivity and efficacy of SGP while minimizing systemic toxicity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Protein design has emerged as a powerful approach in biotechnology and medicine, enabling the creation of artificial proteins with tailored functions not found in nature. Among the most studied structural motifs in protein engineering are coiled-coil proteins, which consist of two or more alpha-helices wound around each other in a superhelical arrangement stabilized by hydrophobic interactions. Helix bundle proteins, another class of structured proteins, comprise multiple alpha-helices packed together to form a compact core, often involved in membrane association or pore formation. In contrast, β-structural proteins rely on beta-sheets for stability and function, commonly observed in enzymes and structural proteins. De novo design of proteins involves the rational construction of amino acid sequences that fold into predetermined three-dimensional structures with desired biological activities. This approach has enabled the development of synthetic proteins with novel functionalities, such as catalysis, ligand binding, and membrane disruption.

A notable example of such an engineered protein is the small globular protein (SGP), a synthetic polypeptide designed to adopt a defined tertiary structure capable of interacting with lipid bilayers. Despite its promise, SGP exhibits certain limitations that must be addressed for therapeutic utility. Specifically, early versions of SGP demonstrated non-selective cytotoxicity against both normal and malignant cells in vitro, raising concerns about potential off-target effects. Furthermore, the mechanism by which SGP disrupts membranes remains incompletely understood, and its activity appears to be modulated by extracellular matrix components, which may influence its in vivo behavior. These limitations highlight the need for further refinement of SGP-based therapeutics to improve selectivity, reduce toxicity, and enhance clinical applicability.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel application of protein engineering through the development of a small globular protein (SGP) that functions as a pore-forming protein with potent membrane-disrupting properties. Unlike naturally occurring pore-forming toxins, SGP is a synthetically designed molecule created through de novo protein design principles. The membrane-disrupting properties of SGP result in rapid cell lysis, characterized by immediate loss of plasma membrane integrity without classical features of apoptosis or necrosis at high concentrations. At lower concentrations, SGP induces apoptotic pathways, as evidenced by caspase activation and nuclear condensation. Importantly, SGP demonstrates significant anti-tumor activity in vivo, effectively inhibiting both primary tumor growth and metastatic spread in human xenograft models.

The proposed mechanism of action of SGP involves physical disruption of membrane architecture rather than enzymatic degradation or receptor-mediated signaling, distinguishing it from traditional chemotherapeutic agents. Although SGP lacks intrinsic selectivity toward cancerous versus normal cells in vitro, its activity in vivo is selectively restricted to tumor tissues due to the protective effect of extracellular matrix components present in healthy tissues. This differential susceptibility underlies the observed therapeutic window in animal models. Compared to other pore-forming peptides and proteins—such as magainin, cecropin, granulysin, NK-lysin, and verotoxin 1—SGP exhibits superior in vivo efficacy, likely due to its optimized structural stability and resistance to proteolytic degradation. Therapeutic applications of SGP include localized treatment of unresectable or metastatic tumors, particularly in anatomical sites where conventional therapies are ineffective or contraindicated.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

As used herein, the term “small globular protein” (SGP) refers to a synthetically designed polypeptide of approximately 70–100 amino acids that folds into a compact, stable tertiary structure enriched in alpha-helical content. The structure of SGP is characterized by a central hydrophobic core flanked by amphipathic helices that facilitate membrane insertion. Functional derivatives of SGP include any variant, analog, or modified form of the parent protein that retains the essential membrane-disrupting activity. The term “functional derivative” encompasses substitutions, deletions, insertions, or chemical modifications that do not abolish the protein’s ability to interact with and disrupt lipid bilayers. Examples of functional derivatives include SGP-L, wherein the central alanine-rich helix is replaced with a leucine-rich helix, and SGP-E, in which lysine residues are substituted with glutamic acid to alter charge distribution.

Conservative variations of SGP refer to amino acid substitutions that preserve the physicochemical properties of the original residue, thereby maintaining structural and functional integrity. A conservative variation is defined as the replacement of one amino acid with another having similar size, charge, hydrophobicity, or side chain functionality. Examples of conservative substitutions include valine for isoleucine, aspartate for glutamate, and serine for threonine. Substantially identical or highly conserved polypeptides are those sharing significant sequence homology with SGP, typically exhibiting at least 80% identity over a defined comparison window of at least 20 contiguous amino acids. Sequence homology and identity are determined using standard alignment algorithms, such as BLAST, which evaluates local alignments between query and subject sequences. The BLAST algorithm employs scoring matrices (e.g., BLOSUM62) to assign values to matches and mismatches, and calculates statistical significance using the smallest sum probability (P(N)) to assess the likelihood of observed similarity arising by chance. Five specific BLAST programs—blastp, blastn, blastx, tblastn, and tblastx—are available for different query types, with blastp being most relevant for protein comparisons.

An “effective amount” of SGP is that quantity sufficient to achieve a desired biological effect, such as tumor regression or cell lysis, without causing unacceptable toxicity. The effective amount typically ranges from 1 μM to 1 mM in local administration settings. A “biological membrane” refers to the phospholipid bilayer surrounding cells or organelles, and “membrane architecture” denotes the structural organization of this bilayer, including lipid composition, fluidity, and embedded proteins. Methods for disrupting membrane architecture involve contacting the membrane with SGP or its derivatives, resulting in pore formation, ion leakage, and eventual cell lysis. Cell lysis is defined as the irreversible breakdown of the plasma membrane, leading to cytoplasmic release and cell death. Selective disruption of cell membranes is achieved in vivo due to the shielding effect of extracellular matrix components, which inhibit SGP activity in healthy tissues but not in tumors lacking dense matrix coverage.

Methods for treating a tumor include administering SGP directly to the tumor site, where “treating” encompasses reducing tumor volume, preventing metastasis, or inducing tumor cell death. Tumors amenable to treatment include carcinomas (e.g., breast, prostate, lung), sarcomas (e.g., Kaposi’s sarcoma), and melanomas. “Administering” refers to delivering SGP via routes such as intratumoral, intradermal, or interstitial infusion, using formulations containing non-toxic, pharmaceutically acceptable carriers such as saline, buffered solutions, or liposomes. Targeted-delivery systems, including liposomes, enhance localization and reduce systemic exposure. Liposomes are formed by hydrating lipid films composed of phospholipids and cholesterol, encapsulating SGP within their aqueous core or bilayer. Alternative delivery approaches include intrathecal injections for central nervous system tumors, surgical implants for sustained release, and interstitial infusion for deep-seated lesions.

Chemical modification, such as omega-3 derivatization, may improve agent absorption and bioavailability. Peptide transport vectors and site-specific macromolecules can be conjugated to SGP via covalent bonding to enhance tissue targeting. Cationic macromolecular carriers facilitate cellular uptake, particularly in combination with anti-cancer drugs. Acid-sensitive intermediate molecules allow controlled release in the acidic tumor microenvironment. A “therapeutically effective amount” is the dose required to produce a clinically meaningful response, such as tumor shrinkage or symptom palliation. SGP induces non-necrotic, non-apoptotic cell death at high concentrations, but triggers apoptosis at lower doses, confirmed by caspase activation. Inhibition of primary and metastatic tumor growth is demonstrated across multiple xenograft models. Formulations may include lyophilized powders, aqueous solutions, or depot preparations for prolonged action.

### EXAMPLE 1

SGP, SGP-L, and SGP-E were synthesized using Fmoc solid-phase peptide synthesis on Fmoc-Leu-PEG resin with a Miligen Model 9050 synthesizer. Cleavage was performed with trifluoroacetic acid, followed by purification via HPLC on an ODS column using a water/acetonitrile gradient with 0.1% trifluoroacetic acid. Amino acid analysis confirmed expected composition, and mass spectrometry verified molecular weight (observed 7555.1 Da, calculated 7554.8 Da). Peptide concentrations were quantified by UV absorbance at 280 nm based on Trp and Tyr content.

### EXAMPLE 2

A computer model of SGP was generated using Insight II software on an Octane SSE workstation, revealing a compact globular fold with three amphipathic helices arranged around a hydrophobic core, consistent with pore-forming capability.

### EXAMPLE 3

Human cell lines—including KS1767 (Kaposi’s sarcoma), MDA-MB-435 (breast carcinoma), PC-3 (prostate cancer), H358 (lung carcinoma), and dermal microvessel endothelial cells—were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and antibiotics.

### EXAMPLE 4

Cells were treated with SGP, SGP-L, or SGP-E at varying concentrations. Viability was assessed morphologically using acridine orange/ethidium bromide staining. SGP induced rapid, concentration-dependent cell death: >10 μM caused immediate lysis, 5–10 μM induced necrosis within 60 min, and <5 μM triggered apoptosis over 24 h. LC50 values for SGP-L and SGP-E were at least 10-fold higher than SGP, confirming structural dependence. SGP showed no selectivity between tumor and normal cells in vitro.

### EXAMPLE 5

Human tumor xenografts were established in nude mice by implanting 10⁶ tumor cells subcutaneously. SGP (100 μM or 1 mM) was injected directly into tumors in 40 μl total volume. After four weekly treatments, SGP-treated mice showed 5-fold reduction in tumor volume compared to PBS controls. Similar efficacy was observed across breast, prostate, and lung carcinoma models, with no recurrence for up to 4 months.

### EXAMPLE 6

Histological analysis of SGP-treated tumors revealed widespread apoptosis (condensed nuclei, caspase-3 activation) without damage to extracellular matrix. Major organs showed no pathology, confirming localized action.

### EXAMPLE 7

Intradermal injection of 100 μM SGP in nude mice produced no skin toxicity over 2 weeks, unlike conventional cytotoxic agents.

### EXAMPLE 8

A matrigel assay mimicked extracellular matrix. Without matrigel, SGP caused >90% cell death within 10 min. With matrigel or polymeric fibronectin, SGP was inactive, while doxorubicin and ethanol remained cytotoxic. This explains the in vivo selectivity: tumors lack dense matrix, allowing SGP access, whereas healthy tissues are protected.
