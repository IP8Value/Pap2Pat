## FIELD

The present invention relates to recombinant Aspergillus fungi that have been genetically engineered to modulate the production of organic acids, particularly itaconic acid, aconitic acid, and 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3-HP). More specifically, the invention provides isolated recombinant strains of Aspergillus, including but not limited to Aspergillus pseudoterreus, in which the cadA gene encoding cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase has been genetically inactivated, thereby eliminating itaconic acid biosynthesis and redirecting metabolic flux toward the accumulation of aconitic acid or, when further engineered with heterologous biosynthetic pathways, toward the production of 3-HP. The invention also encompasses methods for producing aconitic acid and 3-HP using these engineered fungal strains, as well as compositions and kits comprising such strains for industrial bioproduction applications.

## BACKGROUND

Itaconic acid (IA) is a five-carbon unsaturated dicarboxylic acid produced industrially by filamentous fungi of the genus Aspergillus, particularly Aspergillus terreus and its close relatives such as Aspergillus pseudoterreus. IA serves as a valuable platform chemical for the synthesis of polymers, resins, and synthetic fibers due to its reactive vinyl group and two carboxylic acid functionalities. The biosynthesis of itaconic acid occurs via the decarboxylation of cis-aconitic acid, an intermediate of the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. This reaction is catalyzed by the enzyme cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase, encoded by the cadA gene. The cadA gene is part of a conserved gene cluster involved in itaconic acid production, which also includes transcription factor genes (e.g., tf), mitochondrial transporter genes (e.g., mttA), and major facilitator superfamily transporter genes (e.g., mfsA). Expression of this gene cluster is tightly regulated in response to environmental conditions such as carbon source availability, pH, and oxygen levels, with maximal expression typically occurring during the late exponential or early stationary phase of fungal growth under acidic, glucose-rich conditions. Despite decades of industrial use, the precise regulatory mechanisms governing the IA gene cluster remain incompletely understood, though transcriptomic analyses have indicated coordinated upregulation of all cluster genes during high-level IA production.

## SUMMARY

The present invention is based on the discovery that genetic inactivation of the cadA gene in Aspergillus species abolishes itaconic acid production and leads to the accumulation of aconitic acid, both in its cis and trans isomeric forms, thereby providing a novel biological route to this industrially relevant compound. Further, the invention demonstrates that the ΔcadA strain serves as an ideal chassis for the heterologous production of 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3-HP) via the beta-alanine pathway. Expressed sequence tag (EST) analysis revealed strong co-expression of genes within the IA cluster during itaconic acid production, supporting the functional linkage of cadA with other cluster components. Gene deletion analysis confirmed that loss of cadA function eliminates itaconic acid synthesis without impairing fungal viability, while simultaneously enabling the secretion of aconitic acid into the culture medium. In parallel, the invention introduces recombinant ΔcadA Aspergillus strains engineered to express exogenous nucleic acid molecules encoding key enzymes of the 3-HP biosynthetic pathway: aspartate 1-decarboxylase (panD), β-alanine-pyruvate aminotransferase (BAPAT), and 3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase (HPDH). These heterologous genes are operably linked to strong fungal promoters, such as the gpdA or eno1 promoters from Aspergillus niger, and integrated into the fungal genome using standard transformation vectors. The genetic inactivation of cadA may be achieved through various means, including complete deletion of the coding region, insertional mutagenesis, antisense RNA expression, or CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout. The resulting compositions include isolated recombinant fungal cells, spores, and culture broths, as well as kits containing such materials along with instructions for use in fermentation processes. Methods are provided for the production of aconitic acid by culturing ΔcadA strains under optimized conditions, and for the production of 3-HP by culturing ΔcadA strains additionally expressing panD, BAPAT, and HPDH, optionally combined with deletion of competing catabolic genes such as Apald6 (malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase) to enhance yield and stability of 3-HP.

### Overview

Aspergillus pseudoterreus is a filamentous fungus closely related to A. terreus and is known for its high-yield production of itaconic acid under acidic fermentation conditions. Itaconic acid biosynthesis proceeds from glucose through glycolysis to acetyl-CoA, entry into the TCA cycle, formation of citric acid, isomerization to cis-aconitic acid, and finally decarboxylation to itaconic acid by cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (CadA). The genes encoding CadA (cadA), a transcription factor (tf), a mitochondrial tricarboxylate transporter (mttA), and a plasma membrane efflux pump (mfsA) are clustered in the genome and co-regulated. When the cadA gene is deleted (ΔcadA strain), itaconic acid production ceases entirely. Instead, cis-aconitic acid accumulates intracellularly and is partially isomerized to trans-aconitic acid, both of which are secreted into the culture medium. This metabolic redirection demonstrates that the ΔcadA strain functions as an efficient whole-cell biocatalyst for aconitic acid production, offering a sustainable alternative to energy-intensive chemical synthesis routes. Analysis of expressed sequence tags (ESTs) confirmed that tf, cadA, mttA, and mfsA are coordinately upregulated during peak itaconic acid production, validating the functional integrity of the cluster. Construction of targeted deletion mutants for each gene in the cluster established that only cadA deletion abolishes itaconic acid synthesis. The ΔcadA strain produces significant quantities of aconitic acid, a compound recognized by the U.S. Department of Energy as one of the top 30 potential building blocks for biobased chemicals due to its utility in polymer synthesis. Thus, the invention provides isolated recombinant Aspergillus fungi, particularly A. pseudoterreus, with a genetically inactivated cadA gene, enabling robust aconitic acid biosynthesis.

### Recombinant ΔcadA Fungi

Recombinant ΔcadA fungi are Aspergillus strains, preferably A. pseudoterreus, in which the endogenous cadA gene has been functionally inactivated. Genetic inactivation may be achieved by homologous recombination replacing the cadA open reading frame with a selectable marker (e.g., ptrA), by CRISPR/Cas9-induced frameshift mutations, or by insertional mutagenesis. Reduced or absent CadA enzymatic activity results in no detectable itaconic acid and elevated aconitic acid titers. Additional genes, such as those encoding competing dehydrogenases (e.g., Apald6), may also be inactivated to optimize product yield. Exogenous nucleic acid molecules encoding panD, BAPAT, and HPDH are introduced to enable 3-HP production. These genes are codon-optimized for Aspergillus and placed under control of strong constitutive or inducible promoters (e.g., gpdA, eno1). Expression cassettes may be integrated at the cadA locus or elsewhere in the genome. The cre-lox system can be employed for marker recycling. Transformation is typically performed via protoplast preparation using lysing enzymes, followed by polyethylene glycol-mediated DNA uptake. Gene inactivation is verified by PCR, Southern blotting, or qRT-PCR showing absence of cadA mRNA. Aconitic acid and 3-HP are quantified using HPLC with refractive index detection or LC-MS. CadA protein sequences are defined by conserved decarboxylase domains; variants with conservative substitutions (e.g., hydrophobic for hydrophobic residues) retain structural integrity. Similarly, panD, BAPAT, and HPDH variants exhibiting substantial sequence identity (>60%) and catalytic activity are encompassed. Codon optimization enhances expression without altering amino acid sequence. Up-regulation of heterologous genes is confirmed by targeted proteomics (e.g., SRM). Transgene constructs include terminators (e.g., trpC) and selection markers (ptrA). Combined cadA inactivation and heterologous pathway expression yields strains capable of high-titer 3-HP production.

### Methods of Producing Aconitic Acid

Aconitic acid is produced by culturing recombinant ΔcadA Aspergillus fungi in a suitable medium, such as minimal medium containing 100 g/L glucose, ammonium sulfate, and trace minerals, adjusted to pH 3.4. Cultivation is carried out at 30°C with agitation (200 rpm) in shake flasks or in bioreactors with controlled pH (maintained at 2.8 via KOH addition) and aeration (0.4 vvm). After 5–8 days of growth, the culture broth is harvested by filtration. Aconitic acid is isolated from the supernatant using ion-exchange chromatography, crystallization, or solvent extraction, followed by purification via recrystallization or preparative HPLC. Yields in ΔcadA strains exceed 5 g/L, significantly higher than negligible levels in wild-type strains, which primarily produce itaconic acid.

### Methods of Producing 3-HP

3-HP is produced by culturing recombinant ΔcadA Aspergillus strains expressing panD, BAPAT, and HPDH under similar conditions as above. The medium composition may be optimized for nitrogen balance to support transamination reactions. Cultivation in 0.5-L bioreactors at 30°C, pH 2.8, and high aeration (600 rpm stir rate) yields up to 0.88 g/L 3-HP in strains also lacking Apald6, compared to <0.2 g/L in parental strains. Harvest occurs at peak titer (typically day 7). 3-HP is purified from the broth using electrodialysis, nanofiltration, or acid precipitation, leveraging its low pKa (4.5) for efficient separation in acidic conditions.

### Compositions and Kits

Compositions comprise isolated recombinant ΔcadA Aspergillus fungi, either as spores, mycelial fragments, or lyophilized biomass, suspended in cryoprotectants (e.g., 15% glycerol) or lyophilization buffers. Kits include such compositions along with culture media (e.g., CM agar, minimal medium powder), transformation reagents, and instructional manuals for fermentation and product recovery.

### Example 1

Strains and vectors: A. pseudoterreus ATCC 32359 was used. Vector pZD4028 contained codon-optimized TcPAND, BcBAPAT, and EcHPDH under gpdA/eno1 promoters, with ptrA selection. Growth: Spores were inoculated into minimal medium (100 g/L glucose, pH 3.4) and grown at 30°C, 200 rpm. Protoplasts were prepared by digesting mycelia with lysing enzymes in osmotic stabilizer (1.2 M MgSO4), washed, and transformed with linearized vector DNA using PEG/CaCl2. Transformants were selected on pyrithiamine-containing plates. Deletion mutants were constructed via fusion PCR: 5′ and 3′ cadA flanking regions were amplified and fused to ptrA, then transformed. Dry mass was measured after freeze-drying filtered mycelia. HPLC analysis used an Aminex HPX-87H column with 0.0045 M H2SO4 eluent. RNA was isolated using TRIzol, and qRT-PCR was performed with gene-specific primers to assess transcript levels.

### Example 2

EST data from A. pseudoterreus cultures producing itaconic acid showed high read counts for tf, cadA, mttA, and mfsA, confirming co-expression of the entire IA gene cluster during active production phases, consistent with coregulation.

### Example 3

Knockout strains for tf, cadA, mttA, and mfsA were generated. Only ΔcadA completely abolished itaconic acid production, while Δtf reduced it by >90%, indicating tf’s role as a master regulator.

### Example 4

Kinetic analysis showed Δtf strains produced 0.5 g/L IA versus 25 g/L in wild-type, confirming tf’s essential role. Example 5 demonstrated that tf deletion drastically reduced mRNA levels of all cluster genes. Example 6: cadA deletion eliminated IA and yielded 6.2 g/L aconitic acid. Example 7: DNA fragments for panD/BAPAT/HPDH were assembled into pZD4028 via Gibson Assembly and transformed into ΔcadA strain, establishing 3-HP production capability.
