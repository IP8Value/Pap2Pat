## BACKGROUND

Cellular differentiation is a fundamental biological process by which unspecialized cells, such as stem or progenitor cells, acquire specialized functions and morphologies to form the diverse cell types that constitute multicellular organisms. This process is tightly regulated by intrinsic transcriptional programs and extrinsic signaling cues that guide cells through hierarchical developmental trajectories toward terminal fates. Understanding the molecular mechanisms governing cellular differentiation is critical for advancing regenerative medicine, elucidating disease pathogenesis, and developing targeted therapeutics. In recent years, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has emerged as a transformative technology that enables high-resolution profiling of gene expression at the individual cell level, thereby revealing previously unappreciated heterogeneity within seemingly homogeneous cell populations. Despite its power, scRNA-seq data are inherently noisy due to technical limitations, most notably the phenomenon of “dropout events,” wherein transcripts are present in a cell but fail to be detected due to low capture efficiency or amplification bias. These dropouts introduce spurious zeros into the expression matrix, obscuring true biological signals and complicating downstream analyses such as clustering, trajectory inference, and lineage reconstruction. Conventional analytical methods, including principal component analysis (PCA) and standard non-negative matrix factorization (NMF), often fail to adequately account for this technical noise, leading to inaccurate representations of cellular states and misleading interpretations of developmental hierarchies. Moreover, existing approaches typically lack a robust quantitative framework to distinguish multipotent progenitor cells from lineage-committed cells based solely on transcriptomic data, thereby limiting their utility in mapping differentiation landscapes. There remains a pressing need for computational tools that can effectively model dropout noise, extract biologically meaningful gene expression signatures, and provide a principled measure of cellular plasticity to enable accurate reconstruction of developmental trajectories from scRNA-seq data.

## SUMMARY

The present invention addresses the limitations of current scRNA-seq analysis methods by providing a novel computational pipeline, termed “dpath,” that enables robust decomposition of single-cell transcriptomic data while accounting for technical noise, particularly dropout events. The invention is motivated by the need for a molecular definition of cellular differentiation states that transcends reliance on pre-defined marker genes and instead derives intrinsic transcriptional signatures directly from the data. Central to the invention is the use of Etv2-EYFP transgenic embryos, which allow for the specific isolation of endothelial and hematopoietic lineage cells during early murine embryogenesis, thereby providing a well-defined biological system for method development and validation. The dpath pipeline performs single-cell transcriptome analyses through a series of integrated computational steps that collectively yield a high-resolution map of cellular states and their developmental relationships. A key innovation of the invention is the introduction of “metagene entropy,” a quantitative measure derived from the heterogeneity of metagene expression within individual cells, which serves as an indicator of cellular plasticity and differentiation potential. The invention further encompasses a software implementation of the dpath algorithm, embodied in a machine-readable medium containing instructions executable by a processor to perform the disclosed methods. The core analytical approach involves decomposing the single-cell expression profile matrix using a weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization (wp-NMF) model that explicitly accounts for the probability of dropout events, thereby yielding a parts-based representation of gene expression in the form of metagenes and their corresponding coefficients. Genes are prioritized based on their contribution to defining progenitor versus committed cellular states, and individual cells are ranked according to their alignment with specific lineage trajectories. This comprehensive framework enables the accurate inference of developmental pathways and the identification of novel regulatory genes and signaling pathways involved in cell fate decisions.

### ASPECTS OF THE INVENTION

Ets variant 2 (Etv2) is a master transcriptional regulator essential for the development of endothelial and hematopoietic lineages during embryogenesis. The invention leverages the biological role of Etv2 by utilizing Etv2-EYFP transgenic embryos, wherein enhanced yellow fluorescent protein (EYFP) expression is driven by the Etv2 promoter, enabling fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) of Etv2-expressing cells. Studies involving Etv2 mutants and overexpression models have demonstrated its necessity and sufficiency for vasculogenesis and hematopoiesis, respectively. During murine embryogenesis, Etv2 expression is dynamically regulated, initiating around embryonic day 7.25 (E7.25) and persisting through E8.25, marking a critical window of mesodermal specification and lineage commitment. The invention employs single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to profile the transcriptomes of FACS-sorted Etv2-EYFP+ cells across these developmental time points. To address the pervasive issue of dropout events in scRNA-seq data, the invention introduces a mathematical solution in the form of weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization (wp-NMF). This model treats observed gene expression as a mixture of a true Poisson-distributed signal and a low-magnitude Poisson process representing dropouts, assigning differential weights to each entry in the expression matrix based on the estimated probability of being a true detection. The wp-NMF decomposition yields a set of metagenes—latent variables representing co-expressed gene modules—and corresponding metagene coefficients that describe the contribution of each metagene to each cell. Building upon this, the invention introduces metagene entropy, calculated as the Shannon entropy of the normalized metagene coefficients for a given cell, as a metric of transcriptional heterogeneity and thus cellular plasticity. Cells with high metagene entropy are inferred to be multipotent progenitors, whereas those with low entropy are considered lineage-committed. The self-organizing map (SOM) algorithm is employed to project cells into a two-dimensional hexagonal grid of metacells, where each metacell represents a prototype expression profile, thereby visualizing the continuum of cellular states. To infer lineage relationships, the invention implements a random walk with restart (RWR) algorithm on a heterogeneous graph comprising metacells and metagenes, which prioritizes cells based on their proximity to specified cellular states (e.g., progenitor or committed). The dpath program integrates all these components into a unified software package, offering functionalities for data preprocessing, matrix decomposition, entropy calculation, SOM mapping, RWR-based prioritization, and gene ranking. Comparative analyses demonstrate that wp-NMF outperforms conventional factorization methods like PCA in separating biologically distinct cell populations. ### Materials and Methods

Cells were isolated from Etv2-EYFP transgenic embryos harvested at E7.25, E7.75, and E8.25. Embryos were screened for EYFP expression under a fluorescence microscope, and EYFP-positive embryos were dissociated using TrypLE Express. The resulting single-cell suspension was stained with propidium iodide to exclude dead cells and sorted by FACS to collect viable EYFP+ cells. Sorted cells were loaded onto a Fluidigm 10–17 μm integrated fluidics circuit for single-cell capture, viability screening, lysis, and library amplification on a C1 Single-Cell Auto Prep System. Libraries were sequenced on an Illumina MiSeq platform using 75-bp paired-end reads. Low-quality reads and cells with fewer than 100,000 reads were filtered out. Transcripts per million (TPM) were estimated using TopHat and Cufflinks. A noise model was fitted to the TPM data to identify and remove genes with high technical noise, as well as ubiquitously expressed genes with low coefficient of variation. The weighted Poisson non-negative matrix factorization (wp-NMF) model was defined by an objective function that maximizes a weighted log-likelihood, where weights are derived from the probability of a gene being expressed versus a dropout event. The optimization was performed using a gradient ascent method with multiplicative update rules, initialized via weighted NMF. Metagene entropy was computed as the Shannon entropy of the normalized metagene coefficients. The number of metagenes (K) was selected based on cophenetic correlation coefficients from consensus clustering. Performance was evaluated using leave-one-out cross-validation (LOO-CV) error and within-cluster sum of squares (WSS) to total sum of squares (TSS) ratio. Cells were clustered into metacells using a 15×15 hexagonal SOM, and the SOM was partitioned into discrete clusters using the Partitioning Around Medoids (PAM) algorithm. A heterogeneous metagene–metacell graph was constructed, and metacells were prioritized with respect to cellular states using the RWR algorithm.

### Results

The dpath pipeline was applied to 281 Etv2-EYFP+ cells from E7.25–E8.25 embryos. wp-NMF decomposition into four metagenes revealed biologically coherent signatures: MG1 (endothelium), MG2 (hematopoiesis), MG3 (endocardium), and MG4 (mesodermal progenitors), validated by known marker genes and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). wp-NMF outperformed PCA, diffusion maps, and t-SNE in separating spatially distinct Emcn+, Gata1+, and Tbx20+ subpopulations, as confirmed by immunohistochemistry. Metagene entropy was significantly higher in earlier-stage cells (E7.25 > E7.75 > E8.25), establishing it as a quantitative measure of progenitor state. The SOM organized cells into a metacell landscape where high-entropy progenitors occupied the center and low-entropy committed cells localized to the periphery. PAM clustering identified eight cell clusters (C1–C8), with C2, C4, C5, and C6 enriched in early time points and exhibiting high entropy. T+ cells in C5 expressed Sox7 and Runx1, marking them as immature progenitors. C2, expressing endocardial markers Cgnl1 and Dok4, was verified as endocardial cushion progenitors transitioning to C1 via endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition. Two waves of hematopoiesis were identified: C5→C6→C7 (primitive) and C4→C7 (definitive), with Runx1 preceding Gata1 expression. Pathway analysis of progenitor clusters (C2, C5, C6) implicated the Sonic Hedgehog (SHH) pathway, which was experimentally validated using SHH agonist (SAG) and antagonist (cyclopamine) in an ES/EB differentiation model—SAG promoted and cyclopamine suppressed hematopoietic and endothelial progenitors. Developmental trajectories were inferred using RWR on a metacell–metagene graph, with pseudotime strongly correlated with embryonic age (Kendall τ = 0.798), outperforming Monocle (0.213) and Wishbone (0.375). GSEA and Etv2 ChIP-seq data confirmed the biological relevance of predicted progenitor and committed states.

### DISCUSSION

The dpath pipeline represents a significant advancement in scRNA-seq data analysis, offering three major technical breakthroughs: (1) wp-NMF for robust matrix decomposition that mitigates dropout noise, (2) metagene entropy as a quantitative measure of cellular plasticity, and (3) a 2D SOM for intuitive visualization of lineage hierarchies. Unlike PCA, which produces holistic representations, NMF’s parts-based decomposition allows genes to participate in multiple metagenes, reflecting biological reality. Metagene entropy, derived from the uncertainty in metagene usage, provides the first entropy-based method to rank differentiation potential in scRNA-seq. The SOM’s flexibility accommodates multiple progenitor states and asynchronous differentiation, avoiding the oversimplification of tree-like models. Applied to Etv2+ cells, dpath revealed high-entropy progenitors of hematopoietic and endothelial lineages, consistent with the model that mesodermal cells ingress through the primitive streak and commit rapidly based on local signals. The dynamic expression of SHH pathway components and its functional validation underscore dpath’s ability to uncover novel regulators. The pipeline’s accuracy in pseudotime inference, superior to existing tools, highlights its utility for reconstructing developmental trajectories. By providing a full transcriptome of individual Etv2+ cells, dpath identifies novel markers to improve population purity and reveals signaling pathways driving lineage bifurcation. The availability of dpath as an R package ensures broad applicability, making it an essential tool for extracting meaningful biological insights from the ever-growing volume of single-cell RNA-seq data.
