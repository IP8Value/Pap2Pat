# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to enhancer elements for promoters, particularly those derived from human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6), and more specifically to an enhancer element comprising the first intron of the immediate early 1 (IE1) gene of HHV-6 that significantly enhances promoter activity. The invention further encompasses nucleic acid constructs incorporating such enhancer-promoter combinations, expression vectors utilizing these constructs, and methods for enhancing heterologous gene expression in mammalian cells, especially T-cell lines. The disclosed technology provides a novel regulatory system for driving high-level, cell-type-preferential expression of transgenes in gene therapy, vaccine development, recombinant protein production, and functional genomics applications.

## BACKGROUND ART

Gene therapy represents a promising approach for treating a wide range of diseases by introducing therapeutic genes into target cells to correct genetic defects, modulate immune responses, or deliver cytotoxic agents. A critical component of successful gene therapy is the selection of appropriate regulatory elements that can drive sufficient and sustained expression of the therapeutic transgene in the desired cell types while minimizing off-target effects. Viral promoters have been widely employed due to their strong transcriptional activity, with the human cytomegalovirus (HCMV) major immediate early (MIE) promoter being one of the most commonly used regulatory elements in both research and clinical applications. However, the HCMV MIE promoter exhibits variable activity across different cell types and may be subject to silencing over time, particularly in certain hematopoietic lineages. Moreover, its activity in T lymphocytes—the primary target cells for many immunotherapeutic approaches—is often suboptimal compared to other cell types. These limitations have created a need for alternative promoter systems that can provide enhanced and cell-type-specific expression profiles suitable for therapeutic applications targeting specific cellular populations, particularly T cells which are central to adaptive immunity and numerous disease processes including viral infections, cancers, and autoimmune disorders.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The present invention addresses the longstanding problem in the field of gene therapy and recombinant protein expression regarding the lack of strong, reliable promoter systems that exhibit preferential activity in T-cell lineages. While the HCMV MIE promoter has served as a workhorse for heterologous gene expression, its relatively weak performance in T-cell lines compared to other cell types represents a significant limitation for applications targeting these critical immune cells. Additionally, existing promoter systems often fail to maintain consistent expression levels across different cellular contexts or may be susceptible to epigenetic silencing mechanisms that diminish their long-term utility. There exists a compelling need for novel regulatory elements that can overcome these limitations by providing enhanced transcriptional activity specifically in T cells while maintaining robust expression in other relevant cell types.

### Solutions to the Problems

The present invention provides a solution to these problems through the identification and characterization of a novel enhancer element derived from the first intron of the immediate early 1 (IE1) gene of human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6). This enhancer element, when combined with the HHV-6 major immediate early (MIE) promoter, creates a regulatory system that demonstrates significantly enhanced transcriptional activity compared to conventional promoters, particularly in T-cell lines. The invention introduces the use of this intronic sequence as a potent enhancer that dramatically boosts promoter activity when positioned downstream of the transcription start site. The enhancer activity of this first intron has been demonstrated to increase HHV-6 MIE promoter activity substantially across multiple cell types, with particularly striking enhancement observed in T-cell lines where the combination outperforms the standard HCMV MIE promoter by a considerable margin. When compared directly with the CMV promoter, the HHV-6 MIE promoter containing the first intron (designated herein as 6MIE-in1) exhibits superior activity in Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1 T-cell lines while maintaining comparable activity in adherent cell lines such as MRC-5 and MeWo cells. The invention further encompasses the combination of this enhanced promoter system with the 6MIE promoter architecture, creating a versatile regulatory element suitable for diverse biotechnological applications. The advantages of this invention include: (1) significantly enhanced promoter activity in T-cell lines compared to conventional viral promoters; (2) maintenance of robust expression levels in non-T-cell lines, providing broad applicability across different cellular contexts; (3) provision of a novel regulatory element derived from a human pathogen with natural tropism for T cells, thereby leveraging evolutionary optimization for T-cell-specific expression; (4) creation of a modular system that can be readily incorporated into various vector backbones for gene therapy, vaccine development, and recombinant protein production; and (5) demonstration of functional utility in complex viral systems, as evidenced by successful heterologous gene expression in recombinant varicella-zoster virus.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention provides numerous advantageous effects that address critical limitations in current gene expression technologies. The HHV-6 MIE promoter containing the first intron enhancer element demonstrates superior transcriptional activity in T-cell lines compared to the widely used HCMV MIE promoter, thereby enabling more efficient gene delivery and expression in these therapeutically important cells. This enhanced activity translates to improved efficacy in T-cell-targeted gene therapy applications, potentially allowing for lower vector doses and reduced associated risks. The invention also provides a regulatory system that maintains robust expression across diverse cell types, offering versatility for applications requiring broad cellular tropism. Furthermore, the demonstrated functionality of this promoter-enhancer combination in recombinant viral systems, specifically varicella-zoster virus, establishes its utility for vaccine development and oncolytic virotherapy applications. The identification of specific regulatory elements within the promoter region, including NF-κB binding sites that are critical for activity, provides opportunities for further engineering and optimization of this system for specialized applications.

## DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention provides novel enhancer elements for promoters, particularly those derived from human herpesvirus 6, that significantly enhance transcriptional activity when combined with appropriate promoter sequences.

### (Enhancer for Promoter)

An enhancer for promoter as described herein refers to a nucleic acid sequence that increases the transcriptional activity of a promoter when positioned in proximity to it, regardless of orientation or exact location relative to the transcription start site. The enhancer of the present invention comprises the first intron of the immediate early 1 (IE1) gene of human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6), specifically spanning nucleotide positions +1 to +262 relative to the IE1 transcription start site. This enhancer element exhibits a unique structure characterized by the presence of multiple transcription factor binding sites, including two CCAAT enhancer binding protein (C/EBP) binding sites and an OCT-1-binding site, which contribute to its potent enhancing activity. The primary function of this enhancer is to dramatically increase the transcriptional output of associated promoters, particularly the HHV-6 major immediate early (MIE) promoter, resulting in significantly higher levels of gene expression compared to the promoter alone. The enhancer demonstrates remarkable selectivity, providing the greatest enhancement in T-cell lines such as Jurkat, Molt-3, and SupT1, which aligns with the natural tropism of HHV-6 for T lymphocytes. Applications of this enhancer include gene therapy vectors targeting T cells, recombinant protein production systems requiring high-level expression, and vaccine platforms where robust antigen presentation is desired. The key advantage of this enhancer lies in its ability to convert the HHV-6 MIE promoter into a regulatory element that outperforms the widely used HCMV MIE promoter specifically in T-cell contexts while maintaining competitive activity in other cell types. Prior art limitations include the lack of strong, T-cell-preferential promoter systems and the variable performance of existing viral promoters across different cellular environments. The unexpected effect of this enhancer is its dramatic boosting of promoter activity specifically when combined with the HHV-6 MIE promoter, with the combination showing superior performance to either element alone. The nucleotide sequence of the enhancer corresponds to the first intron of HHV-6 IE1 (positions +1 to +262 relative to transcription start site), and variations may include conservative substitutions that preserve transcription factor binding sites while maintaining enhancing activity. The biological activity of the enhancer is demonstrated by its ability to increase luciferase reporter expression by several-fold in multiple cell lines, with the most pronounced effects observed in T-cell lines. The identity of the enhancer is defined by its sequence homology to the HHV-6 IE1 first intron, with functional variants maintaining at least 80% sequence identity while preserving enhancing activity. The region of enhancer effect encompasses the entire first intron sequence, with specific contributions from the C/EBP and OCT-1 binding sites. The enhancer is used by positioning it downstream of the transcription start site in expression constructs, typically immediately following the start codon of the gene of interest or within the 5' untranslated region.

### (Promoter)

A promoter with improved activity as described herein refers to the HHV-6 major immediate early (MIE) promoter that has been enhanced through the incorporation of the first intron enhancer element. The structure of this improved promoter consists of the native HHV-6 MIE promoter region (spanning approximately -983 to -13 relative to the IE1 transcription start site) combined with the first intron sequence (+1 to +262). This structural arrangement creates a composite regulatory element that leverages both upstream promoter elements and downstream enhancer sequences to achieve maximal transcriptional activity. The primary function of this improved promoter is to drive high-level, sustained expression of heterologous genes in mammalian cells, with particular strength in T-cell lineages. The promoter contains critical regulatory elements including an NF-κB binding site within the R3 repeat region (positions -381 to -552) that is essential for its activity, as demonstrated by mutational analysis showing dramatic reduction in promoter strength upon deletion of this site.

### (Gene Construct)

A gene construct as described herein refers to a recombinant nucleic acid molecule comprising the enhanced HHV-6 MIE promoter containing the first intron enhancer element operatively linked to a heterologous gene of interest. The structure of this gene construct typically includes the enhanced promoter region, the coding sequence of the foreign gene, and appropriate termination and polyadenylation signals. The primary function of this gene construct is to enable high-level, cell-type-preferential expression of the encoded gene product, particularly in T-cell lines where conventional promoters show suboptimal activity. Applications of this gene construct span gene therapy, vaccine development, recombinant protein production, and functional genomics studies requiring robust transgene expression. The key advantages of this gene construct include its superior activity in T cells compared to standard viral promoters, its demonstrated functionality in complex viral systems such as varicella-zoster virus, and its modular design that facilitates incorporation into various vector backbones. Prior art limitations addressed by this construct include the lack of strong, T-cell-specific expression systems and the variable performance of existing promoters across different cellular contexts. The unexpected effect of this gene construct is its ability to drive heterologous gene expression in recombinant varicella-zoster virus at levels sufficient for detectable protein production, demonstrating its versatility across different viral platforms. The nucleotide sequence of the gene construct includes the HHV-6 MIE promoter region (-983 to -13) fused to the first intron (+1 to +262) and the coding sequence of the gene of interest. Variations of this construct may include different heterologous genes, modified promoter regions with altered regulatory elements, or different termination signals while maintaining the core enhancer-promoter architecture. The biological activity of the gene construct is demonstrated by its ability to drive expression of reporter genes such as luciferase or DsRed2 at levels significantly exceeding those achieved with conventional promoters in T-cell lines. The identity of the gene construct is defined by the presence of the enhanced HHV-6 MIE promoter containing the first intron, with functional variants maintaining the essential regulatory architecture while accommodating different coding sequences. The region of gene construct effect encompasses the entire regulatory cassette from the upstream promoter elements through the first intron enhancer to the coding sequence. The gene construct is used by incorporating it into appropriate expression vectors for delivery to target cells, with particular utility in applications requiring strong T-cell expression. Cells targeted by this gene construct include primarily T lymphocytes and their derivatives, though the construct maintains functionality in various other mammalian cell types. The foreign gene in the gene construct can encode any protein of interest, including therapeutic proteins, antigens, reporter molecules, or regulatory factors.

### (Method for Enhancing Expression of Gene in Cell)

The method for enhancing expression of a gene in a cell according to the present invention involves introducing into the cell a nucleic acid construct comprising the enhanced HHV-6 MIE promoter containing the first intron enhancer element operatively linked to the gene of interest. This method achieves significantly higher levels of gene expression compared to conventional promoter systems, particularly in T-cell lines. The kit for enhancing gene expression includes the nucleic acid construct of the invention, along with appropriate delivery reagents such as transfection reagents, viral packaging components, or other means for introducing the construct into target cells.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments and applications of the present invention without limiting its scope.

### Example 1

The major immediate early (MIE) promoter of human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6) was identified and characterized through systematic analysis of the genomic region upstream of the immediate early 1 (IE1) gene. Materials and methods involved cloning the 971-bp region upstream of the IE1 transcription start site (positions -983 to -13 relative to the start site at position 139442 of the HHV-6 strain HST genome) into the pGL3-basic reporter vector. Reporter assays were conducted using the luciferase gene expression system in various cell lines including MRC-5 (human lung fibroblasts), MeWo (human melanoma), U373 (human astrocytoma), and T-cell lines Molt-3, SupT1, and Jurkat. Co-transfection with pRL-TK plasmid enabled normalization of transfection efficiency. Results of the reporter assay demonstrated that the HHV-6 MIE promoter exhibited weaker activity than the CMV promoter in adherent cell lines (MRC-5, U373, MeWo) but significantly stronger activity in T-cell lines (Molt-3, SupT1, Jurkat). The implications of these results indicate that the HHV-6 MIE promoter possesses inherent T-cell-preferential activity, consistent with the natural tropism of HHV-6 for T lymphocytes, making it a valuable tool for applications requiring enhanced expression in these therapeutically important cells.

### Example 2

The polyadenylation signal derived from bovine growth hormone (BGH) was examined for its compatibility with the HHV-6 expression system. Materials and methods involved constructing expression cassettes containing the enhanced HHV-6 MIE promoter (including the first intron), the DsRed2 reporter gene, and the BGH poly(A) signal sequence. These cassettes were incorporated into the varicella-zoster virus Oka strain bacterial artificial chromosome (vOka-BAC) genome using Tn7-mediated site-specific transposition. Expression of DsRed2 was confirmed through fluorescence microscopy and Western blot analysis following reconstitution of infectious virus from the modified BAC DNA. The successful expression of DsRed2 demonstrated that the BGH poly(A) signal functions effectively in the context of the HHV-6 promoter system and supports proper mRNA processing and stability in VZV-infected cells.

### Example 3

A specific deletion system was constructed to investigate the contribution of individual regulatory elements to promoter activity. Cells for the experiment included the same panel used in Example 1 (MRC-5, MeWo, U373, Molt-3, SupT1, Jurkat). An siRNA expression retrovirus vector was produced to target specific transcription factors potentially involved in regulating the HHV-6 MIE promoter. Cells were infected with the retrovirus vector to achieve stable knockdown of target genes. Gene introduction was confirmed through antibiotic selection and PCR analysis of genomic DNA. Knockdown of gene expression was verified through quantitative RT-PCR and Western blot analysis for the targeted transcription factors. This system enabled functional validation of specific regulatory elements within the promoter region and their contribution to cell-type-specific activity.

### Example 4

A nucleic acid molecule encoding a gene for expression was introduced under the control of the enhanced HHV-6 MIE promoter containing the first intron. The construct included the complete regulatory cassette (promoter plus intron enhancer) fused to the coding sequence of interest, followed by appropriate termination signals. This molecule was incorporated into various vector backbones including plasmid, viral, and BAC systems for different applications. Expression analysis confirmed high-level production of the encoded protein across multiple cell types, with particularly robust expression observed in T-cell lines, validating the utility of this system for diverse biotechnological applications.
