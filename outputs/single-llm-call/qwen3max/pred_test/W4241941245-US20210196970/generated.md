# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to systems and methods for treating cancer with cold atmospheric plasma. More specifically, the invention provides a novel therapeutic approach for the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma by combining a modified FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy regimen with cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) therapy, either as an adjuvant during surgical resection or as a standalone multimodal intervention. The invention encompasses both the method of administration and the specialized medical device configured to deliver CAP in a controlled, reproducible manner at temperatures compatible with human tissue viability.

### Brief Description of the Related Art

Cholangiocarcinoma is a rare and highly aggressive malignancy that originates from the epithelial cells of the intrahepatic or extrahepatic biliary tract. Due to its insidious onset and nonspecific clinical presentation, cholangiocarcinoma is frequently diagnosed at advanced stages when curative interventions are no longer feasible. Consequently, the prognosis remains poor, with a five-year survival rate consistently reported below twenty percent. Surgical resection or liver transplantation represents the only potentially curative options; however, even in early-stage disease, recurrence rates following resection are alarmingly high, often exceeding fifty percent within two years. This high recurrence underscores the limitations of current surgical approaches and highlights the urgent need for effective adjuvant therapies capable of eradicating microscopic residual disease.

Chemoresistance further complicates the management of cholangiocarcinoma, rendering conventional systemic therapies largely ineffective in many patients. Historically, gemcitabine and fluorouracil have served as the backbone of palliative chemotherapy, either as monotherapies or in combination regimens. While these agents offer modest survival benefits, their efficacy is constrained by intrinsic and acquired resistance mechanisms inherent to cholangiocarcinoma biology. In recent years, the FOLFIRINOX protocol—a four-drug combination comprising fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin—has emerged as a more potent alternative, initially validated in metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma where it demonstrated superior overall survival compared to gemcitabine alone. Despite its promise, the adoption of FOLFIRINOX in cholangiocarcinoma has been limited by significant toxicity, including high rates of thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and febrile neutropenia, which often necessitate dose reductions or treatment discontinuation.

To mitigate these toxicities, numerous clinical studies have explored modified dosing schedules, such as FOLFOX-4, FOLFOX-5, and FOLFOX-7, which adjust the concentrations and infusion durations of individual components while preserving synergistic anti-tumor activity. These modifications aim to achieve a balance between efficacy and tolerability, particularly in patient populations with compromised performance status. A phase II–III clinical trial evaluating FOLFIRINOX in biliary tract cancers, including cholangiocarcinoma, is currently underway to establish its role in this setting and to refine patient-tailored dosing strategies.

Concurrently, cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) has emerged as a novel modality in oncology with demonstrated anti-cancer effects across a spectrum of solid tumors. CAP is a partially ionized gas generated at or near room temperature and atmospheric pressure, composed of reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, charged particles, photons, and electric fields. Preclinical studies have established that CAP selectively induces apoptosis, DNA damage, and cell cycle arrest in malignant cells while sparing adjacent normal tissues, particularly when administered at low doses. This selectivity is attributed to differences in redox homeostasis between cancerous and non-cancerous cells, with tumor cells exhibiting heightened susceptibility to oxidative stress. Notably, indirect CAP treatment—where plasma is applied to a liquid medium that subsequently contacts tumor cells—has shown efficacy against cholangiocarcinoma cell lines, preferentially killing malignant biliary epithelial cells over normal hepatocytes.

Moreover, emerging evidence suggests that CAP can act synergistically with conventional anti-neoplastic agents. Studies in melanoma, breast cancer, pancreatic cancer, and glioblastoma models have demonstrated enhanced cytotoxicity when CAP is combined with chemotherapeutics such as oxaliplatin, gemcitabine, or temozolomide. This synergy allows for reduced drug dosages while maintaining or even improving therapeutic outcomes, thereby potentially alleviating systemic toxicity. The Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS), paired with a dedicated plasma scalpel, represents a clinically translatable platform currently under evaluation in a Phase I FDA Investigational Device Exemption trial. This system delivers CAP at temperatures between 26°C and 31°C, ensuring thermal safety during intraoperative application to surgical margins immediately following tumor resection. Given the unmet need in cholangiocarcinoma therapy and the complementary mechanisms of action between FOLFIRINOX and CAP, there exists a compelling rationale for their combined use—a strategy heretofore unexplored in the scientific literature.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

Cholangiocarcinoma presents formidable treatment challenges due to late diagnosis, high recurrence after surgery, intrinsic chemoresistance, and the significant toxicity associated with aggressive chemotherapy regimens such as FOLFIRINOX. While FOLFIRINOX offers improved efficacy over older standards, its clinical utility is often limited by adverse effects that preclude its use in frail or elderly patients. Cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) has recently gained attention as a selective anti-cancer agent capable of inducing tumor cell death without damaging surrounding healthy tissue, particularly when applied at controlled, low temperatures.

The present invention introduces a synergistic therapeutic strategy that combines a modified, low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen with CAP treatment to enhance anti-tumor efficacy while reducing systemic chemotherapeutic burden. Specifically, the invention demonstrates for the first time that the concurrent or sequential application of CAP and FOLFIRINOX results in significantly greater reduction in cholangiocarcinoma cell viability than either modality alone. This synergy enables the use of submaximal FOLFIRINOX doses—thereby minimizing toxicity—while achieving equivalent or superior tumor control through the adjunctive action of CAP.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for treating cholangiocarcinoma in a subject comprising administering a therapeutically effective amount of a FOLFIRINOX regimen followed by or co-administered with cold atmospheric plasma applied directly to the tumor site or surgical margins. The FOLFIRINOX regimen may be administered systemically or locally, and the CAP is delivered via a medical device configured to produce plasma at temperatures not exceeding 35°C, ensuring tissue compatibility.

Preferred embodiments of the invention include: (1) a perioperative method wherein CAP is applied to the resection cavity immediately after surgical removal of a cholangiocarcinoma tumor, with the patient having received a low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen preoperatively; (2) a neoadjuvant approach involving FOLFIRINOX priming followed by CAP ablation of unresectable lesions; and (3) a device-enabled delivery system that integrates real-time monitoring and precise control of plasma parameters to ensure consistent therapeutic output. The invention thus offers a paradigm shift in cholangiocarcinoma management by leveraging the complementary mechanisms of chemotherapy and physical plasma to overcome resistance and improve clinical outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

Cholangiocarcinomas are notoriously difficult to treat due to their anatomical location, aggressive biology, and resistance to conventional therapies. Cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) represents a promising physical modality that can be integrated into existing treatment algorithms to enhance efficacy. The FOLFIRINOX regimen, though potent, is often poorly tolerated; however, when used at reduced doses in combination with CAP, it can achieve robust anti-tumor effects with diminished toxicity. The present invention outlines a comprehensive method for treating cholangiocarcinoma using this synergistic combination.

FOLFIRINOX is an effective combination cancer drug treatment consisting of four agents: oxaliplatin, irinotecan, leucovorin, and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU). In the context of this invention, a low-dosage FOLFIRINOX regimen is employed, defined herein as a concentration of 5-FU ranging from 6.7 µM to 13.5 µM, corresponding to proportional reductions in the other three components based on standard clinical ratios. This dosage is selected to maintain biological activity while avoiding severe hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicities.

The method for treating cancer with a combination of FOLFIRINOX and CAP involves administering the FOLFIRINOX regimen to the patient—either systemically via intravenous infusion or regionally via hepatic arterial perfusion—followed within 24 to 72 hours by localized CAP application. In cases of surgical resection, the cancerous tumor is first removed using standard oncologic techniques. Immediately thereafter, CAP is applied directly to the surgical margins and the tumor bed using a specialized plasma applicator to eliminate residual microscopic disease and reduce the risk of local recurrence.

A preferred embodiment of the invention utilizes a CAP-enabled generator specifically designed for intraoperative oncology applications. This generator includes a housing with a removable cover for maintenance access, a front panel equipped with a touchscreen display for user interface, and multiple connectors for power, gas supply, and data output. Internally, the generator houses a power supply, a central processing unit (CPU), and memory modules that store operational protocols and treatment logs. A gas control module regulates the flow of a carrier gas—typically helium or argon—at a constant rate of 3 liters per minute, while a power module modulates electrical energy to sustain plasma generation at a stable output of 28.7 watts (corresponding to power setting 120p).

The CAP applicator, which connects to the generator via flexible conduits for gas and electrical transmission, is configured as a handheld scalpel-like instrument with an insulated tip that emits plasma plume at a distance of 1.5 cm from the target tissue. Alternative arrangements may include endoscopic or laparoscopic probes for minimally invasive delivery. Critically, the system is engineered to produce cold atmospheric plasma at temperatures below 35°C, typically between 26°C and 31°C, thereby preventing thermal injury to adjacent structures. The plasma exerts its anti-tumor effects through the generation of reactive species that induce oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, DNA damage, and ultimately, apoptotic or necrotic cell death in cholangiocarcinoma cells, while simultaneously sensitizing them to the cytotoxic effects of FOLFIRINOX.

### Experiments

To validate the invention, a series of in vitro experiments were conducted using the human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma cell line KKU-055. Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin, and seeded in 12-well plates at densities of 50,000 to 100,000 cells per well. After 24 hours of incubation at 37°C and 5% CO₂, cells were treated with varying concentrations of FOLFIRINOX, prepared by diluting clinical-grade drugs in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and then in culture media to achieve final micromolar concentrations corresponding to 5-FU levels of 0.8 to 53.8 µM.

Cold plasma treatment was delivered using the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System paired with the Canady Helios Cold Plasma Scalpel, operating at a helium flow rate of 3 L/min and a power setting of 120p (28.7 W). Treatment durations ranged from 1 to 7 minutes, with the plasma tip held 1.5 cm above the culture medium surface. FOLFIRINOX pretreatment lasted 24 hours, after which CAP was applied, and cells were returned to the incubator for up to 72 hours post-treatment.

Cell viability was assessed 48 hours after CAP exposure using the MTT assay, wherein formazan crystals formed by metabolically active cells were quantified spectrophotometrically at 570 nm. Confocal microscopy was employed to evaluate proliferation via Ki-67 immunofluorescence staining: cells grown on fibronectin/collagen-coated coverslips were fixed, stained with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated anti-Ki-67 antibody, counterstained with DAPI, and imaged using a Zeiss LSM 510 confocal microscope. Cell cycle progression was monitored in real time using the IncuCyte Live-Cell Analysis System, utilizing a stable KKU-055 line transduced with a lentiviral reporter that fluoresces red in G1 phase and green in S/G2/M phases.

Statistical analysis was performed using Microsoft Excel 2019. Data are presented as mean ± standard error of the mean. Student’s unpaired t-tests and two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni-corrected post hoc Fisher exact tests were used to determine significance, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. The experimental design included at least three independent cohorts with duplicate intra-experimental replicates (n ≥ 6 per condition).

Results confirmed that FOLFIRINOX alone reduced KKU-055 viability in a dose-dependent manner, with significant effects observed at 5-FU concentrations ≥3.4 µM. CAP treatment also induced dose-dependent cytotoxicity, with 7 minutes of exposure reducing viability to 3%. Most importantly, combination treatment—particularly 6.7 µM 5-FU FOLFIRINOX with 5 minutes of CAP—achieved 91% cell death, significantly exceeding the effects of either monotherapy. This combination also markedly decreased Ki-67-positive cells and induced profound cell cycle arrest, as evidenced by IncuCyte imaging showing accumulation in G1 and reduction in mitotic populations.

### Results

The FOLFIRINOX regimen significantly reduced cholangiocarcinoma cell viability in a concentration-dependent fashion. At the highest tested dose (53.8 µM 5-FU), viability dropped to 19% ± 1.9%, whereas lower doses showed graded responses. Cold atmospheric plasma similarly exhibited dose dependence, with 1-minute exposure reducing viability to 78% and 7-minute exposure to 3%. When FOLFIRINOX and CAP were combined, synergistic effects were evident: the 6.7 µM 5-FU dose with 5-minute CAP yielded 91% cell death, far exceeding additive predictions.

Two-way ANOVA confirmed significant main effects for both FOLFIRINOX dose (p < 0.001) and CAP duration (p < 0.001), as well as a significant interaction term (p < 0.01), indicating true synergy. Post hoc testing revealed that combinations involving 6.7 µM 5-FU and ≥1 minute CAP were significantly more effective than either treatment alone (p < 0.001). Ki-67/DAPI co-staining demonstrated a marked reduction in proliferating cells at 6 hours post-combination treatment, with total cell counts decreasing by over 50% compared to controls.

IncuCyte live-cell imaging revealed that combination treatment halted cell confluence progression and induced morphological changes consistent with apoptosis, including cellular shrinkage and debris formation. Cell cycle analysis showed that while untreated cells progressed normally through G1, S, and M phases, combination-treated cells accumulated in G1 and failed to enter S phase, indicative of G1/S arrest. At 5-minute CAP doses, rapid cell death occurred within 6 hours, precluding long-term cycle analysis but confirming acute cytotoxicity. Statistical validation across all assays consistently supported the superiority of the combination approach.

### Discussion

The poor prognosis of cholangiocarcinoma necessitates innovative therapeutic strategies. FOLFIRINOX, though promising, is limited by toxicity; CAP offers a physical modality with tumor selectivity and minimal systemic impact. Phase I and II trials of FOLFIRINOX in biliary cancers suggest clinical activity but underscore the need for dose optimization. CAP has demonstrated safety in early human studies, including a cohort of oral cancer patients, and selectivity in CCA xenografts.

Prior studies combining CAP with gemcitabine or oxaliplatin support the concept of chemo-plasma synergy. The present work extends this to FOLFIRINOX—the most potent modern regimen for gastrointestinal cancers—and demonstrates, for the first time, synergistic cytotoxicity in cholangiocarcinoma. The mechanism likely involves CAP-induced oxidative stress overwhelming DNA repair pathways already taxed by FOLFIRINOX-mediated damage, leading to catastrophic cell failure. Importantly, synergy was achieved at low FOLFIRINOX doses, suggesting clinical feasibility for patients unable to tolerate full regimens.

While limited to in vitro models, these findings provide a strong rationale for clinical translation, particularly in the adjuvant surgical setting where CAP can be applied directly to margins. Future work should explore in vivo models, optimal sequencing, and molecular pathways involved.

### Conclusion

The combination of cold atmospheric plasma and a low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen demonstrates potent synergistic anti-tumor activity against cholangiocarcinoma cells in vitro. This approach significantly enhances cell death, reduces proliferation, and induces cell cycle arrest beyond what is achievable with either modality alone. CAP thus holds promise as an adjuvant therapy that could allow dose de-escalation of FOLFIRINOX, expanding treatment eligibility and improving quality of life. Future studies should focus on in vivo validation, mechanistic elucidation, and clinical trial design to translate this innovation into patient care.
