# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to optoelectronic integrated devices, and more particularly to large-scale silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulator arrays fabricated on a heterogeneously integrated platform. The invention provides a novel architecture for high-performance, ultra-compact photonic integrated circuits (PICs) that combine the superior light-guiding properties of silicon with the strong electro-optic response of thin-film lithium niobate. These devices are particularly suited for applications requiring high modulation bandwidth, low drive voltage, and dense integration, such as microwave photonics, optical neural networks, and high-speed optical communication systems.

## BACKGROUND ART

Electro-optic modulators are essential components in modern photonic systems, enabling the conversion of electrical signals into optical signals through the modulation of light intensity, phase, or polarization. Traditional approaches to fabricating such modulators have relied heavily on doped silicon waveguides incorporating p-n or p-i-n junctions to induce changes in the refractive index via the plasma dispersion effect. However, these silicon-based modulators suffer from inherent limitations, including low modulation efficiency, limited bandwidth due to carrier dynamics, high optical loss, and nonlinear behavior at high signal powers. These drawbacks significantly constrain their utility in next-generation photonic systems that demand both high speed and high fidelity.

Lithium niobate (LN) has long been recognized as a superior material for electro-optic modulation due to its strong linear Pockels effect, which enables high-speed, low-loss, and highly efficient modulation without the need for charge carriers. Nevertheless, conventional bulk lithium niobate modulators exhibit poor optical mode confinement, resulting in large device footprints and low interaction efficiency between the optical mode and the applied electric field. This makes them unsuitable for large-scale photonic integration where compactness and high component density are critical.

To overcome these limitations, recent efforts have focused on thin-film lithium niobate platforms, which offer enhanced optical confinement and improved electro-optic performance. One prominent approach involves heterointegration of silicon and lithium niobate, either by bonding pre-patterned LN chips onto silicon-on-insulator (SOI) wafers (LN-SOI) or by fabricating silicon waveguides directly on top of a thin-film LN wafer (SOI-LN). While these methods have demonstrated promising results—such as modulation bandwidths exceeding 100 GHz and half-wave voltage-length products (VπL) below 7 V·cm—they remain hampered by significant challenges. In particular, the bonding of discrete LN pieces introduces process complexity, alignment difficulties, and scalability issues, thereby impeding wafer-scale manufacturing and large-scale integration.

Moreover, existing SOI-LN configurations often utilize silicon nitride or silicon waveguides with relatively large bend radii (e.g., 300 μm), which severely limits the integration density of photonic circuits. The inability to achieve tight bends and close waveguide spacing without excessive crosstalk or loss undermines the potential for complex, multi-component PICs. Consequently, there remains a critical need for a scalable, wafer-level integration technology that leverages the complementary strengths of silicon and lithium niobate while enabling ultra-compact routing, high-efficiency modulation, and large-bandwidth operation within a single, manufacturable platform.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The object of the present invention is to provide a large-scale silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulator array that overcomes the limitations of prior art by enabling ultra-compact photonic integration with high modulation efficiency and bandwidth exceeding 350 GHz. The invention achieves this through a novel heterogeneously integrated architecture that strategically differentiates the waveguide dimensions for light routing and light modulation, thereby optimizing each function independently while maintaining compatibility with standardized silicon fabrication processes.

The invention discloses a large-scale silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulator array comprising a silicon crystal substrate layer, a silicon dioxide film layer formed thereon, one or more silicon waveguide layers patterned atop the silicon dioxide film, an adhesive layer, a lithium niobate film layer bonded to the silicon waveguide layers via the adhesive layer, a direct-current bias electrode layer, and a radio-frequency electrode layer configured as a coplanar waveguide. The array integrates multiple electro-optic modulators, optical splitters, and optical couplers monolithically on a single wafer.

The structure of the array is designed such that light-routing waveguides feature a silicon cross-section of 600 nm in width and 220 nm in height, enabling a small bend radius of 10 μm and edge-to-edge waveguide separation as low as 0.7 μm with negligible crosstalk. In contrast, light-modulating waveguides employ a reduced silicon height of 90 nm and width of 480 nm to maximize optical mode confinement within the lithium niobate film, thereby enhancing the electro-optic interaction. Mode transition between these two waveguide types is achieved through a bilevel taper structure that ensures high coupling efficiency (>98%) with minimal loss.

The silicon crystal substrate layer provides mechanical support and thermal stability. The silicon dioxide film layer acts as a buried oxide that optically isolates the silicon waveguides from the substrate. The silicon waveguide layers serve dual purposes: as low-loss, high-confinement paths for light routing and as evanescent couplers to the lithium niobate film for modulation. The adhesive layer, formed during wafer bonding, ensures robust mechanical and optical contact between the silicon and lithium niobate layers without requiring etching of the lithium niobate.

The lithium niobate film layer, preferably 700 nm thick and oriented along the x-cut crystal axis, provides the active electro-optic medium wherein the Pockels effect induces refractive index changes under an applied electric field. The direct-current bias electrode layer enables static phase control for bias point stabilization, while the radio-frequency electrode layer, arranged in a ground-signal-ground (GSG) coplanar waveguide configuration with a 4.5 μm gap and 400 nm gold thickness, supports traveling-wave modulation with velocity matching to the optical group index.

Each individual modulator in the array operates as a Mach-Zehnder interferometer, incorporating multimode interference (MMI) couplers for splitting and recombining optical signals. The fabrication process leverages standard silicon processing—including thermal oxidation, polycrystalline silicon deposition, photolithography, reactive ion etching, ion implantation for PN junctions, and metal deposition—followed by wafer-level bonding of the lithium niobate film. This approach eliminates the need for post-bonding LN etching or piecewise assembly, thereby enabling scalable, high-yield manufacturing.

The working principle of each modulator relies on the push-pull application of a radio-frequency signal across the two arms of the Mach-Zehnder interferometer, inducing a differential phase shift via the Pockels effect in the lithium niobate. The resulting interference at the output converts phase modulation into intensity modulation. The design achieves a half-wave voltage-length product (VπL) of 1.76 V·cm and a modulation bandwidth exceeding 350 GHz for a 5 mm interaction length, representing a significant improvement over existing technologies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The integration method of the silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulator array begins with a silicon crystal substrate layer, which is thermally oxidized to form a silicon dioxide film layer of approximately 2–3 μm thickness. This buried oxide layer serves as an optical cladding and electrical insulator. Polycrystalline silicon is then deposited and patterned using deep ultraviolet lithography and reactive ion etching to form the silicon waveguide layers. Within these layers, optical splitters and optical couplers—specifically multimode interference (MMI) structures with a width of 2.7 μm and length of 6.6 μm—are defined to enable power division and recombination with insertion loss below 0.1 dB.

Ion implantation is performed to create PN junctions across selected segments of the silicon waveguides, which function as direct-current bias elements for fine-tuning the operating point of the modulator. Metal connection lines, typically composed of aluminum or copper, are subsequently deposited and patterned above these PN junctions to provide electrical access.

Separately, a lithium niobate wafer is prepared, preferably with a 700 nm thick x-cut thin-film layer. Periodic ridge structures are not required; instead, the lithium niobate film remains planar. The wafer is then aligned such that the regions intended for modulation coincide precisely with the optical couplers in the silicon waveguide layers. An adhesive layer—comprising a thin polymer or oxide-based bonding medium—is applied, and the lithium niobate film is bonded to the silicon waveguide layers using wafer-level fusion or adhesive bonding techniques at elevated temperatures, ensuring uniform contact and minimal interfacial defects.

Following bonding, a metal layer—preferably gold due to its low microwave loss and high conductivity—is deposited across the entire surface of the lithium niobate film. Photolithography and lift-off or etching processes are then employed to remove excess metal, leaving behind precisely defined radio-frequency electrode structures. These electrodes form a coplanar waveguide with a signal electrode width of 7 μm, ground electrodes on either side, and a 4.5 μm gap between signal and ground, optimized for 50 Ω impedance matching and velocity synchronization with the optical mode.

The structure of one silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulator comprises the aforementioned layers, each serving a distinct function. The silicon crystal substrate layer provides mechanical rigidity and thermal management. The silicon dioxide film layer prevents optical leakage into the substrate and reduces parasitic capacitance. The silicon waveguide layer guides light efficiently during routing and facilitates evanescent coupling into the lithium niobate during modulation. The adhesive layer ensures optical continuity and mechanical integrity between the dissimilar materials. The lithium niobate film layer acts as the active electro-optic medium, where the applied electric field induces a refractive index change via the Pockels effect, primarily leveraging the r₃₃ coefficient due to the x-cut orientation. The direct-current bias electrode layer enables static phase adjustment, while the radio-frequency electrode layer delivers high-frequency signals with minimal loss and reflection.

The principles and processes of operation involve splitting input light via an MMI coupler into two arms, each containing a light-modulating waveguide section. A radio-frequency signal is applied differentially to the two arms through the GSG electrodes, generating opposing electric fields that produce a net phase difference. Simultaneously, a direct-current bias voltage may be applied to set the quadrature operating point. The bilevel taper transitions the optical mode from the high-confinement routing waveguide (600 nm × 220 nm Si) into the modulating waveguide (480 nm × 90 nm Si), where 83% of the mode resides in the lithium niobate, maximizing the electro-optic overlap. After modulation, the mode is transitioned back to the routing waveguide for low-loss propagation and further processing.

The wafer-level large-scale silicon waveguide layers are designed to accommodate hundreds or thousands of modulators interconnected via compact bends and dense parallel waveguides. The modulator arrangement follows a regular grid or serpentine layout to maximize space utilization. The waveguide interconnection structure employs 10 μm bend radii and 0.7 μm edge-to-edge spacing, enabled by the high effective index (n_eff = 2.6) and small mode area (0.16 μm²) of the routing waveguides. The optical splitters and couplers are optimized for low loss and high uniformity across the array. The direct-current bias electrode layer is patterned with vias and pads for individual or grouped control. The lithium niobate film layer is uniformly thick across the wafer, ensuring consistent performance. The radio-frequency electrode layer is designed with impedance-matched tapers and terminations to minimize reflections.

A key advantage of the invention is its compatibility with standardized silicon-based integration technology, including CMOS-compatible processes. This enables co-integration with electronic control circuits, high-volume manufacturing, and seamless scaling to large arrays, thereby fulfilling the demands of emerging applications in microwave photonics and optical computing.
