## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of virology and therapeutic medicine, specifically relating to the treatment and prevention of viral infections caused by severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2). More particularly, the invention provides novel methods and pharmaceutical compositions for inhibiting SARS-CoV-2 infection by targeting the interaction between the viral spike protein and liver/lymph node-specific ICAM-3 grabbing nonintegrin (L-SIGN), a C-type lectin receptor expressed on specific endothelial cell populations. The invention further encompasses diagnostic and therapeutic strategies aimed at mitigating SARS-CoV-2–induced coagulopathy, thrombosis, and associated organ dysfunction, particularly in the liver and lymphatic system, through modulation of L-SIGN–mediated viral entry and endothelial activation.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Coronaviruses are a family of enveloped, positive-sense RNA viruses known to cause a spectrum of diseases in humans and animals, ranging from mild upper respiratory tract infections to severe and potentially fatal lower respiratory illnesses. Among these, severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) emerged in late 2019 as the causative agent of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), which rapidly evolved into a global pandemic. While many infected individuals remain asymptomatic or experience mild symptoms, a significant proportion develop severe disease characterized by viral pneumonia, acute respiratory distress syndrome, and multiorgan dysfunction affecting the heart, kidneys, and liver. This systemic involvement is a major determinant of disease severity and mortality.

SARS-CoV-2 initiates host cell entry through the binding of its surface-exposed spike (S) glycoprotein to the angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) receptor, which is expressed on epithelial cells of the lung, kidney, intestine, and other tissues. However, a critical clinical feature of severe COVID-19 is a profound coagulopathy and a high incidence of thrombotic events, which are strongly associated with poor prognosis. Patients with severe disease exhibit elevated plasma levels of D-dimer, von Willebrand factor (vWF), and coagulation factor VIII (FVIII), markers indicative of endothelial cell activation and a prothrombotic state. Paradoxically, single-cell RNA sequencing studies have consistently shown minimal to no expression of ACE2 on vascular endothelial cells, raising the question of whether direct viral infection of the endothelium contributes to this life-threatening coagulopathy.

The SARS-CoV-2 spike protein is heavily glycosylated, forming a “glycan shield” that helps the virus evade immune surveillance. This glycosylation includes both complex-type and high-mannose–type N-linked glycans. Previous research on the related SARS-CoV virus identified an alternative entry receptor, L-SIGN (also known as CD209L or CLEC4M), a C-type lectin that binds to high-mannose glycans in a calcium-dependent manner. L-SIGN is known to be expressed on specific endothelial cell subsets, but its role as a functional receptor for SARS-CoV-2 and its contribution to the pathogenesis of extrapulmonary complications, particularly coagulopathy, remained unknown. In light of the unmet medical need for therapies that address the systemic and thrombotic complications of severe COVID-19, there exists a critical need for novel compositions and methods that target alternative viral entry pathways beyond ACE2, thereby preventing direct viral injury to key endothelial cell populations responsible for maintaining vascular homeostasis.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for treating a SARS-CoV-2 viral infection in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject an inhibitor of the binding between the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein and the L-SIGN receptor. This inhibitor is a key component of a pharmaceutical composition that is formulated for systemic administration and is designed to reduce or prevent SARS-CoV-2–mediated tissue damage, particularly in organs where L-SIGN is expressed. The invention is predicated on the discovery that L-SIGN serves as a functional receptor for SARS-CoV-2 on liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) and lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs), and that this interaction drives a pathological cascade leading to endothelial activation and a procoagulant state.

The pharmaceutical composition of the invention comprises a therapeutically effective amount of the L-SIGN inhibitor and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The inhibitor may be selected from the group consisting of a monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to L-SIGN, a recombinant soluble L-SIGN/Fc fusion protein, and a high-mannose–binding carbohydrate polymer such as mannan. Administration of the composition is intended to block the initial attachment of the virus to L-SIGN–expressing cells, thereby interrupting the viral life cycle at its earliest stage and preventing downstream cellular activation.

A primary objective of the invention is the reduction of SARS-CoV-2–induced tissue damage, with a specific focus on vascular deficits and organ dysfunction. The method is particularly effective for treating liver dysfunction, which manifests in severe COVID-19 patients as a consequence of LSEC infection and activation. Similarly, the method is applicable to treating lymph node dysfunction arising from infection of LECs. The timing of administration is crucial; the composition is preferably administered after a diagnosis of SARS-CoV-2 infection but before the onset of severe coagulopathy or multiorgan failure, although it may also be used as an adjunct therapy in already hospitalized patients. The treatment regimen may involve repeated administration of the pharmaceutical composition to maintain effective inhibitory concentrations in the bloodstream over a clinically relevant period.

The dosage and infusion rate are carefully calibrated to achieve a therapeutically effective amount while minimizing potential side effects. For instance, a monoclonal anti–L-SIGN antibody may be administered intravenously at a dose ranging from 1 to 10 mg/kg of body weight, infused over a period of 30 to 120 minutes. The invention thus offers a targeted therapeutic strategy to mitigate the severe systemic complications of COVID-19 by specifically interfering with a newly identified viral entry pathway that is central to the development of coagulopathy and endothelial dysfunction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The scope of the present invention encompasses methods and compositions for treating SARS-CoV-2 infection by targeting the L-SIGN receptor. As used herein, the term “treating” or “therapy” refers to an approach for obtaining beneficial or desired clinical results, including, but not limited to, alleviating symptoms, slowing disease progression, preventing complications, and promoting recovery. A “therapeutically effective amount” is that quantity of a compound or composition which is sufficient to result in the desired therapeutic effect without causing unacceptable levels of toxicity. The precise dosage and route of administration will be determined by a physician based on the subject's age, weight, general health, and the severity of the infection. The pharmaceutical composition may be administered via intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular routes, with intravenous infusion being preferred for rapid systemic distribution. The composition is formulated in a carrier solution, such as sterile saline or a buffered aqueous solution, that is compatible with parenteral administration and maintains the stability of the active ingredient.

The term “subject” as used herein refers to a human patient diagnosed with or at risk of SARS-CoV-2 infection. The phrase “pharmaceutically acceptable” denotes those carriers, diluents, excipients, and salts that are compatible with the active ingredient and suitable for use in a human. An “effective dose” is synonymous with a therapeutically effective amount and is established through conventional methods of dose-ranging clinical trials. The invention leverages the discovery that SARS-CoV-2 can utilize L-SIGN, in addition to ACE2, as a functional entry receptor. L-SIGN is a C-type lectin that binds to the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein in a manner that is strictly dependent on the presence of high-mannose–type N-glycans on the spike protein and on extracellular calcium ions (Ca2+).

Experimental evidence demonstrates that a recombinant Spike/Fc protein, comprising the receptor-binding domain of the SARS-CoV-2 spike fused to an immunoglobulin Fc domain, binds specifically to cells expressing either L-SIGN or ACE2. This binding to L-SIGN-expressing cells is abolished by chelation of Ca2+ with EGTA or by competition with mannan, confirming its Ca2+-dependent, lectin-mediated nature. Pseudotyped lentiviral particles bearing the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein (CoV2-type virus) efficiently infect cells engineered to express L-SIGN, with infection efficiency correlating directly with the level of L-SIGN expression on the cell surface. This L-SIGN–mediated infection is distinct from ACE2-mediated infection, as it is sensitive to inhibitors of mannose-binding but not to inhibitors of ACE2.

Critically, L-SIGN is endogenously expressed at high levels on liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) and lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs) in human liver and lymph node tissues, respectively, as confirmed by immunohistochemistry on biopsy and autopsy samples. In stark contrast, L-SIGN is not detected on blood endothelial cells from various human tissues, including the lung, resolving a key point of contention in the field. LSECs and LECs are known to be major physiological sources of the procoagulant proteins vWF and FVIII. Analysis of liver autopsy samples from deceased COVID-19 patients revealed the presence of SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid protein within LSECs, along with a significant upregulation of vWF and a trend toward increased FVIII expression, and a loss of the quiescence marker Lyve-1, all hallmarks of endothelial cell activation.

The invention’s core embodiments include the use of specific inhibitors to block this pathogenic interaction. These include a monoclonal antibody that binds to the extracellular domain of L-SIGN, a recombinant soluble L-SIGN/Fc fusion protein that acts as a decoy receptor, and mannan, a polysaccharide that competitively inhibits the binding of high-mannose glycans to L-SIGN. In vitro studies confirm that these agents dose-dependently inhibit both pseudotyped and authentic SARS-CoV-2 infection of L-SIGN–expressing LSECs.
