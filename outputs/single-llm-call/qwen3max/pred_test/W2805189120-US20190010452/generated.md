# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to methods for differentiating human pluripotent stem cells into hypothalamic neurons, particularly arginine vasopressin (AVP)-secreting neurons, and to the production of cellular structures comprising hypothalamic tissue, either alone or in functional integration with pituitary tissue. The invention further encompasses optimized culture conditions, including the use of specific signaling molecules and inhibitors, that enable the efficient generation of dorsal or ventral hypothalamic progenitors from human pluripotent stem cells, followed by their maturation into hormone-producing neurons capable of regulated secretion in response to physiological stimuli. This technology provides a robust platform for regenerative medicine applications targeting neuroendocrine disorders such as central diabetes insipidus, as well as for disease modeling, drug screening, and fundamental studies of human hypothalamic development.

## BACKGROUND ART

The hypothalamus is a critical region of the diencephalon that governs essential homeostatic functions including thermoregulation, osmoregulation, circadian rhythms, feeding behavior, and reproductive physiology through its neuroendocrine outputs. It synthesizes and releases key neuropeptides such as arginine vasopressin (AVP), oxytocin (OXT), corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH), thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH), and others that act directly on peripheral organs or modulate anterior pituitary hormone secretion via the hypophyseal portal system. Dysfunction of hypothalamic neurons—particularly AVP-producing magnocellular neurons in the paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei—leads to central diabetes insipidus (CDI), characterized by polyuria, polydipsia, and risk of severe dehydration. Current treatment relies on exogenous desmopressin, which carries risks of hyponatremia and fails to restore physiological feedback regulation, especially in patients with impaired osmoreceptor function.

Hormone replacement therapy, while life-sustaining, does not address the underlying neuronal loss and lacks dynamic responsiveness to physiological cues. Consequently, there is a compelling need for regenerative approaches that can replace lost hypothalamic neurons with functional, integrated cellular grafts. Human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs), including both embryonic stem cells (ESCs) and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), represent a promising cell source due to their capacity for unlimited self-renewal and differentiation into any somatic cell type. However, prior attempts to generate hypothalamic neurons from hPSCs have suffered from low efficiency, poor maturation, and lack of demonstrated hormone secretion. For instance, Merkle et al. reported AVP neuron differentiation but failed to confirm AVP release or functional responsiveness, limiting utility for therapy or disease modeling.

Earlier protocols often relied on default neural induction without precise spatial patterning, resulting in heterogeneous neural populations contaminated with telencephalic or retinal fates. The hypothalamus arises from the rostral-most neuroectoderm under the influence of opposing morphogen gradients: bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) from the dorsal ectoderm and sonic hedgehog (Shh) from the ventral midline. Recapitulating these developmental cues in vitro is essential for generating authentic hypothalamic identity. Additionally, the transition from progenitor to mature, hormone-secreting neuron requires stage-specific microenvironmental support, including appropriate extracellular matrix, oxygen tension, and neurotrophic factors—conditions not adequately addressed in previous methods. Thus, despite recognition of the hypothalamus’s therapeutic and research value, the field has lacked a reliable, high-efficiency method to produce functional hypothalamic neurons from hPSCs, hindering progress in both basic science and clinical translation.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

Existing methods for differentiating human pluripotent stem cells into hypothalamic neurons are inefficient, yielding low proportions of target cells with incomplete maturation and no demonstrated capacity for regulated hormone secretion. These limitations stem from inadequate recapitulation of embryonic hypothalamic development, particularly the failure to precisely control dorsal-ventral patterning and the absence of optimized culture conditions for terminal neuronal maturation. Furthermore, no prior art enables the construction of a cellular structure integrating both hypothalamic and pituitary tissues—a configuration essential for modeling the hypothalamic-pituitary axis or developing advanced regenerative therapies for complex neuroendocrine disorders.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention solves these problems by providing a stepwise, developmentally informed protocol that achieves high-efficiency differentiation of human pluripotent stem cells into functional hypothalamic neurons, including AVP-secreting magnocellular neurons. The method begins with the formation of uniform aggregates in a serum-free medium supplemented with knock-out serum replacement (KSR) to support initial survival and aggregation. Hypothalamic fate is then induced by the synergistic action of BMP4, which activates the bone morphogenetic protein signal transduction pathway, and a substance acting on the Shh signaling pathway (e.g., smoothened agonist, SAG), applied at precisely optimized concentrations and timing to generate RAX+ hypothalamic progenitors with over 80% efficiency. Dorsal or ventral hypothalamic identity is selectively promoted by tuning the BMP4-to-SAG ratio: lower SAG concentrations (e.g., 0.5 μM) favor PAX6+ dorsal progenitors, the origin of AVP neurons, while higher SAG (e.g., 1.0 μM) induces NKX2.1+ ventral progenitors that give rise to MCH or orexin neurons.

To enhance rostral hypothalamic specification, an Akt inhibitor is added during early patterning, improving the yield of dorsal hypothalamic precursors without compromising overall RAX induction. Following progenitor specification, aggregates are transitioned to a dissociation culture on Matrigel/poly-D-lysine/laminin-coated surfaces in a medium containing BDNF, NT-3, and LM22A-4, which supports neuronal maturation and neurite outgrowth. This two-dimensional culture is critical for the emergence of AVP+ neurons, which express mature markers such as Neurophysin II and exhibit soma morphology consistent with magnocellular neurons. Crucially, these neurons secrete AVP in a regulated manner, as demonstrated by increased hormone release upon potassium chloride-induced depolarization. The method also generates other hypothalamic neuron subtypes—including OXT, CRH, TRH, NPY, AgRP, POMC, MCH, and orexin neurons—confirming broad applicability.

Moreover, the invention enables the construction of an integrated hypothalamic-pituitary cellular structure by modulating BMP4 and SAG concentrations to simultaneously induce hypothalamic neuroepithelium and surface ectoderm (marked by E-cadherin and pan-cytokeratin), the latter giving rise to anterior pituitary lineages. Sequential culture steps involving FGF2 and serum alternatives further promote pituitary differentiation, resulting in a co-developed tissue that mimics the native hypothalamic-pituitary unit. The entire process uses chemically defined or growth-factor-free media where possible, ensuring reproducibility and clinical compatibility. In summary, the invention provides a comprehensive, tunable platform for producing functional hypothalamic neurons and integrated neuroendocrine tissues, overcoming the inefficiencies and immaturity of prior approaches.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### 1. Method for Producing Cellular Structure Including Hypothalamic Tissue

The hypothalamus is a diencephalic structure located below the thalamus, comprising numerous nuclei that integrate neural and endocrine signals to maintain homeostasis. Hypothalamic tissue, as used herein, refers to a three-dimensional cellular assembly containing neurons and supporting cells derived from hypothalamic progenitors, expressing markers such as RAX, PAX6 (dorsal), or NKX2.1 (ventral), and capable of producing hypothalamic hormones. The method for producing such tissue begins with human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs)—defined as cells capable of unlimited self-renewal and differentiation into derivatives of all three germ layers, including embryonic stem cells (ESCs) derived from blastocyst inner cell mass, embryonic germ cells (EGCs) from primordial germ cells, and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) reprogrammed from somatic cells via factors like OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, and c-MYC. Pluripotency is confirmed by expression of OCT4, NANOG, SSEA-4, and TRA-1-60, and by teratoma formation assays.

hPSCs are maintained feeder-free in serum-free medium on vitronectin- or laminin-coated surfaces, or on mitomycin C-inactivated mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) in DMEM/F12 with 20% KSR, bFGF, and supplements. For differentiation, hPSCs are dissociated to single cells using TrypLE Express with DNase I and ROCK inhibitor Y-27632 to prevent anoikis, then seeded at 10,000 cells/well in V-bottom, low-adhesion 96-well plates in growth-factor-free chemically defined medium (gfCDM) supplemented with 5% KSR, non-essential amino acids, pyruvate, and 20 μM Y-27632 to form uniform aggregates within 24 hours. This rapid aggregation (SFEBq method) ensures homogeneity, with aggregate quality assessed by size consistency and absence of necrosis.

In step (2), aggregates are cultured in suspension with daily medium changes. From day 6, BMP4 (0.5–2.0 nM) and SAG (0.1–1.0 μM) are added to induce RAX+ hypothalamic progenitors; optimal dorsal induction uses 1.0 nM BMP4 and 0.5 μM SAG, while ventral induction employs 1.5 nM BMP4 and 1.0 μM SAG. An Akt inhibitor (0.5 μM) may be included from day 6 to enhance rostral identity. Culture occurs in static conditions in oxygen-permeable vessels at 40% O₂ to prevent central necrosis, with medium based on gfCDM or DMEM/F12 supplemented with N2/B27. After 30 days, over 80% of cells express RAX, with minimal FOXG1 (telencephalic) or VSX2 (retinal) contamination.

Step (3) involves dissociating day-60 aggregates with Accumax/DNase I/Y-27632, plating on Matrigel/poly-D-lysine/laminin-coated coverslips in DFNB medium (DMEM/F12 + glucose + N2 + B27) supplemented with 10% FBS, BDNF (20 ng/mL), NT-3 (10 ng/mL), LM22A-4 (1 μM), and Y-27632 (10 μM). Cultures are maintained at 40% O₂ for 60–90 days, with half-medium changes every other day. This yields mature AVP+ neurons that secrete AVP basally and in response to KCl stimulation, alongside other hypothalamic neuron types.

For integrated hypothalamic-pituitary structures, step (i) uses 1.0 nM BMP4 and 0.5 μM SAG from day 6 to induce RAX+ hypothalamic neuroepithelium and E-cadherin+/pan-cytokeratin+ surface ectoderm. Step (ii) (days 12–30) adds FGF2 (10 ng/mL) at high O₂ to expand pituitary precursors. Step (iii) (days 30–60) continues with SAG (0.5 μM) and serum alternative (e.g., B27) to mature ACTH+ corticotrophs and other pituitary lineages adjacent to CRH+ hypothalamic neurons. The resulting structure models hypothalamic-pituitary connectivity.

Applications include transplantation therapy for CDI using autologous iPSC-derived AVP neurons, drug screening for neuroendocrine disorders, and toxicity testing. Cells may be prepared as dissociated neurons or intact aggregates, implanted into the hypothalamus or posterior pituitary, and sourced from patient-derived iPSCs to avoid immune rejection.

### EXAMPLES

Applying the Wataya et al. SFEBq method to hESCs initially failed due to poor aggregation in gfCDM alone; adding 5% KSR enabled survival but induced FOXG1+ telencephalic cells. Systematic testing revealed that combining BMP4 (1.0 nM) and SAG (0.5 μM) from day 6 yielded RAX+/VSX2− hypothalamic progenitors, with PAX6+ dorsal or NKX2.1+ ventral fates controlled by SAG concentration. Timing was critical: BMP4/SAG before day 6 caused non-neural differentiation, while delayed addition favored telencephalon.

Dorsal hypothalamic induction (1.0 nM BMP4, 0.5 μM SAG) produced OTP+/BRN2+ AVP precursors only after transfer to Transwell gas-liquid interface culture at day 60. Further maturation required dissociation onto ECM-coated surfaces, yielding AVP+ neurons by day 120. Adding Akt inhibitor (0.5 μM) from day 6 doubled AVP+ neuron numbers without affecting RAX/PAX6 induction. Ventral conditions (1.5 nM BMP4, 1.0 μM SAG) generated MCH+ neurons, whose pro-MCH expression increased with NGF/dexamethasone/LiCl stimulation.

AVP secretion was confirmed by radioimmunoassay: basal release was 2.1 pg/mL, rising to 5.8 pg/mL after KCl stimulation (n=10, p<0.01). Other neurons—OXT, CRH, TRH, NPY, AgRP, POMC, orexin—were identified by immunostaining, all TUJ1+. Integrated hypothalamic-pituitary structures showed CRH+ neurons adjacent to ACTH+ pituitary cells, with functional coupling evidenced by stimulated ACTH release. Medium optimization (e.g., B27 instead of FBS) improved viability and maturation.

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary

Integrated structures were generated by inducing surface ectoderm alongside hypothalamic neuroepithelium using 1.0 nM BMP4 and 0.5 μM SAG. Fluorescent antibody staining confirmed RAX+/PAX6+ hypothalamic regions adjacent to E-cadherin+/pan-cytokeratin+ ectoderm, which differentiated into ACTH+ pituitary cells under FGF2 and serum alternative. CRH+ hypothalamic neurons and ACTH+ pituitary cells were spatially organized, mimicking the native hypothalamic-pituitary portal system.

### 3. Conclusion

The invention establishes novel, high-efficiency conditions for differentiating hPSCs into functional hypothalamic neurons, surpassing prior art in yield, maturity, and secretory capacity. Key innovations include precise BMP4/SAG titration for dorsal/ventral patterning, Akt inhibition for rostral enhancement, and dissociation culture for terminal maturation—enabling the first demonstration of KCl-responsive AVP secretion from hPSC-derived neurons.

### 1. Appearance of Hypothalamic AVP Neuron

AVP+ neurons appeared after 120 days in dissociation culture, exhibiting large somas (~40×20 μm), Neurophysin II positivity, and absence of CRH co-expression—consistent with magnocellular identity. Over 30% of neurons were AVP+ under optimized conditions.

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary

Pituitary tissue matured alongside hypothalamus, with ACTH+ cells showing glucocorticoid-responsive regulation. Adjacency enhanced pituitary function, and the integrated structure released both AVP and ACTH in response to physiological stimuli, confirming functional coupling.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The cellular structures and methods of the invention are applicable in regenerative medicine for treating hypothalamic disorders like CDI, in pharmaceutical screening for neuroendocrine drugs, and in toxicity testing. The scope includes all embodiments described, particularly the use of Akt inhibitors, specific BMP4/SAG concentrations, and dissociation culture for AVP neuron maturation.
