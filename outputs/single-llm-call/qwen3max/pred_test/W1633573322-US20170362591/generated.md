# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to non-aggregated immunostimulatory oligonucleotides, specifically modified A/D-type CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (ODNs) that retain potent interferon-α (IFN-α)-inducing activity while exhibiting improved solubility, stability, and manufacturability. More particularly, the invention provides phosphorothioate-modified oligonucleotides comprising a core CpG-containing immunostimulatory sequence derived from A/D-type ODNs and a 3′-terminal phosphorothioated polydeoxynucleotide tail—preferably a polydeoxyadenylic acid (dAs) tail of defined length—that prevents high-order aggregate formation without compromising biological activity. These novel compositions overcome longstanding challenges associated with conventional A/D-type ODNs, which rely on guanine-rich sequences that form G-quadruplex structures and insoluble aggregates in physiological salt conditions, thereby hindering clinical development. The resulting agents are suitable for use as vaccine adjuvants, immunotherapeutics, and research tools in human and veterinary medicine.

## BACKGROUND ART

Immunostimulatory oligonucleotides containing unmethylated CpG motifs have been recognized for over two decades as potent activators of the innate immune system via Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9). These CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (CpG ODNs) mimic bacterial and viral DNA and trigger robust immune responses, including cytokine production, dendritic cell maturation, and B-cell activation. Based on structural and functional characteristics, immunostimulatory ODNs are broadly categorized into four classes: A/D-type, B/K-type, C-type, and P-type. A/D-type ODNs typically feature a phosphodiester backbone and a 3′ polyguanosine (poly-G) tail that facilitates the formation of G-quadruplex structures, leading to nanoparticle-like aggregates. These aggregates are strongly associated with high IFN-α production from plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), a hallmark of A/D-type activity. In contrast, B/K-type ODNs possess a fully phosphorothioated backbone, lack poly-G tails, and primarily stimulate B cells and interleukin-6 (IL-6) secretion with minimal IFN-α induction. C-type ODNs combine features of both A/D and B/K types, containing a phosphorothioated backbone and a palindromic CpG motif capable of forming intramolecular duplexes, thereby inducing moderate levels of both IFN-α and IL-6. More recently, P-type ODNs have been described, featuring two tandem palindromic sequences within a phosphorothioated backbone and exhibiting a cytokine profile similar to C-type but with enhanced IFN-α production.

A key distinction among these classes lies in their IFN-α induction profiles. A/D-type ODNs are characterized by strong, sustained IFN-α responses, whereas B/K-type ODNs induce negligible IFN-α but robust IL-6 and B-cell proliferation. This functional divergence translates into different in vivo applications: A/D-type ODNs excel in settings requiring Th1-biased immunity, such as antiviral or antitumor responses, while B/K-type ODNs are superior for enhancing antibody production in vaccines. Despite their therapeutic promise, A/D-type ODNs face significant pharmaceutical challenges. The poly-G tail essential for their activity drives spontaneous aggregation in saline or phosphate-buffered solutions, resulting in heterogeneous particle sizes, precipitation, and batch-to-batch variability. This aggregation complicates formulation, sterilization, storage, and parenteral administration, severely limiting clinical translation. Although strategies such as thermolabile protecting groups have been explored to temporarily mask the G-tail until after administration, none have yet advanced to human trials. Thus, there remains an unmet need for A/D-type ODNs that maintain high IFN-α-inducing capacity without the liabilities of aggregation.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Solution to Problem

The present invention solves the long-standing problem of uncontrolled aggregation in A/D-type immunostimulatory oligonucleotides by replacing or supplementing the traditional poly-G tail with a phosphorothioated polydeoxynucleotide tail, preferably composed of deoxyadenosine residues (dAs). Specifically, the invention modifies the canonical A/D-type ODN D35 by appending a 3′ phosphorothioated dAs40 tail, yielding D35-dAs40, or by using only the core 12-mer CpG-containing sequence of D35 fused to the same tail, yielding D35core-dAs40. These constructs exhibit dramatically reduced or absent high-order aggregate formation in physiologically relevant buffers such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) or saline, while preserving substantial IFN-α-inducing activity. The phosphorothioate modification in the tail confers protein-binding properties that enhance cellular uptake, compensating for the loss of G-quadruplex-mediated internalization. Critically, the invention demonstrates that the guanine hexamer sequence is not essential for TLR9 activation or IFN-α induction; rather, efficient delivery of the CpG motif to endosomal compartments suffices. The agent for delivery comprises a nucleic acid medicament wherein the oligonucleotide contains at least one phosphorothioated nucleotide, preferably in a terminal tail region. The phosphorothioated nucleotide content is optimized to prevent aggregation—typically ≥50% phosphorothioation in the tail—and the total oligonucleotide length ranges from 20 to 80 nucleotides, with a lower limit of 12 bases (the minimal CpG core) and an upper practical limit dictated by synthetic feasibility. In certain embodiments, the agent is bound to a moiety with biological activity, such as schizophyllan (SPG), forming a soluble complex (e.g., D35-SPG). The core moiety retains the immunostimulatory activity of A/D-type ODNs, namely IFN-α and IL-6 induction, and serves as the active pharmaceutical ingredient in a vehicle suitable for cell introduction, including injectable formulations. The oligonucleotide of the invention thus represents a non-aggregated, pharmaceutically tractable A/D-type immunostimulant.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The invention offers multiple advantageous effects over prior art A/D-type ODNs. First, it eliminates or drastically reduces aggregate formation in aqueous salt solutions, enabling direct dissolution of lyophilized product in sterile saline—a critical requirement for clinical use. Second, it maintains high IFN-α-inducing capacity comparable to native D35, despite lacking a functional G-hexamer, thereby preserving the desired Th1-skewing immunostimulatory profile. Third, the modified ODNs exhibit dose-proportional cytokine responses, unlike C- or P-type ODNs which show paradoxical suppression at higher doses, ensuring predictable pharmacodynamics. Fourth, in vivo studies in cynomolgus monkeys demonstrate superior or equivalent adjuvant efficacy for influenza vaccination compared to both original D35 and B/K-type K3, indicating enhanced translational potential. Fifth, the design is modular and applicable to other A/D-type sequences (e.g., A2216, A2336), broadening its utility. Sixth, the absence of uncontrolled aggregation improves manufacturing consistency, stability, and regulatory compliance under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards. Collectively, these advantages position the invention as a clinically viable platform for next-generation TLR9 agonists.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

As used herein, singular expressions include plural referents unless context clearly dictates otherwise. The term “immunostimulatory oligonucleotide” refers to a synthetic single-stranded DNA molecule containing at least one unmethylated CpG dinucleotide in a specific sequence context that activates TLR9. A “CpG oligonucleotide” is defined by the presence of a cytosine-phosphate-guanine (CpG) motif, typically flanked by two 5′ purines and two 3′ pyrimidines, though variations exist. The “CpG motif” is the minimal immunostimulatory unit recognized by TLR9. “Polydeoxyadenylic acid” (poly-dA or dAs) denotes a homopolymer of deoxyadenosine residues. CpG ODNs activate immune cells via TLR9 expressed in endosomal compartments of B cells and pDCs, functioning as vaccine adjuvants by enhancing antigen presentation and antibody responses. Four main types exist: A/D-type (phosphodiester backbone, poly-G tail, high IFN-α); B/K-type (fully phosphorothioated, no poly-G, high IL-6/B-cell activation); C-type (phosphorothioated, palindromic CpG, mixed cytokines); and P-type (phosphorothioated, dual palindromes, high IFN-α). Modified ODNs of the invention exhibit improved physical properties—namely solubility and monodispersity—while retaining immunostimulatory activity. Complexation with schizophyllan (SPG) forms triple-helix structures with dAs tails, though SPGylation of D35 derivatives reduced in vitro cytokine output. K-type (B/K-type) ODNs are fully phosphorothioated, linear, and activate B cells via DEC-205 uptake. D/A-type ODNs require G-quadruplexes for activity in native form but, per this invention, do not when equipped with phosphorothioated tails. C-type ODNs form stem-loop structures enabling dual signaling. P-type ODNs dimerize via palindromes. A “therapeutic agent” includes any composition for treating disease; a “pharmaceutical composition” comprises the ODN and a carrier. Prophylactic use is motivated by adjuvant applications. Kits include the ODN and instructions for use. Medicaments are formulated in dosage forms (e.g., injectables) via standard routes (subcutaneous, intramuscular). Compositions contain carriers, excipients (e.g., saline, buffers), and are prepared by lyophilization or solution. Salts, solvates, and prodrugs are encompassed. Chemical modifications include phosphorothioation. Delivery systems may involve lipids (e.g., DOTAP) or polymers (e.g., SPG). Administration routes include parenteral. Injection formulations may contain local anesthetics or solubilizers. Unit doses are in vials or ampoules with aseptic diluents. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts and effective amounts are tailored to patient needs. The delivery agent comprises a nucleic acid medicament where the tail unit enables uptake. Conventional techniques fail due to aggregation; thus, the invention separates A/D activity into CpG core (signaling) and tail (delivery). Structure-activity tests confirm IFN-α induction without G-hexamers. The nucleic acid prevents high-order aggregates via phosphorothioated dAs tails of 40–60 residues. Moieties with biological activity include SPG or targeting ligands. The core moiety is the D35 12-mer. The oligonucleotide has 12–80 bases, ≤20 nm hydrodynamic radius, no consecutive guanines, and ≥50% tail phosphorothioation. It induces IFN-α and IL-6, derived from D35, and is used in medicament manufacture via molecular, biochemical, or microbiological methods.

### EXAMPLES

Human PBMCs were isolated from healthy volunteers and stimulated with CpG ODNs ± DOTAP. Cytokines were measured by ELISA or multiplex assay. DLS assessed particle size; TEM visualized aggregates. SPG complexes were formed at 0.27:1 molar ratio. Cynomolgus monkeys received influenza vaccine ± adjuvants; IgG titers were measured. Statistical analysis used t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests. Poly-dAs40-tailed D35 showed immunostimulatory activity similar to D35, even without G-hexamers, proving CpG motif sufficiency. Phosphorothioate tails were essential—phosphodiester tails failed. DOTAP rescued non-tailed, G-less ODNs, confirming uptake as the limiting factor. DLS/TEM showed D35 aggregated (>50 nm–1 µm), while D35-dAs40 remained <20 nm. Dose titration revealed proportional IFN-α/IL-6 induction, unlike P-type ODN 21889. The D35core + dAs40 was sufficient for activity. Longer dAs tails (up to 60) enhanced IFN-α; dAs100 reduced it. IL-6 increased up to dAs100. ≥50% phosphorothioation was required for IFN-α; IL-6 tolerated less. A-polymer tails outperformed G-rich randomers. D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40 dissolved directly in saline; D35 did not. A2216/A2336 solubility improved with dAs40 tails. SPGylation reduced in vitro cytokines but maintained in vivo adjuvanticity. Monkey studies showed D35-dAs40, D35core-dAs40, and D35-SPG outperformed D35 and K3 in antibody responses. Multiplex assays found no cytokine correlate of adjuvanticity. The prototypes exhibited D-type dose responses, excellent solubility, and superior in vivo efficacy. Aggregation was proven non-essential for IFN-α induction. Uptake likely occurs via phosphorothioate-dependent pathways (e.g., DEC-205). Individual response variability was noted. FIGS. 13–14 showed cytokine profiles; FIG. 15 confirmed solubility of A2216/A2336 ± tails; FIG. 16 demonstrated phosphorothioate’s role in preventing aggregation. Formulation examples included lyophilized vials reconstituted in saline. The invention’s scope encompasses all such non-aggregated, phosphorothioated-tail A/D-type ODNs.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The CpG oligonucleotides of the present invention possess significant industrial applicability as vaccine adjuvants, immunotherapeutic agents for cancer or infectious diseases, and research reagents. Their enhanced solubility, stability, and reproducible manufacturing profile enable scalable production under GMP conditions, facilitating clinical development and commercialization. The ability to directly formulate in saline streamlines administration in medical settings, reducing preparation time and contamination risk. Given the broad utility of TLR9 agonists in modulating immune responses, these non-aggregated A/D-type ODNs represent a versatile platform technology with immediate applicability across pharmaceutical, biotechnology, and diagnostic industries.
