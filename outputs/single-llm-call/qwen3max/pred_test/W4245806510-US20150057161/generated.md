# DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to methods and systems for the high-resolution characterization of human macrophage polarization states through RNA sequencing (RNA-seq)-based transcriptome analysis. Macrophages are highly plastic immune cells that adopt distinct functional phenotypes in response to environmental cues, commonly classified as classically activated M1-like or alternatively activated M2-like states. Accurate discrimination between these states is critical for understanding immune responses in health and disease, yet existing methodologies relying on limited surface markers or microarray-based gene expression profiling suffer from insufficient resolution, dynamic range, and biological insight. The invention provides a comprehensive, RNA-seq-driven framework that enables the identification of novel polarization-specific markers—including cell surface proteins, alternative splice variants, differential promoter usage, and non-canonical transcripts—with unprecedented sensitivity and specificity. This approach facilitates improved diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic strategies in conditions involving dysregulated macrophage activity, such as cancer, autoimmune disorders, chronic inflammation, and transplant rejection.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Macrophages are phagocytic cells of the innate immune system that reside in virtually all tissues and play essential roles in maintaining homeostasis, clearing pathogens, and orchestrating inflammatory and reparative responses. Their functional versatility stems from their ability to undergo phenotypic reprogramming—termed polarization—in response to specific microenvironmental signals. This plasticity allows macrophages to adopt context-dependent effector functions tailored to the immunological challenge at hand.

M1-like macrophages represent the classically activated phenotype, typically induced by interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), lipopolysaccharide (LPS), or tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α). These cells are potent mediators of pro-inflammatory responses, characterized by high secretion of interleukin-12 (IL-12), IL-23, IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α, coupled with low production of anti-inflammatory IL-10. Phenotypically, M1-like macrophages express surface markers such as CD86, CD64 (FcγRI), and CD16, and are instrumental in host defense against intracellular pathogens and tumor surveillance.

In contrast, M2-like macrophages denote a heterogeneous group of alternatively activated cells, commonly induced by interleukin-4 (IL-4) or IL-13. These macrophages exhibit an anti-inflammatory, pro-repair, and immunoregulatory profile, marked by low IL-12/IL-23 and high expression of molecules like CD23 (FcεRII), CCL17, CCL18, and CCL22. M2-like macrophages contribute to tissue remodeling, wound healing, parasite containment, and suppression of adaptive immunity, but can also promote tumor progression and fibrosis in pathological contexts.

Transcriptional reprogramming underpins these functional shifts, involving coordinated changes in gene expression networks governed by specific transcription factors and epigenetic modifications. Historically, microarray-based gene expression profiling has been employed to map these transcriptional landscapes, yielding valuable insights into macrophage biology. However, such approaches are constrained by predefined probe sets, limited dynamic range, cross-hybridization artifacts, and inability to detect novel transcripts or isoforms.

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) has emerged as a transformative technology that overcomes many limitations of microarrays. By enabling whole-transcriptome interrogation at single-nucleotide resolution, RNA-seq provides quantitative data on known and novel transcripts, alternative splicing events, fusion genes, differential promoter usage, and transcription start sites. Despite its advantages, the systematic application of RNA-seq to human macrophage polarization has been limited, and the full potential of this technology for uncovering biologically and clinically relevant markers remains underexploited.

Prior art methods for macrophage classification rely heavily on a small set of surface markers or low-resolution transcriptomic data, which fail to capture the complexity and continuum of macrophage states. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for a high-fidelity, comprehensive method to define macrophage polarization states with molecular precision, enabling accurate identification, isolation, and functional modulation of specific macrophage subsets in research and clinical settings.

## SHORT DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

There is a pressing need for high-resolution transcriptome data to fully elucidate the molecular mechanisms governing human macrophage polarization and to identify robust, functionally relevant biomarkers that distinguish M1-like from M2-like states. Conventional approaches based on microarray analysis or limited protein markers lack the sensitivity, dynamic range, and isoform-level resolution required for precise macrophage characterization.

The present invention addresses this need by applying RNA sequencing to in vitro polarized human macrophages, thereby generating a comprehensive, high-fidelity transcriptomic map of M1-like and M2-like states. This approach reveals not only differentially expressed genes with greater accuracy and breadth than microarrays but also uncovers novel regulatory layers, including alternative splicing, differential promoter usage, and non-annotated transcripts.

New insights into human macrophage biology are provided, including the identification of previously unrecognized gene families—such as apolipoprotein L (APOL) and leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptors (LILRs)—that are specifically enriched in M1-like macrophages. Additionally, the invention demonstrates that RNA-seq enables superior network-based analysis, revealing central regulatory hubs and functional modules missed by microarray data.

The invention introduces a method for identifying M1-like and M2-like macrophages based on RNA-seq-derived transcriptional signatures. This method comprises isolating human monocytes, differentiating them into macrophages using granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), polarizing them with IFN-γ (for M1-like) or IL-4 (for M2-like), and performing RNA-seq to generate genome-wide expression profiles. Differential expression analysis, combined with exon-level resolution and isoform quantification, allows for the precise classification of macrophage states.

Another preferred embodiment of the method involves the identification and validation of novel cell surface markers differentially expressed between M1-like and M2-like macrophages. These include CD120b, SLAMF7, TLR2 (enriched in M1-like), and CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226 (enriched in M2-like). These markers can be used in flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, or diagnostic assays to distinguish macrophage subsets in clinical samples.

In summary, the invention provides a robust, RNA-seq-based platform for high-resolution characterization of human macrophage polarization, enabling the discovery of novel biomarkers, regulatory mechanisms, and therapeutic targets with applications in immunology, oncology, autoimmunity, and regenerative medicine.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Macrophages are central players in innate immunity and tissue homeostasis, exhibiting remarkable functional plasticity through polarization into distinct phenotypic states. Understanding these states at the molecular level is essential for manipulating immune responses in disease. To achieve this, high-resolution transcriptomic analysis is indispensable.

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) was motivated as the optimal technology due to its ability to provide unbiased, quantitative, and isoform-resolved transcriptome data. In the present invention, RNA-seq was applied to human macrophages polarized under standardized M1-like (IFN-γ) and M2-like (IL-4) conditions, revealing extensive transcriptional reprogramming.

RNA-seq results demonstrated significantly higher information content compared to microarray data, with increased detection of expressed genes, greater dynamic range of fold-changes (spanning six orders of magnitude versus four in microarrays), and identification of 900 additional differentially expressed genes not captured by arrays. This enhanced resolution enabled the discovery of novel marker genes with high specificity for each polarization state.

Among these, several novel marker genes were identified, including members of the APOL and LILR families in M1-like macrophages, validated at the RNA and protein levels. These genes were undetectable by microarray due to probe design limitations or low expression below array detection thresholds.

Differential promoter usage was observed in multiple genes, indicating that polarization signals can redirect transcription initiation. Similarly, alternative transcription start sites (TSS) were identified, suggesting context-dependent regulation of 5′ untranslated regions and potential impacts on mRNA stability and translation.

Differential splicing variants were a key finding. For example, the gene PDLIM7, encoding a PDZ-LIM domain scaffold protein, exhibited isoform switching: variant 1 was enriched in M1-like macrophages, while variant 2 predominated in M2-like cells. This was confirmed by isoform-specific qPCR and suggests a mechanism linking cytoskeletal organization and transcriptional regulation to macrophage function.

PDLIM7 exemplifies how RNA-seq reveals functional nuances invisible to microarrays. Its role in modulating p53 activity and Tbx5 signaling implies that isoform-specific expression may fine-tune macrophage responses in inflammation or cancer.

RNA-seq advantages include detection of intergenic and antisense transcripts, precise quantification of low-abundance mRNAs, and elimination of cross-hybridization noise. In contrast, microarray limitations include reliance on pre-designed probes, saturation at high expression levels, high background at low levels, and inability to resolve isoforms or novel exons.

Cell surface marker identification was a major objective. By intersecting RNA-seq data with the human surfaceome, 475 differentially expressed surface molecules were found. Method of aspect (1) involves designing primers for qPCR validation of these markers. Primer design follows standard criteria: amplicon size 80–150 bp, Tm ~60°C, spanning exon-exon junctions to avoid genomic DNA amplification.

Method of aspect (2) entails probe design for in situ hybridization or Nanostring assays. Probes are 50-mer oligonucleotides targeting unique exonic sequences, avoiding SNPs and repetitive elements, with melting temperatures optimized for hybridization stringency.

Method of aspect (3) describes a hybridization array comprising probes for the newly identified markers (e.g., CD120b, SLAMF7, CD93), enabling multiplexed detection in clinical samples. The array is fabricated on a glass slide with covalently attached oligonucleotides and includes controls for housekeeping genes and spike-in RNAs.

Method of aspect (4) involves binding molecules such as monoclonal antibodies or recombinant ligands specific to the novel surface markers. These are used in flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, or ELISA formats to detect and isolate macrophage subsets.

Method of aspect (5) focuses on functional validation of markers. CD120b (TNFR2), a TNF receptor family member, was found enriched in M1-like macrophages. Unlike TNFR1, CD120b signals primarily via non-canonical NF-κB, potentially sustaining inflammatory loops in chronic disease.

SLAMF7, originally described in NK cells, was robustly upregulated in M1-like macrophages. Its association with transplant rejection suggests utility as a biomarker in graft monitoring.

CD1a and CD1b, typically linked to dendritic cell antigen presentation, were unexpectedly elevated in M2-like macrophages, implying a role in lipid antigen presentation in tissue repair or tumor microenvironments.

CD93, involved in adhesion and phagocytosis, was M2-enriched. Its soluble form promotes monocyte-to-macrophage differentiation, suggesting autocrine regulation in alternative activation.

CD226, known for T-cell cytotoxicity, was identified as an M2 marker in macrophages, potentially regulating endothelial transmigration in wound healing or metastasis.

RNA-seq benefits extend beyond marker discovery to systems-level insights. Network analysis using a priori knowledge revealed that RNA-seq uncovers 73% of M1 and 54% of M2 network components missed by arrays, including critical hubs like MYC and TP53 in M2 states.

In conclusion, RNA-seq offers unparalleled advantages for macrophage biology: comprehensive transcript detection, isoform resolution, dynamic quantification, and discovery of novel regulatory mechanisms. This positions RNA-seq as the gold standard for defining macrophage polarization states in research and diagnostics.

### EXAMPLES

Standard abbreviations include PBMC (peripheral blood mononuclear cells), M-CSF (macrophage colony-stimulating factor), GM-CSF (granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor), LPS (lipopolysaccharide), FACS (fluorescence-activated cell sorting), qPCR (quantitative polymerase chain reaction), PCA (principal component analysis), RPKM (reads per kilobase per million), and TSS (transcription start site).

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells were isolated from healthy donor buffy coats using density gradient centrifugation with Pancoll. CD14+ monocytes were purified from PBMCs using magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS) with anti-CD14 beads, achieving >95% purity.

Immature macrophages were generated by culturing CD14+ monocytes in RPMI1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum and either GM-CSF (500 U/ml) or M-CSF (100 U/ml) for three days. Medium was refreshed on day three, and polarization was induced for an additional three days using IFN-γ (200 U/ml) for M1-like or IL-4 (1,000 U/ml) for M2-like states.

Cells were stained with fluorochrome-conjugated monoclonal antibodies against surface markers after Fc receptor blockade. Antibodies included anti-CD1b, CD23, CD93, CD226, CD120b, SLAMF7, CD1a, CD86, CD64, and isotype controls. Flow cytometry was performed on an LSR II instrument, and data were analyzed using FlowJo software.

Total RNA was extracted using TRIZOL reagent, and quality was assessed by Nanodrop spectrophotometry (A260/A280 ratio) and agarose gel electrophoresis for 18S/28S ribosomal RNA integrity.

Quantitative PCR was conducted using GAPDH as a reference gene, with cDNA synthesized from 500 ng RNA. TaqMan or SYBR Green chemistry was employed, and relative expression was calculated by the 2−ΔΔCT method.

Microarray-based transcriptional profiling utilized Illumina Human HT-12 V3 BeadChips. Biotin-labeled cRNA was hybridized, scanned, and processed using BeadStudio and R/Bioconductor packages, including quantile normalization and filtering by coefficient of variation.

Microarray data analysis involved principal component analysis and hierarchical clustering to assess sample relationships. Differentially expressed genes were defined by fold-change ≥2.0 and adjusted p-value <0.05.

RNA-seq libraries were prepared using the Illumina TruSeq kit, with poly-A selection, fragmentation, cDNA synthesis, adapter ligation, and size selection. Paired-end 100 bp sequencing was performed on a HiScanSQ, yielding 15–20 million reads per sample.

RNA-seq data were aligned to hg19 using TopHat/Bowtie, and transcript abundance was quantified as RPKM using Cufflinks. Differential expression was assessed by Cuffdiff, with thresholds of RPKM ≥0.3, fold-change ≥1.5, and p<0.05.

A priori information-based network analysis was performed using EGAN software, integrating literature-curated interactions to visualize gene connectivity in M1 and M2 states. Networks built from RNA-seq data were overlaid with microarray expression values to compare information content.

Statistical analysis employed Student’s t-tests for pairwise comparisons, with normality and variance assumptions verified. Correlation between RNA-seq and microarray fold-changes was evaluated by linear regression. Significance was defined as p<0.05.

These examples collectively demonstrate that RNA-seq provides superior resolution, dynamic range, and biological insight compared to microarray analysis in characterizing human macrophage polarization.

### Example 1

A human macrophage model system was established by differentiating CD14+ monocytes with GM-CSF or M-CSF, followed by polarization with IFN-γ (M1-like) or IL-4 (M2-like). Surface marker expression was analyzed by flow cytometry, confirming expected patterns: CD64 and CD86 were elevated in M1-like cells, while CD23 was induced in M2-like macrophages. Notably, GM-CSF-differentiated cells showed stronger polarization responses than M-CSF-derived macrophages, leading to the selection of GM-CSF for subsequent experiments. However, limitations were observed: some canonical markers showed donor variability, and no single marker perfectly segregated M1 and M2 states, underscoring the need for multi-parameter or transcriptomic approaches.

### Example 2

Microarray-based gene expression profiling was performed on macrophages from seven donors. Principal component analysis and hierarchical clustering clearly separated M1, M2, and unpolarized (M0) samples, validating the model. Known M1 markers (e.g., CXCL10, IL1B, CD64) and M2 markers (e.g., CCL18, CD23, F13A1) were differentially expressed as expected. A priori network analysis revealed distinct M1 and M2 gene modules, with minimal overlap. However, limitations emerged: the dynamic range was compressed, low-abundance transcripts were undetected, and alternative isoforms could not be resolved, restricting biological interpretation.

### Example 3

RNA-seq was applied to M1 and M2 macrophages from three donors. Over 11,000 genes were detected per condition, with high concordance to microarray data at the global level (Pearson r >0.8). However, RNA-seq identified significantly more differentially expressed genes (1,736 vs. 834 for M1-upregulated), particularly at high fold-changes (>4x). Visualization of marker genes (e.g., CD64, CD23) confirmed polarization fidelity. Crucially, RNA-seq detected transcripts absent from microarray probe sets, including APOL and LILR family members.

### Example 4

Differential expression analysis revealed RNA-seq’s superior dynamic range: fold-changes spanned 10^6-fold versus 10^4-fold in microarrays. Genes like DUOX1, TBX21, and GBP7 were exclusively detected as differentially expressed by RNA-seq. Venn analysis showed 900 genes unique to RNA-seq, while 308 were array-specific—these latter showed trends in RNA-seq but lacked statistical power due to lower replicates. This highlights RNA-seq’s sensitivity despite smaller sample sizes.

### Example 5

Exon-resolution analysis of CD68, CD64, and CD23 demonstrated RNA-seq’s ability to quantify expression at the exon level. For CD64, RNA-seq showed complete absence in M2 macrophages across all exons, matching protein data. Microarrays and qPCR showed similar trends but with higher variance. RNA-seq also detected M1/M2 markers not represented on arrays, proving its utility for novel gene discovery.

### Example 6

Network analysis using RNA-seq data uncovered dense, biologically coherent modules for M1 and M2 states. When microarray data were overlaid onto RNA-seq-derived networks, only 73% (M1) and 54% (M2) of nodes were informative. Critical hubs like APOL3 and LILRB1 were missed by arrays but validated by qPCR and flow cytometry, demonstrating RNA-seq’s advantage in systems biology.

### Example 7

Cufflinks/Cuffdiff analysis identified 9 genes with alternative promoters, 28 with alternative TSS, and 20 with differential coding sequences. PDLIM7 exhibited isoform switching: v1 in M1, v2 in M2. Isoform-specific qPCR confirmed this, revealing a layer of post-transcriptional regulation invisible to microarrays, with potential functional implications in cytoskeletal signaling.

### Example 8

Screening the human surfaceome in RNA-seq data identified 475 differentially expressed surface molecules. Novel M1 markers included CD120b and SLAMF7; M2 markers included CD1a, CD1b, CD93, and CD226. These were validated by flow cytometry across differentiation conditions (GM-CSF vs. M-CSF), confirming their robustness as polarization indicators for diagnostic and research applications.
