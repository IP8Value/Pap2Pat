## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Invention

Membrane proteins constitute a critical class of biomolecules that mediate essential cellular functions, including signal transduction, ion transport, nutrient uptake, cell adhesion, and intercellular communication. These proteins are embedded within lipid bilayers of cellular membranes and often possess complex topologies involving multiple transmembrane domains, extracellular loops, and cytoplasmic regions that interact dynamically with intracellular signaling machinery. Despite their biological significance, the study of membrane protein trafficking, localization, and functional regulation has been historically hampered by technical limitations in detection methodologies. Traditional approaches for monitoring membrane proteins frequently rely on epitope tagging followed by immunofluorescence labeling, which necessitates cell fixation, permeabilization, and multiple washing steps that can perturb native protein conformation, disrupt subcellular architecture, and introduce artifacts unrelated to physiological behavior. Reporter-tagged membrane proteins have emerged as valuable tools to circumvent these issues by enabling live-cell imaging and real-time tracking of protein dynamics; however, existing reporter systems suffer from significant drawbacks. Many conventional fluorescent protein tags, such as green fluorescent protein (GFP) and its variants, exhibit constitutive fluorescence regardless of cellular compartmentalization, thereby precluding selective visualization of surface-exposed versus intracellular pools of the target protein. Moreover, these reporters often interfere with proper folding, trafficking, or function of the membrane protein due to steric hindrance, misfolding induced by fusion topology, or unintended interactions with cellular quality control mechanisms. Existing reporter polypeptides, while useful in certain contexts, lack the modularity, specificity, and dynamic range required for high-throughput screening applications or quantitative assessment of pharmacological rescue in protein misfolding diseases. Consequently, there exists a pressing need for novel reporter-tagged membrane protein constructs that enable selective, sensitive, and non-invasive detection of cell surface-localized proteins in living cells without compromising native structure or function. The present invention addresses these deficiencies by introducing a genetically encoded biosensor system based on fluorogen-activating proteins (FAPs) fused to membrane proteins via engineered membrane-spanning linkers, thereby providing a robust platform for monitoring dynamic trafficking processes, evaluating corrector efficacy in cystic fibrosis and other conformational disorders, and accelerating drug discovery pipelines through improved assay fidelity and throughput.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides recombinant protein constructs comprising a membrane protein operably linked to a reporter polypeptide via a membrane-spanning linker domain. As used herein, a “membrane protein” refers to any polypeptide that is natively integrated into a biological membrane, including but not limited to ion channels, transporters, receptors, and adhesion molecules. A “reporter polypeptide” denotes a protein moiety capable of generating a detectable signal upon interaction with a cognate ligand, such as a fluorogen, chromogen, or enzyme substrate. Central to the invention is the inclusion of a membrane-spanning linker, which ensures proper topological orientation of the reporter relative to the membrane, thereby enabling selective exposure of the reporter to either the extracellular or intracellular milieu depending on design intent. In preferred embodiments, the membrane-spanning linker is derived from the platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR), although synthetic or naturally occurring transmembrane segments from other sources may also be employed. The membrane protein may be modified at its N-terminus or C-terminus, or within an extracellular loop, to accommodate the reporter fusion without disrupting native folding or trafficking. Exemplary membrane proteins include the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR), particularly wild-type and F508del mutant forms, as well as glucose transporter 4 (GLUT4) and beta-2 adrenergic receptor (B2AR). The reporter polypeptide is preferably a fluorogen-activating protein (FAP), such as HL1.1-TO1 or HL4-MG, which binds non-fluorescent small-molecule dyes (fluorogens) and induces a dramatic increase in fluorescence quantum yield upon binding. Epitope tags may be incorporated for orthogonal validation, but are not required for primary detection. The invention enables two complementary assay modalities: a surface fluorescence depletion assay using cell-impermeant fluorogens (e.g., MG-11p) to selectively label plasma membrane-resident protein, and an internal fluorescence accumulation assay using cell-permeant fluorogens (e.g., MG-ester) to visualize total cellular protein distribution. Together, these features constitute a genetically encoded biosensor system that facilitates real-time, quantitative monitoring of membrane protein trafficking, internalization, and pharmacological rescue in live cells with high signal-to-noise ratio and minimal experimental perturbation.

## EXPERIMENTAL

Materials and methods employed in the development and validation of the invention are detailed below, including molecular cloning, cell culture, transduction, functional assays, imaging, and data analysis protocols.

### EXAMPLE 1

An expression vector designated pDisplaySacLac2 was constructed to facilitate modular assembly of FAP-tagged membrane proteins. The SacB gene, encoding levansucrase from Bacillus subtilis, was amplified using polymerase chain reaction (PCR) with primers incorporating appropriate restriction sites for directional cloning. The resulting amplicon was purified and ligated into the pDisplayBlue backbone vector, which contains regulatory elements for mammalian expression, including a cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter and a bovine growth hormone polyadenylation signal. The ligation mixture was transformed into chemically competent Escherichia coli, and positive clones were identified by colony PCR and restriction digest analysis. Plasmid DNA was isolated using standard alkaline lysis methods and verified by Sanger sequencing to ensure fidelity of the inserted sequence and absence of PCR-induced mutations.

### EXAMPLE 2

A retroviral expression vector, pBabeSacLac2, was engineered to enable stable integration and long-term expression of FAP-tagged constructs in mammalian cells. Vector inserts containing open reading frames of interest were generated with SfiI restriction sites flanking the coding sequence to allow seamless ligation into the pBabeSacLac2 backbone. PCR amplification of target genes—including CFTR (wild-type and F508del), GLUT4, and B2AR—was performed using high-fidelity DNA polymerase and gene-specific primers designed to introduce SfiI sites without altering the amino acid sequence. Amplicons were cloned into intermediate vectors pCR2.1-TOPO or pCR-Blunt II-TOPO for propagation and sequence verification. Verified inserts were excised and ligated into pBabeSacLac2 digested with SfiI. Seven distinct open reading frame constructs were prepared, with templates sourced from established cDNA libraries or obtained as gifts from collaborating laboratories. A seventh fragment, designed for insertion into the fourth extracellular loop of CFTR, was prepared with BsmI sites to facilitate precise integration at the desired locus. Cell lines NIH 3T3 (mouse fibroblasts), C2C12 (mouse myoblasts), and HEK 293 (human embryonic kidney) were maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and antibiotics. Transient transfections were carried out using Minis TransIT1-LT1 Transfection Reagent according to the manufacturer’s protocol: cells were seeded at 70–80% confluence, and DNA-lipid complexes were added dropwise after 4–6 hours of serum-free incubation, followed by replacement with complete medium after 6 hours. For stable expression, retroviral particles were produced using the Phoenix Ecotropic Packaging System: Phoenix-ECO cells were transfected with pBabeSacLac2 constructs, and viral supernatant was harvested 48 hours post-transfection, filtered through a 0.45-μm syringe filter, and flash-frozen. Target cells were infected in the presence of hexadimethrine bromide (polybrene) to enhance transduction efficiency. Fluorescence microscopy was performed using an inverted epifluorescence microscope equipped with appropriate filter sets; images were acquired every 15 seconds during perfusion experiments. B2AR internalization was assessed by treating cells expressing FAP-B2AR with isoproterenol and monitoring loss of surface fluorescence over time. Expression vectors pDisplaySacLac2 and pBabeSacLac2 contain a translation unit comprising a Kozak sequence, signal peptide, FAP tag, membrane-spanning linker, and target membrane protein. These vectors enable rapid generation of FAP fusions for diverse applications. Fluorescence micrographs confirmed surface localization of B2AR, GLUT4, and CFTR fusions. FAP-tagged CFTR constructs exhibited corrector-dependent rescue of F508del trafficking, as visualized by MG-11p labeling. Functional activity was validated by iodide efflux assays using SPQ dye. Flow cytometry quantified surface expression levels across corrector conditions. Immunoblotting with CFTR-specific antibody #596 confirmed glycosylation maturation. Microscope image acquisition utilized confocal systems with EMCCD cameras. Statistical analysis employed Student’s t-tests. Reagents included correctors C4, CFFT-002, and C18 from the CFFT panel, fluorogens from MBIC, and Cellstripper for non-enzymatic cell detachment. The FAP-CFTR platform enabled selective, wash-free detection of surface protein, demonstrated corrector efficacy consistent with functional assays in HBE cells, and provided a modular framework adaptable to other trafficking-deficient membrane proteins.

### Summary of Corrector Efficacy from FAP Labeling and Functional Assays

Corrector efficacy was systematically evaluated using FAP-based surface labeling and functional anion transport assays. FIG. 13A presents flow cytometry data showing mean fluorescence intensity of HEK293 cells expressing FAP-F508del-CFTR treated with various correctors; C18 alone produced a 3.5-fold increase over C4, while combinations C4+C18 and CFFT-002+C18 yielded 9.3- and 8.6-fold improvements, respectively. FIG. 13B displays analogous data for F508del-CFTR EL4-FAP, where corrector combinations enhanced surface density to 1.29–1.37 relative units compared to 0.51 for C4 alone. C4 showed minimal rescue across assays, confirming its limited potency. CFFT-002 performed similarly to C4. C18 emerged as the most effective single agent, with robust activity in both HEK293 and primary human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells. Combinations of CFFT-002 and C18 or C4 and C18 produced synergistic effects, indicating distinct mechanisms of action. The FAP reporter polypeptide offers advantages over traditional methods by enabling instantaneous, single-step labeling with high dynamic range. Immunoblot analysis corroborated trafficking rescue through appearance of mature glycosylated CFTR bands after corrector treatment. C4’s limitations were evident in both biochemical and functional readouts. C18’s superior performance suggests direct interaction with CFTR or conserved quality control pathways. To further validate modularity, a FAP reporter was appended to the cytosolic C-terminus of CFTR via a flexible linker; this construct was transfected into HEK293 cells and imaged with cell-permeant MG-ester, confirming intracellular retention and demonstrating adaptability for alternative topological configurations.
