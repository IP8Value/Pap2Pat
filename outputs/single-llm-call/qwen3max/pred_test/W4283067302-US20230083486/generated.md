# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Reinforcement learning is a computational framework for training agents to make sequential decisions through interaction with an environment. In this paradigm, an agent observes the state of its surroundings, selects actions according to a policy, and receives scalar feedback signals known as rewards that indicate the desirability of its behavior. The objective of the agent is to learn a policy that maximizes the cumulative reward over time. However, in many real-world scenarios, extrinsic rewards are sparse, delayed, or entirely absent during initial phases of learning, rendering conventional reinforcement learning algorithms ineffective due to insufficient learning signals. This challenge has motivated the development of exploration strategies that enable agents to discover informative regions of the environment without relying solely on external reward cues. Among these strategies, curiosity-driven exploration has emerged as a powerful approach wherein agents generate intrinsic rewards based on prediction errors from a learned model of the environment—commonly referred to as a world model. Traditional implementations of curiosity-driven methods often employ separate mechanisms for representation learning and intrinsic reward generation, leading to architectural complexity and potential misalignment between the learned representations and the exploration objectives. Moreover, existing approaches frequently rely on pixel-level prediction losses or fixed random projections, which may be ill-suited for high-dimensional visual observations or fail to capture meaningful environmental dynamics. Consequently, there remains a need for a unified, efficient, and generalizable reinforcement learning system that jointly optimizes both the internal representation of the environment and the exploratory policy using a single, coherent learning objective derived from self-supervised predictive modeling.

## SUMMARY

The present invention describes a reinforcement learning system that integrates representation learning and curiosity-driven exploration through a unified self-supervised objective. The system employs a neural network architecture comprising an environment representation neural network and an action selection policy neural network, both trained concurrently to optimize a shared predictive loss function. The environment representation neural network processes sequences of observations to generate latent representations of the agent’s history, while the action selection policy neural network uses these representations to determine actions that maximize cumulative reward. To enhance learning efficiency and representation quality, the system further incorporates auxiliary neural networks that perform forward and backward prediction tasks in latent space, thereby providing additional supervisory signals during training. Specifically, the environment representation neural network is trained by receiving current and future observation inputs from agent-environment interactions, generating a future latent representation from the future observation via a target encoder, and producing a predicted future latent representation from the current observation and intervening actions via an online encoder and recurrent dynamics model. An objective function evaluates the discrepancy between the predicted and actual future latent representations, and gradients derived from this evaluation are used to update the parameters of the environment representation neural network. Similarly, the system generates future internal representations of the agent’s state history and predicts these representations from current internal states; the resulting prediction error informs updates to the latent embedding network parameters. During policy training, the action selection policy neural network processes the current internal representation to output a probability distribution over possible actions, and a reinforcement learning loss—computed from extrinsic and intrinsic rewards—is backpropagated to update the policy parameters. Critically, the intrinsic reward is derived directly from the same prediction error used to train the world model, ensuring alignment between representation learning and exploration. The agent is controlled using a fixed action selection policy during data collection, enabling stable and consistent trajectory generation. This integrated approach eliminates the need for separate representation and exploration modules, reduces architectural complexity, and improves sample efficiency across diverse environments, including those with sparse rewards, partial observability, and high-dimensional sensory inputs.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The reinforcement learning system of the present invention operates by enabling an agent to interact with an environment through a sequence of observations and actions. At each discrete time step, the agent receives an observation from the environment and selects an action to execute, thereby influencing subsequent environmental states. Observations may include high-dimensional sensory data such as images, object positions, or raw sensor readings, while actions may consist of control inputs, torques, or discrete commands depending on the nature of the agent and environment. The environment may be a real-world physical setting, such as a robotic workspace or autonomous vehicle operating domain, or a simulated environment, including virtual 3D worlds, chemical synthesis simulators, or drug design platforms. In real-world applications, the agent may control mechanical equipment like industrial robots or manage computational resources in distributed systems, where observations include system metrics and actions involve task scheduling or routing decisions. Simulated environments may also encompass domains such as integrated circuit layout optimization or data packet routing in communication networks, where the agent is a software entity making decisions based on abstract state representations. Rewards provided by the environment are typically scalar numerical values reflecting progress toward a goal, though they may be sparse or absent in early learning stages. To address this, the system generates intrinsic rewards based on prediction uncertainty from its internal world model.

Referring to FIG. 1, the reinforcement learning system (100) includes an agent control subsystem (160) that orchestrates data flow and computation. Central to the system is an environment representation neural network (110), which encodes observations into latent representations and maintains a recurrent hidden state capturing historical context. Coupled with this is an action selection policy neural network (130) that maps internal representations to action distributions. A value prediction neural network estimates expected future returns, aiding policy optimization. A training engine (116) coordinates the learning process using stored trajectories from a trajectory buffer (114), employing both on-policy and off-policy reinforcement learning techniques as appropriate. Network parameters (118) are updated through gradient-based optimization. Auxiliary neural networks (150A-D) support representation learning through self-supervised prediction tasks.

The environment representation neural network (110) processes a current observation input to produce a current internal representation, which is updated recurrently with each new observation-action pair. The action selection policy neural network (130) takes this internal representation as input and outputs a probability distribution over possible actions, from which an optimal action is sampled or selected via argmax. The value prediction neural network computes Q-values or state-value estimates to guide policy improvement. Exploration is facilitated through stochastic policies or entropy regularization. The recurrent architecture enables the system to handle partially observable environments by maintaining a compressed memory of past interactions.

Training proceeds by interleaving representation learning and policy optimization. For the forward prediction auxiliary task, the system receives a current observation and a future observation separated by k time steps. The environment representation neural network processes the current observation along with intervening actions through an open-loop recurrent unroll to generate a predicted future latent representation. Concurrently, a target encoder—whose parameters are an exponential moving average of the online encoder—processes the actual future observation to produce a target future latent representation. The cosine distance between these two representations defines a first auxiliary task objective function, whose gradient drives updates to the environment representation parameters. Similarly, for internal state prediction, the system generates a future internal representation from the full history up to time t+k and predicts it from the internal state at time t; the resulting error updates the latent embedding network parameters. Backward prediction tasks may also be employed, where past states are predicted from future ones, enhancing temporal consistency.

During reinforcement learning training, the action selection policy neural network processes the current internal representation to compute action probabilities. A reinforcement learning loss—derived from a combination of extrinsic rewards and intrinsic rewards proportional to the world model’s prediction error—is computed using algorithms such as VMPO or PPO. This loss is backpropagated to update the policy parameters, while updates to the environment representation parameters are applied separately or jointly depending on whether representation sharing is enabled. Training continues until a termination criterion is met, such as convergence of performance metrics or exhaustion of computational budget.

The system may be implemented using digital electronic circuitry, computer software, firmware, or hardware, including data processing apparatus executing computer programs stored on computer-readable media. Components may be integrated into back-end, middleware, or front-end modules communicating over networks. Clients and servers may exchange data representing observations, actions, and rewards. The invention encompasses packaged software products, alternative embodiments with modified architectures, and subcombinations of features. Multitasking and parallel processing may be employed to accelerate training. The scope of the invention includes all variations and feature combinations that achieve the described technical effects, particularly the unification of representation learning and curiosity-driven exploration through a single self-supervised objective.
