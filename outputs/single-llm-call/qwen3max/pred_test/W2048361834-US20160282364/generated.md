# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) is a highly conserved zinc metalloprotease that plays a central role in the catabolism of multiple peptide hormones, including insulin, glucagon, amylin, and calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP). IDE is ubiquitously expressed in mammalian tissues, with particularly high levels in the liver, kidney, brain, and muscle. Its primary physiological function involves the regulated degradation of insulin following receptor-mediated endocytosis, thereby terminating insulin signaling and maintaining systemic glucose homeostasis. Beyond its canonical role in insulin metabolism, IDE has emerged as a critical modulator of other metabolic and neuroendocrine pathways through its ability to cleave structurally diverse substrates.

The enzymatic activity of IDE is tightly regulated by conformational dynamics, allosteric effectors, and subcellular localization. Under normal physiological conditions, IDE ensures rapid clearance of circulating insulin postprandially, preventing chronic hyperinsulinemia and associated insulin resistance. However, dysregulation of IDE activity—either through genetic polymorphisms, altered expression, or impaired catalytic efficiency—has been implicated in the pathogenesis of type 2 diabetes mellitus, Alzheimer’s disease, and other metabolic disorders. Notably, genome-wide association studies have identified IDE as a susceptibility locus for type 2 diabetes, underscoring its therapeutic relevance.

Despite its biological significance, the development of pharmacological inhibitors targeting IDE has been historically challenging. Early-generation inhibitors, such as bacitracin and N-ethylmaleimide, lack specificity and exhibit off-target effects due to their reactive chemical nature. More recently, substrate-mimetic peptides like Ii1 have demonstrated improved selectivity but suffer from poor pharmacokinetic properties, including low oral bioavailability, rapid proteolytic degradation, and limited tissue penetration. These limitations have hindered the clinical translation of IDE inhibition as a viable therapeutic strategy for diabetes and related metabolic syndromes.

Current approaches to diabetes management primarily focus on enhancing insulin secretion (e.g., sulfonylureas), improving insulin sensitivity (e.g., metformin, thiazolidinediones), or exogenous insulin replacement. However, these strategies do not address the underlying defect in postprandial insulin clearance mediated by IDE. Transient pharmacological inhibition of IDE offers a novel mechanism to prolong endogenous insulin action, thereby improving glucose tolerance without inducing chronic hyperinsulinemia. This approach requires inhibitors that are potent, selective, metabolically stable, and capable of achieving transient target engagement aligned with meal timing.

There remains a critical unmet need for next-generation IDE inhibitors that overcome the limitations of existing compounds. Such inhibitors must exhibit high affinity for IDE, minimal cross-reactivity with other metalloproteases, favorable pharmacokinetic profiles, and the ability to modulate multiple hormone pathways—including insulin, glucagon, amylin, and CGRP—in a coordinated manner. The discovery of macrocyclic small molecules that fulfill these criteria represents a significant advancement in the field, enabling precise temporal control over IDE activity and offering a new paradigm for the treatment of diabetes and related disorders.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses the discovery and characterization of novel macrocyclic compounds that function as potent and selective inhibitors of insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE). Central to this invention is the identification of a specific macrocyclic inhibitor, designated herein as 6bK, which exhibits high-affinity binding to IDE and demonstrates superior selectivity over other proteases. The co-crystal structure of IDE bound to a close analog, 6b, reveals a unique binding pocket located at the interface between the N- and C-terminal domains of IDE, distinct from the conventional catalytic site occupied by substrate-mimetic inhibitors such as Ii1. This structural insight confirms a non-competitive mechanism of inhibition and explains the observed synergistic effects when 6bK is co-administered with other IDE modulators.

The invention further provides competitive binding assays for the identification and validation of IDE-binding compounds. These assays utilize a labeled probe—specifically, a macrocyclic IDE-binding molecule conjugated to a detectable label—to quantify displacement by candidate compounds. In one embodiment, the probe is labeled with a fluorophore, enabling detection via fluorescence anisotropy or polarization. Upon binding to IDE, the rotational mobility of the fluorophore decreases, resulting in increased anisotropy; displacement by a competing compound restores mobility and reduces anisotropy, allowing quantitative determination of binding affinity.

Importantly, the disclosed IDE inhibitors modulate not only insulin but also glucagon and amylin signaling. Biochemical and in vivo studies demonstrate that 6bK stabilizes both glucagon and amylin by inhibiting their IDE-mediated degradation, thereby enhancing their physiological effects. Glucagon signaling is potentiated, leading to improved hepatic glucose output regulation, while amylin stabilization enhances satiety and delays gastric emptying—both beneficial effects in the context of postprandial glucose control. Furthermore, 6bK modulates CGRP signaling, as evidenced by its ability to augment CGRP-induced hypotension and bradycardia in murine models, indicating broader neuroendocrine regulatory roles.

The invention encompasses methods for identifying IDE-binding compounds through high-throughput screening of chemical libraries using the aforementioned probe-based assays. The probe composition includes a macrocyclic scaffold derived from DNA-templated synthesis, functionalized with a linker and a detectable moiety. Detectable labels include fluorescent dyes (e.g., fluorescein, rhodamine), isotopic tags (e.g., ¹³C, ¹⁵N), biotin for streptavidin-based capture, or immune moieties such as haptens. Fluorophore-based detection is particularly advantageous due to its compatibility with homogeneous assay formats, eliminating the need for separation steps.

Fluorescence anisotropy is calculated by measuring the emission intensities parallel (I∥) and perpendicular (I⊥) to the plane of vertically polarized excitation light, using the formula: r = (I∥ – G·I⊥)/(I∥ + 2G·I⊥), where G is an instrument-specific correction factor. This method enables real-time monitoring of binding kinetics and determination of dissociation constants across a dynamic IDE concentration range, typically from 0.1 nM to 10 µM.

The invention also includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the IDE inhibitors, formulated for transient inhibition—defined as suppression of IDE activity for less than six hours—to align with meal-related metabolic demands. Administration is timed in temporal proximity to meals, with dosing schedules that may be skipped if meals are omitted, thereby minimizing unintended hormonal perturbations. Kits are provided for both therapeutic administration and compound screening, containing the inhibitor, instructions for use, and, in the case of screening kits, the labeled probe and reference standards.
