# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to *Helicobacter pylori* strains, particularly isolated and genetically characterized strains of *H. pylori* that exhibit low pathogenicity while retaining the ability to colonize the gastric mucosa of mammalian hosts, including mice, without prior host adaptation. These strains are suitable for use as live bacterial delivery vehicles for biologically active agents, such as antigens, immunomodulators, or therapeutic molecules, and may be employed in pharmaceutical compositions for vaccination, immunotherapy, or targeted drug delivery to the gastrointestinal tract.

## BACKGROUND

Live bacteria have long been explored as delivery vehicles for heterologous antigens and therapeutic molecules due to their intrinsic capacity to interact with host immune systems and deliver payloads directly to mucosal surfaces. Among bacterial vectors, attenuated pathogens such as *Salmonella*, *Listeria*, and *Mycobacterium* have been widely studied. However, these systems often suffer from significant limitations, including residual virulence, poor colonization efficiency, rapid clearance by the host immune system, and limited ability to establish persistent infection—features that compromise their utility for sustained antigen presentation or chronic therapeutic delivery.

A major challenge in the development of bacterial delivery platforms is achieving a balance between safety and efficacy. Attenuated strains are typically engineered to reduce virulence, but this attenuation frequently impairs their ability to colonize host tissues effectively, thereby diminishing their immunogenic potential or therapeutic impact. Moreover, many attenuated strains require complex genetic modifications that may destabilize the bacterial genome or alter essential metabolic pathways, leading to unpredictable behavior in vivo.

*Helicobacter pylori* presents a compelling alternative as a delivery vehicle. This Gram-negative bacterium naturally and persistently colonizes the human gastric mucosa in over half the global population, often for life, without causing overt disease in the majority of infected individuals. Its unique biology—including acid resistance, niche-specific tropism, and capacity to modulate host immune responses—makes it ideally suited for mucosal delivery applications. Unlike conventional attenuated vectors, certain *H. pylori* strains can establish chronic infection in animal models without genetic manipulation or host adaptation, offering a platform that combines natural persistence with low pathogenicity. The present invention capitalizes on this biological advantage by identifying and characterizing specific clinical isolates of *H. pylori* that are both naturally transformable and capable of robust, long-term colonization in diverse murine models, thereby enabling their use as safe and effective live vectors.

## SUMMARY

The invention provides isolated *Helicobacter pylori* strains that are suitable for use as delivery vehicles for biologically active agents. These strains are characterized by low pathogenicity, the ability to naturally transform exogenous DNA, and the capacity to colonize the gastric mucosa of mammalian subjects—including multiple mouse strains—without prior host adaptation. The invention further encompasses recombinant derivatives of these strains, wherein isolated nucleic acids encoding biologically active agents are integrated into the bacterial genome, enabling stable expression and delivery of therapeutic or immunogenic molecules.

In a first aspect, the invention includes isolated *H. pylori* strains designated OND737, OND738, OND739, OND740, OND248, and OND256. Each of these strains has been derived from clinical isolates and exhibits superior colonization properties in murine models compared to reference strains such as X47. Specifically, strain OND737 demonstrates high-level colonization across multiple mouse genotypes and establishes chronic infection lasting at least ten months. Strain OND738 shows comparable colonization efficiency with enhanced stability in acidic environments. Strain OND739 is notable for its natural transformability and consistent expression of heterologous antigens. Strain OND740 exhibits reduced induction of pro-inflammatory cytokines while maintaining robust mucosal persistence. Strains OND248 and OND256 display broad-spectrum compatibility with diverse murine genetic backgrounds, including C57BL/6, DBA/2J, C3H, FVB/n, CBA, 129/s, and Swiss mice, particularly when administered with a casein-rich diet and acidified water.

In a second aspect, the invention provides methods for using these strains as delivery vehicles. This includes the integration of isolated nucleic acids encoding biologically active agents—such as antigens, cytokines, antibodies, or enzyme inhibitors—into the genome of the *H. pylori* strains via natural transformation. The resulting recombinant bacteria can be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions for oral administration, where they colonize the stomach and deliver their payload locally or systemically via immune activation.

The invention thus offers a novel platform for mucosal vaccination, cancer immunotherapy, treatment of gastrointestinal disorders, and targeted delivery of biologics, leveraging the unique biology of non-pathogenic, colonizing *H. pylori* strains.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

The present patent application discloses isolated *Helicobacter pylori* strains and methods of their use as delivery vehicles for biologically active agents. The scope of the invention encompasses the strains themselves, their genetically modified derivatives, nucleic acids derived therefrom, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising them. All documents cited herein, including scientific publications, patents, and patent applications, are incorporated by reference in their entirety for all purposes.

Conventional molecular biology techniques, including PCR, cloning, transformation, and protein analysis, are employed in accordance with standard protocols known in the art. As used herein, “isolated” refers to a biological material that has been removed from its natural environment and is at least partially purified. A “mammalian subject” includes humans and non-human mammals, particularly laboratory animals such as mice, rats, and rabbits, used for experimental or therapeutic purposes.

The *H. pylori* strains of the invention are characterized by “low pathogenicity,” meaning they do not induce severe gastritis, ulceration, or neoplastic changes in infected hosts under standard conditions. This is distinct from highly virulent strains that carry cytotoxin-associated gene A (*cagA*) or vacuolating cytotoxin A (*vacA*) alleles associated with disease. The strains described herein may lack functional *cag* pathogenicity islands or express benign variants of virulence factors.

“Chronic infection” refers to bacterial colonization persisting for at least three months, preferably six months or longer, in the gastric mucosa without clearance by the host immune system. The ability to infect mice without host adaptation means the strains colonize standard laboratory mouse strains upon primary inoculation, without requiring serial passage or genetic modification to adapt to murine physiology.

Clinical isolates of *H. pylori* were obtained from human gastric biopsies and stored in glycerol stocks. These isolates were resurrected by plating on selective blood agar and screened for genotype, natural transformability, and colonization capacity. “Natural transformation” denotes the inherent ability of *H. pylori* to take up exogenous DNA from the environment and integrate it into its genome via homologous recombination—a feature exploited to generate recombinant strains expressing heterologous proteins.

Mutants or derivatives include strains in which native genes have been disrupted, replaced, or supplemented with foreign sequences. Delivery of “biologically active agents” encompasses proteins, peptides, RNA, or small molecules that exert a physiological effect upon expression or release in the host. An “isolated nucleic acid” is a DNA or RNA molecule separated from genomic context, optionally operably linked to regulatory elements for expression in *H. pylori*.

Pharmaceutical use involves formulating recombinant *H. pylori* into oral suspensions, capsules, or lyophilized powders, optionally with stabilizers or adjuvants. The method of inducing an antibody response entails oral administration of the strain, leading to mucosal and systemic IgG production against the delivered antigen.

Integration of isolated nucleic acid into the genome is achieved via natural transformation using PCR constructs flanked by homologous sequences. Suitable strains are identified by screening clinical isolates for transformability, followed by in vivo testing in mouse models. Colonization is assessed by quantitative culture of gastric homogenates, and chronicity is confirmed by persistence beyond three months.

Examples demonstrate the isolation of clinical strains, screening for natural transformation, and validation in murine infection models. Strains K4, K8, K12, K18, and others (corresponding to OND designations) were identified as robust colonizers. Notably, K4 (OND737), K8 (OND738), and K18 (OND739) showed broad-spectrum colonization across six mouse strains when fed a semi-synthetic, casein-rich diet with acidified water. In contrast, K11 and K12 exhibited poorer colonization. Table 3 and FIG. 8 summarize these findings, confirming that dietary optimization enhances strain performance.

Experimental details included orogastric inoculation with 10^9 CFU, stomach homogenization, and plating on selective media. Data analysis involved statistical comparison of bacterial loads over time. Results demonstrated that select strains establish chronic infection and elicit strong IgG responses, as measured by ELISA against *H. pylori* antigens. Significance lies in the identification of safe, persistent, and genetically tractable *H. pylori* vectors. Applications include vaccine development against gastric pathogens, cancer antigens, or autoimmune targets. Advantages over existing systems include natural mucosal tropism, long-term antigen presentation, and minimal inflammation. Limitations include strain-specific variability, which is addressed by the disclosed screening pipeline. Future directions involve engineering strains to express checkpoint inhibitors or tolerogenic antigens. The correlations between diet, genetics, and colonization inform personalized delivery strategies. Overall, the invention provides a versatile, clinically relevant platform for next-generation bacterial therapeutics.

### Isolation of Clinical Strains

Clinical isolates of *H. pylori* were obtained from anonymized human gastric biopsy specimens collected during endoscopic procedures. These isolates were initially cultured on selective blood agar plates containing antibiotics to suppress contaminating flora and stored long-term in 15% glycerol at −80°C. For experimental use, isolates were resurrected by streaking from glycerol stocks onto Brain Heart Infusion (BHI) agar supplemented with 5% horse blood and incubated under microaerobic conditions (5% CO₂, 90% N₂, 5% O₂) for 48–72 hours. Individual colonies were then subcultured to obtain pure cultures. Genotyping was performed by PCR amplification and sequencing of key loci, including *glmM*, *ureA*, *cagA*, and *vacA*, to confirm species identity and virulence profile. Isolates were subsequently screened for natural transformability by exposing them to exogenous DNA encoding antibiotic resistance markers and selecting for transformants on appropriate media. Only strains yielding reproducible transformants at frequencies above 10⁻⁴ were advanced to in vivo testing.

### Example 2

To evaluate colonization capacity, clinical isolates were tested in the C57BL/6 mouse model. Eight-week-old, Helicobacter-free mice were orogastrically inoculated with approximately 1×10⁹ colony-forming units (CFU) of each strain. Four weeks post-inoculation, mice were euthanized, and stomachs were harvested, homogenized, and plated on selective media to quantify bacterial load. Strains exhibiting colonization levels exceeding 10⁴ CFU per stomach were classified as colonizers. This screen identified multiple candidate strains, including those later designated OND737 through OND256, which consistently achieved high-level colonization.

### Example 3

To distinguish transient colonizers from true persistent strains, a secondary screen was conducted. Mice were infected as above and monitored for eight weeks. Stomachs were analyzed at four and eight weeks to assess bacterial retention. Strains showing stable or increasing CFU counts over time were selected as robust colonizers, eliminating those with declining titers indicative of immune clearance.

### Example 4

Long-term colonization was assessed by maintaining infected mice for up to ten months. At monthly intervals, subsets of mice were sacrificed, and gastric colonization was quantified. Strains OND737, OND738, and OND739 maintained colonization above 10⁴ CFU throughout the study period, confirming their ability to establish chronic infection without host adaptation.

### Example 5

Immunogenicity was evaluated in DBA/2J mice infected with strains K6 and K8 (corresponding to OND248 and OND738). Serum samples collected at three and ten months post-infection were analyzed by ELISA for *H. pylori*-specific IgG. Both strains induced strong antibody responses, with titers increasing over time, demonstrating their capacity to engage the adaptive immune system durably.

### Example 6

Screening in the C57BL/6 model identified additional colonizers, including K4 and K12 (OND737 and OND256). These strains achieved colonization levels comparable to the reference strain X47 but with reduced inflammatory pathology, supporting their suitability as delivery vectors.

### Example 7

Optimization of the mouse model revealed that a casein-rich semi-synthetic diet combined with acidified drinking water (pH 2.5–3.0) significantly enhanced colonization efficiency across all tested strains. This dietary regimen mimics aspects of the human gastric environment and improves bacterial survival during transit.

### Example 8

Broad-spectrum colonization was confirmed by testing strains in six additional mouse models: C3H, FVB/n, CBA, DBA/2, 129/s, and Swiss. Using the optimized diet and acidified water, strains K4 (OND737), K8 (OND738), and K18 (OND739) consistently colonized all strains, whereas K11 and K12 showed variable or poor colonization. Results, summarized in Table 3 and FIG. 8, demonstrate that these lead strains are not restricted by host genetics. Experimental details included standardized inoculation, diet control, and blinded CFU enumeration. Data analysis employed ANOVA with post-hoc testing, revealing statistically significant superiority of K4, K8, and K18. Conclusions affirm their robustness as universal vectors. Implications include applicability across diverse preclinical models. Limitations involve minor strain-specific differences in immune modulation. Future directions include humanized mouse studies. Significance lies in overcoming a major barrier in bacterial vector development—host restriction. Relevance extends to translational research requiring reliable in vivo platforms. Applications encompass mucosal vaccines for influenza, HPV, or SARS-CoV-2. Advantages include oral delivery, no cold chain, and self-adjuvanting properties. Disadvantages are limited to gastric localization, though this is advantageous for GI-targeted therapies. Comparisons show superiority over *Salmonella* vectors in persistence. Correlations between diet and colonization inform clinical formulation. Relationships between bacterial load and antibody titer support dose-response predictability. Interactions with dendritic cells suggest built-in adjuvanticity. Synergies with checkpoint blockade are being explored. In summary, the data validate OND737–OND256 as a new class of safe, persistent, and versatile *H. pylori* delivery vehicles.
