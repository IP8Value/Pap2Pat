# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutics for muscular dystrophy, particularly Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). More specifically, the invention provides methods and pharmaceutical compositions for the treatment of muscular dystrophy by administering small molecule compounds that activate the utrophin promoter, thereby increasing utrophin expression or activity in muscle tissue. The invention further encompasses methods for reducing symptoms associated with muscular dystrophy, correcting dystrophin deficiency through functional compensation by upregulated utrophin, and enhancing utrophin promoter activity using orally or parenterally administered small molecules. These small molecules include, but are not limited to, nabumetone, piperine, apigenin, chrysin, riluzole, hesperetin, resveratrol, phenazopyridine, tiabendazole, leflunomide, kawain, clorgyline, equilin, and their analogs, derivatives, prodrugs, isomers, and pharmaceutically acceptable salts. The invention also includes methods of identifying and validating such compounds through cell-based assays utilizing reporter constructs linked to the human utrophin A promoter. By leveraging existing pharmacological agents with known safety profiles, the invention offers a clinically translatable strategy for addressing the underlying pathophysiology of muscular dystrophy without the immunogenic risks associated with dystrophin replacement therapies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Muscular dystrophy refers to a group of inherited neuromuscular disorders characterized by progressive muscle weakness and degeneration. Among these, Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is the most severe and prevalent form, affecting approximately 1 in 3,500–5,000 male births worldwide. DMD arises from mutations in the dystrophin gene located on the X chromosome, leading to the complete absence or nonfunctional expression of dystrophin protein. Dystrophin plays a critical structural role in skeletal and cardiac muscle by anchoring the intracellular actin cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix via the dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC). In its absence, the sarcolemma becomes fragile and susceptible to mechanical stress during muscle contraction, resulting in recurrent cycles of muscle fiber necrosis, inflammation, failed regeneration, and eventual replacement of muscle tissue with fibrotic and adipose deposits. This pathological cascade leads to progressive loss of ambulation, respiratory insufficiency, cardiomyopathy, and premature death, typically in the third or fourth decade of life.

Despite decades of research, there remains no cure for DMD, and current therapeutic approaches are largely palliative. Emerging strategies such as exon-skipping antisense oligonucleotides, stop-codon readthrough agents, and gene therapy face significant limitations including mutation-specific applicability, delivery challenges, immune responses, and regulatory hurdles due to the large size of the dystrophin coding sequence. An alternative and promising therapeutic paradigm involves the upregulation of utrophin, an autosomal paralog of dystrophin that shares 74% amino acid sequence homology and a nearly identical domain architecture. During fetal development, utrophin is widely expressed in muscle fibers and functionally substitutes for dystrophin. In healthy adults, utrophin expression becomes restricted primarily to neuromuscular and myotendinous junctions, while remaining constitutively expressed in non-muscle tissues such as liver, kidney, and lung. Critically, studies in the mdx mouse model of DMD have demonstrated that transgenic overexpression of utrophin throughout the muscle fiber membrane can fully rescue the dystrophic phenotype, restoring muscle integrity and function. Because utrophin is naturally present in DMD patients and recognized as "self" by the immune system, its therapeutic upregulation avoids the risk of immune rejection—a major concern with dystrophin-replacement strategies. Thus, pharmacological activation of the endogenous utrophin gene represents a mutation-agnostic, safe, and potentially curative approach to treating DMD and related muscular dystrophies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for treating muscular dystrophy comprising administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a small molecule compound capable of activating the utrophin promoter. The small molecule compound may be administered orally or parenterally, and includes specific embodiments such as compounds of formula (I), which encompass structurally diverse agents identified through high-throughput screening for utrophin promoter activation. The invention is applicable to various forms of muscular dystrophy, including but not limited to Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), Becker muscular dystrophy (BMD), limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, and other dystrophin-deficient or dystrophin-dysfunctional conditions. In one aspect, the method reduces symptoms of muscular dystrophy—such as muscle weakness, fatigue, contractures, and cardiopulmonary decline—by enhancing utrophin expression to compensate for dystrophin deficiency. In another aspect, the invention provides a method for increasing utrophin promoter activity, which directly leads to elevated transcription of the utrophin A isoform, the predominant form in mature myofibers. This increased promoter activity results in higher levels of utrophin mRNA and protein, thereby restoring the linkage between the cytoskeleton and extracellular matrix via the DAPC. The small molecule compounds of the invention include nabumetone, piperine, apigenin, chrysin, riluzole, hesperetin, resveratrol, phenazopyridine, tiabendazole, leflunomide, kawain, clorgyline, equilin, and their analogs, derivatives, prodrugs, stereoisomers, and pharmaceutically acceptable salts. Each of these compounds has been shown to dose-dependently activate the human utrophin A promoter in validated cellular assays. The invention further includes methods wherein the small molecule is formulated for oral or parenteral delivery and used either as monotherapy or in combination with other utrophin-modulating agents. By targeting the root cause of muscle fragility through endogenous utrophin upregulation, the invention offers a broadly applicable, safe, and clinically feasible therapeutic strategy for muscular dystrophy.
