# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to pine cone extracts, and more specifically to a polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) derived from the cones of *Pinus sylvestris*. The invention further pertains to methods of using such extracts to modulate immune responses, particularly by enhancing T cell proliferation during the primary phase of an antigen-specific immune response. The disclosed compositions and methods are useful in vaccine adjuvant applications, immunotherapeutic strategies, and as agents for promoting cellular immunity against infectious agents or neoplastic cells.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The medicinal use of pinecone extracts has deep historical roots, with early documentation appearing in ancient Greek literature. Pedanius Dioscorides, a prominent Greek physician, pharmacologist, and botanist who practiced in Rome during the first century AD, authored *De Materia Medica*, a foundational text in the history of pharmacology and herbal medicine. In this seminal work, Dioscorides described the therapeutic application of pinecone extracts for treating kidney and digestive disorders, as well as topical use for skin conditions resembling psoriasis. His meticulous observations, based on direct examination of plants in their natural habitats and empirical knowledge of their medicinal properties, established *De Materia Medica* as a precursor to modern pharmacopeias and one of the most influential botanical texts in recorded history.

Centuries later, traditional Japanese folk medicine on the island of Kyushu independently recognized the broad therapeutic potential of aqueous extracts prepared from pinecones. Local practitioners employed this “pinecone tea” to address a spectrum of ailments, including infectious diseases and cancer. The breadth of these anecdotal claims suggested that the extract’s mechanism of action likely involved systemic modulation of host defense mechanisms, particularly the immune system.

Scientific inquiry into these traditional uses has yielded evidence supporting immunomodulatory activity. Previous studies have demonstrated that an orally bioavailable polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC), isolated from *Pinus sylvestris* cones, exerts potent effects on adaptive immunity. Specifically, PPC administration at the time of vaccination biases the immune response toward a T helper type 1 (Th1) phenotype and significantly amplifies the generation of antigen-specific CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) during secondary immune responses. This effect has been consistently observed across diverse vaccine platforms, including DNA, protein, and dendritic cell-based formulations. In vitro experiments further revealed that PPC exposure rapidly induces the differentiation of human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and murine bone marrow progenitors into dendritic-like cells, suggesting a role in antigen-presenting cell development.

Moreover, PPC has been shown to suppress Th2-type responses. In murine models immunized with the model allergen ovalbumin (OVA), concurrent oral PPC administration prevented the development of IgE-mediated allergic sensitization. Splenocytes from these mice failed to produce interleukin-4 (IL-4) upon OVA rechallenge but exhibited markedly elevated interferon-gamma (IFNγ) secretion, consistent with a Th1-skewed response. ELISPOT analysis confirmed a two-fold increase in OVA-specific CD8+/IFNγ+ T cells in PPC-treated animals compared to controls.

Despite these promising findings and accumulating anecdotal reports of enhanced anti-cancer efficacy when PPC is co-administered with conventional therapies, the precise molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying PPC’s immunostimulatory effects remained incompletely understood. In particular, it was unclear whether PPC influenced the expansion, contraction, or survival phases of T cell responses, or whether its effects were mediated through modulation of apoptosis, oxidative stress, or cytokine networks. A detailed mechanistic understanding is essential for optimizing clinical applications and developing next-generation adjuvants. The present invention addresses this gap by elucidating how PPC enhances primary T cell responses without interfering with activation-induced cell death, thereby providing a rational basis for its use in vaccination and immunotherapy.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

It is an object of the present invention to provide a composition comprising a polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complex (PPC) derived from *Pinus sylvestris* pinecones for use in enhancing antigen-specific T cell responses. Another object is to provide methods for using PPC as an adjuvant to improve the efficacy of vaccines by promoting robust primary T cell expansion. A further object is to disclose the mechanism by which PPC augments CD8+ T cell numbers without suppressing activation-induced apoptosis during the contraction phase of the immune response.

The invention demonstrates that PPC possesses potent adjuvant properties that manifest during the primary phase of T cell activation. Specifically, oral administration of PPC significantly increases the number of both CD4+ and CD8+ T cells bearing the Vβ8 T cell receptor variant following stimulation with Staphylococcal enterotoxin B (SEB), a superantigen that selectively activates Vβ8+ T cells. This enhancement occurs without altering the intrinsic susceptibility of activated T cells to apoptosis, indicating that PPC acts primarily by amplifying the proliferative burst rather than by prolonging T cell survival.

Additionally, the invention reveals that PPC promotes T cell differentiation toward a Th1 phenotype, as evidenced by increased systemic IFNγ production and reduced IL-2 levels, while leaving IL-12p70 unaffected. These cytokine shifts support cellular immunity and are conducive to effective anti-viral and anti-tumor responses.

The invention further encompasses systems for vaccination wherein PPC is co-administered with an antigen to enhance the magnitude and quality of the resulting T cell response. Such systems are applicable to prophylactic and therapeutic vaccines targeting infectious diseases, cancers, and other conditions requiring robust cellular immunity. The disclosed methods leverage PPC’s ability to act early in the immune cascade—potentially through effects on dendritic cell maturation or function—to maximize clonal expansion of antigen-specific T cells during initial antigen encounter.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

Building upon prior work demonstrating PPC’s capacity to enhance secondary CD8+ T cell responses and induce dendritic-like cell differentiation in vitro, the present invention investigates its impact on the dynamics of a primary T cell response using the well-established SEB mouse model. SEB binds directly to MHC class II molecules on antigen-presenting cells and specific Vβ regions of the T cell receptor, triggering polyclonal activation and expansion of Vβ8+ T cells without the need for classical antigen processing. This model provides a controlled system to dissect the phases of T cell activation, proliferation, and contraction.

Experiments revealed that oral administration of PPC significantly enhanced the primary T cell response to SEB. Mice treated with SEB plus PPC exhibited a marked increase in the total number of CD3+/Vβ8+ T cells in inguinal lymph nodes 48 hours post-immunization—the peak of the proliferative phase—compared to mice receiving SEB alone. This enhancement was evident in both CD4+/Vβ8+ and CD8+/Vβ8+ subsets, with statistically significant increases observed for each population. Notably, PPC administration in the absence of SEB had no effect on baseline T cell numbers, confirming that PPC does not cause nonspecific lymphoproliferation but rather potentiates antigen-driven expansion.

To determine whether PPC also influences the contraction phase, lymph node cellularity was assessed at 96 hours post-SEB injection, a timepoint corresponding to active T cell deletion via activation-induced cell death (AICD). While CD4+/Vβ8+ T cell numbers had contracted to near-naïve levels in both SEB and SEB+PPC groups, CD8+/Vβ8+ T cell counts remained significantly elevated in the PPC-treated cohort. This differential survival aligns with known biological distinctions between CD4+ and CD8+ T cell fates post-activation and suggests that the initial proliferative advantage conferred by PPC translates into greater CD8+ T cell persistence.

Analysis of serum cytokines at 48 hours showed that PPC reduced IL-2 levels while significantly increasing IFNγ production, consistent with a Th1-polarizing effect. IL-12p70 levels were unchanged, indicating that PPC’s cytokine modulation occurs downstream of dendritic cell IL-12 secretion or through alternative pathways.

Given the established role of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in mediating AICD, the invention examined whether PPC’s polyphenolic constituents—known antioxidants—might suppress ROS and thereby inhibit apoptosis. Flow cytometry using CM-H2DCFDA staining demonstrated that PPC significantly reduced both the frequency and intensity of ROS in SEB-activated CD3+ T cells. However, despite this reduction, intracellular Bcl-2 protein levels in Vβ8+ T cells were equally suppressed in SEB and SEB+PPC groups, as were Bcl-2 mRNA levels. This indicates that the observed ROS reduction was insufficient to rescue Bcl-2 expression, a key anti-apoptotic regulator.

Further gene expression analysis revealed that SEB—but not PPC—induced a four-fold increase in pro-apoptotic Bim mRNA, with no significant changes in Bax, Bcl-xL, or Bcl-3 in activated T cells. Interestingly, PPC alone upregulated Bcl-3 mRNA in naïve mice, a finding consistent with reports linking Bcl-3 to T cell survival, though this effect was absent in the context of SEB activation.

Annexin V staining confirmed robust induction of early apoptosis in Vβ8+ T cells 48 hours after SEB injection, with no difference between SEB and SEB+PPC groups. This verifies that PPC does not interfere with AICD, reinforcing the conclusion that its primary mechanism is enhancement of the expansion phase.

The invention also notes preliminary evidence of increased CD11c+ dendritic cells in lymph nodes of SEB+PPC-treated mice, suggesting that PPC may promote dendritic cell maturation or migration, thereby increasing the number of antigen-presenting cells available to stimulate T cells. Future studies will explore this hypothesis in detail.

## EXAMPLES

### Preparation of PPC

PPC was prepared from commercially sourced *Pinus sylvestris* pinecones. The cones were thoroughly washed in deionized water, shredded, and subjected to aqueous extraction under alkaline conditions (pH 12.0) at elevated temperature (121°C for 30 minutes) in a stainless steel reaction vessel. The resulting liquid extract was standardized by UV absorbance at 280 nm using an extinction coefficient of 22.8 L·g⁻¹·cm⁻¹ for polyphenylpropanoids. The concentration of active ingredients was calculated as:  
PPC concentration (mg/mL) = OD₂₈₀ × (1 / 22.8 L·g⁻¹·cm⁻¹).  
The extract was rigorously tested for contaminants including herbicides, pesticides, heavy metals, endotoxin, and microorganisms, then spray-dried into a stable, dark brown powder. For experimental use, the powder was reconstituted in sterile water to 25 mg/mL, centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 20 minutes, filtered through a 0.2 μm nylon membrane, and diluted to a working stock of 200 μg/mL polyphenylpropanoids.

### In Vivo Immune Stimulation

Six- to seven-week-old male Balb/c mice were injected intraperitoneally with 100 μg Staphylococcal enterotoxin B (SEB) in 100 μL PBS. Mice in the SEB+PPC group received oral gavage of 100 μL of 200 μg/mL PPC three times daily on Days 0 and 1 (six total doses) and had PPC (200 μg/mL) provided ad libitum in drinking water. Control groups included naïve (PBS only), SEB only, and PPC only. Mice were euthanized at 48 or 96 hours post-SEB for tissue and serum collection.

### ELISA Assays

Serum levels of IL-2, IFNγ, and IL-12p70 were quantified using commercial ELISA kits per manufacturer instructions. Absorbance was read at 450 nm, and concentrations were interpolated from standard curves generated using GraphPad Prism software.

### Lymph Node and Spleen Cell Isolation

Inguinal lymph nodes and spleens were homogenized in HBSS using a Stomacher®80, and single-cell suspensions were prepared. Cell counts and viability were determined using Muse Count and Viability reagent and a Muse Cell Analyzer. Cells were resuspended in complete RPMI media at 4 × 10⁶ cells/mL for downstream assays.

### Flow Cytometry

Lymph node cells were stained with fluorescently labeled antibodies against CD3 (APC), CD4 (FITC), CD8 (PE), and Vβ8 (PerCP-eFluor®710) in FACS buffer containing Fc block. After 30-minute incubation on ice, cells were washed and analyzed on a BD FACSCalibur using CellQuest Pro and FloJo software.

### Detection of Apoptosis

Apoptosis was assessed using Annexin V-FITC in conjunction with anti-CD3-APC and anti-Vβ8-PerCP-eFluor®710. Cells were stained per kit protocol, incubated 10 minutes at room temperature, and analyzed by flow cytometry.

### Detection of Reactive Oxygen Species

Intracellular ROS was measured using CM-H2DCFDA. Cells were incubated with the probe and surface antibodies for 15 minutes at 37°C, washed, and analyzed by flow cytometry. Data are presented as percentage of ROS+ CD3+ T cells and fluorescence intensity histograms.

### Detection of Intracellular Bcl-2

Following surface staining, cells were permeabilized with 0.03% saponin and stained intracellularly with FITC-labeled anti-Bcl-2 antibody for 60 minutes on ice. After washing, cells were analyzed by flow cytometry.

### Analysis of Gene Expression

Total RNA was isolated from lymph node cells using RNeasy kits. One microgram of RNA was reverse-transcribed, and qPCR was performed using SYBR Green master mix and primers for Bcl-2, Bcl-3, Bcl-xL, Bax, Bim, and β-actin. Relative mRNA levels were calculated by the ΔΔ-Ct method, normalized to β-actin in naïve controls. Statistical significance was assessed by ANOVA with Newman-Keuls post-test.

### Example I: Oral Delivery of PPC Enhances T Cell Response to SEB

At 48 hours post-SEB, PPC co-administration doubled the number of CD4+/Vβ8+ and CD8+/Vβ8+ T cells in lymph nodes compared to SEB alone (p < 0.01). PPC alone had no effect.

### Example II: PPC Enhances CD8+ T Cell Survival During Contraction Phase

At 96 hours, CD8+/Vβ8+ T cell numbers remained significantly higher in SEB+PPC mice versus SEB-only (p < 0.05), while CD4+ populations had fully contracted in both groups.

### Example III: PPC Affects Cytokine Production

PPC reduced serum IL-2 (p < 0.05) and increased IFNγ (p < 0.005) at 48 hours, with no change in IL-12p70.

### Example IV: PPC Reduces ROS Levels in T Cells

PPC significantly decreased both the proportion and intensity of ROS in CD3+ T cells (p < 0.01).

### Example V: PPC's Effect on Bcl-2 Expression

Despite lower ROS, Bcl-2 protein and mRNA were equally suppressed in SEB and SEB+PPC groups, indicating no rescue of anti-apoptotic signaling.

### Example VI: PPC's Effect on Bcl-3, Bim, Bax, and Bcl-xL Expression

SEB induced a 4-fold increase in Bim mRNA; PPC did not alter this or affect Bax, Bcl-xL, or Bcl-3 in activated T cells. PPC alone upregulated Bcl-3 in naïve mice.

### Example VII: PPC Does Not Suppress SEB-Enhanced Apoptosis

Annexin V staining showed equivalent apoptosis in Vβ8+ T cells from SEB and SEB+PPC groups (p > 0.05), confirming no inhibition of AICD.

### Summary of Results

Collectively, the examples demonstrate that PPC enhances primary T cell expansion without blocking contraction-phase apoptosis. The increased T cell numbers result from amplified proliferation, not prolonged survival.

### Discussion of Implications

These findings clarify that PPC’s adjuvant effect stems from boosting the initial clonal burst, likely via dendritic cell modulation, making it ideal for prime-boost vaccination strategies.

### Limitations of Study

The SEB model, while informative, involves non-specific T cell activation; future work will test PPC with true peptide antigens.

### Future Directions

Ongoing studies focus on dendritic cell trafficking, transcriptomic profiling, and combinatorial use with checkpoint inhibitors.

### Conclusion

PPC enhances antigen-specific T cell responses by augmenting primary expansion, offering a safe and effective adjuvant platform for next-generation vaccines.
