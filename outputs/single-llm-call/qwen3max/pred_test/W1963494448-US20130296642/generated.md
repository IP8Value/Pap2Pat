## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of surgical wound healing and hernia risk assessment, and more specifically to methods and kits for determining a patient’s risk of developing an incisional hernia following abdominal surgery based on the expression levels of specific biomarkers in skin or fascial tissue. Incisional hernias represent a common and clinically significant complication following abdominal surgery, with reported incidence rates ranging from 2% to 11% and recurrence rates as high as 50% in certain patient populations. Despite advances in surgical technique and the widespread use of prosthetic mesh reinforcement, a substantial number of patients continue to experience recurrent hernias, leading to increased morbidity, repeated hospitalizations, and significant healthcare expenditures estimated at $2.5 billion annually in the United States alone. Current clinical strategies for managing incisional hernias are largely reactive rather than preventive, and there remains a critical unmet need for predictive tools that can identify patients at elevated risk prior to their initial surgical intervention.

The related art includes various attempts to correlate clinical and demographic factors—such as obesity, advanced age, male gender, smoking status, diabetes, and postoperative wound infection—with the development of incisional hernias. However, these factors lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity to reliably predict individual patient outcomes. Moreover, while alterations in collagen composition, particularly a reduced ratio of type I to type III collagen, have been associated with impaired wound strength and hernia formation, existing methodologies for assessing collagen profiles are largely qualitative, inconsistent, and not amenable to routine clinical implementation. Additionally, although hereditary connective tissue disorders like Ehlers–Danlos syndrome and Marfan’s syndrome are known to predispose individuals to hernia formation, the majority of incisional hernia cases occur in otherwise healthy individuals without such diagnoses, suggesting the existence of subtler molecular determinants that remain poorly understood.

A major limitation of current incisional hernia repair strategies is the absence of a preoperative or intraoperative diagnostic method capable of stratifying patients according to their intrinsic biological risk of poor wound healing or hernia recurrence. Surgeons are therefore often forced to apply a one-size-fits-all approach to mesh selection, suture technique, and postoperative management, which may be suboptimal for high-risk individuals. Furthermore, existing tissue sampling protocols typically focus on the hernia site itself, which may be confounded by local inflammation, fibrosis, or ongoing repair processes that obscure the patient’s baseline molecular phenotype. This limitation underscores the need for a standardized, minimally invasive method to obtain biologically relevant tissue samples from anatomically remote sites that reflect the patient’s systemic wound-healing capacity.

Recognizing these shortcomings, the inventors identified a clear need for improved methods to assess the molecular underpinnings of incisional hernia susceptibility. Specifically, there is a pressing demand for a reliable, objective, and quantifiable diagnostic platform that leverages gene expression profiling to predict hernia risk before or during the initial surgical procedure. Such a method would enable personalized surgical planning, including the selection of appropriate mesh types, reinforcement strategies, and adjunctive therapies tailored to the patient’s molecular risk profile, thereby reducing recurrence rates and improving long-term outcomes. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel diagnostic and prognostic framework based on the differential expression of specific biomarkers—including GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10—in non-herniated skin and fascial tissue obtained from abdominal surgical sites.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for determining a subject’s risk level for developing an incisional hernia following abdominal surgery. In one embodiment, the method comprises measuring the expression level of one or more genes selected from the group consisting of GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10 in a tissue sample obtained from the subject’s skin or fascia. The measured expression level is then compared to a control value derived from a reference population of subjects who do not have a history of incisional hernia. Based on this comparison, a risk level for incisional hernia is determined, wherein a decreased expression of GREM1 or an increased expression of COL3A1, COL1A2, or IL10 relative to the control is indicative of an elevated risk. The subject may then be treated according to their determined risk level, for example by selecting a reinforced surgical mesh, employing laparoscopic techniques, or implementing enhanced postoperative monitoring.

The invention also encompasses a diagnostic method for assessing incisional hernia risk, which similarly involves measuring the expression of the aforementioned biomarkers in a skin or fascial tissue sample, comparing the results to a control, and classifying the subject as low-risk or high-risk based on predefined thresholds. The tissue samples used in the method are preferably obtained from a site remote from any existing hernia or prior surgical incision, such as a planned trocar insertion site during laparoscopic surgery, to avoid confounding effects from local pathology. Gene expression may be measured using any suitable nucleic acid detection assay, including quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), microarray hybridization, RNA sequencing, or PCR array analysis.

In another aspect, the invention provides a product for diagnosing or prognosing incisional hernia risk, comprising a biochip array on which nucleic acid probes complementary to GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and/or IL10 are immobilized. Also provided are kits for use in the diagnostic or prognostic methods described herein. These kits may include PCR primers specific for the target biomarkers, instructions for correlating expression levels with risk categories, reagents for RNA isolation and amplification, and optionally, a DNA microarray containing complements of the biomarker sequences. In some embodiments, the kit includes a substrate with an adsorbent capable of binding the protein products of the biomarker genes, along with detection reagents such as antibodies for immunohistochemical or immunoassay-based analysis. The invention further includes a biochip comprising isolated biomarkers or their nucleic acid complements arranged in a spatially defined format for high-throughput screening of patient samples.

### Methods

The invention provides methods for determining a subject’s risk of developing an incisional hernia following abdominal surgery. These methods are based on the discovery that patients with recurrent incisional hernias exhibit distinct gene expression profiles in non-herniated skin and fascial tissue compared to control subjects. Specifically, the expression levels of GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10 were found to be significantly altered in recurrent hernia patients. The method involves obtaining a tissue sample from the subject’s skin or fascia—preferably from a site distant from any hernia or prior incision, such as a planned trocar site—and measuring the expression levels of one or more of these biomarkers.

Gene expression profiling may be performed using microarray experiments, which revealed differential expression of over 160 genes in the skin of recurrent hernia patients. Among the most significant findings was the consistent downregulation of GREM1 in both skin and fascia, with fold changes of 0.49 and 0.23, respectively. PCR array data further validated these findings and identified additional differentially expressed genes involved in extracellular matrix remodeling and immune regulation. The method entails quantifying the expression level of each biomarker using a nucleic acid detection assay, such as qRT-PCR, and comparing it to a control value derived from a reference population of hernia-free individuals.

The control value may be a mean or median expression level established from a cohort of normal control subjects, optionally adjusted for demographic variables such as age, sex, or body mass index. A subject is classified as high-risk if their GREM1 expression is below the control threshold, or if their COL3A1, COL1A2, or IL10 expression exceeds the control threshold. Risk determination may also be based on ratios, such as the COL1A2/COL3A1 ratio, which was found to be significantly reduced in recurrent hernia patients. Once the risk level is determined, the subject may be treated accordingly—for example, by affixing a reinforced surgical mesh to the abdominal wall during closure, performing a laparoscopic repair to minimize tissue trauma, or prescribing postoperative interventions to support wound healing.

Quantitative assessment of gene expression may be achieved through hybridization-based methods (e.g., microarrays) or amplification-based methods (e.g., qPCR). Differential expression is defined as a statistically significant change in expression level, typically with a fold change of at least 1.5 and a false discovery rate-adjusted p-value (q-value) below 0.30. Upregulation refers to increased expression relative to control, while downregulation indicates decreased expression. Treatment options based on risk level may include the use of lightweight, macroporous mesh for low-risk patients and heavyweight, composite mesh for high-risk patients, as well as modifications to suture technique or postoperative activity restrictions.

### Kits

The invention provides kits for the prognosis and diagnosis of incisional hernia risk. These kits are designed to facilitate the measurement of biomarker expression in clinical or laboratory settings. One embodiment includes a set of PCR primers specific for GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10, along with instructions for performing qRT-PCR and interpreting the results in the context of hernia risk. The kit may further include reagents for RNA isolation, reverse transcription, and amplification, such as DNase, reverse transcriptase, and DNA polymerase.

Another embodiment comprises a DNA microarray containing immobilized nucleic acid probes complementary to the target biomarkers. The kit may include labeling reagents, hybridization buffers, and wash solutions, as well as software for data analysis and risk classification. In alternative embodiments, the kit includes a substrate with an adsorbent material capable of binding the protein products of the biomarker genes, along with detection reagents such as enzyme-conjugated antibodies for colorimetric or fluorescent readout. For protein-based assays, the kit may also contain pre-fractionation spin columns to enrich for low-abundance proteins.

A further embodiment provides a product in the form of a purified biomarker panel bound to a microarray surface, enabling simultaneous detection of multiple analytes. The kit may be configured for use with automated platforms and include calibration standards and quality control samples. Instructions for use specify the correlation between biomarker expression levels and risk categories, enabling clinicians to make informed decisions regarding surgical planning and postoperative care. The biochip array may be fabricated using photolithography, inkjet printing, or robotic spotting, and is compatible with standard fluorescence or chemiluminescence detection systems.

## EXAMPLES

### Patient Samples and Tissue Acquisition

Patient samples were collected under institutional review board approval with informed consent. Thirty-three patients participated: 18 with recurrent incisional hernias (RH) and 15 healthy controls undergoing laparoscopic cholecystectomy (NC). Approximately 1 cm² of skin and fascia was excised from a trocar site remote from hernias or prior incisions. Tissues were divided and stored in RNALater™ for RNA preservation or formalin for histology.

### RNA Isolation and RNA Amplification

Total RNA was isolated using the RNeasy® Lipid Tissue Mini Kit with on-column DNase treatment. RNA integrity was assessed via Bioanalyzer, yielding RIN values of 5–8. RNA was amplified using the WT-Ovation™ Pico system to generate sufficient cDNA for microarray hybridization. Eight RH and eight NC samples with high-quality RNA were selected for genome-wide expression analysis.

### Immunohistochemistry

Formalin-fixed tissues were embedded in paraffin and sectioned at 4 μm. Immunohistochemistry was performed using an automated horseradish peroxidase system with antibodies against COL1A1 and COL3A1. Staining intensity was qualitatively assessed to corroborate gene expression findings.

### Statistical Analysis of Microarray Data

Microarray data were analyzed using R software. Genes undetectable in >50% of samples were excluded. Differential expression was assessed using moderated t-tests with Benjamini-Hochberg correction (q < 0.30). A fold change ≥1.5 was required for significance. GO enrichment analysis identified overrepresented biological processes, revealing associations with wound healing, immune response, and cell adhesion.

### Demographics

The study cohort was predominantly female (26/33). RH and NC groups were comparable in age, BMI, and smoking status, though RH patients had higher rates of diabetes and prior surgery (p = 0.03 and 0.01, respectively). Microarray subsets showed similar demographic distributions.

### Gene Ontology Analysis of Differentially Expressed Genes

GO analysis of skin samples revealed 53 enriched biological processes, including “response to wounding” and “regulation of immune response.” Key genes like COL3A1 and FBN1 appeared in multiple categories, underscoring their multifunctional roles in tissue integrity and inflammation. Molecular function terms included “receptor binding” and “enzyme inhibitor activity,” while cellular component terms highlighted extracellular matrix localization.

### COL1/COL3 Ratio by Microarray, PCR Array, and Immunohistochemistry

Microarray showed a reduced COL1A2/COL3A1 ratio in RH skin (0.59 vs. 0.79, p = 0.02). PCR array confirmed elevated COL3A1 and reduced COL1A2 in RH patients. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated stronger COL3A1 staining in RH tissues. Quadratic discriminant analysis using GREM1 and COL3A1 expression correctly classified 86% of patients, demonstrating strong diagnostic potential.
