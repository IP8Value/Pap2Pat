# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to antibodies and antigen-binding fragments that specifically bind to the human prolactin receptor (PRLR). These antibodies and fragments are useful for targeting tumor cells that express PRLR, particularly in the context of breast cancer. The invention encompasses isolated monoclonal antibodies, including full-length immunoglobulins and their antigen-binding portions, as well as nucleic acid molecules encoding such antibodies, recombinant expression vectors, host cells for production, and pharmaceutical compositions comprising these antibodies. The antibodies may be used alone or conjugated to therapeutic moieties such as cytotoxic agents or radioisotopes to form antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) with enhanced antitumor activity. The invention further includes methods of treating cancers characterized by PRLR expression, including triple-negative breast cancer and other molecular subtypes, through the administration of these anti-PRLR antibodies or ADCs.

## BACKGROUND

The prolactin receptor (PRLR) is a single-pass transmembrane receptor belonging to the type I cytokine receptor superfamily, which also includes the growth hormone receptor. PRLR is activated upon binding of its ligand, prolactin (PRL), a polypeptide hormone primarily secreted by the pituitary gland but also produced by the adrenal and mammary glands. Upon ligand binding, PRLR initiates intracellular signaling cascades through multiple kinases, including Jak2/Stat5, MAPK, and PI3K pathways, which are implicated in cell proliferation, survival, and differentiation. While PRLR signaling is essential for normal physiological functions such as lactation, aberrant activation of this pathway has been associated with oncogenesis in hormone-responsive tissues.

PRLR is overexpressed in malignant breast epithelium relative to normal breast tissue and is expressed across various molecular subtypes of human breast cancer, including estrogen receptor-positive (ER+), progesterone receptor-positive (PR+), HER2-positive, and even triple-negative breast cancers (TNBC). Although the role of PRLR as a driver of advanced disease remains debated, its elevated expression in tumors compared to limited expression in normal tissues—restricted mainly to mammary glands, placenta, adrenal gland, and kidney—makes it an attractive therapeutic target. Previous strategies to inhibit PRLR signaling, such as PRL-based antagonists like G129R-hPRL and its Pseudomonas exotoxin A conjugate, have faced clinical limitations due to short serum half-life, immunogenicity, and toxicity. More recently, monoclonal antibodies such as LFA-102 and 005-C04 have demonstrated the ability to block PRL-mediated signaling in preclinical models, but a Phase I clinical trial of LFA-102 showed limited antitumor efficacy despite good tolerability, suggesting that mere blockade of PRLR signaling may be insufficient for robust clinical activity.

Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) represent a promising approach to overcome this limitation by enabling the selective delivery of highly potent cytotoxic agents directly to tumor cells expressing the target antigen. ADCs such as Kadcyla (adotrastuzumab emtansine), which targets HER2 and delivers the maytansinoid DM1, have demonstrated significant clinical benefit in metastatic breast cancer. Given the internalization properties of PRLR and its tumor-selective overexpression, the development of an anti-PRLR ADC offers a rational strategy to achieve potent antitumor effects while minimizing systemic toxicity. The present invention addresses this unmet need by providing high-affinity, fully human anti-PRLR antibodies capable of efficient internalization and conjugation to cytotoxic payloads, resulting in potent in vitro and in vivo antitumor activity against PRLR-expressing breast cancers.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides antibodies and antigen-binding fragments that specifically bind to the human prolactin receptor (PRLR). These antibodies are useful for targeting tumor cells that express PRLR, particularly in breast cancer, and may be administered alone or as part of an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC). The antibodies may be full-length immunoglobulins or comprise only the antigen-binding portion, and they may be modified to alter effector function, stability, or pharmacokinetic properties. Exemplary anti-PRLR antibodies include those designated herein as REGN2878 and related variants.

The invention further provides specific amino acid sequences for the heavy chain variable region (HCVR) and light chain variable region (LCVR) of these antibodies, as well as the corresponding complementarity-determining regions (CDRs): HCDR1, HCDR2, HCDR3, LCDR1, LCDR2, and LCDR3. In particular embodiments, the antibodies comprise a HCVR having an amino acid sequence selected from a defined set, and a LCVR having an amino acid sequence selected from another defined set. Alternatively, the antibodies may be defined by their CDR sequences, such as a combination of HCDR3 and LCDR3, or a complete set of six CDRs.

Corresponding nucleic acid molecules encoding the HCVR, LCVR, and individual CDRs are also provided, along with recombinant expression vectors containing these nucleic acids and host cells engineered to express the anti-PRLR antibodies. The invention includes methods for producing the antibodies in suitable host systems, such as Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells. Additionally, the antibodies may be engineered to have modified glycosylation patterns, which can influence Fc-mediated effector functions or serum half-life.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the anti-PRLR antibodies or antigen-binding fragments are provided, along with methods for their therapeutic use in treating cancers characterized by PRLR expression. The invention also encompasses ADCs formed by conjugating the anti-PRLR antibodies to therapeutic moieties such as cytotoxic agents (e.g., DM1, MMAE, DM4) or radioisotopes, using either cleavable or non-cleavable linkers. These ADCs are particularly effective against tumor cells expressing low to moderate levels of PRLR, expanding the therapeutic window beyond what is achievable with signaling inhibitors alone.
