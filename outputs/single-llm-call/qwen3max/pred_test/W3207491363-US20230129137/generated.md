# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Heart disease remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, with myocardial infarction (MI) representing a particularly devastating manifestation due to the irreversible loss of functional cardiomyocytes. The adult mammalian heart possesses an extremely limited capacity for self-renewal, rendering it incapable of adequately replacing the vast number of cardiomyocytes lost during ischemic injury. This inherent limitation results in the formation of non-contractile fibrotic scar tissue, which compromises cardiac function and often leads to progressive heart failure. Despite advances in medical and surgical interventions, current therapeutic strategies primarily aim to mitigate symptoms or slow disease progression rather than restore lost myocardial tissue. Consequently, there is a critical unmet need for regenerative approaches capable of stimulating endogenous cardiomyocyte proliferation and promoting structural and functional recovery following MI.

The concept of cardiac regeneration has been significantly informed by observations in neonatal mammals, where apical resection surgery performed shortly after birth elicits a robust regenerative response that culminates in complete restoration of cardiac structure and function with minimal scarring. Notably, this regenerative capacity is largely driven by the proliferation of preexisting cardiomyocytes, suggesting that adult cardiomyocytes retain a latent ability to re-enter the cell cycle under appropriate conditions. These findings have spurred intense research into the molecular mechanisms governing cardiomyocyte proliferation, with the goal of identifying therapeutic targets that can reactivate this regenerative potential in the adult heart.

Among the key signaling pathways implicated in cardiac regeneration is the Hippo signaling pathway, which plays a central role in regulating organ size during development and maintaining tissue homeostasis in adulthood. In the context of the heart, the Hippo pathway functions as a potent inhibitor of cardiomyocyte proliferation by phosphorylating and sequestering its downstream effectors, YES-associated protein (YAP) and transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ), in the cytoplasm. When Hippo signaling is suppressed, YAP and TAZ translocate to the nucleus, where they interact with transcription factors such as TEA domain (TEAD) family members to activate the expression of genes involved in cell proliferation, survival, and tissue repair. Given its pivotal role in controlling cardiomyocyte cell cycle activity, modulation of the Hippo/YAP/TAZ axis represents a promising strategy for promoting cardiac regeneration following injury.

Pharmaceutical agents targeting this pathway have emerged as potential therapeutic candidates, with TT-10—a fluorine-substituted derivative of TAZ-12—demonstrating notable pro-proliferative effects on cardiomyocytes. Initial studies showed that systemic administration of TT-10 reduced infarct size in murine models of MI, but these benefits were transient and failed to prevent the subsequent decline in cardiac function. This limitation is likely attributable to the pharmacokinetic profile of free TT-10, which suffers from rapid systemic clearance, poor retention at the site of injury, and suboptimal bioavailability. As a result, achieving sustained therapeutic concentrations of TT-10 within the myocardium using conventional delivery methods requires high doses that may increase the risk of off-target effects and complicate clinical translation. These challenges underscore the necessity for advanced drug delivery systems capable of enhancing the local concentration, duration of action, and overall efficacy of TT-10 while minimizing systemic exposure.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel nanoparticle compositions comprising poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles encapsulating the small molecule TT-10, designed to overcome the limitations of conventional TT-10 administration and enhance its therapeutic potential for the treatment of myocardial infarction and other cardiac disorders. These nanoparticle compositions enable sustained and localized release of TT-10 at the site of cardiac injury, thereby prolonging its biological activity and improving its efficacy in promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation, reducing apoptosis, and enhancing angiogenesis. The method of the invention involves administering the disclosed nanoparticle compositions directly to the myocardium, preferably via intramyocardial injection, to improve cardiac function following ischemic injury.

Administration of the nanoparticle compositions is typically performed shortly after the onset of myocardial infarction, although the timing may be adjusted based on clinical considerations. The sustained release profile of TT-10 from the PLGA nanoparticles ensures that therapeutic concentrations are maintained over an extended period—typically up to four weeks—thereby supporting prolonged activation of regenerative pathways without the need for repeated dosing. This approach not only enhances the durability of the therapeutic effect but also allows for the use of significantly lower total doses of TT-10 compared to conventional systemic administration, thereby reducing the potential for adverse effects and improving the safety profile of the treatment.

In addition to improving cardiac function, the disclosed nanoparticle compositions offer several other advantages. The biocompatible and biodegradable nature of PLGA ensures that the nanoparticles are safely metabolized and cleared from the body over time, minimizing long-term toxicity concerns. Furthermore, the nanoparticle platform is amenable to integration with minimally invasive delivery techniques, such as catheter-based or echo-guided transthoracic myocardial injection, facilitating clinical translation and broadening the applicability of the therapy. Collectively, these features position the disclosed nanoparticle compositions as a transformative approach for the treatment of heart disease, with the potential to shift the paradigm from palliative care to true cardiac regeneration.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to nanoparticle compositions comprising biodegradable poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) nanoparticles encapsulating the small molecule TT-10 (C11H10FN3OS2), a fluorine-substituted derivative of TAZ-12 known to modulate the Hippo signaling pathway. PLGA is a copolymer composed of lactic acid and glycolic acid monomers, and its properties can be finely tuned by adjusting the molecular weight and the ratio of lactide to glycolide units. In the disclosed compositions, the PLGA used typically has a molecular weight ranging from 10,000 to 100,000 Daltons, with a preferred molecular weight of approximately 50,000 Daltons. The ratio of lactide to glycolide in the PLGA polymer is selected to optimize the degradation rate and drug release kinetics, with ratios of 50:50, 65:35, 75:25, or 85:15 being particularly suitable. A 50:50 lactide-to-glycolide ratio is especially preferred due to its balanced degradation profile, which supports sustained release over a period of up to four weeks.

The nanoparticles encapsulating TT-10 are prepared using established methods such as single-emulsion solvent evaporation, resulting in particles with a narrow size distribution and high encapsulation efficiency. The average diameter of the TT-10-loaded nanoparticles is typically between 100 and 200 nanometers, with a preferred average diameter of approximately 155 nanometers. Empty nanoparticles, prepared without TT-10, exhibit a similar size profile, with an average diameter of about 123 nanometers. The loading efficiency of TT-10 in the nanoparticles is approximately 2.5 micrograms of TT-10 per milligram of PLGA, ensuring that a therapeutically effective dose can be delivered with a minimal amount of carrier material.

The method for improving cardiac function according to the invention involves administering the disclosed nanoparticle compositions to a subject suffering from myocardial infarction or at risk of developing cardiac dysfunction. Administration is preferably performed via direct intramyocardial injection into the peri-infarct region, although alternative routes such as intracoronary or intravenous delivery may be considered in certain contexts. The target site of administration is the border zone surrounding the infarcted myocardium, where the presence of viable but stressed cardiomyocytes provides an optimal environment for regenerative intervention. The duration of TT-10 release from the nanoparticles is designed to span up to four weeks, aligning with the critical window for post-infarct remodeling and regeneration.

The dosage of the nanoparticle composition is carefully calibrated to deliver a therapeutically effective amount of TT-10 while minimizing systemic exposure. In preclinical models, a total dose of 67.2 nanograms of TT-10 per mouse has been shown to be highly effective, representing a more than 3700-fold reduction compared to doses used in prior studies with free TT-10. This dramatic reduction in required dose is made possible by the enhanced local retention and sustained release afforded by the nanoparticle formulation. The effect of the method on cardiac function is profound and multifaceted, encompassing improvements in both structural and functional parameters of the heart.

Specifically, the method improves key cardiac functions and properties, including left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS), and reductions in left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVESD) and left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD). Infarct size, a critical measure of myocardial damage, is significantly reduced—by approximately 20%—in subjects treated with TT-10-loaded nanoparticles compared to controls. LVEF and LVFS, which reflect the heart’s pumping efficiency, are markedly improved and remain stable over time in treated subjects, whereas they tend to deteriorate in untreated or control-treated groups. Additional cardiac functions that benefit from the treatment include enhanced vascularity in the peri-infarct region, as evidenced by increased density of endothelial and smooth muscle cell-lined vessels, and reduced cardiomyocyte apoptosis during the acute phase following infarction.

Heart damage resulting from myocardial infarction is primarily caused by the occlusion of coronary arteries, leading to ischemia, necrosis of cardiomyocytes, and subsequent inflammatory and fibrotic responses. The disclosed method addresses this cascade by promoting the survival and proliferation of remaining cardiomyocytes through TT-10-mediated activation of the Hippo/YAP signaling pathway. TT-10, as a modulator of this pathway, inhibits the phosphorylation of YAP, allowing it to translocate to the nucleus and drive the expression of genes involved in cell cycle progression and anti-apoptotic signaling. By encapsulating TT-10 in PLGA nanoparticles, the invention ensures that this molecular intervention is delivered precisely where and when it is needed most, thereby maximizing therapeutic impact while minimizing off-target effects.

### ASPECTS

The invention encompasses a nanoparticle composition comprising a biocompatible polymer, specifically PLGA, encapsulating TT-10. The composition is designed for sustained release of TT-10 over a period of up to four weeks. A method for improving cardiac function is provided, involving the administration of the nanoparticle composition to a subject with myocardial infarction. The method increases the levels of markers associated with cardiomyocyte proliferation, such as Ki67, PH3, and nuclear YAP, while reducing markers of apoptosis. Administration is typically performed via intramyocardial injection into the peri-infarct region. The method also improves cardiac properties including LVEF, LVFS, infarct size, and vascularity.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope. Example 1 demonstrates that TT-10 promotes cell cycle activity in human induced pluripotent stem cell–derived cardiomyocytes (hiPSC-CMs), as evidenced by increased expression of Ki67, BrdU incorporation, histone H3 phosphorylation (PH3), and Aurora B. TT-10 also reduces apoptosis, as measured by TUNEL staining, and upregulates YAP signaling by decreasing phosphorylated YAP levels. Nanoparticle-mediated delivery of TT-10 enhances cellular uptake, as confirmed by fluorescent labeling, and provides sustained release over nine days. Echocardiographic assessments in a mouse MI model show that TT-10-loaded nanoparticles significantly improve LVEF and LVFS while reducing infarct size and heart weight/body weight ratio compared to controls.

Example 2 elaborates on the mechanism of action, highlighting that cardiomyocyte renewal occurs primarily through proliferation of preexisting cells. The Hippo pathway, via YAP and TAZ, regulates this process. TT-10 promotes cardiomyocyte survival and proliferation by inhibiting YAP phosphorylation. NP-mediated delivery enables low-dose, slow-release administration, resulting in increased Ki67, PH3, and nuclear YAP expression, reduced apoptosis, and enhanced angiogenesis in the peri-infarct region.

Example 3 details the preparation of PLGA nanoparticles using single-emulsion techniques, with characterization confirming particle size and TT-10 release kinetics. hiPSC-CMs are differentiated and maintained using standard protocols. Cellular uptake of NPs is verified by fluorescence microscopy. In a mouse MI model, intramyocardial injection of TT-10-NPs improves cardiac function as assessed by echocardiography and cardiac MRI. Infarct size is evaluated histologically, and immunofluorescence confirms increased cardiomyocyte proliferation and reduced apoptosis. Western blot analysis validates YAP pathway activation. A pig model further demonstrates the efficacy of TT-10-PLGA nanoparticles, with cMRI and LGE-cMRI showing reduced scar size and improved function. The examples collectively emphasize the versatility and therapeutic potential of the disclosed embodiments, with variations and modifications readily apparent to those skilled in the art.
