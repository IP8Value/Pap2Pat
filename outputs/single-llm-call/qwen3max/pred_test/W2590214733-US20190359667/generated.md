## FIELD

The present disclosure relates to the field of molecular biology and therapeutic protein engineering, specifically to engineered transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) monomers that function as dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. The invention further pertains to pharmaceutical compositions comprising such engineered monomers, methods for their production, and their use in the treatment of diseases and disorders associated with aberrant TGF-β signaling, including fibrosis, cancer, and other pathological conditions driven by excessive or dysregulated TGF-β activity.

## BACKGROUND

Transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) is a multifunctional cytokine that plays a central role in regulating cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, migration, and extracellular matrix production. The TGF-β superfamily includes several isoforms, with TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3 being the most prominent in mammals. These isoforms are synthesized as inactive precursors that require proteolytic cleavage and conformational changes to become biologically active. In their mature form, TGF-β ligands function as disulfide-linked homodimers that bind to a heteromeric complex of type I and type II serine/threonine kinase receptors on the cell surface. Upon ligand binding, the type II receptor (TβRII) phosphorylates and activates the type I receptor (TβRI), which then propagates the signal intracellularly via Smad-dependent and Smad-independent pathways.

While TGF-β signaling is essential for normal development, tissue homeostasis, and immune regulation, its dysregulation is implicated in a wide array of human diseases. In early stages of tumorigenesis, TGF-β acts as a tumor suppressor by inhibiting epithelial cell proliferation. However, in advanced cancers, TGF-β often switches to a tumor-promoting role, facilitating epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, immune evasion, angiogenesis, and metastasis. Moreover, excessive TGF-β activity is a key driver of fibrotic diseases affecting the lung, liver, kidney, and heart, where it stimulates the accumulation of extracellular matrix components leading to organ dysfunction. Despite decades of research and numerous clinical trials targeting the TGF-β pathway—using approaches such as neutralizing antibodies, soluble receptor traps, and small-molecule kinase inhibitors—no TGF-β-targeted therapy has yet been approved for clinical use, largely due to issues of specificity, bioavailability, toxicity, or insufficient efficacy.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides engineered TGF-β monomers that function as potent and specific dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. These engineered monomers are derived from naturally occurring TGF-β isoforms but have been structurally modified to abolish their ability to activate downstream signaling while retaining high-affinity binding to the TGF-β type II receptor (TβRII). By sequestering TβRII without recruiting or activating TβRI, these monomers effectively block endogenous TGF-β ligands from initiating signal transduction.

In one aspect, the invention features a recombinant TGF-β monomer that lacks the interchain disulfide bond-forming cysteine residue and includes a deletion of amino acid residues corresponding to the α-helical 3 region (also referred to as the “heel helix”), which is critical for both non-covalent dimerization and TβRI binding. In specific embodiments, the engineered monomer comprises a substitution of Cys-77 with serine and a deletion of residues 52–71 relative to the mature wild-type TGF-β sequence. Additional amino acid substitutions may be introduced to enhance solubility, stability, or receptor-binding affinity.

The invention further encompasses fusion proteins and pharmaceutical compositions comprising the engineered TGF-β monomers. These compositions may include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or stabilizers and are formulated for systemic or local administration. The engineered monomers may also be conjugated to polyethylene glycol (PEG) or other moieties to improve pharmacokinetic properties.

Methods of inhibiting TGF-β signaling are also provided, wherein the method comprises administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of an engineered TGF-β monomer as described herein. Such methods are particularly useful for treating or preventing diseases or disorders characterized by aberrant TGF-β signaling, including but not limited to cancer, fibrosis, Marfan syndrome, scleroderma, and chronic inflammatory conditions.

### III. Overview of Several Embodiments

The invention provides recombinant TGF-β monomers engineered to inhibit TGF-β signaling by acting as dominant negative antagonists. These monomers are designed to bind TβRII with high affinity but are incapable of recruiting TβRI or forming functional signaling complexes. In one embodiment, the engineered monomer is derived from human TGF-β2 and includes a C77S substitution to prevent covalent dimerization, along with a deletion of residues 52–71 that removes the α-helical 3 region responsible for non-covalent dimerization and TβRI interaction. This deletion is flanked by additional substitutions that increase the net charge of the monomer at physiological pH, thereby enhancing solubility.

Specific amino acid substitutions are introduced to confer high-affinity binding to TβRII. For example, L51R and A73K substitutions are included to optimize interactions with the receptor. In a preferred embodiment, the engineered monomer contains seven substitutions relative to wild-type TGF-β2: K25R, R26K, L51R, L89V, I92V, N94R, and T95K, resulting in a variant designated mmTGF-β2-7M. These substitutions correspond to residues found in high-affinity isoforms such as TGF-β1 and TGF-β3 and are located within or near the TβRII-binding interface. The resulting protein, which may be represented by SEQ ID NO: 8 or SEQ ID NO: 10, exhibits nanomolar affinity for TβRII, complete loss of signaling activity, and potent inhibitory function against all three major TGF-β isoforms.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: An Engineered TGF-β Monomer that Functions as a Dominant Negative to Block TGF-β Signaling

#### BACKGROUND

Wild-type TGF-β1 and TGF-β3 can be converted to monomeric forms (mTGF-β1 and mTGF-β3) by substituting the interchain cysteine (Cys-77) with serine. Although these monomers lack covalent dimerization, they retain significant signaling activity due to their ability to non-covalently dimerize via the amphipathic α-helix 3 (“heel helix”), which also mediates TβRI binding. To overcome this limitation, an engineered mini-monomeric TGF-β (mmTGF-β) was designed by deleting residues 52–71 (encompassing α-helix 3 and flanking regions) and introducing charge-enhancing substitutions to improve solubility. Additionally, key residues in the TβRII-binding interface were mutated to match those of high-affinity isoforms.

Bacterial expression constructs were generated for mmTGF-β1, mmTGF-β2, and mmTGF-β3. Among these, mmTGF-β2 showed superior folding and yield. To enhance TβRII affinity, seven substitutions (K25R, R26K, L51R, L89V, I92V, N94R, T95K) were introduced, yielding mmTGF-β2-7M. Proteins were expressed in *E. coli* as inclusion bodies, refolded in CHAPS-containing buffer, and purified by cation-exchange chromatography.

Structural characterization by X-ray crystallography confirmed that mmTGF-β2 and mmTGF-β2-7M retained the overall TGF-β fold, with the deleted region replaced by a flexible loop. NMR spectroscopy verified native-like secondary structure and increased dynamics in the loop region. Solubility assays demonstrated that mmTGF-β variants remained soluble at neutral pH, unlike wild-type dimers or full-length monomers. SPR analysis revealed that mmTGF-β2-7M bound TβRII with high affinity (K_D ~100 nM), comparable to TGF-β1 and TGF-β3.

Functional assays in HEK293 cells expressing a CAGA12-luciferase reporter showed that mmTGF-β2-7M had no intrinsic signaling activity even at micromolar concentrations. In competition assays, it potently inhibited TGF-β1, -β2, and -β3 signaling with IC₅₀ values of 68 nM, 19 nM, and 21 nM, respectively. Mechanistic studies using SPR, AUC, and TR-FRET confirmed that mmTGF-β2-7M binds TβRII but cannot recruit TβRI or form higher-order signaling complexes. AUC indicated weakened self-association (K_D ~40 μM vs. ~4 μM for mTGF-β3), while TR-FRET showed no ternary complex formation.

These results demonstrate that mmTGF-β2-7M functions as a dominant negative inhibitor by sequestering TβRII without enabling TβRI recruitment, thereby blocking signal initiation by endogenous ligands.

#### Discussion

TGF-β signaling drives numerous pathological processes, yet clinical translation of inhibitors has been challenging. The engineered mmTGF-β2-7M offers several advantages: high specificity for TβRII, small size (~10 kDa) for improved tissue penetration, intrinsic stability from four intramolecular disulfides, and water solubility at physiological pH. Unlike kinase inhibitors or antibodies, it exploits the natural ligand-receptor interface, potentially reducing off-target effects. Its dominant negative mechanism—binding TβRII without activating signaling—is uniquely enabled by structural removal of the heel helix, which abrogates both dimerization and TβRI engagement.

The cooperative assembly model of TGF-β receptor complexes explains why even weakly dimerizing monomers like mTGF-β3 remain potent agonists: receptor binding stabilizes transient dimers. By contrast, mmTGF-β2-7M’s inability to form the composite TβRI-binding interface ensures it remains inert. This design principle may be extended to other TGF-β superfamily members, enabling tailored inhibitors for activins, BMPs, or AMH. Given its potency, specificity, and favorable biophysical properties, mmTGF-β2-7M represents a promising therapeutic candidate for fibrosis, cancer, and other TGF-β-driven diseases.
