# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of catalytic conversion of bio-derived alcohols, particularly ethanol, into hydrocarbon blendstocks suitable for use as transportation fuels or chemical feedstocks. More specifically, the invention relates to a method and catalyst system for the direct conversion of aqueous ethanol streams—including those derived from fermentation processes without extensive purification—into high-value hydrocarbons comprising C3+ olefins, paraffins, and aromatic compounds, with concomitant reduction in light hydrocarbon (C2) byproducts and benzene content. The invention further encompasses the design, synthesis, and application of heterobimetallic zeolite catalysts, particularly indium-vanadium co-exchanged ZSM-5 (InV-ZSM-5), which exhibit enhanced selectivity toward desirable liquid hydrocarbons and improved durability under reaction conditions compared to monometallic or proton-form zeolite catalysts.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Current methods for converting bio-alcohols such as ethanol into hydrocarbon fuels typically rely on dehydration over acidic zeolites like H-ZSM-5 to produce ethylene, followed by oligomerization and aromatization steps to form higher hydrocarbons. However, these conventional approaches suffer from significant limitations. A primary drawback is the excessive production of ethylene (C2 hydrocarbons), which represents a low-value product stream when the objective is to generate liquid fuel-range hydrocarbons (C5+). Moreover, the dehydration step is highly endothermic, requiring substantial energy input, and often leads to rapid catalyst deactivation due to coking.

A critical obstacle in practical implementation is the requirement for highly purified, anhydrous ethanol as feedstock. Fermentation-derived ethanol streams typically contain 5–15% water even after initial distillation, and complete dewatering via molecular sieves or extractive distillation is energy-intensive and costly. Prior art suggests that water in the feed adversely affects catalyst performance, particularly by suppressing aromatization and promoting undesired side reactions. Consequently, integration of ethanol production and upgrading has remained economically unfeasible at scale.

Additionally, hydrocarbon products derived from alcohol conversion often contain elevated levels of benzene, a known carcinogen regulated in gasoline blends worldwide. Conventional zeolite catalysts, especially those with strong Brønsted acidity like H-ZSM-5, tend to favor benzene formation through cyclization and dehydrogenation pathways. Reducing benzene content without sacrificing overall yield or octane number presents a significant technical challenge. Thus, there exists a long-felt need for a catalytic system capable of efficiently converting wet ethanol directly into a high-octane, low-benzene, C3+-rich hydrocarbon blendstock with minimal preprocessing and extended catalyst lifetime.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel catalytic method for converting bio-alcohols, particularly ethanol, into valuable hydrocarbon products without requiring prior purification of the alcohol feedstream. This is achieved through the use of a heterobimetallic zeolite catalyst, specifically indium-vanadium exchanged ZSM-5 (InV-ZSM-5), which enables high selectivity toward C3+ hydrocarbons while minimizing ethylene (C2) formation. The invention further discloses that aqueous ethanol streams containing up to 90% water can be directly fed to the conversion process without detrimental effects on product distribution or catalyst stability.

In one embodiment, the invention employs a two-stage catalytic process wherein a metal-loaded zeolite catalyst facilitates initial deoxygenation and oligomerization, followed by a benzene alkylation catalyst that converts residual benzene into higher alkylated aromatics such as toluene, ethylbenzene, and xylenes, thereby reducing benzene content in the final hydrocarbon fraction. Alternatively, a single-stage process utilizes a physical mixture or co-exchanged formulation combining both functionalities within a single reactor.

The metal-loaded zeolite catalyst comprises a microporous or hierarchical ZSM-5 framework ion-exchanged with both indium and vanadium cations, wherein the synergistic interaction between these metals modulates acid site density, suppresses ethylene formation, enhances aromatization, and improves resistance to coking. The benzene alkylation catalyst may be a separate zeolite-based material, such as a modified beta or mordenite zeolite, or integrated into the same particle via dual functionalization.

Reactor embodiments include fixed-bed, fluidized-bed, or staged reactors operating in continuous or batch mode, with temperature ranges of 250–450°C and pressures from atmospheric to 30 bar. The resulting hydrocarbon product exhibits high research octane number (>100), low benzene content (<1 wt%), and suitability as a gasoline blendstock or petrochemical feedstock.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

As used herein, the term “about” refers to a variation of up to ±10% of the stated numerical value. The term “alcohol” denotes organic compounds containing one or more hydroxyl (–OH) groups attached to a saturated carbon atom. Bio-alcohols are alcohols produced from renewable biomass sources via biological or thermochemical routes; ethanol derived from corn, sugarcane, or cellulosic fermentation is a primary example. Other alcohols include methanol, propanol, butanol, and mixtures thereof, though ethanol is preferred due to its abundance and compatibility with existing infrastructure.

Fermentation streams refer to the crude output of microbial conversion of sugars into ethanol, typically comprising 5–15 wt% ethanol, 85–95 wt% water, and trace impurities such as acetaldehyde, methanol, diacetyl, ethyl acetate, and acetic acid (collectively <0.5 wt%). These streams may be partially concentrated via flash distillation to 30–60 wt% ethanol but need not undergo complete dewatering. The concentration of fermentation streams used in the present invention ranges from 10% to 100% ethanol by weight, with no adverse impact on conversion efficiency or product selectivity.

Dewatering of fermentation streams, if performed, may involve conventional techniques such as distillation, membrane separation, or adsorption, but the invention explicitly obviates the necessity of achieving anhydrous ethanol. The term “hydrocarbon” refers to compounds consisting exclusively of hydrogen and carbon atoms. Saturated hydrocarbons include alkanes, which may be linear (e.g., n-pentane) or branched (e.g., isooctane). Other hydrocarbon products encompass olefins (e.g., propylene, butenes), naphthenes (cycloalkanes), and aromatics (e.g., toluene, xylenes).

The hydrocarbon product serves as a fuel or blendstock for gasoline, exhibiting high octane numbers and favorable volatility characteristics. Its composition typically includes 3–10% paraffins, 20–30% iso-paraffins, 5–10% olefins, 5–10% naphthenes, 50–70% aromatics, and minor unidentified components. Applications extend beyond transportation fuels to include solvents, lubricants, and chemical intermediates. The process demonstrates versatility by accommodating variable feed compositions and operating conditions.

The core innovation lies in the metal-loaded zeolite catalyst, specifically InV-ZSM-5, prepared by sequential ion exchange of NH₄-ZSM-5 with vanadium(III) chloride followed by indium(III) nitrate, then calcined at 500°C. This catalyst contains 0.6 wt% vanadium and 1.6 wt% indium, confirmed by elemental analysis. Concurrently, a benzene alkylation catalyst—such as a phosphorus-modified ZSM-5 or a gallium-exchanged beta zeolite—is employed either in a second reactor stage or as a blended component.

In the two-stage process, the first stage utilizes InV-ZSM-5 at 250–450°C to convert ethanol into a hydrocarbon mixture rich in C3–C5 olefins and aromatics, with minimal C2. The effluent, containing trace benzene, is then passed to a second stage containing the alkylation catalyst at 200–350°C, where benzene reacts with light olefins (e.g., ethylene, propylene) to form alkylated benzenes. Reaction temperatures are maintained between 250°C and 450°C in the first stage and 200–350°C in the second, with pressures from 1 to 30 bar. Reactor designs include fixed beds with catalyst particles sized 250–500 μm, operated continuously with weight hourly space velocities (WHSV) of 1–10 h⁻¹.

Alternatively, a single-stage process employs a homogeneous mixture of InV-ZSM-5 and benzene alkylation catalyst in a 1:1 to 10:1 weight ratio. This configuration simultaneously achieves deoxygenation, oligomerization, aromatization, and benzene alkylation in one reactor. The resulting alkylated benzene product includes toluene, ethylbenzene, cumene, and xylenes, with total benzene content reduced to less than 1 wt% in the hydrocarbon fraction, satisfying regulatory standards.

The term “reduced in benzene content” means the benzene concentration in the hydrocarbon product is ≤1 wt%, preferably ≤0.5 wt%, as measured by gas chromatography. Integration with fermentation facilities enables direct coupling of biomass-to-ethanol and ethanol-to-hydrocarbon processes. Biomass reactors convert lignocellulosic or starchy feedstocks into fermentable sugars, which are then converted to ethanol via yeast or bacterial fermentation. Alternative sugar sources include molasses, syngas-derived ethanol, or waste-derived alcohols.

The zeolite portion of the catalyst is ZSM-5 with a silica-to-alumina ratio of 20–200, preferably 23. It possesses a MFI topology with micropores (~0.55 nm), optionally modified to include mesopores (2–50 nm) via desilication or templating for improved diffusion. Cationic species include H⁺, Na⁺, or NH₄⁺ prior to metal exchange. Catalytically active metals include indium and vanadium, loaded via aqueous ion exchange. Trivalent metal ions such as In³⁺ and V³⁺/V⁵⁺ are incorporated at 0.5–2.0 wt% each. Zeolite catalysts may also include combinations of main group (e.g., Ga³⁺, Al³⁺) or lanthanide (e.g., La³⁺, Ce³⁺) trivalent ions.

Synthesis involves forming a slurry of zeolite in metal salt solution, refluxing at 80°C for 16 h, filtering, drying at 105°C, and calcining at 500°C (1°C/min ramp). Catalyst forms include extrudates, pellets, coatings on monoliths (e.g., honeycomb ceramics), or fibrous meshes. Support materials comprise refractory oxides (alumina, silica) or corrugated metal sheets in layered configurations.

Examples demonstrate that InV-ZSM-5 yields 33% C5+ liquid with RON 105.7, while benzene content is reduced below detection limits when combined with alkylation catalyst. The scope encompasses all variations in metal loading, zeolite structure, and process integration, affirming broad industrial applicability.

### Example 1

A mixed catalyst was prepared by physically blending InV-ZSM-5 (0.6 wt% V, 1.6 wt% In) with a phosphorus-modified H-Beta zeolite (P/Beta) in a 4:1 weight ratio. This mixture (0.2 g, 250–500 μm) was loaded into a quartz reactor and activated under helium at 450°C. Aqueous ethanol (40 wt%) was fed at WHSV = 2.0 h⁻¹ and 350°C. The product stream, analyzed by GC/MS and SGS, showed 98% ethanol conversion, C2 selectivity of 8%, C3+ selectivity of 85%, and benzene content of 0.3 wt%. The liquid fraction had RON = 103 and contained 58% aromatics, predominantly toluene and xylenes.

### Example 2

Catalyst loading was configured as a dual-bed system: top bed (0.15 g) of InV-ZSM-5, bottom bed (0.05 g) of Ga-ZSM-5 alkylation catalyst. Pure ethanol was fed at WHSV = 4.0 h⁻¹, 350°C. Product analysis revealed C2 selectivity of 7%, C3–C4 olefins at 20%, C3–C4 paraffins at 34%, and C5+ at 36%. Carbon atom distribution plotted against chain length showed a sharp decline at C2, peak at C6–C8 (aromatics), and negligible C9+, confirming selective oligomerization-aromatization without heavy coke precursors. Benzene was undetectable (<0.1 wt%).
