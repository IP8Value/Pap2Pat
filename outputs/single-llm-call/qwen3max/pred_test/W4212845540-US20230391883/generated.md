# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to antibodies for cancer treatment. More specifically, the invention provides anti-TIGIT monoclonal antibodies, including humanized and fully human forms, that exhibit high affinity for human TIGIT and possess functional Fc domains capable of engaging Fcγ receptors. The invention further encompasses methods of treating cancer by administering such anti-TIGIT antibodies alone or in combination with other immunotherapeutic agents, particularly anti-PD-1 antibodies. The disclosed antibodies are designed to modulate the tumor microenvironment through multiple mechanisms of action, including blockade of TIGIT–ligand interactions, depletion of immunosuppressive regulatory T cells (Tregs), activation of natural killer (NK) cells and antigen-presenting cells, and induction of trogocytosis-mediated removal of TIGIT from the surface of immune cells. These therapeutic antibodies are particularly suited for the treatment of solid tumors and hematological malignancies where TIGIT is overexpressed on tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes.

## BACKGROUND

TIGIT (T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains), also known as VSIG9, VSTM3, or WUCAM, is a type I transmembrane protein belonging to the immunoglobulin superfamily of receptors. It is expressed on activated CD8+ and CD4+ T cells, natural killer (NK) cells, and regulatory T cells (Tregs) in both murine and human immune systems. Structurally, TIGIT comprises an extracellular immunoglobulin variable (IgV)-like domain, a type I transmembrane region, and a cytoplasmic tail containing an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM) and an immunoglobulin tail tyrosine (ITT)-like motif. This structural configuration enables TIGIT to transmit inhibitory signals upon ligand engagement.

TIGIT expression is dynamically regulated and is significantly upregulated in the tumor microenvironment across multiple cancer types. Its expression is particularly enriched on exhausted CD8+ T cells and tumor-infiltrating Tregs, often co-expressed with other immune checkpoint molecules such as PD-1, TIM-3, and LAG-3. Functionally, TIGIT exerts immunosuppressive effects through several mechanisms: it delivers direct inhibitory signals to T cells and NK cells via its ITIM and ITT motifs; it competes with the activating receptor DNAM-1 (CD226) for binding to shared ligands, thereby disrupting stimulatory signaling; it enhances the immunosuppressive function and stability of Tregs; and it promotes the generation of tolerogenic dendritic cells through reverse signaling via PVR on antigen-presenting cells.

The primary ligands for TIGIT are poliovirus receptor (PVR, also known as CD155) and PVR-related 2 (PVRL2, also known as CD112). TIGIT binds PVR with high affinity and PVRL2 with lower but significant affinity. Both ligands are frequently overexpressed on tumor cells and antigen-presenting cells within the tumor microenvironment, facilitating immune evasion. Upon binding, TIGIT initiates intracellular signaling cascades that suppress T cell receptor (TCR) and NK cell activation pathways, leading to reduced cytokine production, impaired cytotoxicity, and diminished proliferation.

In the context of immune responses, TIGIT serves as a critical negative regulator that maintains immune homeostasis but can be co-opted by tumors to evade immune surveillance. In cancer, elevated TIGIT expression on tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes correlates with poor clinical outcomes and resistance to existing immunotherapies. Similarly, in chronic viral infections such as HIV and CMV, persistent antigen exposure leads to TIGIT upregulation on virus-specific T cells, contributing to T cell exhaustion and impaired viral control.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 axis, such as pembrolizumab and nivolumab, have revolutionized cancer treatment. However, a significant proportion of patients exhibit limited response or develop acquired resistance to single-agent therapy. This limitation has spurred interest in targeting alternative or complementary checkpoints like TIGIT. Preclinical and early clinical data suggest that TIGIT blockade can restore T cell and NK cell function, particularly when combined with PD-1 inhibition. Given the frequent co-expression of TIGIT and PD-1 on exhausted T cells, dual blockade represents a rational strategy to overcome resistance and enhance anti-tumor immunity. The present invention addresses this need by providing anti-TIGIT antibodies optimized for both checkpoint blockade and Fc-mediated effector functions, enabling superior therapeutic efficacy in combination regimens.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides anti-TIGIT antibodies for the treatment of cancer. In one embodiment, the antibody is a humanized monoclonal antibody comprising a heavy chain variable region (VH) and a light chain variable region (VL) that specifically bind to human TIGIT with high affinity. The VH domain includes complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) derived from a murine precursor antibody, grafted onto a human IgG1 framework. The VL domain similarly comprises murine CDRs integrated into a human kappa light chain framework. The antibody binds to human TIGIT with an equilibrium dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 0.135 nM, as determined by surface plasmon resonance.

The antibody of the invention comprises a human IgG1 constant region, which confers full effector functions, including binding to Fcγ receptors (FcγRI, FcγRIIA, FcγRIIB, and FcγRIIIA) and the ability to mediate antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP), and trogocytosis. This functional Fc domain is essential for the antibody’s optimal anti-tumor activity, as demonstrated by comparative studies with an Fc-silent variant (BGB-A1217MF) bearing mutations (L234A, L235A, P329A) that abrogate FcγR binding.

The invention further provides a method of treating cancer in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering a therapeutically effective amount of the anti-TIGIT antibody described herein. In a preferred embodiment, the method further comprises co-administering an anti-PD-1 antibody, such as pembrolizumab (BGB-A317), nivolumab, or a biosimilar thereof. The anti-PD-1 antibody may be a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody that blocks the interaction between PD-1 and its ligands PD-L1 and PD-L2.

Specific structural embodiments of the anti-TIGIT antibody include the humanized antibody designated BGB-A1217, which comprises the VH sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO:1 and the VL sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO:2, or variants thereof with conservative amino acid substitutions. The anti-PD-1 antibody may comprise the heavy and light chain variable regions of pembrolizumab or nivolumab.

The method is applicable to a wide range of cancer types, including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC), esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), cervical cancer, melanoma, renal cell carcinoma, and colorectal cancer. The anti-TIGIT antibody is administered at a dose ranging from 1 mg/kg to 20 mg/kg, typically 3 mg/kg to 10 mg/kg, every three to five days (Q3D to Q5D) or weekly (QW). When used in combination, the anti-PD-1 antibody is administered at a standard dose (e.g., 200 mg pembrolizumab every three weeks).

The method may further include administration of chemotherapy or chemoradiotherapy. Chemotherapeutic agents include platinum-based drugs (e.g., cisplatin, carboplatin), taxanes (e.g., paclitaxel, docetaxel), and antimetabolites (e.g., 5-fluorouracil). Chemoradiotherapy involves concurrent administration of radiation and chemotherapy, commonly used in locally advanced cancers. The dosing regimen for the anti-TIGIT antibody remains consistent whether used as monotherapy or in combination with other modalities.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

As used herein, “conservative amino acid substitutions” refer to the replacement of one amino acid with another having similar physicochemical properties, such as hydrophobicity, charge, or size, without substantially altering the biological activity of the protein. Examples include substitution of leucine for isoleucine, aspartic acid for glutamic acid, or serine for threonine.

Technical and scientific terms used herein have the meanings commonly understood by those skilled in the art unless otherwise defined. The singular forms “a,” “an,” and “the” include plural referents unless the context clearly dictates otherwise. The term “or” is inclusive unless specified otherwise, and “and/or” means one or all of the listed elements.

The terms “comprise,” “comprising,” “include,” and “including” are open-ended and do not exclude additional, unrecited elements. “TIGIT” refers to the human T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains, including isoforms and allelic variants.

“Administration,” “administering,” “treating,” and “treatment” refer to the provision of a therapeutic agent to a subject to prevent, ameliorate, or cure a disease or condition. A “subject” is a mammal, preferably a human, diagnosed with or at risk of developing cancer.

An “antibody” is an immunoglobulin molecule comprising at least one heavy chain and one light chain, capable of specifically binding an antigen. A “monoclonal antibody” is derived from a single clone of B cells and exhibits uniform specificity. Monoclonal antibodies may be produced by hybridoma technology or recombinant DNA methods.

The “hypervariable region” or complementarity-determining region (CDR) is the portion of the antibody variable domain responsible for antigen binding. The “framework” (FR) residues provide structural support for the CDRs. An “antibody fragment” or “antigen-binding fragment” includes Fab, Fab’, F(ab’)2, scFv, and other portions retaining antigen-binding capability.

Antibody “specificity” denotes the ability to bind a particular epitope with high selectivity. “pH-dependent binding” refers to differential affinity at varying pH levels, typically higher affinity at neutral pH (e.g., pH 7.4) and lower affinity at acidic pH (e.g., pH 6.0), which may facilitate recycling and reduce target-mediated drug disposition. Binding affinity under different pH conditions is measured using surface plasmon resonance (SPR).

A “human antibody” is an antibody wherein the entire sequence is of human origin. A “humanized antibody” is derived from a non-human antibody (e.g., murine) by grafting the CDRs onto human framework regions, optionally with back-mutations to retain affinity.

“Cancer” or “tumor” encompasses malignant neoplasms of any histological type, including carcinomas, sarcomas, lymphomas, and leukemias. “Cancer treatment” includes reduction in tumor burden, prevention of metastasis, and prolongation of survival.

A “therapeutically effective amount” is the quantity of an agent sufficient to achieve a desired therapeutic effect without undue toxicity. This amount is determined based on factors including disease severity, patient weight, and route of administration.

### Anti-TIGIT Antibodies

The invention provides anti-TIGIT antibodies that specifically bind human TIGIT and block its interaction with PVR and PVRL2. These antibodies are characterized by high binding affinity (Kd ≤ 1 nM), potent ligand blockade (IC50 ≤ 5 nM), and functional Fc domains enabling engagement with Fcγ receptors. Exemplary antibodies include BGB-A1217, which comprises VH and VL sequences derived from murine clone mu1217, humanized via CDR grafting. Mutated variants may include substitutions in CDRs or framework regions to improve stability, solubility, or pharmacokinetics, provided antigen-binding specificity is retained. These antibodies are useful for depleting TIGIT-expressing Tregs, activating NK cells and myeloid cells, and inducing trogocytosis in the tumor microenvironment.

### Anti-PD1 Antibodies

Anti-PD1 antibodies for use in combination therapy include clinically approved agents such as pembrolizumab and nivolumab, or biosimilars thereof. These antibodies block the PD-1/PD-L1 interaction, reversing T cell exhaustion. They are typically human IgG4 antibodies engineered to minimize Fc effector functions, focusing activity on checkpoint blockade.

### Further Alteration of the Framework of Fc Region

The Fc region of the anti-TIGIT antibody may be altered to modulate effector functions. For instance, amino acid residues may be replaced to enhance or reduce binding to Fcγ receptors or C1q. U.S. Pat. Nos. 5,624,821 and 5,648,260 describe methods to alter Fc-mediated effector functions. To reduce complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), mutations such as those in U.S. Pat. No. 6,194,551 (e.g., K322A) may be introduced. PCT Publication WO 94/29351 discloses modifications to alter complement fixation. Alternatively, amino acids may be replaced with allotypic variants to optimize pharmacokinetics. In a preferred embodiment, the antibody is of IgG1 subclass and kappa isotype, preserving strong ADCC activity. To further enhance ADCC, residues may be modified per PCT Publication WO 00/42072 (e.g., S298A, E333A, K334A). Binding sites on human IgG1 for FcγRI, FcγRII, FcγRIII, and FcRn are well-characterized and may be targeted for specific modulation.

### Antibody Production

Anti-TIGIT antibodies and fragments are produced by recombinant expression, chemical synthesis, or enzymatic digestion. Full-length monoclonal antibodies are obtained via hybridoma fusion or recombinant methods. Host cells, including prokaryotic (e.g., *E. coli*) and eukaryotic (e.g., CHO, HEK293) cells, harbor expression vectors encoding antibody chains. Polynucleotides encoding the VH and VL domains exhibit at least 90% sequence identity to reference sequences. Expression vectors contain promoters (e.g., CMV, EF-1α), enhancers, and other regulatory elements to drive high-level expression in mammalian cells.

### Methods of Detection and Diagnosis

The presence of TIGIT in a biological sample (e.g., tumor biopsy, blood) is detected using labeled anti-TIGIT antibodies in immunoassays (e.g., ELISA, flow cytometry). Disorders associated with TIGIT overexpression, such as cancer or chronic infection, are diagnosed by comparing TIGIT levels in test cells to control cells from healthy tissue.

### Methods of Treatment

TIGIT-associated disorders, particularly cancer, are treated by administering an anti-TIGIT antibody or fragment. Administration routes include parenteral (intravenous, subcutaneous), intrapulmonary, and intranasal. Dosing schedules vary (e.g., 3–10 mg/kg Q5D). The antibody is formulated alone or with other agents, such as anti-PD1 antibodies (e.g., pembrolizumab, nivolumab). A therapeutically effective amount is determined empirically, with initial candidate dosages based on preclinical data. Repeated administrations maintain therapeutic levels. Combination therapy enhances efficacy by targeting non-redundant immune checkpoints.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Formulations

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the anti-TIGIT antibody and one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers (e.g., buffers, stabilizers, surfactants). Formulations include solutions, lyophilized powders, and sustained-release preparations. Sterility is ensured for parenteral administration.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Kits

Pharmaceutically acceptable compositions are provided in dosage forms suitable for the intended route (e.g., vials for IV infusion). Kits may include the antibody, instructions for use, and diluents.

## EXAMPLE

### Example 1: Generation of Anti-TIGIT Monoclonal Antibody

Anti-TIGIT monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) were generated using hybridoma fusion technology. Mice were immunized with recombinant human TIGIT fused to murine IgG2a Fc (TIGIT-mIgG2a) and adjuvant. Splenocytes were fused with murine myeloma SP2/0 cells. Hybridoma clones were screened by ELISA for binding to TIGIT and by FACS using cells expressing full-length human or monkey TIGIT. Clones with high binding activity were verified in functional assays. Stable hybridomas were adapted to serum-free medium, and mAbs were purified by protein A chromatography.

### Example 2: Cloning and Sequence Analysis of TIGIT Antibodies

VH and Vk regions of murine mAbs were cloned and sequenced. Sequence homology analysis grouped mAbs into families based on germline gene usage. Clone mu1217 was selected for humanization due to high affinity and functional activity.

### Example 3: Affinity Determination of Purified Murine Anti-TIGIT Antibodies by SPR

Binding kinetics of purified murine antibodies were characterized by surface plasmon resonance (SPR). Association (Kon) and dissociation (Koff) rates were measured, and Kd values were calculated. Mu1217 exhibited high affinity (Kd = 0.135 nM).

### Example 4: Humanization of the Murine Anti-Human TIGIT mAb mu1217

Mu1217 was humanized by CDR-grafting onto human germline IgG1 and kappa frameworks. The humanized antibody, hu1217-2-2 (BGB-A1217), was expressed in CHO cells and purified. It retained high binding affinity and functional activity on primary human immune cells.

### Example 5: Binding Activities of Different Versions of 1217 to Native TIGIT

Binding to native TIGIT on living cells was evaluated by flow cytometry. BGB-A1217 showed strong binding to TIGIT-expressing T cells and NK cells.

### Example 6: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies Block the Interactions of TIGIT with its Ligands PVR and PVR-L2

Ligand blockade was assessed by competition assay. BGB-A1217 blocked TIGIT-PVR and TIGIT-PVR-L2 interactions with IC50 values of 4.53 nM and 1.69 nM, respectively.

### Example 7: Activation of CMV-Specific Human T Cells by Anti-TIGIT Antibodies

CMV-specific T cells co-cultured with pp65-pulsed HCT116 cells showed enhanced IFN-γ secretion upon BGB-A1217 treatment, indicating restored T cell function.

### Example 8: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies Enhanced NK Cell-Mediated Cytotoxicity

BGB-A1217 promoted NK-mediated cytotoxicity against PVR-expressing SK-MES-1 cells, as measured by LDH release and CD107a expression.

### Example 9: Anti-TIGIT Antibodies can Reduce the Surface Expression of TIGIT Receptor Via FcγR-Mediated Trogocytosis

Trogocytosis was demonstrated by co-culturing Jurkat/TIGIT cells with HEK293 cells expressing FcγRs. BGB-A1217, but not BGB-A1217MF, induced TIGIT removal from T cells in an FcγR-dependent manner. FcγRIIB played a key role. ADCC assays using human PBMCs showed moderate Treg reduction with BGB-A1217, but no significant lysis of total T cells. CDC assays confirmed no complement-mediated lysis. pH-dependent binding studies revealed higher affinity at pH 6.0, suggesting potential for improved pharmacokinetics. Clinical trials in NSCLC, NPC, ESCC, and cervical cancer demonstrated tumor regression with BGB-A1217 and BGB-A317 combination. Early results support the therapeutic potential of this regimen.
