# DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to recombinant protein production, particularly in the context of mammalian cell-based biomanufacturing systems. Recombinant protein therapeutics, including monoclonal antibodies, enzymes, hormones, and cytokines, represent a cornerstone of modern biopharmaceutical development. Efficient, scalable, and stable production of these proteins is essential for commercial viability and regulatory compliance. Among available host systems, mammalian cells—especially Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells—are widely preferred due to their capacity for proper post-translational modifications, such as glycosylation, which are critical for the biological activity, stability, and safety of therapeutic proteins.

CHO cells have become the industry standard for recombinant protein production owing to their well-characterized biology, robust growth in suspension culture, adaptability to serum-free media, and extensive regulatory acceptance. The CHO-DHFR (dihydrofolate reductase) expression system, often coupled with methotrexate (MTX) selection, is among the most commonly employed platforms for achieving high-level recombinant gene amplification and protein expression. In this system, transfected cells are subjected to increasing concentrations of MTX, which inhibits endogenous DHFR activity and thereby selects for cells that have amplified the recombinant DHFR gene along with the gene of interest, typically located on the same expression vector.

Despite the widespread use of CHO cells and the DHFR/MTX system, a persistent challenge in cell line development is the inherent genomic instability of CHO cells, which can lead to unstable recombinant protein production over time. This instability manifests as a progressive decline in both recombinant gene copy number and protein productivity during prolonged culture, especially in the absence of selective pressure. Such instability arises from chromosomal rearrangements, homologous recombination events, and epigenetic silencing mechanisms, including promoter methylation. Studies have reported that between 8% and 63% of recombinant CHO (rCHO) cell lines exhibit unstable production phenotypes under standard cultivation conditions, significantly impeding efficient cell line development pipelines.

This problem is exacerbated by conventional clone screening methods, which typically assess only initial productivity and growth characteristics without predictive insight into long-term stability. As a result, significant time and resources are invested in expanding and characterizing clones that ultimately prove unsuitable for manufacturing due to productivity loss during scale-up or extended culture. Previous efforts to address this issue have included monitoring apoptosis markers, measuring recombinant mRNA levels via Northern blotting, or assessing transgene integration sites; however, none have provided a robust, early-stage, transcriptome-based method for reliably predicting stability across diverse clones.

The present invention solves this long-standing problem by providing a novel method for the early identification of suitable candidate cell clones that are likely to exhibit stable recombinant protein production over extended cultivation periods. This method is based on the discovery that the expression levels of specific endogenous genes in CHO cells—measured prior to or shortly after transfection—correlate strongly with the future stability of recombinant protein output. By analyzing the expression profiles of a defined set of marker genes, one can distinguish, at an early stage, between clones that will maintain consistent productivity (stable clones) and those that will experience significant decline (unstable clones).

The method comprises determining the expression level of RNA corresponding to one or more specific endogenous genes in at least two candidate CHO cell clones. While the method may be applied using a single gene, preferred embodiments involve the analysis of multiple genes to enhance predictive accuracy. The genes of interest include, but are not limited to, *E130203B14*, *BX842664.2/Hist1h3c*, *Ptpre*, *Cspg4*, and *Fgfr2*. Notably, the expression of these genes has been shown to be unaffected by the presence or absence of MTX in the culture medium, making them reliable indicators independent of selection conditions. An optional gene, *Vsnl1*, may also be included, though experimental data indicate it may not consistently differentiate stable from unstable clones.

Expression levels are preferably determined using quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR), though other sensitive and specific nucleic acid detection methods may be employed. The RNA analyzed is endogenous to the host cell and not derived from the recombinant construct. Critically, the method requires a minimum of two cell clones for comparative analysis, though in practice, dozens or even hundreds of clones may be screened in parallel to identify the most promising candidates. The analysis can be performed prior to transfection—using untransfected parental or subcloned host cells—as a pre-screening step to select a favorable cellular background, or it may be conducted after transfection but before extensive expansion or stability testing.

The type of recombinant protein being produced is not relevant to the method’s applicability; however, antibodies—particularly immunoglobulin G (IgG)—are preferred embodiments due to their commercial importance. Ideally, all clones under comparison express the same recombinant protein to eliminate confounding variables, though the method remains effective even when comparing clones expressing different proteins, provided the marker gene expression is interpreted within appropriate experimental contexts.

Expression levels may be determined in parallel across all clones under identical culture conditions—preferably during the early log phase of growth—to minimize environmental variability. The host cells are typically CHO-derived, with CHO-K1 being a particularly preferred cell line due to its widespread use and well-documented performance in bioproduction. RNA expression values are processed through log2 transformation and normalized against stable reference genes (e.g., *Actb* and *Gapdh*) to enable accurate cross-clone comparisons.

Statistical analysis, such as Welch’s t-test or multivariate modeling, is then applied to identify significant differences in gene expression between clones. Clones exhibiting expression profiles associated with stability—such as upregulation of *Fgfr2* and *Hist1h3c* or downregulation of *Ptpre* and *Cspg4*—are selected for further expansion. The “best” candidate clone is defined as the one whose combined expression profile across multiple marker genes most closely aligns with the stability-associated signature identified through empirical validation.

In particularly preferred embodiments, the expression values of multiple genes are combined using multivariate statistical techniques such as principal component analysis (PCA). A three-dimensional representation of the first three principal components often reveals clear clustering of stable versus unstable clones, enabling robust classification even before long-term stability data are available. Unsupervised clustering algorithms, such as k-nearest neighbor or k-means, can further automate the selection process.

Once a suitable candidate clone is identified, it undergoes further expansion under standard bioprocessing conditions, optionally with MTX selection to maintain transgene amplification. The entire method is conducted ex vivo or in vitro and does not involve human subjects or in vivo manipulation.

The invention also encompasses host cells for recombinant protein expression wherein the expression of one or more of the aforementioned marker genes has been artificially modified—either through overexpression or downregulation—to enhance stability. Such engineered host cells may serve as improved platforms for future transfections. The recombinant protein is encoded by a heterologous gene introduced via transfection, and preferred proteins include monoclonal antibodies, Fc-fusion proteins, and other biotherapeutics.

As used herein, the term “comprising” is open-ended and permits the inclusion of additional elements not explicitly recited. Articles such as “a” and “an” are understood to mean “at least one” unless context clearly indicates otherwise. A “suitable candidate cell clone” refers to a clonally derived population of host cells that, based on its endogenous gene expression profile, is predicted to maintain stable recombinant protein production over extended culture periods.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: General Outline of the Experiment

To establish a transcriptome profile predictive of stability, six high-producing CHO clones were selected following standard transfection and MTX amplification protocols. These clones were split into two parallel cultures—one maintained with MTX and one without—and cultivated for ten weeks. Samples were collected at weeks 1, 2, 9, and 10 for RNA and DNA isolation. Transcriptome analysis via DNA microarray revealed 295 differentially expressed genes between stable and unstable clones. From these, 14 top candidates were validated by RT-qPCR, and five (*E130203B14*, *BX842664.2/Hist1h3c*, *Ptpre*, *Cspg4*, *Fgfr2*) were confirmed as MTX-independent markers suitable for early prediction.

### Example 2: Detailed Outline of the Experiment

An expression vector containing the recombinant gene of interest and a DHFR selection marker was constructed and linearized. This vector was transfected into CHO-der1 host cells via nucleofection. Following recovery, cells were subjected to antibiotic selection and stepwise MTX amplification to enrich for high producers. Six resulting clones were isolated using ClonePix FL technology. Each clone was then split into two culture conditions: with and without MTX. Cultures were maintained in serum-free media under identical environmental conditions for ten weeks, with sampling at defined intervals to assess productivity and molecular characteristics.

### Example 3: Cell Culture

The CHO-der1 host cell line, derived from ECACC-sourced CHO-der cells and adapted to in-house serum-free media, was used throughout. Nucleofection was performed using Lonza’s Nucleofector system with a CHO-specific program. Post-transfection, cells were cultured in T-flasks until sufficient biomass was obtained for cloning. Clonal populations were expanded in 30 mL working volumes in shaking flasks at 37°C and 10% CO₂, with cell densities maintained between 2.0–3.0 × 10⁵ cells/mL through biweekly passaging. All manipulations adhered to aseptic technique to prevent contamination.

### Example 4: DNA Microarray

Total RNA was isolated from all 48 samples (6 clones × 2 conditions × 4 time points) using QiaCube automation and RNeasy kits. RNA integrity was verified via Bioanalyzer. Biotinylated cRNA was synthesized and hybridized to a proprietary CHO-specific Affymetrix microarray containing 61,223 probe sets. Raw data were processed in GeneSpring GX and normalized using robust multichip average. Non-expressed genes (background in >80% of samples) were filtered out. Differential expression analysis using limma identified 295 genes significantly altered between stable and unstable groups (corrected *P* < 0.05).

### Example 5: Quantitative Real-Time PCR (RT-qPCR)

RNA from the same isolations was treated with DNase I and reverse-transcribed. Custom TaqMan assays were designed for 14 candidate genes and two reference genes (*Actb*, *Gapdh*). Reactions were run in triplicate on 384-well plates using an ABI PRISM 7900 system. Relative expression was calculated using the ΔΔCq method, normalized to geometric mean of reference genes. Copy number of the recombinant gene was determined via absolute quantification using a standard curve generated from known quantities of vector DNA and host gDNA, with *Glucagon* as a single-copy endogenous reference. Recombinant gene copies per cell were derived from the ratio of transgene to *Gluc*.

### Example 6: Results

Productivity and copy number data revealed two distinct clone groups: stable (≤9% productivity decline) and unstable (≥67% decline) over ten weeks. Transcriptome comparison identified 212 genes specifically associated with stability, independent of MTX effects. RT-qPCR confirmed differential expression in 13 of 14 candidates. PCA of the five MTX-insensitive genes explained >92% of variance in the first three components. Three-dimensional plotting showed clear separation between stable and unstable clones. k-NN clustering correctly grouped unstable clones into a distinct cluster, even with suboptimal cluster numbers, demonstrating robustness of the signature.

### Example 7: Presence of MTX

To ensure marker reliability, gene expression was compared between cultures grown with and without MTX. Only five genes (*E130203B14*, *BX842664.2/Hist1h3c*, *Ptpre*, *Cspg4*, *Fgfr2*) showed no significant response to MTX (*P* > 0.05), confirming their suitability as stability predictors regardless of selection pressure. PCA incorporating MTX status confirmed that the primary source of variation was stability phenotype, not drug presence, validating the independence of the marker signature from culture conditions.

### Example 8: CRISPR/CAS9 Experiment

A CRISPR/Cas9 experiment is proposed to functionally validate the role of identified marker genes in stability. Guide RNAs would be designed to knock out or modulate expression of *Fgfr2*, *Ptpre*, or *Cspg4* in CHO host cells. These engineered lines would then be transfected with the same expression vector and subjected to the stability assay. It is predicted that clones with edited stability-associated genes will show altered productivity trajectories—e.g., *Fgfr2* knockout leading to instability—thereby confirming causal roles and enabling rational host cell engineering for improved platform performance.
