## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and therapeutics, specifically to single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) aptamers that bind with high affinity and specificity to the nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB) RelA subunit. More particularly, the invention provides novel ssDNA aptamers useful for the selective enrichment, detection, quantification, and functional modulation of activated RelA protein complexes in biological samples. The invention further encompasses methods for utilizing these aptamers in analytical assays, including stable isotope dilution-selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry (SID-SRM-MS), as well as potential applications in imaging and therapeutic intervention of diseases involving dysregulated NF-κB signaling.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Single-stranded DNA aptamers are short oligonucleotide sequences that fold into specific three-dimensional structures capable of binding target molecules with high affinity and selectivity, often rivaling or exceeding that of antibodies. These nucleic acid ligands are generated through an iterative in vitro selection process known as Systematic Evolution of Ligands by EXponential enrichment (SELEX), which allows for the isolation of aptamers against a wide range of targets, including proteins, small molecules, and even whole cells. Due to their chemical stability, ease of synthesis and modification, and lack of immunogenicity, ssDNA aptamers have emerged as powerful tools in both diagnostic and therapeutic contexts.

In the realm of diagnostics, ssDNA aptamers serve as highly specific capture reagents in biosensors, microarrays, and affinity purification platforms, enabling the detection and quantification of low-abundance biomarkers in complex biological matrices. Therapeutically, aptamers can function as antagonists or agonists by modulating the activity of their target proteins, thereby influencing downstream signaling pathways. Several aptamer-based therapeutics have already entered clinical use, underscoring the translational potential of this technology.

U.S. Patent Application No. 20080261225 discloses methods for selecting nucleic acid ligands that bind to transcription factors, including members of the NF-κB family, using modified SELEX protocols. The application emphasizes the use of RNA and modified nucleic acids but does not describe ssDNA aptamers with the specificity and functional properties disclosed herein. U.S. Pat. No. 6,867,289 describes aptamers that bind to the p50 subunit of NF-κB and inhibit its DNA-binding activity, primarily focusing on RNA-based ligands and their use in anti-inflammatory therapy. Similarly, U.S. Pat. No. 7,309,786 relates to thioate-modified double-stranded DNA decoys that mimic NF-κB binding sites and act as competitive inhibitors; however, these are not single-stranded aptamers and do not enable selective enrichment or quantitative mass spectrometric analysis of activated RelA complexes.

Despite these advances, there remains a critical need for reagents that can distinguish between inactive (IκBα-bound) and activated (free, post-translationally modified) forms of RelA, the principal transactivating subunit of NF-κB. Existing antibodies often fail to differentiate these states with sufficient precision, and conventional DNA decoys lack the specificity required for analytical applications. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a novel ssDNA aptamer, P028F4, which selectively binds activated RelA and enables its isolation, quantification, and functional interrogation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) aptamers that specifically bind to the NF-κB RelA protein, particularly in its activated state. In one embodiment, the invention features an ssDNA aptamer comprising the sequence of SEQ ID NO: 9, which exhibits high-affinity binding to RelA with a dissociation constant (K_D) in the sub-nanomolar range. This aptamer demonstrates significant homology to SEQ ID NO: 21, particularly within a conserved GGGAC motif that corresponds to half of the canonical NF-κB binding site.

The invention includes methods for quantifying RelA in biological samples, both in vitro and in vivo. A key aspect of the invention is the development of an analytical method for RelA quantification that integrates aptamer-based affinity enrichment with stable isotope dilution-selected reaction monitoring mass spectrometry (SID-SRM-MS). This method enables precise measurement of the activated fraction of endogenous RelA, distinguishing it from the IκBα-inhibited pool.

Specifically, the invention provides a method for quantifying RelA comprising: (a) contacting a biological sample with an ssDNA aptamer that binds RelA, wherein the aptamer is optionally conjugated to a solid support; (b) allowing the aptamer to bind activated RelA; (c) washing away unbound material; (d) releasing the bound RelA; and (e) assaying the released RelA by mass spectrometry, preferably SRM-MS. The aptamer may be biotin-conjugated to facilitate capture on streptavidin-coated magnetic beads.

The invention further introduces a method for improving SRM-MS analysis of RelA by incorporating an aptamer-mediated enrichment step prior to trypsin digestion and mass spectrometric analysis. This approach significantly enhances the signal-to-noise ratio, enabling detection of low-abundance RelA isoforms and associated complexes.

Additionally, the invention provides a method for enriching activated RelA from a complex protein mixture, comprising: (a) adding a biotin-conjugated oligonucleotide sequence comprising SEQ ID NO: 9 to the mixture; (b) capturing the aptamer-RelA complexes on streptavidin-coated beads; (c) washing the beads to remove non-specifically bound proteins; and (d) releasing the bound RelA for downstream analysis.

In another aspect, the invention encompasses a method for modulating the innate immune response by administering a composition comprising the ssDNA aptamer of the invention. The aptamer functions by inhibiting NF-κB-mediated gene expression, thereby attenuating inflammatory responses. This therapeutic approach is particularly relevant in conditions characterized by chronic or dysregulated NF-κB activation, such as autoimmune diseases, cancer, and sepsis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to single-stranded DNA aptamers that specifically recognize and bind the NF-κB RelA subunit in its activated conformation. As used herein, “activated RelA” refers to the form of RelA that is not complexed with IκBα inhibitory proteins and is capable of nuclear translocation, DNA binding, and transcriptional activation. The term “aptamer” denotes a synthetic oligonucleotide, typically 20–100 nucleotides in length, that adopts a defined tertiary structure enabling high-affinity interaction with a specific molecular target.

A central embodiment of the invention is the ssDNA aptamer designated P028F4, which comprises the sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO: 9. This aptamer was isolated through multiple rounds of in vitro selection against recombinant human RelA (amino acids 1–313) and exhibits exceptional specificity for free RelA over IκBα-bound RelA. P028F4 contains a conserved GGGAC motif within its variable region, which partially mimics the natural NF-κB binding site (GGGACTTTCC), thereby enabling competitive inhibition of RelA-DNA interactions.

P028F4 has been successfully integrated into an enrichment-mass spectrometric quantification assay, termed aptamer-SID-SRM-MS. In this assay, P028F4 serves as an affinity capture reagent to isolate activated RelA from cytoplasmic or nuclear extracts of mammalian cells. Following on-bead trypsin digestion, signature peptides derived from RelA are quantified by SRM-MS using isotopically labeled internal standards, enabling absolute quantification of activated RelA molecules with high precision and sensitivity.

Binding studies confirm that P028F4 selectively interacts with activated RelA, as evidenced by its inability to bind recombinant RelA pre-complexed with IκBα. Moreover, P028F4 effectively reduces TNFα-stimulated endogenous NF-κB-dependent gene expression in human A549 pulmonary epithelial cells, demonstrating its functional antagonism of RelA transcriptional activity.

The incorporation of P028F4 as an affinity isolation step represents a significant advancement over traditional immunoprecipitation methods, as it avoids antibody cross-reactivity and enables selective enrichment of post-translationally modified RelA isoforms, including those phosphorylated at Ser536—a key activation mark. The resulting aptamer-tandem affinity purification (ATAP) technique captures RelA-p300 coactivator complexes while excluding IκBα-containing inhibitory complexes.

The development of a stable isotope dilution (SID)-SRM-MS assay for RelA quantification leverages the specificity of P028F4 to measure the activated fraction of endogenous RelA in subcellular compartments. This approach has enabled, for the first time, the estimation that approximately 5% of total cytoplasmic RelA exists in an activated state in unstimulated cells, increasing sixfold upon TNFα stimulation, with equivalent amounts of activated RelA detected in the nucleus post-stimulation.

The innate immune response (IIR) is a fundamental defense mechanism governed by the NF-κB signaling pathway. Nuclear factor-κB (NF-κB) is a family of transcription factors that regulate the expression of genes involved in inflammation, immunity, cell survival, and proliferation. NF-κB activity is tightly controlled by stimulus-induced protein interactions. In resting cells, NF-κB/RelA is sequestered in the cytoplasm by IκB inhibitory proteins, which mask its nuclear localization signal and DNA-binding domain. Upon activation by ligand-bound cytokine receptors (e.g., TNF receptor) or pattern recognition receptors (e.g., Toll-like receptors), the IκB kinase (IKK) complex phosphorylates IκBα, targeting it for proteasomal degradation. This liberates RelA, allowing its nuclear translocation.

Once in the nucleus, RelA undergoes post-translational modifications, including phosphorylation at Ser536 or Ser276, which enhance its transcriptional activity by promoting association with the p300/CBP coactivator complex. The tandem affinity purification (TAP) technique, adapted herein using P028F4, facilitates the identification of the RelA interactome under activating conditions.

Aptamer selection was performed using a randomized ssDNA library incubated with GST-tagged RelA(1–313). After 12 rounds of enrichment, individual clones were screened, leading to the identification of P028F4. Binding affinity was confirmed by surface plasmon resonance (SPR), yielding a K_D of 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁰ M. Competition studies demonstrated that P028F4 competes with natural dsNF-κB binding sites for RelA interaction, as shown by electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSAs).

Confocal microscopy revealed that fluorescently labeled P028F4 colocalizes with EGFP-RelA in the nuclei of TNFα-stimulated cells, confirming in situ binding to activated RelA. Chromatin immunoprecipitation (ChIP) assays further showed that P028F4 blocks RelA recruitment to endogenous promoters of NF-κB target genes such as Groβ and TNFAIP3/A20.

Critically, P028F4 binds only free RelA and not IκBα-associated RelA, as demonstrated by EMSA and co-immunoprecipitation. Aptamer-TAP purification enriches for IκBα-free, Ser536-phosphorylated RelA and associated p300 complexes, while excluding IκBα. SRM assay optimization included selection of high-response Q1/Q3 transitions, collision energy tuning, and validation of peak assignment certainty through retention time and fragment ion intensity matching with SIS peptides.

The lower limit of quantification was established at 200 amol, with a linear dynamic range exceeding 1000-fold. Aptamer enrichment increased the signal-to-noise ratio for RelA detection by 36-fold compared to crude extracts. Quantitative analysis revealed that in TNFα-stimulated cells, the number of activated RelA molecules reaches approximately 50,000 per nucleus, representing the majority of nuclear RelA.

Other aptamers binding NF-κB/RelA have been described, but none combine the specificity for activated RelA, compatibility with mass spectrometry, and functional inhibitory capacity of P028F4. The invention’s scope extends beyond P028F4 to include variants with at least 80% sequence identity that retain RelA-binding function.

Potential applications include diagnostic imaging—by conjugating P028F4 to contrast agents for detecting NF-κB activation in inflammatory diseases or tumors—and targeted therapy, such as nanoparticle-encapsulated aptamers or aptamer-drug conjugates delivering anti-proliferative agents to NF-κB-hyperactive cells.

In summary, the P028F4 aptamer enables unprecedented precision in the enrichment, quantification, and functional modulation of activated NF-κB/RelA, offering transformative tools for research, diagnostics, and therapeutics.
