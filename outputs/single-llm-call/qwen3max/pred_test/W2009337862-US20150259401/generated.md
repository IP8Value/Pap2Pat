# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to antibodies with increased half-life, particularly in the context of ferret models used for preclinical evaluation of therapeutic antibodies. More specifically, the invention provides modified ferret immunoglobulin G (IgG) molecules comprising amino acid substitutions within the Fc region that enhance binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) and thereby extend serum half-life in ferrets. These engineered antibodies are especially useful for improving the pharmacokinetic profile of therapeutic candidates during in vivo studies in ferrets, a model species widely employed for evaluating antiviral and respiratory therapeutics due to its physiological and immunological similarity to humans in response to pathogens such as influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). The invention further encompasses chimeric, humanized, ferretized, and veneered antibodies incorporating these modified Fc regions, as well as antibody fragments, fusion proteins, and immunoglobulin constructs that retain the enhanced half-life properties conferred by the disclosed substitutions.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ferrets (Mustela putorius furo) have emerged as a critical laboratory model for the study of human respiratory diseases, including influenza and RSV, owing to their susceptibility to human viral strains and the clinical manifestations they exhibit, which closely mirror those observed in infected humans. Their use extends to the evaluation of monoclonal antibody (mAb) therapeutics, where they serve as a bridge between rodent models and non-human primates. However, a significant limitation of the ferret model has been the unusually short serum half-life of human IgG antibodies when administered in vivo. Studies have shown that human mAbs exhibit terminal half-lives of approximately 30 hours in ferrets—substantially shorter than the typical 10–21 days observed in humans or even the 5–10 days seen in cynomolgus monkeys. This rapid clearance not only necessitates frequent dosing, complicating experimental design, but also increases variability in pharmacokinetic data and may obscure true efficacy signals. Moreover, the administration of human antibodies to ferrets often elicits anti-drug antibody (ADA) responses, which can accelerate clearance through immune-mediated mechanisms and confound interpretation of results. While chimeric antibodies—comprising human variable regions and ferret constant regions—have been proposed to mitigate immunogenicity, the intrinsic half-life of such chimeras remains suboptimal due to suboptimal interactions between the ferret Fc region and the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), the key mediator of IgG recycling and long serum persistence. Consequently, there exists a clear and unmet need for novel IgG variants engineered specifically for the ferret model that combine reduced immunogenicity with significantly extended half-life, thereby enabling more reliable, cost-effective, and physiologically relevant preclinical assessment of therapeutic antibodies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a modified ferret IgG Fc region engineered to exhibit increased half-life in ferrets through enhanced binding to ferret FcRn at acidic pH, while maintaining efficient release at neutral pH. This modification is achieved by introducing specific amino acid substitutions at position 252 of the ferret heavy chain constant region, as numbered according to the EU numbering system based on homology to human IgG1. In particular, substitution of the native serine residue at position 252 with either methionine (S252M) or tyrosine (S252Y) results in markedly improved FcRn binding affinity and prolonged serum persistence. The S252Y substitution, alone or in combination with additional substitutions such as S254T and T256E (collectively referred to as the YTE modification), demonstrates the most pronounced effect, extending the terminal half-life of chimeric antibodies in ferrets to over 200 hours—approximately threefold longer than the wild-type chimeric counterpart. The invention further encompasses an isolated antibody comprising a modified ferret Fc region containing one or more of these substitutions, wherein the antibody retains specific antigen-binding capability, typically via human or humanized Fab regions. Also provided is an isolated immunoglobulin construct comprising an antibody fragment—such as a Fab, scFv, or F(ab′)₂—fused to a modified Fc region with enhanced half-life properties. Additionally, the invention includes immunoglobulin fusion proteins in which a bioactive molecule (e.g., a cytokine, enzyme, or receptor ectodomain) is linked to the modified Fc region, thereby conferring extended circulation time to the fused moiety. The antibodies and constructs of the invention may be chimeric (human variable/ferret constant), fully ferret, ferretized (non-ferret antibody framework residues replaced with ferret counterparts), or veneered (surface residues humanized or ferretized to reduce immunogenicity). In a preferred embodiment, the antibody specifically binds to the RSV F glycoprotein, though the platform is broadly applicable to any target. The resulting molecules exhibit not only extended half-life but also reduced immunogenicity in ferrets, as evidenced by the absence of precipitous drops in serum concentration indicative of ADA responses, thereby enabling more robust and reproducible preclinical studies.

### DEFINITIONS

A “corresponding” unmodified antibody refers to an antibody identical in sequence except for the absence of the half-life-extending Fc substitutions. An “epitope” is the specific molecular structure on an antigen that is recognized and bound by an antibody. An “antibody” is a glycoprotein comprising at least one immunoglobulin variable domain capable of specifically binding an antigen. The immunoglobulin or antibody molecule typically consists of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, each containing variable and constant domains. The Fc region, derived from the heavy chain constant domains, mediates effector functions and FcRn binding. It includes the CH2 and CH3 domains, with the hinge region connecting the Fab arms to the Fc. The Fab (antigen-binding fragment) contains the variable domains of one heavy and one light chain. An scFv (single-chain variable fragment) links the heavy and light chain variable domains via a peptide linker. An “isolated biomolecule” is separated from its natural environment, and a “substantially purified biomolecule” is free of contaminants to a degree suitable for therapeutic or diagnostic use. An “immunoglobulin fusion protein” comprises an immunoglobulin domain (e.g., Fc) fused to a heterologous polypeptide. “In vivo half-life” is the time required for the serum concentration of an antibody to decrease by half, and “increased half-life” means a statistically significant extension relative to a corresponding unmodified antibody. “Recombinant” indicates production via genetic engineering. “Specifically binds” denotes selective interaction with a target with high affinity (typically KD < 10⁻⁷ M) and minimal cross-reactivity. Cross-reactivity refers to binding to non-target molecules. KD (equilibrium dissociation constant) quantifies binding affinity, with lower values indicating higher affinity. An “immunoglobulin construct” is any engineered molecule containing immunoglobulin domains. The “constant region” includes the constant domains of the heavy or light chain. A “constant fragment” is a portion thereof, such as Fc. FcRn is the neonatal Fc receptor, a heterodimer of an alpha chain and beta2 microglobulin (β2m), responsible for IgG recycling. Residue numbering follows the EU index as aligned by homology.

### Amino Acid Substitutions

Amino acid substitutions are introduced via site-directed mutagenesis, PCR-mediated mutagenesis, or other methods known in the art. Site-directed mutagenesis employs oligonucleotide primers encoding the desired mutation, while PCR-mediated approaches use overlapping primers. Thermostable ligases may be used in assembly methods. Mutagenic agents like error-prone PCR are also applicable. Mutants are screened for FcRn binding and half-life. Exemplary substitutions include S252Y, S252M, and the YTE triple mutant (S252Y/S254T/T256E). Numbering follows the EU system applied to ferret sequences via alignment.

### Antibodies of the Invention

Antibodies according to the invention comprise a modified ferret Fc region with substitutions enhancing FcRn binding and half-life. They retain antigen specificity, typically via human or humanized variable regions, and exhibit improved pharmacokinetics in ferrets without inducing significant ADA responses.

### Ferret Antibody Derivatives

Ferret antibody derivatives include molecules with conservative amino acid substitutions—those preserving physicochemical properties (e.g., leucine to isoleucine)—in framework or CDR regions. Silent or neutral missense mutations do not alter function, whereas non-neutral ones may require functional validation. Mutant molecules are designed in silico, expressed, and tested for binding and half-life. Covalent attachments (e.g., PEGylation, biotinylation) and chemical modifications (e.g., acetylation) are possible. Peptides may be chemically synthesized. Non-classical amino acids (e.g., selenocysteine) can be incorporated. Immunoconjugates link antibodies to drugs, toxins, or labels.

### Immunoglobulin Constructs of the Invention

An immunoglobulin construct comprises an Fc region—wild-type or modified—fused to another protein domain. Examples include Fc-fusion proteins and antibody fragments (Fab, scFv, diabodies) linked to modified Fc for half-life extension.

### Antibody Preparation

Antibodies are prepared via hybridoma technology (for murine mAbs) or recombinant methods. Monoclonal antibodies derive from a single clone. Hybridomas are produced by fusing B cells with myeloma cells and screened for antigen binding. Fragments are generated by proteolysis or genetic engineering. Phage display libraries enable selection of binders. Antibody coding regions are isolated by RT-PCR and cloned for recombinant expression.

### Recombinant Production of Antibodies and Immunoglobulin Constructs

Recombinant production involves isolating DNA encoding the antibody, sequencing it, and inserting it into expression vectors under control of promoters (e.g., CMV). Host cells (e.g., HEK293, CHO) are transfected and cultured. Vectors contain signal sequences (prokaryotic, yeast, or mammalian), promoters, enhancers, and transcription terminators. DNA is inserted into replicable vectors, which are introduced into host cells via transformation or transfection. Cells are cultured in bioreactors to produce antibodies, which are purified by chromatography (e.g., Protein A/G).

### Chimeric Antibodies

Chimeric antibodies combine variable regions from one species (e.g., human) with constant regions from another (e.g., ferret). They are made by recombinant DNA techniques linking human VH/VL to ferret CH/CL. Ferret antibodies contain fully ferret sequences. Ferretized antibodies are non-ferret antibodies engineered to contain ferret framework residues, often via CDR-grafting. Veneering or resurfacing replaces surface-exposed residues to reduce immunogenicity. Chain shuffling optimizes pairing. Binding is confirmed by immunoassays (ELISA, SPR). Fusion proteins are made by fusing Fc to bioactive moieties. Polynucleotides encode the modified antibodies. Half-life is assayed in ferrets via PK studies. Modified antibodies are used in preclinical testing to reduce dosing frequency and immunogenicity.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A pharmacokinetics study was conducted to evaluate the half-life of a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody specific for RSV F glycoprotein in ferrets. Male ferrets received a single intravenous dose of 2 mg/kg. Plasma samples were collected over 21 days and analyzed by electrochemiluminescent assay. The average terminal half-life was 31 ± 10 hours. Two animals exhibited abrupt drops in concentration, and anti-drug antibodies were detected in one, confirming immunogenicity as a contributing factor to rapid clearance.

### Example 2

Ferret IgG heavy and light chain constant region cDNAs were cloned from kidney, lung, liver, and spleen RNA using degenerate primers based on mink sequences. Sequencing revealed a consensus heavy chain sequence 89% identical to mink, with three hinge-region differences suggesting multiple isotypes. The light chain was 94% identical to mink and matched the database sequence.

### Example 3

Chimeric antibodies were prepared by fusing human anti-RSV F variable regions to ferret constant regions. Variants with S252M, S252Y, YTE, and N434S substitutions were generated by site-directed mutagenesis. Antibodies were expressed in HEK293F cells and purified using tandem Protein A/G columns. SDS-PAGE and SE-HPLC confirmed purity and integrity. Antigen binding was retained, with IC50 values comparable to the human parent.

### Example 4

Soluble ferret FcRn was produced by co-expressing the cloned extracellular alpha chain (with His-tag) and β2-microglobulin in HEK293E cells. The complex was purified via Ni-NTA and size exclusion chromatography. SDS-PAGE showed bands at expected sizes, with a doublet for the alpha chain likely due to glycosylation heterogeneity.

### Example 5

Chimeric mAbs were tested for ferret FcRn binding at pH 6.0 using an AlphaScreen competitive assay. Wild-type chimeric mAb showed twofold better binding than human IgG. S252M and S252Y variants exhibited 6-fold and 24-fold improved affinity, respectively. The YTE variant showed similar affinity to S252Y alone, indicating no additive effect from the additional substitutions.

### Example 6

Pharmacokinetics of chimeric mAb variants were evaluated in ferrets after single and repeat 2 mg/kg IV doses. Terminal half-lives were: WT, 65 ± 27 h; S252M, 127 ± 38 h; S252Y, 206 ± 44 h; YTE, 213 ± 28 h. No abrupt concentration drops were observed, suggesting absence of ADA responses. Bridging assays for ADAs were inconclusive due to interference by residual antibody, but consistent clearance kinetics support low immunogenicity.
