# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

*Chlamydia trachomatis* is a Gram-negative obligate intracellular bacterium that represents one of the most prevalent sexually transmitted infections (STIs) worldwide. Epidemiological data indicate that approximately 92 million new cases of *C. trachomatis* infection occur annually, with significant implications for public health, particularly in relation to female reproductive health. The infection is frequently asymptomatic, especially in women, which contributes to its high transmission rate and the potential for serious sequelae, including pelvic inflammatory disease, ectopic pregnancy, and tubal factor infertility. Despite the widespread availability of effective antibiotics such as azithromycin and doxycycline, limitations in antibiotic administration persist. These include challenges related to patient compliance, the inability of antibiotics to reverse established tissue damage, and the risk of reinfection due to the lack of durable immunity following treatment. Moreover, the emergence of antibiotic-resistant strains, although not yet widespread, remains a concern for long-term management strategies. Consequently, the development of a prophylactic vaccine against *C. trachomatis* has been identified as a critical public health priority.

The resolution of *Chlamydia* infection in both humans and animal models is primarily mediated by CD4+ T helper cells, with a dominant Th1-type cell-mediated immune response being essential for bacterial clearance. This response is characterized by the production of interferon-gamma (IFNγ), which activates macrophages and induces indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase expression in epithelial cells, thereby limiting the availability of tryptophan—an essential nutrient for *Chlamydia* replication. In contrast, antibody responses, while detectable during infection, appear to play a secondary or supportive role in protection, particularly at mucosal surfaces. Historically, the major outer membrane protein (MOMP) of *Chlamydia* has been considered a leading candidate antigen for vaccine development due to its surface exposure, immunodominance, and conservation across serovars. MOMP contains variable domains that elicit serovar-specific neutralizing antibodies and constant domains that are targets for cross-reactive T-cell responses. However, efforts to develop an effective MOMP-based vaccine have been hampered by the difficulty of maintaining its native conformation—a requirement for inducing protective immunity—and the lack of suitable adjuvants capable of driving robust Th1 responses at mucosal sites.

A significant barrier to successful *Chlamydia* vaccine development has been the absence of delivery platforms that can effectively stimulate mucosal immunity without inducing excessive inflammation. Mucosal surfaces, including the genital and respiratory tracts, are interconnected through the common mucosal immune system, such that immunization at one site—such as the nasal mucosa—can elicit protective immune responses at distant mucosal sites, including the female reproductive tract. Nasal immunization is particularly advantageous because it targets the nasal-associated lymphoid tissue (NALT), a key inductive site for mucosal immunity. However, the hostile environment of mucosal surfaces, characterized by enzymatic degradation, mucus barriers, and rapid clearance mechanisms, necessitates the use of protective carriers that can deliver antigens to underlying dendritic cells while preserving their immunogenic integrity. To date, no FDA-approved adjuvant exists that can reliably induce strong cell-mediated Th1 immunity in mucosal tissues without triggering damaging inflammatory responses. Therefore, there remains a compelling need for a novel vaccine platform that can serve as a “smart adjuvant”—one that enhances antigen-specific Th1 responses while minimizing non-protective inflammation. The present invention addresses this unmet need through the use of engineered vault-like particles as a mucosal vaccine delivery system for *Chlamydia* antigens.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods for treating or preventing *Chlamydia* infection in a subject by administering a pharmaceutical composition comprising vault-like particles encapsulating an immunogenic peptide derived from *Chlamydia trachomatis* or a homologous organism such as *Chlamydia muridarum*. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising vault-like particles that incorporate a major outer membrane protein (MOMP) or a fragment thereof, wherein the MOMP is fused to a vault-targeting domain to enable its encapsulation within the vault cavity. These vault-like particles are administered via a mucosal route, preferably intranasally, to stimulate protective immunity in distant mucosal tissues, including the genital tract. The vault-like particles may be further modified to include an Fc-binding domain, such as a staphylococcal protein A-derived “z” peptide, appended to the C-terminus of the major vault protein (MVP), thereby enhancing uptake by dendritic cells through interaction with immunoglobulins present in mucosal secretions. The invention also provides methods for immunizing a subject against *Chlamydia* infection, reducing the incidence of infertility associated with chronic infection, eliciting a Th1-biased cell-mediated immune response, and preventing immune-mediated pathology by selectively activating inflammasome pathways without stimulating Toll-like receptors (TLRs). Additionally, the invention includes kits for immunizing or treating subjects, comprising lyophilized or liquid formulations of vault-like particles, along with instructions for mucosal administration.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to the development of vault-based vaccines for the prevention and treatment of *Chlamydia* infection. Vaults are naturally occurring, evolutionarily conserved ribonucleoprotein complexes found in eukaryotic cells, including human dendritic cells and mucosal epithelia. Native vaults consist of multiple copies of three proteins—major vault protein (MVP), telomerase-associated protein 1 (TEP1), and vault poly(ADP-ribose) polymerase (VPARP)—along with several copies of a small untranslated vault RNA (vRNA). The MVP, a 97 kDa protein, self-assembles into barrel-shaped nanoparticles when expressed recombinantly, forming vault-like particles that retain the structural integrity of native vaults but lack TEP1, VPARP, and vRNA. These recombinant vault-like particles possess a large internal cavity capable of encapsulating exogenous proteins, peptides, or other bioactive molecules, making them ideal candidates for antigen delivery.

In the context of the present invention, vault-like particles are engineered to carry immunogenic peptides derived from *Chlamydia* antigens, particularly the major outer membrane protein (MOMP). MOMP fragments, such as the full-length mature protein without its signal sequence, are fused to a minimal interaction domain (mINT) derived from VPARP (amino acids 1563–1724), which facilitates packaging into the vault interior. Homologous proteins from related species, such as *C. muridarum* MOMP, may be used interchangeably due to high sequence conservation and shared immunogenic epitopes. The resulting chlamydia-vault-like particles (CVLPs) are administered intranasally, leveraging the common mucosal immune system to generate protective immunity in the genital tract. This route of administration targets nasal-associated lymphoid tissue (NALT), leading to the induction of antigen-specific CD4+ T cells that migrate to distal mucosal sites.

The immunogenicity of these vault-based vaccines is enhanced through strategic modifications to the vault structure. For instance, the C-terminus of MVP may be fused to an Fc-binding domain, such as the 33-amino acid “z” peptide from staphylococcal protein A, which binds the Fc region of IgG. This modification promotes dendritic cell uptake via Fc receptor-mediated endocytosis, a mechanism shown to be critical for Th1 response induction in *Chlamydia* infection. Upon internalization, CVLPs are processed by dendritic cells, leading to the presentation of MOMP-derived peptides on MHC class II molecules and the activation of CD4+ T cells. Critically, protein-loaded vaults—but not empty vaults—induce the secretion of interleukin-12p70 (IL-12p70) and CXCL10, cytokines and chemokines that drive Th1 differentiation and recruitment, respectively. Simultaneously, they trigger inflammasome activation, resulting in caspase-1-dependent cleavage and secretion of IL-1β, a process that occurs independently of Toll-like receptor signaling. This selective immune activation—characterized by robust Th1 responses without the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNFα or neutrophil-attracting chemokines like CXCL2—defines vaults as “smart adjuvants” that promote protective immunity while minimizing tissue-damaging inflammation.

The term “subject” as used herein refers to any mammal, including humans, non-human primates, rodents, and livestock, susceptible to *Chlamydia* infection. “Administering” encompasses any method of delivery, with intranasal administration being preferred for mucosal immunization. Dosage regimens typically involve 100–500 µg of vault-like particles per dose, administered two to three times at biweekly intervals. The vault-like particles may be co-administered with additional agents, such as stabilizers or permeation enhancers, though they function effectively as standalone immunogens. Kits for clinical or veterinary use include pre-measured doses of lyophilized CVLPs, reconstitution buffers, and administration devices.

Vault-like particles are produced using recombinant baculovirus expression in insect cells (e.g., Sf9 cells). Polynucleotide sequences encoding modified MVP (e.g., cp-MVP-z) and MOMP-mINT fusion proteins are cloned into baculovirus transfer vectors, used to generate recombinant bacmids, and transfected into insect cells. Following infection, vaults are purified by sucrose gradient centrifugation and resuspended in sterile buffer. Modifications to MVP, VPARP, or TEP1 may include N- or C-terminal fusions of targeting moieties (e.g., EGF, TAT, anti-CEA scFv), sensors (e.g., fluorescent proteins), or binding domains (e.g., GAL4, MS2, polynucleotide-binding peptides). These engineered vaults can also be used for non-vaccine applications, such as environmental detoxification (e.g., by incorporating arsenate reductase) or targeted drug delivery.

Definitions: “Vault” refers to the native ribonucleoprotein complex; “vault-like particle” denotes recombinant particles formed primarily from MVP. “MVP,” “VPARP,” “TEP1,” and “vRNA” are as defined in the literature. “Human” includes all stages of development. “Environmental medium” encompasses soil, water, or air. “Comprise” is inclusive and non-limiting. “MS2” refers to the bacteriophage coat protein used for RNA binding. The invention thus provides a versatile platform for mucosal vaccination against *Chlamydia*, with broad applicability to other mucosal pathogens.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Chlamydia-vault-like particles (CVLPs) were prepared by co-expressing a MOMP-mINT fusion construct and cysteine-modified MVP bearing a C-terminal Fc-binding “z” peptide (cp-MVP-z) in Sf9 insect cells using the Bac-to-Bac baculovirus system. The MOMP coding sequence (366 amino acids, excluding the signal peptide) was PCR-ligated to the mINT domain (VPARP residues 1563–1724) and cloned into pFastBac. Sf9 cells were infected with recombinant baculoviruses at an MOI of 0.01 for 65 hours, lysed, and mixed to allow mINT-mediated incorporation of MOMP into pre-formed cp-MVP-z vaults. Purification via sucrose gradient centrifugation yielded homogeneous CVLPs, confirmed by negative stain electron microscopy and SDS-PAGE. Western blot analysis using anti-MOMP antibodies demonstrated that the MOMP-INT fusion retained conformational epitopes, as evidenced by recognition of the VD1 domain. The FcR-mediated delivery mechanism, facilitated by the “z” peptide, was shown to enhance dendritic cell uptake and promote a Th1-polarized immune response, as MOMP-vaults induced high levels of IL-12p70 and IFNγ without triggering TLR-dependent inflammation.

### Example 2

Female C57BL/6 mice were intranasally immunized three times at two-week intervals with 200 µg of MOMP-vaults, GL-vaults (negative control), or a single dose of 10³ IFU of live *C. muridarum* (positive control). Two weeks after the final immunization, mice were hormonally synchronized and challenged intravaginally with 1.5×10⁵ IFU of *C. muridarum*. Vaginal swabs collected every three days revealed that MOMP-vault immunization reduced bacterial burden by approximately 1 log compared to both GL-vault controls and live-infection-immunized mice, demonstrating superior protection. Splenocytes and iliac lymph node cells were analyzed by intracellular cytokine staining, showing a significant increase in IFNγ⁺IL-4⁻CD4⁺CD3⁺ T cells in MOMP-vault-immunized mice, indicative of a Th1 response. Serum antibody analysis revealed high titers of MOMP-specific IgG, with an IgG2c:IgG1 ratio >1, consistent with Th1 bias. Notably, MOMP-vault immunization did not elevate IL-4 or IL-17, confirming the absence of Th2 or Th17 skewing. Humoral and cellular responses were assessed via ELISA, Luminex cytokine arrays, and flow cytometry, confirming that CVLPs elicit robust, protective mucosal immunity.

### Example 3

To evaluate dendritic cell uptake, bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs) from C57BL/6 mice were incubated with GL-vaults or FITC-dextran. Flow cytometry showed that after 30 minutes, FITC-dextran exhibited higher surface adherence, but by 60 minutes, GL-vault internalization matched dextran uptake. Confocal microscopy confirmed that GL-vaults were internalized into the cytoplasm, whereas dextran remained largely surface-bound. BMDCs were differentiated using GM-CSF over seven days, yielding >94% CD11c⁺ cells with low MHC class II and CD40 expression, confirming immaturity. This assay demonstrated that vaults are efficiently internalized by dendritic cells, a prerequisite for antigen presentation and T-cell priming.

### Example 4

Mice immunized intranasally with MOMP-vaults developed serum IgG levels comparable to those induced by live infection, as measured by ELISA and Western blot. However, unlike live infection, MOMP-vaults elicited antibodies specific only to MOMP, not to other chlamydial proteins. Surprisingly, MOMP-vaults—but not GL-vaults or mCherry-vaults—broke immune tolerance to MVP, inducing anti-vault antibodies. This was attributed to MOMP’s unique ability to enhance inflammasome activation, as evidenced by elevated IL-1β. Intracellular cytokine staining confirmed Th1 polarization, with minimal Th2 or Th17 responses. These findings establish vault nanoparticles as a novel mucosal vaccine platform that reduces inflammation and immune-mediated pathology while providing superior protection against *Chlamydia*.
