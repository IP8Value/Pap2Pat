# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Machine learning has become a cornerstone of modern decision-making systems across diverse domains, including financial services, healthcare, criminal justice, and employment screening. These systems often rely on predictive models trained on historical data to classify individuals or events into discrete outcomes—such as fraudulent versus legitimate transactions, high-risk versus low-risk recidivism, or high-income versus low-income earners. While such models can enhance efficiency and scalability, they also carry the risk of perpetuating or even amplifying societal biases present in training data, leading to discriminatory outcomes against protected groups defined by attributes such as race, gender, nationality, or socioeconomic status. Regulatory frameworks, particularly in the United States, recognize two primary doctrines of discrimination: disparate treatment and disparate impact. Disparate treatment prohibits explicit use of protected attributes in decision logic, which is commonly addressed in machine learning by excluding such attributes from model inputs. However, disparate impact addresses indirect discrimination arising from seemingly neutral policies that produce unequal outcomes across protected groups—a challenge not resolved by mere exclusion of sensitive features. Existing fairness-aware machine learning techniques attempt to mitigate disparate impact through constraints like demographic parity, equalized odds, or equal opportunity. Yet these approaches suffer from significant practical limitations: they often assume binary protected attributes, require multiple model retrainings or sanitizations, enforce rigid equality conditions that ignore real-world cost asymmetries between error types, and fail to scale efficiently to high-arity or multi-attribute settings common in industrial applications such as global fraud detection. Moreover, many methods operate during model training, limiting their applicability to post-hoc correction of already-deployed models. Consequently, there exists a critical need for a flexible, efficient, and model-agnostic framework that enforces a relaxed yet meaningful notion of fairness—balancing equity with operational feasibility—while preserving model fidelity, privacy, and computational efficiency in real-world deployment scenarios.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel fairness deviation constraint that relaxes the strict equality requirements of traditional fairness metrics such as equalized odds by permitting performance rates—specifically false positive rates (FPR) and true positive rates (TPR)—to vary within statistically bounded deviations from a population-wide mean. This constraint is operationalized through a decision matrix that encodes calibrated decision thresholds tailored to individual values of one or more protected attributes. The system enforces the fairness deviation constraint by iteratively analyzing model performance across sub-populations, pruning threshold candidates that violate the deviation bounds, and selecting optimal thresholds based on a user-defined accuracy metric such as F0.5, F1, or F2 scores, thereby aligning fairness objectives with business-specific error cost structures. Critically, the fairness deviation constraint offers flexibility: users may choose to constrain only FPRs, only TPRs, or both, depending on whether false positives or false negatives carry greater operational or ethical weight. This adaptability enables the system to conform to varying regulatory, commercial, or social priorities without requiring model retraining or access to raw feature data, thus supporting post-hoc, differentially private, and model-agnostic bias correction. The invention further extends this framework to multi-attribute settings through both strong (joint sub-population) and weak (marginal attribute-wise) fairness enforcement strategies, enhanced by attribute pruning based on statistical dependence to reduce computational complexity. By replacing rigid equality with a principled deviation tolerance grounded in standard statistical measures—mean and standard deviation—the invention achieves a pragmatic balance between fairness, accuracy, efficiency, and deployability across real-world machine learning systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention discloses a bias correction system designed to mitigate algorithmic bias in machine learning models while preserving predictive performance, privacy, and computational efficiency. Conventional bias mitigation systems suffer from several critical limitations. First, they often enforce strict equality of performance metrics—such as identical false positive or true positive rates—across all sub-populations defined by protected attributes. This rigidity ignores real-world variability in ground truth distributions; for instance, in fraud detection, countries inherently exhibit different baseline fraud rates due to jurisdictional, economic, or infrastructural factors. Enforcing identical outcome rates under such conditions forces the model to either over-accept fraud in low-fraud regions or over-reject legitimate transactions in high-fraud regions, degrading both fairness and utility. Second, conventional systems frequently conflate type-1 errors (false positives) and type-2 errors (false negatives) as equally costly, despite business contexts where one error type may be significantly more damaging—for example, accepting a fraudulent transaction may incur direct financial loss, whereas declining a genuine user may only result in temporary customer inconvenience. Third, many existing approaches, such as those implementing equalized odds or equalized opportunity, are designed for binary protected attributes and require separate model sanitizations for each attribute value, leading to exponential computational overhead in high-arity settings like country or currency codes. This inefficiency is compounded when multiple protected attributes are involved, as naive enumeration of all attribute combinations results in combinatorial explosion. Furthermore, repeated sanitizations undermine system fidelity by distorting original model outputs, compromise privacy by necessitating access to granular user-level data, and reduce flexibility by locking fairness enforcement into rigid, non-adaptive protocols.

To overcome these drawbacks, the present invention introduces a fairness deviation constraint that replaces strict equality with a statistically grounded tolerance band. Specifically, for a protected attribute with K values, the system computes the mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ) of a selected performance metric—such as FPR or TPR—across all K sub-populations. The fairness deviation constraint then requires that each sub-population’s metric lies within [μ − nσ, μ + nσ], where n is a configurable parameter (e.g., n=2). This relaxed condition acknowledges natural variation while preventing extreme disparities. The system learns a decision matrix comprising decision thresholds—one per attribute value—that, when applied to model output probabilities, yield classifications satisfying the fairness deviation constraint. These thresholds are determined through an iterative process: first, a grid of candidate thresholds is initialized; second, for each candidate, performance metrics are computed per sub-population; third, candidates violating the fairness deviation constraint are pruned; fourth, among remaining candidates, the threshold maximizing a user-specified accuracy metric (e.g., F0.5 if false positives are costlier) is selected. This loop repeats until convergence, ensuring the final decision matrix satisfies fairness while optimizing task-specific accuracy.

The system extracts features from input samples, generates classification probabilities via a pre-trained machine learning model, and applies the learned decision thresholds to produce sanitized predictions. It analyzes multiple values across multiple data attributes simultaneously, enabling determination of distinct decision matrices for complex sub-populations. Multiple approaches—including random initialization, heuristic seeding, or optimization-based search—may be used to generate initial thresholds, enhancing robustness. Computational complexity is reduced through attribute pruning: dependent attributes (e.g., country and currency, measured via chi-square statistics) are identified and redundant ones removed, minimizing the attribute space. The system provides user interfaces allowing flexible definition of fairness deviation constraints—selecting which metrics to constrain (FPR, TPR, or both), setting the deviation multiplier n, and choosing the accuracy metric—thereby improving accuracy, flexibility, and efficiency relative to conventional systems. Privacy and security are enhanced because the method operates solely on aggregate performance statistics, enabling differential privacy via noise addition without accessing raw features. Model fidelity remains intact as only output thresholds—not internal model parameters—are modified.

The system environment comprises client devices, servers, databases, and networks. Client applications submit digital inputs (e.g., transaction records) to a machine learning system hosting the bias correction system. The latter trains or interfaces with pre-trained models, generates sanitized predictions, and supports various prediction types and classes. It dynamically determines new attribute value sets, regenerates classification probabilities, computes new thresholds, and produces updated sanitized outputs—even for samples unassociated with specific users. The bias correction system initializes a decision matrix, selects randomized or heuristic-based initial thresholds, and iteratively refines them by comparing classification probabilities against thresholds, evaluating performance against ground truth, and computing mean and deviation of metrics per attribute value. Pruning occurs when sub-population metrics exceed the fairness deviation constraint; new thresholds are selected using an accuracy model that scores candidates based on the chosen metric (e.g., F1). This iteration continues until all sub-populations satisfy the constraint.

For multi-dimensional populations, the system supports both strong fairness (enforcing constraints on all joint attribute combinations) and weak fairness (enforcing constraints per attribute marginally). In strong mode, sub-populations with insufficient sample sizes (e.g., <100) are pruned using scalable data structures like Frequent Pattern Trees. Dependency metrics guide attribute pruning to avoid redundancy. The system includes specialized managers: a sample manager handles input data; a fairness deviation constraint manager configures and enforces bounds; a machine learning model manager interfaces with predictors; a performance metric manager computes FPR/TPR; a decision matrix manager stores and updates thresholds; and a storage manager persists components. Communication between modules occurs via APIs or message queues. Components may be implemented as standalone applications or cloud services, supporting service models (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS) and deployment models (private, public, hybrid cloud).

In operation, the system generates enhanced digital outputs—such as fairer fraud decisions—via a flowchart-driven process: predicted probabilities are generated; fairness deviation constraints are determined via user selection or default rules; performance rates (FPR/TPR) are computed; thresholds are modified iteratively; and sanitized classifications are output. Acts include generating probabilities, determining constraints via mean/deviation or performance windows, pruning attributes, identifying sub-populations, initializing and modifying thresholds, combining thresholds for multi-attribute cases, and applying thresholds differentially privately. Computer-readable media store instructions for these acts, executable on systems with processors, memory, and storage, deployable in cloud environments. The invention thus delivers a comprehensive, efficient, and flexible solution for real-world fairness-aware machine learning.
