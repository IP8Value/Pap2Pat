## BACKGROUND

The present invention resides in the field of oncology, specifically in the diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic management of metastatic cancer through the identification and utilization of novel biomarkers associated with tumor cell invasion and dissemination. Metastasis—the spread of malignant cells from a primary tumor to distant organs—is the principal cause of mortality in patients diagnosed with epithelial cancers, including breast carcinoma. Despite advances in early detection and localized treatment, the molecular mechanisms governing the transition from localized disease to systemic metastasis remain incompletely characterized, thereby limiting the development of effective interventions for advanced-stage disease.

Cancer cell invasion is a critical step in the metastatic cascade, wherein tumor cells breach the basement membrane and infiltrate the surrounding stromal tissue. This invasive behavior is driven by dynamic reorganization of the actin cytoskeleton, which enables the formation of specialized membrane protrusions that facilitate directional migration through the extracellular matrix (ECM). Among these structures, lamellipodia—broad, sheet-like extensions enriched in branched actin networks—are essential for mesenchymal-type migration observed in many aggressive carcinomas. Lamellipodium formation is orchestrated by a complex interplay of signaling molecules and cytoskeletal regulators that respond to chemotactic cues such as epidermal growth factor (EGF) present in the tumor microenvironment.

Lamellipodin (Lpd), a recently characterized adaptor protein, plays a pivotal role in lamellipodium dynamics by serving as a scaffold that links upstream signaling pathways to downstream actin nucleation and elongation machinery. Lpd localizes to the leading edge of migrating cells and directly interacts with key actin regulatory complexes, including the Ena/VASP family of proteins and the Scar/WAVE complex. Ena/VASP proteins promote the processive elongation of unbranched actin filaments, while the Scar/WAVE complex activates the Arp2/3 complex to generate branched actin networks necessary for protrusive force generation. Through these interactions, Lpd coordinates the spatial and temporal assembly of actin structures required for efficient cell motility.

In the context of cancer biology, Lpd’s function extends beyond basic cell migration to actively promote metastatic progression. Experimental evidence demonstrates that Lpd is upregulated in invasive and metastatic breast cancer cell lines compared to non-invasive counterparts, and its expression correlates with poor clinical outcomes in human patients. Moreover, functional studies using genetic knockdown models reveal that Lpd is indispensable for tumor cell invasion, intravasation into blood vessels, and subsequent metastatic colonization of distant organs such as the lungs. These findings position Lpd not merely as a structural component of the motility apparatus but as a central node in pro-metastatic signaling pathways activated by oncogenic kinases such as c-Src and c-Abl.

Despite the recognized importance of metastasis in cancer lethality, current diagnostic and prognostic tools suffer from significant limitations. Conventional biomarkers often lack specificity for early metastatic potential or fail to distinguish indolent tumors from those likely to disseminate aggressively. Imaging modalities can detect macroscopic metastases but are insensitive to micrometastatic disease or pre-invasive states. Consequently, there exists an urgent need for reliable, molecularly defined biomarkers that can accurately predict metastatic risk at the time of initial diagnosis, thereby enabling personalized therapeutic strategies aimed at preventing systemic spread.

The limitations of existing biomarkers underscore the necessity for novel approaches grounded in mechanistic insights into metastasis. Proteins like Lpd, whose expression levels and subcellular localization directly influence invasive capacity and correlate with patient survival, represent promising candidates for next-generation diagnostics. By integrating quantitative assessment of Lpd expression—whether at the mRNA or protein level—with clinical parameters, clinicians may gain unprecedented insight into a tumor’s metastatic propensity, facilitating earlier intervention and improved patient stratification.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides compositions and methods for assessing, diagnosing, and prognosing metastatic cancer based on the detection and quantification of Lamellipodin (Lpd) expression in biological samples. Lpd has been identified as a critical regulator of cancer cell invasion and metastasis, with elevated levels strongly correlating with reduced metastasis-free and disease-free survival in breast cancer patients. Accordingly, the invention exploits Lpd as a novel biomarker for metastatic potential, offering a robust platform for clinical decision-making.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for assessing metastatic cancer in a subject, comprising obtaining a biological sample from the subject, determining the level of Lpd protein or gene expression in the sample, and correlating the determined level with the metastatic state of the cancer. Elevated Lpd expression—relative to a reference or control value—is indicative of an increased likelihood of metastasis or a more aggressive disease course. This correlation enables both diagnostic confirmation of metastatic status and prognostic prediction of future metastatic events.

An alternative embodiment of the method involves detecting Lpd protein in the biological sample using an Lpd-specific antibody. The amount of Lpd protein is then quantified through immunohistochemical, immunofluorescent, or immunoassay-based techniques, and this quantitative measure is correlated with the metastatic state. For instance, high membrane-localized Lpd staining intensity in tumor tissue sections, as assessed by histoscore, is significantly associated with reduced metastasis-free survival, providing a clinically actionable metric.

Another embodiment entails detecting Lpd gene expression by measuring mRNA levels via nucleic acid hybridization or amplification techniques such as reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). The expression level of Lpd mRNA is compared to a reference threshold derived from non-metastatic or healthy tissue, and deviations above this threshold signal heightened metastatic risk. This approach is particularly valuable in archival tissue analysis or liquid biopsy contexts where protein integrity may be compromised.

A further method encompasses extracting total RNA or protein from a biological sample, determining Lpd protein or gene expression levels using appropriate analytical platforms, and correlating these levels with the metastatic state to inform diagnosis or prognosis. This flexible framework accommodates diverse sample types—including formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissues, fresh frozen biopsies, blood, or fine-needle aspirates—and integrates seamlessly with existing laboratory workflows.

In therapeutic applications, the invention contemplates administering a therapeutic agent to a subject identified as having elevated Lpd levels, thereby targeting the Lpd-mediated pro-metastatic pathway. Such agents may include small molecule inhibitors of c-Src or c-Abl kinases, siRNA or shRNA constructs designed to suppress Lpd expression, or monoclonal antibodies that neutralize Lpd function or block its interactions with Ena/VASP or Scar/WAVE complexes. Additionally, the invention provides diagnostic kits containing Lpd-specific antibodies, nucleic acid probes, control reagents, and instructions for use, enabling standardized assessment of Lpd status in clinical or research settings.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS

Figure 1A shows Western blot analysis demonstrating higher Lpd protein levels in invasive and metastatic basal breast cancer cell lines compared to non-invasive luminal lines. Figure 1B presents Kaplan–Meier survival curves from the NKI295 cohort, illustrating that high Lpd mRNA expression correlates with reduced metastasis-free survival. Figure 1C displays similar survival data from the Loi cohort, confirming the association between elevated Lpd mRNA and poor clinical outcome. Figure 1D shows representative immunohistochemical staining of Lpd in a breast tumor tissue microarray, highlighting cytoplasmic and membrane localization. Figure 1E provides Kaplan–Meier curves indicating that high plasma membrane Lpd abundance (Histoscore 3) is significantly associated with reduced disease-free and metastasis-free survival. Figure 1F depicts cytoplasmic Lpd histoscores and their correlation with breast cancer-specific mortality. Figure 1G illustrates the inverse relationship between Lpd membrane intensity and Her2 expression. Figures 1H–1J show validation of Lpd knockdown efficiency and controls used in subsequent functional assays.

Figure 2 introduces in vivo metastasis models. Figures 2A–2E demonstrate that Lpd knockdown in LM2 breast cancer cells does not affect primary tumor growth but drastically reduces lung metastasis incidence and burden in orthotopic mouse models. Figures 2F–2I present intravital imaging data showing that Lpd-deficient tumors exhibit fewer motile cells with impaired directional migration and protrusion dynamics. Figures 2J–2K utilize a zebrafish embryo model to show that Lpd overexpression enhances cancer cell seeding and dissemination. Figures 2L–2P confirm that Lpd depletion does not impair later metastatic steps such as extravasation, as tail vein injection experiments yield comparable lung colonization.

Figure 3 focuses on cellular protrusion dynamics. Figures 3A–3B quantify reduced protrusion persistence and distance in Lpd-depleted MTLn3 cells upon EGF stimulation. Figures 3C–3F show rapid recruitment of Lpd to the cell edge following EGF treatment and delayed lamellipodial initiation in knockdown cells. Figures 3G–3K provide kymographic and time-lapse analyses supporting these conclusions.

Figure 4 demonstrates Lpd’s role in actin polymerization and chemosensing. Figures 4A–4B show reduced barbed-end formation in Lpd-depleted cells after EGF stimulation. Figures 4C–4F reveal impaired recruitment of Mena and Arp2/3 to protruding edges. Figures 4G–4H and supplementary videos illustrate defective chemotactic responses in micropipette gradient assays. Figure 4I confirms that Lpd’s interaction with Ena/VASP is essential for chemosensing, as an Ena/VASP-binding-deficient Lpd mutant fails to rescue the phenotype.
