# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to electrical machines, and more particularly to high-power-density electric motors suitable for demanding applications such as electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft propulsion systems. The invention specifically concerns a novel stator architecture that integrates magnetics, thermal management, and electrical insulation into a unified structure to enhance reliability, power density, and thermal performance under transient high-load conditions typical of aerospace electric drive systems.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Electric transportation systems, especially those employed in advanced air mobility platforms like eVTOL aircraft, impose stringent requirements on electric motor performance. During critical flight phases such as takeoff and landing, the propulsion system may experience peak power demands that are three to ten times greater than those encountered during steady-state cruise operation. This dramatic increase in electrical loading results in elevated current densities within the motor windings, which in turn leads to significant copper losses and rapid temperature rise in the conducting regions. Without effective thermal management, these transient thermal excursions can compromise the integrity of electrical insulation systems, potentially leading to premature failure or catastrophic breakdown. Conventional active cooling approaches—such as liquid cooling jackets or forced-air systems—often add complexity, weight, and maintenance burden, which are undesirable in weight-sensitive aerospace applications. Moreover, traditional passive cooling strategies relying solely on conduction through ferromagnetic laminations suffer from inherently low thermal conductivity in electrical steel, thereby limiting heat extraction from the windings to the external heatsink. To address these challenges, phase change materials have been explored for transient heat storage; however, they do not provide a continuous thermal pathway and are unsuitable for sustained high-power operation. There remains a need for an integrated solution that simultaneously improves electromagnetic performance, enhances thermal conduction from loss-generating regions, and reinforces dielectric robustness without compromising manufacturability or adding excessive mass.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is motivated by the recognition that interleaving ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic materials within the stator yoke can create parallel thermal conduction paths that significantly reduce the overall thermal resistance between the windings and the external cooling interface. In one aspect, the invention provides a stator body comprising a laminated stack of alternating ferrous and nonferrous layers, wherein the nonferrous layers are selected for their high thermal conductivity while maintaining compatibility with magnetic flux guidance. This architecture improves thermal conductivity along the radial direction from the coil region to the outer heatsink, thereby mitigating hotspots and enabling higher transient current densities. The nonferrous material is preferably a highly conductive metal such as copper or aluminum, though other thermally conductive, nonmagnetic materials may be employed. In preferred embodiments, the volume ratio of nonferrous to ferrous material in the lamination stack is maintained below 30%, and more preferably at approximately 20%, to balance thermal enhancement against electromagnetic performance degradation due to reduced magnetic cross-section. Examples of suitable nonferrous materials include oxygen-free high-conductivity copper (C10100 or C11000), aluminum alloys, or thermally conductive ceramics, though metallic options are favored for their superior thermal transport properties. The laminations are oriented such that their plane is substantially perpendicular to the axis of rotation, forming axial stacks that extend the full active length of the machine. The axial thickness of individual laminations is selected to minimize eddy current losses while facilitating manufacturability; typical values range from 0.1 mm to 0.5 mm for both ferrous and nonferrous layers. In a further refinement, the motor employs a slotless design, eliminating teeth and associated magnetic saturation points, thereby allowing the armature winding to occupy a larger fraction of the airgap circumference and increasing electrical loading. Complementing this, a coil carrier or holder is introduced, fabricated from a material exhibiting high thermal conductivity and excellent dielectric strength, such as aluminum nitride (AlN) or boron nitride (BN). This coil carrier surrounds the form-wound Litz wire conductors and provides an additional lateral thermal shunt path from the winding surface directly to the stator yoke or heatsink, bypassing the otherwise resistive insulation layers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

In a preferred embodiment, the invention is implemented in an electric propulsion system for an aircraft, particularly an eVTOL platform. The system includes an electric motor mechanically coupled to a propulsor and electrically connected to a motor drive, which in turn communicates with an aircraft controller responsible for managing flight dynamics and power distribution. The motor is configured as a slotless-winding, outer-rotor, air-cooled permanent magnet synchronous machine (PMSM), chosen for its high torque density, smooth torque production, and suitability for direct-drive propeller integration. The rotor, mounted on the exterior, carries a Halbach array of permanent magnets that concentrate flux on the airgap side while minimizing back-iron requirements. The stator, positioned radially inward, comprises a laminated yoke structure surrounding the armature windings. A key innovation is the incorporation of a coil form or holder made from a high-thermal-conductivity, electrically insulating ceramic such as aluminum nitride. This coil form supports the pre-formed Litz wire coils and establishes a robust thermal bridge from the conductor surfaces laterally outward to the stator yoke. The coil construction utilizes multi-strand Litz wire to mitigate skin and proximity effects at high frequencies, wound in a form-wound geometry to maximize packing factor and mechanical stability. Magnetic flux is generated by the interaction between the rotating permanent magnet field and the stator currents, with the flux primarily conducted through the ferromagnetic laminations of the yoke. To enhance thermal performance without severely degrading magnetic circuit efficiency, the flux conductor is constructed from a laminated stack that interleaves conventional electrical steel laminations with thin sheets of high-conductivity nonferromagnetic material, such as copper. The lamination materials are selected to ensure minimal eddy current generation; the ferrous laminations are typically grain-oriented or non-oriented silicon steel with insulating coatings, while the nonferrous laminations are high-purity copper foils with surface treatments to promote bonding and prevent galvanic corrosion. The thermal conductivity of copper (~400 W/m·K) vastly exceeds that of electrical steel (~25–50 W/m·K), enabling rapid lateral heat spreading. Nonferromagnetic laminations are distributed axially throughout the stack at regular intervals—for example, one copper lamination inserted for every two or three steel laminations—to maintain structural integrity and magnetic continuity while establishing effective thermal shunts. The proportion of nonferromagnetic laminations is carefully optimized; empirical and simulation studies indicate that a copper fill factor of approximately 20% yields a 40% reduction in thermal resistance with less than a 1% reduction in torque output. An external heatsink, optionally featuring radial fins or additively manufactured geometries for enhanced airflow, is affixed to the outer diameter of the stator yoke. Airflow, either natural or forced via vehicle motion or auxiliary fans, passes over the heatsink fins, convectively removing heat. Heat dissipation mechanisms thus include conduction through the ceramic holder and interleaved laminations, followed by convection at the heatsink surface. The manufacturing process involves precision stacking of laminations, which may be bonded using thermally stable epoxies such as EB-548, followed by insertion of the ceramic coil forms and winding of the armature coils. Potting forms and resin impregnation may be employed to secure windings and improve environmental protection. An alternative construction may utilize segmented ceramic holders assembled axially from waterjet-cut plates to accommodate complex geometries. The motor drive continuously monitors power consumption and estimates winding temperature using a thermal model that accounts for copper loss, thermal resistances, and cooling conditions. When the predicted temperature approaches a predefined safety limit—typically set below the insulation class rating—the controller issues a warning and may throttle down power output to prevent overheating. Advanced implementations project future temperature trajectories based on load profiles and display a “thermal margin” to the operator or flight management system. While particularly advantageous in aerospace applications, the invention is broadly applicable to any high-performance electric machine requiring enhanced thermal resilience, including automotive traction motors, industrial drives, and marine propulsion systems.
