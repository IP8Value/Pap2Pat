# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to wireless communication systems, and more specifically to methods and apparatuses for beam management in fifth-generation (5G) and beyond 5G mobile communication systems operating in millimeter wave (mmWave) frequency bands. The invention particularly addresses the design of sub-chain beam codebooks for user equipment (UE) that enable dynamic adjustment of active antenna elements to manage power consumption and thermal conditions while preserving downlink-uplink (DL-UL) beam correspondence. This technical field encompasses antenna array architectures, beamforming techniques, codebook design methodologies, and adaptive beam management procedures that facilitate efficient mmWave communication without compromising link reliability or requiring frequent fallback to lower-frequency radio access technologies.

## BACKGROUND

Wireless communication systems have evolved significantly to support increasing data rate demands, culminating in the deployment of 5G networks capable of delivering gigabit-per-second throughput. A key enabler of such high data rates is the use of mmWave spectrum, which offers vast bandwidth resources unavailable in traditional sub-6 GHz bands. However, mmWave communication presents unique challenges, particularly regarding power consumption and thermal management at the user equipment (UE) level. The high path loss characteristic of mmWave frequencies necessitates the use of directional beamforming with large antenna arrays to achieve sufficient signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) for reliable communication. While these antenna arrays enable high-gain beams that compensate for path loss, they also consume substantial power, especially during uplink transmission where power amplifiers must operate at high output levels. This power consumption leads to rapid heating of the UE, which can trigger thermal throttling mechanisms that either reduce performance or force the device to fall back to sub-6 GHz operation. Such fallback operations are undesirable because they result in significant throughput degradation, increased latency due to reconfiguration overhead, and potential service disruption. Existing approaches to power management in mmWave systems have focused primarily on reducing transmission power levels across all active antennas, but this approach fails to address the fundamental issue that each active power amplifier consumes a base amount of power regardless of its output level. Consequently, there remains a critical need for improved wireless communication techniques that enable dynamic power management while maintaining the beam correspondence essential for efficient 5G beam management procedures.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel user equipment (UE) beam codebook design that enables adaptive power management through sub-chain beam operation while preserving downlink-uplink beam correspondence. In one embodiment, the UE comprises an antenna array with multiple antenna elements connected to RF chains, processing circuitry configured to implement beamforming operations, and memory storing multiple beam codebooks including full-chain and sub-chain configurations. The UE components include transmit and receive processing circuitry, RF transceivers, and a controller/processor that manages beam selection based on thermal conditions and operational requirements. The UE functionality enables dynamic switching between full-chain and sub-chain beam operations without requiring additional beam sweeping procedures when beam correspondence is maintained. The invention also introduces a method embodiment comprising steps for designing sub-chain beam codebooks using three distinct optimization metrics: similarity score maximization, spherical coverage maximization, and beam correspondence spherical coverage maximization. The method steps include generating candidate beam patterns, evaluating optimization metrics, selecting optimal beam configurations, and implementing adaptive beam management based on real-time conditions. Other technical features include temperature-based chain reduction algorithms, power consumption modeling, and maximum permissible exposure (MPE) considerations. Certain words and phrases are defined throughout the disclosure, including "sub-chain beam" referring to beam configurations that activate only a portion of the available antenna array, "full-chain beam" referring to configurations that activate the entire array, and "beam correspondence" referring to the maintenance of consistent beam indices between downlink and uplink operations.
