# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the combination of decanoic acid and perampanel or another AMPA receptor inhibitor for use in the treatment of neurological and neurodegenerative disorders. More specifically, the invention provides a novel therapeutic approach that exploits a synergistic interaction between decanoic acid—a medium-chain fatty acid found in medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) formulations—and perampanel, a clinically approved non-competitive antagonist of AMPA (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid) receptors. This combination is particularly useful in the treatment of epilepsy, but also extends to other conditions mediated by excessive AMPA receptor activity, including amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), cerebral ischemia, certain forms of cancer, and Alzheimer’s disease. The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, kits, methods of treatment, and products comprising this synergistic combination, offering enhanced efficacy at lower doses of each component, thereby potentially reducing side effects associated with monotherapy.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Epilepsy is a chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures resulting from abnormal, excessive, or synchronous neuronal activity in the brain. Seizures can manifest in various forms, ranging from brief lapses of attention to severe convulsions, and significantly impair quality of life. Despite the availability of numerous antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), approximately one-third of patients suffer from drug-resistant epilepsy, necessitating alternative or adjunctive therapeutic strategies. Current treatment options include pharmacological agents targeting ion channels, neurotransmitter systems, or synaptic transmission, as well as non-pharmacological interventions such as surgery, vagus nerve stimulation, and dietary therapies.

Among dietary approaches, the ketogenic diet has long been recognized as an effective treatment for refractory epilepsy, particularly in pediatric populations. The classical ketogenic diet is high in fat and low in carbohydrates and protein, inducing a metabolic state of ketosis. However, adherence to this strict regimen is challenging. The medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) ketogenic diet was developed as a more palatable alternative, leveraging the rapid hepatic metabolism of MCTs into ketone bodies. Interestingly, recent evidence suggests that the antiseizure effects of the MCT diet may not solely depend on ketosis; rather, specific medium-chain fatty acids, notably decanoic acid, exert direct inhibitory effects on neuronal excitability.

Decanoic acid, a saturated 10-carbon fatty acid, has been shown to non-competitively inhibit AMPA receptors—ligand-gated ion channels critical for fast excitatory synaptic transmission in the central nervous system. Overactivation of AMPA receptors is implicated in seizure generation and propagation, making them a key therapeutic target. Perampanel (Fycompa®), a selective, non-competitive AMPA receptor antagonist, is approved as an adjunctive therapy for partial-onset and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures. However, its clinical utility is limited by dose-dependent neuropsychiatric side effects, including dizziness, somnolence, and aggression.

Although both perampanel and decanoic acid act on AMPA receptors, they bind to distinct molecular sites: perampanel interacts with the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linkers of the receptor, while decanoic acid binds within the transmembrane M3 domain. This spatial separation of binding sites provides a mechanistic basis for potential synergy. Prior art does not disclose or suggest that combining these two agents would result in enhanced inhibition of AMPA receptor currents beyond simple additive effects. The limitations of the MCT diet—including gastrointestinal intolerance, variable compliance, and uncertain brain bioavailability of decanoic acid—further underscore the need for a refined, pharmacologically optimized strategy that harnesses the benefits of decanoic acid without relying solely on dietary delivery.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the unexpected discovery that decanoic acid and perampanel act synergistically to inhibit AMPA receptor activity, resulting in significantly enhanced suppression of epileptiform activity compared to either agent alone. This synergy arises from their distinct but complementary mechanisms of action on the AMPA receptor complex, allowing for greater efficacy at lower concentrations of each compound. Consequently, the combination enables effective seizure control while minimizing the risk of adverse effects typically associated with higher doses of perampanel. The invention thus provides a superior therapeutic regimen for epilepsy and other AMPA receptor-mediated pathologies, offering improved clinical outcomes through rational polypharmacy grounded in molecular pharmacology.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Combination

As used herein, a “combination” refers to the co-administration of decanoic acid and perampanel or an AMPA receptor inhibitor, whether administered simultaneously in the same formulation, sequentially in separate formulations, or separately via different routes or at different times, provided that both agents are present in the body during a therapeutically relevant window to achieve synergistic AMPA receptor inhibition. Administration may be simultaneous, where both agents are delivered at the same time; sequential, where one agent is given before or after the other with overlapping pharmacokinetic profiles; or separate, where formulations are taken independently but within a dosing schedule designed to maintain concurrent therapeutic levels. Co-formulation involves a single pharmaceutical dosage form containing both active ingredients, such as a tablet, capsule, or liquid suspension. Alternatively, separate formulations may be provided, for example, decanoic acid in an oil-based medical food and perampanel as an oral tablet. A kit according to the invention comprises at least two preparations: one containing decanoic acid and another containing perampanel or an AMPA receptor inhibitor, optionally with instructions for combined use. The product may be presented as a combined preparation for packaging, sale, or prescription purposes, even if physically separate. Alternative embodiments include sustained-release formulations, enteric-coated tablets, or emulsified delivery systems to enhance bioavailability or reduce gastrointestinal side effects.

### Decanoic Acid and Compositions Comprising the Same

Decanoic acid (capric acid, C10:0) is a saturated medium-chain fatty acid naturally occurring in coconut oil, palm kernel oil, and dairy fats. It may be used in free acid form or as part of a triglyceride. Medium-chain triglycerides (MCTs) are esters of glycerol and medium-chain fatty acids (typically C6–C12), with decanoic acid being a predominant component. Fatty acid moieties in MCTs may include caproic (C6), caprylic (C8), capric (C10), and lauric (C12) acids. Homotriglycerides contain three identical fatty acid chains (e.g., tridecanoin), whereas heterotriglycerides contain mixed chains. In preferred embodiments, the composition is free from or substantially free from lauric acid (C12) and long-chain fatty acids (≥C14), as these may interfere with ketogenic efficacy or AMPA receptor modulation. Natural MCT sources include fractionated coconut oil enriched in C8 and C10. Synthetic MCTs can be produced via esterification of glycerol with purified decanoic acid. Compositions may optionally include long-chain triglycerides (LCTs) in controlled ratios to modulate energy density or palatability. Additional substances may include emulsifiers (e.g., lecithin), antioxidants (e.g., tocopherols), flavorings, and stabilizers. Essential minerals such as sodium, potassium, magnesium, and calcium may be added to prevent electrolyte imbalances. Vitamins, particularly B-complex and fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), may be included to support nutritional adequacy. The composition may be formulated as an oil-in-water emulsion, with total fat content ranging from 50% to 90% by weight and energy value of 8–9 kcal/g. Delivery may occur as part of a medically supervised ketogenic diet, with a fat-to-(protein + carbohydrate) ratio of at least 2:1, preferably 3:1 or 4:1. Forms include oils, powders, capsules, beverages, and medical foods. Administration routes include oral, enteral, or parenteral (e.g., intravenous emulsion). Food substances may be incorporated into bars, shakes, or meal replacements designed for epilepsy management.

### Perampanel

Perampanel (3-(2-cyano-4-fluorophenyl)-7-fluoro-1H-indole-2-carboxylic acid) is a selective, non-competitive AMPA receptor antagonist approved for adjunctive epilepsy treatment. Its chemical structure features a fluorinated indole core with cyano and carboxylic acid substituents. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts include sodium, potassium, or meglumine salts to improve solubility. Perampanel is typically administered orally once daily, with clinical doses ranging from 2 mg to 12 mg. It functions as an AMPA receptor antagonist by binding to an allosteric site at the interface of the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linkers of GluA subunits, stabilizing the closed-channel conformation. AMPA receptor inhibitors that bind to the same site as perampanel—such as GYKI-52466 derivatives—are encompassed within the term “AMPA receptor inhibitor” as used herein. Techniques for identifying such inhibitors include radioligand binding assays, fluorescence-based conformational sensors, and computational docking. Electrophysiological techniques, particularly whole-cell patch clamp and two-electrode voltage clamp in recombinant or native neurons, are used to assay AMPA receptor activity. An IC50 value for AMPA receptor inhibition of less than 10 μmol/L is indicative of potent activity. Inhibitors may be small molecules (<900 Da) or engineered polypeptides, though small molecules are preferred for blood-brain barrier penetration.

### Treatment

“Treatment” as used herein includes prophylaxis, mitigation, amelioration, or cure of a disease or condition. “Prevent” refers to reducing the likelihood or delaying the onset of a disorder. Subjects for treatment include humans and non-human mammals diagnosed with or at risk of epilepsy, ALS, ischemia, cancer, or Alzheimer’s disease. Epilepsy is motivated by the high prevalence of drug resistance and the need for safer, more effective therapies. AMPA receptors mediate fast excitatory transmission; their overactivation contributes to seizure initiation and spread. The combination directly suppresses this hyperexcitability. For ALS, excessive glutamate signaling via AMPA receptors causes motor neuron excitotoxicity; thus, dual inhibition offers neuroprotection. Ischemia triggers glutamate release and Ca²⁺ influx through AMPA receptors, leading to neuronal death; the combination mitigates this cascade. In certain cancers (e.g., glioblastoma), AMPA receptors promote proliferation and invasion; inhibition may suppress tumor growth. In Alzheimer’s disease, AMPA receptor dysregulation contributes to synaptic loss and cognitive decline; modulation may preserve function. The combination is applicable across these indications due to shared AMPA receptor pathology.

### Administration

Administration routes include oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, intranasal, and rectal. Oral administration is preferred for chronic conditions like epilepsy. Intravenous delivery may be used in acute settings such as status epilepticus or stroke. Formulations are tailored to route: tablets/capsules for oral, solutions for IV, sprays for intranasal. Dosing regimens are adjusted based on age, weight, disease severity, and concomitant medications.

## EXAMPLES

Conventional techniques in molecular pharmacology, electrophysiology, and ex vivo tissue modeling were employed, consistent with standards in the field (see, e.g., *Journal of Neuroscience Methods*, *Epilepsia*).

### Example 1—Methods

Direct AMPA receptor currents were recorded from Xenopus oocytes expressing human GluA1/2 or GluA2/3 subunits using two-electrode voltage clamp. Seizure-like activity was induced in rat entorhinal cortex-hippocampal slices by perfusion with pentylenetetrazol (PTZ, 5 mM), and discharge frequency was quantified via extracellular field potential recordings.

### Example 2—Direct Inhibition of AMPA Receptors by Perampanel and Decanoic Acid

Perampanel inhibited GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 currents with IC50 values of 6.27 and 8.11 μmol/L, respectively, in a non-competitive manner. A GluA3 mutant resistant to GYKI showed reduced sensitivity to perampanel but unchanged response to decanoic acid, confirming distinct binding sites. When tested together, decanoic acid’s IC50 decreased 3- to 10-fold in the presence of perampanel, and vice versa. Isobolographic analysis confirmed true synergy, with combination data points falling below the additive line for both receptor subtypes.

### Example 3—Effects of Perampanel and Decanoic Acid on Seizure Models

In rat hippocampal slices, 100 nmol/L perampanel plus 10 μmol/L decanoic acid significantly reduced PTZ-induced seizure activity compared to either agent alone (P < 0.05). The IC50 of decanoic acid dropped from 352 to 122 μmol/L with 500 nmol/L perampanel. In human neocortical slices from epilepsy patients, the combination virtually abolished epileptiform activity (P < 0.0001 vs. monotherapies), demonstrating translational relevance.

## SUMMARY

The data demonstrate that the combination of decanoic acid and perampanel produces direct synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors, evidenced by significant leftward shifts in concentration-response curves and isobolographic validation. This synergy translates into enhanced suppression of seizure-like activity in both rodent and human ex vivo brain slice models, with near-complete abolition of pathological network activity in human tissue. The clinical relevance is substantial: by leveraging dietary or supplemental decanoic acid alongside reduced doses of perampanel, patients may achieve superior seizure control with fewer behavioral side effects. This combinatory approach represents a paradigm shift in epilepsy management, merging dietary and pharmacological strategies through a unified molecular mechanism. The invention thus offers a scientifically grounded, clinically actionable advancement with broad applicability across multiple neurological disorders.
