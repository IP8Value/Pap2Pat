# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of protein engineering and biologics development, specifically to engineered polypeptides that incorporate variant Fc regions derived from immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies. These engineered polypeptides are designed to exhibit reduced or abrogated effector functions while maintaining favorable biophysical properties, antigen-binding capabilities, and pharmacokinetic profiles. The invention is particularly relevant to the development of therapeutic antibodies and Fc fusion proteins intended for use in indications where immune-mediated cytotoxicity, inflammation, or complement activation is undesirable or potentially harmful. By modulating the interaction between the Fc region and immune effector components such as Fc gamma receptors (FcγRs) and complement C1q, the disclosed polypeptides offer improved safety and manufacturability without compromising stability, solubility, or expression yield. The engineered polypeptides find application in the treatment of autoimmune disorders, inflammatory diseases, oncology (particularly in T cell-engaging bispecific formats), and other conditions where precise targeting without collateral immune activation is therapeutically advantageous.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Biologics, including therapeutic monoclonal antibodies, represent a major class of modern pharmaceuticals due to their high specificity, potency, and favorable pharmacokinetic properties. Monoclonal antibodies function by binding to specific antigens on target cells or soluble mediators, thereby neutralizing pathogenic activity or marking targets for immune clearance. A key structural and functional component of IgG antibodies is the Fc (fragment crystallizable) region, which mediates interactions with the immune system through binding to Fc receptors (FcRs) expressed on immune cells and to components of the complement cascade. The Fc region of human IgG1, the most commonly used subclass in therapeutics, comprises the CH2 and CH3 domains and includes a lower hinge region connecting the Fab and Fc portions. This region is critical for engaging activating Fcγ receptors—namely FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIa/c (CD32a/c), and FcγRIIIa (CD16a)—which trigger effector functions such as antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) and antibody-dependent cell-mediated phagocytosis (ADCP). Additionally, the Fc region binds the complement protein C1q, initiating the classical complement pathway and leading to complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). While these effector functions are beneficial in oncology applications where tumor cell lysis is desired, they can be detrimental in other therapeutic contexts, such as in autoimmune or inflammatory diseases, or when using T cell-engaging bispecific antibodies, where off-target cytotoxicity or cytokine release may cause severe adverse events.

The neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) plays a distinct role by binding IgG in a pH-dependent manner within endosomes, facilitating recycling and thereby extending serum half-life. Importantly, FcRn binding occurs at the CH2-CH3 interface and is structurally distinct from the FcγR and C1q binding sites located in the lower hinge and proximal CH2 domain. Protein A (ProA), a bacterial protein used in antibody purification, also binds the Fc region at the CH2-CH3 junction, making its retention critical for manufacturing. Over the years, numerous efforts have been made to engineer IgG Fc variants with reduced effector function. Early approaches included switching to IgG2 or IgG4 subclasses, which naturally exhibit lower FcγR and C1q binding; however, these subclasses suffer from poor biophysical properties, including increased aggregation propensity under acidic conditions encountered during manufacturing. Subsequent strategies focused on introducing point mutations into the IgG1 Fc region to disrupt FcγR and C1q interactions while preserving FcRn and ProA binding. Notable examples include the L234A/L235A (LALA) mutation, which reduces but does not eliminate effector function, and more advanced variants such as L234A/L235A/P329G (LALA-PG) and L234F/L235E/P331S (LFLE-PS). Despite progress, many existing Fc-silencing mutations compromise developability—manifested as reduced thermal stability, increased hydrophobicity, or acid-induced aggregation—thereby complicating large-scale production and formulation. Thus, there remains a compelling need for engineered Fc variants that achieve complete abrogation of effector function while maintaining or improving the biophysical and pharmacokinetic properties of wild-type IgG1.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel polypeptides comprising a variant Fc region derived from human IgG1 that exhibits significantly reduced or undetectable binding to activating Fc gamma receptors (FcγRs) and complement C1q, while retaining robust binding to FcRn and Protein A. These polypeptides are engineered to possess superior biophysical developability characteristics, including resistance to acid-induced aggregation and high thermal stability, making them particularly suitable for therapeutic applications requiring minimal immune activation. In one embodiment, the invention features a polypeptide comprising an Fc region with the amino acid substitutions L234A, L235A, and P329A (referred to herein as “LALA-PA”). In another embodiment, the polypeptide comprises the substitutions L234A, L235E, and P329G (“LALE-PG”). Both LALA-PA and LALE-PG variants demonstrate complete loss of binding to FcγRI, FcγRIIa, and FcγRIIIa under highly sensitive assay conditions, including avid immune complex formats, and show no detectable C1q binding. Critically, these variants maintain wild-type-like melting temperatures (Tm) for the CH2 domain and exhibit minimal aggregation following low-pH stress, a key attribute for compatibility with standard viral inactivation steps in antibody manufacturing. The polypeptides may further comprise an antigen-binding region, such as a variable heavy and light chain pair, forming a full-length monoclonal antibody, or may be part of an Fc fusion protein. Optional features include incorporation into bispecific or multispecific antibody formats, conjugation to payloads, or formulation with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients. The invention also encompasses methods for producing these polypeptides, typically involving recombinant expression in mammalian host cells such as HEK293 or CHO cells, followed by purification via Protein A affinity chromatography. Pharmaceutical compositions containing the disclosed polypeptides are provided for administration to subjects in need thereof, particularly in indications where effector function silencing enhances therapeutic index.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

As used herein, technical terms are given their ordinary meaning in the art unless otherwise defined. The term “about” refers to a range of ±10% of the stated value, unless context dictates otherwise. Amino acid positions in the Fc region are designated according to the EU numbering system, which aligns residues based on the mature human IgG1 sequence. The transitional phrases “comprising,” “consisting essentially of,” and “consisting of” are interpreted in accordance with standard patent practice: “comprising” is open-ended, “consisting essentially of” excludes elements that materially affect basic and novel characteristics, and “consisting of” is closed. The invention centers on a variant Fc domain engineered to reduce effector function while preserving developability. A “variant Fc” refers to an Fc region derived from a parent Fc—typically human IgG1—with one or more amino acid substitutions that diminish binding to FcγRs and/or C1q. The Fc region includes the CH2 and CH3 domains and the lower hinge, the latter defined as residues 226–238 (EU numbering). The “parent Fc” is the unmodified Fc sequence from which variants are derived. “Effector function” encompasses ADCC, ADCP, and CDC, mediated respectively by natural killer cells, macrophages, and the complement cascade upon Fc engagement with FcγRs or C1q. FcγRs include the activating receptors FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIa/c (CD32a/c), and FcγRIIIa (CD16a); FcRn is a heterodimer of β2-microglobulin and an MHC class I-like heavy chain that binds IgG at acidic pH. Desirable therapeutic properties of the variant Fc include abolished FcγR/C1q binding, retained FcRn/ProA binding, high expression titer, low polyspecificity, minimal self-interaction, and resistance to aggregation. ADCC is assessed using NK cell-based cytotoxicity assays; ADCP via phagocytosis assays with macrophages; CDC through complement deposition or cell lysis assays. Fc receptor binding is quantified by surface plasmon resonance (SPR) or flow cytometry. The LALA-PA variant (L234A/L235A/P329A) and LALE-PG variant (L234A/L235E/P329G) were shown to eliminate FcγR binding even under high-avidity conditions and abolish C1q binding, while maintaining CH2 Tm within 1–2°C of wild-type and exhibiting <2% aggregate formation after pH 3.5 stress. An “Fc fusion protein” comprises the variant Fc linked to a non-immunoglobulin polypeptide. An “antibody” includes full-length immunoglobulins and antigen-binding fragments such as Fab, scFv, or diabodies. A “monoclonal antibody” is derived from a single clone. “Antigen-binding fragments” retain the ability to bind antigen via complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) within variable heavy (VH) and light (VL) domains. Multispecific antibodies, including bispecifics, bind two or more epitopes. An “epitope” is the specific molecular structure recognized by an antibody. Antibody structure includes two heavy and two light chains, each with variable and constant domains; CDRs (three per VH/VL) confer specificity, flanked by framework regions (FRs). IgG subclasses differ in effector function; IgG1 is preferred for engineering due to superior developability. “Developability” refers to manufacturability attributes: desirable traits include high solubility, low viscosity, thermal stability, and resistance to aggregation; undesirable traits include polyspecificity, hydrophobicity, and low Tm. Developability is assessed via PSR binding, HIC, AC-SINS, SEC, DSF, and pH stress. Polyspecific antibodies bind unintended targets; improved expressability denotes high titers in transient or stable expression systems. Examples include anti-CD3 antibodies incorporating LALA-PA or LALE-PG, which retain antigen affinity while exhibiting silenced effector function and excellent biophysical profiles.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Antibodies

Anti-CD3ε monoclonal antibody ADI-26906 was engineered to incorporate Fc variants including LALA-PA (L234A/L235A/P329A) and LALE-PG (L234A/L235E/P329G). Variants were generated by site-directed mutagenesis of the IgG1 Fc-encoding sequence and expressed in HEK293 cells via transient transfection. Antibodies were purified using Protein A affinity chromatography, yielding >95% monomeric protein as confirmed by size-exclusion chromatography (SEC). All variants retained high-affinity binding to human and cynomolgus CD3ε, as measured by biolayer interferometry (BLI), with KD values comparable to wild-type IgG1, confirming that Fc modifications did not impair antigen recognition.

### Example 2: THP-1 Cell Binding and C1q Binding to Antibodies

Binding of Fc variants to FcγRs on THP-1 cells (which express FcγRI and FcγRII) was assessed under standard (100 nM uncomplexed IgG) and high-avidity conditions (pre-complexed with multivalent CD3ε-BSA). Wild-type IgG1 showed strong binding under both conditions. LALA and LALE hinge variants reduced binding to near-background levels under standard conditions but retained detectable binding under high-avidity conditions. In contrast, LALA-PA and LALE-PG showed no detectable binding above background in either assay. C1q binding was evaluated using a Dynabead-based assay: wild-type IgG1 bound C1q robustly, whereas LALA-PA and LALE-PG showed no detectable binding, consistent with complete abrogation of complement engagement.

### Example 3: FcγR Binding by Antibodies

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) on a Biacore 8K+ system was used to measure binding kinetics to recombinant human FcγRI, FcγRIIa, and FcγRIIIa. Wild-type IgG1 bound all three receptors with nanomolar affinity. LALA and LALE variants showed >10-fold reduction in binding. LALA-PA and LALE-PG exhibited no measurable binding response even at 1000 nM analyte concentration, confirming elimination of FcγR engagement across the activating receptor family.

### Example 4: Developability Assays

A comprehensive panel of developability assays was performed. Polyspecificity reagent (PSR) binding scores ranged from 0.33 (WT) to 0.41 (LALA-PA), indicating low non-specific interactions. Hydrophobic interaction chromatography (HIC) retention times were equivalent to WT, confirming unchanged surface hydrophobicity. Affinity-capture self-interaction nanoparticle spectroscopy (AC-SINS) showed Δλmax <5 nm for all variants, indicating minimal self-association. Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) revealed >98% monomer content pre-stress. After pH 3.5 stress (simulating viral inactivation), LALA-PA and LALE-PG showed only 1.2% and 1.5% aggregate formation, respectively, versus 9.6% for LALA-D265A. Differential scanning fluorimetry (DSF) yielded CH2 Tm values of 66.8°C (LALA-PA) and 66.5°C (LALE-PG), compared to 67.5°C for WT. Expression titers in HEK cells exceeded 50 mg/L, and ForteBio BLI confirmed retained CD3 affinity (KD ~1 nM).

### Example 5: ADCC Activity Assay

ADCC activity was assessed using a luciferase-based reporter assay with engineered Jurkat cells expressing FcγRIIIa and NFAT-luciferase. Wild-type IgG1 induced strong luminescence, indicating potent ADCC. LALA and LALE variants showed >90% reduction. LALA-PA and LALE-PG produced signals indistinguishable from buffer control, confirming complete abrogation of ADCC.

### Example 6: Complement Activity Assay

CDC activity was measured by incubating antibody-opsonized CD3+ PC3 cells with human complement and quantifying C3b deposition via flow cytometry. Wild-type IgG1 induced robust C3b deposition. LALA-PA and LALE-PG showed no detectable C3b signal, confirming elimination of complement activation.
