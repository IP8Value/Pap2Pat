# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of bioluminescent imaging and molecular biology, specifically to engineered luciferase enzymes and their use in conjunction with novel luciferin substrates for enhanced near-infrared (NIR) bioluminescence. More particularly, the invention provides variant polypeptides derived from click beetle red luciferase (CBR), which exhibit improved luminescent properties, altered emission spectra, and modified substrate specificities when used with naphthyl-based luciferin analogs such as amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin (NH₂-NpLH₂) and hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin (OH-NpLH₂). These engineered CBR variants enable highly sensitive and spatially resolved in vivo imaging, especially in deep tissues such as the brain, where conventional bioluminescent systems suffer from poor photon penetration due to absorption by hemoglobin, melanin, and other endogenous chromophores. The invention further encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding these variant luciferases, expression vectors, host cells, transgenic non-human animals, fusion proteins, kits, and methods for utilizing these components in diagnostic, research, and therapeutic applications, including bioluminescence resonance energy transfer (BRET), protein-protein interaction assays, transcriptional reporting, and multispectral tomographic reconstruction.

## BACKGROUND

Bioluminescence imaging (BLI) has become a cornerstone technique in preclinical research, enabling non-invasive monitoring of biological processes in living organisms through the detection of light emitted by luciferase enzymes upon reaction with their cognate substrates. The most widely used system employs firefly luciferase (Luc2) and D-luciferin (D-LH₂), which generates light peaking around 600 nm at physiological temperatures. While effective for superficial tissues, this system is severely limited in deep-tissue applications—such as imaging within the brain, lungs, or bone—due to strong absorption of visible light by hemoglobin, melanin, lipids, and water. This attenuation drastically reduces signal intensity and spatial resolution, hindering accurate quantification and localization of biological events in vivo.

To overcome these limitations, significant efforts have focused on shifting bioluminescent emission into the near-infrared (NIR) window (approximately 650–900 nm), where tissue absorption and scattering are minimized, allowing for deeper photon penetration and improved imaging fidelity. Approaches have included mutagenesis of existing luciferases to red-shift their emission spectra and the development of synthetic luciferin analogs with extended π-conjugation to lower the energy gap of the excited-state oxyluciferin. However, prior attempts have yielded only modest improvements: mutant luciferases often suffer from reduced catalytic efficiency or stability, while many NIR-emitting substrates produce weak signals or exhibit poor bioavailability, cytotoxicity, or instability in physiological conditions.

For instance, although substrates like AkaLumine-HCl and iLH₂ have demonstrated NIR emission with firefly luciferase, their utility remains constrained by suboptimal kinetics, limited solubility, or insufficient brightness relative to D-LH₂/Luc2 under in vivo conditions. Moreover, even when NIR emission is achieved, the absolute photon flux may be too low for robust detection, particularly in deep anatomical structures. Additionally, many engineered systems lose compatibility with native substrates, limiting their versatility in multiplexed or comparative assays. Thus, despite progress, there remains a critical unmet need for a bioluminescent system that simultaneously offers high brightness, true NIR emission (>700 nm), excellent enzyme stability, favorable pharmacokinetics, and compatibility with both conventional and novel substrates.

The present invention addresses these challenges by combining rational protein engineering of click beetle red luciferase (CBR) with the design of naphthothiazole-based luciferin analogs. Unlike previous strategies that rely on alkene-extended conjugation (prone to photodegradation), the naphthyl-fused scaffold provides stable, rigid π-extension, enabling efficient NIR photon generation. Coupled with targeted mutagenesis of key active-site residues involved in hydrogen bonding and substrate orientation, this approach yields a synergistic enhancement in both spectral properties and enzymatic performance. The resulting system not only outperforms existing technologies in deep-tissue imaging but also enables unprecedented resolution in bioluminescence tomography, thereby fulfilling a long-standing need in the field of molecular imaging.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel click beetle luciferase variant polypeptides exhibiting enhanced luminescence, altered light emission wavelength, and modified substrate specificity compared to wild-type click beetle red luciferase (CBR). In particular, the invention features a CBR variant polypeptide comprising amino acid substitutions at positions corresponding to R334 and G351 of wild-type CBR, wherein the substitutions result in improved catalytic efficiency with naphthyl-based luciferin analogs, notably NH₂-NpLH₂ and OH-NpLH₂. One exemplary variant, designated CBR2, contains the substitutions R334S and G351R, which collectively reconfigure the hydrogen-bonding network in the active site to better accommodate the bulkier naphthothiazole moiety of the novel substrates.

This CBR2 variant demonstrates enhanced luminescence when assayed with OH-NpLH₂ and exhibits a dramatic red-shift in emission maximum—up to 730 nm—with NH₂-NpLH₂, representing one of the most red-shifted bioluminescent systems reported to date without reliance on energy transfer mechanisms. Furthermore, the CBR2 polypeptide retains activity with native D-luciferin, enabling dual-substrate utility. The invention also includes a codon-optimized nucleic acid sequence (CBR2opt) encoding the CBR2 polypeptide, designed for high-level expression in mammalian cells, which significantly improves protein accumulation and thermal stability (t₁/₂ > 10 hours at 37°C) compared to Luc2 (t₁/₂ < 30 minutes).

The CBR2 variant exhibits a Km of approximately 0.5 µM for PBI-4813 (a designation for NH₂-NpLH₂) and 22 µM for PBI-4739 (a designation for D-LH₂), reflecting a 44-fold higher affinity for the NIR substrate relative to D-LH₂. Relative Vmax values indicate that while absolute turnover with NH₂-NpLH₂ is lower than with D-LH₂, the combination of improved cellular stability, membrane permeability of the substrate, and reduced tissue absorption of NIR photons results in superior in vivo performance. The invention further encompasses polynucleotides encoding the CBR variant, expression vectors comprising such polynucleotides under control of suitable promoters, and host cells—prokaryotic or eukaryotic, including mammalian cells—transformed or transfected with these vectors.

Also provided are non-human transgenic animals harboring the CBR variant polynucleotide or expressing the CBR variant polypeptide in specific tissues, useful for disease modeling and therapeutic evaluation. The CBR variant may be engineered as a circularly permuted luciferase or fused to heterologous polypeptides (e.g., HALOTAG, epitope tags, signal sequences, or proteins of interest) to create biosensors, reporters, or tools for protein proximity assays. The invention includes a near-infrared bioluminescence system comprising the CBR variant polypeptide and a luciferin derivative such as PBI-4813 or PBI-4739, enabling deep-tissue imaging with high sensitivity and resolution.

Methods for producing the CBR variant polypeptide via recombinant expression in host cells are disclosed, as well as kits containing the polynucleotide, vector, or purified polypeptide for research or diagnostic use. The system is compatible with bioluminescence resonance energy transfer (BRET), where the CBR variant serves as an energy donor to fluorescent acceptors, facilitating real-time monitoring of molecular interactions. Methods for measuring bioluminescence intensity, enzymatic activity, and substrate kinetics are also encompassed, providing standardized protocols for characterizing and applying the invention in diverse experimental contexts.
