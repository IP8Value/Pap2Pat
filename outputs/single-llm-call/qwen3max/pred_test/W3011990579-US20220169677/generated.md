# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present disclosure relates to novel compounds that function as inhibitors of the interaction between vascular endothelial growth factor A165 (VEGF-A165) and neuropilin-1 (NRP-1). More specifically, the disclosure pertains to peptidomimetic compounds comprising a C-terminal arginine residue in accordance with the C-end rule (CendR) and further incorporating a cysteine residue at or near the N-terminus, which together confer enhanced binding affinity for NRP-1. These compounds are useful in the prevention and treatment of diseases mediated by VEGF-A165/NRP-1 signaling, including pathological angiogenesis and cancer. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing such compounds, methods for their preparation, and their therapeutic uses.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) is a transmembrane glycoprotein that functions as a co-receptor for multiple ligands, including class 3 semaphorins and various growth factors such as vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), epidermal growth factor (EGF), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), placental growth factor (PlGF), hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), and transforming growth factor β (TGF-β). Although NRP-1 lacks intrinsic enzymatic activity, it enhances signaling through receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) by forming complexes with them, thereby modulating cellular processes such as axon guidance, vascular development, and tumor angiogenesis. Among the VEGF isoforms, VEGF-A165 is the predominant and most biologically active form involved in pathological angiogenesis, particularly in cancer. VEGF-A165 binds to both VEGFR-2 and NRP-1, with NRP-1 acting to stabilize and potentiate VEGFR-2 signaling. Elevated expression of NRP-1 on tumor cells and tumor-associated endothelial cells is frequently associated with poor prognosis, underscoring its role as a therapeutic target.

The interaction between VEGF-A165 and NRP-1 is primarily mediated by the C-terminal hexapeptide sequence encoded by exons 7 and 8 of VEGF-A165, specifically the motif CDKPRR. Structural studies have revealed that the b1 domain of NRP-1 contains a shallow binding pocket that accommodates the C-terminal arginine residue of the ligand. This requirement has been formalized as the C-end rule (CendR), which stipulates that high-affinity NRP-1 ligands must terminate in an R/KXXR/K motif, with a free carboxyl group on the C-terminal basic residue. Peptides conforming to this rule—such as A7R (ATWLPPR), tuftsin (TKPR), and the iRGD peptide—have demonstrated anti-angiogenic activity by competitively inhibiting VEGF-A165 binding to NRP-1.

Despite these advances, existing anti-angiogenic strategies based on VEGF pathway inhibition face limitations, including modest potency, poor pharmacokinetic profiles, and the development of resistance. Small-molecule VEGF inhibitors and monoclonal antibodies like bevacizumab have shown clinical benefit but are often associated with systemic toxicity and incomplete efficacy. Peptidic inhibitors, while more specific, typically suffer from rapid enzymatic degradation and suboptimal binding affinity. Prior art peptides, including linear and branched analogues of the CendR motif, have achieved IC50 values in the micromolar range, which may be insufficient for robust therapeutic effect. Moreover, the structural features beyond the CendR motif that contribute to high-affinity binding remain incompletely understood, limiting rational design efforts. There is therefore a compelling need for novel compounds that exhibit significantly enhanced affinity for NRP-1, improved metabolic stability, and the potential for clinical translation in oncology and other angiogenesis-dependent disorders.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a compound of formula (I):

H-R1-A-R2-OH

wherein R1 is selected from the group consisting of H-Cys-Lys(hArg)- and H-Lys(Cys-hArg)-; R2 is Arg; and A is a tripeptide linker selected from -Dab-ΔPro- and -Dap-Dhp-, wherein Dab is 2,4-diaminobutyric acid, Dap is 2,3-diaminopropionic acid, ΔPro is dehydroproline, and Dhp is 3,4-dehydroproline. In certain embodiments, R5 is H, where R5 corresponds to the hydrogen atom of the C-terminal carboxylic acid group. The groups R1 and R4 are defined such that R4 represents the side chain of the lysine residue in the branched architecture, which may be substituted with hArg (homoarginine) or Cys-hArg, thereby defining the branching point. The groups R2 and R5 are defined as the C-terminal arginine and the terminal hydrogen, respectively, ensuring compliance with the CendR motif. The group A serves as a conformationally constrained spacer that optimizes the spatial orientation of the N-terminal cysteine-containing moiety relative to the C-terminal arginine, thereby enhancing receptor engagement.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSURE

### Compounds of the Disclosure

The present disclosure introduces novel VEGF-A165/NRP-1 inhibitors of formula (I): H-R1-A-R2-OH. The compound of formula (I) is characterized by a C-terminal arginine residue essential for NRP-1 binding per the CendR rule, and an N-terminal modification incorporating a cysteine residue either in the main chain or on a lysine side chain. Groups R1 and R4 are defined such that R1 includes a lysine residue whose α-amino group or ε-amino group is functionalized with a cysteine-homoarginine (Cys-hArg) dipeptide or a homoarginine (hArg) residue, respectively. Specifically, R1 may be H-Cys-Lys(hArg)-, where the cysteine is attached to the α-amino terminus of lysine bearing hArg on its side chain, or H-Lys(Cys-hArg)-, where the cysteine-hArg dipeptide is attached to the ε-amino group of lysine. Groups R2 and R5 are defined as R2 = Arg and R5 = H, ensuring a free C-terminal carboxylate. Group A is a tripeptide linker comprising non-natural amino acids that confer conformational rigidity; A may be -Dab-ΔPro- (A1) or -Dap-Dhp- (A2), where Dab is 2,4-diaminobutyric acid, Dap is 2,3-diaminopropionic acid, ΔPro is (Z)-dehydroproline, and Dhp is 3,4-dehydroproline. The disclosure explicitly excludes the peptide CKPPR. All chemical groups are non-substituted unless otherwise specified. The residue for R1 and R4 includes the lysine backbone and its substituents, while the residue for R2 and R5 encompasses the arginine side chain and terminal hydrogen. Subfamilies include compounds of formula (I-A1) with A = A1 and (I-A2) with A = A2. Abbreviations follow standard IUPAC and biochemical conventions. The compounds may exist as isomeric forms, including the isomeric form of compound (I′), which may differ in stereochemistry at non-chiral centers introduced by dehydroamino acids. Protecting groups such as Fmoc, Boc, Pbf, and Alloc are used during synthesis and subsequently removed. Examples of protecting groups include tert-butyloxycarbonyl (Boc) for amines and trityl for thiols. The compounds bind NRP-1 with high affinity, as demonstrated by IC50 values in the nanomolar range. VEGF-A165 is the 165-amino acid splice variant of VEGF-A, and NRP-1 is the human neuropilin-1 receptor. The sulfhydryl group of cysteine enables additional interactions with NRP-1, such as hydrogen bonding or S-π interactions. The cysteine residue, in conjunction with the C-terminal arginine, is critical for enhanced binding. Proline analogues like ΔPro and Dhp introduce conformational constraints that optimize the peptide backbone. Embodiments with A as A1 or A2, and with R2/R5 or R1/R4 as defined, exhibit superior inhibitory activity. Additional compounds include formulas (I-A) through (I-D), representing specific structural variants. The compounds may be formulated as acid-addition salts using pharmaceutically acceptable acids such as hydrochloric, sulfuric, or trifluoroacetic acid; the trifluoroacetate salt is particularly suitable for purification. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts, hydrates, solvates, and mixtures thereof are included. The compounds may exist as racemic, enantiomeric, diastereoisomeric, or tautomeric forms, all of which are encompassed.

### Methods for Preparing Compounds of Formula (I)

The compounds of formula (I) are prepared via solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS). A first preparation method is used for compounds with A = A1 (-Dab-ΔPro-). The synthesis begins with Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin. Step a) involves Fmoc deprotection with 20% piperidine/DMF. Step b) couples Fmoc-ΔPro-OH using HATU/DIPEA. Step c) deprotects Fmoc and couples Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH. Step d) deprotects Fmoc and couples Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-OH. Step e) removes the Alloc group with Pd(0)/PhSiH3 and couples Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH. Step f) cleaves the peptide from the resin with TFA:DTT:H2O:TIS (88:5:5:2) and purifies by HPLC. A second method is used for A = A2, following analogous steps with Fmoc-Dhp-OH and Fmoc-Dap(Boc)-OH, and coupling Boc-Lys(Fmoc)-OH before Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH.

## EXAMPLES

### Example

The synthesis of VEGF-A165/NRP-1 inhibitors was performed via solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) on Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin. Functional groups were protected with Fmoc (α-amine), Boc (side-chain amines of Dab/Dap/Lys), Pbf (guanidine of Arg/hArg), and Alloc (ε-amine of Lys for branching). Wang resin was used as the solid support. Peptide bonds were formed using HATU/DIPEA in DMF. Cleavage from the resin employed TFA:DTT:H2O:TIS (88:5:5:2) to reduce disulfide formation. Purification was achieved by preparative HPLC, followed by conversion to the trifluoroacetate salt. For compound H-Cys-Lys(hArg)-Dab-ΔPro-Arg-OH, synthesis started with Fmoc-Arg(Pbf)-Wang resin. Fmoc deprotection was monitored by Kaiser test. Fmoc-ΔPro-OH was coupled, with reaction progress tracked by acetaldehyde/chloranil test. Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH was then coupled, followed by Fmoc deprotection and washing. Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-OH was coupled, and Alloc was removed with Pd(0)/PhSiH3. Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH was coupled, and final Fmoc deprotection completed the chain. For H-Lys(Cys-hArg)-Dab-ΔPro-Arg-OH, synthesis began similarly. After coupling Fmoc-ΔPro-OH and Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH, Boc-Lys(Fmoc)-OH was coupled to introduce the branching lysine. Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH was then coupled to the ε-amine after Fmoc removal from lysine, followed by Cys coupling to the α-amine. Final cleavage, purification by HPLC, and analysis by HRMS confirmed identity and purity.
