## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of plant biotechnology, specifically in the genetic modification of potato plants to restore or enhance reproductive capabilities, particularly female fertility, through the expression of a cellulose binding domain (CBD) protein derived from the oomycete pathogen *Phytophthora infestans*. The invention further relates to methods for utilizing such genetically modified plants in breeding programs to introduce desirable agronomic traits—such as disease resistance and enhanced nutritional characteristics—into elite cultivars that are otherwise reproductively limited.

Cellulose binding domains (CBDs) are modular components of carbohydrate-active enzymes, commonly found in saprophytic microorganisms that degrade plant cell walls. These domains facilitate the binding of enzymes to cellulose, thereby enhancing enzymatic efficiency in breaking down complex polysaccharides. CBDs are classified into families based on structural and sequence homology, with Family 1 CBDs being among the most well-characterized. Notably, while CBDs are prevalent in microbial cellulases, they are generally absent from both plant-encoded and plant-pathogen-encoded cellulolytic systems. An exception is the *P. infestans* CBD1 protein, which exists as an independent, non-enzymatic protein tightly associated with the hyphal cell wall of this devastating potato pathogen. This protein has been hypothesized to contribute to cell wall integrity or host interaction during infection.

The global potato industry faces significant challenges due to the narrow genetic base of many commercially important cultivars. Bintje, a century-old European cultivar first released in 1904, exemplifies this limitation. Despite its excellent agronomic performance, tuber quality, and culinary versatility, Bintje is functionally female-sterile—it flowers normally but fails to develop ovaries or seed berries after pollination, rendering it incapable of serving as a maternal parent in conventional breeding. This sterility severely restricts efforts to introgress valuable traits such as late blight resistance, drought tolerance, or enhanced phytonutrient content into the Bintje genetic background. Current breeding strategies often rely on lengthy backcrossing or somatic hybridization, which are inefficient and may introduce undesirable linkage drag.

In response to these limitations, there is a pressing need for innovative approaches to overcome reproductive barriers in elite potato germplasm. The present invention addresses this need by introducing a novel strategy: the expression of the *P. infestans* CBD1 protein in Bintje potato plants. Contrary to initial expectations that this transgene might alter host-pathogen interactions, its expression unexpectedly restored female fertility, enabling the formation of seed berries and viable seeds upon cross-pollination. This breakthrough provides a powerful tool for expanding the genetic diversity of Bintje and other sterile cultivars without permanent transgene integration in the final progeny.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for restoring female fertility in otherwise sterile potato cultivars through the stable expression of a nucleic acid molecule encoding the *Phytophthora infestans* CBD1 protein. In a preferred embodiment, the CBD1 cDNA is operably linked to a constitutive promoter and introduced into *Solanum tuberosum* cv. Bintje via *Agrobacterium*-mediated transformation. The primary object of the invention is to provide a reproducible and effective strategy for expressing the CBD1 gene in potato plants to overcome inherent reproductive limitations.

Transformed Bintje plants exhibit marked changes in morphology, most notably the development of ovaries and seed berries—structures that are absent in non-transformed Bintje. Remarkably, these transgenic plants produce seed berries even in the absence of fertilization, indicative of parthenocarpic development, and yield viable seeds when pollinated with compatible donor pollen. Additionally, the transgenic plants display altered ovule development, enabling successful embryo formation post-fertilization.

The invention further outlines a method for obtaining new, improved potato plants by crossing CBD1-expressing Bintje as the female parent with donor lines carrying desirable traits such as late blight resistance (e.g., Sarpo Mira) or enhanced tuber color and nutritional value (e.g., Peter Wilcox). Progeny from these crosses inherit the desired traits, and through segregation, individuals lacking the CBD1 transgene can be selected, resulting in non-transgenic, improved Bintje derivatives.

In summary, the objects of the invention include: (1) providing a means to restore female fertility in sterile potato cultivars; (2) enabling the introgression of multigenic traits into elite germplasm; (3) facilitating the development of new potato varieties with enhanced agronomic and nutritional profiles; and (4) offering a transient genetic tool that need not be retained in the final commercial product.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention begins with the transformation of potato plants, specifically the cultivar Bintje, with a recombinant DNA construct comprising the *P. infestans* CBD1 gene. Potato production worldwide is constrained by the genetic uniformity of leading cultivars, which limits adaptive potential to emerging pests, diseases, and climate stresses. Bintje, despite its widespread cultivation, suffers from complete female sterility, preventing its use in sexual hybridization. This limitation motivated the exploration of CBD1 expression as a means to modulate plant cell wall dynamics and potentially influence reproductive development.

The choice of CBD1 was informed by its unique origin and structure. Unlike typical microbial CBDs fused to catalytic domains, CBD1 is a standalone protein associated with the *Phytophthora* hyphal wall. Initial hypotheses suggested it might interfere with host-pathogen enzymatic interactions, but empirical testing revealed an unanticipated effect on plant reproduction. In transgenic Bintje, CBD1 expression led to the formation of seed balls—berry-like fruits containing seeds—following cross-pollination. Even without pollination, ovary-like structures developed, suggesting a role in parthenocarpic fruit initiation.

Ovum development in wild-type Bintje is arrested post-anthesis, with floral abscission occurring before ovary enlargement. In contrast, CBD1-expressing lines such as B-7 and B-48 exhibited persistent ovaries that matured into seed berries. When pollinated with viable pollen from fertile cultivars, these berries produced numerous viable seeds, confirming functional female fertility. Pollen from other potato varieties, including late blight-resistant or pigmented types, successfully fertilized the transgenic Bintje, yielding hybrid progeny.

The transgene also influenced seed ball structure. Immunolocalization confirmed CBD1 protein accumulation throughout the ovary wall and locule tissues, suggesting direct interaction with cell wall components. This localization correlates with the observed morphological changes, implying that CBD1 may modulate cellulose organization or signaling during ovary development.

The discovery of CBD1’s role stems from investigations into *Phytophthora* virulence mechanisms. The CBD1 protein, encoded by gene EU179903, functions in hyphal integrity but, when expressed in planta, exerts a profound developmental effect. Its ability to bind cellulose may alter cell wall mechanics, mimicking hormonal signals such as auxin, which is known to promote cell wall loosening and fruit set. This mechanistic insight motivated testing CBD1’s influence on plant growth, revealing stage-specific effects—enhanced height in later development and, crucially, restored reproductive capacity.

CBDs exhibit variable effects in transgenic plants. For instance, a bacterial Family 3 CBD promoted early growth in Desiree potatoes, while tandem CBMs stunted tobacco. The unusual feature of *Phytophthora* CBD1 in Bintje is its specific induction of ovary development—a phenotype not observed with other CBDs, including the related CBD4.

This parthenocarpic development resembles that achieved in tomatoes via auxin manipulation. Auxin induces cell wall-modifying enzymes that facilitate expansion; CBD1 may act analogously by perturbing cellulose architecture, triggering developmental pathways. This opens avenues for engineering fruit set in other Solanaceous crops.

The invention enables strategic expansion of the Bintje gene pool. Current Bintje lacks resistance to late blight and other pathogens. By using CBD1-transformed Bintje as a female parent, breeders can introgress resistance genes (e.g., the RB gene from *S. bulbocastanum* via Sarpo Mira) and anthocyanin biosynthesis pathways for colored tubers. Critically, progeny can be selected that lack the CBD1 transgene, satisfying regulatory and market preferences for non-GMO products.

Transformation is defined as the stable integration of exogenous DNA into a plant genome. *Agrobacterium*-mediated transformation is the preferred method, using disarmed strains like LBA4404 harboring binary vectors such as pBI121. Alternative methods include particle bombardment (“gene gun”), protoplast transfection, or viral vectors. The isolated polynucleotide encoding CBD1 is incorporated into recombinant constructs comprising plant expression vectors, which contain regulatory sequences for transcription and translation.

A plant expression vector includes a promoter (e.g., CaMV 35S), a multiple cloning site, and a terminator. The nucleic acid molecule—defined as any DNA or RNA sequence, including fragments—is operably linked to regulatory sequences, meaning positioned to allow expression. “Isolated polynucleotide” refers to DNA separated from its natural context. “Recombinant” denotes molecules formed by laboratory manipulation. A “construct” or “chimeric gene” combines heterologous elements. “Expression” means transcription and, optionally, translation. “Altered levels” indicate increased or decreased expression relative to wild-type. “Encoding” signifies the nucleotide sequence specifies the amino acid sequence. “Operably linked” implies functional association between regulatory and coding regions. “Regulatory sequences” include promoters, enhancers, and terminators. A “promoter” initiates transcription. An “RNA transcript” is the RNA product. A “protein” or “polypeptide” is a chain of amino acids.

## EXAMPLES

### Plant Transformation and Selection

Four-week-old Bintje tissue culture plantlets were used for transformation. Leaf explants were co-cultivated with *Agrobacterium tumefaciens* LBA4404 carrying pBI121-CBD1. After selection on kanamycin-containing media, regenerated plantlets were screened by PCR using 35S promoter primers. Positive transformants were transferred to soil and grown in a greenhouse. Height measurements showed increased growth in select lines. Genome walking confirmed single or low-copy T-DNA integration, with B-48 integrating into a non-coding intronic region of an odorant-related gene, minimizing disruption.

### Example 2

Breeding studies crossed CBD1-Bintje (B-7 and B-48) as females with Sarpo Mira (male). Control Bintje flowers abscised; transgenics formed seed berries. Harvested seeds were germinated, and progeny were tested for late blight resistance. Detached leaf assays with *P. infestans* US-11 showed 12 of 16 progeny were fully resistant, demonstrating successful inheritance of the RB-mediated resistance. Segregation patterns indicated dominant resistance gene transfer.

### Example 3

Crosses between CBD1-Bintje and Peter Wilcox (purple skin, yellow flesh) yielded progeny with diverse tuber phenotypes—red, purple, and yellow skins, varying shapes, and flesh colors. This demonstrated transmission of multigenic traits controlling anthocyanin and carotenoid pathways. The ability to combine Bintje’s culinary qualities with enhanced nutrition underscores the breeding utility of the CBD1 fertility restoration system.
