# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of electrochemical energy conversion and storage systems, and more particularly to reversible protonic ceramic electrochemical cells (PCECs) capable of operating in both electrolysis and fuel cell modes. The invention specifically concerns novel triple-conducting oxide electrode materials, electrochemical cell architectures incorporating such materials, and integrated systems for the production of hydrogen gas and generation of electricity using renewable energy sources. The disclosed technology enables efficient, durable, and self-sustainable operation at intermediate temperatures ranging from approximately 400°C to 600°C, thereby addressing critical limitations associated with conventional solid oxide electrolysis cells and proton-conducting fuel cells.

## BACKGROUND

The increasing global demand for clean and sustainable energy has driven significant interest in hydrogen as a versatile energy carrier that can be produced from water using renewable electricity and subsequently converted back into electrical power with only water as a byproduct. Hydrogen gas production via high-temperature electrolysis offers substantial thermodynamic and kinetic advantages over low-temperature alkaline or polymer electrolyte membrane electrolysis, including higher efficiency, faster reaction kinetics, and reduced reliance on precious metal catalysts. Among high-temperature electrolysis technologies, solid-oxide electrolysis cells (SOECs) have emerged as promising candidates due to their ability to operate at elevated temperatures (typically 700–1000°C), which enhances ionic conductivity and reduces electrical energy requirements. However, conventional oxygen-ion conducting SOECs suffer from material degradation, thermal stress, and high operational costs associated with prolonged exposure to extreme temperatures.

Protonic ceramic electrolysis cells (PCECs) represent an advanced subclass of SOECs that utilize proton-conducting electrolytes instead of oxygen-ion conductors. In PCECs, water is fed to the oxygen electrode where it is oxidized to produce oxygen gas and protons; the protons then migrate through the electrolyte to the hydrogen electrode, where they combine to form hydrogen gas. This configuration avoids the need to transport gaseous hydrogen through porous electrodes and enables operation at lower temperatures (500–700°C) compared to traditional SOECs. Despite these advantages, the widespread deployment of PCECs has been hindered by the lack of highly active, stable, and reversible oxygen electrodes capable of efficiently catalyzing both the water oxidation reaction (WOR) during electrolysis and the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) during fuel cell operation. Conventional mixed ionic-electronic conducting (MIEC) electrodes confine electrochemical reactions to the triple-phase boundaries (TPBs)—regions where the electrode, electrolyte, and gas phase intersect—limiting active surface area and performance, especially at reduced temperatures.

Recent efforts have focused on developing triple-conducting oxides (TCOs) that simultaneously conduct electrons, oxygen ions, and protons, thereby extending the electrochemically active zone from the TPB into the entire electrode bulk. However, the design of such materials is challenging due to the conflicting requirements for high electronic conductivity, sufficient oxygen vacancy concentration for hydration, and low proton migration barriers. Existing TCO candidates, such as doped cerates and zirconates or layered perovskites like PrBa₀.₅Sr₀.₅Co₂O₅₊δ, exhibit limited proton conductivity or insufficient stability under high-steam conditions. Moreover, most reported materials lack comprehensive experimental validation of proton conduction mechanisms. Thus, there remains a critical need for novel electrode compositions that enable high-performance, durable, and reversible PCEC operation at intermediate temperatures without external hydrogen supply—a capability essential for grid-scale energy storage and distributed power generation.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention provides an electrochemical cell comprising a first electrode, a second electrode, and a proton-conducting electrolyte disposed between the first and second electrodes. The first electrode comprises a triple-conducting layered perovskite material having the formula PrNi₀.₅Co₀.₅O₃₋δ (PNC), which exhibits simultaneous conduction of protons (H⁺), oxygen ions (O²⁻), and electrons (e⁻). The second electrode comprises a cermet material, such as Ni-BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃ (Ni-BCZYYb4411), configured to facilitate hydrogen evolution and oxidation reactions. The invention further encompasses a system for producing hydrogen gas and generating electricity, which includes a steam source, an electrochemical apparatus containing one or more of the disclosed electrochemical cells, and a controllable power source capable of applying a potential difference across the cell to operate in either electrolysis mode or fuel cell mode.

In electrolysis mode, the system splits steam into hydrogen and oxygen gases by applying an external voltage, with hydrogen produced at the second electrode and oxygen evolved at the first electrode. In fuel cell mode, the system generates electricity by oxidizing hydrogen at the second electrode and reducing oxygen at the first electrode, producing water as a byproduct. The system may further include heating apparatuses, gas storage units, and thermal energy recovery mechanisms to enhance overall efficiency. A key feature of the invention is its ability to switch rapidly between electrolysis and fuel cell modes, enabling self-sustainable operation wherein hydrogen generated during electrolysis is immediately consumed to produce electricity without requiring external hydrogen feed. The disclosed electrochemical cell structure, material compositions, and operational methods collectively enable high Faradaic efficiencies, exceptional thermal cycling durability, and stable performance under high-steam concentrations at temperatures as low as 400°C.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

In specific embodiments of the invention, the electrochemical cell is designed for reversible operation between electrolysis and fuel cell modes. The negative electrode, also referred to as the hydrogen electrode, is composed of a cermet material that combines a proton-conducting ceramic phase with a metallic phase to provide electronic conductivity and catalytic activity for hydrogen reactions. The positive electrode, or oxygen electrode, is formed from the triple-conducting oxide PrNi₀.₅Co₀.₅O₃₋δ (PNC), which enables extended reaction zones beyond traditional triple-phase boundaries. The electrolyte is a dense proton-conducting membrane, typically composed of doped barium cerate-zirconate compounds such as BaCe₀.₄Zr₀.₄Y₀.₁Yb₀.₁O₃ (BCZYYb4411), which selectively transports protons while blocking electron and gas permeation.

Materials used in the cell must be chemically and thermally compatible to prevent interfacial reactions during sintering and long-term operation. Spatially relative terms such as “between,” “adjacent to,” and “opposing” are used to describe physical relationships within the cell architecture and should be interpreted in the context of the assembled device. The terms “a” and “an” are intended to mean one or more unless otherwise indicated, and “and/or” denotes inclusive disjunction. The term “configured” implies structural or functional adaptation for a specified purpose, while “substantially” indicates a condition that is largely but not necessarily completely achieved. “About” or “approximately” refers to values within ±10% of the stated number.

Electrochemical cell 100 comprises first electrode 102, proton-conducting membrane 104, and second electrode 106. First electrode 102 is a triple-conducting layered perovskite with the general formula PrNiₓCo₁₋ₓO₃₋δ, where x is approximately 0.5. This material adopts an orthorhombic perovskite structure (space group Pbnm) with lattice parameters a = 5.405 Å, b = 5.380 Å, and c = 7.617 Å. The crystal lattice features corner-sharing BO₆ octahedra (B = Ni, Co) with Pr³⁺ ions occupying the A-sites. The presence of Ni²⁺ dopants induces oxygen vacancies, which serve as sites for water dissociation and proton incorporation upon hydration.

Proton-conducting membrane 104 is fabricated from proton-conducting ceramics such as BCZYYb4411, BaZr₀.₈Y₀.₂O₃₋δ, or SrCe₀.₉Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ. These materials exhibit high proton conductivity in humid atmospheres due to the incorporation of water molecules into oxygen vacancies, forming mobile hydroxide defects. Second electrode 106 is a cermet composed of nickel metal dispersed in a proton-conducting ceramic matrix, providing porosity for gas diffusion, electronic pathways, and catalytic surfaces for hydrogen reactions. Examples of suitable cermet materials include Ni-BCZYYb4411, Ni-BaZr₀.₈Y₀.₂O₃₋δ, and Ni-SrCe₀.₉Yb₀.₁O₃₋δ.

The electrodes and membrane may be fabricated as thin films or dense layers with thicknesses ranging from 10 to 500 micrometers, and may adopt planar, tubular, or monolithic geometries. Cell 100 is formed by co-sintering or sequential firing of component layers to ensure strong interfacial adhesion. In system 300 for H₂ production and electricity generation, steam source 302 supplies humidified gas to the oxygen electrode side of electrochemical apparatus 304, which houses one or more electrochemical cells. Power source 308 applies a controllable potential difference to drive electrolysis or harvest electricity. Optional components include heat exchangers, gas separators, and control units.

The steam source provides water vapor at concentrations from 3% to 50% by volume, delivered as a continuous steam stream to the internal chamber of the housing structure. During electrolysis mode, H₂O is oxidized at the first region (oxygen electrode) to produce O₂ gas and H⁺ ions, which permeate through the electrolyte to the second region (hydrogen electrode), where they combine to form H₂ gas. Electrons flow externally from the oxygen to the hydrogen electrode. In fuel cell mode, H₂ is oxidized at the second electrode, releasing H⁺ and e⁻; H⁺ migrates through the electrolyte while e⁻ flows through an external circuit, and O₂ is reduced at the first electrode to form H₂O. Multiple cells may be stacked in series or parallel to scale output.

Switching between modes can occur rapidly (<1 minute) or with controlled delays to manage thermal and chemical transients. Produced H₂ and O₂ gases may be stored, utilized for heating, or vented. Thermal energy from exothermic fuel cell operation or exhaust streams can be recovered to preheat incoming steam, improving system efficiency. The disclosed PNC electrode demonstrates superior hydration capacity, low proton migration barriers (as confirmed by DFT calculations), high hydrogen permeation fluxes, and excellent stability under thermal cycling and high-steam conditions. These attributes enable record-breaking current densities (e.g., 1.72 A/cm² at 1.4 V and 600°C) and self-sustainable reversible operation without external hydrogen, representing a significant advancement in intermediate-temperature electrochemical energy systems.

### EXAMPLES

Example 1 demonstrates electrolysis mode performance of a PCEC with a standard PNC oxygen electrode and Ni-BCZYYb4411 hydrogen electrode. At 600°C and 1.4 V, the cell achieved a current density of 1.31 A/cm² with humidified air (~10% H₂O) at the oxygen electrode and 10% H₂/90% Ar at the hydrogen electrode. Performance remained stable over 120 hours at both 1.4 V and 1.6 V, with a slight increase in current density attributed to improved interfacial contact. Faradaic efficiency was measured at 79.3% under these conditions.

Example 2 compares the electrolysis performance of the PNC-based cell with state-of-the-art SOECs. The PNC cell exhibited current densities of 1.72 A/cm² at 600°C, 1.44 A/cm² at 550°C, and 0.84 A/cm² at 500°C (all at 1.4 V), surpassing all reported proton-conducting and oxygen-ion conducting cells in the same temperature range. When operated with a nanostructured mesh-like PNC electrode, performance increased further to 1.72 A/cm² at 1.4 V and 600°C, with peak power density in fuel cell mode reaching 611 mW/cm².

In summary, the disclosed invention provides a robust, high-performance, and reversible electrochemical system for hydrogen production and electricity generation. The scope of the disclosure encompasses the specific PNC composition, its use as a triple-conducting electrode, the integrated cell and system architectures, and the methods of operation enabling self-sustained energy conversion at intermediate temperatures.
