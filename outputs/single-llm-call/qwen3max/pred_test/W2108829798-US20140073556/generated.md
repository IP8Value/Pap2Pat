# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and neuropharmacology, specifically to novel peptide-based antagonists of the interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI). More particularly, the invention concerns truncated polypeptide fragments derived from the human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein (IL1RA), which retain the ability to bind IL-1RI with high affinity and inhibit downstream inflammatory signaling pathways. These peptides, exemplified by Ilantide (SEQ ID NO:1), exhibit improved pharmacokinetic properties over full-length recombinant IL1RA proteins such as anakinra, including enhanced solubility, stability, synthetic accessibility, and—critically—the capacity to cross the blood–brain barrier. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these peptides, methods for their use in treating inflammatory and neurodegenerative disorders, and kits for their administration.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Interleukin-1 (IL-1) is a pivotal proinflammatory cytokine that orchestrates innate immune responses and contributes to the pathogenesis of numerous acute and chronic inflammatory diseases. IL-1 exists in two primary bioactive isoforms, IL-1α and IL-1β, both of which signal through the interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1RI). Upon ligand binding, IL-1RI recruits the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein (IL-1RAcP), forming a ternary complex that initiates intracellular signaling cascades, predominantly via nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways. This activation leads to the transcription of genes encoding additional cytokines, chemokines, adhesion molecules, and enzymes involved in tissue destruction and inflammation amplification.

To counterbalance this potent inflammatory stimulus, the body produces the interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein (IL1RA), a naturally occurring competitive inhibitor that binds IL-1RI with high affinity but fails to recruit IL-1RAcP, thereby preventing signal transduction. Recombinant forms of IL1RA, such as anakinra (marketed as Kineret), have been developed as therapeutics for conditions like rheumatoid arthritis, autoinflammatory syndromes, and type 2 diabetes. Despite clinical utility, anakinra suffers from significant limitations: it is a large (~17 kDa), non-glycosylated protein requiring daily subcutaneous injections; it exhibits poor penetration across the blood–brain barrier (BBB), rendering it ineffective against central nervous system (CNS) inflammation; and it can induce local injection site reactions and systemic side effects.

In response to these drawbacks, research has focused on identifying smaller, more druggable mimetics of IL1RA. Peptides with IL-1R antagonist activity offer several theoretical advantages: lower molecular weight, enhanced BBB permeability, reduced immunogenicity, simplified manufacturing, and greater chemical stability. The international patent application WO05086695A2 disclosed certain peptide fragments derived from IL1RA, suggesting their potential as anti-inflammatory agents. However, these fragments lacked comprehensive characterization of binding kinetics, functional efficacy in neuronal models, or demonstration of CNS penetration. The present invention addresses these gaps by identifying and validating specific short peptide sequences—most notably SGRKSSKMQA (SEQ ID NO:1)—that not only effectively antagonize IL-1RI but also promote neurite outgrowth and neuronal survival, properties absent in prior art peptides. The advantages of these short peptides include increased solubility in aqueous formulations, resistance to proteolytic degradation relative to larger proteins, significantly lower cost of chemical synthesis compared to recombinant biologics, and the critical ability to access brain parenchyma following systemic administration.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The invention provides truncated forms of the human interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein (IL1RA) that function as potent and selective antagonists of IL-1RI. These truncated polypeptides, typically ranging from 5 to 20 amino acid residues in length, are derived from key receptor-binding regions of native IL1RA and retain the essential structural motifs required for high-affinity interaction with IL-1RI. Upon binding, they competitively inhibit the association of IL-1β (and IL-1α) with IL-1RI, thereby preventing the recruitment of IL-1RAcP and subsequent activation of downstream inflammatory signaling pathways, including NF-κB and MAPK. This inhibition results in the suppression of proinflammatory mediator production, such as tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α), interleukin-6 (IL-6), and other cytokines.

Remarkably, the peptides of the present invention exhibit beneficial effects beyond mere anti-inflammatory action. They have been demonstrated to stimulate neurite outgrowth and enhance the survival of primary neurons in culture, indicating a direct neurotrophic or neuroprotective role. This dual functionality—anti-inflammatory and neuroregenerative—is particularly advantageous for the treatment of disorders where neuroinflammation and neuronal dysfunction coexist, such as Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, multiple sclerosis, and other neurodegenerative conditions. Consequently, the invention provides methods for treating a broad spectrum of inflammatory disorders, including but not limited to rheumatoid arthritis, Behçet’s disease, type 2 diabetes mellitus, and systemic juvenile idiopathic arthritis, as well as neurodegenerative diseases characterized by IL-1-mediated pathology. The peptides may be administered alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents to achieve synergistic or complementary effects.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Inflammation

Inflammation is a complex biological response of vascularized tissues to harmful stimuli, such as pathogens, damaged cells, or irritants. It is a protective mechanism aimed at eliminating the injurious agent and initiating tissue repair. Inflammation is broadly classified into acute and chronic types. Acute inflammation is the immediate, short-term response characterized by vasodilation, increased vascular permeability, and leukocyte infiltration. Chronic inflammation is a prolonged response involving lymphocytes and macrophages, often leading to tissue destruction, fibrosis, and angiogenesis, and is implicated in numerous diseases including rheumatoid arthritis, atherosclerosis, and neurodegenerative disorders.

### Interleukins

Interleukins (ILs) are a class of cytokines primarily produced by leukocytes that mediate communication between white blood cells. They regulate immune responses, hematopoiesis, and inflammation. Among them, IL-1 is a master regulator of the inflammatory cascade. IL-1α is constitutively expressed in epithelial and endothelial cells and acts as an alarmin upon cellular damage. IL-1β, synthesized as an inactive precursor (pro-IL-1β), requires cleavage by caspase-1 within the inflammasome for activation and is predominantly secreted by monocytes, macrophages, and dendritic cells. Both isoforms bind IL-1RI, a transmembrane protein composed of three extracellular immunoglobulin (Ig)-like domains, a single transmembrane helix, and an intracellular Toll/IL-1 receptor (TIR) domain essential for signal transduction. IL-1RI expression is upregulated during inflammation on various cell types, including fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and neurons.

### Peptide Fragments of the Present Invention

The present invention discloses truncated forms of IL1RA, specifically short peptides that mimic the receptor-binding interface of the full-length protein. These peptides exhibit improved properties over both native IL1RA and anakinra, including increased aqueous solubility, enhanced thermal and proteolytic stability, and significantly lower production costs due to straightforward solid-phase peptide synthesis. Crucially, their low molecular weight (typically <2 kDa) facilitates passage across the blood–brain barrier (BBB)—a highly selective semipermeable border of endothelial cells that protects the brain from circulating toxins and pathogens but restricts the entry of most macromolecules. Molecular weight is inversely correlated with BBB penetration; thus, these small peptides achieve therapeutic concentrations in the central nervous system after systemic administration.

The core peptide of the invention is SGRKSSKMQA (SEQ ID NO:1), termed Ilantide. Also encompassed are related peptides such as RIWDVNQKT (SEQ ID NO:29), TAMEADQPVS (SEQ ID NO:35), and GPNA, as well as functional fragments or variants thereof. A “fragment” is a contiguous subsequence of the parent peptide, while a “variant” includes substitutions, insertions, or deletions that preserve biological activity. Amino acid sequences are represented using standard one-letter or three-letter codes, with the N-terminal residue at the left and C-terminal at the right. Peptides may comprise at least five of the twenty proteinogenic amino acids and may incorporate unnatural amino acids to enhance stability or affinity. Preferred embodiments are 8–12 residues long. Sequence identity is calculated by aligning sequences and determining the percentage of identical residues; conservative substitutions (e.g., leucine for isoleucine) are often tolerated, whereas non-conservative changes may abolish activity. Posttranslational modifications (e.g., acetylation, amidation) and isolation from reaction mixtures yield pharmaceutically suitable “isolated peptides.”

### Synthetic Preparation

Peptides of the invention are prepared by conventional solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS) using Fmoc (fluorenylmethyloxycarbonyl) or Boc (tert-butyloxycarbonyl) chemistry on polymeric resins such as TentaGel. After sequential coupling of protected amino acids, the peptide is cleaved from the resin and deprotected under acidic conditions. Purification is achieved via reversed-phase high-performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC) or gel filtration, yielding products of >85% purity. Multimeric constructs, such as dendrimers with four peptide arms linked to a lysine core, are synthesized using branched scaffolds to enhance avidity.

### Compound of the Present Invention

The invention provides compounds comprising or consisting of a peptide as described herein, optionally formulated with excipients, carriers, or stabilizers. These compounds may be salts, esters, or prodrugs designed to improve bioavailability.

### Multimeric Compound

A “multimeric compound” contains two or more copies of the active peptide. Dimers, trimers, and tetramers can be linear or branched. Dendrimeric embodiments, where multiple peptides radiate from a central core (e.g., lysine), exhibit increased functional affinity (avidity) due to multivalent binding to IL-1RI clusters.

### Methods

The invention includes methods for stimulating neurite outgrowth and promoting neuronal survival by administering a peptide of the invention to neuronal cultures or subjects in need thereof. This is particularly relevant for neurodegenerative conditions where synaptic loss and neuronal death are pathological hallmarks.

### Pharmaceutical Formulation

Pharmaceutical formulations of the invention may be solid (tablets, capsules) or liquid (solutions, suspensions). Parenteral administration (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous) is preferred for peptides due to poor oral bioavailability. Topical, transdermal, ocular (eye lotions), nasal, pulmonary, and bronchial routes are also viable. Aerosol formulations—either liquid (nebulized) or dry powder (inhalable)—are suitable for respiratory delivery. Propellants, pH buffers (e.g., phosphate, citrate), and stabilizers (e.g., sugars, surfactants) ensure product integrity. All components must be pharmaceutically acceptable.

### Dosage

Dosage regimens depend on disease severity, patient weight, and route of administration. For parenteral delivery, daily doses of 0.1–100 mg/kg are typical, with 1–20 mg/kg being preferred. Unit dosage forms contain a predetermined effective amount. Inter-individual variability in pharmacokinetics necessitates dose titration. Effective levels are defined by plasma or tissue concentrations that achieve >50% receptor occupancy. Parenteral routes bypass first-pass metabolism, enhancing peptide delivery. Co-administration with anti-inflammatory, anti-rheumatoid, or anti-neurodegenerative drugs (e.g., TNF inhibitors, levodopa, memantine) may optimize outcomes. Kits of parts facilitate such combination therapies.

Inflammatory disorders driven by IL-1 include Behçet’s disease (characterized by oral/genital ulcers, uveitis, and skin lesions) and neurodegenerative diseases like Alzheimer’s (amyloid plaques, neurofibrillary tangles, cognitive decline), Parkinson’s (dopaminergic neuron loss, motor deficits), Huntington’s (CAG repeat expansion, chorea), and multiple sclerosis (demyelination, autoimmune attack). IL-1 exacerbates these conditions by activating microglia, promoting protease release, stimulating acute-phase proteins, and disrupting neuronal function. Blocking IL-1 signaling with the peptides of this invention mitigates these pathological processes.

The human IL1RA protein (UniProt P01584) exists in multiple isoforms, including a secreted form and intracellular variants. The invention’s peptides are derived from its N-terminal domain, specifically the Ilantafin sequences: Ilantafin-1 (SGRKSSKMQA), Ilantafin-2 (RIWDVNQKT), Ilantafin-3 (TAMEADQPVS), Ilantafin-4 (GPNA), and others (Ilantafin-5, -6).

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Structural modeling of the IL1RA–IL-1RI complex (PDB:1IRA) identified a ten-residue motif at the N-terminus of IL1RA (residues 1–10: SGRKSSKMQA) as critical for receptor interaction. This sequence, designated Ilantide (Ilantafin-1), was selected for synthesis and testing.

### Example 2

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis demonstrated that Ilantide-t (dendrimeric form) binds the ectodomain of IL-1RI with a Kd of 2.75×10⁻⁸ M, comparable to full-length IL1RA (Kd=4.8×10⁻⁸ M) and superior to IL-1β (Kd=1.71×10⁻⁷ M). No binding was observed to control proteins (NCAM Ig1-2), confirming specificity.

### Example 3

In HEK-Blue IL-1β reporter cells, Ilantide-m and Ilantide-t dose-dependently inhibited IL-1β-induced NF-κB activation, while scrambled or reversed-sequence controls were inactive. This confirms sequence-specific antagonism of IL-1 signaling.

### Example 4

Ilantide-t suppressed IL-1β-induced TNF-α secretion from AMJ2-C8 macrophages in a dose-dependent manner, with efficacy matching IL1RA and sIL-1RI. IFN-γ-induced TNF-α was unaffected, underscoring pathway selectivity.

### Example 5

In primary cerebellar granule neurons, Ilantide-t stimulated neurite outgrowth comparably to IL1RA and sIL-1RI, but unlike IL-1β. This effect was reversed by co-administration of IL-1β, proving dependence on IL-1RI blockade.

### Example 6

Ilantide-t reduced neuronal cell death induced by potassium withdrawal, achieving survival rates similar to insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), a known neurotrophic factor.

### Example 7

In a rat collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) model, daily subcutaneous Ilantide-t (10 mg/kg) significantly attenuated clinical arthritis scores compared to vehicle, demonstrating in vivo anti-inflammatory efficacy.

### Example 8

Pharmacokinetic studies showed Ilantide-t reached plasma concentrations of ~6 μg/ml within 20 minutes post-subcutaneous injection and penetrated the BBB, achieving CSF levels of 125 ng/ml at 30 minutes (plasma/CSF ratio ~55).

### Example 9

In hippocampal neurons exposed to kainic acid (an excitotoxin), Ilantide-t pretreatment significantly improved cell viability and preserved nuclear morphology versus untreated controls. Its efficacy surpassed full-length IL1RA, highlighting superior neuroprotection.
