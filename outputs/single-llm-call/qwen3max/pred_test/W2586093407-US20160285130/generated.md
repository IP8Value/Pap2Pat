## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a metal hydride battery comprising a novel non-aqueous electrolyte composition that enables enhanced electrochemical performance and expanded operational voltage windows compared to conventional aqueous alkaline systems. Specifically, the invention discloses an electrolyte composition comprising at least one ionic liquid combined with a protic acid or an organic solvent, which together facilitate proton conduction through both vehicle and Grotthuss mechanisms. This electrolyte system replaces traditional potassium hydroxide (KOH) aqueous solutions used in nickel/metal hydride (Ni/MH) batteries, thereby overcoming the intrinsic limitations imposed by the narrow electrochemical stability window of water. The disclosed battery embodiments utilize this advanced electrolyte in conjunction with compatible cathode and anode materials, including but not limited to AB5-type metal hydride alloys, to achieve improved energy density, cycle life, and safety characteristics. By leveraging the wide electrochemical stability window and high proton conductivity of the formulated electrolyte, the invention enables the use of high-potential electrode materials previously incompatible with aqueous environments, thus significantly advancing the state-of-the-art in rechargeable proton-conducting batteries.

## DETAILED DISCLOSURE

The metal hydride battery of the present invention is a secondary electrochemical cell that operates via reversible proton insertion and extraction at the electrodes, facilitated by a non-aqueous electrolyte medium. Unlike conventional Ni/MH batteries that rely on aqueous KOH electrolytes wherein hydrogen transport occurs as hydroxide ions (OH⁻), the inventive system employs a proton-conductive electrolyte based on ionic liquids (ILs) blended with protic acids or organic solvents, enabling proton migration primarily through the Grotthuss mechanism—structural diffusion via hydrogen-bond networks. This fundamental shift in charge carrier mechanism expands the usable voltage range beyond the thermodynamic limit of water decomposition (~1.23 V), thereby permitting higher cell voltages and greater energy densities.

The electrolyte composition central to the invention comprises at least one ionic liquid selected from protic or aprotic varieties, combined with a proton-donating species such as acetic acid, phosphoric acid, or other organic protic acids, or alternatively with organic solvents that enhance ion dissociation and reduce viscosity. The ionic liquid serves as a non-volatile, thermally stable, and non-flammable matrix that provides structural integrity and ionic conductivity, while the added acid introduces exchangeable protons essential for proton-hopping conduction. In preferred embodiments, the ionic liquid is an imidazolium-, pyridinium-, phosphonium-, or ammonium-based salt, and the protic acid is selected from carboxylic acids, sulfonic acids, or mineral acids capable of donating protons without decomposing under operating conditions.

ABx-type alloys constitute a critical class of hydrogen storage materials suitable for use as the negative electrode (anode) in the disclosed battery. These alloys are defined by their stoichiometric ratio of constituent elements, where “A” represents one or more electropositive metals (e.g., La, Ce, Nd, Mg, Ti, Zr) and “B” denotes one or more transition metals (e.g., Ni, Co, Mn, Al, Cr, V). Specific examples include AB5-type alloys such as La₁₀.₅Ce₄.₃Pr₀.₅Nd₁.₄Ni₆₀.₀Co₁₂.₇Mn₅.₉Al₄.₇; AB-type alloys like Mg₅₂Ni₃₉Co₃Mn₆ (designated AR3); and body-centered cubic (BCC) alloys such as Ti₄₀V₃₀Cr₁₅Mn₁₃Mo₂ (BCC-B08). Ti—V—Mn alloys, known for their high hydrogen capacity and moderate equilibrium pressures, are particularly effective in acidic non-aqueous environments due to their corrosion resistance. Similarly, Ti—V—Zr—Ni quaternary alloys offer tunable thermodynamics and kinetics for hydrogen absorption/desorption. More complex formulations such as (Vᵧ′₋ᵧNiᵧTiₓ′₋ₓZrₓCr_z)_aM_b, where M may be Fe, Co, or Mn, provide further optimization of plateau pressure and hysteresis.

Zr-modified Ni-based alloys (ZrModNie) and ZrMn_wV_xM_yNi_z systems exhibit enhanced surface catalysis and bulk hydrogen diffusivity. LaNi₅-type alloys remain foundational due to their well-understood electrochemistry and ease of activation, though they require surface modification in non-aqueous media. TiV₂₋ₓNiₓ alloys balance capacity and stability, while TiaZrbNicCrdMx variants (M = Mn, Fe, Co) offer multi-phase microstructures that improve cycle life. Ti₁₋ₓZrₓMn₂₋ᵧ₋zCrᵧV_z alloys demonstrate compositional flexibility for tailoring hydrogen affinity. LnM₅ alloys (Ln = lanthanides) and broader V-, Ti-, Zr-, Ni-, Cr-, and Mn-containing systems are encompassed within the scope of operable anodes.

Additional alloy classes include (metal alloy)_aCo_bFe_cAl_dSn_e compositions, AB, AB₂, AB₅, and A₂B-type structures, and modified forms of ZrNi, ZrCo, TiNi, and TiCo. Mg₂Ni and its derivatives, though prone to oxidation, can be stabilized in dry IL environments. AB₂ Laves phase alloys, LaNi₅ modifications with partial substitutions, and TiMn₂-based systems are also viable. Notably, the complex alloy Zr₃.₆₃Ti₂₉.₈V₈.₈₂Cr₉.₈₅Mn₃₉.₅Ni₂.₀Fe₅.₀ exemplifies high-capacity BCC-type materials. A₅B₁₉ and A₂B₇ type hydrogen storage alloys, often multiphase, provide intermediate hydrogen capacities with good kinetics. Compositions such as Ln₁₋ₓMgₓNi_a₋b₋cAl_bZ_c (Z = Co, Mn, Fe) and Ln₁₋ₓMgₓ(Ni₁₋ᵧTᵧ)_z (T = Co, Al, Mn) enable fine control over electrochemical properties. Alloys containing La, Nd, Mg, Ni, and Al are especially effective in forming catalytically active surface layers.

The general formula Ti_AZr_B–X_YX_VC_Ni_DME defines a broad family of tunable alloys, where subscripts denote atomic percentages constrained by phase stability. ABx high-capacity alloys, particularly those rich in Mg and Ni, are included provided they are protected from hydrolysis. Metal hydride alloys are defined herein as intermetallic compounds capable of reversibly absorbing and desorbing hydrogen at near-ambient conditions, with hydrogen storage capacities typically exceeding 200 mAh·g⁻¹ in aqueous systems and demonstrably functional in non-aqueous IL-based electrolytes. The atomic ratio of Mg to Ni in Mg–Ni alloys is preferably between 1:1 and 2:1 to stabilize the Mg₂Ni phase. Incorporation of Co and/or Mn enhances surface reactivity and corrosion resistance. Additional elements such as Al, Fe, Cr, or Cu may be introduced in minor amounts (<10 at.%) to refine microstructure or suppress pulverization.

Prior art MH alloys developed for alkaline environments are adapted for use in the disclosed system through surface engineering or compositional tuning. Hydrogen storage capacities of these alloys in the inventive electrolyte range from 50 to over 300 mAh·g⁻¹, depending on composition and activation protocol. A generalized formula for metal hydride alloys is A_mB_n, where A is a hydrogen-absorbing element or mixture (Ti, Zr, V, La, Mg, etc.) and B is a catalytic/transition metal (Ni, Co, Mn, Fe, Cr, etc.), with m/n ratios adjusted to achieve desired plateau pressures.

Cathode materials compatible with the weakly acidic, non-aqueous electrolyte include nickel hydroxide (Ni(OH)₂) in various morphologies. Multi-phase disordered nickel hydroxide matrices, often doped with Group II elements (e.g., Ca, Sr, Ba), improve proton diffusion and electronic conductivity. Prior art cathodes based on sintered or pasted Ni(OH)₂ are modified by incorporating cobalt compounds (e.g., CoO, CoOOH) and carbon powder to enhance charge transfer. Metal oxides or hydroxides of Co, Ca, Ag, Mn, Zn, V, Sb, Cd, Y, Sr, or Ba may be added as modifiers to stabilize the β-Ni(OH)₂ phase and suppress oxygen evolution. Cathode preparation methods involve co-precipitation, impregnation, or mechanical mixing followed by thermal treatment. Particles of nickel hydroxide or manganese hydroxide, optionally coated with conductive carbons, serve as active materials. The separator is typically a microporous polyolefin or non-woven fabric, wetted by the ionic liquid-based electrolyte.

Ionic liquids employed in the invention consist of organic cations and inorganic or organic anions. Cations include imidazolium (e.g., 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium [EMIM]⁺, 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium [BMIM]⁺), pyridinium, phosphonium (e.g., triethylmethylphosphonium), and ammonium (e.g., diethylmethylammonium [DEMA]⁺). Anions encompass carboxylates (e.g., acetate [Ac]⁻), imides (e.g., bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide [TFSI]⁻), methides, bifluoride (HF₂⁻), halides (Cl⁻, Br⁻), borates (e.g., tetrafluoroborate [BF₄]⁻), phosphates, sulfonates (e.g., trifluoromethanesulfonate [TfO]⁻), sulfates, carbonates, and aluminates. Protic ionic liquids (PILs), formed by proton transfer from a Brønsted acid to a base, inherently possess mobile protons, whereas aprotic ionic liquids (AILs) require addition of external proton sources. Organic solvents such as propylene carbonate, dimethyl carbonate, tetrahydrofuran, diglyme, γ-butyrolactone, ethylene glycol, methyl formate, sulfolane, N-methylpyrrolidone, ethanol, acetone, nitromethane, and acetonitrile may be co-solvents to adjust viscosity and dielectric constant.

The electrolyte composition is optimized to balance ionic conductivity, electrochemical stability, and interfacial compatibility. In preferred embodiments, the weight/weight ratio of ionic liquid to protic acid ranges from 95:5 to 50:50, and the ratio of ionic liquid to organic solvent (when used) is between 90:10 and 60:40. Additives include corrosion inhibitors (e.g., benzotriazole), solid electrolyte interface (SEI) improvers (e.g., vinylene carbonate), self-discharge inhibitors (e.g., KF), anti-gassing agents (e.g., Li₂CO₃), viscosity reducers (e.g., linear carbonates), cathode protection agents (e.g., ZnO), salt stabilizers (e.g., molecular sieves), and conductivity enhancers (e.g., LiTFSI). Specific embodiments (E9–E15) define precise combinations: E9 uses an ionic liquid with anions selected from carboxylates, imides, methides, bifluoride, halides, borates, phosphates, sulfonates, sulfates, carbonates, or aluminates; E10 specifies perfluoroalkylcarboxylates; E11 includes bis(perfluoroalkylsulfonyl)imides; E12–E15 enumerate cation-anion pairs such as [EMIM][Ac], [BMIM][TfO], [DEMA][TfO], and phosphonium fluorophosphates.

Battery embodiments operate within a nominal open-circuit voltage range of 1.0 to 2.0 V, enabled by the extended stability window of the electrolyte (>2.5 V vs. ~1.8 V for aqueous KOH). Cathode active materials may further include oxides, hydroxides, oxyhydroxides, or fluorides of Sc, Ti, V, Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu, Zn, Y, Zr, Nb, Mo, Tc, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag, Cd, Lu, Hf, Ta, W, Re, Os, Ir, Pt, or Au, though nickel-based systems remain preferred for cost and performance. Water content is maintained below 1000 ppm to prevent parasitic reactions. The final electrolyte composition embodiments specify cation groups Rw, Rx, Ry, Rz (alkyl, aryl, or fluorinated variants) and anion groups OO defined by formulas involving variables y, m, z to denote substitution patterns. For instance, when X is B or Al, the anion may be [B(CN)₄]⁻ or [Al(OR)₄]⁻, with R being hydrocarbyl groups interrupted by heteroatoms if needed.

Hydrocarbyl is defined as alkyl, alkenyl, cycloalkyl, cycloalkenyl, aryl, or aralkyl, optionally substituted with halogens or functional groups and optionally interrupted by O, S, or N. Alkyl includes methyl, ethyl, propyl, butyl, etc.; partly or fully fluorinated groups include CF₃CH₂–; perfluoroalkyl denotes CₙF₂ₙ₊₁–; alkenyl includes vinyl; cycloalkyl includes cyclohexyl; aryl includes phenyl; aralkyl includes benzyl; halogen includes F, Cl, Br, I. Protic acids include H₂SO₄, H₃PO₄, CH₃COOH, p-toluenesulfonic acid, and oxonium salts such as H₃O⁺[SbF₆]⁻. Organic solvents span carbonates (ethylene carbonate), ethers (1,2-dimethoxyethane), ortho esters (trimethyl orthoformate), polyalkylene glycols (PEG-200), lactones (γ-valerolactone), glycols (diethylene glycol), formates (ethyl formate), sulfones (tetramethylene sulfone), sulfoxides (DMSO), amides (DMF), alcohols (isopropanol), ketones (cyclohexanone), nitro compounds (nitrobenzene), and nitriles (adiponitrile).

## EXAMPLES

Electrolyte compositions were prepared by mixing dried ionic liquids (<1000 ppm H₂O) with anhydrous acids or solvents under inert atmosphere. Conductivity was measured at 25°C using a calibrated conductivity meter.

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Acetic Acid

Binary mixtures of [EMIM][Ac], [BMIM][Ac], [EMIM][TfO], [BMIM][TfO], [EMIM][TFSI], [BMIM][TFSI], [EIM][TFSI], and [DEMA][TfO] with glacial acetic acid (0–6 M) were prepared. Conductivity increased with acid concentration, peaking at ~14 mS·cm⁻¹ for [DEMA][TfO] + 6 M acetic acid. Swagelok cells with AB5 anodes and sintered Ni(OH)₂ cathodes showed reversible cycling, with [EMIM][Ac] + 2 M acetic acid delivering 161.9 mAh·g⁻¹ and stable 1.2 V discharge plateau.

### Electrolyte Composition Comprising Ionic Liquid and Solvent

A ternary blend of [EMIM][Ac] + 2 M acetic acid + 20 wt% propylene carbonate exhibited enhanced wettability and reduced viscosity, yielding 20% higher conductivity than binary counterpart and improved rate capability in half-cell tests.

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Protic Acid

Phosphoric acid (up to saturation) was dissolved in [EMIM][TfO] at 50°C. Despite high theoretical proton conductivity, the resulting electrolyte showed poor electrode compatibility, with rapid capacity fade within 30 cycles due to cathode dissolution and anode corrosion.

### Electrolyte Composition Containing 2 M Acetic Acid in EMIM Ac

Full cells cycled between 0.2–1.8 V at 4 mA·g⁻¹ achieved 28.6 mAh·g⁻¹ after 25 activation cycles, retaining 23.9 mAh·g⁻¹ at 100 cycles. Charge/discharge plateaus stabilized at 1.6 V/1.2 V, confirming low polarization.

### Example 5

Cyclic voltammetry in 2 M acetic acid/[EMIM][Ac] revealed a hydrogen adsorption peak at −1.0 V vs. Ag/AgCl, with a 0.6 V margin before electrolyte reduction, indicating sufficient stability for efficient cycling. Conductivity of the ternary electrolyte ([EMIM][Ac] + acetic acid + propylene carbonate) reached 12.5 mS·cm⁻¹, supporting high-rate operation.
