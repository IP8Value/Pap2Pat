## FIELD

The present invention relates to a computer-implemented method for predicting behavior in dynamical systems involving multiple interacting agents. More specifically, the invention pertains to a method that leverages graph neural networks within a deep state-space modeling framework to generate multimodal predictive distributions over future trajectories of agents while maintaining computational efficiency through deterministic moment propagation techniques.

## BACKGROUND INFORMATION

Predicting the behavior of agents in complex dynamical systems has long been a fundamental challenge across numerous domains including autonomous driving, robotics, crowd simulation, and financial modeling. Traditional approaches have often relied on either purely deterministic models that fail to capture the inherent stochasticity of real-world interactions or probabilistic models that require computationally expensive sampling procedures to approximate predictive distributions. In multi-agent scenarios, these challenges are exacerbated by the need to model not only the individual dynamics of each agent but also the complex interactions between them. While graph neural networks have emerged as a powerful tool for capturing such interactions by propagating information through relational structures, their integration with probabilistic state-space models has typically required Monte Carlo methods that become prohibitively expensive as the number of agents increases. Furthermore, many real-world applications demand multimodal predictions that can account for multiple plausible future outcomes, such as different driver intentions in autonomous driving scenarios, which cannot be adequately captured by unimodal distributional assumptions. The computational complexity of maintaining full covariance structures across multiple agents further compounds these challenges, particularly in resource-constrained environments such as embedded vehicle control systems. Consequently, there exists a significant need for methods that can efficiently generate expressive multimodal predictive distributions while accounting for agent interactions and maintaining computational tractability.

## SUMMARY

The present invention achieves precise prediction of behavior in multi-agent dynamical systems through a novel combination of graph neural networks and deterministic moment matching within a deep state-space modeling framework. The method determines a value of a first moment of a first distribution representing the initial latent state of agents in the system. It further determines a value of a second moment of the first distribution, which captures the uncertainty and correlations in the initial state. The invention then determines an expected value for a first moment of a second distribution, which represents the propagated latent state at a future time point, by applying deterministic moment matching rules through graph neural network layers. Additionally, the method determines a second moment of a third distribution corresponding to the observation space, which enables the computation of predictive likelihoods in the observable domain. The invention determines a sum of the third distributions across all mixture components to form a comprehensive predictive distribution over future observations. This process culminates in determining a prediction of behavior that accounts for multiple plausible future trajectories while maintaining computational efficiency. The method recursively determines the value of the first moment through successive time steps by propagating the mean through the graph neural network transition model. Similarly, it recursively determines the value of the second moment by propagating the covariance through the same transition model. The invention efficiently determines the expected value of nonlinear transformations through neural network layers using analytical moment matching rules rather than sampling-based approximations. The method determines the covariance of the first moment of the second distribution by accounting for both deterministic and stochastic components of the transition dynamics. It further determines an expected value for the second moment of the second distribution, which captures the evolution of uncertainty through the system dynamics. The invention determines the second moment of the third distribution by propagating uncertainty through the emission model that maps latent states to observations. The method considers a context variable that encodes auxiliary information such as historical trajectories, relational structures, and environmental features that influence agent behavior. It determines a history of the dynamic system from observed trajectories of agents over a predetermined time window. The invention considers the neighborhood of agents through graph-based representations where edges encode relationships between agents based on proximity or other interaction criteria. The method models latent states of agents in a coupled manner through shared graph neural network parameters that capture interaction effects. Finally, the invention enables control of an agent depending on the prediction by providing calibrated uncertainty estimates that can inform decision-making processes in autonomous systems.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EXAMPLE EMBODIMENTS

The present invention introduces a device for predicting behavior of agents in complex dynamical systems, particularly suited for applications involving multiple interacting entities such as autonomous vehicles in traffic scenarios. The device comprises several key components that work in concert to generate efficient and accurate behavioral predictions. The device includes an interface and sensor system that receives observational data from the environment, which may include position, velocity, and acceleration measurements of multiple agents over time. The sensor system capabilities extend beyond basic kinematic measurements to include environmental context such as road geometry, traffic rules, and weather conditions that may influence agent behavior. The device further comprises an actuator and control command module that can translate predictions into actionable commands for autonomous systems, though in some embodiments this component may be external to the prediction device itself. A computer program stored on non-transitory computer-readable medium implements the core prediction algorithms described herein, enabling the device to process input data and generate behavioral predictions in real-time.

To illustrate the operation of the invention, consider a dynamic system example involving traffic participants at a roundabout intersection. In this scenario, the agents in the dynamic system correspond to individual vehicles navigating the roundabout, each with their own behavioral patterns and intentions. The roundabout example presents a particularly challenging prediction problem due to the multiple possible exit choices available to each vehicle and the complex interactions between vehicles as they negotiate right-of-way. The device processes observed trajectories of these vehicles over a historical time window, typically represented as dashed lines in visualization outputs, to infer their current states and potential future behaviors. Based on this historical information, the device generates predicted trajectories for each vehicle, shown as solid lines with associated confidence intervals that capture the uncertainty in predictions.

The invention represents the predictive distribution as a Gaussian mixture distribution, which enables the modeling of multiple distinct behavioral modes that may correspond to different driver intentions. For each mode and traffic participant, the device computes and displays confidence intervals, typically at the 95% level, that provide calibrated uncertainty estimates around the predicted trajectories. These predictions can include various kinematic quantities such as distance, velocity, or acceleration, depending on the requirements of the specific application. In the autonomous vehicle example, these predictions enable the ego-vehicle to anticipate the behaviors of surrounding traffic participants and plan safe maneuvers accordingly.

The core of the invention's predictive capability lies in a machine learning model that combines graph neural networks with deep state-space modeling principles. The model introduces a latent variable X that represents the hidden state of the system, which may include quantities not directly observable in the input data such as driver intentions or vehicle dynamics parameters. The observed variable Y corresponds to the measurable quantities such as vehicle positions and velocities. The model begins with an initial value for the latent state that is inferred from historical observations through an embedding function implemented as a graph neural network. The latent state evolves over time through both deterministic change governed by neural network functions and stochastic change that captures unpredictable variations in agent behavior.

The context variable I plays a crucial role in the model by encoding auxiliary information that influences agent behavior, including historical trajectories, relational structures between agents, and environmental features such as road maps. The observed variable Y follows a normal distribution conditioned on the latent state X, with parameters determined by neural network functions. Specifically, the mean of this distribution is computed by a neural network g that maps latent states to expected observations, while the variance is determined by another neural network Γ that captures observation uncertainty.

The deterministic change in the latent state is modeled by a neural network f that takes the current latent state and context variable as inputs and produces the mean update for the next time step. Similarly, the stochastic change is modeled by a neural network L that determines the variance of the transition noise. Both of these neural networks are updated during training to optimize predictive performance. The model also incorporates message passing between agents through graph neural network operations, where each agent receives information from its neighbors through an aggregation operation AGG that is permutation invariant and can handle varying numbers of neighbors.

The edges of the interaction graph are dynamically constructed based on agent proximity or other relevant criteria, enabling the model to adapt to changing interaction patterns over time. For prediction at a specific time point T, the model computes the marginal probability p(yT|I) by integrating over the latent state distribution. This involves computing the kernel p(xT|x0,I) that represents the transition from initial to final latent states, combined with the initial distribution p(x0|I) which is modeled as a Gaussian mixture model to capture multimodal initial conditions.

The Gaussian mixture model p(x0|I) assigns weights to different mixture components that may correspond to different behavioral hypotheses about the agents' intentions. Each component is characterized by its mean value and covariance matrix, which together determine the first and second moments of the distribution. The model computes expected values and covariances through the transition dynamics using moment matching rules that propagate these statistics through neural network layers without requiring sampling. Cross-covariance terms between latent states and their transformations are computed using Jacobian matrices that capture local linear approximations of the neural network functions.

The method for prediction p(yT|I) employs a bidimensional moment matching approach that combines horizontal moment matching along the time axis with vertical moment matching across neural network layers. This is implemented through an inner loop that handles moment propagation through network layers and an outer loop that handles temporal propagation. The neural network for moments is specifically designed to compute output statistics efficiently, taking advantage of structured covariance approximations to maintain computational tractability.

The context variable I is associated with neighborhood structures Nm for each agent m, which define the set of agents that influence agent m's behavior. Trajectories are formally defined as sequences of observed states over time, which serve as both input for inference and output for prediction. The operation AGG implements the message passing mechanism that enables information flow between agents, with the motivation for using graph neural networks stemming from their ability to naturally model interaction effects in multi-agent systems.

In the computational implementation, step 404 determines the first moment recursively by propagating the mean through the transition model using analytical moment matching rules. A specialized tool for expected value and covariance computation implements the operation AGG efficiently by leveraging the structured nature of graph neural network operations. The expected value and covariance are calculated using closed-form expressions for common neural network operations such as affine transformations and ReLU activations. The Jacobi matrix is determined to compute cross-covariance terms that arise in the moment propagation equations. Affine transformations are handled through exact moment computations that exploit their linear structure.

The expected value and covariance are calculated recursively through time steps by applying the moment matching rules at each transition. Step 406 determines the second moment recursively by propagating the covariance through both deterministic and stochastic components of the transition model. The covariance of the deterministic change is calculated by propagating uncertainty through the mean update function f, while the expected value of the stochastic change is computed by averaging the variance update function L over the current state distribution. Step 408 determines the expected value of the first moment and the covariance of the first moment for the observation distribution by propagating statistics through the emission model. Step 410 determines the first moment of the normal distribution in observation space, which provides the predicted mean trajectory for each agent. Step 412 determines the expected value of the second moment, which captures the total uncertainty in predictions including both aleatoric and epistemic components. Step 414 determines the second moment of the normal distribution in observation space, completing the specification of the predictive distribution. The outer loop coordinates these computations across time steps to produce the final prediction for the target time horizon T.
