# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of organ transplantation and, more specifically, to systems and methods for predicting post-transplant survival rates of prospective organ transplant recipients. The invention provides a predictive analytics platform that leverages historical transplant data, machine learning algorithms, and recipient-donor characteristics to generate personalized survival rate estimates for individual patients awaiting organ transplants. This technology enables improved decision-making in organ allocation by quantifying expected survival outcomes for specific donor-recipient pairings, thereby facilitating more equitable and effective distribution of scarce transplantable organs. The system is particularly applicable to kidney transplantation but may be adapted for use with other solid organs including liver, heart, lung, and pancreas. The invention further encompasses graphical user interfaces, data processing architectures, and communication protocols designed to support real-time survival prediction and interactive decision support for transplant clinicians, organ procurement organizations, and prospective recipients.

## BACKGROUND

Organ transplantation represents a life-saving intervention for patients suffering from end-stage organ failure. Despite significant medical advances, the demand for transplantable organs far exceeds supply, resulting in lengthy waiting lists and substantial mortality among candidates awaiting transplantation. In the United States alone, over 100,000 individuals are currently listed for organ transplantation, with kidneys representing the most commonly needed organ. The Organ Procurement and Transplantation Network (OPTN), operated under contract with the Health Resources and Services Administration (HRSA), maintains the national transplant waiting list and oversees organ allocation through a complex system designed to balance medical urgency, geographic proximity, and expected post-transplant outcomes.

Current organ allocation systems rely on relatively simplistic scoring mechanisms to prioritize candidates. For kidney transplantation, the Estimated Post Transplant Survival (EPTS) score has been used since 2013 to match high-quality kidneys with recipients most likely to benefit from long-term graft survival. However, these existing models suffer from several critical limitations. First, they typically employ a single statistical model applied uniformly across all patient populations, failing to account for heterogeneity in how different patient subgroups respond to transplantation. Second, they incorporate a limited number of variables—often fewer than ten—which may not capture the full complexity of factors influencing post-transplant outcomes. Third, many current models exclude donor characteristics entirely or treat them as secondary considerations, preventing true optimization of donor-recipient matching. Finally, existing systems provide static scores rather than dynamic, personalized survival curves that could better inform clinical decision-making and patient counseling.

These limitations have significant consequences for both individual patients and the broader transplant system. Suboptimal matching can lead to premature graft failure, increased healthcare costs, and reduced quality of life for recipients. At the system level, inefficient allocation reduces the total number of life-years gained from available organs, effectively wasting precious transplant opportunities. There exists a pressing need for an improved system that can generate more accurate, personalized survival predictions by leveraging advanced machine learning techniques, comprehensive datasets, and sophisticated modeling approaches that adapt to different patient subpopulations.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a predictive organ transplant survival rate system that addresses the limitations of current allocation methodologies through an innovative ensemble modeling approach. The system calculates survival rates by receiving multiple datasets containing historical transplant information, including characteristics of previous recipients, donors, and actual post-transplant outcomes. From these datasets, the system calculates a first set of estimated survival rates based on comparisons with previous persons who received transplants and a second set of estimated survival rates based on comparisons with previous persons who did not receive transplants but shared similar characteristics with the prospective recipient.

The system generates a graph displaying both sets of estimated survival rates, which is then transmitted to user devices for display to clinicians and patients. The characteristics considered include demographic factors, medical history, laboratory values, and organ-specific metrics for both previous recipients and donors, as well as detailed attributes of the prospective recipient and the available organ. Survival rates are calculated differently based on organ type, recognizing that different organs have distinct failure patterns and risk factors.

A key innovation of the system is its ability to assign dynamic weights to various characteristics based on their predictive importance within specific patient subgroups. The system compares characteristics between prospective recipients and historical cases to identify congruent matches, then calculates a comprehensive transplant score that integrates multiple predictive dimensions. The underlying predictive algorithm is continuously updated as new transplant outcomes become available, ensuring that predictions remain current and accurate.

When an organ becomes available, the system calculates survival rates for multiple potential recipients, identifies the candidate most likely to achieve the longest post-transplant survival, and generates a personalized graph for that individual. An offer notification containing the survival graph is sent to the prospective recipient's device, and their response—acceptance or denial—is recorded and used to refine future predictions. If the initial offer is declined, the system automatically determines the next optimal recipient, generates a new survival graph, and repeats the offer process until the organ is accepted or reallocated.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive system for predictive survival rates of prospective organ transplant recipients that integrates advanced machine learning techniques with real-world transplant data. The system architecture comprises user devices, computing devices, and external servers working in concert to deliver personalized survival predictions. User devices include smartphones, tablets, and computers equipped with specialized transplant applications that enable recipients and clinicians to view survival graphs and respond to organ offers. Computing devices serve as the analytical backbone, housing the predictive algorithms and processing capabilities necessary for complex survival modeling. External servers maintain centralized databases and facilitate data exchange between system components.

The database contains extensive historical transplant records, including characteristics of previous prospective organ transplant recipients such as age, sex, ethnicity, comorbidities, laboratory values, and immunological profiles. It also stores characteristics of previous organs received, including donor age, cause of death, organ quality metrics, and preservation details. Additionally, the database includes information about previous persons in need of transplantation who did not receive organs, along with their eventual outcomes, providing crucial counterfactual data for survival modeling.

The system calculates two distinct sets of actual survival rates: a first set derived from previous transplant recipients and a second set from non-recipients with similar characteristics. By identifying congruence between prospective recipients and these historical cohorts, the system calculates corresponding estimated survival rates that reflect both the benefits of transplantation and the risks of remaining on the waiting list.

Graph generation represents a critical user interface component, translating complex statistical predictions into intuitive visual representations that facilitate informed decision-making. These graphs are transmitted to external servers for secure download by authorized user devices, accompanied by instructions for proper display and interpretation. User responses—including acceptance or denial of organ offers—are captured and fed back into the system to continuously refine the predictive algorithm and update characteristic weights.

When determining organ recipients, the system processes comprehensive datasets containing both historical transplant records and real-time information about available organs and waiting list candidates. For each prospective recipient, the system calculates dual survival rate estimates and analyzes these predictions to identify the candidate most likely to achieve optimal post-transplant outcomes. Personalized graphs are generated and transmitted alongside organ offers, with response tracking enabling efficient reallocation if necessary.

The system maintains strict data security protocols, storing personally identifiable information separately from analytical datasets and requiring authentication for access to sensitive information. Graphs and supporting documentation are made available for download only after proper identity verification, ensuring patient privacy while maintaining clinical utility.

The method for determining predictive organ transplant survival rates follows a systematic workflow: receiving datasets from multiple sources, calculating dual survival rate estimates, generating visual representations, transmitting results to stakeholders, capturing responses, and continuously updating the predictive model. This process is implemented through specialized software applications running on both user devices and computing devices, featuring intuitive graphical user interfaces with interactive elements that enable seamless navigation and decision-making.

Key terms used throughout this specification are carefully defined to ensure clarity and precision. The disclosed technology encompasses multiple implementations that share core algorithmic principles while adapting to different organ types and clinical contexts. The patentable scope extends to both the specific technical implementations described herein and broader applications of the underlying predictive methodology to organ transplantation and related medical decision-support domains.
