# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of agricultural biotechnology, specifically in the development and application of synthetic microbial preparations comprising endophytic fungi and bacteria for the enhancement of seed vitality, germination efficiency, and stress resilience in crop plants. The invention further relates to novel compositions comprising agriculturally acceptable carriers and selected endophytes capable of colonizing seed tissues and modulating plant gene expression to improve agronomic traits such as drought tolerance, heat resistance, biomass accumulation, and yield stability. Additionally, the invention encompasses methods for inoculating seeds with defined endophytic consortia, formulations suitable for commercial-scale seed treatment, and systems for ensuring uniformity and performance consistency in agricultural fields through microbiome-mediated prenatal care of crop seeds.

## BACKGROUND

Endophytes are microorganisms—primarily fungi and bacteria—that reside within plant tissues without causing apparent disease symptoms. Historically, endophytes were regarded as passive inhabitants of plant interiors, but contemporary research has revealed their active role in shaping plant physiology, development, and environmental adaptability. The concept of endophytism dates back to the late 19th century, with early observations of fungal structures inside healthy plant tissues. However, it was not until the latter half of the 20th century that the ecological and functional significance of endophytes began to be systematically explored, particularly in natural ecosystems and non-cultivated plants.

Endophytes play multifaceted roles in plant biology. They contribute to nutrient acquisition, phytohormone modulation, pathogen defense, and abiotic stress mitigation. In particular, certain endophytic fungi have been shown to enhance seed germination under suboptimal conditions by influencing hormonal balances, scavenging reactive oxygen species, and improving water-use efficiency. Seed germination is a critical developmental checkpoint that determines the success of seedling establishment and, ultimately, crop productivity. Under climate change scenarios characterized by increasing frequency of drought and heat extremes, the ability to ensure reliable and vigorous germination becomes paramount.

Endophyte colonization typically occurs either vertically (transmission from parent to offspring via seeds) or horizontally (acquisition from the environment during plant growth). In agricultural contexts, horizontal colonization is often inconsistent due to soil variability, chemical inputs, and microbial competition. This inconsistency limits the reproducibility and scalability of endophyte-based benefits. While some endophytes, such as arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi and rhizobacteria, are already used in commercial agriculture for nutrient uptake enhancement, their application is largely restricted to root systems and post-germination stages. The potential of seed-colonizing endophytes to act as “prenatal” agents—modulating seed physiology before and during germination—remains largely untapped.

Despite documented benefits, prior art suffers from significant limitations. Existing endophyte products lack specificity for seed tissues, fail to ensure consistent colonization across seed lots, and do not reliably confer stress resilience under field conditions. Moreover, many commercially available microbial inoculants are incompatible with standard agricultural chemicals, limiting their integration into conventional farming systems. There is also a paucity of standardized methods for evaluating endophyte efficacy in modulating seed vigor, germination kinetics, and early seedling traits under controlled stress regimes.

The importance of endophytes in sustainable agriculture cannot be overstated. As global food demand rises and arable land faces increasing environmental pressure, harnessing the plant microbiome offers a non-transgenic route to enhance crop resilience. Endophytes represent a natural extension of the plant’s genetic and physiological repertoire, effectively functioning as a “second genome” that can be engineered through selection and formulation. Seedling establishment—the transition from quiescent seed to autotrophic seedling—is highly vulnerable to environmental fluctuations. Endophytes that accelerate this transition while buffering against stress provide a strategic advantage in both conventional and organic farming.

Given these challenges and opportunities, there exists a clear and urgent need for new methods and compositions that enable precise, reliable, and scalable delivery of beneficial endophytes to seeds. Such innovations must address compatibility with agrichemicals, ensure heritable trait improvements, and support uniform performance across diverse crop genotypes and environmental conditions. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing synthetic endophyte preparations specifically designed for seed application, with demonstrated efficacy in enhancing germination, stress tolerance, and yield-related traits through targeted modulation of plant gene expression and physiology.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces synthetic preparations comprising selected endophytic microorganisms formulated for application to seeds to enhance plant performance. These compositions offer significant advantages over prior art by ensuring consistent colonization of seed tissues, compatibility with agricultural practices, and reproducible improvement of key agronomic traits. The invention is applicable to a wide range of plant hosts, including monocots such as wheat, barley, maize, and rice, as well as dicots such as soybean, pea, lentil, chickpea, canola, and flax.

Symbiotic efficacy is evaluated through parameters including colonization rate in cortical cells, enhancement of 50% germination time, increase in seedling fresh weight, and modulation of stress-responsive genes. Germination assessment is performed using hydrothermal time models that integrate temperature and water potential effects, enabling precise prediction of germination under stress. The synthetic preparations significantly improve seed vitality, defined as the sum of germination capacity, vigor, and stress resilience.

Improved plant traits conferred by the invention include increased drought tolerance, heat resistance, cold tolerance, salt tolerance, pest and disease resistance, biomass accumulation, root and shoot elongation, seed or fruit number, and overall plant vigor. These traits arise from targeted gene modulation involving pathways related to phytohormone biosynthesis (e.g., gibberellins and abscisic acid), systemic acquired resistance (e.g., MYB transcription factors), and oxidative stress protection (e.g., superoxide dismutase, proline biosynthesis).

The core composition comprises one or more endophytes in a physiologically active or dormant form, suspended or embedded in an agriculturally acceptable carrier. Endophytes may be filamentous fungi (Ascomycota or Basidiomycota) or bacteria (e.g., Streptomyces, Pseudomonas), selected for their ability to form symbiotic structures within seed and root tissues without eliciting pathogenic responses. These structures include intracellular hyphal coils, symbiosomes, and intercellular networks that facilitate molecular exchange.

Seed coating is a primary delivery method, wherein the composition is applied to the seed surface as a liquid, gel, or powder, optionally with adjuvants such as tackifiers, surfactants, and desiccants to ensure adherence and shelf stability. The coated seeds are packaged in moisture-barrier materials to maintain microbial viability until planting. Upon sowing, the endophytes colonize the emerging radicle and coleorhiza, establishing a symbiotic system that persists through early seedling development.

Endophyte selection is based on rigorous screening for traits including compatibility with agrichemicals, rapid colonization kinetics, stress-induced bioactivity, and absence of toxin production. Strains may be wild isolates, spontaneously adapted variants, or genetically modified to enhance specific functions (e.g., plasmid-borne stress tolerance genes). The invention supports both single-strain and multi-strain formulations, enabling synergistic interactions across different plant tissues.

The resulting plant-fungus symbiosis exhibits anti-aging and anti-senescence effects, prolonging photosynthetic activity and delaying leaf yellowing under stress. Pathogen protection is achieved through induced systemic resistance and direct antagonism. The invention further provides a method for improving seed vitality by inoculating seeds with the composition, cultivating first-generation plants, and selecting for heritable improvements in stress resilience.

Altered traits are measurable at morphological, physiological, and molecular levels. Gene expression changes are confirmed via quantitative PCR of markers such as RSG (repression of shoot growth), KAO (ent-kaurenoic acid oxidase), and MYB resistance genes. Phytohormone levels, particularly GA/ABA ratios, are shifted to favor germination under stress. Collectively, these modifications render germination a predictable and robust process even under combined heat and drought stress, thereby ensuring reliable crop stand establishment and yield stability.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The synergy between endophytes and plants represents a co-evolved partnership that transcends simple microbial association. Plants share fundamental physiological and developmental pathways, allowing beneficial endophytes to exert conserved effects across diverse species. Endophytes are classified by interaction type: mutualistic, commensal, or latent pathogenic, with the present invention focusing exclusively on mutualistic strains that enhance host fitness without compromising growth.

Endophytes possess sophisticated informational systems, including quorum sensing, volatile organic compound signaling, and effector protein secretion, which mediate dialogue with host cells. These systems enable dynamic responses to host status and environmental cues. The benefits of endophyte colonization are manifold: enhanced nutrient solubilization, phytohormone production, antioxidant synthesis, and induction of defense priming.

Seed germination is a tightly regulated process involving water uptake, metabolic reactivation, and radicle emergence. Endophytes stimulate this process by modulating the balance between abscisic acid (dormancy-promoting) and gibberellins (germination-promoting). They also lower the base water potential (ψmin) and thermal time required for germination, effectively expanding the environmental window for successful emergence.

Beyond germination, endophytes confer additional benefits including improved root architecture, enhanced photosynthetic efficiency, and increased nutrient use efficiency. These effects stem from sustained colonization of cortical and vascular tissues, where endophytes form specialized structures that facilitate metabolite exchange. The association is non-destructive and reversible, aligning with the plant’s developmental needs.

In conclusion, endophyte-plant associations represent a scalable, sustainable platform for crop improvement. By targeting the seed—the foundational unit of plant life—this invention leverages natural symbiosis to deliver predictable, field-ready enhancements in crop resilience and productivity, addressing the urgent challenges of climate-resilient agriculture.

### Novel Compositions and Seeds

The invention provides novel compositions comprising endophytic microorganisms selected for their ability to colonize seed tissues and enhance plant performance. An endophyte, as used herein, refers to a fungus or bacterium that can live asymptomatically within plant tissues, including seeds, and confer a benefit to the host. The composition includes an agriculturally acceptable carrier—such as diatomaceous earth, talc, alginate, or plant-based oils—that stabilizes the microorganism and facilitates adherence to the seed surface.

Specifically, the composition includes coleorhiza-activating endophytes, which preferentially colonize the protective sheath of the emerging radicle, thereby accelerating germination under stress. The synthetic preparation may contain heterologous endophytes—strains not naturally associated with the host plant—selected for superior performance in target crops. Endophytes are disposed on the seed surface or within seed tissue via coating, soaking, or injection, ensuring immediate contact upon imbibition.

The resulting seeds exhibit improved functional traits, including resistance to biotic stresses (e.g., Fusarium head blight, leaf spot pathogens) and abiotic stresses (e.g., drought, heat, salinity). Yield is measured as grain number, biomass, or harvest index, with improvements observed across multiple generations, indicating heritability. The invention applies to all major agricultural seed types, including monocots (wheat, barley, maize, rice) and dicots (soybean, pea, canola), with tailored formulations for each.

The amount of composition applied ranges from 10^4 to 10^8 colony-forming units per seed, optimized for colonization without inhibiting germination. Endophyte types include Ascomycota (e.g., *Fusarium*, *Trichoderma*), Basidiomycota (e.g., *Piriformospora*), and Actinobacteria (e.g., *Streptomyces*). Structures formed include intracellular hyphal coils, symbiosomes, and intercellular networks, distinct from arbuscules of mycorrhizae.

Novel compositions are defined by specific strains, such as SMCD2215 (*Streptomyces* sp.), SMCD2204, SMCD2206, and others, with DNA sequences provided in deposited strains. These endophytes modulate expression of genes involved in plant growth (e.g., *RSG*, *KAO*), systemic resistance (e.g., *MYB*), and oxidative stress (e.g., *MnSOD*, *Pro*). Changes in gene expression are quantified via qPCR, showing upregulation of germination promoters and downregulation of dormancy genes.

Compositions may include multiple endophytes with complementary properties—for example, one strain enhancing drought tolerance and another conferring pathogen resistance. Colonization targets different tissues: some strains specialize in coleorhiza, others in root cortex or shoot meristems. Coating ensures uniform distribution, validated by quantitative PCR of seed washes or homogenates.

Soil-based inoculation is also possible, but seed coating provides superior control. Formulations are liquid, gel, or powder, with concentrations adjusted for field-scale applicators. Endophytes may be modified via mutagenesis, serial selection, or genetic engineering to enhance agrichemical compatibility—e.g., tolerance to fungicides like azoxystrobin or antibiotics like streptomycin. Spontaneous isolates from chemically treated fields are preferred for inherent compatibility.

Packaging uses moisture-resistant bags with labels indicating strain, concentration, and expiration. Agricultural products comprise predetermined seed counts or weights, ensuring dosing accuracy. The article of manufacture includes both packaging and seeds, ready for commercial distribution. Inoculation protocols specify hydration-dehydration cycles to activate metabolism prior to planting, maximizing colonization efficiency.

### Methods

The invention provides methods for enhancing seed vitality by inoculating seeds with endophyte-containing compositions. The seed is treated exogenously, then cultivated into a first-generation plant. The endophyte replicates within plant tissue, colonizing cortical cells at levels exceeding 0.1% colonization frequency, as confirmed by microscopy or qPCR.

Endophytes are selected for their ability to promote germination, particularly under stress, and may be heterologous to the host. Upon colonization, they form symbiotic structures and modulate phytohormone levels—elevating gibberellins, reducing abscisic acid—thereby accelerating radicle emergence. The symbiosis exhibits anti-aging effects, delaying senescence and maintaining chlorophyll content under heat stress.

Pathogen protection is achieved through induced systemic resistance, with upregulation of PR proteins and MYB transcription factors. Colonization levels vary by tissue and strain but are sufficient to alter plant traits measurably. Improved functional traits include increased yield, seedling establishment, growth rate, and tolerance to drought, heat, cold, salt, and pests.

Biomass, root/shoot length, fresh weight, seed number, and plant vigor are all enhanced. Gene expression is improved in pathways governing stress response and development. The 50% germination rate is accelerated by at least 12 hours under control conditions and by 24–48 hours under stress. The method is scalable to industrial seed treatment facilities and compatible with existing planting equipment.

### Formulations/Seed Coating Compositions

Formulations combine endophytes with carriers selected for stability, wettability, and dispersability. Solid carriers include silica, loam, and diatomaceous earth; liquid carriers include water, plant oils, or fertilizer solutions. Wetting agents (e.g., esterified seed oils) and tackifiers (e.g., alginate, starches) ensure adhesion. Surfactants such as nitrogen-surfactant blends enhance spreading.

Desiccants like trehalose or sucrose protect microbes during drying. Fungicides or antibacterial agents may be added if the endophyte is resistant, enabling co-application with standard seed treatments. Emulsions (e.g., water-in-oil) encapsulate microbes for controlled release. Granules, pellets, and gels offer alternative delivery formats for different cropping systems.

### Populations of Seeds

The invention provides substantially uniform seed populations, defined by consistent microbial density (±20% CV) and germination performance. Uniformity is validated by growing subsamples and measuring height, flowering time, or biomass. Seeds are packaged in labeled containers, suitable for air seeders or precision planters. Soil inoculation is possible but less reliable than seed coating.

### Increased Uniformity in Populations of Plants/Agricultural Fields

Uniform microbial composition leads to reduced standard deviation in plant height and flowering time, enhancing harvest efficiency. Genetic uniformity of the microbial inoculant ensures consistent physiological responses across the field.

### Decreased Uniformity in Populations of Plants/Agricultural Fields

In some cases, increased phenotypic diversity (e.g., staggered flowering) may be desirable for risk mitigation; the invention can be tuned to achieve this by blending strains with differential effects.

### Commodity Plant Product

Commodity products include nonviable materials such as meal, oil, or fiber derived from endophyte-treated crops. Industrial uses include biodiesel from canola oil or protein isolates from pulse crops, with no residual microbial concerns.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Endophytes SMCD2215 (*Streptomyces* sp.), SMCD2204, SMCD2206, etc., were isolated from prairie soils and classified via 16S rDNA sequencing. Strains exhibited robust growth under heat and drought, with SMCD2215 showing highest ACC deaminase activity.

### Example 2

Compatibility assays showed root hair proliferation and endodermal colonization. Symbiotic indices confirmed mutualism, with no growth penalty.

### Example 3

In wheat, endophytes formed symbiosomes in root cells, visualized via light microscopy, confirming mycovitalism.

### Example 4

Under heat/drought stress, SMCD2206-colonized wheat showed 30% higher germination and 25% greater fresh weight versus controls. Hydrothermal time modeling confirmed reduced ψmin and Tmin.

### Example 5

Field trials demonstrated 15–20% yield increase in wheat and barley under drought, with strain-specific effects on kernel number.

### Example 6

Phytotron studies showed biomass increases of up to 40% in heat-stressed wheat with SMCD2215.

### Example 7

In greenhouse drought trials, pea plants with SMCD2204 produced 35% more pods.

### Example 8

SMCD2215 enhanced *Rhizobium* nodulation in legumes, indicating cross-kingdom synergy.

### Example 9

Pulse crops showed improved germination energy and frequency with endophyte treatment, linked to GA/ABA gene modulation.

### Example 10

Yield improvements of 18% were observed in lentil under field drought, with reduced pest damage.

### Example 11

Biological stratification with SMCD2206 matched cold stratification in breaking dormancy, with superior stress resilience.

### Example 12

GA biosynthesis genes (*KAO*) were upregulated; ABA catabolism genes were induced, shifting hormonal balance.

### Example 13

MYB resistance genes and MnSOD were upregulated, enhancing oxidative stress tolerance.

### Example 14

Nitric oxide accumulation in radicles, visualized via DAF-2T fluorescence, confirmed signaling role in mycovitalism.

### Example 15

Field trials across three sites showed consistent yield gains, reduced Fusarium incidence, and uniform flowering.

### Example 16

Greenhouse trials with canola and flax confirmed cross-species efficacy.

### Example 17

HPLC-MS/MS detected elevated GA4 and reduced ABA in colonized seeds.

### Example 18

Fungal biomass was scaled in bioreactors, formulated with talc, and applied via rotary coater.

### Example 19

In-vitro assays confirmed phosphate solubilization, siderophore production, and antifungal activity.
