# DESCRIPTION

## INTRODUCTION

Multiple myeloma is a hematologic malignancy arising from terminally differentiated plasma B cells and remains an incurable disease despite recent therapeutic advances. The clinical course of multiple myeloma is typically preceded by a premalignant condition known as monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS), which may progress through an intermediate smoldering phase before evolving into overt, symptomatic myeloma. This stepwise progression underscores the importance of early detection and molecular characterization of genetic events that drive malignant transformation. Genetic alterations present in MGUS are considered initiating events in myelomagenesis and often persist throughout disease evolution. These initiating lesions fall broadly into two mutually exclusive primary subtypes: hyperdiploid multiple myeloma, characterized by trisomies involving multiple odd-numbered chromosomes, and non-hyperdiploid multiple myeloma, which is frequently defined by immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) translocations that dysregulate oncogenes through juxtaposition with potent B-cell-specific enhancer elements within the IGH locus. Canonical IGH translocation partners include WHSC1/FGFR3 at t(4;14), CCND1 at t(11;14), CCND3 at t(6;14), MAF at t(14;16), and MAFB at t(14;20). Secondary genetic events, which are absent in MGUS but emerge during progression to active myeloma, include MYC translocations, recurrent single nucleotide variants (SNVs) in genes such as KRAS, NRAS, and DIS3, and copy number alterations (CNAs) involving gains of 1q or losses of 1p, 6q, 13q, 14q, or 17p. These secondary hits contribute to clonal expansion, treatment resistance, and disease aggressiveness.

Recent efforts have integrated these diverse mutational classes into refined prognostic models. Notably, the International Staging System–Mutational (ISS-MUT) model combines traditional clinical staging with genomic data on CNVs, SNVs, and translocations to improve risk stratification and predict early mortality more accurately than clinical staging alone. Additional studies have revealed context-dependent interactions among mutation types; for example, trisomies of chromosomes 3 or 5 can mitigate the adverse prognosis typically associated with t(4;14) translocations. Such findings highlight the necessity of comprehensive genomic profiling that captures all major classes of somatic alterations—translocations, SNVs, and CNVs—in a single assay to inform personalized therapeutic strategies. While whole-exome sequencing has been used in research settings to achieve this breadth, its high cost, computational burden, and longer turnaround time limit clinical utility. Targeted sequencing approaches offer a pragmatic alternative by focusing on disease-relevant genomic regions, thereby enabling deeper coverage, faster analysis, and reduced costs. Existing targeted panels for multiple myeloma, such as amplicon-based assays covering 47 to 77 genes, detect SNVs and CNVs but are limited in their ability to comprehensively capture structural variants like IGH and MYC translocations. Capture-based methods have shown promise in detecting IGH rearrangements, particularly for minimal residual disease monitoring or translocation discovery, but lack integration across all three mutation classes in a clinically scalable format. Thus, there remains a critical unmet need for a unified, capture-based sequencing platform specifically optimized for multiple myeloma that simultaneously and accurately detects SNVs, CNVs, and translocations—including both canonical and novel IGH and MYC rearrangements—with high sensitivity and specificity.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel capture-based next-generation sequencing platform specifically designed for comprehensive genomic profiling of multiple myeloma. This platform overcomes limitations of existing methods by integrating detection of single nucleotide variants (SNVs), copy number variations (CNVs), and chromosomal translocations—including those involving the immunoglobulin heavy chain (IGH) and MYC loci—within a single assay. A key advantage of this approach over whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing is its ability to deliver deep, uniform coverage of disease-relevant genomic regions at lower cost and with faster turnaround, making it suitable for clinical implementation. The platform enables the generation of personalized treatment plans by providing a complete mutational landscape that informs prognosis, therapeutic selection, and disease monitoring. Central to the invention is a custom-designed set of oligonucleotide probes that selectively enrich genomic regions of interest from tumor and matched normal DNA samples. These probes were meticulously selected based on stringent criteria: inclusion of genes recurrently mutated in multiple myeloma at a frequency exceeding 3% in published cohorts, genes annotated in cancer databases such as COSMIC or identified by MutSig as significantly mutated, genes involved in DNA repair or B-cell biology, and genes harboring clustered hotspot mutations. This rational gene selection process ensures maximal relevance to myeloma pathogenesis while maintaining a focused target space.

The discovery platform was engineered to serve dual purposes: clinical diagnostics and biological discovery. Unlike prior targeted panels that restrict probe placement to exonic regions or known breakpoints, the present platform employs a tiling strategy across entire genomic loci of interest. Specifically, probes are tiled across the full IGH locus—including variable (IGHV), diversity (IGHD), joining (IGHJ), constant, and switch regions—to enable unbiased detection of translocations regardless of breakpoint location. Additionally, exonic regions of canonical IGH translocation partners (CCND1, CCND3, FGFR3, MAF, MAFB, WHSC1, WWOX) are targeted to facilitate partner identification. Similarly, the MYC locus is comprehensively tiled across exonic and intronic regions to capture both intra- and inter-chromosomal rearrangements. The method involves preparing DNA sequencing libraries from both tumor (e.g., CD138+ bone marrow plasma cells) and non-tumor (e.g., peripheral blood) samples, hybridizing these libraries to the custom DNA capture array, and performing high-depth sequencing. The same steps are repeated for the matched normal sample to enable somatic variant calling. Bioinformatic analysis then identifies somatic mutations by comparing tumor and normal sequences. Variations of the method include alternative probe configurations, such as expanded coverage of regulatory elements or inclusion of additional cancer-associated genes.

The platform targets a total of 465 genes spanning approximately 3.3 megabases of genomic space, including the IGH and MYC loci. This gene set encompasses canonical drivers of myeloma, including KRAS, NRAS, TP53, RB1, FAM46C, DIS3, TRAF3, CYLD, and BRAF, as well as DNA repair genes such as ATM and BRCA2. It also includes B-cell signaling and differentiation genes like CLIP1, CSMD3, EP400, and notably IGLL5, which was discovered herein to be recurrently mutated and translocated in myeloma. The probe design ensures detection of at least 400 genes, with specific embodiments targeting exactly 465 or 467 genes, always remaining below 500 to balance comprehensiveness with cost-efficiency. The DNA capture array comprises biotinylated oligonucleotide probes synthesized using NimbleGen technology, each designed to hybridize specifically to complementary genomic sequences under stringent conditions. The array enables simultaneous detection of SNVs, focal and arm-level CNVs, and balanced translocations. In particular, probes tiled around the IGH locus allow detection of translocations involving any region of IGH, while probes targeting exonic regions of known partners aid in annotation. Probes across the MYC locus similarly support translocation detection. Additional genes covered include those involved in cell cycle regulation, epigenetic modification, and RNA processing, ensuring a holistic view of myeloma genomics. This precise gene configuration, validated across 95 patient samples, represents a significant advance over previous platforms that either omitted structural variant detection or covered fewer genes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention provides a DNA capture array comprising a plurality of oligonucleotide probes designed to hybridize to specific genomic regions associated with multiple myeloma pathogenesis. An oligonucleotide probe, as used herein, refers to a single-stranded DNA molecule of defined sequence, typically 60–120 nucleotides in length, synthesized to be complementary to a target genomic segment. The method of identifying multiple myeloma-associated mutations begins with the preparation of a DNA sequencing library from tumor cells, typically isolated from bone marrow aspirates via CD138+ selection to enrich for malignant plasma cells. Concurrently, a DNA sequencing library is prepared from non-tumor cells, usually peripheral blood mononuclear cells, serving as a germline control. The DNA capture array, containing the custom-designed oligonucleotide probes, is then provided. The tumor-derived sequencing library is hybridized to this array under conditions that promote specific binding between probes and complementary genomic fragments. Following hybridization, the captured library is sequenced to a maximum average depth of at least 100×, preferably between 100× and 150×, to ensure robust variant detection. The same hybridization and sequencing steps are performed on the non-tumor library to generate a matched normal dataset. Variants present in the tumor but absent in the normal sample are identified as somatic alterations.

This approach offers significant advantages over whole-exome sequencing, including higher on-target efficiency, deeper coverage at equivalent cost, and simplified bioinformatic analysis focused on clinically actionable genes. The method detects three major classes of genomic alterations: single nucleotide variants (including missense, nonsense, and splice-site mutations), copy number changes (ranging from focal deletions/amplifications to whole-chromosome aneuploidies), and balanced chromosomal translocations. Data generated from the array are analyzed using integrated computational pipelines to call each variant type, and the resulting mutational profile is used to assess prognosis and guide therapy selection—for instance, by identifying high-risk features such as del(17p), t(4;14), or biallelic inactivation of TP53. The platform may be combined with gene expression profiling to further refine risk stratification or identify druggable pathways. The custom capture design allows flexible updating of the gene panel as new myeloma drivers are discovered. Moreover, the method enables systematic investigation of patterns of mutual exclusivity and co-occurrence among mutations, which can reveal functional relationships and synthetic lethal interactions. For example, statistical testing can determine whether mutations in NRAS, KRAS, and IGLL5 occur less frequently together than expected by chance, suggesting they operate in the same oncogenic pathway. Similarly, the method can test whether hyperdiploidy and non-MYC IGH translocations are mutually exclusive, as observed in prior studies. Co-occurrence analyses may reveal synergistic relationships, such as between del(13q) and IGLL5 mutations or between DNA repair gene defects and specific CNAs. Pairwise statistical tests between any two specified genes or alteration types can be performed to uncover novel biological insights.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The custom capture sequencing platform was designed to target 3.3 megabases of genomic space encompassing 465 genes and the IGH locus. Probes were specifically designed for the IGH and MYC loci to enable comprehensive translocation detection. The IGH probe set tiles continuously across the entire locus, including V, D, J, constant, and switch regions, based on the hypothesis that endonucleolytic cleavage of free DNA ends during class switch recombination or V(D)J recombination can occur outside canonical segments, necessitating unbiased coverage. Probes were designed to lie entirely outside, entirely inside, or partially overlapping annotated genomic elements to maximize breakpoint capture. Libraries were constructed as automated dual-indexed Illumina-compatible libraries to enable multiplexing. Library pools were hybridized with a biotinylated NimbleGen probe set, and post-capture concentration was determined by quantitative PCR (qPCR) to ensure balanced sequencing. Sequencing was performed on HiSeq2000 or HiSeq2500 instruments, generating paired-end reads of 100 or 125 base pairs.

Bioinformatic analysis began with alignment of reads to the human reference genome GRCh37-lite using the Burrows-Wheeler Aligner (BWA). Single nucleotide variants were called using an ensemble approach combining samtools, SomaticSniper, MuTect, Strelka, and VarScan2, with consensus calls requiring support from multiple callers. Translocations were detected using LUMPY, a structural variant caller that integrates split-read and discordant read-pair evidence. To reduce false positives, a machine learning filter was developed: for IGH translocations, a support vector machine (SVM) was trained on available fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) data, with parameters tuned via grid search and validated on held-out test samples. For MYC translocations, where FISH validation data were unavailable, an SVM was trained to distinguish somatic events by filtering out translocations also present in matched normal samples. Copy number variants were called using CopyCAT2, parameterized with a Gaussian mixture model to estimate noise from diploid regions and set dynamic thresholds for calling gains and losses. Average depth and on-target efficiency were calculated using Genome Modeling System utilities, with per-base coverage assessed across the target space. On-target efficiency exceeded 80% at 20× depth in most samples.

CNV calls were annotated to distinguish focal events (<3 Mb) from arm-level or whole-chromosome alterations. Hyperdiploid status was inferred from the presence of trisomies in multiple odd-numbered chromosomes. Focal CNVs were annotated with affected genes using RefSeq annotations. Translocation calls were parsed to extract events involving IGH or MYC, and each breakpoint was annotated with the nearest cancer-associated gene within 1 Mb. Novel translocations, such as t(14;22) involving IGLL5, were validated experimentally: genomic DNA was subjected to PCR using primers flanking the predicted breakpoint, products were separated by electrophoresis, and Sanger sequencing confirmed the fusion junction. Breakpoint coordinates for IGH switch and enhancer regions were defined using UCSC Genome Browser and validated via BLAT alignment. Mutual exclusivity and co-occurrence analyses were performed using Fisher’s exact test, excluding hypermutator samples to avoid bias. Survival analysis employed Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests to associate mutations like IGLL5 with progression-free survival. RNA-seq data were integrated to assess overexpression of genes near translocation breakpoints, such as DERL3 in t(14;22) cases. Genes for inclusion on the panel were filtered based on expression in myeloma, mutation frequency, and functional relevance, ensuring clinical and biological utility.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The oligonucleotide probe array was designed for a multiple myeloma-specific custom capture sequencing platform targeting 465 genes and the IGH locus across 3.3 Mb of genomic space. The probe set enabled simultaneous detection of copy number variations (CNVs), single nucleotide variants (SNVs), and chromosomal translocations in multiple myeloma samples. Probes were tiled across the entire IGH locus, including V, D, J, constant, and switch regions, and across exonic regions of canonical translocation partners. The MYC locus was similarly tiled to capture secondary translocations. This design facilitated comprehensive genomic profiling in a single assay.

### Example 2

Ninety-five paired tumor and normal cell DNA samples were sequenced using the custom capture platform. Tumor DNA was derived from CD138+ plasma cells isolated from bone marrow aspirates, and normal DNA from peripheral blood. The cohort included samples previously analyzed by exome sequencing (n=44) and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) for IGH translocations (n=22), enabling direct validation of the platform’s accuracy. Mean sequencing depth exceeded 100× in both tumor and normal samples, ensuring high sensitivity for variant detection.

### Example 3

Copy number alterations with established prognostic significance were reliably identified, including chromosome-level hyperdiploidy, arm-level events such as amp(1q) and del(13q), and focal deletions like homozygous loss of BRCA2. CNV calls were filtered using a noise threshold derived from diploid regions to minimize false positives. Somatic mutations were detected in all tumor samples, with a mean of 20 non-silent variants per sample. Deleterious mutations were predicted using PolyPhen-2 and SIFT algorithms. Multi-hit genes exhibiting bi-allelic inactivation, such as TP53 and RB1, were identified, highlighting potential therapeutic vulnerabilities.

### Example 4

IGH translocations were detected using the LUMPY structural variant caller. Putative translocations were filtered using thresholds on split-read and discordant read-pair support, further refined by a support vector machine (SVM) trained on FISH validation data. This achieved 100% precision and 64% recall for canonical translocations, including t(11;14), t(4;14), and t(14;16), at frequencies consistent with literature. Translocations were validated using PCR amplification across breakpoint junctions followed by Sanger sequencing, confirming reciprocal rearrangements.

### Example 5

Novel IGH translocations were prioritized as potential driver events by identifying cancer-associated genes within 1 Mb of breakpoints. A t(14;22)(q32.33;q11.22) translocation involving IGLL5 was validated by PCR and sequencing. RNA-seq analysis revealed DERL3 overexpression in samples with putative t(14;22), suggesting enhancer hijacking. Breakpoints on derivative chromosome 14 were mapped relative to IGH enhancers and switch regions. Additional IGLL5 translocations were sought by relaxing filters, and DERL3 expression was assessed across a cohort of 84 myeloma patients, confirming outlier expression in translocation-positive cases.

### Example 6

Intra- and inter-chromosomal MYC translocations were detected in 6 of 95 samples (6%). Due to high false-positive rates in normal samples, an SVM filter was trained to remove germline or artifactual events. Validated somatic translocations included rearrangements with PVT1 and POU5F1B, consistent with known MYC amplicon structure. Filtering using the SVM significantly improved specificity, enabling confident detection of secondary MYC-driven events.

### Example 7

Non-silent single nucleotide variants were identified in all 95 tumor samples. Each sample harbored a median of 20 coding mutations, with 94 samples containing at least one variant predicted deleterious by PolyPhen-2 or SIFT. Frequently mutated genes included KRAS, NRAS, IGLL5, and FAM46C. COSMIC-annotated mutations were observed in known oncogenes, confirming the platform’s ability to detect clinically relevant alterations.

### Example 8

To assess the prevalence of deeply subclonal variants, 15 tumor-normal pairs underwent deep sequencing (mean depth >1,200×). Comparison with initial sequencing (mean depth ~100×) showed high correlation in variant allele frequencies (R²=0.80) for shared variants. Few additional high-confidence variants were discovered at greater depth, and most unique variants had low allele frequencies (<10%). Downsampling experiments confirmed that depths beyond 300× yield diminishing returns, supporting the use of ~100× depth for clinical applications.

### Example 9

Integrative analysis across mutation types revealed significant patterns of mutual exclusivity and co-occurrence. Hyperdiploidy was mutually exclusive with t(11;14) (p<0.05). IGLL5 mutations were mutually exclusive with RAS mutations (p=0.006) and enriched for activation-induced cytidine deaminase (AID) mutational signatures (p=2.7×10⁻⁶). IGLL5 mutations were associated with shorter progression-free survival (hazard ratio=1.46, p=0.03). Co-occurrence was observed between del(13q) and amp(1q), and between del(14q) and t(4;14). These findings demonstrate the platform’s utility in uncovering biologically and clinically relevant genomic interactions.
