# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Vaccines represent one of the most effective public health interventions in human history, having significantly reduced morbidity and mortality from infectious diseases worldwide. Traditional vaccine administration routes, such as intramuscular or subcutaneous injection, have proven efficacious for eliciting systemic immune responses but often fail to induce robust mucosal immunity, which is critical for protection against pathogens that enter the body through mucosal surfaces—such as the respiratory, gastrointestinal, or genitourinary tracts. Mucosal vaccination offers a compelling alternative by potentially stimulating both local and systemic immune responses, thereby providing a first line of defense at the site of pathogen entry. However, the development of effective mucosal vaccines has been hampered by significant challenges related to delivery, stability, and immunogenicity.

A major limitation of current vaccine delivery methods lies in the hostile environment of the gastrointestinal tract. Oral administration, while highly desirable due to its non-invasive nature, patient compliance, and potential for mass distribution, confronts numerous physiological barriers. The acidic milieu of the stomach can rapidly degrade protein-based antigens or viral vectors before they reach the intestinal immune inductive sites. Furthermore, enzymatic degradation in the upper small intestine, coupled with rapid transit and dilution effects, often results in insufficient antigen exposure to gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT), particularly Peyer’s patches and mesenteric lymph nodes, which are essential for initiating adaptive immune responses. Conventional oral formulations lack the precision to target specific regions of the intestine where immune induction is most efficient, leading to inconsistent or suboptimal immunogenicity.

Additionally, existing oral vaccine platforms often rely on live-attenuated or replication-competent vectors, which raise safety concerns, especially in immunocompromised individuals. Non-replicating vectors, such as recombinant adenoviruses, offer a safer alternative but require precise delivery to immune-rich regions of the gut to overcome their lower intrinsic immunogenicity compared to replicating systems. Without targeted release, these vectors may be degraded or fail to engage sufficient antigen-presenting cells, resulting in weak or undetectable immune responses. Moreover, traditional enteric coatings, while designed to resist gastric acid, often disintegrate prematurely in the duodenum or jejunum, missing the more immunologically active ileum, where Peyer’s patches are denser and dendritic cell populations are optimized for antigen sampling and presentation.

These limitations underscore the need for an advanced oral vaccine delivery system capable of protecting the immunogenic payload through the stomach, precisely releasing it in the ileum—the region of the small intestine with the highest concentration of inductive immune structures—and enhancing the magnitude and quality of both systemic and mucosal immune responses. The invention described herein addresses these unmet needs by integrating a recombinant adenoviral vector encoding a viral antigen with a double-stranded RNA adjuvant, formulated within a pH-dependent enteric-coated tablet or a radio-controlled capsule engineered for site-specific release in the ileum, thereby maximizing immune induction while maintaining safety and manufacturability.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel immunogenic compositions and methods for their delivery to the human ileum to elicit robust systemic and mucosal immune responses. The immunogenic compositions comprise an immunogenic biological agent, which may include a recombinant adenoviral vector encoding a heterologous antigen, such as influenza hemagglutinin (HA), operably linked to a promoter and co-formulated with double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) to enhance immunogenicity. The expression vector is replication-incompetent, ensuring safety while retaining the capacity to transduce antigen-presenting cells in the gut-associated lymphoid tissue. The dsRNA functions as a Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR-3) agonist, acting as a potent adjuvant to stimulate innate immune pathways and amplify adaptive responses.

These immunogenic compositions are formulated for oral delivery using advanced delivery systems designed to target the ileum. In one embodiment, the composition is encapsulated in a tablet or capsule coated with an enteric coating that remains intact in the acidic environment of the stomach and the proximal small intestine but disintegrates at a pH characteristic of the distal small intestine, specifically the ileum (pH ≥ 6.8). The enteric coating comprises pH-sensitive polymers such as methacrylic acid copolymers (e.g., Eudragit L100 or S100), cellulose acetate phthalate, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate, or polyvinyl acetate phthalate, applied in a thickness ranging from 50 to 200 micrometers to ensure controlled disintegration kinetics. In another embodiment, the composition is housed within a radio-controlled capsule equipped with a telemetry system that allows real-time monitoring of its position via gamma scintigraphy and remote triggering of content release upon reaching the ileum.

The delivery system may also incorporate microparticles or nanoparticles to further protect the immunogenic agent and facilitate uptake by M cells overlying Peyer’s patches. Adjuvants, including lipid-based (e.g., monophosphoryl lipid A) or non-lipid TLR agonists (e.g., poly(I:C) as a dsRNA analog), are included to enhance dendritic cell maturation and cytokine production, thereby promoting T-cell priming and B-cell differentiation. Upon ileal delivery, the composition is released, allowing the adenoviral vector to transduce local antigen-presenting cells, which then present the encoded antigen and initiate a coordinated humoral and cell-mediated immune response.

This targeted delivery results in significantly increased immune responses compared to non-directed oral delivery or delivery to the jejunum. Specifically, the invention elicits higher frequencies of antigen-specific antibody-secreting cells (ASCs), elevated serum hemagglutination inhibition (HAI) and microneutralizing (MN) antibody titers, and robust mucosal IgA responses in nasal and fecal secretions. Critically, it induces a population of α4β7<sup>high</sup> CCR9<sup>+</sup> IgA<sup>+</sup> B cells in peripheral blood, which are programmed to home to the intestinal lamina propria, thereby establishing long-term mucosal immunity. The method enables effective immunization against influenza and other mucosal pathogens through a simple, needle-free oral tablet, offering a scalable, stable, and patient-friendly alternative to injectable vaccines.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

As used herein, the term “immunogenic” refers to the ability of a substance to provoke an adaptive immune response in a subject. A “biological agent” encompasses any molecule derived from a biological source, including proteins, polypeptides, peptides, carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids, or combinations thereof. An “immunogenic biological agent” is a biological agent capable of inducing an immune response, either alone or in combination with an adjuvant. An “antigen” is a specific molecular structure recognized by the immune system, typically a portion of a pathogen or tumor cell that elicits antibody or T-cell responses. An “immunogenically effective dose” is the amount of an immunogenic agent required to elicit a measurable adaptive immune response in a subject.

“Humoral immune responses” refer to antibody-mediated immunity involving B cells and secreted immunoglobulins, whereas “cell-mediated immune responses” involve T lymphocytes, including CD4<sup>+</sup> helper and CD8<sup>+</sup> cytotoxic T cells. The “ileum” is the final and longest segment of the small intestine, extending from the jejunum to the ileocecal valve, and is characterized by a high density of Peyer’s patches and gut-associated lymphoid tissue. An “enteric coating” is a polymer layer applied to a pharmaceutical dosage form that resists dissolution in the acidic environment of the stomach but dissolves at higher pH levels found in the small intestine. “Disintegrate” means the physical breakdown of a solid dosage form into smaller particles or complete dissolution in the gastrointestinal fluid.

“Chimeric or recombinant” describes molecules or vectors engineered by combining genetic material from different sources. An “expression vector” is a DNA construct used to introduce and express a gene of interest in a host cell. A “promoter” is a nucleic acid sequence that initiates transcription of a downstream gene, and “operably linked” means that a promoter is positioned relative to a coding sequence such that it directs its transcription. “Heterologous” refers to a nucleic acid or protein sequence not naturally associated with the host cell or vector backbone. A “nucleic acid” is a polymer of nucleotides, which may be DNA or RNA, and a “nucleotide” is a monomeric unit comprising a sugar, phosphate, and nitrogenous base.

“Conservatively modified variants” of nucleic acids or amino acid sequences are those that do not significantly alter the function or structure of the original molecule; for amino acids, this includes substitutions with residues of similar physicochemical properties (e.g., leucine for isoleucine). A “therapeutic dose” is the amount of an agent required to achieve a desired clinical effect. An “antibody” is an immunoglobulin produced by B cells that specifically binds an antigen. “T cells” are lymphocytes involved in cell-mediated immunity. The “adaptive immune response” is the antigen-specific defense mechanism involving memory and effector cells. “Antigen presenting cells” (APCs) include dendritic cells, macrophages, and B cells that process and present antigens to T cells.

An “adjuvant” is a substance that enhances the immune response to an antigen. “Polypeptide,” “peptide,” and “protein” are used interchangeably to denote chains of amino acids, with “polypeptide” typically referring to longer chains. An “amino acid” is a building block of proteins, and “amino acid analogs” or “mimetics” are synthetic or natural compounds that mimic the structure or function of natural amino acids. “Selectively hybridize to” describes nucleic acid strands that bind under stringent conditions to a complementary sequence. “Polynucleotide variants” include insertions, deletions, or substitutions that retain biological activity. “Identical or percent identity” refers to the degree of sequence similarity between two nucleic acids or proteins.

A “control” is a reference sample used for comparison in experiments. “Diagnosis,” “prognosis,” “therapy,” “treatment,” and “amelioration” refer to the identification, prediction, management, intervention, or improvement of a disease state. “Subject,” “patient,” and “individual” denote a human or animal recipient of the invention. Examples of conservatively modified variants include substitutions such as aspartic acid for glutamic acid (both acidic), lysine for arginine (both basic), or valine for alanine (both hydrophobic). Common amino acid substitutions preserving charge, hydrophobicity, or size are well known in the art and are encompassed by the invention.

### II. Immunogenic Biological Agents

Immunogenic biological agents suitable for use in the invention include polypeptides (e.g., viral surface proteins like influenza HA, HIV gp120, SARS-CoV-2 spike), carbohydrates (e.g., bacterial capsular polysaccharides), lipids (e.g., mycobacterial cord factor), and polynucleotides (e.g., DNA or RNA vaccines). These agents may elicit direct immune responses by being recognized by B-cell receptors or indirect responses when processed and presented by APCs to T cells. Administration may be oral, intranasal, or transdermal, but the invention focuses on oral delivery to the ileum.

Expression vectors, particularly virally-derived vectors such as adenoviral, adeno-associated viral, or poxviral vectors, are preferred. These are rendered replication-incompetent by deletion of essential genes (e.g., E1/E3 in adenovirus), ensuring safety. Adenoviral vectors, such as human serotype 5 (Ad5), are exemplified due to their high transduction efficiency in epithelial and dendritic cells. Transcriptional control sequences include viral promoters (e.g., CMV immediate-early promoter, SV40 promoter) or inducible promoters to regulate antigen expression.

Immunogens include antigens from viruses (e.g., influenza, RSV, norovirus), bacteria (e.g., *Salmonella*, *E. coli*), parasites (e.g., *Plasmodium*), fungi (e.g., *Candida*), and cancer (e.g., MUC1, PSA). Adjuvants enhance immunogenicity: lipid adjuvants include squalene emulsions (MF59) or TLR4 agonists (MPLA); non-lipid adjuvants include aluminum salts, CpG oligonucleotides (TLR9 agonist), or dsRNA (TLR3 agonist, e.g., poly(I:C)). The dsRNA component is critical, as it mimics viral infection, activating interferon pathways and promoting Th1-biased responses essential for antiviral immunity.

### III. Targeted Delivery Systems

Enteric coatings are essential for ileal targeting. pH-sensitive polymers such as methacrylic acid-methyl methacrylate copolymers (Eudragit L100, dissolving at pH ≥ 6.0; Eudragit S100, dissolving at pH ≥ 7.0) are used alone or in combination with biodegradable polymers (e.g., polylactic-co-glycolic acid) to fine-tune release kinetics. Combination polymers, such as Eudragit L100/S100 blends, allow disintegration specifically in the ileum (pH 6.8–7.4). Coating thickness (50–200 µm) controls the time to disintegration, preventing premature release in the jejunum (pH ~6.0–6.5).

Osmotic-controlled release oral delivery systems (OROS) may be incorporated to provide sustained release post-disintegration. Components may include water-soluble pore formers (e.g., PEG) or enzymatically degradable linkers to respond to ileal-specific enzymes (e.g., bile salt hydrolases). The therapeutic composition is formed into a matrix with excipients (e.g., microcrystalline cellulose, sucrose) to stabilize the adenoviral vector.

Radio-controlled capsules (RCCs) represent an alternative delivery method. Early models used mechanical valves triggered by external radio signals; updated versions integrate radiolabeling (e.g., <sup>99m</sup>Tc) for real-time tracking via gamma scintigraphy. Upon confirmation of ileal positioning, a signal triggers capsule opening, releasing the liquid vaccine formulation. This technology validates site-specific efficacy and informs enteric coating design.

Formulations are suitable for both prophylactic and therapeutic purposes. Pharmaceutical compositions include tablets, capsules, or microparticles containing 1×10<sup>10</sup> to 1×10<sup>12</sup> infectious units of adenoviral vector, 1–100 µg of dsRNA, and standard excipients.

### IV. Immune Responses and Vaccines

The pharmaceutical compositions are designed to elicit comprehensive immune responses. As vaccines, they prevent or treat infections caused by influenza, RSV, norovirus, HIV, or *Helicobacter pylori*. Prior diagnosis via PCR or serology may guide administration. Active immunotherapy involves repeated dosing to boost memory responses. Administration frequency ranges from single to multi-dose regimens, with doses spaced 2–8 weeks apart.

Immune responses are monitored via standard assays: ELISA for total IgG/IgA, HAI and MN for functional antibodies, ELISPOT for ASCs, and flow cytometry for homing receptors (α4β7, CCR9). Detection of immunocomplexes (e.g., antigen-antibody complexes in serum or mucosa) indicates therapeutic efficacy. Evaluation includes geometric mean titers (GMT), fold-rise in titers, and responder rates (e.g., ≥4-fold increase in HAI titer).

### V. Examples

Oral vaccine delivery to the intestine is motivated by the need for needle-free, scalable immunization. Current methods suffer from poor stability and inconsistent targeting. Radio-controlled capsules (RCCs) were used to compare jejunum vs. ileum delivery of a recombinant adenovirus (rAd) encoding influenza HA and dsRNA. In Example 1, 24 subjects received 1×10<sup>11</sup> IU of rAd-HA-dsRNA in liquid form via RCC. Gamma scintigraphy confirmed release in jejunum (n=12) or ileum (n=12). ASC assays on day 7 showed significantly higher IgG ASCs in the ileum group (median 200 vs. 111 per 10<sup>6</sup> PBMCs, p=0.023). T-cell responses, though not detailed, were inferred from B-cell activation. MN titers showed 11/12 responders in ileum vs. 7/12 in jejunum.

Example 2 describes a tablet formulation with Eudragit S100 enteric coating (150 µm thick). In vitro dissolution testing confirmed disintegration only at pH ≥ 7.0, mimicking ileal conditions. Performance matched RCC delivery in preclinical models.

Example 3 details a clinical study using GMP-grade rAd-HA-dsRNA tablets. Manufacturing involved dialysis and concentration in sucrose buffer, followed by compression into tablets with enteric coating. ASC responses mirrored RCC data, with 100% responder rates in the ileum-targeted group. Neutralizing antibodies showed >5-fold GMT increase post-vaccination.

Example 4 presents in vitro dissolution testing across pH 1.2–7.4. Disintegration time was >2 hours at pH ≤6.5 but <30 minutes at pH 7.0, confirming ileal specificity.

Example 5 reports a Phase 1 clinical trial (NCT01761123). HAI responses showed 67% seroconversion in ileum-delivered subjects, comparable to injectable vaccines. Durability was assessed at 6 months, with sustained MN titers (GMT >80). Geometric mean titers for HAI reached 1:80 in responders. Results confirmed superior immunogenicity of ileal delivery.

In discussion, antibody responses were measured via ELISA EC50, with 12/12 ileum subjects showing >1.3-fold rise. Compared to historical studies, ileal delivery induced stronger mucosal homing (α4β7<sup>high</sup> B cells) and fecal IgA. Tablet stability was confirmed under ICH conditions (25°C/60% RH for 12 months). MN titers correlated with protection in challenge models. HAI durability exceeded 6 months, suggesting long-lived plasma cells. Materials and methods included IRB-approved protocols, randomization, GMP vaccine composition, PBMC isolation, and statistical analysis (Mann-Whitney, Wilcoxon). Endpoints were ASC frequency, HAI/MN titers, and homing receptor expression. The data conclusively demonstrate that ileum-targeted oral delivery of rAd-HA-dsRNA elicits robust, durable, and mucosally targeted immunity, validating the invention’s utility.
