# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glass-forming alloys. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel quaternary and optionally higher-order Zr-Co-Ni-Al-based metallic glasses exhibiting a synergistic combination of high glass-forming ability, superior mechanical toughness, and controlled optical appearance suitable for cosmetic and luxury applications. These alloys are engineered to possess critical plate thicknesses exceeding conventional limits while simultaneously achieving specific CIELAB color coordinates that align with commercial aesthetic requirements, particularly in consumer electronics, wearable devices, and high-end decorative components. The invention further encompasses methods for producing these metallic glasses through controlled thermal processing and thermoplastic forming techniques that preserve both structural integrity and desired surface characteristics.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Prior art Zr-Co-Ni-Al alloys have been investigated primarily for their potential as bulk metallic glasses (BMGs) due to the favorable atomic size mismatch and negative heat of mixing among constituent elements, which promote amorphous phase formation upon rapid solidification. Early studies identified compositional windows within the Zr-Co-Ni-Al system capable of forming glasses with critical rod diameters up to 3–4 mm, typically centered around eutectic compositions such as Zr57Co15Ni8Al20 or similar variants. These alloys demonstrated high strength, elastic strain limits, and corrosion resistance, making them attractive for structural applications. However, their utility has been constrained by several interrelated limitations that hinder broader adoption, especially in markets where aesthetics and user experience are paramount.

A primary limitation of prior art Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glasses is their inherently dull, grayish appearance, which lacks the luster and chromatic richness demanded in consumer-facing products. This poor optical quality stems from high surface roughness after casting, strong light scattering due to internal heterogeneities, and intrinsic electronic structure that results in low reflectivity. Attempts to improve surface finish through polishing or coating often compromise the corrosion resistance or introduce delamination risks. Furthermore, the color of these alloys, when quantified using the CIELAB color space—a standardized perceptual color model—typically exhibits low L* values (indicating low luminance or reflectivity), near-zero a* values (neutral red-green balance), and slightly negative b* values (slight bluish tint), collectively yielding an unappealing "industrial" look unsuitable for premium goods.

Another significant drawback lies in the mechanical brittleness of monolithic Zr-based BMGs. While they exhibit high compressive strength, their notch toughness—the resistance to crack propagation under stress concentration—is often insufficient for applications involving impact or cyclic loading. Traditional strategies to enhance toughness, such as introducing ductile crystalline phases to form metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs), have proven challenging in the Zr-Co-Ni-Al system due to the tendency for competing intermetallic phases to nucleate during cooling, disrupting the desired microstructure. Moreover, even when MGMCs are successfully formed, the resulting color becomes mottled or non-uniform if the secondary phase features exceed the resolution limit of the human eye (~20–30 μm), rendering the material visually unacceptable for cosmetic use.

Additionally, the processing window for these alloys remains narrow. Achieving full amorphization requires cooling rates that are difficult to maintain in sections thicker than a few millimeters, limiting part geometry and scalability. Thermoplastic forming above the glass transition temperature (Tg) offers a route to net-shape manufacturing, but many prior compositions exhibit high viscosity or narrow supercooled liquid regions (ΔT = Tx – Tg), leading to incomplete filling or premature crystallization during molding. Consequently, there exists a long-felt but unresolved need for Zr-Co-Ni-Al-based metallic glasses that simultaneously satisfy stringent criteria for glass-forming ability, mechanical robustness, and visual appeal—properties that have historically been considered mutually exclusive.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention introduces novel Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glass-forming alloys that overcome the aforementioned limitations by precisely tailoring composition to achieve a unique triad of properties: enhanced glass-forming ability, high notch toughness, and controlled optical appearance characterized by specific CIELAB color coordinates. These alloys are defined by the general formula Zr_aCo_bNi_cAl_d, wherein the atomic percentages a, b, c, and d are constrained within empirically determined ranges that enable the concurrent optimization of structural and aesthetic performance.

Specifically, the alloy composition satisfies 50 ≤ a ≤ 60, with the subrange 52 ≤ a ≤ 58 being particularly effective when combined with a critical plate thickness of at least 2.0 mm. The cobalt content b ranges from 8 to 18, and more preferably from 10 to 16 when the CIELAB L* value exceeds 75, ensuring high surface reflectivity. The nickel content c is maintained between 5 and 15, and optimally between 7 and 12 when the CIELAB b* value is greater than −2.0, thereby avoiding undesirable bluish tones. The aluminum content d falls within 15 to 25, with refined subranges offering synergistic benefits: 18 ≤ d ≤ 22 when L* > 75; 17 ≤ d ≤ 23 when notch toughness exceeds 25 MPa·m^1/2; 16 ≤ d ≤ 24 when critical plate thickness ≥ 2.0 mm; and most advantageously, 19 ≤ d ≤ 21 when all three conditions—L* > 75, notch toughness > 25 MPa·m^1/2, and critical plate thickness ≥ 2.0 mm—are simultaneously satisfied.

Optional elements such as Cu (0.5–3 at.%), Nb (0.1–2 at.%), or Ti (0.5–4 at.%) may be incorporated to further refine glass stability or color without degrading key properties. The alloys are formed into metallic glasses via rapid solidification techniques, including melt spinning, copper mold casting, or suction casting, followed by optional thermoplastic shaping. In the latter process, the amorphous alloy is heated to a temperature To within its supercooled liquid region, typically 50–100°C above Tg, where its viscosity ranges from 10^3 to 10^6 Pa·s, enabling deformation under modest forces. Heating may be accomplished through induction, resistance, or infrared methods, and the shaped article is subsequently cooled below Tg to retain the amorphous structure.

Exemplary compositions include Zr55Co12Ni10Al23, Zr57Co14Ni8Al21, and Zr53Co16Ni12Al19, each demonstrating critical plate thicknesses of 2.5 mm or greater, L* values above 78, b* values between −1.5 and +1.0, and notch toughness exceeding 28 MPa·m^1/2. In summary, the invention provides a new class of Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glasses that reconcile engineering performance with cosmetic desirability, enabling their use in applications previously inaccessible to amorphous metals.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present disclosure introduces a family of Zr-Co-Ni-Al-based metallic glass-forming alloys engineered to exhibit an unprecedented combination of high glass-forming ability, mechanical toughness, and aesthetically pleasing optical properties. For clarity and illustrative purposes, the following description details specific embodiments, though it is understood that the scope of the invention extends beyond these examples.

Critical plate thickness, denoted t_c, is defined as the maximum thickness of a plate-shaped sample that can be fully amorphized under a given cooling condition, typically measured via copper mold casting with water cooling. It serves as a practical metric for glass-forming ability (GFA), which is intrinsically linked to the critical cooling rate (R_c)—the minimum cooling rate required to bypass crystallization during solidification. Lower R_c corresponds to higher GFA and thus larger t_c. Metallic glass-forming alloys are broadly categorized as non-glass formers (t_c < 0.1 mm), marginal glass formers (0.1 ≤ t_c < 1 mm), and bulk glass formers (t_c ≥ 1 mm). The present alloys fall decisively into the bulk category.

Zr-based systems are among the most studied BMG families due to Zr’s large atomic radius and strong affinity for metalloids like Al. Prior Zr-Co-Ni-Al alloys exhibited moderate GFA but suffered from poor color and brittleness. The invention overcomes this by identifying compositional regions where Co and Ni concentrations balance electronic and topological effects to suppress nucleation while Al content is tuned to modify both atomic packing and surface oxide characteristics.

Critical rod diameter (d_c) is another GFA metric, related to t_c by geometric considerations; for similar thermal boundary conditions, t_c ≈ 0.7·d_c. Thus, a t_c of 2.5 mm implies d_c ≈ 3.6 mm.

Color is quantified using the CIELAB coordinate system, a perceptually uniform color space where L* represents lightness (0 = black, 100 = white), a* denotes red-green axis (+a* = red, −a* = green), and b* indicates yellow-blue axis (+b* = yellow, −b* = blue). In luxury goods, high L* (>75) conveys brightness and premium feel, while b* near zero avoids cold (bluish) or overly warm (yellowish) casts. Traditional Zr-BMGs exhibit L* ≈ 60–70 and b* ≈ −3 to −5, appearing dull and gray-blue.

The disclosed alloys achieve L* ≥ 75 and b* ≥ −2.0 through precise Al control, which influences native oxide thickness and refractive index. Notch toughness, measured via three-point bend tests on notched specimens, exceeds 25 MPa·m^1/2—significantly higher than typical Zr-BMGs (<20 MPa·m^1/2)—due to nanoscale chemical fluctuations that impede shear band propagation.

Processing involves arc melting pure elements (Zr ≥ 99.9, Co ≥ 99.9, Ni ≥ 99.95, Al ≥ 99.99 wt.%) under argon to form ingots, followed by re-melting and injection into copper molds to produce plates or rods. Thermoplastic forming heats samples to To = Tg + 60°C, where viscosity η ≈ 10^4 Pa·s, allowing deformation under <10 MPa pressure, then rapid cooling.

Three exemplary alloys are presented:

**First Composition**: Zr55Co12Ni10Al23  
- t_c = 2.8 mm  
- DSC shows Tg = 410°C, Tx = 470°C (ΔT = 60°C)  
- CIELAB: L* = 79.2, a* = −0.8, b* = −1.2  
- Notch toughness = 29 MPa·m^1/2  

**Second Composition**: Zr57Co14Ni8Al21  
- t_c = 3.1 mm  
- Tg = 405°C, Tx = 465°C  
- L* = 80.5, a* = −0.5, b* = −0.9  
- Toughness = 31 MPa·m^1/2  

**Third Composition**: Zr53Co16Ni12Al19  
- t_c = 2.5 mm  
- Tg = 415°C, Tx = 475°C  
- L* = 78.1, a* = −1.1, b* = −1.5  
- Toughness = 27 MPa·m^1/2  

All exhibit fully amorphous XRD patterns and homogeneous surfaces. The data confirm that strategic adjustment of d (Al) within 19–23 at.% enables simultaneous optimization of t_c, L*, and toughness.

### Methods of Processing the Alloy Ingots of the Sample Metallic Glass-Forming Alloys

Alloy ingots were prepared using a water-cooled copper hearth arc melter under high-purity argon atmosphere (oxygen < 1 ppm). Stoichiometric amounts of elemental Zr (99.9 wt.%), Co (99.9 wt.%), Ni (99.95 wt.%), and Al (99.99 wt.%) were weighed and loaded into the melter. The mixture was melted repeatedly (at least five times) with flipping between melts to ensure homogeneity. The resulting button ingot was approximately 50 mm in diameter and 10 mm thick.

### Methods of Processing the Sample Metallic Glass Plates

Metallic glass plates were fabricated via the melt-and-pour technique. The arc-melted ingot was placed in a quartz crucible and inductively heated to 1100°C under argon flow. The molten alloy was then injected under 0.2 MPa argon pressure into a water-cooled copper mold with rectangular cavities (20 mm × 20 mm × t, where t = 1–3 mm). Rapid solidification yielded fully amorphous plates with mirror-like as-cast surfaces.

### Method for Measuring the CIELAB Color Coordinates

CIELAB color coordinates were measured using a Konica Minolta CM-700d spectrophotometer equipped with an 8-mm measurement aperture and D65 illuminant (daylight). Measurements were taken at four corners of each 20 mm × 20 mm plate, and the average L*, a*, and b* values were reported. Samples were cleaned with ethanol prior to measurement to remove surface contaminants.

### Method for Performing Differential Scanning Calorimetry

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) was conducted on a TA Instruments Q2000 calorimeter under high-purity nitrogen flow (50 mL/min). Samples weighing 10–15 mg were heated at a constant rate of 20 K/min from room temperature to 600°C. The glass transition temperature (Tg) was identified as the onset of the endothermic step change in heat flow, while the crystallization temperature (Tx) was taken as the peak of the exothermic crystallization event.
