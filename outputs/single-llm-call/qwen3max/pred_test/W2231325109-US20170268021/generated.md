# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of regenerative medicine and cellular reprogramming, specifically relating to methods and compositions for the direct transdifferentiation of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic precursor (iDP) cells. These iDP cells are neural progenitor-like cells that are lineage-restricted to differentiate exclusively into functional midbrain dopaminergic neurons, without concomitant generation of glial or non-dopaminergic neuronal subtypes. The invention further encompasses therapeutic applications of such iDP cells in the treatment of neurodegenerative disorders, particularly Parkinson’s disease, as well as their use in drug screening, disease modeling, and mechanistic studies of dopaminergic neuron development and pathology. The core technological advancement disclosed herein involves the ectopic expression of a defined combination of transcription factors—Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2—in somatic cells, such as dermal fibroblasts, to directly convert them into expandable, transplantable, and functionally competent dopaminergic precursors that exhibit molecular, morphological, and electrophysiological hallmarks of authentic midbrain dopaminergic progenitors.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Neurodegenerative disorders, including Parkinson’s disease (PD), Alzheimer’s disease, Huntington’s disease, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, are characterized by the progressive loss of specific neuronal populations, leading to debilitating motor, cognitive, and behavioral impairments. Among these, PD is primarily caused by the selective degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta, resulting in dopamine deficiency in the striatum and consequent motor dysfunction such as bradykinesia, rigidity, resting tremor, and postural instability. Current pharmacological treatments, such as levodopa administration, provide symptomatic relief but do not halt disease progression and often lose efficacy over time while inducing dyskinesias. Cell replacement therapy has long been envisioned as a potential curative strategy by restoring lost dopaminergic circuitry through transplantation of healthy, functional dopaminergic neurons.

Stem cell biology has undergone transformative breakthroughs in recent decades, enabling the derivation of patient-specific pluripotent stem cells via nuclear reprogramming (e.g., induced pluripotent stem cells or iPSCs) and the directed differentiation of embryonic stem cells (ESCs) or iPSCs into dopaminergic neurons. However, these approaches face significant challenges, including tumorigenic risk from residual undifferentiated pluripotent cells, heterogeneity in differentiated cell populations, low yield of authentic midbrain dopaminergic neurons, and ethical concerns associated with ESC use. Direct reprogramming, or transdifferentiation, offers an alternative paradigm by converting one differentiated somatic cell type directly into another without passing through a pluripotent intermediate state, thereby potentially circumventing safety and efficiency issues inherent in pluripotent-based strategies.

Despite progress in generating induced neurons or induced neural progenitor cells (iNPCs) from fibroblasts using transcription factors such as Brn2, Sox2, and others, a critical limitation remains: the resulting cells often lack regional identity and subtype specificity. For instance, prior iNPCs derived from fibroblasts exhibit broad neural potential but inefficiently differentiate into midbrain dopaminergic neurons even when exposed to patterning morphogens like Sonic Hedgehog (SHH) and Fibroblast Growth Factor 8 (FGF8). This inefficiency stems from incomplete epigenetic remodeling and absence of key fate-determining transcription factors that establish midbrain dopaminergic identity during embryogenesis. Foxa2 and Lmx1a are master regulators of midbrain dopaminergic neuron development, acting downstream of SHH and Wnt signaling, respectively, to specify floor plate-derived dopaminergic progenitors. While their roles in development are well-established, their utility in direct reprogramming has been underexplored, and no method has yet achieved the generation of dopaminergic-restricted precursors that bypass the need for exogenous morphogen supplementation.

Thus, there exists a pressing need for a robust, safe, and efficient method to generate dopaminergic precursors that are intrinsically programmed for midbrain identity, capable of high-yield terminal differentiation into functional dopaminergic neurons, amenable to controlled expansion, and suitable for autologous transplantation. The present invention addresses this unmet need by identifying a minimal set of transcription factors—Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2—that collectively reprogram somatic cells into induced dopaminergic precursors (iDPs) with these desirable properties.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides an induced dopaminergic precursor (iDP) cell generated by the direct transdifferentiation of a somatic cell through ectopic expression of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. The iDP composition comprises a population of neural progenitor-like cells that express canonical neural progenitor markers such as Nestin, Sox1, and CD133, as well as midbrain dopaminergic progenitor-specific markers including Aldh1A1, Corin (Lrp4), Lmx1a, Otx2, Ngn2, Pitx3, and Nkx6.1, while lacking expression of forebrain identity markers such as FoxG1, GSX2, and Nkx2.1. These iDP cells are characterized by their lineage restriction to the dopaminergic neuronal fate, as evidenced by their inability to differentiate into astrocytes or oligodendrocytes under standard glial-inducing conditions, and their capacity to differentiate into tyrosine hydroxylase-positive (TH+) neurons at efficiencies exceeding 90%, even in the absence of SHH and FGF8.

The iDP cells further exhibit functional maturity upon differentiation, expressing key dopaminergic proteins including aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC), dopamine transporter (DAT), and vesicular monoamine transporter 2 (VMAT2), and demonstrating electrophysiological properties of mature neurons, including voltage-gated sodium and potassium currents and the ability to fire action potentials. A population of iDPs according to the invention is homogeneous, clonally expandable, and stable in culture, making it suitable for therapeutic-scale production.

The invention also discloses a method of transdifferentiating somatic cells into iDPs by introducing nucleic acids encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 into the somatic cells, thereby inducing direct conversion without an intervening pluripotent state. This method may be practiced using viral vectors, synthetic mRNA, or other gene delivery systems, and is applicable to human and non-human somatic cells, particularly dermal fibroblasts. Additionally, the invention provides a method of treating a neurodegenerative disease, such as Parkinson’s disease, by administering a therapeutically effective amount of iDP cells to a subject in need thereof, where the iDPs survive, differentiate into dopaminergic neurons in vivo, integrate into host circuitry, and alleviate motor deficits.

Furthermore, the invention includes a method of identifying regulatory agents that modulate dopaminergic differentiation or survival by contacting iDP cells or their derivatives with candidate compounds and assessing changes in marker expression, morphology, or function. This platform enables high-throughput screening for neuroprotective or neurorestorative therapeutics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for the transdifferentiation of somatic cells into induced dopaminergic precursor (iDP) cells through the coordinated ectopic expression of three transcription factors: Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. This triad of factors synergistically reprograms the epigenetic and transcriptional landscape of somatic cells, such as fibroblasts, to adopt a stable, self-renewing, and lineage-restricted neural progenitor state with intrinsic midbrain dopaminergic identity. Unlike previous reprogramming strategies that yield multipotent neural progenitors requiring extensive exogenous patterning cues, the iDPs of the present invention are pre-specified toward the dopaminergic fate and differentiate efficiently into functional neurons without dependence on SHH or FGF8.

A key advantage of the present disclosure over currently available technologies is the elimination of pluripotency-associated risks, such as teratoma formation, and the achievement of near-complete dopaminergic lineage restriction, which enhances therapeutic predictability and safety. Moreover, the iDPs can be modified to enable controlled proliferation—for example, through conditional expression of L-Myc under a Tet-On system—allowing for scalable expansion while maintaining differentiation competence and genomic stability. Upon withdrawal of the proliferative signal, the iDPs cease dividing and terminally differentiate into mature dopaminergic neurons.

The iDP cells are particularly suited for cell or tissue replacement therapy in neurodegenerative diseases characterized by dopaminergic neuron loss, most notably Parkinson’s disease. Preclinical studies in the MPTP mouse model of PD demonstrate that transplanted iDPs survive for at least six weeks, differentiate predominantly into TH+ neurons without forming tumors or glial scars, restore striatal dopaminergic innervation, and partially reverse motor deficits as measured by rotarod performance. These findings underscore the therapeutic potential of iDPs as an engraftable cell source.

Beyond Parkinson’s disease, the iDP platform may be adapted for the treatment of other conditions involving dopaminergic dysfunction, such as certain forms of depression, schizophrenia, or drug addiction, although primary application is envisioned for neurodegenerative movement disorders. The therapeutic efficiency of iDP cells is enhanced by their autologous potential—patient-derived skin fibroblasts can be reprogrammed into iDPs, expanded, and reintroduced without immune rejection—thereby enabling personalized regenerative medicine.

### Definitions

As used herein, “transdifferentiation” or “reprogramming” refers to the direct conversion of one differentiated somatic cell type into another differentiated or progenitor cell type without passage through a pluripotent state. A “somatic cell” is any cell of the body other than germ cells, including but not limited to fibroblasts, keratinocytes, hepatocytes, and blood cells. A “dopaminergic precursor” or “dopaminergic progenitor” is a neural progenitor cell committed to the dopaminergic neuronal lineage, expressing markers such as Corin, Aldh1A1, and Lmx1a, and capable of differentiating into tyrosine hydroxylase-expressing neurons. An “endogenous DP cell” is a naturally occurring dopaminergic progenitor in the developing or adult brain. An “induced dopaminergic precursor cell” (iDP) is a cell generated by artificial reprogramming that exhibits the molecular and functional characteristics of a dopaminergic progenitor.

“Ectopic expression” means the expression of a gene in a cell type or developmental stage where it is not normally expressed, typically achieved by introducing an exogenous nucleic acid construct. “Expression” and “expression products” refer to the production of RNA or protein from a gene. “Brn2” (POU3F2), “Brn4” (POU3F4), “Sox2,” “Foxa2,” and “Lmx1a” are transcription factors known in the art; their sequences are publicly available and include naturally occurring isoforms and engineered variants. A “variant of a polypeptide” is a polypeptide with at least 80% amino acid sequence identity to the reference sequence and retains biological activity.

“Operably linked” describes a configuration wherein a promoter is positioned to drive expression of a downstream coding sequence. “Viral vectors” include retroviral, lentiviral, adenoviral, and adeno-associated viral vectors used for gene delivery. A “transcription factor” is a protein that binds DNA and regulates gene expression. Terms such as “decrease,” “reduced,” etc., indicate a statistically significant reduction relative to a control, while “increased,” “enhance,” etc., denote a statistically significant elevation.

A “subject,” “individual,” or “patient” is a human or non-human animal, preferably a mammal, more preferably a human. “Non-human animals” and “non-human mammals” include rodents, primates, and livestock. “Treat,” “treating,” or “treatment” means administering a therapy to ameliorate symptoms, delay progression, or cure a disease. “Prophylactic treatment” is administered to prevent disease onset. An “effective treatment” produces a measurable beneficial outcome.

The terms “comprising,” “consisting essentially of,” and “consisting of” have their standard legal meanings in patent law, with “comprising” being open-ended. “About” indicates ±10% of a stated value. The articles “a” and “an” mean one or more unless context dictates otherwise. “Or” is inclusive unless specified. “And/or” includes all combinations. “Primary somatic cell types” are cells isolated directly from tissue, not immortalized lines. “Isolation and culture methods” refer to standard enzymatic and mechanical dissociation followed by maintenance in appropriate media.

“Parental cell species” denotes the organism from which the somatic cell is derived. A “human individual” is a member of Homo sapiens. “Fully differentiated and/or restricted somatic cells” are post-mitotic or lineage-committed cells. “Partially or terminally differentiated somatic cells” include cells with limited plasticity. “Transdifferentiated somatic cells” are reprogrammed cells as defined above. “Fibroblast cells” are mesenchymal cells commonly found in connective tissue. “iDP cells” are as defined throughout.

### Somatic Cells

Fibroblasts, particularly dermal fibroblasts, are preferred primary somatic cells due to their accessibility via minimally invasive skin biopsy, ease of culture, and robust reprogramming efficiency. Non-limiting examples of other primary somatic cell types suitable for reprogramming include hepatocytes, adipocytes, keratinocytes, peripheral blood mononuclear cells, and urinary epithelial cells. Isolation methods vary by tissue: fibroblasts are obtained by explant culture or collagenase digestion of skin biopsies; hepatocytes by perfusion of liver tissue; blood cells by density gradient centrifugation. Culture conditions are optimized for each cell type to maintain viability and prevent senescence prior to reprogramming.

### Reprogramming (Transdifferentiation)

Transdifferentiation is achieved by introducing master transcription factors that override the somatic cell’s gene regulatory network and activate a new lineage program. For iDP generation, the master transcription factors are Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. Ectopic expression is mediated by viral vectors (e.g., retrovirus, lentivirus), synthetic modified mRNA, plasmid DNA, or protein transduction. Endogenous gene expression is modulated as the reprogramming factors activate native neural and dopaminergic genes while silencing somatic programs. Epigenetic modifications, including histone acetylation and DNA demethylation at neural loci, accompany this transition.

Vector design may incorporate multiple transcription factor cassettes in a single vector or co-deliver separate vectors. Integration at safe harbor loci (e.g., AAVS1) minimizes genotoxicity. Synthetic modified mRNA offers transient, non-integrating expression, reducing long-term safety concerns. iDP characteristics include expression of neural progenitor and midbrain dopaminergic markers, lineage restriction, and functional maturation potential. Applications include cell therapy, disease modeling, and drug screening. Current cell therapies suffer from heterogeneity and poor survival; direct reprogramming aims to generate pure, safe, and functional cell populations.

### iDP Production

Parkinson’s disease results from dopaminergic neuron loss in the substantia nigra. Current cell therapies using fetal tissue or stem cell derivatives show variable efficacy and safety. Direct reprogramming seeks to generate authentic, patient-matched dopaminergic precursors. Foxa2 and Lmx1a are essential for midbrain DA neuron development; Foxa2 specifies floor plate identity and activates Lmx1a. Using a Tet-On/Off system, Foxa2 and Lmx1a expression in 5F-iNPCs enhanced TH+ neuron yield tenfold. Foxa2 alone, with Brn2 and Sox2, sufficed for direct conversion.

Adult dermal fibroblasts from Nestin-EGFP mice were infected with retroviruses encoding Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2. EGFP+ colonies emerged by day 14, yielding expandable iDP clones. iDPs expressed high levels of neural progenitor markers (Sox1, Nestin) and dopaminergic progenitor markers (Aldh1A1, Corin, Lmx1a, Otx2), but not forebrain markers (FoxG1). Thus, fibroblasts acquired mesencephalic identity via forced expression of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2.

iDPs expressed PSA-NCAM, DCX, and Corin, confirming neuronal-restricted precursor status. Upon differentiation with BDNF/GDNF/AA, >90% of βIII-tubulin+ neurons were TH+, even without SHH/FGF8. No GFAP+ or O4+ cells formed, unlike wild-type NPCs. Glial genes (Glast, GFAP) were minimally expressed. Differentiated neurons expressed synaptophysin, AADC, DAT, and VMAT2, and exhibited voltage-gated currents and action potentials.

Conditional L-Myc expression via Tet-On enhanced Ki67+ proliferation without altering DP markers, enabling scalable expansion. In MPTP mice, GFP-labeled iDPs grafted into striatum survived six weeks without tumors. At three weeks post-transplant, iDPs increased TH+ fiber density by 20% and differentiated into Tuj+/TH+ neurons, not astrocytes. Rotarod performance improved, indicating partial functional recovery.

Human iDPs were produced from both Parkinson’s patient and fetal fibroblasts using the same factor combination, demonstrating clinical translatability and potential for autologous therapy.

### Human iDP Upon Ectopic Expression of Brn2, Sox2 and Foxa2

Human induced dopaminergic precursors (iDPs) were successfully generated by ectopic expression of Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2 in dermal fibroblasts derived from both a Parkinson’s disease patient and a human fetus. The reprogramming protocol, adapted from murine studies, involved retroviral delivery of the three transcription factors followed by culture in neural progenitor medium. The resulting human iDPs exhibited morphological and molecular characteristics analogous to their murine counterparts, including expression of neural progenitor markers (Nestin, Sox1) and midbrain dopaminergic progenitor markers (Corin, Lmx1a, Otx2). Critically, these human iDPs demonstrated lineage restriction, differentiating efficiently into TH+ neurons under maturation conditions while showing negligible glial differentiation. This successful derivation from patient cells underscores the feasibility of creating autologous, disease-relevant cell sources for transplantation and in vitro modeling of Parkinson’s disease pathogenesis and drug response.

## DISCUSSION

Stem cell-based therapy represents a promising avenue for treating neurodegenerative disorders, yet current approaches using ESCs or iPSCs face hurdles in safety, purity, and scalability. Direct conversion of somatic cells into specialized neural subtypes offers a compelling alternative by bypassing pluripotency. Prior efforts generated induced DA neurons or multipotent iNPCs, but with low dopaminergic yield and dependence on morphogens. The present invention introduces a novel triad of transcription factors—Brn2, Sox2, and Foxa2—that directly reprogram fibroblasts into induced dopaminergic precursors (iDPs) with intrinsic midbrain identity.

Foxa2 and Lmx1a are pivotal in midbrain DA development; our data confirm their role in enhancing DA specification in iNPCs. Remarkably, Foxa2 alone, combined with Brn2 and Sox2, suffices for full reprogramming into stable iDPs. These iDPs are dopaminergic-restricted, differentiating into functional TH+ neurons at >90% efficiency without SHH/FGF8, and express mature markers like DAT and VMAT2. Electrophysiology confirms neuronal maturity. In vivo, iDPs survive, differentiate into DA neurons, restore striatal innervation, and improve motor function in PD mice without tumor formation.

Patient skin fibroblasts can be reprogrammed into iDPs, enabling autologous therapy and disease modeling. Conditional L-Myc expression allows controlled expansion, addressing scalability. Collectively, this work establishes iDPs as a safe, efficient, and clinically relevant cell source for Parkinson’s disease treatment, with broad implications for regenerative neurology.

## Methods

### Cell Preparation, Retroviral Packaging, Infection and Direct Reprogramming

Mouse skin fibroblasts were isolated from adult Nestin-EGFP transgenic mice aged 5.5–7.0 weeks under IACUC approval. Fibroblasts were cultured in DMEM with 10% FBS and used at passages 2–5. Brn2 and Foxa2 ORFs were cloned into pMXs-retroviral vectors; Sox2 vector was obtained from Addgene. Retroviruses were packaged in Plat-E cells via Lipofectamine transfection. Virus-containing supernatants were collected at 48h and 72h post-transfection, filtered, and mixed with polybrene. Fibroblasts were infected overnight, then switched to NeuroCult NSC Basal Medium with bFGF and EGF. iDP colonies were monitored by EGFP fluorescence from day 9–14.

### Quantitative Real-Time RT-PCR

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol and RNeasy kits. cDNA was synthesized using a first-strand kit. qRT-PCR was performed with SYBR Select Master Mix on a real-time PCR system using gene-specific primers. Cycling conditions included initial denaturation at 95°C for 2 min, followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 15 sec, annealing at gene-specific temperatures for 15 sec, and extension at 72°C for 1 min. Triplicate samples were analyzed, and data normalized to housekeeping genes.

### Immunocytochemistry

Cells were fixed in 4% formaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100, and blocked with 2% BSA. Primary antibodies against Nestin, TH, GFAP, etc., were applied overnight at 4°C. After washing, fluorescent secondary antibodies were incubated for 2h at room temperature. Images were acquired using a Zeiss 710 Confocal Microscope.

### Differentiation

Astrocyte differentiation: iDPs cultured in DMEM/F12 with 10% FBS for 7 days. Oligodendrocyte differentiation: iDPs treated with PDGF, FGF-2, and forskolin for 4 days, then T3 and ascorbic acid for 7 days. Neuronal differentiation (SHH/FGF8-independent): iDPs plated on coated coverslips in DMEM/F12 with N2, B27, BDNF, GDNF, and ascorbic acid for 4 weeks. SHH/FGF8-dependent protocol included 6 days of SHH/FGF8 pre-patterning before switching to maturation medium.

### Recording of Action Potentials and Total Currents

Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings were performed using an Axonpatch 200B amplifier and pClamp 10.2 software. Pipette solution contained K-aspartate, KCl, CaCl2, MgATP, HEPES, EGTA, and glucose. Bath solution contained NaCl, KCl, MgCl2, CaCl2, HEPES, glucose, and sucrose. Action potentials were elicited by 1-s current injections from 20–100 pA. Voltage steps from −70 to +70 mV evoked total currents. Data were sampled at 10 kHz and filtered at 5 kHz.

### Motor Function and Behavior Tests

Rotarod testing assessed motor coordination. Mice were trained on an accelerating rod (2–12 rpm) for 5 min over three days. Baseline performance was recorded twice pre-MPTP at 10 rpm. Post-transplant, mice were tested twice weekly for three weeks. Performance was calculated as latency to fall, normalized to baseline and PBS control group.

### Statistical Analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD. Statistical significance was determined by ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test, with p < 0.05 considered significant. All experiments were repeated at least twice with triplicate samples.

### Cell Labeling, Transplantation and Histology

iDPs were labeled with GFP-expressing lentivirus and selected with puromycin. For transplantation, 250,000 GFP+ iDPs were injected bilaterally into the striatum of MPTP-treated SCID or C57BL/6J mice using stereotaxic coordinates. Mice were perfused 3–6 weeks post-transplant. Brains were sectioned and stained for TH, GFP, Tuj1, and GFAP. TH+ fiber density in striatum was quantified by digital image analysis in a blinded manner.
