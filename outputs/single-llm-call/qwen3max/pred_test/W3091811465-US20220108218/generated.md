# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention resides in the technical field of quantum computing and machine learning, specifically relating to methods and apparatus for quantum-assisted machine learning (QAML) using tensor network models deployable on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices. More particularly, the invention provides a comprehensive framework for designing, training, optimizing, and compiling generative quantum machine learning models based on matrix product states (MPS), a class of tensor networks, into executable quantum circuits tailored for specific NISQ hardware architectures with constrained qubit connectivity, limited gate sets, and significant noise profiles. The invention further encompasses classical preprocessing of data, classical optimization of quantum model parameters, and post-processing of quantum measurement outcomes to enable practical machine learning applications on current and near-term quantum hardware.

## BACKGROUND

Current quantum computing platforms are characterized as noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices, which possess a limited number of physical qubits—typically ranging from a few to several dozen—and operate without full quantum error correction. These devices are subject to significant hardware limitations that pose substantial challenges for executing complex quantum algorithms. A primary limitation is the presence of high levels of noise, including decoherence, gate infidelity, and readout errors, which accumulate rapidly over the course of a quantum computation and corrupt the final result. Another critical constraint is the restricted qubit connectivity topology; not all pairs of qubits can interact directly, necessitating the insertion of additional SWAP gates to mediate interactions between non-adjacent qubits, which further increases circuit depth and error susceptibility. Furthermore, NISQ devices support only a limited native gate set, often comprising arbitrary single-qubit rotations and a specific two-qubit entangling gate such as the controlled-NOT (CNOT) or iSWAP. Generic quantum compilation methods, designed for fault-tolerant machines, often produce circuits that are too deep and contain too many entangling operations to be viable on NISQ hardware, as the accumulated noise renders the output meaningless. These combined limitations—the noise, the sparse connectivity, and the restricted gate set—severely restrict the class of quantum algorithms that can be successfully executed and necessitate the development of specialized, resource-efficient, and noise-resilient approaches for practical applications like machine learning.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel and robust framework for quantum-assisted machine learning (QAML) that is specifically engineered to overcome the inherent limitations of noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices. At its core, the invention leverages the mathematical structure of tensor networks, particularly matrix product states (MPS), to design highly resource-efficient quantum machine learning models. An embodiment of the invention provides an apparatus for implementing QAML, comprising processing circuitry configured to execute a multi-stage workflow. This workflow begins by encoding a set of classical data vectors into unentangled quantum states within an exponentially large Hilbert space. A quantum model, structured as an MPS, is then trained on this encoded data using classical optimization techniques to maximize a log-likelihood objective function, resulting in a set of optimized tensors. These tensors are subsequently converted into a sequence of isometric operations that define a sequential state preparation scheme, requiring only a constant number of physical qubits independent of the input data size. Finally, a key innovation of the invention is a specialized compilation stage that translates these isometries into a shallow, hardware-specific quantum circuit. This compilation exploits the inherent gauge freedom and basis ambiguities of the MPS representation to minimize the number of entangling gates, thereby producing a noise-resilient program suitable for execution on a target NISQ device with a given qubit topology and native gate set.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The following detailed description presents non-limiting example embodiments of the invention, providing a complete and enabling disclosure for a person of ordinary skill in the art. The terms used throughout this disclosure are defined in their broadest reasonable sense consistent with the specification. For instance, a “tensor network” (TN) refers to a factorization of a high-dimensional tensor into a network of lower-dimensional tensors connected by contracted indices, a powerful formalism for representing and manipulating complex, high-dimensional data structures. A “matrix product state” (MPS) is a specific one-dimensional TN topology widely used in quantum physics and, as disclosed herein, adapted for quantum machine learning. A “noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) device” denotes a current-generation quantum processor with tens of qubits, lacking full error correction, and exhibiting significant operational noise and connectivity constraints. The motivation for using TNs in the context of NISQ devices stems from their ability to provide a compact, variational representation of quantum states with controlled complexity, governed by the bond dimension, which directly translates to efficient use of scarce quantum resources.

The invention describes methods for quantum-assisted machine learning using TNs and classical data sets. The process begins with a collection of classical data vectors, which are first preprocessed and mapped into a quantum Hilbert space via a data embedding map; for binary data, this is a simple phase-like encoding where each bit is mapped to a computational basis state of a qubit. The quantum model is then defined using a TN architecture, specifically an MPS, whose parameters (the tensors) are learned or trained on a classical computer. This training is performed by optimizing a cost function, such as the negative log-likelihood of the training data under a Born machine probability model, using a DMRG-inspired sweeping algorithm that updates local blocks of tensors via gradient descent. The trained MPS model is a purely classical object at this stage but encodes a quantum state that can generate samples resembling the training data.

A critical step is the deployment of this classically trained TN model on a gate-based quantum device. To achieve this, the MPS is converted into a resource-efficient sequential preparation scheme. This scheme requires only a single “physical” or “readout” qubit, which is measured to produce a data sample, coupled to a small ancillary register of qubits whose size scales logarithmically with the MPS bond dimension. The conversion relies on placing the MPS in a left-canonical form, from which a sequence of isometric operators can be directly extracted. Each isometry acts on the physical qubit and the ancilla, preparing the state for the next measurement. However, these isometries are abstract mathematical objects and must be compiled into a sequence of native quantum gates for a specific hardware platform.

This compilation is a central challenge addressed by the invention. The disclosure motivates the use of specialized heuristics for translating the TN-derived isometries into abstract quantum circuits, as generic compilation methods are ill-suited for NISQ constraints. The invention introduces a framework for this compilation that leverages the unique properties of the isometries. First, the isometries are “cleaned” by applying a local SVD compression to the MPS, truncating singular values below a tolerance to remove numerical noise from the classical optimization, which prevents the compiler from wasting resources on irrelevant details. Second, the invention introduces the concept of a “diagonal gauge” for the MPS. By exploiting the unitary gauge freedom inherent in the MPS representation—which exists because the bond (ancilla) degrees of freedom are never measured—an optimization is performed to permute the ancilla basis states. The goal of this permutation is to make the isometries as “diagonal” as possible, meaning that the quantum operations preferentially leave qubits in their initial state, thereby minimizing the need for complex entangling operations during compilation.

The compilation of the cleaned and gauge-optimized TN models into quantum programs for NISQ devices is performed using a greedy heuristic search. The compiler defines a cost function that measures the distance between a candidate unitary circuit (built from the hardware’s native gate set) and the target isometry, but only over the subspace where the isometry is defined. Starting with simple circuits of only single-qubit gates, the compiler iteratively appends entangling gates and associated single-qubit rotations, building a priority queue of candidate circuits sorted by their cost. It selects the shallowest circuit that meets a specified tolerance, effectively trading off perfect fidelity for a significant reduction in circuit depth and entangling gate count—a crucial advantage on noisy hardware. The advantages of these TN-based quantum programs are manifold: they are highly qubit-efficient, their circuit depth is minimized through intelligent compilation, and they are robust to the specific noise profile of the target device.

The applicability of these example methods extends to various contexts, including generative modeling, classification, and feature extraction. The invention introduces a complete QAML workflow on NISQ devices, which includes a framework for designing and optimizing TN-based QAML models. A key element of this framework is the generalized canonical form for MPS models, which facilitates the sequential preparation scheme. The greedy heuristics for compiling TN models are designed to work with a given hardware topology and gate set, ensuring practical deployability. To validate the framework, the invention introduces a solvable benchmark problem—an MPS generative model for data vectors with a single ‘1’—which allows for exact analytical solutions and rigorous performance assessment.

The invention further describes the impacts of real-world hardware constraints on model performance. Parametric studies are conducted to assess the effects of depolarization noise and readout noise fluctuations on the fidelity of the generated samples, using hardware simulators to model these imperfections. The description also covers the fundamentals of gate-based quantum computing and its limitations, reiterating the necessity of the invention’s resource-efficient approach for viable machine learning applications. The design of quantum circuits for these applications is centered on the TN architecture, which provides a principled way to control model expressivity and resource usage. The TN-based QAML algorithms offer distinct advantages, including a smooth transition from classically simulable to potentially classically intractable models and the ability to use the classically trained model as a “preconditioner” for hybrid quantum-classical optimization loops on future hardware.

The framework for QAML with TNs explicitly includes the translation of classical data into quantum states, for example, using a binary encoding for discrete data. The optimization of the TN models is performed entirely on classical devices using well-established techniques like the sweeping algorithm with SVD-based updates. A pivotal innovation is the conversion of the optimized MPS model into a resource-efficient sequential preparation scheme, which decouples the qubit resource requirement from the input data size. The subsequent compilation and optimization of the isometric operations into native hardware operations is achieved by leveraging the gauge freedom of the MPS to simplify the isometries before applying the greedy compilation heuristic.

The techniques for the compilation stage are specifically aimed at TN models for QAML on NISQ devices. A key technique is the permutation of auxiliary quantum degrees of freedom (the ancilla basis states) in the TN to optimize the mapping to the physical hardware resources. This is followed by heuristics for the translation of isometries into native operations that explicitly aim to minimize the number of entangling operations, which are the dominant source of error. An example method 100 for the QAML workflow is described, which systematically executes the steps of data encoding, classical model training, model-to-circuit conversion, and hardware-specific compilation.

The invention also touches upon quantum-inspired ML applications, where the TN structure is used to design powerful classical models. The data encoding uses a phase-like encoding, specifically a binary map for discrete data, to embed classical vectors into a quantum Hilbert space. The quantum model is defined using TN models, with a focus on MPS topologies due to their maturity, efficiency, and sequential preparability. The properties of MPSs, such as their ability to be placed in canonical forms and their efficient classical simulability for moderate bond dimensions, make them ideal for this application. The invention applies these TN methods, originally developed in condensed matter physics, to the domain of machine learning, leveraging the sequential preparation scheme to achieve minimal qubit resource requirements. For a register of N qubits representing the data, the scheme requires only a single physical qubit and an ancilla of size log₂(χ).

The isometric operators that constitute the sequential preparation scheme are formally defined, and the process ensures the decoupling of the final qubit states from the ancilla. The quantum data representation is implemented via a Born machine, where the probability of a data sample is given by the squared amplitude of its corresponding quantum state. The training objective is to maximize the average log-likelihood of the training data, which is equivalent to minimizing the negative log-likelihood. The optimization proceeds by updating a local block of tensors in the MPS and sweeping back and forth across the network until convergence. The trained MPS model is then converted into a sequence of isometric operations that can be executed on a quantum processor.

The quantum compilation process involves optimizing the tensor network structure (e.g., via the diagonal gauge) and then mapping the isometries to hardware gates. Plots of the isometry matrices are used to visualize their structure before and after optimization. The basis ordering for the physical and ancilla qubits is carefully defined to facilitate this process. The isometry acts on the physical qubit and the ancilla, and the cleaning process filters the MPS to remove noise from the classical training. This is done via local SVD compression and truncation of the bond dimension based on a singular value cutoff. The MPS is converted into left-canonical form, and the gauge freedom is used to produce models that are more amenable to compilation.

The matrix of overlaps in the ancilla space is defined, and its diagonalization is used to guide the basis permutation. The transformation to the diagonal gauge involves permuting the ancilla basis states to maximize diagonality, with a specific procedure to handle cases where multiple matrix elements have the same absolute value. A cost function is constructed based on the binary representation of the state indices to select the optimal permutation that minimizes the required qubit operations. The compilation of isometries for QAML models leverages this gauge freedom to simplify the resulting quantum operations. A distance metric between basis states is defined, and a cost function is introduced to drive the greedy compilation heuristic.

The compilation heuristic implements right-moving and left-moving updates to transform the entire MPS into the diagonal gauge, setting a specific “diagonality center.” Sign ambiguities are fixed to maintain consistency. The isometries are then transformed into quantum operations for a target hardware, which is defined by its qubit topology and native gate set (e.g., single-qubit rotations and CNOTs). A compilation heuristic is introduced that uses a cost function to evaluate candidate circuits built from a tree of possible gate sequences. This cost function is optimized using methods like BFGS, and a priority queue is formed to manage the search space efficiently. Longer gate sequence motifs, such as two-qubit rotation gates, are introduced to accelerate convergence, with a penalty function applied to balance their cost against simpler gates.

The greedy compilation procedure is illustrated with examples, showing how it can produce circuits with an order of magnitude fewer CNOTs than generic methods like those in QISKIT. The procedure is tolerant of a specified error threshold, recognizing that over-compilation is detrimental on noisy hardware. Successive optimization rounds refine the circuit, which is then compiled into the native gate set, with single-qubit rotations collected and optimized. Alternative compilation methods, such as those for permutation gates, are discussed for comparison. The invention’s framework is validated using an exactly solvable benchmark model, a two-qubit MPS Born machine, which is converted to a sequential preparation scheme via QR decomposition and compiled using both hand-optimized and auto-compiled methods.

The performance of the QAML workflow is assessed on real cloud-based quantum hardware (e.g., IBMQ devices). A jackknife statistical procedure is implemented to account for day-to-day noise fluctuations, and a measurement noise filter is applied for calibration. The Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence is used as a metric to compare the ideal and experimental probability distributions, demonstrating that the auto-compiled circuits from the invention generally outperform hand-compiled ones due to their shallower depth. The invention is also applied to a more complex problem: generative modeling of features extracted from the MNIST handwritten digit dataset. The model is trained classically, compiled using the disclosed methods, and its performance is analyzed under simulated depolarization and readout noise, showing its resilience and practical utility.

Finally, the invention describes an apparatus for implementing QAML, comprising processing circuitry, memory for storing QAML data and instructions, and a processor for executing the QAML workflow. The configuration of the processing circuitry is specialized to handle the unique demands of the QAML module, which orchestrates the encoding, training, compilation, and execution stages. The operation of this module is detailed, from the initial encoding of classical data vectors to the final sequential preparation of the MPS on the quantum hardware, utilizing the diagonal gauge and greedy heuristics to ensure an efficient and robust implementation on the target NISQ device.
