# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular diagnostics and, more specifically, to the identification and use of plasma protein biomarkers for the detection, diagnosis, and monitoring of lymphoma in human subjects. In particular, the invention provides novel methods and systems for detecting Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL) in individuals infected with the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) through the analysis of specific plasma proteins that are differentially expressed in the presence of HL. The technical field encompasses proteomics-based biomarker discovery, immunoassay development, clinical diagnostics, and personalized medicine strategies aimed at improving early detection and management of lymphoma in high-risk populations, especially those with underlying immunosuppressive conditions such as HIV/AIDS. The invention further pertains to diagnostic kits and analytical platforms designed to measure the levels of these biomarkers in biological samples, thereby enabling non-invasive, blood-based screening for lymphoma.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Lymphoma represents a heterogeneous group of hematologic malignancies originating from lymphoid cells, with Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL) being a distinct subtype characterized by the presence of rare malignant Reed-Sternberg cells within a reactive inflammatory microenvironment. In the context of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection, HL is classified as a non-AIDS-defining cancer but occurs at a significantly elevated incidence—estimated to be 5.6- to 14.7-fold higher than in the general population. This increased risk is attributed to chronic immune dysregulation and persistent viral co-infections, particularly Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), which plays a well-established oncogenic role in immunocompromised individuals. Despite advances in antiretroviral therapy that have extended the lifespan of HIV-positive patients, the burden of non-AIDS-defining malignancies like HL continues to rise, underscoring an urgent unmet clinical need for effective surveillance strategies.

Current methods for lymphoma detection rely heavily on clinical symptomatology, imaging modalities such as computed tomography (CT) or positron emission tomography (PET), and invasive tissue biopsies for histopathological confirmation. These approaches are inherently reactive, typically initiated only after patients present with overt signs such as lymphadenopathy, fever, night sweats, or weight loss. Consequently, diagnosis often occurs at advanced disease stages, necessitating aggressive chemotherapy and radiation regimens that carry substantial morbidity, particularly in HIV-infected individuals who may have compromised organ function and heightened susceptibility to treatment-related toxicities. Moreover, existing laboratory markers such as lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), beta-2-microglobulin, and C-reactive protein (CRP) lack sufficient specificity and sensitivity for early-stage HL detection, especially in the complex immunological milieu of HIV infection where chronic inflammation can confound interpretation.

The limitations of current diagnostic paradigms highlight a critical gap in preventive oncology: the absence of a routine, non-invasive, blood-based screening test for HL in high-risk populations. Early detection of HL could enable timely intervention with less intensive therapeutic regimens, thereby reducing treatment-related complications and improving long-term outcomes. Given that HL is fundamentally associated with a profound systemic inflammatory response—even though malignant cells constitute less than 2% of the tumor mass—the circulating proteome offers a rich reservoir of potential biomarkers reflective of both neoplastic activity and host immune engagement. Thus, there exists a compelling rationale for developing a proteomics-driven, plasma-based assay capable of identifying molecular signatures specific to HL in HIV-positive individuals, thereby facilitating proactive clinical management and personalized risk stratification.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel biomarkers and methods for the detection, diagnosis, and monitoring of Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL) in human subjects, particularly those infected with HIV. Specifically, the invention identifies a panel of 60 plasma proteins that are significantly differentially expressed in HIV-infected individuals with HL compared to matched HIV-infected controls without lymphoma. These proteins, many of which are implicated in inflammatory pathways and cancer biology, serve as candidate biomarkers for non-invasive lymphoma screening. The invention further discloses a method for detecting HL comprising obtaining a plasma sample from a subject, quantifying the abundance of one or more of the identified biomarker proteins, and comparing the measured levels to reference values derived from control populations to determine the likelihood of HL presence.

In addition, the invention encompasses a diagnostic test kit configured for use in clinical or laboratory settings to facilitate the detection of HL. The kit includes reagents, antibodies, or affinity agents specific for at least one of the disclosed biomarker proteins—such as C-reactive protein, serum amyloid A, beta-2-microglobulin, lactate dehydrogenase, or any of the 57 newly identified candidate proteins—and instructions for performing the assay. The kit may be adapted for use with immunoassay platforms (e.g., ELISA, lateral flow assays), mass spectrometry, or multiplexed protein detection systems. Furthermore, the invention provides a comprehensive diagnostic system integrating sample processing modules, analytical instrumentation, data analysis algorithms, and interpretive software to automate the detection and classification of HL based on the biomarker profile. This system enables rapid, objective, and reproducible assessment of lymphoma risk, supporting early clinical decision-making and longitudinal monitoring of disease progression or treatment response in high-risk cohorts.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Materials and Methods

The methods employed in the development of the present invention were conducted in strict accordance with ethical standards and regulatory requirements. The study protocol received full approval from the George Washington University Medical Center Institutional Review Board, and written informed consent was obtained from all human participants prior to sample collection. Additional ethical oversight was provided by the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL) Institutional Review Board, which reviewed and endorsed the original IRB approval before the transfer and analysis of biological specimens. Human plasma samples were sourced from the AIDS and Cancer Specimen Resource, with cases consisting of HIV-infected individuals diagnosed with HL and controls comprising HIV-infected individuals without lymphoma. Subjects were matched for age and gender to minimize demographic confounding. Plasma samples were processed under blinded conditions, with randomization based on HL status to prevent analytical bias.

Plasma preparation involved depletion of the twelve most abundant proteins using Proteome Purify™ 12 immunodepletion resin, followed by trichloroacetic acid precipitation to concentrate lower-abundance proteins. The resulting protein pellets were solubilized in denaturing buffer containing urea, thiourea, and dithiothreitol, reduced, alkylated, and digested with sequencing-grade trypsin. Peptides were purified via solid-phase extraction on C18 columns and stored at −20°C until analysis. Liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) was performed using a custom-built nanocapillary HPLC system coupled online to an LTQ-Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Peptide separation was achieved on a 65-cm reversed-phase C18 column using a gradient elution program, and high-resolution mass spectra were acquired over a defined m/z range with stringent acquisition parameters to ensure data quality.

Data analysis utilized the Accurate Mass and Time (AMT) tag approach, wherein observed peptide features were matched against a pre-existing human plasma AMT tag database containing over 30,000 validated tryptic peptides. False discovery rates were controlled at ≤6% using decoy database searches. Statistical evaluation involved normalization of peptide intensities across runs using regression-based methods and median absolute deviation scaling. Differential abundance was assessed using a non-parametric Mann-Whitney U test for continuous intensity data and a modified Chi-square (G-test) for presence-absence patterns. Protein inference was performed using RRollup within the DAnTE software platform, requiring a minimum of two unique peptides per protein and prioritizing characterized over hypothetical proteins. Biological interpretation of the resulting protein list was conducted using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA), which applied Fisher’s Exact Test to identify enriched functional categories such as “inflammatory response” and “cancer.”

### Results and Discussion

Proteomic profiling of plasma from 36 HIV-infected individuals—18 with HL and 18 without—yielded a robust dataset comprising 3814 quantified peptides corresponding to 629 proteins. Statistical analysis identified 647 peptides significantly altered in association with HL, mapping to 152 proteins. Of these, 60 proteins exhibited both statistical significance (p ≤ 0.05) and a minimum 1.5-fold change in abundance, forming the core biomarker panel of the invention. This panel included established inflammatory mediators such as C-reactive protein and serum amyloid A, as well as enzymes like lactate dehydrogenase and structural proteins with emerging roles in immune regulation. Ingenuity Pathway Analysis confirmed strong enrichment for biological functions directly relevant to HL pathogenesis, particularly “inflammatory response” and “cancer,” consistent with the known biology of HL as a malignancy driven by cytokine-mediated leukocyte recruitment rather than autonomous tumor cell proliferation.

Notably, three proteins in the panel—C-reactive protein, beta-2-microglobulin, and lactate dehydrogenase—had been previously reported as elevated in HL patients in the general population, thereby providing independent validation of the proteomic approach despite the unique HIV-positive cohort. Although HIV disease stage (as indicated by CD4+ T-cell count) showed a non-significant trend toward greater immunosuppression in HL cases, the replication of known HL biomarkers suggests that the observed protein changes are primarily attributable to lymphoma rather than HIV-related inflammation alone. The remaining 57 proteins represent novel candidate biomarkers with plausible mechanistic links to HL, including acute-phase reactants, complement factors, and proteins involved in leukocyte adhesion and migration. While potential confounding effects of concurrent infections or medications cannot be entirely excluded, the rigorous matching of cases and controls and the consistency of biological pathway enrichment support the specificity of the signature for HL.

In conclusion, the results demonstrate that plasma proteome analysis can uncover a reproducible molecular signature of HL in HIV-infected individuals. The identified biomarkers reflect the systemic inflammatory state induced by the tumor microenvironment and offer a foundation for developing a blood-based diagnostic test. Future validation in larger, prospective cohorts will be essential to confirm clinical utility, but the current findings establish a strong rationale for translating this proteomic signature into a practical tool for early lymphoma detection in high-risk populations.
