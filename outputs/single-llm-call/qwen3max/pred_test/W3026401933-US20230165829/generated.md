# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of oncology and molecular pharmacology, specifically to the identification and use of flavagline derivatives as inhibitors of KRAS oncogene activation. KRAS is a member of the RAS family of small GTPases that functions as a critical node in intracellular signal transduction pathways governing cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. Mutations in the KRAS gene, particularly at codons 12, 13, and 61, result in constitutive activation of the protein, rendering it insensitive to normal regulatory mechanisms and thereby driving uncontrolled cellular growth—a hallmark of numerous human cancers, including pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, non-small cell lung cancer, and colorectal carcinoma. Despite decades of research, direct pharmacological inhibition of KRAS has remained a formidable challenge due to its high affinity for GTP and the absence of well-defined druggable pockets. The invention disclosed herein addresses this unmet medical need by providing a novel class of compounds—specifically, structurally defined flavagline derivatives—that effectively suppress KRAS activation by interfering with its membrane-associated nanoclustering and GTP-loading processes, thereby offering a promising therapeutic strategy for cancers driven by aberrant RAS signaling.

## BACKGROUND ART

RAS proteins are small monomeric GTPases that cycle between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state, acting as binary molecular switches that relay signals from activated receptor tyrosine kinases to downstream effectors such as the RAF-MEK-ERK and PI3K-AKT pathways. This cycling is tightly regulated by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs), which promote GDP release and GTP binding, and GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs), which accelerate the intrinsic GTP hydrolysis activity of RAS, returning it to its inactive state. Oncogenic mutations, most commonly at glycine 12, glycine 13, or glutamine 61, impair GAP-mediated hydrolysis, leading to persistent RAS activation and sustained proliferative signaling. Among the three major RAS isoforms—HRAS, NRAS, and KRAS—KRAS is the most frequently mutated in human cancers. Alternative splicing of the KRAS pre-mRNA yields two isoforms, KRAS4A and KRAS4B, which share identical N-terminal G-domains but differ in their C-terminal hypervariable regions (HVRs). KRAS4B is the predominant isoform expressed in most tissues and malignancies. Posttranslational modifications, including farnesylation, proteolysis, methylation, and in the case of KRAS4A and HRAS/NRAS, palmitoylation, govern the subcellular localization of RAS proteins to specific plasma membrane microdomains, where they assemble into nanoscale signaling platforms known as nanoclusters. These nanoclusters are essential for efficient effector recruitment and signal amplification. KRAS4B, lacking palmitoylation sites, relies on a polybasic lysine-rich region for electrostatic interactions with anionic phospholipids such as phosphatidylserine (PS) and phosphatidic acid (PA), which facilitate its partitioning into disordered lipid domains conducive to nanocluster formation. Recent breakthroughs have led to the development of allele-specific covalent inhibitors targeting the KRAS G12C mutant, such as sotorasib and adagrasib, which trap the protein in its inactive GDP-bound conformation. However, these agents are ineffective against other prevalent KRAS mutants like G12D or G12V, and resistance often emerges through adaptive feedback mechanisms involving wild-type RAS reactivation. Flavaglines, a class of natural compounds derived from Aglaia plants, have been reported to possess anti-tumor properties by targeting prohibitin 1 (PHB1) and eukaryotic initiation factor 4A (eIF4A). Prior studies indicated that certain flavaglines disrupt the PHB1-CRAF interaction, thereby inhibiting RAS-driven tumorigenesis, but their precise mechanism of action on RAS activation remained unclear. Moreover, kinase inhibitors targeting downstream components of the MAPK pathway often suffer from limited efficacy and toxicity due to pathway redundancy and compensatory signaling.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The present invention introduces a subset of flavagline derivatives as potent and selective inhibitors of KRAS activation, irrespective of the specific oncogenic mutation present. Through systematic screening of structurally diverse flavaglines, the inventors have identified that only those bearing specific substituents on the cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core exhibit robust inhibitory activity against KRAS GTP-loading. These active derivatives conform to a general formula (I), wherein the substituent at position R8 is selected from —H, —OCH₃, —Br, —F, or —Cl, and the substituent at position R9 is either —O—CH₃ or —O—(CH₂)₂—NH—CH₃. Among the tested compounds, rocaglamide, FL3, FL10, FL13, FL15, FL19, FL23, FL32, FL37, FL40, and FL42 demonstrated significant inhibition of KRAS activation, with rocaglamide exhibiting an IC₅₀ of approximately 25 nM in quantitative NanoBiT assays. Critically, these compounds inhibit the formation of KRAS nanoclusters at the plasma membrane without affecting HRAS or NRAS nanoclustering, indicating a KRAS-specific mechanism. Furthermore, they disrupt the interaction between activated KRAS mutants—including G12V, G12D, and G12C—and the RAS-binding domain (RBD) of CRAF, thereby preventing downstream signal propagation. The invention encompasses the use of these flavagline derivatives for specifically inhibiting the proliferation of cell populations dependent on KRAS signaling, such as those harboring oncogenic KRAS mutations. A pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a flavagline derivative of formula (I), optionally in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient, is provided for the prevention or treatment of diseases characterized by pathological RAS signaling, including but not limited to pancreatic, lung, and colorectal cancers. Preferred embodiments include compositions wherein the flavagline derivative is rocaglamide or FL42, administered systemically or locally to achieve nanomolar concentrations sufficient to suppress tumor growth in vivo, as demonstrated in autochthonous mouse models of KRAS-driven non-small cell lung cancer.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The efficacy of the claimed flavagline derivatives was rigorously validated through a series of in vitro and in vivo experiments. In vitro, treatment of KRAS-mutant cancer cell lines—including HCT-116 (colorectal, KRAS G12D), ASPC-1 (pancreatic, KRAS G12D), and Calu-1 (lung, KRAS Q61K)—with rocaglamide or FL42 resulted in potent inhibition of cell proliferation, as measured by MTT assays, with IC₅₀ values ranging from 6 to 20 nM. Soft agar colony formation assays further confirmed the suppression of anchorage-independent growth, a hallmark of oncogenic transformation, upon flavagline treatment. Mechanistically, these compounds were shown to target plasma membrane-associated prohibitins (PHB1/PHB2), which serve as scaffolds facilitating the segregation of anionic phospholipids such as PS and PA. By disrupting the PHB-phospholipid-KRAS complex, flavaglines prevent the GTP-loading of KRAS and inhibit the formation of functional KRAS nanoclusters, as evidenced by fluorescence lifetime imaging–Förster resonance energy transfer (FLIM-FRET) and immunogold electron microscopy. Active Ras pull-down assays using CRAF-RBD or Ral-GDS-RA domains confirmed reduced levels of GTP-bound KRAS following flavagline exposure, independent of the RBD used. In vivo, the therapeutic potential was demonstrated in an autochthonous mouse model of KRAS G12D-driven non-small cell lung cancer. Mice harboring a doxycycline-inducible KRAS G12D transgene were treated with rocaglamide (2.5 mg/kg, intraperitoneally, three times per week) for six weeks following two months of transgene induction. This regimen significantly reduced the number and size of lung nodules compared to vehicle-treated controls, without inducing detectable hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity, as assessed by serum liver enzyme and creatinine levels. Materials and methods employed included standard cell culture techniques using authenticated human cancer cell lines maintained in appropriate media supplemented with fetal bovine serum; transient transfection of plasmid DNA or siRNA using polyethylenimine or Lipofectamine 2000; active Ras pull-down assays performed with CRAF-RBD-conjugated agarose beads followed by SDS-PAGE and western blotting using isoform-specific anti-RAS antibodies; and quantitative assessment of KRAS-effector interactions via the NanoBiT luminescence complementation system. These comprehensive studies establish that selected flavagline derivatives represent a novel class of natural anti-tumor agents capable of suppressing KRAS-driven oncogenesis through a unique mechanism involving disruption of membrane nanodomain organization and KRAS activation.
