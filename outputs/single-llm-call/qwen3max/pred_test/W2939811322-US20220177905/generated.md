## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of molecular genetics and genome engineering, specifically relating to systems and methods for achieving super-Mendelian inheritance of specific allelic variants through CRISPR-Cas9-mediated homology-directed repair mechanisms. More particularly, the invention provides novel allelic-drive systems that enable biased transmission of desired point mutations or small sequence variants across generations without requiring large transgenic insertions, thereby facilitating applications in agriculture, disease vector control, and functional genomics.

## BACKGROUND

Gene drive systems represent a transformative approach in genetic engineering, enabling the biased inheritance of genetic elements beyond classical Mendelian expectations. Such systems hold promise for addressing global challenges including vector-borne diseases, agricultural pest resistance, and conservation biology. Conventional CRISPR-Cas9-based gene drives operate by inserting a cassette encoding both Cas9 endonuclease and a guide RNA (gRNA) into a target genomic locus, thereby converting heterozygous individuals into homozygotes through homology-directed repair (HDR) following Cas9-induced double-strand breaks. However, these canonical gene drives are inherently limited to propagating relatively large DNA cargo sequences—typically ranging from one to ten kilobases—which restricts their utility when the desired trait arises from subtle allelic differences involving only single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) or minor sequence alterations. Many agriculturally and medically relevant traits, such as insecticide sensitivity, drought tolerance, or pathogen resistance, are governed by such fine-scale genetic variations rather than large insertional transgenes. Consequently, there exists a critical need for genetic technologies capable of driving the inheritance of specific allelic variants with high efficiency and precision. The limitations of standard CRISPR-Cas9 approaches become especially apparent in scenarios where the goal is not to insert foreign DNA but to replace one naturally occurring allele with another at an endogenous locus, necessitating innovative strategies that decouple the drive mechanism from bulk transgene propagation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel class of genetic systems termed “allelic gene drives,” which enable the super-Mendelian inheritance of specific allelic variants through two distinct yet complementary mechanisms: copy-cutting and copy-grafting. In the copy-cutting allelic drive system, a dual gRNA cassette is employed wherein a first gRNA mediates conventional gene drive at a linked locus while a second gRNA selectively targets a non-preferred allele at a separate genomic site for cleavage, thereby promoting its replacement via HDR using a co-inherited cleavage-resistant preferred allele as a repair template. This strategy exploits natural allelic differences—such as PAM site disruptions or gRNA core mismatches—that render the favored allele insensitive to Cas9 cleavage. The copy-grafting allelic drive represents a more universally applicable approach wherein a short genomic interval encompassing a desired allelic variant is copied onto a cleavage-sensitive receiver chromosome during HDR-mediated repair initiated at a nearby cut site; critically, the preferred allele need not itself confer cleavage resistance so long as it resides within the region of local gene conversion flanking the break. A key feature enhancing the efficacy and safety of both allelic-drive modalities is the phenomenon of lethal mosaicism, wherein progeny inheriting non-functional NHEJ-induced alleles of essential genes—such as Notch—are eliminated due to somatic mosaicism caused by maternally deposited Cas9-gRNA complexes acting on the wild-type paternal allele. The invention further encompasses methods for constructing allelic-drive elements, introducing them into host organisms, and leveraging these systems for applications including reversal of pesticide resistance, assembly of complex multi-allelic genotypes, and population-level dissemination of beneficial traits in plants, insects, and other eukaryotes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention incorporates by reference the disclosures of U.S. Patent Nos. 9,840,713; 10,150,975; and 10,450,582, as well as published applications US2018/0023087A1 and US2020/0157591A1, for teachings regarding CRISPR-Cas systems, homology-directed repair, and gene drive architectures. As used herein, technical terms shall have the meanings ascribed to them in the art unless otherwise defined. Conventional molecular biology techniques—including PCR amplification, restriction enzyme digestion, ligation, transformation, and sequencing—are performed according to standard protocols known to those skilled in the art. The allelic-drive copy-cutting method involves introducing into a host organism a nucleic acid construct comprising at least two guide polynucleotides: a first guide polynucleotide configured to direct cleavage and copying of a drive element at a first genomic locus, and a second guide polynucleotide configured to selectively cleave a non-preferred allele at a second genomic locus while sparing a co-inherited preferred allele that differs by one or more nucleotides within the PAM site or gRNA-binding region. Repair of the cleaved non-preferred allele occurs via HDR using the intact preferred allele on the homologous chromosome as a template, resulting in super-Mendelian transmission of the desired variant. The allelic-drive copy-grafting method similarly employs a single gRNA to induce a double-strand break near—but not necessarily within—the sequence of a desired allelic variant; during HDR, a segment of DNA spanning the variant is copied from the donor chromosome to the receiver chromosome, effectively grafting the favored allele onto the repaired locus. Insertion of allelic-drive elements is achieved via standard transgenesis methods including embryo microinjection, viral delivery, or particle bombardment, depending on the host species. Conversion efficiency is influenced by factors including homology arm length, chromosomal proximity between drive and target loci, and the presence of inversions that disrupt pairing. Integration of Cas endonuclease may be provided in cis (within the drive element) or in trans (from an unlinked transgene), with perduring Cas9-gRNA complexes enabling residual “shadow-drive” activity in subsequent generations even after segregation of the Cas9 source. Progeny survival is significantly impacted by lethal mosaicism, a process wherein maternal deposition of active Cas9-gRNA complexes causes mosaic biallelic disruption of essential genes in heterozygous offspring carrying NHEJ-induced loss-of-function alleles, leading to dominant lethality. This mechanism effectively purges non-functional resistance alleles from the population. Essential gene modification is thus coupled with functional recoded sequences that preserve gene function while conferring cleavage resistance. Gene examples suitable for allelic drive include Notch, voltage-gated sodium channels, and acetylcholinesterase—loci associated with dominant phenotypes and fitness costs upon mutation. Selective elimination of undesired alleles is achieved through strategic gRNA design targeting critical exonic regions. Germline editing fidelity is enhanced by ensuring that cleavage-sensitive alleles are efficiently repaired using homologous templates. Allele assembly in plants and insects is facilitated by iterative crossing schemes incorporating allelic-drive cassettes. Transitional phrases such as “comprising” are inclusive, whereas “consisting of” excludes additional elements. Ranges are expressed as “about X to Y,” where “about” denotes ±10%. Nucleic acids include DNA, RNA, and analogs such as locked nucleic acids (LNAs). Cas proteins encompass Cas9, Cas12, and engineered variants with altered PAM specificities. Guide polynucleotides consist of variable targeting (VT) domains complementary to target sequences and constant effector-recruiting (CER) domains that bind Cas proteins. Homology arms typically span 0.5–2 kb with ≥95% identity to the target locus. Genetic modifications may include SNPs, indels, or epitope tags. Delivery into host cells employs microinjection, electroporation, or Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. Applications span combating malaria via Anopheles modification, reversing glyphosate resistance in weeds, and stacking yield-enhancing alleles in polyploid crops like wheat. Model organisms include Drosophila melanogaster, Arabidopsis thaliana, Mus musculus, and Danio rerio. The scope of the invention encompasses all embodiments consistent with the disclosed principles.

### EXAMPLES

Experimental validation of the invention was conducted in Drosophila melanogaster using the X-linked Notch locus, which harbors both loss-of-function (N−) and gain-of-function Abruptex (NAx) alleles exhibiting dominant wing phenotypes. A DsRed-marked y<ccN> CopyCat element was engineered to carry two gRNAs: gRNA-y targeting the yellow locus for self-copying, and gRNA-N+ designed to cleave the wild-type Notch+ allele but not the NAx16 variant due to a G→A substitution disrupting the PAM site. F1 “master females” heterozygous for y<ccN> NAx16 and a vasa-Cas9 transgene exhibited 93.6% transmission of NAx16 to F2 males—significantly exceeding Mendelian expectations—alongside 85.3% transmission of the DsRed marker. Molecular analysis confirmed conversion of receiver chromosomes via HDR, while NHEJ-induced N− alleles were eliminated in males due to hemizygous lethality. In F2 females, lethal mosaicism—caused by maternal Cas9-gRNA complexes acting on the paternal N+ allele in N−/+ heterozygotes—prevented recovery of N− lines unless fathers carried cleavage-insensitive N+IS alleles. Crossing master females to N+IS males yielded 22% N−/+ females with characteristic wing notching, confirming the lethal mosaicism hypothesis. Further tests showed 100% lethality when ccN was combined with N− alleles in the presence of Cas9. Shadow-drive activity persisted into the F3 generation, with ~30% conversion rates attributable to perduring Cas9-gRNA complexes. Co-drive analysis revealed strong correlation between y<ccN> and NAx16 inheritance (92.2% co-conversion vs. 51.6% without y<ccN> copying), dependent on normal chromosome pairing as inversions abolished co-drive. Copy-grafting was demonstrated by driving a wild-type N+IS allele over a cleavage-sensitive NAxE2 variant located 21 bp upstream of the cut site, achieving 78.2% conversion—comparable to copy-cutting. Cage trials showed sustained increase in N+IS frequency over five generations. These results confirm that both allelic-drive strategies are highly efficient, with lethal mosaicism providing a built-in safeguard against resistance. The invention’s broad applicability is evidenced by successful drive in cis and trans configurations, compatibility with multiple Cas9 sources (vasa, nanos), and feasibility in diverse taxa. Run-away NHEJ is mitigated by co-drive linkage and lethal mosaicism, ensuring preferential propagation of functional alleles. Thus, allelic-drive systems offer a robust platform for precision genetic manipulation across biological contexts.
