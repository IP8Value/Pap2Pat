# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the discovery and functional characterization of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) in eukaryotic organisms, particularly their essential roles in growth, development, and reproduction. More specifically, the invention encompasses novel biological pathways, regulatory mechanisms, and molecular components involved in mmBCFA biosynthesis, metabolism, and homeostasis in non-human animals, with a primary focus on the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans as a model system. The invention further provides methods for modulating mmBCFA levels through genetic, dietary, or pharmacological interventions, and discloses applications of mmBCFAs and their derivatives—particularly methyl esters—as nutraceuticals, pharmaceuticals, and research tools for investigating lipid biology, insulin signaling, developmental regulation, and metabolic disease.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Fatty acids constitute a fundamental class of biomolecules that serve structural, energetic, and signaling functions across all domains of life. While straight-chain saturated and unsaturated fatty acids have been extensively studied in eukaryotes, branched-chain fatty acids, particularly monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs), have received comparatively little attention despite their widespread presence in nature. In bacteria, mmBCFAs such as C15ISO and C17ISO are well-documented components of membrane lipids, where they modulate fluidity, permeability, and adaptation to environmental stressors like temperature extremes. These bacterial mmBCFAs are synthesized de novo from branched-chain amino acid precursors—leucine, isoleucine, and valine—via the branched-chain α-keto-acid dehydrogenase (BCKAD) complex, which generates branched acyl-CoA primers for fatty acid elongation by fatty acid synthase (FAS).

In eukaryotes, mmBCFAs have been sporadically detected in fungi, plants, and animals, including humans. They are found in specialized tissues such as skin, sebaceous glands, hair, brain, blood, and even cancer cells. Notably, C21anteISO is covalently bound to hair proteins and contributes to hydrophobicity, while C17anteISO esterified to cholesterol has been shown to activate protein biosynthesis enzymes. Moreover, C15ISO exhibits selective pro-apoptotic activity against human cancer cells, suggesting potential therapeutic relevance. Despite these observations, the biosynthetic pathways, regulatory networks, and physiological functions of mmBCFAs in eukaryotes have remained largely uncharacterized due to the absence of identified biosynthetic enzymes and suitable genetic models.

A critical knowledge gap exists regarding whether eukaryotes can synthesize mmBCFAs de novo or rely solely on dietary acquisition. Although mmBCFAs are present in certain foods and commensal bacteria, their endogenous production in animals was previously unconfirmed. Furthermore, the molecular machinery responsible for elongating short-chain mmBCFA precursors into physiologically active long-chain species (e.g., C15ISO and C17ISO) had not been identified in any eukaryotic system. Additionally, the regulatory mechanisms governing mmBCFA homeostasis—including transcriptional control, feedback inhibition, and cross-talk with other metabolic pathways such as insulin signaling and lipid biosynthesis—were entirely unknown. This lack of understanding has hindered the development of diagnostic, therapeutic, or nutritional strategies targeting mmBCFA-related physiology and disease.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a non-human animal model, specifically Caenorhabditis elegans, genetically and biochemically engineered to elucidate the biosynthesis, regulation, and function of monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs). This model enables the study of mmBCFA-dependent processes in a whole-organism context with high genetic tractability. The invention further provides isolated cell models derived from such organisms, modified to overexpress, knock down, or delete key genes involved in mmBCFA metabolism, thereby facilitating high-throughput screening and mechanistic studies.

The invention discloses methods to identify compounds that modulate mmBCFA biosynthesis or function. These methods involve exposing the non-human animal or cell model to test compounds and measuring changes in mmBCFA levels via gas chromatography or reporter gene expression. Compounds that increase or decrease specific mmBCFAs—such as C15ISO or C17ISO—are identified as potential therapeutics or research tools. Complementarily, the invention includes methods to regulate mmBCFA levels through administration of exogenous mmBCFAs or their derivatives, particularly methyl esters, which exhibit enhanced solubility and cellular uptake.

The invention also encompasses formulations comprising mmBCFAs or mmBCFA methyl esters in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, designed for oral, topical, or systemic delivery. These formulations may be used to treat conditions associated with mmBCFA deficiency, such as developmental arrest or metabolic disorders. Additionally, the invention provides a method to increase endogenous mmBCFA production by administering compounds that activate key biosynthetic enzymes or transcription factors, such as LPD-1, the C. elegans homolog of mammalian SREBP-1c.

Repeated iterations of compound identification and mmBCFA regulation methods enable iterative optimization of lead molecules. The invention thus establishes a comprehensive platform for discovering and developing agents that target mmBCFA pathways for therapeutic, nutraceutical, or agricultural applications. Formulations may include purified mmBCFAs extracted from bacterial sources or chemically synthesized, formulated with excipients to enhance stability and bioavailability. The method to increase mmBCFA levels may involve dietary supplementation, genetic intervention, or pharmacological activation of biosynthetic enzymes like ELO-5, ELO-6, or ACS-1.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) are now recognized as essential metabolites in eukaryotes, particularly in the nematode Caenorhabditis elegans, where they are required for normal growth, development, and reproduction. Unlike bacteria, which utilize FAS for mmBCFA elongation, C. elegans employs a distinct set of long-chain fatty acid elongation enzymes—ELO-5 and ELO-6—to produce C15ISO and C17ISO from shorter precursors. Biosynthesis begins with branched-chain amino acids, which are transaminated and decarboxylated by the BCKAD complex to yield branched acyl-CoA primers. RNAi-mediated knockdown of BCKAD components (e.g., Y39E4A.3) significantly reduces C15ISO and C17ISO levels, confirming conservation of early biosynthetic steps between bacteria and eukaryotes.

Genetic and biochemical analyses demonstrate that mmBCFAs are indispensable for C. elegans viability. Suppression of elo-5, which encodes an elongase acting on both C13ISO and C15ISO, causes complete L1 larval arrest in progeny, a phenotype fully rescued only by dietary C17ISO or C17anteISO. Neither straight-chain nor polyunsaturated fatty acids, nor mmBCFAs of other chain lengths, can substitute, underscoring the unique physiological role of 17-carbon mmBCFAs. The rescue is reversible: arrested L1 larvae resume development upon C17ISO supplementation, indicating that mmBCFA deficiency triggers a developmental checkpoint rather than irreversible damage.

Different mmBCFA species exhibit distinct biological activities based on carbon backbone length. C13ISO supports partial rescue of elo-5 mutants but cannot be elongated to C17ISO when administered as a free fatty acid, suggesting that esterified forms (e.g., from bacterial diets like Stenotrophomonas maltophilia) are required for efficient metabolic incorporation. This highlights the importance of delivery format—methyl esters of mmBCFAs show superior solubility and permeability, enabling effective dietary supplementation without toxicity.

The acetyl-CoA synthetase ACS-1 is essential for embryogenesis; its suppression leads to embryonic lethality and defects in eggshell and adult cuticle formation, likely due to impaired activation of mmBCFAs for incorporation into structural lipids. Genetic interactions exist between acs-1 and zen-4, a kinesin-like protein involved in cytokinesis, suggesting that mmBCFA-derived lipids may influence cytoskeletal dynamics or membrane integrity during cell division.

mmBCFAs intersect with the conserved DAF-2/DAF-16 insulin-signaling pathway. Deficiency in C15ISO/C17ISO stimulates nuclear translocation of the FOXO transcription factor DAF-16, upregulates stress-response genes like sod-3, and induces dauer-like L1 arrest—a phenotype mimicking starvation. This positions mmBCFAs as metabolic signals linking nutrient status to developmental decisions. Furthermore, mmBCFA biosynthesis is coupled to protein uptake: RNAi of the oligopeptide transporter pep-2 reduces mmBCFA levels, likely by limiting branched-chain amino acid availability, and downregulates elo-5 transcriptionally.

Regulation occurs at multiple levels. The TOR pathway suppresses elo-5 expression under nutrient-replete conditions, while exogenous mmBCFAs downregulate pantothenate kinase pnk-1, suggesting feedback control linking CoA metabolism to mmBCFA homeostasis. The SREBP homolog LPD-1 directly activates elo-5 and elo-6 transcription; lpd-1(RNAi) reduces mmBCFA levels, and conversely, mmBCFA deficiency upregulates lpd-1 in amphid neurons, revealing a reciprocal regulatory loop.

Human cells can elongate exogenous short-chain mmBCFAs (e.g., C13ISO) into C15ISO and C17ISO, implying conservation of elongation machinery. Key regulatory proteins include NHR-49 (a nuclear hormone receptor regulating fat metabolism), PNK-1 (pantothenate kinase), and RuvB-like DNA-binding proteins, all identified via microarray screens as responsive to mmBCFA status. mmBCFA methyl esters are non-toxic, efficiently incorporated into lipid fractions, and can be sourced from mmBCFA-producing bacteria or chemical synthesis.

The invention enables the creation of modified non-human organisms—via CRISPR, RNAi, or transgenesis—with altered mmBCFA biosynthesis for functional studies or bioproduction. Screening platforms use GFP reporters (e.g., elo-5Prom::GFP) to identify compounds that modulate pathway activity. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising C17ISO methyl ester are proposed for treating Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD), where BCKAD mutations cause mmBCFA deficiency. Such supplements could restore mmBCFA levels, potentially ameliorating symptoms like hair abnormalities or neurological deficits.

Methods for identifying regulatory compounds involve culturing modified C. elegans or mammalian cells, treating with test agents, and quantifying mmBCFAs via gas chromatography or reporter fluorescence. Agonists or antagonists of targets like DAF-2, LPD-1, or ACS-1 are evaluated for their ability to modulate mmBCFA levels and associated phenotypes. Homologues of C. elegans genes in mammals (e.g., SREBP-1c for LPD-1, ACSS2 for ACS-1) allow translation of findings to human physiology.

Experimental validation includes twenty-two key studies: (1) GC profiling confirms mmBCFA presence in wild-type worms; (2) elo-5/6 RNAi reduces C15ISO/C17ISO; (3) BCKAD RNAi phenocopies elo-5 deficiency; (4) elo-5(gk208) null mutant arrests at L1; (5) C17ISO rescues arrest; (6) S. maltophilia feeding rescues via C15ISO elongation; (7) FA staging shows C17ISO accumulation during oogenesis; (8) Correlation analysis links C17ISO to linoleic and vaccenic acids; (9) lpd-1 RNAi reduces mmBCFAs; (10) LPD-1 and LPD-2 have divergent roles; (11) GFP reporters confirm LPD-1 regulation of elo-5/6; (12) FAS RNAi does not affect mmBCFA levels; (13) lpd-1 is upregulated in amphid neurons during mmBCFA deficiency; (14) Microarray identifies pnk-1, nhr-49, acs-1, and C27H6.2 as responsive genes; (15) RNAi of these genes alters FA profiles; (16) acs-1/pnk-1 reporters are upregulated in elo-5(RNAi); (17) pnk-1 is LPD-1-dependent; (18) DiI staining localizes LPD-1 to amphid neurons; (19) Rescue assays validate specificity of C17ISO; (20) Human cell elongation assays demonstrate cross-species functionality; (21) Toxicity assays confirm safety of mmBCFA supplementation; (22) MSUD-relevant models support therapeutic application.

These findings collectively establish mmBCFAs as central regulators of eukaryotic development and metabolism, with broad implications for nutrition, aging, and metabolic disease.
