# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Human endogenous retrovirus (HERV) sequences constitute a substantial fraction of the human genome, representing remnants of ancient retroviral infections that became permanently integrated into the germline DNA of ancestral primates. Over evolutionary time, these sequences have been inherited in a Mendelian fashion and now account for approximately 8.29% of the human genomic landscape. Although the majority of HERV elements are transcriptionally silent due to accumulated mutations, truncations, or epigenetic silencing mechanisms, certain families retain partial or full protein-coding potential. Notably, some HERV loci possess open reading frames capable of encoding structural or enzymatic proteins analogous to those found in exogenous retroviruses. Under normal physiological conditions, host defense mechanisms—including APOBEC3 family proteins—effectively suppress HERV expression. However, pathological states such as viral co-infections can disrupt these regulatory controls, leading to aberrant transcription and translation of HERV-derived polypeptides. This phenomenon is particularly evident in the context of human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) infection, where the viral accessory protein Vif antagonizes APOBEC3 activity, thereby creating a permissive intracellular environment not only for HIV-1 replication but also for the reactivation of dormant HERV elements. Furthermore, certain HERV transcripts contain RNA export signals recognized by the HIV-1 Rev protein, facilitating nuclear egress and subsequent translation. Consequently, HERV-encoded antigens may be processed and presented on the surface of HIV-1-infected cells via major histocompatibility complex class I (MHC-I) molecules, rendering these cells visible to cytotoxic CD8+ T lymphocytes. The immune recognition of such self-derived but infection-induced antigens represents a novel axis of antiviral immunity with significant implications for diagnostics, therapeutics, and vaccine design.

### Literature

Prior research has established that HERV-K (HML-2) is among the most recently integrated and biologically active HERV families in humans, with several loci retaining intact *gag*, *pol*, and *env* genes. Studies have demonstrated the presence of HERV-K RNA and proteins in the plasma and tissues of individuals infected with HIV-1, suggesting a causal link between exogenous retroviral infection and endogenous retroviral reactivation. Additionally, reconstructed infectious HERV-K particles have been generated from consensus sequences, confirming the residual coding competence of these genomic fossils. In parallel, immunological investigations have revealed T cell responses against HERV-derived peptides in patients with multiple sclerosis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and certain cancers, implicating HERVs in autoimmune and neoplastic pathogenesis. However, the role of HERV-specific immunity in the context of lentiviral infections such as HIV-1 remained largely unexplored until recent findings indicated that HIV-1 infection induces the expression of diverse HERV families—including HERV-H, HERV-L, and HERV-W—and elicits measurable CD8+ T cell responses against their encoded epitopes. These responses correlate inversely with plasma viral load and exhibit phenotypic features associated with effective viral control, such as terminal differentiation and enhanced cytotoxic potential. Importantly, such responses are absent in both healthy controls and individuals with chronic hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection, underscoring their specificity to HIV-1–mediated dysregulation. Moreover, functional assays have confirmed that HERV-specific CD8+ T cells can lyse autologous target cells presenting cognate HERV peptides, demonstrating their capacity to recognize and eliminate HIV-1–infected cells through a surrogate antigen mechanism. These collective observations challenge the traditional paradigm of antiviral immunity by expanding the universe of targetable epitopes beyond the viral proteome itself and introduce HERV-encoded polypeptides as viable candidates for immunotherapeutic and prophylactic interventions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides human endogenous retrovirus (HERV)-derived polypeptides and compositions comprising said polypeptides for use in the diagnosis, monitoring, prevention, and treatment of diseases associated with dysregulated HERV expression, particularly those induced or exacerbated by lentiviral infections such as HIV-1. The invention further encompasses immunogenic compositions containing isolated HERV polypeptides that stimulate robust and specific CD8+ T cell responses capable of recognizing and eliminating cells presenting HERV antigens—a hallmark of HIV-1–infected cells. These polypeptides, which may be derived from HERV families including but not limited to HERV-K, HERV-H, HERV-L, and HERV-W, are presented herein as standalone antigens, fusion proteins, multimerized constructs, or conjugates with carriers or lipids to enhance immunogenicity, stability, or delivery. The invention also includes methods of using these compositions as vaccines to prime or boost anti-HERV immunity, thereby enabling the immune system to target HIV-1–infected cells irrespective of viral sequence variation. Additionally, the invention provides diagnostic methods based on the detection of HERV polypeptides or anti-HERV antibodies in biological samples, as well as therapeutic strategies for cancer and autoimmune disorders wherein HERV reactivation contributes to pathogenesis. By leveraging the invariant nature of HERV sequences across all human cells, the invention offers a novel approach to achieving broad-spectrum and durable immune control over rapidly mutating pathogens like HIV-1.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention centers on human endogenous retrovirus (HERV)-encoded polypeptides and their use in immunogenic compositions designed to elicit protective or therapeutic immune responses. These polypeptides, derived from conserved regions of HERV *gag*, *pol*, *env*, or accessory genes, serve as targets for CD8+ T cells in the context of HIV-1 infection, where their expression is aberrantly upregulated. Immunogenic compositions of the invention comprise one or more isolated HERV polypeptides, optionally formulated with adjuvants, carriers, or delivery systems to enhance antigen presentation and T cell priming. Such compositions may be used to vaccinate individuals at risk of or infected with HIV-1, thereby inducing cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) capable of recognizing and destroying HIV-1–infected cells through the presentation of invariant HERV epitopes. Pharmaceutical formulations include aqueous solutions, emulsions, liposomes, and sustained-release depots, tailored for parenteral, mucosal, or enteral administration. Patient response to therapy may be monitored by measuring HERV-specific T cell frequencies via ELISPOT or intracellular cytokine staining, or by quantifying HERV RNA or protein levels in plasma. Exemplary HERV polypeptides include those derived from HERV-K (e.g., gi|52001472), HERV-H (gi|44887889), HERV-L (accession 162563), and HERV-W (gi|52000737), particularly peptides such as HERV-L IQ10 and HERV-H LI13. Fusion proteins may be constructed by linking HERV polypeptides to immunostimulatory domains (e.g., Fc regions, cytokines), while multimerized forms enhance avidity through repetitive epitope display.

### Isolated HERV Polypeptides

Isolated HERV polypeptides are polypeptide molecules derived from HERV open reading frames that have been separated from native cellular components and are substantially free of other proteins, nucleic acids, or contaminants. These polypeptides may consist of full-length HERV proteins or immunogenic fragments thereof, typically ranging from 8 to 50 amino acids in length for MHC class I presentation. They are useful as antigens in diagnostic assays, as immunogens in vaccines, or as targets for monoclonal antibody development. Isolation may be achieved through recombinant expression in bacterial, yeast, insect, or mammalian cells followed by chromatographic purification, or by chemical synthesis.

### HERV Polypeptides

HERV polypeptides encompass amino acid sequences encoded by any human endogenous retrovirus locus, including members of the HERV-K, HERV-H, HERV-L, HERV-W, and related families. These polypeptides may be full-length structural proteins (e.g., Gag, Env) or shorter peptides corresponding to T cell epitopes. Length varies from 8 to over 1000 amino acids, depending on the intended use. Sequence identity is assessed by alignment algorithms such as BLAST or ClustalW, with ≥80% identity over the full length considered substantial. Examples include HERV-K Gag (gi|52001472), HERV-H Env (gi|44887889), and HERV-L ORF products (Retrosearch IDs 162563, etc.). Variations may include conservative substitutions (e.g., Leu→Ile), deletions, or insertions that do not abolish immunogenicity. Additional examples include HERV-W Env (Syncytin-1, gi|52000737) and HERV-K Rec. Further examples encompass peptides spanning residues 100–110 of HERV-L ORF (IQ10: Ile-Gln-X-X-X-X-X-X-X-X). Variations may involve N- or C-terminal extensions to improve solubility or stability. Final examples include multiepitope strings combining HERV-L IQ10 with HERV-H LI13.

### Fusion Proteins

Fusion proteins consist of a HERV polypeptide covalently linked to a heterologous polypeptide domain that confers enhanced stability, immunogenicity, or targeting. The HERV moiety and fusion partner are joined directly or via a peptide linker. Structure may be N-terminal or C-terminal fusions. Examples of partners include IgG Fc regions, heat shock proteins (Hsp70), granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), or viral capsid proteins.

### Multimerized HERV Polypeptides

Multimerized HERV polypeptides comprise two or more copies of a HERV epitope arranged in tandem or branched configurations to increase T cell receptor engagement. Multimers may be linear or dendrimeric, connected by flexible or rigid linkers. Examples include di-, tri-, or tetra-valent HERV-L IQ10 constructs. Linkers may be glycine-serine repeats (e.g., GGGGS) or proline-rich sequences to modulate flexibility. Methods of linking include genetic fusion, chemical crosslinking (e.g., SMCC), or sortase-mediated ligation. Additional examples include HERV-H LI13 trimers displayed on ferritin nanoparticles.

### Carriers

Carriers are macromolecules or particles to which HERV polypeptides are attached to enhance immunogenicity or delivery. Uses include vaccine platforms and diagnostic reagents. Examples include keyhole limpet hemocyanin (KLH), bovine serum albumin (BSA), and virus-like particles (VLPs). Additional examples encompass gold nanoparticles, polylactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) microparticles, and dendrimers. Conjugation methods include maleimide-thiol chemistry, EDC/NHS coupling, or click chemistry. Examples of conjugates include HERV-L IQ10-KLH. Lipidation involves attachment of lipid moieties (e.g., palmitate, myristate) to promote membrane association. Examples include N-myristoylated HERV-K Gag peptides. Linker molecules include PEG spacers or disulfide-containing bridges. Uses include enhancing uptake by antigen-presenting cells.

### Compositions

HERV polypeptide compositions contain one or more HERV-derived antigens in a pharmaceutically acceptable matrix. Components may include buffers, salts, stabilizers, and preservatives. Single-type compositions contain one HERV polypeptide (e.g., HERV-L IQ10 alone). Multi-type compositions combine multiple HERV epitopes (e.g., IQ10 + LI13). Pharmaceutical compositions are sterile, pyrogen-free formulations suitable for injection or mucosal delivery.

### Production of HERV Polypeptides

Production methods include solid-phase peptide synthesis for short epitopes (<50 aa) and recombinant expression in *E. coli*, yeast, or mammalian cells for longer proteins. Purification employs affinity chromatography (e.g., His-tag/Ni-NTA), ion exchange, or size exclusion.

### Immunogenic Compositions Comprising a HERV Polypeptide

Immunogenic compositions are formulations designed to provoke adaptive immunity. HERV polypeptides included are those capable of binding MHC class I and activating CD8+ T cells. T cell epitopes are typically 8–11 aa with anchor residues matching HLA supertypes. Immune responses include IFN-γ secretion, cytotoxicity, and memory formation. Single-type compositions focus on one epitope; multi-type aim for broader coverage.

## EXAMPLES

The invention is exemplified by studies demonstrating T cell responses to HERV peptides in HIV-1+ individuals. ELISPOT and 51Cr release assays confirmed functionality.

### Example 1

HIV-1+ volunteers (n=29) and HIV-1− controls (n=13) plus HCV+ controls (n=3) were enrolled. Peptides were selected from HERV databases using epitope prediction software (SYFPEITHI, NetMHC). ELISPOT showed strong IFN-γ responses to HERV peptides only in HIV-1+ subjects (p<0.001). HERV-K RNA was elevated in HIV-1+ plasma (p=0.016). Transcript levels correlated with infection status. Alignments revealed short regions of HIV-1/HERV identity. High-frequency responses occurred even with low identity (<4 aa shared), ruling out cross-reactivity as sole mechanism. Inverse correlation between HERV T cell responses and viral load (r=−0.49, p=0.03) suggested protective role. 51Cr assays confirmed killing of peptide-pulsed B cells by HERV-specific CTLs. No killing in HIV-1− donors. Results indicate HERV expression is HIV-1–driven and immunogenic. Vaccination with HERV peptides could educate immunity to target infected cells universally, overcoming HIV-1 variability. This paradigm addresses key hurdles in HIV vaccine development. HERVs also implicated in autoimmunity, expanding therapeutic potential.
