## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of people counting systems and methods, particularly those based on radar measurements. More specifically, the invention pertains to a computer-implemented method and system for estimating the number of people present in a monitored environment using radar sensor data processed through a neural network architecture that leverages both macro-Doppler and micro-Doppler features extracted from range-Doppler measurement maps. The invention further encompasses a novel loss function for training such neural networks, designed to enforce an ordered embedding space that reflects the ordinal nature of people count labels, thereby improving regression accuracy and robustness in real-world deployment scenarios.

## BACKGROUND

People counting is a critical task in numerous applications, including occupancy estimation for building automation, surveillance for security and safety, customer traffic analysis in retail environments, and energy management in heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC) systems. Accurate and reliable estimation of the number of individuals in a given space enables intelligent resource allocation, enhanced user experience, and operational efficiency. Traditional approaches have heavily relied on image-based methods using computer vision techniques. While these methods can achieve high accuracy under controlled conditions, they suffer from significant limitations in practical deployments. Chief among these are privacy concerns, as cameras capture identifiable visual information, and performance degradation under adverse environmental conditions such as poor lighting, occlusions, or inclement weather.

Radar-based sensing has emerged as a compelling alternative to optical methods due to its inherent advantages. Radar systems operate effectively in complete darkness, are unaffected by visual obstructions like smoke or fog, and do not capture personally identifiable information, thereby addressing privacy constraints. However, conventional radar-based people counting methods face their own set of challenges, particularly when employing low-cost, short-range radar sensors in complex, confined environments such as vehicle cabins or train carriages. These challenges include low spatial resolution, signal attenuation due to occlusion, and the complex nature of human radar cross-sections, which result from the superposition of reflections from multiple body parts moving at different velocities. This complexity manifests in the radar signal as a combination of macro-Doppler shifts—corresponding to the bulk motion of the body—and micro-Doppler modulations—arising from limb movements and other fine-scale motions. Traditional signal processing techniques often fail to adequately disentangle and exploit these complementary features for accurate counting, especially in dense or dynamic scenes.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method and system for people counting based on radar measurements that overcomes the limitations of prior art by leveraging a dual-path neural network architecture and a novel training methodology. The method comprises determining a first range-Doppler measurement map and a second range-Doppler measurement map from a radar measurement dataset acquired by a radar sensor. The first range-Doppler measurement map is configured to emphasize macro-Doppler features, while the second range-Doppler measurement map is configured to emphasize micro-Doppler features. These two measurement maps are input into a first data processing pipeline and a second data processing pipeline, respectively, of a neural network algorithm. Each pipeline includes one or more range-Doppler convolutional layers configured to extract spatial and velocity features from the respective input maps. The outputs of the two pipelines are then processed in a regression block, which outputs a one-dimensional value predicting the number of people in the scene.

In a specific embodiment, the regression block includes a fully-connected layer with a single neuron to produce the final count prediction. In another embodiment, the regression block outputs a higher-dimensional value representing a feature vector in an embedding space, and the people count is predicted based on the position of this feature vector within the embedding space. The first range-Doppler measurement map is defined as a macro-Doppler range-Doppler measurement map, obtained by applying a high-pass filter to suppress static clutter and emphasize bulk motion signatures. The second range-Doppler measurement map is defined as a micro-Doppler range-Doppler measurement map, obtained by integrating multiple frames to enhance the visibility of subtle, time-varying micro-motions. The velocity resolution of the radar system is determined based on the frame repetition frequency and the number of chirps per frame, ensuring sufficient discrimination of human motion signatures.

The invention further encompasses a computer program product comprising program code stored on a non-transitory computer-readable medium, wherein the program code, when loaded and executed on a computer, causes the computer to perform the computer-implemented method described herein. The method includes determining the first and second range-Doppler measurement maps, estimating the people count by inputting the measurement maps into the neural network algorithm, and applying a tracking filter to track the evolution of the output in the embedding space over time to enhance temporal stability. The neural network algorithm is trained using multiple training radar measurement datasets labeled with ground-truth people counts. The training employs a label-aware ranked loss function, which explicitly exploits the ordinal relationship between count labels to shape the embedding space such that feature vectors corresponding to similar counts are closer together than those corresponding to dissimilar counts, thereby improving regression performance.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS

Electrical devices and circuits for sensing and signal processing are fundamental to modern monitoring and control systems. Their functionality typically involves the acquisition of physical signals, their conversion into electrical form, and subsequent processing to extract meaningful information. However, limitations of conventional electrical devices and circuits, particularly in the context of human sensing, include susceptibility to environmental interference, limited resolution, and an inability to discern complex patterns in raw data without sophisticated post-processing. These limitations motivate the development of people counting systems based on radar measurements, which offer a robust, privacy-preserving alternative to optical methods.

A radar measurement operation begins with the emission of electromagnetic waves by a radar sensor. The sensor, which may be a Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW) radar operating in the 60 GHz band, transmits a series of linear frequency-modulated chirps. The pulsed operation of the radar sensor involves repeating these chirps at a defined chirp repetition time, and grouping them into frames with a specific frame repetition frequency. When these waves encounter objects in the environment, they are reflected back to the sensor. The Doppler frequency shift, which arises from the relative motion between the radar and the target, is a key parameter that allows the estimation of target velocity. This principle is applied to detect and characterize human motion, as people inherently exhibit both translational and rotational movements.

To handle the complexity of human radar signatures, a machine-learning algorithm, specifically a deep neural network, is employed. The input to this algorithm is derived from range-Doppler measurement maps, also known as Range-Doppler Images (RDIs). These RDIs are obtained by performing a two-dimensional Fourier transformation on the raw radar data. The first dimension, fast-time, corresponds to the sampling of the intermediate frequency signal within a single chirp and is used to compute range. The second dimension, slow-time, corresponds to the sequence of chirps within a frame and is used to compute Doppler velocity. To further enhance spatial awareness, 2-D angular measurement maps can be generated using a beamforming algorithm applied across multiple receive antennas, and these can be combined with range information to form range-angle measurement maps.

A critical insight of the present invention is the differentiation between micro-Doppler and macro-Doppler features. Macro-Doppler features correspond to the overall velocity of a person’s torso or center of mass, while micro-Doppler features arise from the periodic motion of limbs, such as walking or gesturing. These features carry complementary information: macro-Doppler is robust for detecting presence and gross motion, while micro-Doppler provides fine-grained details that can help distinguish between multiple closely spaced individuals. To exploit this, the invention introduces a first RDI for macro-Doppler features and a second RDI for micro-Doppler features. The macro-Doppler RDI is formed by applying Moving Target Indication (MTI) filtering—a high-pass filter in the slow-time domain—to suppress static clutter and strong reflections from the environment, followed by a 2-D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). The micro-Doppler RDI is formed by integrating multiple consecutive frames to accumulate the subtle, time-varying micro-motions, and then applying MTI filtering and 2-D FFT. A Hamming window is applied along both the fast-time and slow-time dimensions before each FFT to reduce spectral leakage and improve the clarity of the resulting RDIs.

The neural network architecture is designed with two parallel data processing pipelines: a macro-Doppler data processing pipeline and a micro-Doppler data processing pipeline. Each pipeline consists of an encoder branch with multiple 2-D convolutional layers, which perform spatial contraction to extract increasingly abstract features. The architecture may also include a decoder branch for spatial expansion, though for a regression task like people counting, the encoder is often sufficient. The separate processing of micro-Doppler and macro-Doppler features is motivated by their distinct statistical properties and the need to apply specialized feature extraction strategies. However, to capture the correlations between these two modalities, the pipelines are joined at one or more connecting sections. These connecting sections implement feature fusion, which can be achieved through various mechanisms such as a combination layer with learned filter parameters, a concatenation layer followed by a convolutional layer, or a combination of these. This fusion enhances the robustness of the system against variations in radar sensor pose and environmental conditions.

The outputs of the fused pipelines are fed into an output section, which includes a regression block. This block may consist of one or more fully-connected layers that control the dimensionality of the output. In one embodiment, the final layer is a single neuron that directly outputs the predicted people count. In another, more sophisticated embodiment, the regression block outputs a higher-dimensional feature vector that resides in an embedding space. The people count is then inferred from the position of this vector within the embedding space. To improve the temporal stability of the count prediction, postprocessing techniques are applied. A smoothing filter, such as an exponential moving average filter, is used to avoid artificial, frame-to-frame fluctuations in the count. Additionally, a tracking filter, such as a Kalman filter, is applied to track the evolution of the feature vector in the embedding space over time, using a constant velocity motion model to predict its future position.

The training of the neural network is a crucial aspect of the invention. Multiple sets of training radar measurement datasets, each labeled with a ground-truth people count, are obtained. These datasets are preprocessed to generate the macro-Doppler and micro-Doppler RDIs. The neural network is then trained using a label-aware ranked (LAR) loss function. Unlike conventional losses such as Mean Squared Error (MSE) or standard Deep Metric Learning (DML) losses like Triplet loss or Constellation loss, the LAR loss explicitly incorporates the ordinal nature of the count labels. It does so by assigning a weight to negative sample pairs based on the logarithmic distance between their labels and the anchor label, thereby encouraging the embedding space to be not only discriminative but also ordered. This ordering is achieved by minimizing the angles between embeddings of the same label and maximizing the angles between embeddings of different labels in a manner that respects the rank order of the labels, ideally leading to a uniform angular distribution of class centers on a hypersphere.

The system for people counting comprises a radar sensor, which includes transmit and receive antennas, a signal generator, and an analog-to-digital converter, and a processing device, such as a microcontroller or a dedicated AI accelerator. The processing device executes the data processing pipeline, which includes the neural network and the postprocessing filters. The method is illustrated in a flowchart that begins with obtaining a radar measurement dataset, preprocessing it to obtain the two RDIs, inputting them into the neural network, outputting a prediction, and applying the tracking filter. The use cases for this system are diverse, including monitoring people entering and exiting doorways for access control, gathering customer traffic data in retail stores for business intelligence, and counting people in rooms or vehicles for automated HVAC regulation to optimize energy savings.
