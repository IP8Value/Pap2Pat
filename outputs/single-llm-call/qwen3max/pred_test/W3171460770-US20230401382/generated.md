# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to machine learning, and more particularly to systems and methods for incremental training of machine-learned models in response to evolving data distributions, especially in the context of natural language processing. The invention addresses technical challenges associated with maintaining model performance over time as new tokens emerge and the semantic meaning of existing tokens shifts due to dynamic changes in language usage, particularly in high-velocity domains such as social media. The disclosed techniques enable efficient adaptation of pre-trained transformer-based language models—such as BERT—through intelligent vocabulary updates and selective re-training using hard example mining, thereby reducing computational costs while preserving or enhancing predictive accuracy in production environments.

## BACKGROUND

Machine learning models, despite their widespread success, face significant limitations when deployed in real-world settings where data distributions are non-stationary. Traditional supervised learning assumes that training and inference data are drawn from the same underlying distribution—a condition rarely met in practice. In domains characterized by rapid semantic evolution, such as online discourse on social platforms, this assumption is routinely violated, leading to model deterioration over time. This phenomenon manifests as declining accuracy, increased prediction uncertainty, and reduced relevance of model outputs, ultimately undermining user trust and system utility.

Natural language modeling presents a particularly acute instance of this challenge. Language is inherently dynamic: new words enter common usage (e.g., “Covid,” “Zoom”), while existing terms acquire novel connotations or shift in contextual association (e.g., “flattening the curve”). These changes occur at varying rates across domains, with social media exhibiting especially high volatility. Pre-trained language models like BERT, though powerful, are typically trained on static corpora and lack mechanisms to adapt to linguistic drift post-deployment. Empirical studies confirm that even state-of-the-art bidirectional transformers suffer substantial performance degradation when applied to temporally distant data, primarily due to two interrelated factors: vocabulary shift (the emergence of out-of-vocabulary tokens) and semantic shift (the evolution of meaning for in-vocabulary tokens).

Incremental machine learning offers a promising pathway to address these limitations. Unlike batch retraining—which discards prior knowledge and incurs prohibitive computational expense—incremental approaches aim to update models continuously using newly arriving data while preserving previously acquired competencies. However, naive incremental updates risk catastrophic forgetting or inefficient resource utilization if all incoming data are processed indiscriminately. Thus, there exists a critical need for intelligent sampling strategies that identify the most informative examples—those containing emerging tokens or exhibiting significant semantic divergence—for targeted retraining. Such methods must balance representativeness, computational feasibility, and alignment with evolving task objectives, particularly in online serving scenarios where model responsiveness and stability are paramount.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel machine learning method for incrementally updating pre-trained language models in response to evolving linguistic content. The method begins by obtaining a first version of a machine-learned model, such as a BERT-based architecture, pre-trained on historical data with a fixed vocabulary. As new data become available—e.g., from a real-time social media stream—the system accesses this data and identifies tokens that are either newly emergent or semantically divergent relative to the model’s current understanding.

The method then re-trains the model to obtain a second version, incorporating dynamic vocabulary updates that replace obsolete tokens with high-frequency emerging ones while maintaining constant vocabulary size to ensure parameter efficiency. A key innovation lies in the determination of similarity scores between token or sentence embeddings generated by the first and second model versions. These similarity scores, computed via cosine distance or analogous metrics, serve as indicators of semantic instability. Entities—whether individual tokens or entire sequences—with dissimilar embeddings across model versions are flagged as candidates for focused retraining.

Training examples containing these identified entities are selectively sampled to construct a biased training dataset enriched with hard or informative instances. This dataset is then used to incrementally re-train the second version of the model, thereby reinforcing its capacity to handle linguistic evolution without exhaustive reprocessing of all new data. The invention further encompasses non-transitory computer-readable media storing instructions that, when executed by a processor, perform the operations of obtaining model versions, computing embedding similarities, selecting training examples based on dissimilarity thresholds, and executing incremental retraining.

Additionally, the invention introduces a computing system specifically designed for online hard example mining in dynamic language environments. This system includes components for real-time loss monitoring (e.g., Masked Language Modeling loss), vocabulary management, and adaptive sampling. It supports both online and batch learning settings, enabling flexible deployment across cloud infrastructure, edge devices, or hybrid architectures. Various systems and apparatuses are described, including user devices, server clusters, and dedicated training engines, all interoperating via networks to deliver continuously updated, high-performance language models for tasks ranging from classification to semantic search.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

Incremental training of machine-learned models represents a technically advantageous approach to maintaining model relevance in non-stationary environments. The core motivation stems from the observed mismatch between static model assumptions and the reality of evolving data distributions, particularly pronounced in natural language applications. As societal discourse shifts—driven by events, technology, or cultural trends—the vocabulary and semantics of language adapt accordingly. This necessitates model adaptation mechanisms that go beyond periodic full retraining, which is computationally unsustainable and environmentally costly.

Evolving vocabulary in natural language models poses a dual challenge: the introduction of new lexical items (e.g., hashtags like #maga) and the obsolescence of previously common terms. Traditional subword tokenization (e.g., WordPiece) mitigates but does not eliminate this issue, as fragmented representations of novel tokens often fail to capture their intended semantics. Incremental training emerges as a feasible and efficient solution, allowing models to assimilate new knowledge while retaining stable representations for unchanged concepts.

The benefits of incremental training extend beyond computational savings; it enables continuous service delivery without downtime, supports real-time responsiveness to emerging trends, and reduces carbon footprint associated with large-scale model retraining. Moreover, the approach is broadly applicable beyond text—to domains such as image recognition (where object categories evolve), sensor analytics (with drifting environmental conditions), or financial forecasting (amid shifting market dynamics).

A central aspect of the invention is the identification of entities undergoing semantic shift. By comparing embeddings across model versions, the system detects tokens whose contextual meaning has diverged significantly—e.g., country hashtags whose co-occurring terms change from sports to politics. This detection informs intelligent sampling strategies that prioritize training on examples containing such unstable entities, embodying principles of active learning tailored to temporal drift.

The proposed solutions operate effectively in both online and batch settings. In online learning, the system processes streaming examples, maintains logs of loss values, and triggers retraining when performance degradation exceeds thresholds. In batch mode, periodic updates incorporate curated datasets reflecting recent linguistic trends. Technical advantages include reduced training cost (demonstrated at 76.9% savings versus full retraining), improved downstream task performance, and seamless integration into live-serving pipelines. The technologies are domain-agnostic in principle, though optimized for high-churn textual data, and offer scalable, sustainable model maintenance for enterprise and consumer applications alike.

### Example Methods

The invention provides concrete methods for implementing incremental model updates. Initially, a machine-learned model with a defined vocabulary—e.g., a BERT variant with 65K wordpieces—is obtained, typically pre-trained on historical data. Upon arrival of new training data for a current epoch (e.g., tweets from a new year), the system identifies new entities (tokens appearing with high frequency not present in the prior vocabulary) and obsolete entities (low-frequency tokens unlikely to recur). The model’s vocabulary is then modified by swapping out obsolete tokens for new ones, preserving total size for parameter consistency.

The model is incrementally re-trained using this updated vocabulary and a subset of the new data. To enhance efficiency, the method obtains a first version of the model and new training data, then performs an initial incremental re-training to produce a second version. Both versions process the same set of training examples, generating respective embeddings for tokens or sentences ([CLS] vectors). Similarity scores—computed as cosine distances between corresponding embeddings—are determined. Entities exhibiting low similarity (high dissimilarity) are identified as having undergone potential semantic shift.

Training examples containing these dissimilar entities are selected with elevated probability, forming a biased dataset enriched with hard examples. The second version of the model is then incrementally re-trained on this dataset, refining its representations for evolving concepts. This cycle may repeat iteratively: after each retraining, new similarity comparisons inform subsequent sampling rounds, progressively honing model adaptation.

In deployment, the final model performs its designated task (e.g., country hashtag prediction or offensive content detection). In online settings, the system performs real-time learning: as new examples arrive, it computes and logs loss values (e.g., MLM loss). Examples yielding large losses—indicative of poor model fit—are flagged. When a retraining condition is met (e.g., sustained loss increase), the system triggers incremental training using these high-loss examples, updates the model, and deploys the improved version—all without interrupting live traffic.

### Example Devices and Systems

The invention is implemented within a distributed computing ecosystem. A block diagram depicts interconnected components: user computing devices (smartphones, laptops) equipped with input mechanisms (keyboards, microphones) and network interfaces; server computing systems hosting application logic and model inference; and dedicated training computing systems performing model updates.

The model trainer—a specialized software module—orchestrates incremental learning. It accesses training data (text, images, sensor streams), manages vocabulary evolution, executes embedding comparisons, and applies sampling strategies. Machine-learned models, primarily neural networks (including transformers), are stored and served from model repositories. Training data encompass diverse modalities: natural language (social posts, news), speech transcripts, image pixels, latent encodings from autoencoders, statistical aggregates, or IoT sensor readings.

Generalization techniques—dropout, weight decay, data augmentation—are applied during training to prevent overfitting. Personalization mechanisms allow models to adapt to individual user patterns while preserving global coherence. The network—comprising LANs, WANs, or the Internet—enables data flow between devices and servers.

Models are used across tasks: text classification, named entity recognition, sentiment analysis, image captioning, anomaly detection. Image data undergo convolutional processing; text leverages attention-based encoders; speech uses spectrogram inputs; latent codes are decoded or regressed; statistical features feed into dense layers; sensor streams are modeled via recurrent or temporal convolutional networks. The architecture supports extensibility to other tasks through modular design.

## ADDITIONAL DISCLOSURE

The disclosed system exhibits significant flexibility, accommodating various tokenization schemes, embedding comparison metrics, and retraining triggers. The examples provided—including Twitter hashtag modeling, MLM loss monitoring, and cosine-based similarity—are illustrative而非 limiting. Alternative implementations may employ different language models (e.g., RoBERTa, T5), similarity measures (Euclidean distance, KL divergence), or sampling policies (uncertainty-based, diversity-promoting).

Variations are expressly permitted: vocabulary update rules may prioritize semantic coherence over frequency; embedding comparisons could involve alignment techniques (Procrustes analysis) to mitigate space misalignment; online triggers might combine multiple signals (loss, accuracy drop, drift detectors). The core inventive concept—intelligent, similarity-guided incremental retraining in response to linguistic evolution—remains invariant across embodiments, ensuring robustness and broad applicability in an era of perpetually changing data.
