# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs) and their functional analogs, specifically engineered variants of BMPs that exhibit reduced susceptibility to inhibition by endogenous antagonists such as Noggin. The invention further pertains to modified growth and differentiation factors (GDFs) within the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) superfamily that have been structurally altered to resist Noggin-mediated inhibition. These engineered proteins are designed to enhance therapeutic efficacy in clinical applications involving bone regeneration, cartilage repair, and other tissue engineering contexts where native BMP activity is limited by natural regulatory mechanisms.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Bone morphogenetic proteins are a subgroup of the TGF-β superfamily of secreted signaling molecules that play critical roles in embryonic development, skeletal formation, and postnatal tissue homeostasis. First identified for their ability to induce ectopic bone formation, BMPs regulate a wide range of cellular processes including proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and migration. Among the most clinically relevant BMPs are BMP-2 and BMP-7, which have been approved for use in spinal fusion and non-union fracture repair. Despite their therapeutic utility, these recombinant proteins require high doses to achieve clinical efficacy, which can lead to adverse side effects such as ectopic bone formation, inflammation, and immune responses. A major limitation of current BMP-based therapies is the presence of endogenous extracellular antagonists, particularly Noggin, which binds directly to BMPs and prevents their interaction with cell surface receptors. Noggin is itself transcriptionally induced by BMP signaling, creating a potent negative feedback loop that rapidly attenuates BMP activity in vivo. This self-limiting mechanism significantly reduces the bioavailability and duration of action of administered BMPs, thereby necessitating supraphysiological dosing regimens. While structural and functional studies have elucidated key aspects of BMP-Noggin interactions, strategies to circumvent this inhibitory pathway through protein engineering have remained elusive until now.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides designed bone morphogenetic proteins with enhanced resistance to inhibition by Noggin and related antagonists. Specifically, the invention encompasses modified BMPs containing amino acid substitutions that reduce their susceptibility to Noggin-mediated inhibition while preserving or enhancing their biological activity. In particular, the invention includes modified BMP-2, BMP-4, BMP-5, BMP-7, GDF-5, GDF-6, and GDF-7 proteins wherein specific residues corresponding to position 60 of the mature BMP-6 domain have been substituted with lysine or other residues that confer Noggin resistance. The invention also provides chimeric proteins comprising domains from TGF-β superfamily members fused to wild-type BMP-6 or BMP-9 sequences, resulting in constructs resistant to Noggin inhibition. Additionally, the invention includes nucleic acid sequences encoding these modified BMPs and GDFs, as well as expression vectors and host cells for their production. Pharmaceutical compositions containing the modified proteins are provided for therapeutic applications requiring enhanced BMP signaling. The invention further encompasses methods for modulating the inhibition of BMP or GDF activity by Noggin or Noggin-like proteins through administration of the disclosed modified proteins. Non-naturally occurring peptides derived from BMP or GDF sequences with reduced inhibition by Noggin are also claimed, along with their use in pharmaceutical formulations. The scope of the invention extends to all TGF-β superfamily members that can be engineered to incorporate Noggin-resistance-conferring mutations, particularly those homologous to the identified critical residue at position 60 of BMP-6.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Introduction to the Invention Concept

The present invention is based on the discovery that specific amino acid residues within the mature domain of BMP proteins determine their susceptibility to inhibition by Noggin. Through comparative analysis of BMP-6 and BMP-7, which share 73% sequence identity yet exhibit markedly different resistance to Noggin, the inventors identified lysine at position 60 of the mature BMP-6 domain as a critical determinant of Noggin resistance. This finding enables the rational design of BMP variants with enhanced therapeutic potential by introducing analogous substitutions into other BMP and GDF family members.

### Bone Morphogenetic Proteins

Bone morphogenetic proteins constitute a subfamily of the TGF-β superfamily, which itself belongs to the larger cysteine knot cytokine superfamily. The BMP subfamily includes BMP-2 through BMP-15, as well as growth and differentiation factors GDF-5, GDF-6, and GDF-7, which are sometimes designated as BMP-14, BMP-13, and BMP-12, respectively. These proteins are synthesized as precursor polypeptides containing an N-terminal signal peptide, a pro-domain, and a C-terminal mature domain. Following proteolytic cleavage, the mature domain dimerizes to form the biologically active ligand. BMPs signal through heterotetrameric complexes of type I and type II serine/threonine kinase receptors, leading to phosphorylation of receptor-regulated Smads (Smad1/5/8) that complex with Smad4 and translocate to the nucleus to regulate gene expression. BMP activity is modulated by interactions with various extracellular inhibitors, co-receptors, and matrix components that fine-tune signaling gradients during development and tissue repair.

### Interactions Between BMPs and Their Antagonists

BMP antagonists play crucial roles in spatial and temporal regulation of BMP signaling during development and homeostasis. Among these, Noggin is a high-affinity extracellular inhibitor that binds directly to BMP ligands, preventing their interaction with receptors. The DAN family (including Gremlin, Cerberus, and Dan) and Chordin/SOG family represent additional classes of BMP antagonists with distinct binding mechanisms. While Noggin binds BMP-7 with high affinity, the inventors discovered that BMP-6 exhibits marked resistance to Noggin inhibition despite similar binding affinity in surface plasmon resonance assays. This paradox was resolved by demonstrating that BMP-6 maintains receptor binding capacity in the presence of Noggin, whereas BMP-7 does not. Structural analysis revealed that lysine at position 60 in BMP-6 stabilizes the conformation of the receptor-binding interface, reducing Noggin's ability to block receptor engagement. By introducing this residue into other BMPs through site-directed mutagenesis, the inventors created variants with enhanced Noggin resistance. For example, substitution of glutamic acid at position 60 of BMP-7 with lysine (E60K) conferred significant resistance to Noggin inhibition. Similarly, introduction of lysine at the corresponding position in BMP-2 (P36K) increased its resistance to Noggin. The invention encompasses single, double, triple, and higher-order substitutions in BMP-2, BMP-4, BMP-5, BMP-7, GDF-5, GDF-6, and GDF-7, as well as chimeric proteins incorporating Noggin-resistant domains from BMP-6 or BMP-9. The scope extends to all TGF-β superfamily members that can be engineered to incorporate these resistance-conferring mutations.

### Generating Modified BMPs

Modified BMPs of the present invention are defined as non-naturally occurring variants of wild-type BMP proteins containing amino acid substitutions that reduce susceptibility to Noggin inhibition. These modifications are typically introduced at positions corresponding to residue 60 of the mature BMP-6 domain, though additional substitutions may be included to enhance stability, solubility, or receptor specificity. Positional numbering follows the mature domain of the respective wild-type protein after proteolytic cleavage of the pro-domain. Preferred embodiments include BMP-7 variants with E60K, Y65N, Y78H, R48Q, or combinations thereof, with the triple mutant R48Q/E60K/Y65N showing particularly enhanced properties. The modified proteins maintain at least 70% sequence identity with their wild-type counterparts, preferably at least 80%, and more preferably at least 90%, while exhibiting significantly reduced inhibition by Noggin. Co- or post-translational modifications such as glycosylation, phosphorylation, or PEGylation may be incorporated to further optimize pharmacokinetic properties. Nucleic acids encoding these modified proteins are also provided, enabling recombinant production in various expression systems.

### Preparing Nucleic Acids Encoding Modified BMPs

Nucleic acids encoding modified BMPs are prepared using standard molecular biology techniques, including site-directed mutagenesis, PCR-based assembly, and gene synthesis. The coding sequences are optimized for expression in selected host systems while maintaining the desired amino acid substitutions. Synthetic genes may be codon-optimized for mammalian, bacterial, or yeast expression, and may include appropriate restriction sites for cloning into expression vectors.

### Expression Vectors

Expression vectors for producing modified BMPs contain regulatory sequences necessary for transcription and translation in the chosen host system. These include promoters (such as CMV, EF1α, or inducible promoters), enhancers, polyadenylation signals, and selectable markers. Additional elements may include secretion signals, epitope tags for purification, and internal ribosome entry sites for bicistronic expression. Integrating vectors may be used for stable cell line generation, while episomal vectors are suitable for transient expression.

### Transfection/Transformation

Modified BMP nucleic acids are introduced into host cells using standard methods including calcium phosphate precipitation, lipofection, electroporation, or viral transduction. The choice of method depends on the host cell type and whether transient or stable expression is desired. For large-scale production, stable cell lines are typically generated through selection with appropriate antibiotics.

### Hosts for Expression of Modified BMPs

Host cells for expressing modified BMPs include mammalian cells (such as HEK293, CHO, or COS cells), insect cells, yeast, and bacteria. Mammalian cells are preferred for producing properly folded, glycosylated proteins with full biological activity. Bacterial systems may be used for non-glycosylated variants when appropriate.

### Expression Methods

Modified BMPs are produced by culturing transfected host cells under conditions that support protein expression and secretion. Culture media are supplemented with appropriate nutrients and serum, and conditioned media are collected at optimal time points for protein yield. Large-scale production may employ bioreactors with controlled parameters for pH, temperature, and oxygenation.

### Purification

Purification of modified BMPs employs standard chromatographic techniques including affinity chromatography (such as nickel-IMAC for His-tagged proteins), ion exchange, size exclusion, and hydrophobic interaction chromatography. Ultrafiltration and diafiltration are used for concentration and buffer exchange. The degree of purification required depends on the intended application, with some therapeutic uses requiring >95% purity while research applications may tolerate lower purity. Post-translational modifications may be introduced enzymatically or chemically, including PEGylation to enhance half-life, glycosylation to improve solubility, or labeling with isotopes for tracking. Covalent modifications using bifunctional cross-linking agents may create dimers or conjugates with targeting moieties. Biophysical characterization includes assessment of secondary structure, thermal stability, and aggregation state. Biochemical characterization involves receptor binding assays, Smad phosphorylation assays, and functional assays such as alkaline phosphatase induction in osteoblast precursor cells. Animal models for testing include rat nephrotoxic serum nephritis, chronic cyclosporine-induced nephropathy, unilateral ureteral obstruction, streptozotocin-induced diabetic nephropathy, anti-thy1.1 glomerulonephritis, 5/6 remnant kidney, bile duct ligation, CCl4 plus ethanol liver damage, dimethylnitrosamine cirrhosis, thioacetamide liver damage, ovalbumin airway fibrosis, bleomycin lung fibrosis, monocrotaline pulmonary fibrosis, selective irradiation, 6-OHDA Parkinson's model, MPTP Parkinson's model, ALS models, and stroke models. Pharmaceutical compositions are formulated for parenteral, oral, or local administration using carriers, excipients, and delivery systems that enhance stability and bioavailability. Therapeutic uses include inducing bone and cartilage proliferation, regenerating arthritic cartilage, repairing cartilage tears, promoting bone growth in fractures, and treating osteoporosis.

## EXAMPLES

### BMP Induction of Alkaline Phosphatase Activity

A panel of BMPs was tested for their ability to induce alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity in ROS 17/2.8 rat osteosarcoma cells. BMP-6 demonstrated the highest potency (EC50 = 14 ng/ml), followed by BMP-7, BMP-4, BMP-2, and BMP-5. GDF-5 and GDF-6 showed minimal activity. The steep dose-response curves for BMP-6 and BMP-7 contrasted with the shallower slopes of BMP-2 and BMP-4, suggesting differential regulation by inhibitory mechanisms.

### Noggin Inhibition of a Panel of Exemplary BMPs and Related Proteins

Noggin inhibition assays were performed using ALP activity in ROS 17/2.8 cells and BRE-luciferase reporter activity in A549-BRE cells. BMP-4 was most sensitive to Noggin (IC50 = 300 ng/ml), followed by BMP-2 (IC50 = 500 ng/ml), BMP-7 (IC50 = 800 ng/ml), and BMP-6 (IC50 > 5,000 ng/ml). Similar results were obtained in human mesenchymal stem cells (hMSCs) measuring ID-1 gene expression, confirming BMP-6's intrinsic resistance to Noggin across multiple cell types and assay systems.

### BMP-6 Induction of Downstream Genes in Primary Human Bone Marrow-Derived Mesenchymal Stem Cells is Less Susceptible to Noggin Inhibition

In hMSCs, BMP-6 induction of ID-1, Dlx-5, Msx-2, and noggin genes was largely unaffected by Noggin co-treatment, whereas BMP-7-induced expression of these genes was significantly inhibited. Notably, Noggin's own induction by BMP-7 was more strongly suppressed than other target genes, highlighting the potency of this negative feedback loop.

### BMP-7 Variant Proteins with Marked Resistance to Noggin Inhibition

Chimeric proteins were created by replacing regions of BMP-7 with corresponding sequences from BMP-6. The 80-1 chimera (residues 45-80 replaced) exhibited Noggin resistance comparable to BMP-6. Scale-up production and purification confirmed the chimera's enhanced properties. Further mutagenesis identified lysine 60 as critical for resistance, with the E60K single mutant showing significant improvement over wild-type BMP-7.

### Example 5

Sequence alignment of BMP-6 and BMP-7 mature domains revealed key differences at positions 48, 60, 65, and 78. Revertant mutants of BMP-7 with BMP-6 residues at these positions were tested for Noggin resistance. The E60K mutation alone conferred substantial resistance, while triple mutants (R48Q/E60K/Y65N) showed additive effects. Introducing lysine at the corresponding position in BMP-2 (P36K) also increased Noggin resistance. In vivo testing in rabbit ulnar defect models demonstrated that mutant BMPs induced equivalent bone formation at lower doses compared to wild-type proteins. Histological and radiographic analyses confirmed enhanced osteogenic activity, with biomechanical testing showing improved bone strength. Evaluation in human patients demonstrated successful bone growth induction with reduced protein requirements.
