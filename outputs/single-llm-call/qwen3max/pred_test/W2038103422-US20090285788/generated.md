# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel RNA molecules, particularly short-hairpin RNA (shRNA) molecules comprising a double-stranded stem region of defined length and a non-self-complementary loop sequence, and methods for their generation, screening, and therapeutic application. More specifically, the invention provides recombinant expression vectors encoding libraries of such RNA molecules, wherein the stem region includes a substantially random nucleotide sequence of predetermined length, enabling unbiased functional screening for RNA molecules that modulate biological parameters such as cell survival, viral replication, differentiation, or proliferation. The invention further encompasses methods for generating these RNA molecules through enzymatic and synthetic approaches that overcome limitations of conventional RNA library synthesis, including the precise construction of inverted repeat sequences with controlled mismatches and non-complementary loops. Additionally, the invention includes applications of identified RNA molecules in conferring protection against viral infection, inhibiting viral replication, inducing or sustaining cellular differentiation or pluripotency, and serving as therapeutic agents or research tools in disease models, including cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, and viral pathogenesis.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Conventional methods for synthesizing RNA libraries, particularly those designed for RNA interference (RNAi) applications, suffer from significant technical and functional limitations that restrict their utility in unbiased discovery of therapeutically relevant RNA molecules. Traditional RNAi libraries are typically constructed using rational design algorithms that target known gene sequences, thereby precluding the identification of RNA molecules that act through non-canonical mechanisms or that modulate phenotypes by targeting multiple genes simultaneously. While some efforts have been made to generate random RNA libraries, existing approaches often encode small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) rather than short-hairpin RNAs (shRNAs), resulting in reduced potency due to the transient nature of siRNA activity and inefficient cellular uptake. Furthermore, siRNA-encoding libraries frequently utilize dual opposing promoters to transcribe complementary strands, which can lead to transcriptional interference, reduced expression efficiency, and an inability to incorporate structural features such as mismatches within the RNA duplex—features known to enhance the biological activity of endogenous microRNAs. Another critical limitation lies in the fixed stem length of conventional shRNA constructs, which is typically restricted to 19–21 base pairs, despite evidence demonstrating that shRNAs with longer stems (e.g., 27–29 base pairs) exhibit significantly greater RNAi potency. However, extending stem length in random libraries presents formidable synthetic challenges, including the need to generate the reverse complement of a random sequence on the same DNA strand, insert a defined non-complementary loop, and ensure proper processing by the cellular RNAi machinery. Additionally, conventional cloning strategies often fail to preserve sequence diversity due to bacterial toxicity, recombination events, or selection bias during propagation. These shortcomings collectively hinder the ability to perform comprehensive, mechanism-agnostic screens for RNA molecules capable of eliciting robust and therapeutically relevant phenotypic effects, particularly in complex biological contexts such as apoptosis resistance, viral defense, or stem cell fate determination.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides recombinant expression vectors engineered to express novel RNA molecules, particularly shRNAs featuring a double-stranded stem region of optimal length (e.g., 29 nucleotides) and a non-self-complementary loop sequence, enabling enhanced RNAi potency and functional versatility. The RNA molecule structure comprises a first region of substantially random nucleotide sequence, a second non-self-complementary loop region, and a third region that is substantially complementary to the first region, arranged such that upon transcription, the RNA folds into a hairpin with a double-stranded stem and a single-stranded loop. The invention further encompasses a set or library of such recombinant expression vectors, each encoding a unique RNA molecule with a distinct random stem sequence, thereby facilitating high-throughput functional screening without prior assumptions about target genes or mechanisms of action. A key motivation for the invention is to enable the identification of RNA molecules that confer desirable phenotypes—such as protection against apoptosis, inhibition of viral replication, or induction of cell differentiation—through unbiased, cell-based selection. To this end, the invention describes a method of generating RNA molecules that overcomes prior synthetic hurdles by employing a multi-step enzymatic process involving nucleic acid intermediates. Specifically, the method begins with synthesizing a nucleotide intermediate I containing a random sequence flanked by defined restriction and nicking enzyme sites. A second nucleotide molecule, serving as a primer, is annealed to intermediate I, often with intentional mismatches to preserve critical enzyme recognition sequences. Extension and ligation steps yield a nucleic acid intermediate II, which is then amplified to produce a double-stranded intermediate III. Critically, the design incorporates restriction enzyme site asymmetry—achieved through phosphorothioate linkages or mismatched primers—to enable directional cloning and prevent self-ligation. An alternative method utilizes a hairpin-shaped linker nucleic acid molecule ligated to a staggered end of an extended oligonucleotide, followed by nicking and strand-displacement amplification using polymerases such as phi29 or Bst to generate the full-length insert. The invention also provides a set or library of recombinant viruses, such as retroviruses, packaged from these expression vectors for efficient delivery into mammalian cells. In therapeutic contexts, the expressed RNA molecules can confer protection against viral infection by targeting essential viral genes or host factors, inhibit viral replication through RNAi-mediated degradation of viral RNA, or induce cell differentiation by modulating networks of gene expression involved in lineage commitment.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention centers on RNA molecules possessing a double-stranded variable region formed by intramolecular base-pairing between a random sequence and its reverse complement, separated by a non-self-complementary loop. Sets and libraries of such RNA molecules are generated to enable comprehensive interrogation of sequence-function relationships without reliance on predicted mRNA targets. Methods of generating these RNA molecules involve precise enzymatic assembly to ensure structural fidelity and diversity. These RNA molecules function as therapeutics or modulators of biological parameters—including cell survival, proliferation, differentiation, pluripotency, and pathogen susceptibility—by engaging canonical or non-canonical RNA regulatory pathways. Drug targets for diseases such as cancer, viral infections, and neurodegenerative disorders can be indirectly identified through phenotypic screening of RNA libraries, even when direct mRNA targets remain elusive. Variants of active RNA molecules, generated via random mutagenesis or computational design, may exhibit altered or enhanced biological effects due to changes in seed sequence, stem stability, or mismatch patterns. Recombinant expression vectors, exemplified by modified pSuper or pSiren backbones, are engineered to express individual RNA molecules under the control of RNA polymerase III promoters (e.g., H1), ensuring high-level, constitutive transcription terminated by a poly-T stretch. Each vector expresses one RNA molecule type, minimizing competition for RISC loading. Individual RNA molecules consist of a variable region (6–29 nucleotides, preferably 7–25 nt), a non-self-complementary loop (e.g., CTAAAC), and complementary flanking regions; the second, non-self-complementary region may be positioned at either end, and the double-stranded region length may vary across the library while maintaining a common constant region for PCR retrieval. Random sequences are synthesized using oligonucleotide synthesizers with spiked phosphoramidites to introduce controlled diversity or generated computationally and synthesized en masse. The term “substantially random” denotes sequences where each position is independently chosen from A, C, G, T with near-equal probability, allowing minor biases; “non-self-complementary” indicates the loop sequence does not form stable secondary structures with itself or the stem; “substantially complementary” means ≥80% Watson-Crick base pairing over the stem length. Expression vectors include RNA polymerase promoters, optional RNAi effector genes (e.g., shRNA cassettes), and features like 2H1-promoter cassettes for dual expression. Loop-forming regions are typically 4–12 nucleotides; “palindromic” sequences read the same 5′→3′ on both strands. Nicking endonucleases (e.g., Nb.BbvCI, Nb.BsmI, Nt.AlwI) cleave one DNA strand to initiate strand-displacement synthesis by highly processive polymerases (e.g., phi29, Bst, Vent exo−), which displace downstream strands without dissociating. Restriction enzyme site asymmetry—achieved via phosphorothioate bonds or mismatched primers—ensures unidirectional cloning. Libraries are screened via function-based assays: cells exhibiting desired phenotypes (e.g., survival post-apoptosis induction) are isolated, their integrated vectors PCR-amplified, and shRNA sequences determined. Hits are confirmed by re-infection and phenotypic re-assay. Sub-libraries with randomized mutations (e.g., error-prone PCR) enable hit optimization. Therapeutic RNA molecules may act via translational repression, mRNA cleavage, or non-canonical activation. Biological readouts include flow cytometry (GFP/PI staining), caspase activity, morphology, and surface marker expression. Spacer sequences (e.g., between XcmI and SfiI) maintain proper promoter-transcript spacing. Stem lengths of 19–29 bp are contemplated, with 27–29 bp preferred for potency. Avoiding high-salt solutions preserves enzyme activity during synthesis. Target molecules include mRNAs, viral genomes, or regulatory RNAs. Complementary region lengths of 6–29 nt allow seed-based or extended targeting. Expression in stem cells can sustain pluripotency (via OCT4/NANOG modulation) or drive differentiation (into neurons, cardiomyocytes, etc.). Apoptosis assays use IL3 withdrawal, serum starvation, or glucose oxidase; viral protection assays employ cytopathic viruses. Sequencing employs “half-shRNA” PCR to resolve hairpin structures. Computer programs design sub-libraries based on hit sequences. The method supports identification of RNA molecules that inhibit cancer cell growth, enhance cell survival, or reduce pathogen susceptibility, with applications in regenerative medicine, antiviral therapy, and functional genomics.

### EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS SECTION

The invention was implemented using a retroviral vector system designed for dual RNAi expression, though the primary construct utilized a single shRNA cassette. The vector pQe2 was engineered to contain an H1-RNA polymerase III promoter, a cloning site for shRNA inserts, and a GFP reporter for tracking infection efficiency. Validation of shRNA knockdown was performed by transducing target cells and assessing target mRNA reduction via qPCR or protein levels by Western blot. Modifications were made to the commercial vector pSuper-Retro to accommodate the novel library design: specifically, the native BglII-MluI fragment was replaced with a synthetic BglII-BbsI-MluI fragment to introduce additional restriction sites necessary for asymmetric cloning. This yielded a linearized vector as depicted in FIG. 5, featuring compatible ends for directional insertion of the shRNA cassette. Alternate vectors were developed by replacing the BglII cloning site and optimizing the spacer sequence upstream of the promoter to enhance transcriptional initiation. A spacer sequence was reintroduced between XcmI and SfiI sites to maintain optimal distance between regulatory elements and the transcription start site, as excessive proximity can impair polymerase binding. Other suitable vector/enzyme combinations include pSiren with BglII/NotI or lentiviral vectors with AgeI/EcoRI, provided they support high-titer virus production and robust shRNA expression. The library of partially self-complementary RNA molecules was created by first obtaining single-stranded DNA molecules containing a constant 5′ region, a 29-nucleotide random segment (N28+G), and a 3′ constant region with embedded enzyme sites. A primer mismatched at two positions was annealed to this oligo to preserve PmeI and AarI recognition sequences during extension. Klenow polymerase extended the primer to generate a complementary strand, forming most of the double-stranded stem. A hairpin-loop linker (e.g., 5′-phosphorylated CTAAAC-containing oligo) was ligated to the staggered end of the extended product using T4 DNA ligase, yielding nucleic acid intermediate II—a single-stranded molecule with a double-stranded stem, a loop, and flanking single-stranded tails. This intermediate was PCR-amplified with primers binding the constant regions to generate double-stranded intermediate III, which contained BtgZI and NotI sites at opposite ends. Digestion with BtgZI (asymmetric due to phosphorothioate linkages) and NotI produced compatible ends for ligation into the prepared vector downstream of the H1 promoter, forming circular intermediate IV. Subsequent digestion with PmeI and AarI removed the loop-flanking sequences, and blunt-end ligation closed the vector to create circular product VI, ready for transformation. An alternative approach generated double-stranded intermediate IB by covalently linking the random sequence to its reverse complement via a recessed primer extension, followed by hairpin-loop ligation. A third method employed nicking enzyme Nb.BbvCI to create a nick in intermediate II, which was then extended by strand-displacing Bst polymerase to generate full-length double-stranded intermediate III. BtgZI digestion and Klenow fill-in completed the insert for cloning. Efficacy was confirmed by sequencing inserts and testing in apoptosis assays: 293T cells were treated with CDDO or serum-free medium, or apoptosis was induced via glucose/glucose oxidase to generate reactive oxygen species. Surviving cells were enriched over multiple rounds of selection, and shRNA sequences were retrieved by PCR and retested for protective effects.

### Materials and Experimental Methods

Cell preparation involved culturing FL5.12 pro-B cells in RPMI 1640 supplemented with 10% FBS, antibiotics, β-mercaptoethanol, and 0.3 ng/ml IL3. For retroviral production, 293T cells at 60% confluency were transfected with shRNA vector and pCL-Eco packaging plasmid using Effectene. Viral supernatant was harvested and used to infect target cells in the presence of polybrene, with centrifugation-enhanced transduction. Infection efficiency was monitored by GFP expression via flow cytometry, maintained at ≤30% to ensure single integrations per cell.

### Results

Apoptosis was induced by IL3 withdrawal for 14–22 hours, with cell viability assessed by propidium iodide exclusion in GFP+ populations. Retroviral infection was calibrated to achieve ~30% GFP+ cells initially. FL5.12 cells infected with the 300K shRNA library underwent four cycles of IL3 withdrawal (2–5 days) and recovery (3–8 days), enriching GFP+ survivors to >60%. Genomic DNA was extracted, and shRNA cassettes were PCR-amplified using vector-specific primers with long extension times to traverse hairpin structures. Sequencing revealed enriched clones (1p, 3p, 8p), which upon retesting significantly improved survival versus controls (p<0.0001). Caspase-3 activity was reduced in 3p- and 8p-expressing cells. Hit optimization via spiked oligo synthesis generated sublibrary 9.1 (~1.5 mutations/construct). Stringent selection (up to 5-day starvation) enriched clone 3p05p, which outperformed 3p (p=0.014). Mutational analysis showed the A-to-C change (creating a stem mismatch) was critical; restoring complementarity (G-C) or altering the base (C-T) abolished efficacy. In FRDA fibroblasts, the library enabled isolation of clones supporting long-term proliferation. In ES cells, infection followed by in vivo transplantation and FACS of GFP+ tissues identified shRNAs driving differentiation into multiple lineages. Reversible RNAi confirmed phenotypes. Antiviral screens identified shRNAs protecting against cytopathic viruses. Error-prone PCR and computer-designed sub-libraries yielded improved variants, demonstrating iterative optimization. Pooling, retesting, and secondary assays (e.g., morphology, adherence) validated hits across cell types and stressors, confirming broad applicability.
