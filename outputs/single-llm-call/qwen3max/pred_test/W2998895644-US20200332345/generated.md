## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics, specifically to methods and kits for the rapid, sensitive, and specific detection of infectious bacteria, with particular emphasis on the identification and differentiation of *Francisella tularensis* subspecies and subtypes. The invention provides nucleic acid-based assays capable of distinguishing virulent select agent strains from non-select agent variants and environmental near-neighbors, thereby addressing critical needs in public health surveillance, biodefense, clinical diagnostics, and epidemiological investigations.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Tularemia is a severe zoonotic disease caused by the Gram-negative bacterium *Francisella tularensis*, a facultative intracellular pathogen notable for its extremely low infectious dose—fewer than ten organisms can cause disease via inhalation. Due to its high infectivity, potential for aerosol dissemination, and capacity to cause mass casualties, *F. tularensis* is classified as a Tier 1 Select Agent by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA), indicating it poses the highest risk of deliberate misuse with significant potential for public harm.

*F. tularensis* comprises four recognized subspecies: *F. tularensis* subsp. *tularensis* (Type A), *F. tularensis* subsp. *holarctica* (Type B), *F. tularensis* subsp. *mediasiatica*, and *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida*. Among these, the first three are designated as Select Agents due to their virulence in humans, whereas *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida* is not regulated as a Select Agent despite causing opportunistic infections, primarily in immunocompromised individuals. Type A strains are further subdivided into subtypes A.I and A.II, with A.I exhibiting the highest virulence and mortality rates, historically reaching up to 35% in untreated cases. Transmission occurs through multiple routes—including inhalation of contaminated aerosols (causing pneumonic tularemia), ingestion of contaminated food or water, and vector-borne exposure via ticks, deer flies, or mosquitoes—making early and accurate diagnosis essential for effective medical countermeasures and outbreak containment.

Public health concerns surrounding *F. tularensis* are amplified by its environmental persistence, broad host range (>250 species), and potential use as a biological weapon. Rapid identification of exposure to virulent strains, particularly subtype A.I, is imperative for appropriate antibiotic therapy, patient isolation, and public health intervention. However, current diagnostic platforms suffer from significant limitations. Many existing qPCR assays detect *F. tularensis* at the species level but fail to differentiate among subspecies or subtypes. Other methods rely on complex scoring matrices, gel electrophoresis, or hierarchical testing schemes that are time-consuming, prone to interpretation errors, and incompatible with high-throughput laboratory workflows. Furthermore, cross-reactivity with near-neighbor organisms—such as *Francisella persica* (a tick endosymbiont formerly classified as *Wolbachia persica*) and other *Francisella*-like endosymbionts commonly found in arthropod vectors—leads to false-positive results and diagnostic uncertainty.

For example, environmental samples from *Dermacentor* ticks often contain *Francisella*-like organisms that share high genomic similarity with *F. tularensis*, resulting in misidentification when using non-specific assays. This problem is exacerbated by the fact that *F. tularensis* subsp. *novicida* shares over 98% average nucleotide identity with Select Agent subspecies, yet carries distinct regulatory and clinical implications. The inability to rapidly distinguish between a hypervirulent A.I strain and a non-Select Agent *novicida* isolate could lead to unnecessary public health alerts or, conversely, delayed response to a genuine bioterrorism event. Thus, there exists an urgent and unmet need for a robust, rapid, and unambiguous molecular diagnostic platform capable of simultaneously detecting *F. tularensis* and resolving its subspecies and subtype identity with high sensitivity and specificity.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for the detection and differentiation of *Francisella tularensis* species, subspecies, and subtypes using a suite of highly specific nucleic acid amplification assays. The invention encompasses both singleplex and multiplex quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR) assays that target unique chromosomal regions conserved within specific *F. tularensis* clades but absent in near-neighbor organisms and non-target bacteria. Central to the invention is a two-tier multiplex qPCR strategy that enables rapid classification of unknown isolates into clinically and regulatorily relevant categories in as few as two tests.

The method includes a pan-*F. tularensis* detection assay (4Pan1) that identifies all four subspecies by targeting a 116-bp region within the *ostA* gene (locus tag FTT_0467). Complementing this, a virulent-subspecies-specific assay (3Pan) detects only the three Select Agent subspecies (*tularensis*, *holarctica*, and *mediasiatica*) by amplifying an 83-bp region in a hypothetical protein gene (FTL_1858), thereby excluding *novicida*. Subtype- and subspecies-specific assays include A1d (for *tularensis* subtype A.I, targeting FTT_0516), A2c (for subtype A.II, targeting *mviN*, FTW_1702), B2 (for *holarctica*, targeting FTS_0806), M3 (for *mediasiatica*, targeting FTM_1104), and N1 (for *novicida*, targeting FTN_0003). Each assay employs optimized primer and hydrolysis probe sequences with defined fluorophore-quencher pairs suitable for standard qPCR instrumentation.

The invention further provides a Tier 1 multiplex qPCR assay combining U16S (universal bacterial 16S rRNA control), 4Pan1, 3Pan, and A1d to simultaneously determine: (1) presence of bacterial DNA, (2) confirmation of *F. tularensis* species, (3) identification as a Select Agent strain, and (4) detection of hypervirulent subtype A.I. A complementary Tier 2 multiplex assay includes U16S, A2c, B2, and M3 to resolve the identity of non-A.I Select Agent strains. The invention also encompasses a detection assay kit containing pre-formulated primer-probe mixes, optimized reaction buffers, positive and negative controls, and instructions for use.

Additionally, the invention supports direct DNA sequencing of amplicons generated from the same primer sets used in qPCR, enabling strain-level confirmation and phylogenetic analysis without redesign of reagents. Key advantages include: (1) elimination of gel electrophoresis and complex scoring matrices; (2) limit of detection as low as 2–7 fg genomic DNA (1–3 genome copies) in singleplex format; (3) high specificity against a broad exclusivity panel including *F. philomiragia*, *F. persica*, and *Francisella*-positive tick lysates; (4) compatibility with multiple qPCR platforms (e.g., Applied Biosystems 7500 Fast Dx, 3M Integrated Cycler); and (5) flexibility for use with cultured isolates or crude lysates. Variations of the invention include incorporation of additional subspecies-specific targets (e.g., N2, N3, N4 for *novicida* confirmation), alternative probe chemistries (e.g., TaqMan, Molecular Beacons), and integration with next-generation sequencing workflows. The primary objects and advantages are to provide public health laboratories, biodefense agencies, and clinical microbiology facilities with a rapid, accurate, and regulatory-compliant tool for *F. tularensis* identification that directly addresses gaps in current diagnostic capabilities.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is directed to molecular assays for the detection and differentiation of *Francisella tularensis* at the species, subspecies, and subtype levels. These assays are based on the discovery of unique genomic signatures within chromosomal regions that are differentially conserved across the four *F. tularensis* subspecies and their subtypes. The core methodology involves nucleic acid amplification—specifically quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR)—using primer and probe sets designed to anneal exclusively to these signature regions.

Both singleplex and multiplex qPCR formats are supported. Singleplex assays allow maximum sensitivity and are ideal for confirmatory testing, while multiplex assays enable high-throughput screening with reduced reagent consumption and turnaround time. The assays are compatible with standard real-time thermocyclers, including the Applied Biosystems 7500 Fast Dx and the 3M Integrated Cycler, provided appropriate fluorophore-quencher combinations are used (e.g., FAM-BHQ1, HEX-BHQ1, Cy5-BHQ2). In addition to qPCR, the invention encompasses a sequencing-based genotyping approach wherein amplicons generated using the same primer sets (optionally barcoded for multiplexed sequencing) are subjected to direct DNA sequencing, typically via Ion Torrent PGM or Illumina platforms, to confirm clade identity and detect single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) for fine-scale strain characterization.

The method, referred to herein as Method 20, begins with the acquisition of a suspect specimen, which may be a bacterial culture isolate, a clinical sample, or an environmental swab. Genomic DNA is extracted using standard methods (e.g., CTAB, commercial kits), though crude lysates prepared by heat lysis of colony material may also be used. The user then selects a detection strategy based on required resolution: species-level, subspecies-level, or subtype-level identification. At least one assay is selected from a plurality of available assays, each corresponding to a specific taxonomic group as defined in Table 1 of the research paper.

Each assay targets a unique chromosomal locus. For instance, the 4Pan1 assay targets a region in *ostA* (FTT_0467) present in all four subspecies, yielding a 116-bp amplicon. The 3Pan assay targets FTL_1858, present only in the three virulent subspecies. Subtype-specific assays exploit structural or sequence differences: the A1d assay amplifies a 114-bp fragment in FTT_0516 only when the intervening genomic distance is short (as in A.I), whereas in other clades the primers are too far apart for amplification. Negative control assays include no-template controls (NTCs) to monitor contamination, while positive controls consist of validated genomic DNA from reference strains (e.g., SCHU S4 for A.I). Inhibition is assessed via the universal 16S rRNA assay (U16S), which also serves as an endogenous control for bacterial DNA presence; a threshold cycle (CT) value <31 indicates sufficient bacterial DNA and absence of significant PCR inhibitors.

The U16S assay uses modified universal primers and a Francisella-tuned probe to detect a 159-bp conserved region. The 4Pan1 assay sequences are: forward primer 5′-GCA TTA TCG TTA GCT GCT GC-3′, reverse primer 5′-TGC TGA TAA CGC TGC TAA GG-3′, and FAM-labeled probe 5′-FAM-CCA TCA TCA TGC CCA TGT TGG T-BHQ1-3′. The 3Pan assay sequences target FTL_1858 with forward primer 5′-GCT GAA GAA GGT GAA GGT GA-3′, reverse primer 5′-TCC TGC TTA TCA TCA TCA TCA T-3′, and probe 5′-HEX-CCA TCA TCA TGC CCA TGT TGG T-BHQ1-3′. The A1d assay uses forward primer 5′-GCA TTA TCG TTA GCT GCT GC-3′, reverse primer 5′-TGC TGA TAA CGC TGC TAA GG-3′, and a Cy5-labeled probe. Similarly, A2c targets *mviN* (FTW_1702), B2 targets FTS_0806, M3 targets FTM_1104, and N1 targets FTN_0003 with subspecies-specific sequences.

Interpretation follows a logical flowchart. First, U16S CT <31 confirms bacterial DNA and valid reaction. If 4Pan1 is positive, *F. tularensis* is present. If 3Pan is also positive, the strain is a Select Agent; if negative, it is *novicida*. If A1d is positive, the strain is subtype A.I. If 3Pan is positive but A1d negative, Tier 2 testing is performed: A2c+ indicates A.II, B2+ indicates *holarctica*, M3+ indicates *mediasiatica*. The N4 assay (not detailed in results but implied in outline) would similarly target another *novicida*-specific locus, such as a region in FTN_1234, with primers annealing to unique flanking sequences and a Texas Red-labeled probe.

Reaction preparation involves mixing template DNA with optimized concentrations of primers (0.25–1.0 µM) and probes (0.05–0.7 µM) in a master mix containing thermostable DNA polymerase, dNTPs, MgCl₂, and buffer. Cycling conditions are uniform: 95°C for 2 min, followed by 45 cycles of 95°C for 1 s and 60°C for 20 s. Sequencing reactions use the same primers (with optional barcodes) in conventional PCR, followed by purification and sequencing. Data analysis involves filtering low-quality reads, aligning to reference genomes (e.g., NCBI RefSeq), and confirming identity via BLAST. This facultative sequencing step provides high-confidence strain identification beyond qPCR alone.

### Example 1

The analytical sensitivity of the assays was rigorously evaluated by generating standard curves using ten-fold serial dilutions of reference strain genomic DNA. The limit of detection (LOD) for each singleplex assay was determined as the lowest concentration yielding ≥95% positive replicates. The 4Pan1 assay exhibited an LOD of 3 fg (∼2 genome copies), with a linear standard curve (R² = 0.998) over six logs of DNA concentration. Optimized reaction conditions included 0.5 µM forward primer, 0.75 µM reverse primer, and 0.3 µM FAM-BHQ1 probe. The universal 16S (U16S) assay had an LOD of 0.1 pg, dependent on 16S copy number, with optimized concentrations of 0.5 µM primers and 0.2 µM probe. The 3Pan assay achieved 5 fg LOD (R² = 0.999) with 0.5 µM primers and 0.4 µM probe. Subtype-specific assays showed comparable performance: A1d (7 fg, R² = 0.976, 0.5 µM primers, 0.2 µM probe), A2c (5 fg, R² = 0.999, 0.5/0.25 µM primers, 0.3 µM probe), B2 (5 fg, R² = 0.998, 0.5 µM primers, 0.2 µM probe), M3 (2 fg, R² = 0.997, 1.0/0.75 µM primers, 0.3 µM probe), and N1 (3 fg, R² = 1.00, 1.0/0.75 µM primers, 0.3 µM probe). Graphical representation of CT versus log DNA concentration confirmed linearity and assay robustness across instruments and operators.

### Example 2

A two-tier multiplex qPCR system was developed to maximize information yield per test. Tier 1 combines U16S, 4Pan1, 3Pan, and A1d in a single reaction to classify isolates as: (1) A.I (all four targets positive), (2) non-A.I Select Agent (U16S+, 4Pan1+, 3Pan+, A1d−), or (3) *novicida* (U16S+, 4Pan1+, 3Pan−, A1d−). Tier 2 (U16S, A2c, B2, M3) resolves non-A.I Select Agents into A.II, *holarctica*, or *mediasiatica*. Each 10× stock was formulated for optimal performance: U16S (0.5 µM primers, 0.2 µM probe), 4Pan1 (0.5/0.75 µM primers, 0.3 µM probe), 3Pan (0.5 µM primers, 0.4 µM probe), A1d (0.5 µM primers, 0.2 µM probe) for Tier 1; U16S (same), A2c (0.5/0.25 µM, 0.3 µM), B2 (0.5 µM, 0.2 µM), M3 (1.0/0.75 µM, 0.3 µM) for Tier 2. Specificity was verified against 25 exclusivity organisms, including *Francisella*-positive ticks, with no cross-reactivity. LODs in multiplex format were slightly higher (10–100 fg) due to competitive amplification but remained highly sensitive (5–49 genome copies). Standard curves maintained high linearity (R² > 0.96). This approach eliminates the need for sequential testing or complex interpretation matrices, providing actionable results in under two hours. The invention thus represents a significant advance in molecular diagnostics for high-consequence pathogens, offering speed, accuracy, and regulatory compliance for public health and defense applications.
