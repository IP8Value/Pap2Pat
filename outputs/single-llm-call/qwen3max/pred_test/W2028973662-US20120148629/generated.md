# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to nutritional compositions comprising probiotic microorganisms, particularly strains of the genus *Lactococcus*, for use in the dietary management of allergic conditions. More specifically, the invention provides complete nutritional compositions containing defined amounts of viable or inactivated *Lactococcus lactis* strains, optionally in combination with prebiotics and hydrolyzed proteins, formulated to promote secondary prevention of allergic manifestations in sensitized individuals. The invention is particularly directed toward compositions suitable for administration to infants, children, and other vulnerable populations suffering from food allergies, respiratory allergies, or contact dermatitis, wherein the composition modulates immune responses without altering systemic sensitization status but effectively reduces clinical symptoms associated with allergic reactions.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Allergic diseases have exhibited a marked increase in prevalence over recent decades, affecting up to 30% of the global population, with early-life onset being particularly common. This rise has been attributed to complex interactions between genetic predisposition and environmental factors, including changes in diet, hygiene practices, and microbial exposure during critical windows of immune development. The natural history of allergic disease often follows a trajectory known as the “atopic march,” wherein initial sensitization to food allergens in infancy progresses to eczema, allergic rhinitis, and ultimately asthma in later childhood. Central to this progression is the dysregulation of the immune system, particularly an imbalance in T-helper cell differentiation favoring a Th2-polarized response characterized by elevated production of interleukins such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, which drive IgE synthesis, eosinophil recruitment, and mast cell activation.

Immune system maturation occurs predominantly during the first years of life, coinciding with colonization of the gut microbiota and dietary diversification. Disruptions in this process—such as cesarean delivery, antibiotic use, or formula feeding—can impair the establishment of immune tolerance and increase susceptibility to allergic sensitization. Consequently, interventions aimed at supporting healthy immune development through nutritional means have garnered significant scientific and clinical interest. Among these, probiotics—defined as live microorganisms that confer a health benefit when administered in adequate amounts—have emerged as promising tools for immune modulation. However, the efficacy of probiotic interventions remains highly strain-specific and context-dependent, with inconsistent outcomes reported across studies due to variations in bacterial species, dosage, timing of administration, and target population.

## Prevention of Allergies can be Achieved on Different Levels:

Allergy prevention strategies are broadly categorized into primary and secondary prevention. Primary prevention refers to interventions implemented before any sensitization has occurred, with the goal of inhibiting the initial development of allergen-specific IgE and preventing the establishment of a Th2-skewed immune phenotype. In contrast, secondary prevention targets individuals who are already sensitized to one or more allergens but have not yet developed overt clinical symptoms, or who experience mild or intermittent symptoms; the objective here is to modulate the immune response to prevent progression to more severe or chronic allergic disease. A third approach, often termed tertiary prevention or management, focuses on alleviating established symptoms and improving quality of life in diagnosed allergic individuals.

Modulating sensitization involves influencing the early immune events that lead to IgE production against harmless environmental antigens. This may include promoting regulatory T-cell (Treg) development, enhancing gut barrier integrity, or skewing dendritic cell function toward a tolerogenic phenotype. On the other hand, modulating symptoms entails dampening the effector phase of the allergic response—such as mast cell degranulation, eosinophil infiltration, or smooth muscle contraction—without necessarily reversing underlying sensitization.

Common food allergens include proteins from cow’s milk, egg, wheat, soy, peanut, tree nuts, fish, and shellfish, which trigger IgE-mediated reactions ranging from urticaria to anaphylaxis. Respiratory allergens, such as pollen from grasses, trees, and weeds, as well as house dust mite and animal dander, primarily induce allergic rhinitis and asthma through similar immunological mechanisms.

*Lactococcus* species, particularly *Lactococcus lactis*, are Gram-positive, non-pathogenic lactic acid bacteria widely used in dairy fermentation. Recent research has revealed that certain *L. lactis* strains possess intrinsic immunomodulatory properties beyond their traditional role as starter cultures. Notably, specific strains have demonstrated anti-allergic effects in preclinical models, including suppression of skin inflammation and reduction of airway hyperresponsiveness. These observations suggest that *Lactococcus* may serve as a novel probiotic platform for allergy management.

Despite advances, there remains a critical unmet need for safe, effective, and nutritionally complete interventions that reduce allergic symptoms without compromising growth or development, especially in infants and young children. Conventional hypoallergenic formulas often lack bioactive components necessary for immune education, while pharmacological treatments carry risks of side effects and do not address underlying immune dysregulation. Moreover, many existing probiotic formulations fail to demonstrate consistent clinical benefits due to inadequate strain selection, insufficient dosing, or inappropriate timing relative to the allergic disease phase. There is therefore a pressing demand for a complete nutritional composition that integrates proven anti-allergic probiotics within a balanced matrix capable of delivering both nutritional adequacy and targeted immune modulation during the symptomatic phase of allergy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a complete nutritional composition designed for the dietary management of allergic conditions in sensitized individuals. This composition comprises a defined amount of a probiotic strain belonging to the genus *Lactococcus*, preferably *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287 (also designated SL131) or *L. lactis* NCC 2180 (SL60), optionally combined with prebiotic oligosaccharides and hydrolyzed proteins. The composition is formulated to support secondary prevention of allergic manifestations by reducing the severity and frequency of clinical symptoms upon allergen exposure, without altering systemic sensitization status as measured by allergen-specific IgE levels. The invention is particularly suited for administration during the weaning period and beyond, offering a nutritionally balanced alternative to standard infant formulas or complementary foods for children with diagnosed food allergies, respiratory allergies, or atopic dermatitis. The composition may be provided as a powder, liquid, or semi-solid formulation, including infant formula, follow-on formula, cereal-based baby food, yoghurt drinks, or pet food, and may contain viable or inactivated bacterial cells stabilized through lyophilization or encapsulation to ensure shelf-life and gastric survival.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The term “patients” as used herein refers to human subjects, particularly infants, children, and adults who have been clinically diagnosed with or are at risk of developing allergic conditions, including but not limited to food allergy, allergic rhinitis, asthma, atopic dermatitis, or eosinophilic gastrointestinal disorders. It also encompasses non-human mammals, such as companion animals, suffering from analogous allergic syndromes. A “complete nutritional composition” denotes a formulation that provides all essential macronutrients (proteins, fats, carbohydrates), micronutrients (vitamins and minerals), and optional functional ingredients in proportions sufficient to meet daily dietary requirements for the target age group, thereby serving as a sole or major source of nutrition.

“Symptoms of allergies” include, but are not limited to, cutaneous manifestations (e.g., urticaria, pruritus, eczema), gastrointestinal disturbances (e.g., vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain), respiratory symptoms (e.g., wheezing, nasal congestion, cough), and systemic reactions (e.g., anaphylaxis). “Primary prevention of allergies” involves interventions initiated before any evidence of sensitization or clinical allergy, aiming to prevent the initial development of allergen-specific immune responses. In contrast, “secondary prevention of allergies” refers to strategies applied after sensitization has occurred but before or during the onset of clinical symptoms, with the goal of mitigating disease progression or symptom severity.

The “weaning period” is defined as the developmental stage during which an infant transitions from exclusive milk feeding to the introduction of solid or semi-solid complementary foods, typically beginning around 4–6 months of age. “Sensitization” denotes the presence of allergen-specific IgE antibodies in serum or positive results from skin prick tests, indicating immune recognition of an allergen, regardless of clinical reactivity. An “immune boost” in this context refers to the enhancement of regulatory or Th1-type immune responses that counterbalance pathogenic Th2 activity, rather than a general stimulation of immunity.

A “probiotic” is a preparation containing live or inactivated microorganisms that, when administered in adequate amounts, confers a health benefit on the host through modulation of the gut microbiota or direct interaction with host immune cells. A “prebiotic” is a selectively fermented ingredient that results in specific changes in the composition and/or activity of the gastrointestinal microbiota, thus conferring benefit(s) upon host health, typically non-digestible oligosaccharides such as fructooligosaccharides (FOS) or inulin.

### Effect of the Composition:

The nutritional composition of the invention exerts a beneficial effect on the secondary prevention of allergies by significantly reducing the clinical severity of allergic symptoms upon allergen re-exposure in sensitized individuals. This effect is evidenced by decreased scratching behavior, reduced incidence of diarrhea, improved respiratory function, and lower overall allergic scoring in experimental models. Importantly, the composition does not alter the degree of systemic sensitization, as indicated by unchanged levels of allergen-specific IgE, IgG1, or mast cell protease MMCP-1, thereby distinguishing its mechanism from classical desensitization approaches.

The reduction in symptoms correlates with modulation of local immune responses in the gastrointestinal tract, particularly in the ileum. Specifically, the composition decreases the production of the Th2 cytokine IL-13 by mesenteric lymph node lymphocytes upon antigen restimulation. IL-13 is a key mediator of chronic allergic inflammation, driving eosinophil recruitment, mucus hypersecretion, and epithelial barrier dysfunction. The composition also suppresses the expression of Th2-associated chemokines CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC) in ileal tissue, which are downstream effectors of IL-13 signaling and critical for leukocyte trafficking to sites of inflammation.

Notably, these immunomodulatory effects are spatially restricted to the ileum and are not observed in the jejunum, suggesting site-specific action coinciding with regions of active allergic inflammation. The composition does not influence systemic humoral immunity or mast cell degranulation, indicating a localized, cell-mediated mechanism of action. Long-term administration during the management phase leads to sustained symptom relief without inducing tolerance or eliminating sensitization, positioning the invention as a therapeutic rather than curative intervention.

### Composition of the Invention or for Use in the Invention:

The complete nutritional composition comprises a protein source, lipid source, carbohydrate source, vitamins, minerals, and at least one probiotic strain of the genus *Lactococcus*. The preferred probiotic is *Lactococcus lactis* NCC 2287 (deposited as CNCM I-4154), or alternatively *L. lactis* NCC 2180 (SL60). The probiotic is present in an amount ranging from 10⁶ to 10¹⁰ colony-forming units (CFU) per gram of dry composition, preferably 10⁸ CFU/g. The probiotic may be added as a lyophilized powder post-sterilization to preserve viability, or as inactivated biomass if intended for use in immunocompromised populations.

To enhance stability, the probiotic may be microencapsulated in alginate, chitosan, or lipid matrices, or co-administered with cryoprotectants such as trehalose or skim milk. Inactivated probiotic preparations, obtained by heat treatment, UV irradiation, or chemical fixation, retain immunomodulatory capacity through preserved cell wall structures such as peptidoglycan and lipoteichoic acid.

The composition further includes a prebiotic, preferably a mixture of short-chain fructooligosaccharides (scFOS) and long-chain inulin in a ratio of 1:1 to 9:1, totaling 0.5–5 g per 100 kcal. Apple extract, rich in polyphenols such as phloretin and quercetin, may be included at 0.1–1% w/w to exert synergistic anti-inflammatory effects with the probiotic.

Protein is provided as extensively hydrolyzed whey or casein, produced by enzymatic cleavage using proteases such as trypsin, chymotrypsin, or alcalase, resulting in peptides with molecular weights predominantly below 5 kDa to minimize allergenicity. Alternative protein sources include rice, pea, or soy hydrolysates for dairy-free formulations.

### Target Group:

The target group includes infants and young children diagnosed with IgE-mediated food allergies, particularly to cow’s milk or egg, as well as those with non-IgE-mediated food protein-induced enterocolitis syndrome (FPIES). It also encompasses individuals suffering from respiratory allergies (e.g., seasonal allergic rhinitis to grass pollen) or contact allergies (e.g., atopic dermatitis). Non-human mammals, especially dogs and cats with food-responsive enteropathy or atopic dermatitis, are additional beneficiaries.

The composition is formulated as starter infant formula (for 0–6 months), follow-up formula (6–12 months), weaning cereals, or toddler milk. It may also be adapted into yoghurt drinks for older children and adults, or into complete pet foods containing animal or plant-derived proteins (e.g., hydrolyzed chicken, lamb, or soy), poultry fat or fish oil as fat sources, rice or potato starch as carbohydrates, and fermentable fibers such as beet pulp or chicory root. Moisture content is adjusted to 8–12% for dry pet food, with ash content maintained below 8%. Optional materials include taurine, L-carnitine, omega-3 fatty acids, and antioxidants.

## Experimental Results, Section 1:

In vitro screening identified *L. lactis* NCC 2287 as a potent modulator of human immune responses. Using a Th2-skewed peripheral blood mononuclear cell (PBMC) model induced by anti-CD3/CD28 antibodies and IL-4, the strain significantly enhanced production of IFNγ and IL-10—cytokines associated with Th1 and regulatory responses—while suppressing IL-5 secretion, a key eosinophil-activating factor. These findings were confirmed at the mRNA level via real-time PCR, demonstrating upregulation of *IFNG* and *IL10* transcripts and downregulation of *IL5*.

In an ovalbumin (OVA)-induced murine food allergy model, oral administration of *L. lactis* NCC 2287 (5 × 10⁸ CFU/mL in drinking water) during the management phase (days 43–49 post-sensitization) significantly reduced clinical allergic scores compared to control (1.3 ± 0.37 vs. 2.5 ± 0.31; P = 0.038). Serum levels of mouse mast cell protease-1 (MMCP-1), a marker of systemic anaphylaxis, were markedly elevated in sensitized controls (1107 ± 322 pg/mL) but reduced by 30% in the probiotic group (773 ± 298 pg/mL), though not statistically significant. Ex vivo restimulation of mesenteric lymph node (MLN) cells with OVA revealed a trend toward decreased IL-13 production (P = 0.08), with no effect on IL-4, IL-5, or IL-10, confirming selective Th2 modulation.

## Experimental Results, Section 2:

When tested in PBMCs isolated from grass pollen-allergic donors, *L. lactis* NCC 2287 induced IFNγ and IL-10 and inhibited IL-5 in response to grass pollen extract, mirroring results from non-allergic donors. This indicates that the strain’s immunomodulatory capacity is preserved in the context of pre-existing allergic sensitization. Comparative analysis showed a more pronounced IL-10 response in allergic individuals, suggesting enhanced regulatory potential in inflamed environments. These data support the strain’s applicability to respiratory allergies, where Th2-driven inflammation underlies seasonal symptoms.

## Analytical Methods:

### Reagents and Bacterial Biomass:

*L. lactis* NCC 2287 was cultured in M17 broth at 30°C until early stationary phase (3 h post-entry), harvested by centrifugation, washed twice in cold PBS, and stored at −80°C in 20% glycerol. Viability was confirmed by plating on agar.

### Isolation and Culture of Th2-Skewed Human PBMC:

PBMCs were isolated from heparinized blood via Ficoll density gradient centrifugation. Th2 polarization was achieved by culturing cells with plate-bound anti-CD3 (1 μg/mL), soluble anti-CD28 (0.5 μg/mL), and recombinant IL-4 (20 ng/mL) for 5 days. Cells were then co-cultured with heat-killed *L. lactis* NCC 2287 (10:1 bacteria:cell ratio) for 48 h. Parallel experiments used PBMCs from grass-allergic donors stimulated with Timothy grass pollen extract (10 μg/mL).

### Cytokine ELISA:

Cytokine concentrations in culture supernatants were quantified using commercial ELISA kits for human IFNγ, IL-10, and IL-5, following manufacturer protocols. Optical density was read at 450 nm.

### Evaluation of ELISA Results:

Data were normalized to unstimulated controls and expressed as mean ± SEM. Statistical significance was assessed by Kruskal-Wallis test with Bonferroni-Sidak correction.

### Quantitative Gene Expression Levels by Real-Time PCR:

Total RNA was extracted using SV Total RNA Isolation System. cDNA synthesis employed Multiscribe reverse transcriptase. Gene expression was analyzed on ABI Prism 7900HT using TaqMan assays, normalized to β-actin, GAPDH, and HPRT. Relative expression was calculated as 2^−ΔCt × 10⁶.

## OVA Food Allergy Mouse Model:

Female BALB/c mice were sensitized weekly for 7 weeks with 20 mg OVA + 10 μg cholera toxin by gavage. Challenge occurred one week later with 100 mg OVA. Probiotic was administered in drinking water either during prevention (from day −5) or management (days 43–49).

### Serum MMCP-1:

MMCP-1 was quantified by ELISA (Moredun Scientific) with detection limit of 10 pg/mL.

### Isolation and Culture of Mesenteric Lymph Node (MLN) Lymphocytes:

MLNs were homogenized, filtered, and cultured (3 × 10⁵ cells/well) ± 1 mg/mL OVA for 72 h.

### Cytokines in Supernatant of Mesenteric Lymph Node Cell and Splenocyte Cultures:

Cytokines were measured using Meso Scale Discovery Th1/Th2 multiplex kit and R&D Systems IL-13 ELISA.

### Low Density Array Analyses of Gene Expression in Intestine:

RNA from ileum and jejunum was reverse-transcribed and analyzed on custom Low-Density Arrays (Applied Biosystems) for 48 immune-related genes. Data were normalized to housekeeping genes and expressed as fold-change vs. negative control.

### EXAMPLE 1

An infant formula powder per 100 g contains: hydrolyzed whey protein (12 g), high-oleic sunflower oil (25 g), maltodextrin (40 g), scFOS/inulin blend (3 g, 9:1), *L. lactis* NCC 2287 (10⁸ CFU/g), vitamins, minerals, choline, taurine, and nucleotides. Reconstituted with water to 67 kcal/100 mL.

### EXAMPLE 2

Adapted milk powder for children (1–3 years) per 100 g: hydrolyzed casein (15 g), coconut oil/palm olein/fish oil blend (28 g), lactose (30 g), apple extract (0.5 g), scFOS/inulin (4 g), *L. lactis* NCC 2287 (5 × 10⁷ CFU/g), iron, zinc, vitamin D.

### EXAMPLE 3

Yoghurt drink (200 mL) contains: skimmed milk fermented with *Streptococcus thermophilus* and *L. delbrueckii* subsp. *bulgaricus*, supplemented with *L. lactis* NCC 2287 (10⁷ CFU/mL), inulin (2 g), apple juice concentrate (5%), and vitamin C.

### EXAMPLE 4

Dry dog food per kg: hydrolyzed chicken protein (250 g), rice (300 g), poultry fat (120 g), beet pulp (30 g), fish oil (10 g), *L. lactis* NCC 2287 (10⁶ CFU/g), vitamins, minerals, taurine (0.15 g), moisture (10%), ash (7%). Protein sources include chicken and rice; fat from poultry and fish; carbohydrates from rice and beet pulp; fiber from beet pulp and chicory inulin. Fermentable fiber constitutes 4% of total diet.
