# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of therapeutic agents for the treatment of inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD), and more specifically, to compositions and methods for sequestering high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) protein in the gastrointestinal tract using DNA-conjugated beads. The invention provides a novel approach to managing IBD by orally administering non-absorbable, enteric-coated beads functionalized with specific oligonucleotide sequences that bind HMGB1 with high affinity, thereby neutralizing its pro-inflammatory activity within the intestinal lumen. This method offers a targeted, localized, and minimally invasive strategy to reduce inflammation, restore epithelial barrier integrity, and ameliorate clinical symptoms associated with ulcerative colitis, Crohn’s disease, and other HMGB1-mediated gastrointestinal disorders without systemic immunosuppression or significant toxicity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD), including ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease, are chronic, relapsing inflammatory conditions of the gastrointestinal tract that affect millions of individuals worldwide. These diseases are characterized by mucosal inflammation, epithelial barrier dysfunction, weight loss, diarrhea, and abdominal pain, leading to substantial morbidity and reduced quality of life. Despite advances in biologic and immunomodulatory therapies, many patients exhibit incomplete responses, lose responsiveness over time, or experience significant adverse effects, underscoring the need for novel, safe, and effective treatment modalities.

High mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) is a nuclear protein that, when released extracellularly during cellular stress, injury, or immune activation, functions as a potent damage-associated molecular pattern (DAMP) molecule. Extracellular HMGB1 activates innate immune pathways through receptors such as TLR4 and RAGE, triggering the release of pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6. In both human IBD patients and murine colitis models, HMGB1 is abundantly secreted by inflamed intestinal tissues and is detectable at elevated levels in stool and serum, correlating with disease severity. Neutralization of HMGB1 using monoclonal antibodies or small-molecule inhibitors has been shown to attenuate colitis in preclinical models, establishing HMGB1 as a validated therapeutic target.

However, conventional approaches to HMGB1 inhibition—such as systemic administration of neutralizing antibodies or recombinant proteins—face limitations including high cost, potential immunogenicity, poor bioavailability in the gut lumen, and off-target systemic effects. Direct injection of free DNA into the gastrointestinal tract has also been explored due to HMGB1’s strong affinity for nucleic acids, but this strategy suffers from rapid enzymatic degradation by nucleases, poor retention in the intestinal environment, and lack of controlled delivery. Moreover, unmodified DNA may trigger unintended immune responses via Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9) or cytosolic DNA sensors, potentially exacerbating inflammation rather than mitigating it. These challenges highlight the need for a stable, non-immunogenic, and localized delivery system capable of efficiently sequestering HMGB1 within the harsh and complex milieu of the inflamed gut.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides therapeutic beads comprising a solid, non-absorbable polymeric core coated with specific oligonucleotide sequences that bind HMGB1 with high affinity and selectivity. These beads are designed for oral administration and function as molecular scavengers that sequester extracellular HMGB1 directly within the gastrointestinal lumen, thereby preventing its interaction with immune receptors and downstream inflammatory signaling. The invention further encompasses methods of treating IBD by administering these HMGB1-sequestering beads to subjects in need thereof, resulting in reduced mucosal inflammation, improved epithelial barrier function, decreased cytokine production, and amelioration of clinical disease manifestations. The therapeutic beads are engineered to resist degradation in acidic and enzymatic environments, ensuring stability throughout the gastrointestinal tract while remaining non-toxic and non-immunogenic.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a method of treating inflammatory bowel disease in a subject comprising orally administering an effective amount of therapeutic beads that reduce levels of biologically active HMGB1 in the gastrointestinal tract. The method specifically targets the sequestration of extracellular HMGB1, a key mediator of intestinal inflammation, by exploiting its intrinsic high-affinity binding to certain DNA structures. The therapeutic beads are composed of a non-absorbable, biocompatible bead material functionalized with oligonucleotides selected for their ability to bind multiple redox and acetylation isoforms of HMGB1. Suitable bead materials include cross-linked polysaccharides such as sepharose, agarose, dextran, or synthetic polymers like polystyrene or poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA), with particle diameters ranging from approximately 45 to 165 micrometers, preferably averaging about 90 micrometers, to ensure retention in the gut lumen without systemic absorption.

The beads are coated with HMGB1-binding agents, specifically oligonucleotides comprising linear, kinked, or four-way junction DNA structures known to interact with HMGB1. Preferred embodiments utilize oligonucleotides S1 and S2, which are linear sequences synthesized with phosphorothioate-modified backbones to confer resistance to nuclease degradation. These oligonucleotides are covalently attached to the bead surface via a C6 amino linker conjugated to the 5′ terminus, enabling stable immobilization through standard coupling chemistries such as cyanogen bromide (CNBr) activation. The coating density is optimized to achieve high HMGB1 binding capacity, typically ranging from 4 to 8 nanomoles of DNA per milliliter of drained beads.

Administration is performed orally, allowing the beads to transit through the stomach and into the intestines, where they encounter and bind HMGB1 released from damaged or activated cells in the inflamed mucosa. To enhance gastric stability, the beads may be formulated with an enteric coating that resists dissolution at low pH (e.g., pH 1–3) but dissolves in the neutral to slightly alkaline environment of the small intestine or colon. Critically, the beads are non-absorbable, ensuring that they remain confined to the gastrointestinal lumen and are eventually excreted, minimizing systemic exposure and potential off-target effects.

The HMGB1-binding oligonucleotides may comprise natural phosphodiester backbones or modified backbones such as phosphorothioate, which replaces a non-bridging oxygen with sulfur to increase nuclease resistance. Nucleic acid sequences are selected from those demonstrated to bind HMGB1 with dissociation constants (Kd) in the low nanomolar range, including but not limited to sequences referred to as S1 (12–20.1), S2 (12–20.2), S3 (12-4WJ.1), and S4 (12-DP.1). Attachment to the bead matrix is achieved through covalent linkage, typically via primary amine groups on the oligonucleotide reacting with activated functional groups (e.g., CNBr, NHS esters) on the bead surface, forming stable amide or isourea bonds. This immobilization preserves the conformational integrity of the DNA and maintains high-affinity HMGB1 binding even under physiological conditions.

### EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS

The invention is exemplified by DNA-conjugated sepharose beads wherein oligonucleotides are covalently linked to CNBr-activated sepharose 4 Fast Flow resin. Binding studies demonstrate that HMGB1 binds specifically and saturably to these DNA-beads, with B1 (S1-conjugated) and B2 (S2-conjugated) beads showing superior performance. Materials used include recombinant HMGB1 purified from E. coli with endotoxin levels below 10 pg/mg, lipopolysaccharide (LPS), dextran sulfate sodium (DSS), and standard cell culture reagents. Cell culture methods involve RAW 264.7 macrophages, HeLa, and Caco-2 cells maintained in DMEM with fetal bovine serum and antibiotics, with treatments conducted in serum-free medium.

DNA-bead generation involves coupling amino-modified oligonucleotides to activated sepharose beads, followed by blocking unreacted sites with ethanolamine. Preparation yields beads with defined DNA densities: approximately 8 nmol of S1/S2 or 4 nmol of S3/S4 per mL of resin. The binding ratio of HMGB1 to DNA beads is calculated by incubating fixed bead volumes with increasing HMGB1 concentrations and quantifying bound protein via Western blot; results indicate molar ratios of ~6:1 for B1/B2 and ~12:1 for B3. FAM-labeled B2 DNA-beads are synthesized for fluorescence-based tracking, and ethidium bromide staining confirms DNA integrity on beads.

Concentration-dependent binding assays show that 5 µL of B1/B2 beads capture >90% of 2 µg HMGB1. HMGB1 protein is prepared by expression in E. coli and purified using Triton X-114 phase separation to remove LPS. Cytokine measurements via ELISA confirm that DNA-beads suppress HMGB1-induced TNF release without affecting LPS responses. HMGB1 levels are quantified by ELISA or Western blot. Surface plasmon resonance (Biacore) reveals high-affinity interactions between HMGB1 and immobilized DNA, with Kd values consistent with literature reports.

In vitro binding assays demonstrate that DNA-beads effectively deplete HMGB1 from LPS-stimulated RAW 264.7 supernatants. Depletion and saturation studies confirm specificity and capacity, while time-course experiments show maximal binding within 30 minutes. Stability studies reveal that B1/B2 beads retain HMGB1-binding function after exposure to pH 1–2 or physiological concentrations of DNase I, attributed to phosphorothioate backbone protection. Cytotoxicity assays using LDH release show no toxicity to Caco-2 or HeLa cells.

Animal experiments utilize DSS-induced colitis in BALB/c mice and spontaneous colitis in IL-10 knockout mice. Ex vivo, DNA-beads remove HMGB1 from serum of septic (CLP) mice and from fecal extracts and colonic tissue of colitis mice. Treatment with anti-HMGB1 antibodies validates HMGB1 as a target, reducing disease severity. Oral administration of B2 beads significantly improves body weight, reduces colon shortening, lowers serum and fecal HMGB1, and decreases colonic IL-6 and IL-1β mRNA. Tissues are harvested for histology, HMGB1 analysis, and qPCR.

Histology employs H&E staining and a blinded scoring system (0–14) assessing inflammation severity, extent, crypt damage, and area involvement. qPCR uses HPRT-normalized primers for IL-6, IL-1β, and IFN-β. Statistical analysis employs t-tests or ANOVA with p<0.05 significance.

Custom oligonucleotides are synthesized by Genemed Synthesis, Inc., with 5′ C6 amino linkers [NH2(CH2)6O-P(O)2]. Phosphorothioate backbones are used for S1/S2; phosphodiester for S3/S4. Conjugation efficiency is confirmed by OD260 measurements. Binding studies confirm B1/B2 beads bind all HMGB1 redox isoforms (fully reduced, disulfide, sulfonyl) and hyperacetylated forms found in colitis feces, but not histone H3 or TNF. Specificity is maintained in the presence of heparin, plasma, or acid. Beads remove HMGB1 from RAW supernatants, septic serum, and colitis stool/colon.

Therapeutic efficacy is demonstrated in both acute (DSS) and chronic (IL-10 KO) colitis models, with B2 beads outperforming controls. Advantages include acid stability, nuclease resistance, minimal toxicity, oral deliverability, and selective HMGB1 sequestration without inducing type I interferon. The therapy is feasible, effective, specific, and safe, representing a novel approach to IBD management through localized HMGB1 neutralization in the gut lumen.
