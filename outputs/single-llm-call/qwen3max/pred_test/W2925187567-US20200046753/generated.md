# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a composition and method for sustained inhibition of gene expression in eukaryotic cells, particularly in the context of RNA interference (RNAi)-mediated therapeutics. More specifically, the invention provides a nucleus-independent, cytoplasmic system for prolonged expression of short hairpin RNA (shRNA) through the use of a self-amplifying T7 RNA polymerase-based hybrid mRNA/DNA platform. The invention further encompasses a liposomal delivery vehicle for systemic administration of this hybrid system, enabling efficient and durable gene silencing in target tissues such as tumors without the need for repeated dosing. The disclosed technology is particularly advantageous for applications requiring long-term suppression of disease-associated genes, including cancer therapy, where conventional siRNA or plasmid-based shRNA approaches are limited by transient effects, nuclear dependency, immunogenicity, or inefficient delivery.

## BACKGROUND ART

RNA interference (RNAi) is a naturally occurring cellular mechanism that regulates gene expression by sequence-specific degradation of messenger RNA (mRNA). This process has been harnessed as a powerful tool for both functional genomics and therapeutic intervention. Small interfering RNA (siRNA), typically 21–23 base pairs in length, is the effector molecule of RNAi and can be introduced exogenously into cells to induce targeted gene silencing. However, despite its high specificity and potency, the clinical utility of synthetic siRNA is severely constrained by its short duration of action—typically lasting only 2 to 4 days in cultured cells due to rapid degradation, dilution during cell division, and lack of self-renewal capacity. Consequently, effective therapeutic outcomes require frequent and repeated administration, which poses significant challenges in patient compliance, cost, and potential toxicity.

To overcome the transient nature of siRNA, researchers have developed systems for endogenous expression of short hairpin RNA (shRNA), which is processed intracellularly into functional siRNA by the RNAi machinery. These systems commonly employ plasmid DNA vectors under the control of RNA polymerase III promoters such to U6 or H1, which drive high-level transcription of shRNA in the nucleus. While such approaches can extend the duration of gene silencing compared to synthetic siRNA, they suffer from critical limitations. Most notably, efficient nuclear delivery of plasmid DNA is a major bottleneck; less than 1% of internalized plasmids successfully reach the nucleus, especially in non-dividing or post-mitotic cells where the nuclear envelope remains intact. This inefficiency drastically reduces shRNA expression levels and undermines the reliability of gene silencing in vivo.

Alternative strategies have explored viral vectors—such as adenoviral, lentiviral, or adeno-associated viral systems—for shRNA delivery, which can achieve long-term expression due to efficient nuclear transduction and genomic integration (in the case of lentiviruses). However, these vectors carry significant safety risks, including insertional mutagenesis, immunogenic responses, and difficulties in large-scale manufacturing, thereby limiting their clinical translation. Nonviral delivery methods, such as cationic liposomes or polymers, have also been employed to deliver siRNA or shRNA-encoding plasmids. Yet, cationic carriers often exhibit high cytotoxicity, poor biocompatibility, and non-specific binding to serum proteins or endothelial cells, which compromises their circulation time and targeting efficiency. Moreover, even when delivered, plasmid-based shRNA systems remain dependent on nuclear entry for transcription, perpetuating the fundamental limitation of nuclear dependency.

In an effort to circumvent nuclear barriers, cytoplasmic expression systems have been proposed. For instance, systems utilizing the bacteriophage T7 RNA polymerase (T7pol) have been engineered to transcribe shRNA directly in the cytoplasm under the control of a T7 promoter. In such designs, T7pol is initially expressed from a separate plasmid driven by a eukaryotic promoter (e.g., CMV), which still requires nuclear entry for transcription. Thus, while the shRNA itself is transcribed in the cytoplasm, the initial production of T7pol remains nucleus-dependent, undermining the goal of complete nuclear independence. Furthermore, existing T7-based systems have not demonstrated sustained amplification of T7pol or prolonged shRNA expression beyond a few days.

These collective limitations highlight a critical unmet need: a robust, nonviral, nucleus-independent platform capable of sustaining high-level shRNA expression over extended periods without repeated administration. The present invention addresses this need by introducing a novel hybrid mRNA/DNA system that leverages the autocatalytic self-amplification properties of T7 RNA polymerase, initiated entirely in the cytoplasm, thereby enabling durable and efficient gene silencing without reliance on nuclear processes.

## DISCLOSURE

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inability of existing RNA interference technologies to achieve sustained, efficient, and safe inhibition of target gene expression in eukaryotic cells, particularly in vivo. Conventional siRNA formulations provide only transient silencing, necessitating repeated dosing. Plasmid-based shRNA systems, while offering longer duration, are hampered by inefficient nuclear delivery, especially in non-dividing cells, and may cause cellular toxicity due to saturation of nuclear export pathways. Viral vectors, though effective, pose safety concerns. Existing cytoplasmic expression systems either remain partially nucleus-dependent or fail to sustain expression beyond a few days. There is thus a pressing need for a delivery system that enables long-term, nucleus-independent, high-yield shRNA expression with minimal immunogenicity and cytotoxicity.

### Technical Solution

The present invention provides a composition for sustained inhibition of gene expression, comprising a hybrid mRNA/DNA system that operates entirely in the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells. This system includes a 5′-capped mRNA fragment encoding T7 RNA polymerase, a plasmid DNA construct designed for self-amplification of T7 RNA polymerase, and a DNA fragment encoding a gene expression inhibitor under the control of a T7 promoter. The T7 RNA polymerase is a bacteriophage-derived enzyme that catalyzes RNA synthesis from a T7 promoter without requiring host nuclear factors. The self-amplification of T7 RNA polymerase is achieved through a positive feedback loop: the initially delivered T7pol mRNA is translated into T7 RNA polymerase protein in the cytoplasm; this protein then binds to the T7 promoter on the self-amplification plasmid, transcribing a new mRNA that includes an Internal Ribosome Entry Site (IRES) domain, enabling cap-independent translation of additional T7 RNA polymerase. This cycle continues, generating large quantities of T7 RNA polymerase over time.

The plasmid DNA for self-amplification of T7 RNA polymerase comprises, in operable linkage, a T7 promoter, a gene encoding T7 RNA polymerase, an IRES domain derived from Encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV), a polyadenylation (poly A) tail for mRNA stability, and a T7 termination sequence. The inclusion of the IRES domain is critical, as cytoplasmic transcripts lack the 5′-cap structure normally required for ribosomal recruitment; IRES allows efficient translation initiation in the absence of a cap. The 5′-capped T7pol mRNA is synthesized in vitro using anti-reverse cap analog (ARCA) to ensure proper orientation and enhanced translational efficiency, serving as the initial trigger for the autocatalytic loop without requiring nuclear transcription.

The DNA fragment encoding the gene expression inhibitor contains a T7 promoter upstream of a sequence specifying a short hairpin RNA (shRNA), microRNA, siRNA precursor, or aptamer directed against a target gene. In preferred embodiments, the inhibitor is an shRNA designed to fold into a stem-loop structure that is processed by Dicer into mature siRNA. The target tissue may include tumor tissue, and the composition is delivered via a transporter, specifically a liposome composed of 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidylcholine (DOPC), a neutral lipid that minimizes nonspecific interactions and enhances tumor accumulation via the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect. The liposomal transporter is prepared by mixing the hybrid mRNA/DNA components with DOPC in a defined weight ratio (e.g., oligonucleotides:DOPC = 1:10), followed by lyophilization and rehydration, with removal of unencapsulated nucleic acids by filtration. This formulation achieves high loading efficiency (>90%) and forms nanoparticles of approximately 31 nm in diameter, ideal for systemic circulation and tumor penetration.

In experimental validation, the composition—termed auto_shRFP@LS when targeting red fluorescence protein (RFP)—demonstrated sustained cytoplasmic expression of T7 RNA polymerase for over 9 days, leading to continuous shRNA production and conversion to siRNA for more than 13 days. In B16F10/RFP melanoma cells, this resulted in prolonged suppression of RFP mRNA and protein, far exceeding the duration achieved by synthetic siRNA or nuclear-dependent shRNA systems (e.g., pSuper_shRFP). In vivo, a single intravenous injection of Cy5.5-labeled auto_shRFP@LS accumulated preferentially in tumor tissue and suppressed RFP expression for at least six days, whereas siRNA controls lost efficacy after three days. Critically, the system exhibited no detectable innate immunogenicity in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) or mouse models, as measured by TNF-α and INF-α release, unlike conventional siRNA/lipofectamine complexes.

The method for preparing the transporter involves synthesizing the 5′-capped T7pol mRNA, constructing the self-amplification plasmid and the T7-driven shRNA DNA fragment, mixing these components with DOPC at a molar ratio of pT7/shRNA DNA:T7pol mRNA:T7 autogene plasmid = 0.4:1:1 and a weight ratio of oligonucleotides to DOPC of 1:10, followed by purification to remove free oligonucleotides. This composition enables nucleus-independent, long-term gene silencing with superior duration, efficiency, and biocompatibility compared to existing technologies.

### Advantageous Effects of the Invention

The present invention offers several significant advantages over conventional RNAi technologies. First, it eliminates dependence on nuclear entry for gene expression, enabling efficient shRNA production even in non-dividing or post-mitotic cells. Second, the self-amplifying T7 RNA polymerase system provides sustained, high-level expression of shRNA for more than 13 days, dramatically extending the duration of gene silencing without repeated administration. Third, the use of neutral DOPC liposomes ensures high biocompatibility, low cytotoxicity, and preferential tumor accumulation via the EPR effect, enhancing therapeutic precision. Fourth, the system demonstrates minimal innate immunogenicity, reducing the risk of inflammatory side effects. Finally, the modular design allows easy adaptation to target any gene of interest, making it a versatile platform for long-term RNAi therapeutics in cancer, chronic diseases, and other conditions requiring durable gene suppression.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Example 1. Design and Preparation of pT7/T7 Polymerase System Capable of Sustained Expression of shRNA in Cytoplasm

The auto_T7pol plasmid was constructed to enable sustained cytoplasmic self-amplification of T7 RNA polymerase. It comprises a T7 promoter, followed by the T7 RNA polymerase gene, an EMCV IRES element, a polyA tail, and a T7 terminator sequence. This arrangement allows T7 RNA polymerase to transcribe its own mRNA in the cytoplasm, which is then efficiently translated via IRES-mediated ribosomal recruitment. The pT7/shRNA DNA template was prepared as a 64-bp double-stranded DNA fragment containing a T7 promoter upstream of a 19-nt RFP-targeting sequence, a 9-nt loop, and the reverse complement, ensuring proper shRNA folding. The 5′-capped T7pol mRNA was synthesized in vitro using a dsDNA template with a T7 promoter and ARCA, yielding mRNA with correct 5′-cap orientation for optimal translation. As a control, the pSuper_shRFP plasmid was constructed with an H1 promoter driving shRFP expression, requiring nuclear transcription and processing.

### Example 2. Preparation of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA

DOPC liposomes were prepared by mixing DOPC lipid with the hybrid mRNA/DNA components (pT7/shRNA DNA, T7pol mRNA, and auto_T7pol plasmid) in t-butanol, followed by lyophilization and rehydration in saline. The mixture was filtered to remove unencapsulated nucleic acids, resulting in stable lipoplexes designated auto_shRFP@LS.

### Example 3. Examination of Biophysicochemical Properties of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA

Gel retardation assays confirmed complete complexation of oligonucleotides with DOPC at a 1:10 weight ratio. Dynamic light scattering revealed a hydrodynamic diameter of 31.4 ± 1.2 nm and zeta potential of 1.1 mV. MTT assays showed no cytotoxicity up to 60 μg/mL. Flow cytometry and confocal microscopy confirmed efficient cellular uptake and cytoplasmic localization. ELISA assays on human PBMCs demonstrated no induction of TNF-α or INF-α, indicating low immunogenicity.

### Example 4. Experiment on Sustained Expression of T7 Polymerase Using Modified pT7/T7 Polymerase System

Western blot analysis showed that co-delivery of T7pol mRNA and auto_T7pol plasmid induced T7 RNA polymerase expression for over 9 days, with 4.0-fold higher levels than the pCMV_T7pol control. The IRES element and 5′-capped mRNA were essential for efficient cytoplasmic translation and amplification.

### Example 5. Examination of Long-Term Cytoplasmic Expression of shRNAs by Auto_shRFP@LS and their Conversion to siRNAs

TaqMan assays using a standard curve of synthetic siRFP quantified mature siRNA in transfected cells. Auto_shRFP@LS produced detectable siRFP for 13 days, peaking at day 10, while the auto(−)_shRFP@LS control (lacking auto_T7pol plasmid) showed significantly lower levels, confirming the role of self-amplification in sustained siRNA production.

### Example 6. Examination of In Vitro Gene Silencing by Auto shRNA @LS Nanoparticles

qRT-PCR showed RFP mRNA suppression for 13 days with auto_shRFP@LS, versus 4 days with siRFP@LS. Western blot and flow cytometry confirmed prolonged RFP protein knockdown. Auto_shRFP@LS outperformed both siRNA and nuclear shRNA (pSuper_shRFP) systems in duration and magnitude of silencing.

### Example 7. Examination of Cancer Accumulation Property of Auto_shRNA@LS Nanoparticles in Mouse Xenograft Model

In B16F10 tumor-bearing mice, Cy5.5-auto_shRFP@LS showed strong tumor accumulation 24 hours post-injection, while free Cy5.5-siRFP did not, demonstrating the EPR-mediated tumor targeting of the liposomal formulation.

### Example 8. Examination of Gene Silencing of Auto_shRNA@LS Nanoparticles in Mouse Xenograft Model

A single intravenous injection of auto_shRFP@LS suppressed RFP fluorescence in tumors for six days, as measured by IVIS imaging. Ex vivo analysis showed 2.7-fold lower RFP signal and 43% reduction in RFP mRNA compared to controls. Immunohistochemistry confirmed reduced RFP protein. The system’s efficacy, durability, and safety support its broad applicability for long-term gene silencing in vivo.
