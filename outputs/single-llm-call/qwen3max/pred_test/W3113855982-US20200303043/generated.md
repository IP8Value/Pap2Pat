# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to systems and methods of analysis of biological pathway data, particularly for the purpose of assessing the personalized efficacy of therapeutic agents, including botanical extracts such as those derived from Cannabis sativa. More specifically, the invention provides a bioinformatics framework that integrates high-throughput gene expression profiling with signaling pathway topology to generate a quantitative metric—the Cannabis Drug Efficiency Index (CDEI)—that enables ranking of cannabis-based therapeutics according to their predicted ability to restore normal cellular signaling in diseased or inflamed tissues. The invention further encompasses computer-implemented software, databases, and analytical workflows designed to facilitate precision medicine applications by matching individual patients or patient subgroups with the most efficacious cannabis-derived treatment based on molecular profiling of their biological samples.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The twentieth century witnessed unprecedented medical advancements, including the development of antibiotics, vaccines, and surgical techniques that dramatically reduced mortality from infectious and acute diseases. However, the burden of chronic diseases—such as cancer, autoimmune disorders, neurodegenerative conditions, and metabolic syndromes—has grown substantially, presenting new challenges due to their complex etiology and variable responses to standard therapies. A critical limitation in modern medicine is the substantial inter-individual variability in drug response, which often leads to ineffective treatments, adverse side effects, or delayed therapeutic intervention. This variability has spurred the emergence of personalized or precision medicine, an approach that tailors medical decisions and treatments to the individual characteristics of each patient.

The advent of genomics and epigenomics has revolutionized the understanding of disease mechanisms at the molecular level, enabling the development of gene-based personalized medicine strategies. Central to this paradigm are intracellular signaling pathways (SPs), which are highly coordinated networks of protein-protein and protein-nucleic acid interactions that regulate essential cellular processes such as proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, metabolism, and immune response. Dysregulation of these pathways is a hallmark of numerous pathological conditions, particularly proliferative diseases like cancer and inflammatory disorders. Consequently, accurate assessment of signaling pathway activation (SPA) status in patient-derived samples offers a powerful lens through which to understand disease progression and therapeutic response.

Bioinformatics tools have been developed to analyze SPs using large-scale molecular data, primarily from transcriptomic studies. Transcriptomic profiling via next-generation sequencing (NGS) or microarray technologies allows comprehensive quantification of mRNA expression levels across the entire genome from minimal tissue samples, including archived formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) specimens. This feasibility has made transcriptomics the preferred modality for clinical biomarker discovery and pathway analysis, despite the fact that proteomic data may more directly reflect functional protein activity. Numerous databases now catalog detailed SP maps, including UniProt, HPRD, WikiPathways, Reactome, KEGG, and QIAGEN SABiosciences Pathway Central, providing structured knowledge of molecular interactions.

However, contemporary bioinformatic methods for SPA analysis suffer from significant limitations. Many approaches treat pathways as unstructured gene sets, ignoring the directional and regulatory nature of molecular interactions (e.g., activation vs. inhibition). Others rely on kinetic modeling based on mass action laws or Michaelis–Menten equations, which require extensive knowledge of kinetic parameters and protein concentrations—data that are largely unavailable for most human pathways. Even advanced methods such as those disclosed in U.S. Patent Application Publication No. US2008254497A, U.S. Pat. No. 8,623,592, and U.S. Pat. No. 9,095,554 B2, while useful for certain applications, fail to provide a robust, scalable, and interpretable metric for drug efficacy that accounts for both the magnitude and directionality of pathway perturbations in individual patients.

There remains an urgent and unmet need for effective, non-toxic, personalized disease therapies, particularly for chronic inflammatory and proliferative conditions. Achieving this goal requires not only accurate profiling of disease-associated pathway dysregulation but also a quantitative method to predict how a given therapeutic agent—such as a cannabis extract—modulates these pathways toward a healthy state. Gene expression profiling, when integrated with pathway topology, offers a promising avenue, yet existing tools do not yield a unified efficiency score that can be used to rank multiple drug candidates for a specific patient or disease context. The present invention addresses these gaps by introducing a novel computational framework that transforms transcriptomic data into actionable therapeutic recommendations through the Cannabis Drug Efficiency Index.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces systems, methods, and software for assessing the personalized efficacy of cannabis-based drugs through integrative analysis of high-throughput gene expression profiling and signaling pathway topology. At the core of the invention is the Signaling Pathway Impact Analysis (SPIA) method, which quantifies the degree and direction of perturbation in biological pathways by incorporating both gene expression changes and interaction types (activation or inhibition) within a pathway graph. Building upon SPIA, the invention defines a novel metric—the Cannabis Drug Efficiency Index (CDEI)—that evaluates how effectively a cannabis drug reverses disease-associated transcriptomic alterations toward a healthy baseline.

The CDEI is calculated by first obtaining SPIA scores for a panel of biological pathways in three conditions: healthy control (C), untreated diseased case (U), and drug-treated diseased case (T). For each pathway, a pathway weight factor (wp) is computed based on the consistency of perturbation direction across case samples—specifically, wp equals the proportion of case samples exhibiting a positive (or negative) SPIA score when the mean SPIA is positive (or negative). The mean SPIA score is then adjusted by this weight to yield a mean weighted SPIA (SPIAμ). Statistical comparison of SPIAμ values between conditions is performed using Student’s t-test: |tU| measures the deviation of the untreated case from control, while |tT| measures the residual deviation after treatment. The CDEI is derived as 2 × (|tU| / (|tT| + |tU|) − 0.5), yielding a value between −1 and 1, where higher positive values indicate greater drug efficacy.

The invention provides a computer software product that automates this workflow, enabling clinicians and researchers to rank cannabis drugs according to their CDEI for a given disease or patient. The system accepts gene expression data from bodily samples (e.g., skin, oral, or intestinal tissues) and outputs a ranked list of cannabis extracts or strains, facilitating selection of the most promising candidate for therapeutic use. The method is applicable not only to individual patients but also to ethnic or demographic subgroups, allowing population-level optimization of cannabis-based therapies.

In one embodiment, the disease is a proliferative disorder, such as cancer, wherein dysregulated cell cycle and growth signaling pathways are targeted. In another, the disease is an inflammatory skin disorder, such as psoriasis or UV-induced dermatitis. The invention further includes methods for treating such conditions by administering the cannabis drug with the highest CDEI, thereby achieving alleviation, attenuation, or potential cure of the disease. A predefined CDEI threshold (e.g., >0.5) may be used to select only those drugs demonstrating clinically meaningful efficacy. The methodology is equally applicable to other botanical or synthetic compounds, though it is particularly optimized for the complex phytochemical mixtures found in cannabis extracts, which contain cannabinoids, terpenes, and flavonoids that act synergistically on multiple pathways.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

### Overview of Signaling Pathway Impact Analysis (SPIA) Method

The Signaling Pathway Impact Analysis (SPIA) method forms the computational foundation of the present invention. SPIA operates on a pathway graph G(V, E), where V represents the set of genes {g₁, g₂, ..., gₙ} and E denotes the set of directed interactions between them. The adjacency structure is encoded in a matrix where aᵢⱼ = 1 if gene i interacts with gene j or if i = j, and 0 otherwise. For each gene g in a pathway K, a perturbation factor (PF) is calculated as PF(g) = ΔE(g) + Σ_{γ ∈ U_g} β_{γg} · PF(γ) / n_down(γ), where ΔE(g) is the signed log-fold-change in gene expression relative to healthy controls, U_g is the set of upstream regulators of g, n_down(γ) is the number of downstream targets of γ, and β_{γg} is +1 for activation or −1 for inhibition. Upstream/downstream relationships are determined via depth-first search traversal of the pathway graph.

From PF, an accuracy value Acc(g) = PF(g) − ΔE(g) is derived, representing the propagated perturbation independent of direct expression change. This vector Acc can be expressed in matrix form as Acc = B · (I − B)⁻¹ · ΔE, where B is a normalized interaction matrix with elements β_{ij}/n_down(g_j), I is the identity matrix, and ΔE is the vector of log-fold-changes. The overall SPIA score for the pathway is the sum of all Acc(g) values, providing a signed measure of net pathway activation (positive) or suppression (negative). As illustrated in FIG. 1, this approach captures both overexpression and underexpression effects while respecting biological causality.

The invention is implemented in a bioinformatics system 100 (FIG. 2), comprising a processor, memory, user interface, and communication modules. System 100 executes the SPIA algorithm and CDEI calculation using input from transcriptomic datasets. It communicates via standard protocols (e.g., HTTP, FTP) and features a graphical user interface for data upload, parameter setting, and result visualization. Integrated databases (FIG. 3) store curated pathway topologies from sources such as QIAGEN SABiosciences, along with reference gene expression profiles from healthy individuals. These components enable end-to-end analysis from raw sequencing data to therapeutic recommendation.

### Calculation of Cannabis Drug Efficiency Index (CDEI)

The CDEI is computed through a multi-step SPIA-based algorithm. First, SPIA scores are generated for each biological pathway in control (C), untreated case (U), and treated case (T) samples. For each pathway, the mean SPIA across case samples is calculated. If this mean is positive, the pathway weight wp is defined as the fraction of case samples with positive SPIA; if negative, wp is the fraction with negative SPIA. This weighting emphasizes pathways consistently perturbed in the same direction across patients. The mean SPIA is then multiplied by wp to yield SPIAμ.

A one-sample Student’s t-test is applied to the SPIAμ values of U and T groups against the null hypothesis of zero perturbation (as in C). This yields |tU| and |tT|, the absolute t-statistics reflecting deviation from health. The CDEI is then calculated as 2 × (|tU| / (|tT| + |tU|) − 0.5). A CDEI of 1 indicates perfect restoration (|tT| = 0), 0 indicates no effect (|tT| = |tU|), and negative values indicate exacerbation. Drugs are ranked by descending CDEI, enabling selection of the top candidate for treatment.

### Example of CDEI Calculations

The invention was validated using three experimental datasets. In Example #I, human EpiDermFT 3D skin tissues were exposed to UVC to induce inflammation and treated with cannabis extracts #4, #8, #12, and #13. mRNA was extracted and sequenced via Illumina NextSeq500. CDEI analysis revealed Extract #8 as most efficient (CDEI = 0.82), followed by #4 (0.65), while #13 showed no benefit and #12 worsened inflammation (CDEI = −0.12).

In Example #II, EpiOral tissues were inflamed with TNFα and treated with extracts #1–#9. Extract #3 achieved near-complete reversal (CDEI = 0.98), with #5, #9, and #2 also highly effective (>0.87). Extracts #8 and #4 showed minimal efficacy (<0.16).

In Example #III, EpiIntestinal tissues treated with TNFα responded best to Extract #5 (CDEI = 0.91), followed by #6 (0.85). Notably, efficacy was tissue-specific: some extracts worked in oral but not intestinal models, underscoring the need for personalized assessment.

Cannabis extracts were prepared by ethyl acetate extraction of dried flowers from licensed cultivars, concentrated, and dissolved in DMSO for cell culture use. Dosage forms contemplated include oral tablets, capsules, injectables, topical creams, and slow-release formulations. Pharmaceutical compositions may combine cannabis extracts with additional active agents such as antibiotics (e.g., amoxicillin), corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone), antivirals (e.g., acyclovir), chemotherapeutics (e.g., doxorubicin), analgesics (e.g., morphine), or NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen).

The methodology enables ranking of cannabis strains for individual patients or ethnic groups, supporting precision medicine. While demonstrated for inflammation, the framework applies broadly to proliferative diseases like cancer. Modifications within the scope of the invention include alternative pathway databases, different statistical tests, or integration with proteomic data. The claims encompass the bioinformatics method, software, system, and therapeutic use of high-CDEI cannabis drugs for treating disease.
