# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to imaging methods, and more particularly to noninvasive magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) techniques for quantifying fractional flow reserve (FFR) in blood vessels. The invention provides a method and system for determining pressure gradients across vascular stenoses using multi-dimensional phase-contrast magnetic resonance (PC-MR) sequences, thereby enabling the assessment of hemodynamic significance of coronary artery disease without the need for invasive catheterization.

## BACKGROUND

Current clinical practice for evaluating the functional severity of coronary artery stenoses often relies on invasive fractional flow reserve (FFR) measurements, which require the insertion of a pressure-sensing guidewire into the coronary artery during cardiac catheterization. This procedure, while considered the gold standard, carries inherent risks such as vessel injury, arrhythmias, and contrast-induced nephropathy, and is associated with increased procedural costs and patient discomfort. Noninvasive alternatives, such as computed tomography (CT)-based FFR, have been developed but are limited by ionizing radiation exposure, reliance on contrast agents, and assumptions about microvascular resistance that may not hold true in all patients. Furthermore, existing MRI-based approaches for assessing coronary hemodynamics have been constrained by low spatial resolution, long acquisition times, and inadequate reproducibility in the dynamic and small-diameter coronary arteries. These limitations hinder the clinical translation of noninvasive FFR estimation using MRI. There remains a critical unmet need for a robust, reproducible, and truly noninvasive method to quantify pressure gradients and derive FFR values directly from high-resolution velocity data acquired via MRI, without exogenous contrast or radiation.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a method for quantifying fractional flow reserve (FFR) in a subject by noninvasively determining the pressure gradient within a segment of a blood vessel using magnetic resonance imaging. The method employs a multi-dimensional phase-contrast magnetic resonance (PC-MR) sequence to acquire time-resolved, three-directional velocity-encoded data across multiple slices encompassing a region of interest, such as a suspected stenosis. From this 4D flow dataset, the pressure gradient is calculated using the Navier-Stokes equations of fluid dynamics, which relate local velocity fields to pressure changes. The transtenotic pressure difference derived from this gradient is then correlated to an FFR value, providing a functional assessment of stenosis severity. The invention further encompasses an MRI system specifically configured to execute the multi-dimensional PC-MR sequence, including appropriate ECG-triggering and respiratory navigator-gating to mitigate motion artifacts. Additionally, the invention includes a non-transitory machine-readable medium encoded with instructions that, when executed by a processor, cause the MRI system to perform the sequence, reconstruct the velocity field, compute the pressure gradient, and output the FFR value for clinical interpretation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

For the purposes of this application, “Fractional Flow Reserve (FFR)” is defined as the ratio of the maximum blood flow in a coronary artery in the presence of a stenosis to the theoretical maximum flow in the absence of the stenosis, typically approximated by the ratio of distal to proximal pressure under hyperemic conditions. The FFR technique, as used herein, refers to the noninvasive derivation of this index from MRI-derived pressure gradients. A key advantage of MRI over CT for this application is its lack of ionizing radiation, superior soft tissue contrast, and the ability to directly measure blood flow velocities in three dimensions without the need for iodinated contrast agents. The method of using MRI for quantifying FFR involves acquiring a multi-dimensional PC-MR sequence, which is a specialized pulse sequence that encodes the velocity of moving spins (e.g., blood) along three orthogonal spatial directions (x, y, z) at multiple time points throughout the cardiac cycle. This sequence is designed to calculate the pressure gradient using the steady-state or simplified Navier-Stokes equations, which, under certain assumptions, allow the pressure gradient field (∇P) to be derived from the measured 4D flow velocity field (v) and its spatial and temporal derivatives. Image reconstruction is performed using generic Fourier transform methods to convert the acquired k-space data into the spatial domain. The process yields a 4D flow velocity field, from which the necessary velocity derivatives are computed to solve for the pressure gradient field. The transtenotic pressure difference is then obtained by integrating this pressure gradient along a streamline or path between a proximal and a distal point relative to a stenosis within a defined volume of interest (VOI) in the subject. Imaging parameters are carefully optimized for coronary applications, including an in-plane resolution of 0.58–0.67 mm, a slice thickness of 3.2 mm, and a flip angle of 15°. The acquisition is synchronized to a specific cardiac phase, typically mid-diastole, which represents a quiescent period of minimal coronary motion, with an acquisition window of 65–71 ms per phase. To ensure image quality, the scan employs ECG-triggering to synchronize data acquisition with the cardiac cycle and navigator-gating to compensate for respiratory motion, with a total scan time of 1–3 minutes per slice. The MRI system comprises a main magnet, gradient coils, and RF coils, and is controlled by a processor that executes the PC-MR sequence, performs image reconstruction, and implements the computational fluid dynamics algorithms to derive the pressure gradient and FFR. A computer in communication with the MRI system runs the software for data processing, visualization, and clinical reporting. The invention also includes a non-transitory machine-readable medium, such as a hard drive or solid-state memory, storing the executable instructions for these processes. The method is used for diagnosing cardiovascular disease, particularly by assessing the hemodynamic significance of a stenosis—a pathological narrowing of a blood vessel. Stenoses can be classified as mild (<50% diameter reduction), moderate (50–70%), or severe (>70%), with FFR providing a functional correlate to these anatomical classifications. While the primary embodiment uses a 3T MRI system, alternative imaging systems, including those at different field strengths or with different hardware configurations, may be used. The described methods and materials are understood to have equivalents that would be readily apparent to a person skilled in the art, and such equivalents are considered within the scope of the invention.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A 3D PC-MR sequence was implemented on a 3T MAGNETOM Verio scanner (Siemens Healthineers). The acquisition was prospectively ECG-triggered and combined with a pencil-beam navigator for respiratory gating, ensuring data collection only during end-expiration. The acquisition window was set to 65–71 ms, precisely timed to coincide with the mid-diastolic quiescent period of the coronary arteries. Key imaging parameters included an in-plane resolution of 0.58–0.67 mm, a slice thickness of 3.2 mm, a flip angle of 15°, and a velocity encoding (VENC) value of 30–45 cm/s in each direction, determined from a preliminary VENC scout scan. K-space was filled using a view-sharing scheme to maintain temporal fidelity even when the acquisition window slightly exceeded the quiescent period.

### Example 2

Phantom studies were conducted to validate the reproducibility of the method. A flow phantom was constructed using a silicone tube with an inner diameter of 4.8 mm, incorporating a 40% area stenosis. The phantom was perfused with gadolinium-doped water at a constant flow rate of 300 mL/min. A 3D PC-MR sequence was applied with a VENC of 130 cm/s in the through-plane direction and 30 cm/s in the in-plane directions. The experiment was repeated to assess measurement consistency. Results demonstrated excellent intra-class correlation coefficients (ICCs) for both velocity measurements and the derived pressure gradients (ΔP), confirming the technical robustness of the approach in a controlled environment.

### Volunteer Studies

Four healthy human volunteers underwent two repeat scanning sessions using the 2D PC-MR protocol described above. A total of 19 slices were acquired across the proximal left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery. The averaged maximum through-plane velocity across all subjects was 16.5 ± 4.0 cm/s. Reproducibility analysis showed excellent agreement for through-plane velocities (ICC = 0.93–0.96) and good agreement for in-plane velocities (ICC = 0.78–0.86). The mean transtenotic ΔP among all subjects was 0.1039 ± 0.28 mmHg, with an ICC of 0.51, indicating acceptable reproducibility for a baseline physiological state.

### Example 3

The quantification of the pressure gradient was performed by applying the Navier-Stokes equation to the 4D flow velocity field obtained from the PC-MR data. In healthy human volunteers, the method successfully generated noninvasive FFR measurements that were consistent with expected physiological norms (i.e., FFR ≈ 1.0). The objectives of this approach are to provide a completely noninvasive, radiation-free, and contrast-free method for functional assessment of coronary stenoses. Its advantages include direct measurement of flow physics, high reproducibility as demonstrated in phantom and volunteer studies, and seamless integration into a standard MRI workflow. Various alternative implementations are possible, such as using 4D flow MRI for whole-vessel coverage or employing advanced noise-reduction algorithms. The applicability of the various features—ECG/navigator gating, optimized VENC, and CFD-based pressure calculation—is broad across cardiovascular imaging. A skilled artisan would recognize that the core principles of this invention can be modified and adapted to other vascular territories beyond the coronaries. All such embodiments and modifications that achieve the same functional result are considered to be within the scope of the present invention.
