# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to modified recombinant binding proteins, specifically engineered non-immunoglobulin scaffold proteins that selectively bind pro-angiogenic isoforms of vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A). More particularly, the invention provides recombinant binding proteins based on designed ankyrin repeat protein (DARPin) scaffolds that exhibit high affinity and specificity for VEGF-Axxx isoforms, such as VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121, while demonstrating minimal or no binding to anti-angiogenic VEGF-Axxxb isoforms or other members of the VEGF family. These binding proteins are useful in the inhibition of pathological angiogenesis and vascular permeability associated with ocular diseases, including wet age-related macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, and diabetic macular edema, as well as in oncology and inflammatory conditions. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these binding proteins, methods of their production using recombinant DNA technology, and therapeutic and diagnostic uses thereof.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Angiogenesis—the formation of new blood vessels from pre-existing vasculature—is a tightly regulated physiological process essential for embryonic development, wound healing, and tissue regeneration. However, dysregulated or pathological angiogenesis contributes significantly to the progression of numerous diseases, including cancer, diabetic retinopathy, age-related macular degeneration, rheumatoid arthritis, and psoriasis. Among the key molecular mediators of angiogenesis is the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) family, with VEGF-A being the most potent and well-characterized member. VEGF-A exerts its biological effects primarily through binding to two receptor tyrosine kinases: VEGFR-1 (Flt-1) and VEGFR-2 (KDR/Flk-1), with VEGFR-2 being the principal mediator of pro-angiogenic signaling, including endothelial cell proliferation, migration, survival, and vascular permeability.

In addition to the canonical tyrosine kinase receptors, VEGF-A also interacts with neuropilin-1 and neuropilin-2, which function as co-receptors that enhance VEGF-A binding to VEGFR-2 and modulate downstream signaling. VEGF-A undergoes alternative splicing to generate multiple isoforms, collectively denoted as VEGF-Axxx, where “xxx” indicates the number of amino acids (e.g., VEGF-A121, VEGF-A165, VEGF-A189). These isoforms differ in their heparin-binding affinity, extracellular matrix association, and bioavailability. Notably, VEGF-A165 is the predominant and most biologically active isoform in humans, exhibiting strong pro-angiogenic activity. In contrast, a distinct class of splice variants, termed VEGF-Axxxb (e.g., VEGF-A165b), possesses anti-angiogenic properties due to altered C-terminal sequences that impair receptor activation.

Given the central role of VEGF-A in pathological angiogenesis, significant efforts have been directed toward developing therapeutics that inhibit VEGF-A signaling. Current clinical approaches include monoclonal antibodies (e.g., bevacizumab), antibody fragments (e.g., ranibizumab), and aptamers (e.g., pegaptanib), all of which target VEGF-A to block its interaction with VEGFR-2. While these agents have demonstrated clinical efficacy—particularly in ophthalmology for treating neovascular age-related macular degeneration and diabetic macular edema—they suffer from limitations such as short intraocular half-life, necessitating frequent intravitreal injections, poor corneal penetration limiting topical delivery, and manufacturing complexity. Moreover, some agents lack isoform selectivity or exhibit suboptimal binding kinetics.

To address these shortcomings, alternative protein scaffolds have been explored. Designed repeat domains, such as those described in international patent application WO 02/20565, represent a promising class of engineered binding proteins derived from natural repeat proteins like ankyrins. These scaffolds offer advantages over antibodies, including small size, high stability, solubility, ease of recombinant production, and absence of disulfide bonds or immunoglobulin folds. Despite these advances, there remains a critical need for improved anti-angiogenic agents that combine ultra-high affinity for pathogenic VEGF-A isoforms, resistance to aggregation, extended pharmacokinetics, and suitability for non-invasive administration routes such as topical eye drops. The present invention fulfills this unmet medical and pharmaceutical need.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a recombinant binding protein comprising a binding domain that specifically recognizes and binds pro-angiogenic VEGF-Axxx isoforms, particularly VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121, with high affinity, while exhibiting negligible binding to VEGF-Axxxb isoforms or other VEGF family members such as VEGF-B, VEGF-C, VEGF-D, placental growth factor (PlGF), or platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF). The binding domain is derived from a designed ankyrin repeat protein (DARPin) scaffold and is engineered to lack free cysteine residues, immunoglobulin folds, and immunoglobulin-like domains, thereby ensuring stability, monomeric behavior, and absence of effector functions. In preferred embodiments, the binding protein is conjugated to a polymer moiety—either proteinaceous or non-proteinaceous such as polyethylene glycol (PEG)—to enhance its pharmacokinetic profile, particularly for intraocular applications. The recombinant binding protein effectively inhibits VEGF-A-induced VEGFR-2 phosphorylation, endothelial cell sprouting, and vascular leakage in vitro and in vivo, and is suitable for formulation into pharmaceutical compositions for the treatment of angiogenesis-dependent disorders.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

VEGF-Axxx isoforms refer to the pro-angiogenic splice variants of human vascular endothelial growth factor A, including but not limited to VEGF-A121, VEGF-A165, and VEGF-A189, which contain exon-encoded C-terminal domains that facilitate receptor binding and activation. The binding domain of the invention is specifically selected or engineered to recognize an epitope present on these isoforms, enabling selective neutralization of their biological activity. This binding domain is part of a larger recombinant polypeptide constructed using recombinant DNA technology, wherein a nucleic acid sequence encoding the binding domain is operably linked to regulatory elements and expressed in a host cell such as *Escherichia coli*. The resulting protein may include optional polypeptide tags (e.g., His6-tag, FLAG-tag, Strep-tag) for purification or detection, connected via polypeptide linkers (e.g., (GGGGS)n, (EAAAK)n) that provide flexibility or rigidity as needed.

A polypeptide, as used herein, denotes a linear chain of at least 20 amino acid residues, preferably at least 50, and more preferably between 100 and 200 residues, forming a folded, stable structure. The recombinant binding protein may further comprise a proteinaceous polymer moiety—such as an albumin-binding domain or elastin-like polypeptide—with a molecular weight typically ranging from 5 to 100 kDa, which enhances serum half-life or ocular retention. Alternatively, a non-proteinaceous polymer moiety, exemplified by polyethylene glycol (PEG), polypropylene glycol, or polysialic acid, may be covalently attached via chemical conjugation (e.g., PEGylation at lysine or N-terminus) to improve solubility, reduce immunogenicity, and prolong residence time in the vitreous humor.

The binding protein itself is defined as a monomeric, non-antibody protein that comprises one or more binding domains arranged in tandem or fused to multimerization moieties (e.g., leucine zippers, Fc fragments—though excluded in preferred embodiments to avoid effector functions), with a total molecular weight generally between 15 and 150 kDa. Critically, the binding domain is based on a protein scaffold that is not derived from immunoglobulins; instead, it utilizes repeat protein architectures such as designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins). DARPins consist of repeating structural units—typically 33-amino-acid ankyrin motifs—that stack to form a curved solenoid structure capped by N- and C-terminal capping modules that stabilize the fold. Each repeat unit contains conserved framework residues and variable positions that are randomized in libraries to enable selection of high-affinity binders.

Through ribosome display or phage display screening of naïve DARPin libraries, clones are isolated that bind VEGF-Axxx with dissociation constants (Kd) in the low picomolar range, as determined by surface plasmon resonance (SPR) or competitive ELISA. Preferred binding domains exhibit a Kd ≤ 50 pM for VEGF-A165 and show at least 100-fold selectivity over VEGF-A165b and other VEGF family ligands. These domains are devoid of free cysteine residues to prevent aggregation and maintain monomeric state, and they demonstrate exceptional stability in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), with midpoint thermal denaturation temperatures (Tm) exceeding 65°C and no detectable aggregation after prolonged storage at 40°C.

Functional characterization includes inhibition of human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) spheroid sprouting in collagen matrices, where the binding protein achieves IC50 values ≤ 100 pM, often below 10 pM. In vivo, the protein inhibits VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage in rabbit eyes following intravitreal injection and suppresses corneal or choroidal neovascularization upon topical administration as eye drops. Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the binding protein in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as PBS, with optional excipients (e.g., sucrose, polysorbate 80) and stabilizers, formulated for intraocular injection, sustained-release implants, or topical ocular delivery. For posterior segment diseases like diabetic macular edema or wet AMD, preferred formulations enable monthly or less frequent dosing due to extended half-life, especially when PEGylated.

The invention further provides methods of treating conditions driven by pathological angiogenesis or vascular hyperpermeability, including retinal ischemic diseases, diabetic retinopathy, age-related macular degeneration, pulmonary hypertension, chronic inflammation, and solid tumors. Administration may be via intravitreal injection, subconjunctival depot, or eye drops, with combination therapy possible alongside PlGF inhibitors or other anti-angiogenic agents. The binding proteins are obtained by expressing selected DARPin clones in microbial hosts, purifying via affinity chromatography, and optionally conjugating to PEG. The entire platform enables rapid generation of high-affinity, stable, and manufacturable VEGF-A antagonists superior to current biologics in potency, stability, and delivery flexibility.
