# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of electrochemical sensing, particularly to ion-selective electrodes and solid-state potentiometric sensors for the detection of molecular ions in aqueous and complex environmental media. More specifically, the invention relates to the use of metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) as highly selective ionophores in ion-selective membranes, enabling the development of robust, miniaturizable, and size-selective sensing platforms capable of distinguishing molecular ions from elemental ions based on structural and dimensional compatibility. The invention further encompasses solid-contact ion-selective electrodes (SC-ISEs) and ion-sensitive field-effect transistors (ISFETs) functionalized with MOSC-based mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) for applications in environmental monitoring, biomedical diagnostics, industrial process control, and water quality assessment. The technical domain integrates principles of supramolecular chemistry, materials science, microfabrication, and electroanalytical instrumentation to provide a novel class of ionophores that overcome long-standing limitations in selectivity, stability, and response kinetics associated with conventional ion-sensing technologies.

## BACKGROUND

Ion sensing technology plays a critical role in numerous scientific, industrial, and clinical domains, ranging from environmental pollutant detection to point-of-care diagnostics. Conventional ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) rely on ionophores—molecular receptors embedded within polymeric membranes—that selectively bind target ions and generate a measurable electrical potential difference across the membrane-electrolyte interface. Despite decades of advancement, existing ionophores suffer from significant limitations, including poor selectivity for large molecular ions, susceptibility to leaching, limited chemical and mechanical stability, and suboptimal response characteristics such as slow kinetics or non-Nernstian slopes. Traditional ionophores, such as valinomycin for potassium or calixarenes for alkali metals, are typically optimized for small elemental cations and anions, but they exhibit minimal affinity for structurally complex organic ions like methylene blue (MB⁺) or tetrabutylammonium (TBA⁺). Moreover, many ionophores lack the architectural precision required to discriminate between ions of similar charge but different size or shape, leading to cross-sensitivity and inaccurate measurements in complex matrices. The absence of ionophores capable of size-selective recognition of molecular ions has hindered the development of reliable potentiometric sensors for emerging contaminants, pharmaceuticals, and biomarkers. Consequently, there exists a pressing need for a new class of ionophores that combine structural tunability, high binding affinity, dimensional selectivity, and compatibility with both conventional and solid-state transduction platforms. The present invention addresses this unmet need through the design and application of metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs)—nanoscale, cage-like architectures that function as biomimetic hosts with precisely defined internal and external cavities tailored for the selective encapsulation of molecular ions.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel solid-liquid junction sensing interface based on metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) integrated into ion-selective membranes, thereby enabling highly selective and sensitive potentiometric detection of molecular ions. The invention discloses MOSC-based ion sensing technology wherein MOSCs serve as advanced ionophores in mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) used in both conventional and solid-contact ion-selective electrodes (SC-ISEs). This approach offers significant advantages over prior art, including near-Nernstian response slopes, exceptional size selectivity, resistance to leaching, and compatibility with microfabrication processes. The invention further describes an apparatus for the detection of molecular ions, comprising a sensing element functionalized with a MOSC-containing MMM, a reference electrode, and a high-impedance voltmeter or field-effect transistor readout system. Potential applications span environmental monitoring (e.g., detection of organic dyes in wastewater), biomedical sensing (e.g., neurotransmitter analogs), and food safety (e.g., quaternary ammonium preservatives). The invention also encompasses potentiometric sensors that overcome the limitations of prior art ionophores, which often fail to provide stable, selective, and reversible responses to large organic ions. Central to the invention is the unique structure of MOSCs, which feature a central endo-cavity and multiple peripheral exo-cavities, creating a multi-pore architecture capable of simultaneously accommodating and concentrating target molecular ions. These sensors exhibit a near-Nernstian response—typically 56–60 mV per decade change in ion activity—demonstrating thermodynamic efficiency comparable to ideal ion-selective systems. Furthermore, the MOSC-based sensors display remarkable stability in various aqueous environments, including natural river water, without degradation or loss of performance. The electrical response is measured via open-circuit potential or drain current modulation in ISFET configurations, providing real-time, label-free detection. Example 1 demonstrates proof-of-concept sensing of MB⁺ and TBA⁺ using a MOSC-II-Co-based ISE, showing rapid response, low detection limits, and size discrimination. Example 2 confirms the absence of leaching due to strong host-guest binding within the MOSC cavities. Example 3 validates sensor performance in simulated wastewater, proving robustness in complex real-world samples. Collectively, these examples underscore the transformative potential of MOSC-based ionophores in next-generation electrochemical sensing.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is founded upon the discovery and application of metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) as a new class of ionophores for potentiometric sensing. MOSCs are discrete, three-dimensional coordination cages formed by the self-assembly of metal ions and organic ligands, resulting in well-defined nanocavities with precise geometries. Specifically, the MOSC designated as 1-Co (or MOSC-II-Co) is synthesized from Co(II), p-tert-butylsulfonyl-calix[4]arene, and 1,4-benzenedicarboxylate, yielding an octahedral structure with an outer diameter of 3.3 nm, an inner (endo) cavity of 1.7 nm, and six surrounding (exo) cavities of approximately 0.74 nm in diameter. As illustrated in FIG. 1, this architecture provides multiple binding domains capable of hosting molecular ions through a combination of size exclusion, hydrophobic interactions, and cation-π effects. The multi-pore nature of MOSCs is further detailed in FIG. 2 and FIG. 3, which depict the spatial arrangement of exo-cavities around the central endo-cavity, enabling cooperative binding and local concentration enhancement of target analytes. Critically, the MOSC structure is chemically tunable; by varying the metal nodes, organic linkers, or calixarene substituents, the size, polarity, and binding affinity of the cavities can be precisely engineered for specific ions. This tunability allows MOSCs to function as versatile host systems for a wide range of molecular guests, particularly large organic cations that cannot be effectively recognized by conventional ionophores.

In the context of ion sensing, MOSCs are incorporated into mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) composed of poly(vinyl chloride) (PVC), plasticizer (e.g., bis(2-ethylhexyl)sebacate), ionic additive (e.g., potassium tetrakis(4-chlorophenyl)borate), and the target ion (e.g., methylene blue). As shown in FIG. 4A-B, the MMM is cast onto a substrate—either a conventional pipette tip filled with internal electrolyte or a solid-contact surface such as pyrolyzed SU8-derived carbon—to form a functional ion-selective electrode. Two primary scaffold architectures are employed: the MOSC-MMM scaffold, wherein the MOSC is dispersed homogeneously within the polymer matrix, and the MOSC-solid sensing surface (SSS) scaffold, wherein MOSCs are covalently or adsorptively attached to a conductive solid surface. Both scaffolds leverage the MOSC’s ability to selectively bind target ions at the membrane-electrolyte interface, generating a phase-boundary potential that varies with ion activity. The tunability of MOSC cavities permits customization for specific ion detection; for instance, a MOSC with a 1.7-nm endo-cavity is ideal for MB⁺ (1.6 nm length), while exo-cavities accommodate its width (0.7 nm). The MMM scaffold is used in conventional ISEs, offering ease of fabrication and high signal fidelity, whereas the SSS scaffold enables integration with microelectronics, facilitating miniaturization and mass production.

Electrical response is measured potentiometrically, as illustrated in FIG. 5, where the open-circuit potential between the MOSC-functionalized working electrode and a reference electrode is recorded. In advanced implementations, an ion-sensitive field-effect transistor (ISFET) is employed, as shown in FIG. 6, wherein the gate dielectric is functionalized with the MOSC-MMM. The binding of ions modulates the surface potential, altering the drain current, as depicted in FIG. 7. For SSS-based devices, MOSCs are attached to the solid surface via chemical bonds (e.g., silane or carboxylate linkages), ensuring stable immobilization without leaching. FIG. 8 and FIG. 9 illustrate the integration of SSS scaffolds into planar and microfluidic sensor platforms, respectively. The key advantages of MOSC-based ion sensors include near-Nernstian response, exceptional size selectivity, resistance to leaching, rapid response times (<1 s at high concentrations), and compatibility with both liquid and solid-contact transducers. These features collectively enable reliable, selective, and robust detection of molecular ions in complex environments, surpassing the performance of all known prior art ionophores.

### EXAMPLE 1: PROOF-OF-CONCEPT STUDY WITH MB+ AND TBA+

This example demonstrates the feasibility of using MOSC-II-Co as an ionophore for the potentiometric detection of molecular ions. The MOSC-II-Co molecule, synthesized as described, features a central endo-cavity and six exo-cavities optimized for binding planar and spherical organic cations. Materials and methods followed standard protocols for MMM preparation, involving dissolution of PVC, DOS, KTpClPB, and MOSC-II-Co in THF, followed by addition of methylene blue (MB⁺) to form a dark blue membrane solution. A conventional ISE was fabricated by capillary filling of the MMM into a pipette tip, insertion of an Ag/AgCl pellet, and conditioning in 10 μM MB⁺ overnight. Potentiometric measurements revealed a near-Nernstian response of 56.8 ± 2.5 mV/p[MB⁺], with a detection limit of ~300 nM, as shown in FIG. 10. Impedance spectroscopy (FIG. 11) confirmed a two-fold reduction in charge-transfer resistance due to MOSC-mediated ion transfer. Size-selectivity was evaluated by testing against TBA⁺, which yielded a 60.0 ± 1.5 mV/p[TBA⁺] response (FIG. 12), confirming that cavity dimensions—not just chemistry—govern selectivity. Response time studies (FIG. 15) showed sub-second response above 30 μM, with slower kinetics at lower concentrations due to diffusion-limited exchange. Potential stability was excellent, with drift <5 mV/h. Control experiments with smaller ions (K⁺, Ag⁺, NH₄⁺) showed negligible response (FIGS. 13–16), underscoring the size-exclusion principle.

### EXAMPLE 2: LEECHING STUDY

To assess membrane stability, a leeching test was conducted by immersing MOSC-containing and MOSC-free MMMs in deionized water for one week. As shown in FIG. 17, the MOSC-free MMM released MB⁺ into the water, turning it blue, whereas the MOSC-containing MMM retained all MB⁺, with no color change observed. This confirms that the MOSC acts as a non-leaching host, immobilizing the ionophore-analyte complex within the membrane matrix—a critical feature for long-term sensor reliability.

### EXAMPLE 3: SIMULATED WASTE WATER SAMPLE

The sensor’s performance in real-world conditions was tested using Fyris River water spiked with MB⁺. As shown in FIG. 18, the response curve remained near-Nernstian, though the detection limit shifted slightly due to partial reduction of MB⁺ by microbial activity in the river water. UV-vis analysis confirmed lower MB⁺ concentration in river water versus DI water, yet the sensor responded only to the cationic form, demonstrating specificity and resilience in complex matrices.

### EXAMPLE 4: MOLECULAR ION SENSING

This example details the use of a silicon nanowire ISFET (SiNW-ISFET) functionalized with MOSC-based MMM for molecular ion detection. The MMM was prepared as before and conditioned in MB⁺ solution. Electrical measurements were performed using a standard setup with source, drain, and gate contacts. Transfer characteristics (Id-Vg curves) showed a clear threshold voltage (VTH) shift upon MB⁺ binding. Surface functionalization with MMM created a potential at the SiO₂/electrolyte interface, modified by the MOSC/electrolyte interaction. The VTH difference between functionalized and control SiNW-ISFETs confirmed sensing activity. Gate leakage remained negligible, and the surface potential (SS) was unaffected by MMM alone, indicating that the response arises from ion binding. Using MB⁺-MMM1, a near-Nernstian ΔVTH of ~58 mV/decade was observed with increasing MB⁺ activity (αMB), with a lower detection limit of 300 nM. An overshoot after sample addition reflected transient binding kinetics. At high αMB, deviation from ideal response occurred due to site saturation. Selectivity tests against K⁺, Na⁺, and NH₄⁺ showed minimal interference, confirming superior selectivity for molecular ions. In contrast, a SiNW-ISFET without MOSC showed no stable response, highlighting the necessity of the MOSC host. A second membrane (MB⁺-MMM2) achieved a lower detection limit of 100 nM. Elemental ion sensing was also demonstrated using Na⁺-MMM, yielding a near-Nernstian response to αNa and a selectivity coefficient KNa,KPot of 10⁻³. Cross-sensitivity to molecular ions was negligible. Finally, multiplexed analysis was achieved by simultaneously detecting MB⁺ and Na⁺ in a complex sample, proving the platform’s versatility for multi-analyte sensing.
