# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of regenerative medicine, specifically to methods and compositions for the production and quality assessment of corneal endothelial cells suitable for transplantation. Corneal endothelial cells (CECs) constitute a monolayer of specialized cells located on the inner surface of the cornea, adjacent to Descemet’s membrane, and are essential for maintaining corneal transparency through active regulation of stromal hydration. Due to their limited proliferative capacity in vivo, loss of CECs resulting from disease, trauma, or aging can lead to irreversible corneal edema and vision impairment. The invention provides novel means for identifying, producing, and validating cell populations that exhibit functional and molecular characteristics of native human corneal endothelial cells, thereby enabling reliable and scalable cell-based therapies for corneal endothelial dysfunction.

## BACKGROUND ART

Corneal endothelial cell damage represents a major cause of corneal decompensation and visual loss worldwide. Human CECs are post-mitotic under physiological conditions, exhibiting minimal regenerative capacity following injury or attrition. Consequently, when CEC density falls below a critical threshold—typically around 500–700 cells/mm²—the pump-leak balance necessary for corneal deturgescence is disrupted, leading to stromal swelling, opacification, and eventual blindness. Current standard treatment relies on allogeneic corneal transplantation, including penetrating keratoplasty or endothelial keratoplasty procedures such as Descemet’s stripping automated endothelial keratoplasty (DSAEK) or Descemet’s membrane endothelial keratoplasty (DMEK). However, this approach is severely constrained by the global shortage of high-quality donor corneas, immune rejection risks, surgical complications, and long-term graft failure rates.  

Efforts to develop alternative therapies have focused on in vitro expansion of primary human CECs or derivation of CEC-like cells from pluripotent stem cells, including embryonic stem (ES) cells and induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells. Despite progress, significant challenges remain. Cultured primary CECs often undergo endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EnMT), losing their characteristic hexagonal morphology, tight junction integrity, and ion transport function, thereby rendering them unsuitable for clinical use. Similarly, stem cell-derived CECs frequently exhibit incomplete differentiation, heterogeneous phenotypes, or residual pluripotency markers, raising safety and efficacy concerns. A critical bottleneck in these approaches is the absence of robust, standardized criteria to determine whether a given cell population possesses the molecular and functional identity of authentic human CECs. While certain structural proteins like Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, ZO-1, and collagen type VIII have been used as qualitative markers, they lack specificity and do not reliably correlate with functional maturity. Thus, there exists an urgent need for objective, quantitative biomarkers that reflect the transcriptional identity of bona fide corneal endothelial cells and can be integrated into manufacturing and quality control protocols for cell therapy products.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of reliable, objective, and quantifiable means to identify and produce cell populations that are functionally and molecularly equivalent to native human corneal endothelial cells for use in transplantation therapies. Existing methods for generating CECs from stem cells or expanding primary CECs in culture yield heterogeneous populations whose suitability for clinical application cannot be confidently assessed due to the absence of definitive molecular markers that distinguish mature, functional CECs from progenitor states or off-target differentiated cells.

### Solution to Problem

The invention provides a comprehensive solution by establishing a gene expression-based framework for the identification, production, and validation of corneal endothelial cell populations suitable for transplantation. Specifically, the invention discloses that the expression levels of certain genes—namely POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B—serve as highly specific and sensitive indicators of corneal endothelial cell identity and maturation status. The invention provides means for identifying cells suitable for corneal transplantation by measuring the expression of these genes in a candidate cell population. Furthermore, it provides means for producing such cells by culturing stem cells—including iPS cells, ES cells, EG cells, GS cells, or somatic stem cells—under conditions conducive to differentiation into corneal endothelial lineage cells.  

A method for producing a cell population suitable for corneal transplantation comprises culturing undifferentiated or progenitor cells in a serum-free medium optimized for corneal endothelial differentiation, optionally supplemented with defined growth factors, and continuing the culture until the expression levels of POU6F2, LMX1B, and/or TFAP2B reach predetermined reference values established from native human corneal endothelial tissues or validated differentiated cell populations. Gene expression is measured using quantitative techniques such as qRT-PCR, RNA sequencing, or CAGE analysis, with particular emphasis on promoter-specific activity, especially p3@POU6F2, p2@LMX1B, and p2@TFAP2B. The method further defines criteria for determining cell population suitability: a population is deemed suitable if POU6F2 expression is significantly upregulated relative to progenitor states and is detectable at levels comparable to those in native CECs, while maintaining appropriate expression of LMX1B and TFAP2B consistent with neural crest-derived ocular mesenchymal identity.  

The cell population may be prepared in various administrable forms, including a suspension, a confluent sheet, or a three-dimensional spheroid, and is administered to a patient suffering from corneal endothelial dysfunction via intracameral injection or surgical implantation. The invention also provides efficient means for scaling production by enabling real-time monitoring of differentiation progress through gene expression profiling, thereby reducing batch failure rates and ensuring product consistency. Critically, the use of POU6F2 as a maturation marker allows discrimination between immature progenitors and functionally competent CECs, addressing a key gap in current differentiation protocols.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The present invention offers multiple advantageous effects. First, it enables the objective, quantitative assessment of corneal endothelial cell identity using molecular markers with high tissue specificity, particularly POU6F2, which is virtually exclusive to corneal endothelium among all human tissues. Second, it facilitates the development of robust, reproducible differentiation protocols by providing clear molecular endpoints for process optimization. Third, it enhances the safety and efficacy of cell-based corneal therapies by ensuring that only fully differentiated, non-proliferative, and functionally mature cell populations are transplanted. Fourth, it supports regulatory compliance by offering standardized quality control metrics for cell therapy manufacturing. Finally, by enabling the production of transplant-ready CECs from renewable stem cell sources, the invention alleviates dependence on scarce donor corneas and expands access to sight-restoring treatments globally.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

The invention encompasses a cell population comprising cells that express POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B at levels indicative of corneal endothelial identity and functional maturity. Differentiation into corneal endothelial cells is induced by culturing pluripotent or progenitor cells under defined conditions that mimic the developmental niche of neural crest-derived periocular mesenchyme. The culture medium is serum-free to prevent uncontrolled differentiation and fibroblastic drift, and may include Knockout Serum Replacement (KSR), DMEM/F12, non-essential amino acids, L-glutamine, 2-mercaptoethanol, and antibiotics. Examples of usable media include modified DMEM formulations supplemented with bFGF during proliferation phases and FBS-containing media during terminal differentiation. The medium is substantially free of exogenous differentiation inducers or promoters that could drive off-target lineages, ensuring lineage fidelity.  

Preferred medium composition maintains a pH of approximately 7.2–7.4 and is incubated at 37°C in 5% CO₂. Culture duration typically spans 21–28 days for terminal differentiation. Cells are seeded onto culture containers—such as multiwell plates, flasks, or bioreactors—whose inner surfaces are coated with extracellular matrix proteins like laminin-511E8 or FNC coating mix to support adhesion and polarization. Starting cell types include human iPS cells reprogrammed from somatic cells, ES cells derived from blastocysts, EG or GS cells from germ lineages, or somatic stem cells isolated from ocular tissues. Corneal endothelial cells may also be isolated directly from donor corneas by enzymatic digestion of Descemet’s membrane.  

Central to the invention is the measurement of gene expression in the cultured population. The genes POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B are quantified using qRT-PCR with primers targeting specific transcript isoforms or promoter regions, notably p3@POU6F2, which exhibits strict corneal endothelial specificity. Expression levels are normalized to housekeeping genes such as GAPDH and compared against reference values derived from native human CECs. A population is deemed suitable for transplantation if POU6F2 expression is markedly elevated—indicating terminal maturation—and LMX1B and TFAP2B are expressed at levels consistent with neural crest-derived ocular identity. If expression thresholds are not met, the culture is continued under differentiation conditions until benchmarks are achieved.  

The validated cell population is formulated into a pharmaceutical preparation comprising cells suspended in a carrier such as balanced salt solution, hyaluronic acid, or a biocompatible hydrogel. Scaffold materials like temperature-responsive polymers or decellularized Descemet’s membrane may be incorporated to support sheet formation. Solubilizing agents and other pharmaceutically acceptable carriers ensure stability and injectability. The preparation is administered to human patients diagnosed with Fuchs’ endothelial corneal dystrophy, bullous keratopathy, or other endothelial disorders via intracameral injection or surgical placement.  

Method B for determining suitability involves parallel assessment of multiple CEC-specific promoters identified through CAGE analysis, including but not limited to SLC4A11, COL8A2, and MIR184, in addition to the core transcription factors. This multi-gene signature enhances diagnostic confidence. Upon confirmation of suitability, the population is used as a cell therapy product to regenerate corneal endothelium, restoring pump function and corneal clarity.

### EXAMPLES

Human comeoscleral rims were obtained from eye banks within 24 hours postmortem. Descemet’s membrane with attached endothelium was microsurgically dissected and immediately stabilized in RNAlater. Total RNA was extracted using miRNeasy kits, and Heliscope CAGE libraries were prepared by cap-trapping, linker ligation, and single-molecule sequencing. Sequence reads were mapped to the hg19 genome, and tags aligned to promoter regions were counted. Comparison with the FANTOM5 atlas identified promoters with >10 tpm in CECs and <3 tpm in all other tissues. Among these, POU6F2 (p3 promoter), LMX1B (p2), and TFAP2B (p2) showed exceptional specificity. Their expression was validated across 22 non-ocular and multiple ocular tissues via qRT-PCR, confirming restriction to corneal endothelium for POU6F2 and enrichment in neural crest-derived ocular tissues for LMX1B and TFAP2B. These genes serve as the foundation for the invention’s quality control framework.
