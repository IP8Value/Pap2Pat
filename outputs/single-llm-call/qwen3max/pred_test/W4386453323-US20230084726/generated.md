## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of quantum sensing, and more particularly to methods and systems for enhanced detection of physical signals using ensembles of solid-state quantum defects. Specifically, the invention provides a quantum logic-enhanced sensing protocol that leverages hybrid electronic-nuclear spin systems within nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond to achieve significant improvements in signal-to-noise ratio and sensitivity for alternating current (AC) magnetic field detection and other physical observables.

## BACKGROUND

Quantum sensing based on nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond has emerged as a powerful platform for high-precision magnetometry, thermometry, and electrometry at the nanoscale. A key limitation in ensemble-based NV sensing arises from the relatively poor optical readout fidelity of the electronic spin state, which is fundamentally constrained by photon shot noise and limited spin contrast. While dynamical decoupling sequences such as XY8 or DROID-60 can extend coherence times and improve spectral resolution, the ultimate sensitivity remains bottlenecked by the single-shot readout inefficiency. Prior approaches have attempted to mitigate this through repeated measurements or advanced pulse sequences, but these often fail to address the intrinsic trade-off between measurement duration and signal degradation due to spin relaxation. Ensemble sensing further complicates matters, as inhomogeneous broadening and spin-spin interactions limit coherence times, especially in moderately doped diamond samples. Consequently, there exists a critical need for a method that decouples the sensing duration from the readout limitations, thereby enabling longer integration times without sacrificing fidelity. The use of nuclear spins as quantum memory elements offers a promising route, as their long coherence lifetimes can preserve encoded information while the electronic spins are reset and reused for repeated optical interrogation. However, practical implementation of such schemes in macroscopic ensembles has remained elusive due to challenges in control fidelity, cross-talk, and scalability. The present invention overcomes these limitations by introducing a robust, globally controlled quantum logic protocol that exploits the natural two-qubit structure of each NV center—comprising an electronic spin and its proximal nitrogen nuclear spin—to achieve orders-of-magnitude enhancement in both signal-to-noise ratio and absolute sensitivity.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a method of quantum sensing that enables high-fidelity detection of target signals by leveraging a hybrid quantum system comprising electronic and nuclear spin states within an ensemble of quantum defects. In accordance with the invention, information regarding a target signal—such as an AC magnetic field—is first encoded into the electronic spin states of the quantum defects during a sensing stage. This information is then mapped onto long-lived nuclear spin states via a coherent SWAP operation, effectively transferring the sensor state into a quantum memory register. Following this mapping, a light pulse is applied to reset the electronic spin states to a known polarization, thereby preparing them for subsequent readout cycles without disturbing the stored nuclear spin information. The readout stage is then repeated multiple times: in each cycle, the information stored in the nuclear spin states is mapped back onto the electronic spin states using a controlled-NOT (CNOT) gate, after which an optical readout pulse interrogates the electronic spin state via spin-dependent fluorescence. This repetitive readout protocol continues for a number N of cycles, where N is limited primarily by the nuclear spin lifetime T₁. The resulting series of fluorescence measurements is then processed—preferably with optimal weighting based on signal amplitude and noise variance—to reconstruct the target signal with enhanced fidelity. The invention specifically employs an ensemble of quantum defects, preferably nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers in diamond, with a density ranging from 0.5 to 20 parts per million (ppm) of substitutional nitrogen, yielding an NV concentration sufficient for ensemble averaging while maintaining adequate coherence properties. A static magnetic bias field is applied parallel to the NV symmetry axis, with a magnitude selected to suppress flip-flop relaxation channels and extend the nuclear spin T₁; in preferred embodiments, this field ranges from 1700 Gauss to 4000 Gauss, and most preferably is approximately 3700 Gauss. The quantum sensor system implementing this method comprises integrated microwave and radiofrequency delivery, optical excitation, fluorescence collection, and real-time control electronics, all coordinated by a central controller to execute the multi-stage quantum sensing protocol.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The quantum sensor system of the present invention comprises a diamond substrate containing an ensemble of nitrogen-vacancy (NV) centers, wherein each NV center forms a natural two-qubit system consisting of an electronic spin (S=1) and a proximal nitrogen nuclear spin (I=1/2 for ¹⁵N). The NV center ensemble layer is engineered to contain between 10⁹ and 10¹² NV centers within the optical excitation volume, ensuring sufficient signal strength for ensemble-averaged detection while minimizing deleterious dipolar interactions. An external magnetic bias field is applied along the [111] crystallographic axis of the diamond, which aligns with the symmetry axis of the NV centers, to lift degeneracies and suppress spin relaxation mechanisms. This bias field is generated by a feedback-stabilized electromagnet and maintained at a magnitude between 1700 G and 4000 G, with a preferred operating point of 3700 G, where the ¹⁵N nuclear spin T₁ reaches 3.44(12) ms. An antenna, configured as a single-loop shorted coil of 1 mm diameter, delivers both microwave (MW) pulses for electronic spin manipulation and radiofrequency (RF) pulses in the 1–3 MHz range for nuclear spin control. These MW and RF pulse signals are synthesized with high temporal precision—on the order of 83 picoseconds—and combined before delivery to the sample. A laser source emitting at 532 nm with a power of 130 mW provides optical pulses of 3 µs duration for spin initialization and readout; the resulting spin-state-dependent fluorescence is collected via a liquid light guide and detected by a photodetector. A test coil, comprising four loops of 10 mm diameter, generates calibrated AC magnetic fields for experimental validation. The nuclear memory spin lifetime T₁ is critically dependent on both the bias magnetic field and laser illumination intensity; under the specified conditions, T₁ follows a power-law dependence on field with an exponent of 1.8(2), consistent with theoretical predictions. The MW and RF pulse signals are varied in phase, amplitude, and timing to implement dynamical decoupling sequences (e.g., XY8-6 or DROID-60) and quantum logic gates (SWAP and CNOT e|n). Optical pulses serve dual roles: initializing the electronic spin into the mₛ=0 state and reading out its population via fluorescence intensity. A central controller orchestrates the entire quantum sensing protocol, which proceeds through distinct stages. The preparation stage initializes all electronic spins. The sensing stage applies a dynamical decoupling sequence to encode the target AC magnetic field into the electronic spin phase. The swap stage executes a SWAP gate to transfer this phase information to the nuclear spin memory. The readout stage then repeats N times: each iteration applies a CNOT e|n gate to map nuclear spin information back to the electronic spin, followed by optical readout. This repetitive readout enhances the effective signal-to-noise ratio by a factor of up to 33.3(9) for N=2000, as demonstrated experimentally. Compared to prior art, which relies on single-shot readout, the present method decouples sensing duration from readout inefficiency. Sensitivity enhancements η_QLE of up to 11.3(3) are achieved in correlation spectroscopy, and up to 5.6(3) in single-sequence magnetometry, by optimizing N relative to the overhead times (T_SWAP = 16.5 µs, T_QLR = 3 µs). Experimental results confirm that sensitivity improvement occurs when the sensing duration T_sense exceeds T_SWAP, a condition readily met in modern NV metrology. Alternative implementations may employ different defect systems (e.g., silicon-vacancy centers), varied nuclear species, or modified pulse sequences. The disclosed method is broadly applicable to any physical quantity detectable via NV spin dephasing, including temperature, strain, and electric fields. In conclusion, this disclosure provides a scalable, ensemble-compatible quantum logic protocol that significantly advances the state of the art in solid-state quantum sensing.
