# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Bottlebrush polymers represent a distinctive class of macromolecular architectures characterized by a linear polymeric backbone densely grafted with polymeric sidechains, thereby resembling the bristles of a bottlebrush. These polymers have garnered significant scientific interest due to their unique structural and physical properties that distinguish them from conventional linear or branched polymers. The dense grafting of sidechains along the backbone imparts high rigidity, large hydrodynamic volumes, and resistance to chain entanglement, which collectively enable precise control over molecular dimensions and solution behavior. Over the past decade, advances in synthetic methodologies—particularly graft-through polymerization strategies involving macromonomers—have facilitated the preparation of well-defined bottlebrush polymers with tailored compositions, block sequences, and functionalities. Among these methods, ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-based macromonomers has emerged as a particularly powerful and versatile approach, owing to its high efficiency, functional group tolerance, and ability to produce polymers with controlled molecular weights and narrow dispersities.

The properties of bottlebrush polymers render them highly suitable for a broad spectrum of applications, including but not limited to drug delivery, molecular imaging, self-assembled nanostructures, and the fabrication of mechanically robust polymer networks. Their architectural precision allows for the engineering of amphiphilic structures capable of forming stable micelles or vesicles in aqueous environments, which can encapsulate therapeutic agents with high efficiency. Moreover, the modular nature of bottlebrush synthesis enables the incorporation of stimuli-responsive segments—such as those exhibiting temperature-, pH-, or redox-dependent behavior—thereby facilitating the development of "smart" delivery systems that respond dynamically to physiological cues. Despite these advantages, the exploration of multi-block bottlebrush architectures, particularly triblock variants, remains underdeveloped relative to their linear counterparts.

Extended release technologies are critical in modern pharmacotherapy, as they enhance patient compliance, reduce dosing frequency, minimize peak-trough fluctuations in drug concentration, and improve therapeutic outcomes by maintaining effective drug levels over prolonged periods. Polymers play a central role in extended release formulations, serving as matrices, coatings, or carriers that modulate the rate of drug diffusion, degradation, or erosion. Among polymeric delivery platforms, injectable hydrogels have gained prominence due to their ability to form localized depots at the site of administration, thereby enabling sustained release while limiting systemic exposure. These hydrogels can be engineered to undergo sol-gel phase transitions in response to environmental triggers such as temperature, ionic strength, or pH, allowing for minimally invasive delivery via syringe followed by in situ gelation.

However, existing hydrogel systems often suffer from limitations including poor mechanical integrity, rapid erosion, burst release of payloads, or complex synthesis protocols that hinder scalability and reproducibility. There remains a pressing need for new classes of polymers that combine ease of synthesis, structural precision, biocompatibility, and tunable thermoresponsive behavior to overcome these challenges. In particular, the development of bottlebrush polymers with triblock architectures incorporating distinct functional domains—hydrophobic, hydrophilic, and thermoresponsive—holds untapped potential for creating next-generation injectable hydrogels with superior performance in controlled drug delivery applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel thermally-responsive nanomaterials based on triblock bottlebrush copolymers that exhibit lower critical solution temperature (LCST) behavior and are uniquely suited for biomedical applications, particularly in the field of controlled drug delivery. Specifically, the invention encompasses ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymers comprising three distinct polymeric blocks arranged sequentially along a polymeric backbone, wherein each block is composed of polymeric sidechains that may independently exhibit LCST behavior, hydrophobicity, or hydrophilicity. These copolymers are designed to self-assemble in aqueous media at temperatures below the LCST into well-defined nanoparticles, and to undergo a reversible sol-gel phase transition upon heating above the LCST, forming physically crosslinked hydrogels that serve as localized depots for sustained therapeutic release.

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the invention are synthesized via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers, enabling precise control over block sequence, composition, and molecular weight. In one embodiment, the A block comprises polyester sidechains (e.g., polylactic acid, PLA), the B block comprises polyether sidechains (e.g., polyethylene glycol, PEG), and the C block comprises poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM) sidechains, which exhibit a sharp LCST near physiological temperature (~32 °C). The resulting ABC architecture enables compartmentalized self-assembly: the hydrophobic PLA domains form micellar cores at room temperature, the hydrophilic PEG segments constitute the corona, and the thermoresponsive PNIPAM chains remain hydrated below the LCST but dehydrate and aggregate above it, driving physical crosslinking and hydrogel formation.

The invention further provides Formula (I), which defines the general structure of these triblock bottlebrush copolymers, wherein the backbone is derived from a polymerizable monomer unit, and each of the A, B, and C blocks consists of repeating units bearing sidechains of defined chemical composition. As used herein, “same polymer” refers to blocks composed of identical repeating units and sidechain chemistries, whereas “different polymer” denotes blocks with distinct chemical identities. A specific subclass of the invention is defined by Formula (I-d), which incorporates polyester and polyether groups in designated blocks and includes polymeric sidechains exhibiting LCST behavior, such as PNIPAM or related poly(N-alkylacrylamide)s.

Methods for preparing the triblock bottlebrush copolymers involve sequential addition of macromonomers to a ROMP initiator, typically a ruthenium-based catalyst such as Grubbs third-generation complex, followed by quenching and purification. The resulting copolymers spontaneously self-assemble in water to form nanoparticles of uniform size (typically 90–120 nm in diameter), which can encapsulate a wide range of therapeutic agents, including small molecules, proteins, and nucleic acids. Upon heating above the LCST, these particles interconnect via hydrophobic interactions between dehydrated LCST-responsive blocks, yielding injectable hydrogels that remain localized at the site of administration.

The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical formulations comprising these triblock bottlebrush copolymers, either as free polymers, drug-loaded particles, or pre-formed hydrogels, as well as kits containing such formulations together with instructions for use in the treatment of various diseases. These materials offer significant advantages over existing delivery systems, including low critical micelle concentrations, high drug loading capacity, tunable release kinetics, and enhanced therapeutic index through localized, sustained delivery with reduced systemic toxicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS

### Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers

The invention provides triblock bottlebrush copolymers comprising an ABC architecture, wherein A, B, and C represent distinct polymeric blocks arranged sequentially along a polymeric backbone. Each block consists of repeating backbone units, each bearing a polymeric sidechain. The backbone is typically derived from a polymerizable monomer such as norbornene, and the sidechains are covalently attached via linkers. The A block is selected to be hydrophobic (e.g., polyester such as PLA), the B block hydrophilic (e.g., polyether such as PEG), and the C block thermoresponsive with LCST behavior (e.g., poly(N-alkylacrylamide) such as PNIPAM). Lower critical solution temperature (LCST) behavior refers to the phenomenon wherein a polymer is soluble below a critical temperature and phase-separates above it; PNIPAM exhibits an LCST near 32 °C.

The polymeric sidechains may be homopolymers or copolymers, linear or branched, with molecular weights typically ranging from 1 kDa to 20 kDa. Polyester sidechains include PLA, PGA, PCL; polyether sidechains include PEG, PPG. Polyacrylamide sidechains encompass poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM), poly(N,N-diethylacrylamide), and derivatives. The copolymers are defined by Formula (I):

Backbone-[Sidechain_A]_n-[Sidechain_B]_m-[Sidechain_C]_p

wherein n, m, p are integers defining block lengths, and Sidechain_A, _B, _C are as described. “Different polymer” means the chemical composition of sidechains differs between blocks; “same polymer” implies identity. Specific embodiments include Formula (I-d), where A is polyester, B is polyether, and C is polyacrylamide with LCST. Linkers L1–L4 connect backbone to sidechains and may be alkylene, ester, amide, or ether groups. GA, GB, GC denote sidechain types, with GA = polyester (e.g., -[OCH(CH3)CO]_q-), GB = polyether (e.g., -[OCH2CH2]_r-), GC = polyacrylamide (e.g., -[N(RN)C(O)CH=CRc]_s-). q ranges 5–100; r 10–500; s 20–300. RN is isopropyl for PNIPAM; Rc is H or methyl. Additional formulas (I-a to I-e, II-a to II-g) define variations in backbone, linkers, and sidechain termini. Block ratios (n:m:p) are typically 1:1:1 to 1:3:1. The copolymers self-assemble into micelles in water below LCST and form hydrogels above LCST, offering advantages in drug delivery due to compartmentalized functionality and reduced primary loop formation in networks.

### Methods for Preparing Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers

Triblock bottlebrush copolymers are prepared by sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of macromonomers. First, norbornene-functionalized macromonomers (e.g., Nor-PLA, Nor-PEG, Nor-PNIPAM) are synthesized by coupling amine-terminated polymers to norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl ester. ROMP is initiated by a ruthenium catalyst, preferably Grubbs third-generation bispyridyl complex, in anhydrous solvent under inert atmosphere. The first macromonomer (e.g., Nor-PLA) is added and polymerized to completion, followed by sequential addition of the second (Nor-PEG) and third (Nor-PNIPAM) macromonomers. Polymerization is quenched with ethyl vinyl ether, and the product is purified by dialysis (MWCO 50 kDa) and lyophilization. This method ensures controlled block sequence and high fidelity, as confirmed by SEC and NMR.

### Particles and Hydrogels

Triblock bottlebrush copolymers self-assemble in aqueous solution (1 mg/mL) into nanoparticles of 90–120 nm diameter, as measured by DLS and TEM. Particles comprise a hydrophobic core (PLA), hydrophilic shell (PEG), and thermoresponsive corona (PNIPAM). Therapeutic agents—small molecules (e.g., paclitaxel, dexamethasone), proteins (e.g., ovalbumin)—are encapsulated during self-assembly via nanoprecipitation: polymer and drug are dissolved in DMSO, then diluted with water/saline. Encapsulation efficiency correlates with drug hydrophobicity. Above the LCST (~32 °C), particles aggregate into hydrogels (10 wt%) that are elastic solids (G′ > G′′ at 37 °C), as confirmed by rheology and SAXS. Hydrogels provide sustained release over 3–5 days and remain localized in vivo, unlike non-thermoresponsive diblock controls.

### Formulations, Kits, and Administration

Formulations contain 1–20 wt% triblock copolymer, with drug:polymer ratios of 1:1 to 1:10. Excipients include water, PBS, saline. Administration routes: intratumoral, ophthalmic, subcutaneous. Kits include vials of lyophilized polymer-drug particles, diluent, and instructions. Dosage is adjusted for age, condition; combination therapy with other agents is enabled. Solid dosage forms use standard excipients (e.g., lactose, cellulose); liquid forms use oils or surfactants. Effective amounts vary by agent; e.g., PTX 5–20 mg/kg, R848 1–5 mg/kg.

### Methods of Treatment and Use

The invention treats proliferative diseases (e.g., CT26 colon cancer), inflammatory conditions, ocular disorders, and angiogenesis-related pathologies. Administering drug-loaded hydrogels intratumorally cures tumors and induces immune memory, as shown in murine models. Non-biomedical uses include sensors and actuators leveraging thermoresponsive behavior.

## EXAMPLES

### Thermoresponsive ABC Bottlebrush Copolymer Amphiphiles

ABC triblock copolymers (PLA-PEG-PNIPAM) self-assemble into ~100 nm micelles that encapsulate hydrophobic drugs (PTX, R848). Heating above 32 °C triggers hydrogel formation, enabling localized, sustained release.

### General Methods and Instruments

LC/MS quantified drug loading; GPC (THF, PS standards) and 1H NMR (CDCl3) characterized polymers.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-N-Hydroxysuccinimidyl (NHS)-Ester

4-Aminobutyric acid reacted with cis-5-norbornene-exo-2,3-dicarboxylic anhydride, then NHS/DCC to yield norbornene-NHS ester, purified by column chromatography.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PEG Macromonomer (Nor-PEG)

Amine-PEG (5 kDa) coupled to norbornene-NHS ester in DMF, purified by precipitation.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PNIPAM Macromonomer (Nor-PNIPAM)

Amine-PNIPAM (5.5 kDa) coupled similarly to Nor-PEG.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-Propanol

3-Amino-1-propanol reacted with norbornene anhydride to yield alcohol-terminated macromonomer.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PLA Macromonomer (Nor-PLA)

Lactide underwent ring-opening polymerization initiated by norbornene-alcohol, yielding Nor-PLA.

### Ring-Opening Metathesis (ROMP) Polymerization

Grubbs G3 catalyst initiated sequential ROMP: Nor-PLA → Nor-PEG → Nor-PNIPAM. Quenched with EVE, purified by dialysis/filtration, lyophilized. Drug particles formed by nanoprecipitation; characterized by DLS (size, PDI), TEM (morphology), and drug release assays (dialysis/LC-MS). Dexamethasone and ofloxacin loaded similarly. Gelation confirmed by tube inversion and rheology.
