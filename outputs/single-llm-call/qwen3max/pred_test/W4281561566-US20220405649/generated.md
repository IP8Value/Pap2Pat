# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Decision support systems have become integral to modern data-driven industries, enabling automated or semi-automated classification, prediction, and risk assessment across domains such as finance, healthcare, logistics, and cybersecurity. These systems typically rely on machine learning models trained on historical datasets to infer patterns and make decisions about new, unseen data. The efficacy of such systems hinges on the model’s capacity to capture complex, non-linear relationships among input features while maintaining generalizability and robustness over time. Classical approaches—such as logistic regression, random forests, gradient boosting machines (e.g., XGBoost), and support vector machines—have demonstrated strong empirical performance but often reach performance plateaus when faced with highly entangled or high-dimensional feature interactions that are not easily separable in classical Euclidean space. This limitation has motivated exploration into alternative computational paradigms capable of accessing richer representational spaces.

Quantum machine learning (QML) models, particularly those based on quantum kernel methods, offer a promising avenue for overcoming these limitations by leveraging the exponential dimensionality of quantum Hilbert spaces. In such models, classical data is encoded into quantum states via parameterized quantum circuits known as feature maps, and similarity between data points is computed through quantum kernel functions derived from inner products of these states. Quantum support vector machines (QSVMs), for instance, exploit this quantum-enhanced feature space to construct decision boundaries that may be inaccessible to classical counterparts. Early experimental implementations on superconducting quantum processors have validated the feasibility of such approaches, though practical demonstrations of consistent quantum advantage—i.e., superior predictive performance on real-world datasets—remain elusive. While theoretical frameworks suggest potential benefits from quantum entanglement and interference, current QML models often suffer from sensitivity to noise, limited qubit coherence times, and suboptimal feature map selection, which collectively constrain their empirical utility.

A critical limitation of existing quantum machine learning models lies in their reliance on manually designed or physically inspired feature maps, such as those derived from Ising Hamiltonians, which may not be optimal for arbitrary datasets. Moreover, the selection of hyperparameters—including Pauli rotation factors, regularization strengths, and circuit depth—is typically performed through exhaustive grid searches or heuristic tuning, processes that are both computationally expensive and inaccessible to users without deep expertise in quantum physics. Additionally, most QML models are evaluated as single learners, neglecting ensemble strategies that have proven indispensable in classical machine learning for improving stability, reducing variance, and enhancing generalization. Although preliminary work has explored quantum ensembles in the context of adiabatic quantum computing or parallelized classifier evaluation, these approaches do not fully integrate the iterative, adaptive nature of boosting algorithms—where misclassified samples are reweighted to guide subsequent learner training—nor do they automate the discovery of diverse quantum kernel architectures across boosting iterations. Consequently, there remains a significant gap in the art: a need for an automated, adaptive, and performance-driven ensemble framework for quantum kernel-based learners that systematically explores heterogeneous quantum feature spaces while maintaining compatibility with near-term quantum hardware.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel ensemble generation methodology specifically tailored for quantum kernel-based machine learning models, designed to overcome the limitations of single-learner quantum approaches and manual feature map selection. At its core, the invention employs an adaptive boosting procedure that iteratively trains a sequence of quantum kernel-based learners—such as quantum support vector machines—on reweighted training data, while dynamically selecting optimal quantum kernel configurations from a constrained search space at each iteration. This process ensures exploration of diverse quantum feature representations and prevents redundancy by excluding previously selected kernels in subsequent rounds.

In one embodiment, the invention is implemented as a computer system comprising a processor, memory, and specialized software modules configured to execute the ensemble generation method. The system receives classical input data, splits it into training, validation, and testing subsets, and initializes sample weights uniformly. During each boosting iteration, the system performs a constrained grid search over a predefined set of quantum feature maps, Pauli rotation parameters, and classical SVM hyperparameters to identify the best-performing quantum kernel-based learner on the validation set. Upon selection, the corresponding feature map is excluded from future searches to enforce architectural diversity, and sample weights are updated based on classification errors to emphasize misclassified instances in subsequent rounds.

A key advantage of this system embodiment is its ability to autonomously discover high-performing quantum model architectures without requiring user expertise in quantum circuit design. By integrating hyperparameter optimization with iterative ensemble construction, the system achieves higher predictive accuracy and greater robustness than single QSVM models or classical baselines on certain datasets, particularly those exhibiting circular or XOR-like decision boundaries that benefit from quantum-enhanced feature mappings.

The invention further encompasses a computer-implemented method that operationalizes the above ensemble generation process. This method includes steps for initializing data partitions and weights, executing iterative boosting cycles with dynamic kernel selection, updating sample and learner weights, and terminating the procedure upon meeting predefined stopping criteria—such as perfect classification, random-guess-level performance, or maximum ensemble size. The resulting ensemble combines individual quantum learners via weighted voting, where each learner’s contribution is proportional to its performance-derived weight.

An advantage of this computer-implemented method is its adaptability to both classification and regression tasks, as well as its compatibility with various quantum hardware platforms, including superconducting, trapped-ion, and photonic qubit systems. Moreover, the method inherently mitigates overfitting through early stopping and ensemble pruning based on validation performance.

Additionally, the invention includes a computer program product comprising non-transitory computer-readable media storing instructions that, when executed by a processor, cause the system to perform the disclosed method. This program product enables deployment across cloud infrastructures, enterprise servers, or hybrid classical-quantum computing environments.

A further advantage of the computer program product is its modularity: it can be integrated into existing machine learning pipelines as a drop-in enhancement for quantum-aware decision support systems. The program product also supports simulation on classical hardware during development, facilitating rapid prototyping before execution on physical quantum processors.

Central to the invention is a modified plurality of quantum kernels generated through the boosting procedure. Unlike conventional approaches that fix a single kernel or randomly sample from a pool, the invention systematically explores the kernel space under constraints that promote diversity and performance. Each kernel is defined by a unique combination of feature map topology, Pauli rotation scaling, and SVM regularization, and is evaluated using a performance metric—such as classification accuracy or mean squared error—computed on a held-out validation set.

The advantage of this modified plurality of quantum kernels is twofold: first, it enables the ensemble to capture complementary aspects of the data manifold through distinct quantum embeddings; second, it reduces the risk of local optima by preventing repeated selection of identical or highly similar kernels. Empirical results demonstrate that ensembles constructed via this method achieve statistically significant performance gains over single QSVMs, with improvements of up to 7.5% in classification accuracy on synthetic benchmarks, thereby advancing the practical viability of quantum machine learning for real-world applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present patent application discloses a comprehensive system, method, and program product for generating ensembles of quantum kernel-based learners through an adaptive boosting procedure. The scope of embodiments encompasses any implementation that integrates classical boosting logic with quantum kernel evaluation, regardless of the underlying quantum hardware platform or specific machine learning task. This includes, but is not limited to, binary and multiclass classification, regression, anomaly detection, and clustering, provided that a quantum kernel function can be defined for the task.

Conventional approaches to feature space discovery in quantum machine learning suffer from several critical flaws. Many rely on fixed, physics-inspired feature maps—such as those derived from Ising models—that may not align with the statistical structure of arbitrary datasets. Others perform unconstrained searches over vast parameter spaces, leading to redundant or suboptimal kernel selections and excessive computational overhead. Furthermore, existing quantum ensembles often lack mechanisms to enforce diversity among learners or adaptively reweight training samples, thereby failing to replicate the error-correcting properties of classical boosting algorithms like AdaBoost.

To address these shortcomings, the present invention introduces a boosting procedure specifically adapted for quantum kernel-based learners. This procedure is defined as an iterative algorithm that, at each step, selects the highest-performing quantum kernel from a dynamically constrained search space, updates sample weights based on prediction errors, and accumulates a weighted ensemble of learners. The boosting procedure begins with uniform sample weights and a full set of candidate feature maps. After each iteration, the selected feature map is removed from the candidate pool to ensure architectural diversity in subsequent rounds.

The advantages of this boosting procedure are manifold. It automates the discovery of effective quantum feature representations without user intervention, promotes exploration of orthogonal regions of Hilbert space, and incrementally focuses learning capacity on difficult-to-classify samples. This leads to ensembles that are both more accurate and more interpretable than monolithic quantum models.

Building upon this procedure, the invention introduces an ensemble of quantum kernel-based learners, each instantiated as a quantum support vector machine or analogous kernel method. These learners are applied to standard machine learning tasks by encoding classical input vectors into quantum states via parameterized circuits, computing kernel matrices through quantum state overlaps, and solving the resulting optimization problem classically.

The invention further encompasses computer processing systems, methods, apparatus, and program products configured to execute the disclosed techniques. These implementations provide technical improvements over conventional quantum machine learning models by integrating ensemble diversity, adaptive reweighting, and automated hyperparameter selection—features absent in prior art. Specifically, the system avoids the stagnation common in single-kernel QSVMs by forcing exploration of alternative quantum embeddings, thereby increasing the likelihood of discovering a kernel that aligns with the intrinsic geometry of the data.

The boosting algorithm proceeds as follows: initialize sample weights; for each iteration, train all candidate learners, select the best performer, exclude its kernel, compute learner weight α = log((1−err)/err), update sample weights proportionally to misclassification, and repeat until termination (e.g., max learners or perfect fit). The final ensemble combines learners via weighted voting.

This process detects overfitting by monitoring validation performance and can be combined with classical models in hybrid stacks. Input data and feature maps are received, learners are trained, metrics generated, selections made, iterations repeated, and the ensemble deployed for inference and reporting.
