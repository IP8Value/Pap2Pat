# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to doping engineered hole transport layers for use in optoelectronic devices, particularly in organometal halide perovskite-based solar cells. More specifically, the invention pertains to a triple-layer hole transport layer architecture comprising an n-type doped sublayer, an intrinsic (undoped) sublayer, and a p-type doped sublayer, wherein each sublayer is formed by vacuum evaporation under controlled vapor pressure conditions to achieve optimal energy level alignment with the underlying perovskite absorber layer. The invention further encompasses methods for fabricating such hole transport layers using integrated or sequential vacuum deposition systems that enable precise control over dopant concentration, film morphology, and interfacial properties, thereby enhancing both the power conversion efficiency and long-term environmental stability of perovskite solar cells.

## BACKGROUND ART

Solar cells are semiconductor devices designed to convert incident photons from sunlight into electrical energy through the photovoltaic effect. At the heart of most solar cell architectures lies the p-n junction, a built-in electric field formed at the interface between p-type and n-type semiconductors that facilitates the separation of photogenerated electron-hole pairs. The efficiency of this energy conversion process is governed by several key parameters, including the open-circuit voltage (Voc), short-circuit current density (jsc), fill factor (FF), and ultimately the power conversion efficiency (PCE). Over the past decade, organometal halide perovskites have emerged as a revolutionary class of light-absorbing materials due to their exceptional optoelectronic properties—such as high absorption coefficients, long carrier diffusion lengths, tunable bandgaps, and low exciton binding energies—which collectively enable rapid improvements in PCE from below 4% to over 25% in single-junction configurations.

Despite these remarkable advances, the practical deployment of perovskite solar cells has been hindered by challenges related to device stability and reproducibility, many of which stem from limitations in existing fabrication techniques. Conventional solution-processing methods for depositing critical charge transport layers, such as the hole transport layer (HTL), often result in films with pinholes, non-uniform thickness, and poor interfacial contact. These morphological defects not only impede efficient charge extraction but also serve as pathways for moisture and oxygen ingress, accelerating perovskite degradation. Moreover, solution-processed HTLs typically require post-deposition chemical doping with hygroscopic additives like lithium salts and organic bases, which further compromise device stability under ambient conditions. These shortcomings motivate the development of alternative fabrication strategies that can produce dense, pinhole-free, and compositionally controlled HTLs without reliance on liquid-phase processing or unstable dopants.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides an optoelectronic device structure comprising a substrate, an electron transport layer, a perovskite photoactive layer, a triple-layer hole transport layer, and a top electrode. The triple-layer hole transport layer consists of three sequentially deposited sublayers: an n-type doped hole transport material (HTM) sublayer adjacent to the perovskite layer, an intrinsic (undoped) HTM sublayer interposed between the doped layers, and a p-type doped HTM sublayer adjacent to the top electrode. This n-i-p structured HTL is fabricated entirely by vacuum evaporation under precisely controlled vapor pressures for both the HTM and dopant species, enabling fine-tuning of the highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) energy levels across the stack to align optimally with the valence band maximum (VBM) of the perovskite absorber. The resulting energy-level staircase enhances hole extraction while suppressing recombination losses, leading to improved Voc, FF, and overall PCE. Furthermore, the absence of pinholes in the vacuum-evaporated HTL acts as an effective barrier against environmental degradation, significantly extending device lifetime under ambient air exposure.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

Organometal halide perovskite film materials, such as methylammonium lead iodide chloride (MAPbI₃₋ₓClₓ), exhibit excellent light-harvesting capabilities and favorable charge transport characteristics suitable for high-performance optoelectronic devices. These perovskite films typically adopt a crystalline structure characterized by distinct X-ray diffraction peaks corresponding to (110) and (220) planes, confirming phase purity and crystallinity essential for efficient photovoltaic operation. In perovskite-based solar cells, the perovskite layer functions as the primary photoactive component where photon absorption generates electron-hole pairs that must be efficiently separated and collected at respective electrodes.

The hole transport layer (HTL) plays a critical role in extracting photogenerated holes from the perovskite layer and transporting them to the anode. Traditional HTLs, such as spiro-OMeTAD, are commonly deposited via spin-coating from solution, followed by chemical doping to enhance conductivity. However, this approach suffers from inherent limitations: solution-processed spiro-OMeTAD films frequently contain pinholes due to incomplete surface coverage, which not only reduce charge collection efficiency but also expose the underlying perovskite to ambient air, triggering irreversible decomposition. Pinholes in the HTL thus represent a major failure point in device stability and performance.

To overcome these issues, the present invention employs a vacuum evaporation method for forming the HTL. Vacuum-evaporated spiro-OMeTAD films demonstrate superior morphological integrity—being continuous, pinhole-free, and amorphous—with a high glass transition temperature (~121 °C) that enhances thermal and environmental robustness. Crucially, vacuum deposition allows for precise control over doping concentration through regulation of dopant vapor pressure during co-evaporation, enabling systematic engineering of the electronic structure of the HTL.

Doping engineering is a fundamental technique for modulating the electronic properties of semiconductors, including organic HTMs. In the context of the present invention, n-type doping introduces electron donors that raise the Fermi level toward the conduction band, effectively lowering the work function and shifting the HOMO level closer to the Fermi level. Conversely, p-type doping employs electron acceptors that lower the Fermi level toward the valence band, increasing the work function and bringing the HOMO level nearer to the Fermi edge. By strategically combining n-type, intrinsic, and p-type doped sublayers, a graded energy landscape is created that facilitates directional hole transport while minimizing interfacial energy barriers.

Exemplary n-dopants include dimethylcyclooctatetraene (DMC), while representative p-dopants encompass tetrafluorotetracyanoquinodimethane (F4-TCNQ) and molybdenum trioxide (MoO₃). Suitable HTMs beyond spiro-OMeTAD may include PTAA, P3HT, or other π-conjugated small molecules or polymers compatible with vacuum processing. Energy band diagrams derived from ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS) measurements confirm that the HOMO level of the n-doped spiro-OMeTAD sublayer can be tuned to match the VBM of the perovskite (approximately 2.6 eV below EF), ensuring minimal hole injection barrier. The intrinsic layer serves as a diffusion barrier between n- and p-dopants, preserving the integrity of the energy gradient, while the p-doped layer ensures ohmic contact with the metal electrode (e.g., Au, Ag, or Al).

Optimal n-type doping concentration is achieved at a DMC vapor pressure of 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr, yielding a HOMO level 2.5 eV below EF—precisely aligned with the perovskite VBM. Current density–voltage (J–V) characteristics demonstrate that devices incorporating the n-i-p HTL exhibit enhanced PCE, primarily driven by higher Voc and FF compared to reference devices with solution-processed HTLs. Performance metrics consistently show improved reproducibility and reduced hysteresis.

The fabrication process begins with patterned fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass, cleaned and etched to define electrode patterns. A compact TiO₂ electron transport layer (~100 nm) is deposited via spray pyrolysis and annealed at 480 °C. The perovskite layer (MAPbI₃₋ₓClₓ) is then solution-processed and thermally annealed in an inert atmosphere. Subsequently, the n-i-p HTL is formed in a high-vacuum system (base pressure ~10⁻⁸ Torr). First, n-doped spiro-OMeTAD is co-evaporated with DMC at a controlled vapor pressure (e.g., 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ Torr), monitored by a quartz crystal microbalance to achieve a target thickness of ~30 nm. Next, intrinsic spiro-OMeTAD (~20 nm) is deposited without dopant. Finally, p-doped spiro-OMeTAD (~30 nm, 2 wt.% F4-TCNQ) is evaporated, either in the same chamber or a second connected chamber to prevent cross-contamination.

The vacuum evaporation system comprises a deposition chamber coupled to a high-vacuum pump unit, a temperature-controlled substrate stage, multiple evaporation sources (e.g., Knudsen cells or resistive boats) for HTM and dopants, deposition rate monitors (quartz crystal or optical), and precision valves or shutters to regulate vapor flux. For volatile dopants like DMC, an all-metal leak valve connected to a heated ampule enables stable vapor delivery. Alternative configurations employ dual chambers with independent control, allowing sequential deposition without breaking vacuum. This setup ensures uniform, stoichiometrically controlled films even for thermally sensitive or reactive dopants.

Stability studies reveal that devices with n-i-p HTL retain >95% of initial PCE after 840 hours in ambient air, whereas reference devices degrade to ~50%. Notably, some n-i-p devices exhibit a gradual PCE increase over time, attributed to benign dopant redistribution or mild ambient-induced doping of the intrinsic layer, reducing series resistance. UPS and XRD confirm structural and energetic stability, while AFM/SEM verify pinhole-free morphology. The invention is extendable to other perovskite compositions and optoelectronic applications, including LEDs and photodetectors. Unlike prior art in organic electronics that uses doping gradients in emissive layers, the present invention uniquely applies predetermined n-doping in the HTL to achieve energy-level matching—a novel approach not previously disclosed for perovskite photovoltaics.
