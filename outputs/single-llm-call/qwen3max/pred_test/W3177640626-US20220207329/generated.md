# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to generative adversarial networks (GANs), and more particularly to systems, methods, and apparatuses for compressing image-to-image translation models based on GAN architectures. The invention provides a novel framework for jointly performing model compression and knowledge distillation using a single teacher network that incorporates an inception-based residual block design. This approach enables the efficient generation of high-fidelity synthetic images with significantly reduced computational complexity, memory footprint, and power consumption compared to conventional GAN implementations. The disclosed techniques are especially advantageous for deployment on resource-constrained computing platforms such as mobile devices, embedded systems, and edge computing environments where real-time performance, low latency, and energy efficiency are critical requirements. By eliminating the need for separate supernet training and reducing hyperparameter tuning complexity, the invention facilitates practical large-scale deployment of generative models in commercial applications including but not limited to image editing tools, augmented reality experiences, virtual try-on systems, medical imaging, autonomous vehicle perception, and creative content generation platforms.

## BACKGROUND

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) represent a class of deep learning architectures comprising two neural networks—a generator and a discriminator—that are trained simultaneously through adversarial processes. The generator learns to produce synthetic data samples that mimic real data distributions, while the discriminator learns to distinguish between real and generated samples. Through this competitive training dynamic, GANs have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in generating high-quality, photo-realistic images and videos across various domains. Conditional GAN variants extend this framework by incorporating additional input signals such as segmentation maps, class labels, or sketches to control the generation process, enabling targeted image synthesis for specific applications. Despite their impressive performance, conventional GAN implementations suffer from substantial computational complexity and large model sizes that render them impractical for deployment on resource-constrained platforms. The massive memory requirements, high power consumption, and slow inference speeds associated with these models present significant barriers to real-world adoption, particularly in mobile and edge computing scenarios where computational resources are limited. Previous attempts to address these limitations through model compression techniques—including weight pruning, channel slimming, layer skipping, patterned pruning, and network quantization—have primarily focused on discriminative models for classification, detection, or segmentation tasks rather than generative models. When applied to GANs, these approaches typically result in degraded image quality compared to the original models, creating an undesirable trade-off between computational efficiency and generation fidelity. Existing GAN compression methods that employ neural architecture search (NAS) require training additional supernets or utilize iterative pruning procedures with multiple hyperparameters, leading to prohibitively expensive search costs and complex implementation requirements. Furthermore, knowledge distillation techniques used in prior art often introduce auxiliary learnable layers to match feature dimensions between teacher and student networks, potentially causing information leakage and suboptimal knowledge transfer. These limitations highlight the need for a more efficient, effective, and practical approach to GAN compression that maintains or even improves generation quality while dramatically reducing computational requirements.

### Time-Based Access Limitation Architecture

The system implements sophisticated access-limiting mechanisms to enforce temporal constraints on content availability. Ephemeral messages are automatically removed after expiration of their associated duration parameters, with message timers tracking remaining access time. Ephemeral message groups aggregate multiple messages under shared temporal policies defined by group duration parameters and group participation parameters that govern member access rights. The ephemeral timer system coordinates removal operations across client and server components, ensuring consistent enforcement of time-based restrictions. Upon expiration, ephemeral messages are permanently deleted from all storage locations, and ephemeral message groups are dissolved when their collective duration elapses. Communication with the messaging system maintains synchronization of timer states across distributed components, while indicium displays provide visual feedback about remaining access time to users.

### Generative Adversarial Networks

The invention leverages generative adversarial network (GAN) architectures comprising generator and discriminator neural networks trained through adversarial processes. The generator produces synthetic images from input conditions such as segmentation maps or sketches, while the discriminator classifies inputs as real or fake to guide generator improvement. Both networks typically employ convolutional neural network architectures optimized for image processing tasks. The generator outputs synthetic images that approximate real data distributions, with quality measured through metrics like Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Kernel Inception Distance (KID). The discriminator functions as a binary classifier distinguishing between authentic training samples and generator outputs, with discriminator loss quantifying classification error during training. Discriminator training alternates with generator updates to maintain balanced adversarial dynamics. Generator loss combines adversarial objectives with reconstruction and feature matching terms to ensure output fidelity and structural accuracy. Pre-trained GAN models serve as starting points for compression procedures, with residual blocks forming fundamental building units in generator architectures. The invention introduces inception-based residual blocks that incorporate multiple parallel convolution paths with varying kernel sizes (1×1, 3×3, 5×5) alongside depth-wise convolution blocks to create rich feature representations with computational efficiency. Normalization layers within these blocks include learnable scaling parameters that facilitate subsequent pruning operations. Output channel configurations are carefully balanced across parallel branches to maintain computational budgets while preserving representational capacity. During compression, residual blocks are selectively pruned by removing entire branches or reducing channel counts based on scaling factor magnitudes. The method for generating compressed image-to-image models begins with training a first GAN incorporating inception-based residual blocks, followed by systematic channel pruning to achieve target computational budgets measured in Multiply-Accumulate Operations (MACs).
