# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to an integrated platform for the generation and engineering of antibodies, particularly single-domain antibodies such as variable heavy chain domains derived from camelid heavy chain-only antibodies (VHHs or nanobodies). More specifically, the invention provides a cell-free in vitro system—termed CeVICA (Cell-free VHH Integrated Construction and Affinity maturation)—that enables the design, construction, selection, and optimization of high-affinity, stable, and functional VHH antibodies against target antigens without reliance on animal immunization or cellular transformation. The platform encompasses methods for constructing highly diverse synthetic VHH libraries with fully randomized complementarity-determining regions (CDRs), performing ribosome display-based selections under controlled stringency, computationally clustering post-selection sequences to identify unique binder families, and executing iterative affinity maturation cycles to enhance binding affinity and neutralizing potency. The invention is particularly suited for rapid development of therapeutic and diagnostic agents targeting viral pathogens such as SARS-CoV-2, including emerging variants with mutations like N501Y and E484K, and provides a scalable, automatable, and generalizable framework for antibody discovery and engineering.

## BACKGROUND

Traditional methods for generating monoclonal antibodies have long relied on the immunization of animals—typically mice or rabbits—followed by hybridoma technology to immortalize B cells producing antigen-specific antibodies. While effective, these approaches are time-consuming, labor-intensive, and limited by species-specific immune tolerance, which may preclude the generation of antibodies against highly conserved human proteins or toxic antigens. Moreover, murine antibodies often require humanization to reduce immunogenicity in clinical applications, adding complexity to development pipelines. Alternative in vivo strategies using transgenic animals expressing human immunoglobulin loci have improved compatibility but remain constrained by biological variability, ethical considerations, and lengthy timelines.

In response, in vitro antibody generation methods have emerged as powerful alternatives that decouple antibody discovery from the immune system. Phage display, yeast display, and ribosome display enable the screening of large synthetic or naïve antibody libraries against virtually any target antigen under controlled conditions. Among these, ribosome display offers distinct advantages: it operates entirely in a cell-free environment, supports library diversities exceeding 10^10 unique members—far beyond the transformation capacity of bacterial or yeast systems—and links genotype (mRNA) directly to phenotype (nascent polypeptide) through stalled ribosomal complexes. However, existing in vitro platforms often suffer from limitations in library design fidelity, inefficient recovery of full-length functional clones, and inadequate computational frameworks for interpreting high-throughput sequencing data from selection outputs.

Recent advances in antibody library design have sought to mimic natural sequence features observed in immune repertoires to bias libraries toward functional folds. For instance, synthetic nanobody libraries have incorporated amino acid profiles derived from natural camelid VHH sequences, particularly in CDR1 and CDR2, under the assumption that such biases enhance structural stability and binding competence. Nevertheless, these assumptions have not been rigorously validated, and overly restrictive designs may exclude novel, high-performing binders that deviate from natural norms. Furthermore, conventional analysis of selection outputs often relies on frequency-based ranking of individual sequences, which fails to account for mutational noise, PCR artifacts, and the combinatorial nature of CDR interactions. There remains a critical need for an integrated, end-to-end platform that combines rational yet permissive library design, robust cell-free selection, and intelligent computational clustering to comprehensively map the landscape of potential binders and efficiently isolate optimal candidates for therapeutic or diagnostic use.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides an antibody or antigen-binding fragment thereof, wherein the antibody comprises complementarity determining regions (CDRs) selected or derived from clusters identified through a CDR-directed computational clustering method applied to output sequences from a ribosome display selection campaign. In specific embodiments, the antibody is a VHH domain, and representative examples include SR1, SR2, SR4, SR6, SR8, SR12, SR15, SR18, SR25, SR30, and SR38. Further optimized variants include SR6v15, SR6v7, SR38, SR6c3, SR4t13, and SR2c3. The invention particularly encompasses heavy chain antibodies or isolated VHH domains that bind the receptor-binding domain (RBD) of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein. Notably, SR38 exhibits preferential binding to the N501Y mutant RBD compared to wild-type, making it uniquely suited for detection or neutralization of this variant. SR6v15 and SR6v7 demonstrate enhanced neutralizing potency against pseudotyped SARS-CoV-2.

The invention further includes camelid heavy chain antibodies and humanized forms thereof. Humanization is achieved by substituting framework residues in FR2—specifically positions 42, 49, 50, and 52 according to IMGT numbering—with the corresponding residues found in human IGHV genes, such as IGHV3-23, thereby reducing potential immunogenicity while preserving binding function. The antibody or antigen-binding fragment may be modified via fusion to another antibody, antibody fragment, Fc region, albumin, or other polypeptides to enhance half-life, avidity, or effector functions. Such constructs are useful in the treatment of SARS-CoV-2 infection, wherein administration of SR38 or SR6v15—alone or in multimeric form—effectively neutralizes viral entry. These molecules also serve as detection reagents in immunoassays for SARS-CoV-2, with SR38 enabling specific identification of N501Y-containing variants.

Additionally, the invention provides a method for generating a VHH library comprising PCR amplification of frame templates followed by sequential ligation-mediated randomization of CDR2, CDR1, and CDR3 using primers containing NNB codons (where N = A/T/G/C and B = C/G/T), under conditions that preserve reading frame integrity and minimize recombination. Primer sequences and cycling conditions are optimized to maintain hairpin structures that prevent spurious ligation and ensure directional assembly. The method further includes identifying CDRs through alignment to consensus frame sequences and applying a match score threshold based on BLOSUM62 to define sequence similarity for clustering purposes. This integrated approach enables the rapid isolation of high-affinity, stable, and therapeutically relevant antibodies against challenging targets.

### OVERVIEW

The invention introduces a cell-free antibody engineering platform designated CeVICA, which integrates library design, ribosome display selection, and computational analysis into a seamless workflow. CeVICA utilizes linear DNA libraries encoding artificial VHH domains with fully randomized CDRs, performs iterative rounds of in vitro selection under controlled stringency, and applies CDR-directed clustering to distill millions of sequencing reads into discrete binder families. This platform enables the discovery of high-affinity neutralizing antibodies against SARS-CoV-2 and its variants without animal immunization, offering speed, scalability, and reproducibility unmatched by traditional methods.

### Therapeutic Antibodies or Binding Fragments of an Antibody

Antibodies are immunoglobulin molecules comprising two heavy and two light chains, each containing variable and constant domains. Antigen-binding fragments include Fab, Fab′, F(ab′)₂, scFv, and single-domain antibodies such as VHHs. Therapeutic antibodies neutralize pathogens, block receptor-ligand interactions, or modulate immune responses. Neutralizing antibodies against SARS-CoV-2 inhibit viral entry by binding the spike protein’s RBD, preventing interaction with ACE2. SARS-CoV-2 variants—including Alpha (B.1.1.7), Beta (B.1.351), Gamma (P.1), and Delta (B.1.617.2)—are classified by PANGO lineages based on phylogenetic analysis of global sequences. VHHs, derived from camelid heavy-chain-only antibodies, consist of a single variable domain with extended CDR3 loops enabling access to cryptic epitopes. CDRs are hypervariable loops responsible for antigen contact, flanked by conserved framework regions (FRs). Clustering of CDR sequences identifies families with shared binding modes. Amino acid substitutions in CDRs or FRs can enhance potency, stability, or human compatibility. Monoclonal antibodies are homogeneous populations derived from a single clone; polyclonal mixtures contain multiple specificities. Humanized antibodies retain non-human CDRs grafted onto human FRs, minimizing immunogenicity. Chimeric antibodies fuse non-human variable domains to human constant regions. Human IGHV genes, particularly IGHV3-23, provide templates for humanizing VHH frameworks. Epitope-binding proteins include designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins) and fibronectin domains, but the invention focuses on immunoglobulin-derived scaffolds. IgG subclasses (IgG1–4) differ in effector functions; IgG1 is preferred for neutralization. Single-chain immunoglobulins link VH and VL via a peptide spacer. A “domain” is a structurally independent folding unit; “region” denotes a segment with shared function. “Conformation” refers to three-dimensional structure essential for binding. “Specific binding” indicates preferential interaction with a target over unrelated molecules. “Affinity” is the strength of monovalent interaction, measured by KD. Blocking antibodies prevent ligand-receptor engagement. Agonist antibodies activate receptors; antagonists inhibit. Receptor- or ligand-specific antibodies modulate signaling pathways. Antibodies may be modified via conjugation to carrier proteins (e.g., KLH), ligands (e.g., folate), or other antibodies to create bispecifics. PEGylation, polysialylation, HESylation, recombinant PEG mimetics, Fc fusion, albumin fusion, nanoparticle attachment, encapsulation, cholesterol fusion, iron fusion, acylation, amidation, glycosylation, oxidation, phosphorylation, biotinylation, surfactant addition, amino acid mimetics, unnatural amino acids, analogs, spacers, or linkers alter pharmacokinetics, stability, or activity. Modified therapeutic antibodies include those with N-terminal protecting groups, deaminated termini, or chemically altered side chains. Polymers such as PEG, cholesterol, or palmitate may be attached to any residue, especially the N-terminus. Conservative substitutions (e.g., Leu→Ile) preserve function; non-conservative changes may enhance properties. Antibodies are produced in vitro (cell-free systems) or in vivo (mammalian, bacterial, or yeast cells). Derivatives include covalent conjugates with glycosyl, acetyl, PEG, phosphate, or amide groups. Simple binding assays (ELISA, SPR) detect interactions. Administration routes include intravenous, subcutaneous, or inhalation; dosing regimens vary by indication. Vectors (viral or plasmid) deliver antibody-encoding nucleic acids for in vivo expression. Immunoassays (sandwich, competitive) use labeled antibodies (enzymes, fluorophores, radioisotopes) for detection via spectrophotometry, fluorescence, or autoradiography. The CeVICA platform generates VHH libraries via PCR and ligation, screens by ribosome display, identifies families via clustering, validates binding/neutralization, and performs affinity maturation through error-prone PCR and stringent selection.

### EXAMPLES

The CeVICA platform was developed to engineer SARS-CoV-2-neutralizing VHHs. It comprises: (1) design of VHH DNA templates based on consensus frames from 298 natural nanobodies; (2) three-stage PCR/ligation to randomize CDR2 (5 aa), CDR1 (7 aa), and CDR3 (6–13 aa) using NNB codons; (3) ribosome display with anti-Myc pre-enrichment to boost full-length sequences from 25.3% to 51.9%; (4) three-round selection against RBD or EGFP immobilized via anti-Flag beads; (5) high-throughput sequencing and CDR-directed clustering yielding 862 RBD-specific clusters; (6) validation of top 14 clusters (SR1–14) showing 3 strong binders and 6 neutralizers (>30% inhibition at 1 µM); (7) affinity maturation of six leads via error-prone PCR, identifying beneficial mutations (e.g., in SR6c3); (8) generation of SR6v15 with KD = 2.18 nM and IC50 = 62.7 nM; (9) creation of dimeric SR6v15.d with IC50 = 0.329 nM; (10) discovery of SR38 from a low-abundance cluster (rank 862) that preferentially neutralizes N501Y pseudovirus; (11) biophysical characterization confirming >90% monomericity, thermal stability (Tm = 72°C), and refolding capability after denaturation; and (12) demonstration that fully random CDRs do not impair fitness, as output profiles correlate more strongly with input than natural sequences. The platform’s generalizability, automation potential, and ability to recover rare high-performers like SR38 establish CeVICA as a transformative tool for in vitro antibody engineering.
