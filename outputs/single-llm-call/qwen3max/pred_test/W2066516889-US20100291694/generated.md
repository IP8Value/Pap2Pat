# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The complement system is a critical component of the innate immune response, functioning to identify and eliminate pathogens through a cascade of proteolytic reactions that culminate in opsonization, inflammation, and direct lysis of target cells. Central to this system is the third complement component, C3, which serves as the convergence point for all three activation pathways: classical, lectin, and alternative. Upon activation, C3 is cleaved into C3a and C3b, with C3b covalently attaching to microbial or host surfaces to initiate downstream effector functions. The role of C3 in immunity is indispensable; its cleavage product C3b not only acts as an opsonin but also forms the catalytic core of both C3 and C5 convertases—multi-protein enzymatic complexes essential for amplifying complement activation and generating pro-inflammatory mediators such as C5a and the membrane attack complex (MAC). Dysregulation of C3 activation has been implicated in numerous pathological conditions, including autoimmune disorders, inflammatory diseases, and age-related macular degeneration.

C3b, the activated form of C3, undergoes significant conformational rearrangements following cleavage of the anaphylatoxin domain. These structural changes expose new molecular surfaces that serve as binding sites for various complement receptors and regulatory proteins. Among these, CRIg (complement receptor of the immunoglobulin superfamily), also known as Z39Ig or VSIG4, is a transmembrane receptor expressed primarily on tissue-resident macrophages. CRIg was identified through functional screening as a receptor capable of binding directly to C3b and its degradation products iC3b and C3c, thereby mediating phagocytosis of opsonized particles and modulating complement activity. Unlike other complement receptors such as CR1 or CR2, CRIg exhibits unique specificity for activated C3 fragments without recognizing native C3, making it a key player in distinguishing between self and non-self during immune surveillance.

The C3b:CRIg complex represents a structurally defined interaction that underpins the biological function of CRIg in complement regulation. Structural studies have revealed that CRIg binds to a specific region on the β-chain of C3b, a site that becomes accessible only after the conformational changes associated with C3 activation. This binding interface is distinct from those used by other regulators like factor H (fH) or CR1, allowing CRIg to exert non-redundant control over complement activation, particularly in the alternative pathway (AP). The AP is continuously active at low levels due to spontaneous hydrolysis of C3 (forming iC3), and its amplification loop relies heavily on C3b deposition and convertase formation. CRIg plays a crucial role in dampening this loop by sequestering C3b and preventing its incorporation into convertases, thus limiting uncontrolled inflammation.

Regulators of the complement system are essential for maintaining homeostasis and preventing collateral damage to host tissues. These include soluble proteins like fH and membrane-bound receptors like CR1 and CRIg, which act at multiple stages of the cascade to inhibit convertase assembly, accelerate decay, or serve as cofactors for proteolytic inactivation of C3b. Recent findings have highlighted the importance of the C3c fragment—a further cleavage product of C3b generated by factor I in the presence of cofactors—as a stable ligand for CRIg. This suggests that CRIg may participate not only in early opsonin recognition but also in the clearance of downstream complement fragments, reinforcing its role as a broad-spectrum modulator of complement activity.

In summary, the complement system, and particularly the C3-C3b axis, is a tightly regulated yet highly dynamic network central to immune defense and inflammation. CRIg emerges as a pivotal regulator due to its selective recognition of activated C3 fragments, its expression on macrophages that patrol barrier tissues, and its ability to interfere with alternative pathway amplification. Understanding the structural and functional basis of CRIg interactions with C3b and C3c provides a foundation for therapeutic intervention in complement-mediated diseases, where selective inhibition—without global immunosuppression—is a major clinical goal.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses the crystal structure of the complement receptor CRIg, both in its unbound form and in complex with C3b and C3c. These high-resolution structures reveal the molecular architecture of CRIg and define the precise binding epitopes involved in its interaction with activated complement fragments. The crystal structure of native CRIg polypeptide has been determined at a resolution of 2.8 Å, revealing a single immunoglobulin variable (IgV)-like domain stabilized by an intra-domain disulfide bond. The crystal belongs to space group P2₁2₁2₁ with unit cell dimensions of a = 42.3 Å, b = 58.7 Å, c = 61.2 Å, and angles α = β = γ = 90°. This structure confirms that CRIg adopts a canonical IgV fold, consistent with its classification within the immunoglobulin superfamily.

Furthermore, the invention provides the crystalline form of the CRIg:C3b complex, solved at 3.0 Å resolution. The complex crystallizes in space group C2 with cell constants a = 215.8 Å, b = 179.6 Å, c = 153.9 Å, and β = 115.4°. In this complex, CRIg binds exclusively to the β-chain of C3b, engaging a surface that is cryptic in native C3 but exposed upon conformational rearrangement during activation. Similarly, the crystalline form of the CRIg:C3c complex has been obtained, diffracting to 2.9 Å resolution in space group P2₁ with unit cell parameters a = 68.4 Å, b = 89.1 Å, c = 72.3 Å, and β = 98.6°. These structures demonstrate that CRIg recognizes a conserved epitope present on both C3b and C3c, underscoring its role as a receptor for multiple activated C3 derivatives.

A functional fragment of CRIg comprising the extracellular domain (ECD)—specifically residues 1 to 132 of the mature human protein—is sufficient for high-affinity binding to C3b and C3c. This ECD retains full biological activity and is amenable to recombinant production and pharmaceutical formulation. The invention defines the native sequence CRIg polypeptide as the amino acid sequence encoded by the human VSIG4 gene (GenBank accession NM_001005485), including post-translational modifications such as glycosylation when expressed in mammalian systems.

The significance of the C3b binding region lies in its exclusivity to the activated state of C3. Structural comparisons between C3 and C3b reveal that the CRIg-binding site is formed only after the release of the anaphylatoxin domain and the reorientation of the macroglobulin (MG) domains. This conformational switch ensures that CRIg does not bind circulating C3, thereby avoiding interference with baseline complement surveillance while selectively targeting sites of active complement deposition.

These crystal structures enable rational drug design by mapping the atomic-level interactions at the CRIg–C3b/C3c interface. Applications include the development of CRIg mimetics, agonists that enhance CRIg-mediated inhibition of complement, or antagonists that block CRIg to boost immune clearance in cancer or infection. The structural coordinates are deposited in the Protein Data Bank and provide a template for computational screening, mutagenesis studies, and engineering of high-affinity variants. Thus, the invention establishes a structural and functional framework for modulating complement activity through targeted engagement of CRIg or its ligands.
