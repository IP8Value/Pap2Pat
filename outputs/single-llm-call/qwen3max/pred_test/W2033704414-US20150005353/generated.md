# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Sphingosine kinase 2 (SphK2) inhibitors represent a promising class of therapeutic agents for the treatment of cancer and other diseases associated with dysregulation of sphingolipid metabolism. Sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), a bioactive lipid mediator, is synthesized from sphingosine through the action of two isoenzymes, sphingosine kinase-1 (SphK1) and sphingosine kinase-2 (SphK2). S1P functions as a critical signaling molecule that regulates diverse cellular processes including cell proliferation, migration, survival, angiogenesis, and immune responses. The biosynthetic precursors of S1P—ceramide and sphingosine—are generally associated with pro-apoptotic and growth-inhibitory effects, whereas S1P promotes cell survival and proliferation. This dynamic balance between pro-apoptotic and pro-survival sphingolipid metabolites, known as the “sphingolipid rheostat,” plays a central role in determining cellular fate under physiological and pathological conditions.

SphK1 and SphK2, while sharing significant sequence homology, exhibit distinct biological roles, subcellular localizations, and tissue distributions. SphK1 is predominantly cytosolic and has been consistently linked to oncogenic signaling, with its overexpression observed in numerous cancers. It promotes tumor growth, metastasis, and resistance to therapy by enhancing S1P production and activating downstream survival pathways such as ERK and Akt. In contrast, SphK2 is localized primarily in the nucleus, endoplasmic reticulum, and mitochondria, and its functional roles are more complex and context-dependent. Early studies suggested SphK2 might be pro-apoptotic, but accumulating evidence now indicates that SphK2 also supports tumor cell survival, particularly through nuclear S1P-mediated inhibition of histone deacetylases (HDACs) and modulation of mitochondrial function. Genetic ablation or pharmacological inhibition of SphK2 has been shown to suppress tumor growth in multiple preclinical models, underscoring its potential as a therapeutic target.

Despite the growing recognition of SphK2’s role in cancer biology, the development of selective SphK2 inhibitors has lagged behind that of SphK1 inhibitors. Several potent and selective SphK1 inhibitors have been reported, including SK1-I and PF-543, which have advanced our understanding of SphK1’s contributions to disease. However, only a limited number of SphK2 inhibitors exist, such as ABC294640, SG-12, R-FTY720-OMe, and trans-12, and these often suffer from moderate potency, poor selectivity, or suboptimal pharmacokinetic properties. The lack of robust, selective chemical tools for SphK2 has hindered efforts to fully elucidate its biological functions and therapeutic potential. Therefore, there remains a critical need for novel, potent, and highly selective SphK2 inhibitors with favorable drug-like properties that can serve both as research tools and as leads for anticancer drug development.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel 5-alkylidenethiazolidine-2,4-dione and 3-alkylidene-1,3-diazolidine-2,4-dione compounds that act as selective inhibitors of sphingosine kinase 2 (SphK2). These compounds are represented by Formula I:

**Formula I:**  
A compound of the structure wherein a thiazolidine-2,4-dione ring is substituted at the 3-position with a 2-aminoethyl group and at the 5-position with an alkylidene moiety bearing a substituted aryl group. In Formula I, R¹ is a C₁–C₁₀ alkyl, alkenyl, alkynyl, or aryl group, optionally substituted with one or more substituents selected from hydroxyl, alkoxy, halo, amino, nitro, cyano, or carboxyl; R², R³, R⁴, and R⁵ are independently hydrogen, halogen, C₁–C₆ alkyl, C₁–C₆ alkoxy, or hydroxyl; V is a bond, —CH₂—, —CH₂CH₂—, or —CH=CH—; X and Y are each independently O, S, or NH; Z is a substituted or unsubstituted phenyl, naphthyl, or heteroaryl ring; and W is NH₂, NHR, or NR₂, where R is C₁–C₆ alkyl.

The invention further encompasses compounds of Formula II, which are structurally related analogues featuring modifications to the core scaffold. In Formula II, R¹⁰ through R¹⁸ are independently selected from hydrogen, halogen, alkyl, alkoxy, hydroxyl, amino, and other common organic substituents, and X is as defined above. The invention includes methods of treating cancer by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a compound of Formula I or Formula II to a subject in need thereof. Specific embodiments include the use of these compounds for treating leukemia, breast cancer, glioblastoma, and other malignancies wherein SphK2 activity contributes to pathogenesis.

The invention also provides methods of inhibiting SphK2 activity, comprising contacting SphK2 with a compound of Formula I or Formula II. In preferred embodiments, W is NH₂, and R¹ is an alkoxyl substituent containing 4, 7, or 8 carbon atoms, such as butoxy, heptoxy, or octoxy. Additionally, the invention includes compounds of Formula III and Formula IV, which represent specific subclasses within the broader scope of Formula I and II. Notably, the compounds KL145 and KLIII016 are exemplified as particularly potent and selective SphK2 inhibitors. Methods of treating cancer using Formula III and Formula IV compounds, as well as methods of inhibiting SphK2 with these compounds, are disclosed. The overarching objective of the invention is to provide new, orally bioavailable, and selective SphK2 inhibitors that demonstrate significant anti-tumor efficacy in vitro and in vivo with minimal toxicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention discloses a series of novel thiazolidine-2,4-dione derivatives that function as selective SphK2 inhibitors. In Formula I, R¹ is preferably a C₄–C₈ alkoxy-substituted alkyl chain, such as butoxy, heptoxy, or octoxy, which enhances binding affinity and selectivity for SphK2. R² through R⁵ are typically hydrogen, though halogen or methoxy substitutions may be tolerated. V is most advantageously —CH₂CH₂—, forming a propylidene linker between the thiazolidine core and the aryl group. X and Y are preferably both oxygen, maintaining the thiazolidine-2,4-dione structure. Z is a para-substituted phenyl ring, with the substituent being the R¹-containing alkoxy group. W is optimally NH₂, as the primary amine is critical for forming key hydrogen bonds with Asp344 in the SphK2 active site.

Formula II represents guanidine or other nitrogen-containing analogues of the core structure, with R¹⁰–R¹⁸ defined to allow for structural diversity while preserving inhibitory activity. The compounds of Formula I and Formula III share a common pharmacophore: a planar thiazolidinedione ring capable of π-stacking interactions, a basic terminal amine for ionic/hydrogen bonding, and a hydrophobic aryl-alkyl tail that occupies a lipophilic pocket in SphK2. Structural characterization by NMR and mass spectrometry confirms the identity and purity of synthesized compounds. Among the disclosed compounds, KL145 (3-(2-aminoethyl)-5-[3-(4-butoxyphenyl)propylidene]thiazolidine-2,4-dione) exhibits an IC₅₀ of 4.30 µM against SphK2 and shows no inhibition of SphK1 at concentrations up to 10 µM, demonstrating exceptional selectivity. Other compounds, such as KLIII016, also display potent and selective SphK2 inhibition.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising a compound of the invention and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier are provided for treating diseases associated with SphK2 overactivity, particularly cancer. The composition may include various additives and excipients to enhance stability, solubility, and bioavailability. Acidifying agents (e.g., citric acid), alkalinizing agents (e.g., sodium bicarbonate), adsorbents (e.g., activated charcoal), aerosol propellants (e.g., HFA-134a), and air displacement agents (e.g., nitrogen) may be used in specific formulations. Antifungal preservatives (e.g., parabens), antimicrobial preservatives (e.g., benzyl alcohol), antioxidants (e.g., ascorbic acid), binding materials (e.g., cellulose), buffering agents (e.g., phosphate salts), carrying agents (e.g., lactose), chelating agents (e.g., EDTA), colorants, clarifying agents, emulsifying agents (e.g., polysorbates), encapsulating agents (e.g., gelatin), fillers (e.g., starch), flavorants, and humectants (e.g., glycerin) are among the excipients that may be incorporated.

The compounds are administered in an effective amount, typically ranging from 1 mg/kg to 100 mg/kg body weight, via oral, intraperitoneal, or other suitable routes. Treatment regimens may involve daily dosing for several weeks. The effective amount is that which achieves sufficient SphK2 inhibition to reduce tumor growth without causing significant toxicity. In vivo studies demonstrate that KL145, administered at 15–50 mg/kg, significantly inhibits tumor growth in U937 xenograft and JC syngeneic models, with tumor growth inhibition (TGI) values exceeding 50%. The compounds are effective against a range of cancers, including leukemia, breast cancer, and glioblastoma. Patient subjects include mammals, particularly humans, diagnosed with SphK2-associated malignancies.

Methods of inhibiting SphK2 involve administering a compound that reduces SphK2 enzymatic activity by at least 50% at micromolar concentrations. Inhibition is measured using recombinant enzyme assays or cellular S1P quantification. Selective inhibition is defined as >10-fold greater potency for SphK2 versus SphK1. The compounds also inhibit cancer cell proliferation and induce apoptosis, as demonstrated in U937 cells. Diseases treatable by SphK2 inhibition include not only cancer but also inflammatory and autoimmune disorders where SphK2/S1P signaling is implicated.

### In Vitro and In Vivo Characterization
KL145 inhibited SphK2 with IC₅₀ = 4.30 µM and showed no effect on SphK1 or ceramide kinase. Kinetic studies revealed competitive inhibition with sphingosine (Kᵢ = 6.4 µM). In U937 cells, KL145 reduced S1P levels, induced late apoptosis, and suppressed p-ERK and p-Akt. In vivo, KL145 (15 mg/kg i.p.) achieved 44.2% TGI in U937 xenografts; oral administration (50 mg/kg) yielded 51.25% TGI. In JC syngeneic models, doses of 20 and 35 mg/kg produced >50% tumor volume reduction. Molecular modeling confirmed preferential binding to SphK2 via H-bonding with Asp344 and hydrophobic interactions with Phe350, Ala336, and Val340.
