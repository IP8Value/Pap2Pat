# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of molecular biology and drug discovery, specifically relating to high-throughput screening (HTS) platforms for identifying modulators of intracellular protease activity. More particularly, the invention provides genetically engineered yeast-based systems that reconstitute mammalian protease activation networks, enabling functional assays for both chemical compound libraries and cDNA expression libraries. These systems are designed to overcome significant limitations inherent in traditional biochemical approaches to protease inhibitor discovery.

Proteolytic processing of proteins represents an irreversible post-translational modification that plays a pivotal role in numerous biological processes, including cell division, programmed cell death (apoptosis), cell differentiation, innate immunity, host-pathogen interactions, and intracellular protein trafficking. Given their central roles in cellular physiology and pathology, proteases have emerged as highly promising targets for therapeutic intervention across a broad spectrum of human diseases, including cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, ischemic conditions, inflammatory diseases, and infectious diseases. However, the development of effective screening methodologies for identifying compounds that modulate protease activity has been fraught with technical challenges that have significantly hampered drug discovery efforts in this area.

Caspases represent a particularly compelling example of a family of intracellular cysteine proteases for which novel high-throughput screening tools are urgently needed. The human genome encodes at least ten distinct caspase proteins, which function in complex hierarchical networks involving upstream initiator caspases and downstream effector caspases. These proteases collaborate through intricate proteolytic cascades where upstream members cleave and activate downstream family members, ultimately executing critical cellular functions such as apoptotic cell death and cytokine-mediated inflammation. The activation of upstream initiator caspases typically occurs through protein-protein interactions that facilitate the assembly of large multi-protein complexes, a process that is notoriously difficult to recapitulate in vitro using purified components. This complexity presents a major obstacle to conventional biochemical screening approaches, which often rely on isolated catalytic domains rather than physiologically relevant full-length proteins within their native activation contexts.

There exists a critical need for alternative screening methods that can identify compounds targeting not only the protease active sites themselves but also the upstream regulatory proteins and activation mechanisms that control protease activity in vivo. Traditional high-throughput screening assays using purified proteases face several fundamental limitations: they are generally restricted to identifying inhibitors rather than activators; they often require large quantities of purified, stable enzyme that may be difficult to produce recombinantly; they typically utilize only catalytic domains, thereby missing opportunities to discover allosteric modulators; they fail to capture the physiological context of multi-component activation complexes; and they struggle to achieve selectivity among closely related protease family members that share highly similar active site architectures. The development of cell-based screening platforms that can faithfully reconstitute entire protease activation pathways would address these limitations and provide a more physiologically relevant approach to drug discovery.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel genetic systems for monitoring and modulating protease activity in living yeast cells, specifically designed for high-throughput screening applications. These systems exploit the ability to reconstitute mammalian protease activation pathways in the simple eukaryotic organism Saccharomyces cerevisiae, thereby creating powerful platforms for both chemical library screening and functional cDNA library screening.

The invention encompasses a Single Component system wherein active proteases are expressed directly in yeast cells alongside cleavable reporter constructs. In this configuration, the protease of interest is expressed at carefully titrated levels sufficient to achieve activation without causing excessive cellular toxicity, while a membrane-tethered transcription factor containing a protease-specific cleavage site is co-expressed. Upon protease-mediated cleavage, the transcription factor is released from its membrane anchor and translocates to the nucleus, where it activates reporter genes that enable facile detection of protease activity. This readout method employs dual reporter systems, typically comprising LEU2 (enabling growth on leucine-deficient media) and lacZ (producing β-galactosidase activity detectable by colorimetric assays), providing both selection-based and quantitative measurement capabilities. A specific embodiment of this Single Component system enables drug screening assays for all ten human caspases, with each caspase demonstrating characteristic substrate specificity profiles that validate the physiological relevance of the assay format.

The invention further provides a Two-Component system that more accurately recapitulates the physiological activation mechanisms of proteases. In this configuration, the inactive zymogen form of the protease is expressed at low levels alongside its specific upstream activator protein, which is expressed at higher levels to drive activation. For caspases, this includes combinations such as pro-caspase-1 with ASC, pro-caspase-2 with RAIDD, pro-caspase-9 with gain-of-function Apaf-1 mutants, and pro-caspase-8 or -10 with FADD. Expression levels are precisely engineered through the use of promoters with varying strengths, plasmid copy number control (low-copy CEN/ARS versus high-copy 2µ origins), and careful optimization of reporter gene operator numbers to achieve optimal signal-to-noise ratios. An embodiment of this Two-Component system provides robust drug screening assays that specifically require both components for reporter activation, thereby enabling the identification of compounds that interfere with either the protease itself or its upstream activation mechanism.

The invention additionally encompasses a Plural-Component system that reconstitutes entire mammalian protease activation pathways in yeast. These complex networks include three or more components that faithfully reproduce physiological signaling cascades, such as the extrinsic apoptosis pathway (Fas/FADD/caspase-8) or inflammasome complexes (NLRP1/ASC/caspase-1). Expression levels of each component are meticulously engineered using the same promoter strength and plasmid copy number strategies employed in the simpler systems, but with additional complexity management to ensure proper pathway functionality without excessive background activation. An embodiment of this Plural-Component system enables comprehensive drug screening assays that capture the full complexity of physiological protease activation networks, providing unprecedented opportunities to identify pathway-specific modulators.

The Two-Component and Plural-Component systems are particularly valuable for identifying caspase activation protease networks, as they enable functional dissection of hierarchical relationships between pathway components. By systematically omitting individual components and testing various combinations, these systems can elucidate the specific requirements for protease activation and identify novel regulatory interactions. Chemical library screens can be performed using these systems to identify compounds that modulate specific steps within complex activation pathways, providing greater mechanistic insight than traditional single-target approaches.

The invention facilitates automated screening processes that are compatible with high-throughput formats, particularly 384-well microtiter plates. These automated processes integrate robotic liquid handling systems for compound dispensing, cell seeding, and reagent addition, coupled with automated plate reading for quantitative measurement of reporter gene activity. Data analysis is performed using standardized statistical methods, including calculation of Z′ factors to assess assay quality and reliability, dose-response curve fitting for potency determination, and hit validation through counter-screening against related protease systems. An embodiment of library screens demonstrates successful identification of both known and novel caspase modulators from diverse chemical libraries, validating the platform's utility for drug discovery.

Optimization of screening concentrations is achieved through systematic empirical testing of multiple variables, including initial cell seeding density, culture duration, substrate concentration, and carbon source composition. These optimizations ensure maximal signal-to-noise ratios while maintaining assay robustness and reproducibility. The HTS assays developed according to the invention demonstrate excellent performance characteristics, with Z′ factors consistently exceeding 0.6, indicating suitability for large-scale screening campaigns. These assays enable the rapid identification and characterization of protease modulators with unprecedented efficiency and physiological relevance.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Proteolytic processing of proteins represents one of the most important irreversible post-translational modifications in biological systems, governing a vast array of cellular processes ranging from cell division and differentiation to programmed cell death and immune responses. Proteases, the enzymes responsible for this processing, have consequently emerged as critical therapeutic targets for numerous human diseases. However, the development of effective screening methodologies for identifying compounds that modulate protease activity has been significantly hindered by technical challenges associated with traditional biochemical approaches. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing genetically engineered yeast-based systems that reconstitute mammalian protease activation networks in a simple eukaryotic host, enabling physiologically relevant high-throughput screening platforms.

Proteases play essential roles in virtually all aspects of cellular physiology, including intracellular protein sorting and trafficking, host-pathogen interactions, innate immunity, cell differentiation, cell division, and programmed cell death. Their dysregulation is implicated in a wide spectrum of human pathologies, making them attractive targets for therapeutic intervention. Despite their importance, high-throughput screening for protease modulators faces several fundamental challenges. Many proteases require complex multi-component activation systems that are difficult to reconstitute in vitro, and purified proteases often suffer from stability issues that compromise assay reliability. Furthermore, the structural similarity of active sites among closely related protease family members makes achieving selective inhibition extremely challenging using traditional competitive inhibitor approaches.

The production and purification of proteases for biochemical assays presents significant difficulties, particularly for full-length proteins that may require specific post-translational modifications or membrane association for proper function. Many proteases are initially synthesized as inactive zymogens that require specific activation mechanisms, which are often lost when working with isolated catalytic domains. This limitation severely restricts the discovery of allosteric modulators and compounds that target regulatory proteins involved in protease activation rather than the catalytic site itself. The present invention overcomes these challenges by providing cellular platforms that maintain the physiological context of protease activation while enabling high-throughput screening capabilities.

The invention creates unprecedented opportunities for discovering allosteric modulators and compounds that target other proteins involved in protease activation pathways. By reconstituting entire activation networks in yeast, the platform enables identification of compounds that interfere with protein-protein interactions, oligomerization events, or other regulatory mechanisms that control protease activity in vivo. This approach provides a more comprehensive view of potential therapeutic intervention points compared to traditional single-target biochemical assays.

Caspases serve as a specific and exemplary embodiment of intracellular cysteine proteinases that benefit from the present invention's platform. These proteases are conserved throughout the animal kingdom and play central roles in both apoptotic cell death and inflammatory responses. The human genome contains at least ten caspase genes that function in complex hierarchical networks, making them ideal candidates for the multi-component screening systems described herein.

### Caspases

Caspases are defined as intracellular cysteine proteases that exhibit strict specificity for aspartic acid residues at the P1 position of their cleavage sites. This unique substrate specificity distinguishes them from other protease families and provides a foundation for developing selective detection and screening methodologies. Caspases play essential roles in programmed cell death (apoptosis), serving as the primary executioners of this highly regulated cellular process. The caspase family is organized into upstream initiator caspases and downstream effector caspases, with the former responsible for activating the latter through specific proteolytic cleavage events.

Upstream initiator caspases become activated through protein interaction-mediated assembly of large multi-protein complexes, such as the death-inducing signaling complex (DISC) for caspases-8 and -10, or the apoptosome for caspase-9. These activation mechanisms involve homotypic domain interactions, such as death effector domain (DED) or caspase recruitment domain (CARD) interactions, that facilitate proximity-induced dimerization and subsequent autoproteolytic activation. Downstream effector caspases, once activated by upstream initiators, execute apoptosis through the irreversible cleavage of specific protein substrates that dismantle the cell in an orderly fashion.

The three-dimensional structure of the active sites of most human caspases reveals a conserved catalytic mechanism centered around a reactive cysteine residue, but with notable differences in the geometry and side-chain composition of amino acids surrounding the active site pocket. These structural variations account for the distinct substrate preferences observed among different caspase family members, despite their shared requirement for aspartic acid at the P1 position. Activity profiles for most human caspases have been well characterized, revealing preferences for specific tetrapeptide sequences that reflect their physiological roles and substrate specificities.

The known cleavage sites of endogenous proteins hydrolyzed by specific caspases provide critical validation for the screening systems described herein. For example, caspases-3 and -7 preferentially cleave after DEVD sequences, caspase-8 and -10 favor LETD motifs, caspase-9 cleaves LEHD sites, and inflammatory caspases-1, -4, and -5 recognize WEHD sequences. These well-established substrate specificities enable the design of highly selective reporter constructs that accurately reflect the physiological activity of each caspase family member.

### Substrate Specificity of Caspases

The three-dimensional structure of the active sites of most human caspases reveals a conserved catalytic core with variable substrate-binding pockets that determine substrate specificity. While all caspases require aspartic acid at the P1 position, the preferences for amino acids at positions P2, P3, and P4 vary significantly among family members, creating distinct substrate recognition profiles. These structural differences in the active site architecture enable the development of selective substrates and inhibitors that can distinguish between closely related caspase family members.

Activity profiles for most human caspases have been extensively characterized using peptide library screening and physiological substrate identification. Caspase-1, -4, and -5 (inflammatory caspases) show strong preference for WEHD sequences, while caspase-2 favors DEHD motifs. Caspases-3 and -7 (effector caspases) efficiently cleave DEVD-containing substrates, caspase-6 prefers TEVD sequences, caspases-8 and -10 recognize LETD sites, and caspase-9 cleaves LEHD motifs. These well-defined specificity profiles provide the foundation for designing selective reporter constructs that enable accurate discrimination between different caspase activities in the yeast-based screening systems described herein.

### Mechanisms of Activating Upstream Initiator Caspases

The activation of upstream initiator caspases occurs through sophisticated protein interaction networks that assemble large multi-protein complexes in response to specific cellular signals. The TNF family of cytokine receptors plays a central role in extrinsic apoptosis induction, with receptors such as Fas (CD95) containing cytoplasmic Death Domains (DD) that serve as docking sites for adapter proteins. FADD (Fas-associated death domain protein), also known as Mort-1, functions as a critical adapter protein that bridges death receptors to downstream caspases through its bipartite domain structure containing both a Death Domain (DD) and a Death Effector Domain (DED).

Caspases-8 and -10 contain N-terminal DED domains that interact with the DED of FADD, facilitating their recruitment to the activated death receptor complex. Within this death-inducing signaling complex (DISC), caspases undergo proximity-induced dimerization followed by autoproteolytic activation, converting the inactive zymogen into the active tetrameric enzyme composed of two large and two small subunits. This Extrinsic Pathway of apoptosis induction is triggered by death ligands such as FasL or TRAIL binding to their respective receptors, leading to receptor trimerization and DISC assembly.

Anti-apoptotic proteins containing DED domains, such as c-FLIP (cellular FLICE-inhibitory protein), can modulate this activation process by competing with caspases for binding to FADD or by forming heterodimers with caspases that alter their activity. The nucleotide-binding oligomerization domains found in caspase-binding proteins represent another important class of regulatory elements that control caspase activation through ATP-dependent oligomerization mechanisms.

The prototype for caspase activation was first elucidated in Caenorhabditis elegans, where the CED-4 protein functions as an ATP-binding adapter that oligomerizes to activate the caspase CED-3. Apaf-1 serves as the human homologue of CED-4, containing a C-terminal WD-40 repeat domain that functions as a negative-regulatory domain in the absence of activating signals. Cytochrome c, released from mitochondria during intrinsic apoptosis, binds to Apaf-1 and induces a conformational change that relieves autoinhibition, enabling Apaf-1 oligomerization and formation of the apoptosome complex that activates caspase-9 as the apical caspase in the cytochrome c/mitochondrial pathway.

### Phenotypes of Animal Caspases Expressed in Yeast

The expression of animal caspases in yeast has revealed important insights into their activation mechanisms and cellular effects. Many caspases, when overexpressed in yeast, undergo spontaneous activation due to their inherent capacity for dimerization and autoproteolysis, leading to growth suppression or cell death phenotypes that mirror their pro-apoptotic functions in mammalian cells. These phenotypes have provided valuable lessons regarding the careful titration of expression levels required for functional assays that do not result in excessive cellular toxicity.

The observation that caspase expression can lead to yeast growth inhibition or death has highlighted the possibility of devising cleavable reporter systems that separate the detection of protease activity from its cytotoxic consequences. By engineering expression levels to achieve sub-lethal activation thresholds, researchers can create sensitive reporter systems that accurately reflect protease activity without compromising cellular viability. This approach forms the foundation of the present invention's screening platforms, which balance sufficient protease activation for detection with minimal cellular toxicity to enable robust high-throughput screening.

## EXAMPLE 1

### Development and Testing of a Cleavable Reporter Gene System for Assaying Protease Activity in Living Yeast

The development of a cleavable reporter gene system for assaying protease activity in living yeast began with the design of a membrane-tethered transcription factor construct based on the human Fas (CD95) receptor. This reporter gene system was engineered such that the cytosolic death domain of Fas was replaced with a chimeric transcription factor comprising the DNA-binding domain of LexA fused to the transactivation domain of B42. Between the transmembrane domain of Fas and the LexA-B42 transcription factor, various tetrapeptide sequences known to be recognized and cleaved by specific caspase family members were inserted. As a critical control, parallel constructs were prepared in which the essential aspartic acid residue at the P1 position was substituted with glycine, rendering the sequence uncleavable by caspases.

This reporter system was tested with various caspases by co-expressing the cleavable transcription factor constructs with active forms of caspases-1 through -10 in yeast strains EGY48 or EGY191, which contain stably integrated LEU2 and episomal lacZ reporter genes under the control of LexA operator sequences. The system demonstrated remarkable specificity, with each caspase activating only those reporter constructs containing its cognate cleavage sequence. For instance, caspases-1, -4, and -5 specifically activated the WEHD-containing reporter, while caspases-3 and -7 activated the DEVD-containing reporter. This precise substrate specificity validated the physiological relevance of the assay system and established its utility for distinguishing between different caspase activities.

### A Genetic System for Monitoring Caspase 1 Activity in Yeast Cells.

A specific genetic system for monitoring caspase-1 activity in yeast cells was created by engineering yeast to express a Fas-LexA-B42 fusion protein containing the caspase-1 target site WEHD. A glycine substitution control construct (WEHG) was also generated to confirm the specificity of cleavage. Active caspase-1 was expressed in these yeast cells using moderate-strength promoters to achieve sufficient activation without causing excessive growth inhibition. The reporter gene system employed dual readouts: LEU2 expression enabled growth on leucine-deficient medium, while lacZ expression produced β-galactosidase activity detectable by X-gal colorimetric assays.

Testing with active caspase-1 resulted in robust activation of both reporter genes, with yeast transformants growing on leucine-deficient medium and developing blue coloration due to β-galactosidase activity. In contrast, testing with catalytically inactive caspase-1 (containing cysteine-to-alanine mutations in the active site) failed to activate either reporter, confirming that proteolytic activity was required for reporter activation. The signal-to-noise ratio was optimized by empirically adjusting promoter strengths, plasmid copy numbers, and the number of LexA operators in the reporter gene promoters. Results showed clear growth on leucine-deficient medium only when both active caspase-1 and the WEHD-containing reporter were present, while β-galactosidase activity measurements provided quantitative confirmation of caspase-1 activity levels.

### Substrate Specificities of the Caspases Expressed in Yeast.

To comprehensively characterize substrate specificities, multiple versions of the Fas-d-S1-TA reporter construct were created, each containing different caspase cleavage sites: WEHD (caspase-1), DEHD (caspase-2), DEVD (caspase-3/7), TEVD (caspase-6), LETD (caspase-8/10), and LEHD (caspase-9). These constructs were co-expressed with LacZ and LEU2 reporter genes in yeast cells alongside individual caspases-1 through -10. Substrate specificity testing revealed precise matching between caspase identity and preferred cleavage sequences, with inflammatory caspases (1, 4, 5) showing strong preference for WEHD, effector caspases (3, 7) for DEVD, and initiator caspases (8, 10) for LETD. Caspase-2 demonstrated activity against both DEHD and DEVD sequences, consistent with previous biochemical characterizations. The results confirmed that the yeast-based system accurately recapitulates the known substrate specificities of human caspases, validating its utility for functional studies.

### Development of Two-component Systems Demonstrating Function of Caspase Activators in Yeast

The concept of two-component systems for caspase activators was developed to more accurately model physiological activation mechanisms. These systems express the inactive zymogen form of a caspase at low levels alongside its specific upstream activator protein at higher levels, requiring both components for reporter activation. This approach mimics the natural requirement for adapter proteins in caspase activation and enables the study of specific protein-protein interactions that drive protease activation.

### Schematic Representation of a Two-component System for Caspase1 Activators in Yeast Cells.

The two-component system for caspase-1 activators in yeast cells comprises several key elements: a zymogen pro-caspase-1 protein expressed from a weak promoter on a low-copy plasmid, a cleavable reporter containing the S1 (WEHD) site, and co-expression of the adapter protein ASC from a strong promoter on a high-copy plasmid. Upon co-expression, ASC facilitates the oligomerization and activation of pro-caspase-1 through CARD-CARD interactions. The activated caspase-1 then cleaves the membrane-tethered transcription factor at the WEHD site, releasing the LexA-B42 domain which translocates to the nucleus and activates reporter genes containing LexA operator sequences.

Additional two-component systems were tested for other caspase-activator pairs, including pro-caspase-2 with RAIDD, pro-caspase-9 with gain-of-function Apaf-1 mutants, and pro-caspase-8/-10 with FADD. Results demonstrated that each system required both components for reporter activation, with neither the pro-caspase alone nor the activator alone producing significant reporter gene expression. Validation experiments confirmed that catalytically inactive caspase mutants failed to activate reporters, and mismatched activator-caspase pairs (such as ASC with pro-caspase-8) also failed to produce activation, demonstrating the specificity of these two-component systems.

### Development of a Plural-component System—Mammalian Protease Activating Pathways Reconstituted in Yeast

The plural-component system represents the most sophisticated embodiment of the invention, reconstituting entire mammalian protease activation pathways in yeast. The extrinsic apoptosis pathway was successfully reconstituted by expressing Fas (death receptor), FADD (adapter protein), and pro-caspase-8 or -10 (effector protease) together with a LETD-containing cleavable reporter. Additionally, γ-secretase activity was reconstituted by expressing the four-component complex of Presenilin-1, Nicastrin, APH-1, and PEN-2 with an appropriate APP-derived cleavable reporter. Testing of these plural-component systems demonstrated faithful recapitulation of physiological activation mechanisms, with reporter activation requiring all pathway components and showing appropriate sensitivity to pathway-specific inhibitors.

### Schematic Representation of Plural-component Systems for Caspase-8 Activation in Yeast.

The plural-component system for caspase-8 activation in yeast comprises Fas (CD95) death receptor, FADD adapter protein, and pro-caspase-8, along with a LETD-containing cleavable reporter. Two alternative approaches were developed: one expressing low levels of FADD with high levels of Fas to mimic ligand-independent receptor oligomerization, and another expressing low levels of pro-caspase-8 with high levels of FADD, supplemented with death receptor expression. Both approaches successfully reconstituted the death-inducing signaling complex (DISC) and demonstrated caspase-8 activation dependent on all three components.

### Testing of Yeast-based, Plural-component Systems for Activating Caspases-8 or -10.

Yeast-based systems for activating caspases-8 or -10 were tested using yeast strain EGY48 engineered to express various combinations of pathway components. Caspase-8/-10-cleavable substrates containing LETD sequences were expressed alongside low levels of pro-caspase-8 or -10 and FADD from plasmids with weak promoters. Transformation with empty vector or Fas-expressing plasmids demonstrated that Fas expression enhanced caspase activation, with resulting cell growth and blue color development on selective media. Alternative configurations expressing LETD-containing substrates, low levels of pro-caspase-8 or -10, and Fas, followed by transformation with empty vector or FADD-expressing plasmids, confirmed the essential role of FADD in bridging receptor and caspase activation.

Substrate cleavage and caspase-8/-10 activation were quantitatively measured through β-galactosidase activity assays, demonstrating robust activation only when all pathway components were present. Expression of LETD-containing substrates enabled screening of various cDNA libraries, leading to the characterization of positive clones encoding fragments of DR4, DR5, caspase-8, and caspase-2. Assays were performed in duplicate to ensure reproducibility, and successful creation of plural-component caspase-activating networks enabled the study of both apoptotic and pro-inflammatory pathways.

The pro-inflammatory plural-component system successfully reconstituted the NALP1 inflammasome by expressing pro-caspase-1, adapter protein ASC, and gain-of-function mutant NALP1(ΔLRR). A reporter protein containing the caspase-1 cleavage sequence WEHD was activated upon NALP1(ΔLRR) expression, with exclusion of any of the three components preventing reporter activation. The yeast assay for NALP1 demonstrated specific cleavage of substrates with WEHD linkers, while WEHG controls remained uncleaved. Expression of wild-type or Cys285-mutated caspase-1 confirmed the requirement for catalytic activity.

High-throughput screening implementation of yeast-based protease network reconstitution assays was achieved by optimizing signal-to-noise ratios in microtiter plates. Validation using the pharmacological caspase inhibitor zVAD-fmk demonstrated dose-dependent inhibition with IC50 values ranging from 1-10 µM. High-throughput screening of the yeast-based NALP1 inflammasome assay identified specific inhibitors with appropriate selectivity profiles. The yeast-based systems for activating caspases demonstrated substrate sequence- and caspase activity-dependent cleavage patterns that accurately reflected known biochemical specificities.

Flow charts for cDNA library screening using the reporter gene strategy illustrated systematic approaches for identifying novel caspase activators and regulators. The one-component yeast-based caspase activity assay provided a foundation for validating effector caspase activators, while two and three-component systems enabled comprehensive analysis of initiator caspase activation mechanisms. Validation of yeast-based assays for initiator caspase activators demonstrated their utility for identifying novel regulatory proteins and pathway components.

The specificity of upstream activators of initiator caspases was confirmed through systematic testing of various combinations, with yeast transformants showing appropriate activation patterns only when correct activator-caspase pairs were expressed. Three-component yeast-based caspase assays were validated through comprehensive testing, demonstrating their reliability for functional screening applications. Flow charts for cDNA library screening illustrated systematic approaches for identifying novel pathway components, with examples of successful cloning results including death receptors, adapter proteins, and regulatory molecules.

Signal-to-noise ratio optimization in microtiter plates was achieved through systematic testing of multiple variables, including cell density, incubation time, and substrate concentration. Plasmids for co-expression of caspases and substrate-cleavable transcription factors were engineered with appropriate promoter strengths and copy number control elements. Plasmids for expression of upstream activators of caspases and lacZ reporter genes were similarly optimized for maximum assay performance. Yeast expression plasmids for functional screening of cDNA libraries were constructed with appropriate selectable markers and promoter elements to enable efficient library screening.

## Experimental Designs & Methods Used

Chemical library screens were performed using the optimized yeast-based systems to identify novel caspase modulators. Assay performance was validated through comprehensive testing of positive and negative controls, demonstrating robust signal-to-noise ratios and appropriate sensitivity to known inhibitors. Assay conditions were optimized through systematic empirical testing of multiple variables, including cell density, incubation time, substrate concentration, and carbon source composition. Hit rates and test reliability were determined through pilot screening campaigns, with Z′ factors consistently exceeding 0.6, indicating excellent assay quality suitable for high-throughput applications.

β-galactosidase activity was measured using colorimetric X-gal substrate in both solid and liquid media formats, with absorbance readings at 620 nm providing quantitative measurements of reporter gene activation. Robotic liquid handling systems were integrated into the screening workflow to enable automated compound dispensing, cell seeding, and reagent addition in 384-well microtiter plate formats. The Z′ factor was calculated for each plate using standard statistical methods to assess assay quality and reliability, with values consistently above the threshold of 0.5 required for high-throughput screening applications.

### Vectors Encoding Caspase Cleavable Transcription Factors:

Reporter proteins were generated using PCR and recombinant DNA techniques to create membrane-tethered transcription factors containing specific caspase cleavage sites. The Fas-d-S1-TA construct served as the foundation, with various tetrapeptide linkers (WEHD, DEHD, DEVD, TEVD, LETD, LEHD) inserted between the Fas transmembrane domain and the LexA-B42 transcription factor. Control constructs with glycine substitutions at the P1 position were also generated to confirm cleavage specificity.

### Plasmids for Expressing Caspases and Their Upstream Activators:

Plasmids were derived from the pRS series vectors and modified to enable precise control of expression levels. Promoter strength was adjusted using constitutive promoters of varying activity (CYC1, ADH, TEF, GPD) or inducible GAL1 promoters, combined with different plasmid replication origins (CEN/ARS for low copy, 2µ for high copy) to achieve optimal expression levels. Caspases and upstream activators were cloned with epitope tags (HA, FLAG) for convenient detection and verification of expression.

### Complex Plasmids Constructions.

Complex plasmids containing three or more genes were constructed to enable co-expression of multiple pathway components from single vectors. Diagrams of these complex plasmids illustrated the arrangement of promoters, coding sequences, and termination elements necessary for balanced expression of multiple proteins. cDNAs encoding human caspases and adapter proteins were systematically cloned into these vectors, and expression cDNA libraries were constructed using directional cloning strategies with appropriate promoter elements.

### Reporter Gene Assays.

Plasmids were introduced into yeast by lithium acetate transformation, with transformants selected on appropriate dropout media. Reporter gene assays were performed on solid media by streaking transformants on selective plates containing X-gal substrate, with growth and blue color development monitored over 4-6 days. Liquid media assays were performed in 384-well plates using optimized conditions, with β-galactosidase activity measured by absorbance at 620 nm after appropriate incubation periods.

### HTS Assays.

High-throughput screening assays were performed for both Fas/FADD/caspase-8 and NLRP1/ASC/caspase-1 pathways using optimized conditions. Yeast cells were cultured and prepared according to standardized protocols, with HTS assays assembled in 384-well plates using robotic liquid handling systems. Absorbance readings at 620 nm were taken after 48 hours of incubation, with data analyzed using standardized statistical methods to identify hits and calculate potency values.

### Yeast-based Counter-screen Assays.

Counter-screen assays were developed to detect compounds that directly inhibit the lacZ reporter gene or alter β-galactosidase activity independent of caspase modulation. These assays employed yeast strains constitutively expressing the LexA/B42 transcription factor or containing empty vectors, enabling identification and elimination of false-positive compounds that interfere with the reporter system rather than the target pathway.

### Cloning Systems for Caspase Activators.

Comprehensive cloning systems were developed for identifying caspase activators through cDNA library screening. cDNAs encoding caspase-1 activators were cloned and transformed into yeast strain EGY191, with selection on histidine-deficient plates. Similar approaches were employed for caspases-2, -3, and -4 using appropriate yeast strains and selection conditions. Screens for cDNAs encoding proteases, caspase-8 activators, and adapter proteins successfully identified known pathway components and novel regulatory molecules, with activation of caspases-8 and -10 confirmed through comprehensive validation experiments.
