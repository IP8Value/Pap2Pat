## BACKGROUND

Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) is a devastating inflammatory bowel disease that predominantly affects premature infants, characterized by acute intestinal necrosis, systemic sepsis, and high mortality rates approaching thirty percent. The pathogenesis of NEC arises from a confluence of risk factors including prematurity, enteral feeding with formula rather than breast milk, and bacterial colonization of the immature gastrointestinal tract. Current clinical understanding posits that NEC develops primarily in the postnatal period as a consequence of exaggerated innate immune responses to luminal bacteria, particularly through toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) signaling on the intestinal epithelium. TLR4 is expressed at significantly higher levels in the premature intestinal epithelium compared to term infants, and its activation by bacterial lipopolysaccharide leads to mucosal injury, epithelial apoptosis, and bacterial translocation across the compromised intestinal barrier. Despite decades of research, therapeutic interventions for NEC remain largely supportive, focusing on bowel rest, antibiotics, and surgical resection in severe cases. Preventive strategies have been limited to promoting breast milk feeding and probiotic supplementation, but these approaches are not universally effective and do not address the fundamental molecular mechanisms driving disease pathogenesis. Critically, all existing preventive measures are implemented after birth, reflecting the prevailing dogma that NEC is exclusively a postnatal disorder. However, emerging clinical observations challenge this paradigm, suggesting that in utero processes may predispose infants to NEC development, thereby opening new avenues for prenatal intervention. For instance, infants born after intrauterine infection or inflammation exhibit increased incidence and severity of NEC, while certain maternal dietary patterns correlate with reduced NEC risk. These findings collectively indicate that the developmental trajectory toward NEC may begin before birth, highlighting an urgent need for novel therapeutic approaches that target the prenatal period to modulate intestinal development and immune maturation in ways that confer protection against this life-threatening condition.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present patent application is structured to provide a comprehensive disclosure of the invention, including detailed descriptions of the chemical compounds, methods of treatment, pharmaceutical compositions, and definitions of key terms. The purpose of this detailed description is to enable any person skilled in the art to make and use the invention without undue experimentation, while also providing sufficient detail to support the claims that define the scope of patent protection sought. Aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) ligands represent a class of compounds that have gained significant attention for their immunomodulatory properties, particularly in the context of inflammatory and autoimmune disorders. The AHR is a ligand-activated transcription factor that recognizes a diverse array of environmental and dietary compounds, including those derived from cruciferous vegetables and microbial metabolism. Recent research has revealed that AHR signaling plays a critical role in maintaining intestinal homeostasis by regulating epithelial barrier function, immune cell differentiation, and inflammatory responses. The motivation for using AHR agonists in the treatment of inflammatory disorders stems from the discovery that impaired AHR signaling contributes to the pathogenesis of several gastrointestinal diseases, including inflammatory bowel disease and necrotizing enterocolitis. Particularly relevant to the present invention is the finding that AHR activation in the intestinal epithelium can specifically attenuate toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) signaling, which is a key driver of inflammation in premature infants susceptible to NEC. The administration of AHR agonists to pregnant mothers represents a novel therapeutic strategy that leverages maternal-fetal signaling to confer protection against NEC in offspring. This approach is based on the discovery that AHR ligands can cross the placental barrier and activate AHR signaling in the developing fetal intestine, thereby programming the neonatal gut for enhanced resistance to inflammatory insults. The compounds of formulas (I-III) and their analogues described herein represent a new class of AHR agonists that have been specifically optimized for potency, selectivity, and pharmacokinetic properties suitable for both prenatal and postnatal administration.

### A. Compounds

The invention provides compounds of formula (I) as defined above, wherein the variables X₁, Y₁, R₁, R₂, R₃, R₄, R₅, and R₆ are selected to optimize AHR agonist activity while maintaining favorable pharmaceutical properties. Specific embodiments of formula (I) include compounds where X₁ is S, Y₁ is absent (representing a direct bond), R₁ is fluoro, methyl, or cyano, R₂ is hydrogen, and R₃ and R₄ are methyl and trifluoroethoxy, respectively. These specific substitutions have been found to enhance binding affinity to the AHR ligand-binding domain while maintaining metabolic stability. The invention also encompasses compounds of formula (II), which feature a quinazoline core structure substituted with benzimidazole moieties through various linker groups. In formula (II), preferred embodiments include those where the linkage between the quinazoline and benzimidazole rings is through a thioether or ether bond, with specific substitution patterns on both ring systems that enhance AHR activation potency. Representative compounds of formula (II) include 4-(((1H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-yl)methyl)thio)quinazoline and its N-methylated derivative 4-((1-methyl-1H-benzo[d]imidazol-2-yl)methoxy)quinazoline, which demonstrate potent AHR agonist activity in cellular assays. Additionally, the invention provides compounds of formula (III), which represent further structural modifications of the quinazoline-benzimidazole scaffold with various substituents at the 2-position of the quinazoline ring. Specific embodiments of formula (III) include compounds where the 2-position substituent is ethyl, hydroxy, or trifluoromethyl, with the latter demonstrating particularly potent AHR activation and TLR4 inhibition in preclinical models. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutically acceptable salts of all disclosed compounds, which are formed by reaction with appropriate acids or bases to enhance solubility, stability, or bioavailability. Acid addition salts may be formed with inorganic acids such as hydrochloric acid, sulfuric acid, or phosphoric acid, or with organic acids such as acetic acid, citric acid, or tartaric acid. Base addition salts may be formed with alkali metal hydroxides, alkaline earth metal hydroxides, or organic bases such as triethylamine or diisopropylethylamine.

### B. Methods of Treatment

The present invention provides methods for treating inflammatory disorders by administering AHR agonist compounds to subjects in need thereof. These methods are based on the discovery that activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor can modulate inflammatory responses, particularly in the gastrointestinal tract. Inflammatory disorders as used herein include conditions characterized by excessive or dysregulated immune activation, with particular emphasis on necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), inflammatory bowel disease, colitis, and other gastrointestinal inflammatory conditions. The treatment of necrotizing enterocolitis represents a primary application of the disclosed methods, given the critical role of TLR4 signaling in NEC pathogenesis and the ability of AHR activation to specifically attenuate this pathway. The term "subject" as used herein refers to any animal that may benefit from the disclosed treatments, including human subjects and animal subjects used in preclinical studies or veterinary applications. Human subjects encompass individuals of all ages, with particular relevance to infants, newborns, and premature infants who are at highest risk for NEC. Animal subjects include laboratory animals such as mice, rats, and piglets used in experimental models of inflammatory disorders. The term "infant" refers to children from birth to twelve months of age, while "newborn" specifically denotes infants during the first twenty-eight days of life. "Premature infant" refers to infants born before completion of 37 weeks of gestation, who are at particularly high risk for developing NEC due to immature intestinal development and immune function. The treatment of human subjects according to the disclosed methods involves administration of AHR agonist compounds via enteral routes, which include oral, gastric, or intestinal delivery. An "effective amount" refers to the quantity of compound required to achieve the desired therapeutic effect, which may vary depending on the specific compound, route of administration, and patient characteristics. The disclosed methods also encompass combination therapy, wherein AHR agonists are administered together with additional therapeutic agents such as antibiotics, anti-inflammatory drugs, or probiotics. Combination therapy may involve simultaneous administration, where both agents are delivered at the same time, or sequential administration, where agents are delivered at different times to achieve optimal therapeutic synergy. Synergistic effects occur when the combined effect of two agents exceeds the sum of their individual effects, which can be quantified using a synergy index calculated from dose-response curves. Antagonism refers to situations where the combined effect is less than expected, while additivity describes effects that are simply cumulative. A "synergistically effective amount" refers to the specific quantities of each agent required to achieve synergistic interaction. The disclosed methods also include prophylactic treatment to reduce the risk of NEC development in high-risk populations, particularly premature infants. This prophylactic approach may involve administration during pregnancy to the mother or directly to the neonate, with the goal of reducing both the incidence and severity of NEC.

### C. Infant Nutritional Formula Comprising Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) Agonists for the Prevention and Treatment of Inflammatory Disorders

The invention provides infant nutritional formulas comprising AHR agonist compounds for the prevention and treatment of inflammatory disorders, particularly necrotizing enterocolitis. These specialized formulas are designed to deliver therapeutic concentrations of AHR agonists to infants at risk for or suffering from inflammatory gastrointestinal conditions. The formula composition includes standard nutritional components such as proteins, carbohydrates, fats, vitamins, and minerals, supplemented with effective amounts of AHR agonist compounds of formulas (I-III) or their pharmaceutically acceptable salts. The formulas may be provided in various forms including liquid ready-to-feed formulations, concentrated liquids requiring dilution, or powdered formulations requiring reconstitution with water. Liquid formulations are particularly advantageous for immediate administration to hospitalized infants, while powdered formulations offer extended shelf life and ease of transport. Commercial infant formulas such as Similac Advance, Enfamil, or Gerber Good Start may serve as base formulations for incorporation of AHR agonists. Enteral administration of these specialized formulas ensures direct delivery of AHR agonists to the gastrointestinal tract, where they can activate AHR signaling in the intestinal epithelium to exert their anti-inflammatory effects. This approach is particularly beneficial for premature infants who cannot receive breast milk and are therefore at highest risk for NEC development.

### D. Maternal Administration of an Aryl Hydrocarbon Receptor (AHR) Agonist

The invention provides methods for preventing inflammatory disorders in offspring through maternal administration of AHR agonist compounds during pregnancy. This approach leverages the discovery that AHR ligands can cross the placental barrier and activate AHR signaling in the developing fetal intestine, thereby programming the neonatal gut for enhanced resistance to inflammatory insults. Administration of AHR agonists to pregnant mothers represents a novel prenatal intervention strategy that addresses the emerging understanding that NEC pathogenesis may begin in utero. The method involves administering effective amounts of AHR agonist compounds to pregnant women at risk of delivering premature infants, with the goal of reducing the incidence and severity of NEC in their offspring. This prophylactic approach is particularly relevant in cases of threatened preterm labor, where administration of AHR agonists could provide immediate protection to the developing fetal intestine.

### Example 1

Abbreviations used herein include NEC (necrotizing enterocolitis), AHR (aryl hydrocarbon receptor), TLR4 (toll-like receptor 4), I3C (indole-3-carbinol), LPS (lipopolysaccharide), and Cyp1a1 (cytochrome P450 1A1). Necrotizing enterocolitis represents a critical unmet medical need in neonatal intensive care, with mortality rates remaining stubbornly high despite advances in supportive care. The current dogma regarding NEC pathogenesis emphasizes postnatal factors including formula feeding, bacterial colonization, and exaggerated TLR4 signaling in the premature intestine. However, this paradigm fails to account for clinical observations suggesting in utero contributions to disease susceptibility. The presently disclosed subject matter focuses on AHR signaling as a critical regulatory pathway that can be targeted for NEC prevention and treatment. AHR activation in the newborn gut, whether achieved through maternal diet during pregnancy or direct administration to neonates, reduces TLR4 signaling and expression, thereby attenuating the inflammatory cascade that leads to intestinal necrosis. Administration of the natural AHR ligand I3C during pregnancy induces Cyp1a1 expression in both maternal and fetal intestines, confirming successful AHR activation across the placental barrier. This prenatal intervention significantly reduces NEC severity in offspring, as evidenced by improved histological scores, reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression (IL6, TNF-α), and decreased intestinal epithelial apoptosis. The presence of AHR ligands in maternal fluids including amniotic fluid, breast milk, and serum provides a natural mechanism for maternal-fetal signaling that protects against NEC. Importantly, AHR expression is significantly reduced in the intestines of human infants, mice, and piglets with NEC compared to controls, suggesting that impaired AHR signaling is a universal feature of the disease. Genetic deletion studies confirm that AHR signaling specifically in the intestinal epithelium, rather than in myeloid cells, is required for protection against NEC. Administration of I3C to neonatal mice reduces NEC severity in wild-type but not Ahr−/− mice, demonstrating the specificity of this protective effect. The discovery of the synthetic AHR ligand A18 provides a clinically relevant compound that activates AHR and reduces TLR4 signaling in human intestinal tissue ex vivo, offering promise for translation to human therapy. A18 belongs to the benzimidazole class of compounds and demonstrates potent AHR agonist activity in both cellular and animal models of NEC.

### Example 2

Necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality in premature infants, driven by excessive TLR4 signaling in the immature intestinal epithelium. The motivation for AHR activation stems from the discovery that this pathway can specifically attenuate TLR4 signaling and expression. The initial AHR agonist identified, designated A18, demonstrated potent activity but required optimization for clinical use. Structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies led to the identification of compound 169, which showed improved potency and pharmacokinetic properties. Representative compounds were synthesized following general procedures involving condensation reactions between appropriately substituted diamines and carbonyl compounds, followed by functionalization of the resulting heterocyclic cores. The synthesis began with preparation of key intermediates including 5-fluoro-1,2-diaminobenzene, which was converted to 5-fluoro-2-thiobenzimidazole through cyclization with carbon disulfide. Similarly, 5-methyl-2-thiobenzimidazole and 5-cyano-2-thiobenzimidazole were prepared from their corresponding diamines. Pyridine N-oxide intermediates were prepared from 2,3-dimethylpyridine through oxidation with meta-chloroperbenzoic acid, followed by electrophilic substitution to introduce various alkoxy, aryloxy, and benzyloxy groups at the 4-position. Reduction of the N-oxide and functionalization provided key pyridine intermediates for coupling with benzimidazole derivatives. The final compounds were prepared by alkylation of benzimidazole thiones or alcohols with appropriately substituted pyridyl halides or mesylates. Products were extracted using standard organic solvent systems, purified by column chromatography or recrystallization, and characterized by NMR spectroscopy, high-resolution mass spectrometry (HRMS), and infrared (IR) spectroscopy. Spectral data were consistent with assigned structures and comparable to literature values where available. Additional quinazoline derivatives were prepared through condensation of anthranilic acid derivatives with formamide or urea, followed by functionalization at various positions. All compounds were tested for AHR agonist activity using luciferase reporter assays and induction of Cyp1a1 expression in intestinal epithelial cells.

### Example 3

AHR activation represents a promising therapeutic strategy for inflammatory disorders, particularly necrotizing enterocolitis. The search for potent AHR agonists led to the identification of compound A18, which demonstrated significant protection against NEC in mouse models when administered either prenatally or postnatally. Administration of A18 to pregnant mice induced Cyp1a1 expression in offspring intestines and significantly reduced NEC severity, as measured by histological scoring and pro-inflammatory cytokine expression. Synthesis and testing of A18 analogues through systematic SAR studies identified compounds #169, #243, and #247 as having improved potency and selectivity. In vivo testing in mouse NEC models confirmed that these analogues maintained protective activity while demonstrating improved pharmacokinetic profiles. Additional analogues were synthesized and tested, with compound #263 showing particularly promising results in reducing TLR4 expression and NF-κB activation in intestinal epithelial cells. Mouse experiments followed standardized protocols involving induction of NEC through formula feeding, bacterial supplementation, and hypoxia exposure. RNA isolation from intestinal tissues was performed using commercial kits, followed by quantitative real-time PCR analysis of target genes including Cyp1a1, Il6, Tnf-α, and Tlr4, with normalization to housekeeping genes. These studies confirmed that the protective effects of AHR agonists correlate with reduced TLR4 signaling and expression in the intestinal epithelium, providing a clear mechanistic basis for their therapeutic activity.
