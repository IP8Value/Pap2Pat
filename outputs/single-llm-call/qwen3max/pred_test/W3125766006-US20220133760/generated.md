## BACKGROUND

Nucleosomes constitute the fundamental repeating unit of eukaryotic chromatin and are composed of an octamer of core histone proteins—two copies each of histones H2A, H2B, H3, and H4—around which approximately 147 base pairs of DNA are wrapped in a left-handed superhelix. This structural organization serves to compact genomic DNA while simultaneously regulating access to genetic information during essential nuclear processes such as transcription, replication, and repair. Perturbations in nucleosome architecture can profoundly influence genome stability and gene expression programs, thereby contributing to disease states including cancer.

Recent advances in cancer genomics have revealed that single-allele mutations in genes encoding canonical histones can drive oncogenesis. These so-called “oncohistones” are typically heterozygous missense substitutions that occur within highly conserved regions of the histone fold domain (HFD), which mediates critical inter-histone and histone-DNA interactions necessary for nucleosome integrity. Notably, oncohistone mutations do not act as solitary oncogenic drivers but rather function synergistically with other genetic lesions to promote neoplastic transformation by altering chromatin landscapes in ways that facilitate aberrant transcriptional programs.

Among the most frequently mutated residues in canonical H2A are arginine 29 (R29) and glutamic acid 121 (E121). Substitutions at R29, such as R29Q or R29F, disrupt direct contacts between H2A and the DNA minor groove, thereby reducing nucleosome stability. Similarly, mutations at E121, located within the acidic patch of H2A that mediates internucleosomal interactions, impair higher-order chromatin folding. These destabilizing effects collectively enhance chromatin accessibility, which may potentiate the activity of coexisting oncogenes by facilitating inappropriate transcription factor binding or RNA polymerase II elongation.

In parallel to these findings, a distinct class of histone variants known as short histone H2A variants (sH2As) has been identified in mammals. Unlike canonical histones, sH2As are expressed predominantly in the testis during spermatogenesis and exhibit rapid evolutionary divergence. The best-characterized member of this family, H2A.B (also referred to as H2A.Bbd), possesses a truncated C-terminus, a divergent HFD, and a mutated acidic patch—all features that compromise nucleosome stability. In vitro and in vivo studies have demonstrated that H2A.B-containing nucleosomes wrap only ~120 bp of DNA, significantly less than the canonical 147 bp, and display increased dynamics that correlate with enhanced transcriptional activity and altered RNA splicing.

Given the structural and functional parallels between oncohistone mutations in canonical H2A and the inherent properties of sH2As, it is plausible that ectopic expression of sH2As in non-testicular tissues could recapitulate oncohistone-like phenotypes. Indeed, emerging evidence suggests that H2A.B is aberrantly reactivated in several malignancies, including Hodgkin’s lymphoma and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), where its expression correlates with accelerated cell proliferation and sensitivity to DNA-damaging agents. These observations raise the possibility that sH2As represent a novel class of endogenous oncohistones whose dysregulated expression contributes to oncogenesis through mechanisms involving nucleosome destabilization, transcriptional dysregulation, and splicing dysfunction.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides methods for treating lymphoma in a subject by detecting the expression of short histone H2A (sH2A) variants, particularly H2A.B, in a biological sample from the subject and administering an anthracycline agent to the subject upon confirmation of sH2A expression. In one aspect, the invention relates to a method for treating lymphoma comprising: (a) obtaining a biological sample from a subject having or suspected of having lymphoma; (b) determining whether the sample exhibits elevated expression of an sH2A variant relative to a control; and (c) administering an anthracycline agent to the subject if elevated sH2A expression is detected.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for inducing cytotoxicity in lymphoma cells, the method comprising administering an anthracycline agent to a subject whose lymphoma cells express an sH2A variant. The anthracycline agent may be selected from the group consisting of doxorubicin, daunorubicin, epirubicin, idarubicin, aclarubicin, and analogs thereof. The administration of the anthracycline agent results in selective cytotoxicity toward sH2A-expressing lymphoma cells, thereby inhibiting tumor growth and promoting therapeutic response.

Further, the invention encompasses a method for inhibiting cancer cell proliferation in a subject diagnosed with or suspected of having lymphoma, the method comprising detecting the presence or level of sH2A expression in a biological sample from the subject and administering a therapeutically effective dose of an anthracycline agent to the subject when sH2A expression is above a predetermined threshold. The detection step may be performed using molecular techniques such as quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR), RNA sequencing, immunohistochemistry, or Western blotting, and the biological sample may include blood, serum, plasma, tumor tissue, or biopsy specimens.

These methods exploit the discovery that sH2A-expressing lymphomas exhibit heightened sensitivity to anthracycline-induced DNA damage, likely due to pre-existing chromatin instability conferred by sH2A incorporation into nucleosomes. By stratifying patients based on sH2A expression status, the invention enables precision therapeutic intervention with anthracyclines, thereby improving clinical outcomes while minimizing unnecessary exposure in non-responsive individuals.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Short histone H2A variants (sH2As) are a family of evolutionarily divergent histone proteins encoded by X-linked genes in humans, including H2AFB1 (H2A.B.2), H2AFB2 (H2A.B.1.1), H2AFB3 (H2A.B.1.2), H2A.P (HYPM), and H2A.Q. Unlike canonical H2A, sH2As possess truncated C-terminal tails, altered histone fold domains, and disrupted acidic patches, all of which contribute to reduced nucleosome stability. These structural features enable sH2As to form loosely wrapped nucleosomes that facilitate rapid transcriptional activation and RNA polymerase II elongation, functions that are physiologically relevant during spermatogenesis but potentially oncogenic when misexpressed in somatic tissues.

Aberrant upregulation of sH2As, particularly H2A.B paralogs, has been observed across a broad spectrum of human cancers, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL), uterine corpus endometrial carcinoma (UCEC), bladder urothelial carcinoma (BLCA), and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL). Transcriptomic analyses of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) datasets reveal that H2A.B expression occurs in up to 50% of DLBCL cases and is virtually absent in normal non-testicular tissues, indicating tumor-specific reactivation. This ectopic expression is not attributable to global X-chromosome dysregulation or gene amplification but rather reflects locus-specific transcriptional activation of individual H2AFB paralogs.

Based on these findings, the present invention posits that sH2As constitute a novel class of endogenous oncohistones whose expression confers a unique vulnerability to DNA-damaging chemotherapeutics, particularly anthracyclines. The method for treating lymphoma according to the invention involves first confirming the presence of sH2A expression in a subject diagnosed with or suspected of having lymphoma. “Lymphoma” as used herein refers to a heterogeneous group of hematologic malignancies originating from lymphoid lineage cells, including but not limited to Hodgkin’s lymphoma, non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma, DLBCL, follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, Burkitt lymphoma, and adult T-cell leukemia/lymphoma. The method is particularly applicable to Hodgkin’s lymphoma and DLBCL, wherein H2A.B expression is most prevalent.

Preferred embodiments include treating adult or pediatric forms of lymphoma, with particular emphasis on aggressive subtypes such as activated B-cell-like (ABC) and germinal center B-cell-like (GCB) DLBCL. The method comprises collecting a biological sample—such as peripheral blood, bone marrow aspirate, lymph node biopsy, or formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tumor tissue—from the subject. The sample is then analyzed for sH2A expression, specifically H2A.B, using any suitable molecular technique. Quantitative thresholds for positivity may be defined as >1.5 transcripts per million (TPM) by RNA-seq, or equivalent signal intensity by qRT-PCR or immunodetection.

Detection methods include RNA-based assays (e.g., RNA-seq, NanoString, RT-qPCR using primers specific to H2AFB1, H2AFB2, or H2AFB3), protein-based assays (e.g., immunohistochemistry with anti-H2A.B antibodies, Western blotting, or mass spectrometry), or hybrid approaches. A positive result triggers administration of an anthracycline agent. “Anthracycline agent” refers to a class of chemotherapeutic compounds derived from Streptomyces bacteria that intercalate into DNA, inhibit topoisomerase II, and generate reactive oxygen species, leading to double-strand DNA breaks and apoptosis. Exemplary anthracyclines include doxorubicin, daunorubicin, epirubicin, idarubicin, and aclarubicin, as well as pharmaceutically acceptable salts, esters, or analogs thereof.

Aclarubicin, in particular, has demonstrated superior cytotoxicity against H2A.B-expressing lymphoma cell lines compared to conventional agents like etoposide or doxorubicin, suggesting enhanced efficacy in sH2A-positive contexts. The therapeutically effective dose may range from 20–100 mg/m² administered intravenously, with dosing schedules tailored to standard regimens (e.g., every 3 weeks) or adjusted based on patient tolerance and disease burden. Routes of administration include intravenous, intra-arterial, or intratumoral delivery, though systemic intravenous infusion is preferred for disseminated lymphomas.

The method further induces selective cytotoxicity in sH2A-expressing lymphoma cells by exploiting their inherent chromatin fragility. Nucleosomes containing H2A.B are less stable and more permissive to DNA damage, rendering these cells hypersensitive to anthracycline-induced genotoxic stress. This synthetic lethal interaction provides a mechanistic basis for targeted therapy, improving tumor response while sparing normal tissues that lack sH2A expression.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the discovery and validation of sH2A as a biomarker and therapeutic target in lymphoma. Comparative sequence analysis revealed that wild-type sH2A variants, including H2A.B.1.1 (H2AFB2), H2A.B.1.2 (H2AFB3), and H2A.B.2 (H2AFB1), naturally harbor amino acid substitutions at positions corresponding to recurrent oncohistone mutations in canonical H2A, such as R29Q/F and truncation at E121. These changes are conserved across primates, underscoring their functional importance in nucleosome destabilization.

Transcriptomic interrogation of TCGA data showed H2A.B reactivation in diverse cancers, with the highest frequency (50%) in DLBCL. Analysis of B-ALL datasets confirmed H2A.B expression in 6–13% of cases. CCLE screening identified H2A.B-positive lymphoma cell lines, including HL (70%) and non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (25%). Expression was locus-specific, without evidence of X-chromosome-wide dysregulation.

H2A.B-positive tumors exhibited distinct splicing signatures, including reduced alternative cassette exon inclusion and proximal polyadenylation site usage, consistent with H2A.B’s role in RNA binding and splicing modulation. In B-ALL, retained intron levels were significantly decreased. Despite shared splicing alterations, gene expression profiles varied by cancer type, though Cancer-Testis Antigens (CTAs) were commonly upregulated in HNSC, LUSC, UCEC, and B-ALL.

Immune infiltrate analysis revealed no consistent enrichment in H2A.B-positive tumors, supporting tumor-intrinsic expression. CRISPR and shRNA screens demonstrated that H2A.B loss impairs proliferation in DLBCL, HL, and ALCL cell lines. Notably, aclarubicin induced potent cytotoxicity in H2A.B-expressing cells, exceeding the effects of doxorubicin or etoposide, with minimal off-target toxicity.

Collectively, these data establish sH2As as endogenous oncohistones whose expression defines a therapeutically actionable subset of lymphomas amenable to anthracycline-based therapy.
