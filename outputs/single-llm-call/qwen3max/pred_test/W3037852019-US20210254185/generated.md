# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for detecting undifferentiated cells, particularly residual induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), in populations of differentiated cells derived from human pluripotent stem cells. More specifically, the invention provides a highly sensitive and robust approach for identifying, quantifying, and excluding undifferentiated iPSCs that may persist as contaminants in differentiated cell products intended for therapeutic use. This method is applicable across all three embryonic germ layers—endoderm, mesoderm, and ectoderm—and enables enhanced safety assurance in regenerative medicine applications by ensuring the absence of tumorigenic undifferentiated cells prior to transplantation or clinical deployment.

## BACKGROUND ART

Conventional methods for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell populations have relied on a variety of strategies, including morphological observation, surface marker-based cell sorting, reporter gene systems, and expression analysis of known pluripotency-associated genes such as OCT4, SOX2, NANOG, and LIN28A. Some approaches involve culturing differentiated cells under conditions permissive for iPSC maintenance to allow residual undifferentiated cells to form visible colonies, which are then manually counted or immunostained for pluripotency markers. Other techniques utilize flow cytometry with antibodies against cell surface antigens like TRA-1-60 or SSEA-4, or employ quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) to measure transcript levels of pluripotency genes. Additionally, functional assays such as teratoma formation in immunodeficient mice have been used as a gold standard for assessing pluripotency, though this method is time-consuming, ethically complex, and unsuitable for routine quality control.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

### Problem for Solution by the Invention

A critical challenge in the clinical translation of iPSC-derived cell therapies is the risk posed by residual undifferentiated iPSCs, which retain the capacity to form teratomas upon transplantation. Existing detection methods suffer from significant limitations: many pluripotency markers (e.g., LIN28A, SOX2, OCT4) are either expressed in certain differentiated lineages or lack sufficient sensitivity to detect very low levels (<0.01%) of contamination. Furthermore, most methods are lineage-specific and cannot be universally applied across endodermal, mesodermal, and ectodermal derivatives. There is thus an unmet need for a rapid, sensitive, specific, and broadly applicable method to detect and quantify residual undifferentiated iPSCs in any differentiated cell product.

### Means to Solve the Problem

The inventors identified a set of novel marker genes—ESRG (Embryonic Stem Cell Related), SFRP2 (Secreted Frizzled Related Protein 2), VSNL1 (Visinin Like 1), THY1 (Thy-1 Cell Surface Antigen), SPP1 (Secreted Phosphoprotein 1), USP44 (Ubiquitin Specific Peptidase 44), and CNMD (Chondromodulin, also known as LECT1)—that exhibit high and specific expression in undifferentiated iPSCs but are rapidly and robustly downregulated upon differentiation into any of the three germ layers. An ideal marker gene for this purpose must satisfy three criteria: (1) exclusive or highly enriched expression in undifferentiated iPSCs relative to differentiated progeny, (2) sufficiently high absolute expression in iPSCs to enable detection at very low contamination levels, and (3) minimal or absent expression in target differentiated cells to avoid false positives. Among the identified genes, ESRG and CNMD emerged as superior markers due to their exceptional specificity, high dynamic range, and consistent performance across diverse differentiation protocols and germ layer lineages.

The present invention encompasses a method for detecting undifferentiated cells in a population of differentiated cells, comprising measuring the expression level of at least one of the aforementioned marker genes in the cell population and correlating elevated expression with the presence of residual undifferentiated iPSCs. The invention further includes a method for selecting a highly safe undifferentiated cell clone by screening iPSC lines for stable downregulation of these markers upon differentiation, thereby identifying clones with inherently lower risk of residual contamination. Additionally, the invention provides a method for detecting undifferentiated cells within tissue samples, such as organoids or transplanted grafts, using in situ hybridization or immunohistochemistry targeting the encoded proteins or transcripts. The marker genes disclosed herein are explicitly claimed for use as undifferentiation markers in quality control assays for cell therapy products. A kit for detecting undifferentiated cells is also provided, comprising reagents for measuring the expression of ESRG, CNMD, SFRP2, VSNL1, THY1, SPP1, USP44, or combinations thereof. Such reagents include nucleic acid probes, primers for PCR, antibodies against the encoded proteins, reporter constructs, and labeled detection molecules.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention achieves a significant technical advance by providing a universal, highly sensitive, and robust method for detecting residual undifferentiated iPSCs in differentiated cell products derived from any of the three embryonic germ layers. Unlike conventional markers such as LIN28A—which exhibits lineage-dependent expression and poor correlation with actual residual cell numbers—the disclosed markers, particularly ESRG and CNMD, demonstrate detection limits as low as 0.005% and strong quantitative correlation with colony-forming residual cells. This enables reliable safety validation of iPSC-derived therapeutics, reduces the risk of teratoma formation, and facilitates regulatory compliance. The invention also streamlines quality control workflows by offering compatibility with standard laboratory techniques including qPCR, droplet digital PCR, single-molecule fluorescence in situ hybridization (smFISH), flow cytometry, and immunostaining.

## BEST MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The method of detecting undifferentiated cells according to the invention is carried out by assessing the expression of one or more of the specified marker genes in a sample comprising differentiated cells. The undifferentiated cell targets are primarily human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) or embryonic stem cells (ESCs) that may persist as contaminants. A differentiated cell population is defined as a culture or tissue in which the majority of cells have undergone commitment to a specific lineage and no longer exhibit pluripotency. Endodermal differentiated cells include hepatocytes, pancreatic beta cells, and intestinal epithelial cells. Mesodermal differentiated cells encompass cardiomyocytes, endothelial cells, mesenchymal stromal cells, chondrocytes, and osteoblasts. Ectodermal differentiated cells include neural stem cells, neurons, glial cells, retinal pigment epithelial cells, and keratinocytes.

For example, residual iPSCs in hepatic endoderm cells can be detected by measuring ESRG expression via qPCR; a signal significantly above background indicates contamination. Similarly, in mesenchymal cells derived from iPSCs, downregulation of CNMD confirms successful differentiation, whereas persistent expression suggests residual undifferentiated cells. In vascular endothelial cells, SFRP2 serves as a reliable marker, while in neural stem cells, ESRG and CNMD—but not SFRP2—are effective. The same applies to mature neural cells and neural crest derivatives. Measurement of marker gene expression is performed using standard molecular biology techniques. Methods for measuring gene expression include quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR), droplet digital PCR (ddPCR), RNA sequencing, microarray analysis, Northern blotting, and single-molecule FISH (smFISH).

Detection of undifferentiated cells is achieved by comparing the measured expression level to a predetermined threshold established using negative controls (pure differentiated populations) and positive controls (spike-in samples with known percentages of iPSCs). Selection of highly safe undifferentiated cell clones involves differentiating multiple iPSC lines and identifying those that exhibit the most complete and rapid downregulation of the marker genes, indicating a lower propensity to retain residual undifferentiated cells. Measurement of marker gene expression in differentiated cells follows the same protocols, with emphasis on normalization to housekeeping genes such as 18S rRNA.

Detection of undifferentiated cells in tissue—such as 3D organoids or biopsy samples—is performed using smFISH or immunohistochemistry with antibodies against ESRG, CNMD, or other marker proteins. The use of these genes as undifferentiation markers is central to the invention’s utility in quality assurance. A kit for detecting undifferentiated cells comprises reagents for gene expression measurement, including sequence-specific primers and probes for ESRG, CNMD, SFRP2, VSNL1, THY1, SPP1, and USP44. Reagents for measuring promoter activity include reporter vectors in which the promoter of a marker gene drives expression of a detectable protein such as GFP, luciferase, or β-galactosidase.

Reagents may be labeled with fluorophores, enzymes, biotin, or radioisotopes for detection. Detection of target molecules using primary and secondary antibodies follows standard immunoassay protocols. Reporter proteins include green fluorescent protein (GFP), red fluorescent protein (RFP), luciferase, and secreted alkaline phosphatase (SEAP). Vectors for measuring promoter activity are plasmid or viral constructs containing the promoter region of a marker gene operably linked to a reporter gene. Additional kit components may include lysis buffers, reverse transcription enzymes, PCR master mixes, hybridization buffers, wash solutions, and blocking agents. Written instructions detail sample preparation, assay execution, data interpretation, and safety precautions. Control samples include RNA from pure iPSCs, fully differentiated cells, and spike-in mixtures. Instruments included may comprise microcentrifuges, thermal cyclers, fluorescence microscopes, or flow cytometers. The best modes thus provide comprehensive, reproducible, and scalable methods for ensuring the safety of iPSC-derived products.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the practice of the invention without limiting its scope. Materials and methods were as described in the research paper. iPSC lines (TkDA3-4, 1231A3, etc.) were maintained on laminin-511 and differentiated using established protocols. Mesodermal differentiation yielded endothelial and mesenchymal cells; ectodermal differentiation produced neural stem and neural crest cells. Residual undifferentiated cells were quantified by the re-seeding method: differentiated cells were replated in iPSC medium, cultured for 7 days, and SOX2+ colonies counted. Colony counting was performed manually on whole-well fluorescence images. Hepatocyte differentiation followed a four-stage protocol from definitive endoderm to mature hepatocytes. Immunostaining used anti-SOX2 and anti-TRA-1-60 antibodies with fluorescent secondaries. Microscopy employed a KEYENCE BZ-X710 system. qPCR used RNeasy RNA extraction and Universal ProbeLibrary assays. Spike-in experiments mixed iPSCs into hepatic endoderm at 0.0025%–5%. Statistics used Student’s t-test (p<0.05 significant).

Results showed LIN28A is unsuitable for hepatic differentiation due to endogenous expression in hepatic progenitors. Single-cell RNA-seq identified 12 candidate genes; qPCR confirmed ESRG, SFRP2, CNMD, SOX2, THY1, USP44, VSNL1, and SPP1 as highly iPSC-specific. These were validated across multiple iPSC lines. ddPCR estimated RNA copies per cell. Correlation analysis revealed ESRG, CNMD, and SFRP2 strongly correlated (r≥0.9) with actual and spiked-in residual iPSC numbers, unlike OCT4 or LIN28A. Detection limits were 0.005% for ESRG, 0.025% for CNMD/SFRP2—far superior to SOX2 (1%) or NANOG (5%). Testing in mesodermal (endothelial, mesenchymal) and ectodermal (neural) lineages confirmed ESRG and CNMD are universally applicable. STEMdiff kit-derived trilineage cells further validated robustness. smFISH enabled flow cytometric detection of ESRG+ cells.

Example 2 incorporated a reporter gene (e.g., GFP) under the ESRG promoter into iPSCs, enabling live monitoring of undifferentiated cells. Example 3 screened iPSC clones for those exhibiting fastest marker downregulation upon differentiation, selecting “highly safe” clones with minimal residual risk. Example 4 demonstrated application in 3D liver bud organoids, confirming safety of all-iPSC-derived tissues.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The invention has broad industrial applicability in the field of regenerative medicine and cellular therapy. It enables pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies to implement rigorous quality control for iPSC-derived cell products—such as hepatocytes for liver disease, cardiomyocytes for heart failure, dopaminergic neurons for Parkinson’s disease, and pancreatic islets for diabetes—by reliably detecting and excluding tumorigenic undifferentiated contaminants. The method supports compliance with regulatory guidelines (e.g., FDA, EMA) requiring demonstration of product purity and safety. Diagnostic laboratories can adopt the kits for batch release testing. The invention also facilitates the development of safer iPSC banks and accelerates clinical translation of stem cell therapies worldwide.
