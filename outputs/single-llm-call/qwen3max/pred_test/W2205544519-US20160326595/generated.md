# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Prostate cancer (CaP) represents one of the most prevalent malignancies among men in the United States and globally, with an estimated 248,530 new cases and 34,130 deaths projected in 2021 alone. Despite advances in early detection and treatment, significant disparities persist in both incidence and mortality rates across racial and ethnic groups. Notably, men of African ancestry experience a disproportionately higher burden of disease, exhibiting approximately 1.7 times the incidence rate and more than twice the mortality rate compared to men of European descent. These disparities are not solely attributable to differences in access to healthcare or socioeconomic status but are increasingly recognized to reflect underlying biological and genetic distinctions in tumor biology between populations. While socioeconomic factors—including limited access to screening, delayed diagnosis, and reduced availability of high-quality treatment—contribute meaningfully to outcome disparities, emerging genomic evidence suggests that intrinsic molecular differences in prostate tumors may also play a critical role. Specifically, tumors arising in men of African descent often display distinct mutational landscapes, structural variations, and gene expression profiles that diverge from those commonly observed in Caucasian patients. For example, the well-characterized TMPRSS2-ERG gene fusion, which occurs in nearly half of all prostate cancers in men of European ancestry, is significantly less frequent in African American men. Similarly, deletions or mutations in key tumor suppressor genes such as PTEN appear to be underrepresented in African American prostate cancers at early stages, suggesting divergent oncogenic pathways. These observations underscore the inadequacy of applying diagnostic and therapeutic paradigms developed primarily from studies of Caucasian populations to all patient groups. Historically, large-scale genomic studies such as The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) have predominantly enrolled patients of European descent, thereby limiting the generalizability of findings to racially diverse populations. Consequently, there exists an urgent need to identify population-specific biomarkers that can improve risk stratification, inform prognosis, and guide personalized therapeutic decisions for underrepresented groups, particularly men of African ancestry who face the highest risk of aggressive disease and poor outcomes. The identification of novel genomic alterations that are enriched in specific ancestral backgrounds not only enhances our understanding of prostate cancer heterogeneity but also opens avenues for the development of targeted diagnostics and therapeutics tailored to the unique molecular features of each patient subgroup.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses a novel genomic rearrangement involving the ZBTB20 and LSAMP genes located within chromosome region 3q13.31, which has been identified as a recurrent and clinically significant alteration specifically enriched in prostate cancers arising in men of African descent. This rearrangement, herein referred to as the ZBTB20/LSAMP genomic rearrangement, manifests as deletions, duplications, or other structural variants that disrupt the normal architecture of the 3q13 locus, leading to the formation of chimeric transcripts or loss of tumor-suppressive function associated with the LSAMP gene. The invention provides methods for diagnosing and prognosing prostate cancer by detecting the presence of this genomic rearrangement in biological samples obtained from patients. In particular, the detection of the ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement is strongly associated with aggressive forms of prostate cancer, including tumors with Gleason scores of 6–7 that exhibit biochemical recurrence or progression to metastatic disease following radical prostatectomy. The invention further encompasses compositions and kits designed to facilitate the detection of this rearrangement, including polynucleotide probes, primers, and double-stranded oligonucleotide duplexes specifically configured to hybridize to or amplify unique junction sequences formed by the rearrangement. Of particular importance are sequences derived from exon 3* and exon 0* of the LSAMP locus, which represent alternative transcriptional start sites or splice variants uniquely generated as a consequence of the rearrangement and absent in normal tissue or non-rearranged cancers. Additionally, the invention addresses the differential prevalence of PTEN deletion—a well-established driver alteration in prostate cancer—between ethnic groups, revealing its virtual absence in early-stage tumors from men of African descent. This finding has critical implications for treatment selection, as it suggests that therapies targeting the PI3K/PTEN/Akt/mTOR signaling pathway may be ineffective or inappropriate for this patient population. Accordingly, the invention includes methods for selecting targeted prostate cancer treatments based on the combined assessment of ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement status and PTEN deletion status. Alternative embodiments include isolated antibodies that specifically bind to chimeric ZBTB20/LSAMP fusion proteins or truncated LSAMP polypeptides resulting from the rearrangement, as well as pharmaceutical compositions comprising such antibodies for therapeutic use. The invention thus provides a comprehensive platform for ethnically informed precision oncology in prostate cancer, enabling improved diagnosis, risk stratification, and treatment personalization for men of African ancestry.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### DEFINITIONS

As used herein, the term “of African descent” refers to individuals whose genetic ancestry is predominantly derived from populations indigenous to Sub-Saharan Africa, as confirmed by self-reporting, genealogical records, or objective genomic analyses such as principal component analysis using reference panels like YRI (Yoruba in Ibadan, Nigeria) or ASW (African Ancestry in Southwest USA). Conversely, “of Caucasian descent” denotes individuals with predominant genetic ancestry from European populations, typically aligning with CEU (Utah residents with Northern and Western European ancestry) in genomic clustering. An “antibody” is a glycoprotein produced by B cells that specifically binds to an antigen via its antigen-binding domain, which may be a full-length immunoglobulin or an engineered fragment thereof. The “antigen-binding domain” comprises the variable regions of the heavy and light chains responsible for epitope recognition, while an “antigen-binding fragment” includes Fab, Fab′, F(ab′)₂, scFv, or other derivatives retaining binding specificity. “Detecting” or “detection” means identifying the presence, absence, or quantity of a target molecule (e.g., nucleic acid, protein) in a sample using analytical methods such as PCR, sequencing, hybridization, or immunoassay. A “therapeutically effective amount” is the dose of a compound sufficient to elicit a desired clinical response without undue toxicity. A “gene expression profile” refers to the pattern of mRNA or protein levels for a set of genes in a given cell or tissue. “Isolated” indicates that a molecule has been separated from its natural environment and is at least partially purified. The terms “polypeptide,” “peptide,” and “protein” are used interchangeably to denote chains of amino acids linked by peptide bonds, regardless of length or post-translational modification. A “polypeptide probe” is an isolated polypeptide used to detect binding partners, while a “primer” is a short oligonucleotide that initiates DNA synthesis in amplification reactions. A “variation” or “variant” encompasses any deviation from a reference sequence, including SNPs, indels, or structural changes. The “genomic rearrangement of the ZBTB20 and LSAMP genes” refers to somatic structural alterations in chromosome 3q13.31 that juxtapose sequences from ZBTB20 and LSAMP, resulting in fusion genes, chimeric transcripts, or allelic loss. The “ERG gene” (ETS-related gene) encodes a transcription factor frequently activated by fusion with TMPRSS2 in prostate cancer. An “aggressive form of prostate cancer” is defined by clinical features such as Gleason score ≥7, pathological stage ≥pT3, biochemical recurrence, or metastasis. “Gleason 6–7” refers to tumors graded using the Gleason scoring system, where primary pattern 3 with secondary pattern 3 (score 6) or 4 (score 7) indicates intermediate-risk disease. The ZBTB20/LSAMP genomic rearrangement was discovered through whole genome sequencing of matched tumor-normal pairs from African American and Caucasian American prostate cancer patients, revealing recurrent deletions or duplications at 3q13.31 exclusively in African American tumors. Next-generation sequencing techniques, including Illumina paired-end sequencing with ≥30× coverage, generated high-quality genome data aligned to GRCh37/hg19. Comparative cohort analysis identified this rearrangement as significantly associated with African ancestry and aggressive clinical behavior. The LSAMP gene (Limbic System-Associated Membrane Protein), located at 3q13.31, encodes a neuronal adhesion molecule implicated as a tumor suppressor in multiple cancers; its inactivation via deletion is a hallmark of poor prognosis in African American prostate cancer. The ZBTB20 gene (Zinc Finger and BTB Domain Containing 20), adjacent to LSAMP, encodes a transcriptional repressor with potential oncogenic roles when dysregulated. Methods for diagnosing or prognosing CaP involve collecting biological samples (e.g., prostate tissue, blood, urine, exosomes) and detecting the ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement via nucleic acid or protein assays. Detection may include assessing chromosomal deletion at 3q13 by FISH, identifying chimeric mRNA/cDNA by RT-PCR or RNA-Seq, or measuring expression of exon 3* or exon 0*—novel LSAMP exonic sequences generated by alternative splicing or promoter usage due to the rearrangement. Concurrently, PTEN (Phosphatase and Tensin Homolog), located at 10q23.31 (NCBI Accession NM_000314.8 for mRNA; NP_000305.3 for protein), is a critical tumor suppressor frequently deleted in Caucasian prostate cancer but rarely in African American cases. Whole genome sequencing of CD (Caucasian descent) and AD (African descent) samples confirmed this disparity, with FISH validation showing PTEN deletion in 63% of CD vs. 15% of AD tumors. This finding mandates exclusion of PI3K/PTEN/Akt/mTOR pathway inhibitors (e.g., ipatasertib, capivasertib, everolimus) for AD patients lacking PTEN loss. Diagnostic methods combine detection of ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement and PTEN status to guide therapy. Nucleic acid detection employs hybridization probes under stringent conditions (e.g., 65°C in 0.1× SSC for high stringency; 50°C in 2× SSC for moderate stringency) or PCR using primers spanning fusion junctions. Amplification products confirm rearrangement presence. Immunoassays (sandwich, competitive, non-competitive) or antibody arrays detect fusion proteins using synthetic or monoclonal antibodies. Mass spectrometry, including selected reaction monitoring, quantifies fusion peptides. Computer-implemented models integrate molecular and clinical data to output user-readable prostate cancer determinations. Kits contain polynucleotide probes for 3q13 deletion, LSAMP exons 3*/0*, ZBTB20/LSAMP fusion, and control genes (e.g., ERG, PCA3, PSA), along with labeled antibodies, instructional materials, and reagents for amplification. Double-stranded oligonucleotide duplexes serve as hybridization controls or standards. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising anti-fusion antibodies are administered via intravenous, subcutaneous, or localized routes, with efficacy and toxicity evaluated preclinically. All referenced patents, applications, and publications are incorporated herein by reference.

### Examples

Comparative genomic DNA analysis was performed on 14 prostate cancer specimens (7 African American, 7 Caucasian American) from radical prostatectomies, with tumors manually microdissected to >80% purity. Whole genome sequencing yielded ≥30× coverage, and somatic variants were called using Strelka, BreakDancer, and cn.MOPs. Structural variants (SVs) and copy number variations (CNVs) were scored based on read-depth and split-read evidence. Ethnic-specific associations revealed exclusive ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangements in African American tumors: two deletions (23 Mb and 1 Mb) and one duplication creating a fusion transcript. RNA-Seq and 5′-RACE validated chimeric mRNAs initiating from ZBTB20 or LSAMP promoters, with nested PCR confirming junction sequences. FISH assays using locus-specific probes (500 kb spanning ZBTB20-LSAMP; 600 kb centered on LSAMP) showed LSAMP deletion in 26% of African American vs. 7% of Caucasian tumors (p=0.007), correlating with biochemical recurrence. In TCGA data (41 AA, 279 CA), LSAMP deletion occurred in 27% vs. 13% (p=0.023). Conversely, PTEN deletion was rare in African Americans (15% by FISH; 7% in Gleason 6) versus Caucasians (63%; 53% in Gleason 6; p=0.004). Sanger sequencing validated breakpoints. Primers were designed to amplify exon 3* and exon 0*—unique LSAMP sequences absent in reference genomes—enabling specific detection of rearrangement-derived transcripts. These findings establish ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement as a novel biomarker for aggressive prostate cancer in men of African descent and redefine therapeutic eligibility based on PTEN status.
