# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of environmental biotechnology, specifically in the area of microbial bioremediation of herbicide-contaminated soils. More particularly, the invention relates to the use of a novel atrazine-degrading bacterium, *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* liulou 1 (also referred to herein as strain DnL1-1), for the remediation of soils contaminated with s-triazine herbicides such as atrazine and its derivatives. The invention further encompasses methods for isolating, identifying, maintaining, and applying this bacterium as a biological agent to enhance the degradation of atrazine in agricultural and non-agricultural environments, thereby reducing phytotoxicity and environmental persistence of this widely used herbicide.

Prior art in the field of atrazine biodegradation has largely focused on the isolation and characterization of soil bacteria capable of metabolizing atrazine through either hydrolytic or dealkylation pathways. While several bacterial strains belonging to genera such as *Pseudomonas*, *Rhodococcus*, and *Arthrobacter* have been reported to degrade atrazine, their practical application in field conditions has been limited by poor survival, inadequate colonization of plant roots, and insufficient competitiveness against indigenous soil microbiota. Traditional approaches to bioremediation often require high inoculum densities, repeated applications, and supplementation with exogenous carbon sources to sustain microbial activity—factors that significantly increase cost and complexity. Moreover, many previously described atrazine-degrading bacteria are isolated from bulk soil and lack rhizosphere competence, which restricts their ability to establish stable populations in the root zone where herbicide concentrations are often highest due to root exudation and proximity to applied agrochemicals. Additionally, existing methods for delivering degraders to contaminated sites do not leverage the natural ecological niche of the rhizosphere, resulting in inefficient colonization and suboptimal degradation kinetics. There remains a critical need for a robust, rhizosphere-competent, atrazine-degrading microorganism that can be easily introduced into agricultural systems via seed inoculation and that effectively mitigates atrazine toxicity without requiring extensive agronomic interventions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is motivated by the discovery of a novel atrazine-degrading bacterium, *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* liulou 1 (strain DnL1-1), which exhibits exceptional rhizosphere colonization capacity, motility, and efficient degradation of atrazine and its primary metabolites. This strain was isolated from the rhizosphere of maize grown in atrazine-exposed agricultural soils in Shandong Province, China, and demonstrates superior adaptability to both nutrient-limited and herbicide-stressed environments. The bacterium has been deposited at the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC) under accession number CGMCC 9667, ensuring public availability and reproducibility.

Phylogenetic identification of strain liulou 1 was confirmed through 16S rRNA gene sequencing, which revealed >99% sequence identity to the type strain of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens*, thereby establishing its taxonomic classification. The physical properties of the bacterium include rod-to-coccus morphological transitions typical of the genus *Arthrobacter*, Gram-positive cell wall structure, aerobic metabolism, and the ability to utilize atrazine as a sole nitrogen source. Critically, strain liulou 1 harbors the *trzN* and *atzBC* genes, enabling it to degrade atrazine via the hydrolytic pathway, converting it first to hydroxyatrazine (HA) and subsequently to cyanuric acid, thereby avoiding the accumulation of persistent and potentially toxic dealkylated metabolites such as desethylatrazine (DEA) and desisopropylatrazine (DIA).

The bacterium was isolated from the rhizosphere of maize using a direct plating method on selective mineral medium (SM agar) supplemented with atrazine as the sole nitrogen source. The isolation protocol involved soil sampling from the root-adhering zone, serial dilution, and colony purification based on clearing halo formation indicative of atrazine degradation. Pure cultures of strain liulou 1 are maintained on SM or SMY agar at 28°C and can be stored long-term in glycerol stocks at −80°C without loss of viability or degradative function.

The invention provides a biological agent for the remediation of atrazine-contaminated soils, comprising viable cells of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* liulou 1. This agent is applied by inoculating seeds of crop plants—including but not limited to wheat, maize, and alfalfa—with a suspension of the bacterium prior to sowing. The method ensures targeted delivery of the degrader to the rhizosphere, where it colonizes root surfaces, proliferates in response to atrazine presence, and accelerates herbicide breakdown. This approach eliminates the need for high-dose soil amendments or repeated applications, offering a cost-effective and scalable solution for sustainable agriculture and environmental protection.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS

### Isolation of Strain Liulou 1

Strain liulou 1 was isolated using SM agar, a mineral medium containing atrazine as the sole nitrogen source, which selectively supports the growth of atrazine-degrading bacteria. Soil samples were collected from the rhizosphere of maize plants grown in atrazine-treated fields in Shandong Province. The rhizosphere soil was gently shaken from roots, homogenized, and serially diluted in sterile buffer. Aliquots of the dilutions were spread onto SM agar plates and incubated at 28°C for 5–7 days. Colonies exhibiting clearing halos—indicative of atrazine hydrolysis—were selected for purification. Purification was achieved through repeated streaking on fresh SM agar until morphologically uniform colonies were obtained. The purified strain, designated DnL1-1, was characterized by its ability to grow on atrazine, form circular, smooth, cream-colored colonies, and maintain stability over multiple subcultures. Physiological tests confirmed its Gram-positive nature, aerobic respiration, and optimal growth at 28°C.

### Phylogenetic Identification of Strain Liulou 1

Genomic DNA was extracted from pure cultures of strain liulou 1, and the 16S rRNA gene was amplified using universal bacterial primers 27F and 1492R via polymerase chain reaction (PCR). The PCR protocol included an initial denaturation at 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 30 cycles of denaturation at 94°C for 30 seconds, annealing at 55°C for 30 seconds, and extension at 72°C for 90 seconds, with a final extension at 72°C for 10 minutes. The resulting amplicon (~1,500 bp) was purified and sequenced using Sanger sequencing. The nucleotide sequence was analyzed using BLASTn against the NCBI database, revealing 99.8% identity to *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* strain CGMCC 1.1897T. Phylogenetic analysis was performed using MEGA5 software with the neighbor-joining method and 1,000 bootstrap replicates. The resulting phylogenetic tree placed strain liulou 1 within the *A. ureafaciens* clade with strong bootstrap support (>95%), confirming its taxonomic assignment.

### Taxonomic and Individual Identification of Strain Liulou 1 by Repetitive Elements Sequence-Based PCR (Rep-PCR)

To distinguish strain liulou 1 from other *A. ureafaciens* isolates at the strain level, repetitive element sequence-based PCR (Rep-PCR) was employed using BOXA1R and ERIC2 primers. Genomic DNA served as the template in 25-μL reactions containing Taq polymerase, dNTPs, and appropriate buffers. The thermal cycling conditions consisted of an initial denaturation at 95°C for 2 minutes, followed by 35 cycles of 94°C for 30 seconds, 60°C for 30 seconds, and 72°C for 2 minutes, with a final extension at 72°C for 10 minutes. Amplified products were resolved on 1.5% agarose gels stained with ethidium bromide. BOX-PCR generated a unique banding pattern comprising 8–10 distinct fragments ranging from 200 to 2,000 bp, while ERIC-PCR produced a complementary profile with 6–8 bands. These fingerprint patterns were consistent across multiple isolations and differed from those of the type strain, confirming the genetic uniqueness of strain liulou 1 and enabling its reliable identification in environmental samples.

### Identification of the Genes for Atrazine Degradation in Strain Arthrobacter ureafaciens Liulou 1

Multiplex PCR was used to detect key atrazine degradation genes (*trzN*, *atzB*, *atzC*) in strain liulou 1. Primer sets specific to each gene were combined in a single reaction with genomic DNA. PCR conditions included 95°C for 5 minutes, followed by 35 cycles of 94°C for 30 seconds, 60°C for 30 seconds, and 72°C for 1 minute, ending with a 10-minute extension at 72°C. Amplicons were analyzed by gel electrophoresis, revealing bands of expected sizes: 500 bp for *trzN*, 700 bp for *atzB*, and 600 bp for *atzC*. Sequencing of these products confirmed 100% identity to known atrazine hydrolase genes. The presence of *trzN* and *atzBC* indicates that strain liulou 1 degrades atrazine via hydrolysis to HA, then to N-isopropylammelide, and finally to cyanuric acid—a non-toxic end product. This genetic configuration explains the strain’s efficient atrazine mineralization and absence of persistent dealkylated metabolites in degradation assays.

### Example 5

Degradation of atrazine by *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* liulou 1 was evaluated in liquid SM medium supplemented with 2 mg/L atrazine. Cultures were inoculated at 1:1000 dilution and incubated at 28°C without shaking. Samples taken at 24, 48, and 72 hours were analyzed by HPLC-MS/MS. Atrazine was undetectable after 48 hours, with HA peaking at 14 mol% of initial atrazine and gradually declining over 25 days. In parallel experiments, DEA and DIA were completely degraded within 3–4 days, while DAA remained unchanged, confirming the strain’s specificity for the hydrolytic pathway.

Motility was assessed in liquid and semisolid SA medium (0.2% agar). Microscopic observation revealed rapid directional movement of rod-shaped cells in liquid culture. On semisolid SA plates, colonies expanded radially, reaching plate edges in 14 days, indicating swimming motility. No migration occurred on SM agar, suggesting chemotaxis toward asparagine.

Root colonization was tested in a soil-sand tube assay. Germinated wheat seeds were inoculated with bacterial suspensions (10⁶–10⁷ CFU/seed), sown in tubes containing atrazine-spiked sand-soil mixtures, and incubated for 21 days. Roots were vortexed in buffer, and suspensions plated on SM agar. Populations reached 6.26 ± 0.41 lg CFU/g fresh root at 175 μg/tube atrazine, even with low inoculum, demonstrating atrazine-enhanced colonization.

Survival on dry seed was evaluated by inoculating dry wheat seeds, air-drying, and storing at room temperature. Viable counts after 7 days showed 4.81 ± 0.02 lg CFU/seed, confirming suitability for commercial seed treatment.

In pot experiments, inoculated wheat seeds sown in atrazine-contaminated soil (17.5 kg/ha) showed 97.9% atrazine degradation after 28 days, versus 42–46% in controls. Root-associated populations reached ~6 lg CFU/g, and no degraders were found on control roots. HPLC-MS/MS analysis showed reduced levels of DEA, DIA, and DAA, confirming preferential hydrolytic degradation.

Root growth promotion was observed in alfalfa: inoculated seeds exhibited 25% longer roots than controls after 14 days. Phosphate solubilization was confirmed on Pikovskaya agar, with clear halos indicating organic acid production.

The 16S rRNA gene sequence of strain liulou 1 (SEQ ID NO:1) is provided in the sequence listing. The strain also degraded atrazine derivatives: HA, DEA, and DIA were metabolized, but DAA was not.

In soil remediation trials, wheat seeds inoculated with liulou 1 and sown in atrazine-contaminated soil showed near-complete herbicide elimination and prevention of phytotoxicity. Extraction and HPLC-MS/MS analysis confirmed residual atrazine <0.5 μg/tube in most treatments. The data conclusively demonstrate that *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* liulou 1 is a highly effective, rhizosphere-competent bioremediation agent suitable for large-scale agricultural deployment.
