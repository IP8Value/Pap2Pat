## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to ion-selective membranes, and more particularly to nanostructured polyelectrolyte-coated nanoporous membranes fabricated via Layer-by-Layer (LbL) deposition for precise control of ionic transport properties. These membranes are engineered to exhibit tunable ionic selectivity and conductivity, making them suitable for applications including electrodialysis, water purification, energy storage in flow batteries and fuel cells, and other electrochemical separation processes requiring selective ion transport through porous media.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ion-exchange membranes are critical components in a wide array of electrochemical technologies, serving as selective barriers that permit the passage of specific ions while restricting others. Conventional ion-exchange membranes rely on fixed charged functional groups embedded within a polymeric matrix to achieve ion selectivity, typically through Donnan exclusion principles. In electrodialysis—a process widely used for desalination and chemical separations—such membranes are arranged in alternating cation- and anion-exchange configurations between electrodes to drive the migration of ions from dilute to concentrated compartments under an applied electric field. The efficiency of this process is heavily dependent on the membrane’s ability to selectively transport one ionic species over another while maintaining high ionic conductivity and mechanical stability.

The fundamental mechanism of ion transport in nanoporous systems hinges on the interplay between pore geometry, surface charge density, and electrolyte concentration. In nanopores where the Debye length is comparable to or larger than the pore radius, the electrical double layers from opposing pore walls overlap, leading to enhanced surface conduction and selective ion transport dictated by the sign and magnitude of the surface charge. This phenomenon enables fine control over ion mobility when the pore wall chemistry is tailored appropriately. However, conventional electrodialysis membranes often suffer from limitations such as insufficient selectivity at low salt concentrations, swelling-induced loss of mechanical integrity, fouling, and high manufacturing costs—particularly when high-performance perfluorinated polymers like Nafion are employed.

To overcome these challenges, recent research has focused on nanostructured polyelectrolyte architectures deposited onto inexpensive nanoporous supports. Such approaches leverage the high surface-to-volume ratio of nanopores to amplify the influence of surface charge on bulk ion transport. Prior art includes the use of responsive polymers grafted onto pore walls to modulate charge in response to pH, temperature, or light, as well as geometric modifications such as conical or asymmetric pores to induce ion rectification. Additionally, nanoporous membranes fabricated from track-etched polycarbonate, anodic aluminum oxide, or mesoporous silica have served as scaffolds for polyelectrolyte coatings. Despite these advances, existing methods often lack precise, independent control over both ionic selectivity and conductivity, and many fail to provide long-term chemical stability under operational conditions.

The importance of controlling ion transport cannot be overstated, especially in emerging applications like redox flow batteries, where crossover of active species must be minimized without sacrificing ionic conductivity, or in resource-limited settings where low-cost, scalable membrane fabrication is essential. Thus, there remains a pressing need for ion-selective membranes that combine tunable transport properties, robustness, and manufacturability—objectives directly addressed by the present invention through the strategic application of Layer-by-Layer polyelectrolyte assembly combined with targeted crosslinking chemistries.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel class of ion-selective membranes fabricated by Layer-by-Layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolytes onto nanoporous supports, followed by selective chemical crosslinking to independently tune ionic selectivity and conductivity. The LbL method enables precise control over film thickness, composition, and charge density by sequentially adsorbing oppositely charged polyelectrolytes—specifically poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) and poly(ethyleneimine) (PEI)—onto both planar and nanoporous substrates. By varying the number of bilayers and employing distinct crosslinking agents such as glutaraldehyde (GA) or N-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)-N′-ethylcarbodiimide (EDC), the net fixed charge within the polyelectrolyte film can be systematically adjusted. GA crosslinking preferentially consumes primary amine groups on PEI, thereby increasing the net negative charge and enhancing cation selectivity, whereas EDC forms amide bonds between carboxylic acid and amine groups, preserving charge neutrality and yielding moderate selectivity.

A key advantage of the invention lies in its decoupling of ionic selectivity from conductivity: thicker films (e.g., five bilayers) crosslinked with GA exhibit significantly enhanced cation transference numbers without proportionally reducing ionic conductivity, which may even recover relative to thinner or uncrosslinked counterparts due to improved structural integrity and pore filling. Furthermore, crosslinking prevents polymer dissolution or surface diffusion during operation, ensuring long-term stability. The method is scalable, cost-effective, and compatible with large-area (e.g., 64 cm²) membrane production using aqueous polymer solutions and simple dip-coating procedures. The resulting membranes demonstrate superior performance in electrodialysis-relevant metrics, offering a versatile platform for next-generation electrochemical separations and energy conversion devices.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Large-scale industrial applications such as municipal water desalination, industrial wastewater treatment, and grid-scale energy storage demand ion-selective membranes that are not only highly selective and conductive but also inexpensive and durable. The present invention meets these criteria through a bottom-up fabrication strategy based on Layer-by-Layer (LbL) deposition, a technique renowned for its simplicity, versatility, and compatibility with diverse substrates and polymers. LbL deposition involves the sequential, alternating immersion of a substrate into solutions of oppositely charged polyelectrolytes, resulting in the spontaneous formation of multilayered thin films driven by electrostatic and secondary interactions such as hydrogen bonding. This approach offers unparalleled control over film architecture at the nanoscale, enabling the engineering of interfacial properties critical for ion transport.

Polymers suitable for LbL assembly include those bearing ionizable functional groups such as amines, carboxylic acids, or sulfonates. In the preferred embodiment, poly(ethyleneimine) (PEI), a cationic polymer rich in primary, secondary, and tertiary amines, is paired with poly(acrylic acid) (PAA), an anionic polymer containing carboxylic acid moieties. These polyelectrolytes are selected not only for their strong mutual attraction but also for the chemical reactivity of their functional groups, which permits post-assembly crosslinking to enhance mechanical and chemical stability. A variety of substrates may serve as supports, including track-etched polycarbonate (PC), anodic aluminum oxide, mesoporous silica, and inverse opal structures. Notably, nanoporous PC membranes with pore diameters on the order of tens of nanometers are particularly advantageous due to their commercial availability, mechanical robustness, and ease of surface activation.

On planar surfaces, LbL film growth typically follows linear or exponential kinetics depending on polymer molecular weight, ionic strength, and pH. However, on nanoporous surfaces, film formation is more complex, involving not only surface adsorption but also infiltration into the pore lumen. Cooperative elements—functional groups that drive self-assembly through complementary interactions—are essential; in this case, protonated amines on PEI and deprotonated carboxylates on PAA form electrostatic pairs, while hydrogen bonding between neutral species further stabilizes the structure. To lock the assembled architecture in place and prevent delamination or dissolution during operation, crosslinking is introduced. Two primary strategies are employed: (1) amine-to-amine crosslinking using glutaraldehyde, which reacts selectively with primary amines to form Schiff base linkages, thereby reducing cationic charge density; and (2) carboxylic acid-amine coupling via EDC-mediated amide bond formation, which consumes equal numbers of anionic and cationic groups, preserving overall charge balance.

These crosslinking options directly influence ionic selectivity and conductivity. Membranes crosslinked with GA exhibit enhanced cation selectivity due to a net increase in negative fixed charge, while EDC-crosslinked membranes maintain near-neutral charge character. Conductivity is initially reduced upon polyelectrolyte deposition due to partial pore occlusion but can be partially restored in thicker, crosslinked films, likely due to complete pore filling and formation of continuous ion-conducting pathways. The invention thus enables independent optimization of selectivity and conductivity—two parameters traditionally in tension—through rational design of polyelectrolyte architecture and crosslinking chemistry.

### Example: LbL Deposition of Bilayer PAA and PEI Polyelectrolytes

In a representative embodiment, ion-selective membranes are fabricated by LbL deposition of PAA and PEI onto track-etched polycarbonate (PC) supports with nominal pore diameters of 50 nm. The process begins with pretreatment of the PC membrane to remove residual surfactants and activate the surface. Specifically, the membrane is etched in 4.5 M sodium hydroxide for five minutes to eliminate a polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) layer, rinsed thoroughly with deionized water, and then subjected to UV-ozone treatment for ten minutes per side to introduce hydroxyl and carboxyl functionalities that promote initial polyelectrolyte adhesion.

The LbL assembly is initiated by immersing the activated membrane in a 0.1 wt% aqueous solution of branched PEI (pH 10.4) for five minutes, followed by rinsing with deionized water to remove loosely bound polymer. The membrane is then immersed in a 0.2 wt% aqueous solution of PAA (pH 3.2) for five minutes, completing the first bilayer (BL). Subsequent bilayers are deposited using one-minute immersion times per polymer solution to accelerate processing. Membranes with one, three, or five bilayers are prepared, and subsets are crosslinked either with 25% glutaraldehyde (GA) for twelve hours or with 100 mM EDC in water for twelve hours, followed by extensive rinsing.

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) confirms successful deposition and crosslinking. Spectra of coated membranes show characteristic peaks at 1714 cm⁻¹ (C=O stretch of PAA carboxylic acid) and 1550 cm⁻¹ (N–H bend of PEI amines), which diminish upon crosslinking. In GA-crosslinked samples, the N–H peak decreases disproportionately, indicating selective consumption of amines, whereas EDC treatment reduces both peaks equally, consistent with amide bond formation. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) reveals that uncrosslinked 3BL membranes exhibit non-uniform coverage with exposed pores, while crosslinked variants—especially those with three or more bilayers—display smooth, continuous coatings. Cross-sectional SEM images confirm that polyelectrolytes infiltrate and fill nanopores, forming tubular or rod-like structures within the membrane matrix.

Electrochemical characterization demonstrates tunable ionic transport. Conductivity measurements via impedance spectroscopy show that bare PC membranes exhibit the highest conductivity, which decreases upon initial coating but stabilizes or slightly recovers in thicker, crosslinked films. Ionic selectivity, assessed by measuring transmembrane potential across NaCl concentration gradients, reveals that all polyelectrolyte-coated membranes are cation-selective. GA-crosslinked membranes display the highest cation transference numbers (e.g., t⁺ ≈ 0.62 for 5BL GA), surpassing even the bare PC membrane, due to increased net negative charge. In contrast, EDC-crosslinked and uncrosslinked 3BL membranes show similar, moderate selectivity, confirming that charge-neutral crosslinking preserves the intrinsic selectivity of the as-deposited film.

Collectively, these results validate the invention’s core principle: that LbL deposition combined with selective crosslinking provides a powerful, scalable route to engineer ion-selective membranes with independently optimized transport properties and enhanced durability for real-world applications.
