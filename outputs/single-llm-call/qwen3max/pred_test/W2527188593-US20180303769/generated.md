# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Protein interaction networks represent a fundamental framework for understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying cellular function and disease pathogenesis. Within these networks, proteins do not operate in isolation but rather engage in complex, dynamic interactions that govern signal transduction, metabolic regulation, gene expression, and cell fate decisions. In the context of infectious diseases, pathogenic agents—including bacterial toxins, plant toxins, and viruses—have evolved sophisticated strategies to hijack host protein interaction networks to facilitate their entry, replication, and dissemination while simultaneously inducing host cell death or immune evasion. These pathogenic interventions often target central nodes within the host network, referred to as “hub” proteins, which serve as critical conduits for multiple signaling pathways. The targeting of such hubs enables pathogens to exert maximal disruptive effects with minimal molecular investment, thereby enhancing their virulence and efficiency.

A key structural feature of many biological networks, including those perturbed during infection, is the so-called “small-world” property. This characteristic describes a topology wherein most nodes are not neighbors of one another, yet can be reached from any other node by a small number of steps or connections. In disease networks, this property implies that even though distinct pathogens may exploit seemingly unrelated host pathways, these pathways are often interconnected through shared hub proteins. Consequently, disruption at a single hub can propagate through the network, amplifying the pathogenic effect. This interconnectivity also presents a therapeutic opportunity: interventions directed at hub proteins may simultaneously neutralize the effects of multiple pathogens that rely on overlapping or convergent host pathways. Traditional antimicrobial strategies, which focus on direct inhibition of pathogen-specific components (e.g., bacterial enzymes or viral polymerases), are inherently narrow in scope and vulnerable to resistance development. In contrast, a host-oriented approach that targets conserved cellular machinery exploited by diverse pathogens offers the potential for broad-spectrum efficacy and reduced susceptibility to resistance.

Motivated by these principles, multiplex therapeutic approaches have emerged as a promising paradigm for combating complex and evolving biological threats. Rather than designing a unique inhibitor for each pathogen, multiplex strategies seek to identify host-directed therapeutics that interfere with common vulnerability points across multiple pathogenic pathways. Such approaches are particularly valuable in scenarios involving emerging or re-emerging pathogens, where rapid deployment of countermeasures is essential, and de novo drug development is impractical. By leveraging the inherent connectivity of host protein interaction networks, multiplex therapies can achieve simultaneous inhibition of multiple pathogenic processes, thereby enhancing therapeutic breadth and resilience. The present invention embodies this philosophy by identifying a repurposed clinical compound—Bithionol—that functions as a potent inhibitor of a select subset of host caspases, which serve as critical hub proteins exploited by a wide array of bacterial toxins, plant toxins, and viruses, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, anthrax toxin, ricin, botulinum neurotoxin A, and Zika virus.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for inhibiting pathogenic agents by administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of Bithionol or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt or derivative thereof. The method is particularly effective against pathogenic agents whose cytotoxicity or pathogenicity is mediated, at least in part, through the activation of host caspases, specifically caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9. By inhibiting these executioner and inflammatory caspases, Bithionol disrupts the downstream cellular death pathways that are co-opted by diverse pathogens, thereby preserving host cell viability and limiting disease progression. The method is applicable to a broad spectrum of pathogenic agents, including but not limited to bacterial exotoxins (e.g., Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, diphtheria toxin, anthrax lethal factor), plant toxins (e.g., ricin), neurotoxins (e.g., botulinum neurotoxin A), and viruses (e.g., Zika virus), all of which have been shown to induce caspase-dependent cell death as a mechanism of virulence.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition for inhibiting pathogenic agents, comprising Bithionol in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, diluent, or excipient. The composition may be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, or topical delivery, depending on the clinical context and the nature of the pathogenic threat. The composition is designed to achieve systemic concentrations of Bithionol sufficient to inhibit the activity of the target caspases without causing undue toxicity, leveraging the compound’s established safety profile from prior human use as an anti-helminthic agent. In certain embodiments, the composition may further include one or more additional therapeutic agents, such as antibiotics, antivirals, or immunomodulators, to provide synergistic or complementary effects in the treatment of complex infections.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is based on a cell-based multiplex approach that integrates both cellular and biochemical screening methodologies to identify host-directed therapeutics capable of neutralizing multiple pathogenic agents through a common mechanism of action. This approach, termed “Disparate Dual Multiplexing,” involves parallel screening of a Clinical Compound Library (CCL) against multiple toxins in live cells while simultaneously assessing the ability of candidate compounds to inhibit specific host protease activities implicated in toxin-mediated cytotoxicity. By requiring hits to demonstrate efficacy in both cellular protection and target inhibition assays, the method ensures that identified compounds act through a defined molecular mechanism and possess broad-spectrum potential.

Bithionol, a compound historically used as an anti-parasitic agent, was identified through this multiplex screening strategy as a potent inhibitor of pathogenic agents. Originally developed for the treatment of helminth infections, Bithionol’s mechanism of action in parasites was thought to involve disruption of oxidative phosphorylation. However, the present invention reveals that Bithionol also functions as a direct inhibitor of a select subset of human caspases, specifically caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9. These caspases are key mediators of programmed cell death (apoptosis and pyroptosis) and are exploited by numerous pathogenic agents to induce host cell demise. By inhibiting these caspases, Bithionol effectively blocks the final execution steps of toxin- and virus-induced cell death, thereby preserving cellular integrity and function.

As used herein, the term “pathogen” refers to any biological agent capable of causing disease in a host organism, including bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, and multicellular parasites. The term “pathogenic agent” encompasses not only whole pathogens but also their virulence factors, such as exotoxins, endotoxins, neurotoxins, and other secreted or injected effector molecules that directly mediate host damage. The terms “prevent” and “treat” are used broadly to include any intervention that reduces the incidence, severity, duration, or pathological consequences of infection or intoxication, whether administered before, during, or after exposure to the pathogenic agent.

Bithionol has been shown to inhibit caspase-dependent pathogenic agents by directly binding to and inhibiting the proteolytic activity of caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9. This inhibition occurs in a dose-dependent manner, with half-maximal inhibitory concentrations (IC50) ranging from 11 to 21 μM for the various caspases tested. Importantly, Bithionol does not inhibit other host proteases involved in toxin processing, such as furin or cathepsin B, indicating a selective mechanism of action focused on the caspase cascade.

The invention demonstrates that Bithionol inhibits a range of specific pathogenic agents, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A, diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, anthrax lethal toxin, ricin, botulinum neurotoxin A, and Zika virus. In each case, Bithionol reduces cytotoxicity or viral replication in vitro with EC50 values between 5.5 and 10 μM, well below the serum concentrations achievable in humans following oral administration.

In vivo analysis of Bithionol confirmed its therapeutic efficacy in a murine model of botulinum neurotoxin A intoxication. Oral administration of Bithionol at 6.0 mg/kg completely protected mice from a lethal dose of BoNT/A, with no observed symptoms of paralysis or mortality, whereas untreated controls exhibited 90% lethality within three days. This demonstrates that Bithionol is bioavailable, crosses relevant physiological barriers, and exerts protective effects in a living organism.

The effective dosage of Bithionol for inhibiting pathogenic agents in humans is expected to range from 1 to 50 mg/kg, administered orally or parenterally, based on historical use and pharmacokinetic data showing serum concentrations up to 480 μM after a 50 mg/kg dose—approximately 30-fold higher than the in vitro EC50 values.

The method of inhibiting caspase-dependent pathogenic agents comprises administering Bithionol to a subject exposed to or at risk of exposure to a pathogenic agent that induces caspase activation. The composition for inhibiting caspase-dependent pathogenic agents comprises Bithionol formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable vehicle suitable for systemic delivery.

Administration of the Bithionol composition may be performed via oral, intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous routes, with oral administration being particularly advantageous due to Bithionol’s known gastrointestinal absorption and historical use in this form.

### Combination Compositions and Treatments

The therapeutic efficacy of Bithionol may be enhanced by combining it with conventional antimicrobial agents, such as antibiotics, to provide a dual mechanism of action: direct pathogen killing combined with host pathway protection. This strategy is especially relevant for bacterial infections where toxin production contributes significantly to pathology, such as in Pseudomonas aeruginosa or Bacillus anthracis infections.

A composition including Bithionol and an antibiotic, such as ciprofloxacin or doxycycline, may be formulated as a single dosage unit or as separate components administered concurrently. The mixture of Bithionol and antibiotic may be prepared in aqueous or lyophilized form for parenteral administration, ensuring rapid systemic delivery in acute intoxication scenarios.

Parenteral administration of the composition—via intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous injection—is preferred in emergency settings where rapid onset of action is critical, such as in bioterrorism-related exposures to anthrax or ricin.

The dosage of the combination composition is tailored to achieve synergistic effects, with Bithionol administered at 1–50 mg/kg and the antibiotic at standard therapeutic doses. For example, in anthrax prophylaxis, Bithionol may be co-administered with ciprofloxacin at 500 mg twice daily.

The method of treating specific pathogenic agents using the combination composition involves simultaneous inhibition of pathogen growth and blockade of host-mediated cell death, thereby reducing both microbial burden and tissue damage.

This application references U.S. Patent No. [Octodrine patent number], which discloses related compositions for neurological disorders; however, the present invention is distinct in its focus on infectious and toxicological indications mediated by caspase activation.

### Administration of the Bithionol Composition

Parenteral administration, as used herein, refers to non-oral routes of drug delivery, including intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and intraperitoneal injection, which bypass the gastrointestinal tract and provide direct access to systemic circulation.

Systemic administration denotes delivery of the drug to the bloodstream, enabling distribution throughout the body to reach target tissues, including the brain, liver, and peripheral organs affected by pathogenic agents.

Administering the Bithionol composition may be performed as a single dose or repeated dosing regimen, depending on the duration of exposure and the pharmacokinetic profile of the compound. Given Bithionol’s half-life and historical dosing schedules, twice-daily oral administration is contemplated for sustained protection.

Preparation of pharmaceutical doses involves dissolving or suspending Bithionol in appropriate vehicles, such as saline, dextrose solution, or buffered formulations, with optional inclusion of solubilizers, stabilizers, or preservatives to ensure sterility and stability for injectable products.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Identification of Host Hub Proteins Exploited by Multiple Pathogenic Toxins

Host hub proteins were identified by analyzing the convergence of cellular pathways targeted by diverse cytotoxic agents, including Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A (PE), anthrax toxin, ricin, and diphtheria toxin. Despite utilizing distinct entry mechanisms—such as endosomal acidification or ER-associated degradation—these toxins ultimately activate overlapping host death pathways centered on caspase proteases. Through genetic and functional studies, caspases-3, -6, and -7 were pinpointed as critical hubs mediating toxin-induced apoptosis. These proteins were then used as molecular targets in high-throughput drug screens designed to discover compounds capable of simultaneously inhibiting multiple toxin pathways by blocking this common node.

### Example 2. The Effect of Caspase Mutations on the Sensitivity of Human B-Cells to P. aeruginosa Exotoxin A

Genetic variation in caspase genes was found to correlate with differential sensitivity to PE in human B-lymphoblastoid cells derived from 234 individuals across four global populations. A 200-fold range in PE lethality was observed, with heritability confirmed through parent-child trio analysis (P < 0.0001). Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in CASP3 and CASP7 showed statistically significant associations with PE sensitivity in European and East Asian cohorts, respectively. These findings establish caspases as genetically determined modulators of toxin susceptibility and validate them as high-value therapeutic targets for host-directed intervention.

### Example 3. A Multiplexed Cellular Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Cytotoxic Activities of Bacterial Toxins Exploiting Unique but Interconnected Host Pathways

A Clinical Compound Library (CCL) was screened in RAW264.7 and C32 cells challenged with PE, cholera toxin, and diphtheria toxin. Compounds that increased cell survival by ≥20 standard deviations above toxin-only controls—without intrinsic cytotoxicity—were classified as hits. Multiplex hits, defined as compounds active against ≥2 toxins, included Bithionol and Pyrogallol. Bithionol demonstrated consistent protection across all three toxins, reducing cell death by >80% at 33 μM, thereby qualifying as a lead candidate for broad-spectrum anti-toxin therapy.

### Example 4. A Multiplex Protein Function-Based Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Proteolytic Activities of Host Caspase-3, -6, and -7

Parallel biochemical screening employed fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) assays with caspase-specific substrates (DNLD for caspase-3, VEID for caspase-6, DQTD for caspases-3/7). Compounds inhibiting ≥80% of caspase activity at 33 μM were considered hits. Bithionol emerged as a multiplex hit, inhibiting all three caspase activities with IC50 values of 21, 13, and 11 μM, respectively. This dual validation—in both cellular protection and enzymatic inhibition—confirmed Bithionol’s mechanism as a caspase inhibitor.

### Example 5. Bithionol Reduces the Pathogenicity of a Range of Toxins by Inhibiting Host Caspases

Dose-response studies confirmed Bithionol’s anti-toxin activity with EC50 values of ~10 μM against PE, cholera, and diphtheria toxins in vitro. It also protected PE-sensitive HapMap B-cell lines from 80% lethal doses. Broader profiling revealed inhibition of five caspases (1, 3, 6, 7, 9) among ten tested, establishing Bithionol as a selective polypharmacological caspase inhibitor with pan-toxin protective potential.

### Example 6. Bithionol Inhibits Cytotoxic Activity of Anthrax Toxins

In RAW264.7 cells, Bithionol (33 μM) conferred >80% protection against lethal factor–protective antigen (LF-PA) intoxication. FRET assays showed complete suppression of caspase-1 activation induced by anthrax toxin. Western blot and enzymatic assays confirmed no inhibition of LF, furin, or cathepsin B, proving that protection is mediated solely through caspase-1 blockade.

### Example 7. Bithionol Inhibits Ricin and Botulinum Neurotoxin A-Induced Death In Vitro and In Vivo

Bithionol reduced ricin cytotoxicity in K562 cells with an EC50 of 10 μM. In Swiss Webster mice, oral Bithionol (6.0 mg/kg) provided 100% survival against a lethal BoNT/A dose, with no paralysis observed. FRET assays confirmed Bithionol does not inhibit BoNT/A protease activity, demonstrating host-targeted protection. Western blot analysis verified preservation of full-length SNAP-25 in Bithionol-treated, intoxicated neurons.

### Example 8. Bithionol Acts as a Zika Virus Inhibitor

Bithionol inhibited both African (DAK AR D 41525) and Asian (PRVABC59) Zika virus strains in Vero E6 cells and human astrocytes, with EC50 values of 5.5–6.7 μM. High-content imaging showed >90% reduction in viral envelope protein expression. Since ZIKV induces caspase-3–dependent neuronal apoptosis, Bithionol’s caspase inhibition disrupts viral propagation and cytopathology, positioning it as a novel antiviral candidate.

### Example 9. Material and Methods

Chemicals included toxins from List Biological Laboratories and Bithionol from Sigma-Aldrich. Cell lines (RAW264.7, C32, Vero E6, etc.) were cultured in standard media. Human B-cell sensitivity to PE was assessed via Alamar Blue assay across 234 HapMap lines. Statistical analysis used GraphPad Prism. Drug screens employed MTT and FRET assays with caspase substrates DNLD, VEID, and DQTD. Kinetic data were analyzed via Excel LINEST. Caspase inhibition was tested against purified recombinant caspases 1–10. In vivo studies used Swiss Webster mice with IACUC approval. Zika infection assays utilized MOIs of 0.5–15, with detection by anti-flavivirus antibody and Opera high-content imaging. All methods followed institutional biosafety and ethical guidelines.
