# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, particularly to methods and kits for the detection and diagnosis of liver cancer. More specifically, the invention provides novel approaches for the early and precise identification of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC), a primary malignancy arising from the epithelial cells of the intrahepatic bile ducts. The invention leverages the discovery of specific glycan alterations associated with ICC, utilizing lectin-based recognition of these glycans in combination with protein-specific antibodies to enable highly sensitive and specific detection in clinical specimens such as bile and blood. This technology addresses a critical unmet need in hepatobiliary oncology, where current diagnostic modalities often fail to distinguish ICC from other liver malignancies or benign biliary conditions at an early, treatable stage.

## BACKGROUND ART

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide, and early detection is paramount to improving patient outcomes. Among primary liver cancers, hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) represent the two most common histological subtypes, each with distinct etiologies, molecular profiles, and therapeutic implications. HCC typically arises in the context of chronic liver disease and cirrhosis, whereas ICC originates from the cholangiocytes lining the intrahepatic bile ducts and is often associated with chronic biliary inflammation, such as that seen in primary sclerosing cholangitis or hepatolithiasis.

Hepatocellular carcinoma is the most prevalent form of primary liver cancer, accounting for approximately 75–85% of cases. It is characterized by its origin in hepatocytes and is frequently diagnosed using serum biomarkers like alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), although AFP lacks sufficient sensitivity and specificity for early-stage disease. In contrast, intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma constitutes about 10–15% of primary liver cancers but has seen a rising incidence globally. ICC is notoriously difficult to diagnose early due to its asymptomatic presentation and lack of reliable non-invasive biomarkers. Its prognosis is generally poor, with a five-year survival rate below 20%, largely because it is often detected at an advanced stage when curative resection is no longer feasible.

Current detection methods for liver cancer rely heavily on imaging modalities such as ultrasound, computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), often supplemented by serum tumor markers. For HCC, AFP, des-gamma-carboxy prothrombin (DCP), and more recently, glypican-3 (GPC3), have been used as serological indicators. Genes and their encoded polypeptides, including GPC3, have been explored as tumor markers, with anti-glypican-3 antibodies employed in immunohistochemical staining to aid pathological diagnosis. Additionally, glycan biomarkers—alterations in the sugar moieties attached to proteins—have emerged as promising indicators of malignancy, reflecting underlying changes in glycosyltransferase expression during carcinogenesis.

For cholangiocarcinoma, including ICC, the most commonly used serum marker is carbohydrate antigen 19-9 (CA19-9). However, CA19-9 is elevated in numerous benign biliary conditions, such as cholangitis and choledocholithiasis, leading to low specificity. Other proposed markers include MUC5AC, Mac-2-binding protein, and cytokeratin fragments, but none have achieved widespread clinical utility due to inadequate performance characteristics. Genomic approaches have identified mutations in genes like IDH1/2, FGFR2 fusions, and KRAS as potential diagnostic or therapeutic targets, yet these require tissue biopsy and complex molecular analysis, limiting their use in routine screening.

Cytokeratin and epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM) are well-established tissue markers used in immunohistochemistry to differentiate ICC from HCC, as ICC cells express biliary cytokeratins (e.g., CK7, CK19) and EpCAM, while HCC cells typically do not. Despite their utility in pathological confirmation, these markers are not applicable to non-invasive liquid biopsies. A major limitation of existing tumor markers is their inability to reliably detect ICC in its early stages or to distinguish it from benign biliary diseases or combined HCC-ICC tumors, which pose significant diagnostic challenges.

Consequently, there exists a compelling need for novel, highly specific, and sensitive biomarkers that can be detected in accessible body fluids such as bile or blood. Such markers should reflect the unique molecular pathology of ICC, particularly its characteristic glycosylation changes, and enable simple, cost-effective, and robust diagnostic assays suitable for clinical implementation.

### Prior Art Documents

Relevant patent documents include Japanese Patent Application Laid-Open No. 2005-126456, which discloses the use of glypican-3 as a diagnostic marker for HCC; U.S. Patent No. 7,892,756, describing monoclonal antibodies against MUC1 for cancer detection; and WO 2010/123123, which relates to lectin microarray technology for glycan profiling. Non-patent literature includes Matsuda et al., “Glycomic analysis of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma identifies WFA-reactive glycans as a specific marker,” published in Hepatology (2015); Hakomori, “Aberrant glycosylation in tumors and tumor-associated carbohydrate antigens,” in Advances in Cancer Research (1989); and Hirabayashi et al., “Lectin microarray for glycomic analysis,” in Methods in Molecular Biology (2010).

These prior art documents collectively describe the use of lectins, antibodies, and glycoproteins in cancer diagnostics but fail to disclose or suggest the specific combination of Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA) and an antibody against a WFA-binding glycoprotein—particularly sialylated MUC1—for the detection of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma in bile or blood. None of the cited references teach the sandwich assay format employing a glycan-binding substance (lectin) and a protein-binding substance (antibody) to achieve high-precision discrimination of ICC from benign biliary diseases or other liver cancers.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a method for the early and accurate detection of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, a malignancy that is frequently diagnosed at an advanced stage due to the absence of reliable non-invasive biomarkers. A particular challenge lies in distinguishing ICC from benign biliary conditions, such as hepatolithiasis or bile duct stones, as well as from hepatocellular carcinoma or combined HCC-ICC tumors, which require different therapeutic strategies. The invention aims to overcome the limitations of current tumor markers, which suffer from low sensitivity, poor specificity, or dependence on invasive tissue sampling, by introducing a novel glycan-based diagnostic approach applicable to easily obtainable clinical specimens like bile and blood.

### Means to Solve the Object

The invention achieves this object through the identification of a specific glycan biomarker for intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma: glycans recognized by the lectin Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA). Using lectin microarray analysis of paired cancerous and normal bile duct epithelial tissues from the same patients, WFA was found to exhibit significantly higher binding to ICC lesions compared to normal tissue, with a tumor-to-normal ratio of 4.6 and a p-value < 0.0001. This discovery led to the development of a detection method based on the principle that ICC-specific glycoproteins carry WFA-reactive glycans, notably on sialylated MUC1.

The core of the invention is a sandwich detection method wherein a glycan-binding substance (specifically, WFA) and a protein-binding substance (an antibody that recognizes the protein moiety of the WFA-binding glycoprotein, such as the monoclonal antibody MY.1E12 against sialylated MUC1) are used in tandem. In a preferred embodiment, WFA is immobilized on a solid phase—such as a microtiter plate, magnetic beads, or a membrane—via covalent bonding, ionic interaction, or physical adsorption. A clinical specimen (e.g., bile or serum) is then applied, allowing WFA to capture glycoproteins bearing its specific glycan ligand. Subsequently, a labeled antibody (e.g., enzyme-, fluorescent-, radioactive-, or biotin-conjugated) that binds to the protein backbone of the captured glycoprotein is introduced, enabling detection through various readout systems.

The invention encompasses multiple assay formats, including enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), immunochromatography, radioimmunoassay, fluoroimmunoassay, chemiluminescent immunoassay, and evanescent wave excited fluorescence detection. The labeling substances may include horseradish peroxidase, alkaline phosphatase, fluorescent dyes (e.g., Cy3, Alexa Fluor), radioactive isotopes, or biotin for signal amplification. The solid support can be a plate, beads, or membrane, functionalized to immobilize either the lectin or the antibody.

Antibodies for use in the invention can be polyclonal or monoclonal, produced by immunizing animals with the full-length WFA-binding glycoprotein, its protein moiety, or synthetic peptides derived from candidate molecules such as CA125, N-CAM-L1, Maspin, or MUC1. Glycan profiling to identify suitable lectins can be performed via frontal affinity chromatography, lectin microarray, or mass spectrometry (MS/MSn).

The invention further provides a diagnostic kit comprising WFA and an antibody that recognizes a WFA-binding glycoprotein, pre-immobilized or provided in solution for user-defined solid-phase preparation. This kit enables the detection and/or distinction of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma from other liver pathologies with high precision, facilitating early intervention and personalized treatment planning.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention enables the early detection of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma with high sensitivity (90.0%) and a strong area under the ROC curve (AUC = 0.86), significantly outperforming conventional biliary cytology and existing serum markers like CA19-9. The method is simple, robust, and adaptable to standard clinical laboratory platforms, making it suitable for routine diagnostic use. The diagnostic kit offers a practical tool for clinicians to differentiate ICC from benign biliary diseases and other liver cancers, thereby improving diagnostic accuracy, guiding appropriate therapy, and ultimately enhancing patient prognosis.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The method for detecting intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma according to the present invention centers on the use of WFA-binding glycoprotein as a cancer-specific marker. This glycoprotein, identified as sialylated MUC1 in exemplary embodiments, carries terminal N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues that are specifically recognized by WFA. The detection process begins with the immobilization of WFA onto a solid substrate, such as a streptavidin-coated microtiter plate, to which biotinylated WFA is bound. Alternatively, WFA may be directly adsorbed onto polystyrene surfaces.

Clinical specimens—including bile obtained via percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage or during surgery, or serum from peripheral blood—are prepared by centrifugation to remove cellular debris and adjusted to a standardized protein concentration. The specimen is then incubated in the WFA-coated well, allowing WFA to capture glycoproteins bearing its cognate glycan structure. After washing, a detection antibody—such as the monoclonal antibody MY.1E12, which specifically binds to sialylated MUC1—is added. This antibody may be directly labeled with an enzyme (e.g., horseradish peroxidase) or detected via a secondary labeled antibody.

In a lectin/antibody sandwich ELISA format, the amount of captured glycoprotein is quantified by measuring the enzymatic conversion of a chromogenic substrate, with optical density correlating to the concentration of the ICC marker. The method can also be adapted to immunochromatographic strips for point-of-care testing or to evanescent wave biosensors for real-time, label-free detection.

A diagnostic kit is provided, containing lyophilized or liquid formulations of WFA, anti-WFA-binding glycoprotein antibody (e.g., MY.1E12), assay buffers, wash solutions, and detection reagents. The kit may include pre-coated plates or devices for rapid deployment in clinical settings. Patient-derived test specimens are processed according to standardized protocols to ensure reproducibility and reliability.

## EXAMPLE 1

Lectin array analysis was performed on formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections from 45 ICC cases and 38 normal bile duct epithelia. Tissue fragments (1.0 mm²) were microdissected, and total proteins were labeled with Cy3-succinimidyl ester. The labeled proteins were applied to a lectin microarray slide containing 45 different lectins. Fluorescence signals were scanned and normalized, with the strongest signal set to 1.0. Statistical analysis revealed that WFA exhibited the highest tumor-to-normal ratio (4.6, P < 0.0001). ROC curve analysis confirmed WFA’s superior discriminatory power, with an AUC of 0.93, 87.4% sensitivity, and 92.1% specificity, establishing WFA as a potential specific marker for ICC.

## EXAMPLE 2

Tissue staining with biotinylated WFA was conducted on 90 ICC specimens, 25 normal bile ducts, 25 HCCs, and 10 combined HCC-ICCs. WFA staining was observed in 92.2% of ICCs and 80% of ICC components in combined tumors, but in only 32% of normal bile ducts and 0% of HCCs. The staining was localized to the apical surface of neoplastic cholangiocytes. Comparison with clinical classification showed consistent expression across ICC subtypes. Immunohistochemistry confirmed the specificity of WFA for ICC, demonstrating its utility in pathological diagnosis and differential diagnosis from HCC.

## EXAMPLE 3

Detection of ICC using cells in bile was explored by applying the WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich principle to exfoliated biliary cells. Bile samples were cytospun onto slides, fixed, and subjected to dual staining with WFA and MY.1E12. Colocalization of signals on malignant cells provided a cytological correlate to the biochemical assay, enhancing diagnostic confidence in equivocal cytology cases.

## EXAMPLE 4

To identify the WFA-binding glycoprotein, WFA-positive tissue areas were microdissected, and proteins were extracted. WFA-binding proteins were captured using streptavidin beads conjugated with biotinylated WFA. Eluted proteins were separated by agarose gel electrophoresis and visualized by protein staining. Bands corresponding to high-molecular-weight glycoproteins were excised and analyzed by mass spectrometry. Database searching yielded candidate molecules, with MUC1, CA125, and Maspin receiving high scores based on peptide coverage and spectral counts, identifying them as dominant candidates for WFA-binding proteins in ICC.

## EXAMPLE 5

Candidate WFA-binding proteins were validated by tissue staining. Antibodies against MUC1, CA125, N-CAM-L1, and Maspin were used on ICC tissue sections. Only anti-MUC1 (MY.1E12) showed strong colocalization with WFA staining, categorizing MUC1 as the primary carrier of WFA-reactive glycans in ICC. Other candidates showed weak or inconsistent expression, confirming the specificity of the WFA-MUC1 axis.

### EXAMPLE 6

A sandwich detection analysis was developed using bile. WFA was coated onto streptavidin plates, and bile (10 µg protein) was added. After binding, MY.1E12 was applied, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibody. Optical density was measured at 450 nm. The assay distinguished 30 CC cases from 38 benign controls (P = 0.0004). ROC analysis gave an AUC of 0.86, with 90% sensitivity and 76.3% specificity at a cutoff of S/N = 6.64, outperforming biliary cytology (55.6% sensitivity).

### EXAMPLE 7

An improved sandwich ELISA used optimized blocking and washing conditions to reduce background. The protocol was identical to Example 6 but included enhanced sample dilution and extended incubation times. Results showed increased signal-to-noise ratios and improved specificity (82%), with comparable sensitivity. ROC analysis confirmed robust performance (AUC = 0.88), demonstrating assay refinement without altering core components.

### EXAMPLE 8

Detection using blood was evaluated by applying the WFA-MY.1E12 system to serum samples. WFA-coated plates captured circulating glycoproteins, and MY.1E12 detected sialylated MUC1. Although signal intensity was lower than in bile, significant elevation was observed in ICC patients versus controls (P < 0.01). ROC analysis yielded an AUC of 0.79, indicating feasibility for serum-based screening, albeit with reduced sensitivity compared to bile, supporting bile as the preferred specimen for maximal diagnostic accuracy.
