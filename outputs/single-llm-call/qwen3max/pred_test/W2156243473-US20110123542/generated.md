# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapy, particularly to the treatment of cancers that are dependent on or influenced by the chemokine CCL20 and its receptor CCR6. More specifically, the invention provides novel therapeutic compositions and methods for treating cancer by administering CCL20-neutralizing antibodies, either alone or in combination with other agents, to inhibit tumor growth, vascularization, invasion, and metastasis. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, kits, and medicaments comprising such antibodies, as well as methods for identifying and selecting patients who may benefit from such therapy based on the expression of CCL20, CCR6, and/or CXCR4 in their tumors. The disclosed approaches are applicable to a broad spectrum of malignancies, including but not limited to prostate cancer, glioma, leukemia, uterine cancer, colon carcinoma, and other epithelial and hematopoietic cancers that exhibit dysregulation of the CCL20/CCR6 axis, often in conjunction with CXCR4 overexpression.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Chemokines constitute a family of small, secreted proteins ranging from 5 to 20 kDa in size that primarily mediate the directional migration, or chemotaxis, of immune cells such as lymphocytes and leukocytes to specific lymphoid tissues or sites of inflammation, infection, or tissue damage. Beyond their classical role in immune cell trafficking, chemokines have been implicated in a wide array of biological processes, including angiogenesis, embryogenesis, hematopoiesis, lymphopoiesis, and even viral pathogenesis, such as in the case of HIV. In the context of oncology, it has become increasingly evident that cancer cells co-opt chemokine signaling pathways to facilitate multiple stages of tumor development, progression, and metastasis. Among the various chemokine receptors, CXCR4 stands out due to its remarkably broad expression across at least 23 different cancer types of epithelial, mesenchymal, and hematopoietic origin, including gliomas, breast carcinomas, certain leukemias, uterine cancers, Burkitt’s lymphoma, neuroblastomas, and pancreatic cancer. CXCR4 and its sole canonical ligand, CXCL12 (also known as SDF-1), play a critical role in promoting tumor growth, invasion, and metastasis in malignancies such as breast, prostate, and renal cell carcinoma. Neutralizing the CXCR4/CXCL12 interaction has been shown to impede these oncogenic processes, underscoring its therapeutic relevance.

Another key player in the chemokine network is CCL20, also known as macrophage inflammatory protein-3α (MIP-3α), liver and activation-regulated chemokine (LARC), or exodus-1. CCL20 is the unique ligand for the chemokine receptor CCR6 and is constitutively expressed at low levels in tissues such as skin, intestinal mucosa, liver, and tonsils, but is strongly upregulated by pro-inflammatory stimuli like IL-1β, TNF-α, and LPS. CCL20 functions primarily to recruit CCR6-expressing immune cells—including immature dendritic cells, memory T cells, TH17 cells, and natural killer cells—to sites of inflammation. In cancer, CCL20 expression has been documented in a variety of human neoplasms, including colorectal, lung, pancreatic, and breast adenocarcinomas, as well as malignant glioma, leukemia, lymphoma, and melanoma. However, the role of CCL20 in tumorigenesis remains controversial. While some studies suggest that CCL20 may enhance anti-tumor immunity by attracting antigen-presenting dendritic cells, other evidence indicates that CCL20 promotes tumor growth, invasiveness, and immune evasion by fostering an immunosuppressive microenvironment or acting directly as a growth factor on cancer cells that express CCR6.

Prior art has explored the use of CCL20 or CCR6 modulation in cancer therapy, but these efforts have been inconsistent and often contradictory, failing to establish a clear therapeutic paradigm. Moreover, the functional interplay between the CXCR4/CXCL12 and CCL20/CCR6 axes in cancer has not been adequately elucidated. There remains a significant unmet medical need for effective, targeted therapies that can disrupt the pro-tumorigenic functions of CCL20 without exacerbating immune suppression or promoting metastasis. The present invention addresses this gap by revealing a novel mechanistic link between CXCR4 and CCL20, demonstrating that CXCR4 signaling upregulates CCL20 expression in multiple cancer types, and that CCL20, in turn, drives tumor proliferation, adhesion, vascularization, and invasion—particularly in cancers that co-express CCR6. This discovery provides a strong rationale for targeting CCL20 therapeutically and establishes CCL20-neutralizing antibodies as a promising new class of anti-cancer agents.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a composition for treating cancer, based on the surprising discovery that neutralizing antibodies against CCL20 exert potent anti-tumor effects. It has been found that CCL20 plays a critical role in promoting tumor growth, adhesion, vascularization, and invasion, particularly in cancers that express its receptor CCR6 and/or the chemokine receptor CXCR4. Contrary to prior assumptions, CCL20 acts not merely as an immune modulator but as a direct driver of oncogenic behavior in CCR6-expressing tumor cells. The invention thus introduces CCL20-neutralizing antibodies as effective anti-cancer agents capable of inhibiting these pathological processes.

In one aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a CCL20-neutralizing antibody, or an antigen-binding fragment thereof, in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The antibody may be monoclonal, polyclonal, chimeric, humanized, or fully human, and is specifically designed to bind to CCL20 with high affinity and neutralize its biological activity, thereby blocking its interaction with CCR6. The invention further encompasses various embodiments of such antibodies, including those derived from hybridoma MAB360, single-chain variable fragments (scFv), Fab miniantibodies, and recombinant constructs produced via phage display or other biotechnological methods.

The therapeutic methods of the invention are applicable to a wide range of cancers, including but not limited to glioma, leukemia, uterine cancer, prostate cancer, colon carcinoma, breast cancer, pancreatic cancer, and melanoma—particularly those that exhibit CCL20 dependency, as evidenced by expression of CCL20, CCR6, and/or CXCR4. A method of treating cancer is provided, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of a CCL20-neutralizing antibody. This method may be used to inhibit tumor growth and progression, inhibit tumor vascularization, and induce tumor regression. In addition, the invention includes a pharmaceutical pack or kit comprising the CCL20-neutralizing antibody together with instructions for its use in the treatment of CCL20-dependent cancers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the identification of anti-CCL20 antibodies as effective agents for the treatment of cancer. Specifically, the invention provides a method for treating CCL20-dependent cancer by administering a CCL20-neutralizing antibody to a subject. CCL20-dependent cancers are defined as those in which tumor cells express CCR6 and/or CXCR4, and in which CCL20 signaling contributes to tumor growth, survival, adhesion, vascularization, or metastasis. The method is particularly effective in inhibiting tumor growth and/or progression, inhibiting tumor vascularization, and inducing or enhancing tumor regression. Exemplary cancer types amenable to this therapy include glioma, leukemia, and uterine cancer, as well as prostate and colon carcinomas.

A key embodiment of the invention involves the use of the monoclonal antibody MAB360, which specifically binds to and neutralizes human CCL20. This antibody may be administered by injection or infusion, preferably subcutaneously, intravenously, or intraperitoneally, in a therapeutically effective dose. The method is also effective in inhibiting the metastasis of CCL20-dependent tumors, particularly in subjects whose tumors express CCR6 and/or CXCR4 on the cell surface, as confirmed by immunohistochemistry, flow cytometry, or PCR-based assays.

The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a CCL20-neutralizing antibody in a pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. Such compositions may be formulated as liquid solutions, suspensions, liposomal formulations, or lyophilized powders for reconstitution. The composition serves as a medicament for inhibiting tumor progression, growth, or vascularization, and for reducing tumor size or inhibiting tumor invasiveness. A pharmaceutical kit is also provided, comprising the CCL20-neutralizing antibody and instructions for its use in treating cancers such as glioma, leukemia, or uterine cancer.

The antibodies of the invention specifically antagonize the interaction between CCL20 and CCR6. As used herein, “antibody” includes full-length immunoglobulins as well as fragments such as Fab, F(ab′)₂, scFv, and single-domain antibodies, provided they retain the ability to bind CCL20 with high specificity and neutralize its activity. The antigen-binding domain recognizes a specific epitope on CCL20, and the antibody may be engineered as a chimeric, humanized, or fully human molecule to reduce immunogenicity in human subjects. Methods for generating such antibodies include immunization of animals followed by hybridoma technology, phage display from human libraries, recombinant DNA techniques, and CDR grafting to produce humanized variants. Humanized antibodies offer the advantage of reduced risk of anti-drug antibody responses during chronic administration.

CCL20 is defined as the human chemokine CCL20 (UniProt P78556), and “CCL20-neutralizing antibodies” are those that inhibit CCL20-mediated signaling through CCR6, as demonstrated by in vitro assays such as inhibition of CCL20-induced cell adhesion or proliferation. Pharmaceutical compositions are formulated for systemic or local administration and may include buffers, stabilizers, surfactants, and other excipients. Dosage regimens are tailored to the individual, typically ranging from 1 to 100 mg per dose, administered once to three times weekly. The invention also contemplates gene therapy or cell therapy approaches wherein nucleic acids encoding the antibody are delivered to the patient. Ultimately, the CCL20-neutralizing antibody is used to prepare a medicament for the treatment of CCL20-dependent cancers, offering a novel and effective strategy to combat tumor progression and metastasis.

## EXAMPLES

### Experimental Procedures

Human prostate cancer cell lines PC3, LNCaP, 22RV1, and DU145 were cultured under standard conditions. To generate stable overexpression models, PC3 cells were transduced with lentiviral vectors encoding human CXCR4 or CCL20, followed by selection and isolation of single-cell clones using limiting dilution. Secreted CCL20 protein in culture supernatants was quantified by ELISA. Surface expression of CXCR4 was analyzed by flow cytometry using anti-CXCR4 monoclonal antibody 12G5. For immunohistochemistry, formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tumor sections were stained with anti-CCL20, anti-CCR6, or anti-CXCR4 antibodies, and staining intensity was scored blindly by two independent observers. Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent, reverse-transcribed into cDNA, and subjected to semi-quantitative or real-time PCR using gene-specific primers for CCL20, CCR6, CXCR4, and β-actin as a housekeeping control. Cell proliferation was assessed by propidium iodide staining and FACS analysis or by BrdU incorporation. Cell adhesion assays were performed by allowing cells to adhere to fibronectin- or collagen I-coated plates, followed by washing and quantification of adherent cells. Tumor xenografts were established by subcutaneous injection of 5×10⁶ cancer cells into SCID/bg mice. Tumor size was measured regularly with calipers, and vascular functionality was assessed by MRI using ΔSo2 mapping during carbogen challenge. Statistical significance was determined by two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test, with p<0.05 considered significant.

### Example 1

CCL20 and CCR6 expression was characterized across four human prostate cancer cell lines. Only PC3 cells expressed both CCR6 mRNA and surface protein, and secreted detectable CCL20 into the culture medium. Treatment of PC3 cells with recombinant CCL20 (5–50 ng/ml) significantly increased cell proliferation, as measured by viable cell count and BrdU incorporation, while higher concentrations (250 ng/ml) had no effect, likely due to receptor desensitization. CCL20 also enhanced adhesion of PC3 cells to collagen I in a dose-dependent manner, an effect abolished by pertussis toxin, indicating G-protein coupling. Stable transfection of PC3 cells with CCL20 yielded clones secreting varying levels of the chemokine; clones with moderate CCL20 secretion (e.g., PC3-CCL20.30) exhibited increased proliferation and adhesion to collagen I compared to mock-transfected controls. Similar CCL20 responsiveness was observed in CCR6-expressing HT-29 colon carcinoma and HL60 leukemia cells, but not in CCR6-negative lines, confirming the specificity of the CCL20/CCR6 axis in driving tumor cell behavior.

### Example 2

To evaluate the in vivo role of CCL20, SCID/bg mice were injected subcutaneously with mock-transfected or CCL20-overexpressing PC3 cells (clone 30). Mice bearing PC3-CCL20.30 tumors developed significantly larger, more invasive, and highly vascularized tumors compared to controls. Histological analysis revealed extensive invasion into muscle and dermis, necrosis, and aberrant blood vessel formation. MRI-based assessment of vessel functionality (ΔSo2) demonstrated significantly higher oxygen delivery in CCL20-overexpressing tumors, even at early stages, whereas control tumors showed poor perfusion restricted to the periphery. These results establish that CCL20 overexpression drives tumor vascularization and aggressive growth in vivo.

### Example 3

Neutralization of CCL20 with monoclonal antibody MAB360 abolished CCL20-induced adhesion of PC3-CCL20.30 cells to collagen I in vitro. In vivo, SCID/bg mice injected with PC3-CCL20.30 cells and treated with anti-CCL20 antibody (20 µg, three times weekly, subcutaneously) showed markedly reduced tumor incidence and growth compared to isotype control-treated mice. Similarly, treatment of mice bearing CXCR4-overexpressing PC3 tumors with anti-CCL20 antibody delayed tumor onset and inhibited growth, demonstrating that CCL20 mediates CXCR4-driven tumorigenesis. In a colon cancer model, anti-CCL20 antibody also suppressed HT-29 tumor growth and invasion, confirming the broad applicability of this approach.

### Example 4

Immunohistochemical analysis of human prostate cancer tissue arrays (n=52) revealed CCR6 expression in 96% and CCL20 in 65% of tumors, with co-expression in the majority of CCL20-positive cases. High CCL20 expression correlated significantly with higher Gleason score (8.75 vs. 7.72, p=0.02), indicating association with tumor aggressiveness. In a second array (n=48), co-expression of CXCR4 and CCL20 was observed in 79.5% of samples, with similar staining intensities, supporting a functional link between these pathways in human prostate cancer. These findings validate the clinical relevance of targeting CCL20 in prostate and other cancers that co-express CCR6 and CXCR4.
