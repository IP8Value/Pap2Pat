## BACKGROUND

Entity resolution is a critical data processing task that involves identifying records across disparate datasets that correspond to the same real-world entity, such as a person, organization, or transaction. In many practical scenarios, these datasets are held by different organizations—such as hospitals, financial institutions, or government agencies—that are legally or ethically prohibited from sharing raw identifying information due to privacy regulations like HIPAA, GDPR, or CCPA. This constraint necessitates the development of privacy-preserving techniques that allow collaborative analysis without exposing sensitive quasi-identifiers such as names, addresses, dates of birth, or medical histories. Traditional entity resolution methods often rely on exact matching of unique identifiers like social security numbers, but such identifiers are frequently absent, inconsistent, or deliberately obscured across systems. Moreover, even when present, they may be subject to typographical errors, formatting differences, or intentional obfuscation, rendering exact matching ineffective. To address this challenge, approximate or "fuzzy" matching techniques have been developed, which evaluate similarity between records based on multiple fields using metrics such as edit distance, cosine similarity, or the Jaccard index. However, directly applying these similarity measures in a cross-organizational setting risks exposing private data during the comparison process. Private set intersection (PSI) is a cryptographic protocol that enables two parties to compute the intersection of their private sets without revealing any additional information about elements not in the intersection. While PSI is well-suited for exact matching scenarios, it cannot natively handle approximate matches required for real-world entity resolution. Locality-sensitive hashing (LSH) offers a complementary approach by mapping similar inputs to the same hash bucket with high probability, thereby enabling efficient similarity search in high-dimensional spaces. LSH functions, particularly those based on min-hashing and the Jaccard similarity, provide a probabilistic guarantee that similar records will collide in at least one hash bucket, while dissimilar records are unlikely to do so. The combination of LSH and PSI presents a promising avenue for privacy-preserving record linkage, but existing approaches either suffer from computational inefficiency, reliance on trusted third parties, or inadequate formal security guarantees. The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a novel protocol that seamlessly integrates LSH-based fuzzy matching with PSI-based privacy protection in a two-party setting without third-party involvement.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a private data intersection method that enables two or more parties to securely identify matching records across their respective private datasets without revealing non-matching information. The method begins by performing private set intersection over transformed representations of the data, specifically using locality-sensitive hash (LSH) values derived from each record. To enhance precision and reduce false positives, the protocol removes identical intersecting records that may arise from duplicate entries within a single dataset prior to the main matching phase. The core of the invention involves computing locality-sensitive hash values for each record in both datasets, where the LSH is configured using min-hash functions applied to k-shingles of normalized record fields. These min-hash values are then grouped into bands, and each band is hashed to produce a compact band signature; the collection of these signatures forms the LSH tuple for a record. The parties then jointly perform private set intersection over the multiset of all band signatures generated from their respective datasets. Matching records are determined based on whether their LSH tuples share at least one common band signature, indicating a high probability of underlying record similarity according to the Jaccard index. The invention also encompasses a computer program product comprising a non-transitory computer-readable storage medium having program instructions embodied therewith, the program instructions executable by one or more processors to implement the private data intersection method. These instructions include modules for data preprocessing, shingle generation, min-hash computation, band signature creation, PSI execution, and match determination. Additionally, the invention provides a computer system comprising one or more processors, memory, and network interfaces configured to execute the private data intersection method, supporting deployment in cloud, on-premise, or hybrid environments. The system architecture ensures that no party learns anything beyond the intended output—namely, the set of matching records or their count—while preserving the privacy of all non-matching data through cryptographic guarantees rooted in the hardness of the discrete logarithm problem and the properties of collision-resistant hash functions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Entity resolution and private set intersection (PSI) represent two foundational pillars in modern data interoperability and privacy-preserving computation. Entity resolution seeks to link records referring to the same real-world entity across databases, while PSI enables secure computation of set intersections without data leakage. The motivation for privacy-preserving record linkage arises in numerous domains: healthcare institutions wishing to pool patient data for rare disease research without violating confidentiality, financial regulators detecting cross-border fraud while respecting client privacy, or electoral commissions deduplicating voter rolls across jurisdictions. Existing protocols for this task face significant limitations—they often require trusted third parties, incur prohibitive computational overhead, leak auxiliary information through side channels, or fail to scale to datasets with millions of records. To overcome these challenges, the present invention proposes a novel integration of Locality-Sensitive Hashing (LSH), a technique designed to amplify collisions among similar inputs, with PSI, a cryptographic primitive ensuring minimal information disclosure. LSH possesses key properties that make it ideal for this fusion: it is data-independent, meaning its behavior does not adapt to the input distribution, thereby simplifying security analysis; it provides tunable trade-offs between false positive and false negative rates via configurable parameters; and it operates in linear time relative to dataset size, enabling scalability. The invention specifically applies LSH to private data matching by first transforming each record into a set of k-shingles—overlapping substrings of fixed length—derived from normalized quasi-identifier fields such as name, address, and date of birth. From these shingles, min-hash values are computed using multiple independent hash functions, leveraging the mathematical property that the probability of min-hash equality equals the Jaccard similarity between the original shingle sets. These min-hash values are then partitioned into B bands of R values each, and each band is hashed to produce a band signature; the resulting LSH tuple comprises B such signatures. The application of PSI to LSH proceeds by treating each party’s collection of LSH tuples as a multiset of band signatures and executing a PSI protocol over this multiset. Because PSI reveals only the intersection of band signatures—not their source records or non-intersecting elements—the privacy of non-matching records is preserved. Matching records are declared when at least one band signature is shared between the LSH tuples of two records, a condition that probabilistically corresponds to a Jaccard similarity above a threshold determined by B and R. To improve accuracy, the invention incorporates optimization methods such as empirical parameter tuning and analytical formulas for selecting B and R to achieve desired false positive/negative rates. The Jaccard similarity index, central to this approach, is computed as the ratio of the size of the intersection to the size of the union of two shingle sets. Shingles play a crucial role in approximating string similarity through set operations, enabling efficient comparison without direct string exposure. Critically, the invention proposes using PSI not only for initial matching but also to privately compute the Jaccard index for candidate pairs post-matching, by performing an additional PSI over the shingle sets to obtain intersection and union sizes without revealing the shingles themselves.
