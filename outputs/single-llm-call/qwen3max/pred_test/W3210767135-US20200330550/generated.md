# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for modulating insulin-independent glucose transport in skeletal muscle cells. More specifically, the invention provides a novel therapeutic approach for enhancing cellular energy availability by administering teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP), particularly TCAP-1, to increase glucose uptake into skeletal muscle cells without reliance on insulin signaling pathways. This method offers significant potential for treating metabolic disorders characterized by impaired glucose homeostasis, including type 1 and type 2 diabetes mellitus, glycogen storage diseases, and conditions involving muscle trauma or fatigue where enhanced energy availability would be therapeutically beneficial. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising isolated TCAP peptides and methods for their administration to achieve improved skeletal muscle function through modulation of glucose metabolism.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Teneurins represent an evolutionarily ancient family of transmembrane proteins that play crucial roles in cell adhesion, neuronal development, and metabolic regulation. These proteins are characterized by their unique structural organization, featuring multiple functional domains including epidermal growth factor-like repeats, YD-shell repeats, and a distinctive β-barrel structure at their carboxyl terminus. The teneurin family comprises four paralogous members in vertebrates (teneurin-1 through teneurin-4), each encoded by separate genes but sharing significant structural homology. The evolutionary origin of teneurins is particularly noteworthy, as evidence suggests their introduction into metazoan genomes occurred through lateral gene transfer from prokaryotic organisms, positioning them as fundamental components of early intercellular communication systems that linked sensory and motor functions with cellular metabolism.

Associated with the distal extracellular region of teneurins is a bioactive peptide fragment known as teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP). This peptide possesses primary structural similarity to members of the secretin superfamily of peptides, including vasoactive intestinal peptide and growth hormone-releasing hormone. Among the four TCAP isoforms corresponding to each teneurin paralogue, TCAP-1 has been most extensively studied and demonstrates significant biological activity in various physiological contexts. Previous research has established that TCAP-1 can modulate neural function, behavior, and reproductive physiology in rodent models, indicating its independent biological functionality beyond its role as part of the full-length teneurin protein.

Skeletal muscle represents one of the most critical tissues for whole-body glucose homeostasis, accounting for approximately 40% of glucose-associated energy requirements and responsible for up to 80% of insulin-stimulated glucose uptake under normal physiological conditions. The proper functioning of skeletal muscle is intrinsically linked to its metabolic capacity, with disruptions in glucose metabolism leading to impaired muscle performance and degradation. This relationship is particularly evident in metabolic disorders such as type 2 diabetes mellitus, where patients exhibit reduced skeletal muscle function as demonstrated by decreased grip strength compared to non-diabetic individuals. Similarly, type 1 diabetes mellitus, characterized by absolute insulin deficiency, results in severe impairments in glucose utilization by peripheral tissues including skeletal muscle.

Glycogen storage diseases represent another class of metabolic disorders affecting skeletal muscle function, where genetic defects in enzymes involved in glycogen metabolism lead to abnormal glycogen accumulation or depletion, resulting in muscle weakness, exercise intolerance, and other clinical manifestations. Current therapeutic approaches for these conditions remain limited, highlighting the need for novel treatment strategies that can enhance glucose availability and utilization in skeletal muscle independently of traditional insulin-dependent pathways. The discovery that TCAP peptides can modulate glucose transport through insulin-independent mechanisms presents a promising avenue for addressing these unmet medical needs, offering potential benefits for patients with various forms of diabetes, glycogen storage disorders, and other conditions characterized by impaired skeletal muscle energy metabolism.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel method for increasing energy available to skeletal muscle cells through the administration of teneurin C-terminal associated peptide (TCAP), particularly TCAP-1. This approach provides a means to enhance cellular energy metabolism without dependence on insulin signaling pathways, thereby offering therapeutic potential for various metabolic disorders. The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising isolated TCAP peptides formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, as well as methods for their administration to achieve improved skeletal muscle function through modulation of glucose metabolism.

The isolated TCAP peptide utilized in the present invention corresponds to the naturally occurring sequence derived from the carboxyl-terminal region of teneurin proteins. Specifically, the TCAP-1 peptide employed in the disclosed methods comprises an amino acid sequence that includes an N-terminal pyroglutamic acid residue to protect against N-terminal-directed peptidases and a C-terminal amidated residue consistent with the expected genomic sequence. This specific modification enhances the stability and bioavailability of the peptide while maintaining its biological activity. The carboxy terminal end of the TCAP peptide is particularly important for its biological function, as it contains structural elements essential for receptor binding and signal transduction.

Administration of TCAP peptides according to the present invention results in increased glucose uptake into skeletal muscle cells through insulin-independent mechanisms. This enhanced glucose transport leads to improved cellular energy availability, which is particularly advantageous in conditions where increased energy supply would be therapeutically beneficial. Such conditions include, but are not limited to, exercise performance enhancement, where improved energy availability can delay fatigue and improve endurance; diabetes management, where insulin-independent glucose uptake can help maintain glycemic control; and recovery from muscle trauma or exercise-induced injury, where enhanced energy supply supports tissue repair and regeneration processes.

The method of the present invention is particularly effective for promoting long-term energy availability in skeletal muscle tissue, making it suitable for both acute and chronic therapeutic applications. In the context of diabetes prevention and treatment, TCAP administration offers a novel approach to managing hyperglycemia by facilitating glucose disposal into peripheral tissues without requiring functional insulin signaling pathways. This is especially relevant for patients with type 1 diabetes who lack endogenous insulin production and those with type 2 diabetes who exhibit insulin resistance. The use of TCAP-1 specifically has been demonstrated to effectively modulate glucose metabolism in skeletal muscle cells, providing a foundation for its therapeutic application in various clinical settings where enhanced energy availability would be beneficial.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this patent application, the term "administering to the cell(s)" refers to the delivery of TCAP peptides to target cells either directly or indirectly through systemic circulation, resulting in contact between the peptide and its cellular receptors. An "effective amount" denotes the quantity of TCAP peptide sufficient to produce a measurable biological response in the target cells, while a "therapeutically effective amount" specifically refers to the dosage required to achieve a desired clinical outcome in the treatment or prevention of a particular condition. "Homeostasis" describes the maintenance of stable internal physiological conditions despite external changes, particularly with respect to glucose and energy balance in skeletal muscle cells.

"Insulin resistance" is defined as a pathological condition characterized by reduced responsiveness of target tissues, particularly skeletal muscle, to the actions of insulin, leading to impaired glucose uptake and utilization. "Muscle fatigue" refers to the decline in the ability of muscles to generate force during prolonged or intense activity, often associated with energy depletion and metabolic disturbances. "Muscle trauma" encompasses any physical injury to skeletal muscle tissue, including strains, contusions, and exercise-induced damage, which may benefit from enhanced energy availability during the recovery process.

"Neuronal cells" are specialized cells of the nervous system responsible for transmitting electrical and chemical signals, though the present invention primarily focuses on skeletal muscle applications. "Obvious chemical equivalents" include modifications to the TCAP peptide structure that maintain its biological activity, such as conservative amino acid substitutions or minor structural alterations that do not significantly affect receptor binding or signal transduction. A "pharmaceutically acceptable carrier" refers to any excipient, diluent, or vehicle suitable for delivering the active TCAP peptide to target tissues while maintaining its stability and bioavailability.

"Skeletal muscle cells" are multinucleated contractile cells responsible for voluntary movement and postural support, characterized by their striated appearance and high metabolic demands. "TCAP" and its variants encompass the family of peptides derived from the carboxyl-terminal region of teneurin proteins, including naturally occurring isoforms (TCAP-1 through TCAP-4) and synthetic analogues designed to maintain or enhance biological activity while improving pharmacokinetic properties.

### Applications: The Use of TCAP to Increase Energy Available to Skeletal Muscle Cells

The present invention discloses the use of TCAP peptides, particularly TCAP-1, to increase energy available to skeletal muscle cells through modulation of glucose metabolism. This application is based on the critical importance of glucose uptake in skeletal muscle cells for maintaining cellular energy homeostasis and supporting contractile function. Under normal physiological conditions, skeletal muscle accounts for the majority of insulin-stimulated glucose disposal, making it a key target tissue for interventions aimed at improving whole-body glucose metabolism.

In conditions characterized by insulin resistance, such as type 2 diabetes mellitus, the ability of skeletal muscle cells to take up glucose in response to insulin is significantly impaired, leading to hyperglycemia and reduced cellular energy availability. The TCAP-mediated pathway described herein provides an alternative mechanism for glucose uptake that operates independently of insulin signaling, thereby circumventing the limitations imposed by insulin resistance. This insulin-independent glucose transport mechanism offers significant therapeutic potential for both type 1 and type 2 diabetes management, as it can facilitate glucose disposal into peripheral tissues regardless of insulin status.

Beyond diabetes management, the application of TCAP peptides extends to enhancing exercise performance by improving energy availability during physical activity. By increasing glucose uptake into working muscles, TCAP administration can delay the onset of fatigue and improve endurance capacity, making it valuable for athletic performance enhancement and rehabilitation programs. Additionally, the promotion of recovery from muscle trauma or exercise-induced injury represents another important application, as enhanced energy availability supports the metabolic demands of tissue repair and regeneration processes.

The potential applications of TCAP peptides also encompass the treatment of glycogen storage disorders, where genetic defects in glycogen metabolism enzymes lead to abnormal energy storage and utilization in skeletal muscle. By providing an alternative pathway for glucose uptake and metabolism, TCAP administration may help compensate for these enzymatic deficiencies and improve muscle function. Furthermore, the ability of TCAP to enhance cellular energy availability makes it suitable for treating hypoxic conditions, where oxygen limitation impairs mitochondrial ATP production and necessitates alternative energy sources.

Detailed studies have demonstrated that TCAP administration significantly increases glucose uptake in skeletal muscle cells through mechanisms involving enhanced GLUT4 transporter expression and translocation to the plasma membrane. This effect is mediated through calcium-dependent signaling pathways that activate downstream metabolic processes, ultimately leading to increased ATP and NADH production. The comprehensive range of potential applications for TCAP peptides in increasing energy available to skeletal muscle cells underscores their therapeutic value across multiple clinical indications where improved energy metabolism would be beneficial.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Modes of Administration

The present invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising TCAP peptides, particularly TCAP-1, formulated with appropriate pharmaceutically acceptable carriers to ensure stability, bioavailability, and targeted delivery to skeletal muscle tissue. These compositions are designed to facilitate contact between the administered TCAP peptide and its sites of action within skeletal muscle cells, thereby enabling the desired modulation of glucose metabolism and energy availability.

Various modes of administration are contemplated for delivering TCAP peptides to target tissues, including parenteral routes such as subcutaneous, intravenous, intramuscular, and intraperitoneal injection, as well as non-invasive methods such as oral administration, inhalation, transdermal application, and rectal administration. Each route presents distinct advantages depending on the intended therapeutic application and desired pharmacokinetic profile. For instance, subcutaneous administration has been demonstrated to provide sustained release of TCAP peptides with favorable bioavailability characteristics, making it particularly suitable for chronic therapeutic applications.

To protect TCAP peptides from degradation by digestive enzymes and gastric acids when administered orally, specialized coatings and formulation strategies may be employed. These include enteric coatings that resist dissolution in the acidic environment of the stomach but dissolve in the more neutral pH of the small intestine, as well as encapsulation techniques using liposomes or nanoparticles that shield the peptide from enzymatic degradation while facilitating absorption across the intestinal epithelium. Direct administration to the desired site of action, such as local injection into affected muscle groups, may be advantageous for treating specific areas of muscle trauma or localized metabolic dysfunction.

Alternative delivery strategies involve the use of nucleic acids encoding TCAP peptides, which can be introduced into target cells using viral or non-viral vectors to enable endogenous production of the therapeutic peptide. This approach offers the potential for sustained, long-term expression of TCAP within target tissues, reducing the need for frequent administrations. Various vehicles or carriers may be utilized to enhance the delivery and stability of TCAP peptides, including polymeric matrices, hydrogels, and microparticle formulations that provide controlled release profiles tailored to specific therapeutic requirements.

Pharmaceutical compositions containing TCAP peptides can be prepared using well-established methods known in the art of pharmaceutical formulation, ensuring appropriate sterility, stability, and compatibility with the chosen route of administration. Inactive viral carriers, such as adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) or lentiviruses, may be employed for gene therapy approaches involving nucleic acid delivery, providing efficient transduction of target cells while minimizing immunogenic responses. The versatility of administration routes and formulation strategies available for TCAP-based therapeutics enables customization of treatment protocols to meet the specific needs of different clinical applications and patient populations.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Peptide Synthesis

The synthesis of mouse TCAP-1 peptide was accomplished through solid-phase peptide synthesis using automated methodology. The determined sequence of mouse TCAP-1 was identical to that of rat TCAP-1, allowing for cross-species application in experimental studies. A mouse paralogue of the putative peptide sequence was synthesized to confirm structural conservation across species. The synthesis employed an automated peptide synthesizer utilizing continuous flow Fmoc (9-fluorenylmethoxycarbonyl) chemistry, which provides high efficiency and purity for peptide production. The coupling reactions were performed using diisopropyl ethyl amine as the base and Fmoc-amino acid derivatives as building blocks, ensuring proper stereochemistry and sequence fidelity.

Deprotection of the Fmoc group was achieved using piperidine solution, followed by thorough washing steps to remove residual reagents before subsequent coupling cycles. Upon completion of the linear peptide chain assembly, the final peptide was cleaved from the resin support and simultaneously deprotected using standard cleavage cocktails containing trifluoroacetic acid and scavenging agents to prevent side reactions. The crude peptide product was then subjected to desalting procedures to remove residual salts and organic solvents, followed by lyophilization to obtain a stable powder form suitable for storage and subsequent use in biological assays.

Additional peptide variants were synthesized to support various experimental objectives, including rat K37-OT K8-TCAP-1 for structural studies and biotinylated K37-TCAP-1 for detection and localization experiments. The biotinylated peptide was purified using affinity chromatography techniques to ensure high purity and proper biotinylation efficiency. A scrambled TCAP control peptide was also produced, maintaining the same amino acid composition as native TCAP-1 but with randomized sequence order, serving as a negative control to distinguish specific biological effects from non-specific interactions.

Antisera against TCAP-1 were generated by conjugating synthetic TCAP-1 peptides to keyhole limpet hemocyanin (KLH) carrier protein to enhance immunogenicity. Rabbits were immunized with the conjugated peptides following standard protocols, and antisera were collected at appropriate intervals to assess antibody titers and specificity. Binding specificity of the generated antisera was rigorously evaluated using competitive binding assays and Western blot analysis to ensure recognition of native TCAP-1 without cross-reactivity to unrelated peptides.

Fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC)-conjugated TCAP-1 was prepared for cellular localization studies by reacting purified TCAP-1 with FITC under controlled conditions to achieve optimal labeling efficiency while preserving biological activity. The FITC-TCAP-1 conjugate was purified using size-exclusion chromatography and characterized by spectroscopic methods to confirm proper conjugation and fluorescence properties. Uptake studies of TCAP-1 in plasma were conducted by administering the peptide intravenously or subcutaneously to experimental animals and collecting blood samples at predetermined time points for analysis of peptide concentration and stability.

Autoradiography procedures were developed to visualize TCAP-1 distribution in tissues using radiolabeled peptide preparations. Functional positron emission tomography (PET) studies were designed to assess glucose metabolism changes following TCAP-1 administration, with detailed protocols established for image acquisition and analysis. RNA extraction for RT-PCR analysis was optimized using TRIzol reagent, followed by reverse transcription and PCR amplification using gene-specific primers to evaluate teneurin and latrophilin expression patterns. Signal transduction studies focused on MEK-ERK-AMPK phosphorylation cascades, with specific assays developed for DAG and IP3 measurements to elucidate downstream signaling events. Various biochemical assays including BCA protein quantification, ELISA procedures, and Fluo-4 fluorescence measurements for calcium imaging were standardized to support comprehensive mechanistic investigations of TCAP-1 biological activity.

### Example 2—In-Vivo Experiments

Animal studies were conducted in accordance with institutional animal care guidelines to investigate the pharmacokinetics and biological effects of TCAP-1 administration. Male Wistar rats were selected for these experiments and maintained under controlled environmental conditions with appropriate monitoring for health and welfare throughout the study period. The experimental design for TCAP-1 uptake studies involved both intravenous (IV) and subcutaneous (SC) administration routes to compare bioavailability and tissue distribution profiles.

Following administration of radiolabeled 125I-TCAP-1, plasma samples were collected at multiple time points to measure peptide concentration and calculate pharmacokinetic parameters including plasma clearance half-life. Area under the curve (AUC) analysis was performed to compare systemic exposure between IV and SC administration routes, providing insights into relative bioavailability. Brain uptake studies were conducted to assess central nervous system penetration, with specific brain regions analyzed for 125I-TCAP-1 accumulation using gamma counting techniques.

Functional positron emission tomography (fPET) scan protocols were implemented to evaluate glucose metabolism changes in response to TCAP-1 treatment. Animals received intrascapular SC injections of TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) or vehicle control, followed by intravenous administration of [18F]-2-deoxyglucose radiotracer three days post-treatment. PET imaging was performed using a Siemens Inveon microPET scanner with standardized acquisition parameters, including body temperature maintenance and static emission data collection over 20 minutes. Image analysis was conducted using Amira 5.5.0 software, with regions of interest (ROIs) delineated based on standardized uptake values to quantify glucose metabolism in specific brain regions and peripheral tissues.

Glucose uptake measurements in various tissues were compared between treatment groups to assess the systemic effects of TCAP-1 administration. Statistical analysis revealed significant differences in glucose metabolism patterns between TCAP-1-treated and control animals, with particular emphasis on skeletal muscle and brain regions. The results demonstrated enhanced glucose uptake in hind-limb skeletal muscle following TCAP-1 administration, confirming the peptide's ability to modulate peripheral glucose metabolism. These findings were consistent with observed decreases in serum glucose levels and provided strong evidence for TCAP-1's role in regulating whole-body energy homeostasis through insulin-independent mechanisms.

### Example 3—Effect of TCAP on Blood Glucose Levels

In vivo experiments investigating the effects of TCAP-1 on blood glucose levels were designed to evaluate both acute and chronic responses to peptide administration. Male Sprague-Dawley rats were randomly assigned to treatment groups receiving either TCAP-1 (10 nmoles/kg) or vehicle control via subcutaneous injection. Blood collection protocols were standardized to minimize stress-induced glucose fluctuations, with samples obtained through jugular vein catheterization at predetermined time points following treatment administration.

Serum processing procedures included immediate centrifugation to separate plasma from cellular components, followed by storage at -80°C until analysis. Hematology analysis was performed to assess potential systemic effects of TCAP-1 treatment on blood parameters. Muscle and liver tissue samples were collected at sacrifice for glycogen content determination using established enzymatic assays. Blood glucose levels were monitored over time using glucometer measurements and confirmed through laboratory-based glucose oxidase assays.

Comparison of blood glucose levels between treatment groups revealed significant reductions in TCAP-1-treated animals compared to vehicle controls. Experiment 1 demonstrated acute glucose-lowering effects within 30 minutes of TCAP-1 administration, with maximal effects observed at 2 hours post-injection. Experiment 2 evaluated longer-term effects, showing sustained glucose reduction over 24 hours following single-dose administration. Experiment 3 investigated chronic treatment effects, with daily TCAP-1 administration for five consecutive days resulting in progressive improvements in glucose tolerance and insulin sensitivity indices.

The results consistently demonstrated TCAP-1's ability to lower blood glucose levels through mechanisms independent of insulin secretion or action. Glycogen content analysis revealed increased storage in both liver and muscle tissues of treated animals, suggesting enhanced glucose disposal and utilization. These findings were corroborated by improved glucose tolerance test results and reduced fasting glucose levels in chronically treated animals. The implications of these results support TCAP-1's therapeutic potential for managing hyperglycemia in diabetic conditions, particularly where insulin resistance or deficiency limits conventional treatment options.

### Example 4—Glucose Insulin and Glucagon Changes in Blood

The investigation into the effects of TCAP-1 on glucose, insulin, and glucagon dynamics in blood revealed complex regulatory interactions that contribute to improved glucose homeostasis. Following TCAP-1 administration, blood samples were analyzed for concurrent changes in these key metabolic hormones to understand the peptide's mechanism of action. The results demonstrated that while blood glucose levels decreased significantly in TCAP-1-treated animals, insulin concentrations remained relatively stable compared to vehicle controls, confirming the insulin-independent nature of TCAP-1's glucose-lowering effects.

Interestingly, glucagon levels showed a modest but statistically significant reduction in TCAP-1-treated animals, suggesting potential modulation of pancreatic alpha-cell function or hepatic glucose production. This finding indicates that TCAP-1 may influence multiple aspects of glucose regulation beyond peripheral tissue uptake, potentially contributing to its overall efficacy in maintaining glycemic control. The dissociation between glucose lowering and insulin secretion provides strong evidence for TCAP-1's unique mechanism of action, distinguishing it from conventional antidiabetic agents that primarily target insulin pathways.

### Example 5—Liver and Muscle Glycogen Content in Extracted Liver of TCAP-1 Treated Wistar Rats

Analysis of glycogen content in liver and muscle tissues from TCAP-1-treated Wistar rats provided direct evidence of enhanced glucose storage following peptide administration. Liver glycogen content measurements revealed a significant 2.5-fold increase in TCAP-1-treated animals compared to vehicle controls, indicating improved hepatic glucose disposal and storage capacity. This finding correlates with the observed reductions in blood glucose levels and suggests that TCAP-1 promotes glycogen synthesis in the liver through mechanisms that may involve activation of glycogen synthase or inhibition of glycogen phosphorylase.

Muscle glycogen content analysis demonstrated an even more pronounced effect, with TCAP-1-treated animals showing a 3.2-fold increase in skeletal muscle glycogen stores compared to controls. This substantial enhancement in muscle glycogen deposition aligns with the demonstrated increase in glucose uptake into skeletal muscle tissue and supports the hypothesis that TCAP-1 facilitates glucose utilization for energy storage in peripheral tissues. The preferential accumulation of glycogen in muscle tissue versus liver may reflect the higher density of TCAP receptors or more robust signaling responses in skeletal muscle cells.

### Example 6—Haematology Assays

Haematology assays conducted on blood samples from TCAP-1-treated animals revealed no significant adverse effects on hematological parameters, supporting the safety profile of the peptide for therapeutic applications. Complete blood counts, including red blood cell count, hemoglobin concentration, hematocrit, white blood cell differential, and platelet count, remained within normal physiological ranges across all treatment groups. These findings indicate that TCAP-1 administration does not induce hematological toxicity or immune system activation at the tested doses, providing reassurance regarding its potential for clinical development.

### Example 7—Localization of TCAP in Muscle Tissue

The localization of TCAP-1 in muscle tissue was investigated using FITC-conjugated TCAP-1 in binding assays to determine specific cellular targets and distribution patterns. Results from these studies demonstrated specific binding of FITC-TCAP-1 to sarcolemmal regions of skeletal muscle fibers, with punctate staining patterns consistent with receptor localization at specialized membrane domains. The binding was competitively inhibited by excess unlabeled TCAP-1, confirming specificity of the interaction. Co-localization studies with β-dystroglycan revealed overlapping staining patterns, suggesting association of TCAP receptors with the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex at the sarcolemma.

### Example 8—Localization of TCAP-1 Relative to β-Dystroglycan in Skeletal Cells

Further investigation into the localization of TCAP-1 relative to β-dystroglycan in skeletal cells confirmed their close spatial relationship at the sarcolemmal membrane. Immunofluorescence co-staining experiments demonstrated that TCAP-1 immunoreactivity was predominantly localized to regions coincident with β-dystroglycan expression, supporting the hypothesis that TCAP signaling complexes associate with the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex. This association may facilitate mechanical coupling between extracellular matrix components and intracellular cytoskeletal elements, potentially contributing to TCAP's effects on muscle contractile function and structural integrity.

The localization experiment examining TCAP-1 relative to rapsyn in skeletal cells revealed significant co-localization at the sarcolemmal membrane. Immunoreactivity co-staining of TCAP-1 and rapsyn demonstrated that both proteins exhibited overlapping distribution patterns along the sarcolemma, with TCAP-1 immunoreactivity clearly visible on the sarcolemmal surface. Similarly, rapsyn localization was observed on the sarcolemmal membrane, consistent with its known role in clustering acetylcholine receptors at neuromuscular junctions. The co-localization of TCAP-1 and rapsyn suggests potential functional interactions between these proteins in regulating neuromuscular transmission or postsynaptic organization.

Investigations into brain glucose metabolism demonstrated TCAP-1's ability to enhance glucose uptake in hypothalamic neurons through GLUT3 upregulation, with corresponding increases in pyruvate levels and ATP production.

Studies in diabetic Goto-Kakizaki (GK) rats demonstrated TCAP-1's therapeutic potential for managing hyperglycemia in insulin-resistant conditions. Chronic TCAP-1 treatment resulted in significant reductions in plasma glucose levels without affecting insulin secretion, confirming its insulin-independent mechanism of action. Functional PET studies in GK rats revealed enhanced glucose uptake in peripheral tissues, particularly skeletal muscle, supporting the peptide's ability to bypass insulin resistance mechanisms. These findings collectively establish TCAP-1 as a promising therapeutic agent for diabetes management and other conditions characterized by impaired glucose metabolism.

The comprehensive investigation of TCAP-1's effects on glucose metabolism, spanning from molecular mechanisms to whole-animal physiology, provides compelling evidence for its therapeutic potential across multiple clinical indications. The demonstration of TCAP-1's ability to enhance glucose uptake through insulin-independent mechanisms, coupled with its favorable safety profile and diverse physiological effects, positions it as a novel therapeutic approach for managing metabolic disorders and improving skeletal muscle function in various pathological conditions.
