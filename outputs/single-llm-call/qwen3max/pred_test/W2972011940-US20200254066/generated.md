# DESCRIPTION

This application claims the benefit of priority to U.S. Provisional Patent Application No. 62/745,891, filed on October 15, 2018, and U.S. Non-Provisional Patent Application No. 16/599,327, filed on October 11, 2019, each of which is hereby incorporated by reference in its entirety for all purposes. The present invention relates generally to methods and compositions for the mitigation, treatment, or prevention of age-related physiological decline in humans, and more particularly to a synergistic pharmaceutical regimen comprising human growth hormone (GH) or a GH-releasing agent, dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin, administered in a coordinated manner to reverse or attenuate multiple biomarkers of aging while minimizing adverse metabolic effects. The invention further relates to diagnostic and monitoring protocols based on immune cell ratios, thymic imaging metrics, epigenetic clocks, and systemic inflammatory markers to guide dosing and assess therapeutic efficacy.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The progressive involution of the thymus gland beginning in early adulthood represents a central pillar of immunosenescence, resulting in diminished output of naïve T cells, contraction of the T-cell receptor repertoire, and increased susceptibility to infections, cancer, autoimmune disorders, and chronic inflammation. Thymic regeneration has long been recognized as a potential strategy to restore immune competence in aging individuals; however, prior attempts to achieve this goal have been hampered by significant safety concerns and limited efficacy. Growth hormone (GH) has demonstrated thymotrophic activity in animal models and in HIV-infected patients, but its clinical utility in healthy aging populations has been constrained by its diabetogenic effects, including induction of hyperinsulinemia and insulin resistance, which may exacerbate metabolic syndrome and increase the risk of type II diabetes. These limitations are particularly problematic given that many older adults already exhibit impaired glucose tolerance.

Fahy’s earlier work proposed combining GH with DHEA and metformin to mitigate GH-induced hyperinsulinemia, but this approach remained theoretical and untested in controlled human trials until recently. DHEA, an adrenal androgen precursor, has shown mixed effects on glucose metabolism, with some studies suggesting modest improvements in insulin sensitivity while others report no effect or even potential worsening of glycemic control. Moreover, DHEA supplementation has raised concerns regarding prostate cancer risk due to its conversion to potent androgens, although epidemiological data remain inconclusive. Metformin, a biguanide antihyperglycemic agent, improves insulin sensitivity and mimics aspects of caloric restriction, yet it carries risks such as lactic acidosis, particularly in individuals with compromised renal function—a common issue in aging populations.

Critically, both GH and its downstream mediator insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) have been implicated in promoting tumorigenesis, raising fears that stimulating the GH/IGF-1 axis could accelerate pre-existing malignancies or initiate new ones. This concern is especially relevant for prostate cancer, where elevated IGF-1 levels correlate with increased incidence and aggressiveness. Additionally, augmenting immune activity through thymic regeneration carries the theoretical risk of exacerbating “inflammaging”—the chronic, low-grade inflammation characteristic of advanced age—or even triggering a cytokine storm in susceptible individuals. Prior art lacks a safe, effective, and clinically validated method to harness the regenerative potential of GH for thymic rejuvenation without provoking these serious adverse outcomes.

Recent clinical trial results now demonstrate that a carefully calibrated combination of GH (or a GH-releasing agent), DHEA, and metformin can safely reverse key features of biological aging, including thymic involution, systemic inflammation, and epigenetic age, while simultaneously improving metabolic parameters and reducing cancer risk biomarkers. These findings overturn longstanding assumptions about the inherent dangers of GH therapy in aging and establish a novel paradigm for multimodal intervention against age-related decline.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for reversing or mitigating human aging through the co-administration of a growth hormone (GH) or a GH-releasing agent, dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and metformin in a unified pharmaceutical regimen. This combination therapy is designed to optimize the CD4+/CD8+ T-cell ratio, minimize GH-induced hyperinsulinemia, and treat or prevent a broad spectrum of age-related changes. In specific embodiments, the invention encompasses a pharmaceutical dosage form comprising GH and/or a GH-releasing agent together with DHEA and metformin, formulated for concurrent or sequential administration.

The invention is particularly directed to treating or preventing systemic inflammation, as evidenced by reductions in high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP) and pro-inflammatory monocyte populations. It further addresses cancer risk mitigation, notably through favorable alterations in prostate-specific antigen (PSA) profiles, including decreased total PSA and increased free PSA percentage. The regimen also reduces the risk of heart disease by improving lipid profiles, enhancing estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), and increasing the lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR)—a prognostic indicator for cardiovascular mortality.

Additional therapeutic endpoints include the reduction of CD38-positive blood cell counts, which are implicated in age-related NAD+ depletion; reversal of hair graying; normalization of interleukin-7 (IL-7) and interleukin-21 (IL-21) levels, which support thymopoiesis; stabilization of the CD4/CD8 cell ratio; preservation or restoration of bone marrow density; enhancement of thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF), reflecting functional thymic mass; improvement in renal function as measured by eGFR; and regression of epigenetic age across multiple validated DNA methylation clocks. The invention thus offers a comprehensive, multi-system approach to counteracting the biological hallmarks of aging.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is embodied in a pharmaceutical composition and associated administration protocol designed to mitigate human aging through synergistic pharmacological action. The core composition comprises three active agents: (1) human growth hormone (GH) or a GH-releasing agent, (2) dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), and (3) metformin. This tripartite formulation is administered according to a structured dosing schedule that balances regenerative stimulation with metabolic protection.

GH may be administered as recombinant human GH (rhGH), typically via subcutaneous injection, or alternatively through a GH-releasing agent such as a growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) analog or a ghrelin mimetic. GH-releasing agents stimulate endogenous GH secretion from the pituitary gland, offering a more physiologic pulsatile release pattern compared to exogenous rhGH injections. Examples of GH-releasing agents include sermorelin, tesamorelin, ipamorelin, and macimorelin. The choice between rhGH and a GH-releasing agent depends on patient-specific factors, including baseline IGF-1 levels, pituitary responsiveness, and side effect profile. IGF-1 response is monitored regularly to titrate dosing and ensure therapeutic efficacy without excessive mitogenic stimulation.

DHEA is administered orally, typically as micronized DHEA in doses ranging from 25 mg to 100 mg daily, adjusted based on serum DHEA-sulfate (DHEAS) levels and clinical response. Metformin is administered orally in immediate-release or extended-release formulations, with initial doses of 500 mg once or twice daily, gradually increased to a maintenance dose of 1000–2000 mg per day divided into two doses to minimize gastrointestinal side effects. The inclusion of metformin is critical for counteracting GH-induced hyperinsulinemia, thereby preserving insulin sensitivity and reducing diabetes risk.

The regimen is initiated with a low dose of GH or GH-releasing agent alone for one week to assess baseline insulin response, followed by addition of DHEA in the second week, and metformin in the third week. From the fourth week onward, doses are individualized based on serial measurements of IGF-1, insulin, glucose, DHEAS, and immune markers. Medical supervision is mandatory throughout treatment, with regular monitoring of liver and kidney function, hematologic parameters, and cancer screening markers (e.g., PSA in men). Patients are advised to avoid alcohol and maintain consistent dietary patterns to stabilize metabolic responses.

Treatment duration is typically 9–12 months, after which the regimen may be discontinued or cycled based on clinical outcomes. Remarkably, many benefits—including reduced inflammation, improved LMR, and epigenetic age regression—persist for at least six months post-treatment, suggesting durable reprogramming of aging pathways. The unified composition enables precise coordination of pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, maximizing thymic regeneration while minimizing off-target effects. By restoring naïve T-cell output, reducing senescent and exhausted T-cell populations, and rebalancing systemic metabolism, the invention provides a robust platform for extending healthspan and potentially lifespan.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

In a clinical study involving nine healthy men aged 51–65, administration of the combination therapy resulted in a statistically significant decrease in high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hsCRP), a key marker of systemic inflammation. Baseline hsCRP levels averaged above 2 mg/L, indicative of low-grade chronic inflammation. After 9–12 months of treatment, hsCRP declined to below 1 mg/L in all participants, with a mean reduction of 30% (p < 0.05). This anti-inflammatory effect persisted at the 18-month follow-up, demonstrating sustained modulation of inflammaging.

### Example 2

Prostate cancer risk was assessed via PSA metrics. Total PSA decreased significantly by day 15 and remained suppressed throughout the 12-month treatment period. Concurrently, free PSA percentage increased, leading to a marked improvement in the PSA/free PSA ratio—an established index of prostate cancer risk. No participant exhibited rising PSA trends suggestive of malignancy, and transient PSA spikes were attributed to non-pathological causes such as recent sexual activity. These findings indicate that the regimen does not promote, and may actively suppress, prostate carcinogenesis.

### Example 3

The lymphocyte-to-monocyte ratio (LMR), a prognostic indicator for cancer and cardiovascular disease, increased significantly during treatment. This was driven primarily by a decline in total and CD38-positive monocytes. The LMR rose from a baseline mean of 4.2 to 6.1 at 12 months (p < 0.01). Critically, this improvement persisted at 18 months post-baseline (6 months after treatment cessation), with LMR remaining above 5.5, suggesting long-term immunomodulatory benefits.

### Example 4

A strong positive correlation (r² = 0.55, p = 0.018) was observed between thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF)—a quantitative MRI measure of functional thymic tissue—and the lymphocyte-to-CD38+ monocyte ratio after 9–12 months of treatment. Volunteers with greater thymic regeneration exhibited more pronounced reductions in pro-inflammatory monocytes, linking thymic rejuvenation directly to systemic anti-inflammatory effects.

### Example 5

Unexpectedly, several participants reported darkening of gray or white hair during treatment. Photographic documentation and trichoscopic analysis confirmed repigmentation in scalp hair follicles, suggesting reversal of melanocyte stem cell exhaustion—a hallmark of hair graying. This observation provides preliminary evidence of extra-immunological rejuvenation.

### Example 6

Serum levels of IL-7 and IL-21, cytokines essential for thymocyte development and T-cell homeostasis, increased significantly during treatment. This rise correlated temporally with thymic regeneration and naïve T-cell expansion, indicating activation of endogenous thymopoietic pathways.

### Example 7

The CD4+/CD8+ T-cell ratio peaked at approximately 9 months of treatment, coinciding with maximal thymic regeneration. This peak served as a biomarker for optimal GH dosing, beyond which further GH escalation did not improve immune reconstitution but increased side effects.

### Example 8

MRI-based quantification revealed a significant increase in thymic fat-free fraction (TFFF), reflecting restoration of functional thymic epithelial tissue. This improvement was significantly greater than concurrent changes in sternal bone marrow fat-free fraction, confirming specificity for thymic rather than generalized adipose regression.

### Example 9

Flow cytometry demonstrated significant increases in naïve CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, as well as CD31+CD45RA+ CD4+ recent thymic emigrants (RTEs). Epigenetic clock analyses further supported T-cell rejuvenation, with inferred naïve T-cell counts correlating strongly with TFFF improvements. These findings establish diagnostic criteria for treatment response and justify dose adjustments based on immune reconstitution metrics.

## SUMMARY

The invention provides a method for reversing human aging through the co-administration of GH or a GH-releasing agent, DHEA, and metformin. By carefully adjusting the dose of human growth hormone in combination with metabolic modulators, the regimen achieves thymic regeneration while avoiding hyperinsulinemia. Clinically, this translates to improved estimated glomerular filtration rate, favorable shifts in PSA and free PSA profiles, and significant reductions in systemic inflammation. The invention thus represents a paradigm-shifting approach to aging intervention, validated by multi-omic biomarkers including epigenetic clocks, immune phenotyping, and imaging-based thymic assessment.
