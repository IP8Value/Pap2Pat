## BACKGROUND

Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) is a complex neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent impairments in social communication and interaction, as well as restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities. The diagnostic criteria for ASD, as defined in the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition (DSM-5), encompass a broad range of symptom severity and functional impairment, reflecting the heterogeneous nature of the disorder. Epidemiological studies indicate that approximately one in 68 children in the United States is diagnosed with ASD, with prevalence rates continuing to rise over recent decades. Despite advances in behavioral and educational interventions, the current clinical diagnostic process for ASD remains largely reliant on observational assessments, developmental history, and standardized psychological evaluations, which typically culminate in a formal diagnosis only after the age of four years. This diagnostic delay significantly impedes the timely initiation of evidence-based early intervention strategies, which are known to yield substantial improvements in cognitive, communicative, and social outcomes when implemented during critical periods of neuroplasticity in early childhood.

The limitations of current diagnostic approaches for ASD are multifaceted. First, the reliance on subjective behavioral observations introduces variability in diagnostic accuracy across clinicians and settings. Second, the absence of objective biological markers necessitates prolonged evaluation periods, often involving multiple clinical visits and multidisciplinary assessments. Third, the heterogeneity of ASD phenotypes complicates the identification of consistent diagnostic criteria applicable across diverse populations. Although genetic studies have identified numerous risk variants associated with ASD, these account for only a minority of cases—approximately 20%—and fail to capture the influence of environmental, epigenetic, and metabolic factors that contribute to the disorder’s pathogenesis. Moreover, genomic approaches exhibit limited predictive power, with reported accuracy rates ranging from 56% to 70%, and are susceptible to confounding by population stratification. Critically, existing diagnostic paradigms do not incorporate biochemical or metabolic profiling, thereby overlooking potential endophenotypic signatures that may reflect the integrated effects of genetic predisposition, environmental exposures, and physiological dysregulation. These shortcomings underscore the urgent need for an objective, biologically grounded method capable of facilitating earlier and more reliable identification of individuals at risk for ASD, thereby enabling prompt therapeutic intervention and improved long-term prognosis.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for identifying a metabolomic signature associated with autism spectrum disorder (ASD) through the comprehensive analysis of biosamples using orthogonal high-resolution mass spectrometry techniques. In accordance with the invention, biosamples are obtained from subjects diagnosed with ASD and from non-autistic control subjects. These biosamples are assayed using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS) to detect and quantify small molecule metabolites. Concurrently, the same biosamples are analyzed using untargeted liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (LC/HRMS) to capture a broader and complementary set of metabolites. Differentially produced metabolites—those exhibiting statistically significant abundance differences between the ASD and control groups—are identified from each analytical platform. The metabolites detected by GC-MS and LC/HRMS are then combined into a unified dataset, from which a subset of metabolites demonstrating statistically significant differential abundance is selected to define a metabolomic signature for ASD.

In an alternative embodiment, the invention encompasses a generalized method for identifying a metabolomic signature for ASD by assaying biosamples using two or more distinct analytical methodologies, such as different chromatographic separation modes or ionization techniques. Differentially produced metabolites are identified independently from each methodology, and the resulting metabolite sets are integrated. A subset of metabolites exhibiting statistically significant abundance differences across the combined dataset is then selected to constitute the metabolomic signature. Variations of the method include the use of specific biosample types, such as blood plasma, serum, or dried blood spots, and the focus on metabolites within a defined molecular weight range, typically between 50 and 1,500 Daltons. The selection of metabolites for inclusion in the signature may be performed using univariate statistical tests, multivariate analysis, or machine learning algorithms, including support vector machines (SVM) or partial least squares discriminant analysis (PLS-DA).

The metabolomic signature for ASD, as defined by the invention, comprises a specific panel of metabolites whose abundance levels are consistently altered in individuals with ASD relative to non-autistic controls. This signature may include, but is not limited to, homocitrulline, glutaric acid, saccharopine, 5-aminovaleric acid, lactate, and succinate. The signature may consist of at least 40 metabolites, or more specifically, between about 80 and about 160 metabolites. The method for determining the metabolomic signature involves quantifying the abundance of these metabolites in a biosample obtained from a subject, comparing the measured abundances to reference values derived from non-autistic control subjects, and interpreting deviations as indicative of increased risk for ASD. The biosample may be sourced from blood, plasma, serum, urine, or cerebrospinal fluid, with blood plasma being particularly preferred due to its stability and accessibility.

The invention further provides a method for assessing the risk of ASD in a subject by quantifying the abundance of one or more metabolites included in the defined metabolomic signature. The method involves obtaining a biosample from a subject within a specified age range, preferably between 1 and 6 years of age, and measuring the concentration of signature metabolites using GC-MS, LC/HRMS, or other validated analytical platforms. The measured abundances are compared to those observed in a reference population of non-autistic individuals, and a statistically significant difference in the abundance of one or more metabolites is interpreted as indicating an increased risk of ASD. In certain embodiments, the risk assessment is based on the ratio of two or more metabolites, or on a composite score derived from the combined abundances of multiple metabolites. The method may be tailored to specific phenotypic subpopulations, such as individuals with high-functioning or low-functioning autism, by defining sub-signatures that reflect the metabolic profiles characteristic of these subgroups.

For the purposes of this specification, the term “metabolite” refers to any small molecule involved in or resulting from metabolic processes, including amino acids, organic acids, fatty acids, sterols, and microbial co-metabolites. The term “biosample” denotes any biological material suitable for metabolomic analysis, including whole blood, plasma, serum, urine, saliva, or tissue extracts. The phrase “statistically significant abundance difference” indicates a difference in metabolite concentration between ASD and control groups that meets a predefined threshold of significance, typically a p-value less than 0.05, with or without correction for multiple comparisons. Preferred embodiments of the invention utilize biosamples collected under standardized fasting conditions to minimize dietary confounders, and employ rigorous quality control measures during sample preparation and analysis. The method may be implemented as a discrete series of steps or as an integrated workflow combining sample processing, metabolite quantification, statistical analysis, and risk interpretation. Representative examples include assays using C8-positive, C8-negative, HILIC-positive, and HILIC-negative LC/HRMS modes, either individually or in combination, to maximize metabolome coverage. The invention also contemplates kits comprising reagents, standards, and instructions for performing the disclosed methods, thereby facilitating clinical translation and commercial deployment.
