# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to methods and systems for the real-time evaluation of illness severity in critically ill patients through the quantitative measurement of adenosine 5′-triphosphate (ATP) levels in biological samples. More specifically, the invention provides a novel diagnostic approach that utilizes ATP concentration—extracted using a phenol-based reagent—as a primary biomarker, optionally in combination with intermediate metabolites such as lactic acid and ketone bodies, to generate composite severity scores that reflect the patient’s energetic and metabolic status. This method enables rapid, objective, and continuous assessment of disease progression, treatment response, and mortality risk in intensive care settings, offering significant advantages over conventional scoring systems that rely on complex clinical parameters and retrospective data aggregation.

## BACKGROUND ART

Adenosine 5′-triphosphate (ATP) serves as the principal energy currency in all living cells, underpinning essential physiological processes including ion transport, biosynthesis, muscle contraction, and signal transduction. Cellular ATP is predominantly generated through mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, glycolysis, and fatty acid β-oxidation. In critical illness—such as sepsis, multiple organ failure, or severe trauma—mitochondrial dysfunction often leads to impaired ATP synthesis, resulting in cellular energy depletion. Concurrently, anaerobic metabolism increases lactate production, while disrupted fatty acid oxidation elevates ketone body levels. These metabolic shifts are hallmarks of bioenergetic crisis and correlate strongly with poor clinical outcomes.

Conventional methods for assessing illness severity in intensive care units (ICUs) include the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation II (APACHE II) and the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) scoring systems. While these tools incorporate physiological, laboratory, and demographic variables to estimate mortality risk, they suffer from significant limitations: they are time-consuming to calculate, require data not always immediately available from monitoring equipment, and exhibit inter-observer variability. Moreover, both APACHE II and SOFA are static assessments typically performed within fixed time windows (e.g., first 24 hours of ICU admission), rendering them poorly suited for real-time monitoring of dynamic changes in patient condition during treatment.

A major obstacle in leveraging ATP as a clinical biomarker has been the technical challenge of accurate extraction and quantification from high-protein biological matrices such as whole blood. Traditional ATP extraction reagents—including trichloroacetic acid, perchloric acid, and ethylene glycol—are chaotropic agents that cause protein denaturation and co-precipitation of ATP, leading to substantial underestimation of true intracellular concentrations, particularly in samples rich in hemoglobin and other proteins. This limitation has historically precluded reliable use of blood ATP levels in clinical diagnostics.

To address this, a revolutionary ATP extraction technique employing a phenol compound solution—specifically Tris-EDTA-saturated phenol—has been developed. This method efficiently liberates ATP from blood cells without co-precipitation, enabling highly reproducible and sensitive quantification even in complex biological fluids. Coupled with the luciferin-luciferase bioluminescence assay, this approach yields precise ATP measurements that reflect the net balance of cellular energy production, consumption, and release.

Given the urgent need for real-time, objective, and metabolically grounded evaluation methods in critical care, there exists a compelling opportunity to exploit ATP and its associated metabolic intermediates as dynamic biomarkers. The present invention fulfills this unmet need by introducing a novel framework for illness severity assessment based on quantifiable biochemical parameters rather than aggregated clinical scores.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

The present invention aims to provide a novel, real-time, and biochemically grounded method for evaluating the severity of illness in critically ill patients. Conventional scoring systems such as APACHE II and SOFA are inadequate for dynamic monitoring due to their reliance on static, multi-parameter assessments that are labor-intensive and delayed. The invention solves this problem by introducing ATP—measured via a robust extraction protocol—as a direct indicator of cellular bioenergetic status, thereby enabling immediate, objective, and serial evaluation of patient condition and prognosis.

### Means to Solve the Object

The invention achieves its objective through a multi-step analytical process. First, a biological sample—preferably whole blood—is collected from the patient. ATP is then extracted from the sample using a phenol compound solution, specifically phenol saturated with Tris-EDTA buffer, which effectively solubilizes ATP without co-precipitating it with denatured proteins. To enhance extraction efficiency, a protein denaturant may be added to the phenol solution, although the phenol itself acts as a potent denaturant. The pH of the phenol compound solution is maintained within the range of 7.0 to 9.0, preferably around 8.0, to stabilize ATP and prevent hydrolysis.

Following extraction, the ATP concentration in the aqueous phase is quantified using a bioluminescent reagent containing luciferin and luciferase, wherein light emission is directly proportional to ATP amount. In parallel, the levels of lactic acid and/or ketone bodies in the same or a corresponding serum sample are measured using standard enzymatic or colorimetric assays. From these measurements, composite scores are calculated: the ATP-Lactate Energy Score (A-LES), defined as the ratio of lactic acid concentration (mM) to ATP concentration (mM); and the ATP-Ketone Energy Score (A-KES), defined as the ratio of total ketone body concentration (mM) to ATP concentration (mM). These ratios amplify the diagnostic signal by integrating energy deficit (low ATP) with metabolic stress markers (elevated lactate or ketones).

### Effect of the Invention

The invention enables accurate, real-time evaluation of illness severity based on fundamental bioenergetic principles. By measuring ATP alone, clinicians gain insight into the patient’s cellular energy reserves. When combined with lactic acid or ketone body levels, the A-LES or A-KES provides a more comprehensive and sensitive indicator of metabolic crisis, strongly correlating with organ dysfunction, treatment response, and mortality risk. Unlike APACHE II or SOFA, these scores can be updated continuously with each blood draw, offering dynamic feedback on therapeutic efficacy and disease trajectory. Clinical validation demonstrates that A-LES values above 20 are highly predictive of non-survival, while values below 5.5 correlate with favorable outcomes, establishing clear prognostic thresholds.

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

Severity of illness, as used herein, refers to the degree of physiological derangement and organ dysfunction in a critically ill patient, which correlates with risk of morbidity and mortality. The invention provides a method to quantify this severity through biochemical markers rather than clinical aggregates. ATP level measurement is central to this approach. Biological samples are collected via standard venipuncture or arterial line access, with anticoagulants such as EDTA or heparin to prevent clotting. Acceptable sample types include whole blood, plasma, or serum, though whole blood is preferred due to the high ATP content in erythrocytes.

The method is applicable to a wide range of critical illnesses, including septic shock, post-cardiac surgery complications, acute respiratory distress syndrome, multiple organ failure, severe trauma, and viral-induced myocarditis or encephalopathy. ATP extraction begins by mixing 0.1 mL of blood with 1.3 mL of phenol-TE solution (Tris-EDTA-saturated phenol), followed by vigorous shaking for 20 seconds. The mixture is then centrifuged at 10,000 × g for 5 minutes at 4°C to separate phases. The upper aqueous layer, containing solubilized ATP, is diluted 10,000-fold and assayed via luciferin-luciferase reaction in a luminometer. Light output is converted to ATP concentration (mM) using a calibration curve.

In healthy individuals, the mean blood ATP level is approximately 0.62 mM (standard deviation 0.19), with no statistically significant differences between sexes or age groups under 60 years, though a mild decline may occur in elderly subjects. The normal ATP range is defined as 0.43 to 0.81 mM (mean ±1 SD). Severity is evaluated as follows: mild abnormality is indicated by ATP levels between 0.30 and 0.43 mM; severe abnormality by levels between 0.15 and 0.30 mM; and high mortality risk by levels below 0.15 mM.

Lactic acid is measured in serum using enzymatic assays, with normal levels below 1.19 mM. The A-LES is calculated as [lactate (mM)] / [ATP (mM)]. A normal A-LES is below 2.00. Mild abnormality corresponds to A-LES values of 2.00–5.49; severe abnormality to 5.50–19.99; and values ≥20.0 indicate severe abnormality with high risk of death.

Ketone bodies (acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone) are measured collectively, with normal total levels below 0.5 mM. The A-KES is [ketone bodies (mM)] / [ATP (mM)]. An abnormal A-KES is defined as >0.8, reflecting significant metabolic stress from fatty acid dysregulation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Blood samples were collected from 42 ICU patients at serial time points. ATP and lactic acid levels were measured using the phenol extraction and bioluminescence/enzymatic methods described. A-LES values were calculated for each time point. At admission, severely ill patients (APACHE II ≥20) exhibited median ATP of 0.31 mM and A-LES of 13.37, versus 0.56 mM and 4.74 in moderately ill patients. Over time, survivors showed progressive ATP recovery and A-LES decline to median 0.52 mM and 2.7 at discharge. Non-survivors maintained low ATP (0.23 mM) and high A-LES (38.9). Time-dependent A-LES trajectories clearly distinguished treatment responders from non-responders. Strong inverse correlation was observed between A-LES and survival (p<0.01), confirming its prognostic utility.

### Example 2

APACHE II scores and A-LES values were compared in the same cohort. While APACHE II showed limited dynamic range in severe cases (scores 20–46), A-LES varied widely (3–89.7), providing finer resolution of deterioration. Spearman correlation coefficient between admission A-LES and APACHE II was rs=0.68 (p<0.001), confirming association, but A-LES better reflected real-time changes during therapy, as APACHE II remained static after initial calculation.

### Example 3

In a murine model of influenza A infection, blood ATP, lactate, and ketone bodies were measured. Infected mice showed ATP depletion (0.28 mM vs. 0.60 mM in controls), elevated lactate (3.1 mM vs. 1.0 mM), and increased ketones (0.8 mM vs. 0.3 mM). Corresponding A-LES rose from 1.7 to 11.1, and A-KES from 0.5 to 2.9. Both scores correlated strongly (rs=0.92), and values >10 predicted mortality. This confirms the cross-species applicability and mechanistic link between bioenergetic failure and metabolite accumulation.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The testing method of the present invention is readily implementable in clinical laboratories and point-of-care settings using standardized reagents and luminometers. It offers rapid turnaround (<30 minutes), minimal training requirements, and compatibility with existing blood collection protocols. Benefits include real-time severity monitoring, early detection of metabolic crisis, objective treatment guidance, and improved triage decisions. By replacing or augmenting subjective scoring systems with quantifiable bioenergetic metrics, the invention enhances precision medicine in critical care, with broad applicability across hospitals, emergency departments, and field medicine.
