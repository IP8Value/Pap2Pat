# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel method and pharmaceutical composition for the selective enhancement of anabolic effects of testosterone on skeletal muscle while simultaneously inhibiting or preventing its undesirable proliferative effects on the prostate gland. More specifically, the invention provides a combination therapy comprising testosterone or a biologically active analogue, derivative, or prodrug thereof, together with an inhibitor of ornithine decarboxylase (ODC), particularly α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO) or other pharmaceutically acceptable ODC inhibitors. This therapeutic strategy exploits the differential activation of signaling pathways by testosterone in muscle versus prostate tissue, wherein follistatin mediates the anabolic actions in muscle without stimulating prostate growth, and wherein ODC activity is essential for testosterone-induced prostate proliferation but dispensable for its myogenic effects. The invention further encompasses diagnostic kits, controlled-release formulations, and methods for treating low-testosterone-associated disorders—including sarcopenia, physical dysfunction, hypogonadism, and age-related functional decline—while mitigating the risk of benign prostatic hyperplasia, prostate cancer progression, or other androgen-dependent adverse events.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Testosterone is the principal male sex hormone and an androgenic steroid that plays a central role in the development of male reproductive tissues, secondary sexual characteristics, and the maintenance of muscle mass, bone density, libido, and overall metabolic health. In both men and women, testosterone contributes to energy levels, mood regulation, cognitive function, and erythropoiesis. However, testosterone deficiency, also known as hypogonadism, is a well-documented clinical condition characterized by serum total testosterone levels below 300–350 pg/mL in men and associated with a constellation of symptoms including fatigue, reduced muscle strength, increased adiposity, depressed mood, diminished libido, erectile dysfunction, osteoporosis, and impaired physical performance. These symptoms are particularly prevalent in aging men, where a gradual, age-dependent decline in testosterone production—often termed “andropause” or late-onset hypogonadism—affects up to 30% of men over the age of 70.

Current testosterone replacement therapies (TRT) include transdermal gels, patches, intramuscular injections, buccal tablets, subcutaneous pellets, and oral formulations such as testosterone undecanoate. While effective in restoring physiological testosterone levels and alleviating symptoms of deficiency, these therapies carry significant risks, most notably the stimulation of prostate growth, which may exacerbate benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) or potentially promote the progression of occult prostate cancer. Additional side effects include polycythemia, sleep apnea, acne, fluid retention, and suppression of endogenous gonadotropin secretion leading to testicular atrophy and infertility. In women, exogenous testosterone can cause virilization, including hirsutism, voice deepening, and clitoromegaly, limiting its therapeutic utility despite evidence of benefits in hypoactive sexual desire disorder and certain forms of ovarian insufficiency.

Epidemiological studies have consistently correlated low testosterone levels with increased risks of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, frailty, and all-cause mortality. Despite these associations, the long-term safety of TRT remains controversial due to concerns about prostate-related morbidity. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet medical need for therapeutic approaches that preserve or enhance the beneficial anabolic and metabolic effects of testosterone—particularly on muscle mass, physical function, and quality of life—while selectively blocking its proliferative actions on the prostate. The present invention addresses this need through a rationally designed combination therapy that leverages newly discovered molecular distinctions in how testosterone signals in different target tissues.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the unexpected discovery that the anabolic effects of testosterone on skeletal muscle and its proliferative effects on the prostate are mediated through divergent downstream signaling pathways. Specifically, the inventors have demonstrated that follistatin, a secreted glycoprotein induced by testosterone via the androgen receptor (AR)/β-catenin axis in muscle progenitor cells, is both necessary and sufficient for mediating testosterone’s myogenic actions. Administration of recombinant follistatin (rFst) robustly increases lean body mass and muscle weight in murine models without altering prostate size or stimulating markers of prostate cell proliferation such as PCNA expression or S-phase entry in LNCaP cells. In stark contrast, testosterone activates additional pathways in the prostate that are not engaged by follistatin, among which the polyamine biosynthetic pathway—catalyzed by ornithine decarboxylase (ODC)—has been identified as a key mediator of androgen-driven prostate growth.

Microarray transcriptome analysis of levator ani muscle and prostate tissue from castrated mice treated with either testosterone or rFst revealed that while both agents regulate overlapping sets of genes in muscle related to growth and differentiation, only testosterone significantly upregulates ODC and other components of polyamine metabolism in the prostate. Critically, ODC expression is restored by testosterone—but not by rFst—in castrated animals, and its induction correlates temporally with prostate regrowth. Based on these findings, the inventors hypothesized that pharmacological inhibition of ODC would uncouple testosterone’s anabolic effects from its prostatic effects.

This hypothesis was confirmed experimentally: co-administration of testosterone with DFMO, an irreversible and specific ODC inhibitor, fully restored skeletal muscle mass in castrated mice to levels seen in intact controls, while completely preventing testosterone-induced prostate enlargement. Histological analysis further showed that DFMO blocked testosterone-mediated epithelial hyperplasia and luminal expansion in the prostate. These results establish that ODC activity is essential for testosterone’s proliferative actions in the prostate but dispensable for its anabolic effects on muscle.

Accordingly, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of testosterone or a biologically active analogue, derivative, ester (e.g., testosterone undecanoate, testosterone propionate), or prodrug thereof, in combination with a therapeutically effective amount of an ODC inhibitor, such as DFMO (eflornithine), or other selective ODC inhibitors including but not limited to SAM486A, CGP 48664, or novel compounds disclosed herein. The composition may be formulated for oral, transdermal, intramuscular, subcutaneous, buccal, intranasal, or parenteral administration, and may include controlled- or delayed-release systems to maintain stable plasma concentrations. The invention further provides a method for treating low-testosterone-associated disorders—including hypogonadism, sarcopenia, physical dysfunction, mobility limitation, and age-related functional decline—in a subject in need thereof, by administering the combination therapy to increase muscle mass and physical performance while preventing or reducing prostate-related side effects.

The invention also encompasses diagnostic kits containing the combination formulation along with instructions for use, patient selection criteria (e.g., serum testosterone <350 pg/mL), and monitoring parameters. By selectively targeting the ODC pathway, the invention achieves a functional dissociation of androgen action, offering a safer, more targeted approach to androgen replacement that preserves sexual, metabolic, and musculoskeletal benefits without compromising prostate health. This strategy is particularly advantageous for older men, those with a history of BPH, or individuals at elevated risk for prostate cancer, and represents a significant advance over existing selective androgen receptor modulators (SARMs) by utilizing clinically validated agents with established safety profiles.
