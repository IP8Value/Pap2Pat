# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention pertains to the field of biotechnology and therapeutic antibody development, specifically relating to fully human monoclonal antibodies that bind with high specificity and affinity to the receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 2 (ROR2). The invention further encompasses antibody-based therapeutics derived from these anti-ROR2 antibodies, including antibody drug conjugates (ADCs), antibody effector conjugates (AECs), chimeric antigen receptors (CARs), and bispecific or multispecific antibody formats. These molecules are designed for the diagnosis, detection, and targeted treatment of neoplastic diseases characterized by aberrant or elevated expression of ROR2 on tumor cells. The invention also includes methods for producing such antibodies and their derivatives, as well as pharmaceutical compositions and kits comprising them for clinical and diagnostic applications.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, despite significant advances in therapeutic modalities. While conventional chemotherapy continues to serve as a standard of care for many malignancies, its utility is often limited by systemic toxicity and insufficient selectivity for malignant cells. In response, the development of targeted therapies—particularly those leveraging the specificity of monoclonal antibodies—has emerged as a promising strategy to enhance therapeutic efficacy while minimizing off-target effects. Among these approaches, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) represent a sophisticated class of therapeutics that combine the tumor-targeting precision of antibodies with the potent cytotoxic activity of small-molecule payloads.

Genetic alterations underlie the pathogenesis of most cancers, driving uncontrolled proliferation, evasion of apoptosis, and metastatic dissemination. Among the molecular targets implicated in oncogenesis are cell surface receptors that are overexpressed or aberrantly activated in tumor cells but exhibit limited expression in healthy adult tissues. The receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptors ROR1 and ROR2 have garnered attention as such targets due to their restricted normal tissue distribution and frequent upregulation in diverse carcinomas. ROR1 and ROR2 share structural homology and belong to the same receptor family, yet their expression patterns in cancer are largely non-overlapping, with only rare instances of co-expression reported.

ROR2, in particular, is expressed during embryonic development but is largely silenced in most adult tissues. However, it becomes re-expressed or upregulated in a variety of human cancers, including renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, gastrointestinal stromal tumors, breast cancer, colorectal cancer, oral squamous cell carcinoma, and pancreatic cancer. Elevated ROR2 expression has been correlated with aggressive disease phenotypes and poor clinical outcomes. Functionally, ROR2 acts as a co-receptor in noncanonical Wnt signaling pathways, promoting cell migration, invasion, and tumor progression upon interaction with ligands such as Wnt5a. Paradoxically, in certain contexts driven by canonical Wnt signaling, ROR2 may function as a tumor suppressor, highlighting its complex biological role.

Structurally, human ROR2 is a type I transmembrane protein comprising an extracellular region with three domains: an N-terminal immunoglobulin-like domain, a cysteine-rich Frizzled domain, and a membrane-proximal Kringle domain, followed by a transmembrane segment and an intracellular tyrosine kinase-like domain. This extracellular architecture presents multiple potential epitopes for antibody recognition. However, the development of high-quality, fully human anti-ROR2 antibodies has been challenging due to the need for exquisite specificity, favorable binding kinetics, efficient internalization upon antigen engagement, and cross-reactivity with orthologs in preclinical species for toxicological evaluation.

In normal physiology, hROR2 plays critical roles in skeletal and neural development, but its postnatal expression is minimal, making it an attractive tumor-associated antigen with a potentially wide therapeutic window. Despite this promise, the generation of therapeutic-grade anti-ROR2 antibodies has been hindered by technical hurdles, including the difficulty of isolating fully human antibodies with optimal functional properties directly from immune repertoires without extensive engineering. There exists a clear and unmet need for high-affinity, fully human monoclonal antibodies that bind ROR2 with high specificity, induce robust internalization, and can serve as effective scaffolds for ADCs, CAR-T cells, and other advanced therapeutic modalities.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides fully human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to human ROR2 with high affinity and selectivity. These antibodies are capable of recognizing conformational epitopes on the extracellular domain of ROR2 and exhibit favorable kinetic profiles, including slow dissociation rates. Critically, the antibodies of the invention demonstrate efficient internalization upon binding to ROR2-expressing cells, rendering them particularly suitable for use as components of antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) and other targeted therapeutics. The invention further includes antigen-binding fragments, derivatives, and engineered formats of these antibodies, as well as polynucleotides encoding them, expression vectors, host cells, and methods for their production and therapeutic application.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Introduction and Non-Limiting Embodiments

The invention is directed to novel fully human anti-ROR2 antibodies and their functional derivatives, which have been isolated using an integrated discovery platform combining immunization of human immunoglobulin transgenic mice with a mammalian cell display system enabling direct functional screening for ADC activity. These antibodies bind specifically to human ROR2 and, in preferred embodiments, also cross-react with cynomolgus monkey and/or mouse ROR2, facilitating preclinical development. The antibodies may be used in a variety of therapeutic and diagnostic contexts, including as naked antibodies, conjugated to cytotoxic agents (ADCs), fused to effector molecules (AECs), or engineered into chimeric antigen receptors (CARs) for adoptive T-cell therapy.

### Antibody Selection Process

The antibodies were discovered through a streamlined process involving immunization of H2L2 transgenic mice—engineered to express fully human variable regions—with the extracellular domain of human ROR2 fused to a Twin-Strep-tag. Antigen-specific B cells were enriched from splenocytes via magnetic-activated cell sorting (MACS), and libraries of human heavy and kappa light chain variable domains were constructed. These libraries were cloned into transposable vectors and introduced into murine pro-B L11 cells, generating cellular libraries displaying fully human IgG1 antibodies on the cell surface. Clones binding hROR2 were isolated by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), and supernatants from single-cell clones were screened not only for antigen binding by ELISA but also for functional ADC activity using a secondary killing assay with a PNU-159682-conjugated anti-human Fc reagent. This dual-screening approach enabled the direct identification of antibodies with both high affinity and potent internalization-mediated cytotoxicity, bypassing the need for sequence recovery and re-expression prior to functional assessment.

### Antibody Drug Conjugates (ADCs)

The anti-ROR2 antibodies of the invention serve as ideal platforms for ADC development. In preferred embodiments, the antibodies are site-specifically conjugated to potent cytotoxic agents such as derivatives of PNU-159682 via sortase-mediated conjugation (SMAC technology). The resulting ADCs exhibit high drug-to-antibody ratios (DARs) of approximately 3–4 and demonstrate nanomolar potency against ROR2-positive cancer cell lines, including breast and multiple myeloma models, while sparing ROR2-negative cells. The conjugation employs C-terminal LPETG sortase recognition motifs engineered into the heavy and/or light chains, enabling precise attachment of glycine-functionalized toxins.

### Antibody Effector Conjugates (AECs)

Beyond cytotoxic payloads, the antibodies may be conjugated to effector molecules such as cytokines, radioisotopes, enzymes, or imaging agents to create AECs for immunomodulation, radiotherapy, prodrug activation, or diagnostic imaging. The same site-specific conjugation strategies apply, ensuring homogeneity and controlled stoichiometry.

### Chimeric Antigen Receptors (CARs)

The antigen-binding domains of the anti-ROR2 antibodies—particularly the variable heavy (VH) and variable light (VL) chains or single-chain variable fragments (scFvs)—can be incorporated into CAR constructs. These CARs, when expressed in T cells or other immune effector cells, redirect cellular cytotoxicity toward ROR2-expressing tumor cells. The invention includes nucleic acids encoding such CARs, viral or non-viral vectors for their delivery, and methods for manufacturing and administering ROR2-targeted CAR-T cells.

### Bi- or Multispecific Antibodies

The invention further encompasses bispecific or multispecific antibodies wherein one binding arm targets ROR2 and the other engages a second antigen, such as CD3 (to recruit T cells), PD-L1 (to block immune checkpoints), or another tumor-associated antigen. These formats enhance therapeutic versatility by enabling dual targeting or immune cell redirection.

### Isolated Polynucleotides and Expression Vectors

The invention includes isolated polynucleotides encoding the VH and VL domains of the anti-ROR2 antibodies, as well as full-length heavy and light chain sequences. These polynucleotides are operably linked to promoters and regulatory elements in expression vectors suitable for transient or stable expression in eukaryotic host cells, such as HEK293T or CHO cells. Vectors may include secretion signals, selection markers (e.g., puromycin, hygromycin resistance), and tags (e.g., Twin-Strep, His) for purification.

### Therapeutic and Diagnostic Uses

The antibodies and their derivatives are useful for treating neoplastic diseases characterized by ROR2 overexpression. Methods of treatment involve administering a therapeutically effective amount of the antibody, ADC, CAR-T cell, or pharmaceutical composition to a subject in need thereof. Additionally, the antibodies enable detection of ROR2 in biological samples (e.g., tumor biopsies, serum) via immunoassays or imaging, facilitating diagnosis, patient stratification, and monitoring of disease progression or treatment response.

### Definitions

A “human antibody” refers to an antibody wherein the variable and constant regions are derived from human germline immunoglobulin sequences. An “antibody-based binding protein” encompasses any protein scaffold that utilizes immunoglobulin-derived domains for antigen recognition, including full-length antibodies, fragments, and engineered variants. A “modified antibody format” includes scFvs, Fab, F(ab′)₂, dsFv, dAbs, and single-domain antibodies. An “antibody derivative or fragment” retains antigen-binding capability but may lack effector functions.

An intact antibody is a tetrameric glycoprotein composed of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, each containing variable and constant regions. Immunoglobulin genes undergo V(D)J recombination to generate diversity. Heavy chains are classified into isotypes (IgG, IgA, etc.), with IgG1 being preferred herein. Light chains are kappa or lambda; the disclosed antibodies use kappa chains.

Variable regions contain framework regions (FRs) and complementarity-determining regions (CDRs), with CDR3 being the most diverse. CDRs are defined according to Kabat, Chothia, or IMGT conventions. A “single chain antibody” (scFv) links VH and VL via a peptide linker. “Antigen-binding fragments” include Fab (one arm), F(ab′)₂ (two arms, hinge-linked), Fd (VH-CH1), Fv (VH-VL non-covalent pair), and disulfide-stabilized Fvs (dsFvs).

“Single domain antibodies” (dAbs) consist of a single VH or VL domain, including camelid VHHs. An “isolated CDR” may retain binding if properly scaffolded. Antibodies may be generated via hybridoma, phage/yeast/mammalian display, or transgenic animals. “Conservatively modified variants” substitute amino acids with similar physicochemical properties. “Percent identity” is calculated over aligned sequences, excluding gaps.

### Detailed Description of Embodiments

The invention features fully human anti-ROR2 antibodies defined by specific VH and VL CDR sequences. Exemplary VH CDR1 sequences include SEQ ID NO:2–5; VH CDR2: SEQ ID NO:6–9; VH CDR3: SEQ ID NO:10–13. VL CDR1: SEQ ID NO:14–17; VL CDR2: SEQ ID NO:18–21; VL CDR3: SEQ ID NO:22–25. Preferred combinations are listed in Table 3, with triplets such as (SEQ ID NO:2,6,10) for VH and (14,18,22) for VL. Antibodies bind human ROR2 with Kd ≤ 347 nM, preferably ≤10 nM, as measured by SPR (kon, koff, Kd reported).

Cross-reactivity with cynomolgus and mouse ROR2 is advantageous for toxicology studies; antibodies like GK-5A1 bind all three orthologs. Polynucleotides encoding these antibodies are expressed in HEK293T or CHO cells using CMV or EF1α promoters, with Igκ or IgH signal peptides. Stable pools or clones are selected with antibiotics.

ADCs use cleavable linkers (e.g., Val-Cit-PAB, LPXTGn) conjugated to PNU-159682 or other toxins (auristatins, maytansinoids). SMAC technology enables site-specific conjugation via sortase A recognition of LPETG motifs, yielding homogeneous DAR 3–4 products. CARs comprise anti-ROR2 scFv fused to CD3ζ and costimulatory domains (e.g., CD28, 4-1BB).

Pharmaceutical compositions contain the antibody or derivative in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier (e.g., PBS, histidine buffer) with stabilizers (sucrose, polysorbate). Administered intravenously or subcutaneously, alone or with checkpoint inhibitors. Kits include the antibody conjugated to a label (e.g., fluorophore, radionuclide) for imaging or ELISA-based ROR2 detection in patient samples.

Methods for detecting neoplastic disease involve contacting a biological sample with the antibody, measuring bound antibody, and comparing to control ROR2 levels. For therapy, ROR2-positive subjects receive ADCs or CAR-T cells, with response monitored via imaging or biomarker assays.

Examples detail library construction from H2L2 mice, FACS sorting, ELISA/secondary killing screens, SPR affinity measurements, and in vitro cytotoxicity of ADCs on EMT6-hROR2 and T47D-hROR2 cells. IC50 values range from 12.3–168 ng/ml, confirming target-dependent killing.
