# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD AND BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Beta-cell replacement therapy represents a promising curative approach for the treatment of insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus, particularly Type 1 diabetes, wherein autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells results in an absolute deficiency of insulin. The transplantation of functional islets or purified beta cells can restore endogenous insulin production and glucose homeostasis, thereby alleviating the need for exogenous insulin administration and reducing the risk of long-term diabetic complications. However, the clinical application of beta-cell replacement is severely constrained by the limited availability of donor pancreata, the inefficiency of current isolation protocols, and the inability to expand human beta cells in vitro without compromising their differentiated phenotype and insulin-secreting capacity. These limitations underscore the urgent need for methods that enable the large-scale expansion of functional adult human beta cells while preserving or restoring their identity and function.

Efforts to overcome these challenges have focused on culturing dissociated human islet cells under conditions that support proliferation. While such approaches have demonstrated the capacity of adult human beta cells to undergo limited replication in vitro, this expansion is invariably accompanied by dedifferentiation—a loss of beta-cell-specific gene expression, including insulin (INS), pancreatic and duodenal homeobox 1 (PDX1), neurogenic differentiation 1 (NEUROD1), and other transcription factors essential for beta-cell identity and function. Dedifferentiated beta cells acquire mesenchymal-like characteristics, exhibit enlarged nuclei, and lose their ability to secrete insulin in a glucose-responsive manner, rendering them therapeutically ineffective unless redifferentiated.

The NOTCH signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in cell fate determination, proliferation, and differentiation during embryonic development and in adult tissues. In the developing pancreas, NOTCH signaling maintains progenitor cells in an undifferentiated state through lateral inhibition, preventing premature endocrine differentiation. Activation of NOTCH receptors (NOTCH1–4) by ligands such as Delta-like and Jagged leads to proteolytic cleavage by gamma-secretase, releasing the intracellular domain of NOTCH (NICD), which translocates to the nucleus and forms a complex with the DNA-binding protein RBPJκ to activate transcription of downstream effectors, notably the Hairy and Enhancer of Split (HES) family of transcriptional repressors, including HES1. HES1, in turn, represses genes required for endocrine differentiation, such as NGN3, and promotes progenitor cell maintenance.

In the adult pancreas, NOTCH signaling is largely quiescent but can be reactivated under pathological or regenerative conditions. Recent studies have shown that components of the NOTCH pathway, particularly HES1, are upregulated in cultured human islet cells undergoing dedifferentiation and proliferation. This reactivation appears to facilitate the transition of beta cells from a quiescent, differentiated state to a proliferative, dedifferentiated state, mirroring aspects of embryonic progenitor behavior. Prior art has described the use of serum-containing media to expand islet cells and the application of soluble factors such as nicotinamide, exendin-4, and activin A to promote redifferentiation. However, these methods yield only modest restoration of beta-cell function and fail to achieve efficient, scalable redifferentiation of expanded populations. Moreover, no prior art has recognized the central role of HES1 upregulation as a barrier to redifferentiation or proposed targeted inhibition of the NOTCH pathway—specifically HES1—as a means to reverse dedifferentiation and restore functional beta-cell identity in expanded adult human islet cells.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for generating functional adult islet beta cells suitable for transplantation by expanding dedifferentiated beta cells in culture and subsequently redifferentiating them through inhibition of the NOTCH signaling pathway. In one embodiment, the method comprises contacting expanded adult islet beta cells with a NOTCH pathway inhibitor, particularly an agent capable of downregulating HES1 expression or activity, in a serum-free medium to promote redifferentiation. The cells are incubated in a defined culturing medium that supports the reacquisition of beta-cell markers and insulin production.

The invention further encompasses a method for treating diabetes in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of redifferentiated adult islet beta cells generated by the aforementioned method. In certain embodiments, the treatment involves transplanting the redifferentiated cells into the subject, preferably under the kidney capsule or into the liver via portal vein infusion, where they engraft and restore glucose-responsive insulin secretion.

Additionally, the invention provides a method for purifying dedifferentiated beta cells from a heterogeneous population of cultured islet cells. This method involves permanently tagging primary beta cells prior to or during initial culture using a lineage-tracing system, such as a Cre-loxP-based reporter construct driven by a beta-cell-specific promoter (e.g., insulin promoter). Following expansion, the tagged dedifferentiated beta cells are isolated based on the expression of the reporter (e.g., enhanced green fluorescent protein, eGFP), yielding a purified population suitable for redifferentiation or screening.

The invention also includes isolated populations of dedifferentiated adult islet beta cells that have been genetically labeled and purified, as well as methods for identifying agents that affect the proliferation or redifferentiation of these cells. Such screening methods involve exposing the purified populations to candidate compounds and assessing changes in beta-cell marker expression, insulin content, or functional glucose responsiveness, thereby enabling the discovery of novel therapeutic agents for diabetes.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The present invention is directed to redifferentiated populations of expanded adult islet beta cells and their use in cell replacement therapy for insulin-dependent diabetes. The core principle underlying the invention is that adult human beta cells, although terminally differentiated in vivo, retain the capacity to proliferate in vitro when induced to dedifferentiate, and crucially, can be redifferentiated into functional insulin-producing cells through targeted molecular intervention. This addresses a critical limitation of prior art, which has failed to provide a robust, scalable source of functional beta cells due to the irreversible loss of identity during expansion.

Prior approaches relying on human pancreas donors are inherently limited by organ scarcity, immune rejection, and variability in islet quality. The present invention overcomes these barriers by enabling the ex vivo expansion of beta cells from a single donor to generate sufficient quantities for multiple recipients, followed by redifferentiation to restore function. Novel culture conditions, particularly the combination of NOTCH pathway inhibition with serum-free media supplemented with specific soluble factors, significantly increase insulin content and beta-cell marker expression in dedifferentiated expanded cells.

Purification of dedifferentiated beta cells is achieved through a genetic lineage-tracing strategy. Primary beta cells within freshly isolated human islets are permanently tagged by infecting the cells with two lentiviral vectors: one expressing a tamoxifen-inducible Cre recombinase under the control of the rat insulin promoter (RIP-Cre/ER), and another containing a loxP-flanked STOP cassette upstream of an eGFP reporter gene. Upon addition of 4-hydroxytamoxifen, Cre-mediated recombination excises the STOP sequence only in insulin-expressing beta cells, leading to permanent eGFP expression regardless of subsequent dedifferentiation. This allows for the isolation of pure populations of dedifferentiated beta cells (eGFP⁺) from non-beta islet cells (eGFP⁻) using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS).

Key to the redifferentiation process is the downregulation of components of the NOTCH signaling pathway. During in vitro expansion, cultured human islet cells exhibit significant upregulation of HES1, NOTCH1, NOTCH2, and NOTCH3 transcripts, correlating with loss of insulin expression and acquisition of a proliferative phenotype. Inhibition of HES1 expression using short hairpin RNA (shRNA) delivered via lentivirus prevents this dedifferentiation and induces redifferentiation, as evidenced by increased expression of INS, PDX1, NEUROD1, MAFA, NKX6.1, and other beta-cell genes, reduced nuclear size, formation of islet-like clusters, and growth arrest mediated by upregulation of cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitors p57 (CDKN1C) and p27 (CDKN1B). Critically, HES1 shRNA treatment alone is sufficient to prime expanded cells for further functional maturation in vivo, as demonstrated by their ability to reverse hyperglycemia in diabetic immunodeficient mice.

These findings establish that agents capable of downregulating NOTCH pathway components—particularly HES1—can be used to redifferentiate expanded adult islet beta cells. The invention thus provides a comprehensive method for ex vivo expansion and redifferentiation: adult islet beta cells are isolated from autologous or allogeneic mammalian donors (preferably human), dispersed into single-cell suspension, and cultured under non-apoptotic conditions with supplements supporting viability (e.g., antibiotics, antifungals, serum or defined substitutes). After expansion, cells are redifferentiated by contacting them with a NOTCH pathway inhibitor in serum-free medium, optionally supplemented with redifferentiation factors such as high glucose, nicotinamide, exendin-4, and activin A.

“Adult islet beta cells” refer to insulin-producing endocrine cells derived from the islets of Langerhans of postnatal mammals, excluding embryonic or stem cell-derived beta-like cells. “Expanded adult islet beta cells” are those that have undergone at least one round of cell division in culture, typically exhibiting reduced insulin content and dedifferentiated morphology. “Redifferentiating” means restoring expression of beta-cell-specific genes and functional characteristics, including glucose-stimulated insulin secretion.

Downregulation of the NOTCH pathway may be achieved through various agents: gamma-secretase inhibitors (e.g., DAPT), neutralizing antibodies against NOTCH receptors or ligands, or nucleic acid-based silencing agents. RNA silencing agents include small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs), and microRNAs (miRNAs) designed to target HES1, NOTCH1, or other pathway components. siRNAs are typically 19–25 bp double-stranded RNAs that guide the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) to cleave complementary mRNA. shRNAs are expressed from plasmid or viral vectors and processed by Dicer into siRNAs. Long dsRNAs may be used in certain cell types if engineered to avoid interferon and PKR activation. Chemically modified nucleotides or conjugation to cell-penetrating peptides (e.g., TAT, penetratin) can enhance stability and delivery.

Alternative downregulation strategies include antisense oligonucleotides, ribozymes, DNAzymes (e.g., “10-23” motif cleaving specific mRNA sequences), and triplex-forming oligonucleotides (TFOs) that bind genomic DNA to inhibit transcription. Polypeptide or polynucleotide agents may be delivered via lipid nanoparticles, polymers, or viral vectors (lentiviral, adenoviral, retroviral). For transplantation, cells may be encapsulated in biocompatible microcapsules (e.g., alginate-polylysine) to prevent immune rejection, or co-administered with immunosuppressive agents.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising redifferentiated beta cells or NOTCH pathway inhibitors are formulated with physiologically acceptable carriers and administered via injection, transmucosal, oral, or buccal routes. Dosage is determined based on efficacy and toxicity studies, aiming for a therapeutically effective amount that restores euglycemia without adverse effects.

The purification and tagging methodology enables high-throughput screening of candidate agents affecting beta-cell proliferation or redifferentiation. Expression constructs for permanent tagging include mammalian vectors with beta-cell-specific promoters, enhancer elements, polyadenylation signals, and selectable markers. Lentiviral vectors are preferred for stable integration and long-term expression.

In vivo studies confirm that HES1-inhibited, expanded human islet cells, upon transplantation into hyperglycemic NOD-SCID mice, further differentiate, express insulin, secrete human C-peptide, and normalize blood glucose levels. This demonstrates the therapeutic potential of the invention for diabetes treatment.

### Examples

The invention is further illustrated by the following examples. Human islets were dissociated and cultured in CMRL 1066 medium with 10% FBS. Lineage tracing was performed using RIP-Cre/ER and pTrip-loxP-STOP-loxP-eGFP lentiviruses, with 4-hydroxytamoxifen induction. HES1 was inhibited using shRNA cloned in pLKO.1 lentivirus. Redifferentiation media included serum-free CMRL with BSA, ITS, high glucose, nicotinamide, exendin-4, and activin A.

RNA analyses by qPCR showed that cultured beta cells upregulate HES1, NOTCH1–3, and downregulate NOTCH4 and ligands. HES1 inhibition increased INS, PDX1, NEUROD1, p57, and p27 transcripts, while reducing proliferation (BrdU incorporation) and nuclear size. Immunofluorescence confirmed C-peptide expression specifically in eGFP⁺ (beta-cell-derived) cells after HES1 shRNA treatment, with no hormone co-expression, indicating mature differentiation.

In vivo, HES1-inhibited cells transplanted under the kidney capsule of diabetic mice restored normoglycemia and secreted human C-peptide. Graft analysis revealed high INS, NKX2.2, and PC1/3 expression. Statistical analysis (Student’s t-test with Bonferroni correction) confirmed significance (p < 0.05).

These results demonstrate that HES1 upregulation is a key mediator of beta-cell dedifferentiation in culture and that its inhibition is sufficient to drive redifferentiation. The combined use of HES1 shRNA and redifferentiation medium synergistically enhances beta-cell recovery, offering a scalable platform for diabetes cell therapy. Future work will focus on non-viral HES1 inhibitors and GMP-compliant manufacturing.
