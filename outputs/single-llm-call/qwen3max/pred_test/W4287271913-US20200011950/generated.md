## BACKGROUND

Tumor heterogeneity presents a significant challenge in the accurate diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic management of solid malignancies, particularly aggressive cancers such as glioblastoma (GBM). Unlike homogeneous disease models, real-world tumors exhibit spatially and temporally varying cellular compositions, genetic profiles, and microenvironmental interactions that collectively influence clinical outcomes. This heterogeneity extends beyond the visibly demarcated tumor margins into adjacent tissue regions that appear radiologically normal but are nonetheless biologically altered—a phenomenon known as the “tumor field effect.” In GBM, one of the most lethal primary brain cancers, this field effect manifests through infiltrative tumor cells, biomechanical mass effect, and structural deformation of surrounding brain parenchyma, often leading to recurrence even after maximal surgical resection. Conventional imaging and clinical metrics fail to capture the full extent of this field effect, resulting in suboptimal risk stratification and treatment planning. Existing prognostic models rely heavily on molecular markers (e.g., MGMT methylation, IDH mutation status) or gross tumor volume, which are either inconsistently available or insufficiently reflective of the dynamic interplay between tumor biology and host tissue response. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for non-invasive, imaging-based methods capable of quantifying both morphological and biomechanical manifestations of tumor heterogeneity to improve survival prediction and personalize therapeutic strategies in GBM and other solid tumors.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Cancer, particularly glioblastoma (GBM), is not a spatially confined disease but rather a systemic process that disrupts local and distal tissue architecture through infiltration and mechanical displacement. This disruption gives rise to the tumor field effect, wherein regions of the brain that appear normal on routine magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) are nonetheless influenced by the presence of the tumor. The field effect in GBM is clinically significant because it contributes to treatment resistance, tumor recurrence, and poor overall survival. Despite advances in neuro-oncology, current approaches to predicting overall survival in GBM remain limited by their reliance on static, macroscopic features such as tumor size or enhancement pattern, which inadequately reflect the underlying biological aggressiveness and spatial heterogeneity of the disease.

Radiomic features—quantitative descriptors derived from medical images—offer a promising avenue to capture intra-tumoral heterogeneity by converting visual patterns into mineable data. However, prior radiomic studies have predominantly focused on textural and shape-based features extracted from the enhancing tumor core and peritumoral edema, neglecting the biomechanical consequences of tumor growth on surrounding normal-appearing brain tissue. The present invention addresses this gap by introducing a novel integrated descriptor, termed r-DepTH (radiographic Deformation and Textural Heterogeneity), which synergistically combines measurements of subtle tissue deformations in the brain around tumor (BAT) region with high-order textural features from tumoral and peritumoral compartments.

The r-DepTH descriptor is grounded in the hypothesis that more aggressive tumors exert greater mechanical stress on adjacent parenchyma, resulting in quantifiable deformations that correlate with poor prognosis. To capture this, the invention employs diffeomorphic image registration between a patient’s MRI and a healthy brain atlas to compute voxel-wise deformation magnitudes in annular sub-regions extending up to 60 millimeters from the tumor boundary. These deformation features are complemented by 3D gradient-based textural descriptors—specifically, the Co-occurrence of Local Anisotropic Gradient Orientations (CoLIAGe)—computed within the enhancing tumor and peritumoral hyperintense regions on T1-weighted and FLAIR MRI sequences, respectively.

In specific embodiments, the method begins by accessing a radiological image associated with a GBM patient, typically a multi-sequence MRI scan including Gd-enhanced T1w, T2w, and FLAIR sequences. The tumoral region is segmented manually or via automated algorithms to delineate the enhancing lesion (I_T) and the peritumoral region (I_P) characterized by T2/FLAIR hyperintensity. The peritumoral region is defined using morphological transformations: the tumoral boundary is dilated outward in successive 5-millimeter increments to generate concentric annular rings (e.g., 12 rings spanning 5–60 mm), thereby establishing a structured representation of the BAT region. A parenchymal region (I_B) is then defined as the union of these annular sub-volumes, excluding the tumoral and immediate peritumoral zones.

To compute the deformation heterogeneity feature descriptor, the patient’s brain image is non-rigidly registered to a healthy brain atlas (e.g., MNI152) using symmetric normalization (SyN) with mutual information as the similarity metric, while masking out the tumor region to avoid bias. The inverse transformation yields a deformation field mapping each voxel in the patient’s parenchyma to its corresponding location in the atlas. The magnitude of deformation at each voxel is calculated as the Euclidean norm of the displacement vector. First-order statistics (mean, median, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis) are then computed for each annular sub-region, producing a deformation feature vector.

Concurrently, tumoral and peritumoral 3D gradient-based texture descriptors are generated using the CoLIAGe framework. For each voxel within I_T and I_P, intensity gradients in three spatial directions are computed, and local gradient orientation matrices are constructed. From these, co-occurrence matrices of gradient orientations are derived, and Haralick texture statistics (e.g., energy, contrast, correlation, sum variance) are calculated. First-order statistics of these Haralick features across each region yield the tumoral (F_T) and peritumoral (F_P) CoLIAGe descriptors.

The r-DepTH descriptor is formed by concatenating the deformation heterogeneity descriptor (F_B), the tumoral CoLIAGe descriptor (F_T), and the peritumoral CoLIAGe descriptor (F_P), resulting in a comprehensive feature vector that encapsulates both biomechanical and morphological aspects of tumor field effect. This descriptor is then provided as input to a machine learning classifier—such as a LASSO-regularized Cox proportional hazards model—trained to distinguish long-term survivors (LTS) from short-term survivors (STS) based on overall survival thresholds (e.g., median survival of 15 months).

In operation, the system accesses a patient’s MRI, segments the tumor, defines peritumoral annuli, registers the image to an atlas, computes deformation and texture features, constructs the r-DepTH descriptor, and outputs a survival probability or risk classification. The classification is displayed alongside the original image and key descriptors to assist clinicians in treatment planning. The machine learning classifier is trained on a retrospective cohort of GBM patients with known survival outcomes, using cross-validation to optimize feature selection and model parameters. Performance is evaluated via Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, log-rank tests, hazard ratios, concordance index (C-index), and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves.

Illustrative examples demonstrate that patients with poor survival exhibit higher skewness in deformation magnitudes near the tumor margin and elevated kurtosis in CoLIAGe energy features, reflecting greater asymmetry and extremity in tissue response and texture heterogeneity, respectively. The r-DepTH system thus facilitates improved risk stratification, enabling personalized treatment plans that account for the full spatial and biomechanical footprint of the tumor.

The invention further encompasses an apparatus comprising a processor, memory storing executable instructions, and specialized circuits for image acquisition, region definition, descriptor computation, survival prediction, and display. The system may be implemented on a general-purpose computer or integrated into a computer-aided diagnosis (CADx) or personalized medicine platform, operable in networked environments with remote data sources. Non-transitory computer-readable media encode the method steps, ensuring reproducibility across clinical sites.

In summary, the disclosed embodiments provide a technically superior solution for predicting overall survival in GBM by integrating radiographic deformation and textural heterogeneity into a unified prognostic biomarker, thereby advancing the precision of oncologic care in solid tumors.
