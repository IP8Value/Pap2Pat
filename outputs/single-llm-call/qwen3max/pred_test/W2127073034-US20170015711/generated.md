# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The global effort to develop a vaccine against human immunodeficiency virus type 1 (HIV-1) infection remains one of the most pressing challenges in modern medicine. Despite decades of research, no effective prophylactic vaccine has been approved for clinical use. A central strategy in HIV-1 vaccine development involves the induction of high-titer broadly neutralizing antibodies (bNAbs) that can block viral entry into host cells by targeting the envelope glycoprotein (Env) complex on the surface of the virion. The Env complex, which mediates both receptor attachment and membrane fusion, is the sole target for bNAbs and thus represents the critical antigenic focus for vaccine design. However, the native Env spike is inherently unstable when removed from the viral membrane and tends to adopt non-native conformations that expose epitopes recognized by non-neutralizing antibodies (non-NAbs), thereby diverting immune responses away from protective bNAb targets. To address this problem, researchers have developed engineered soluble versions of the Env trimer, such as the BG505 SOSIP.664 construct, which incorporates specific stabilizing modifications to mimic the native spike structure. These include a disulfide bond (SOS) between gp120 and gp41 ectodomain (gp41ECTO) subunits and an I559P substitution in gp41ECTO that enhances trimer stability. Additionally, proteolytic cleavage at the gp120–gp41 junction is essential for achieving native-like conformation, which is typically facilitated by co-expression of the cellular protease furin. While transient transfection systems have enabled the production of high-quality SOSIP trimers for preclinical research, they are not scalable or consistent enough for clinical manufacturing. Soluble gp140 constructs lacking these modifications are often misfolded, heterogeneous, and prone to degradation, rendering them unsuitable as immunogens. The BG505 SOSIP.664 trimer—derived from an HIV-1 subtype A transmitted/founder virus—has demonstrated exceptional structural and antigenic fidelity to the native Env spike, displaying key bNAb epitopes while occluding non-NAb epitopes. Nevertheless, the absence of a robust, stable, and scalable production platform has hindered its progression toward human clinical trials. Existing methods relying on transient transfection suffer from batch-to-batch variability, low yields, and impracticality under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) conditions. Therefore, there exists a critical need for improved methods to produce homogeneous, cleaved, native-like HIV-1 Env glycoprotein trimers in a consistent, scalable, and GMP-compliant manner suitable for vaccine development.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel expression vector designed for the co-expression of a stabilized, cleavable HIV-1 Env glycoprotein and the furin protease in mammalian cells. This vector enables the generation of stable, isogenic mammalian cell lines that constitutively produce high yields of fully cleaved, native-like Env trimers, such as the BG505 SOSIP.664 variant. The invention further encompasses a method for producing these Env glycoproteins by transfecting a mammalian host cell—preferably HEK 293T or Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells—with the expression vector, followed by selection and clonal expansion of high-producing cell lines using the Flp-In™ recombination system. The resulting cell lines stably integrate the expression cassette at a defined genomic locus, ensuring genetic uniformity and long-term expression stability. The method allows for the continuous, scalable production of Env trimers that are biochemically and antigenically indistinguishable from those produced by transient transfection, but with significantly higher yields and suitability for GMP-compliant manufacturing. This approach overcomes the limitations of prior art by providing a reliable, reproducible platform for the production of clinical-grade HIV-1 Env immunogens.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is centered on a recombinant expression vector engineered to drive the coordinated expression of an HIV-1 Env glycoprotein and the furin protease in mammalian cells. The expression vector is derived from the pcDNA5/FRT backbone, which contains a Flp Recombination Target (FRT) site enabling site-specific integration into the genome of Flp-In™ host cells. The vector includes a plasmid configuration wherein the gene encoding a modified HIV Env glycoprotein is placed under the control of a strong constitutive promoter, such as the human cytomegalovirus (CMV) immediate-early enhancer/promoter, to maximize protein expression. Concurrently, the furin gene is operably linked to a weaker promoter, such as the elongation factor 1-alpha (EF1α) promoter, to ensure sufficient proteolytic activity for Env cleavage without inducing cytotoxicity. The HIV Env glycoprotein is a trimeric complex composed of three gp120 surface subunits non-covalently associated with three gp41 transmembrane subunits; in the soluble form used herein, the transmembrane and cytoplasmic domains of gp41 are replaced with a truncated ectodomain (gp41ECTO) and a stop codon, yielding a secreted gp140 protein. The Env glycoprotein may be derived from various HIV-1 strains, including but not limited to subtype A (e.g., BG505), subtype B (e.g., B41), or subtype C isolates. Preferred Env glycoproteins incorporate the SOSIP modifications: a disulfide bond between residues A501C in gp120 and T605C in gp41ECTO (SOS), and an I559P substitution in gp41ECTO (IP), which together stabilize the pre-fusion trimeric conformation. Furin, a proprotein convertase, functions to cleave the Env precursor at the canonical REKR site between gp120 and gp41, a step essential for proper folding and antigenic authenticity. The vector also includes optimized polyadenylation signals, such as a synthetic poly(A) sequence modeled after the herpes simplex virus thymidine kinase gene, to enhance mRNA stability and translation efficiency. Transcriptional control is further refined through the inclusion of appropriate transcription termination signals. The gp41ECTO domain retains key structural elements, including the heptad repeat regions, while excluding the membrane-proximal external region (MPER) and transmembrane domain. Post-translational modifications, particularly N-linked glycosylation, occur naturally in mammalian cells and are critical for proper folding and immune evasion. The BG505 SOSIP.664 and B41 SOSIP.664 variants are exemplary preferred embodiments. The expression vector components include not only the Env and furin expression cassettes but also a selectable marker, such as hygromycin resistance, linked to the FRT site for stable clone selection. Promoter options include both constitutive (e.g., CMV, EF1α, SV40) and inducible systems (e.g., tetracycline-inducible), though constitutive promoters are preferred for continuous production. Promoter activity is defined relative to a reference standard, with CMV exhibiting the highest activity among commonly used mammalian promoters. Promoter strength can be empirically determined by reporter assays measuring luciferase or GFP expression. The preferred embodiment utilizes CMV for Env and EF1α for furin to balance high yield with cellular health. The vector is transfected into mammalian host cells, such as HEK 293T or CHO Flp-In™ cells, which contain a single genomic FRT site and express the lacZ-Zeocin fusion gene for background selection. Suitable mammalian cells may be further modified to enhance protein folding, glycosylation, or secretion. Transfection is achieved via lipid-based reagents or electroporation, and integration occurs in a Flp-recombinase-dependent manner when co-transfected with a plasmid encoding Flp recombinase (e.g., pOG44). The Flp-In™ system ensures single-copy, site-specific integration, eliminating positional effects and promoting clonal uniformity. Transformed cells are selected using hygromycin, and high-producing clones are isolated by limiting dilution and screened via flow cytometry or ELISA using anti-Env antibodies such as 2G12. Stable cell lines are adapted for serum-free, suspension culture to facilitate GMP compliance. The method for producing cleaved Env glycoproteins involves culturing the selected cell line in appropriate growth medium—such as DMEM for 293T or Ham’s F-12 for CHO cells—supplemented with necessary nutrients and antibiotics, under controlled temperature, CO₂, and humidity conditions. Supernatants are harvested periodically, and Env trimers are purified using affinity chromatography (e.g., 2G12-coupled resin) followed by size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) to isolate homogeneous trimeric species. Purification may also employ anti-Env glycoprotein antibodies specific for conformational epitopes to ensure structural integrity.

### EXAMPLE 1

In a specific embodiment, the expression vector pAM/C BG505 was designed to co-express the BG505 SOSIP.664 gp140 Env glycoprotein and human furin protease from a single plasmid. The Env gene was positioned downstream of the CMV promoter to drive high-level expression, while the furin gene was placed under the control of the EF1α promoter to provide moderate, non-cytotoxic levels of protease activity. This dual-promoter configuration within the pcDNA5/FRT backbone allowed for balanced co-expression, ensuring efficient cleavage of the Env precursor without compromising cell viability. The vector included a synthetic polyadenylation signal derived from the HSV thymidine kinase gene to enhance transcript stability. The resulting plasmid, designated pAM/C BG505, was constructed by cloning the BG505 SOSIP.664 sequence and the furin coding sequence into the HindIII and NotI sites of the vector, respectively, maintaining optimal spacing and orientation for independent transcription.

### EXAMPLE 2

Stable 293T and CHO cell lines were generated by co-transfecting the pAM/C BG505 vector with the pOG44 plasmid, which encodes the Flp recombinase, into Flp-In™ 293T and CHO cells. Following transfection, cells were subjected to hygromycin selection to isolate integrants. Initial pools of resistant cells were screened for Env expression by dot blot using monoclonal antibody 2G12. The highest-expressing pools—293T line 13 and CHO line B—were subjected to limiting dilution to ensure clonality and eliminate genetic heterogeneity. Single-cell clones were expanded and analyzed by flow cytometry using FITC-labeled 2G12. Clone 13 #3-5 from 293T and clone B-D7 from CHO exhibited the highest intracellular and secreted Env levels. Trimer production in culture supernatants was quantified by a 2G12-capture ELISA with PGT145 detection, yielding 2.1 μg/ml and 1.7 μg/ml, respectively, confirming their suitability for large-scale production.

### EXAMPLE 3

The stability of Env expression in the selected clones was monitored over ten sequential passages (approximately five weeks). Intracellular Env levels, assessed by flow cytometry with FITC-2G12 in the presence of Brefeldin A, remained constant across all passages. Similarly, trimer yields in supernatants, measured by ELISA, showed no decline, averaging 2–3 μg/ml throughout the culture period. Cell viability, assessed by trypan blue exclusion, remained above 90% for 293T and 85% for CHO lines, indicating that neither Env nor furin expression imposed significant cytotoxic stress. These data demonstrate that the engineered cell lines maintain stable, high-level production of Env trimers over extended periods, a critical feature for industrial-scale manufacturing.

### EXAMPLE 4

Biochemical characterization confirmed that trimers from stable cell lines matched those from transient transfection in quality and homogeneity. Purification by 2G12-affinity chromatography followed by SEC yielded >90% trimeric protein, as shown by Blue Native PAGE (BN-PAGE) and Coomassie staining. Yields from stable lines were dramatically higher: 12 mg per 10⁹ cells for both 293T and CHO, compared to 1.25–1.5 mg and 0.375 mg from transiently transfected counterparts, representing 10-fold and 32-fold improvements, respectively. Reducing SDS-PAGE confirmed complete cleavage into gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits. Western blotting with ARP 3119 (anti-gp120) and HIV-Ig revealed no degradation products—specifically, no V3-clipped fragments—in contrast to monomeric gp120 or uncleaved gp140 controls, which showed extensive proteolysis and aggregation. The structural compactness of SOSIP.664 trimers likely shields vulnerable sites like V3 from proteases, explaining their resistance to degradation.

### EXAMPLE 5

Antigenic authenticity was verified using surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and ELISA adapted for non-tagged trimers. In SPR, immobilized bNAb PGT145 (trimer-specific) bound strongly to all trimer preparations, while non-NAb F105 (CD4bs-directed, occluded on native trimers) showed minimal binding. Similarly, in 2G12-capture ELISA, PGT145 detected abundant trimers, whereas F105 reactivity was absent, confirming the absence of misfolded or uncleaved contaminants. These results were consistent across trimers from stable 293T, stable CHO, and transiently transfected cells, demonstrating that the stable lines produce structurally authentic immunogens.

### EXAMPLE 6

Negative-stain electron microscopy (EM) of purified trimers revealed homogeneous, native-like morphology across all sources. Reference-free 2D class averages showed the characteristic three-lobed structure of the Env spike, with 81–100% of particles classified as native-like. No significant differences were observed between stable-line and transient-transfection products, confirming that the production method does not alter trimer architecture at the resolution of negative-stain EM.

### EXAMPLE 7

Thermal stability was assessed by differential scanning calorimetry (DSC). Trimers from stable 293T and CHO cells exhibited sharp, single thermal transitions at 66.9°C and 66.3°C, respectively, comparable to the 68.1°C transition of transiently produced trimers. This narrow melting profile indicates high conformational homogeneity and stability, consistent with a well-folded, native-like state.

### EXAMPLE 8

The vector platform was extended to other Env genotypes. The B41 (subtype B) and a subtype C Env gene were modified with SOSIP.664 mutations and cloned into the pAM/C backbone. The B41 SOSIP.664-D7324 variant included a C-terminal tag for purification, and monomeric B41 gp120 was also prepared as a control. These constructs demonstrated the versatility of the system for producing diverse SOSIP trimers.

### EXAMPLE 9

B41 SOSIP.664 trimers were expressed in HEK293T cells by transient transfection with co-delivered furin. When cultured in fetal calf serum (FCS)-containing medium, western blotting revealed V3-clipped gp120 fragments, indicating proteolytic degradation. However, switching to serum-free medium eliminated clipping, and thrombin addition restored it, implicating serum proteases as the source. In contrast, BG505 trimers showed no clipping even in FCS, highlighting strain-dependent stability. SEC and BN-PAGE confirmed trimer purity, and comparison with BG505 showed similar cleavage efficiency. These results underscore that the BG505 sequence is inherently more resistant to proteolysis, making it superior for stable production.
