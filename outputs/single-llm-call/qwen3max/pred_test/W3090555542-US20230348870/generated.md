# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention resides in the field of molecular biology and gene therapy, specifically relating to the treatment of genetic muscular disorders through targeted genome editing. More particularly, the invention pertains to compositions and methods for the correction of mutations in the dystrophin gene associated with Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) using CRISPR/Cas9-based gene-editing systems delivered via adeno-associated viral vectors. The invention further encompasses the use of muscle-specific promoters to drive expression of Cas9 nuclease in satellite cells, thereby enabling durable and therapeutically relevant restoration of dystrophin expression in skeletal muscle tissue. The disclosed technology represents a significant advancement in the development of long-term, potentially curative therapies for DMD by targeting the self-renewing satellite stem cell compartment, which serves as a reservoir for sustained muscle regeneration.

## INTRODUCTION

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a severe X-linked recessive genetic disorder affecting approximately 1 in 5,000 live male births, characterized by progressive and ultimately fatal skeletal muscle degeneration due to the absence of functional dystrophin protein. Dystrophin is a critical structural component of the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex that stabilizes the sarcolemma during muscle contraction. In the absence of dystrophin, muscle fibers become susceptible to mechanical stress-induced damage, leading to chronic cycles of necrosis and regeneration. Initially, the regenerative capacity of satellite cells—muscle-resident stem cells—compensates for this damage; however, over time, the constant demand for regeneration exhausts the satellite cell pool, resulting in replicative senescence, fibrosis, fatty infiltration, and irreversible loss of muscle function. Current therapeutic approaches for DMD, including corticosteroids, exon-skipping antisense oligonucleotides, and stop-codon readthrough agents, offer only symptomatic relief or partial restoration of dystrophin and do not address the underlying genetic defect. These treatments are often limited by transient efficacy, variable patient response, high cost, and inability to halt disease progression. Consequently, there remains an unmet medical need for a durable, potentially curative therapy that corrects the root genetic cause of DMD. Gene editing, particularly using CRISPR/Cas9 technology, offers a promising strategy to permanently restore the dystrophin reading frame by excising mutated exons, thereby enabling the production of a truncated but partially functional dystrophin protein. However, the success of such an approach hinges on efficient delivery to and editing of the satellite cell population, which is essential for long-term therapeutic benefit due to its role in lifelong muscle maintenance and repair.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a vector composition comprising nucleic acid sequences encoding a CRISPR/Cas9-based gene-editing system designed to correct mutations in the dystrophin gene associated with Duchenne muscular dystrophy. The composition includes a guide RNA (gRNA) component comprising at least one gRNA molecule targeting intronic regions flanking a mutated exon of the dystrophin gene, and a Cas9 component encoding a Cas9 nuclease, such as Staphylococcus aureus Cas9 (SaCas9), operably linked to a promoter. Critically, the promoter is selected from muscle-specific promoters, including but not limited to MHCK7, CK8e, and SPc5-12, which drive expression preferentially in skeletal and cardiac muscle, including satellite cells. The invention further encompasses viral vector embodiments, particularly adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors, such as AAV8 and AAV9, which exhibit high tropism for satellite cells in vivo. The vector composition may be configured as a single vector co-expressing both Cas9 and gRNA, or as multiple vectors, such as a dual-vector system wherein one vector encodes Cas9 and another encodes the gRNA(s). The gRNA and Cas9 are encoded in expressible form, meaning they are under the control of appropriate transcriptional regulatory elements to ensure functional expression in target cells. In certain embodiments, the invention includes multiple gRNA molecules designed to induce double-strand breaks at two distinct sites to excise a specific exon, such as exon 23 in the mdx mouse model or exons 45–55 in human DMD patients. The Cas9 protein may be wild-type or engineered variants, including those with nuclear localization sequences (NLS) or altered protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) specificities. The invention also provides cell compositions comprising satellite cells genetically modified using the disclosed vector compositions, as well as kits containing the vector compositions and instructions for their use in gene correction. Methods of the invention include administering the vector composition to a subject in need thereof to correct the mutant dystrophin gene in satellite cells, thereby enabling sustained dystrophin expression following muscle injury and regeneration. Additionally, the invention includes methods for screening AAV serotypes and promoters for optimal satellite cell targeting and gene-editing efficiency.

### 2. Dystrophin Gene

The dystrophin protein is a large cytoskeletal protein essential for maintaining muscle fiber integrity during contraction. It is encoded by the DMD gene, one of the largest human genes, spanning approximately 2.4 megabases on the X chromosome and containing 79 exons. Mutations in the DMD gene, most commonly large deletions or duplications, disrupt the reading frame, leading to the production of a truncated, nonfunctional protein or no protein at all, resulting in DMD. In contrast, in-frame deletions often produce a partially functional dystrophin, as seen in the milder Becker muscular dystrophy. A significant proportion of DMD patients harbor mutations within exons 45–55, and deletion of this entire region can restore the reading frame, yielding a functional Becker-like dystrophin. Exon 51 skipping is a clinically approved therapeutic strategy applicable to ~13% of DMD patients. The mutant dystrophin gene in DMD lacks a continuous ORF, while the wild-type gene maintains it. Restoring the disrupted dystrophin gene through exon excision via gene editing can convert a DMD phenotype to a milder Becker-like phenotype, offering substantial clinical benefit.

### 3. CRISPR/Cas9-Based Gene Editing System

The CRISPR/Cas9-based gene editing system of the invention utilizes a Cas9 protein, such as SaCas9, which is smaller than SpCas9 and thus more suitable for AAV packaging. Cas9 functions as an RNA-guided endonuclease that creates double-strand breaks at target DNA sequences complementary to the gRNA and adjacent to a PAM sequence (e.g., NNGRRT for SaCas9). The system includes gRNA, which consists of a scaffold domain binding Cas9 and a targeting domain (typically 20 nucleotides) complementary to the target DNA. For exon excision, two gRNAs target intronic regions flanking the exon to be deleted. The donor sequence, if used for HDR, is not required for NHEJ-mediated exon deletion. NHEJ is advantageous for DMD as it is active in post-mitotic cells and leads to indels or deletions that can restore the reading frame. The Cas9 protein may be codon-optimized for mammalian expression and fused to functional domains such as transcriptional activators or repressors, though for gene correction, nuclease-active Cas9 is preferred. The gRNA and Cas9 are encoded in synthetic polynucleotides, and the system is delivered via AAV vectors.

### 4. Genetic Constructs

The genetic constructs of the invention are recombinant nucleic acid molecules, such as plasmids or AAV vectors, encoding the CRISPR/Cas9 components. They may encode one or two gRNA molecules under U6 promoter control and Cas9 under a muscle-specific promoter. The construct includes regulatory elements: a promoter, initiation codon, stop codon, and polyadenylation signal. Muscle-specific promoters like MHCK7, CK8e, and SPc5-12 are used to restrict expression to muscle tissues, reducing off-target effects and immune responses. Coding sequences are codon-optimized for stability and expression. The construct may be a linear minichromosome or a viral vector, and it functions to deliver and express the gene-editing machinery in target cells.

### 5. Pharmaceutical Compositions

The pharmaceutical compositions comprise the genetic construct formulated in a pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The formulation may be isotonic and include vasoconstriction agents to reduce vector dispersion. Excipients include buffers, salts, and stabilizers suitable for intramuscular or systemic administration.

### 6. Administration

The genetic construct is administered to cells in vivo via viral infection, particularly AAV serotypes 8 or 9. Alternative methods include electroporation, nucleofection, or nanoparticle delivery, though AAV is preferred for in vivo muscle targeting.

### 7. Kits

Kits for restoring dystrophin function include the genetic construct or pharmaceutical composition, along with instructions for use in CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing of satellite cells.

### 8. Methods

The method of correcting a mutant dystrophin gene in a cell involves delivering the vector composition to satellite cells, where Cas9 and gRNA are expressed, inducing double-strand breaks and NHEJ-mediated exon deletion, thereby restoring the reading frame and enabling dystrophin expression.

## 9. EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Plasmid design involved cloning muscle-specific promoters upstream of SaCas9 in AAV transfer plasmids, and gRNA cassettes were constructed for exon 23 excision in mdx mice. AAV9 vectors were produced and titrated. Animal models included Pax7nGFP;Ai9;mdx mice for satellite cell tracking. In vivo AAV administration via intramuscular or tail-vein injection was performed. Cre-mediated recombination in satellite cells was assessed using the dual reporter system. AAV9-CRISPR experiments showed exon 23 deletion in satellite cells. Serial injury with BaCl2 induced muscle degeneration, and AAV-CRISPR cell transplantation demonstrated engraftment of corrected satellite cells. Satellite cells were isolated by FACS based on GFP expression. Genomic DNA analysis via PCR and Tn5-mediated sequencing quantified editing. Histology and immunofluorescence confirmed dystrophin expression. Western blots detected SaCas9 and dystrophin. Oligonucleotide sequences for gRNAs and primers were designed. Results showed AAV9 efficiently transduces satellite cells, and CRISPR editing leads to sustained dystrophin after injury.

### Example 2

The Ai9 mouse allele enabled permanent tdTomato labeling upon Cre recombination. Pax7-nGFP mice labeled satellite cells. AAV serotype profiling compared AAV1, 2, 5, 6.2, 8, and 9. Results showed AAV8 and AAV9 had highest satellite cell tropism after systemic injection, with 20–30% recombination efficiency across muscle types.

### Example 3

AAV9-CRISPR constructs targeting exon 23 were delivered systemically. Gene editing in satellite cells was confirmed by PCR and sequencing. Results demonstrated detectable exon deletion in satellite cells from hindlimb and diaphragm muscles eight weeks post-treatment.

### Example 4

Muscle-specific promoters (MHCK7, CK8e, SPc5-12) drove Cre or SaCas9 expression. Recombination efficiency in satellite cells was 11–16%, lower than CMV (33%) but significant. Gene editing with muscle-specific promoters restored dystrophin, with MHCK7 showing highest efficiency (73% dystrophin+ fibers vs. 35% for CMV).

### Example 5

Serial injury with BaCl2 was performed three times. After vector loss (confirmed by Western blot), dystrophin expression persisted at ~10% of fibers, indicating contribution from edited satellite cells.

### Example 6

In serial transplantation, CRISPR-corrected satellite cells from donor mdx mice were transplanted into irradiated mdx hosts. Results showed 5.95 dystrophin+ fibers/mm² vs. 1.46 in controls. Deletion PCR and sequencing confirmed edited origin. Two methods—serial injury and transplantation—validated long-term dystrophin expression from edited satellite cells. Tn5 sequencing quantified editing at ~1% in satellite cells. The invention demonstrates that targeting satellite cells with AAV-CRISPR enables durable therapy for DMD.
