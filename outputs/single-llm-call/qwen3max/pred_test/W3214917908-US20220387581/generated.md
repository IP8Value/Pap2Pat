# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) represents a significant and ongoing threat to global swine production, causing severe enteric disease characterized by acute watery diarrhea, vomiting, dehydration, and high mortality—particularly in neonatal piglets less than seven days of age, where mortality rates can approach 100%. First identified in Europe in the early 1970s, PEDV re-emerged with devastating impact in Asia around 2010 and was introduced into the United States in 2013, rapidly spreading across North America and resulting in substantial economic losses estimated in the hundreds of millions of dollars. The virus belongs to the family Coronaviridae and is an enveloped, positive-sense, single-stranded RNA virus with a genome approximately 28 kilobases in length. PEDV primarily targets mature enterocytes lining the small intestinal villi, leading to villous atrophy, malabsorption, and life-threatening secretory diarrhea. Despite its well-characterized pathogenesis and the urgent need for effective countermeasures, current vaccine options remain suboptimal. Conditionally licensed vaccines in North America, including those based on inactivated whole virus or RNA platforms, have demonstrated only marginal efficacy in field conditions, failing to consistently induce robust mucosal immunity or provide reliable passive protection to nursing piglets. This limited effectiveness stems in part from the inability of injectable vaccines to stimulate sufficient secretory IgA responses in the gut mucosa—the primary site of viral replication—and the logistical challenges associated with mass parenteral administration in large commercial herds. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for a safe, stable, cost-effective, and orally deliverable vaccine capable of eliciting both systemic and mucosal immune responses, thereby conferring durable passive immunity to offspring through lactogenic transfer.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel plant-based vaccine platform for the prevention and control of porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) in swine, specifically utilizing transgenic maize grain as a bioreactor for the production and oral delivery of immunologically relevant fragments of the PEDV spike (S) protein. The vaccine is produced by expressing a codon-optimized nucleic acid sequence encoding the S1 subunit or core neutralizing epitope (COE) domain of the PEDV spike glycoprotein in maize embryos, resulting in high-level accumulation of the recombinant antigen within the grain. This maize-derived antigen is administered orally to pregnant sows during gestation, eliminating the need for purification, cold-chain storage, or injection. The invention leverages the natural bioencapsulation properties of maize grain to protect the antigen from degradation in the gastrointestinal tract, enabling efficient uptake by gut-associated lymphoid tissue and subsequent induction of mucosal and systemic immune responses. Experimental results demonstrate that sows fed low-dose maize expressing the S1 antigen exhibit significantly elevated levels of neutralizing antibodies (NABs) in serum and, critically, in milk post-farrowing. These NABs are transferred to nursing piglets via lactation, correlating with markedly improved survival rates following PEDV challenge. Furthermore, the vaccine modulates the maternal immune response by reducing levels of granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF)—a pro-inflammatory cytokine associated with disease severity—in both serum and milk. Notably, lower GM-CSF concentrations in milk were strongly correlated with higher piglet survival, suggesting an immunomodulatory component to the protective effect. In addition to its therapeutic utility, the expressed S1 polypeptide or its derivatives may serve as a diagnostic reagent for detecting anti-PEDV antibodies in biological samples, facilitating serological surveillance and vaccine efficacy monitoring. The invention thus provides a practical, scalable, and economically viable solution to a major veterinary health challenge, combining ease of administration, long-term antigen stability at ambient temperatures, and dual functionality as both a prophylactic vaccine and a diagnostic tool.

## DESCRIPTION

Porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) is a member of the genus Alphacoronavirus within the family Coronaviridae, closely related to other porcine coronaviruses such as transmissible gastroenteritis virus (TGEV) and porcine respiratory coronavirus (PRCV). The virus has a monopartite, positive-sense, single-stranded RNA genome of approximately 28,000 nucleotides, flanked by a 5′ cap and a 3′ poly-A tail, and encodes both structural and non-structural proteins through a nested set of subgenomic mRNAs. The genome organization includes a 5′ untranslated region (UTR), followed by two large overlapping open reading frames (ORF1a and ORF1b) that encode the replicase polyproteins, and then a series of downstream genes encoding the structural proteins: spike (S), envelope (E), membrane (M), and nucleocapsid (N), as well as accessory proteins such as ORF3. The spike (S) glycoprotein is a type I transmembrane protein that forms homotrimers on the viral surface and mediates receptor binding and membrane fusion. It is synthesized as a single precursor that is cleaved by host proteases into two functional subunits: S1 and S2. The S1 subunit, located at the N-terminus, contains the receptor-binding domain (RBD) and harbors multiple neutralizing epitopes, including the core neutralizing epitope (COE) spanning amino acids 499–638, which is highly conserved among virulent strains and serves as a primary target for neutralizing antibodies. The S2 subunit mediates fusion between the viral and host cell membranes following receptor engagement. Cleavage of the S protein at the S1/S2 boundary, often facilitated by furin-like proteases, is critical for viral infectivity and is influenced by specific motifs such as the GPRLQPY sequence found in highly virulent U.S. outbreak strains. The M protein is the most abundant structural component, playing a central role in virion assembly, while the N protein encapsidates the viral RNA genome. The ORF3 gene encodes an ion channel protein that modulates virulence; deletions or mutations in ORF3 are frequently associated with attenuated phenotypes. PEDV strains are classified into two major genogroups: Genogroup 1 (G1), historically circulating in Europe and Asia, and Genogroup 2 (G2), which includes the highly virulent strains responsible for recent pandemics in Asia and the Americas. G2 is further subdivided into G2a (classical U.S. strains) and G2b (emerging variants with insertions/deletions in the S gene). Clinical signs of infection include acute vomiting, profuse watery diarrhea, and rapid dehydration, with histopathological examination revealing severe villous atrophy in the jejunum and ileum. Current vaccines include live-attenuated and inactivated formulations, but these suffer from poor immunogenicity, safety concerns, or inadequate mucosal immunity. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a plant-produced S1 polypeptide expressed in maize grain. The nucleic acid molecule encoding the S1 antigen is codon-optimized for expression in plants, particularly Zea mays, using degenerate codon substitutions that maintain the amino acid sequence while enhancing translational efficiency. The optimized sequence is operably linked to a seed-preferred promoter, such as a glutamine synthetase or embryo-specific promoter, and a termination signal, and cloned into an expression vector suitable for Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. Transgenic maize lines, such as Hi II, are generated using modified Ishida protocols involving co-cultivation with Agrobacterium strain LBA4404 harboring plasmid pTOK162 or EHA101, followed by selection on bialaphos-containing media. The resulting transgenic plants accumulate the S1 protein in the embryo, which is enriched via milling and separation. The polypeptide may be used as a full-length S1, a COE fragment, or a conservatively modified variant exhibiting at least 85% amino acid identity, and may include signal peptides for endoplasmic reticulum retention (e.g., KDEL) to enhance stability. The invention encompasses isolated nucleic acids, expression cassettes, vectors, transgenic plants, and methods of producing the antigen, as well as compositions for oral administration to induce passive immunity in offspring.

### EXAMPLES

The spike protein of PEDV is the primary immunogen due to its surface exposure and role in receptor binding, making it the logical focus for vaccine development. Prototype candidates based on S1, S2, or the COE domain have shown immunogenicity, but large-scale production of full-length S protein has been hindered by low yields and instability in conventional expression systems. Oral vaccination is particularly advantageous for enteric pathogens like PEDV, as it stimulates mucosal immunity at the site of infection. Precedent exists for oral PEDV vaccines using probiotic bacteria, but plant-based systems offer superior scalability and stability. Maize grain is an ideal platform because it naturally protects recombinant proteins from gastric degradation, allows ambient storage, and integrates seamlessly into animal feed. Early studies with TGEV S protein in maize demonstrated efficacy in pigs and safety in humans, validating the platform. In this work, the COE domain of PEDV S1 was codon-optimized and synthesized as overlapping fragments, which were assembled and cloned into the pSB11 binary vector under the control of a seed-specific promoter. Transgenic maize line PDC18 was generated via Agrobacterium-mediated transformation of Hi II immature embryos, followed by selection and regeneration. Western blot analysis confirmed high-level S1 accumulation in the germ fraction. For the animal trial, sixteen PEDV-naïve sows were divided into four groups: injected commercial vaccine (INJ), unvaccinated controls (CON), low-dose oral maize (LOV; 10 mg S1/kg feed), and high-dose oral maize (HOV; 50 mg S1/kg feed). Vaccinations occurred during three 3-day windows in gestation. Post-farrowing, litters were challenged with virulent PEDV. Piglets from LOV sows exhibited 53% survival, significantly higher than CON (10%) and HOV (7%), suggesting a dose-dependent tolerance effect. Serum and milk from LOV sows showed elevated neutralizing antibodies, particularly in milk, which correlated strongly with piglet survival. Additionally, GM-CSF levels were significantly reduced in LOV sow serum and milk, and low milk GM-CSF was inversely correlated with mortality. These findings establish that oral delivery of maize-produced S1 antigen induces protective lactogenic immunity and modulates inflammatory responses, offering a practical and effective strategy against PEDV.

## Example 2

Survival rates among treatment groups revealed a clear protective effect of the low-dose oral vaccine. On day 11 post-challenge, piglets from sows in the LOV group demonstrated a survival rate of 53%, which was statistically superior to both the control group (10% survival) and the high-dose oral vaccine group (7% survival). The injected vaccine group showed intermediate protection with a 37% survival rate. Logistic regression analysis confirmed that treatment group and piglet body weight at challenge were significant predictors of survival, with LOV conferring the highest odds of survival compared to all other groups. Notably, the inverse relationship between vaccine dose and efficacy—where the high-dose group performed worse than controls—suggests the induction of oral tolerance at elevated antigen concentrations, a phenomenon observed in prior TGEV vaccine studies. This dose-response profile underscores the importance of antigen titration in oral vaccine design and highlights the therapeutic window within which the maize-based S1 antigen elicits optimal protective immunity without triggering immunosuppressive mechanisms.

## Example 3

Cytokine profiling revealed a significant immunomodulatory effect of the S1 antigen, particularly on granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF). Sows in both the LOV and INJ groups exhibited significantly lower concentrations of GM-CSF in serum compared to control animals. More importantly, milk GM-CSF levels were markedly reduced in LOV sows, and this reduction was strongly correlated with increased piglet survival. Specifically, milk GM-CSF concentrations below 436 pg/mL were associated with high survival rates, whereas levels exceeding 1193 pg/mL corresponded with near-total mortality in control litters. Statistical correlation analysis confirmed that each standard deviation increase in milk GM-CSF was associated with a substantial decrease in the odds of piglet survival. No other cytokines among the 13 measured—including interferon-gamma, interleukin-6, and tumor necrosis factor-alpha—showed significant differences between groups, indicating a specific regulatory effect on GM-CSF. Given GM-CSF’s established role in amplifying inflammatory responses in diseases such as inflammatory bowel disease and severe coronavirus infections, its downregulation likely contributes to mitigating immunopathology in infected piglets, thereby enhancing survival. This dual mechanism—induction of neutralizing antibodies coupled with suppression of a key pro-inflammatory cytokine—represents a novel and advantageous feature of the plant-based vaccine platform.
