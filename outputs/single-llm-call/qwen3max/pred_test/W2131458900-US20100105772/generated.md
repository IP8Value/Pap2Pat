# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of ophthalmic therapeutics and, more specifically, to the use of specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators—particularly aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analogs (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1)—for the treatment and prevention of corneal neovascularization. Corneal neovascularization is a pathological condition characterized by the abnormal growth of blood vessels into the normally avascular cornea, often resulting from inflammation, infection, trauma, or surgical intervention. This condition can severely impair vision and is a leading cause of corneal graft failure following transplantation. The invention provides novel therapeutic agents that act through dual mechanisms: first, by resolving inflammatory responses through modulation of innate immune cell infiltration and cytokine production; and second, by directly inhibiting angiogenic signaling pathways, particularly those mediated by vascular endothelial growth factor-A (VEGF-A) and its receptor VEGFR2. The scope of the invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising these lipid mediators, methods for their topical or local ocular administration, and their use in treating diseases associated with pathological angiogenesis and chronic inflammation in the eye, especially within the corneal tissue.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Corneal neovascularization (CNV) is a sight-threatening complication arising from a breakdown in the cornea’s natural avascularity, which is essential for maintaining optical clarity. Under normal physiological conditions, the cornea is devoid of both blood and lymphatic vessels due to a delicate balance of pro-angiogenic and anti-angiogenic factors. However, this equilibrium is disrupted during inflammatory insults such as infection, chemical burns, autoimmune disorders, or surgical procedures like suture placement. In response to such stimuli, pro-inflammatory cytokines—including interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α)—are upregulated, triggering the expression of potent angiogenic mediators, most notably members of the vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) family. VEGF-A drives hemangiogenesis (blood vessel formation), while VEGF-C and VEGF-D promote lymphangiogenesis (lymphatic vessel formation). The resultant ingrowth of new vessels compromises corneal transparency, induces scarring, and increases the risk of immune rejection in corneal transplants.

Current therapeutic approaches for CNV are limited and often inadequate. Corticosteroids and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are commonly used to suppress inflammation but do not specifically target angiogenesis and carry significant side effects with prolonged use, including elevated intraocular pressure, cataract formation, and delayed wound healing. Anti-VEGF agents such as bevacizumab or ranibizumab have shown some efficacy in reducing corneal blood vessel growth when administered topically or via subconjunctival injection; however, they primarily address only the angiogenic component and fail to resolve the underlying inflammatory cascade. Moreover, these biologics exhibit poor corneal penetration, short residence time, and potential systemic absorption risks. Laser photocoagulation and fine-needle diathermy are invasive mechanical interventions that may cause collateral tissue damage and are unsuitable for diffuse or recurrent neovascularization. There remains a critical unmet need for therapeutics that simultaneously resolve inflammation and inhibit pathological angiogenesis without compromising corneal integrity or inducing significant adverse effects. The discovery of endogenous lipid mediators—such as lipoxins and resolvins—that actively promote the resolution phase of inflammation offers a promising new paradigm for addressing this complex pathophysiology.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention introduces a novel class of therapeutic agents derived from specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs), specifically aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1), for the treatment of corneal neovascularization and associated inflammatory conditions. These compounds are generated endogenously during the resolution phase of inflammation from omega-3 and omega-6 polyunsaturated fatty acids and function as potent dual-action molecules: they suppress acute inflammation while actively promoting tissue repair and homeostasis. Central to the invention is the discovery that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 significantly reduce corneal hemangiogenesis by modulating key components of the innate immune response and angiogenic signaling pathways. Experimental results demonstrate that these lipid mediators downregulate the infiltration of neutrophils and macrophages into inflamed corneal tissue, suppress the expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β and TNF-α, and inhibit the mRNA expression of VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2—critical drivers of pathological blood vessel formation. Notably, ATLa exhibits superior efficacy by also directly inhibiting VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis, a property not shared by RvD1 or RvE1, thereby positioning it as the most potent agent among the tested mediators for controlling corneal neovascularization.

The therapeutic applications of the invention extend to the management of various ocular disorders characterized by inflammation-driven angiogenesis, including post-surgical corneal haze, infectious keratitis, chemical burns, and corneal graft rejection. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions formulated for topical, subconjunctival, or intraocular delivery, ensuring targeted action with minimal systemic exposure. Related patents in the field of SPMs describe their anti-inflammatory roles in systemic diseases, but none disclose their specific application in corneal angiogenesis or their differential effects on VEGF-mediated pathways in the eye. By harnessing the body’s natural resolution mechanisms, the disclosed agents offer a physiologically aligned strategy that avoids the immunosuppressive pitfalls of conventional therapies. The invention thus provides a groundbreaking approach to preserving corneal clarity and visual function through the coordinated regulation of inflammation and angiogenesis.

In the context of ocular pathology, lymphangiogenesis refers to the formation of new lymphatic vessels, while angiogenesis denotes the formation of new blood vessels; together, they constitute neovascularization, a hallmark of chronic inflammatory disease in the cornea. The VEGF family includes VEGF-A, VEGF-B, VEGF-C, VEGF-D, and placental growth factor (PlGF), which signal through three primary tyrosine kinase receptors: VEGFR1 (Flt-1), VEGFR2 (KDR/Flk-1), and VEGFR3 (Flt-4). VEGFR2 is the principal mediator of VEGF-A-induced hemangiogenesis, whereas VEGFR3 is critical for lymphangiogenesis driven by VEGF-C and VEGF-D. Lipoxins are trihydroxy-containing eicosanoids biosynthesized from arachidonic acid via transcellular metabolism involving lipoxygenases; aspirin-triggered lipoxins (ATLs) are epimeric forms generated in the presence of acetylated cyclooxygenase-2. ATLa, a stable synthetic analog of 15-epi-lipoxin A4, exhibits enhanced metabolic stability and prolonged biological activity compared to native lipoxin A4. Experimental data reveal that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 each reduce neutrophil and macrophage infiltration into inflamed corneas, suppress inflammatory cytokine expression (notably IL-1β and TNF-α), and downregulate VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2 mRNA levels. Crucially, only ATLa demonstrates direct inhibition of VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis, highlighting its unique dual mechanism of action.

### Results

Receptor expression analysis confirmed that Fpr-rs2 (a murine homolog of the human ALX/FPR2 lipoxin receptor) and ChemR23 (the resolvin E1 receptor) are expressed in corneal epithelial cells, stromal keratocytes, and infiltrating CD11b+ immune cells, establishing the molecular basis for the observed biological effects. Treatment with ATLa, RvD1, or RvE1 significantly reduced neutrophil and macrophage infiltration at 24 and 72 hours post-injury, as quantified by immunofluorescence staining for NIMP-R14 and F4/80 markers. Real-time PCR demonstrated greater than 50% reduction in IL-1β mRNA and significant suppression of TNF-α expression, particularly with RvE1 at later time points. All three mediators uniformly downregulated VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2 transcripts without affecting VEGF-D or VEGFR3, indicating selective inhibition of hemangiogenesis over lymphangiogenesis. In vivo assessment using slit lamp biomicroscopy and corneal flatmount immunostaining (CD31 for blood vessels, LYVE-1 for lymphatics) showed that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 each reduced suture-induced blood vessel density by approximately 60–65% compared to vehicle controls, with no significant effect on lymphatic vessel density. Furthermore, in micropocket assays, ATLa—but not RvD1 or RvE1—significantly suppressed VEGF-A-induced hemangiogenesis, confirming its direct anti-angiogenic activity. Additional experiments revealed that ATLa regulates hyaluronan (HA) production induced by IL-1β and VEGF-A, suggesting modulation of extracellular matrix remodeling as an ancillary mechanism.

### Discussion

The collective findings establish that ATLa, RvD1, and RvE1 exert potent anti-angiogenic effects in the cornea primarily through early resolution of innate immune responses. By blocking neutrophil infiltration—the initial wave of inflammation—they disrupt the cytokine cascade that amplifies macrophage recruitment and VEGF expression. The reduction in macrophage numbers further attenuates sustained VEGF production, creating a feedback loop that limits hemangiogenesis. ATLa’s unique ability to directly inhibit VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis likely stems from its interaction with Fpr-rs2 on endothelial cells or keratocytes, leading to suppression of endothelial cell adhesion, migration, and proliferation. Its structural stability confers a longer duration of action compared to native lipoxins, enhancing therapeutic efficacy. The selective regulation of VEGF-A/-C and VEGFR2—without affecting lymphangiogenic pathways—suggests a targeted mechanism that preserves immune surveillance while curbing pathological vessel growth. Importantly, ATLa’s suppression of IL-1β and VEGF-A-stimulated HA production implicates modulation of the pericellular matrix, which influences endothelial cell behavior. These multiple, complementary mechanisms underlie ATLa’s superior potency in controlling corneal neovascularization and position it as a leading candidate for clinical translation in ophthalmic therapeutics.

### Methods

The experimental framework employed a well-established murine model of suture-induced inflammatory corneal angiogenesis, wherein three 11-0 nylon sutures were placed intrastromally to provoke a reproducible neovascular response peaking at 14 days. Complementary corneal micropocket assays involved implantation of Hydron pellets containing IL-1β or VEGF-A to isolate cytokine- versus growth factor-driven angiogenesis. Compounds were administered via subconjunctival injection (100 ng/10 µL every 48 hours) in a masked protocol. RNA isolation from microdissected corneal layers and infiltrating CD11b+ cells enabled cell-type-specific receptor profiling via RT-PCR, confirming Fpr-rs2 and ChemR23 expression in epithelium, keratocytes, and immune cells. Real-time PCR quantified cytokine and VEGF pathway gene expression, normalized to GAPDH. Immunohistochemistry on whole-mount corneas used CD31/LYVE-1 co-staining to distinguish blood and lymphatic vessels, with morphometric analysis via ImageJ. Statistical comparisons used two-tailed t-tests. The motivation for targeting ocular inflammation stems from the eye’s immune privilege and the devastating consequences of neovascularization in anterior segment diseases. Unlike the posterior chamber, the cornea lacks vasculature, making it uniquely susceptible to inflammatory disruption. Current retinal anti-VEGF therapies are ineffective in the cornea due to poor penetration and differing pathophysiology. Lipoxins represent a novel class of agents that align with endogenous resolution programs, offering advantages over broad immunosuppressants. Their suitability for topical formulation addresses limitations of current treatments, which suffer from instability, toxicity, or incomplete mechanism coverage. In corneal transplantation, where suture-induced inflammation drives graft failure, lipoxins could improve outcomes by preventing neovascularization without impairing wound healing. Chemical definitions provided herein—including alkyl, alkenyl, alkynyl, heteroalkyl, aryl, heteroaryl, halogen, and protecting groups—are standard in medicinal chemistry and support the broad claim scope covering analogs and derivatives. Chirality, double bond geometry, and metabolic stability considerations inform the design of optimized lipoxin-based therapeutics.

### Lipoxins

Lipoxins are endogenous eicosanoids derived from arachidonic acid that signal through G-protein-coupled receptors to promote inflammation resolution. Prior art describes their biosynthesis and anti-inflammatory roles, but not their application in corneal angiogenesis. The invention encompasses lipoxin analogs defined by structural formulas I–XI, wherein variables R1–R6, Y1–Y2, and T represent defined substituents including alkyl, alkenyl, alkynyl, aryl, heteroaryl, halogen, and phenoxy groups. Preferred embodiments include compounds where R1 is a C1–C6 alkyl, R2 and R3 are hydroxyl or protected hydroxyl groups, R4 is hydrogen or methyl, R5 is carboxylic acid or ester, and Y1/Y2 denote specific stereochemical configurations. Pharmaceutically acceptable salts, esters, and prodrugs are included to enhance solubility, stability, or bioavailability. Synthetic schemes yield high-purity compounds (>95% by LC-MS), with stereoisomers separated by chiral chromatography. The “active region” of lipoxins refers to the trihydroxy motif essential for receptor binding, while the “recognition site” involves the conjugated tetraene system. Metabolic transformation regions are modified to resist rapid inactivation (e.g., 15-epi configuration in ATLa). Labeled analogs incorporating detectable moieties (e.g., fluorescent tags) enable pharmacokinetic studies. Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the lipoxin analog dissolved or suspended in carriers suitable for ophthalmic use—such as buffered saline, hydrogels, or nanoemulsions—with optional additives like antioxidants (e.g., ascorbic acid), preservatives, or viscosity enhancers. Formulations include solutions, ointments, gels, and sustained-release implants. Dosage forms are prepared by standard methods: tablets by compression, capsules by filling, injectables by sterile filtration. For ocular delivery, unit doses contain 0.1–100 µg of active compound, administered topically, subconjunctivally, or intracamerally. The invention thus provides a robust platform for developing next-generation therapeutics that harness the body’s natural resolution pathways to treat blinding corneal diseases.
