# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Keratinocyte culturing represents a foundational technique in regenerative medicine, dermatological research, and clinical cell therapy. Human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) are the predominant cell type in the epidermis and serve critical roles in maintaining skin barrier integrity, wound healing, and immune defense. The ability to expand these cells ex vivo has enabled life-saving treatments for patients with extensive burns, genetic skin disorders such as junctional epidermolysis bullosa, and other debilitating epithelial conditions. However, despite decades of advancement, the standard methodology for expanding HEKs remains heavily reliant on xenogeneic components that pose significant safety, regulatory, and reproducibility challenges.

The current gold standard protocol for keratinocyte expansion was established by Rheinwald and Green (R&G) in the 1970s. This method involves co-culturing primary human keratinocytes with lethally irradiated mouse 3T3-J2 fibroblasts, which act as a feeder layer to support keratinocyte proliferation and prevent terminal differentiation. The culture medium used in this system—typically a 3:1 mixture of Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) and Ham’s F12—contains fetal bovine serum (FBS), cholera toxin, epidermal growth factor, insulin, hydrocortisone, adenine, and triiodothyronine. While effective for laboratory-scale expansion and certain clinical applications, this system incorporates multiple animal-derived components classified as high-risk ancillary materials under U.S. Pharmacopeia guidelines. Specifically, murine feeders, bovine serum, and bacterial cholera toxin fall into Risk Tier 4, presenting potential hazards of zoonotic pathogen transmission, immunogenic reactions, and batch-to-batch variability.

These limitations have hindered broader clinical adoption of keratinocyte-based therapies. Regulatory agencies such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) restrict the use of R&G-cultured epithelial autografts to compassionate or emergency scenarios—typically for burns covering more than 30% of total body surface area—due to concerns over xenogeneic contamination. Moreover, the undefined nature of the co-culture system complicates process validation, quality control, and compliance with Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards. Numerous attempts to develop xeno-free and serum-free alternatives have been reported, including the use of human feeder cells, conditioned media, recombinant proteins, and extracellular matrix coatings such as fibronectin, collagens, and vitronectin. However, most of these approaches either fail to sustain long-term proliferation without initial 3T3 expansion or still require undefined supplements like bovine pituitary extract to maintain progenitor phenotypes.

Consequently, there remains an urgent and unmet need for a fully defined, xeno-free, and serum-free culture system that supports robust, scalable, and clinically compliant expansion of human epidermal keratinocytes from primary isolation through multiple passages. Such a system must eliminate all animal-derived components while preserving the proliferative capacity, genetic stability, and functional potency of keratinocytes—attributes essential for both therapeutic applications and reliable scientific investigation.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION

The present invention introduces a novel, chemically defined, and xeno-free method for culturing human epidermal keratinocytes without the use of feeder cells or animal-derived sera. This system is based on the discovery that specific laminin isoforms—namely Laminin-511 and Laminin-421—can effectively replace murine 3T3 fibroblasts as a substrate for keratinocyte adhesion, proliferation, and maintenance of progenitor identity. The invention provides a complete culture platform comprising a defined laminin-coated substrate and a serum-free, animal component-free cell culture medium.

In one embodiment, tissue culture surfaces are coated with recombinant human Laminin-511 or Laminin-421 at a concentration of approximately 2.5 µg/cm². These laminins are selected based on their presence in the native epidermal basement membrane or their expression by conventional feeder cells, respectively. The coated substrates support immediate adhesion and long-term expansion of freshly isolated primary keratinocytes without requiring prior adaptation on feeder layers.

The cell culture medium employed in this system is Keratinocyte Growth Medium-Chemically Defined (KGM-CD), which contains no animal sera, pituitary extracts, or microbial toxins. Instead, it comprises a basal medium supplemented with precisely defined concentrations of insulin, hydrocortisone, epidermal growth factor, transferrin, ethanolamine, phosphoethanolamine, selenium, and other small molecules necessary for keratinocyte survival and proliferation. This formulation ensures reproducibility, regulatory compliance, and suitability for clinical manufacturing.

Cells cultured using this method express canonical keratinocyte markers, including keratin 5 (KRT5), keratin 14 (KRT14), and transcription factor p63, confirming their basal progenitor identity. Furthermore, they retain colony-forming efficiency, genetic stability over multiple passages, and the ability to stratify into multilayered epidermis in organotypic and in vivo models.

The plating and culturing process begins with enzymatic isolation of keratinocytes from human skin biopsies, followed by direct seeding onto laminin-coated plates at densities ranging from 1×10⁴ to 9×10⁴ cells/cm². Cultures are maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 10% CO₂. Upon reaching confluence, cells are passaged using TrypLE Select or similar recombinant trypsin substitutes, avoiding animal-derived trypsin.

The trypsinization process is optimized to minimize cellular stress: incubation times range from 8 to 16 minutes at 37°C, followed by neutralization with serum-free medium. This gentle dissociation preserves cell viability and enables serial passaging without loss of proliferative potential.

The overall system maintains keratinocytes in an undifferentiated, proliferative state for at least ten passages, with population doubling rates comparable to the R&G method. Critically, the absence of xenogeneic components eliminates biosafety risks and facilitates regulatory approval for therapeutic use.

Additionally, the invention encompasses a kit for culturing keratinocytes, comprising pre-coated culture vessels with Laminin-511 or Laminin-421, vials of KGM-CD medium, and instructions for use. This kit enables standardized, off-the-shelf implementation in research laboratories and GMP-compliant manufacturing facilities.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present patent application discloses compositions and methods for the xeno-free, serum-free, and feeder-free culture of human epidermal keratinocytes. The accompanying drawings, if included, illustrate experimental data supporting the efficacy of the disclosed system, including immunofluorescence images, growth curves, RNA-seq heatmaps, and histological sections; however, the scope of the invention is not limited by the figures.

As used herein, the term “keratinocyte” refers to epithelial cells derived from the epidermis of human skin, including basal progenitor cells capable of self-renewal and differentiation into suprabasal layers. Keratinocytes function as the primary structural and protective cells of the skin, forming a stratified barrier against environmental insults, pathogens, and dehydration. In primary culture, however, keratinocytes rapidly undergo senescence or terminal differentiation unless supported by appropriate extracellular cues—a major limitation of conventional systems.

The basement membrane (BM) is a specialized extracellular matrix underlying the epidermis, composed of laminins, type IV collagen, perlecan, agrin, and nidogen. Among these, laminins are heterotrimeric glycoproteins consisting of α, β, and γ chains that assemble into distinct isoforms. Laminin isoforms are designated by numbers corresponding to their constituent chains (e.g., Laminin-511 = α5β1γ1). Key laminins in human skin include Laminin-332 (α3β3γ2), Laminin-311 (α3β1γ1), Laminin-321 (α3β2γ1), Laminin-511 (α5β1γ1), and Laminin-521 (α5β2γ1). Additionally, Laminin-411 (α4β1γ1) and Laminin-421 (α4β2γ1) are expressed by murine 3T3-J2 fibroblasts but not normally found in human epidermal BM.

The methods of the present disclosure involve culturing keratinocytes on matrices composed of recombinant human laminins, specifically Laminin-511, Laminin-521, Laminin-411, or Laminin-421. Of these, Laminin-511 and Laminin-421 demonstrate superior performance in supporting long-term proliferation and progenitor maintenance. The cell culture substrate consists of tissue culture plastic or glass coated with a purified laminin solution at 2.5 µg/cm², incubated overnight at 4°C.

Effective laminins include those that engage α3β1 and α6β4 integrins—key receptors mediating keratinocyte-BM adhesion. Laminin combinations (e.g., 511 + 421) were tested but showed no additive benefit, indicating single laminin isoforms suffice. Laminin-511 is defined as the heterotrimer α5β1γ1; Laminin-521 as α5β2γ1; Laminin-421 as α4β2γ1; and Laminin-411 as α4β1γ1.

The cell culture medium is a chemically defined formulation free of animal sera, pituitary extracts, and microbial products. Its ingredients include a basal medium (e.g., DMEM/F12), insulin (5 µg/mL), hydrocortisone (0.4 µg/mL), epidermal growth factor (10 ng/mL), transferrin (5 µg/mL), ethanolamine (0.1 mM), phosphoethanolamine (0.1 mM), sodium selenite (10 nM), adenine (0.18 mM), triiodothyronine (2 nM), and antibiotics. Optional ingredients may include Rho kinase inhibitors during initial seeding, though they are not required.

This system enables a complete, defined, and clinically translatable platform for keratinocyte expansion, eliminating all xenogeneic risks while maintaining functional equivalence to legacy methods.

### EXAMPLES

Human epidermal keratinocytes were isolated from surgical waste skin following ethical approval. Tissue was treated with Dispase II overnight at 4°C, epidermis separated mechanically, and keratinocytes released via 0.05% trypsin-EDTA digestion. Cells were resuspended in KGM-CD and seeded directly onto laminin-coated six-well plates at 9×10⁴ cells/cm². Control plates were either uncoated or used the R&G 3T3 co-culture method with FAD medium containing 10% FBS and cholera toxin.

Cumulative population doublings were calculated using PD = log(N/N₀)/log₂, where N is harvested cell count and N₀ is seeded count. Cells on Laminin-511 and Laminin-421 achieved population doublings comparable to 3T3 controls over 10 passages.

For qPCR, total RNA was extracted using RNeasy kits, cDNA synthesized from 500 ng RNA, and real-time RT-PCR performed with SYBR Green and gene-specific primers. GAPDH served as reference. Expression of KRT5, KRT14, KRT1, and KRT10 was monitored across passages.

FACS analysis involved fixation, permeabilization, blocking with goat serum, and staining with primary antibodies against keratinocyte markers, followed by fluorophore-conjugated secondary antibodies. Data confirmed >90% of cells retained basal markers on laminin substrates.

Immunostaining of fixed cells showed strong expression of KRT5, KRT14, and p63. Organotypic cultures were prepared using de-epidermalized dermis seeded first with fibroblasts, then with HEKs. After 7 days submerged, cultures were raised to air-liquid interface for 14 days to induce stratification. Cryosections were stained with H&E and antibodies.

RNA was isolated from cultured cells or whole skin using microRNA kits. RNA-seq libraries were prepared with TruSeq Stranded Total RNA kits, sequenced on Illumina HiSeq2000, and aligned to hg38. Differential expression analysis used DESeq2 with patient as covariate; functional enrichment used GSEA with Hallmark gene sets.

Karyotyping revealed normal chromosomal integrity in cells cultured on laminins. Transcriptome analysis showed near-identical profiles between Laminin-511 and Laminin-421, but significant differences versus 3T3 co-culture—including downregulation of inflammatory pathways and upregulation of MYC targets.

In vivo, grafts on nude mice formed stratified human epidermis expressing Ku80 (human-specific nuclear marker) and continuous laminin γ2 at the dermal-epidermal junction, confirming functional BM deposition. Epidermal thickness matched 3T3-derived grafts.

Collectively, Examples 1–6 demonstrate that the disclosed laminin-based system supports keratinocyte adhesion, proliferation, genetic stability, marker expression, stratification, and in vivo functionality—without xenogeneic components.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides improved methods for culturing human epidermal keratinocytes that overcome the critical limitations of current protocols. Traditional approaches rely on murine feeder layers, bovine serum, and bacterial toxins, introducing unacceptable risks for clinical translation. The disclosed method eliminates all animal-derived materials by utilizing recombinant human laminins—specifically Laminin-511 and Laminin-421—as defined substrates in conjunction with a chemically defined, serum-free medium.

This system supports robust expansion of primary keratinocytes from initial isolation through multiple passages, maintaining progenitor identity, genetic stability, and functional potency. Cultured cells form stratified epidermis in vitro and in vivo, express appropriate basal and differentiation markers, and deposit functional basement membrane proteins.

The advantages of the disclosed method include enhanced safety, regulatory compliance, reproducibility, scalability, and suitability for GMP manufacturing. Applications extend to regenerative medicine (e.g., burn treatment, genetic skin disease therapy), drug screening, toxicology testing, and basic research.

The scope of this disclosure encompasses the use of Laminin-511, Laminin-421, or functionally equivalent laminin isoforms; defined culture media formulations; coating protocols; passaging procedures; and kits integrating these components for standardized keratinocyte culture.
