# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of breast cancer diagnosis, particularly to methods and kits for the non-invasive detection and diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer through the quantification of specific microRNAs (miRNAs) in whole blood. More specifically, the invention provides a diagnostic approach based on the identification and measurement of circulating miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652, whose expression levels are significantly altered in the blood of individuals diagnosed with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. The invention further encompasses the use of these miRNAs as biomarkers for early detection, risk stratification, and potential monitoring of disease progression or therapeutic response in patients with this prevalent molecular subtype of breast cancer.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed malignancy among women globally, with an estimated incidence exceeding 1.3 million new cases annually. It remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women across Europe and North America. Despite significant advances in treatment modalities—including hormonal therapies for estrogen receptor (ER)-positive tumors and targeted agents such as trastuzumab for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/neu)-positive disease—metastatic dissemination continues to be the primary cause of death. Early detection is therefore critical to improving survival outcomes, as localized disease is highly curable with current interventions.

Mammography is currently the standard screening modality for breast cancer; however, it suffers from limitations including variable sensitivity in dense breast tissue, high false-positive rates leading to unnecessary biopsies, and an inability to provide molecular subtyping information. Definitive diagnosis and subtyping require invasive tissue biopsy followed by immunohistochemical or genomic analysis, which delays treatment initiation and imposes patient discomfort. Consequently, there exists a pressing clinical need for minimally invasive, blood-based biomarkers that can complement imaging modalities and facilitate early, subtype-specific diagnosis.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding RNA molecules that regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally and have emerged as promising candidates for cancer biomarkers. They are remarkably stable in circulation, resistant to degradation by RNases, and can be readily detected in whole blood, serum, or plasma. In breast cancer, miRNA expression profiles have been shown to correlate with molecular subtypes, hormone receptor status, and clinicopathological features. Importantly, tumor-derived miRNAs can be released into the bloodstream via exosomes, microvesicles, or protein complexes, offering a “liquid biopsy” that reflects underlying tumor biology.

Breast cancer is a heterogeneous disease, broadly classified into molecular subtypes including Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-enriched, and basal-like. Luminal A tumors—characterized by ER and progesterone receptor (PR) positivity and HER2/neu negativity—account for over 70% of all breast cancers and generally carry a favorable prognosis. However, even within this group, overtreatment remains a concern, prompting tools like the Oncotype DX® assay to guide adjuvant therapy decisions. A circulating biomarker specific to Luminal A-like disease could enhance early detection, reduce unnecessary procedures, and support personalized management strategies.

Prior art has explored circulating miRNAs in breast cancer, but these studies often lack subtype specificity, utilize inconsistent sample types (e.g., serum vs. plasma vs. whole blood), and fail to validate findings in independent cohorts. Moreover, no single miRNA has demonstrated sufficient sensitivity and specificity for standalone clinical use. The present invention addresses these gaps by identifying a panel of three miRNAs—miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652—that are consistently down-regulated in the whole blood of women with Luminal A-like breast cancer and collectively yield robust diagnostic performance, distinguishing affected individuals from healthy controls with high accuracy.

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION

It is an object of the present invention to identify novel, circulating microRNA biomarkers that are specifically associated with Luminal A-like breast cancer and to provide a reliable, minimally invasive method for the early detection and diagnosis of this molecular subtype. The invention aims to overcome the limitations of current diagnostic approaches by offering a blood-based test that can be integrated into routine clinical workflows, thereby enabling timely intervention and personalized therapeutic planning without the need for invasive tissue sampling at the initial diagnostic stage.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a diagnostic kit for detecting Luminal A-like breast cancer in a human subject, comprising reagents for quantifying the expression levels of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 in a whole blood sample obtained from the subject. The kit includes specific reverse transcription primers and quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) probes for each of the three miRNAs, along with instructions for performing RNA extraction, reverse transcription, and qPCR amplification. Optionally, the kit may include a reference miRNA control, such as miR-16, for normalization of expression data. The invention further encompasses a method of diagnosing Luminal A-like breast cancer by measuring the expression levels of these three miRNAs in a blood sample and comparing them to established thresholds derived from healthy control populations. A diagnostic algorithm, such as binary logistic regression, may be employed to generate a composite score based on the combined expression profile, which is then used to classify the subject as likely having or not having Luminal A-like breast cancer. The kit and method are designed for use in conjunction with standard imaging techniques like mammography to improve overall diagnostic accuracy and support early clinical decision-making.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is based on a comprehensive study designed to discover and validate circulating miRNA biomarkers specific to Luminal A-like breast cancer. The study cohort comprised 110 women, including 54 patients with histologically confirmed Luminal A-like breast cancer (ER+, PR+, HER2/neu−) and 56 age-matched healthy controls with no history of malignancy or active inflammatory disease. Whole blood was collected in EDTA-containing vacutainers and processed for total RNA extraction using TRI Reagent BD, a method optimized for miRNA recovery. RNA integrity and concentration were assessed using NanoDrop spectrophotometry and Agilent Bioanalyzer, ensuring high-quality input material for downstream analyses.

Initial discovery-phase profiling was conducted using TaqMan Low Density Array (TLDA) microfluidic cards, which enabled simultaneous quantification of 667 human miRNAs across 20 samples (10 cancer, 10 control). To address the high dimensionality and non-linear relationships inherent in miRNA array data, a feed-forward artificial neural network (ANN) with back-propagation learning was employed. This algorithm, featuring a three-layer architecture and early stopping to prevent overfitting, ranked miRNAs based on their ability to discriminate between cancer and control samples in blind validation sets. From this analysis, 76 miRNAs exhibited differential expression, with the top candidates selected for validation.

Validation was performed using real-time quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) in an independent cohort of 44 cancer patients and 46 controls. Ten candidate miRNAs were assayed using TaqMan miRNA assays, with reactions run in triplicate on 96-well plates and normalized to miR-16, a stably expressed endogenous control in breast cancer. Expression levels were calculated using the ΔCT method, and statistical significance was determined using non-parametric tests due to non-normal data distribution. The validation confirmed significant down-regulation of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 in the blood of Luminal A-like patients (p < 0.01). Notably, miR-181a and miR-652 were also under-expressed in matched tumor tissue compared to tumor-associated normal tissue, suggesting a potential tumor origin, although the correlation between tissue and circulating levels was not absolute, highlighting the complexity of miRNA release mechanisms.

### Validation Study Statistical Analysis

Statistical analysis was rigorously performed using Minitab version 16.0. Normality of data was assessed via the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, and log10 transformation was applied where necessary. Group comparisons employed Mann-Whitney U tests for non-parametric data, while associations with clinicopathological variables (tumor size, grade, nodal status, stage) were evaluated using Spearman’s rho or Pearson correlation coefficients. Binary logistic regression was used to model the probability of cancer presence based on miRNA expression, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to assess diagnostic performance. Cross-validation techniques ensured model generalizability, and p-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

### Results

RT-qPCR validation confirmed that circulating levels of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were significantly lower in Luminal A-like breast cancer patients compared to healthy controls. ROC curve analysis demonstrated that the combination of these three miRNAs yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.80, with 77% sensitivity and 74% specificity—performance metrics superior to many existing clinical biomarkers. Individually, each miRNA contributed to discrimination, but the panel effect was synergistic. Furthermore, the down-regulation was consistent across early- and late-stage disease, indicating utility in both screening and diagnostic contexts. A negative correlation was observed between miR-181a levels and invasive tumor size (r = −0.429, p = 0.059), suggesting a potential link to tumor burden. These findings establish miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 as robust, circulating biomarkers for the non-invasive detection of Luminal A-like breast cancer.
