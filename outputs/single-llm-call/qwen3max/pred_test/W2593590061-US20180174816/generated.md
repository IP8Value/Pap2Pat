# DESCRIPTION

Mass spectrometry is a foundational analytical technique widely employed across scientific disciplines for the identification and quantification of chemical species. Among various mass spectrometric methodologies, time-of-flight (TOF) mass spectrometry has gained prominence due to its inherent capability for rapid, high-sensitivity, and high-resolution analysis. TOF instruments operate by measuring the time required for ions to traverse a known flight path under the influence of an electric field, with lighter ions arriving at the detector sooner than heavier ones. This principle enables the simultaneous acquisition of full mass spectra, offering a multiplexing advantage over scanning instruments. However, conventional TOF systems face limitations in resolving power, duty cycle, dynamic range, and throughput, particularly when interfaced with continuous ion sources such as electrospray ionization (ESI) or atmospheric pressure chemical ionization (APCI). To address these constraints, advanced architectures incorporating multi-reflecting time-of-flight (MR-TOF) analyzers, orthogonal acceleration, and novel ion manipulation strategies have been developed. The present invention builds upon these foundations by introducing a suite of integrated innovations—ranging from encoded frequent pulsing and open trap electrostatic analyzers to parallel trap arrays and hybrid detectors—that collectively enable high-throughput, high-resolution, and high-dynamic-range mass spectral analysis. These advancements are particularly suited for complex mixture analysis, tandem mass spectrometry (MS-MS), and applications requiring robust data-independent acquisition, such as proteomics, metabolomics, and petroleomics.

## MR-TOF with Frequent Pulsing

Multi-reflecting time-of-flight (MR-TOF) mass spectrometers employ a folded ion path achieved through repeated reflections between electrostatic mirrors, thereby extending the effective flight distance within a compact physical footprint. This configuration significantly enhances mass resolving power, with modern implementations achieving resolutions exceeding 200,000 and approaching 1,000,000 in specialized “zoom” modes. The folded flight path is typically stabilized by periodic ion lenses that confine the ion beam along the mean trajectory, mitigating divergence in the drift direction. However, conventional MR-TOF systems coupled with orthogonal acceleration (OA) converters suffer from a low duty cycle—often below 1%—because only a narrow temporal slice of the continuous ion beam is sampled per pulse. This inefficiency is exacerbated at higher resolutions, where longer flight times necessitate even sparser pulsing to avoid spectral overlap. Alternative approaches using axial trap converters improve ion utilization but are limited by space charge capacity and are unsuitable for high-flux MS-only analyses. To overcome these limitations, the present invention introduces an open trap electrostatic analyzer, which eliminates the periodic lens array, allowing natural angular divergence to produce multiple reflection peaks per ion species. This multiplexed signal can be decoded due to the non-systematic nature of peak overlaps, thereby improving dynamic range and duty cycle. Furthermore, the invention incorporates a method of encoded frequent pulsing (EFP), wherein the orthogonal accelerator is triggered at precisely defined, non-uniform time intervals. This encoding ensures that any spectral overlap between pulses is unique and non-repetitive, enabling computational deconvolution of the composite signal. The combination of EFP with MR-TOF dramatically improves sensitivity, dynamic range, and analysis speed by up to two orders of magnitude, while maintaining high resolution. An improved EFP-MR-TOF system is proposed, integrating these features into a robust platform capable of handling high ion fluxes without saturation, thereby enabling comprehensive analysis of complex samples at femtogram levels.

## Comprehensive MS-MS (C-MS-MS)

Conventional tandem mass spectrometers, such as triple quadrupoles or quadrupole-time-of-flight (Q-TOF) instruments, rely on sequential selection of precursor ions, resulting in significant ion loss during scanning and limiting throughput in discovery-mode analyses. Comprehensive MS-MS (C-MS-MS) aims to circumvent this by enabling parallel fragmentation and detection of all precursor ions within a sample. Prior art approaches, such as the Comprehensive TOF-TOF (CTT) method, utilize a first MR-TOF stage to separate precursors in time, followed by rapid fragmentation and analysis in a second TOF. However, these systems are constrained by space charge-induced ion packet expansion in the MR-TOF analyzer, leading to substantial ion loss at the fragmentation cell entrance—particularly at ion packet densities above a few thousand charges. The LT-TOF described in U.S. Pat. No. 7,507,953 attempts to address throughput but lacks the resolution and dynamic range required for complex mixtures. The present invention proposes a novel solution: a dual-stage tandem architecture comprising either an MS-EFP-MRTOF or an MS-CID/SID-EFP-MRTOF configuration. In the former, a high-capacity first-stage mass separator (e.g., a quadrupole or ion mobility device) performs crude separation, while the EFP-MRTOF provides high-resolution fragment analysis with enhanced duty cycle. In the latter, collision-induced dissociation (CID) or surface-induced dissociation (SID) is integrated between stages, with SID offering superior transmission due to its gas-free, wide-aperture design. Critically, the EFP method enables rapid, high-fidelity reconstruction of fragment spectra even when precursor windows are broad (10–30 amu), as the precise timing of each OA pulse allows correlation of fragment ions to their precursors via time-profile deconvolution. This approach achieves near-lossless precursor transmission, high sensitivity, and unit mass resolution in parent selection, overcoming the fundamental limitations of prior C-MS-MS systems.

## Parallel Mass Separators

Analytical quadrupole mass spectrometers (Q-MS) and ion trap mass spectrometers (ITMS) are widely used for mass filtering and ion storage, respectively. Q-Trap instruments combine both functionalities but remain constrained by sequential scanning and limited space charge capacity. To enable truly parallel, high-throughput precursor separation, the present invention proposes a novel mass separator comprising an array of radio-frequency traps (TA). Each trap in the array operates independently, capable of accumulating, storing, and ejecting ions based on mass-to-charge ratio. The TA embodiment may consist of 30–50 linear RF traps arranged coaxially, each 10–30 cm in length, providing a collective space charge capacity approaching 10⁹ ions per second. Mass-selective ejection is achieved through opposing quadrupolar DC and RF fields, a method less susceptible to space charge perturbations than resonant excitation. This configuration supports rapid (10 ms) scanning with moderate resolution (30–50), sufficient for coupling with high-resolution MR-TOF detection. The TA array functions as a lossless, all-mass precursor separator, eliminating the duty cycle penalty of scanning filters and enabling comprehensive MS-MS without precursor omission. Embodiments may include planar or cylindrical geometries, with ion injection and ejection synchronized to downstream analysis stages. The parallel architecture ensures that even at extreme ion fluxes, individual traps operate below saturation, preserving spectral fidelity and dynamic range.

## Resistive Ion Guides

Ion guides are critical for transporting and focusing ion beams through regions of varying pressure and field conditions. Conventional resistive ion guides, composed of resistive rods or coatings, provide smooth DC gradients for ion propulsion but suffer from thermal instability, electrical arcing, and limited control over ion cloud density. The present invention proposes an improved resistive ion guide featuring precisely engineered resistive rods with resistance values optimized for stable DC gradient formation under high ion flux. The rods are fabricated from doped ceramics or metal oxides with tailored resistivity (10⁶–10⁹ Ω·cm), ensuring uniform potential distribution while minimizing Joule heating. RF voltages are capacitively coupled to the rods to provide radial confinement, while segmented DC biasing enables dynamic control of ion transit time and compression. This design enhances ion transmission efficiency, reduces space charge blowup, and supports continuous ion flow compatible with high-throughput EFP-MRTOF operation.

## TOF Detectors

Current time-of-flight detectors, including microchannel plates (MCPs) and Daly detectors, face limitations in dynamic range, lifetime, and saturation at high ion fluxes. The present invention introduces a hybrid TOF detector combining a metal converter, magnetic steering, and a dual photomultiplier tube (PMT) arrangement. Ions strike a metal converter, generating secondary electrons that are accelerated and magnetically steered toward a scintillator. The scintillator is coated with a photon-transparent conductive mesh to prevent charging, and emits light detected by two PMTs with different solid angles—one optimized for high gain (low flux) and one for high linearity (high flux). Additionally, an isochronous Daly detector variant is proposed, featuring an improved scintillator material with faster decay time and higher quantum yield, ensuring precise timing and extended operational life. An alternative embodiment replaces the PMTs with a microchannel plate stack coupled to a delay-line anode, providing single-ion counting with sub-nanosecond timing. These innovations collectively extend dynamic range by three orders of magnitude and increase detector lifetime by reducing surface degradation.

## Data System

Conventional TOF data systems digitize transient signals at fixed sampling rates, often insufficient for resolving overlapping peaks in frequent-pulsing regimes. The present invention proposes a dedicated data system for EFP-MRTOF, featuring high-speed analog-to-digital conversion (≥5 GS/s), real-time pulse-sequence correlation, and adaptive deconvolution algorithms. The system stores raw time-domain data and applies decoding based on the known EFP timing pattern, reconstructing individual mass spectra by identifying consistent peak hypotheses across pulse intervals. Machine learning modules assist in distinguishing true signals from random overlaps, particularly at low intensities. The architecture supports parallel processing for multi-channel or array-based instruments, ensuring scalability without latency.

## Conclusion

The proposed solutions—encompassing EFP-MRTOF, open trap analyzers, parallel trap arrays, resistive ion guides, hybrid detectors, and advanced data systems—collectively address the core limitations of prior mass spectrometric technologies. By enabling high charge throughput, lossless precursor separation, extended dynamic range, and ultra-high resolution, the invention establishes a new paradigm for high-throughput, comprehensive mass spectral analysis.

## SUMMARY

Mass spectrometers are essential tools for chemical analysis, yet prior art systems suffer from low duty cycle, limited dynamic range, and ion loss during tandem operations. The present invention proposes a novel method and apparatus for high charge throughput mass spectral analysis. Ions are generated across a wide m/z range and subjected to crude separation in time by a first-stage mass separator. The ion flow is then conditioned and injected into a multi-reflecting time-of-flight (MR-TOF) analyzer via an orthogonal accelerator triggered by time-encoded frequent pulses. This minimizes spectral overlaps and avoids saturation of the TOF analyzer or detector. A portion of the ion flow may bypass the first separator for direct analysis of abundant species. The ion beam is split into multiple channels, accumulated in a multichannel trap, and sequentially ejected with damping to form narrow packets. These are spatially confined, accelerated orthogonally, and analyzed in the MR-TOF. Signals are recorded post-separation and processed via time deconvolution. The tandem mass spectrometer apparatus integrates fragmentation cells (CID/SID) between stages, enabling comprehensive MS-MS with high sensitivity and resolution.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The mass spectrometer comprises an ion source (e.g., ESI, MALDI, or conditioned glow discharge), a high-throughput front-end, a dual cascade MS system, and a detection subsystem. The ion source generates ions over a broad m/z range. The high-throughput design employs a dual cascade method: a crude mass separator (e.g., quadrupole, IMS, or trap array) performs initial time-based separation, while a comprehensive separator (EFP-MRTOF) provides high-resolution analysis. A conditioner modulates ion flow between stages. The pulsed accelerator uses encoded frequent pulsing (EFP)—non-uniform time intervals between pulses—to multiplex ion packets without systematic overlap. The MR-TOF analyzer, with folded flight path and gridless mirrors, achieves resolving power >200,000. The ion detector features extended lifetime via hybrid design. An optional fragmentation cell (CID or SID) enables MS-MS. In operation, the first MS cascade separates precursors in time; ions are pulsed into the MR-TOF with EFP, avoiding signal overlap. Dual-stage MS improves sensitivity and dynamic range. A numerical example shows 100-fold gain in minor species detection. The system alternates between dual MS (for MS-MS) and single MS (for full-scan) modes. Another method uses the fragmentation cell in transmission mode for zoomed MS. EFP generates multiplexed signals on the MR-TOF detector, which are deconvolved using time-correlation algorithms. A trap array embodiment replaces the first separator with an array of RF traps. Planar or cylindrical arrangements distribute ion flow across channels. Continuous ion flow is buffered in ion traps meeting specific capacity and ejection requirements. The trap array accumulates and sequentially ejects ions with quadrupolar DC/RF fields. Ion guides use resistive rods (10⁶–10⁹ Ω·cm) for stable DC gradients. Orthogonal accelerators form narrow packets with <1 ns spread. The MR-TOF requires isochronous mirrors and periodic lenses. In dual cascade mode, the system achieves high throughput; in standard mode, it operates as a conventional MR-TOF. Dynamic range issues are mitigated by splitting ion flow and using EFP. As a comprehensive MS-MS, the system fragments mass-selected precursors. CID cells operate at high pressure for efficient fragmentation. EFP mode uses 100 kHz pulsing with unique intervals. The C-MS² method achieves dynamic range >10⁵. Novel traps use quadrupole DC ejection, avoiding resonance limitations. Simulations show stable ion trajectories in linear and quadrupolar traps. Resonant ejection is also supported. The novel trap surpasses LTMS in throughput. Space charge capacity is derived from Poisson’s equation, with dual-stage traps proposed for high flux. Trap arrays use planar or cylindrical configurations. Ion collection employs multistage funnels and resistive multipoles. Mechanical design includes slotted cylinders and insulated RF rods. The TOF detector uses a scintillator with conductive mesh, metal converter, and dual PMTs. Lifetime exceeds 10⁹ counts. Variations include MCP-based detectors and alternative ion sources.
