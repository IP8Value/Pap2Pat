# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of immunology and molecular therapeutics, specifically relating to the treatment and diagnosis of autoimmune and inflammatory diseases. More particularly, the invention concerns the use of insulin-like 6 (Insl6) protein, its functional fragments, derivatives, variants, and fusion constructs—especially those fused to immunoglobulin Fc domains—as therapeutic agents for modulating immune responses in subjects suffering from or at risk of developing autoimmune conditions such as myositis, including polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and inclusion body myositis. The invention further encompasses methods for diagnosing these disorders based on the detection of Insl6 expression levels in biological samples, as well as pharmaceutical compositions and gene therapy approaches designed to deliver or upregulate Insl6 activity in vivo. The disclosed technology leverages the newly discovered immunomodulatory and muscle-protective properties of Insl6, positioning it as a novel biologic for addressing unmet clinical needs in chronic inflammatory and autoimmune pathologies characterized by T-cell dysregulation and tissue damage.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Myositis refers to a heterogeneous group of idiopathic inflammatory myopathies characterized by progressive skeletal muscle weakness, infiltration of immune cells into muscle tissue, and elevated serum levels of muscle enzymes such as creatine kinase. The major subtypes include polymyositis (PM), dermatomyositis (DM), and inclusion body myositis (IBM). Polymyositis is primarily mediated by cytotoxic CD8+ T cells that invade non-necrotic muscle fibers within the endomysium, whereas dermatomyositis involves perivascular and perimysial inflammation driven largely by CD4+ helper T cells and complement-mediated microvascular injury. Despite advances in understanding their immunopathogenesis, current therapeutic options—such as corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, and intravenous immunoglobulins—are often inadequate, associated with significant side effects, and fail to halt disease progression in many patients. Moreover, the precise molecular mechanisms governing immune tolerance breakdown and sustained inflammation in myositis remain incompletely elucidated. Recent evidence implicates deficiencies in regulatory T cells (Tregs) and imbalances in pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-α and IFN-γ as central contributors to disease severity. However, no endogenous factor has been previously identified that simultaneously dampens pathogenic T-cell activation, reduces inflammatory cytokine production, and supports muscle regeneration in the context of autoimmune myositis. This gap underscores the urgent need for novel therapeutic targets that can restore immune homeostasis while preserving or enhancing tissue integrity—a need directly addressed by the present invention through the discovery and application of Insl6.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides methods and compositions for treating, preventing, and diagnosing autoimmune and inflammatory diseases, particularly myositis, through the administration or modulation of insulin-like 6 (Insl6) protein or its biologically active derivatives. Insl6 is a secreted member of the insulin-like/relaxin family, herein demonstrated to possess potent anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory activities. The invention discloses that Insl6 regulates T-regulatory (Treg) cell function and suppresses the activation and proliferation of pathogenic T cells, thereby mitigating immune-mediated tissue damage. A key embodiment involves a fusion protein comprising Insl6 linked to a human IgG1 Fc domain (Insl6-Fc), which enhances plasma half-life and bioavailability while retaining full biological activity. The invention further provides methods for treating autoimmune diseases—including polymyositis, dermatomyositis, and other Th1-dominant or non-Th1-dominant autoimmune conditions—by administering a therapeutically effective amount of an Insl6 protein, fragment, or nucleic acid encoding same. Additionally, the invention enables diagnosis of myositis by quantifying reduced levels of Insl6 mRNA or protein in muscle biopsy samples relative to healthy controls, establishing Insl6 deficiency as a biomarker of disease presence and severity. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising Insl6 agents are formulated for systemic or local delivery via parenteral, oral, or transdermal routes. Soluble Insl6 fragments containing the B- and A-domains, modified RNA (modRNA) encoding Insl6, and viral or non-viral vectors for gene therapy are also encompassed. The invention thus offers a multifaceted platform for both therapeutic intervention and diagnostic stratification in immune-mediated muscle disorders and related inflammatory conditions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is founded upon the unexpected discovery that Insl6 protein exerts protective effects in experimental autoimmune myositis by modulating T-cell responses and promoting muscle homeostasis. Insl6, a secreted polypeptide normally upregulated during acute muscle injury, functions as an endogenous immunosuppressant that limits excessive inflammation. Insl6-deficient mice exhibit exacerbated disease in a murine model of C-protein-induced myositis (CIM), showing increased CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell infiltration, elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IFN-γ), and severe motor dysfunction. Conversely, transgenic overexpression or hydrodynamic delivery of Insl6—particularly as an Fc-fusion protein—significantly ameliorates pathology. Mechanistically, Insl6 suppresses T-cell proliferation and IL-2 production, both when expressed intracellularly and when added exogenously as conditioned medium, indicating a direct effect on lymphocyte activation. Critically, human myositis biopsy samples reveal significantly reduced Insl6 transcript levels compared to controls, with lower expression correlating with greater myofiber atrophy, thereby validating its clinical relevance. The invention therefore provides methods for treating autoimmune diseases by administering Insl6 protein, functional fragments (e.g., those retaining B- and A-domains), or nucleic acids encoding them. Treatment may involve recombinant Insl6-Fc fusion proteins administered intravenously or subcutaneously, or gene therapy using adeno-associated viral vectors under muscle-specific promoters. Autoimmune diseases targetable by this approach include not only myositis subtypes but also rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, multiple sclerosis, and transplant rejection. Diagnosis is enabled by measuring Insl6 expression in muscle biopsies via qRT-PCR or immunoassay, with levels below a defined reference threshold indicating disease susceptibility or active pathology. Pharmaceutical compositions are formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, stabilizers, and, if needed, co-administered immunosuppressants, ensuring optimal delivery and efficacy across diverse patient populations.

### DEFINITIONS

For clarity and consistency, the following terms are defined as used herein. “Insulin-like 6” or “Insl6” refers to the human or murine polypeptide encoded by the INSL6 gene, including its precursor and mature forms, and having the amino acid sequence set forth in SEQ ID NO:1 or a biologically active variant thereof. “Wild type” denotes the naturally occurring, non-mutated form of a protein or gene. A “soluble Insl6 polypeptide” is an Insl6 molecule lacking transmembrane or membrane-anchor domains, capable of existing in extracellular fluid. A “polynucleotide encoding Insl6” is a DNA or RNA sequence that directs the synthesis of Insl6 protein, including genomic DNA, cDNA, and synthetic constructs. “Human Fc” refers to the crystallizable fragment of human immunoglobulin G, preferably IgG1, as defined by SEQ ID NO:3. A “mutant” or “variant” is a polypeptide or polynucleotide differing from wild type by one or more substitutions, insertions, or deletions. “Muscle” includes skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle tissues. An “autoimmune disease” is a disorder arising from an inappropriate immune response against self-antigens, including myositis, lupus, and type 1 diabetes. “Th1-dominant autoimmune diseases” are those driven primarily by T-helper 1 cells and associated cytokines (e.g., IFN-γ), such as multiple sclerosis; others, like systemic lupus erythematosus, are not strictly Th1-dominant. “Immunosuppressive agents” include corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors, and biologics that dampen immune activity. “Inflammatory disorders” encompass conditions involving chronic or acute inflammation, whether autoimmune or not. “Cytokines” are signaling proteins mediating immune responses; “pro-inflammatory cytokines” (e.g., TNF-α, IL-6) promote inflammation, whereas “anti-inflammatory cytokines” (e.g., IL-10) suppress it. A “fragment of a peptide” is a contiguous subsequence retaining biological activity; a “functional fragment of Insl6” inhibits T-cell activation or promotes muscle repair. “Derivatives” and “analogs” include chemically modified or structurally altered forms with similar activity. “Conservative substitutions” replace an amino acid with another of similar physicochemical properties (e.g., leucine for isoleucine); “non-conservative” changes alter charge, polarity, or size. “Sequence identity” is calculated by aligning sequences and determining the percentage of identical residues over the aligned region, typically using BLAST or PILEUP algorithms. “Substantially homologous” sequences share at least 70% identity. “Treating” means reducing symptom severity or halting progression; a “therapeutically effective amount” is the dose producing such an effect without undue toxicity. “Administering” includes any route of delivery, especially parenteral (intravenous, subcutaneous). “Pharmaceutically acceptable” refers to carriers, excipients, or salts suitable for human use. These definitions ensure precise interpretation of the invention’s scope across all embodiments.

### Insulin-Like 6 (Insl6) Agents

Insl6 agents encompass the full-length Insl6 protein, its isoforms, variants, fragments, and derivatives. Insl6, also known as insulin-like peptide 6, is synthesized as a preprohormone comprising a signal peptide, B-domain, C-peptide, and A-domain. Post-translational processing removes the signal peptide and C-domain, yielding a mature, disulfide-bonded heterodimer of B- and A-chains analogous to insulin. Functional fragments include those containing the B- and A-domains, either linked or separate, provided they retain immunomodulatory activity. Nucleic acids encoding Insl6 include cDNA, genomic constructs, and modified RNA (modRNA) engineered for enhanced stability and translation. RNA activator (RNAa) molecules that upregulate endogenous INSL6 transcription are also included. Insl6 may be produced recombinantly in mammalian, bacterial, or insect cells, or by chemical synthesis. Derivatives may feature non-natural amino acids, D-amino acid residues (as in retro-inverso peptides), or post-translational modifications (e.g., glycosylation). Delivery systems include liposomes, nanoparticles, and viral vectors. Administration routes span intravenous, subcutaneous, intramuscular, and oral, with formulations tailored to maximize bioavailability and target engagement in muscle or lymphoid tissues.

### Insl6-Fc Fusion Proteins

Insl6-Fc fusion proteins consist of Insl6 or a functional fragment thereof covalently linked to an Fc domain of human IgG1 (SEQ ID NO:3), optionally via a flexible peptide linker. This fusion enhances serum half-life through neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn)-mediated recycling and facilitates purification via protein A/G affinity chromatography. Alternative fusion partners include albumin, polyethylene glycol (PEG), or other long-circulating proteins. PEGylation of Insl6 increases hydrodynamic size, reducing renal clearance and proteolytic degradation, though monodisperse PEG species are preferred to avoid heterogeneity. Targeting ligands—such as agrin, M-cadherin domains, or anti-MuSK antibodies—may be incorporated to direct Insl6 specifically to muscle cells. Conjugation methods employ chemical crosslinkers (e.g., SMCC) or genetic fusion. The resulting constructs maintain Insl6’s ability to suppress T-cell activation while achieving sustained systemic exposure, making them ideal for chronic autoimmune conditions.

### Methods to Treat Autoimmune Diseases and Immune-Related Diseases and Disorders

The invention provides methods for treating autoimmune and inflammatory diseases by administering Insl6 agents to reduce pathogenic immune cell infiltration and pro-inflammatory cytokine production. In myositis, this entails decreasing CD4+/CD8+ T cells and TNF-α/IFN-γ levels in muscle, thereby improving motor function. Treatment extends to sepsis, transplant rejection, and other immune dysregulation syndromes. Diagnosis involves quantifying Insl6 in muscle biopsies: low mRNA (via RT-PCR using primers for SEQ ID NO:2) or protein (via ELISA, Western blot, or immunohistochemistry) indicates myositis. Automated platforms (e.g., FACS, SELDI-TOF-MS) enable high-throughput analysis. Subjects with reduced Insl6 expression are identified as candidates for therapy, with prognosis informed by baseline levels.

### Administration of Pharmaceutical Compositions

Insl6 agents are administered in dosage regimens tailored to disease severity, typically ranging from 0.1 to 10 mg/kg body weight, given weekly or biweekly via subcutaneous or intravenous injection. Effective amounts are determined by monitoring clinical endpoints (e.g., muscle strength) and biomarker reduction. Local administration (e.g., intramuscular) may be used for focal disease. Dosing is adjusted based on patient response, circulating drug levels, and tolerability, adhering to good medical practice.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

Compositions contain Insl6 agent in sterile, isotonic, buffered solutions with stabilizers (e.g., sucrose), surfactants (e.g., polysorbate 80), and preservatives. Liposomal or multivesicular formulations enable controlled release. Solid forms (tablets, capsules) include diluents (lactose) and lubricants (magnesium stearate). Topical gels or transdermal patches are suitable for localized delivery. Combinations with corticosteroids (e.g., prednisone) or other immunomodulators are contemplated.

### Gene Therapy

Gene therapy employs viral vectors (AAV, lentivirus) or non-viral methods (lipofection, electroporation) to deliver INSL6-encoding nucleic acids under constitutive (CMV) or muscle-specific (MCK) promoters. This ensures sustained Insl6 expression in target tissues, offering a potential cure for chronic autoimmune myopathies.

### Kits

Kits include Insl6 agent (protein, nucleic acid, or vector), instructions for use, and optional ancillary materials (syringes, diluents). They facilitate standardized diagnosis (measuring Insl6 levels) and treatment (administering therapeutics) in clinical or research settings.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A chronic inflammatory myositis (CIM) model was established in C57BL/6 mice by immunizing with human skeletal myosin-binding C-protein emulsified in complete Freund’s adjuvant plus pertussis toxin. Insl6-overexpressing transgenic mice (MCK-Insl6-TG) exhibited significantly reduced muscle inflammation, lower CD4+/CD8+ T-cell infiltration, and improved Rotarod performance compared to wild-type controls. An Fc-Insl6 fusion protein was constructed by fusing human IgG1 Fc to murine Insl6 and delivered via hydrodynamic tail vein injection, resulting in serum levels up to 1,800 ng/ml and marked attenuation of CIM pathology. These findings were corroborated in Insl6-knockout mice, which displayed exacerbated disease, confirming Insl6’s protective role.
