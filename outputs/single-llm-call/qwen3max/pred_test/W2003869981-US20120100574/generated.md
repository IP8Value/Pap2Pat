## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of virology and antiviral drug discovery, specifically to systems and methods for producing replication-competent hepatitis C virus (HCV) particles in mammalian cells without requiring HCV RNA replication. The invention further provides novel platforms for assaying HCV entry into host cells, identifying cellular and viral factors essential for HCV infection, and screening test compounds for their ability to inhibit various stages of the HCV life cycle, including entry, assembly, and release.

## BACKGROUND

Hepatitis C virus (HCV) infects an estimated 2–3% of the global population, representing a major public health burden. Chronic HCV infection significantly increases the risk of developing severe liver complications, including cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. HCV is classified within the genus *Hepacivirus* of the *Flaviviridae* family and exhibits substantial genetic diversity, with at least six major genotypes identified. Subtypes 1a and 1b account for more than two-thirds of diagnosed infections worldwide and are notably less responsive to existing antiviral therapies compared to other genotypes. Despite decades of research, no effective vaccine against HCV has been approved, underscoring the urgent need for improved experimental models to study viral pathogenesis and facilitate therapeutic development.

Current in vitro models for HCV research face significant limitations. While subgenomic replicons derived from genotypes 1a and 1b have enabled studies of HCV RNA replication in highly permissive human hepatoma cell lines such as Huh-7.5, these systems lack structural genes and therefore cannot produce infectious virions. The advent of the JFH-1 strain (genotype 2a), which supports a full infectious cycle in cell culture (termed HCVcc), marked a major advance. However, this model is largely restricted to genotype 2a or chimeric constructs and remains problematic for the clinically dominant genotype 1 strains. Attempts to propagate genotype 1 isolates in vitro invariably lead to the accumulation of adaptive mutations that enhance RNA replication but impair particle assembly, infectivity, or in vivo fitness. Consequently, consensus-sequence HCV genomes known to be infectious in chimpanzees often fail to replicate or produce infectious particles in standard cell culture systems.

HCV is an enveloped, positive-sense single-stranded RNA virus. Its genome encodes a single polyprotein that is co- and post-translationally processed into ten mature proteins: three structural proteins—core, envelope glycoprotein 1 (E1), and envelope glycoprotein 2 (E2)—and seven non-structural proteins (NS2 through NS5B). Viral entry into hepatocytes involves a complex, multi-step process mediated by several host cell surface molecules, including scavenger receptor class B type I (SR-BI), CD81 tetraspanin, claudin-1, and occludin. Pseudotyped retroviral particles bearing HCV envelope glycoproteins (HCVpp) have been widely used to study entry mechanisms but do not recapitulate authentic virion structure or contain HCV genomic RNA.

The complete HCV life cycle occurs in the cytoplasm and is intimately linked to virus-induced rearrangements of intracellular membranes, particularly those derived from the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). These membrane alterations serve as platforms for both RNA replication and virion assembly. However, the precise mechanisms governing HCV particle morphogenesis, maturation, and egress remain incompletely understood, partly due to the lack of robust systems capable of producing high yields of authentic, replication-competent HCV particles—especially for genotype 1—without the confounding influence of adaptive mutations.

Given these challenges, there exists a critical need for alternative antiviral strategies and, equally important, for innovative experimental platforms that can faithfully reproduce the production of wild-type, infectious HCV particles. Such systems would enable more accurate studies of viral biology, host-pathogen interactions, and the evaluation of potential therapeutics targeting all stages of the HCV life cycle, from entry to release.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel mammalian cell-based system for the production of replication-competent hepatitis C virus (HCV) particles that does not rely on HCV RNA replication, thereby avoiding the emergence of cell culture-adaptive mutations. This system comprises a first plasmid encoding an HCV polynucleotide sequence under the control of a bacteriophage promoter, and a second plasmid encoding a cytoplasmic RNA amplification system based on reciprocal bacteriophage DNA-dependent RNA polymerases. When co-transfected into a specialized host cell line—specifically, a non-hepatic mammalian cell line stably harboring a flavivirus subgenomic replicon, such as baby hamster kidney (BHK) cells containing a West Nile virus (WNV) subgenomic replicon (designated BHK-WNV cells)—this system drives high-level cytoplasmic transcription of full-length or modified HCV genomic RNA. The resulting RNA serves as a template for translation of HCV structural and, optionally, non-structural proteins, leading to the assembly and release of infectious HCV particles.

The invention further provides isolated replication-competent HCV particles produced by this method, as well as isolated HCV structural proteins. Additionally, the invention encompasses a versatile system for assaying HCV particle entry into target cells. This assay system utilizes a first host cell line (e.g., BHK-WNV) transfected with a plasmid encoding an HCV genome that includes a reporter gene or a gene encoding a bacteriophage RNA polymerase (e.g., SP6 polymerase) in place of most non-structural genes. The resulting HCV reporter particles (HCVrp) are then used to infect a second host cell line (e.g., Huh-7.5 or HepG2-CD81). Entry is quantified by measuring the expression of a secondary reporter gene (e.g., firefly luciferase or EGFP) in the target cells, which is activated only upon delivery of the incoming viral RNA encoding the bacteriophage polymerase.

This platform enables the identification of cellular proteins necessary for HCV entry. In one embodiment, the second host cell line is transfected with small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting a candidate cellular protein prior to exposure to HCVrp; a reduction in reporter gene expression indicates the protein’s involvement in viral entry. Alternatively, the second host cell may be transduced with a recombinant lentivirus expressing a candidate cellular protein to assess whether its overexpression enhances entry. Similarly, the system allows identification of HCV glycoproteins essential for entry by pre-incubating HCV particles with antibodies specific to candidate glycoproteins (e.g., anti-E1, anti-E2, or anti-hypervariable region 1 antibodies) and measuring subsequent inhibition of reporter signal.

The invention also provides methods for identifying test compounds effective at blocking HCV entry or viral genome uncoating. In this approach, the second host cell line is incubated with a test compound prior to infection with HCV particles, and a decrease in reporter gene expression signifies inhibitory activity. Furthermore, the system facilitates the discovery of cellular factors involved in HCV particle release. A clone of the first host cell line (e.g., BHK-WNV) that fails to produce infectious HCV is isolated, and cDNA from this clone is subjected to subtractive hybridization against cDNA from the parental productive line to identify host cell-specific genes whose absence correlates with defective particle release.

Additionally, the invention includes methods for identifying test compounds that inhibit HCV assembly or maturation. Here, the first host cell line is incubated with a test compound during particle production; harvested particles are then used to infect the second host cell line, and reduced reporter signal indicates interference with assembly or release. Finally, the invention provides a method for studying HCV spreading in real time by incorporating a tetracysteine tag into a non-structural viral protein (e.g., NS5A) and tracking newly synthesized viral proteins in live infected cells using biarsenical dyes such as FIAsH or ReAsH.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS

As used herein, the singular forms “a,” “an,” and “the” include plural referents unless the context clearly dictates otherwise. The term “about” means plus or minus 10% of the indicated value. A “promoter” refers to a DNA sequence capable of directing the initiation of transcription of an operably linked coding sequence in a host cell. “Operably linked” describes a functional relationship between a nucleic acid regulatory sequence and a coding sequence such that the regulatory sequence directs appropriate expression of the coding sequence. A “reporter gene” is a gene whose product is readily detectable and quantifiable, such as firefly luciferase, Renilla luciferase, or enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP). A “transformed cell” or “transfected cell” is a cell into which exogenous DNA has been introduced, resulting in stable or transient expression of the encoded gene products. “Transformation” or “transfection” encompasses any method of introducing nucleic acids into cells, including chemical, physical, or viral means.

A “replicon” is an RNA molecule capable of autonomous replication in a host cell, typically containing viral non-structural genes required for RNA synthesis. A “sub-genomic flavivirus replicon” is a replicon derived from a flavivirus (e.g., WNV, dengue virus) that lacks structural genes but retains the non-structural genes and cis-acting elements necessary for RNA replication, often engineered to express a reporter gene. A “test compound” is any chemical, biological, or natural substance evaluated for its effect on HCV entry, assembly, release, or replication. “Subtractive hybridization” is a molecular technique used to identify differentially expressed genes between two populations of mRNA by selectively removing common sequences.

It is to be understood that the embodiments described herein are illustrative and not limiting. The invention encompasses all variations, modifications, and equivalents falling within the scope of the appended claims.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a mammalian cell transformed with a plasmid encoding an HCV polynucleotide under a bacteriophage promoter, wherein the cell also stably expresses a flavivirus subgenomic replicon. In a preferred embodiment, the host cell is a BHK-21 cell line stably harboring a WNV subgenomic replicon (BHK-WNV). The recombinant mammalian cell may further comprise additional plasmids encoding bacteriophage RNA polymerases (e.g., T7 and SP6) to enable cytoplasmic amplification of HCV RNA. The system produces isolated replication-competent HCV particles containing full-length or bicistronic HCV RNA genomes, which are infectious for human hepatoma cells and primary human liver tissue. Isolated HCV structural proteins (core, E1, E2) may also be obtained from culture supernatants or cell lysates.

The invention further provides a system for assaying HCV particle entry into a cell, comprising: (i) a first host cell line (e.g., BHK-WNV) transfected with a plasmid encoding an HCV genome that includes a gene for a bacteriophage RNA polymerase (e.g., SP6pol) in place of HCV non-structural genes; (ii) a second host cell line (e.g., Huh-7.5) susceptible to HCV entry; and (iii) a reporter plasmid in the second host cell containing a reporter gene (e.g., firefly luciferase) under the control of tandem bacteriophage promoters (e.g., T7 and SP6). Upon entry of HCV particles into the second host cell, the delivered SP6pol RNA is translated, leading to amplification of the reporter signal. Actinomycin D may be added to block host RNA polymerase II-dependent transcription, reducing background noise. Reporter genes such as firefly luciferase or EGFP provide quantitative or visual readouts of entry efficiency.

This system enables identification of cellular proteins necessary for HCV entry. For example, siRNA directed against candidate receptors (e.g., SR-BI, CD81, ASGP-R subunit 1, ASGP-R subunit 2, or claudin-1) is transfected into the second host cell prior to infection; a significant decrease in reporter signal confirms the protein’s role in entry. Conversely, overexpression of a candidate protein via plasmid transfection or lentiviral transduction in a normally non-permissive cell (e.g., HepG2) can confer susceptibility to HCV entry, as measured by increased reporter activity.

The invention also allows identification of HCV glycoproteins essential for entry by pre-incubating HCV particles with neutralizing antibodies (e.g., anti-E2, anti-HVR1) and measuring inhibition of reporter expression. Similarly, test compounds that block entry or uncoating are identified by pre-treating the second host cell with the compound and assessing reduced reporter signal.

To identify cellular factors involved in HCV release, a non-productive clone of the first host cell line is isolated. cDNA from this clone is compared to that of the parental line via two-round subtractive hybridization to pinpoint host genes whose loss impairs particle production. This approach has identified candidates such as Rab1, SERF2, and TUBA4.

Methods for studying HCV spreading employ HCV genomes encoding a tetracysteine tag in NS5A. Infected live cells are stained with membrane-permeable biarsenical dyes (e.g., FIAsH), enabling real-time visualization of viral protein synthesis and dissemination. The system supports the production of authentic wild-type HCV (e.g., H77 strain, genotype 1a) without adaptive mutations, which can infect human liver slices ex vivo, demonstrating physiological relevance. The platform is thus uniquely suited for dissecting all phases of the HCV life cycle and accelerating antiviral discovery.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and Methods

T7 DNA-dependent RNA polymerase (DdRP) protein was produced by cloning the T7pol gene into an expression vector under an inducible promoter and purifying the recombinant protein from bacterial lysates. Plasmid constructs were engineered to enable cytoplasmic RNA amplification and HCV expression. The pCR-T7p/SP6pol plasmid contains the SP6 RNA polymerase gene downstream of a T7 promoter and an EMCV IRES, while pSL-SP6p/T7pol encodes T7 RNA polymerase under an SP6 promoter and EMCV IRES. HCV bicistronic constructs, such as p684-SG(L+I)-HDV, encode HCV 5′-UTR through NS2 fused to a subgenomic replicon, whereas p90-T7p/H77FL-HDV encodes the full-length consensus H77 genome. Reporter constructs like pCMV(−)T7p/HCV-SP6pol-HDV replace HCV non-structural genes with SP6pol, and pT7-SP6p2/EGFPLuc contains a dual-promoter-driven EGFP-luciferase fusion. Additional plasmids include pHCVp7 (encoding core-E1-E2-p7) and pIRES1hyg (selection marker).

Antibodies used included monoclonal anti-E1 (A4), anti-E2 (ALP98, AP33), anti-core (C7-50), anti-CD81 (JS-81), anti-SR-BI, anti-ASGPR-1 (8D7), anti-claudin-1, and polyclonal anti-NS5A (raised against a 48-aa H77 peptide). Rabbit anti-HVR1 antibody was used for neutralization assays. Cell lines included BHK-21 and derivative BHK-WNV1 (stably expressing WNV subgenomic replicon with Renilla luciferase), HuH-7.5 and derivative replicon-containing lines, HEK-293, and HepG2. HepG2-CD81 cells were generated by lentiviral transduction with hCD81 cDNA using ViraPower Lentiviral Expression Systems.

HCV particles were produced by co-transfecting BHK-WNV1 cells with HCV-encoding plasmids and the p2B polymerase system. Supernatants were harvested at 72 hours, clarified, and ultracentrifuged. Particles were analyzed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM) after negative staining or immuno-gold labeling. Glycosylation status was assessed by Endo H treatment followed by Western blot. Inhibitors like deoxyribojirimycin and brefeldin A were used to probe glycan processing and secretory pathway dependence.

RNA analysis employed RT-qPCR with primers specific for HCV 5′-UTR or WNV replicon. Total RNA was extracted from cells or pelleted supernatants using Trizol LS. Infectivity was measured by TCID50 assays in Huh-7.5 cells, with NS5A-positive foci quantified by immunofluorescence. Indirect immunofluorescence used primary antibodies against NS5A or core, followed by Alexa Fluor-conjugated secondaries. Bromo-uridine (BrU) incorporation assays involved actinomycin D pre-treatment, BrUTP transfection, and anti-BrU staining to visualize active RNA replication sites.

Live-cell imaging utilized HCVbp-4-cys or HCVwt-4-cys particles encoding a tetracysteine tag in NS5A. Infected cells were stained with FIAsH or ReAsH dye and imaged by confocal microscopy. Entry assays used the HCVrp reporter system: target cells were co-transfected with p2B and pT7-SP6p2/EGFPLuc, infected with HCVrp, and luciferase activity measured. WNV SG-replicon RNA association with HCV particles was tested by RT-PCR of gradient fractions. siRNA knockdown in HuH-7.5 cells targeted candidate receptors, and effects on HCVrp infectivity were quantified. HepG2 cells were rendered permissive by lentiviral hCD81 transduction, confirmed by flow cytometry.

Antiviral drug treatments (interferon-α/β + ribavirin) were applied to BHK-WNV1 cells to assess impact on WNV replicon activity (Renilla luciferase) versus HCV particle production. Subtractive hybridization compared mRNA from productive BHK-WNV1 cells and non-productive clones using a modified two-round protocol to enrich for differentially expressed genes. Rab1 knockdown by siRNA in BHK-WNV1 cells was used to validate its role in HCV production. The amplified reporter system was validated by RT-qPCR for WNV 5′ UTR in pelleted supernatants and Enduren-based RNL activity assays in target cells.

### Example 2

HCV-like particles (HCV-LPs) were produced in BHK-21 cells expressing HCV structural genes under a CMV promoter. Co-expression of flavivirus subgenomic replicons (WNV or dengue serotype 2) dramatically enhanced particle release compared to parental BHK-21 cells, as measured by core protein in supernatants. However, when WNV or dengue subgenomic replicon RNAs were tested for packaging into these particles by RT-PCR and infectivity assays in Huh-7.5 cells, no trans-encapsidation was detected, confirming that the released particles contained only the input HCV-based RNA and not the flavivirus replicon RNA.

### Example 3

BHK cells expressing HCV structural proteins were treated with glycosylation inhibitors deoxyribojirimycin (inhibits glucosidase I/II) or brefeldin A (blocks ER-to-Golgi transport). Western blot analysis showed that inhibitor treatment prevented the appearance of fully cleaved, mature E1 and E2 glycoproteins, indicating that N-linked glycosylation and proper trafficking through the secretory pathway are essential for correct processing and secretion of HCV structural proteins.

### Example 4

BHK-WNV1 cells were co-transfected with p684-SG(L+I)-HDV (encoding HCVbp) and the p2B system. TEM revealed large vesicles near dilated ER and mitochondria, filled with 50–60 nm enveloped particles, absent in control-transfected cells. Sucrose gradient analysis showed HCV RNA and structural proteins co-migrating at densities of 1.05–1.20 g/cm³. HCVbp infected Huh-7.5 cells, inducing NS5A expression and BrU incorporation into nascent RNA, confirming active replication. HCVbp-4-cys particles enabled live-cell tracking of infection via FIAsH staining. Infectivity peaked at 1.08–1.13 g/cm³ and was blocked by anti-CD81 antibodies, demonstrating CD81-dependent entry. TCID50 reached 2.5×10⁵ units/ml, confirming production of infectious HCV.

### Example 5

BHK-WNV1 cells transfected with p90-T7p/H77FL-HDV (encoding wild-type H77 genome) produced HCVwt particles. Negative-stain EM of gradient fractions (1.08–1.13 g/cm³) showed 50–60 nm spherical particles, recognized by anti-HCV antibodies from cured patients in immuno-EM. HCVwt infected human liver slices ex vivo, with specific staining by patient sera and monoclonal antibodies in 2–5 lobules by day 8. HCVwt-4-cys infection was visualized in live slices using biarsenical dyes, showing periportal and mediolobular clusters of positive cells. These results confirm that BHK-WNV1 cells produce authentic, infectious wild-type HCV capable of infecting physiologically relevant human tissue.

### Example 6

HepG2 cells, naturally CD81-negative and non-permissive to HCV, were transduced with lentivirus-hCD81 to express human CD81 on their surface. Incubation with HCVwt resulted in detectable HCV RNA accumulation, which increased over time and was inhibited by interferon/ribavirin, confirming productive infection dependent on CD81 expression.

### Example 7

Comparative analysis of HCV particle release in BHK-21 versus BHK-WNV1 cells demonstrated that the WNV subgenomic replicon induces cellular changes—potentially involving membrane remodeling, lipid metabolism, or suppression of innate immunity—that create a permissive environment for HCV assembly and egress, enhancing particle yield by over 100-fold.

### Example 8

BHK-WNV1 cells treated with interferon-α/β and ribavirin showed >90% reduction in WNV replicon activity (Renilla luciferase) but unchanged HCV particle production, confirming that HCV assembly in this system is independent of flavivirus RNA replication. TEM of treated cells revealed altered membrane structures, suggesting that antivirals affect intracellular trafficking pathways. Despite these changes, infectious HCV particles were still formed, highlighting the robustness of the production system.

### Example 9

Naïve BHK-21 cells were transfected with the WNV SG-replicon and selected to generate stable clones. Clone BHK-WNV2.13 was isolated and subjected to differential mRNA screening via subtractive hybridization, yielding cDNA clones representing genes upregulated or uniquely expressed in the WNV-replicon-bearing cells, which may contribute to HCV permissiveness.

### Example 10

BHK-WNV1 cells were treated with siRNAs targeting candidate factors identified in Example 9. Knockdown of SERF2 and TUBA4 significantly reduced HCV particle production, suggesting roles for these proteins in viral assembly, maturation, or egress, potentially through interactions with the cytoskeleton or ER membrane dynamics.

### Example 11

Analysis of HCV envelope proteins from BHK-WNV1-produced particles showed sensitivity to Endo H digestion, indicating the presence of high-mannose glycans typical of ER-resident proteins. This suggests that HCV envelope glycoproteins in these particles may not undergo full Golgi-mediated maturation, which could influence particle density, infectivity, and interaction with lipoproteins—a feature consistent with the low buoyant density observed and the enhancement of infectivity by exogenous VLDL/LDL.
