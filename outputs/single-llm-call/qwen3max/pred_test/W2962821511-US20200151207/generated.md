# DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to the field of natural language processing, specifically to systems and methods for detecting and tracking topics in temporally ordered collections of text documents. In an era of exponentially increasing textual data—ranging from scientific publications and news articles to social media posts and corporate communications—there is a critical need for automated tools that can identify thematic structures, monitor their evolution, and characterize how specific terms or concepts rise, fall, or transform over time. Traditional approaches to topic modeling have largely treated document collections as static entities, ignoring the temporal dimension that often holds the key to understanding societal, technological, or scientific trends. The invention described herein addresses this gap by introducing a novel neural dynamic topic model that integrates the representational power of undirected stochastic graphical models with the temporal reasoning capabilities of recurrent neural networks. This integration enables the system to not only detect coherent topics at individual time points but also to model how these topics emerge, evolve, merge, or fade across a sequence of time-stamped document collections. The resulting framework provides a robust, scalable, and interpretable solution for applications ranging from scientific trend analysis and market intelligence to public health monitoring and policy evaluation.

## BACKGROUND

Topic detection systems are computational frameworks designed to uncover latent thematic structures within large corpora of text. These systems operate under the assumption that documents are generated as mixtures of underlying topics, each of which is characterized by a probability distribution over words. Early efforts in this domain, such as Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), established a probabilistic foundation for unsupervised topic discovery by modeling each document as a mixture of topics drawn from a Dirichlet prior. While effective for static corpora, such models fail to account for the temporal dynamics inherent in real-world document streams, where topics do not remain fixed but instead evolve in response to external events, technological advancements, or shifts in discourse. Subsequent attempts to incorporate time into topic models, such as the Dynamic Topic Model (DTM), introduced temporal dependencies by chaining topic distributions across time slices. However, these approaches suffer from several limitations: they assume a fixed number of global topics that persist across all time steps, making it difficult to capture the emergence of entirely new themes; they rely on complex variational inference procedures that are computationally intensive and sensitive to initialization; and they do not explicitly model the usage patterns of individual keywords over time, which is crucial for understanding how specific concepts gain or lose prominence within a topic.

## SUMMARY AND DESCRIPTION

The scope of the present invention encompasses a method and system for topic discovery and tracking in time-ordered text document collections using a two-layered Recurrent Neural Network–Replicated Softmax (RNN-RSM) model. This invention is motivated by the need to move beyond static topic representations and to develop a framework that can dynamically adapt to the changing nature of discourse over time. The core innovation lies in the introduction of explicit latent topical dependencies that link the hidden topic representations at each time step to the entire history of prior topic states through a deterministic recurrent neural network. This architecture enables the system to perform three interrelated tasks: Topic Structure Detection (TSD), which identifies the main thematic clusters in a document collection at a given time; Topic Evolution Detection (TED), which tracks how these topics change in composition, popularity, or novelty over successive time intervals; and Temporal Topic Characterization (TTC), which analyzes the usage trends of specific keywords within each topic across time. The method begins by sorting a text document collection according to timestamps, thereby establishing a chronological sequence. For each time step, a bag-of-words vector is generated from the corresponding document subset. This vector is then processed through the RNN-RSM model to calculate a hidden topic vector that encodes the latent thematic content of the documents at that time. The hidden topic vector is derived by conditioning the stochastic hidden units of an RSM layer on the deterministic hidden state of an RNN layer, which accumulates information from all previous time steps. The resulting topic vectors are used to derive topic trends, including measures of topic popularity, drift, and keyword span, which together provide a comprehensive temporal overview of the evolving discourse landscape. The system outputs both the discovered topics—represented as ranked lists of salient terms—and their associated temporal trends, enabling users to visualize and interpret long-term thematic shifts.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The topic discovery system of the present invention comprises a repository or database configured to store a time-stamped text document collection (TDC), where each document is associated with a timestamp (TS) indicating its temporal position within the sequence. The system includes a processing unit or processor operatively connected to a memory that stores the two-layered RNN-RSM model. A predefined dictionary, containing a fixed set of K words or phrases extracted from the entire TDC, serves as the vocabulary for representing documents. The text document collection is first sorted by timestamp to form a chronologically ordered sequence. A text document generation unit processes each time-slice of the collection to produce a bag-of-words vector v, where each element corresponds to the frequency or binary occurrence of a dictionary term in the documents of that time slice. The dictionary selection process ensures that only terms meeting certain frequency or relevance criteria are retained, thereby optimizing the signal-to-noise ratio in topic representation. The hidden topic vector h is then calculated by applying the RNN-RSM model to the bag-of-words vector v. This vector h is a binary stochastic vector of size F, representing the activation pattern of F latent topics at the current time step. Concurrently, the RNN hidden state vector u, a deterministic continuous vector, is updated based on the previous RNN state and the current topic representation, thereby encoding the temporal context. Topic trends are derived by analyzing sequences of h and u vectors across time, including metrics such as cosine similarity between topic vectors at adjacent time steps, keyword appearance/disappearance patterns, and average span of term persistence. The system outputs the discovered topics—each defined by its top-ranking terms—and their associated temporal trends, which may be visualized as timelines or heatmaps.

The text document collection may be provided as a continuous data stream, in which case the system processes incoming documents in real-time, updating the RNN state and topic vectors incrementally. The hidden topic vector h is calculated using the trained RNN-RSM model, which consists of two interconnected layers: an RSM layer and an RNN hidden layer. The RSM layer is an undirected stochastic graphical model that defines a joint probability distribution over visible word units and hidden topic units via an energy function. The RNN hidden layer is a deterministic recurrent network that maintains a summary of past topic states and modulates the biases of the RSM layer at each time step. The joint probability distribution of the entire model is factorized such that the conditional distribution of visible units given hidden units follows a softmax function, while the conditional distribution of hidden units given visibles follows a logistic function. The biases b of the RSM layer are time-dependent and are computed as a function of the RNN hidden state u(t−1) from the previous time step. The RNN hidden state u(t) is updated using standard recurrent equations involving weight matrices W_uu, W_uv, and W_uh, along with a bias term b_u. The energy of the state in the RSM is defined as a bilinear form involving the interaction weights W_vh and the scaled biases, where the scaling accounts for document length to ensure stability across variable-sized inputs. The probability of a state is inversely related to its energy via the Boltzmann distribution.

The RNN-RSM model is trained using a combination of Contrastive Divergence (CD) and Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT). The cost function is defined as the negative log-likelihood of the observed document collections. During training, the deterministic hidden units u(t) are propagated forward in time, while the RSM parameters—including the weight matrix W_vh and the time-varying biases b_v(t) and b_h(t)—are updated using k-step Gibbs sampling to generate negative samples. The gradient of the cost with respect to the RSM parameters is approximated using CD, and this gradient is then back-propagated through the RNN connections to update the recurrent weights and biases. The reconstruction of visibles is used to assess model fidelity, and the gradient estimation is refined iteratively until convergence. The average span of selected keywords is calculated as the length of the longest contiguous sequence of time steps in which a keyword appears in the top terms of any discovered topic. This metric serves as a quantitative measure of a model’s ability to capture persistent or trending concepts. Use cases of the system include detecting emerging research themes in scientific literature, tracking public sentiment in news media, and monitoring technical terminology in engineering documentation. The system can trigger control or monitoring routines when a topic exceeds a predefined threshold of popularity or drift, or initiate processes such as predictive maintenance in industrial settings based on detected shifts in operational reports. Discovered topics may be further evaluated for coherence, relevance, or actionability using downstream analytical modules.
