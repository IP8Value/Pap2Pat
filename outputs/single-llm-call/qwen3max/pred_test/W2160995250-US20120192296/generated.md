# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ectodermal dysplasias, a group of inherited disorders characterized by developmental abnormalities of tissues derived from the ectoderm, including hair, teeth, sweat glands, and other skin appendages. More specifically, the invention pertains to the development and use of agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies for the treatment of X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED), a severe form of ectodermal dysplasia caused by mutations in the EDA gene. The invention provides novel monoclonal antibodies that bind to and activate the EDAR receptor, thereby mimicking the biological activity of the natural ligand ectodysplasin-A1 (EDA1). These antibodies are capable of rescuing the developmental defects associated with XLHED in animal models and hold significant therapeutic potential for human patients suffering from this debilitating condition.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ectodermal dysplasias encompass a heterogeneous group of genetic disorders affecting the development of ectodermally derived structures such as hair, teeth, nails, and sweat glands. Among these, X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED) represents one of the most severe forms, characterized by sparse hair, missing or malformed teeth, and an inability to sweat due to absent or dysfunctional sweat glands. XLHED is caused by mutations in the EDA gene, which encodes ectodysplasin, a member of the tumor necrosis factor (TNF) ligand family. The EDA gene produces two major protein isoforms through alternative splicing: EDA1 and EDA2. EDA1 specifically binds to the EDAR receptor, while EDA2 interacts with the related receptor XEDAR. Mutations disrupting EDA1 function lead to XLHED, as the EDAR signaling pathway is essential for the proper development of ectodermal appendages.

Studies in Tabby mice, which carry a loss-of-function mutation in the Eda gene, have provided valuable insights into the pathophysiology of XLHED and potential therapeutic approaches. These mice exhibit phenotypic features closely resembling those seen in human XLHED patients, including absence of tail hairs, defective tooth development, and lack of sweat glands. Early attempts to treat XLHED focused on recombinant EDA1 protein therapy, as described in U.S. Patent Application Publication No. 2005/152,872. Additional patents, including U.S. Pat. Nos. 6,355,782 and 7,115,555, disclosed DNA and amino acid sequences of EDA proteins and their therapeutic applications. U.S. Patent Application Publication No. 2003/0023991 further detailed methods for treating ectodermal dysplasias using recombinant EDA1.

The CH11 monoclonal antibody, which exhibits agonist activity toward the Fas receptor, demonstrated that monoclonal antibodies could potentially serve as receptor agonists. Similarly, the APO-1 monoclonal antibody showed agonist properties for Fas signaling. However, commercial polyclonal anti-EDAR antibodies have proven problematic due to batch-to-batch variability and inconsistent biological activity. Moreover, EDA1-dependent biological assays are technically challenging and often yield variable results. EDAR-deficient mice, such as the OVE1B strain, have been developed to study EDAR function and provide a valuable platform for generating specific antibodies against EDAR.

Despite these advances, there remains a critical need for effective treatments for XLHED that can address the full spectrum of developmental defects, particularly those affecting tooth formation and glandular structures. The present invention aims to fulfill this need by providing novel agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies that demonstrate potent biological activity in both in vitro and in vivo models of XLHED. The primary objectives of this invention are to provide isolated agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies, methods for their production, pharmaceutical compositions containing these antibodies, and their use in treating XLHED and related conditions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for producing agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies that effectively activate the EDAR receptor and rescue developmental defects associated with XLHED. The method begins with the production of an EDAR antigen or fragment thereof, preferably comprising the extracellular domain of human or mouse EDAR fused to an Fc region to enhance stability and immunogenicity. This antigen is then used to immunize EDAR-deficient mice, specifically the OVE1B strain, which lacks endogenous EDAR expression and therefore can generate high-affinity antibodies against conserved epitopes of the EDAR receptor.

Following immunization, serum samples are collected and tested for the presence of anti-EDAR antibodies using standard immunoassays such as ELISA. Spleen or lymph node cells from immunized mice demonstrating robust anti-EDAR antibody responses are then fused with myeloma cells to produce hybridomas. These hybridomas are screened for the secretion of anti-EDAR antibodies, and positive clones are identified through binding assays. Crucially, the hybridomas are further evaluated for agonist activity using both in vitro and in vivo assays. The in vitro assay employs EDAR-Fas reporter cells that undergo apoptosis upon EDAR activation, while the in vivo assay involves administration of hybridoma supernatants to newborn Tabby mice to assess rescue of tail hair formation.

Hybridoma lines demonstrating both binding specificity and agonist activity are selected for further development. These lines are cloned and sub-cloned through limiting dilution to ensure monoclonality and stability. The resulting hybridomas are then used to produce large quantities of agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies, which are purified using standard techniques such as protein A or G affinity chromatography. The invention encompasses the isolated agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies produced by this method, as well as pharmaceutical compositions containing these antibodies and their use in treating XLHED and related conditions characterized by EDAR signaling deficiencies.

The method for producing agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies involves several key steps. Step a) entails the production of an EDAR antigen, preferably as an Fc fusion protein comprising the extracellular domain of human or mouse EDAR. Step b) involves immunizing EDAR-deficient mice, specifically the OVE1B strain, with this antigen to generate an immune response. Steps c) through h) include harvesting immune cells, generating hybridomas, screening for antibody production, identifying agonist activity, selecting appropriate hybridoma lines, cloning and sub-cloning these lines, and purifying the resulting monoclonal antibodies.

Preferred EDAR antigens include human EDAR-Fc and mouse EDAR-Fc fusion proteins comprising amino acid residues 29-183 of the respective EDAR extracellular domains. An alternative approach to steps b), c), and d) involves using transgenic animals expressing human EDAR or employing phage display libraries to generate human antibodies directly. Binding assays to detect anti-EDAR antibodies include direct or capture ELISA formats, where plates are coated with EDAR-Fc and detected using secondary antibodies against mouse immunoglobulins.

Biological in vitro assays measure apoptosis induction in EDAR-Fas-expressing Jurkat cells, where EDAR activation triggers the Fas-mediated apoptotic pathway. Biological in vivo assays assess tail hair formation in newborn Tabby mice following intraperitoneal injection of test antibodies. Purification of antibodies is typically accomplished using protein A or G affinity chromatography, followed by additional purification steps if necessary.

Methods for preparing immunogens involve mixing EDAR-Fc with adjuvants such as STIMUNE to enhance immunogenicity. Immunization protocols typically involve subcutaneous injection at the base of the tail, followed by booster injections at specified intervals. The preferred embodiment for obtaining agonist anti-EDAR antibodies involves producing human and mouse EDAR-Fc fusion proteins, immunizing OVE1B mice, detecting anti-EDAR antibodies in serum by ELISA, generating hybridomas, and identifying clones producing agonist antibodies through both in vitro and in vivo assays.

Unanticipated results observed during development include the finding that divalent monomeric antibodies are sufficient for agonist activity, contrary to expectations based on other TNF receptor family members. This observation suggests that EDAR may exist in pre-assembled complexes that can be activated by bivalent engagement. ## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

An EDAR-Fc expression vector was developed by cloning the extracellular domain of human or mouse EDAR (amino acid residues 29-183) into a mammalian expression vector containing the Fc region of human IgG1. This construct was transfected into HEK 293 cells, which were then selected to generate stable cell lines expressing the EDAR-Fc fusion protein. The recombinant protein was purified from culture supernatants using protein A affinity chromatography, yielding highly pure EDAR-Fc preparations suitable for immunization.

Female OVE1B EDAR-deficient mice were immunized subcutaneously at the base of the tail with 150 μg of purified EDAR-Fc mixed with STIMUNE adjuvant. Booster immunizations were administered between days 10 and 14, followed by a final boost with antigen in PBS alone on day 40. Serum samples collected on day 30 were tested by ELISA for anti-EDAR antibody titers, and mice demonstrating strong responses were selected for hybridoma generation.

Three days after the final boost, lymph node cells were harvested and fused with SP2/0 myeloma cells using standard polyethylene glycol-mediated fusion techniques. The resulting hybridomas were cultured in complete RPMI 1640 medium over a feeder layer of mouse macrophages and selected in HAT medium. Supernatants from 96-well plates were screened by ELISA for antibody secretion against EDAR-Fc, and positive clones were identified.

Positive hybridoma clones were sub-cloned twice by limiting dilution to ensure monoclonality and then gradually adapted to serum-free Opti-MEM medium without macrophage support or HAT supplementation. Monoclonal antibodies were purified from conditioned supernatants using protein G-Sepharose affinity chromatography, yielding highly pure IgG preparations.

Fab fragments were generated from purified monoclonal antibodies using immobilized ficin digestion followed by protein A chromatography to remove Fc fragments and undigested IgG. The resulting Fab fragments were further purified by size exclusion chromatography. Binding and dissociation constants were determined using surface plasmon resonance with human EDAR-Fc captured on anti-human Fc chips.

EDAR-Fas-expressing Jurkat cells were generated by retroviral transduction of Jurkat-JOM2 cells with pMSCVpuro-hEDAR-Fas or pMSCVpuro-mEDAR-Fas constructs. 293T cells were co-transfected with these plasmids along with pHIT60 and VSV-G packaging plasmids to produce replication-incompetent retroviruses. After 24 hours of incubation, virus-containing supernatants were collected and used to infect Jurkat cells, which were then selected with puromycin. Clones were tested for sensitivity to Fc-EDA1-induced apoptosis to confirm functional EDAR-Fas expression.

Agonist activity was assessed by measuring apoptosis induction in EDAR-Fas-expressing Jurkat cells following antibody treatment. In vivo activity was evaluated by injecting hybridoma supernatants into newborn Tabby pups within 24 hours of birth. Pups were labeled by footpad puncture with tattoo ink for identification. Tail hair formation was examined and photographed at 20-22 days post-injection, with hair presence scored on a standardized scale. Sweat gland development was assessed using the starch-iodine assay to detect functional eccrine glands.

### Example 2

Monoclonal antibodies designated mAbEDAR1 through mAbEDAR9 were tested for their ability to bind both human and mouse EDAR expressed on the surface of transfected 293T cells. Flow cytometry analysis demonstrated that all nine antibodies recognized both human and mouse EDAR with comparable intensity, indicating cross-reactivity across species. Specificity testing against unrelated receptors confirmed that the antibodies did not bind to control proteins such as TRAILR3 or BCMA, establishing their specificity for EDAR.

### Example 3

Epitope mapping was performed using a series of EDAR-Fc truncation mutants containing different combinations of cysteine-rich domains (CRDs). ELISA plates were coated with goat anti-human Fc antibodies to capture various EDAR-Fc constructs from cell supernatants. Antibody binding was then assessed using the panel of anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies. Results indicated that some antibodies recognized CRD1 alone, others required both CRD1 and CRD2, while one antibody bound only to constructs containing all three CRDs. None of the antibodies bound to constructs lacking CRD1, suggesting that this domain is essential for proper folding or contains critical epitope components.

### Example 4

Competition binding assays were conducted to evaluate whether the anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies competed with APO200, a known EDAR-binding agent, for receptor binding. ELISA plates were coated with EDAR-Fc and pre-incubated with varying concentrations of APO200 before adding the anti-EDAR antibodies. Results demonstrated that none of the monoclonal antibodies showed significant competition with APO200, indicating that they recognize distinct, non-overlapping epitopes on the EDAR receptor.

### Example 5

Commercial polyclonal anti-EDAR antibodies were tested for agonist activity using both the in vitro EDAR-Fas reporter assay and the in vivo Tabby mouse model. Despite demonstrating binding to EDAR in ELISA and flow cytometry, these polyclonal preparations failed to induce apoptosis in EDAR-Fas-expressing cells or rescue tail hair formation in newborn Tabby mice, highlighting the unique agonist properties of the monoclonal antibodies described herein.

### Example 6

The half-life of anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies 1 and 3 was determined by administering the antibodies to adult Tabby mice and collecting serum samples at various time points post-injection. Antibody concentrations were measured using ELISA with EDAR-Fc-coated plates. Pharmacokinetic analysis revealed half-lives of 10.5 and 11 days, respectively, indicating favorable persistence in circulation for sustained therapeutic effect.

### Example 7

Therapeutic dose-response relationships were established by administering graded doses of anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies to newborn Tabby mice within 24 hours of birth. Hair density on the tail was scored at 3-4 weeks post-injection using a standardized scoring system. EC50 values for tail hair reversion ranged from 0.1 to 0.7 mg/kg for most antibodies, with one antibody showing reduced potency (EC50 ~3 mg/kg). Sweat gland development was similarly assessed using the starch-iodine assay, yielding comparable EC50 values.

### Example 8

Pregnant Tabby mice were treated intravenously with anti-EDAR monoclonal antibody 3 at embryonic days 13 and 20 (E13/E20) or E9/E17. Offspring were analyzed at 6 months of age for correction of the Tabby phenotype. Macroscopic examination revealed the presence of tail hairs, hair behind the ears, and normalized eye appearance. Histological studies confirmed the development of functional sweat glands, mucus-secreting tracheal glands, and other ectodermal structures. Skin abnormalities characteristic of XLHED were completely corrected in treated animals.

### Example 9

Dentition analysis of Tabby mice treated in utero with anti-EDAR monoclonal antibody 3 revealed that first molars appeared similar to those of wild-type mice, with proper cusp formation and enamel deposition. To assess effects on hair growth in normal animals, anti-EDAR monoclonal antibody was injected into newborn wild-type mice. Treated mice exhibited a ruffled and greasy fur appearance, with thinning of zig-zag and awl hairs and increased hair pigmentation. Growth curves remained normal, and the modified hair phenotype gradually disappeared over time, indicating transient effects in animals with intact EDAR signaling.
