## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunoprophylaxis and immunotherapy against Gram-negative bacterial pathogens, specifically *Klebsiella pneumoniae* (KP) and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (PA). More particularly, the invention provides novel multivalent glycoconjugate vaccines comprising covalently linked O-polysaccharide (OPS) antigens from prevalent serotypes of *Klebsiella pneumoniae* and flagellin proteins from *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. The invention further encompasses methods for producing these conjugates, pharmaceutical compositions containing them, and their use in eliciting protective immune responses against nosocomial infections caused by these multidrug-resistant pathogens.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

*Klebsiella pneumoniae* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* are two Gram-negative bacterial pathogens of critical concern due to their increasing prevalence in healthcare-associated infections and their alarming rates of antimicrobial resistance, including resistance to last-resort antibiotics such as carbapenems and colistin. These organisms are responsible for a wide spectrum of severe infections, including pneumonia, bloodstream infections, urinary tract infections, surgical site infections, and wound infections, particularly in immunocompromised or critically ill patients. Despite the urgent clinical need, there are currently no licensed vaccines available for either pathogen in humans.

*Klebsiella pneumoniae* expresses two major surface polysaccharides: the capsular polysaccharide (K antigen) and the O-polysaccharide (OPS), which is part of the lipopolysaccharide (LPS) in the outer membrane. While over 80 distinct K serotypes have been identified, presenting a formidable barrier to capsule-based vaccine development, only nine OPS structures are known, with four—O1, O2, O3, and O5—accounting for more than 80% of clinical isolates worldwide. Importantly, antibodies targeting OPS have been shown to be protective in preclinical models through opsonophagocytic killing, even in the presence of a capsule, as the OPS remains accessible on the bacterial surface.

*Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, a ubiquitous opportunistic pathogen, utilizes a single polar flagellum for motility, which is composed primarily of a polymer of the flagellin protein (Fla). Only two major flagellin serotypes exist: FlaA (including subtypes A1 and A2) and FlaB. Flagella are not only essential for motility but also serve as adhesion factors and potent activators of innate immunity via Toll-like receptor 5 (TLR5). Antibodies against flagellin can inhibit bacterial motility, block adhesion, and confer protection in animal models of infection. However, immunity is largely serotype-specific, necessitating inclusion of both FlaA and FlaB for broad coverage.

Traditional polysaccharide vaccines are T-cell-independent antigens that elicit poor immune responses, lack immunological memory, and are ineffective in infants and young children. This limitation is overcome by glycoconjugate technology, wherein the polysaccharide is covalently linked to a carrier protein, converting the response to T-cell-dependent and thereby enhancing immunogenicity, inducing class switching, affinity maturation, and long-lasting memory. Historically, carrier proteins such as tetanus toxoid or diphtheria toxoid have been used, but pre-existing immunity to these carriers can suppress the anti-polysaccharide response. The use of a pathogen-relevant, heterologous carrier protein offers a strategic advantage by simultaneously providing protective immunity against multiple targets.

Previous attempts at developing vaccines for KP and PA have faced significant challenges. Capsule-based KP vaccines require an impractically large number of serotypes for broad coverage. Whole-cell killed or purified LPS vaccines are limited by the endotoxic activity of lipid A. Similarly, while PA flagellin vaccines have shown promise in clinical trials, a monovalent approach is insufficient due to serotype specificity. The co-administration of separate multivalent conjugate vaccines for each pathogen is logistically complex and may lead to immune interference. Thus, there exists a critical unmet need for a simplified, broadly protective, and safe vaccine that addresses both pathogens simultaneously.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides, in non-limiting example embodiments, a single, multivalent conjugate vaccine for the prevention and treatment of infections caused by *Klebsiella pneumoniae* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. In one embodiment, the invention is a conjugate vaccine comprising at least one *Klebsiella pneumoniae* O-polysaccharide (OPS) antigen covalently linked to at least one *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin protein. In a specific embodiment, the vaccine is a quadrivalent formulation comprising four individual conjugates: *Klebsiella pneumoniae* O1 OPS linked to *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* FlaB, O2 OPS linked to FlaA, O3 OPS linked to FlaB, and O5 OPS linked to FlaA. In another embodiment, the invention is a single quadrivalent conjugate molecule incorporating all six antigens.

The invention further provides a method for preparing this conjugate vaccine, which involves chemically conjugating the reducing end of a purified *Klebsiella pneumoniae* OPS to lysine residues on a recombinant *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin protein using a bifunctional linker chemistry, such as thioether-aminooxy coupling. In another aspect, the method for preparing the vaccine employs recombinant expression vectors to produce the flagellin protein in a heterologous host, such as *Escherichia coli*, and engineered *Klebsiella pneumoniae* strains for the scalable production of OPS.

The invention also encompasses a method for passive immunization, comprising administering to a subject a composition comprising purified immunoglobulins or polyclonal sera raised against the conjugate vaccine, which can elicit a rapid, protective immune response. A method for eliciting an active immune response is provided, which involves administering to a subject an immunologically effective amount of the conjugate vaccine composition, alone or in a prime/boost regimen.

Furthermore, the invention includes a method for constructing the conjugate vaccine by genetically engineering attenuated *Klebsiella pneumoniae* strains that are guanine auxotrophs and acapsular, facilitating the safe and efficient production of OPS. The method for producing the conjugate vaccine may also utilize bacterial expression systems, such as *Salmonella enterica* or *E. coli*, to express and secrete high yields of recombinant flagellin protein for subsequent purification and conjugation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Conjugate

The present invention is directed to a conjugate for use in a vaccine against *Klebsiella pneumoniae* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* bacterial infections. This conjugate comprises a *Klebsiella pneumoniae* surface polysaccharide antigen covalently linked to a *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin protein. The *Klebsiella pneumoniae* surface polysaccharide antigen is specifically the O-polysaccharide (OPS), which is the repeating oligosaccharide unit of the lipopolysaccharide (LPS) that is attached to the core oligosaccharide. The OPS is derived from clinically relevant *Klebsiella pneumoniae* serovars, preferably serovars O1, O2, O3, and O5, which together represent the vast majority of pathogenic isolates globally. The *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin is the primary structural protein of the bacterial flagellum, and the invention specifically utilizes the two major serotypes, type A (FlaA) and type B (FlaB). The conjugate composition is designed such that the OPS is linked, typically at its reducing end, to the flagellin protein, which serves a dual role as both a carrier protein to enhance the immunogenicity of the OPS and as a protective antigen in its own right. The ratio of surface polysaccharide to flagellin in the conjugate is optimized for immunogenicity, typically ranging from 0.5:1 to 2:1 (w/w). The conjugation is achieved through chemical crosslinking, creating a stable covalent bond between the two moieties. Examples of such conjugates include, but are not limited to, a conjugate of *K. pneumoniae* O1 OPS with *P. aeruginosa* recombinant FlaA (rFlaA) or rFlaB. The invention also contemplates the use of fragments or derivatives of the *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin protein that retain the key T-cell epitopes necessary for carrier function and the conformational B-cell epitopes required for protective antibody induction, where a derivative is defined as an amino acid sequence that is at least 80% identical to the native flagellin sequence.

### Compositions

The invention provides vaccine compositions comprising the aforementioned conjugates. These compositions may contain a single conjugate or, more preferably, a multivalent mixture of conjugates to provide broad coverage against multiple serotypes of both pathogens. In a preferred embodiment, the composition is a multivalent conjugate vaccine that links *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellins (FlaA and/or FlaB) to *Klebsiella pneumoniae* OPS antigens from serovars O1, O2, O3, and O5. To further enhance the immune response, the composition may also include an amount of unconjugated flagellin protein, which can act as an additional source of T-cell help and as a built-in adjuvant due to its TLR5-stimulating properties. The unconjugated flagellin is selected to match the serotypes present in the conjugate (i.e., FlaA and FlaB).

The vaccine composition is formulated as a pharmaceutical composition by admixing the conjugate(s) with a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, which may be a liquid, semi-solid, or solid vehicle suitable for the intended route of administration. To improve efficacy, the composition may further comprise an adjuvant. Suitable adjuvants include aluminum salts (e.g., alum), oil-in-water emulsions (e.g., MF59), saponins (e.g., QS-21), and toll-like receptor agonists. Notably, the flagellin protein itself can serve as an adjuvant due to its intrinsic ability to activate TLR5. The composition can be formulated for various routes of administration, including nasal, parenteral (e.g., intramuscular, subcutaneous), oral, transdermal, mucosal, and pulmonary delivery. For parenteral administration, the composition is typically prepared as a sterile, isotonic aqueous solution or suspension. For pulmonary delivery, it may be formulated as a nebulizer solution, a metered-dose inhaler formulation, or a dry powder for a powder inhaler. The composition may be supplied as a liquid dosage form, a freeze-dried product for reconstitution, a liposome-encapsulated solution, or a gel. The formulation may also contain standard excipients such as pH adjusters (e.g., phosphate buffers), emulsifiers, buffering agents, preservatives (e.g., thimerosal, phenol), and thickening agents (e.g., carboxymethylcellulose) to create a stable, viscous composition suitable for injection. In a commercial embodiment, the invention provides a kit comprising the vaccine composition in a container, along with instructions for its administration, and optionally, a delivery device (e.g., a syringe, an inhaler) and storage instructions.

### Methods of Treatment

The invention provides a method for inducing a protective immune response in a subject against *Klebsiella pneumoniae* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*. This method comprises administering to the subject a composition comprising a conjugate of a *Klebsiella pneumoniae* surface polysaccharide antigen (specifically OPS from serovars O1-O5) covalently linked to a *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin protein (FlaA and/or FlaB). The administration may involve a single conjugate or, more effectively, multiple conjugates representing the different OPS and flagellin serotypes. The subject is a mammal, preferably a human, and the patient population includes individuals at high risk for nosocomial infections, such as those undergoing surgery, intensive care unit patients, or the immunocompromised. Administration can occur prophylactically prior to a known risk event (e.g., surgery), therapeutically during an active infection, or as a post-exposure prophylaxis.

The induced immune response is characterized by the production of high-titer, opsonic IgG antibodies against the OPS antigens, which mediate bacterial clearance via phagocytosis, and functional antibodies against the flagellin proteins, which inhibit bacterial motility and adhesion. An immunologically-effective amount is administered, which is a dose sufficient to elicit this protective response without causing undue toxicity. Dosage ranges for humans are expected to be from 1 to 50 micrograms of polysaccharide per serotype per dose. The composition may be administered as a single dose or in a sequential multi-dose regimen, such as a prime/boost strategy. A heterologous prime/boost strategy is also contemplated, for example, involving an initial oral prime with a live attenuated vector (e.g., *Salmonella*) expressing the antigens, followed by a parenteral boost with the purified conjugate vaccine.

### Methods of Making the Conjugate

The conjugate of the present invention is produced by methods adapted from established glycoconjugate vaccine technology. An embodiment of the production method involves the covalent binding of a purified *Klebsiella pneumoniae* surface polysaccharide antigen (OPS) to a purified *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin protein. The OPS is isolated from engineered *K. pneumoniae* strains, often by mild acid hydrolysis of LPS to cleave the ketosidic linkage between the OPS and the core oligosaccharide, yielding a free reducing end. The flagellin protein is produced recombinantly in a heterologous Gram-negative bacterium (GNB) such as *E. coli*, using an expression vector under the control of an inducible promoter (e.g., trc promoter induced by IPTG). The recombinant flagellin is then purified from the soluble fraction of the bacterial lysate using affinity chromatography (e.g., Ni-NTA for a His-tagged protein), followed by ion-exchange and tangential flow filtration steps.

Conjugation is achieved by first introducing a reactive functional group onto each component. For instance, the reducing end of the OPS can be modified with an aminooxy-thiol linker, while lysine residues on the flagellin can be derivatized with a maleimide-containing crosslinker (e.g., GMBS). The two activated components are then mixed under controlled conditions (e.g., 4°C, pH 6.8, for 24-48 hours) to allow the formation of a stable thioether bond between them. Alternative conjugation chemistries include the use of 1-cyano-4-dimethylaminopyridinium tetrafluoroborate (CDAP) to activate hydroxyl groups on the polysaccharide for direct coupling to protein amines, or the use of a bifunctional linker such as adipic acid dihydrazide (ADH). The final conjugate product is purified from unreacted starting materials and reagents by size-exclusion chromatography and/or ion-exchange chromatography to ensure homogeneity and remove potential contaminants like endotoxin.

### Genetically Engineered Strains

The efficient and safe production of the vaccine components relies on the use of genetically engineered microbial strains. For *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, the invention provides modified strains that are attenuated for use as reagent strains in OPS production. These strains contain deletions in the *guaBA* locus, which encodes enzymes for guanosine biosynthesis, rendering the bacteria guanine auxotrophs that cannot replicate in vivo, thus ensuring safety. Additionally, they contain deletions in the *wza-wzc* locus, which is essential for capsule export, resulting in an acapsular phenotype that simplifies OPS purification and increases its yield. Specific examples include a *K. pneumoniae* O1:K2 strain with deletions in both *guaA* and *guaB* genes and the entire *cps* (capsule) gene cluster.

For the production of *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin, the invention provides modified Gram-negative bacterial strains, such as *Salmonella enterica* or *E. coli*, that have been engineered for high-level, secreted expression of the heterologous flagellin. These strains may contain attenuating mutations, such as in the *guaBA* locus or in the *clpP/clpX* protease genes, to enhance safety and protein stability. The strains are transformed with an expression plasmid containing a codon-optimized *P. aeruginosa fliC* gene (encoding FlaA or FlaB) fused to a strong promoter and, optionally, a secretion signal sequence to promote hyper-secretion of the flagellin into the culture supernatant, greatly simplifying downstream purification. A prototype attenuated *S. Enteritidis* reagent strain may contain inactivating mutations in its native *fliC* gene to prevent expression of its own flagellin, combined with a plasmid for heterologous expression of *P. aeruginosa* FlaA or FlaB, along with *guaBA* and *clpPX* locus deletions for attenuation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The present invention is exemplified by the construction and evaluation of conjugate vaccines comprising *Klebsiella pneumoniae* surface polysaccharides and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* flagellin proteins. A key step was the creation of specialized reagent strains to enable the safe, scalable, and economical purification of the required antigens. An attenuated *Salmonella enterica* serovar strain was developed as a platform to purify heterologous flagellins, and robust upstream and downstream bioprocesses were established. These processes were successfully scaled up to a 20-liter fermentation volume for two different *Salmonella* serovars, demonstrating their industrial feasibility. A fully chemically defined medium was used for growth, and flagellin was captured directly from the fermentation supernatants onto cation exchange membranes, followed by anion exchange purification and tangential flow filtration, yielding highly pure protein with low endotoxin.

Similarly, for OPS production, core oligosaccharide-plus-O-polysaccharide (COPS) was extracted from engineered *Klebsiella* strains using organic extraction steps and purified by ion-exchange chromatography. Methods were then developed to conjugate the purified OPS with the recombinant flagellin. In a model system, *Salmonella* COPS was conjugated directly to the epsilon-amino groups of lysine residues on *Salmonella* FliC, with the linkage occurring at random hydroxyl groups along the polysaccharide chain, generating "sun-type" conjugates where the polysaccharide radiates from the protein core. Unconjugated components and reagents were removed by a combination of size-exclusion and ion-exchange chromatography.

The immunogenicity and efficacy of these model conjugates were validated in mice. Immunization with *S. Enteritidis* COPS conjugated to its homologous FliC elicited high titers of both LPS- and FliC-specific serum IgG antibodies. Dose-ranging studies with 10 μg and 2.5 μg of conjugate demonstrated robust seroconversion and conferred significant protection against a fatal intraperitoneal challenge with a virulent *S. Enteritidis* strain. Importantly, the conjugation process was found to abolish the TLR5-stimulating activity of the flagellin, which is desirable to prevent excessive inflammatory responses upon vaccination.

To translate this platform to the target pathogens, recombinant reagent strains were engineered to produce large amounts of *Klebsiella pneumoniae* OPS and *P. aeruginosa* flagellin safely. A *K. pneumoniae* B5055 (O1:K2) strain was genetically modified by deleting the *guaBA* genes using lambda red recombination, creating a guanine auxotroph, and by deleting the capsule (*cps*) genes spanning from *wza* to *wzc*, creating an acapsular mutant. This double mutant, designated CVD 3001, was confirmed to be attenuated, non-encapsulated by India Ink staining, and a guanine auxotroph. Parallel efforts created a recombinant *S. Enteritidis* strain that expresses either Type A or Type B *P. aeruginosa* flagellin by cloning the respective *flaA* or *flaB* genes into an expression vector (pSEC10) and transforming it into an attenuated *Salmonella* background (CVD 1947). Secretion of the heterologous flagellins was confirmed by SDS-PAGE.

Using these reagents, a monovalent conjugate vaccine, KP-O1 OPS:PA-FlaA, was constructed. This approach was motivated by the high clinical burden of KP and PA and the limitations of current, non-existent vaccine strategies. Flagellins were chosen as carrier proteins due to their proven efficacy and dual functionality. The monovalent conjugate was used to immunize mice, and the resulting antisera were shown to contain high levels of anti-LPS and anti-Fla antibodies that were functional in opsonophagocytic assays. Protection studies in mouse models of invasive KP infection and PA burn wound infection demonstrated the therapeutic potential of this strategy.

This work was expanded to generate a total of 16 different candidate conjugates covering the four key KP OPS types and both PA flagellin types. These candidates were screened for their ability to elicit functional antibodies, and the most promising monovalent conjugates were selected to formulate a quadrivalent vaccine. This quadrivalent formulation was rigorously assessed for its ability to protect against both invasive systemic infections and localized wound infections in mice. Its therapeutic utility was further evaluated in passive transfer studies, where immune sera from vaccinated rabbits were administered to naïve mice, which were then challenged with lethal doses of KP or PA. The results confirmed that the antibodies induced by the quadrivalent conjugate were both opsonic for KP and motility-inhibiting for PA, providing significant protection in multiple disease models.

To support clinical translation, a porcine model of KP and PA wound infection was established. The goal was to determine the effective dose (ED50) for protection in a large animal model that more closely recapitulates human skin physiology and immune responses. Animals were immunized with the quadrivalent conjugate, and their serum antibody titers and functional activity were measured. They were then subjected to a standardized wound infection challenge. The statistical power of this model was calculated to ensure a clear demonstration of vaccine efficacy, and the expected results are predicted to confirm the protective capacity of the vaccine in a clinically relevant setting. In conclusion, this body of work demonstrates a novel, broadly protective, and manufacturable conjugate vaccine platform that addresses a critical unmet medical need posed by multidrug-resistant *Klebsiella pneumoniae* and *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*.
