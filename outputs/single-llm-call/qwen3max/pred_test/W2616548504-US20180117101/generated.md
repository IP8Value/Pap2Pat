## BACKGROUND

The present invention relates to a genetically modified probiotic organism engineered to express phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL), an enzyme capable of metabolizing phenylalanine (phe) into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia. This engineered probiotic is designed for oral administration as a therapeutic intervention for phenylketonuria (PKU), a rare autosomal recessive metabolic disorder caused by mutations in the gene encoding phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH). In individuals with PKU, the absence or dysfunction of PAH leads to toxic accumulation of phenylalanine in the bloodstream, resulting in severe neurological impairments if left untreated. The invention leverages the natural resilience and biocompatibility of probiotic bacteria—specifically strains from the genera *Lactobacillus* and *Lactococcus*—to deliver functional PAL enzyme directly to the gastrointestinal tract, where dietary phenylalanine is absorbed. By expressing PAL within a live microbial vector that survives gastric transit and colonizes the gut transiently, the invention provides a novel, non-invasive, and potentially cost-effective approach to reducing systemic phenylalanine levels without requiring strict dietary restriction or invasive medical procedures.

## Advantages Over Conventional Technologies

Phenylketonuria (PKU) affects approximately 1 in 15,000 individuals worldwide and remains a significant clinical challenge due to the limitations of existing therapeutic modalities. The current standard of care involves lifelong adherence to a severely restricted low-phenylalanine diet, supplemented with synthetic medical foods devoid of natural protein. While effective in preventing acute neurological damage, this regimen is burdensome, expensive, and often poorly tolerated, leading to non-compliance, particularly among adolescents and adults. Liver transplantation offers a potential cure by restoring endogenous PAH activity; however, it is associated with high morbidity, mortality, organ scarcity, and the necessity for lifelong immunosuppression, rendering it impractical for widespread use. Gene therapy approaches, though promising in preclinical models, face substantial hurdles including vector immunogenicity, insertional mutagenesis risks, inconsistent transduction efficiency, and regulatory barriers that have delayed clinical translation. Cofactor therapy using tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) benefits only a small subset of patients with residual PAH activity and is ineffective in classic PKU. Polyethylene glycol-conjugated phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PEG-PAL), while approved in some jurisdictions, requires frequent subcutaneous injections, induces neutralizing antibodies that diminish efficacy over time, and carries risks of hypersensitivity reactions. Yeast-derived PAL formulations suffer from poor stability in the gastrointestinal environment and rapid proteolytic degradation. Oral administration of naked PAL enzyme has been explored but necessitates co-administration of antacid buffers to protect the enzyme from gastric acid and remains vulnerable to intestinal proteases, significantly limiting bioavailability and therapeutic durability. In contrast, the genetically modified probiotic of the present invention circumvents these drawbacks by encapsulating PAL within a living, acid-resistant bacterial chassis that naturally thrives in the gut, thereby enabling sustained, localized enzymatic activity without systemic exposure or immune activation.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a genetically modified probiotic organism comprising a nucleic acid construct encoding a functional phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL) enzyme, operably linked to regulatory elements enabling expression in a probiotic host. In preferred embodiments, the probiotic is selected from *Lactobacillus reuteri*, *Lactobacillus acidophilus*, or *Lactococcus lactis*, and is engineered to express Anabaena variabilis PAL (AvPAL) optimized for codon usage in the host. The construct includes a strong constitutive promoter, a ribosome binding site with an optimized spacer sequence, and a transcriptional terminator to ensure stable mRNA and high-level protein production. Optionally, a secretion tag may be fused to AvPAL to direct extracellular localization. The probiotic is formulated as a freeze-dried powder for oral delivery, demonstrating survival through gastric acid, metabolic activity in the small intestine, and significant reduction of plasma phenylalanine in a murine PKU model without eliciting a systemic anti-AvPAL antibody response. This platform represents a safe, orally administered, enzyme-replacement therapy that addresses the root cause of phenylalanine accumulation at the site of nutrient absorption.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The invention centers on a genetically modified probiotic (GMP) designed to deliver functional phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL) enzyme to the gastrointestinal tract for the treatment of phenylketonuria (PKU). The GMP is engineered to express PAL—preferably the Anabaena variabilis PAL (AvPAL)—within a probiotic bacterial host that is safe for human consumption, resistant to gastric acid, and metabolically active in the small intestine. The probiotic strain is selected based on its well-documented safety profile, ability to transiently colonize the gut, and compatibility with genetic manipulation. *Lactobacillus* and *Lactococcus* species are particularly suitable due to their long history of use in fermented foods and probiotic supplements, with *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C serving as an exemplary host. The GMP is produced in vitro under controlled anaerobic conditions using deMan, Rogosa, and Sharpe (MRS) broth, followed by transformation via electroporation with a plasmid encoding the optimized AvPAL gene. Codon optimization of the AvPAL sequence is performed to match the preferred codon usage of the host bacterium, enhancing translational efficiency and protein yield. The gene construct includes a constitutive promoter derived from the erythromycin resistance B (*ermB*) gene to drive continuous expression, a ribosome binding site (RBS) with a spacer sequence of optimal length (typically 5–9 nucleotides) to facilitate ribosomal initiation, and a transcriptional terminator from the *ermGT* gene to prevent read-through and stabilize mRNA. A secretion signal peptide, such as the Usp45 leader sequence, may be added N-terminal to AvPAL to promote extracellular enzyme release, although intracellular retention is also functional and may reduce proteolytic degradation. The DNA construct is assembled using standard molecular cloning techniques: PCR amplification of gene fragments, restriction digestion (e.g., with NcoI and SmaI), ligation into a shuttle vector (e.g., pSLERGT), and transformation into *E. coli* for plasmid propagation before electroporation into the probiotic host. High-retention plasmids with origins of replication functional in both *E. coli* and lactobacilli, along with selectable markers such as erythromycin resistance, ensure stable maintenance of the construct. In vitro validation confirms PAL activity via spectrophotometric detection of trans-cinnamic acid production from phenylalanine. The final product is purified by centrifugation, washed, and freeze-dried into a stable powder, eliminating culture medium components that could interfere with dosing or stability. Dosage is calculated based on colony-forming units (CFU) per gram of powder, with typical doses ranging from 10⁶ to 10⁹ CFU per administration. Animal studies in the PAHenu2 PKU mouse model demonstrate significant plasma phenylalanine reduction after oral administration mixed into powdered chow, with no detectable anti-AvPAL IgG, indicating immune ignorance. The freeze-dried formulation ensures room-temperature stability during shipping and storage, enhances patient compliance due to ease of oral ingestion, and offers a cost-effective alternative to injectable or dietary therapies.

### EXAMPLE 1

In a specific embodiment, *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C, a rodent-adapted strain with proven gut colonization capacity and safety in murine models, was selected as the probiotic chassis. This strain lacks native plasmids, minimizing genetic interference. The AvPAL gene from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413 was chosen for its high catalytic efficiency, thermal stability, and ability to fold autonomously without chaperones. The native AvPAL coding sequence was fully codon-optimized for *L. reuteri* using bioinformatic algorithms to replace rare codons with host-preferred synonyms. An RBS derived from the *ermB* gene, with a 7-nucleotide spacer (AGGAGGU), was inserted upstream of the start codon to maximize translation initiation. A transcriptional terminator from the *ermGT* resistance gene was appended downstream of the stop codon to ensure proper mRNA termination. The optimized gene, designated FuzErmAvPAL, was synthesized commercially and cloned into the shuttle vector pSLERGT, which contains an *E. coli* origin of replication (pUC ori), a lactobacillal replicon (pGT232), and an erythromycin resistance marker (*ermB*). The vector was first propagated in Top10 *E. coli* with ampicillin selection, then verified by restriction digest and sequencing. Electroporation of *L. reuteri* 100-23C was performed using cells harvested at mid-log phase (OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.9), washed in sucrose-magnesium buffer, and pulsed at 12.5 kV/cm. Transformants were selected on MRS agar with 5 μg/mL erythromycin under anaerobic conditions. The resulting strain, termed “pHENOMMenal,” was grown in MRS broth, harvested at OD₆₀₀ 0.6–0.8, and freeze-dried to yield a stable powder containing ≥10⁹ CFU/g, retaining >90% viability after six months at –20°C.

### EXAMPLE 2

Oral administration of the pHENOMMenal probiotic was achieved by mixing freeze-dried powder into powdered standard mouse chow at a concentration of 2.0 × 10⁷ CFU per gram of food for male mice and 1.0 × 10⁷ CFU/g for females. Fresh food was prepared daily to maintain probiotic viability. Plasma phenylalanine levels were monitored via fluorometric assay before treatment and at intervals during administration (days 3, 7, and 14). Blood was collected via minimally invasive cheek bleed into heparinized tubes, and plasma was stored at –20°C until analysis. Dosage control was maintained by measuring daily food intake and adjusting powder incorporation to ensure consistent CFU delivery per animal.

### EXAMPLE 3

Functional activity of AvPAL in pHENOMMenal lysates was confirmed by measuring trans-cinnamic acid production spectrophotometrically at 280 nm. Lysates from engineered *L. reuteri* incubated with 10 mM phenylalanine showed a time-dependent increase in absorbance, significantly exceeding background from control lysates (P < 0.001 at 30 minutes), demonstrating robust enzymatic conversion of phenylalanine to trans-cinnamic acid in vitro.

### EXAMPLE 4

*Lactobacillus acidophilus* strains were similarly engineered with the FuzErmAvPAL construct. Gene integration was verified by PCR and sequencing. Trans-cinnamic acid production was quantified using a standard curve generated from known concentrations (0–400 μM) in PBS. Experimental lysates incubated with phenylalanine exhibited dose-dependent increases in 280 nm absorbance, confirming functional AvPAL expression. Data from triplicate assays were analyzed using linear regression against the standard curve, revealing enzyme kinetics comparable to *L. reuteri*-derived constructs, thus validating cross-species applicability.
