# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and genetic screening, particularly for the detection of deletions in the DMD gene associated with Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) and Becker Muscular Dystrophy (BMD). More specifically, the invention provides methods, compositions, and kits for the rapid and reliable identification of hemi- or homozygous deletions of specific exons of the DMD gene—namely exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54—in biological samples, including dried blood spots. The invention is particularly directed toward enabling early diagnosis of DMD in newborns who are amenable to exon-skipping therapies targeting exons 45, 51, or 53, thereby facilitating timely therapeutic intervention. The disclosed methods employ multiplex polymerase-based nucleic acid amplification, such as quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR), using specifically designed primer pairs and oligonucleotide probes that allow for the simultaneous detection of multiple target exons in a single reaction. This approach supports integration into newborn screening programs and offers a cost-effective, high-throughput solution for identifying patients eligible for currently approved antisense oligonucleotide-based treatments.

## BACKGROUND

Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) is the most prevalent and severe form of inherited muscular dystrophy, affecting approximately 1 in 5,000 male births. It is characterized by progressive muscle degeneration and weakness, typically manifesting before the age of three, with loss of ambulation occurring by early adolescence and life-threatening cardiac or respiratory complications emerging in the second or third decade of life. DMD is caused by pathogenic variants in the DMD gene located on the X chromosome, which encodes the structural protein dystrophin. The absence of functional dystrophin disrupts the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex, leading to sarcolemmal instability and eventual myofiber necrosis. In contrast, Becker Muscular Dystrophy (BMD), a milder allelic variant, results from in-frame mutations that permit production of a partially functional, truncated dystrophin protein.

The majority of DMD-causing mutations are large deletions or duplications of one or more exons, with deletions accounting for approximately 60–70% of cases. These structural variants often disrupt the translational reading frame, resulting in premature termination codons and complete loss of dystrophin. Current therapeutic strategies aim to restore the reading frame and enable production of a BMD-like dystrophin isoform. Exon skipping, mediated by antisense oligonucleotides (AONs), represents a leading precision medicine approach. FDA-approved drugs such as eteplirsen (targeting exon 51) and golodirsen (targeting exon 53) induce skipping of specific exons to convert out-of-frame deletions into in-frame transcripts, thereby rescuing dystrophin expression. Additional AONs targeting exon 45 (e.g., casimersen) are under development or recently approved, expanding the treatable population.

Gene therapy strategies, including adeno-associated virus (AAV)-mediated delivery of microdystrophin transgenes, are also advancing through clinical trials. However, these approaches face limitations in neonates due to vector dilution during growth and the inability to re-administer AAV vectors because of immune responses. Consequently, early intervention with exon-skipping agents—ideally from birth—has emerged as a critical strategy to preserve muscle function prior to irreversible damage.

Existing newborn screening (NBS) methods for DMD rely primarily on measuring creatine kinase (CK) or its muscle-specific isoform (CKMM) in dried blood spots. While elevated CK levels indicate muscle damage, they are non-specific, influenced by gestational age, birth trauma, and other conditions, leading to false positives and false negatives. Moreover, CK-based screening does not identify the genetic defect or determine eligibility for mutation-specific therapies. Molecular confirmation via multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA) or sequencing is required post-screening, delaying diagnosis and treatment initiation. There remains a pressing need for a direct, DNA-based, high-throughput assay capable of identifying deletions amenable to exon-skipping therapies at the point of newborn screening, enabling immediate stratification of patients for targeted intervention.

## SUMMARY

The present invention addresses the unmet need for rapid, accurate, and scalable detection of DMD gene deletions that are amenable to exon-skipping therapies targeting exons 45, 51, or 53. The invention provides improved methods and tools for analyzing the DMD gene in biological samples, particularly dried blood spots collected during routine newborn screening. Central to the invention is the detection of hemi- or homozygous deletions of specific flanking exons—namely exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54—whose absence indicates a genomic context potentially responsive to exon-skipping treatment. By focusing on these exons, the method identifies approximately 19.7% of all DMD patients who could benefit from currently approved or emerging therapies, while also diagnosing an additional 16.8% of patients with deletions not currently treatable but who would still benefit from early supportive care.

The invention employs multiplex polymerase-based nucleic acid amplification, preferably real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR), to simultaneously analyze multiple target exons in a single reaction. This approach significantly increases throughput and reduces cost compared to sequential single-plex assays. The method is optimized for use with minimal DNA input from dried blood spots, aligning with standard NBS workflows. Amplification is achieved using specifically designed primer pairs that selectively amplify each target exon, with detection mediated by exon-specific oligonucleotide probes labeled with distinct fluorophores. The absence of amplification signal for a given exon, confirmed by a normalized fluorescence ratio (NFR) below a defined threshold (e.g., <0.2), indicates a deletion.

The invention further provides compositions comprising sets of amplification primer pairs and oligonucleotide probes, as well as kits of parts containing all necessary reagents for performing the assay. These kits include instructions for use, enabling implementation in public health laboratories without specialized expertise. The method delivers 100% sensitivity and specificity in validation studies, outperforming CK-based screening in accuracy. Importantly, the platform is modular and can be expanded to include additional exons as new exon-skipping therapies are developed, ensuring long-term clinical relevance. By enabling early, genotype-informed diagnosis, the invention facilitates timely initiation of disease-modifying treatments, physiotherapy, and corticosteroid regimens, thereby improving long-term outcomes for DMD patients.

The invention is grounded in the discovery that deletions of exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54 of the human DMD gene serve as reliable proxies for identifying patients eligible for exon-skipping therapies targeting exons 45, 51, or 53. The DMD gene (GenBank accession NG_012232.1) spans approximately 2.4 megabases and contains 79 exons. Pathogenic deletions disrupting the reading frame cause DMD, while in-frame deletions typically result in BMD. Exon-skipping therapies work by masking splice sites, causing the splicing machinery to skip a targeted exon and restore the reading frame. For example, skipping exon 51 can rescue deletions of exons 45–50, 48–50, 50, 52, or 52–63, among others.

Early diagnosis is critical, as muscle degeneration begins in infancy, and treatment initiated before symptom onset yields superior outcomes. The present method enables analysis of DNA extracted from dried blood spots—a standard NBS sample type—using a multiplex qPCR assay. The assay utilizes primer pairs and probes designed to avoid known polymorphic regions (per dbSNP and LOVD-DMD databases) to minimize false negatives. Each primer pair is optimized for similar melting temperatures (~60–62°C) and amplicon size (80–150 bp) to ensure balanced amplification in multiplex format. Self-dimers and cross-dimers are computationally minimized using established algorithms.

A “sample” as used herein includes any biological material containing genomic DNA, with preferred samples being whole blood, dried blood spots, buccal swabs, or saliva. Dried blood spots are particularly advantageous for NBS due to stability, ease of transport, and compatibility with existing infrastructure. Sample preparation involves punching a 3.1-mm disc from a DBS card and extracting DNA using silica-column or magnetic-bead-based methods, yielding sufficient template for qPCR without quantification.

The core method involves forming a reaction mixture containing genomic DNA, a thermostable DNA polymerase (e.g., Hot Start Taq), dNTPs, buffer, Mg²⁺, and a set of primer pairs and probes. The primer pairs consist of forward and reverse oligonucleotides, each 18–25 nucleotides in length, with sequences complementary to intronic or exonic regions flanking the target exon. Probes are dual-labeled oligonucleotides (e.g., FAM, Yakima Yellow, ROX, Atto 647N, Atto 700) with a 5′ reporter dye and 3′ quencher (e.g., BHQ), designed to hybridize within the amplified region. During amplification, probe cleavage by the 5′→3′ exonuclease activity of the polymerase generates a fluorescent signal proportional to amplicon accumulation.

Specific primer pairs include: for exon 44, SEQ ID NO: 11 (forward) and SEQ ID NO: 12 (reverse); for exon 46, SEQ ID NO: 13 and 14; for exon 50, SEQ ID NO: 15 and 16; for exon 52, SEQ ID NO: 17 and 18; for exon 54, SEQ ID NO: 19 and 20. Additional primer pairs for exons 7, 43, 45, 49, and 51 are also provided (SEQ ID NOs 46–55). Probes include: for exon 44, FAM-labeled probe (SEQ ID NO: 21); for exon 46, Yakima Yellow probe (SEQ ID NO: 22); for exon 50, ROX probe (SEQ ID NO: 23); for exon 52, Atto 647N probe (SEQ ID NO: 24); for exon 54, Atto 700 probe (SEQ ID NO: 25).

The reaction is run on a real-time thermocycler (e.g., CFX96) with cycling conditions: 95°C for 5 min (initial denaturation), followed by 40 cycles of 95°C for 30 s and 61°C for 75 s (annealing/extension). Endpoint fluorescence is measured and normalized to the median of control samples in the same run to calculate NFR. An NFR <0.2 indicates deletion. Internal controls (e.g., RPP30) may be co-amplified to confirm DNA integrity.

The invention also encompasses kits comprising lyophilized or liquid reagents, including primer/probe mixes, master mix, and instructions. Methods for diagnosing DMD and selecting patients for exon-skipping therapy are provided, as well as methods for treating DMD using the identified genotype to guide therapy selection. The assay may be combined with second-tier MLPA or RNA analysis for definitive genotyping. The platform is extensible to additional exons as new therapies emerge, ensuring broad applicability in the evolving DMD treatment landscape.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—DMD Genotyping Assay

A DMD genotyping assay was developed to detect deletions of exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54 in dried blood spots. Samples were collected from 120 individuals classified into three groups: a “deleted group” of 51 male DMD patients (34 with deletions overlapping target exons, 17 with non-target deletions), a “carrier group” of 50 female carriers (32 with target-exon deletions), and a “control group” of 19 healthy individuals. DNA was extracted from 3.1-mm DBS punches using a commercial kit. qPCR was performed in 25 µL reactions containing 1 µL DNA, 5× Takyon Master Mix, and primer/probe sets at concentrations listed in Table 3. Reactions were run on a CFX96 system with 40 cycles of amplification. Data were analyzed by calculating the Normalized Fluorescence Ratio (NFR) for each exon per sample. All 34 patients with target-exon deletions showed NFR <0.2 for the deleted exon(s), with clear amplification for non-deleted exons. All 17 patients with non-target deletions and all 69 carrier/control individuals showed robust amplification (NFR >0.5) for all five exons. The assay achieved 100% sensitivity and 100% specificity. Deletion patterns included isolated exon 44, 46, 50, or 52 deletions, as well as multi-exon deletions (e.g., 46–54, 50–54). The assay reliably distinguished deleted from non-deleted samples, demonstrating feasibility for newborn screening.

### Example 2—DMD Genotyping Assay

A second validation study was conducted under ethical approval (2019/278 and CEH 84/19) with informed consent. Subjects included 51 DMD males, 50 female carriers, and 19 controls, genotyped by MLPA. qPCR was performed as in Example 1, with technical replicates. Interpretation used NFR thresholds: NFR ≥0.5 = present, NFR ≤0.2 = deleted, 0.2 < NFR <0.5 = indeterminate (requiring repeat). All samples were correctly classified: deleted exons showed no amplification curves or flat lines, while non-deleted exons showed sigmoidal curves. Endpoint fluorescence scatter plots clearly separated deleted from non-deleted groups. No false positives or false negatives were observed. The assay detected 10 distinct deletion patterns, including complex rearrangements. Performance metrics confirmed 100% concordance with MLPA. The method proved robust across operators and runs, with inter-assay CV <5% for NFR. These results support deployment in high-throughput NBS programs, with potential to identify ~1 in 15,000 male newborns eligible for exon-skipping therapy.
