# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of computational modeling of disease progression using observational databases, and more specifically to systems, methods, and computer program products for generating probabilistic disease progression models from real-world longitudinal data derived from heterogeneous observational sources such as disease registries and electronic health records. The invention particularly addresses the technical challenges inherent in modeling the natural history of chronic diseases—especially those that are rare, multifactorial, or lack established biomarkers—by leveraging advanced statistical frameworks, including continuous-time hidden Markov models, to infer latent disease states and their temporal dynamics. The invention further pertains to the integration, preprocessing, and feature engineering of high-dimensional clinical data to enable robust, data-driven staging of disease trajectories that span both premanifest and manifest phases, thereby facilitating improved clinical decision-making, patient stratification, and therapeutic development.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel computational framework for constructing disease progression models from observational data, addressing a critical gap in the ability to systematically characterize the natural history of chronic diseases, particularly those lacking well-established staging systems or biomarkers. Traditional approaches to tracking disease progression are often limited by reliance on single-domain assessments, subjective diagnostic criteria, or incomplete longitudinal coverage, which collectively impede accurate modeling of complex, multifaceted disease trajectories. The invention overcomes these limitations by providing a unified, data-driven methodology that integrates multimodal clinical observations across motor, functional, and cognitive domains to infer a sequence of latent disease states that reflect the underlying progression process. Central to this approach is the application of a Continuous-Time Hidden Markov Model (CTHMM), which accommodates irregularly sampled, sparse, and heterogeneous observational data while enforcing clinically plausible progression constraints, such as unidirectional or limited-order state transitions. The system employs a two-step process: first, it determines the optimal number of disease states through a data-driven model selection procedure based on out-of-sample log-likelihood; second, it constructs a final disease progression model that captures the probabilistic transitions between states and the associated distributions of clinical features. The resulting model enables not only population-level insights into expected disease durations and progression patterns but also individual-level staging and trajectory prediction. A computer program product embodying the invention includes executable instructions stored on a non-transitory computer-readable medium that, when executed by a processor, cause the system to receive observational data, perform feature extraction and filtering, generate a disease progression model via CTHMM optimization, identify discriminative clinical features, and output a refined staging system with quantified state durations and transition probabilities. This invention thus provides a scalable, generalizable platform for transforming real-world observational data into actionable clinical knowledge for chronic disease management.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The embodiments described herein are provided for illustrative purposes and are not intended to limit the scope of the invention, which is defined solely by the appended claims. The detailed description serves to enable any person skilled in the art to make and use the invention by disclosing the principles, structures, and operational sequences of the disclosed system without undue experimentation. At the core of the invention is a disease progression model that represents the natural course of a chronic condition as a sequence of discrete, yet temporally continuous, latent states. This model is motivated by the clinical need to track disease severity over time, especially in conditions like Huntington’s Disease (HD), where symptoms evolve slowly across multiple domains long before formal diagnosis. However, tracking disease progression using real-world observational data presents significant difficulties: observations are sparse, irregularly timed, and often cover only fragments of an individual’s full trajectory; moreover, data from different patients may not align temporally, and the absence of universal biomarkers complicates objective staging. To address these challenges, the invention introduces system 100, a specialized computing architecture designed to ingest, process, and model longitudinal observational data. System 100 comprises several key components interconnected via a system bus 106, including a processor 102, memory 104, a receiving component 108, a model generation component 110, an identification component 112, a ranking component 114, and a disease progression model 116. The processor 102, which may be a central processing unit (CPU), graphics processing unit (GPU), or other integrated circuit, executes instructions stored in memory 104—a combination of volatile (e.g., RAM) and nonvolatile (e.g., SSD, hard disk) storage—to orchestrate the system’s functionality. The receiving component 108 is configured to accept observational data from diverse sources, such as disease registries and electronic health records, which may include structured clinical assessments, imaging results, and genetic information. The model generation component 110 implements the CTHMM framework to infer latent disease states and their transition dynamics, while the identification component 112 isolates discriminative clinical features that best distinguish between states. The ranking component 114 evaluates and orders these features by their predictive power, and the disease progression model 116 encapsulates the final learned representation of disease evolution. Current state-of-the-art methods are limited by their focus on single endpoints or post-diagnosis stages, whereas the subject innovation provides a holistic, pre-to-post-diagnosis model grounded in probabilistic inference. The invention encompasses computer processing systems, computer-implemented methods, specialized apparatuses, and computer program products, all designed to execute the disclosed functionality. Referring to FIG. 1, system 100 operates by first receiving raw observational data through component 108, which may suffer from missing values, noise, and domain heterogeneity. Observational data, though rich, is inherently limited by its passive collection nature, lacking controlled experimental conditions. The model generation component 110 then applies Bayesian latent variable analysis to extract composite features—such as factor scores from motor, functional, and cognitive domains—thereby reducing dimensionality while preserving disease-relevant signals. The identification component 112 filters out non-discriminative features, and the ranking component 114 assigns weights based on statistical divergence between states. The disease progression model 116, once trained, outputs state assignments, expected durations, and transition probabilities. FIG. 2 illustrates a filtering component 202 that performs initial feature selection based on availability and variance, while FIG. 3 depicts a pooling component 302 that aggregates domain-specific features into a unified representation. The composite feature engineering step ensures balanced representation across clinical domains, preventing bias toward any single symptom category. The final disease progression model 116 supports utility-based analysis, allowing clinicians to assess risk or prognosis based on inferred state. System 100 is designed with flexibility to accommodate different disease assumptions—for instance, enforcing a forward-chain progression constraint for irreversible conditions like HD. Artificial intelligence techniques, including probabilistic and statistical-based analysis, underpin the classification schemes used to segment disease states. Real-world examples include data from Enroll-HD, REGISTRY, TRACK-HD, and PREDICT-HD, which together form a comprehensive HD observational database. Disease registry data, while extensive, often lacks granularity; electronic health records offer depth but suffer from inconsistency. The invention generates a refined database by integrating these sources, applying initial feature filtering, composite feature engineering, and disease progression modeling to assign each observation to a probabilistic disease stage. Input data comprises both observational data (e.g., UHDRS scores) and knowledge data (e.g., CAG repeat length), which inform model priors. The system analyzes observational data to extract features specifically related to disease progression, distinguishing them from features relevant to comorbidities or unrelated tasks. The output is a reduced database containing a discriminative features sub-database that optimally characterizes state transitions. The computer-implemented method follows a flow diagram: build a preliminary model, identify discriminative features, iterate until convergence on a suitable reduced subset, rank features by discriminative power, and generate the final model.
