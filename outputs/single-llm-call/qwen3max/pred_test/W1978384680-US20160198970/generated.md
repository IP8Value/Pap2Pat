# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the heart, and more particularly to methods and systems for determining optimal cardiac gating windows based on high-temporal-resolution monitoring of ventricular septal motion. The invention further pertains to the acquisition of high-quality magnetic resonance coronary angiography (MRCA) images by synchronizing data collection with periods of minimal cardiac motion—specifically, the diastasis phase of the cardiac cycle—as identified through a novel Septal Scout technique. This approach enables improved spatial fidelity in coronary artery imaging by mitigating motion-induced blurring artifacts that commonly degrade image quality in conventional cine-based cardiac gating protocols.

## BACKGROUND

Cardiac MRI, particularly MRCA, offers a non-invasive alternative to x-ray angiography for diagnosing coronary artery disease (CAD), eliminating exposure to ionizing radiation and avoiding the procedural risks associated with catheterization. However, the clinical utility of MRCA is constrained by long acquisition times that necessitate synchronization with phases of minimal cardiac motion to prevent motion artifacts. Prospective cardiac gating remains the primary strategy for achieving this synchronization, wherein image data are acquired only during a predefined quiescent period within each cardiac cycle—typically the diastasis phase, when coronary artery velocities fall below 10 mm/s. Despite its widespread use, conventional cardiac gating suffers from limitations inherent to the calibration methods employed to define the diastasis window.

Prospective cardiac gating traditionally relies on two components: electrocardiogram (ECG) detection of ventricular systole onset via the R-peak, and pre-scan cine cardiovascular MR (CMR) imaging to determine the temporal offset between the R-peak and the diastasis period. Cine-CMR provides a time-resolved video of the cardiac cycle but is limited by insufficient temporal resolution due to the trade-off between spatial coverage, signal-to-noise ratio, and scan duration. Typical cine acquisitions achieve temporal resolutions of 50–70 ms, which may be inadequate to resolve rapid transitions into and out of diastasis, especially in segments of the coronary tree exhibiting higher velocities during early diastole. Moreover, cine-CMR is vulnerable to temporal data mixing when heart rate variability (HRV) occurs during the multi-heartbeat acquisition, leading to inaccurate estimation of the true diastasis window.

Calibration of gating parameters using cine-CMR typically involves post-processing techniques such as frame-to-frame correlation analysis to identify a plateau period during diastole. While useful, these empirical approaches remain constrained by the underlying temporal sampling limitations of the cine sequence itself. Consequently, there exists a need for a more precise, high-temporal-resolution method to characterize cardiac motion dynamics and accurately delineate the diastasis period.

This need motivates the development of septal motion-based cardiac gating, wherein the motion of the basal interventricular septum (IVS)—a structure whose long-axis displacement correlates strongly with global epicardial motion—is monitored at millisecond-scale temporal resolution. By focusing on a single dimension of motion and sacrificing one spatial encoding dimension, it becomes possible to acquire motion data at rates exceeding 100 frames per second, far surpassing the capabilities of conventional cine imaging. Such an approach enables robust identification of diastasis timing even in the presence of HRV and provides a foundation for more accurate prospective gating in MRCA and other high-resolution cardiac MRI applications.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel method for determining the timing of the diastasis period in the cardiac cycle with high temporal precision, thereby enabling improved prospective cardiac gating for MRI. The method comprises acquiring a series of one-dimensional (1D) projection images along a prescribed Septal Scout Plane that intersects the basal interventricular septum. These projections are acquired rapidly—every 3 to 10 milliseconds—over multiple consecutive heartbeats, triggered by the R-peak of the ECG or an alternative systole detection mechanism.

From the acquired Septal Scout data, a displacement graph is generated by averaging signal intensities within a region of interest (ROI) centered on the basal septum across the spatial dimension and plotting the result over time. This intensity-time plot serves as a pseudo-displacement function reflecting tissue motion through the ROI. A velocity graph is then derived by computing the temporal derivative of the displacement graph, revealing characteristic E and A waves corresponding to early and late diastolic filling, respectively. The diastasis period is defined as the interval between the inflection points (i.e., zero-crossings of the second derivative) that lie between the E and A wave peaks on the velocity graph.

In alternative embodiments, the diastasis period may be defined using different criteria, such as threshold-based velocity minima, duration constraints, or statistical plateaus in displacement variance. The method further accounts for heart rate variability by analyzing diastasis timing across multiple heartbeats and selecting a consensus window that remains valid throughout the expected range of heart rates during a breath-hold acquisition. This multi-heartbeat diastasis window ensures robustness against transient changes in cardiac rhythm that would otherwise compromise gating accuracy in conventional cine-based approaches.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present patent application is structured to describe a comprehensive system and method for high-precision cardiac gating based on septal motion monitoring. Key terms used herein are defined as follows: “diastasis” refers to the mid-diastolic period of minimal ventricular wall motion; “Septal Scout” denotes a 1D projection MRI sequence targeting the basal interventricular septum; “gating window” is the temporal interval within each cardiac cycle during which image data are acquired; and “trigger delay” is the time elapsed between a detected systolic event (e.g., R-peak) and the start of the gating window.

In the medical imaging context, cardiac MRI requires synchronization with cardiac motion to avoid blurring. Cardiac gating achieves this by restricting data acquisition to quiescent phases. The RR interval—the time between successive R-peaks—defines the cardiac cycle length, while the trigger delay specifies when within that cycle imaging begins. Gating error arises when the assumed diastasis window does not align with actual minimal motion, often due to inadequate temporal resolution in calibration scans.

The Septal Scout technique addresses this by acquiring 1D projections every TR (repetition time), typically 3–10 ms, along a Scout Plane prescribed from a 4-chamber long-axis view to intersect the basal septum throughout the cardiac cycle. This plane is orthogonal to the standard cine imaging plane but optimized for motion sensitivity along the heart’s long axis.

Displacement measurements are derived by averaging signals within a ±2.5 mm ROI around the septum, enhancing SNR. The resulting intensity-time plot undergoes optical flow processing to estimate relative tissue displacement. Temporal differentiation yields a velocity graph featuring E and A waves; diastasis is bounded by inflection points between them. Alternative definitions may use velocity thresholds or duration filters.

Two-dimensional (2D) RF excitation pulses can refine spatial selectivity, exciting only a cylindrical volume along the septum to reduce contamination from static tissues. Though longer pulse durations may increase TR, maintaining TR ≤10 ms preserves sufficient temporal resolution.

Phase images from the Septal Scout can also be analyzed, as phase shifts encode motion-induced velocity. These complement magnitude-based displacement graphs, offering additional validation.

End-systole detection—critical for defining the start of diastole—is currently ECG-dependent but may be replaced by direct motion analysis of the Septal Scout, detecting peak septal contraction.

Free-breathing MRI is enabled by integrating respiratory navigators that monitor diaphragm position. Real-time Septal Scout acquisition allows continuous motion tracking, facilitating dynamic adjustment of gating windows.

The MRI-based Cardiac Gating System (MRI-CGS) incorporates a calibration scan to establish baseline diastasis timing and a calibration check to verify stability before main imaging. Ventricular systole detection may shift from ECG to Septal Scout-derived motion signatures, improving reliability in noisy ECG conditions.

Heart rate variability (HRV) is tracked beat-by-beat via RR intervals. An HRV check ensures the selected gating window remains valid across observed heart rates. The system flowchart includes steps for scout acquisition, motion analysis, window determination, and adaptive gating.

Alternative systole detection uses ascending aorta phase-intensity graphs, where systolic ejection causes sharp signal changes. Comparison with Septal Scout confirms timing consistency.

In coronary stenosis imaging, MRA using Septal Scout gating yields sharper vessel boundaries than cine-calibrated or conventional MRI. X-ray angiography serves as anatomical reference, but MRA with precise gating matches diagnostic quality without radiation.

Unlike traditional navigators that monitor respiration, the Septal Scout specifically targets cardiac motion, offering a complementary or superior gating signal. In conclusion, the invention provides a robust, high-resolution method for diastasis detection, significantly enhancing MRCA image quality and paving the way for ECG-independent cardiac MRI.
