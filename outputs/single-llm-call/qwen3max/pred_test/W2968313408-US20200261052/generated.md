# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Transcranial Doppler ultrasound (TCD) is a well-established, noninvasive diagnostic modality used to assess cerebral blood flow velocity (CBFV) through the major intracranial arteries, particularly the middle cerebral artery (MCA). By emitting and receiving high-frequency acoustic waves through the temporal bone window, TCD enables real-time monitoring of hemodynamic parameters critical for evaluating cerebrovascular health. The resulting CBFV waveform is pulsatile in nature, reflecting the cardiac cycle, and its morphological characteristics—such as peak timing, systolic complex shape, and diastolic decay—carry significant clinical information regarding vascular patency, resistance, and compliance. TCD has been widely applied in the diagnosis and management of numerous neurological conditions, including acute ischemic stroke, intracranial hypertension, sickle cell disease, and mild traumatic brain injury. In the context of stroke, TCD is especially valuable for detecting large vessel occlusions (LVOs), which involve partial or complete blockage of the internal carotid artery (ICA) or proximal segments of the MCA and are associated with high morbidity and mortality due to the extensive brain territories they supply. Despite its utility, traditional TCD interpretation relies heavily on subjective visual assessment by trained clinicians, limiting its scalability and reliability in prehospital or resource-constrained settings. The most commonly used framework for morphological evaluation—the Thrombolysis in Brain Ischemia (TIBI) grading system—categorizes waveforms into grades 0 through 5 based on qualitative features such as peak presence, systolic upstroke sharpness, and timing of maximal velocity. While TIBI grades have demonstrated strong correlation with angiographic findings, their assignment requires expert judgment and lacks objective, reproducible criteria. Consequently, there exists a critical need for an automated, data-driven approach to TCD waveform analysis that preserves the clinical insights of morphological assessment while eliminating subjectivity and enabling real-time decision support in acute stroke care.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel method and system for the automated categorization and visualization of transcranial Doppler (TCD) waveforms based on objective morphological variables derived from cerebral blood flow velocity (CBFV) signals. The method begins by processing a signal containing ultrasound-derived CBFV data to identify key morphological variables that characterize the shape and structure of individual cardiac-cycle waveforms. These variables include an onset variable representing the normalized temporal position of peak systolic velocity, a canopy variable quantifying the duration of the systolic complex relative to the cardiac cycle, and a peaks variable measuring the number and prominence of auxiliary peaks and troughs within the waveform. Once extracted, these morphological variables are mapped to respective axes in a multidimensional feature space, enabling quantitative comparison across waveforms. The invention then categorizes each waveform by assigning it to one of several predefined categories, each corresponding to a distinct region or cluster within this feature space. These categories are derived through unsupervised machine learning—specifically spectral clustering—applied to a representative dataset of waveforms from both healthy controls and patients with confirmed cerebrovascular pathologies such as large vessel occlusion (LVO). The probability of a given waveform belonging to each category is computed based on its proximity to cluster centroids or archetype waveforms, allowing for probabilistic rather than deterministic classification. Each category is clinically interpretable and corresponds to specific hemodynamic states: for example, one category reflects normal flow with early systolic peak and robust secondary peaks, another indicates blunted flow with delayed peak and diminished structure, and a third suggests stenotic or severely obstructed flow with narrow systolic complex and minimal oscillatory features. The invention further includes a dedicated device for visualizing this categorization, comprising an ultrasound probe, a controller executing signal processing and clustering algorithms, and an output interface that displays the categorized waveform alongside its probability distribution across categories. This system enables rapid, objective assessment of cerebral hemodynamics at the point of care, supporting timely triage decisions in acute neurological emergencies such as stroke.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The following detailed description sets forth exemplary embodiments of the invention with sufficient particularity to enable those skilled in the art to practice the invention without undue experimentation. Reference is made throughout to the appended drawings, which illustrate key components and operational sequences but are not drawn to scale and should not be interpreted as limiting the scope of the claims.

The invention encompasses systems and methods for automated flow type classification using transcranial Doppler (TCD) ultrasound. Central to this approach is the objective assessment of TCD waveform morphology through machine learning techniques, which overcome the limitations of subjective visual grading systems like TIBI. The process begins with the acquisition of CBFV signals using standard 2 MHz ultrasound probes positioned transtemporally to insonate the middle cerebral artery. These signals are recorded over 30-second intervals at depths between 45–60 mm to ensure vessel specificity. An automated signal processing pipeline then extracts individual cardiac-cycle waveforms from the raw data stream. Beats are identified by detecting systolic upstroke onsets, and outliers with excessive noise or artifact are rejected using iterated interquartile range exclusion combined with cross-correlation metrics. Accepted beats from each recording interval are aligned at systolic onset, padded to uniform length, and averaged to produce a single representative waveform per depth.

To facilitate cross-subject comparison, all waveforms undergo preprocessing: they are resampled to a common native sampling rate (e.g., 125 Hz), smoothed using a Hanning window to suppress high-frequency noise beyond physiological relevance (~10 Hz), and normalized in both time and velocity dimensions. Temporal normalization enforces a fixed number of samples per cardiac cycle (e.g., 100 via cubic spline interpolation), effectively decoupling morphology from heart rate. Velocity normalization scales each waveform to the [0,1] interval by subtracting the diastolic minimum and dividing by the resultant systolic-diastolic range.

From each normalized waveform, three morphological features are extracted. The **onset** variable is defined as the time-normalized index at which peak velocity occurs, capturing whether maximal flow is achieved early or late in systole—a key discriminator in TIBI grading. The **canopy** variable counts the number of samples where velocity exceeds 25% of the systolic-diastolic range, quantifying the breadth of the systolic complex. The **peaks** variable computes a weighted sum of true peaks (zero-crossings of the first derivative) and pseudo-peaks (points where the derivative magnitude falls below a threshold, e.g., 0.01), with weights inversely proportional to derivative magnitude to account for flattened but structurally significant inflections.

These three features form a three-dimensional feature space. To ensure equitable contribution from each dimension, z-score normalization is applied across the entire dataset. Spectral clustering—an unsupervised learning algorithm robust to non-convex cluster shapes—is then employed to partition the feature space into natural groupings. The optimal number of clusters is determined using the gap statistic method, which compares observed intra-cluster dispersion against a null reference distribution generated via bootstrap resampling. In validation studies, four clusters consistently emerged as optimal.

Each cluster yields an archetypal waveform, computed as the average of the five most centrally located members (by mean intra-cluster Euclidean distance). Cluster I (early onset, wide canopy, strong peaks) predominantly contains control waveforms and aligns with TIBI grade 5 (normal flow). Cluster II (late onset, wide canopy, strong peaks) also favors controls and may reflect altered peripheral resistance. Cluster III (early onset, narrow canopy, weak peaks) is overwhelmingly composed of LVO cases and resembles stenotic flow. Cluster IV (late onset, wide canopy, weak peaks) shows mixed composition but strong LVO association and matches TIBI grade 2 (blunted flow).

The invention further comprises a waveform visualization system. This system includes a headset device equipped with a robotic positioning mechanism that holds and precisely maneuvers an ultrasound probe against the patient’s temple. The probe features a concave transducer surface for acoustic focusing and a threaded base for secure coupling to the robotics. A structural support stabilizes the patient’s head during scanning. The controller—comprising a processor, memory, and robotic control circuit—receives raw ultrasound data, executes the aforementioned signal processing and clustering pipeline, and determines morphological indicators. An input device allows operator commands, while a network interface enables data transmission to remote systems. The output device, which may be integrated into the headset as a heads-up display or presented on an external screen, renders the categorized waveform alongside a probability histogram indicating likelihood of membership in each cluster.

In operation, the method processes incoming CBFV data in real time, extracts morphological variables, references the predetermined cluster model, computes category probabilities, assigns the waveform to the most probable category, and displays the result. Categories correspond directly to hemodynamic states: normal perfusion, elevated resistance, stenosis, or proximal occlusion. Overlap between category boundaries is acknowledged through probabilistic outputs, enhancing clinical nuance. The system supports both manual probe operation and fully automated robotic scanning, including autonomous localization of the MCA via depth and velocity optimization algorithms. This comprehensive framework transforms TCD from a subjective interpretive tool into an objective, automated diagnostic platform for cerebrovascular pathology.
