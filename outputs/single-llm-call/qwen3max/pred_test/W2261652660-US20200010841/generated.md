# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of plant biotechnology and, more specifically, to an expression system for transgene expression in plants. The invention provides a tightly regulated, reversible, and tissue-specific expression platform that enables high-level transcriptional control of a gene of interest in the postmeiotic tapetum of flowering plants. This system is particularly useful for the development of male-sterile female lines and their corresponding fertility-restored F1 hybrid progeny, thereby facilitating efficient heterosis breeding and commercial hybrid seed production. The invention leverages a novel molecular architecture comprising a modified TATA-box binding protein (TBPm3), a truncated light-responsive transcription factor fragment (HFR1NT131), and a nuclear-localized mutant of the E3 ubiquitin ligase COP1 (COP1L105A) to achieve stringent on/off switching of transgene expression in a spatially and temporally controlled manner within the anther tapetum.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION AND PRIOR ART

Hybrid vigor, or heterosis, refers to the phenomenon wherein the offspring of two genetically distinct parental lines exhibit superior agronomic traits such as increased yield, enhanced stress tolerance, and improved uniformity compared to either parent. This biological advantage has been extensively exploited in modern agriculture, particularly in major crops like maize, rice, sorghum, and sunflower, where hybrid varieties consistently outperform conventional inbred lines. The successful deployment of heterosis hinges upon the reliable production of hybrid seeds, which requires the prevention of self-pollination in the female parent to ensure cross-fertilization with pollen from the designated male parent.

Historically, hybrid seed production has relied on manual or mechanical emasculation—the physical removal of anthers from the female parent prior to pollen release. However, this method is labor-intensive, costly, and impractical for large-scale cultivation, especially in species with small or numerous flowers. To overcome these limitations, genetic approaches to induce male sterility have been developed. Male sterility is a condition in which a plant is unable to produce functional pollen while retaining normal female fertility, thereby enabling it to serve as an ideal female parent in hybrid breeding programs.

Male sterility can be broadly categorized into cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS) and genetic male sterility (GMS). CMS arises from mutations in mitochondrial DNA that disrupt pollen development and is maternally inherited. While widely used in crops like rice and sunflower, CMS systems often suffer from instability under varying environmental conditions and require specific nuclear restorer-of-fertility (Rf) genes to restore pollen function in the hybrid. GMS, on the other hand, results from nuclear gene mutations and follows Mendelian inheritance. Although more stable, conventional GMS systems are difficult to maintain because homozygous sterile lines cannot self-propagate, necessitating complex breeding schemes involving maintainer lines.

To address these challenges, transgenic strategies for inducing conditional male sterility have emerged. These systems typically involve the tapetum-specific expression of cytotoxic or pro-apoptotic genes that disrupt pollen development. The tapetum is a specialized secretory cell layer surrounding the developing microspores in the anther and plays a critical role in pollen wall formation and nutrient supply. Ablation of tapetal cells during the postmeiotic phase leads to complete male sterility without affecting vegetative growth or female fertility. Several genes have been employed for this purpose, including BARNASE (a bacterial ribonuclease), RNase T1, diphtheria toxin A-chain, the mammalian uncoupling protein, the pro-apoptotic protein BAX, and the autophagy-related gene AtBECLIN1. Additionally, melon ethylene receptor genes and inhibitors of apoptotic proteins or cysteine proteases have also been explored.

Despite these advances, a major limitation of existing transgenic male sterility systems is the requirement for a corresponding restorer gene—such as BARSTAR for BARNASE—that must be precisely matched and co-expressed to neutralize the sterility-inducing agent in the F1 hybrid. The identification and optimization of such restorer components are often inefficient, leading to incomplete fertility restoration and reduced seed set. Moreover, many systems lack tight regulatory control, resulting in leaky expression of the sterility gene in non-target tissues or developmental stages, which can impair plant fitness. Consequently, there remains a pressing need for a versatile, tightly regulated, and reversible expression system that enables robust male sterility in the female parent and complete fertility restoration in the F1 hybrid without reliance on protein-protein neutralization mechanisms.

### LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR ART

Existing transgenic male sterility systems suffer from several critical limitations that hinder their broad applicability in commercial hybrid seed production. First, many systems rely on the expression of toxic proteins such as BARNASE or diphtheria toxin A-chain, which raise biosafety concerns in certain jurisdictions and may lead to unintended pleiotropic effects if expressed ectopically. Second, fertility restoration often depends on the co-expression of a specific antidote protein (e.g., BARSTAR), and the efficiency of restoration is highly sensitive to the relative expression levels of the toxin and antidote, making it difficult to achieve consistent 100% fertility in F1 hybrids. Third, the promoters used for tapetum-specific expression—such as TA29 or A9—are not always sufficiently stringent, leading to low-level “leaky” expression in vegetative tissues that can reduce plant vigor or yield. Fourth, some systems are environmentally sensitive (e.g., temperature- or chemical-inducible), limiting their reliability across diverse growing conditions. Finally, none of the prior art systems provide a mechanism for transcriptional abolition of the sterility gene in the F1 generation; instead, they rely on post-transcriptional or post-translational interference, which is inherently less efficient and more prone to failure.

### Objectives of the Present Invention

It is therefore an objective of the present invention to develop a tightly regulated, reversible expression system for transgenes in plants that overcomes the limitations of prior art. Specifically, the invention aims to provide a tapetum-specific expression system that achieves high-level, postmeiotic, and spatially restricted transgene expression without ectopic activity. Another objective is to engineer a regulatory component that enables stringent transcriptional control over the expression system through targeted protein degradation mediated by the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. A further objective is to utilize the TA29 promoter, modified with a TGTA mutation in its TATA box, in combination with a complementing mutant TATA-box binding protein (TBPm3), to drive robust and specific expression exclusively in the tapetum. Yet another objective is to fuse the N-terminal 131-amino acid fragment of the Arabidopsis transcription factor HFR1 to TBPm3, rendering the fusion protein susceptible to degradation by the E3 ubiquitin ligase COP1, thereby providing a molecular switch to turn off transgene expression. Finally, the invention seeks to enhance the nuclear localization of COP1 through a L105A point mutation (COP1L105A) to ensure complete degradation of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein and thus achieve full transcriptional silencing of the sterility gene in F1 hybrids.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a male sterility–fertility restoration system for plants based on a reversible, tapetum-specific expression platform. In one aspect, the invention provides a male sterility (MS) DNA construct comprising a first expression cassette wherein a gene of interest—such as BECLIN1—is operably linked to a tapetum-specific promoter containing a TGTA-mutated TATA box, and a second regulatory cassette encoding an N-terminal fragment of a transcription factor (HFR1NT131) fused to a mutant TATA-box binding protein (TBPm3), wherein the fusion protein complements the TGTA-mutated promoter to drive high-level expression of the gene of interest specifically in postmeiotic tapetal cells, resulting in complete male sterility. In another aspect, the invention provides an expression system (ES) DNA construct identical in structure to the MS construct but designed for general transgene expression rather than sterility induction. In a further aspect, the invention provides a restoration system (RS) DNA construct comprising a third expression cassette wherein a restorer gene—encoding a nuclear-localized mutant of COP1 (COP1L105A)—is operably linked to a tapetum-specific promoter (e.g., A9), enabling targeted degradation of the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein in F1 progeny and thereby abolishing transcription of the sterility gene and restoring male fertility.

The invention further provides DNA vectors comprising any of the aforementioned MS, ES, or RS DNA constructs, as well as recombinant host cells—such as Agrobacterium tumefaciens—harboring these vectors. Transgenic plants, plant parts, and seeds containing these constructs are also provided, including male-sterile female lines (carrying the MS construct), restorer male lines (carrying the RS construct), and F1 hybrid plants generated by crossing the two, which exhibit restored fertility and normal seed set. Methods for producing such transgenic and hybrid plants are disclosed, including Agrobacterium-mediated transformation, selection of transformants, and controlled crossing. The system is reversible: when the MS and RS constructs are combined in the F1 generation, the COP1L105A protein degrades the HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion, preventing assembly of the transcriptional pre-initiation complex on the TGTA-mutated promoter, thereby switching off expression of the sterility gene. This approach eliminates the need for protein-based antidotes and enables complete, transcription-level control over male fertility, making it ideal for scalable hybrid seed production.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The scope of the present invention encompasses a modular, three-component genetic system for achieving tightly regulated, reversible, and tissue-specific transgene expression in plants, with primary application in hybrid seed production through male sterility and fertility restoration. The system comprises three distinct DNA constructs: a male sterility (MS) construct, an expression system (ES) construct, and a restoration system (RS) construct. Each construct is designed for integration into the plant genome via standard transformation techniques and functions in a coordinated manner to enable on-demand activation or silencing of a gene of interest in the tapetum.

The ES construct serves as the foundational expression module. It includes a first expression cassette in which a gene of interest—such as a reporter gene (e.g., gusA) or a sterility-inducing gene (e.g., BECLIN1)—is placed under the control of a modified tapetum-specific promoter, such as TA29(TGTA), wherein the native TATA box (TATATATG) is mutated to TGTA. This mutation abolishes recognition by endogenous TATA-box binding protein (TBP), rendering the promoter inactive in wild-type plants. However, the construct also includes a second regulatory cassette encoding a chimeric protein consisting of the N-terminal 131 amino acids of the Arabidopsis transcription factor HFR1 (HFR1NT131) fused to a three-amino-acid mutant form of TBP (TBPm3: Ile152Phe, Val161Thr, Leu163Val). This HFR1NT131-TBPm3 fusion protein specifically binds the TGTA-mutated promoter and drives high-level transcription exclusively in postmeiotic tapetal cells (stages 2–3 of anther development), as confirmed by fluorimetric and histochemical GUS assays. The fusion does not impair TBPm3 function, and transgenic plants exhibit normal growth, development, and fertility when a non-toxic gene (e.g., gusA) is expressed.

The MS construct is a specialized version of the ES construct wherein the gene of interest is a male sterility effector, such as Arabidopsis BECLIN1, an autophagy-related protein whose overexpression in the tapetum triggers premature tapetal hypertrophy, delayed degeneration, and complete pollen abortion, resulting in 100% male sterility while preserving female fertility. Transgenic plants carrying the MS construct produce no viable pollen but can be fertilized by wild-type pollen to set normal seeds.

The RS construct provides the fertility restoration function. It contains a third expression cassette in which a restorer gene—encoding a nuclear-enriched mutant of the E3 ubiquitin ligase COP1 (COP1L105A)—is driven by a tapetum-specific promoter such as Arabidopsis A9. The COP1L105A mutation enhances nuclear retention without compromising enzymatic activity. In F1 hybrids generated by crossing MS (female) and RS (male) parents, COP1L105A interacts with the HFR1NT131 domain of the fusion protein and targets it for proteasomal degradation. This depletes cellular TBPm3, preventing transcription initiation at the TGTA promoter and completely abolishing expression of the sterility gene. Consequently, tapetal development proceeds normally, and full male fertility is restored in the F1, as evidenced by pollen viability, germination rates, and seed set comparable to wild-type controls.

The invention further defines key molecular components: a “cloning vector” refers to any plasmid suitable for subcloning DNA fragments; a “reporter gene” (e.g., gusA) enables visual or quantitative detection of expression; a “promoter” is a DNA sequence that directs transcription initiation; a “constitutive promoter” drives expression in all tissues (e.g., CaMV 35S); a “tapetum-specific promoter” (e.g., TA29, A9) restricts expression to the anther tapetum; an “expression cassette” comprises a promoter, coding sequence, and terminator; a “transcription factor” regulates gene expression; a “recombinant vector” carries foreign DNA; an “expression vector” facilitates transgene expression; a “binary vector” is used in Agrobacterium-mediated plant transformation; “programmed cell death (PCD)” and “autophagy” are cellular degradation processes; “explants” are plant tissues used for transformation; and “photomorphogenesis” is light-regulated development.

The method of generating transgenic plants involves Agrobacterium-mediated transformation of explants (e.g., leaf discs), selection on antibiotic media (e.g., kanamycin for NPTII, hygromycin for HPTII), regeneration, and molecular confirmation via PCR and qRT-PCR. Hybrid seeds are produced by crossing homozygous MS female lines with homozygous RS male lines. The system is generic: any sterility gene (e.g., BARNASE, BAX) can replace BECLIN1 in the MS construct, and the RS construct remains universally applicable due to its transcriptional silencing mechanism. Alternative embodiments include variations in promoter choice (e.g., other anther-specific promoters), restorer genes (e.g., other nuclear-localized E3 ligases), and fusion architectures. The invention thus provides a robust, scalable, and biosafe platform for hybrid crop breeding.

### EXAMPLES

The following working examples illustrate the practice of the invention but do not limit its scope. Total RNA was extracted from Arabidopsis thaliana leaves or seedlings using a Plant Spectrum Total RNA Isolation Kit. cDNA was synthesized using SuperScript Reverse Transcriptase. The Beclin1 coding sequence was amplified via PCR using gene-specific primers, cloned into pBluescript SK+, and verified by sequencing and BLAST homology analysis. Similarly, full-length COP1 and the N-terminal 131-amino acid fragment of HFR1 (HFR1NT131) were cloned. Site-directed mutagenesis introduced the L105A substitution into COP1 using QuikChange XL, with primers COPM_f1/r1 and COPM_f2/r2, and mutants were confirmed by sequencing.

Chimeric constructs were assembled in binary vectors (pBI101, pCAMBIA1300). Construct 1370 (ES) contained TA29(TGTA)::gusA and Pcec::HFR1NT131-TBPm3. Construct 1371 (MS) replaced gusA with Beclin1. Construct 1372 (RS-wt) contained PA9::COP1, while 1373 (RS-mut) contained PA9::COP1L105A. Tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum cv. Petit Havana) was transformed via Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404. Transgenic lines were selected on kanamycin (300 mg/L) or hygromycin (50 mg/L), and integration was confirmed by PCR.

Fluorimetric GUS assays showed high activity (20–22 nmol/mg/min) in stages 2–3 anthers of 1370 lines, absent elsewhere. Crossing 1370 (♀) × 1372 (♂) yielded F1 with 4–6-fold reduced GUS activity; crossing with 1373 (♂) abolished GUS entirely. 1371 lines exhibited 0% pollen viability and complete male sterility. TEM revealed tapetal hypertrophy and pollen collapse from stage 2 onward. Crossing 1371 (♀) × 1373 (♂) produced F’1 with 96% pollen viability, normal morphology (SEM), and seed set (78.89 mg/pod vs. 74.23 in WT). qRT-PCR confirmed ~21-fold BECLIN1 reduction in F1 (with COP1) and complete abolition in F’1 (with COP1L105A). Anther histology of F’1 showed normal tapetal degeneration, confirming BECLIN1-dependent sterility. These results validate the invention’s utility for complete male sterility and fertility restoration in hybrid seed production.
