# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase S (EndoS), particularly to mutant forms of EndoS exhibiting enhanced transglycosylation activity and reduced hydrolysis activity. These engineered enzymes are useful in the chemoenzymatic remodeling of glycoproteins, especially immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies, by enabling efficient transfer of defined glycan structures onto the conserved N297-linked glycosylation site in the Fc region. The invention further encompasses methods for producing such mutant enzymes, as well as their application in one-pot transglycosylation reactions using natural glycopeptide donors such as sialoglycopeptide (SGP), thereby obviating the need for chemically synthesized sugar oxazoline intermediates.

## BACKGROUND ART

Glycoproteins are proteins that contain covalently attached carbohydrate moieties, commonly referred to as glycans or sugar chains. These posttranslational modifications play critical roles in protein folding, stability, solubility, trafficking, and biological activity. In particular, the glycosylation profile of therapeutic antibodies profoundly influences their effector functions, including antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). Antibodies, especially IgG, possess a highly conserved N-linked glycan at asparagine 297 (N297) in the Fc domain, and the composition of this glycan—particularly the presence or absence of core fucose, galactose, and sialic acid—modulates interactions with Fcγ receptors and complement proteins.

Posttranslational modification via glycosylation is a non-template-driven process, leading to heterogeneity in glycoforms when antibodies are produced in conventional expression systems. This heterogeneity can compromise therapeutic consistency and efficacy. To address this, various strategies have been developed to produce homogeneous glycoforms, including genetic engineering of host cells and in vitro enzymatic remodeling. Among these, chemoenzymatic approaches using endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidases (ENGases) have emerged as powerful tools for precise glycan editing.

ENGases catalyze the cleavage of the β-1,4-glycosidic bond in the chitobiose core of N-linked glycans. Certain ENGases, such as Endo-S from *Streptococcus pyogenes*, exhibit specificity for IgG Fc glycans and can also perform transglycosylation—the transfer of an intact glycan from a donor substrate (typically a glycan oxazoline) to a GlcNAc-containing acceptor. However, wild-type ENGases often retain significant hydrolytic activity toward the transglycosylation product, limiting reaction yields and requiring excess donor substrate.

Several ENGases have been characterized, including EndoA (*Arthrobacter protophormiae*), EndoD (*Streptococcus pneumoniae*), EndoM (*Mucor hiemalis*), EndoH (*Streptomyces plicatus*), EndoF2 (*Elizabethkingia meningoseptica*), EndoE (*Enterococcus faecalis*), and EndoS (*S. pyogenes*). Among these, EndoS is uniquely selective for native IgG and does not act on denatured proteins or other glycoproteins, making it ideal for antibody engineering. A catalytically impaired mutant, EndoS D233Q, was previously developed to reduce hydrolysis while retaining transglycosylation activity. Similarly, EndoS2, a homolog from *S. pyogenes* serotype M49, has been engineered (e.g., D184M mutant) for improved performance.

Despite these advances, existing EndoS variants still require high molar equivalents of synthetic oxazoline donors (often 20-fold or more relative to antibody), which are costly and unstable. Moreover, the residual hydrolysis activity of even mutant enzymes leads to product degradation over time, complicating large-scale manufacturing. There remains a pressing need for EndoS mutants with further reduced hydrolysis, enhanced transglycosylation rates, and compatibility with natural, non-oxazoline glycan donors.

In response to this need, the present inventors have developed novel EndoS mutants through structure-guided mutagenesis. Additionally, they have devised a one-pot transglycosylation system that leverages complementary ENGase specificities—using an EndoS mutant for IgG remodeling and an auxiliary ENGase mutant (e.g., from Endo-M or Endo-CC) for in situ generation of the active glycan donor from sialoglycopeptide (SGP). This approach eliminates the requirement for chemical synthesis of oxazoline and enables efficient, scalable glycoengineering of therapeutic antibodies.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the inefficiency and impracticality of current chemoenzymatic methods for antibody glycoengineering. Existing EndoS-based systems suffer from residual hydrolytic activity that degrades the transglycosylation product, necessitating high concentrations of expensive and unstable synthetic glycan oxazolines to drive reactions to completion. Furthermore, the reliance on chemically synthesized donors increases cost, complexity, and regulatory burden, hindering large-scale therapeutic manufacturing. There is thus a critical need for engineered EndoS enzymes with minimal hydrolysis, high transglycosylation efficiency at low donor-to-acceptor ratios, and compatibility with natural glycan donors.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention provides novel mutant forms of EndoS that overcome these limitations. These mutants are derived from the EndoS D233Q background and include additional amino acid substitutions at specific positions identified through structural analysis and functional screening. The key mutations include, but are not limited to, substitutions at positions H122, P184, D279, Y282, Q303, Y348, E350, Y402, D405, and R406. Specific exemplary mutations include H122A, P184A, D279S, Y282F, Q303L, Y348F, E350Q, Y402F, D405A, and R406A. These mutations are introduced individually or in combination to modulate substrate binding, transition state stabilization, and product release, thereby decoupling transglycosylation from hydrolysis.

Particularly effective mutants include double mutants such as D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, and D233Q/D405A, as well as triple mutants like D233Q/Q303L/E350Q and D233Q/Y402F/D405A. These variants exhibit transglycosylation efficiencies exceeding 90% at donor-to-acceptor ratios as low as 5:1 (based on monomeric Fc), with negligible hydrolysis even after 48 hours of incubation. The invention further encompasses methods for producing these mutant enzymes recombinantly in microbial hosts, as well as their use in one-pot transglycosylation reactions employing natural glycopeptide donors such as sialoglycopeptide (SGP) or SG-Asn, in combination with auxiliary ENGase mutants (e.g., Endo-M N175Q or Endo-CC N180H).

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The mutant EndoS enzymes of the present invention offer several significant advantages. First, they enable high-yield transglycosylation with substantially reduced amounts of glycan donor, lowering material costs and simplifying reaction setup. Second, their minimal hydrolytic activity ensures product stability over extended reaction times, facilitating process control and scalability. Third, when used in conjunction with auxiliary ENGase mutants, they support one-pot glycan transfer directly from natural glycopeptides like SGP, eliminating the need for chemical synthesis of oxazoline intermediates. This not only reduces cost and complexity but also enhances the sustainability and regulatory acceptability of the manufacturing process. Collectively, these improvements represent a major advancement toward the industrial-scale production of homogeneous, glycoengineered therapeutic antibodies.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

Amino acid residues are denoted using standard single-letter or three-letter codes as established in biochemistry. The term “N297-linked sugar chains” refers to N-linked glycans attached to asparagine residue 297 in the CH2 domain of the IgG heavy chain. These glycans typically consist of a pentasaccharide core (Man3GlcNAc2) that may be further elaborated with antennae containing galactose, sialic acid, fucose, and bisecting N-acetylglucosamine. The core fucose is α1,6-linked to the innermost GlcNAc and is a key determinant of ADCC activity.

Sugar donors for transglycosylation are typically activated glycan derivatives, most commonly glycan oxazolines, which mimic the oxazolinium ion intermediate formed during enzymatic catalysis. However, the present invention also enables the use of natural glycopeptides such as sialoglycopeptide (SGP)—a glycopeptide isolated from egg yolk containing a disialylated biantennary complex-type glycan—as a donor when combined with an auxiliary ENGase mutant.

Acceptor molecules are glycoproteins or fragments thereof bearing a single GlcNAc residue at the N-glycosylation site, generated by prior deglycosylation with an ENGase such as wild-type EndoS. For IgG, this results in (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-IgG, where the core fucose remains attached to the GlcNAc.

EndoS is a 95-kDa enzyme from *S. pyogenes* comprising a catalytic (β/α)8-barrel domain and a carbohydrate-binding module. It specifically hydrolyzes the β-1,4 linkage between the two GlcNAc residues in the chitobiose core of IgG Fc glycans. Wild-type EndoS exhibits both hydrolysis and transglycosylation activities, but the latter is inefficient due to rapid product hydrolysis. The D233Q mutation ablates a key catalytic residue involved in proton donation, thereby reducing hydrolysis while preserving transglycosylation capability.

EndoS-related enzymes include EndoS2 and other bacterial ENGases with structural homology. The mutant enzymes of the present invention are engineered variants of EndoS that incorporate the D233Q mutation plus one or more additional substitutions designed to fine-tune substrate binding and catalytic partitioning.

### <Mutant Enzyme of the Present Invention>

The mutant enzymes of the present invention are based on EndoS D233Q and include additional amino acid alterations at positions that influence glycan binding or catalytic efficiency. Essential additional mutation sites include H122, P184, D279, Y282, Q303, Y348, E350, Y402, D405, and R406. These residues are located in or near the substrate-binding cleft and modulate interactions with different regions of the glycan.

For instance, H122 and E350 are proximal to the α1–3 mannose arm; mutations such as H122A or E350Q weaken binding to this arm, reducing hydrolysis. Q303 lies near the catalytic center; Q303L enhances transglycosylation by altering local hydrophobicity. D279S and Y282F affect interactions with the chitobiose core. Y402, D405, and R406 contact the α1–6 arm or core regions; Y402F and D405A reduce hydrolysis without compromising transglycosylation. Y348F and P184A further modulate binding pocket flexibility.

Preferred combinations include D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, D233Q/D405A, D233Q/Y402F, D233Q/D279S, and triple mutants such as D233Q/Q303L/E350Q or D233Q/Y402F/D405A. These mutants exhibit hydrolysis activity below 20% even after 48 hours, while achieving transglycosylation yields of 90–97% within 24 hours at low donor concentrations.

### <Gene, Host Cell, Method of Producing Enzyme>

The recombinant gene encoding the mutant EndoS enzyme is constructed by site-directed mutagenesis of the wild-type *endoS* gene, typically cloned into an expression vector such as pGEX-4T-3 for GST fusion. The construct is transformed into *E. coli* BL21(DE3) and expressed upon IPTG induction at 16°C. The enzyme is purified via glutathione affinity chromatography, followed by size-exclusion chromatography to remove elution agents. Nucleotide sequence modifications correspond precisely to the desired amino acid substitutions, confirmed by DNA sequencing.

### <Sugar Remodeling>

Sugar remodeling involves the enzymatic transfer of a defined glycan onto a GlcNAc-bearing acceptor. The method comprises: (1) preparing an acceptor molecule (e.g., deglycosylated trastuzumab) by treating IgG with wild-type EndoS; (2) incubating the acceptor with a mutant EndoS enzyme and a sugar donor (e.g., SG-oxazoline or SGP) under buffered conditions (50 mM Tris-HCl, pH 7.4); (3) conducting hydrolysis at 30–37°C for 1–4 hours if using SGP with an auxiliary ENGase; and (4) performing transglycosylation at 28–37°C for 2–48 hours. Reactions are monitored by capillary electrophoresis or LC-MS to assess conversion. Preferred sugar donors include SG-oxazoline or SGP, with SGP used in combination with Endo-M N175Q or Endo-CC N180H for in situ donor activation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

EndoS mutant enzymes were designed by introducing point mutations into the D233Q background via QuikChange mutagenesis. Genes were expressed in *E. coli* BL21(DE3) in 1 L cultures induced with 0.1 mM IPTG at 16°C for 20 h. Cells were lysed by sonication in PBS with lysozyme and DNase I. Supernatants were applied to Glutathione Sepharose 4B, washed, and eluted with 10 mM glutathione. Eluates were buffer-exchanged into PBS and further purified by size-exclusion chromatography on Superdex 200. Yields ranged from 2–7 mg/mL, with purity >95% by SDS-PAGE. Enzyme solutions were stored at −80°C.

### Example 2

SG-Oxa (sialylglycan oxazoline) was synthesized chemically from SGP by periodate oxidation, β-elimination, and oxazoline formation as described in literature. Structure was confirmed by 1H-NMR and MALDI-TOF MS, showing characteristic oxazoline ring signals and correct mass.

### Example 3

Trastuzumab was deglycosylated by incubation with wild-type EndoS (500 μg per 100 mg antibody) in 50 mM phosphate buffer, pH 6.0, at 37°C for 4 h. The reaction mixture was purified sequentially on Protein A and hydroxyapatite (CHT) columns, yielding (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-trastuzumab. LC-ESI-MS confirmed a mass of 49,506 Da for the heavy chain, consistent with retention of core fucose.

### Example 4

Hydrolysis activity was measured by incubating each mutant (10 μg) with trastuzumab (500 μg) in Tris-HCl buffer, pH 7.4, at 30°C. Aliquots taken at intervals were analyzed by Experion Pro260 electrophoresis. Percent hydrolysis was calculated from the deglycosylated heavy chain peak area. Transglycosylation assays used deglycosylated trastuzumab (800 μg), SG-oxazoline (5 eq), and mutant enzyme (16 μg) under identical conditions. Percent transglycosylation was derived from the glycosylated product peak. Products were purified by Protein A and CHT chromatography and confirmed by LC-MS, showing expected mass increases corresponding to the transferred glycan.
