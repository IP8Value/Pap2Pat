# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of wireless communication systems and, more specifically, to machine learning based flow service classification for improving quality of service in mobile networks. The invention provides a novel framework for real-time identification of streaming video traffic flows, particularly HTTP Adaptive Streaming (HAS) services, by analyzing only downlink packet headers without requiring deep packet inspection or bidirectional flow monitoring. This technical approach enables cellular base stations to dynamically classify user traffic into specific service categories and further determine application-level characteristics such as video player state and resolution quality. The classified information is then utilized to implement experience-aware scheduling policies that prioritize users experiencing poor quality of experience, thereby enhancing overall network performance while maintaining compatibility with existing infrastructure. The invention represents a significant advancement in the integration of machine learning techniques with radio access network resource management, specifically addressing the growing challenge of managing exponentially increasing video traffic in modern mobile networks.

## BACKGROUND

The rapid proliferation of mobile video streaming services has fundamentally transformed the landscape of wireless data consumption, with projections indicating that streaming video and immersive multimedia will constitute over 80% of all mobile data traffic. This dramatic shift presents substantial challenges for mobile network operators who must manage constrained bandwidth resources while meeting escalating user expectations for high-quality video experiences. Traditional approaches to traffic management have proven inadequate in this new paradigm, as they typically treat all best-effort traffic equally regardless of the underlying application characteristics or user experience implications. Conventional scheduling algorithms, such as proportional fair schedulers, lack awareness of application-specific requirements and cannot differentiate between users experiencing severe quality degradation and those receiving satisfactory service. 

Streaming video delivery through over-the-top (OTT) services like Netflix and YouTube predominantly employs HTTP Adaptive Streaming (HAS) protocols, which operate over standard TCP connections and are therefore classified as best-effort traffic in most cellular networks. This classification fails to account for the unique temporal characteristics and quality sensitivity of video streaming applications. When network congestion occurs, HAS users frequently experience prolonged playback delays, frequent resolution changes, and rebuffering events that significantly degrade their quality of experience. Prior attempts to address these issues have relied on either deep packet inspection (DPI) techniques or rule-based classification schemes, both of which suffer from critical limitations. DPI methods require access to encrypted application payloads, making them ineffective against modern end-to-end encryption, while rule-based approaches are vulnerable to spoofing and cannot adapt to evolving application behaviors. 

More recent machine learning-based solutions have attempted to overcome these limitations but often introduce new challenges related to implementation complexity and latency. Many existing approaches require bidirectional packet monitoring, inter-packet arrival time information, or extensive feature extraction that creates unpredictable processing delays and computational overhead. These requirements make them impractical for deployment in real-time base station schedulers where low latency and minimal resource consumption are essential. Furthermore, some solutions necessitate modifications to existing network infrastructure or physical layer implementations, creating significant barriers to adoption. The need remains for a lightweight, efficient, and deployable solution that can accurately classify streaming video traffic and extract relevant application characteristics using only readily available information from standard packet headers, thereby enabling proactive quality-of-experience improvements without compromising network performance for other traffic types.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces an apparatus for improving service quality in wireless communication networks through intelligent traffic classification and experience-aware scheduling. The apparatus comprises a communication interface configured to receive downlink packets from a network, wherein each packet includes standard IP and TCP/UDP headers. A processor is operatively coupled to the communication interface and configured to detect the start of a new traffic flow by monitoring incoming packet streams for unique flow identifiers. Upon detection of a new flow, the processor buffers the initial packets belonging to that flow to enable feature extraction without disrupting normal packet processing.

The processor is further configured to extract features exclusively from the UDP/IP or TCP/IP headers of downlink packets, avoiding any requirement for payload inspection or uplink packet monitoring. These extracted features are then processed by a flow service classifier, which determines the service type of the traffic flow from among predetermined categories including streaming video, file download, web browsing, and other application types. The invention also introduces a method for improving service quality that follows a systematic sequence of operations: first detecting the initiation of a traffic flow, then buffering the initial packets of that flow, extracting header-based features, applying a trained machine learning classifier to determine the service type, and finally implementing appropriate scheduling policies based on the classification results.

Additionally, the invention encompasses a non-transitory computer-readable medium containing program code that, when executed by a processor, causes the processor to perform the aforementioned method steps. The program code functionality includes modules for flow detection, packet buffering, feature extraction from IP/TCP headers, service classification using machine learning models, and scheduler weight adjustment based on classification outcomes. The medium stores the necessary classifier models, including Random Forest decision trees trained on header-based features, along with configuration parameters that define the relationship between classification results and scheduling priorities. This comprehensive approach enables real-time, lightweight traffic classification that can be seamlessly integrated into existing base station implementations without requiring hardware modifications or significant computational overhead.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The computing system implementing the present invention operates within a network environment that facilitates communication between multiple server and client devices. The network infrastructure supports various communication protocols and enables the exchange of data packets between content providers and end-user devices. Server devices host streaming video content and other services, while client devices include smartphones, tablets, and other mobile terminals that consume these services. Communication between servers and clients occurs through indirect paths that may involve multiple network elements including base stations, routers, and gateways.

A view field-aware point cloud streaming service represents one example of the advanced multimedia applications that benefit from the invention's classification capabilities. The server components responsible for delivering such services include specialized processors and memory systems optimized for handling large-scale multimedia content. The server's communications interface manages connections with multiple client devices simultaneously, while input/output units handle various peripheral interactions. Client devices incorporate radio frequency transceivers connected to antennas for wireless communication, along with dedicated transmission and reception processing circuitry for signal modulation and demodulation. Audio interfaces include microphones and speakers for voice communication, while visual interfaces comprise touchscreens and displays for user interaction and content presentation.

The core innovation lies in the machine learning algorithms deployed at both server and client components. Machine learning classifiers implement passive classification by inspecting only UDP/IP or TCP/IP headers without requiring payload decryption or bidirectional flow analysis. This approach enables accurate service classification while maintaining compatibility with encrypted traffic and minimizing computational overhead. The classifiers specifically target streaming video applications, detecting player states such as buffering, steady-state, and depleting conditions, as well as determining video resolution levels ranging from 144p to 1080p.

Random Forest decision trees serve as the primary classification mechanism due to their superior performance characteristics compared to alternative machine learning approaches. The training process involves selecting relevant features from IP/TCP headers, including port numbers, packet counts with specific flags set, and median packet sizes. Classification performance evaluations demonstrate high precision and recall rates exceeding 95% for service classification and player state detection, with slightly lower but still effective accuracy for resolution classification. The feature selection process carefully balances classification accuracy against feature extraction overhead, ensuring that the solution remains practical for real-time deployment.

The system architecture integrates multiple specialized classifiers working in concert: a flow service classifier identifies streaming video traffic among other service types, a video player state classifier determines whether users are in buffering or steady-state conditions, and a video resolution classifier assesses the current quality level of video playback. These classification results feed into an experience-aware scheduler that modifies traditional proportional fair scheduling metrics by applying user-specific weights based on detected experience conditions. Unlike conventional schedulers that treat all best-effort traffic equally, the experience-aware scheduler prioritizes users identified as "unhappy" – those either waiting for playback to commence or experiencing sub-threshold video quality.

The passive inspection of IP packet headers represents a significant advantage over deep packet inspection methods, as it eliminates the need for payload decryption while maintaining high classification accuracy. This approach reduces computational overhead substantially, making the solution suitable for deployment on existing base station hardware without requiring specialized processing units. The detailed implementation includes specific configurations for base station components, with the flow service classifier operating on the first five downlink packets of newly detected flows. Video player state classification employs sampling intervals of 0.1 seconds with five samples to achieve 95% accuracy, while resolution classification uses longer sampling intervals of 1 second with fifteen samples to reach 90% accuracy. The multi-layer perceptron architecture was evaluated as an alternative but found to require significantly more computational resources without providing commensurate performance improvements. The experience-aware scheduler implements both strict priority and weighted proportional fair schemes, with simulation results demonstrating substantial improvements in video mean opinion scores and reduced playback delays while maintaining acceptable performance for non-video traffic classes.
