# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to genetically encoded death indicator (GEDI) polypeptides. These polypeptides are engineered biosensors that detect irreversible loss of calcium homeostasis in cells, thereby serving as early and specific markers of cell death. GEDI polypeptides are derived from modified genetically encoded calcium indicators (GECIs), particularly those originally designed for high-affinity or organelle-targeted calcium sensing, such as CEPIA or GCaMP variants. By re-engineering these indicators—specifically by removing subcellular localization signals and tuning calcium-binding affinity—the resulting GEDI polypeptides exhibit minimal response to physiological calcium transients but undergo a robust and sustained increase in fluorescence upon catastrophic calcium influx associated with plasma membrane permeabilization during cell death. The invention encompasses isolated nucleic acid sequences encoding GEDI polypeptides, expression vectors containing such sequences, host cells expressing GEDI constructs, transgenic non-human animals, and methods for using GEDI polypeptides to monitor cell death in vitro, in tissue, and in vivo. The GEDI platform enables high-throughput, longitudinal, single-cell resolution tracking of cell death across diverse biological contexts, including neurodegenerative disease models, drug screening assays, and developmental studies.

## BACKGROUND

Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) play a central role in cellular physiology, acting as critical second messengers in processes ranging from neurotransmission and muscle contraction to gene expression and apoptosis. In healthy cells, cytosolic Ca²⁺ concentrations are tightly regulated and maintained at nanomolar levels (~100 nM), while extracellular Ca²⁺ concentrations and those within intracellular stores such as the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and mitochondria are significantly higher—typically in the millimolar range. This steep electrochemical gradient is actively preserved by ATP-dependent pumps, exchangers, and channels. Disruption of this gradient, particularly a sustained and uncontrolled rise in cytosolic Ca²⁺ due to plasma membrane rupture or failure of homeostatic mechanisms, is a hallmark of irreversible cell injury and a point-of-no-return in the cell death cascade. Historically, detection of cell death has relied on exogenous dyes that intercalate into DNA upon membrane permeabilization (e.g., propidium iodide, ethidium homodimer), enzymatic assays (e.g., caspase activation), or morphological criteria. However, these methods suffer from significant limitations: dyes require repeated application, may be toxic, exhibit delayed signal onset, and cannot be used for long-term longitudinal imaging; enzymatic markers are pathway-specific and may reflect reversible processes; and morphological scoring is subjective, prone to human error, and often lags behind the actual biochemical commitment to death. Moreover, in complex tissues or in vivo settings, dye penetration is inconsistent, and the dynamic nature of degeneration makes precise temporal demarcation of death challenging. The need for a genetically encoded, non-toxic, pathway-agnostic, and acute reporter of the moment of irreversible cell death has thus remained unmet—until the discovery that certain calcium indicators, when appropriately engineered, can serve this precise function by responding exclusively to the pathological Ca²⁺ surge that defines cellular demise.

## SUMMARY

The invention provides methods for detecting cell death based on the use of genetically encoded death indicator (GEDI) polypeptides, which fluoresce in response to the loss of calcium homeostasis that occurs during irreversible cell death. Isolated nucleic acid sequences encoding GEDI polypeptides are disclosed, wherein said sequences are operably linked to expression control elements for transcription and translation in host cells. The invention further encompasses expression vectors comprising these nucleic acid sequences, as well as host cells—both prokaryotic and eukaryotic—that have been transformed or transfected with such vectors to express GEDI polypeptides. Methods for monitoring calcium flux are provided, wherein the GEDI polypeptide exhibits minimal fluorescence change in response to physiological calcium transients but undergoes a substantial and sustained increase in fluorescence upon exposure to pathological, millimolar-range calcium concentrations indicative of cell death. Correspondingly, methods for monitoring cell death comprise introducing a nucleic acid encoding a GEDI polypeptide into a cell, expressing the polypeptide, and detecting an increase in fluorescence intensity as a marker of cell death. The invention also includes methods for screening agents that modulate cell death, comprising contacting cells expressing a GEDI polypeptide with a test agent and measuring changes in GEDI fluorescence over time relative to untreated controls. Tissue-specific promoters, such as the human synapsin 1 (hSyn1) promoter for neurons or the mnx1 promoter for motor neurons, may be used to restrict GEDI expression to defined cell populations. Fluorescent labels, including but not limited to EGFP, mApple, mRuby, and TagBFP2, are employed as internal reference signals in pseudo-ratiometric constructs, typically linked via self-cleaving P2A peptides to enable normalization of GEDI signal. Distinguishable markers refer to spectrally separable fluorescent proteins that allow multiplexing with other biosensors or reporters. Modified calcium binding motifs, derived from calmodulin or troponin C and engineered for reduced calcium affinity (e.g., Kd in the micromolar to low millimolar range), are key functional domains of GEDI polypeptides. The invention is applicable to a wide range of cell types, including primary neurons, induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived neurons, HEK293 cells, and other mammalian and non-mammalian cells, as well as in whole organisms such as zebrafish.

### Vectors

Vectors are fundamental tools for delivering and expressing GEDI-encoding nucleic acids in target cells. Expression vectors suitable for the invention contain regulatory elements necessary for transcription and translation in the host cell, including promoters, enhancers, polyadenylation signals, and selectable markers. Mammalian expression vectors such as pcDNA3.1, pCMV, and pCAGGS are commonly used. Viral vectors, including adenoviral, adeno-associated viral (AAV), lentiviral, and retroviral vectors, offer high transduction efficiency and stable integration or episomal persistence. Retrovirus-based vectors, derived from murine leukemia virus (MLV), integrate into the host genome but only transduce dividing cells. Lentivirus-based vectors, derived from HIV-1, can transduce both dividing and non-dividing cells and are widely used for neuronal applications. Recombinant retroviruses are produced by co-transfecting packaging cell lines—such as HEK293T—with a transfer vector containing the GEDI expression cassette flanked by long terminal repeats (LTRs) and packaging plasmids encoding gag, pol, and env genes. Examples of packaging cell lines include Phoenix-AMPHO and GP2-293. Retroviral construct components include the Ψ packaging signal, rev-responsive element (RRE), and central polypurine tract (cPPT). Self-inactivating (SIN) lentiviral vectors, which have deletions in the 3' LTR U3 region to enhance safety, are preferred. Virus vector plasmids such as pLenti, pLVX, and pRRL are commercially available. Recombinant viruses are produced by transfecting packaging cells using methods like calcium phosphate precipitation, lipofection (e.g., Lipofectamine 2000), or electroporation. Non-viral transfection methods include nucleofection and polymer-based delivery. Expression control sequences include constitutive promoters (e.g., CMV, EF1α), inducible promoters (e.g., Tet-On), and tissue-specific promoters such as hSyn1 for neurons, mnx1 for motor neurons, or albumin for hepatocytes. Enhancers boost transcriptional activity. Detectable markers, such as fluorescent proteins or antibiotic resistance genes, facilitate selection and visualization. In the context of cell death detection, the integrity of the calcium gradient is paramount; GEDI polypeptides exploit the collapse of this gradient as a death signal. Fluorescent detectable signals from GEDI constructs are quantified using microscopy or plate readers. Luminescence or fluorescence assays measure emission intensity, with pseudo-ratiometric standardization achieved by co-expressing a reference fluorophore (e.g., EGFP) via a P2A peptide. Examples of fluorescent markers include EGFP, mCherry, mApple, and BFP variants. RGEDI-P2A-X embodiments consist of a red-shifted GEDI (RGEDI) linked to a reference fluorophore X (e.g., EGFP, 3xBFP) through a P2A self-cleaving peptide, enabling ratiometric quantification. Proteins conferring resistance, such as puromycin N-acetyltransferase, may be included for selection. The calcium binding motif in GEDI is derived from calmodulin or troponin C but is modified to reduce Ca²⁺ affinity. GCaMP variants, which fuse circularly permuted GFP to calmodulin-M13, serve as templates for GEDI engineering. Modified calcium binding motifs exhibit dissociation constants (Kd) in the micromolar to millimolar range, tuned to respond only to pathological Ca²⁺ levels. Sequence identity thresholds of ≥80%, ≥90%, or ≥95% define functional variants. EF-hand motifs are helix-loop-helix structural domains that bind Ca²⁺; troponin C contains multiple EF-hands and is used in some GEDI designs. Optimized signal refers to enhanced dynamic range and brightness. Reduced Ca²⁺ binding affinity ensures insensitivity to physiological transients. Modified calmodulin sequences contain mutations that lower Ca²⁺ affinity. Localization sequences, such as nuclear localization signals (NLS), can be added to target GEDI to specific compartments. GEDI constructs thus represent a versatile class of biosensors for acute cell death detection.

### Cells

Cells as used herein refer to any biological unit capable of expressing recombinant nucleic acids. Specific cell types include primary neurons (e.g., rat or mouse cortical neurons), iPSC-derived neurons (e.g., motor neurons), immortalized cell lines (e.g., HEK293, HeLa), and glial cells. Neural cells encompass neurons, astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, and microglia, with neurons being a primary focus due to their relevance in neurodegeneration. Cardiac cells, including cardiomyocytes, are also within the scope, as they rely heavily on calcium signaling and are susceptible to calcium-mediated death pathways.

### Animals

Animals include non-human vertebrates and invertebrates used as model systems. Animal types specifically contemplated are rodents (mice, rats), zebrafish, and Drosophila. Transgenic animals are generated by introducing GEDI-encoding constructs into the germline, resulting in heritable expression. Methods for making transgenic animals include pronuclear microinjection, embryonic stem (ES) cell manipulation, and CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knock-in. ES cells are pluripotent cells derived from blastocysts that can contribute to all tissues, including germ cells, in chimeric animals. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) are reprogrammed somatic cells that mimic ES cells and can be differentiated into various lineages for transplantation or in vitro modeling. Organotypic slice cultures are ex vivo preparations of brain or other tissues that maintain three-dimensional architecture; brain slice cultures, particularly hippocampal or cortical slices, are used to study neurodegeneration in a more physiologically relevant context. Regions of the brain for such cultures include the hippocampus, cortex, and striatum. Organotypic slice cultures are useful for longitudinal imaging of GEDI-expressing neurons in a tissue-like environment.

### Methods of Use

Methods for monitoring calcium flux involve expressing a GEDI polypeptide in a cell and measuring fluorescence changes in response to Ca²⁺. Unlike conventional GECIs, GEDI shows negligible response to action potential-driven Ca²⁺ transients but robustly fluoresces upon exposure to >10 µM Ca²⁺, mimicking extracellular or organellar concentrations. Methods for monitoring cell death comprise detecting a sustained increase in GEDI fluorescence, which correlates with plasma membrane permeabilization and irreversible commitment to death. Methods for screening agents involve treating GEDI-expressing cells with candidate compounds and quantifying changes in death kinetics or incidence. Optical means for these methods include epifluorescence, confocal, or two-photon microscopy. Automated time-lapse confocal microscopy enables high-throughput, longitudinal tracking of thousands of individual cells. Signal intensity ratios (e.g., RGEDI/EGFP) are used to classify cells as live or dead; a threshold ratio is empirically determined from control populations. Empirical determination involves comparing GEDI ratios in known live and dead cells (e.g., after NaN₃ treatment) and calculating a discriminant value, such as 25% of the difference between mean dead and live ratios added to the mean live ratio.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Genetically encoded calcium indicators (GECIs), such as GCaMP6f, are widely used to detect Ca²⁺ transients associated with neuronal activity. Surprisingly, it was discovered that certain GECIs, when repurposed, could serve as sensitive detectors of cell death. Specifically, rat primary cortical neurons expressing GCaMP6f exhibited strong fluorescence increases not only during electrical field stimulation (30 Hz for 3 seconds) but also upon treatment with the cytotoxin sodium azide (NaN₃), which induces rapid cell death. In contrast, a red-shifted GEDI variant (RGEDI), derived from an ER-targeted CEPIA sensor but lacking ER retention signals, showed no response to electrical stimulation yet robustly fluoresced upon NaN₃ exposure. This demonstrated that RGEDI is selectively responsive to the pathological Ca²⁺ influx of cell death, not physiological signaling. To enable quantitative, high-throughput analysis, a pseudo-ratiometric construct, RGEDI-P2A-EGFP, was developed, wherein RGEDI and EGFP are co-expressed via a self-cleaving P2A peptide. In live neurons, the overlay of red (RGEDI) and green (EGFP) channels appears yellow in the soma, with green extending into neurites. Upon death, RGEDI fluorescence surges throughout the cell, shifting the overlay to red-yellow. Longitudinal imaging revealed that the GEDI ratio increase often preceded overt morphological signs of degeneration. Decay rates of RGEDI and EGFP fluorescence post-death were equivalent, ensuring signal stability. GEDI outperformed traditional death markers like TUNEL or propidium iodide in sensitivity and temporal precision, establishing GEDIs as superior, non-toxic, genetically encoded alternatives for cell death detection.

### Example 2

While GCaMP6f-P2A-mRuby functioned as a GEDI, its green-red spectral overlap limited multiplexing with other green biosensors. To address this, novel GEDI variants were designed. RGEDI-P2A-3xBFP uses a blue fluorescent protein (3xBFP) as the reference, freeing the green channel for concurrent imaging of other reporters. Additionally, GC150—a GEDI based on the high-dynamic-range GCaMP6-150 scaffold but lacking ER targeting—was created. Both variants showed robust, death-specific fluorescence increases with kinetics comparable to RGEDI-P2A-EGFP. Predicted dissociation constants (Kd) for Ca²⁺ binding were in the tens to hundreds of micromolar range, ensuring insensitivity to physiological Ca²⁺ fluctuations (nanomolar to low micromolar) while responding to the millimolar Ca²⁺ surge of cell death. These engineered variants expand the utility of GEDIs across diverse experimental paradigms requiring spectral flexibility or enhanced signal fidelity.

### Example 3

GEDIs were validated as reliable in vitro cell death reporters in rat cortical primary neurons expressing RGEDI-P2A-EGFP. Automated microscopy tracked individual neurons over days, establishing baseline “live” GEDI ratios and elevated “dead” ratios after NaN₃ treatment. A GEDI threshold was computed to objectively classify cell fate. Representative images showed neurons transitioning from green-yellow to red-yellow overlays upon death. Time-lapse plots confirmed irreversible GEDI ratio increases preceding fluorescence loss. GEDI responded to diverse death triggers: KCl-induced excitotoxicity, expression of mutant huntingtin (HttEx1-Q97), and metabolic inhibition by NaN₃. Non-linear regression of GEDI signal increase yielded consistent kinetic parameters. Across neurodegenerative models—Huntington’s (HttEx1-Q97), Parkinson’s (α-synuclein), and ALS/FTD (TDP43)—GEDIs reliably reported death, enabling cumulative risk-of-death analyses that recapitulated known disease toxicities. These results confirm GEDIs as robust, quantitative reporters for longitudinal studies of neurodegeneration.

### Example 4

GEDIs were successfully deployed as in vivo and in tissue cell death reporters. In zebrafish larvae, co-injection of mnx1:Gal4, UAS:GCaMP6f, and UAS:Nitroreductase (NTR) enabled targeted motor neuron ablation upon metronidazole (MTZ) treatment. Under tricaine anesthesia (which blocks neuronal activity), GCaMP6f fluorescence increased specifically in dying motor neurons, functioning as a GEDI. In mammalian tissue, hippocampal slice cultures co-transfected with RGEDI-P2A-EGFP and HttEx1-Q97 showed clear GEDI ratio elevations in degenerating neurons. These experiments demonstrate that GEDIs are effective not only in dissociated cultures but also in intact neural circuits and living organisms, providing unprecedented access to real-time cell death dynamics in physiologically relevant contexts.

### Example 6

GEDIs were integrated with robotic microscopy for high-throughput applications. Neurons expressing RGEDI-P2A-EGFP were automatically classified as dead or alive based on GEDI signal thresholds. Survival analysis using automated GEDI scoring matched or exceeded manual curation in accuracy, with hazard ratios confirming expected toxicities (e.g., glutamate exposure). Statistical analysis revealed that manual scoring occasionally misclassified neurons that GEDI correctly identified as dying, highlighting GEDI’s superior objectivity. In drug screening, neurons exposed to glutamate showed dose-dependent death kinetics quantifiable via GEDI, enabling rapid assessment of neuroprotective compounds. These advantages—automation, scalability, and precision—establish GEDIs as indispensable tools for large-scale phenotypic screening and the identification of physiological determinants of cell survival.
