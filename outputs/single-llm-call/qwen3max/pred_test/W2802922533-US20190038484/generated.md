# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to wheelchairs equipped with inertial sensors for the purpose of detecting potentially destabilizing motion events and automatically providing neuromuscular stimulation to enhance trunk stability in individuals with impaired postural control, particularly those with spinal cord injuries. More specifically, the invention encompasses a wheelchair-integrated system comprising an inertial measurement unit, a controller, and neural stimulating electrodes configured to detect linear and angular motion parameters indicative of collisions or sharp turns and to deliver targeted functional neuromuscular stimulation to appropriate trunk and hip muscles in real time to mitigate postural instability and reduce the risk of falls. The invention further includes methods for monitoring wheelchair motion, evaluating motion signals against predefined thresholds, and activating muscle groups or user restraints in response to detected destabilizing events.

## BACKGROUND

Falls and tips represent the leading cause of injury among manual wheelchair users, particularly those living with spinal cord injuries (SCI). Approximately two-thirds of the over 100,000 annual wheelchair-related injuries treated in emergency departments in the United States are attributable to loss of balance resulting in falls. These incidents often lead to fractures, lacerations, contusions, and in severe cases, death. The rehabilitation burden following such injuries is substantial, typically requiring four to eight weeks of hospitalization and incurring treatment costs ranging from $25,000 to $75,000 per incident. A primary contributor to these falls is the lack of trunk stability experienced by individuals with SCI, who exhibit increased postural sway and are highly susceptible to minor disturbances that can disrupt their upright sitting posture. Common destabilizing events include collisions with obstacles such as curbs or walls, negotiation of sharp turns, traversal of uneven or inclined surfaces, and maneuvering over curb drops. Current strategies to address trunk instability—such as seat belts, specialized cushioning, increased seat dump, and lateral supports—impose significant functional limitations, restrict voluntary movement, contribute to pressure ulcers and skin tears, and suffer from high non-compliance rates due to interference with daily activities. While powered wheelchairs offer more advanced seating systems, they similarly constrain natural motion and are often perceived as markers of greater disability, discouraging transition from manual chairs. Functional neuromuscular stimulation (FNS) has demonstrated efficacy in restoring trunk extension moments and multidirectional stiffness, improving both biomechanical stability and psychological confidence during seated tasks. However, existing FNS applications have largely relied on static posture feedback or continuous stimulation, lacking the capability to dynamically respond to real-world, transient destabilizing events during active wheelchair propulsion. There remains a critical unmet need for a system that can automatically detect the onset of destabilizing motion in real time and deliver precisely timed, event-specific neuromuscular stimulation to preserve or restore stable seated posture without impeding voluntary function.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a wheelchair system integrated with neural stimulation capabilities designed to enhance trunk stability in response to detected destabilizing motion events. The system includes a motion sensor configured to measure at least one motion parameter of the wheelchair, wherein the motion parameter may be a linear motion parameter such as anterior-posterior acceleration or an angular motion parameter such as superior-inferior angular velocity. Neural stimulating electrodes are attached to selected muscles of the user, particularly trunk and hip extensors, flexors, and lateral stabilizers, to deliver functional neuromuscular stimulation upon command. A controller, which may be mounted directly on the wheelchair or worn by the user, receives signals from the motion sensor and processes these signals to evaluate whether a destabilizing event has occurred. The controller comprises a radio frequency receiver for communication with external components, a microprocessor for executing detection algorithms, and circuitry for activating the neural stimulating electrodes. In certain embodiments, the system further includes a seat belt controlled by a motor and a brake controlled by an actuator, both of which may be activated in response to detected events to provide mechanical restraint. A distress indicator may also be incorporated to alert caregivers or emergency services in the event of a fall. The invention also encompasses a method of providing neural stimulation to a wheelchair user, comprising the steps of measuring a motion parameter of the wheelchair, generating a signal indicative of that motion parameter, evaluating the signal against predefined threshold values, and activating at least one muscle in response to the evaluation. Muscles are selected based on the nature of the detected motion parameter—for instance, trunk extensors for forward collisions and lateral stabilizers for sharp turns. In a specific embodiment directed toward collision response, the method includes monitoring linear acceleration, calculating a moving root mean square of the acceleration signal, comparing this value against subject-specific threshold values, and applying neuromuscular stimulation if the threshold is exceeded. Additionally, the invention provides a method of applying user restraint, involving the measurement of wheelchair motion parameters, signal generation, evaluation, and activation of mechanical restraints such as motorized seat belts or brakes. Collectively, these components and methods constitute a comprehensive system for providing real-time assistance to wheelchair users experiencing destabilizing events, thereby enhancing safety, independence, and quality of life.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is embodied in a wheelchair system, designated generally as system 10, which integrates sensing, processing, and stimulation components to maintain or restore trunk stability during manual wheelchair use. Central to the system is a motion sensor 14, preferably an inertial measurement unit (IMU), mounted on a structural component of the wheelchair such as the rear cross bar. The motion sensor 14 is configured to continuously measure tri-axial linear acceleration and angular velocity, providing data on both linear motion parameters (e.g., anterior-posterior, medial-lateral, and superior-inferior acceleration) and angular motion parameters (e.g., pitch, yaw, and roll rates). In particular, anterior-posterior (AP) linear acceleration is used to detect collision events, while superior-inferior (SI) angular velocity is used to identify sharp turning maneuvers. The sensor 14 may incorporate a CMA3000-D01 accelerometer and an LSM330DLC gyroscope, sampling at frequencies of at least 100 Hz to ensure sufficient temporal resolution for real-time event detection. The system 10 further includes a controller 18, which houses a microprocessor 24 programmed with software algorithms to evaluate the signals received from the motion sensor 14. The software implements threshold-based detection logic, wherein subject-specific thresholds are established during calibration trials and stored in memory. When the incoming signal exceeds the predefined threshold—such as AP acceleration surpassing 3.5 g for collisions or SI angular velocity exceeding ±100 deg/s for turns—the controller 18 triggers an output signal. This output activates neural stimulating electrodes 26, which are either implanted intramuscularly or applied as surface electrodes to target muscles including the erector spinae, gluteus maximus, quadratus lumborum, hamstrings, posterior adductors, and iliopsoas. Electrode attachment methods may include percutaneous leads for implanted systems or adhesive pads for surface stimulation, ensuring reliable electrical contact and selective muscle recruitment. The primary goal of system 10 is to minimize postural displacement during destabilizing events and accelerate the return to an erect, stable seated posture through timely, event-appropriate neuromuscular activation. In the event of a collision, the system detects the abrupt deceleration via elevated AP acceleration, and the controller 18 activates trunk and hip extensor muscles to resist forward trunk flexion and assist in postural recovery. For sharp turns, the system identifies the rotational motion via SI angular velocity; left turns produce a positive peak, while right turns yield a negative peak, enabling directional discrimination. In response, the system stimulates the quadratus lumborum on the inside of the turn and the contralateral hip extensor to counteract lateral trunk displacement caused by centrifugal forces. The method of providing neural stimulation involves measuring the wheelchair’s motion parameter, generating a corresponding electrical signal, evaluating that signal against calibrated thresholds, and activating at least one muscle group in real time. Collision event evaluation relies on AP acceleration magnitude, while turning event evaluation depends on the sign and magnitude of SI angular velocity. Referring to FIG. 4, the collision detection algorithm employs a moving root mean square calculation to smooth transient noise and compares the result against a subject-specific threshold derived from calibration trials (mean peak minus two standard deviations). Similarly, FIG. 5 illustrates the method of providing user restraint, which follows an analogous sequence: measure motion parameter, generate signal, evaluate against threshold, and activate a mechanical restraint such as a motorized seat belt or electromagnetic brake. An alternative embodiment, system 66, incorporates additional sensors 74 for enhanced environmental awareness and implements a refined method of providing assistance to user 70. This method includes not only the application of neural stimulation but also its timely removal once stable posture is restored, preventing unnecessary muscle fatigue. Furthermore, the system employs an algorithm for detecting push completion and recovery phases of the manual propulsion cycle, ensuring that stimulation is synchronized with the user’s natural movement patterns and does not interfere with voluntary propulsion efforts. Through these integrated mechanisms, the invention provides a responsive, adaptive, and user-centered approach to maintaining trunk stability in manual wheelchair users with impaired postural control.
