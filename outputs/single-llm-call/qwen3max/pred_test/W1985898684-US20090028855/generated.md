# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The serine/threonine kinase Akt, also known as protein kinase B (PKB), is a central node in the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)/Akt/mTOR signaling cascade, which governs critical cellular processes including proliferation, survival, metabolism, and growth. Akt exists as three highly homologous isoforms—Akt1 (PKBα), Akt2 (PKBβ), and Akt3 (PKBγ)—each encoded by distinct genes but sharing conserved structural domains: an N-terminal pleckstrin homology (PH) domain, a central kinase domain, and a C-terminal regulatory domain. Activation of Akt occurs through a well-characterized two-step phosphorylation mechanism initiated by extracellular stimuli such as growth factors or cytokines. Upon ligand binding to receptor tyrosine kinases, PI3K is recruited and activated, leading to the generation of phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate (PIP3) at the plasma membrane. The PH domain of Akt binds PIP3 with high affinity, facilitating Akt translocation from the cytosol to the plasma membrane. Once localized at the membrane, Akt undergoes phosphorylation at two critical residues: threonine 308 (Thr308) in the activation loop of the kinase domain and serine 473 (Ser473) in the hydrophobic motif of the C-terminal regulatory region. Phosphorylation at Thr308 is primarily mediated by phosphoinositide-dependent kinase 1 (PDK1), while Ser473 phosphorylation is catalyzed by the mammalian target of rapamycin complex 2 (mTORC2). Although other kinases such as DNA-PK and integrin-linked kinase (ILK) have been proposed as candidate Ser473 kinases under specific contexts, mTORC2 remains the predominant physiological regulator.

The tumor suppressor phosphatase and tensin homolog (PTEN) acts as a critical negative regulator of Akt signaling by dephosphorylating PIP3 to phosphatidylinositol (4,5)-bisphosphate (PIP2), thereby reducing membrane PIP3 levels and preventing Akt recruitment and activation. Loss-of-function mutations or epigenetic silencing of PTEN are among the most frequent genetic alterations in human cancers, resulting in constitutive Akt activation. Beyond PTEN loss, Akt hyperactivation arises from activating mutations in PI3K, amplification of Akt isoforms, or gain-of-function mutations within Akt itself, such as the oncogenic E17K substitution in AKT1, which enhances PH domain affinity for PIP3 and promotes aberrant membrane localization. Once activated, Akt phosphorylates a wide array of downstream substrates—including glycogen synthase kinase 3β (GSK3β), forkhead box O (FOXO) transcription factors, BAD, tuberous sclerosis complex 2 (TSC2), and MDM2—to promote cell survival, inhibit apoptosis, stimulate protein synthesis, and drive cell cycle progression. Consequently, dysregulated Akt signaling is implicated in the pathogenesis, progression, and therapeutic resistance of a broad spectrum of malignancies, including breast, ovarian, prostate, pancreatic, and glioblastoma cancers, with aberrant pathway activation observed in nearly fifty percent of all human tumors.

Given its pivotal role in oncogenesis, Akt has emerged as a compelling therapeutic target. Several classes of Akt inhibitors have been developed, including lipid-based PH domain antagonists like perifosine, ATP-competitive kinase inhibitors, and allosteric inhibitors that stabilize inactive conformations. However, many existing agents suffer from limited selectivity, poor pharmacokinetic properties, or insufficient in vivo efficacy. Perifosine, for instance, demonstrated unacceptable toxicity in clinical trials without clear evidence of target modulation. Thus, there remains a significant unmet need for potent, selective, and clinically viable Akt inhibitors capable of effectively suppressing tumor growth with minimal off-target effects.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compounds and pharmaceutical compositions comprising a small molecule designated API-1 (Akt/PKB Inhibitor-1), which functions as a selective inhibitor of the Akt signaling pathway. API-1, chemically identified as pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidine (NSC 177223), exhibits a unique mechanism of action by directly binding to the pleckstrin homology (PH) domain of Akt proteins, thereby preventing their translocation to the plasma membrane and subsequent phosphorylation at both Thr308 and Ser473. This compound demonstrates potent inhibitory activity against all three Akt isoforms—Akt1, Akt2, and Akt3—and effectively suppresses the kinase activity and phosphorylation of both wild-type and constitutively active Akt mutants, including the clinically relevant AKT1-E17K variant. The invention further encompasses methods for inhibiting the survival or proliferation of cancer cells by administering API-1 or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, hydrate, or analog thereof. Additionally, the invention provides methods for treating oncological disorders characterized by hyperactivation of the Akt pathway, particularly those driven by PTEN loss, PI3K mutations, or Akt amplification. API-1 exhibits preferential cytotoxicity toward cancer cells with elevated Akt activity, inducing apoptosis and growth arrest while sparing cells with low basal Akt signaling. In preclinical models, API-1 significantly inhibits tumor growth in xenografts derived from Akt-hyperactivated human cancers without causing systemic toxicity, underscoring its potential as a targeted anti-cancer agent. The selectivity, efficacy, and favorable safety profile of API-1 position it as a promising candidate for the development of precision therapeutics for Akt-driven malignancies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to compounds and pharmaceutical compositions useful for inhibiting the Akt/PKB signaling pathway and treating disorders associated with its dysregulation, particularly cancer. The core compound of the invention is API-1, a pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidine derivative with the chemical name and structure corresponding to NSC 177223. More broadly, the invention includes analogs, derivatives, stereoisomers, hydrates, and pharmaceutically acceptable salts of API-1 that retain the ability to bind the PH domain of Akt and inhibit its membrane translocation and activation. The general structure of these compounds comprises a fused bicyclic heteroaromatic system with specific substitutions that confer high affinity for the Akt PH domain. Specific embodiments include API-1 itself, as well as structurally related molecules wherein alkyl groups (e.g., methyl, ethyl, propyl, butyl, including both saturated and unsaturated variants such as vinyl or allyl), alkoxy groups (e.g., methoxy, ethoxy), or halogen atoms (fluoro, chloro, bromo, iodo) are introduced at defined positions to modulate potency, solubility, or metabolic stability. The term “optionally substituted” refers to moieties that may bear one or more substituents selected from lower alkyl, halo, hydroxy, amino, nitro, cyano, carboxy, or ester groups, provided that biological activity is preserved.

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise an effective amount of API-1 or a derivative thereof in combination with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, diluents, or excipients. These compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration, including oral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, subcutaneous, intramuscular, or topical delivery. Oral formulations may include tablets, troches, pills, or capsules containing inert solid carriers such as lactose, sucrose, starch, or cellulose, along with binders, disintegrants, lubricants, and glidants. Liquid formulations may employ aqueous or non-aqueous vehicles such as water, ethanol, glycerol, or oils, optionally supplemented with suspending agents, emulsifiers, preservatives, or buffering agents. Sterile injectable solutions can be prepared by dissolving the compound in a suitable solvent (e.g., saline, dextrose solution) followed by filtration sterilization; alternatively, sterile powders for reconstitution may be produced by lyophilization. Topical preparations may utilize ointments, creams, or gels incorporating solid or liquid carriers like petrolatum, polyethylene glycol, or mineral oil, often enhanced with permeation enhancers or thickeners such as carbomers. Adjuvants may be included to improve stability or bioavailability.

Therapeutically, the compounds of the invention exhibit potent anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects selectively in cells exhibiting hyperactivated Akt signaling. Methods of treatment involve administering a therapeutically effective dose—typically ranging from 0.1 to 100 mg/kg body weight per day, depending on the route and formulation—to a patient diagnosed with an oncological disorder driven by Akt pathway dysregulation. Such disorders include, but are not limited to, carcinomas of the breast, ovary, prostate, pancreas, lung, and brain, as well as hematologic malignancies. Combination therapy with conventional chemotherapeutic agents (e.g., cisplatin, paclitaxel, doxorubicin) or immunotherapeutic agents (e.g., checkpoint inhibitors) is also envisioned to enhance efficacy. The invention further extends to veterinary applications, benefiting domesticated animals (e.g., dogs, cats), farm animals (e.g., cattle, pigs, poultry), and exotic species suffering from Akt-associated neoplasms. Diagnostic and research kits may be provided, comprising the compound in a sealed container accompanied by instructions for use in in vitro assays to assess Akt inhibition or in vivo tumor models. As used herein, “patient” or “subject” refers to any mammalian species, including humans, amenable to therapeutic intervention.

### MATERIALS AND METHODS

The identification of API-1 originated from a high-throughput screen of the NCI/DTP Open Chemical Repository, which houses a diverse library of 2,300 compounds. The screening strategy employed AKT2-transformed NIH3T3 fibroblasts alongside LXSN vector-transfected control cells plated in 96-well plates. Compounds were tested at a concentration of 5 μM, and cell viability was assessed using the CellTiter 96® AQueous One Solution Cell Proliferation Assay. Agents that selectively inhibited the growth of Akt-transformed cells without affecting control cells were prioritized as candidate Akt inhibitors. Subsequent validation involved a battery of in vitro assays: recombinant Akt kinase activity was measured using purified enzyme and substrate peptides; cell survival was quantified via metabolic activity assays; and apoptosis was detected by annexin V staining followed by flow cytometry. The antitumor efficacy of lead compounds was evaluated in an in vivo nude mouse xenograft model. Human cancer cells (e.g., OVCAR3, PANC-1) were injected subcutaneously into flanks of 8-week-old female athymic nude mice. Once tumors reached approximately 100 mm³, mice were randomized into treatment groups receiving either vehicle (20% DMSO in saline) or API-1 (10 mg/kg/day) via daily intraperitoneal injection. Tumor volumes were monitored over time, and harvested tissues were analyzed for phospho-Akt levels by immunoblotting to confirm target engagement.

### EXAMPLE 1

The development of small molecule Akt inhibitors has been motivated by the high prevalence of Akt pathway activation in human cancers and the therapeutic potential of its suppression. To discover novel inhibitors, we screened the NCI Diversity Set against AKT2-transformed NIH3T3 cells, identifying API-1 as a hit that selectively impaired transformed cell growth. Further characterization revealed that API-1 potently inhibits Akt kinase activity and reduces phosphorylation at both Thr308 and Ser473 in multiple cancer cell lines, with an IC₅₀ of approximately 0.8 μM for pAkt-Ser473. Crucially, API-1 did not directly inhibit recombinant Akt kinase activity in cell-free assays at concentrations effective in cells, indicating an indirect mechanism. Binding studies using API-1 immobilized on Sepharose beads confirmed direct interaction with recombinant Akt1 protein. Collectively, these data establish API-1 as a selective, cell-permeable Akt pathway inhibitor that functions through a non-ATP-competitive mechanism.

### EXAMPLE 2

To determine whether API-1 exerts its effects by targeting upstream activators of Akt, we investigated its impact on PI3K and PDK1. In an in vitro PI3K lipid kinase assay, API-1 failed to inhibit EGF-stimulated PI3K activity, unlike the known inhibitor wortmannin. Similarly, API-1 showed no effect on PDK1 kinase activity in a cell-free system using SGK as a substrate, whereas the PDK1 inhibitor UCN-01 abolished activity. Moreover, API-1 treatment did not reduce autophosphorylation of PDK1 at Ser241 in OVCAR3 cells, a marker of PDK1 activation. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments further demonstrated that API-1 does not disrupt the integrity of mTORC1 or mTORC2 complexes. These findings confirm that API-1 acts downstream of PI3K, PDK1, and mTOR complexes, directly targeting Akt itself.

### EXAMPLE 3

Selectivity profiling revealed that API-1 does not significantly inhibit other AGC kinase family members. In vitro kinase assays showed no reduction in the activity of PKA, PKC, or SGK upon API-1 treatment, even at high concentrations, whereas positive control inhibitors effectively suppressed their respective kinases. Immunoblotting of OVCAR3 cells treated with API-1 confirmed unchanged phosphorylation levels of PKA and PKC activation loop residues. Furthermore, API-1 did not alter the phosphorylation status of key nodes in alternative oncogenic pathways, including ERK1/2, JNK, p38 MAPK, or STAT3. This high degree of specificity underscores API-1’s value as a targeted therapeutic with minimal off-target kinase effects.

### EXAMPLE 4

API-1 was tested against constitutively active Akt mutants, including myristoylated Akt1 (myr-Akt1), Akt1-E40K, myr-AKT2, and the naturally occurring oncogenic mutant AKT1-E17K. Remarkably, API-1 inhibited the kinase activity and phosphorylation of all four forms, despite their membrane-localized, activation-independent nature. This suggests that API-1 interferes with Akt function beyond simple blockade of membrane recruitment, possibly by inducing conformational changes or disrupting protein-protein interactions essential for activity. API-1 also suppressed phosphorylation of canonical Akt substrates such as GSK3β, FOXO3a, and Bad in OVCAR3 and H661 cells, confirming functional inhibition of downstream signaling.

### EXAMPLE 5

The biological consequences of API-1 treatment were assessed in a panel of human cancer cell lines stratified by Akt activation status. In cells with high basal pAkt (e.g., OVCAR3, MDA-MB-468, MCF7), API-1 induced robust PARP cleavage, inhibited proliferation by 50–70%, and triggered 4- to 9-fold increases in apoptosis. In contrast, cells with low Akt activity (e.g., OVCAR5, MDA-MB-435s) exhibited minimal responses. Similar selectivity was observed in Akt1-knockout mouse embryonic fibroblasts reconstituted with either wild-type Akt1 or AKT1-E17K, where the mutant conferred heightened sensitivity to API-1-induced apoptosis. These results demonstrate that API-1 preferentially kills cancer cells dependent on Akt signaling.

### EXAMPLE 6

In vivo efficacy was evaluated using a bilateral xenograft model in nude mice, with Akt-hyperactivated tumors (OVCAR3, PANC-1) implanted on one flank and low-Akt tumors (OVCAR5, COLO357) on the contralateral flank. Daily intraperitoneal administration of API-1 (10 mg/kg) for 21 days resulted in 50–70% growth inhibition of OVCAR3 and PANC-1 tumors, while OVCAR5 and COLO357 tumors were unaffected. Immunohistochemical analysis of excised tumors confirmed a 70% reduction in pAkt levels without changes in total Akt, verifying on-target activity. No adverse effects on body weight, glucose homeostasis, or behavior were observed, supporting a favorable therapeutic index. These data validate API-1 as a selective in vivo inhibitor of Akt-driven tumor growth.
