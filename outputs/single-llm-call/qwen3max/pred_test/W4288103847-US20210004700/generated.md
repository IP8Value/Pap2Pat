# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

### Field of the Disclosure

The present disclosure relates generally to the field of machine learning, and more specifically to systems and methods for active learning in deep neural networks for natural language processing tasks. The invention provides a novel framework for constructing compact, representative training datasets through uncertainty-based query strategies applied with deep text classification models, thereby enabling efficient model training while mitigating sampling bias and preserving classification accuracy.

### Related Art

Active learning has emerged as a critical methodology in machine learning to address the prohibitive costs associated with labeling large datasets required for training deep neural networks. Traditional active learning approaches in natural language processing have relied on uncertainty-based query strategies using classical models such as support vector machines or naive Bayes classifiers. However, these approaches often suffer from significant limitations when applied to modern deep learning contexts. Current methods frequently exhibit sampling bias, particularly in label distribution and feature space representation, which can compromise the representativeness of the acquired training samples. Additionally, many existing approaches require computationally expensive diversity measures or ensemble methods to mitigate redundancy in selected samples, substantially increasing computational overhead without guaranteed improvements in sample quality. The transition from shallow to deep models has introduced new complexities, as properties established for classical active learning do not necessarily translate to deep neural network architectures. Recent studies have produced conflicting results regarding the efficacy of uncertainty-based versus diversity-based query strategies in deep learning contexts, with some suggesting that uncertainty methods perform no better than random sampling while others demonstrate superior performance with ensemble approaches. This lack of consensus, combined with the absence of comprehensive empirical validation on large-scale datasets, has created significant uncertainty regarding optimal active learning methodologies for deep text classification. Furthermore, existing approaches have not adequately addressed the robustness of acquired samples to algorithmic factors such as initial dataset selection, query size variations, and specific query strategy implementations, leaving practitioners without reliable guidelines for deploying active learning in real-world applications.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces a novel query strategy based on posterior uncertainty derived from deep text classification models, specifically utilizing entropy-based acquisition functions applied to single-model architectures rather than computationally expensive ensemble methods. The invention provides a systematic approach for generating training datasets through active learning that achieves remarkable compression ratios while maintaining classification accuracy comparable to models trained on complete datasets. The disclosed method evaluates the efficiency and bias characteristics of actively acquired sample datasets, demonstrating that uncertainty-based sampling with deep models exhibits negligible class bias and favorable bias toward class boundary regions, as evidenced by high overlap with support vectors from independent classification algorithms. The invention trains deep neural networks using these compact, actively acquired datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance with significantly reduced computational requirements and training time. Through extensive empirical validation across eight large-scale text classification datasets containing between 120,000 and 3.6 million samples, the invention establishes that single deep models can produce sample sets comparable to more expensive ensemble approaches while demonstrating robustness to various algorithmic factors including initial set selection, query size variations, and specific uncertainty measurement methodologies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention introduces a comprehensive machine learning system designed to address the challenges of efficient dataset construction for deep neural network training through active learning methodologies. The system leverages the unique properties of deep text classification models to generate compact, representative training datasets that maintain the essential characteristics of the original data while dramatically reducing computational requirements. The motivation for addressing sampling bias and efficiency stems from the practical constraints faced in real-world machine learning applications, where the cost and time required to label massive datasets presents a significant barrier to model development and deployment. Traditional active learning approaches have struggled to balance the competing objectives of sample representativeness, computational efficiency, and robustness to algorithmic variations, often requiring expensive diversity measures or ensemble methods that undermine the very efficiency gains sought through active learning.

The system architecture comprises several interconnected components working in concert to implement the active learning framework. At its core, the system utilizes a pool of unlabeled data from which queries are iteratively selected, labeled by an oracle, and incorporated into a growing training set. The architecture supports multiple deep learning models, with particular emphasis on FastText.zip (FTZ) as a representative deep text classification model that provides competitive performance with exceptional computational efficiency. The system also incorporates traditional models such as Multinomial Naive Bayes (MNB) with TF-IDF features for comparative analysis and baseline establishment.

System components include modules for data management, model training, uncertainty computation, query selection, and performance evaluation. The data management component handles the organization and partitioning of datasets into pool and training sets, while the model training component implements the iterative training process on incrementally constructed training sets. The uncertainty computation module calculates various uncertainty measures from model outputs, and the query selection component implements different acquisition functions to identify the most informative samples for labeling. The performance evaluation component monitors classification accuracy, sampling bias metrics, and computational efficiency throughout the active learning process.

Acquisition function generation represents a critical aspect of the system's operation, with the invention focusing on uncertainty-based approaches that score samples according to their predicted informativeness. The system implements multiple query strategies based on different uncertainty measures, including Least Confidence (LC) and Entropy (Ent) calculations derived from model output probabilities. These strategies operate by identifying samples for which the model exhibits the highest uncertainty in its predictions, under the assumption that such samples will provide the most valuable information for improving model performance.

Uncertainty-based query strategies form the foundation of the invention's approach to active learning, leveraging the inherent uncertainty estimates produced by deep learning models during classification tasks. The system implements specific uncertainty measures including Least Confidence, which selects samples with the lowest maximum predicted probability across all classes, and Entropy, which chooses samples with the highest entropy in their predicted probability distributions. These strategies operate on the principle that samples causing the greatest model uncertainty are likely to be located near class boundaries and thus provide maximal information gain when labeled and incorporated into the training set.

Ensemble models have traditionally been employed in active learning to improve uncertainty estimation through committee-based approaches, but the present invention demonstrates that such expensive methodologies may be unnecessary when working with deep models like FTZ. The system supports both single-model and ensemble-based query strategies, enabling direct comparison of their effectiveness and computational requirements. Experimental results reveal that single deep models can produce sample sets with comparable quality to ensemble approaches, challenging conventional wisdom in the active learning literature.

FTZ ensembles represent one implementation of the ensemble approach supported by the system, utilizing multiple independently trained FastText.zip models to compute aggregated uncertainty measures. However, the invention's empirical findings indicate that the additional computational cost of maintaining and querying multiple models provides minimal improvement in sample quality compared to single-model approaches, suggesting that the inherent properties of deep models like FTZ already capture sufficient uncertainty information for effective active learning.

The data sampling process follows a systematic iterative procedure wherein the system begins with a small randomly selected initial dataset, trains a model on this initial set, computes uncertainty measures for all samples in the unlabeled pool, selects the most uncertain samples according to the chosen query strategy, acquires labels for these samples from an oracle, and incorporates them into the training set. This process repeats for a predetermined number of iterations or until a target training set size is reached, resulting in a final actively acquired dataset that serves as a compact surrogate for the complete original dataset.

The data labeling process involves interaction with an oracle, which may be a human annotator, automated labeling system, or pre-existing ground truth labels in experimental settings. The system is designed to minimize the number of labeling queries required while maximizing the information gained from each labeled sample, thereby optimizing the trade-off between labeling cost and model performance.

Training dataset generation proceeds through the accumulation of actively selected samples across multiple query iterations, with the system maintaining careful records of sample provenance and selection criteria. The resulting datasets are designed to be representative surrogates of the original complete datasets, capturing the essential characteristics necessary for effective model training while achieving compression ratios of 5x to 40x compared to the original data volumes.

Dataset notation within the system follows established conventions from the active learning literature, with datasets denoted as collections of input-label pairs and subsets identified according to their acquisition method and iteration history. The notation facilitates precise tracking of sample origins and enables detailed analysis of sampling bias and efficiency characteristics.

The training process implements standard deep learning methodologies adapted for the incremental nature of actively acquired datasets. Models are retrained at each iteration using the expanded training set, with hyperparameters carefully controlled to ensure consistent performance evaluation across different acquisition strategies and dataset sizes.

Deep neural network (DNN) and traditional network models are both supported within the system architecture, enabling comprehensive comparison of active learning performance across different model families. The invention demonstrates that deep models like FTZ exhibit fundamentally different sampling properties compared to traditional models like MNB, with deep models showing greater robustness to algorithmic variations and reduced susceptibility to sampling bias.

FTZ and MNB models serve as representative examples of deep and traditional approaches respectively, with FTZ providing state-of-the-art text classification performance with exceptional computational efficiency, and MNB serving as a well-established baseline with known characteristics in the active learning literature. The system's comparative analysis reveals significant differences in how these models respond to uncertainty-based query strategies, with FTZ demonstrating superior stability and representativeness in acquired samples.

Efficiency and bias evaluation of sample datasets constitutes a central analytical component of the system, employing multiple metrics to assess both the computational advantages and representational fidelity of actively acquired datasets. The evaluation includes measurements of class bias through label entropy calculations, feature bias through comparison with support vectors from independent classifiers, and stability through intersection analysis of samples acquired under different algorithmic conditions.

Training, testing, and results analysis follows rigorous experimental protocols designed to isolate the effects of individual algorithmic factors while controlling for confounding variables. The system conducts thousands of experiments across multiple datasets, random seeds, and parameter configurations to establish statistically significant conclusions about active learning performance characteristics.

Active learning training datasets and models are systematically compared across different acquisition strategies, with the invention establishing FTZ-Ent (FastText with entropy-based query strategy) as a new state-of-the-art baseline for deep active text classification. This baseline outperforms previous approaches including Bayesian active learning and diversity-based coreset sampling while requiring significantly less training data.
