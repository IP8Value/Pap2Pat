# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of bacterial capsular saccharides, particularly those derived from *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup X. More specifically, the invention relates to conjugates comprising serogroup X capsular polysaccharides chemically linked to carrier molecules, methods for their preparation, pharmaceutical compositions containing such conjugates, and their use in the prevention and treatment of meningococcal disease caused by serogroup X. The technical domain encompasses carbohydrate chemistry, vaccine development, immunology, and pharmaceutical formulation science, with a focus on enhancing the stability, immunogenicity, and manufacturability of glycoconjugate vaccines targeting encapsulated bacterial pathogens.

## BACKGROUND ART

Capsular saccharides have long served as critical components in bacterial vaccines due to their surface exposure and role in pathogenicity. Early polysaccharide-based vaccines, while effective in adults, exhibited poor immunogenicity in infants and young children, failed to induce immunological memory, and did not elicit T cell-dependent immune responses. To overcome these limitations, conjugation of capsular polysaccharides to immunogenic carrier proteins was developed, transforming T cell-independent antigens into T cell-dependent ones. This innovation led to the successful introduction of conjugate vaccines, such as the *Haemophilus influenzae* type b (Hib) vaccine, which dramatically reduced invasive Hib disease worldwide. The Hib conjugate vaccine demonstrated that covalent linkage of the polyribosylribitol phosphate (PRP) capsular polysaccharide to proteins like tetanus toxoid or CRM197 conferred robust, long-lasting immunity even in early infancy.

*Neisseria meningitidis*, a leading cause of bacterial meningitis and septicemia, is classified into multiple serogroups based on capsular polysaccharide structure. Serogroups A, B, C, W, Y, and X account for over 90% of global cases. Conjugate vaccines against serogroups A, C, W, and Y are now widely used, often in tetravalent formulations. However, serogroup X remains a significant public health concern, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, where outbreaks have been reported but no licensed vaccine exists. Unlike other meningococcal serogroups, serogroup X expresses a unique capsular polysaccharide composed of repeating units of →4)-α-D-GlcNAc-(1→4)-α-D-GlcNAc3OAc-(1→3)-β-D-Galp-(1→, with partial O-acetylation at the C3 position of the second GlcNAc residue. This structural complexity presents both an opportunity and a challenge for vaccine design.

Polysaccharide vaccines alone suffer from inherent limitations: they are poorly immunogenic in high-risk populations, do not boost upon revaccination, and offer short-lived protection. Conjugate vaccines address these issues but introduce new complexities in manufacturing, stability, and formulation. While conjugate vaccines against serogroup C have proven highly effective, the development of a serogroup X conjugate vaccine has been hindered by insufficient understanding of optimal conjugation chemistries, stability profiles, and formulation requirements. Moreover, there is a pressing need for aqueous, liquid-stable formulations that eliminate the necessity for lyophilization and reconstitution, thereby improving vaccine accessibility in resource-limited settings where cold chain logistics are challenging. The intrinsic instability of certain capsular polysaccharides, particularly those containing labile linkages, further complicates the development of shelf-stable liquid formulations. Thus, there remains an unmet medical need for a stable, immunogenic, and easily deployable conjugate vaccine against *N. meningitidis* serogroup X.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel conjugates of *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup X capsular polysaccharide with carrier molecules, exhibiting enhanced immunogenicity and stability suitable for inclusion in liquid vaccine formulations. These conjugates elicit robust T cell-dependent immune responses, including functional bactericidal antibodies, and demonstrate superior physicochemical stability compared to native polysaccharides. The invention further encompasses combination vaccines wherein the serogroup X conjugate is co-formulated with other meningococcal or bacterial antigens, enabling broad-spectrum protection.

A first aspect of the invention is a conjugate comprising a serogroup X capsular polysaccharide covalently linked to a carrier molecule. The serogroup X polysaccharide may be native, fragmented, or chemically modified to facilitate conjugation while preserving key immunodominant epitopes. The carrier molecule is preferably a protein capable of eliciting T cell help, such as CRM197, tetanus toxoid, or diphtheria toxoid.

A second aspect of the invention relates to processes for preparing said conjugates. In a first embodiment, the process involves oxidation of primary hydroxyl groups on the polysaccharide—preferably via TEMPO-mediated oxidation—to generate aldehyde functionalities, followed by reductive amination with lysine residues on the carrier protein. This method offers controlled, site-specific conjugation with minimal polysaccharide degradation. In a second embodiment, reductive amination is employed directly on reducing ends of the polysaccharide, optionally after controlled depolymerization. This approach leverages the natural reducing terminus for coupling, avoiding chemical modification of internal sugar residues. A third embodiment involves reduction of the polysaccharide with sodium borohydride to cap reducing ends, followed by oxidative cleavage with periodate to generate dialdehydes at vicinal diol sites, and subsequent reductive amination. This strategy enables conjugation at internal positions, potentially increasing epitope density. A fourth embodiment utilizes direct coupling via bifunctional linkers, such as adipic acid dihydrazide or di-N-hydroxysuccinimidyl adipate (SIDEA), which react with carboxyl or amine groups introduced on the polysaccharide.

Each embodiment offers distinct advantages: the first provides high specificity and stability; the second preserves native structure; the third allows internal labeling; and the fourth enables modular, high-yield conjugation. The resulting conjugates exhibit polysaccharide-to-protein molar ratios typically between 5:1 and 25:1, optimized for immunogenicity and minimal free carrier content.

A third aspect is a pharmaceutical composition comprising the serogroup X conjugate, optionally combined with other antigens—such as conjugates of serogroups A, C, W, Y, or other bacterial polysaccharides—and formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, buffers, and adjuvants. Preferred adjuvants include aluminum salts (e.g., aluminum phosphate), oil-in-water emulsions (e.g., squalene-based MF59), saponins (e.g., QS-21), or combinations thereof. The composition may be presented as a liquid ready-for-use formulation or as a lyophilized powder with a separate diluent, though the invention particularly emphasizes aqueous stability to enable liquid storage.

Additional aspects include intermediates useful in conjugate synthesis, such as oxidized or linker-functionalized polysaccharides, and methods of use for preventing or treating meningococcal disease. The invention also provides a method for assaying serogroup X capsular polysaccharide content in samples (e.g., vaccines, fermentation broths) by acid hydrolysis followed by HPAEC-PAD detection of glucosamine-4-phosphate, using synthetic N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate as a quantitative standard.

Carrier molecules include CRM197, tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, outer membrane proteins, or novel polypeptides such as spr0096 (SEQ ID NO:1) or spr2021 (SEQ ID NO:3), which contain CD4+ T cell epitopes. Hybrid carriers combining domains from multiple proteins are also contemplated, linked via flexible peptide sequences (e.g., GGGGS).

Conjugation chemistries are detailed: TEMPO oxidation selectively targets C6-OH of terminal GlcNAc; reductive amination uses sodium cyanoborohydride at pH 7–8; direct coupling employs NHS-ester linkers. Bifunctional linkers may be homobifunctional (e.g., disuccinimidyl suberate) or heterobifunctional (e.g., SMCC). The invention further describes purification methods (e.g., size-exclusion chromatography, ammonium sulfate precipitation) to remove unconjugated polysaccharide and free carrier.

Pharmaceutical compositions are buffered (e.g., phosphate, histidine) at pH 5.5–7.5, isotonic, and may contain stabilizers (e.g., sucrose, trehalose) for lyophilization. Dosage volumes are typically 0.5 mL, with antigen concentrations adjusted to deliver immunologically effective amounts (e.g., 1–10 µg polysaccharide per dose).

The invention concludes with comprehensive disclosure of all embodiments, emphasizing the unexpected stability and immunogenicity of serogroup X conjugates prepared by the disclosed methods, enabling effective, liquid-stable meningococcal vaccines.

### Particular Embodiments of the Invention

The invention specifically defines a conjugate of *Neisseria meningitidis* serogroup X capsular polysaccharide and a carrier molecule, wherein the polysaccharide is covalently attached via a defined chemical linkage. In one embodiment, the conjugate is obtained by oxidizing primary hydroxyl groups of the polysaccharide—preferably using TEMPO/NaOCl/NaBr system—to convert C6-OH to aldehydes, with an oxidation extent of 10–50% of terminal residues, followed by direct reductive amination with the carrier protein (e.g., CRM197) using NaCNBH₃ at pH 7.2. Alternatively, the polysaccharide is coupled via a bifunctional linker such as SIDEA: the polysaccharide is first functionalized with a primary amine (e.g., at reducing end or via hydrazide chemistry), then reacted with SIDEA to form an NHS-ester intermediate, which subsequently couples to lysine ε-amines on the carrier. The linker moiety is typically adipate (—OC—(CH₂)₄—CO—), and X₁/X₂ denote reactive groups (e.g., NHS ester, hydrazide). Homobifunctional linkers like disuccinimidyl glutarate may also be used. The capsular polysaccharide may be an oligosaccharide with a degree of polymerization (DP) between 4 and 20, optimized for epitope presentation and stability.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The invention is exemplified through detailed experimental protocols. *N. meningitidis* serogroup X is cultured in optimized medium (e.g., Frantz medium), with growth monitored by proton NMR. Capsular polysaccharide is purified from culture supernatants via ethanol precipitation, nuclease treatment, and chromatography. Structural integrity is confirmed by HR-MAS NMR of bacterial pellets and HPAEC-PAD quantification.

For conjugation, polysaccharide is fragmented to avDP ~10–15 by mild acid hydrolysis, monitored by UPLC-SEC and NMR. In the first method, saccharide is oxidized with TEMPO (0.1 mol%), NaOCl (1.1 eq), NaBr (0.1 eq) in water at pH 10, 0°C, yielding aldehyde-terminated chains. Oxidized saccharide is purified by ethanol precipitation and Sephadex G-25 gel filtration, then conjugated to CRM197 (40 mg/mL in 50 mM NaPi, pH 7.2) at a 40:1 active ester:protein ratio overnight at 25°C. Conjugate is purified by Sephacryl S-300 size-exclusion chromatography and verified by SDS-PAGE shift.

In a second method, saccharide is solubilized in sodium acetate buffer (0.1 M, pH 5.5), reacted with SIDEA linker (12-fold molar excess) and triethylamine in DMSO/H₂O, purified by ethyl acetate precipitation, then conjugated to CRM197 as above. Purification uses ammonium sulfate precipitation (40% saturation).

A third method involves reduction with NaBH₄ (1 M, 4°C, 16 h), oxidation with NaIO₄ (5 mM, dark, 2 h), SIDEA coupling, and CRM197 conjugation, followed by ammonium sulfate purification.

Immunogenicity is tested in BALB/c mice (n=10/group): three subcutaneous doses (2 µg saccharide equivalents) with AlPO₄ adjuvant. IgG titers (ELISA vs. MenX CPS) and serum bactericidal activity (SBA using human/rabbit complement) confirm functional immunity.

Stability studies show serogroup X polysaccharide is stable at pH 6–7, 2–8°C, with <10% depolymerization over 24 months. Conjugates maintain >90% integrity under accelerated conditions (40°C, 3 months). Free polysaccharide is quantified by HPAEC-PAD after hydrolysis.

Analytical methods include HPAEC-PAD for saccharide quantification: samples are hydrolyzed in 2M TFA (100°C, 4 h), dried, reconstituted, and analyzed on Dionex CarboPac PA1 column with NaOH/NaOAc gradients and pulsed amperometric detection. Synthetic N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate serves as external standard, enabling precise quantification in vaccines and process intermediates.

These modes demonstrate the feasibility, reproducibility, and superiority of the disclosed conjugates and methods, fulfilling the objects of the invention.
