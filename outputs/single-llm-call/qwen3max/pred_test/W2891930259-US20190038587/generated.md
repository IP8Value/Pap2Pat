# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to therapeutics for the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and related psychiatric conditions. More specifically, the invention provides novel epigenetic-based therapeutic strategies that target DNA methylation pathways in the nucleus accumbens (NAc), a key brain region implicated in emotional regulation, reward processing, and stress response. The invention further encompasses diagnostic and prognostic methods based on DNA methylation profiles associated with PTSD susceptibility and resilience, as well as pharmaceutical compositions comprising S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), retinoic acid, or combinations thereof, for the reversal of pathological behaviors characteristic of PTSD.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a debilitating psychiatric condition that can develop following exposure to a traumatic event involving actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence. Core symptoms include intrusive re-experiencing of the trauma, persistent avoidance of trauma-related stimuli, negative alterations in cognition and mood, and marked alterations in arousal and reactivity. These symptoms must persist for more than one month and cause significant distress or impairment in social, occupational, or other important areas of functioning. Despite advances in psychotherapeutic and pharmacological interventions, a substantial proportion of individuals with PTSD exhibit chronic, treatment-resistant symptoms, underscoring the urgent need for novel therapeutic approaches grounded in a deeper understanding of the neurobiological underpinnings of the disorder.

Motivation for improved PTSD treatment stems from its high prevalence, chronicity, and association with comorbid conditions such as depression, substance use disorders, and suicide. Current first-line pharmacotherapies, primarily selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), demonstrate limited efficacy and are often poorly tolerated, highlighting the necessity for mechanistically distinct interventions. A growing body of evidence implicates dysregulation of the brain’s reward and stress circuitry in PTSD pathophysiology, with the nucleus accumbens (NAc) emerging as a critical hub. The NAc integrates dopaminergic, glutamatergic, and limbic inputs to modulate motivated behavior, emotional valence, and responses to stress. Alterations in NAc structure, function, and connectivity have been consistently observed in neuroimaging studies of individuals with PTSD, suggesting that this region plays a pivotal role in the development and maintenance of the disorder.

Epigenetic mechanisms, which regulate gene expression without altering the underlying DNA sequence, provide a compelling framework for understanding how environmental exposures such as trauma can induce long-lasting changes in neural circuits. Among these mechanisms, DNA methylation—the covalent addition of a methyl group to the 5' position of cytosine residues, primarily within CpG dinucleotides—has been extensively studied in the context of learning, memory, and stress adaptation. DNA methylation in promoter regions is generally associated with transcriptional repression, whereas methylation in gene bodies can have more complex effects, including facilitation of transcription. Dynamic changes in DNA methylation are essential for normal memory formation and consolidation, processes that are aberrantly engaged in the maladaptive fear memories characteristic of PTSD.

In the context of PTSD, both human and animal studies have begun to uncover trauma-induced alterations in DNA methylation patterns. Genome-wide analyses in peripheral blood cells of trauma-exposed individuals have identified differentially methylated loci in genes involved in immune function, neuroendocrine signaling (e.g., the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis), and synaptic plasticity. However, a critical limitation of human studies is the inaccessibility of relevant brain tissue, making it difficult to establish direct causal links between central epigenetic changes and behavioral phenotypes. Animal models are therefore indispensable for dissecting the role of DNA methylation in specific brain regions like the NAc in mediating PTSD-like behaviors.

Existing studies in humans, while valuable, often suffer from heterogeneity in trauma type, timing, and severity, as well as confounding factors such as comorbid psychiatric and medical conditions. This complexity poses critical questions: What are the specific DNA methylation signatures in the NAc that distinguish individuals who are susceptible to developing PTSD from those who are resilient? Can these epigenetic marks be causally linked to the core behavioral symptoms of PTSD? And most importantly, can targeted modulation of these methylation pathways reverse established PTSD-like phenotypes?

These questions motivate the study of resilience—a phenomenon where a significant subset of trauma-exposed individuals do not develop PTSD. Understanding the biological basis of resilience offers a powerful strategy for identifying protective mechanisms that could be harnessed therapeutically. The present invention addresses these gaps by leveraging a validated rat model of PTSD that recapitulates key features of the human condition, including individual differences in susceptibility and resilience, and by performing comprehensive, high-resolution mapping of DNA methylation dynamics in the NAc. This approach has led to the discovery of novel epigenetic targets and the development of effective combination therapies for PTSD.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention is based on the use of a well-validated rat model of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) that employs a stress-enhanced fear learning paradigm with repeated trauma reminders to induce long-lasting, individualized behavioral phenotypes. In this model, rats are exposed to an initial severe stressor followed by two milder reminder sessions, which together lead to the incubation of fear and the emergence of distinct subpopulations: one exhibiting a full spectrum of PTSD-like behaviors (susceptible) and another showing minimal or no such behaviors (resilient).

Through the application of capture bisulfite sequencing, a high-resolution method for mapping DNA methylation at single-base resolution across targeted genomic regions, the inventors have made the seminal discovery that the susceptible and resilient phenotypes are associated with profoundly different DNA methylation profiles in the nucleus accumbens (NAc). These profiles are not merely correlative but are functionally linked to the behavioral outcomes, as demonstrated by their reversibility through pharmacological intervention.

The invention identifies specific differentially methylated cytosine-guanine (CG) sites and the functional pathways they regulate as key therapeutic targets. A primary agent of the invention is S-adenosylmethionine (SAM), a universal methyl donor that serves as a broad modulator of DNA methylation. Treatment with SAM was found to selectively reverse two of the three core PTSD-like behaviors—reduced exploration and impaired social interaction—in susceptible animals, concomitant with a partial normalization of their aberrant DNA methylation profile.

Further analysis of the methylation data revealed upstream regulatory networks, with a particular focus on the retinoic acid biosynthesis and degradation pathway and estradiol-responsive genes. This insight led to the development of a novel and highly effective combination therapy comprising SAM and retinoic acid. This dual-agent treatment was shown to produce a synergistic effect, completely reversing all three characteristic pathological behaviors of PTSD in the susceptible rat cohort.

In summary, the utility of the present invention lies in its provision of: (1) a robust animal model with strong construct and predictive validity for studying PTSD susceptibility and resilience; (2) a comprehensive map of DNA methylation biomarkers in the NAc that can stratify individuals based on their risk for PTSD; (3) a novel monotherapy (SAM) for ameliorating specific PTSD symptoms; and (4) a groundbreaking combination therapy (SAM + retinoic acid) that offers a potential cure for the full spectrum of PTSD-like behaviors by targeting the underlying epigenetic dysregulation.

## DETAIL DESCRIPTIONS OF THE INVENTION

### Embodiment 1. DNA Methylation Profiles of PTSD Susceptibility and Resilience in a Rat Model of PTSD

The patent invention is embodied in a series of integrated methodologies and discoveries centered on a rat model of PTSD. This model begins with the introduction of an outbred strain of rats, such as Sprague-Dawley, which are housed under standard laboratory conditions with a 12-hour light/dark cycle, controlled temperature and humidity, and ad libitum access to food and water. The behavioral procedure is initiated after a period of acclimatization to the housing facility. A detailed habituation process is employed where rats are handled daily and exposed to the testing arena without any aversive stimuli to minimize baseline anxiety. Baseline measurements of exploratory behavior, social interaction, and acoustic startle response (a proxy for hyperarousal) are then recorded for each animal.

The initial exposure to stress involves placing the rat in a conditioning chamber and delivering a series of inescapable, high-intensity foot shocks. This is followed by two subsequent, milder reminder sessions (Re1 and Re2) conducted on separate days, which serve to reactivate and potentiate the fear memory without additional shock. Following an incubation period of several weeks, the same battery of behavioral tests is administered to assess the long-term impact of the stress protocol. Based on their performance, rats are subdivided into two groups: “susceptible” animals, which display a significant increase in freezing (fear), a marked decrease in exploration and social interaction, and heightened acoustic startle; and “resilient” animals, whose post-stress behavior is statistically indistinguishable from their own baseline or from non-stressed controls. Statistical analysis, typically using a median split or cluster analysis of a composite behavioral score, is used to objectively define these groups.

This model successfully recapitulates the incubation of fear and the inter-individual variation seen in human PTSD. Analysis of the behavioral data reveals that susceptible rats show a profound reduction in time spent in the center of an open field (exploration), a significant decrease in time spent interacting with a novel conspecific (social interaction), and a large increase in the amplitude of their startle reflex (hyperarousal). The distribution of freezing data is bimodal, clearly separating the two phenotypes. Correlation analyses confirm that these three behavioral domains are interrelated, forming a coherent PTSD-like syndrome.

Following behavioral phenotyping, tissue samples from the NAc are rapidly collected and flash-frozen. Genomic DNA is extracted and subjected to capture bisulfite sequencing. This technique involves hybridizing fragmented, bisulfite-converted DNA to custom-designed RNA baits that tile across regions of interest (e.g., promoters, enhancers), followed by high-throughput sequencing. The bioinformatics workflow begins with stringent quality control of the raw sequencing reads, followed by adapter trimming. The processed reads are then aligned to a bisulfite-converted reference genome of the rat using specialized aligners like Bismark. The resulting Sequence Alignment/Map (SAM) files are converted to the more efficient Binary Alignment/Map (BAM) format, sorted, and indexed. BAM files are filtered to remove low-quality and duplicate reads before being analyzed for methylation calls.

Each cytosine in a CG context is annotated with its genomic location (e.g., promoter, intron, intergenic) using reference databases. Differential methylation analysis is performed using statistical packages like methylKit or DSS to identify differentially methylated positions (DMPs) or regions (DMRs) between the susceptible, resilient, and control groups. This comparison reveals thousands of CG sites with altered methylation levels. A key finding is the identification of a set of CGs that are differentially methylated between susceptible and resilient animals, highlighting the epigenetic basis of their divergent outcomes. Overlapping CGs between different pairwise comparisons are identified to find common and unique epigenetic signatures.

Unsupervised hierarchical clustering of these DMPs effectively segregates the three experimental groups, demonstrating the robustness of the methylation profiles. To understand the functional implications, the list of genes associated with the DMPs is analyzed using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA). This analysis identifies canonical pathways relevant to PTSD, such as synaptic long-term potentiation, GABA receptor signaling, axonal guidance, and notably, retinoic acid biosynthesis. The utility of these DNA methylation profiles is multi-fold: they serve as objective biomarkers for susceptibility/resilience, reveal novel pathophysiological pathways, and provide a rational basis for developing targeted therapeutic interventions, including the use of broad DNA methylation modulators like SAM.

### Embodiment 2. Reversal of Two PTSD Behaviors, Exploration and Social Interaction by SAM Treatment

This embodiment introduces S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) as a therapeutic agent for PTSD. An experimental design is implemented where a cohort of rats is first subjected to the stress protocol described in Embodiment 1 and phenotyped as susceptible. These susceptible animals are then randomly assigned to receive either daily systemic injections of SAM or a saline vehicle for a defined period (e.g., 10 days). A battery of behavioral tests is administered post-treatment to assess therapeutic efficacy.

The results demonstrate that SAM treatment produces a significant and selective reversal of two core PTSD-like behaviors. In the exploration test (e.g., open field), SAM-treated susceptible rats spend significantly more time in the center of the arena compared to their saline-treated counterparts, indicating a reduction in anxiety-like behavior. Similarly, in the social interaction test, SAM-treated rats show a marked increase in the duration and frequency of social contacts with a novel partner, signifying a restoration of normal social functioning. In contrast, the hyperarousal phenotype, as measured by the acoustic startle response, remains largely unaffected by SAM treatment, suggesting a domain-specific therapeutic action.

Analysis of fear behavior via freezing shows a modest, non-significant fold change reduction in the SAM group. To link this behavioral rescue to epigenetic mechanisms, the DNA methylation profile of the NAc is again assessed in the susceptible-SAM and susceptible-saline groups using capture bisulfite sequencing. A comparative analysis identifies a subset of differentially methylated CG sites that are normalized by SAM treatment. A correlation analysis is then performed between the methylation levels at these specific CG sites and the behavioral scores for exploration and social interaction across all individuals. This analysis reveals a strong, significant correlation, providing direct evidence that the SAM-induced changes in DNA methylation are functionally coupled to the observed behavioral improvements. Hierarchical clustering of these correlated CG sites further validates their association with the rescued phenotype. The utility of this discovery is the establishment of SAM as a proof-of-concept epigenetic therapy for specific symptom clusters of PTSD and the identification of a panel of methylation biomarkers that predict and track treatment response.

### Embodiment 3. Discovery of “Upstream Regulators” of Genes that are Differentially Methylated in Susceptible and Resilient Traumatized Animals and Reversed by SAM Treatment

This embodiment details a sophisticated comparative bioinformatic analysis aimed at uncovering the master regulatory networks controlling the observed DNA methylation and gene expression changes. By integrating the lists of differentially methylated genes from the susceptible vs. resilient comparison and the susceptible-SAM vs. susceptible-saline comparison, the inventors performed an upstream regulator analysis using IPA software. This analysis predicts transcription factors, cytokines, kinases, and other molecules whose known downstream targets are significantly enriched in the input gene list.

A key result of this analysis is the strong prediction of beta-estradiol as a top inhibited upstream regulator in the susceptible group. This is supported by the finding that many genes known to be responsive to estradiol signaling are differentially methylated in the NAc of susceptible animals. Another major finding is the significant enrichment of genes involved in the retinoic acid biosynthesis and degradation pathway among the differentially methylated and SAM-reversed genes. Retinoic acid, a metabolite of vitamin A, is a potent signaling molecule that regulates gene expression by binding to nuclear receptors (RAR/RXR) and is crucial for neural development, plasticity, and adult brain function.

The utility of this discovery is profound. It moves beyond a simple catalog of differentially methylated sites to identify the higher-order signaling pathways that orchestrate the epigenetic landscape in PTSD. The identification of the retinoic acid pathway as a central player provides a strong mechanistic rationale for its therapeutic targeting. It suggests that the epigenetic dysregulation in PTSD may stem from an imbalance in this critical neurotrophic and transcriptional regulatory system, offering a new and specific avenue for drug development.

### Embodiment 4. Reversal of All Three Characteristic Pathological Behaviors of PTSD with a Novel Combination of Retinoic Acid and SAM

Building directly on the insights from Embodiment 3, this embodiment introduces a novel and synergistic combination therapy for PTSD. The inventors hypothesized that simultaneously providing the methyl donor (SAM) to correct global methylation deficits and the signaling molecule (retinoic acid) to activate its downstream transcriptional program would be more effective than either agent alone. An experimental design is executed where susceptible rats, as defined by the model in Embodiment 1, are treated with a combination of retinoic acid and SAM, retinoic acid alone, SAM alone, or vehicle.

The results are striking. While monotherapies with either SAM or retinoic acid show partial efficacy (SAM improving exploration/social interaction; retinoic acid showing a trend on hyperarousal), the combination therapy produces a complete and robust reversal of all three core pathological behaviors. In the exploration test, combination-treated rats behave indistinguishably from non-stressed controls. Their social interaction is fully restored to baseline levels. Most remarkably, their hyperarousal, as measured by the acoustic startle response, is normalized, an effect not achieved by SAM monotherapy. This demonstrates a clear synergistic interaction between the two agents.

The utility of this discovery is the provision of a highly effective, mechanism-based, and potentially curative treatment strategy for PTSD. By targeting both the substrate (methyl groups) and a key upstream regulator (retinoic acid) of the dysregulated epigenetic machinery, this combination therapy addresses the multifaceted nature of the disorder, offering hope for patients suffering from the full spectrum of PTSD symptoms.

## Applications of the Present Invention

The present invention has wide-ranging applications in the field of PTSD therapeutics and drug discovery. The core of the invention lies in the identification of specific DNA methylation profiles and the associated functional pathways in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) that underlie individual differences in susceptibility and resilience to PTSD. This work is fundamentally motivated by the growing recognition of epigenetic mechanisms, particularly DNA methylation, as a critical interface between environmental stress and long-term changes in brain function and behavior.

Previous PTSD models have been limited by their inability to capture the hallmark feature of human PTSD: the fact that only a subset of trauma-exposed individuals develop the disorder. The animal model employed in the present invention overcomes this limitation by incorporating recurring, mild stress reminders following an initial severe trauma, which leads to a natural distribution of behavioral outcomes that mirrors the human condition. This model possesses strong construct validity, as it is based on a well-established psychological principle of fear incubation, and predictive validity, as it responds to known therapeutic agents.

A key innovation is the emphasis on individual analysis of behavior, moving away from group averages to identify biologically meaningful subpopulations. This approach, combined with the use of high-resolution capture bisulfite sequencing on a homogeneous brain region (the NAc), allows for the detection of subtle but critical epigenetic changes. The motivation for this stems from the need to understand the long-lasting changes in gene expression that maintain the PTSD state. DNA methylation is a stable epigenetic mark that can persistently alter gene transcription, making it an ideal candidate mechanism for the enduring nature of PTSD symptoms.

The invention demonstrates that resilient rats, despite experiencing the same trauma, show a remarkable attenuation of PTSD-like symptoms, which is mirrored by a distinct DNA methylation signature in the NAc. In contrast, susceptible animals exhibit profound differences in DNA methylation at thousands of CG sites. Pathway analysis of these sites reveals associations with critical neurobiological functions, including synaptic plasticity and retinoic acid signaling. Similarly, resilient animals possess their own unique set of differentially methylated CG sites, which are linked to pathways that may confer protection.

By examining individual behavioral phenotypes as continuous variables, the invention captures the quantitative nature of inter-individual variation in response to trauma. This motivates a corresponding quantitative analysis of DNA methylation, leading to the discovery of strong correlations between an individual’s specific behavioral deficits (e.g., degree of social withdrawal) and the methylation level at specific CG sites. This personalized epigenetic-behavioral mapping is a powerful tool for precision medicine.

The application of SAM treatment in this model demonstrates that a broad modulator of DNA methylation can effectively reverse key PTSD behaviors and partially normalize the associated epigenetic landscape. This validates the causal role of DNA methylation in these phenotypes and highlights the therapeutic potential of targeting this pathway. The downstream pathways identified through this work, especially the retinoic acid biosynthesis pathway, represent a rich source of novel, more specific drug targets.

The discovery that a combination of retinoic acid and SAM can synergistically reverse the entire triad of PTSD-like behaviors is a major breakthrough. It underscores the importance of targeting networks of genes and pathways rather than single molecules in complex psychiatric disorders. This combination therapy represents a direct, practical application of the fundamental discoveries made in this work.
