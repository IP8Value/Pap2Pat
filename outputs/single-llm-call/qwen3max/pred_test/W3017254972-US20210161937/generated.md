# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to mitochondrial uncoupling and its application in the extension of lifespan, the suppression of cellular proliferation, the restoration of intestinal homeostasis, and the prevention or treatment of cancer. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for activating mitochondrial uncoupling through genetic or pharmacological means, including the administration of mitochondrial uncoupling agents such as small molecules, peptides, or nucleic acids encoding uncoupling proteins. The invention further encompasses kits and pharmaceutical formulations designed to deliver such agents for therapeutic benefit in mammals and other organisms.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Circadian rhythms are endogenous biological oscillations that synchronize physiological processes with the 24-hour day-night cycle. These rhythms are governed by molecular clocks composed of transcriptional-translational feedback loops involving core clock genes. In Drosophila melanogaster, the circadian transcriptional activators Clock (Clk) and Cycle (Cyc) drive the rhythmic expression of repressor genes such as period (per) and timeless (tim), which in turn inhibit Clk/Cyc activity to complete the feedback loop. Disruption of this system leads to arrhythmicity and has been associated with metabolic dysfunction, accelerated aging, and reduced lifespan, particularly when the transcriptional activators are impaired.

However, the consequences of disrupting the repressive arm of the circadian clock—specifically loss of Per or Tim—are less clear and have yielded conflicting results in the literature. Some studies report shortened lifespans and metabolic defects in per-null mutants, while others observe beneficial metabolic phenotypes, including increased metabolic rate, reduced adiposity, and improved insulin sensitivity. These discrepancies may stem from differences in genetic background, diet composition, microbiome status, or environmental conditions. Despite these inconsistencies, emerging evidence suggests that under specific contexts, loss of circadian repressors can confer healthspan advantages.

Notably, circadian regulation extends beyond behavioral outputs to influence fundamental cellular processes, including mitochondrial function. Mitochondria are central to energy metabolism and reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, both of which are tightly linked to aging and age-related diseases. Recent studies indicate that circadian clocks regulate mitochondrial dynamics, biogenesis, and oxidative phosphorylation efficiency. One critical aspect of mitochondrial physiology is uncoupling—the dissipation of the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane without ATP synthesis—mediated by uncoupling proteins (UCPs). Mild mitochondrial uncoupling has been proposed to reduce ROS generation and extend lifespan in various model organisms.

Previous investigations into circadian transcriptional repressors have largely focused on their role in maintaining rhythmicity and metabolic homeostasis but have not elucidated a direct mechanistic link between circadian disruption, mitochondrial uncoupling, and longevity. The present invention addresses this gap by demonstrating that loss of the circadian repressor Period leads to constitutive upregulation of the mitochondrial uncoupling protein UCP4C specifically in the intestine, resulting in decreased ROS levels, suppressed age-related intestinal hyperproliferation, preserved gut barrier integrity, and extended lifespan. This pathway operates independently of canonical longevity mechanisms such as dietary restriction, insulin/IGF signaling, or TOR inhibition, revealing a novel axis connecting circadian regulation, mitochondrial metabolism, and organismal aging.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses that activation of mitochondrial uncoupling confers multiple beneficial effects, including extension of lifespan, reduction of cellular proliferation, restoration of intestinal homeostasis, slowing of tumor growth, and prevention or treatment of cancer. These effects are achieved through the administration of an activator of mitochondrial uncoupling to a subject in need thereof. The activator may be a small molecule, a peptide, or a nucleic acid encoding an uncoupling protein, and it functions by inducing mild mitochondrial uncoupling, thereby reducing mitochondrial membrane potential and lowering reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, particularly in the intestinal epithelium.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method of extending the lifespan of a subject by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling activator. In another embodiment, the invention provides a method of decreasing harmful or aberrant cellular proliferation, such as age-related intestinal stem cell overproliferation or tumor cell growth, by activating mitochondrial uncoupling. Additionally, the invention includes methods for restoring or maintaining intestinal homeostasis, which is often compromised during aging due to barrier dysfunction and dysregulated regeneration.

The invention further encompasses methods for slowing tumor growth or preventing and treating cancer by administering mitochondrial uncoupling activators. These agents suppress proliferative signaling in stem and progenitor cells, thereby inhibiting tumor initiation and progression, as demonstrated in a Drosophila model of Notch-deficient intestinal tumors.

Exemplary mitochondrial uncoupling activators include, but are not limited to, mifepristone (RU486), stearic acid, 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP), butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), niclosamide, nitazoxanide, carbonyl cyanide phenylhydrazones (e.g., CCCP), BAM15, salicylanilide derivatives, and membrane-active peptides such as alamethicin and mastoparan. The invention also includes nucleic acid-based approaches, such as vectors encoding uncoupling proteins like UCP4C, which can be delivered to target tissues, particularly the intestine.

Moreover, the invention provides kits comprising one or more mitochondrial uncoupling activators, along with instructions for their use in practicing the disclosed methods. These kits may include pharmaceutical compositions, delivery vehicles (e.g., liposomes, nanoparticles), and diagnostic tools to monitor efficacy.

### Activation of Mitochondrial Uncoupling has Beneficial Effects

Mitochondrial uncoupling, when induced at mild levels, produces a cascade of beneficial physiological effects. By dissipating the proton motive force across the inner mitochondrial membrane, uncoupling reduces the efficiency of ATP synthesis but concurrently lowers electron transport chain backup, thereby decreasing superoxide and other ROS generation. This reduction in oxidative stress mitigates cellular damage, particularly in long-lived post-mitotic cells and regenerative tissues like the intestine.

In aging organisms, mitochondrial ROS accumulation drives stem cell exhaustion, tissue dysfunction, and mortality. The present invention demonstrates that activation of mitochondrial uncoupling delays aging by preserving intestinal integrity. Specifically, uncoupling in intestinal stem cells (ISCs) and enteroblasts (EBs) suppresses age-related hyperproliferation, maintains proper differentiation, and prevents “smurf” phenotype—a marker of gut barrier failure. Consequently, subjects exhibit extended healthspan and lifespan.

Furthermore, mitochondrial uncoupling slows tumor growth by metabolically constraining proliferative capacity. In a Drosophila model of Notch-deficient intestinal tumors, uncoupling agents reduced tumor burden and extended survival. This anti-proliferative effect stems from lowered ROS-mediated mitogenic signaling, which is essential for ISC expansion during aging and tumorigenesis.

Collectively, mitochondrial uncoupling acts as a metabolic rheostat that balances energy production with redox homeostasis, thereby promoting longevity and suppressing pathological proliferation.

### Methods and Compositions for Activating Mitochondrial Uncoupling

Mitochondrial uncoupling can be activated through chemical, peptide, or genetic means. Chemical uncouplers function as protonophores or fatty acid mimetics that shuttle protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane. Key structural features include moderate hydrophobicity to cross membranes, weak acidity to bind and release protons, and stability under physiological conditions.

Exemplary agents include substituted phenols such as 2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP), which, at low doses, safely induces uncoupling without hyperthermia. Butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), an antioxidant and uncoupler, also extends lifespan. Salicylanilide derivatives like niclosamide—an FDA-approved anthelmintic with a favorable safety profile—and nitazoxanide exhibit potent uncoupling activity. Carbonyl cyanide phenylhydrazones (e.g., CCCP) and newer agents like BAM15 offer alternative scaffolds.

Membrane-active peptides such as alamethicin and mastoparan form pores that dissipate ion gradients, though their use requires careful dosing to avoid cytotoxicity. Signal peptides can also induce uncoupling by altering mitochondrial membrane integrity.

Genetically, nucleic acids encoding uncoupling proteins (e.g., UCP4C) can be delivered via viral or non-viral vectors. Tissue-specific promoters (e.g., intestinal drivers) enable targeted expression. Delivery methods include electroporation, lipid nanoparticles, and adeno-associated viruses (AAVs).

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise a mitochondrial uncoupling activator and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. Carriers include water, saline, dextrose, glycerol, ethanol, lipids, and polymers. Formulations may be solutions, suspensions, emulsions, tablets, capsules, or injectables.

Administration routes include oral, rectal, transdermal, nasal, pulmonary, vaginal, and parenteral (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous). Parenteral vehicles include sterile aqueous solutions and oils. Advanced delivery systems include liposomes—spherical vesicles encapsulating agents—and nanoparticles that enhance bioavailability and targeting.

Therapeutically effective amounts vary by agent, subject, and condition. Dosing considers age, weight, and disease severity. Agents may be co-administered with other therapeutics (e.g., chemotherapeutics). Subjects benefiting include healthy individuals seeking longevity, cancer patients, and those with intestinal disorders (e.g., inflammatory bowel disease) or hyperproliferative conditions.

Kits include one or more uncoupling agents, carriers, delivery devices, and usage instructions.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The following non-limiting examples illustrate the invention using Drosophila models. Fly strains included cyc⁰¹, per⁰¹, tim⁰¹, and UAS-CRISPR lines targeting per or control genes (acp98AB). Flies were reared on standard media; drug-supplemented media contained RU486 (100 µg/mL), DNP (0.01–0.1 mM), or BHT (0.1–1 mM). Lifespan assays involved 30–35 flies/vial, scored every 1–2 days. Starvation resistance was tested on 1% agar.

Western blotting used antibodies against phospho-S6K, phospho-Akt, and actin. qRT-PCR quantified Ucp4 isoforms and per transcripts using Actin5C normalization. Cold shock recovery measured time to three beam breaks after 1h at 4°C. Triglycerides were quantified via thin-layer chromatography. Intestinal barrier integrity was assessed by the smurf assay (2.5% blue dye). Feeding was monitored via CAFE assay.

CO₂ respiration used capillary respirometers. Mitochondria were purified from 50 flies and assayed for oxygen consumption (Oxygraph-2k) and membrane potential (JC-1 dye). Blue-native PAGE assessed complex activity. Locomotor activity was tracked in DAM systems. TMRE and MitoSOX staining visualized mitochondrial potential and ROS in dissected intestines. Phospho-histone H3 immunostaining marked mitotic cells. Notch-RNAi intestines were stained for GFP⁺ tumor area.

Statistical analysis used Prism7: t-tests, ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc, and log-rank tests for survival. Significance: *p<0.05, **p<0.01, ***p<0.001. Data are SEM; n≥4 replicates.

### Loss of the Repressive Arm of the Transcriptional Circadian Clock Extended Lifespan Independent of Dietary Protein Restriction and Reduced Insulin-like Signaling

Four arrhythmic Drosophila mutants were analyzed. cyc⁰¹ and DN-Clock flies exhibited shortened lifespans, confirming prior reports. In contrast, per⁰¹ and tim⁰¹ males showed 15–20% lifespan extension. Restoring Per expression in per⁰¹ mutants reverted lifespan to wild-type, confirming causality. Lifespan extension persisted across yeast concentrations (0.01–10%), indicating independence from dietary restriction. Genetic ablation of insulin-producing cells or inhibition of TOR signaling extended lifespan equally in controls and per⁰¹ mutants, demonstrating that longevity is independent of insulin/IGF and TOR pathways.

### Period Mutants Exhibited a High Metabolic Rate Due to Mitochondrial Uncoupling

per⁰¹ mutants displayed hyperphagia, reduced starvation resistance, and low triglyceride stores. CO₂ production was elevated throughout the circadian cycle. Mitochondrial respiration remained high even with complex I (rotenone) or V (oligomycin) inhibitors, but not with uncouplers (DNP, stearate), suggesting maximal uncoupling. Purified mitochondria showed increased leak respiration, reduced membrane potential (JC-1 assay), and faster cold-shock recovery—hallmarks of uncoupling.

### Endogenous Uncoupling Protein UCP4C was Necessary for Period Mutant Lifespan Extension and Sufficient to Extend Wild-Type Lifespan

Ucp4B/C expression was circadian in controls but constitutively high in per⁰¹ mutants. Disrupting Ucp4B/C via piggyBac mutation or RNAi reverted uncoupling phenotypes and lifespan to wild-type. Conversely, adult-specific UCP4C overexpression mimicked per⁰¹: increased leak respiration, reduced membrane potential, and extended lifespan in wild-type flies, with no additive effect in per⁰¹ mutants, placing UCP4C downstream of Per.

### Lifespan Extension was Mediated by Loss of Per Specifically in the Intestine

Tissue-specific Per rescue revealed that only intestinal (not neuronal) expression reverted per⁰¹ lifespan. CRISPR-mediated per deletion in whole intestine or ISCs/EBs extended lifespan, confirmed by >90% per transcript reduction. Intestinal Ucp4C was circadian-regulated in controls (high at night) but constitutively high in per⁰¹. Knocking down intestinal Ucp4C abolished lifespan extension, while overexpressing it in ISCs/EBs extended wild-type lifespan. Aging assays showed per⁰¹ and UCP4C-overexpressing flies had fewer smurfs and reduced pHH3⁺ cells, indicating preserved barrier function and suppressed proliferation. MitoSOX staining confirmed lower ROS in ISCs/EBs and enterocytes, linking uncoupling to redox homeostasis.
