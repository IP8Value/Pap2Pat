# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, particularly to methods and reagents for the diagnosis and pathological assessment of rheumatoid arthritis. More specifically, the invention provides a novel diagnostic approach based on the quantification of specific microRNAs (miRNAs) in plasma or other blood-derived samples from human subjects. The invention enables the detection, differentiation, and monitoring of rheumatoid arthritis with high sensitivity and specificity, including in patients who are negative for conventional serological markers such as anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA) and rheumatoid factor (RF). The disclosed methods leverage the differential expression profiles of selected miRNAs—namely miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p—to generate a composite diagnostic score, referred to herein as ePRAM (estimated Probability of RA by plasma MiRNAs), which correlates with the presence and pathological state of rheumatoid arthritis while distinguishing it from other arthritic and autoimmune conditions such as osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus.

## BACKGROUND ART

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic, systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by persistent synovial inflammation, progressive joint destruction, and functional disability. It affects approximately 1% of the global population and exhibits significant heterogeneity in clinical presentation, disease progression, and response to therapy. Early and accurate diagnosis is critical, as timely intervention with disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) or biologic agents can substantially improve long-term outcomes and prevent irreversible joint damage. Current diagnostic criteria, such as those established by the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) and the European League Against Rheumatism (EULAR) in 2010, rely on a combination of clinical symptoms, imaging findings, and serological markers, primarily rheumatoid factor (RF) and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA). However, these markers are absent in a substantial subset of patients—up to 30%—leading to diagnostic uncertainty, delayed treatment initiation, and potential misclassification, especially in early disease stages.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding RNA molecules approximately 22 nucleotides in length that regulate gene expression post-transcriptionally by promoting mRNA degradation or translational repression. Over one thousand human miRNAs have been identified, and they are known to modulate a wide array of biological processes, including immune regulation, inflammation, cell proliferation, and apoptosis. Importantly, miRNAs have been found to be remarkably stable in extracellular environments, including blood plasma, serum, and synovial fluid, where they are protected from ribonuclease degradation through association with proteins such as Argonaute 2 (Ago2), encapsulation in exosomes or microvesicles, or binding to high-density lipoproteins (HDL). This stability, combined with their disease-specific expression patterns, has positioned circulating miRNAs as promising non-invasive biomarkers for various pathologies, including cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and autoimmune disorders.

Prior art in the diagnosis of rheumatoid arthritis has explored the use of circulating miRNAs, with studies reporting dysregulation of miRNAs such as miR-16, miR-132, and miR-223 in plasma or peripheral blood mononuclear cells of RA patients. However, these candidates often lack disease specificity, as their expression levels may also be altered in other inflammatory or degenerative joint diseases like osteoarthritis (OA) or systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE). Furthermore, existing miRNA-based approaches have typically focused on single-miRNA signatures, which suffer from limited diagnostic accuracy due to biological variability and the multifactorial nature of RA pathogenesis. Consequently, there remains an unmet need for a robust, specific, and sensitive diagnostic tool capable of reliably identifying RA—even in seronegative cases—and differentiating it from clinically similar conditions, thereby facilitating earlier and more precise therapeutic decision-making.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

Current diagnostic methods for rheumatoid arthritis face significant limitations, particularly in early-stage or seronegative disease. The reliance on clinical criteria and conventional serological markers such as RF and ACPA results in delayed or inaccurate diagnosis in a considerable proportion of patients, especially when fewer than ten joints are involved or when autoantibodies are undetectable. Moreover, existing biomarkers often fail to distinguish RA from other forms of arthritis, such as osteoarthritis—a degenerative condition—or systemic lupus erythematosus—an autoimmune disease with overlapping systemic features. This diagnostic ambiguity impedes timely initiation of appropriate therapy, potentially leading to irreversible joint damage and poor clinical outcomes. Additionally, prior attempts to utilize circulating miRNAs as diagnostic tools have been hampered by insufficient specificity, inconsistent normalization strategies, and inadequate validation across diverse patient cohorts. Therefore, there exists a pressing need for a novel, reliable, and specific molecular signature that can accurately diagnose RA, assess its pathological state, and differentiate it from confounding conditions, even in the absence of traditional serological markers.

### Means of Solving the Problems

The present invention addresses these challenges through a comprehensive analysis of plasma miRNA expression profiles in rheumatoid arthritis patients compared to healthy controls and individuals with other arthritic or autoimmune diseases. The inventors conducted a multi-phase study involving global miRNA profiling, candidate selection, large-scale validation, and specificity testing. This approach led to the identification of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p as significantly elevated in the plasma of RA patients, with high diagnostic specificity. Furthermore, miR-30a-5p was identified as a stably expressed internal reference suitable for normalization. The invention encompasses a method comprising the step of measuring the expression levels of these miRNAs in a blood-derived sample from a test subject. These expression levels are then correlated with the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis through statistical modeling. Specifically, a composite value termed ePRAM is calculated using a multivariate logistic regression formula that integrates the concentrations of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p. This composite value demonstrates superior sensitivity (78.4%) and specificity (92.3%) compared to individual miRNAs or conventional markers. The method is further refined by comparing the measured expression levels or composite value against predetermined cutoff values derived from receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses. Variations of the method include the concurrent measurement of anti-CCP antibody levels and integration into the diagnostic algorithm to enhance accuracy in borderline cases. The invention also provides a diagnostic reagent comprising nucleic acid probes or primers specifically designed to detect and quantify miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p. These reagents may be configured for use in quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), miRNA microarrays, or Northern blotting, and may be provided as part of a kit containing all necessary components, including reverse transcription enzymes, fluorescent labels, and internal control sequences such as synthetic cel-miR-39. Additional variations include immobilized nucleic acid arrays and labeled probes for hybridization-based detection.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention offers several significant advantages over existing diagnostic approaches for rheumatoid arthritis. By leveraging a panel of plasma miRNAs, the method achieves high diagnostic accuracy, effectively differentiating RA from both healthy states and other rheumatic diseases such as osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus. The composite ePRAM score enhances sensitivity and specificity beyond what is achievable with single-miRNA assays or conventional serological tests. Importantly, the invention demonstrates utility in diagnosing ACPA-negative RA, a patient subgroup that is particularly challenging to identify using current criteria. The correlation of miR-24 and ePRAM levels with established disease activity indices, such as DAS28, further enables the assessment of disease severity and monitoring of therapeutic response. The use of a stable internal normalizer (miR-30a-5p or spike-in cel-miR-39) ensures reproducibility and minimizes technical variability. Overall, the invention provides a non-invasive, objective, and highly specific tool for the early diagnosis, differential diagnosis, and pathological evaluation of rheumatoid arthritis, thereby facilitating timely and personalized treatment strategies that can improve patient outcomes and reduce long-term disability.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### 1. Test Method of a Sample Derived From the Blood of a Test Subject for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

The present invention provides a method for determining the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis in a test subject by analyzing a biological sample derived from blood. The method begins with the collection of a blood sample, preferably plasma, from the test subject, which may be a human or other mammal. Rheumatoid arthritis determination according to the invention is based on the discovery that specific miRNAs exhibit altered expression levels in the plasma of RA patients compared to healthy individuals or those with other diseases. The core of the method involves measuring the expression level of at least one miRNA selected from the group consisting of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p. The selection of these miRNAs is grounded in extensive validation showing their differential expression and diagnostic relevance. Measurement is performed using standard molecular biology techniques, with quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) being the preferred method due to its sensitivity and precision. To account for variations in sample handling and RNA extraction efficiency, an internal standard is employed; this may be a synthetic non-human miRNA such as cel-miR-39 spiked into the sample prior to processing, or the endogenous miR-30a-5p, which demonstrates stable expression across samples. The test subject is typically selected based on clinical suspicion of RA, including symptoms such as joint pain, swelling, or morning stiffness. Sample preparation involves centrifugation of whole blood to isolate plasma, which is then stored at –80°C until analysis. Total RNA, including miRNAs, is extracted using phenol-chloroform-based methods or commercial kits optimized for small RNA recovery. Following isolation, the expression level of the target miRNAs is quantified. This can be achieved via hybridization using sequence-specific nucleic acid probes, or more commonly, through amplification using miRNA-specific primers in a PCR reaction. Probes and primers are designed to be complementary to the mature sequence of the target miRNA and may be labeled with fluorescent dyes or other detectable moieties to facilitate quantification. Alternative measurement methods include miRNA microarray analysis, where labeled miRNAs are hybridized to an array of immobilized probes, or Northern blotting, though these are less sensitive than qRT-PCR. Expression levels are normalized to the internal standard, and a composite value, ePRAM, is calculated using the formula ePRAM = exp(–x)/(1+exp(–x)), where x is a linear combination of the normalized concentrations of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p, with miR-30a-5p contributing negatively to the score. The resulting expression levels or composite value are then correlated with the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis. This correlation is established by comparing the measured values to predetermined cutoff thresholds; for example, an ePRAM value greater than 0.50 indicates a high probability of RA. These cutoffs are derived from statistical analyses of large validation cohorts and are set to maximize specificity. The method further allows for the determination of disease state by correlating miRNA levels with conventional clinical indices such as DAS28, CRP, or ESR. Crucially, the method can differentiate RA from osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus, as the expression levels of the specified miRNAs are significantly lower in these conditions. The method is particularly valuable for diagnosing anti-CCP antibody-negative patients, where conventional serology fails. Nucleic acid probes and primers used in the method may include derivatives such as locked nucleic acids (LNAs) to enhance binding affinity and specificity, and are synthesized using standard phosphoramidite chemistry. The method concludes with a determination of the presence or absence of rheumatoid arthritis, its likely activity state, and its distinction from other diseases, providing a comprehensive diagnostic and prognostic assessment.

### 2. Diagnostic Reagent for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis

The invention further provides a diagnostic reagent specifically designed for determining the pathology of rheumatoid arthritis. This reagent comprises at least one nucleic acid probe or primer that is capable of specifically hybridizing to or amplifying a target sequence from miR-24, miR-125a-5p, or miR-30a-5p. Preferable embodiments include sets of primers for use in qRT-PCR, such as forward primers specific to each mature miRNA sequence and a universal reverse primer. The design and function of these reagents are detailed in the test method described above. The reagent may be provided as a kit containing multiple probes or primers for the simultaneous detection of all three miRNAs. For array-based detection, the reagent includes a nucleic acid array wherein probes for the target miRNAs are immobilized on a solid support such as a glass slide or nylon membrane. The constituent elements of the reagent kit may further include buffers, enzymes (e.g., reverse transcriptase, DNA polymerase), dNTPs, and detection labels (e.g., fluorescent dyes, enzymes for chemiluminescence). For real-time PCR applications, the kit includes components such as SYBR Green or TaqMan probes. For miRNA array or Northern blotting, the kit provides hybridization buffers, blocking agents, and detection substrates. Additionally, the reagent kit may contain components for measuring anti-CCP antibody levels, such as antigen-coated plates or beads, enabling a combined serological and miRNA-based diagnostic approach. These reagents are optimized to ensure high specificity, sensitivity, and reproducibility in the clinical or laboratory setting.

## EXAMPLES

### Method

Plasma samples were prepared from whole blood collected in EDTA-containing tubes, followed by centrifugation at 1400 g for 7 minutes and storage at –80°C. Total RNA was isolated from 4 mL of plasma using a phenol-based extraction method with Sepasol-RNA II, and 1 pmol of synthetic cel-miR-39 was added as an external spike-in control. For smaller-scale qRT-PCR validation, 150 µL of plasma was processed using TriPure Isolation Reagent and RNeasy columns, with 25 fmol of cel-miR-39 added. miRNA array analysis was performed using TaqMan Human miRNA Array Cards on an Applied Biosystems 7900 system, with cDNA synthesized from 200 ng of total RNA. Reverse transcription for qRT-PCR was carried out using the NCode VILO kit, and amplification was performed with EXPRESS SYBR GreenER qPCR SuperMix on an Applied Biosystems 7500 system. Clinical indices including DAS28, CRP, ESR, RF, and ACPA were recorded for all participants. Statistical analysis was conducted using JMP 8 software, employing Student’s t-tests, Pearson correlation, and logistic regression. Internal standard miRNAs were identified using geNorm and NormFinder algorithms. Expression levels of candidate miRNAs were analyzed across four phases: initial array screening (n=3 RA, 3 HC), candidate selection (n=8 RA, 8 HC), large-scale validation (n=102 RA, 104 HC), and specificity testing (n=24 OA, 11 SLE).

### Results

Total RNA extraction and miRNA array analysis revealed distinct expression profiles between RA patients and healthy controls (HCs). Initial screening identified numerous differentially expressed miRNAs, leading to the selection of 26 candidates for further validation. In the second phase, qRT-PCR confirmed that 11 miRNAs, including miR-24, miR-26a, and miR-125a-5p, were significantly elevated in RA plasma. Normalization studies established cel-miR-39 as the optimal internal control, with miR-30a-5p as a reliable endogenous alternative. Large-scale validation demonstrated that miR-125a-5p, miR-24, and miR-26a could individually differentiate RA from HC with high specificity (89.5–94.3%). However, multivariate logistic regression showed that a composite score (ePRAM) combining miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p achieved superior diagnostic performance, with an area under the ROC curve (AUC) of 0.89, sensitivity of 78.4%, and specificity of 92.3%. Correlation analysis revealed that miR-24 and ePRAM levels were associated with disease activity indices (DAS28, CRP), while miR-125a-5p levels were independent of general inflammation markers, suggesting a role as a stable disease-specific indicator.

## COMPARATIVE EXAMPLE

To evaluate the specificity of the identified miRNAs for rheumatoid arthritis, the expression levels of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p were examined in plasma samples from patients with osteoarthritis (OA, n=24) and systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE, n=11). The results showed that the plasma concentrations of both miR-24 and miR-125a-5p, as well as the composite ePRAM score, were significantly lower in OA and SLE patients compared to RA patients (P < 0.01 for all comparisons). Specifically, in OA patients, the true negative rate (i.e., correctly identified as non-RA) was 79% for both miR-24 and miR-125a-5p, and 75% for ePRAM. In SLE patients, the true negative rates were 91% for miR-24 and 64% for both miR-125a-5p and ePRAM. These findings conclusively demonstrate that the elevated levels of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p are not merely reflective of general joint degeneration or systemic autoimmunity but are specifically associated with the pathophysiology of rheumatoid arthritis, thereby validating their utility as disease-specific diagnostic markers.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The diagnostic reagent for rheumatoid arthritis disclosed herein possesses significant industrial applicability. It can be manufactured and distributed as a standardized kit for use in clinical laboratories, hospitals, and research institutions worldwide. The reagent enables rapid, accurate, and non-invasive diagnosis of RA, including in seronegative cases, thereby addressing a major unmet clinical need. Its ability to differentiate RA from other arthritic conditions reduces misdiagnosis and supports appropriate therapeutic decisions. The reagent is compatible with widely available laboratory instrumentation, such as real-time PCR machines and microarray scanners, facilitating easy integration into existing diagnostic workflows. Furthermore, the method supports monitoring of disease activity and treatment response, offering longitudinal clinical value. Given the global prevalence of rheumatoid arthritis and the increasing emphasis on early intervention, the diagnostic reagent represents a commercially viable product with substantial market potential in the fields of in vitro diagnostics and personalized medicine.
