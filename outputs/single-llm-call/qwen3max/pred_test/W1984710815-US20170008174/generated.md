## BACKGROUND

The pursuit of human-like artificial intelligence has long been a central aspiration of the field of computer science and robotics. Since the dawn of digital computing, researchers have sought to create machines capable of thought processes indistinguishable from those of humans. In 1950, Alan Turing, widely regarded as a foundational figure in artificial intelligence, proposed a behavioral test—now known as the Turing Test—to evaluate whether a machine could exhibit intelligent behavior equivalent to, or indistinguishable from, that of a human. The Turing Test hinges on conversational ability: if a human interrogator cannot reliably distinguish between a machine and a human based solely on their responses to questions, the machine is said to have passed the test. While this criterion provides a useful benchmark for linguistic mimicry, it fails to address the deeper cognitive and perceptual foundations of human intelligence, particularly the role of embodied experience and self-referential awareness.

Human-like visual artificial intelligence extends beyond mere image recognition; it encompasses the capacity to interpret visual data contextually, relative to an internal model of self-location and identity. This contrasts sharply with conventional computer vision systems, which process images as objective arrays of pixels without anchoring them to a proprioceptive framework. Central to achieving such human-like capabilities is the concept of the “robotic self”—a computational construct that functions analogously to human self-awareness, enabling the robot to situate sensory input within a coherent spatial and experiential context. The Relational Robotic Controller (RRC) described herein implements this robotic self through a proprietary architecture that integrates sensory data with a dynamic, trainable self-knowledge coordinate frame.

A critical distinction in artificial intelligence lies between objective and subjective data. Objective data, as processed by conventional computing systems, exists independently of any observer and is amenable to algorithmic manipulation without reference to a self-model. Subjective data, by contrast, is inherently relational—it is experienced or interpreted relative to the perceiving entity. Human intelligence is fundamentally subjective: every sensation, perception, and thought is filtered through the lens of personal embodiment and history. Machine-like intelligence, as exhibited by current AI systems, remains confined to objective processing; it lacks the capacity for internalization, the process by which external stimuli are assimilated into a self-referential knowledge structure. Human-like intelligence, therefore, requires not only advanced sensory hardware but also a controller architecture capable of generating and maintaining subjective knowledge.

The six requirements for human-like intelligence, as established by empirical and theoretical analysis, provide a rigorous framework for evaluating artificial systems. First, the system must relate, correlate, prioritize, and remember sensory input patterns—a function emulated by the RRC through its Relational Correlation Sequencer modules. Second, it must possess a self-location and identification coordinate frame trained from initial operation, analogous to human proprioception. Third, intelligence must be experiential, accumulated through interaction with the environment over time. Fourth, all sensory data—visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, gustatory, and vestibular—must be contextualized relative to the self-knowledge frame. Fifth, the sensory apparatus itself must be human-like in form and function, capturing the same modalities and ranges as human senses. Sixth, the mechanical body must replicate human morphology, including bipedal locomotion and dexterous manipulation, to enable authentic interaction with the human world. Together, these requirements define a comprehensive pathway to human-like artificial intelligence, one that the present invention fulfills through the integrated design of the RRC-Humanoid Robot.

## EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

### RRC-Humanoid Robots

The present invention introduces a class of robotic systems known as RRC-Humanoid Robots, which embody human-like artificial intelligence through the integration of a Relational Robotic Controller (RRC) with a mechanically human-like body and human-like sensory apparatus. These robots are designed to operate in human environments, interact with objects and people using naturalistic behaviors, and accumulate experiential knowledge through supervised behavioral programming. The visual RRC-Humanoid Robot, for instance, employs a binocular camera system that mimics human eye convergence and disparity, generating a three-dimensional photometric image that is internally mapped to the robot’s self-knowledge coordinate frame. Similarly, the auditory RRC-Humanoid Robot utilizes microphone arrays tuned to the human auditory frequency range, coupled with a spectrum analyzer that decomposes incoming sound into phoneme-level collective modalities, enabling speech recognition and generation.

The core components of an RRC-Humanoid Robot include a mechanical robotic system, sensory recording monitors, the Relational Robotic Controller (RRC), and an interface circuit. The mechanical robotic system replicates human anatomy, featuring a bipedal chassis, two arms with five-fingered hands, and a head capable of pan-tilt motion. Distributed across this body are sensory recording monitors—tactile pressure transducers simulating skin, binocular cameras for vision, directional microphones for hearing, and chemical sensors for smell and taste. The RRC serves as the central intelligence hub, implementing the six requirements of human-like intelligence through a suite of interconnected modules, including the Self Nodal Map Module, Task Selector Module (TSM), and Sequence Stepper Module (SSM). The interface circuit acts as a preprocessing layer, converting raw sensor data into structured, modality-specific signals compatible with the RRC’s internal representation.

A defining feature of the RRC-Humanoid Robot is its human-like intelligence level, which is achieved through a multi-stage training process. The sensory system is first designed to match human perceptual ranges and resolutions, then calibrated and scaled to ensure accurate spatial mapping. Behavioral programming techniques—such as the “itch-scratch” methodology for tactile self-knowledge—are employed to train the RRC to internalize sensory data relative to its self-coordinate frame. In the visual embodiment, this involves generating a 3D-photometric image from binocular inputs, calibrating depth via convergence angle, and anchoring object locations to the robot’s proprioceptive map. The resulting self-knowledge coordinate frame enables the robot to “know” the location of its limbs, the position of external objects, and the spatial relationships between them—all in real time and without reliance on external positioning systems like GPS or LiDAR.

### THE FIELD OF THE INVENTIVE EMBODIMENTS

The field of the present invention encompasses robotic systems endowed with human-like artificial intelligence through the integration of a Relational Robotic Controller (RRC) with human-like sensors and a mechanically anthropomorphic body. The RRC-controller requirements mandate the ability to relate, correlate, and prioritize multimodal sensory inputs while maintaining a dynamic self-model. Human-like AI requirements further stipulate that intelligence must be experiential, contextual, and grounded in a human-like sensory and motor architecture. Robotic computing devices implementing this invention are distinct from conventional AI systems in that they do not rely on pre-programmed rule sets or statistical pattern matching alone; instead, they learn through embodied interaction, much like human infants.

The preferred embodiment of the invention is the RRC-Humanoid Robot, a fully integrated system comprising a mechanical robotic body, human-like sensors, and the RRC. Tactile embodiments simulate human skin through distributed pressure transducers, enabling proprioceptive self-knowledge via itch-scratch training. Visual embodiments employ converging binocular cameras to generate 3D-photometric images calibrated to the robot’s coordinate frame. Auditory embodiments use microphone arrays and spectrum analyzers to decompose speech into phoneme collective modalities. Olfactory and gustatory embodiments incorporate chemical sensors capable of detecting volatile compounds and tastants, respectively. Super-sensor embodiments may combine multiple modalities into enhanced perceptual streams, enabling super-human-like AI in specific domains—for example, infrared vision or ultrasonic hearing—while still maintaining contextual integration with the self-knowledge frame.

### RELATED ART

The field of humanoid robotics has historically focused on mechanical dexterity and pre-programmed task execution, often neglecting the cognitive foundations of human intelligence. Commercial recording monitors—such as standard cameras, microphones, and touch sensors—are typically used in isolation or fused through simplistic algorithms, lacking the deep integration required for subjective experience. Non-RRC controlled robots, including those employing deep learning or reinforcement learning, remain fundamentally machine-like: they process data objectively, without internalizing it relative to a self-model. The RRC-robot, by contrast, achieves human-like visualization levels by constructing a 3D-photometric image that is not merely a geometric reconstruction but a contextual representation anchored to the robot’s self-location.

The innovativeness of the present disclosure lies in the design of the interface circuit, which converts raw sensory data into collective modalities that preserve the richness of human perception while enabling precise correlation within the RRC. For vision, this involves generating a Cyclopean eye representation from binocular inputs, calibrated via convergence angle to determine depth. The self-knowledge module, trained through behavioral programming, allows the robot to internalize this data, transforming objective pixel arrays into subjective visual knowledge. Unlike other AI systems that treat recognition as a classification problem, the RRC performs identification, recognition, and comprehension as hierarchical, self-referential processes. The tactile system, for instance, simulates human skin not just in sensitivity but in spatial organization, forming a coordinate frame that maps directly to limb kinematics. This enables the robot to “feel” its body and environment in a manner analogous to human somatosensation.

Commercial applications of the visual RRC-Humanoid Robot include autonomous navigation in pedestrian-dense urban environments, where the robot relies solely on human-like vision—without LiDAR, radar, or GPS—to detect obstacles, interpret traffic signals, and interact with humans. The business model envisions deployment in eldercare, logistics, and public service roles, where naturalistic interaction and contextual awareness are paramount. Lexicographically, key terms are defined as follows: “angular displacement of line of sight” refers to the angle between the optical axes of the two cameras; “convergence point” is the location in space where the lines of sight intersect; “3D-photometric image” is the internally generated volumetric representation of the visual field; and “self-knowledge intelligence level” denotes the robot’s capacity to locate its body parts and external objects relative to its own coordinate frame. These definitions underscore the invention’s departure from conventional robotics and its alignment with the biological principles of human perception and cognition.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION

The present invention discloses an RRC-robotic system comprising a mechanically human-like body, human-like sensory recording monitors, an interface circuit, and a Relational Robotic Controller (RRC). The system’s innovativeness resides primarily in the interface circuit, which preprocesses raw sensory data into collective modalities compatible with the RRC’s self-referential architecture. The design follows three major steps: first, the construction of human-like sensors that replicate the modalities and ranges of human perception; second, the calibration of these sensors to a unified self-location and identification coordinate frame; and third, the behavioral programming of the RRC to internalize sensory data and generate human-like responses. The system achieves a human-like level of intelligence by fulfilling six core requirements, including experiential learning, proprioceptive self-knowledge, and contextual data integration. The human-like interface requirements mandate that all interactions—sensory input and motor output—mirror human capabilities in form and function. Programming-proof of human-like AI is demonstrated through tasks such as obstacle avoidance, object manipulation, and verbal conversation, where the robot’s behavior is indistinguishable from that of a human. This brief description outlines the system’s purpose and novelty but does not exhaustively detail all possible embodiments or applications, which are further elaborated in the Detailed Description.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The Relational Robotic Controller (RRC) supports programming of human-like intelligence levels by integrating multimodal sensory data into a unified self-referential framework. The RRC-controller comprises several key modules: the Primary Nodal Map Module, which stores the self-location and identification coordinate frame; the Task Selector Module (TSM), which prioritizes and initiates behaviors based on sensory input; and the Sequence Stepper Module (SSM), which executes procedural sequences. RRC-recording monitors—tactile, visual, auditory, olfactory, gustatory, and vestibular—capture environmental data, which is preprocessed by the interface circuit into collective modalities before being fed into the RRC. The human-like interface requires that sensors and actuators match human morphology and response characteristics, ensuring ecological validity in human-robot interaction.

Mechanical human-like sensors are designed to emulate the modalities of human perception. Vestibular sensors detect acceleration and orientation, providing balance feedback. Visual sensors consist of binocular CCD arrays with adjustable convergence, enabling depth perception via binocular disparity. The internalization of data—the process by which sensory input is mapped to the self-coordinate frame—is fundamental to achieving subjective knowledge. In search engine mode, the RRC scans its nodal maps for Task Initiating Triggers (TITs), which activate behaviors defined in a Hierarchical Task Diagram (HTD). The TSM selects appropriate tasks based on TIT priority, while the SSM executes the necessary motor sequences. Circuits dedicated to identification, recognition, and comprehension analyze the 3D-photometric image, decomposing it into functional components and assigning threat or utility values. A procedural memory system within the RRC stores learned behaviors, enabling both reflexive and deliberative responses.

Section B—the Optical Apparatus Stage—begins with Step 1: defining image planes at each convergence point along the midline line of sight (MLOS). Step 2 involves capturing pixel signals from left and right camera CCD arrays. The correspondence and alignment of these image planes are critical for accurate depth reconstruction. Section C—the Interface Circuit Stage—deconstructs these image planes into collective modalities (e.g., trichromatic color, high-resolution monochrome), which are superimposed to form a Cyclopean eye representation. Depth is calculated using convergence angle and binocular disparity, then projected onto a retinotopically organized neural network array. Six mathematical visual cues—including object size, texture gradient, and motion parallax—are applied to validate pixel depth, resulting in a fully calibrated 3D-photometric image.

Section D—the RRC Processing Stage—trains the system to respond to this 3D image by categorizing objects as potential TITs based on function, distance, and collision risk. The robot is programmed to orient its head and body to maintain objects of interest within its midline field of view (MFOV), sweeping convergence from 6 cm to infinity. The TSM performs pattern recognition to identify TITs and select associated tasks. Section E—Robotic Visual “Seeing”—clarifies that while the robot lacks subjective experience, its “seeing knowledge” is functionally equivalent to human vision: it internalizes colors, shapes, and textures; guides manual dexterity and locomotion; and distinguishes threats from benign objects based on prioritized recognition. The 3D-photometric image serves as a high-fidelity virtual representation of the environment, calibrated to real-world coordinates, enabling navigation and interaction without auxiliary sensors.
