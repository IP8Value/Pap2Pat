## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Androgen-resistant prostate cancer represents a significant clinical challenge due to its aggressive nature, resistance to conventional hormonal therapies, and limited treatment options once the disease progresses to castration-resistant stages. Despite advances in chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted agents, patient outcomes remain poor, with median survival often less than two years after diagnosis of metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer. A major obstacle in developing effective molecular therapeutics for this and other solid tumors, including pancreatic cancer, lies in the inefficient intracellular delivery of bioactive payloads such as oligonucleotides, peptide nucleic acids (PNAs), and other macromolecular inhibitors. These agents possess high target specificity and potent biological activity but suffer from poor cellular uptake, rapid degradation, and inadequate subcellular localization—particularly nuclear delivery, which is essential for modulating transcription factors like STAT3.

Current therapeutic approaches rely heavily on viral vectors or lipid-based nanoparticles for delivery, but these systems are often associated with immunogenicity, toxicity, low transfection efficiency in primary cells, and lack of tumor selectivity. Cell-penetrating peptides (CPPs) have emerged as promising alternatives due to their ability to cross plasma membranes and deliver conjugated cargoes into cells. However, many existing CPPs, such as TAT or penetratin, exhibit non-specific uptake across cell types, limited nuclear targeting, and susceptibility to endosomal entrapment, which drastically reduces functional cytosolic or nuclear bioavailability. Furthermore, attempts to use prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-targeted ligands for prostate cancer delivery have shown inconsistent results, particularly in PSMA-negative cell lines like DU-145. There remains an unmet need for novel CPP platforms that combine enhanced cellular internalization, cancer cell selectivity, efficient escape from endosomal compartments, and robust nuclear localization—especially for delivering transcription factor inhibitors such as anti-STAT3 PNAs.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel cell-penetrating peptide (CPP) designed for the targeted delivery of therapeutic and diagnostic agents into cancer cells, particularly androgen-resistant prostate and pancreatic cancer cells. The CPP comprises a glutamate-rich peptide covalently linked via a peptide linker to a nuclear localization signal (NLS) peptide derived from the Oct6 transcription factor. In specific embodiments, the glutamate-rich peptide is positioned at the N-terminus of the NLS peptide, and the linker consists of one or more alanine or glycine residues, or a short peptide sequence that spatially separates the functional domains without interfering with their individual activities.

In a preferred embodiment, the CPP includes the amino acid sequence EEEAA (SEQ ID NO: 1) fused to the N-terminus of the Oct6 NLS sequence PKKKRKV (SEQ ID NO: 2), resulting in the chimeric peptide EEEAA-PKKKRKV (SEQ ID NO: 3). This construct may be further labeled with a detectable moiety such as fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) at the C-terminus for tracking and validation purposes. The invention also encompasses hybrid molecules wherein a therapeutic payload is conjugated to the C-terminus of the NLS peptide. Such payloads include, but are not limited to, nucleic acids (e.g., antisense oligonucleotides, siRNA), peptide nucleic acids (PNAs), liposomes, or diagnostic imaging agents.

The therapeutic payload is selected to inhibit cancer cell proliferation, induce apoptosis, or modulate oncogenic signaling pathways. For example, an anti-STAT3 PNA sequence may be attached to the CPP to suppress STAT3-mediated transcription, leading to growth arrest and programmed cell death in STAT3-dependent tumors. Critically, the CPP of the invention demonstrates superior cellular uptake and nuclear localization compared to either the glutamate peptide or the Oct6 NLS alone. The peptide localizes predominantly to the nucleus of viable cancer cells, as confirmed by live-cell imaging and imaging flow cytometry using nuclear stains such as DRAQ5. This nuclear tropism is essential for the efficacy of payloads targeting nuclear processes, such as transcription factor inhibition.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention is based on the discovery that coupling a glutamate-rich peptide to the N-terminus of the Oct6 nuclear localization signal dramatically enhances both cellular uptake and nuclear accumulation in human prostate and pancreatic cancer cell lines, including DU-145, LNCaP, and PANC-1. While traditional CPPs often suffer from endosomal sequestration or lack organelle specificity, the chimeric peptide described herein overcomes these limitations through a rationally designed architecture that leverages dual functional motifs: a targeting/uptake-enhancing glutamate domain and a nuclear trafficking NLS domain.

The glutamate-rich segment, exemplified by the pentapeptide EEEAA, appears to interact with cell surface receptors or transporters that are overexpressed in malignant cells. Glutamate receptors, including ionotropic and metabotropic subtypes, are aberrantly expressed in various cancers and have been implicated in tumor progression, metabolic reprogramming, and therapy resistance. Although prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) binds C-terminal glutamates, the N-terminal positioning of the glutamate residues in the present CPP suggests engagement with alternative glutamate-sensing mechanisms, possibly involving metabotropic glutamate receptors or other anionic amino acid transporters upregulated in cancer. This interaction facilitates rapid, temperature-dependent internalization that is partially ATP-independent at low concentrations but becomes ATP-dependent when the full chimeric structure—including the Oct6 NLS—is present.

The Oct6 NLS (PKKKRKV) is derived from the POU-domain transcription factor Oct6 (also known as SCIP or Tst-1), which plays roles in neural development and myelination. Its NLS is a classical monopartite signal recognized by importin α/β nuclear transport machinery. When fused to the glutamate peptide via a short alanine-glycine linker or directly, the resulting chimera exhibits synergistic properties: the glutamate domain drives efficient cell entry, while the NLS ensures active nuclear import. Notably, neither component alone achieves comparable nuclear localization—Oct6-FITC shows modest uptake but minimal nuclear accumulation, while Glu-FITC enters cells poorly and remains diffuse in the cytoplasm.

Experimental data demonstrate that the chimeric peptide Glu-Oct6-FITC (SEQ ID NO: 3) enters DU-145 and PANC-1 cells within minutes, with geometric mean fluorescence intensities increasing linearly up to at least 500 nM. Nuclear colocalization studies using DRAQ5 staining reveal approximately 50–70% of the peptide signal localized to the nucleus, far exceeding controls. Substitution of glutamate residues with phenylalanine, asparagine, or tyrosine abolishes uptake, confirming sequence specificity. Competition assays show that unlabeled mutant peptides do not inhibit wild-type peptide internalization, suggesting a non-competitive, receptor-mediated process requiring intact glutamate side chains.

Moreover, the CPP successfully delivers functional payloads. Conjugation to a carboxyfluorescein-labeled PNA results in efficient cellular uptake and predominant nuclear localization of the PNA cargo. When linked to an anti-STAT3 PNA, the CPP induces significant apoptosis in both prostate and pancreatic cancer cells at nanomolar concentrations, whereas the unconjugated PNA is inert. These findings validate the platform’s utility for delivering transcription-targeting therapeutics.

### Examples

The experimental findings confirm that the chimeric peptide Glu-Oct6-FITC (synthesis number 353) exhibits exceptional cell-penetrating and nuclear-localizing capabilities. Peptides were synthesized using standard Fmoc solid-phase chemistry and purified by HPLC; structures were verified by mass spectrometry and sequencing. Key constructs included Glu-FITC (EEEAA-FITC), Glu-Ala-FITC (EEEAA-A-FITC), Glu-Lys-FITC (EEEAA-KKK-FITC), Oct6-FITC (PKKKRKV-FITC), and Glu-Oct6-FITC (EEEAA-PKKKRKV-FITC), along with substitution variants such as Phe-Oct6-FITC (FFF-AA-PKKKRKV-FITC).

Uptake studies in live DU-145 and PANC-1 cells used flow cytometry with viability dyes (7-AAD or PI) to exclude dead cells. Glu-Oct6-FITC showed 100-fold higher uptake than Glu-FITC and 10-fold higher than Glu-Lys-FITC, indicating the Oct6 NLS is critical for maximal internalization. Kinetic analysis revealed rapid entry, with significant fluorescence detectable within 1 hour and continuing to increase over 4 hours at 500 nM. Temperature-dependence experiments showed reduced but still substantial uptake at 4°C, suggesting both energy-dependent and -independent mechanisms. However, sodium azide (NaN₃), an ATP-depleting agent, inhibited uptake of Oct6-containing peptides by over 50%, implicating active transport for the full chimera.

Substitution of glutamates with Phe, Asn, or Tyr eliminated uptake, confirming the necessity of negatively charged residues. Competition with unlabeled mutant peptides did not reduce Glu-Oct6-FITC uptake, ruling out simple receptor saturation and supporting a specific structural requirement. Cytotoxicity assays confirmed that the peptides themselves are non-toxic; observed toxicity with fluorophore-labeled mutants was attributed to hydrophobic aggregation, not inherent peptide activity.

Imaging flow cytometry and live-cell microscopy using DRAQ5 demonstrated clear nuclear colocalization of Glu-Oct6-FITC, unlike control peptides. The hybrid molecule Glu-Oct6-PNA-FITC (comprising the CPP linked to a PNA with inert sequence TATGATCTCCTCCGT-lysyl-FITC) was efficiently internalized by DU-145 and PANC-1 cells, with ~70% nuclear localization. A small, highly fluorescent subpopulation emerged even at 300 nM, suggesting heterogeneous uptake or efflux dynamics.

Most significantly, Glu-Oct6 conjugated to an anti-STAT3 PNA (sequence 13410a) induced 48–69% apoptosis in cancer cells at 600 nM after 48 hours, whereas the unconjugated PNA had no effect. This confirms the CPP’s ability to deliver biologically active payloads to their intranuclear targets, enabling potent therapeutic effects.

## Experimental Procedures

Peptides and PNAs used in the studies are listed in Table 1 of the original research, with synthesis numbers assigned for reference. All peptides were synthesized via Fmoc chemistry on an Applied Biosystems Model 433 synthesizer, cleaved, deprotected, and purified by reverse-phase HPLC. Identity was confirmed by MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry and Edman degradation. FITC or dansyl labels were incorporated at the C-terminus at a 1:1 molar ratio. Peptide-PNA conjugates, including Glu-Oct6-PNA-FITC and anti-STAT3 variants, were synthesized by BioSynthesis, Inc., with C-terminal lysyl-FITC tags due to PNA chemistry constraints.

Cell culture was performed under standard conditions: DU-145 and PANC-1 cells were maintained in DMEM/Ham’s F12 supplemented with 10% newborn bovine serum, while LNCaP cells were grown in RPMI-1640 with 10% fetal bovine serum. All lines were subcultured at 70–80% confluence and used between passages 5–20. Viability was routinely assessed using fluorescein diacetate staining and exceeded 98% at experiment initiation.

For uptake and fluorescence quantification, cells were incubated with peptides at concentrations ranging from 0 to 1000 nM for 1–48 hours at 4°C, 23°C, or 37°C, as specified. Where indicated, sodium azide (0.1–1%) was added to deplete ATP. After incubation, cells were washed, trypsinized, and resuspended in buffer containing 5 mM 7-AAD or PI for live-cell gating. Fluorescence was measured on BD FACScan or LSR II flow cytometers using logarithmic amplification; geometric mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was used for quantitative comparisons. Calibration beads ensured inter-experimental consistency.

Live-cell imaging employed a Zeiss Axiovert 200 inverted epifluorescence microscope. Cells in 12-well plates were treated with 500 nM peptide and 10 µM DRAQ5 for the final hour, then imaged in phenol red-free medium with 10% serum to maintain viability. Images were captured and analyzed using AxioVision software to assess subcellular distribution without fixation artifacts.

Apoptosis was quantified by annexin V-FITC/propidium iodide staining after 48-hour PNA exposure. Cells were analyzed by flow cytometry, with early apoptotic (annexin V⁺/PI⁻) and late apoptotic/necrotic (annexin V⁺/PI⁺) populations summed for total apoptosis percentage.

Statistical analyses were performed using KaleidaGraph 4.2 for graphing and GraphPad InStat3 for ANOVA and post-hoc testing. Significance was defined as p < 0.05, with repeated measures ANOVA used for time-course and dose-response comparisons. All experiments were replicated at least three times with consistent results.
