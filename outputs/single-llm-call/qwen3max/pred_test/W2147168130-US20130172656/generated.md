# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention resides in the field of oncology, particularly in the development of novel therapeutic agents for the treatment of esophageal cancer. More specifically, the invention relates to the use of a naturally derived sesquiterpenoid aromatic ester compound—armillaridin—either alone or in combination with radiation therapy, for inhibiting the proliferation of esophageal cancer cells and enhancing their susceptibility to ionizing radiation. Esophageal cancer remains one of the most aggressive and lethal malignancies worldwide, characterized by rapid progression, early metastasis, and poor response to conventional therapeutic modalities. Despite advances in surgical techniques, chemotherapy, and radiotherapy, the prognosis for patients diagnosed with locally advanced or unresectable esophageal carcinoma remains dismal, with a five-year survival rate consistently below 25%. Current standard-of-care regimens often involve concurrent chemoradiation, yet these approaches are frequently limited by systemic toxicity, tumor resistance, and inadequate local control. The urgent need for safer, more effective adjuvants that can potentiate the efficacy of radiation without exacerbating normal tissue damage has driven the search for novel radiosensitizing agents derived from natural sources.

Radiation therapy is a cornerstone in the management of esophageal cancer, especially for patients who are not candidates for surgical resection due to comorbidities or tumor location. However, the therapeutic window of radiation is inherently narrow, constrained by the tolerance of adjacent critical structures such as the spinal cord, heart, and lungs. Tumor cells often exhibit intrinsic or acquired radioresistance, which significantly compromises treatment outcomes. Conventional chemotherapeutic agents used as radiosensitizers—such as cisplatin—while effective to some degree, are associated with substantial adverse effects including nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity, myelosuppression, and gastrointestinal disturbances. These toxicities frequently necessitate dose reductions or treatment interruptions, thereby undermining the intended synergistic effect with radiation. Moreover, many existing radiosensitizers lack selectivity for malignant cells, leading to collateral damage to healthy tissues. Consequently, there exists a critical unmet need for compounds that can selectively sensitize esophageal cancer cells to radiation while sparing normal cells, thereby improving both local tumor control and patient quality of life during treatment.

## SUMMARY

The present invention is motivated by the unexpected discovery that armillaridin—a protoilludane-type norsesquiterpenoid ester isolated from the medicinal fungus *Armillaria mellea*—exhibits potent anti-proliferative activity against human esophageal cancer cells and significantly enhances their sensitivity to ionizing radiation. This dual functionality positions armillaridin as a promising candidate for integration into multimodal cancer therapy regimens, particularly for esophageal carcinoma, which is notoriously refractory to monotherapy. The anti-proliferative activity of armillaridin has been demonstrated across multiple histological subtypes of esophageal cancer, including both squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma, indicating broad applicability. Mechanistically, armillaridin induces apoptosis through the intrinsic mitochondrial pathway, as evidenced by loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, caspase-3 activation, and characteristic morphological changes such as chromatin condensation and membrane blebbing. Furthermore, armillaridin causes cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase—a stage known to be highly sensitive to radiation—thereby providing a rational basis for its radiosensitizing properties.

In a first aspect, the invention provides a composition comprising armillaridin, or a pharmaceutically acceptable salt or derivative thereof, formulated as a medicament for the treatment of esophageal cancer. This composition may be administered alone or in conjunction with other therapeutic agents to achieve enhanced cytotoxic effects. In a second aspect, the invention encompasses a method of enhancing the susceptibility of esophageal cancer cells to radiation therapy, comprising administering an effective amount of armillaridin prior to or concurrently with radiation exposure. The radiation therapy may be delivered via external beam radiation using clinically relevant parameters, such as a 6 MeV electron beam at a dose rate of approximately 2.4 Gy per minute, with fractionated doses typically ranging from 0.5 to 4 Gy per session. Further embodiments of this method include the co-administration of armillaridin with established chemotherapeutic agents to create synergistic or additive anti-tumor effects while potentially reducing the required radiation dose, thereby minimizing off-target toxicity.

In a third aspect, the invention discloses a method of treating esophageal cancer in a subject, comprising administering armillaridin in an effective amount to inhibit tumor growth and induce apoptosis in cancerous cells. Experimental data demonstrate that armillaridin exerts significant inhibitory effects on human esophageal cancer xenografts in vivo, with tumor volume reduction observed without concomitant weight loss or leukopenia—key indicators of systemic toxicity. Notably, long-term exposure to armillaridin does not appear to induce cumulative adverse effects, suggesting a favorable safety profile suitable for extended treatment regimens. In a fourth aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising armillaridin as the active ingredient, combined with one or more pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, diluents, or carriers. This composition is designed for various routes of administration, including intraperitoneal, intravenous, oral, or topical delivery, depending on the clinical context and desired pharmacokinetic profile.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

For the purposes of this disclosure, the term “treating” or “treatment” refers to the administration of a compound or composition to a subject diagnosed with or at risk of developing esophageal cancer, with the intent to reduce tumor burden, inhibit cancer cell proliferation, induce apoptosis, enhance radiosensitivity, or otherwise ameliorate disease progression. The term “an effective amount” denotes a quantity of the active compound sufficient to produce a measurable therapeutic response without causing unacceptable toxicity; this amount may vary based on factors such as the subject’s age, weight, disease stage, and route of administration. The terms “compound,” “agent,” or “medicament” are used interchangeably to refer to armillaridin (CH-205-O), its derivatives, analogs, or pharmaceutically acceptable salts that retain the core biological activity described herein. “Administered,” “administering,” or “administration” encompass any method by which the compound is introduced into the body, including but not limited to oral, parenteral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, transdermal, or transmucosal routes. A “subject” or “patient” refers to a human or non-human mammal afflicted with or susceptible to esophageal cancer, typically under medical supervision.

The invention stems from the unexpected discovery of a protoilludane norsesquiterpenoid ester compound—designated CH-205-O or armillaridin—derived from *Armillaria mellea*, a basidiomycete fungus traditionally used in Chinese medicine for treating neurological disorders such as vertigo and convulsions. Prior chemical investigations of *Armillaria mellea* focused primarily on polysaccharides and simple phenolics, with limited attention to sesquiterpenoid metabolites. Armillaridin represents a structurally unique entity within this class, characterized by a fused cyclobut(e)indenyl core substituted with chloro, hydroxy, methoxy, and formyl functional groups. Its chemical structure is defined as 3-chloro-6-hydroxy-4-methoxy-2-methyl-,3-formyl-2,2a,4a,5,6,7,7a,7b-octahydro-2a-hydroxy-6,6,7b-trimethyl-1H-cyclobut(e)inden-2-yl ester. The compound is isolated from fungal mycelium through solvent extraction, followed by chromatographic purification, and stored as a dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) stock solution at −20°C; working solutions are prepared fresh to maintain stability, with final DMSO concentrations kept below 0.1% (v/v) to avoid cytotoxic artifacts.

The method of enhancing susceptibility of esophageal cancerous cells to radiation therapy involves pre-treating cells with armillaridin at concentrations ranging from 0.125 to 5.0 μM for 24 hours, followed by irradiation with doses between 0.5 and 4 Gy. Radiation is delivered via a linear accelerator using a 6 MeV electron beam at 2.4 Gy/min, simulating clinical fractionation schedules. This protocol has been validated in human esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (CE81T/VGH, TE-2) and adenocarcinoma (BE-3, SKGT-4) lines, all of which exhibit increased radiosensitivity upon armillaridin exposure, as quantified by clonogenic survival assays yielding sensitizer enhancement ratios (SER) up to 1.6. The method may be augmented with additional anti-cancer agents, including alkylating agents (e.g., cyclophosphamide, temozolomide), anthracyclines (e.g., doxorubicin, epirubicin), plant alkaloids (e.g., vincristine, paclitaxel), topoisomerase inhibitors (e.g., etoposide, irinotecan), cytotoxic antibiotics (e.g., bleomycin, mitomycin C), and tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., erlotinib, gefitinib).

In practicing the method of treating cancer in a subject, armillaridin is administered at an effective dose—such as 80 mg/kg intraperitoneally three times weekly—as demonstrated in murine xenograft models. This regimen achieves significant tumor growth inhibition without altering body weight or white blood cell counts, indicating minimal systemic toxicity. Long-term exposure studies support sustained efficacy over multiple treatment cycles, with no evidence of drug resistance or cumulative organ damage. The pharmaceutical composition comprises armillaridin at a concentration of 0.1% to 99% by weight, blended with excipients such as lactose, starch, cellulose derivatives, or polyethylene glycol to ensure stability, solubility, and bioavailability. The medicament is prepared using standard compounding techniques and may be formulated as tablets, capsules, injectables, creams, or patches. Routes of administration include intraperitoneal, intravenous, oral, topical, and transmucosal delivery. Topical dosage forms include gels, ointments, and sprays, while transmucosal forms encompass buccal films, sublingual tablets, and nasal sprays designed for rapid absorption.

### EXAMPLES

Human esophageal cancer cell lines—including CE81T/VGH and TE-2 (squamous cell carcinoma) and BE-3 and SKGT-4 (adenocarcinoma)—were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, 10 mmol/L NaHCO₃, and 20 mmol/L HEPES under 5% CO₂ at 37°C. Cell viability was assessed via MTT assay after 24–72 hours of armillaridin exposure, revealing IC₅₀ values between 3.4 and 6.9 μM. Trypan blue exclusion confirmed reduced membrane integrity in treated cells, consistent with cytotoxicity. Cell cycle analysis by propidium iodide staining and flow cytometry showed G2/M accumulation (from 5.91% to 14.04%) and increased sub-G1 population (from 4.44% to 8.72%), indicative of apoptosis. Mitochondrial depolarization was detected using DiOC₆(3) dye, while Annexin V-FITC/PI staining confirmed early apoptotic events. Caspase-3 activity was elevated and blocked by Z-VAD-FMK, confirming caspase dependence.

Radiation treatment involved 6 MeV electrons at 0–4 Gy after 24-hour armillaridin pre-treatment. Clonogenic assays demonstrated reduced colony formation, with SER values up to 1.6 at 2.5–5.0 μM armillaridin. In vivo, male nude mice bearing CE81T/VGH xenografts received 80 mg/kg armillaridin intraperitoneally thrice weekly for 12 doses. Tumor volumes were measured biweekly using calipers and calculated as 0.5ab². Armillaridin significantly inhibited tumor growth compared to vehicle controls, matching the efficacy of 2.5 mg/kg cisplatin but without weight loss or leukopenia. Long-term treatment confirmed sustained anti-tumor activity and absence of chronic toxicity, supporting clinical translation. Collectively, these data validate armillaridin as a potent, selective, and safe radiosensitizer for esophageal cancer therapy.
