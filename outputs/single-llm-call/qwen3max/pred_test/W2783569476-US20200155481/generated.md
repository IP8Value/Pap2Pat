# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of malaria prophylaxis and, more specifically, to long-acting injectable formulations of atovaquone for the prevention of malaria in humans. The invention provides a novel method of administering atovaquone via intramuscular injection in a microparticle or nanoparticle formulation that enables sustained release of the drug over an extended period, thereby offering durable protection against Plasmodium infection without the need for daily or weekly oral dosing. This approach is particularly advantageous for travelers to malaria-endemic regions, military personnel, and populations in areas with high transmission risk, where adherence to conventional antimalarial regimens is often suboptimal. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising atovaquone in a non-nanoparticle or microparticle form, suitable for parenteral administration, and methods for their preparation and use in causal prophylaxis of malaria.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Malaria remains one of the most significant global infectious disease burdens, caused primarily by the protozoan parasite Plasmodium falciparum. Each year, hundreds of millions of individuals are infected, resulting in hundreds of thousands of deaths, predominantly among young children in sub-Saharan Africa. Despite decades of public health efforts and the development of various control strategies, including insecticide-treated bed nets and artemisinin-based combination therapies, malaria continues to pose a formidable challenge. Vaccines, such as RTS,S/AS01, have demonstrated only partial and short-lived efficacy, leaving chemoprophylaxis as a critical tool for preventing infection in non-immune individuals, such as international travelers and expatriates. However, current prophylactic regimens rely on oral administration of drugs like mefloquine, doxycycline, or atovaquone-proguanil, which require strict adherence to daily or weekly dosing schedules. Noncompliance is common due to forgetfulness, gastrointestinal side effects, or perceived low risk, significantly undermining the effectiveness of these interventions. Moreover, while some antimalarials act only on the blood-stage parasites (suppressive prophylaxis), others like atovaquone also target the liver stage (causal prophylaxis), offering a more comprehensive barrier to infection. Yet, the pharmacokinetic profile of orally administered atovaquone—characterized by a relatively short half-life and variable absorption—necessitates continued dosing for up to four weeks after potential exposure, further complicating adherence. There exists, therefore, an urgent and unmet need for a long-acting, single-dose prophylactic agent that ensures consistent therapeutic drug levels over an extended duration, thereby eliminating the burden of repeated dosing and improving real-world effectiveness.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention discloses a groundbreaking discovery: that atovaquone, when formulated as a long-acting injectable microparticle or nanoparticle composition and administered intramuscularly, provides sustained plasma concentrations sufficient to confer complete causal prophylaxis against malaria for at least four weeks following a single dose. This method overcomes the limitations of oral atovaquone by ensuring prolonged drug exposure without reliance on patient adherence. In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for preventing malaria in a human subject comprising administering a single intramuscular injection of a pharmaceutical composition comprising atovaquone in a solid drug particle formulation, wherein the formulation comprises at least 20% by weight of atovaquone and is designed for slow release from an intramuscular depot. The microparticle embodiment is specifically engineered using emulsion-templated freeze drying (ETFD) techniques to achieve high drug loading (e.g., 80% by weight) with controlled particle size and release kinetics. Administration frequency is reduced to a single dose prior to anticipated exposure, with follow-up injections required only if protection beyond the effective duration—estimated at one month or longer in humans—is needed. The prophylactic effect duration has been demonstrated in preclinical models to last up to 28 days post-injection, with plasma atovaquone concentrations above 200 ng/mL correlating strongly with protective efficacy. Furthermore, the invention contemplates additional applications, including the prevention of other parasitic infections susceptible to atovaquone, such as Pneumocystis pneumonia or toxoplasmosis, and potential use in combination with other antiparasitic agents for enhanced or broad-spectrum coverage.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The scope of the present invention encompasses methods and compositions for the long-term prevention of malaria through intramuscular delivery of atovaquone in a sustained-release formulation. As used herein, the terms “patient,” “individual,” or “subject” refer to a human being, preferably a non-immune traveler, military personnel, or resident of a malaria-endemic area, who is at risk of Plasmodium infection and in need of prophylaxis. The term “preventing” or “prevention” denotes the inhibition of establishment of malaria infection following exposure to sporozoites, achieved by maintaining systemic concentrations of atovaquone sufficient to eliminate parasites during their liver stage development. “Treatment” or “treating” is not the primary intent of this invention but may be contextually relevant in related applications; however, the focus herein is prophylactic. An “effective” amount refers to a dose of atovaquone that achieves plasma concentrations of at least 200 ng/mL, which has been identified as the threshold for reliable causal prophylaxis. The core of the invention lies in a microparticle formulation of atovaquone, prepared via ETFD, comprising a high drug load (e.g., 20–80% by weight) stabilized by pharmaceutically acceptable polymers and surfactants such as polyvinyl alcohol, poloxamers, or tocopheryl polyethylene glycol succinate. This is distinct from nanoparticle formulations in that the particles may exceed 1000 nm in diameter but are still suitable for intramuscular injection and depot formation. The invention also includes non-nanoparticle formulations that nonetheless provide sustained release. Prophylaxis of malaria, particularly P. falciparum and potentially P. vivax (though not against hypnozoites), is the primary indication, but the composition may also be used to prevent or treat other atovaquone-sensitive parasitic diseases. Combination therapy with other anti-parasitic compounds—such as proguanil, although unnecessary for prophylaxis due to atovaquone’s causal action and the lack of transmissible resistance—is contemplated. Other anti-parasitic agents include chloroquine, lumefantrine, or artemisinin derivatives, though their inclusion is optional. The process for preparing the pharmaceutical product involves dissolving atovaquone in an organic solvent, emulsifying with aqueous solutions of stabilizers, followed by cryogenic freezing and lyophilization to yield a dry, porous monolith that is reconstituted in sterile water prior to injection. The resulting pharmaceutical composition comprises atovaquone and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, which includes parenteral vehicles such as sterile water, saline, or buffered solutions. Formulations for parenteral administration are isotonic, pyrogen-free, and stable upon storage. The choice of carrier is guided by compatibility with the solid drug particles and minimization of injection site irritation. Surfactants are included to stabilize the dispersion and modulate release kinetics. The final product is packaged in unit-dose or multi-dose sealed containers, such as vials or pre-filled syringes, under aseptic conditions. Injectable formulations are designed for intramuscular delivery, typically in volumes of 0.1–2 mL. The amount or dose of atovaquone ranges from 50 mg to 1000 mg per human adult, with preferred doses between 200 mg and 600 mg. For malaria prevention, a single intramuscular dose of 300 mg is expected to provide protection for at least 28 days. Follow-up injections may be administered monthly for extended travel. Specific embodiments include doses of 200 mg, 300 mg, and 400 mg. The dosage range is based on allometric scaling from murine studies showing efficacy at 36–200 mg/kg. Examples demonstrate that ATQSDN7, a lead formulation, provided 100% protection in mice challenged up to 28 days post-injection. Materials and methods involved the use of P. berghei sporozoites for challenge, in vivo pharmacokinetic assessments showing a 105-hour half-life post-injection, washed drug preparations to remove solvents, oral Mepron as a comparator, and a liver stage breakthrough test confirming causal mechanism.

### Examples

Sporozoite parasites for challenge were derived from Anopheles stephensi mosquitoes infected with P. berghei ANKA strain. Salivary glands were harvested 18 days post-infection, homogenized, and sporozoites counted and diluted to 25,000/mL in RPMI medium. Mice were challenged intravenously with 5,000 sporozoites. The test for liver stage breakthrough involved comparing outcomes after sporozoite versus direct blood-stage challenge; only sporozoite-challenged mice remained protected, confirming that efficacy was due to elimination of parasites in the liver before erythrocytic invasion.

### Materials and Methods

Sporozoite parasites for challenge were prepared as described above. In vivo pharmacokinetics and efficacy were assessed in C57BL/6 mice injected intramuscularly with ATQSDN formulations, followed by sporozoite challenge at defined intervals and monitoring for parasitemia over 42 days. Washed drug preparation involved ETFD processing without residual solvents. Oral Mepron administration served as a control, given by gavage at equivalent doses. Atovaquone pharmacokinetics were measured via UPLC-MS/MS, revealing sustained plasma levels. The test for liver stage breakthrough utilized paired challenges with sporozoites and infected erythrocytes, demonstrating that protection occurred only when liver stages were targeted.
