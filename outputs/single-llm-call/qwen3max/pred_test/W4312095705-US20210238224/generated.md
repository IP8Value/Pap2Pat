# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of targeted protein degradation therapeutics, specifically to a novel class of Proteolysis Targeting Chimeras (PROTACs) designed for the treatment of chronic kidney disease (CKD). More particularly, the invention relates to a double-target PROTAC molecule that simultaneously induces the degradation of Smad3—a key mediator of renal fibrosis—and stabilizes hypoxia-inducible factor 2 alpha (HIF-2α)—a renoprotective transcription factor—by selectively recruiting the von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) E3 ubiquitin ligase. This dual-functional molecular architecture leverages the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway not only to eliminate a pathogenic protein but also to exploit the competitive occupancy of an E3 ligase to stabilize a beneficial endogenous substrate. The invention thus represents a strategic advancement in bifunctional degrader design, wherein both arms of the heterobifunctional molecule are intentionally selected to produce synergistic therapeutic outcomes in the context of progressive kidney injury and fibrosis.

## BACKGROUND ART

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is a global health burden characterized by progressive loss of renal function, often culminating in end-stage renal disease requiring dialysis or transplantation. A central pathological feature of CKD is renal fibrosis, driven by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix (ECM), tubular atrophy, interstitial inflammation, and microvascular rarefaction. The transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGF-β1)/Smad3 signaling axis is a well-established master regulator of this fibrotic cascade. Smad3, upon phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, activates transcription of pro-fibrotic genes including collagen I and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), thereby promoting epithelial–mesenchymal transition (EMT) and myofibroblast activation. Consequently, therapeutic strategies aimed at inhibiting Smad3 have demonstrated significant anti-fibrotic effects in preclinical models. However, conventional small-molecule inhibitors of Smad3 typically function by blocking its phosphorylation or DNA-binding activity, necessitating sustained high drug concentrations and raising concerns about off-target effects and incomplete pathway suppression due to the pleiotropic roles of Smad3 in normal physiology.

Current treatments for CKD, such as angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors or angiotensin receptor blockers, primarily slow disease progression but do not halt or reverse established fibrosis. There remains an unmet need for therapies that directly target the core fibrogenic machinery. In this context, PROTACs have emerged as a revolutionary modality. A PROTAC is a heterobifunctional molecule comprising three elements: a ligand that binds a protein of interest (POI), a ligand that recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase, and a chemical linker connecting the two. By bringing the POI into proximity with the E3 ligase, the PROTAC facilitates polyubiquitination of the POI, leading to its degradation by the 26S proteasome. This event-driven mechanism allows substoichiometric dosing and can target proteins traditionally considered “undruggable.”

Despite their promise, existing PROTACs face limitations. Many rely on E3 ligases such as cereblon or VHL without considering the biological consequences of sequestering these enzymes from their natural substrates. For instance, VHL is the primary E3 ligase responsible for oxygen-dependent degradation of HIF-α subunits. Hijacking VHL for POI degradation may inadvertently stabilize HIF-α, which could be either detrimental or beneficial depending on the isoform and disease context. Moreover, most PROTACs are designed solely for POI degradation, overlooking the potential to harness E3 ligase occupancy as a secondary therapeutic lever. This limitation motivates the development of “double-target” PROTACs—molecules engineered not only to degrade a pathogenic protein but also to stabilize a protective endogenous protein by deliberate selection of the E3 ligase and its natural substrate dynamics. Such an approach is particularly compelling in CKD, where Smad3 drives fibrosis while HIF-2α exerts renoprotective, anti-fibrotic, and erythropoietic effects.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention proposes a novel double-target PROTAC designed to concurrently degrade Smad3 and stabilize HIF-2α for the treatment of chronic kidney disease. The method of constructing this PROTAC involves the rational selection of a Smad3-binding small molecule compound (SMC) as the POI recognition ligand, based on prior identification via computer-aided virtual screening and surface plasmon resonance validation. The E3 ligase selected is von Hippel–Lindau (VHL), chosen not only for its high expression in renal tissues but also because it is the exclusive E3 ligase responsible for HIF-2α degradation. A VHL-binding SMC, known to specifically engage the VHL E3 ligase complex, serves as the E3 recognition ligand. Linker optimization was performed by synthesizing variants with different lengths and flexibilities, including polyethylene glycol (PEG) and succinic acid ((CH₂COOH)₂), followed by functional screening to identify the linker that maximizes Smad3 degradation efficiency. The chiral configuration of the Smad3-binding moiety was also evaluated, revealing superior activity for the S-enantiomer over the R-form.

The resulting double-target PROTAC comprises the Smad3-binding SMC in S-configuration linked via a succinic acid-based linker to the VHL-recruiting ligand. This structure enables simultaneous engagement of Smad3 and VHL, leading to ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of Smad3 while competitively inhibiting VHL-mediated ubiquitination of HIF-2α, thereby stabilizing it. The application of this PROTAC is directed toward the prevention or treatment of renal fibrosis, preservation of renal function, amelioration of renal anemia, and restoration of peritubular capillary density in subjects suffering from or at risk of CKD. The benefits of this double-target approach include synergistic anti-fibrotic activity—through direct Smad3 removal and indirect blockade of HIF-1α/Smad3-dependent fibrogenesis—enhanced erythropoiesis via HIF-2α-driven erythropoietin (EPO) production, improved iron mobilization, and angiogenesis via vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) upregulation. In summary, the invention provides a rationally designed, bifunctional degrader that exploits both ends of the PROTAC scaffold to achieve multi-modal renoprotection, representing a paradigm shift in targeted protein degradation therapeutics.

## EMBODIMENTS

The research hypothesis underlying the present invention posits that a PROTAC constructed from a Smad3-binding ligand and a VHL-recruiting ligand will not only degrade Smad3 but also stabilize HIF-2α by occupying VHL, thereby producing combined anti-fibrotic and reparative effects in the kidney. To test this, a Smad3/HIF-2α double-target PROTAC was constructed using a previously identified Smad3-binding SMC that does not inhibit Smad3 phosphorylation—thus permitting its ubiquitination—and a well-characterized VHL ligand. The choice of this Smad3 ligand was critical, as traditional Smad3 inhibitors prevent the conformational changes necessary for ubiquitin transfer, rendering them unsuitable for PROTAC incorporation. The benefits of this dual-targeting strategy are manifold: it directly eliminates a central fibrogenic driver, spares HIF-1α (which is degraded via non-VHL pathways and is pro-fibrotic), selectively stabilizes the renoprotective HIF-2α isoform, and leverages the kidney-enriched expression of VHL to confer tissue selectivity. This results in a therapeutic agent with enhanced efficacy, reduced off-target risk, and multi-faceted action against the complex pathophysiology of CKD.

### Example 1

Small molecules binding to Smad3 were initially screened using a combination of computational docking and surface plasmon resonance (SPR) assays, leading to the identification of a lead compound with high affinity and specificity. The binding site of this Smad3 ligand was mapped to a region distinct from the phosphorylation domain, ensuring that Smad3 remains competent for ubiquitination upon PROTAC-induced ternary complex formation. Concurrently, the binding site of the VHL recognition ligand was confirmed to reside within the hydrophobic pocket of the VHL β-domain, consistent with known VHL-HIF-α interactions. Two distinct linkers were employed to connect these ligands: a flexible, hydrophilic polyethylene glycol (PEG) linker and a shorter, more rigid succinic acid ((CH₂COOH)₂) linker. Chemical synthesis was carried out by stepwise conjugation, and the final PROTAC structures were verified using high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) for purity (>95%) and mass spectrometry (MS) for molecular identity.

In vitro activity screening in NRK-49F rat kidney fibroblasts revealed that the PROTAC bearing the succinic acid linker induced significantly greater Smad3 degradation at lower concentrations (effective at 25 nM) compared to the PEG-linked variant, indicating superior spatial orientation for productive ternary complex formation. Western blot analysis confirmed dose-dependent reduction of Smad3 protein levels without corresponding changes in Smad3 mRNA, verifying post-translational degradation. Furthermore, co-treatment with the proteasome inhibitor MG132 rescued Smad3 levels and revealed higher-molecular-weight ubiquitinated species, confirming degradation via the ubiquitin–proteasome pathway. In parallel, HIF-2α protein levels were markedly elevated in a dose- and time-dependent manner, while HIF-1α remained unaffected—consistent with the cell-type-specific expression of alternative E3 ligases for HIF-1α (e.g., RACK1, HAF, CHIP) in renal tubular cells. The S-enantiomer of the Smad3 ligand conferred significantly greater potency than the R-form in both Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization, leading to its selection for subsequent studies. Collectively, these results demonstrate that rational linker and chirality optimization yields a highly active double-target PROTAC with dual mechanisms of action, fulfilling the objectives of the invention.
