# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Prostate cancer is one of the most prevalent malignancies among men worldwide and represents a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in many developed nations. The disease typically originates in the glandular cells of the prostate and may remain asymptomatic in its early stages, often progressing silently until it reaches an advanced or metastatic state. Early detection is therefore critical to improving patient outcomes and enabling timely therapeutic intervention. Historically, the primary biomarker used for prostate cancer screening has been prostate-specific antigen (PSA), a glycoprotein produced by both normal and malignant prostate epithelial cells. Despite its widespread clinical adoption, the PSA test suffers from significant limitations that undermine its diagnostic reliability. Elevated PSA levels can result from benign prostatic hyperplasia, prostatitis, or even mechanical manipulation of the prostate during digital rectal examination, leading to false-positive results and unnecessary biopsies. Conversely, some aggressive prostate cancers produce minimal PSA, resulting in false-negative diagnoses and delayed treatment. These shortcomings underscore the urgent need for more specific and sensitive diagnostic markers capable of distinguishing indolent from aggressive disease and reducing overtreatment of non-life-threatening conditions.

Current diagnostic approaches beyond PSA include imaging modalities such as multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (mpMRI) and tissue-based histopathological evaluation following biopsy. However, these methods are either invasive, costly, or lack sufficient predictive power for individualized risk stratification. Moreover, existing molecular markers—such as PCA3, TMPRSS2-ERG fusions, and other genomic classifiers—while promising, have not yet achieved universal clinical integration due to variability in performance across diverse patient populations and technical complexities in assay standardization. There remains a compelling unmet need for novel biomarkers that reflect the underlying biology of prostate carcinogenesis and offer actionable insights into disease progression, prognosis, and therapeutic response.

In this context, soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) has emerged as a biologically relevant candidate. Unlike transmembrane adenylyl cyclases (tmACs), which are activated by G-protein-coupled receptors at the plasma membrane, sAC is an intracellular enzyme that generates cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) in response to bicarbonate and calcium ions, functioning independently of hormonal stimulation. Recent evidence suggests that sAC plays a pivotal role in regulating cellular processes such as proliferation, apoptosis, and migration—hallmarks of cancer biology. Given the dysregulation of cAMP signaling pathways in various malignancies, the investigation of sAC expression and activity in prostate cancer offers a promising avenue for both diagnostic innovation and therapeutic targeting. The present invention addresses this gap by providing methods for diagnosing prostate cancer based on sAC overexpression and for treating prostate cancer through the inhibition of sAC activity, thereby offering a non-invasive, mechanistically grounded approach to managing this pervasive disease.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for inhibiting the proliferation of prostate cancer cells by administering an inhibitor of soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) activity. This method exploits the discovery that sAC is overexpressed in prostate carcinoma tissues compared to benign prostatic tissue and that its enzymatic activity supports cancer cell proliferation through an EPAC/Rap1-dependent signaling pathway. Inhibition of sAC—whether by small molecule inhibitors, interfering RNA molecules, or other pharmacological agents—leads to cell cycle arrest at the G2/M checkpoint, suppression of cyclin B1 and CDK1 expression, and ultimately induction of apoptosis in prostate cancer cells, without reliance on protein kinase A (PKA) signaling. Additionally, the invention encompasses diagnostic methods for detecting prostate cancer by assaying biological samples for elevated levels of sAC gene expression or protein production. Such assays may involve immunohistochemical staining, quantitative PCR, Western blotting, or other analytical techniques to compare sAC levels in patient-derived samples against established controls. Based on the diagnostic findings, clinicians can prognosticate disease aggressiveness, calculate Gleason scores, perform staging evaluations, and select appropriate treatment regimens, including monotherapy with sAC inhibitors or combination therapies with existing prostate cancer treatments such as androgen deprivation therapy, chemotherapy, or radiation. Thus, the invention bridges diagnostic precision with targeted therapeutic intervention, offering a comprehensive strategy for the management of prostate cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

Soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) is a unique member of the adenylyl cyclase family that functions as a bicarbonate- and calcium-sensitive enzyme responsible for generating the second messenger cyclic AMP (cAMP) within intracellular compartments. Unlike transmembrane adenylyl cyclases (tmACs), which are tethered to the plasma membrane and activated by G-protein-coupled receptors, sAC is distributed throughout the cytosol and nucleus, enabling localized cAMP signaling that regulates diverse cellular functions including metabolism, ion transport, and gene transcription. In the context of cancer biology, cAMP signaling has long been recognized as a double-edged sword, capable of either promoting or suppressing proliferation depending on the cellular context, subcellular localization of cAMP pools, and downstream effector engagement. While tmAC-derived cAMP often signals through protein kinase A (PKA), sAC-generated cAMP preferentially activates exchange protein directly activated by cAMP (EPAC), a guanine nucleotide exchange factor for the small GTPase Rap1. This distinction is critical, as EPAC/Rap1 signaling has been implicated in mitogenic responses, cytoskeletal reorganization, and cell survival in various tumor types.

The role of cAMP signaling in controlling cellular proliferation and apoptosis has remained controversial due to conflicting reports across different cell systems. Some studies indicate that elevated cAMP promotes apoptosis under stress conditions, while others demonstrate pro-survival effects. This ambiguity arises partly from the compartmentalization of cAMP signals: distinct pools generated by tmACs versus sAC may engage different effectors and elicit opposing biological outcomes. The present invention resolves this uncertainty in prostate cancer by demonstrating that sAC-specific cAMP production drives proliferation via an EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf axis, independent of PKA. This finding motivated the focused study of sAC-dependent cAMP signaling in prostate carcinogenesis, particularly its nuclear localization and transcriptional consequences.

Immunohistochemical analyses revealed that sAC translocates from the cytosol to the nucleus in prostate cancer cells, where it modulates the activity of the cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB), a key transcription factor involved in cell growth and survival. Nuclear accumulation of sAC correlates with increased tumor aggressiveness and is especially pronounced at the invasive front of prostate carcinomas near the prostatic capsule, suggesting a role in local invasion and metastasis. Building upon this observation, the invention introduces a method for inhibiting the proliferation of prostate cancer cells by targeting sAC activity. Prostate cancer, as defined herein, refers to malignant neoplasms arising from the epithelial cells of the prostate gland, including both androgen-sensitive (e.g., LNCaP) and androgen-insensitive (e.g., PC3) subtypes. Prostate cancer cells are characterized by uncontrolled growth, resistance to apoptosis, and potential for metastasis.

The core of the therapeutic method involves administering a substance that inhibits the enzymatic activity of the sAC protein. Such inhibitors may be small molecules, exemplified by KH7, a selective sAC antagonist that reduces intracellular cAMP levels without affecting tmAC or soluble guanylyl cyclase at concentrations up to 100 μM. Other exemplary small molecule sAC inhibitors include analogs of KH7 with improved pharmacokinetic properties or reduced off-target effects. Alternatively, sAC inhibition may be achieved using interfering RNA molecules, such as short interfering RNA (siRNA) or short hairpin RNA (shRNA), which silence sAC gene expression post-transcriptionally. RNA interference (RNAi) is a well-established technique wherein double-stranded RNA triggers sequence-specific degradation of complementary mRNA. In the present context, siRNA duplexes targeting human sAC mRNA (e.g., accession number NM_001167749) effectively reduce sAC protein levels by ≥80%, as confirmed by Western blotting.

Administration of an sAC inhibitor to prostate cancer cells—either in vitro or in vivo—results in dose-dependent suppression of proliferation, induction of G2/M cell cycle arrest, downregulation of cyclin B1 and CDK1, and activation of apoptotic pathways, as evidenced by caspase-3 cleavage and increased sub-G1 DNA content. The invention further provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising an sAC inhibitor in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, excipient, or diluent suitable for systemic or localized delivery. Such compositions may be formulated for oral, intravenous, intratumoral, or other routes of administration and are intended for use in male subjects diagnosed with prostate cancer. A therapeutically effective amount is defined as the quantity of sAC inhibitor sufficient to achieve a measurable reduction in tumor burden, inhibition of cancer cell proliferation, or prolongation of survival, without causing unacceptable toxicity.

Combination therapy is also envisioned, wherein sAC inhibitors are co-administered with standard prostate cancer treatments such as androgen receptor antagonists (e.g., enzalutamide), chemotherapeutic agents (e.g., docetaxel), or radiotherapy, to enhance efficacy or overcome resistance. On the diagnostic front, the invention includes a method for diagnosing prostate cancer by obtaining a biological sample—such as a prostate biopsy, fine-needle aspirate, or liquid biopsy—and assaying it for sAC gene expression or protein production. Gene expression may be measured via quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR), RNA sequencing, or microarray analysis, while protein levels may be detected using immunohistochemistry (IHC), Western blotting, ELISA, or flow cytometry. IHC is particularly advantageous for spatial resolution, allowing assessment of sAC localization (cytoplasmic, nuclear, or apical/luminal) and intensity scoring via H-score methodology.

The human sAC gene undergoes alternative splicing to generate multiple isoforms, notably the full-length somatic isoform (sACfl) and the truncated testicular isoform (sACt). The amino acid sequences of these variants are disclosed herein, enabling the design of isoform-specific detection reagents. Antibodies or antigen-binding fragments—whether polyclonal, monoclonal, chimeric, humanized, or single-chain variable fragments (scFvs)—can be prepared against sAC epitopes using standard hybridoma or recombinant techniques. Polyclonal antibodies are raised by immunizing host animals (e.g., rabbits, goats) with purified sAC protein or peptides, followed by serum collection and purification. Monoclonal antibodies are generated via fusion of spleen cells from immunized mice with myeloma cells to produce immortal hybridomas secreting a single antibody specificity. Chimeric antibodies combine murine variable regions with human constant regions to reduce immunogenicity, while scFvs consist of linked VH and VL domains connected by a flexible peptide linker.

Antibody binding to sAC can be detected using labeled secondary antibodies conjugated to enzymes (e.g., horseradish peroxidase), fluorophores, or radioisotopes, enabling visualization through chromogenic, fluorescent, or luminescent readouts. Specific anti-sAC antibodies, such as clones R21 (recognizing both sACfl and sACt) and R40 (specific for sACt), are particularly useful for differential isoform detection. Overexpression of sAC is defined as a statistically significant increase in gene or protein levels relative to a control—such as benign prostatic tissue or normal prostate epithelial cells (e.g., PNT2 line)—typically quantified by H-score ≥150 or ≥1.5-fold mRNA elevation.

Based on diagnostic results, a method for selecting a treatment option is provided: a prostate cancer cell sample is obtained, assayed for sAC status, compared to control, and used to prognosticate disease course. Gleason scoring—a histological grading system based on glandular architecture—is calculated by summing the primary and secondary patterns (e.g., 3+4=7), with higher scores indicating worse prognosis. Additional staging tests (e.g., CT, bone scan, PSMA-PET) may be performed, and treatment is selected accordingly—ranging from active surveillance for low-risk disease to multimodal therapy for high-risk or metastatic cases. Thus, the invention enables precision oncology guided by sAC biomarker status.

### Example 1

This example demonstrates the immunohistochemical detection of sAC protein in human prostate tissue specimens. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded sections (5 μm thick) were deparaffinized and subjected to heat-induced epitope retrieval using the BOND-III Autostainer platform. Staining was performed with mouse anti-sAC monoclonal antibody R21-IHC at a 1:750 dilution, followed by alkaline phosphatase-based signal amplification and 3,3′-diaminobenzidine (DAB) chromogen development. Slides were evaluated by an experienced urologic pathologist using a semi-quantitative intensity scale: weak (1+), moderate (2+), strong (3+). An H-score was calculated as (%1+ × 1) + (%2+ × 2) + (%3+ × 3), yielding values between 0 and 300. Subcellular localization—cytoplasmic, nuclear, or apical/luminal—was recorded, with nuclear positivity defined by DAB precipitate obscuring hematoxylin-stained nuclei. Analysis of paired benign and malignant tissues from the same patients revealed significantly higher H-scores in carcinoma (mean 189.2) versus benign glands (mean 144.3; p<0.01). Notably, sAC overexpression was most prominent at the tumor’s leading edge near the prostatic capsule, suggesting a role in local invasion. These findings confirm sAC protein overproduction in prostate cancer and validate its utility as a diagnostic biomarker.

### Example 2

This example extends the immunohistochemical analysis to compare staining patterns using three distinct anti-sAC antibodies: R21, R40, and R52. Tissue sections were processed identically to Example 1. R21, which recognizes both sACfl and sACt isoforms, showed strong cytoplasmic and nuclear staining in malignant glands. R40, specific for the sACt isoform, exhibited only weak signal, indicating predominant expression of the somatic isoform in prostate cancer. R52, targeting a different epitope, yielded staining intensity comparable to R21. Quantitative H-scoring confirmed consistent overexpression across tumor samples regardless of antibody used, though absolute intensities varied. Comparison across tumor types—including adenocarcinoma, ductal carcinoma, and neuroendocrine variants—revealed highest sAC levels in conventional acinar adenocarcinoma. Benign tissues showed uniform low-level cytoplasmic staining without nuclear accumulation. These results reinforce that sAC protein is overproduced in prostate cancer, predominantly as the sACfl isoform, and that multiple antibody reagents can reliably detect this abnormality.

### Example 3

This example investigates the functional consequences of sAC inhibition in prostate cancer cell lines. Androgen-sensitive LNCaP and androgen-insensitive PC3 cells, along with normal prostate epithelial PNT2 cells, were cultured under standard conditions. Western blotting confirmed high sAC expression in cancer lines versus PNT2, with minimal sACt detected using R40 antibody. Cells were treated with KH7 (20 μM) or its inactive analog KH7.15 for 48 hours. KH7 reduced intracellular cAMP by >50%, suppressed proliferation (measured by trypan blue exclusion and hemocytometer counting), and induced apoptosis (evidenced by LDH release and caspase-3 cleavage). In parallel, LNCaP cells were transfected with sAC-targeting siRNA or infected with sAC-shRNA adenovirus, achieving ≥80% knockdown. Both genetic approaches replicated KH7’s effects: reduced cell numbers, increased cytotoxicity, and apoptotic markers. Control treatments (scrambled siRNA/shRNA or KH7.15) had no impact. These data establish that sAC activity is essential for prostate cancer cell proliferation and survival, validating sAC inhibition as a viable therapeutic strategy.

### Example 4

This example elucidates the mechanism underlying sAC-mediated proliferation control. LNCaP cells treated with KH7 underwent cell cycle analysis via propidium iodide staining and FACS. Results showed accumulation in G2/M phase (from 15% to 35%) and reduction in G1, indicating G2/M arrest. Western blotting revealed downregulation of cyclin B1 and CDK1 (total and phosphorylated forms), but not G1/S cyclins (D1, D3, E2). To dissect cAMP effectors, cells were co-treated with PKA modulators (N6-benzoyl-cAMP, H-89, Rp-cAMP-S) or EPAC activator 8-pCPT-cAMP. PKA manipulations had no effect on proliferation or cell cycle, whereas 8-pCPT-cAMP reversed KH7-induced arrest and restored Rap1-GTP levels. Rap1 activity assays confirmed KH7 depleted active Rap1 without altering total Rap1 or B-Raf expression, but suppressed phospho-B-Raf. These findings demonstrate that sAC drives proliferation via EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf signaling, not PKA, and that EPAC activation rescues sAC inhibition. All experimental protocols incorporated appropriate controls and statistical validation (n≥3, p<0.05).
