# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the field of nonlinear dynamical systems and, more specifically, to methods and apparatus for generating, stabilizing, and entangling controlled unstable periodic orbits—referred to herein as cupolets—in chaotic systems. The invention further pertains to the creation of entangled pairs and multi-cupolet networks through information-theoretic interactions mediated by exchange functions, and to applications of such entangled states in secure memory devices, logic gates, and classical analogs of quantum phenomena. Chaotic systems, as used herein, refer to deterministic dynamical systems exhibiting sensitive dependence on initial conditions, characterized by a positive maximal Lyapunov exponent and the presence of a strange attractor densely populated with unstable periodic orbits (UPOs). Entanglement, in the context of this invention, denotes a mutually stabilizing interaction between two or more cupolets wherein the stability of each is dynamically maintained by the symbolic output—specifically, the visitation sequence—of its partner(s), without the need for external control once entanglement is established. This form of entanglement is distinct from quantum entanglement but exhibits analogous properties such as measurement sensitivity, state correlation, and entropy reduction, thereby establishing a novel bridge between classical chaos theory and concepts traditionally reserved for quantum mechanics.

## BACKGROUND

Chaos theory has long been recognized for its ability to describe complex, unpredictable behavior arising from simple deterministic rules. Central to this theory is the notion that chaotic systems, despite their apparent randomness, possess an underlying geometric structure composed of an infinite set of unstable periodic orbits (UPOs) embedded within their attractors. These UPOs serve as fundamental building blocks, enabling the characterization of global dynamics through local periodic solutions. Over the past several decades, researchers have developed control strategies to stabilize these otherwise transient orbits, most notably the method proposed by Hayes, Grebogi, and Ott (HGO), which uses small perturbations applied at strategic points—typically defined via Poincaré surfaces of section—to steer a chaotic trajectory onto a desired UPO. Building upon this foundation, the concept of cupolets emerged: controlled, stabilized periodic orbits generated by applying repeating binary control sequences that dictate when macrocontrols (lobe-switching perturbations) and microcontrols (bin-centering corrections) are applied. Cupolets exhibit remarkable properties, including independence from initial conditions and a one-to-one correspondence between control sequence and resulting orbit, making them highly reproducible and suitable for information encoding.

Concurrently, quantum mechanics has revealed phenomena such as entanglement—wherein the states of two or more particles become correlated in ways that defy classical explanation—and wave function collapse upon measurement. While quantum systems are linear and governed by the Schrödinger equation, chaotic systems are inherently nonlinear, raising questions about whether classical systems can exhibit quantum-like behavior. Recent experimental and theoretical work has suggested possible connections: quantum systems initialized in classically chaotic regimes show enhanced entanglement, and signatures of chaos appear in quantum scarring and fidelity decay. However, a rigorous classical analog of entanglement remained elusive until the discovery that interacting chaotic systems, when stabilized onto cupolets, can enter into self-sustaining, mutually stabilizing states—termed chaotic entanglement—wherein each system’s periodicity is preserved by the symbolic dynamics of the other. This phenomenon, mediated by exchange functions that transform visitation sequences into control instructions, not only mimics key aspects of quantum entanglement but also enables practical applications in computing and secure information storage. The background art thus establishes the theoretical basis for controlling chaos and hints at deeper parallels between classical and quantum worlds, yet fails to disclose a systematic method for generating, maintaining, and utilizing entangled cupolet pairs or multi-cupolet networks as described herein.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces cupolets—controlled, stabilized periodic orbits of chaotic systems—and discloses a novel method for creating entangled cupolet pairs through mutual stabilization mediated by an exchange function. The invention further provides circuitry configured to implement this entanglement process using physical realizations of chaotic systems, such as Chua’s double-scroll oscillator implemented in integrated circuits. A method for maintaining entangled cupolets is disclosed, wherein once entanglement is established, no external control is required; instead, each cupolet’s visitation sequence is processed by the exchange function to generate the precise control sequence needed to sustain its partner’s periodic orbit. The invention describes a logic gate architecture wherein an entangled cupolet pair, combined with a logic-oriented exchange function, performs Boolean operations by converting input visitation sequences into emitted control bits that determine output states. Additionally, a secure memory device is enabled by storing information in the control sequences of entangled cupolets, where retrieval requires knowledge of the entanglement protocol to avoid destructive measurement. The invention extends to multi-cupolet entanglement, wherein three or more chaotic systems are interconnected such that each receives control signals derived from the visitation sequences of others, forming stable lattices of mutually sustaining orbits. This is generalized to multiple chaotic systems, potentially of different types, linked via tailored exchange functions. Applications include ultra-secure communication, low-power computing, and classical simulation of quantum information protocols. Key benefits include entropy reduction during entanglement (an information-theoretic reversal of disorder), resilience to minor perturbations when measured knowledgeably, and the potential for spontaneous entanglement in natural systems. In summary, the invention establishes chaotic entanglement as a robust, controllable, and scalable phenomenon with significant technological implications, bridging classical nonlinear dynamics and quantum-inspired information processing.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention is founded upon the concept of cupolets—Chaotic, Unstable, Periodic, Orbit-LETS—which are stabilized periodic trajectories extracted from chaotic systems through the application of binary control sequences. Unlike naturally occurring unstable periodic orbits (UPOs), which require sophisticated numerical techniques like Newton-Raphson iteration for stabilization, cupolets are generated efficiently using an adaptation of the Hayes-Grebogi-Ott (HGO) control scheme. This method involves defining Poincaré surfaces of section intersecting the chaotic attractor, partitioning these surfaces into discrete control bins, and applying perturbations only when the system trajectory intersects a bin. Microcontrols reset the trajectory to the bin center, while macrocontrols—minimal perturbations designed to induce a lobe switch after a specified number of loops—are triggered by ‘1’ bits in the control sequence, with ‘0’ bits invoking only microcontrols. Repeating a binary control sequence causes the system to stabilize onto a unique cupolet, independent of initial conditions, establishing a bijective mapping between control code and orbit.

Prior art includes the HGO method and early cupolet generation, but fails to disclose entanglement or exchange-mediated mutual stabilization. Entanglement between cupolets is defined as a state wherein two cupolets, each belonging to a separate chaotic system, maintain each other’s stability through continuous exchange of symbolic information. This exchange is governed by an exchange function—a deterministic algorithm that transforms a cupolet’s visitation sequence (the binary record of lobe visits) into an emitted sequence, which serves as the control input for the partner system. Pure entanglement occurs when the visitation sequence of one cupolet is identical to the control sequence required by its partner, requiring no transformation (i.e., the exchange function is the identity map). Physical realizability is supported by the fact that many exchange functions model real-world interactions, such as integrate-and-fire dynamics in neurons or photon emission thresholds in lasers.

Cupolets are generated by first simulating a chaotic system, such as the double-scroll oscillator described by Chua’s equations, and applying a repeating binary control sequence via the HGO-derived scheme. Controlling chaotic systems in this manner allows precise navigation of the attractor’s topology. For example, in the double-scroll system—with parameters \(C_1 = 1/9\), \(C_2 = 1\), \(L = 1/7\), \(G = 0.7\), \(m_0 = -0.5\), \(m_1 = -0.8\), \(B_p = 1\)—the attractor comprises two lobes, and control planes are placed perpendicular to the flow near each lobe’s center. A Poincaré surface of section is defined by these planes, and each is partitioned into, e.g., 2000 bins. The coding function maps each bin intersection to a bit (e.g., ‘0’ for left lobe, ‘1’ for right), generating the visitation sequence. Macrocontrols are precomputed to ensure lobe transitions occur as dictated by the control sequence.

Cupolets exhibit diverse spectral signatures and time-domain waveforms, enabling applications in image and music compression by representing complex signals as sequences of cupolet transitions. Theoretically, cupolets can be transformed into wavelet-like basis functions via Fourier decomposition, facilitating signal processing. Transitions between cupolets are achieved by switching control sequences, with transients resolved due to the injective control-orbit mapping.

Entangling two chaotic systems begins by externally stabilizing System I onto cupolet \(C_A\) using control sequence \(S_A\). As \(C_A\) evolves, its visitation sequence \(V_A\) is fed to an exchange function, which outputs emitted sequence \(E_B\), applied to System II to stabilize cupolet \(C_B\). Concurrently, \(V_B\) is processed to yield \(E_A\), which sustains \(C_A\). If \(E_A = S_A\) and \(E_B = S_B\), entanglement is achieved. Properties include mutual stabilization, measurement sensitivity (blind measurements destroy entanglement), and entropy reduction (Kolmogorov entropy drops to zero).

Exchange functions fall into categories: LogicGate (implements AND, OR, etc.), IntegrateAndFire (accumulates bits until threshold), Preponderance (outputs majority bit over window), Complement (inverts bits), NOutOfMtoL (maps N-of-M patterns to L-bit outputs), and ZerosAndOnes (counts runs). For instance, a LogicGate exchange function converts two input bits (from visitation subsequences) into a single output bit via Boolean logic, repeated to form the emitted sequence.

Hardware implementation uses arrays of Chua circuits fabricated on monolithic chips, with nonlinear negative resistance elements (e.g., tunnel diodes) realizing the piecewise-linear \(g(v)\) function. Such platforms enable scalable chaotic entanglement networks. The invention concludes by encompassing all embodiments wherein chaotic systems are entangled via cupolet-mediated symbolic exchange, forming the basis for classical analogs of quantum information processing.
