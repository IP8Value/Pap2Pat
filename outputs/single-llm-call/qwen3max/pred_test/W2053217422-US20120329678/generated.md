# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

High throughput sequencing technologies, also known as next-generation sequencing (NGS) platforms, have revolutionized genomic research by enabling the rapid and cost-effective generation of massive amounts of sequence data. These technologies, including those developed by Illumina, Roche 454, and Applied Biosystems SOLiD, produce millions to billions of short DNA reads in a single run. However, the relatively short read lengths—typically ranging from 36 to 150 base pairs—pose significant challenges for de novo genome assembly, particularly in complex genomes containing repetitive elements, segmental duplications, or structural variants. To overcome these limitations, mate-pair libraries, which provide long-range genomic information by linking two distal regions of a large DNA fragment, are essential for scaffolding contigs, resolving repeats, and detecting structural variations such as insertions, deletions, inversions, and translocations.

Despite their utility, current methods for constructing mate-pair libraries suffer from several critical limitations. Commercial kits, such as Illumina’s Mate Pair Library Prep Kit, are generally restricted to insert sizes of 2–5 kb and rely on intramolecular ligation to circularize large DNA fragments. This ligation-based circularization is inefficient for fragments larger than 10 kb, leading to low library complexity and high rates of chimeric artifacts. Moreover, because the junction between the two distal ends lacks a defined sequence signature, it is impossible to precisely identify the boundary between the paired reads during sequencing. Consequently, longer sequencing reads risk spanning the junction, resulting in chimeric sequences that compromise downstream analysis. Alternative approaches, such as fosmid- or BAC-based cloning, can generate larger inserts (up to 40 kb), but these are clone-based, labor-intensive, and yield limited library complexity due to biases in bacterial transformation and propagation. The Roche 454 Jump Recombi Paired-End kit utilizes the Cre-loxP recombination system to circularize DNA and introduces a defined linker at the junction, enabling unambiguous identification of mate-pair boundaries. However, the 454 platform has low throughput and high cost per base, limiting its widespread adoption. Similarly, the Applied Biosystems SOLiD mate-pair method employs restriction enzymes like EcoP15I to generate paired tags but is constrained to insert sizes under 10 kb and involves multiple ligation steps that reduce efficiency and increase background noise. Collectively, these limitations underscore the need for a robust, scalable, in vitro method capable of generating high-complexity, large-insert mate-pair libraries with a defined junction site, compatible with high-throughput, low-cost sequencing platforms such as Illumina.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for making mate-pair libraries suitable for high-throughput sequencing, particularly on Illumina platforms. The method begins by fragmenting target genomic DNA into large fragments of a desired size range, typically between 5 kb and 22 kb or more, using mechanical shearing or other physical means. The fragmented DNA is then subjected to end-repair to generate blunt, phosphorylated ends suitable for ligation. Following end-repair, the DNA fragments are size-selected to enrich for molecules within the target insert size distribution. Adaptors containing loxP recombination sites and Illumina-compatible primer binding sites (P1 and P2) are ligated to both ends of the size-selected DNA fragments. Critically, these adaptors are designed so that each end of the fragment receives a distinct adaptor—one bearing a P1-loxP sequence and the other a P2-loxP sequence—ensuring directional ligation and proper orientation for downstream processing.

After adaptor ligation, nicks between the DNA fragment and the adaptors are sealed using a DNA polymerase and ligase to ensure covalent continuity. The linear DNA molecules are then circularized not through inefficient ligation, but via Cre recombinase-mediated intramolecular recombination between the loxP sites on opposite ends. This recombination event brings the two distal ends of the original large DNA fragment into proximity, forming a circular molecule in which the original genomic termini are now adjacent and flanked by the P1 and P2 primer sites in opposing orientations. Non-circularized linear DNA is removed enzymatically using an ATP-dependent exonuclease, thereby enriching for successfully recombined circles. The circularized DNA is then fragmented a second time, either by restriction enzyme digestion or random shearing, to generate linear fragments of a size suitable for sequencing. These fragments undergo self-ligation, which preferentially joins compatible ends, including those with overhangs generated by restriction enzymes. Finally, inverse PCR is performed using the P1 and P2 primers, which amplify only those molecules that contain both primer sites in the correct orientation—namely, those derived from the recombined junction of the original mate-pair ends. The resulting amplicons constitute a high-quality mate-pair library fully compatible with Illumina sequencing, featuring a defined junction site that enables accurate demarcation of paired reads and minimizes chimerism.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Definitions

As used herein, a “mate-pair library” refers to a collection of DNA molecules wherein each molecule contains two distal genomic sequences derived from the ends of a large DNA fragment, joined together in a defined orientation and separated by a known or identifiable junction sequence. “Forward and reverse primers” denote oligonucleotides that bind to complementary strands of DNA and initiate amplification in opposite directions; in the context of this invention, the P1 and P2 primers serve as forward and reverse primers for inverse PCR. A “distal site” refers to a genomic location that is physically distant—typically several kilobases or more—from another site on the same DNA molecule. “Circularized DNA” describes a covalently closed DNA molecule formed by joining the two ends of a linear fragment, either by ligation or site-specific recombination. “Primer sites in opposing directions” indicates that the binding sites for the forward and reverse primers are oriented such that amplification proceeds outward from the junction toward the original genomic ends. A “recombinase” is an enzyme that catalyzes site-specific recombination between defined DNA sequences; in the preferred embodiment, Cre recombinase is used. “Recombination sites” are specific DNA sequences recognized by a recombinase; loxP is the canonical 34-bp recombination site for Cre recombinase.

### I. Introduction

The present invention introduces improved methods for generating mate-pair libraries that overcome the inefficiencies and ambiguities of existing protocols. By leveraging the Cre-loxP recombination system, the method achieves highly efficient circularization of large DNA fragments—an advantage over ligation-based approaches that suffer from reduced kinetics at larger insert sizes. Furthermore, the integration of Illumina P1 and P2 primer sites directly into the loxP-containing adaptors enables a streamlined workflow with fewer enzymatic steps. The use of an inverse PCR strategy ensures selective amplification of only those molecules that contain the correctly recombined junction, dramatically reducing background and increasing library specificity. This approach not only enhances library complexity and yield but also introduces a molecularly defined junction site that facilitates bioinformatic separation of true mate-pairs from chimeric artifacts during data analysis.

### II. Generation of Mate-Pair Libraries

The method begins with the fragmentation of source genomic DNA using hydrodynamic shearing, sonication, or nebulization to achieve a target size distribution (e.g., 5 kb, 12 kb, or 22 kb). The fragmented DNA is then end-repaired using a mixture of T4 DNA polymerase, Klenow fragment, and T4 polynucleotide kinase in the presence of dNTPs to generate blunt, 5′-phosphorylated ends. Size selection is performed via agarose gel electrophoresis for smaller inserts or pulse-field gel electrophoresis (PFGE) for larger fragments (>15 kb), followed by purification using silica columns or magnetic beads. Adaptors containing loxP sites fused to Illumina P1 or P2 sequences are ligated to the ends using a rapid ligase such as Quick Ligase. Nicks are filled in using a strand-displacing DNA polymerase like Bst polymerase to ensure complete covalent linkage.

Circularization is achieved by incubating the adaptor-ligated DNA with Cre recombinase, which catalyzes recombination between the loxP sites on opposite ends, forming circular molecules. Linear DNA is degraded using Plasmid-Safe™ ATP-dependent DNase, which selectively digests linear but not circular DNA. The purified circles are then cleaved using a restriction enzyme such as NlaIII, which cuts frequently in the genome and generates 4-bp overhangs, or alternatively by random shearing. The resulting linear fragments undergo self-ligation, a process significantly enhanced by cohesive overhangs. Inverse PCR using P1 and P2 primers amplifies only those fragments containing the recombined P1-loxP-P2 junction. The amplified products are size-selected (typically 300–600 bp) and sequenced on an Illumina platform.

The method is highly adaptable: alternative recombinase systems (e.g., Flp-FRT) may be substituted; different restriction enzymes (e.g., HpyCH4IV for 2-bp overhangs) can be used depending on desired ligation efficiency; various DNA polymerases (Phusion, Q5), ligases (T4 DNA ligase), and exonucleases may be employed; and the adaptors can be modified to include barcodes or platform-specific sequences. Sequencing may be performed on any NGS platform with appropriate primer modifications.

### III. Software and Data Analysis

Post-sequencing, mate-pair reads are analyzed using standard bioinformatic pipelines. Sequence alignment algorithms such as BWA or Bowtie2 map reads to a reference genome. For de novo assembly, DNA assembly algorithms like Velvet, SOAPdenovo, or SPAdes incorporate mate-pair constraints to scaffold contigs. Reads are trimmed at the restriction enzyme recognition site (e.g., CATG for NlaIII) to prevent junction-spanning artifacts, ensuring that each read originates entirely from one end of the original large fragment. This trimming step is critical for maintaining mapping accuracy and avoiding false structural variant calls.

### IV. Kits

Kits for practicing the invention include pre-formulated reagents: loxP-P1 and loxP-P2 adaptors, Cre recombinase, restriction enzymes (e.g., NlaIII), DNA repair enzymes, ligase, PCR master mix with high-fidelity polymerase, and purification beads. Optional components include size-selection gels, PFGE cassettes, and bioinformatic software for read trimming and alignment.

## EXAMPLES

The CLIP-PE (Cre-LoxP Inverse PCR Paired-End) methodology was developed to address the shortcomings of existing mate-pair library construction techniques. Unlike the Illumina jumping method, which relies on inefficient ligation and lacks a defined junction, CLIP-PE uses Cre-loxP recombination for high-efficiency circularization and introduces a recognizable junction via restriction enzyme sites. Existing methods suffer from low complexity, high chimera rates, and insert size limitations; CLIP-PE overcomes these by combining enzymatic precision with selective amplification.

CLIP-PE library construction involves shearing genomic DNA, end-repair, size selection, adaptor ligation (with loxP-P1/P2), nick filling, Cre-mediated circularization, exonuclease treatment, restriction digestion (e.g., NlaIII), self-ligation, and inverse PCR. As illustrated in FIG. 1, this workflow ensures that only correctly recombined molecules are amplified.

When compared to the Illumina jumping method using *Haloterrigena turkmenica* DNA, CLIP-PE produced 20.6% non-redundant mate-pairs versus 8.7%, with far fewer small-fragment artifacts (11% vs. 91%) and lower chimera rates (2.3% vs. 9.2%). Clone coverage was dramatically higher (4,746× vs. 18×), and gap numbers were reduced from 767 to 7.

Library quality was assessed using two criteria: percentage of non-redundant pairs (uniquely mapped, correct insert size) and chimeric rate (misoriented or interchromosomal mappings). CLIP-PE consistently outperformed Illumina’s method across these metrics.

Ligation efficiency critically impacts library quality. Restriction enzymes generating 4-bp overhangs (NlaIII) yielded 11.1% non-redundant pairs, versus 4.0% for 2-bp overhangs (HpyCH4IV) and 2.5% for blunt ends (random shearing). FIG. 4 demonstrates that shorter overhangs increase short-insert background. Despite concerns about genome coverage bias due to non-random restriction sites, empirical testing across six genomes (28–74% GC) showed no detectable bias, likely compensated by initial random shearing.

Combining CLIP-PE libraries with short Illumina reads significantly improved *S. cerevisiae* genome assemblies. Scaffold N50 increased from 102.9 kb (short reads alone) to ~750 kb with CLIP-PE, comparable to simulated ideal mate-pairs. Mis-assembly rates remained low, confirming high fidelity. CLIP-PE is thus highly beneficial for genome assembly and finishing, with potential applications in structural variant detection, metagenomics, and haplotype phasing.

### CLIP-PE Method can Consistently Generate High Quality Mate Pair Libraries

Twelve-kilobase and 22-kb CLIP-PE libraries from *S. cerevisiae* demonstrated high reproducibility and quality. The 12-kb libraries averaged 59% non-redundant pairs, with <0.05% misoriented reads. The 22-kb libraries, though slightly less efficient (11.1% non-redundant), still vastly outperformed conventional methods and enabled effective scaffolding.

### Ligation Efficiency Affects the Productivity and Quality of CLIP-PE Libraries

As detailed above, ligation efficiency directly correlates with overhang length. NlaIII (4-bp) > HpyCH4IV (2-bp) > blunt-end shearing in performance. While restriction-based fragmentation raises theoretical concerns about coverage bias, practical results show uniform representation across diverse genomes. Using multiple enzymes can further mitigate any residual bias.

### Discussion

Large-insert mate-pair libraries are essential for resolving complex genomic architectures. The Cre-loxP system enables efficient circularization of fragments up to 22 kb or more, surpassing ligation-based methods. Unlike in vivo fosmid approaches, CLIP-PE is entirely in vitro, ensuring high complexity and scalability. The inverse PCR strategy minimizes background by amplifying only correct junctions. Compared to 454 and SOLiD methods, CLIP-PE offers superior compatibility with Illumina’s high-throughput, low-cost platform. The defined junction site allows safe use of longer reads (e.g., 2×76 bp), enhancing mappability. Although recombination efficiency may decrease slightly with very large inserts, optimization of Cre concentration and reaction conditions mitigates this. Overall, CLIP-PE represents a versatile, robust, and high-fidelity method for mate-pair library construction with broad applications in genomics.

## Methods

### Illumina Library Preparation

Standard Illumina libraries were prepared using the manufacturer’s paired-end kit without PCR amplification. Jumping libraries used the Mate-Pair Library Prep Kit V2 with 5 µg genomic DNA.

### CLIP-PE Library Preparation

Genomic DNA (5–30 µg) was sheared to target size (5, 12, or 22 kb) using a HydroShear device. Ends were repaired with T4 DNA polymerase, Klenow, PNK, and dNTPs. DNA was purified with Agencourt beads, ligated to loxP-P1/P2 adaptors, and size-selected by gel or PFGE. Nick filling used Bst polymerase. Cre recombination (37°C, 45 min) was followed by Plasmid-Safe™ treatment. Circular DNA was digested with NlaIII, self-ligated, and amplified by inverse PCR with P1/P2 primers. Products were gel-purified (300–600 bp).

### Illumina Sequencing

Libraries were sequenced on GAIIx with 2×76 bp reads.

### Post-Sequencing Analysis

Reads were trimmed after the NlaIII site (CATG); average post-trim length was 70 bp. Alignment used BWA.

### Data Simulations and Genome Assembly

Simulated reads were generated with wgsim; assemblies used Velvet with k-mer optimization. Accuracy was assessed with dnadiff, evaluating relocations, translocations, and inversions.
