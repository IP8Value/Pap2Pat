# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Parallel imaging in magnetic resonance (MR) has become a cornerstone technique for accelerating data acquisition while preserving diagnostic image quality. By exploiting the spatial sensitivity profiles of multiple receiver coils arranged around the region of interest, parallel imaging enables undersampling of k-space—typically along the phase-encoding direction—thereby reducing scan time without proportionally sacrificing spatial resolution. This acceleration is particularly valuable in dynamic imaging applications such as cardiac cine, where rapid physiological motion necessitates high temporal resolution. Over the past two decades, numerous parallel imaging algorithms have been developed, with linear reconstruction methods forming the dominant paradigm in clinical practice.

Linear approaches to parallel imaging, such as SENSE (Sensitivity Encoding) and GRAPPA (GeneRalized Autocalibrating Partially Parallel Acquisitions), rely on pre-estimated or autocalibrated coil sensitivity maps to reconstruct images from undersampled k-space data. In SENSE, coil sensitivities are used to unfold aliased images in the image domain, whereas GRAPPA operates directly in k-space by interpolating missing k-space lines using linear combinations of acquired neighboring points weighted by calibration-derived kernel coefficients. These methods assume that coil sensitivities remain constant over time, which is generally valid for static or slowly varying anatomical structures. However, in highly dynamic imaging scenarios—such as real-time cardiac cine during or immediately after physical exertion—this assumption breaks down due to respiratory motion, changes in heart position, and variations in blood flow, all of which can alter the effective coil sensitivity profiles across time frames.

The limitations of linear parallel imaging approaches become especially pronounced in stress cardiac imaging. Following treadmill exercise, patients often exhibit tachycardia, irregular breathing patterns, and inability to comply with breath-holding instructions, rendering conventional segmented cine acquisitions impractical. Real-time imaging is thus the only viable alternative, but it typically requires high acceleration factors (e.g., R = 4 or greater) to achieve sufficient temporal resolution. At such high acceleration rates, linear methods suffer from noise amplification, residual aliasing artifacts, and reduced signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), particularly when coil sensitivities are inaccurately estimated or assumed static. Moreover, the use of fixed autocalibration signal (ACS) lines averaged over time fails to capture frame-specific sensitivity variations, leading to suboptimal reconstruction fidelity. These shortcomings underscore the need for a more adaptive and temporally aware parallel imaging framework capable of jointly estimating time-varying coil sensitivities and reconstructing high-quality dynamic images from highly accelerated data.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention encompasses preferred embodiments directed to a novel method and system for parallel imaging with temporal sensitivity estimation in magnetic resonance reconstruction, herein referred to as Temporal Sensitivity Parallel Imaging Reconstruction Technique (TSPIRIT). The invention is motivated by the critical need for improved image quality and SNR in real-time cardiac cine imaging under stress conditions, where conventional linear parallel imaging methods fail to adequately account for temporal dynamics in coil sensitivity profiles. To address this, the invention introduces a self-consistent, non-linear reconstruction framework that integrates temporal sensitivity estimation with spatial regularization, thereby enabling robust, high-fidelity image reconstruction even at high acceleration factors and in the presence of significant physiological motion.

A first aspect of the invention provides a method for reconstructing magnetic resonance images from undersampled k-space data acquired using a multi-coil array, wherein coil sensitivity information is dynamically estimated for each temporal frame rather than assumed constant. This temporal sensitivity estimation is performed iteratively within a self-consistent reconstruction loop that jointly optimizes image content and coil sensitivities, ensuring mutual consistency between the reconstructed images and the derived sensitivity maps.

A second aspect of the invention incorporates spatial regularization into the reconstruction process to suppress noise and enhance structural coherence across frames. This regularization term penalizes deviations from expected spatial smoothness or sparsity in transform domains, thereby improving SNR without introducing blurring or loss of diagnostic detail.

A third aspect of the invention enables fully automated, real-time-compatible reconstruction without requiring user-defined parameters or external calibration scans. The method leverages time-interleaved k-space sampling to generate autocalibration data specific to each temporal window, facilitating accurate, frame-wise sensitivity estimation without additional scan time.

It is expressly disclaimed that the invention is limited to cardiac imaging or to any particular MR sequence, field strength, coil configuration, or acceleration factor. The disclosed method is broadly applicable to any dynamic MR application involving time-varying coil sensitivities, including but not limited to abdominal, fetal, or interventional imaging.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DRAWINGS AND PRESENTLY PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

Linear parallel imaging algorithms, such as GRAPPA and SPIRIT (Iterative Self-Consistent Parallel Imaging Reconstruction), form the foundation of many clinical MR protocols. These methods operate under the assumption that coil sensitivity profiles are stationary across the duration of the scan. While adequate for static or quasi-static imaging, this assumption leads to reconstruction errors in dynamic settings where respiration, cardiac motion, or patient movement induce temporal variations in the effective coil sensitivities.

To overcome these limitations, the present invention introduces self-consistent parallel imaging with temporal sensitivity estimation, termed TSPIRIT. TSPIRIT extends the SPIRIT framework by incorporating a temporal dimension into the sensitivity estimation process, allowing coil sensitivities to be updated on a per-frame basis in a manner consistent with the evolving image content.

The TSPIRIT reconstruction begins with the acquisition of magnetic resonance data using a multi-channel receiver coil array, typically comprising 16 to 64 elements. Data are acquired using a time-interleaved k-space sampling scheme, wherein each temporal frame contains a subset of k-space lines, and autocalibration signal (ACS) lines are generated by averaging corresponding k-space locations across multiple frames. This approach preserves temporal fidelity while providing sufficient calibration data for sensitivity estimation.

Following acquisition, an initial linear reconstruction—such as TGRAPPA—is performed to generate a preliminary set of image frames. These frames undergo temporal filtering, for example via Karhunen-Loève Transform (KLT) compression, to isolate dominant temporal modes and suppress noise. The filtered frames are then used to estimate coil sensitivity information through an iterative self-constrained calibration process that enforces consistency between the sensitivity maps and the reconstructed images.

Subsequently, a non-linear reconstruction is executed using a solver that minimizes a cost function comprising data fidelity, spatial regularization, and self-consistency terms. In preferred embodiments, this is implemented using a least-squares QR (LSQR) matrix inversion solver followed by a non-linear conjugate gradient optimizer that refines the solution while enforcing spatial smoothness or sparsity constraints.

The reconstructed complex-valued images from each coil are combined using a sum-of-squares method to produce a final magnitude image series. This output data may undergo further processing, such as intensity normalization or motion correction, before being stored in memory, transmitted to a remote workstation, or displayed on a monitor for clinical interpretation.

The method is implemented as a software program executable on a processor within an MR system, which includes memory for storing raw k-space data, intermediate reconstructions, and final images, as well as a display for visualizing results. The program may be written in C++, Python, MATLAB, or other suitable languages and integrated into the scanner’s reconstruction pipeline.

In clinical testing involving ten healthy volunteers undergoing real-time exercise stress cine imaging at 1.5T with a 32-channel coil and acceleration factor of 4, TSPIRIT demonstrated a mean SNR gain of 38.2% compared to conventional TGRAPPA, without increasing ghosting artifacts attributable to chest wall motion. Quantitative analysis using peak spatial cross-correlation ratios confirmed artifact levels were statistically unchanged (P=0.960), while visual assessment corroborated superior image quality.

The flexibility of the method allows acts in the reconstruction flow—such as filtering, sensitivity estimation, and non-linear optimization—to be reordered, repeated, or adapted based on application-specific requirements. Alternative system configurations may include cloud-based reconstruction servers or embedded GPU accelerators to enable real-time display during scanning.

Thus, TSPIRIT represents a significant advancement in parallel imaging, offering enhanced SNR, robustness to motion, and compatibility with high-acceleration real-time protocols, thereby expanding the clinical utility of stress cardiac MR and other dynamic imaging applications.
