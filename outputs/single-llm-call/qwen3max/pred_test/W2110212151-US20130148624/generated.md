# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Wireless communication systems, particularly those conforming to the Third Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) Long-Term Evolution (LTE) standard, are engineered to deliver high peak data rates, enhanced spectral efficiency, improved cell-edge throughput, and scalable bandwidth capabilities. Central to achieving these performance objectives is the downlink packet scheduler, which governs how physical resources are allocated among multiple user equipment (UE) devices within a cellular network. The downlink scheduler plays a pivotal role in determining overall system performance by balancing competing demands such as throughput maximization, fairness among users, quality of service (QoS) guarantees, and power efficiency. In LTE networks, where all transmissions are inherently packet-switched, the scheduler must dynamically allocate physical resource blocks (PRBs) across both time and frequency domains to meet diverse traffic requirements while operating under strict power constraints at the evolved Node B (eNB).

The importance of the downlink scheduler cannot be overstated, as it directly influences key performance indicators including average user throughput, cell capacity, latency, and energy consumption. Efficient scheduling ensures that available radio resources are utilized optimally, especially in heterogeneous traffic environments comprising both real-time (RT) and non-real-time (NRT) data flows. RT traffic—such as voice or video streaming—typically requires constant bit rate (CBR) delivery with stringent delay constraints, whereas NRT traffic—like web browsing or file downloads—operates on a best-effort basis without hard deadlines but benefits from higher aggregate throughput. A well-designed scheduler must therefore prioritize RT traffic appropriately while maximizing spectral efficiency for NRT traffic using residual resources.

Despite its critical function, conventional downlink schedulers suffer from several limitations. Many existing approaches rely on simplified metrics that do not fully account for the interplay between channel conditions, transmit power, and data volume. For instance, the round-robin (RR) method allocates PRBs cyclically among UEs to ensure absolute fairness but often results in suboptimal throughput due to its disregard for instantaneous channel quality. The maximum throughput (MT) algorithm, conversely, assigns PRBs exclusively based on current channel conditions to maximize spectral efficiency, frequently leading to resource monopolization by UEs near the cell center with favorable signal-to-noise ratios, thereby starving cell-edge users and degrading overall fairness. The proportional fair (PF) method attempts to strike a balance by normalizing instantaneous data rates against historical averages; however, it still fails to consider the actual power cost associated with transmitting each bit, which becomes increasingly significant under limited transmit power budgets or in dense deployment scenarios.

To address scheduling complexity, most practical implementations decompose the process into two sequential phases: time domain packet scheduling (TDPS) and frequency domain packet scheduling (FDPS). The TDPS first identifies a scheduling candidate set (SCS) of UEs based on criteria such as buffer occupancy, delay sensitivity, and channel quality indication (CQI), thereby reducing the computational load for subsequent resource allocation. The FDPS then maps PRBs to members of the SCS according to a defined scheduling metric. While this two-stage architecture improves scalability, the choice of scheduling metric remains crucial. Current metrics used in RR, MT, and PF algorithms primarily focus on data rate or fairness indices without incorporating power efficiency considerations. This omission becomes particularly problematic in mixed-traffic environments where efficient use of transmit power directly impacts both system capacity and battery life at the eNB.

Given these shortcomings, there exists a clear need for a novel scheduling method that integrates power-aware decision-making into the resource allocation process. Such an approach should simultaneously enhance system throughput, improve fairness—especially for cell-edge users—and minimize unnecessary power expenditure, all while maintaining compatibility with existing LTE infrastructure and QoS frameworks.

## SUMMARY

The present invention is motivated by the recognition that conventional downlink scheduling algorithms fail to optimize the trade-off between spectral efficiency, user fairness, and power consumption in modern wireless communication systems. To overcome these deficiencies, the invention introduces a minimum transmit power-based (MP) packet scheduling method that fundamentally redefines the scheduling metric by prioritizing UEs based on the ratio of required transmit power to the number of bits transmitted per physical resource block (PRB). This innovative metric enables more efficient utilization of limited transmit power while concurrently improving both system throughput and fairness across diverse traffic types.

Exemplary embodiments of the invention encompass methods and apparatuses for scheduling downlink packets in a wireless communication system supporting mixed real-time (RT) and non-real-time (NRT) traffic. The primary object of these embodiments is to provide a scheduling framework that minimizes transmit power per bit without compromising quality of service for delay-sensitive applications or sacrificing throughput for best-effort services. By doing so, the invention achieves superior performance compared to conventional round-robin (RR), maximum throughput (MT), and proportional fair (PF) schedulers, particularly in multi-cell environments with varying user distributions and channel conditions.

A key advantage of the exemplary embodiments lies in their ability to dynamically adapt resource allocation based on both channel gain and data volume, ensuring that PRBs are assigned to UEs capable of achieving the highest data transmission efficiency per unit of power. This leads to measurable gains in average user throughput—up to 18% over MT-based schedulers—as well as significant improvements in fairness, with reduced dominance by high-channel-quality users. Additionally, the MP scheduler demonstrates enhanced cell-edge performance, delivering up to 70% higher throughput at the boundary compared to RR scheduling, and enables substantial power savings—approximately 8 dBm—while maintaining equivalent cell throughput levels.

The method of scheduling downlink packets according to the invention involves calculating, for each UE-PRB pair, a scheduling metric defined as the inverse of the ratio of transmit power to the number of transmittable bits. Real-time traffic scheduling operations are executed first, allocating PRBs to RT traffic UEs to satisfy guaranteed bit rate (GBR) requirements under delay constraints. Non-real-time traffic scheduling operations follow, utilizing any remaining PRBs and transmit power to maximize throughput for NRT traffic. The metric calculation operation leverages precomputed functions of channel gain and modulation coding scheme (MCS) levels to efficiently determine the optimal UE-PRB assignments.

In one embodiment, the scheduling metric is expressed using an excess channel gain formulation, which captures how much a given channel exceeds the minimum gain required to support a specific data payload. This allows the scheduler to favor UEs that not only have good channels but also operate efficiently relative to their data demands. The apparatus for scheduling downlink packets includes a scheduling metric calculation unit, a real-time traffic scheduling unit, and a non-real-time traffic scheduling unit, all integrated within the eNB’s medium access control (MAC) layer. Collectively, these components enable a power-conscious, high-performance scheduling solution that addresses the evolving needs of next-generation wireless networks.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EXEMPLARY EMBODIMENTS

The following detailed description sets forth exemplary embodiments of the invention in sufficient detail to enable those skilled in the art to practice the invention. It should be understood that other embodiments may be utilized and that structural, logical, and electrical changes may be made without departing from the scope of the invention. As used herein, the terms “connected” and “include” are to be construed broadly unless otherwise indicated, encompassing direct and indirect connections as well as inclusive relationships among components.

In accordance with the invention, real-time traffic scheduling is performed prior to non-real-time traffic scheduling to honor the stringent delay and bit rate requirements of RT applications such as voice or video. Upon classification of incoming packets into RT and NRT queues by a traffic classifier, the scheduler first processes the RT queue. During each transmission time interval (TTI), the real-time traffic scheduling unit evaluates all RT UEs in the scheduling candidate set (SCS) and allocates PRBs sufficient to meet their guaranteed bit rate (GBR), typically 64 kbps in the described embodiment. Allocation proceeds by selecting, for each available PRB, the RT UE with the highest scheduling metric as defined by the minimum transmit power per bit criterion.

Non-real-time traffic scheduling is then executed using any unallocated PRBs after RT traffic has been served. The non-real-time traffic scheduling unit applies the same MP-based metric to assign remaining PRBs to NRT UEs in a manner that maximizes total throughput under the residual power budget. Because NRT traffic operates on a best-effort basis, no minimum rate is enforced, allowing flexible exploitation of favorable channel conditions.

Referring now to FIG. 2, which illustrates an exemplary downlink scheduler architecture, the scheduler comprises three main functional units: a scheduling metric calculation unit, a real-time traffic scheduling unit, and a non-real-time traffic scheduling unit. The scheduling metric calculation unit receives channel quality indication (CQI) reports from all UEs and computes, for each UE-PRB combination, a metric M(s,n) representing the efficiency of power usage. Specifically, M(s,n) is derived from the excess channel gain Δ<sub>s,n</sub> = g<sub>s,n</sub> − g<sub>min</sub>(b<sub>s,n</sub>), where g<sub>s,n</sub> is the measured channel power for UE s on PRB n, and g<sub>min</sub>(b<sub>s,n</sub>) is the minimum channel gain required to successfully transmit b<sub>s,n</sub> bits using the selected modulation and coding scheme (MCS).

FIG. 3 depicts a flowchart of the downlink packet scheduling method. The process begins by calculating the scheduling metric for all UE-PRB pairs in the SCS. The scheduler then selects the RT traffic flow with the highest metric and assigns it an available PRB. If unallocated RT traffic remains after initial assignment, the process repeats until all RT GBR requirements are met or PRBs are exhausted. Subsequently, if unallocated PRBs remain, they are allocated to NRT traffic flows in descending order of their scheduling metrics.

Finally, the MP scheduler operates by iteratively assigning PRBs to the UE-PRB pair with the largest M(s,n), thereby implementing a minimum transmit power-based resource allocation strategy that optimizes both throughput and fairness in LTE downlink systems.
