# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the fields of medicine, cell biology, molecular biology, and genetics. More specifically, the invention provides novel methods and compositions for the diagnosis, prognosis, treatment, and prevention of dengue virus infection by targeting host cellular pathways that are modulated during viral replication. The invention encompasses the identification and utilization of key components of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, interferon-mediated antiviral responses, and NF-κB-regulated cytokine and chemokine networks as biomarkers and therapeutic targets in dengue infection. It further includes the development of diagnostic assays, pharmaceutical compositions, and screening platforms based on these host-response elements to improve clinical management of dengue fever and its severe manifestations.

## BACKGROUND

Dengue infection is a significant global public health burden, with an estimated 50 to 100 million cases of dengue fever and 250,000 to 500,000 cases of the more severe dengue hemorrhagic fever or dengue shock syndrome occurring annually. Dengue virus, a member of the Flaviviridae family, is a positive-sense, single-stranded RNA virus transmitted primarily by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes. Four distinct serotypes (DENV-1 through DENV-4) circulate in endemic regions, and while primary infection with one serotype confers lifelong homotypic immunity, it does not protect against heterotypic reinfection. In fact, secondary infection with a different serotype is associated with an increased risk of severe disease due to mechanisms such as antibody-dependent enhancement (ADE) and T-cell original antigenic sin.

Dengue virus replication occurs in a variety of human cell types, including Langerhans cells, monocytes, macrophages, endothelial cells, and hepatocytes. The virus enters host cells via receptor-mediated endocytosis, followed by uncoating and translation of its RNA genome into a polyprotein that is processed by both viral and host proteases. Viral replication takes place in association with endoplasmic reticulum-derived membranes, and newly assembled virions are secreted via the exocytic pathway. A critical aspect of dengue pathogenesis is the virus’s ability to antagonize the host innate immune response, particularly by inhibiting type I interferon (IFN-α/β) signaling through suppression of the JAK-STAT pathway, thereby facilitating unchecked viral replication.

Current diagnostic approaches for dengue rely on detection of viral RNA by RT-PCR, NS1 antigen testing, or serological assays for IgM and IgG antibodies. However, these methods suffer from limitations in sensitivity during early or late phases of infection, cross-reactivity among flaviviruses, and inability to predict disease severity. Moreover, there are no approved antiviral therapies for dengue; treatment remains supportive, focusing on fluid management and monitoring for warning signs of progression to severe disease. Although a live-attenuated tetravalent vaccine (Dengvaxia) has been licensed in some countries, its use is restricted to individuals with prior dengue exposure due to safety concerns in seronegative recipients. Thus, there remains an urgent need for novel diagnostic biomarkers that can distinguish dengue from other febrile illnesses and predict clinical outcomes, as well as for targeted therapeutics that disrupt viral replication without causing significant host toxicity.

## SUMMARY

The present invention introduces the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway as a central regulator of dengue virus replication and a promising target for therapeutic intervention. It has been discovered that dengue infection induces significant upregulation of genes involved in this pathway, and that pharmacological inhibition of proteasome activity using compounds such as MG-132 and ALLN markedly reduces viral production in human hepatocytic and lung epithelial cell lines without substantial cytotoxicity at effective concentrations. The invention provides a method for the diagnosis or prognosis of dengue infection based on the detection of expression levels or activity of components of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, interferon-mediated proteins, or NF-κB-regulated cytokines and chemokines in patient samples.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for treating or preventing dengue by modulating the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, either through direct inhibition of proteasome activity or by targeting specific E1, E2, or E3 enzymes involved in ubiquitin conjugation. The invention further identifies specific molecules—such as MG-132, ALLN, and their functional equivalents—as suitable agents for such modulation. Additionally, polynucleotides encoding key regulatory proteins of these pathways may be used for diagnostic or therapeutic purposes, including gene expression profiling to assess infection status or disease severity.

The invention also enables the detection of polymorphisms in genes encoding ubiquitin-proteasome pathway proteins that may correlate with susceptibility to severe dengue, thereby facilitating personalized risk assessment. Screening methods are provided to identify agonists or antagonists of these pathway proteins, which may serve as lead compounds for drug development. Candidate molecules identified through such screens may be administered to animals, including humans, to treat or prevent dengue infection by down-regulating viral function.

Moreover, the invention discloses that interferon-mediated proteins, such as viperin and interferon alpha, play a functional role in restricting dengue replication, and their administration or induction may alleviate disease. Similarly, modulation of NF-κB-mediated cytokine and chemokine responses—particularly involving IP-10 (CXCL10) and I-TAC (CXCL11)—provides diagnostic and prognostic value. Elevated serum levels of IP-10 and I-TAC are strongly associated with acute dengue infection and may serve as early biomarkers to differentiate dengue from other febrile illnesses.

The invention further provides kits for the diagnosis or prognosis of dengue, comprising reagents for detecting IP-10, I-TAC, viperin, or ubiquitin-pathway components, as well as kits for treatment or prevention containing proteasome inhibitors or interferon-inducing agents. Screening assays are described for identifying molecules that bind to or modulate the activity of these host proteins, using techniques such as phage display, X-ray crystallography, or cell-based reporter systems. The invention encompasses the use of equivalent compounds, including chemical derivatives, prodrugs, stereoisomers, and pharmaceutically acceptable salts of MG-132 and ALLN, formulated into pharmaceutical compositions for systemic or localized delivery.

Examples of ubiquitin-proteasome pathway proteins include E1 ubiquitin-activating enzymes, E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, E3 ubiquitin ligases, ubiquitin-specific proteases, and the 26S proteasome complex. Interferon-mediated proteins include STAT1, MX1, OAS family members, IFIH1, and viperin. NF-κB-mediated cytokine/chemokine response proteins include TNF-α, IL-6, CCL5, IP-10, and I-TAC. The comprehensive integration of these host-response elements into diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic strategies represents a significant advance in the management of dengue infection.
