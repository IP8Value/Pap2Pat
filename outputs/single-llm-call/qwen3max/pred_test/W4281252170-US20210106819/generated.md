# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of oncology, specifically to methods and systems for imaging and treating cancer through the modulation and exploitation of phosphatidylserine (PS) exposure on the surface of cancer cells. More particularly, the invention encompasses therapeutic and diagnostic modalities that utilize direct current electric fields to modulate PS expression levels on tumor cell membranes, thereby enhancing the efficacy of PS-targeted imaging agents and therapeutics. The invention further includes compositions comprising PS-binding agents such as SapC-DOPS nanovesicles, bavituximab, and PPS1D1, which may be used alone or in combination with electric field application, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or immunotherapy to improve cancer detection, monitoring, and treatment outcomes across a broad spectrum of malignancies.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of morbidity and mortality worldwide, with millions of new diagnoses and hundreds of thousands of deaths occurring annually. Despite significant advances in surgical techniques, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, and more recently immunotherapy, many patients still face poor prognoses due to tumor recurrence, metastasis, and resistance to conventional treatments. A major limitation of existing therapeutic strategies is their lack of specificity, which often results in substantial off-target toxicity to healthy tissues, thereby limiting dose intensity and compromising patient quality of life. Moreover, the heterogeneity of tumors—both between patients and within individual lesions—presents a formidable challenge to achieving durable responses with monotherapies.

Drug resistance mechanisms further complicate cancer management. Tumors can develop resistance through genetic mutations, epigenetic alterations, activation of alternative signaling pathways, and modulation of the tumor microenvironment. For instance, some cancer cells downregulate drug transporters or upregulate DNA repair mechanisms to evade the cytotoxic effects of chemotherapeutic agents. Additionally, the immunosuppressive nature of many solid tumors limits the effectiveness of immune checkpoint inhibitors and other immunotherapies. These challenges underscore the urgent need for novel therapeutic approaches that are both highly selective for malignant cells and capable of synergizing with existing treatment modalities to overcome resistance and improve clinical outcomes.

In this context, biomarker-driven strategies have emerged as promising avenues for precision oncology. Among potential biomarkers, phosphatidylserine (PS)—a phospholipid normally confined to the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane in healthy cells—has gained attention due to its aberrant externalization on the surface of cancer cells and tumor-associated endothelial cells. This dysregulation of membrane lipid asymmetry provides a unique molecular signature that can be exploited for both diagnostic imaging and targeted therapy. However, the variable and often low levels of PS exposure in certain tumor types or under specific treatment conditions can limit the efficacy of PS-directed agents. Therefore, there exists a critical unmet need for methods that can dynamically modulate PS exposure to enhance the sensitivity and specificity of PS-targeted interventions.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method of treating cancer in a subject by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a PS-targeting agent in combination with the application of a direct current (DC) electric field to the tumor site. The invention further encompasses a method of imaging cancerous cells by administering a detectably labeled PS-targeting agent following or concurrent with the application of a DC electric field to increase surface PS exposure and thereby enhance imaging contrast. In certain embodiments, the method includes co-administration of additional therapeutic agents such as chemotherapeutics or radiotherapeutics to achieve synergistic antitumor effects.

Specifically, the invention introduces the use of low-intensity, non-contact DC electric fields to modulate phosphatidylserine exposure on the outer leaflet of cancer cell membranes without inducing significant cytotoxicity in normal cells. This modulation sensitizes tumor cells to PS-targeted therapeutics such as SapC-DOPS nanovesicles, bavituximab, or PPS1D1. Chemotherapy agents that may be used in conjunction with the disclosed methods include, but are not limited to, temozolomide, gemcitabine, paclitaxel, docetaxel, doxorubicin, and SN38. Radiotherapy agents encompass ionizing radiation delivered via external beam radiotherapy, brachytherapy, or radiopharmaceuticals.

The invention also provides a method of killing or inhibiting the growth of cancer cells by first applying a DC electric field to increase surface PS levels and then administering a PS-targeting cytotoxic agent. Furthermore, the invention includes a method of imaging cancerous tissue by administering a PS-targeting imaging probe—such as a fluorophore-, MRI contrast agent-, or radionuclide-labeled construct—after electric field pretreatment to amplify tumor-specific signal intensity relative to background tissue.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present patent application discloses novel methods and systems for enhancing cancer diagnosis and therapy through the controlled modulation of phosphatidylserine (PS) exposure on tumor cell surfaces using direct current (DC) electric fields. Phosphatidylserine (PS) is a critical anionic phospholipid that plays essential roles in cellular physiology, including signal transduction, membrane trafficking, and apoptosis. In healthy cells, PS is actively maintained on the cytosolic leaflet of the plasma membrane by ATP-dependent flippases. However, in cancer cells, this asymmetry is frequently disrupted, leading to PS externalization—a phenomenon associated with tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance. The extent of PS exposure varies among cancer types and even within clonal populations, influencing susceptibility to different treatment modalities.

The application of electric fields (EFs) represents a non-invasive means to modulate PS dynamics. The invention introduces a combined biomarker-driven therapy wherein a DC electric field is applied to tumor tissue to deliberately alter PS exposure, thereby priming cancer cells for enhanced targeting by PS-specific agents. Experimental data demonstrate that low-amplitude DC electric fields (e.g., 75–150 V/cm) applied via a non-contact parallel-plate capacitor system significantly increase surface PS levels in glioblastoma cells without affecting total cellular PS content or viability of normal cells. Conversely, higher-intensity DC fields may induce excessive PS exposure linked to apoptotic pathways, offering a tunable therapeutic window.

PS-targeted treatments include SapC-DOPS nanovesicles, which exploit the high affinity of saposin C for PS under acidic tumor microenvironment conditions; bavituximab, a monoclonal antibody that binds PS in complex with β2-glycoprotein I; and PPS1D1, a dimeric peptide–peptoid hybrid with selective cytotoxicity toward PS-expressing cancer cells. The electric field device described herein comprises insulated electrodes arranged to generate a uniform field across the tumor region, minimizing thermal effects and collateral damage. The electric field system device may be integrated with imaging or drug delivery platforms for real-time monitoring and feedback-controlled therapy.

Mechanistically, the electric field alters transmembrane potential, activating voltage-gated calcium channels and increasing intracellular Ca²⁺ concentration. Elevated Ca²⁺ inhibits flippase activity and activates scramblases, promoting PS translocation to the outer membrane leaflet. This calcium-dependent pathway was confirmed by measuring intracellular Ca²⁺ flux following EF application and observing concomitant PS externalization and reduced cell proliferation. Apoptosis assays further revealed that EF-primed cells exhibit heightened sensitivity to SapC-DOPS-induced cell death.

### Phosphatidylserine

Phosphatidylserine (PS) is a glycerophospholipid composed of a glycerol backbone, two fatty acid chains, a phosphate group, and a serine headgroup. Its asymmetric distribution is fundamental to cellular homeostasis. In normal physiology, PS exposure serves as an “eat-me” signal during apoptosis, facilitating phagocytic clearance. In cancer, however, PS is aberrantly exposed on viable tumor and endothelial cells, contributing to immunosuppression by polarizing macrophages toward an M2 phenotype and inhibiting dendritic cell maturation. PS overexpression correlates with tumor grade, metastatic potential, and poor prognosis, making it a valuable pan-cancer biomarker. Beyond oncology, PS dysregulation is implicated in neurodegenerative, cardiovascular, and inflammatory diseases, underscoring its broad therapeutic relevance.

### PS-Targeting Drugs

SapC-DOPS nanovesicles consist of saposin C, a lysosomal protein, embedded in dioleoylphosphatidylserine (DOPS) bilayers. These ~200 nm particles selectively bind and internalize into PS-expressing cancer cells, triggering lysosomal membrane permeabilization and caspase-independent cell death. Bavituximab, a chimeric IgG1 monoclonal antibody, recognizes PS/β2GP1 complexes, blocking immunosuppressive signaling and engaging Fcγ receptors to stimulate antitumor immunity. PPS1D1, a synthetic dimeric peptoid, binds PS with high avidity and disrupts mitochondrial function in cancer cells. Preclinical studies confirm the efficacy of these agents across glioblastoma, pancreatic, lung, and breast cancers, with SapC-DOPS showing particular promise due to its dual imaging and therapeutic capabilities.

### Altering the Surface PS to Increase the Efficacy of PS Targeting Modalities

Modulating surface PS levels enhances the binding and cytotoxicity of PS-targeted agents. For example, temozolomide induces DNA damage and apoptosis, increasing PS exposure and thereby sensitizing glioblastoma cells to SapC-DOPS. The present invention extends this principle by using electric fields as a controllable, non-pharmacological means to upregulate PS, enabling dynamic tuning of tumor susceptibility. This approach may allow lower doses of cytotoxic drugs, reducing systemic toxicity while maintaining or improving efficacy.

### Phosphatidylserine as a Tumor Imaging Target

PS serves as an excellent target for non-invasive tumor imaging. SapC-DOPS has been conjugated to near-infrared fluorophores for optical imaging, ultrasmall superparamagnetic iron oxide (USPIO) for MRI, and radioiodinated lipids for PET/SPECT. These constructs enable selective visualization of primary and metastatic lesions, treatment response assessment, and intraoperative guidance. Electric field pretreatment amplifies tumor-to-background ratios by boosting PS availability, thereby improving detection sensitivity.

### Electric Field

Therapeutic electric fields, particularly low-intensity DC fields applied non-invasively, offer a novel strategy to modulate tumor biology without genotoxic stress. Unlike tumor-treating fields (TTFields), which use alternating currents to disrupt mitosis, DC fields act via bioelectrochemical mechanisms to regulate membrane lipid dynamics, providing a complementary approach to enhance targeted therapies.

### Optical Imaging

Optical imaging using SapC-DOPS-CVM (CellVue Maroon) enables real-time, cost-effective tumor visualization in preclinical models. While limited by tissue penetration depth and autofluorescence in humans, it remains invaluable for mechanistic studies and surgical navigation. Electric field enhancement improves signal specificity, particularly in superficial or endoscopically accessible tumors.

### PET/SPECT Imaging

PET and SPECT imaging with radiolabeled PS-targeting agents (e.g., ⁸⁹Zr-PGN635, ⁷⁴As-bavituximab, ¹²⁵I-SapC-DOPS) provide high-sensitivity, quantitative whole-body tumor mapping. These modalities are clinically translatable and benefit significantly from electric field–induced PS upregulation, which increases tracer uptake and improves lesion delineation.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

SapC-DOPS nanovesicles were synthesized by mixing saposin C and dioleoylphosphatidylserine (DOPS) in a 1:10 molar ratio in buffer, followed by extrusion through polycarbonate membranes to form ~200 nm vesicles. Ultrasmall superparamagnetic iron oxide (USPIO) nanoparticles were encapsulated during vesicle formation by adding them to the lipid mixture prior to hydration, yielding SapC-DOPS-IO constructs suitable for MRI.

### Example 2

SapC-DOPS vesicles were labeled with USPIO particles and administered intravenously to mice bearing U87 glioblastoma xenografts. T2*-weighted MRI at 7T demonstrated a significant decrease in tumor signal intensity within 1 hour post-injection, with maximal contrast at 24 hours. Inductively coupled plasma analysis confirmed 5-fold higher iron accumulation in tumors compared to controls receiving free USPIO, validating tumor-selective delivery.

### Example 3

Gd-DTPA-BSA (gadolinium-diethylenetriamine pentaacetate-bis(stearylamide)) was incorporated into SapC-DOPS vesicles to create a T1-weighted MRI contrast agent. Relaxivity measurements yielded a value of 3.32 (s·mM)⁻¹, comparable to clinical Gd-DTPA. In vivo imaging of glioblastoma-bearing mice showed a 9% increase in tumor R1 relaxation rate at 10 hours post-injection, with no significant change in contralateral brain, confirming targeted accumulation. T1 maps revealed reduced relaxation times specifically in tumor regions.

### Example 4

U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells were exposed to a 100 V/cm DC electric field for 30 minutes using a parallel-plate capacitor. Flow cytometry using Annexin V-FITC showed a 2.5-fold increase in surface PS within 1 hour post-exposure. Concurrently, Fluo-4 AM staining revealed a rapid rise in intracellular Ca²⁺, peaking at 15 minutes. Cell viability assays (MTT) indicated no acute toxicity, but cells pre-treated with EF exhibited 60% greater sensitivity to SapC-DOPS 24 hours later, supporting combinatorial therapy.

### Example 5

Cells were treated with hydroxyurea to synchronize in S-phase and then exposed to a high DC electric field (150 V/cm). Flow cytometry analysis showed accelerated transition to G2/M phase within 12 hours. Given that PS exposure fluctuates with cell cycle stage, this suggests that EF-induced cell cycle perturbation may further modulate PS availability. Consequently, SapC-DOPS targeting could be optimized by timing administration relative to EF-induced cell cycle shifts, enhancing therapeutic precision.
