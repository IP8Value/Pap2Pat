## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cellular reprogramming and stem cell biology, particularly to methods for the efficient production of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from adult fibroblasts. More specifically, the invention provides a robust and scalable method for the rapid derivation of high-quality iPSCs through the use of a defined surface marker profile that enables fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) at early stages of reprogramming, thereby significantly improving the purity, efficiency, and reproducibility of iPSC generation compared to conventional manual colony selection techniques.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Stem cells are undifferentiated cells capable of both self-renewal and differentiation into specialized cell types. Embryonic stem cells (ESCs), derived from the inner cell mass of blastocyst-stage embryos, represent the gold standard of pluripotency, possessing the ability to differentiate into all three germ layers—ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm—and thus into any cell type of the adult body. However, the use of ESCs raises significant ethical concerns due to the destruction of human embryos and is further limited by immune incompatibility when used in therapeutic contexts.

Adult stem cells, found in various tissues such as bone marrow, blood, and skin, offer an alternative with fewer ethical constraints but are generally restricted in their differentiation potential, typically giving rise only to cell types of their tissue of origin. These limitations have spurred the development of induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) technology, which reprograms somatic cells back to an ESC-like state without the need for embryos.

The seminal discovery that ectopic expression of four transcription factors—Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc—can reprogram differentiated somatic cells into iPSCs has revolutionized regenerative medicine. iPSCs exhibit morphological, molecular, and functional characteristics nearly identical to ESCs, including the capacity for unlimited self-renewal and trilineage differentiation potential. Critically, iPSCs can be derived from a patient’s own cells, enabling autologous therapies, disease modeling, and personalized drug screening.

Despite these advances, current iPSC production methods suffer from significant drawbacks. Reprogramming of dermal fibroblasts using viral vectors is notoriously slow, typically requiring 4–6 weeks, and highly inefficient, with conversion rates often below 0.01%. Moreover, the identification of bona fide iPSC colonies relies heavily on manual selection under light microscopy based on colony morphology, a process that is subjective, labor-intensive, and requires extensive expertise. This approach frequently results in the co-isolation of partially reprogrammed or contaminating fibroblasts, compromising the quality and homogeneity of derived lines.

Existing techniques for iPSC identification include monitoring morphological changes, expression of pluripotency markers such as SSEA4 and TRA-1-60, and downregulation of somatic markers like CD13. While FACS-based enrichment using positive selection for pluripotency markers has been attempted, it often fails to exclude partially reprogrammed cells that transiently express these markers. Furthermore, the use of integrating viral vectors introduces genomic instability and safety concerns for clinical applications.

There remains a critical unmet need for standardized, efficient, and reliable methods to produce high-purity iPSCs rapidly and reproducibly. Such methods must be compatible with both integrating and non-integrating reprogramming systems and applicable to fibroblasts from diverse sources, including healthy donors and patients with genetic diseases. The present invention addresses this need by introducing a novel FACS-based strategy that combines positive selection for pluripotency markers with negative selection against a fibroblast-specific marker, thereby enabling the isolation of fully reprogrammed iPSCs at early time points with unprecedented purity and efficiency.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides an improved method for the production of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from adult fibroblasts. The method comprises contacting adult fibroblasts with a reprogramming composition comprising factors that induce pluripotency, culturing the contacted fibroblasts under conditions conducive to reprogramming, and then sorting the cultured cells based on a specific combination of cell surface markers to enrich for fully reprogrammed iPSCs. iPSC colonies are subsequently identified and expanded from the sorted population.

The reprogramming composition includes one or more reprogramming factors selected from the group consisting of Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc, delivered via viral vectors (e.g., retroviral or Sendai virus) or non-viral methods. The composition may further include small molecule inhibitors that enhance reprogramming efficiency, such as an ALK5 inhibitor (e.g., SB431542), a MEK inhibitor (e.g., PD0325901), and a ROCK inhibitor (e.g., Thiazovivin or Y-27632).

Following reprogramming, cells are sorted using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) based on the surface marker profile CD13NEG SSEA4POS TRA-1-60POS. This profile enables the simultaneous positive selection for pluripotency markers (SSEA4 and TRA-1-60) and negative selection against the fibroblast marker CD13, thereby excluding parental and partially reprogrammed fibroblasts. iPSC colonies are identified visually by their compact, dome-shaped morphology characteristic of pluripotent stem cells.

The sorted cells are validated using multiple orthogonal assays. Flow cytometry or immunofluorescent microscopy confirms the expression of pluripotency markers and absence of CD13. Genetic integration and transgene silencing are assessed by Southern blotting or quantitative real-time PCR. Functional pluripotency is confirmed by in vitro embryoid body formation and in vivo teratoma formation assays, which demonstrate the ability of the cells to differentiate into derivatives of all three embryonic germ layers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods for the efficient and reproducible production of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from adult fibroblasts. Induced pluripotent stem cells are defined as somatic cells that have been reprogrammed to a pluripotent state, exhibiting self-renewal capacity and the ability to differentiate into cell types of all three germ layers. Adult fibroblasts, typically derived from skin biopsies, serve as a readily accessible and abundant source of somatic cells for reprogramming.

The method begins with the provision of a reprogramming composition containing nucleic acids encoding reprogramming factors, preferably Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc. These factors may be delivered using integrating retroviral vectors or non-integrating Sendai viral vectors, the latter being preferred for clinical applications due to its transient expression and lack of genomic integration. The reprogramming composition may also include small molecule inhibitors that modulate key signaling pathways to enhance reprogramming efficiency and cell survival, such as inhibitors of TGF-β/ALK5 (e.g., SB431542), MEK/ERK (e.g., PD0325901), and ROCK (e.g., Thiazovivin or Y-27632).

Adult fibroblasts are contacted with the reprogramming composition and cultured in a medium supportive of pluripotency, such as human embryonic stem cell medium (HUESM). After 7 to 14 days post-infection, cells are harvested and subjected to FACS using a panel of fluorescently conjugated antibodies. The target population is defined as CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, and TRA-1-60-positive (CD13NEG SSEA4POS TRA-1-60POS). This gating strategy effectively excludes CD13-positive fibroblasts and TRA-1-60-negative partially reprogrammed cells, enriching for a homogeneous population of fully reprogrammed iPSCs.

The sorted cells are plated onto feeder layers (e.g., γ-irradiated murine embryonic fibroblasts) or feeder-free substrates (e.g., Matrigel) in the presence of a ROCK inhibitor to enhance survival. Distinct iPSC colonies emerge within 3–7 days and are manually picked or allowed to expand for further characterization. Alternative embodiments include enriching early reprogrammed fibroblasts using FACS at day 7–10 post-infection and enabling live sorting of fully reprogrammed iPSCs for immediate downstream applications.

The method is applicable to fibroblasts derived from normal individuals as well as those with genetic diseases, including diabetes, neurodegenerative disorders, cardiac conditions, and autoimmune diseases. Fibroblasts may be obtained from fresh biopsies, frozen stocks, or cell repositories, demonstrating broad utility.

Transfection of fibroblasts may be achieved using various methods, including viral transduction, electroporation, lipofection, or cell-penetrating peptides. Antibodies specific for CD13, SSEA4, and TRA-1-60 are employed for FACS, with additional markers such as SSEA3 and CD326 used for confirmation. Genetic analysis via Southern blotting or qRT-PCR verifies transgene integration and silencing, while enzymatic activity and gene expression profiles confirm pluripotency.

Compositions comprising the purified iPSCs are contemplated for use in regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and drug screening. Methods of treating diseases involve differentiating iPSCs into therapeutically relevant cell types and administering them via intravenous, intramuscular, or local injection. The iPSCs may also be used for gene correction studies, wherein disease-causing mutations are repaired ex vivo before transplantation.

Examples demonstrate the successful reprogramming of over 75 fibroblast lines into 228 iPSC lines using this method. Characterization includes Lyoplate assays for surface marker profiling, flow cytometry validation, embryoid body formation, and teratoma assays, all confirming robust pluripotency and genomic stability across multiple passages.

### Rapid Production of iPS Cells and Colonies

The invention enables rapid production of iPSCs and colonies through a streamlined protocol. Fibroblast lines are first derived from 3-mm skin biopsies, minced, and cultured in fibroblast medium until outgrowths appear. Cells are expanded and used within three passages from biopsy or one passage after thawing. Reprogramming is initiated by infecting fibroblasts with either retroviral or Sendai viral vectors carrying the four Yamanaka factors at optimized multiplicities of infection.

For retroviral reprogramming, 1×10⁴ fibroblasts per well are infected in HUESM, with medium exchanged on day 2 to include ALK5, MEK, and ROCK inhibitors. For Sendai virus, 5×10⁵ fibroblasts are infected in fibroblast medium at MOI 3 for two days, followed by daily HUESM exchanges. At 7–10 days post-infection, cells are harvested using Dispase or Accutase, filtered to obtain single-cell suspensions, and stained with anti-CD13, anti-SSEA4, and anti-TRA-1-60 antibodies.

FACS sorting is performed under gentle conditions (100 µm nozzle, 20 psi) to maintain cell viability, with the CD13NEG SSEA4POS TRA-1-60POS population sorted directly onto MEF-coated plates in HUESM supplemented with Y-27632. After 48 hours, the ROCK inhibitor is replaced with the small molecule cocktail (SB431542, PD0325901, Thiazovivin). Colonies are visible by day 3 post-sort and are picked between days 7–10 for expansion.

Characterization includes Lyoplate assays, which profile expression of over 200 cell surface markers, confirming pluripotent identity. Flow cytometry validates the surface marker profile, showing high SSEA4 and TRA-1-60 expression and absence of CD13. Additional examples using fibroblasts from diabetic, neurodegenerative, and cardiac patients confirm consistent reprogramming efficiency and colony formation. The method concludes with the generation of stable, karyotypically normal iPSC lines suitable for research and therapeutic applications.

### Immunofluorescence and Microscopy

Immunofluorescence and microscopy are employed to characterize iPSCs and their differentiated derivatives. Primary antibodies against pluripotency markers (e.g., Nanog, Oct4, TRA-1-60) and lineage-specific markers (e.g., α-fetoprotein for endoderm, smooth muscle actin for mesoderm, βIII-tubulin for ectoderm) are used. RNA is isolated from cells using the RNeasy kit, followed by cDNA synthesis with SuperScript III First-Strand system and oligo(dT) primers. Quantitative real-time PCR is performed using LightCycler SYBR Green Master kit with primers specific for pluripotency and differentiation genes.

For teratoma assays, iPSCs are dissociated with Dispase into clumps of 100–200 cells, resuspended in HUESM with Matrigel, and injected subcutaneously into NOD-SCID Il2rg-null mice. Teratomas are allowed to grow for 6–8 weeks, then recovered, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, sectioned, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) to assess trilineage differentiation.

### Primers

Southern blotting is used to confirm genomic integration and clonality. Probes for human Oct4, Sox2, and KLF4 are generated by PCR using DIG-labeled nucleotides. Genomic DNA is isolated from HUESCs, parent fibroblasts, and iPSCs, digested with BglII, and resolved on agarose gels. DNA is transferred to positively charged nylon membranes, crosslinked by UV, and hybridized with DIG-labeled probes in DIG Easy Hyb buffer. Membranes are washed with DIG wash and block buffer, incubated with anti-DIG antibody, and developed with CDP-Star reagent. Stripping and re-probing allow sequential analysis of multiple loci.

### Comparison of Retrovirus and Sendai Virus Vectors in Reprogramming

The invention is compatible with both retroviral and Sendai viral reprogramming systems. Sendai virus, a non-integrating RNA virus, demonstrates higher reprogramming efficiency, with a greater proportion of SSEA4POS TRA-1-60POS cells emerging by day 14. However, CD13 downregulation is delayed compared to retroviral methods, with CD13POS SSEA4POS TRA-1-60POS cells persisting longer. Despite this, FACS sorting using the CD13NEG SSEA4POS TRA-1-60POS profile effectively isolates fully reprogrammed clones from both systems, with Sendai-derived lines showing complete transgene clearance by passage 10. This confirms the versatility of the method across reprogramming platforms.
