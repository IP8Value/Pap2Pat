# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and pharmaceutical sciences, specifically to engineered polypeptides comprising modified Fc regions derived from immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies. More particularly, the invention encompasses novel polypeptide variants that exhibit selectively enhanced binding affinity for the inhibitory Fc gamma receptor IIb (FcγRIIb) relative to activating Fc gamma receptors, especially FcγRIIa in both its allotypic forms, FcγRIIa-H131 and FcγRIIa-R131. The invention further relates to methods for producing such polypeptides, as well as their use in therapeutic and diagnostic applications. The scope of the invention includes IgG antibodies, Fc fusion proteins, and other molecules incorporating the modified Fc region, which are designed to modulate immune responses through selective engagement of FcγRIIb without concomitant activation of FcγRIIa-mediated pathways. These polypeptides are particularly useful in the treatment or prevention of immunological inflammatory diseases, autoimmune disorders, diseases associated with deficiency of biologically essential proteins, and viral infections, among other conditions where fine-tuned immunomodulation is therapeutically beneficial.

## BACKGROUND ART

Antibodies have emerged as a cornerstone of modern biopharmaceuticals, with monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) representing a major class of therapeutic agents for cancer, autoimmune diseases, and infectious conditions. A critical determinant of antibody efficacy is the interaction between the Fc region of the antibody and Fc gamma receptors (FcγRs) expressed on various immune cells. Among the human FcγR family, FcγRIa (CD64), FcγRIIa (CD32a), and FcγRIIIa (CD16a) function as activating receptors that trigger effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and phagocytosis upon cross-linking by immune complexes. In contrast, FcγRIIb (CD32b) serves as the sole inhibitory FcγR in humans, containing an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM) in its cytoplasmic domain that counteracts activating signals. The balance between activating and inhibitory FcγR signaling profoundly influences immune cell activation, tolerance, and homeostasis.

The IgG1 subclass is commonly used in therapeutic antibodies due to its strong ADCC activity mediated through high-affinity binding to FcγRIIIa. This binding is critically dependent on structural features of the Fc region, particularly the hinge region and CH2 domain, as well as the conserved N-linked glycan attached to Asn297. Engineering efforts have focused on enhancing binding to activating FcγRs to improve antitumor efficacy, but recent interest has turned toward exploiting the immunomodulatory potential of FcγRIIb. Engagement of FcγRIIb can suppress B-cell activation and proliferation when co-ligated with the B-cell receptor (BCR), inhibit dendritic cell maturation and T-cell activation, and serve as a molecular scaffold to enhance the agonistic activity of antibodies targeting members of the tumor necrosis factor receptor (TNFR) superfamily.

Despite the therapeutic promise of FcγRIIb-selective antibodies, a significant challenge arises from the high sequence homology between FcγRIIb and FcγRIIa, particularly at the Fc-binding interface. Both receptors share approximately 95% amino acid identity in their extracellular domains, differing primarily at position 131 (arginine in FcγRIIb and FcγRIIa-R131 versus histidine in FcγRIIa-H131). This similarity makes it extremely difficult to engineer Fc variants that distinguish between these receptors. Previous attempts, such as the S267E/L328F substitution, achieved substantial enhancement of FcγRIIb binding but also increased binding to FcγRIIa-R131 to a comparable extent. This lack of selectivity poses serious clinical concerns, as enhanced FcγRIIa binding can lead to platelet activation and aggregation, potentially causing thromboembolic complications, as observed in clinical trials with certain anti-CD154 and anti-VEGF antibodies. Furthermore, increased binding to FcγRIIa may accelerate antibody clearance through receptor-mediated endocytosis and degradation, reducing therapeutic half-life.

The importance of FcγRIIb in maintaining immune tolerance is underscored by genetic studies linking FcγRIIb polymorphisms to susceptibility to systemic lupus erythematosus and other autoimmune diseases. Consequently, there exists a compelling need for Fc variants that not only enhance binding to FcγRIIb but do so with high selectivity over both FcγRIIa allotypes. Such variants would enable the development of safer and more effective therapeutic antibodies that harness the immunomodulatory functions of FcγRIIb without triggering the adverse effects associated with FcγRIIa activation. The present invention addresses this unmet need by providing novel polypeptide variants with unprecedented selectivity for FcγRIIb, thereby overcoming the limitations of existing Fc engineering approaches.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention

The primary problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of selectivity in existing Fc variants engineered to enhance binding to the inhibitory FcγRIIb receptor. Current approaches, exemplified by the S267E/L328F substitution, significantly increase binding affinity for FcγRIIb but simultaneously enhance binding to the activating FcγRIIa-R131 allotype to a similar or greater extent. This dual enhancement creates serious therapeutic liabilities, including the potential for platelet activation and aggregation leading to thromboembolic events, as well as accelerated antibody clearance through FcγRIIa-mediated internalization and degradation. Additionally, existing polypeptides fail to adequately distinguish between FcγRIIb and both FcγRIIa allotypes (H131 and R131), limiting their utility in diverse patient populations with varying FcγRIIa genotypes. The invention aims to overcome these limitations by providing novel polypeptide variants that achieve selective enhancement of FcγRIIb binding while maintaining or decreasing binding to both FcγRIIa allotypes and other activating FcγRs.

### Means for Solving the Problems

The invention provides polypeptide variants comprising an altered Fc region that exhibits enhanced FcγRIIb-binding activity with high selectivity over FcγRIIa. Specifically, the variants demonstrate a dissociation constant (KD) ratio for FcγRIIb relative to FcγRIIa of at least 10, preferably at least 50, and more preferably at least 100, as measured by surface plasmon resonance. The enhanced FcγRIIb-binding activity is achieved through specific amino acid alterations in the lower hinge and CH2 domain of the Fc region, including but not limited to substitutions at positions 233, 237, 238, 268, 271, 296, and 330 according to EU numbering. The polypeptides maintain or decrease binding to FcγRIIIa and FcγRIa, thereby minimizing unwanted effector functions. The invention encompasses IgG antibodies (particularly human IgG1 and IgG4 isotypes) and Fc fusion protein molecules incorporating these modified Fc regions. Methods for producing such polypeptides involve site-directed mutagenesis of nucleic acids encoding the Fc region, followed by expression in appropriate host cells and purification. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising these polypeptides are formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers for various routes of administration. The polypeptides find application as therapeutic or preventive agents for immunological inflammatory diseases, autoimmune disorders, diseases involving deficiency of biologically essential proteins, and viral infections. They function as agents for suppressing activation of immune cells, particularly B cells, mast cells, basophils, and dendritic cells, through selective engagement of FcγRIIb. Kits for therapeutic and inhibitory methods are provided, along with specific uses of the polypeptides in treating various conditions. The invention also includes methods for suppressing antibody production against supplemented proteins and inhibiting virus replication through antibody-dependent enhancement suppression.

### Effects of the Invention

The polypeptides of the present invention provide several significant advantages over existing Fc variants. By achieving selective enhancement of FcγRIIb binding without increasing FcγRIIa binding, they eliminate the risk of platelet activation and thromboembolic complications associated with non-selective variants. The maintained or decreased binding to other activating FcγRs reduces the potential for unwanted inflammatory responses and cytokine release. The variants retain favorable pharmacokinetic properties, including normal binding to FcRn at acidic pH and comparable in vivo half-life to wild-type IgG1. Despite some reduction in thermal stability of the CH2 domain, the variants demonstrate acceptable storage stability at high concentrations relevant for pharmaceutical formulation. Most importantly, the selective FcγRIIb engagement enables potent immunomodulatory effects, including enhanced suppression of B-cell activation, improved agonistic activity of TNFR superfamily-targeting antibodies, and effective inhibition of mast cell and basophil degranulation, making them highly valuable for treating a wide range of immune-mediated disorders.

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The present invention provides polypeptides with modified Fc regions that exhibit selectively enhanced binding to Fc gamma receptors, particularly FcγRIIb. Fcγ receptors are cell surface proteins that bind the Fc portion of immunoglobulins and mediate various immune responses. The FcγR family includes activating receptors (FcγRIa, FcγRIIa, FcγRIIIa) and the inhibitory receptor FcγRIIb. Polypeptides as used herein refer to molecules comprising an Fc region derived from immunoglobulin heavy chains, including full-length antibodies, antibody fragments containing the Fc region, and Fc fusion proteins. The Fcγ receptors encompass human FcγRI (CD64), FcγRIIA (CD32a), FcγRIIB (CD32b), FcγRIIIA (CD16a), and FcγRIIIB (CD16b), each encoded by specific genes with characteristic polynucleotide and amino acid sequences. FcγRIIa exists in two allotypic forms due to a single nucleotide polymorphism at position 131, resulting in either histidine (H131) or arginine (R131) at the corresponding amino acid position, which affects binding affinity for different IgG subclasses.

The parent polypeptide refers to a naturally occurring IgG antibody, particularly human IgG1 or IgG4, which serves as the template for introducing modifications. The Fc region of naturally occurring IgG comprises the hinge region, CH2 domain, and CH3 domain, extending from approximately residue 216 to the C-terminus according to EU numbering. Binding activity to Fcγ receptors is determined using various analytical methods, with surface plasmon resonance (SPR) being particularly preferred. SPR analysis involves immobilizing the polypeptide on a sensor chip and measuring real-time binding kinetics with soluble Fcγ receptors, generating sensorgrams that allow calculation of association rate constants (ka), dissociation rate constants (kd), and dissociation constants (KD). Alternative methods include ALPHA screening, which utilizes amplified luminescent proximity homogeneous assays, and various inhibition assays that measure competition with reference antibodies.

Polypeptides with decreased FcγR-binding activity are defined as those exhibiting KD values at least 2-fold higher (indicating weaker binding) than the parent polypeptide, with examples showing 5-fold, 10-fold, or even undetectable binding. Conversely, polypeptides with enhanced FcγR-binding activity demonstrate KD values at least 2-fold lower (stronger binding) than the parent, with preferred variants showing 10-fold, 50-fold, 100-fold, or greater enhancement. Maintained binding activity refers to KD values within 2-fold of the parent polypeptide. The invention specifically focuses on polypeptides with increased FcγRIIb-binding activity (KD at least 2-fold lower than parent) combined with maintained or decreased FcγRIIa-binding activity (KD within 2-fold or at least 2-fold higher than parent, respectively). Binding selectivity for FcγRIIb is quantified by the ratio of KD(FcγRIIa)/KD(FcγRIIb), with ratios of at least 10, 50, or 100 being particularly advantageous.

The Fc region can be obtained through standard protein purification techniques, such as re-eluting from protein A column fractions. Antibody constant regions with altered Fc regions are created by introducing specific amino acid substitutions, with additional alterations possible to further enhance FcγRIIb-binding activity or improve other properties. Preferred additional alterations include substitutions at positions 233, 237, 268, 271, 296, and 330. Multiple alterations can be combined to achieve synergistic effects, as demonstrated by the V12 variant containing six specific substitutions. Alterations to improve FcRn-binding activity (important for serum half-life) and antibody heterogeneity or stability may also be introduced, though care must be taken to maintain FcγRIIb selectivity.

IgG antibodies encompass four isotypes (IgG1, IgG2, IgG3, IgG4), with human IgG1 and IgG4 being particularly preferred due to their favorable effector function profiles and clinical track records. The amino acid sequences of heavy-chain constant regions are well-established, though natural genetic polymorphisms exist, particularly in IgG1 and IgG4, which may influence FcγR binding characteristics. Specific alterations for improved FcγRIIb-binding activity include P238D, L328E, E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, Y296D, and A330R, among others identified through comprehensive mutagenesis screening. For maintained or decreased FcγRIIa-binding activity, the same substitutions often achieve this dual objective due to their selective mechanism of action. Alterations for improved FcRn-binding activity typically involve residues in the CH2-CH3 interface (e.g., M252Y, S254T, T256E), while stability improvements may target aggregation-prone regions or introduce stabilizing mutations.

Polypeptides with multiple alterations demonstrate cumulative benefits, such as the V12 variant (E233D/G237D/H268D/P271G/A330R combined with P238D) which achieves over 200-fold enhanced FcγRIIb binding without increasing FcγRIIa binding. These variants maintain acceptable biophysical properties, including solubility, stability, and manufacturability, making them suitable for pharmaceutical development.

### <Substitution>

Amino acid residues are classified into groups based on their physicochemical properties: acidic (aspartic acid, glutamic acid), basic (lysine, arginine, histidine), polar uncharged (serine, threonine, asparagine, glutamine), hydrophobic (alanine, valine, leucine, isoleucine, proline, phenylalanine, tryptophan, methionine), and special cases (glycine, cysteine). Conservative substitutions involve replacing an amino acid with another from the same group, while non-conservative substitutions involve different groups. Methods for producing amino acid sequence alterations include site-directed mutagenesis, PCR-based mutagenesis, and gene synthesis. Post-translational modifications, particularly glycosylation, significantly impact FcγR binding. The conserved N-linked glycan at Asn297 is essential for maintaining the Fc structure required for FcγR interactions. Glycosylation occurs in eukaryotic expression systems (e.g., CHO, HEK293 cells), while prokaryotic systems (e.g., E. coli) produce non-glycosylated antibodies that generally lack FcγR binding activity unless specifically engineered.

### <Antibody>

Antibodies are immunoglobulin molecules capable of specifically binding antigens, including full-length antibodies, antibody fragments (Fab, F(ab')2, scFv), and engineered formats. They can be of various origins: murine, chimeric, humanized, or fully human. Production methods include hybridoma technology, recombinant DNA technology, phage display libraries, and transgenic animals. Humanized antibodies are created by grafting complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) from non-human antibodies onto human framework regions, with possible back-mutations to maintain binding affinity. Genetic engineering techniques involve constructing expression vectors containing heavy and light chain genes under appropriate promoters, transforming host cells, and selecting high-producing clones. Variable regions contain the antigen-binding sites formed by six CDRs (three from heavy chain, three from light chain), which can be modified to alter binding specificity or affinity while maintaining the overall antibody structure. Antigens recognized by therapeutic antibodies include cytokines (e.g., TNFα, IL-6), chemokines, cell surface receptors (e.g., CD20, HER2), immunoglobulins, immune complexes, and various disease-associated proteins.

Heavy chain constant regions determine the antibody isotype and effector functions, with human IgG1 and IgG4 being preferred for therapeutic applications requiring controlled FcγR interactions. Light chains exist as kappa (κ) or lambda (λ) types, with their constant regions also subject to modification. Fc fusion protein molecules consist of a biologically active protein or peptide fused to the Fc region of an immunoglobulin, conferring extended half-life and potential FcγR-mediated functions. Bispecific antibodies, which bind two different antigens simultaneously, can also incorporate the modified Fc regions of the present invention.

Production of polypeptides with modified Fc regions involves cloning nucleic acids encoding the desired heavy and light chains into expression vectors, transfecting host cells (mammalian, insect, yeast, bacterial, or plant cells), and purifying the expressed protein using chromatographic methods (protein A/G, ion exchange, size exclusion). Mammalian expression systems (e.g., CHO, HEK293) are preferred for proper glycosylation and folding. Stable cell lines are generated through selection and amplification of integrated genes, while transient expression allows rapid production for screening purposes. Methods for suppressing antibody production involve creating variants that selectively engage FcγRIIb to inhibit B-cell activation and antibody secretion, particularly useful in autoimmune diseases or when administering replacement proteins to prevent neutralizing antibody formation.

Nucleic acids encoding the modified polypeptides are inserted into vectors (plasmids, viral vectors) under control of appropriate promoters and regulatory elements. Host cells transformed with these vectors express the polypeptides, which are then harvested and purified. The invention encompasses all methods for modifying polypeptide Fc regions to achieve the desired binding profile, including rational design based on structural insights and high-throughput screening approaches.

### <Pharmaceutical Compositions>

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise the modified polypeptides formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents. Parenteral administration is preferred, typically as sterile aqueous solutions or suspensions for injection. Formulations may include vegetable oils, emulsifiers, suspending agents, surfactants, stabilizers, flavoring agents, vehicles, and preservatives. The active ingredient concentration is adjusted based on the intended dose and route of administration. Aqueous formulations often use physiological saline or isotonic solutions buffered to physiological pH, with solubilizers (alcohols, polyalcohols) and non-ionic surfactants to enhance stability. Additional components may include buffers, analgesics, stabilizers, and antioxidants to maintain product integrity.

Administration routes include intravenous, subcutaneous, intramuscular, intranasal, intrapulmonary, and percutaneous delivery. Dosage is determined based on patient factors (age, weight, disease severity) and typically ranges from 0.1 to 20 mg/kg body weight, administered weekly, biweekly, or monthly. The polypeptides suppress B-cell activation by co-engaging FcγRIIb with the BCR, leading to inhibition of IgE, IgM, and IgA production. They suppress mast cell and basophil activation by cross-linking FcγRIIb with IgE receptors, preventing degranulation and mediator release. In dendritic cells, FcγRIIb engagement inhibits activation and costimulatory molecule expression, promoting tolerance.

Therapeutic applications include immunological inflammatory diseases (rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease), autoimmune diseases (systemic lupus erythematosus, multiple sclerosis), diseases with protein deficiencies (hemophilia, enzyme deficiencies), and viral infections (where antibody-dependent enhancement is a concern). Fusion proteins incorporating the modified Fc can suppress autoantibody production or prevent immune responses against therapeutic proteins. Kits for therapeutic and inhibitory methods include the polypeptide, instructions for use, and possibly ancillary reagents.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A comprehensive analysis of Fc variants was conducted by systematically mutating residues in the lower hinge and CH2 region of human IgG1. Over 500 single-substitution variants were generated by replacing each of approximately 30 key residues with every naturally occurring amino acid except cysteine. These variants were screened for binding to human FcγRIIa-H131, FcγRIIa-R131, and FcγRIIb using surface plasmon resonance. Interaction analysis results revealed that most substitutions either decreased binding to all receptors or enhanced binding non-selectively. Remarkably, only two substitutions—P238D and L328E—demonstrated the desired profile of enhanced FcγRIIb binding coupled with decreased binding to both FcγRIIa allotypes. This finding was particularly significant because previously reported variants like S267E and L328F enhanced binding to both FcγRIIb and FcγRIIa-R131. The P238D variant showed the most pronounced effect, substantially enhancing FcγRIIb binding while simultaneously reducing affinity for all activating FcγRs. These results established P238D as a foundational mutation for developing FcγRIIb-selective antibodies.

### Example 2

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis was performed to quantitatively characterize the binding kinetics of Fc variants. The P238D substitution was introduced into the IgG1 antibody framework, and the resulting variant (IgG1-v1) was expressed in FreeStyle™ 293F cells and purified using protein A chromatography. Kinetic analysis of interactions with Fcγ receptors was conducted using a Biacore instrument, with antibodies captured on a CM5 sensor chip via protein A/G immobilization. Binding data were globally fitted to a 1:1 Langmuir binding model to calculate association rate constants (ka), dissociation rate constants (kd), and dissociation constants (KD). The KD values for wild-type IgG1, IgG1-v1 (P238D), IgG1-v2 (L328E), and IgG1-v3 (S267E/L328F) were determined for all human FcγRs. Relative KD values, calculated as KD(IgG1)/KD(variant), demonstrated that IgG1-v1 and IgG1-v2 enhanced FcγRIIb binding by 15-fold and 8-fold, respectively, while decreasing binding to FcγRIIa allotypes. In contrast, IgG1-v3 enhanced both FcγRIIb and FcγRIIa-R131 binding by 355-fold and 864-fold, respectively. Selectivity was evaluated using I/A(R) [KD(FcγRIIa-R131)/KD(FcγRIIb)] and I/A(H) [KD(FcγRIIa-H131)/KD(FcγRIIb)] ratios, which were 25 and >100 for IgG1-v1, compared to 0.4 and 15 for IgG1-v3. These results conclusively demonstrated the superior selectivity of P238D-containing variants for FcγRIIb over both FcγRIIa allotypes.

### Example 3

To explore the potential for combining FcγRIIb-selective binding alterations, additional variants were created by introducing the P238D mutation into different antibody frameworks. The IL6R-G1d-v4 variant incorporated P238D into an anti-interleukin-6 receptor antibody, while IL6R-G1d-v5 combined P238D with other mutations. Binding activities of IgG1, IgG1-v1 (P238D), IgG1-v2 (L328E), and IgG1-v4 were evaluated, confirming the consistent FcγRIIb-selective profile of P238D across different antibody contexts. Similarly, evaluation of IgG1, IgG1-v1, IgG1-v3 (S267E/L328F), and IgG1-v5 demonstrated that combining P238D with S267E/L328F did not yield additive effects, likely due to structural incompatibilities between the different binding interfaces. These experiments highlighted the difficulty of creating Fc variants with excellent selectivity for FcγRIIb, as most combinations either failed to enhance binding or compromised selectivity. The P238D mutation emerged as a unique solution that could not be easily combined with other known FcγRIIb-enhancing mutations, underscoring its distinct mechanism of action.

### Example 4

A comprehensive analysis was undertaken to identify additional mutations that could further enhance FcγRIIb binding when combined with P238D. Motivated by the unique binding interface created by P238D, approximately 400 single-substitution variants were generated on the P238D background, focusing on the hinge and CH2 regions. Variants IL6R-F11 and IL6R-F652 were produced by transient expression in FreeStyle™ 293F cells and purified using protein A chromatography. Interactions with FcγRIIa and FcγRIIb were evaluated by SPR, with binding activity normalized to the parent P238D variant and expressed as relative binding activity values. Analysis results, presented in FIG. 6, identified eleven alterations that enhanced FcγRIIb binding when introduced into the P238D background. The activities of these eleven variants were summarized in Table 6, showing fold-increases ranging from 1.2 to 5.0. Notably, introducing these alterations into the P238D variant yielded significantly greater improvements than when introduced into non-P238D backgrounds, demonstrating the context-dependence of these mutations. KD values for selected variants were measured and summarized in Table 7, calculated using Equation 2 from the binding kinetics data. The analysis revealed that specific combinations, particularly those including P271G, dramatically improved affinity for FcγRIIb while maintaining selectivity, leading to the conclusion that the P238D background enables the identification of mutations that cooperatively enhance FcγRIIb binding selectivity.

### Example 5

X-ray crystallographic analysis was performed to elucidate the structural basis for FcγRIIb selectivity conferred by the P238D mutation. The three-dimensional structure of the Fc(P238D)/FcγRIIb complex was determined and compared with the known structure of the Fc(WT)/FcγRIIa-R131 complex. Fc fragments corresponding to heavy chain residues 216-447 (EU numbering) were cloned, with Cys220 mutated to Ser to prevent disulfide scrambling. FcγRIIb extracellular region was expressed in FreeStyle™ 293F cells in the presence of kifunensine to produce homogeneous Man5GlcNAc2 glycans, then deglycosylated using endoglycosidase F1 to minimize heterogeneity. The Fc(P238D)/FcγRIIb complex was formed by mixing components at a 1:1.2 molar ratio and purified by size exclusion chromatography. Crystals were grown by sitting drop vapor diffusion and flash-frozen for data collection at 2.6 Å resolution. The structure was solved by molecular replacement using known Fc and FcγRIIb structures as search models. Refinement yielded a high-quality model revealing that Pro238 in wild-type IgG1 forms a hydrophobic core with surrounding residues, while Asp238 in the mutant shifts outward to solvent exposure, creating a large cavity in the CH2-B domain. Key interactions included a novel hydrogen bond between Gly237 main chain and FcγRIIb Tyr160, and a salt bridge between Asp270 and FcγRIIb Arg131. These interactions are specific to FcγRIIb because FcγRIIa has Phe160 (cannot form hydrogen bond) and His131 (cannot form salt bridge with Asp270), explaining the molecular basis for selectivity.

### Example 6

Building on the structural insights from Example 5, Fc variants with alterations designed to optimize the Fc(P238D)/FcγRIIb interface were created. Variants IL6R-B3 and IL6R-BF648 incorporated multiple substitutions identified in the P238D background screen. These variants were expressed in FreeStyle™ 293F cells and purified for binding evaluation. Relative binding activity values to FcγRs were calculated and presented in FIG. 13, identifying 24 alterations that improved FcγRIIb binding when combined with P238D. Detailed binding results were compiled in Table 9, and KD values were measured and shown in Table 10. Analysis of binding selectivity revealed that specific residue combinations achieved remarkable FcγRIIb preference. Structural studies indicated that position 233 (Glu→Asp) potentially interacts with FcγRIIb Lys113, position 330 (Ala→Arg) may interact with FcγRIIb Glu86, and position 271 (Pro→Gly) increases loop flexibility to accommodate the Asp270-Arg131 salt bridge. These interactions contribute favorably to binding free energy and selectivity, as the corresponding FcγRIIa residues cannot form equivalent interactions.

### Example 7

The most promising alterations identified in Examples 4 and 6 were combined to create optimized Fc variants. Six effective substitutions (E233D, G237D, H268D, P271G, Y296D, A330R) were introduced into the P238D background to generate the V12 variant. Additional combinations were also tested, including variants with five substitutions. All variants were expressed in FreeStyle™ 293F cells and purified for comprehensive binding evaluation. Relative binding activity values were calculated and presented in Table 11, showing that V12 achieved the highest FcγRIIb binding enhancement (62-fold over P238D). KD values were measured and compiled in Table 12, confirming that V12 exhibited 217-fold enhanced binding to FcγRIIb compared to wild-type IgG1, while maintaining binding to FcγRIIa-R131 and decreasing binding to other activating FcγRs. The binding selectivity for FcγRIIb was exceptional, with I/A(R) and I/A(H) ratios exceeding 200, making V12 the most selective FcγRIIb-binding variant reported to date.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The present invention provides polypeptides with enhanced FcγRIIb-binding activity that exhibit unprecedented selectivity over activating Fcγ receptors, particularly FcγRIIa in both its allotypic forms. These polypeptides are industrially applicable as therapeutic agents for a wide range of immune-mediated disorders, including autoimmune diseases, allergic conditions, inflammatory disorders, and certain cancers. Their selective FcγRIIb engagement enables potent immunomodulatory effects without the thromboembolic risks associated with non-selective Fc variants. The variants maintain favorable pharmacokinetic properties and demonstrate acceptable stability for pharmaceutical development, making them suitable for commercial manufacturing and clinical use. The invention thus represents a significant advancement in antibody engineering with substantial therapeutic and commercial potential.
