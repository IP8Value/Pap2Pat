# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cyclin D1 is a critical regulatory protein that governs progression through the G1 phase of the eukaryotic cell cycle. It functions primarily by forming active complexes with cyclin-dependent kinases 4 and 6 (CDK4/6), which phosphorylate the retinoblastoma protein (Rb), thereby promoting the release of E2F transcription factors and facilitating the transition from G1 to S phase. Cyclin D1 is encoded by the *CCND1* gene and is tightly regulated at multiple levels, including transcriptional activation, post-translational modification, subcellular localization, and proteolytic degradation. Dysregulation of cyclin D1 expression or stability is a hallmark of numerous human cancers, including breast, esophageal, and colorectal carcinomas, where its overexpression drives uncontrolled cellular proliferation and tumorigenesis.

The regulation of cyclin D1 is multifaceted. Transcriptionally, it is induced by mitogenic signals such as growth factors that activate the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK (MAPK) signaling cascade. However, equally important is the post-translational control of cyclin D1 protein stability, which ensures timely degradation to prevent aberrant cell cycle progression. Central to this regulation is the phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at threonine 286 (Thr286), a residue located near its C-terminus. This phosphorylation event serves as a molecular switch that triggers nuclear export and subsequent ubiquitin-mediated proteasomal degradation. Historically, glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK3β) was proposed as the primary kinase responsible for Thr286 phosphorylation. However, recent evidence has challenged this view, particularly in the context of cancer cells where alternative kinases may dominate this regulatory step.

Ubiquitin-mediated degradation of cyclin D1 occurs through the 26S proteasome pathway and is strictly dependent on prior phosphorylation at Thr286. The polyubiquitination process involves a cascade of enzymatic reactions mediated by E1 (ubiquitin-activating), E2 (ubiquitin-conjugating), and E3 (ubiquitin ligase) enzymes. Among these, E3 ligases confer substrate specificity. The SCF (Skp1–Cullin–F-box) family of E3 ligases is particularly notable for recognizing phosphorylated substrates. These complexes consist of four core components: SKP1 (an adaptor), CUL1 or CUL7 (a scaffold), RBX1 (a RING finger protein that recruits E2), and an F-box protein that directly binds the phosphorylated substrate. Despite extensive research, the specific F-box protein responsible for targeting phosphorylated cyclin D1 for degradation remained elusive, with conflicting reports implicating SKP2 or other candidates without conclusive validation across diverse cellular contexts.

FBXW8, an F-box protein containing WD-40 repeat motifs, has emerged as a component of an SCF-like complex that associates with CUL7 and SKP1. While initially linked to insulin-like growth factor signaling and neuronal development, its role in cell cycle regulation—particularly in the turnover of key regulators like cyclin D1—was unknown. The lack of a defined E3 ligase for cyclin D1 has hindered the development of targeted therapies aimed at modulating its stability in cancer. Current strategies often focus on CDK4/6 inhibition but do not address the root cause of cyclin D1 accumulation due to defective degradation. Moreover, the precise kinase responsible for Thr286 phosphorylation in cancer cells, where MAPK signaling is constitutively active, remains inadequately characterized, creating a gap in understanding how oncogenic signals both induce and subsequently terminate cyclin D1 activity during cell cycle progression.

This incomplete understanding of cyclin D1 regulation—specifically, the identity of the kinase that phosphorylates Thr286 in cancer cells and the E3 ligase that mediates its degradation—represents a significant limitation in the field. Without this knowledge, rational drug design to modulate cyclin D1 turnover for therapeutic benefit is severely constrained. The present invention addresses this critical need by identifying the MAPK pathway as the dominant regulator of Thr286 phosphorylation in cancer cells and FBXW8 as the specific E3 ubiquitin ligase that recognizes phosphorylated cyclin D1, thereby providing novel targets and methods for controlling cellular proliferation in neoplastic diseases.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for modulating cellular proliferation by targeting the phosphorylation and ubiquitin-mediated degradation of cyclin D1. Specifically, the invention discloses that the Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK (MAPK) signaling cascade directly phosphorylates cyclin D1 at Thr286 in a manner dependent on a conserved D-domain within cyclin D1, and that this phosphorylation event is recognized by the F-box protein FBXW8, which functions as the substrate-recognition subunit of an SCF-like E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. This complex, comprising FBXW8, SKP1, CUL1 or CUL7, and RBX1, mediates the polyubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal degradation of cyclin D1, particularly during S phase in cancer cells.

The invention encompasses methods for detecting agents that modulate the phosphorylation of cyclin D1, including those that inhibit or enhance the activity of MAPK or other kinases involved in Thr286 phosphorylation. Similarly, it provides methods for identifying agents that modulate the ubiquitination of cyclin D1 by influencing the activity, expression, or assembly of the FBXW8-containing E3 ligase complex. Such methods enable the control of cell proliferation, particularly in hyperproliferative disorders such as cancer, by either stabilizing or destabilizing cyclin D1 as therapeutically appropriate.

In particular, the invention includes methods for modulating FBXW8 activity, whether by altering its transcription, translation, post-translational modification, degradation, or its interaction with phosphorylated cyclin D1. Modulation may be achieved through small molecules, peptides, antibodies, or nucleic acid-based agents such as siRNAs or antisense oligonucleotides. The invention further provides screening methods for test agents that affect the interaction between FBXW8 and phosphorylated cyclin D1, including binding assays, fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET), and co-immunoprecipitation. Additionally, assays are described for detecting the effects of test agents on the ubiquitination of phosphorylated cyclin D1, as well as on the total levels of cyclin D1, phosphorylated cyclin D1, and ubiquitinated cyclin D1 in cells.

The invention also relates to isolated polypeptide complexes comprising FBXW8, SKP1, CUL1 or CUL7, RBX1, and phosphorylated cyclin D1, as well as reaction mixtures suitable for in vitro reconstitution of cyclin D1 ubiquitination. These compositions are useful for high-throughput screening and mechanistic studies. By targeting this newly elucidated regulatory axis—MAPK → Thr286 phosphorylation → FBXW8 recognition → ubiquitination—the invention offers a powerful platform for developing novel therapeutics that restore normal cyclin D1 turnover in cancer cells, thereby inhibiting uncontrolled proliferation.

### FBXW8 Protein (F-BOX WD-40 Domain Protein 8)

FBXW8 is a 592-amino acid human F-box protein containing WD-40 repeats that mediate substrate recognition. It functions as the substrate receptor in an SCF-like E3 ligase complex. FBXW8 binds SKP1 via its F-box domain and CUL7 or CUL1 via its C-terminal region. The protein is expressed in various tissues, with elevated levels in certain cancers. Human FBXW8 nucleic acid sequences are available under GenBank accession NM_001005208. Variants with conservative substitutions in the WD-40 domain may retain function. Regions amenable to modification include the F-box and WD-40 domains. GenBank accessions include NP_001005208 for the protein.

### MAPK (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase)

MAPK, particularly ERK1/2, is a serine/threonine kinase activated by the Ras/Raf/MEK cascade. It phosphorylates substrates with Ser/Thr-Pro motifs and requires a D-domain for efficient binding. MAPK phosphorylates cyclin D1 at Thr286, triggering its degradation. Human MAPK1 (ERK2) sequence is under GenBank NM_002745. Variants with kinase activity are encompassed. GenBank accessions include NP_002736.

### CULLIN 1 (CUL1)

CUL1 is a scaffold protein in SCF complexes, linking SKP1-F-box proteins to RBX1. It is essential for E3 ligase activity. Human CUL1 sequence is NM_003592. Functional variants are included. GenBank accession NP_003583.

### Cullin 7 (CUL7)

CUL7 forms SCF-like complexes with SKP1 and FBXW8. It is involved in cyclin D1 degradation in cancer cells. Human CUL7 sequence is NM_001164260. GenBank accession NP_001157732.

### SKP1 (S-Phase Kinase-associated Protein 1)

SKP1 bridges F-box proteins to CUL1/CUL7. Human SKP1 sequence is NM_001127627. Variants maintaining adaptor function are included. GenBank accession NP_001121099.

### Cyclin D1

Cyclin D1 is a 34 kDa protein regulating G1/S transition. It is phosphorylated by MAPK at Thr286, leading to nuclear export and degradation. Human cyclin D1 sequence is NM_053056. Modifications include point mutations like T286A. Functional assays include kinase activity and degradation kinetics.

Methods included standard cell culture, siRNA transfection, immunoblotting, and kinase assays. Cell lines were synchronized by serum starvation. Vectors expressed wild-type or mutant cyclin D1 and FBXW8. siRNAs targeted FBXW8, CUL1, or CUL7. Pulse-chase used ³⁵S-methionine. Immunofluorescence localized proteins. Key findings: cyclin D1 is destabilized in S phase in cancer cells; degradation is cytoplasmic and proteasome-dependent; MAPK phosphorylates Thr286 via a D-domain; FBXW8 is the E3 ligase recognizing phospho-Thr286; FBXW8 knockdown stabilizes cyclin D1 and inhibits proliferation; nuclear cyclin D1 T286A rescues proliferation.
