# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

All living organisms have evolved sophisticated molecular mechanisms to preserve the integrity of their genomic DNA, which is continuously subjected to endogenous and exogenous sources of damage. Among the most deleterious forms of DNA lesions are double-strand breaks (DSBs), which, if left unrepaired or inaccurately repaired, can lead to chromosomal rearrangements, loss of genetic information, cellular senescence, or malignant transformation. To counteract such threats, eukaryotic cells have developed two primary pathways for DSB repair: non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) and homology-directed repair (HDR). These pathways operate with distinct biochemical mechanisms, fidelity levels, and cell cycle dependencies, often acting in a competitive or antagonistic manner to determine the ultimate fate of a broken DNA molecule.

NHEJ is a rapid and error-prone repair mechanism that functions throughout the cell cycle but predominates in G0/G1 phases. It directly ligates the broken DNA ends without requiring a homologous template, frequently resulting in small insertions or deletions (indels) at the break site due to nucleolytic processing prior to ligation. Core components of the classical NHEJ (C-NHEJ) pathway include the Ku70/Ku80 heterodimer, which binds and protects DNA ends, and DNA ligase IV (LIG4), which catalyzes the final ligation step in complex with XRCC4 and XLF. While C-NHEJ is generally efficient, its mutagenic nature renders it unsuitable for applications requiring precise genetic modifications.

In contrast, HDR is a high-fidelity repair process that utilizes an undamaged homologous DNA sequence—typically the sister chromatid during S/G2 phases—as a template to accurately restore the original sequence at the break site. This pathway involves extensive end resection to generate 3′ single-stranded DNA overhangs, followed by strand invasion, DNA synthesis, and resolution. Key HDR factors include BRCA1, BRCA2, RAD51, CtIP, and cell cycle regulators such as CDK1, which phosphorylates resection nucleases to promote efficient DNA end processing. Despite its precision, HDR is inherently less efficient than NHEJ in most mammalian somatic cells, posing a significant bottleneck for genome editing technologies that rely on donor-templated correction or insertion.

The advent of CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing has revolutionized molecular biology by enabling targeted induction of DSBs at virtually any genomic locus specified by a single guide RNA (sgRNA). Upon Cas9-mediated cleavage, the cell’s endogenous repair machinery processes the break via either NHEJ or HDR. While NHEJ is exploited for gene knockouts through frameshift mutations, HDR is essential for precise knock-ins, point corrections, and other therapeutic genome engineering applications. However, the overwhelming dominance of NHEJ over HDR in mammalian cells severely limits the efficiency and reliability of HDR-dependent editing outcomes.

Numerous strategies have been explored to tilt the balance toward HDR, including chemical inhibition of NHEJ components (e.g., SCR7 targeting LIG4), stimulation of HDR factors (e.g., RS-1 enhancing RAD51 activity), or synchronization of cells in S/G2 phases using cell cycle inhibitors. Yet these approaches suffer from significant drawbacks: small molecules often exhibit cytotoxicity, variable efficacy across cell types, poor bioavailability, or off-target effects; cell cycle manipulation is impractical for in vivo use and may disrupt normal physiology. Consequently, there remains an unmet need for a genetically encoded, tunable, and broadly applicable method to enhance HDR efficiency while simultaneously suppressing error-prone NHEJ, thereby enabling robust and precise genome editing across diverse biological contexts.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for enhancing homology-directed repair (HDR) and/or reducing non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) in cells undergoing CRISPR-Cas9-mediated genome editing. Central to this invention is the use of catalytically dead guide RNAs (dgRNAs)—shortened guide RNAs of 14–15 nucleotides that retain target-binding specificity but lack cleavage activity—to direct transcriptional modulation of key DNA repair genes via CRISPR activation (CRISPRa) and interference (CRISPRi) systems, all while employing a single active Cas9 nuclease for concurrent genome editing.

In one aspect, the invention encompasses an expression vector comprising a dgRNA scaffold engineered with MS2 RNA hairpin loops, operably linked to a promoter, wherein the dgRNA targets a promoter or enhancer region of an HDR-promoting gene such as CDK1 or CtIP. This vector further includes a nucleic acid sequence encoding a CRISPRa effector complex, such as MCP-P65-HSF1 (MPH), which is recruited to the dgRNA-bound genomic locus via MCP-MS2 interactions to activate transcription of the target HDR gene.

In another aspect, the invention provides an expression vector comprising a dgRNA scaffold containing Com RNA binding motifs, operably linked to a promoter, wherein the dgRNA targets a regulatory region of an NHEJ-promoting gene such as LIG4, KU70, or KU80. This vector also encodes a CRISPRi repressor fusion protein, such as COM-KRAB (CK), which is recruited via COM-Com interactions to silence expression of the targeted NHEJ gene.

A further embodiment includes a reporter vector featuring a nonfunctional green fluorescent protein (GFP) variant—such as a broken-frame Venus (bf-Venus)—operably linked to a promoter, wherein restoration of fluorescence occurs only upon precise HDR-mediated correction using an exogenous donor template. This “traffic light reporter” enables quantitative assessment of HDR versus NHEJ outcomes in live cells.

Additionally, the invention features inducible expression systems wherein the CRISPRa and/or CRISPRi effector proteins are under the control of a tetracycline-responsive element (TRE3G) promoter, and the vector further comprises a reverse tetracycline-controlled transactivator (rtTA) sequence. In the presence of doxycycline, rtTA binds TRE3G to drive expression of MPH or CK, allowing temporal control over HDR enhancement and NHEJ suppression.

The invention also includes a composition comprising one or more of the aforementioned vectors together with a Cas9-encoding nucleic acid, forming a complete system for simultaneous genome editing and DNA repair pathway modulation. A host cell stably or transiently transfected with such a composition is also provided.

Methods of the invention involve introducing into a cell: (i) a first vector encoding a dgRNA-MS2:MPH construct targeting an HDR gene; (ii) a second vector encoding a dgRNA-Com:CK construct targeting an NHEJ gene; and (iii) a Cas9 expression construct along with an sgRNA and HDR donor template for a desired genomic edit. In preferred embodiments, both activation and repression plasmids are co-delivered to synergistically enhance HDR efficiency. The method may employ an inducible system where effector expression is activated by doxycycline.

Kits are also disclosed, comprising one or more of the described vectors, a Cas9 expression construct, optional HDR donor templates, and instructional material for use in enhancing HDR during CRISPR-mediated genome editing. These kits facilitate precise genetic modifications in research, biotechnology, and therapeutic applications.

### Description

CRISPR systems, particularly CRISPR-Cas9, have emerged as transformative tools for programmable genome editing. However, their utility for precise modifications is constrained by the low efficiency of HDR relative to NHEJ in mammalian cells. Existing strategies to overcome this limitation—such as small molecule inhibitors or cell cycle synchronization—are often cytotoxic, transient, or incompatible with in vivo applications. The present invention addresses these shortcomings by leveraging dgRNA-mediated CRISPRa/i to genetically reprogram the DNA repair landscape. By simultaneously activating HDR-promoting genes (e.g., CDK1, CtIP) and repressing NHEJ factors (e.g., LIG4, KU70/80) using a single active Cas9, the invention achieves synergistic enhancement of HDR efficiency—up to 8-fold in experimental models—without chemical intervention. This approach is modular, reversible, and adaptable to viral delivery, offering unprecedented control over genome editing outcomes.

### Compositions

The invention provides compositions for enhancing HDR and reducing NHEJ, centered on dgRNA-based transcriptional modulators. Dead guide RNAs (dgRNAs), truncated to 14–15 nt, bind target genomic loci without cleaving DNA, serving as scaffolds for recruitment of effector domains. For activation, dgRNAs are engineered with MS2 stem-loops that bind MCP fused to transcriptional activators P65 and HSF1 (MPH complex). Example HDR targets include CDK1 (promotes end resection) and CtIP (initiates resection). For repression, dgRNAs incorporate Com aptamers that recruit COM fused to KRAB repressor domain (CK complex), targeting NHEJ genes like LIG4, KU70, and KU80.

Inducible systems utilize rtTA under a constitutive promoter (e.g., CMV) and place MPH or CK under a TRE3G promoter. Doxycycline induces rtTA binding to TRE3G, driving effector expression only when needed, minimizing off-target effects. A traffic light reporter vector contains a nonfunctional bf-Venus (disrupted codons 53–63) followed by T2A and frameshifted mCherry. HDR repair restores Venus fluorescence; NHEJ indels (~1/3) restore mCherry. Promoters include U6 for dgRNA, CMV or EF1α for effectors, and TRE3G for inducible expression. Additional elements include nuclear localization signals (NLS), flexible linkers, and polyadenylation signals. Vectors are compatible with lentiviral packaging for stable delivery.

### Methods

Methods involve introducing into a cell: (1) an activation plasmid with dgRNA-MS2 targeting an HDR gene (e.g., CDK1) and MPH effector; (2) a repression plasmid with dgRNA-Com targeting an NHEJ gene (e.g., KU80) and CK effector; (3) Cas9; (4) an sgRNA for the target genomic locus; and (5) an HDR donor template. Transfection or viral transduction delivers components. Inducible systems add doxycycline to activate rtTA-driven expression. sgRNAs are designed with 20-nt spacers complementary to target sites adjacent to PAM sequences. Lentiviral packaging uses psPAX2 and pMD2.G helper plasmids. Administration to animals (e.g., mice) employs hydrodynamic injection, electroporation, or viral vectors. Cas9 recognizes target DNA via sgRNA complementarity and cleaves 3 bp upstream of the PAM (5′-NGG-3′). CRISPRi uses dCas9-KRAB for repression, but here active Cas9 performs editing while dgRNAs recruit separate effectors. Gene transfer uses viral (lentivirus, AAV) or non-viral (lipofection, electroporation) methods. RNA delivery involves in vitro-transcribed mRNA with 5′ cap, poly(A) tail, and modified nucleotides for stability, transfected via electroporation. Cells are sourced from peripheral blood, umbilical cord, or cell lines, isolated by FACS or magnetic sorting, and cryopreserved.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the described vectors or RNA constructs in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier (e.g., saline, liposomes). Administration routes include intravenous, intramuscular, or local injection. Dosage ranges vary by application but typically involve 1–100 μg nucleic acid per kg body weight. Formulations follow conventional techniques for nucleic acid therapeutics, with no limitation to specific excipients.

## Experimental Examples

Experiments validated the invention using HEK293, HEK293T, and HeLa cells. Activation plasmids (dgRNA-MS2:MPH) and repression plasmids (dgRNA-Com:CK) were constructed by cloning MS2 or Com loops into dgRNA scaffolds and fusing MCP-P65-HSF1 or COM-KRAB under CMV promoters. A traffic light reporter (TLR) plasmid was built with bf-Venus-T2A-fs-mCherry under CMV control. Cas9-stable cell lines were generated via lentiviral transduction and antibiotic selection. Lentivirus production used HEK293FT cells co-transfected with transfer, psPAX2, and pMD2.G plasmids. RT-qPCR confirmed CDK1 upregulation (~3-fold) and KU80 downregulation (~50%). Confocal imaging and flow cytometry quantified fluorescence. Genomic DNA sequencing verified precise HDR integration. Cell cycle analysis showed no major perturbations; CCK-8 assays confirmed viability. Sample sizes were n=3; blinding was not used.

### Example 1

HDR enhancement was tested by transfecting dgCDK1-2:MS2-MPH and/or dgKU80-1:Com-CK into HEK293-Cas9-TLR cells. After 24 h, cells received sgVenus-ECFP and EGFP donor. Flow cytometry showed baseline HDR at 2.42%; dgCDK1-2 alone increased to 6.1%, dgKU80-1 to 5.8%, and combination to 15.4%. Similar results occurred at endogenous AAVS1 and ACTB loci, with 4–8-fold HDR boosts. Sequencing confirmed precise SA-T2A-EGFP integration. Inducible TRE-MPH/CK systems showed doxycycline-dependent enhancement. Lentiviral delivery of dgCDK1-MS2:MPH also enhanced HDR, proving adaptability.
