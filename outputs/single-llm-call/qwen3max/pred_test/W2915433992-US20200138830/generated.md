# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The immune system serves as the body’s primary defense mechanism against pathogens, malignancies, and foreign tissues, yet its dysregulation can lead to autoimmune diseases, chronic inflammation, and graft rejection. Recent scientific inquiry has revealed an unexpected intersection between the nervous and immune systems through neuroactive molecules—chemicals traditionally associated with neuronal signaling that also exert profound effects on immune cell function. Among these, gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA), the principal inhibitory neurotransmitter in the central nervous system (CNS), has emerged as a critical modulator of peripheral immune responses. GABA functions primarily through two classes of receptors: ionotropic GABAA receptors, which are ligand-gated chloride channels, and metabotropic GABAB receptors, which are G-protein coupled receptors. In the CNS, activation of GABAA receptors hyperpolarizes neurons by increasing chloride ion influx, thereby reducing neuronal excitability and mitigating conditions such as anxiety, seizures, and insomnia. However, it is now well-established that both GABAA and GABAB receptors are expressed not only in neurons but also in various immune cells, including T lymphocytes, antigen-presenting cells, and pancreatic β-cells, suggesting a broader physiological role for GABA beyond neurotransmission.

GABA’s influence on the immune system is increasingly recognized as a key regulatory mechanism. Studies have demonstrated that GABA signaling can suppress pro-inflammatory immune responses by inhibiting the proliferation and effector functions of pathogenic T-cell subsets such as Th1, Th17, and cytotoxic CD8+ T cells, while simultaneously promoting the differentiation and function of regulatory T cells (Tregs), which maintain immune tolerance. This dual action—dampening inflammation while enhancing regulatory mechanisms—positions GABA as a promising therapeutic candidate for autoimmune disorders like type 1 diabetes (T1D), rheumatoid arthritis, and multiple sclerosis, as well as for preventing allograft rejection following organ transplantation. Despite these promising attributes, the clinical utility of GABA itself is limited by its poor bioavailability and inability to cross the blood-brain barrier (BBB) in significant quantities when administered peripherally, thereby restricting its access to CNS targets but potentially favoring peripheral immune modulation without sedative side effects.

Autoimmune diseases arise from a breakdown in self-tolerance, leading the immune system to attack the body’s own tissues. In T1D, autoreactive T cells destroy insulin-producing β-cells in the pancreatic islets, resulting in lifelong dependence on exogenous insulin. Current interventional strategies have largely failed to achieve durable remission, underscoring the need for combination therapies that simultaneously address autoimmunity and promote β-cell regeneration. Similarly, in the context of solid organ or islet transplantation, the recipient’s immune system often mounts a destructive response against donor tissue, necessitating potent immunosuppression that carries significant risks of infection, malignancy, and metabolic complications. There remains a critical unmet need for safe, effective, and targeted approaches to modulate immune responses—specifically, to reduce inflammatory pathways while bolstering regulatory mechanisms—without causing global immunosuppression or unacceptable neurological side effects. The discovery that GABA receptor signaling can fulfill both immunomodulatory and regenerative roles offers a compelling foundation for novel therapeutic strategies, particularly when enhanced through pharmacological means that amplify endogenous GABA activity without requiring high systemic doses of GABA itself.

## SUMMARY

The present invention is based on the discovery that gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) exerts potent anti-inflammatory and immunoregulatory effects by modulating T-cell responses through GABAA receptor signaling. Specifically, GABA suppresses the proliferation and cytokine production of pro-inflammatory T-cell subsets—including Th1, Th17, and CD8+ T cells—while promoting the expansion and function of regulatory T cells (Tregs). These effects are mediated via GABAA receptors expressed on immune cells, which, upon activation, induce intracellular chloride fluxes that alter cellular activation thresholds and downstream signaling pathways. However, the therapeutic potential of GABA is constrained by its limited bioavailability and rapid metabolism in vivo. To overcome this limitation, the invention introduces the use of positive allosteric modulators (PAMs) of the GABAA receptor, which do not activate the receptor directly but instead enhance the receptor’s response to endogenous or co-administered GABA. When administered in conjunction with a GABA receptor activating ligand—such as GABA itself or other agonists—these PAMs produce synergistic effects that significantly amplify immunomodulatory outcomes at substantially lower doses than would be required for either agent alone. This synergy enables effective suppression of pathogenic immune responses and promotion of regulatory immunity while minimizing the risk of dose-dependent adverse effects, particularly those associated with central nervous system penetration. The invention thus provides a novel, safe, and efficacious strategy for treating or preventing immune-mediated disorders, including autoimmune diseases, graft rejection, and allergic conditions, through the combined administration of a GABAA receptor PAM and a GABA receptor activating ligand.

## Embodiment 1

The invention provides a method for reducing an inflammatory immune response in a subject in need thereof, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a positive allosteric modulator (PAM) of the GABAA receptor. This method leverages the intrinsic ability of GABAA receptor signaling to dampen excessive or misdirected immune activation, particularly in contexts where inflammation contributes to tissue damage or disease progression. The inflammatory immune response targeted by this method includes, but is not limited to, responses mediated by autoreactive T cells in autoimmune disorders, alloreactive T cells in transplant settings, or allergen-specific T cells in hypersensitivity reactions. By enhancing GABAA receptor activity through PAM administration, the method achieves a measurable reduction in the magnitude, duration, or pathological consequences of the inflammatory response, thereby preserving tissue integrity and function. The PAM may be administered alone if sufficient endogenous GABA is present in the microenvironment of immune cells; however, optimal efficacy is typically achieved when the PAM is co-administered with a GABA receptor activating ligand, as detailed in subsequent embodiments.

## Embodiment 2

In a specific embodiment, the positive allosteric modulator (PAM) of the GABAA receptor exerts its effect by potentiating the chloride conductance of the receptor upon binding of GABA or another activating ligand, thereby amplifying the inhibitory signal transduced into the immune cell. This potentiation results in a more pronounced suppression of T-cell receptor signaling cascades, calcium influx, and nuclear factor of activated T cells (NFAT) translocation, all of which are critical for T-cell activation and proliferation. Consequently, the PAM effectively lowers the threshold of GABA concentration required to achieve immunomodulation, enabling robust anti-inflammatory effects even in microenvironments where GABA levels are suboptimal. This mechanism is particularly advantageous in disease states such as early-stage type 1 diabetes, where β-cell mass—and thus endogenous GABA production—is diminished, rendering monotherapy with GABA insufficient. The PAM, by sensitizing GABAA receptors to available GABA, restores effective signaling and re-establishes immune homeostasis without necessitating high systemic GABA concentrations that could pose pharmacokinetic or safety challenges.

## Embodiment 3

The inflammatory immune response reduced by the methods of the invention comprises T cell proliferation, particularly the clonal expansion of antigen-specific T lymphocytes that drive tissue destruction in autoimmune and alloimmune settings. In type 1 diabetes, for example, autoreactive CD4+ and CD8+ T cells proliferate in response to β-cell antigens, infiltrate the islets, and mediate β-cell death through direct cytotoxicity and cytokine release. Similarly, in graft rejection, recipient T cells recognize donor major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules as foreign and undergo vigorous proliferation, leading to graft damage. The invention interrupts this pathogenic cascade by targeting the proliferative phase of T-cell activation. Administration of a GABAA receptor PAM, especially in combination with a GABA receptor activating ligand, significantly diminishes the rate and extent of T-cell division in response to antigenic stimulation, as demonstrated by reduced incorporation of thymidine analogs such as BrdU or 3H-thymidine in vitro and decreased frequencies of Ki67+ T cells in vivo. This antiproliferative effect is selective for activated T cells and does not cause generalized lymphopenia, thereby preserving baseline immune surveillance.

## Embodiment 4

The invention further encompasses methods for promoting a regulatory immune response in a subject, comprising administering a positive allosteric modulator (PAM) of the GABAA receptor. Unlike conventional immunosuppressants that broadly inhibit immune function, this approach actively enhances the body’s natural mechanisms of immune tolerance by boosting the activity and stability of regulatory T cells (Tregs). Tregs, characterized by expression of the transcription factor FoxP3, play a pivotal role in maintaining peripheral tolerance and preventing autoimmunity. GABAA receptor signaling has been shown to support Treg differentiation from naïve CD4+ T cells and to reinforce the suppressive function of existing Tregs. By amplifying GABA-mediated signals through PAM administration, the invention creates a microenvironment conducive to Treg expansion and function, thereby rebalancing the immune system away from inflammation and toward regulation. This shift is particularly beneficial in chronic inflammatory conditions where Treg numbers or function are compromised.

## Embodiment 5

The regulatory immune response promoted by the invention specifically involves the enhancement of CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ regulatory T cells (Tregs). These cells exert their suppressive effects through multiple mechanisms, including the secretion of anti-inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-10 (IL-10) and transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β), direct cell-contact inhibition of effector T cells, and modulation of antigen-presenting cell function. The administration of a GABAA receptor PAM increases the frequency and/or functional capacity of these Tregs in lymphoid tissues and inflamed sites, as evidenced by higher FoxP3 expression levels and greater suppressive activity in co-culture assays. This Treg-centric mechanism ensures that immune modulation is targeted and adaptive, rather than indiscriminate, thereby minimizing the risk of opportunistic infections or loss of tumor surveillance associated with broad immunosuppression.

### Embodiment 6

The reduction in inflammatory immune response achieved by the invention is quantifiable and clinically meaningful, manifesting as a statistically significant decrease in markers of immune activation, tissue infiltration, and cytokine storm. In preclinical models, this reduction translates to improved disease outcomes, such as delayed onset of hyperglycemia in autoimmune diabetes or prolonged graft survival in transplantation. The magnitude of reduction is dose-dependent and is markedly enhanced when the PAM is used in combination with a GABA receptor activating ligand, allowing for therapeutic efficacy at doses that would be subtherapeutic if either agent were used alone.

### Embodiment 7

A key aspect of the invention is the downregulation of pro-inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, which are central mediators of immune pathology. Cytokines such as interleukin-1 (IL-1), IL-6, IL-12, tumor necrosis factor (TNF), interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF), and IL-23 drive inflammation, recruit effector cells to sites of injury, and sustain autoimmune loops. The invention significantly reduces the production and secretion of these molecules by activated T cells, macrophages, and dendritic cells, thereby disrupting the inflammatory cascade at multiple nodes.

### Embodiment 8

Specifically, the invention achieves downregulation of interleukin-1 (IL-1), a master regulator of innate immunity and a potent inducer of fever, acute-phase responses, and Th17 differentiation. Elevated IL-1 levels are implicated in the pathogenesis of type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, and other autoinflammatory syndromes. By suppressing IL-1 production through GABAA receptor potentiation, the invention mitigates one of the earliest and most damaging arms of the inflammatory response.

### Embodiment 9

The invention also specifically downregulates interleukin-12 (IL-12), a heterodimeric cytokine critical for the development of Th1 cells and the production of IFN-γ. IL-12 is produced primarily by antigen-presenting cells and promotes cell-mediated immunity against intracellular pathogens but also contributes to autoimmune tissue destruction. Reduction of IL-12 shifts the immune balance away from Th1 dominance, thereby attenuating organ-specific autoimmunity.

### Embodiment 10

Downregulation of interleukin-6 (IL-6) is another specific effect of the invention. IL-6 is a pleiotropic cytokine involved in B-cell differentiation, acute inflammation, and the transition from innate to adaptive immunity. It also inhibits Treg development and promotes Th17 expansion. By lowering IL-6 levels, the invention simultaneously curbs inflammation and favors regulatory over inflammatory T-cell fates.

### Embodiment 11

Tumor necrosis factor (TNF) is specifically downregulated by the methods of the invention. TNF is a major mediator of systemic inflammation and a key driver of tissue necrosis in autoimmune diseases such as Crohn’s disease and psoriasis. Its suppression is a validated therapeutic strategy, and the invention achieves this through GABAergic modulation without the immunogenicity risks associated with biologic anti-TNF agents.

### Embodiment 12

Interferon-gamma (IFN-γ), the signature cytokine of Th1 cells, is specifically downregulated. IFN-γ activates macrophages, upregulates MHC expression, and enhances antigen presentation—all of which can exacerbate autoimmune responses. Reducing IFN-γ production limits the amplification of autoreactive T-cell responses and decreases target tissue vulnerability.

### Embodiment 13

The invention specifically downregulates both granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) and interleukin-23 (IL-23). GM-CSF is essential for the survival and activation of myeloid cells that sustain inflammation, while IL-23 stabilizes the Th17 phenotype. Dual suppression of these factors disrupts the myeloid–lymphoid inflammatory axis critical in diseases like multiple sclerosis.

### Embodiment 14

Interleukin-23 (IL-23) is specifically downregulated, thereby impairing the maintenance and pathogenicity of Th17 cells. Since IL-23 signaling is non-redundant for Th17-mediated pathology, its reduction is a potent means of controlling diseases driven by this subset.

### Embodiment 15

The invention reduces inflammation-related T-cell types, including those that produce pro-inflammatory cytokines and mediate tissue damage. This includes not only classical subsets like Th1 and Th17 but also less-defined populations such as γδ T cells or mucosal-associated invariant T (MAIT) cells that contribute to inflammation in specific contexts.

### Embodiment 16

CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes, which directly kill target cells including β-cells in type 1 diabetes, are specifically reduced in number or function. The invention impairs their expansion and effector molecule expression (e.g., perforin, granzyme B), thereby protecting vulnerable tissues from immune-mediated destruction.

### Embodiment 17

Th17 cells, a subset of CD4+ T cells defined by RORγt expression and IL-17 production, are specifically reduced. Th17 cells are central to the pathogenesis of numerous autoimmune conditions, and their suppression is a hallmark of effective immunomodulation.

### Embodiment 18

Conversely, the invention increases the frequency and/or function of regulatory T cells (Tregs), particularly CD4+CD25+FoxP3+ cells. This increase is not merely numerical but functional, as evidenced by enhanced suppressive capacity in vitro and greater protection in vivo.

### Embodiment 19

In a preferred embodiment, the method comprises administering a positive allosteric modulator (PAM) of the GABAA receptor in conjunction with a GABA receptor activating ligand. This combination exploits the synergistic interaction between the two agents: the ligand provides the primary activating signal, while the PAM amplifies the receptor’s response, resulting in greater efficacy than either component alone.

### Embodiment 20

The synergy between the PAM and the GABA receptor activating ligand is not merely additive but multiplicative, meaning that the combined effect exceeds the sum of individual effects. This synergy arises from the allosteric nature of PAM action, which increases the affinity and/or efficacy of GABA binding without occupying the orthosteric site.

### Embodiment 21

This synergistic interaction results in a fold increase in effectiveness—typically at least 2-fold, and often 5- to 10-fold or more—compared to monotherapy with either agent at equivalent doses. For instance, a combination may achieve 80% suppression of T-cell proliferation where each agent alone achieves only 30–40%.

### Embodiment 22

Due to this synergy, the invention enables the use of lower dosages of both the GABA receptor activating ligand and the PAM. Lower dosing reduces the risk of off-target effects, particularly those related to central nervous system penetration, while maintaining or even enhancing therapeutic outcomes.

### Embodiment 23

The dosage of the GABA receptor activating ligand can be reduced by at least 50%, and in many cases by 70–90%, when co-administered with a PAM. For example, a dose of GABA that is ineffective alone may become highly effective when paired with a subtherapeutic dose of alprazolam.

### Embodiment 24

Similarly, the dosage of the PAM can be reduced by at least 50%—often to subtherapeutic levels for its traditional indications—when used in combination with a GABA receptor activating ligand. This minimizes sedative, anxiolytic, or addictive potential while retaining immunomodulatory benefits.

### Embodiment 25

The PAM may be administered at a subtherapeutic dosage, defined as a dose below that required to produce its primary pharmacological effect (e.g., anxiolysis for benzodiazepines). At such doses, the PAM lacks significant CNS activity but retains the ability to potentiate GABA signaling in peripheral immune cells.

### Embodiment 26

The subject treated according to the invention is a human. The methods are designed for clinical application in patients suffering from or at risk of immune-mediated disorders.

### Embodiment 27

In a specific application, the human subject has type 1 diabetes. The invention aims to preserve residual β-cell mass, reduce autoimmunity, and potentially delay or reverse disease progression.

### Embodiment 28

The human subject may also be pre-diabetic, exhibiting autoantibodies or impaired glucose tolerance but not yet insulin-dependent. Early intervention with the invention may prevent or delay the onset of clinical diabetes.

### Embodiment 29

The invention is also applicable to non-human mammals, particularly for veterinary use or preclinical research. Rodents, primates, and companion animals with autoimmune or inflammatory conditions may benefit from the disclosed methods.

### Embodiment 30

The synergistic effect of the PAM and GABA receptor activating ligand is reproducible across multiple experimental systems and disease models, confirming its robustness and generalizability as a therapeutic principle.

### Embodiment 31

The PAM is a positive allosteric modulator of the GABAA receptor, which includes several chemical classes: barbiturates, benzodiazepines, neurosteroids, and others. Each class binds to distinct allosteric sites on the receptor complex.

### Embodiment 32

Barbiturate PAMs are encompassed by the invention. These compounds bind to a site distinct from benzodiazepines and can directly activate the receptor at high doses, but at low doses act as pure potentiators.

### Embodiment 33

Specific barbiturate PAMs include phenobarbital, secobarbital, and pentobarbital. These may be used in the invention, particularly in formulations designed for peripheral action.

### Embodiment 34

Benzodiazepine PAMs are a preferred class due to their well-characterized safety profile and selectivity for GABAA receptors containing α1, α2, α3, or α5 subunits.

### Embodiment 35

Specific benzodiazepine PAMs include diazepam, lorazepam, and oxazepam. These are widely available and suitable for repurposing in immunomodulatory regimens.

### Embodiment 36

Alprazolam is a particularly preferred benzodiazepine PAM due to its high potency, selectivity for relevant GABAA receptor subtypes, and lack of affinity for the mitochondrial translocator protein (formerly “peripheral benzodiazepine receptor”).

### Embodiment 37

When used in the invention, alprazolam is administered at a lower dosage than typically used for anxiety—often 10- to 100-fold lower—thereby avoiding sedation while retaining immunomodulatory effects.

### Embodiment 38

A specific dosage of alprazolam for immunomodulation is 0.01 to 0.1 mg/kg/day in humans, corresponding to approximately 0.7 to 7 mg/day for a 70 kg adult, significantly below the standard anxiolytic dose of 0.5–4 mg/day.

### Embodiment 39

Midazolam, another benzodiazepine PAM, is also encompassed by the invention. It is particularly useful in acute settings due to its rapid onset.

### Embodiment 40

Midazolam is administered at a lower dosage than used for sedation—typically 0.005 to 0.05 mg/kg—sufficient for immune modulation but insufficient to cause significant CNS depression.

### Embodiment 41

A specific dosage of midazolam is 0.1 to 1 mg/day in adults, compared to sedative doses of 1–10 mg.

### Embodiment 42

Clonazepam, a long-acting benzodiazepine, is also suitable for chronic immunomodulatory therapy.

### Embodiment 43

Clonazepam is used at reduced dosages, such as 0.1 to 0.5 mg/day, versus typical anticonvulsant doses of 1–20 mg/day.

### Embodiment 44

A specific dosage of clonazepam is 0.25 mg once daily, providing sustained immune modulation with minimal side effects.

### Embodiment 45

Neurosteroid PAMs, which are endogenous or synthetic steroids that modulate GABAA receptors, are also within the scope of the invention. They offer the advantage of natural metabolic pathways and potential tissue selectivity.

### Embodiment 46

Specific neurosteroid PAMs include allopregnanolone, ganaxolone, and brexanolone. These may be formulated for oral or parenteral delivery.

### Embodiment 47

Algiax, a proprietary neurosteroid PAM, is specifically contemplated for use in the invention due to its favorable pharmacokinetic and safety profile.

### Embodiment 48

AP325, another investigational PAM, is included as a specific embodiment. It exhibits high selectivity for immune-relevant GABAA receptor isoforms.

### Embodiment 49

AP325 is administered at a lower dosage than would be required for CNS effects—typically in the range of 0.1 to 1 mg/kg—enabling peripheral immunomodulation without sedation.

### Embodiment 50

GABA itself is a primary GABA receptor activating ligand used in the invention. It is administered orally, intravenously, or via other routes to elevate systemic or local concentrations.

### Embodiment 51

Other GABA receptor activating ligands include muscimol, isoguvacine, and THIP (gaboxadol), which are selective GABAA agonists with varying receptor subtype preferences.

### Embodiment 52

Alcohol (ethanol) is explicitly excluded as a GABA receptor activating ligand in the context of this invention due to its non-selective actions, toxicity, and abuse potential.

### Embodiment 53

Kavalactones, derived from kava plant, are excluded due to hepatotoxicity concerns and lack of specificity.

### Embodiment 54

Skullcap or its constituents (e.g., baicalin) are excluded because their GABAergic effects are indirect and poorly characterized.

### Embodiment 55

Valerian or its constituents (e.g., valerenic acid) are excluded for similar reasons—weak, non-specific modulation with insufficient evidence for reliable immune effects.

### Embodiment 56

Volatile gases such as isoflurane or sevoflurane, though they modulate GABAA receptors, are excluded due to their use only in anesthesia and impracticality for chronic therapy.

### Embodiment 57

The invention is directed to the treatment of inflammatory diseases, broadly defined as conditions characterized by dysregulated or excessive immune activation leading to tissue damage.

### Embodiment 58

Specific inflammatory diseases include type 1 diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis, inflammatory bowel disease, psoriasis, lupus, and uveitis.

### Embodiment 59

The invention is also useful for the prevention of graft rejection following solid organ, islet, or bone marrow transplantation by suppressing alloreactive T-cell responses.

### Embodiment 60

Allergic responses, which involve Th2-driven inflammation and mast cell activation, are also amenable to treatment by the invention through general suppression of T-cell reactivity and cytokine production.

### Embodiment 61

Specific allergic responses include asthma, allergic rhinitis, atopic dermatitis, and food allergies.

### Embodiment 62

The invention explicitly excludes the treatment of psychiatric disorders such as anxiety, depression, or insomnia as primary indications, focusing instead on immune-mediated conditions.

### Embodiment 63

Administration of the PAM for the purpose of anesthesia or sedation is excluded; the invention is solely for immunomodulation at sub-sedative doses.

### Embodiment 64

BBB-permeable PAMs are administered at low doses that achieve peripheral immune effects without significant CNS penetration or activity, leveraging their pharmacokinetics for targeted action.

### Definitions

A “GABA receptor activating ligand” refers to any compound that binds to and activates GABAA or GABAB receptors, including GABA, muscimol, and other orthosteric agonists, but excluding allosteric modulators that lack intrinsic activity. A “positive allosteric modulator of GABAA receptor” (PAM) is a compound that binds to an allosteric site on the GABAA receptor and enhances the receptor’s response to GABA without activating the receptor in the absence of GABA. A “subtherapeutic dosage” is a dose of a drug that is below the threshold required to produce its primary clinical effect (e.g., anxiolysis for benzodiazepines) but sufficient to exert a secondary effect (e.g., immunomodulation) when combined with another agent. The terms “in conjunction with” or “in combination with” mean that two or more agents are administered together, either in the same formulation or separately, with overlapping pharmacokinetic profiles to enable pharmacodynamic interaction. A “regulatory response” refers to an immune response dominated by regulatory T cells and anti-inflammatory cytokines that suppresses effector immunity and maintains tolerance.
