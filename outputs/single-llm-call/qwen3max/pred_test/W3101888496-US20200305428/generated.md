# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of agricultural pathogen control, specifically to novel compounds and methods for enhancing plant immunity against a broad spectrum of phytopathogens. More particularly, the invention provides small molecule inhibitors of cinnamate-4-hydroxylase (C4H), an enzyme in the phenylpropanoid pathway, which unexpectedly enhance plant resistance to plant-parasitic nematodes, fungi, bacteria, and oomycetes without causing significant phytotoxicity or yield penalties. The invention further encompasses agrochemical compositions comprising these C4H inhibitors, methods of their application to crops, and their use in integrated pest management strategies to reduce crop losses caused by economically significant pathogens.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Plant pathogens represent a major constraint to global agricultural productivity, causing substantial pre- and post-harvest losses across diverse crop species. Among the most damaging categories of plant pathogens are bacteria, fungi, and nematodes, each employing distinct mechanisms to colonize host tissues and extract nutrients at the expense of plant health and yield. Bacterial phytopathogens typically invade through natural openings or wounds, multiplying in intercellular spaces and producing toxins, cell wall-degrading enzymes, or effectors that suppress host immunity. Fungal pathogens, which include both biotrophs that derive nutrients from living host cells and necrotrophs that kill host tissue prior to colonization, employ specialized infection structures such as appressoria to breach plant surfaces and establish feeding relationships. Nematodes, particularly plant-parasitic nematodes (PPN), constitute another major class of pathogens that feed on roots or aerial parts, with sedentary endoparasites like root-knot and cyst nematodes forming elaborate feeding sites that reprogram host cell development.

Plant disease symptoms resulting from pathogen infection vary widely depending on the pathogen type, host species, and environmental conditions but commonly include chlorosis, necrosis, wilting, stunting, gall formation, and reduced reproductive output. Pathogenic bacteria such as Pseudomonas syringae, Xanthomonas spp., and Ralstonia solanacearum cause diseases characterized by leaf spots, blights, wilts, and cankers through the deployment of type III secretion system effectors that interfere with host defense signaling. Plant-pathogenic fungi encompass a vast diversity of organisms including Ascomycota, Basidiomycota, and early-diverging lineages that cause rusts, smuts, mildews, rots, and blights through combinations of mechanical penetration, enzymatic degradation, and effector-mediated suppression of immunity.

Nematode lifecycle stages are critical to understanding their pathogenicity and control. Plant-parasitic nematodes exhibit diverse lifestyles, with migratory species like Pratylenchus spp. moving through root tissues while feeding, and sedentary species like Meloidogyne (root-knot) and Heterodera/Globodera (cyst) nematodes establishing permanent feeding sites after initial migration. The second-stage juvenile (J2) is typically the infective stage for sedentary nematodes, which penetrates roots, migrates to vascular tissues, and induces the formation of specialized feeding cells (giant cells or syncytia) that support their development through subsequent molts to adulthood. Plant parasitic nematodes cause damage through direct feeding, disruption of vascular function, and creation of entry points for secondary pathogens, leading to estimated global yield losses of 10–25%.

Biotrophs and necrotrophs represent two fundamental pathogenic strategies among fungi and oomycetes. Biotrophic pathogens maintain host cell viability during infection, often through sophisticated effector arsenals that suppress defense responses and manipulate host metabolism. Necrotrophic pathogens, in contrast, kill host cells rapidly through the production of toxins and lytic enzymes, then feed on the dead tissue. Many pathogens exhibit hemibiotrophic lifestyles, beginning with a biotrophic phase before switching to necrotrophy.

Current control strategies for plant pathogens rely heavily on chemical pesticides, resistant cultivars, cultural practices, and biological control agents. However, these approaches face significant limitations. Chemical nematicides and fungicides often exhibit environmental persistence, non-target toxicity, and increasing regulatory restrictions. The development of resistant crop varieties is hampered by the rapid evolution of pathogen virulence and the limited availability of resistance genes for many pathosystems. Cultural practices such as crop rotation are ineffective against pathogens with wide host ranges or long-lived survival structures. Biological control agents frequently show inconsistent field performance due to environmental variability and complex ecological interactions. These limitations underscore the urgent need for novel, sustainable approaches to enhance plant immunity against diverse pathogens.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compounds and methods for enhancing plant immunity against a broad spectrum of phytopathogens through the targeted inhibition of cinnamate-4-hydroxylase (C4H), a key enzyme in the phenylpropanoid pathway. Contrary to expectations that inhibiting this central metabolic enzyme would compromise plant defense, the inventors have discovered that specific C4H inhibitors unexpectedly enhance resistance to plant-parasitic nematodes, fungi, bacteria, and oomycetes while maintaining acceptable levels of plant growth and yield.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method of inhibiting cinnamate-4-hydroxylase in plants through the exogenous application of small molecule compounds that specifically target this enzyme. These compounds are characterized by their aromatic carboxylic acid or benzoic acid derivative structures, often incorporating benzodioxole moieties with carboxylic acid functional groups. The invention encompasses specific compound formulas, including general Formula I, II, and III structures, which demonstrate potent C4H inhibitory activity at biologically relevant concentrations.

The compounds of the invention are used for preventing infection by phytopathogens and increasing plant resistance through mechanisms that involve the accumulation of trans-cinnamic acid conjugates and compensatory upregulation of defense-related genes. Unlike conventional pesticides that directly target pathogens, these compounds act by modulating plant metabolism to create an unfavorable environment for pathogen establishment and proliferation. The invention further provides agrochemical compositions comprising these C4H inhibitors formulated with agriculturally acceptable carriers, surfactants, and additives to facilitate their application through various methods including foliar spray, seed coating, and soil drench.

The present invention builds upon the understanding of plant defense and immune responses, which involve complex networks of signaling pathways and metabolic reprogramming upon pathogen perception. While the phenylpropanoid pathway has traditionally been viewed as essential for defense through the production of lignin, flavonoids, and other phenolic compounds, the inventors have discovered that strategic inhibition of cinnamate-4-hydroxylase (C4H) leads to unexpected enhancement of plant immunity.

The compounds of the invention demonstrate minimal effects on plant growth when applied at effective concentrations, while simultaneously exhibiting significant effects on pathogen development and reproduction. C4H inhibitors function by blocking the conversion of trans-cinnamic acid to p-coumaric acid, leading to the accumulation of trans-cinnamic acid and its conjugates. This metabolic shift triggers compensatory upregulation of C4H gene expression and activates alternative defense pathways that confer broad-spectrum resistance.

The invention defines C4H inhibitors as compounds that reduce C4H enzyme activity by at least 50% at concentrations of 100 μM or less, as measured by standard enzymatic assays monitoring the conversion of trans-cinnamic acid to p-coumaric acid. Techniques for measuring C4H activity include spectrophotometric assays, high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC), and mass spectrometry-based methods that quantify substrate depletion or product formation.

Primary root elongation assays and lignification impairment assays serve as phenotypic markers for C4H inhibition in planta. The invention encompasses the use of C4H inhibitor precursors that are metabolically converted to active inhibitors within plant tissues. Small molecule C4H inhibitors of the invention are characterized by aromatic carboxylic acid structures, with general Formula I representing core scaffolds that include benzodioxole carboxylic acids.

The invention includes all stereoisomers, tautomers, hydrates, salts, and solvates of the disclosed compounds. General Formula II encompasses compounds with halogen and alkynyl substituents that enhance potency and selectivity. General Formula III represents optimized structures with improved agrochemical properties. Specific compounds of the invention include 3,4-(methylenedioxy)cinnamic acid (MDCA), 3,4-(methylenedioxy)benzoic acid (PA), and structurally related analogs.

Functional equivalents or analogs of the disclosed compounds are included within the scope of the invention, as are prodrugs, salts, oxides, solvates, and inclusion complexes that release the active inhibitor in planta. Agrochemical acceptable salts include sodium, potassium, ammonium, and organic amine salts that improve solubility and formulation stability. Esters of the compounds may serve as prodrugs that are hydrolyzed in plant tissues to release the active carboxylic acid form.

Methods for controlling plant pathogens according to the invention demonstrate efficacy against plant parasitic nematodes, including root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.), cyst nematodes (Heterodera and Globodera spp.), and lesion nematodes (Pratylenchus spp.). The invention defines plant parasitic nematodes as nematodes that feed on plant tissues and cause economic damage, with specific species including M. graminicola, M. incognita, H. schachtii, and P. penetrans.

Phytopathogenic bacteria controlled by the invention include Pseudomonas syringae, Xanthomonas campestris, and Ralstonia solanacearum. Phytopathogenic viruses include tobacco mosaic virus and cucumber mosaic virus. Fungal plant pathogens include Fusarium oxysporum, Botrytis cinerea, and Magnaporthe oryzae. Phytopathogenic oomycetes include Pythium spp. and Phytophthora infestans.

The compounds provide improved resistance to disease through mechanisms that are independent of classical defense hormones like salicylic acid and jasmonic acid, as demonstrated in genetic backgrounds deficient in these pathways. Advantages of the invention include broad-spectrum activity, low environmental impact, compatibility with integrated pest management, and absence of significant phytotoxicity at effective doses.

Bioassay methods for evaluating compound efficacy include in vitro toxicity assays against pathogens and in planta infection assays measuring disease severity parameters. The compounds are isolated through chemical synthesis or extracted from natural sources, with synthetic routes providing consistent quality and scalability. Agriculturally acceptable formulations include aqueous solutions, emulsifiable concentrates, wettable powders, and granules. Liquid diluents include water, alcohols, glycols, and vegetable oils. Fibrous compositions may be used for slow-release applications. Effective amounts range from 0.1 to 1000 ppm active ingredient, with optimal concentrations varying by crop and pathogen. Non-nematicidal and non-fungicidal amounts may still provide resistance enhancement through plant-mediated mechanisms.

Compounds may be used individually or in combination with other pesticides, fertilizers, or biostimulants. Packaging and combination compositions facilitate integrated application strategies. Application methods include foliar spray, soil drench, seed treatment, and drip irrigation. The invention defines plants and crops broadly to include monocots and dicots, with exemplary species including rice, wheat, tomato, sugar beet, and carrot.

Examples demonstrate efficacy across diverse pathosystems, confirming the broad applicability of the invention.

## EXAMPLES

Plant material for experiments included diverse crop species: rice cultivars Nipponbare and IR64, sugar beet cultivar Monohikari, wheat cultivars Chinese Spring and Bobwhite, tomato cultivar Moneymaker, and carrot cultivar Chantenay. Seeds were germinated on moist filter paper and transferred to SAP tubes containing sterile sand for controlled environment studies. Seedlings were watered with Hoagland solution to ensure uniform nutrient status.

Nematode cultures were maintained on susceptible host plants under controlled conditions. Meloidogyne graminicola was extracted from infected rice roots by maceration and sieving, with J2s collected from egg masses hatched in water. Nematode suspensions were counted using a hemocytometer for standardized inoculation. Meloidogyne incognita was similarly extracted from tomato roots, while Heterodera schachtii cysts were harvested from sugar beet roots and hatched in root exudate medium. Pratylenchus penetrans was multiplied on carrot disks and extracted by Baermann funnel.

Fungal cultures were obtained from culture collections and maintained on potato dextrose agar. Spores were harvested by flooding plates with sterile water containing Tween 80 and filtering through Miracloth. Chemical treatments included foliar spray solutions prepared in water with 0.01% Silwet L-77, applied to plants at the 3-4 leaf stage. Seed coatings were prepared by mixing compounds with methyl cellulose and applying to seeds before planting.

Nematode infection experiments involved inoculating plants with standardized numbers of J2s (typically 100-500 per plant) and harvesting at appropriate time points for phenotyping. Nematode and gall counts were performed under dissecting microscopy. Fungal infection experiments used spore suspensions sprayed onto leaves, with lesion counts recorded after incubation in high humidity chambers.

Nematode toxicity assays incubated J2s in compound solutions for 24-72 hours, with motility assessed by counting moving versus immobile nematodes. Fungal toxicity assays spotted spore suspensions on agar plates containing compounds, measuring colony diameter after incubation. Pseudomonas syringae infection used bacterial suspensions infiltrated into leaves, with disease severity scored by chlorosis and necrosis.

### Effect of C4H Inhibition on Growth and Development in Uninfected Crops

Phytotoxic effects of C4H inhibitors were evaluated by applying compounds to uninfected crops at various concentrations. Rice, wheat, and tomato plants treated with MDCA and PA at concentrations up to 100 μM showed no significant reduction in shoot height, root length, or biomass compared to untreated controls. Chlorophyll content and photosynthetic efficiency remained unaffected, indicating that the compounds do not impair basic physiological processes at effective doses.

### C4H Inhibitors have No Effect on Yield and Fertility in Wheat

Wheat plants were treated with PA at 50 μM at tillering stage and grown to maturity under controlled conditions. Treated plants produced similar numbers of spikes per plant, grains per spike, and total grain weight compared to controls. Seed size distribution and germination ability of harvested seeds were equivalent between treated and untreated plants, demonstrating that C4H inhibition does not compromise reproductive fitness or seed quality.

### The Early Phenylpropanoid Pathway and Rice Defense

Rice plants were treated with MDCA or PA and challenged with M. graminicola. Treated plants showed significantly reduced gall counts (60-70% reduction) and impaired nematode development compared to controls. Histological analysis revealed enhanced defense responses including hypersensitive cell death around invading juveniles and restricted giant cell formation. These results demonstrate that C4H inhibition potentiates rice defense against nematodes through mechanisms involving programmed cell death and feeding site disruption.

### Second Infection Experiment in Rice

A second experiment confirmed the protective effects of MDCA and PA in rice. Growth curves showed equivalent plant height and developmental progression between treated and control plants, confirming absence of phytotoxicity. Phenotypic parameters including shoot height and root length were statistically indistinguishable. Gall counts and nematode numbers were reduced by 65-75% in treated plants, with microscopic examination revealing inhibition of juvenile invasion and gall establishment. These results validate the reproducibility and robustness of the protective effect.

### Example 5

C4H inhibition in wheat against root-knot nematodes demonstrated 58% reduction in M. graminicola galls with PA treatment. Additional C4H inhibitors tested against M. graminicola in rice showed varying efficacy, with CF3-BA achieving 72% gall reduction. In tomato, PA reduced M. incognita disease pressure by 63%, with similar efficacy across different application regimens (foliar vs. soil drench). In sugar beet, PA reduced H. schachtii cyst numbers by 55%. In wheat, C4H inhibitors reduced P. penetrans populations by 48%.

Fungal assays showed PA is not directly fungicidal, as it did not inhibit Pythium splendens growth on agar. Analogs of 4-iodobenzoic acid demonstrated C4H inhibitory activity, with CF3-BA phenocopying IBA effects. In OsWRKY45 rice knockdown lines, MDCA/PA still reduced gall counts, indicating WRKY45-independent mechanisms. In NahG-expressing tomato (SA-deficient), PA maintained efficacy against M. incognita. DIECA-treated (JA-biosynthesis inhibited) tomato plants responded normally to PA, demonstrating JA independence.

qPCR analysis showed MDCA/PA treatment upregulated OsPAL4 and OsC4H genes while downregulating OsGNS5. Against P. syringae in tomato, PA reduced disease pressure by 59% without affecting bacterial growth in vitro. PA seed coating improved green bean seedling health against P. splendens without affecting germination. The mechanism involves metabolic reprogramming leading to enhanced pre-penetration resistance and post-penetration defense activation, providing broad-spectrum protection across diverse crops and pathogens.
