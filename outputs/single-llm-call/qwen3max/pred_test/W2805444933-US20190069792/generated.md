# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention pertains to the field of cardiac electrophysiology and, more specifically, to methods and systems for analyzing intracardiac electrograms to detect and characterize abnormalities associated with atrial fibrillation. The invention provides a novel computational framework for identifying regions of abnormal wavefront propagation within the atria by quantifying regional dominant frequency and wave break rate from sequentially acquired intracardiac electrogram signals. This approach enables improved localization of potential drivers or sources of atrial fibrillation, thereby facilitating more effective catheter ablation therapy.

## BACKGROUND

Atrial fibrillation (AF) represents the most prevalent sustained cardiac arrhythmia encountered in clinical practice and constitutes a major risk factor for stroke, heart failure, and other cardiovascular complications. Characterized by rapid, disorganized electrical activity in the atria, AF results in ineffective atrial contraction and irregular ventricular response. Despite significant advances in understanding its pathophysiology, the precise mechanisms underlying AF initiation and perpetuation remain incompletely elucidated, particularly in persistent forms of the disease.

Catheter ablation has emerged as a cornerstone therapeutic intervention for patients with symptomatic AF refractory to pharmacological management. The procedure involves the delivery of radiofrequency energy or cryothermal lesions to specific regions of the atrial myocardium to eliminate or isolate triggers and substrates responsible for sustaining AF. Pulmonary vein isolation (PVI), which targets ectopic foci originating from the pulmonary veins, remains the foundational strategy for paroxysmal AF. However, outcomes for persistent AF are notably suboptimal, prompting the exploration of additional substrate-based ablation targets such as complex fractionated electrograms (CFE), low voltage zones, and dominant frequency (DF) sites.

Conventional approaches to guide ablation rely heavily on the interpretation of intracardiac electrograms (IEGMs), including metrics like CFE mean cycle length, bipolar signal duration, and DF derived from spectral analysis. While these methods have provided some clinical utility, they suffer from significant limitations. CFE mapping is highly susceptible to noise, far-field signals, and variations in catheter contact, leading to poor reproducibility. DF analysis, though theoretically linked to high-frequency drivers, often fails to distinguish between active sources and passive bystander activity due to spatial averaging and temporal instability. Moreover, existing techniques typically analyze individual electrode pairs in isolation, neglecting the spatiotemporal relationships among simultaneously recorded signals that reflect the true nature of wavefront propagation.

These shortcomings underscore a critical need for more robust, mechanistically grounded tools capable of objectively characterizing the dynamic and heterogeneous nature of AF. Specifically, there is an unmet demand for methods that can reliably identify regions exhibiting organized, high-frequency activity amidst surrounding disorganization—features hypothesized to correspond to rotors or focal sources that sustain AF. Such methods must account for the transient and non-stationary characteristics of AF electrograms while minimizing reliance on error-prone activation time annotations. The present invention addresses this need by introducing a novel signal processing paradigm that leverages regional signal fusion and time-frequency analysis to extract meaningful features indicative of wavefront integrity and instability.

## SUMMARY

The invention introduces a method for detecting abnormalities in atrial fibrillation by extracting and fusing features from intracardiac electrograms (IEGMs) using advanced time-frequency analysis. This method enables the identification of regions with abnormal wavefront dynamics, which are likely to represent critical sites for AF perpetuation. The core of the invention lies in the computation of two key metrics: regional dominant frequency (RDF) and wave break rate (WBR). RDF is derived by preprocessing IEGMs from multiple electrodes of a high-density mapping catheter, averaging the processed signals to form a composite regional signal, and then applying short-time Fourier transform (STFT) to estimate the dominant oscillatory frequency over brief time windows. WBR is calculated by detecting transient drops in the instantaneous RDF (iRDF) that exceed predefined thresholds in magnitude and duration, interpreted as manifestations of wavefront discontinuity or collision.

The method systematically excludes irrelevant or artifactual signals through rigorous preprocessing, including baseline removal, morphological simplification via Gaussian pulse replacement, and low-pass filtering using a specialized two-sided exponential finite impulse response (FIR) filter designed to preserve wavefront coherence while attenuating high-frequency noise arising from fragmentation. The resulting RDF and WBR values are assigned color codes based on patient-specific percentiles and displayed as overlaid maps on three-dimensional atrial geometries, providing an intuitive visual representation of regional electrophysiological properties.

Critically, the invention identifies sites characterized by high RDF and low WBR (↑RDF,↓WBR) as putative AF sources. These sites exhibit rapid but stable periodic activity, contrasting with surrounding areas of high WBR that reflect chaotic wave collisions. By correlating these computational findings with procedural outcomes—specifically, locations where ablation resulted in AF termination—the invention demonstrates that ↑RDF,↓WBR sites are strongly associated with successful ablation targets. Furthermore, the method detects changes in wavefront dynamics in real time, enabling dynamic assessment of substrate modification during ablation. The output is presented as a color-coded map integrated into standard electroanatomic mapping systems, thereby offering clinicians a practical, actionable tool for guiding substrate-based ablation beyond conventional PVI.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention encompasses comprehensive methods for analyzing intracardiac electrograms (IEGMs) to determine wavefront characteristics during atrial fibrillation. Central to this approach is the recognition that AF is sustained by complex spatiotemporal interactions of wavefronts, including rotation, block, and collision, which manifest as signal fragmentation and frequency instability in IEGMs. Rather than analyzing individual electrode signals in isolation, the invention processes signals from all electrodes of a high-density circular mapping catheter (e.g., 20-electrode spiral catheter) as an integrated regional unit. As illustrated in the block diagram of FIG. 1A, the method proceeds through sequential stages of preprocessing, feature extraction, fusion, and visualization. First, raw bipolar IEGMs undergo preprocessing to replace complex morphologies with smooth Gaussian-shaped pulses, effectively encoding activation timing without requiring explicit annotation of local activation times—a known source of error in traditional mapping. These preprocessed signals are then averaged across all electrode pairs to generate a single composite signal representing the net regional electrical activity. This averaged signal is further smoothed using a custom-designed two-sided exponential FIR low-pass filter, which preserves coherent wavefronts while suppressing high-frequency components arising from asynchronous activations.

Feature extraction employs time-frequency analysis via STFT with a 1-second window and 95% overlap, optimized for high temporal resolution to capture transient events. From the resulting spectrogram, the instantaneous regional dominant frequency (iRDF) is computed as the frequency with maximum power at each time point. The upper quartile of iRDF over the recording segment is designated as the regional dominant frequency (RDF), providing a robust measure of the prevailing oscillatory rate. Concurrently, wave break (WB) events are identified as iRDF drops exceeding 3 Hz below the RDF (or below 0.5 Hz) and lasting longer than 100 milliseconds. The wave break rate (WBR) is then calculated as the number of such events per second, serving as a quantitative index of wavefront instability.

In a clinical validation study involving twenty patients undergoing catheter ablation for AF, data were collected using a high-density circular mapping catheter in the left atrium during sustained AF. Recordings were obtained prior to ablation, with each site sampled for approximately 30 seconds. Offline processing in MATLAB implemented the aforementioned algorithm to generate RDF and WBR maps, which were subsequently correlated with procedural outcomes. Patients were stratified into paroxysmal and persistent AF groups, and ablation endpoints included acute termination of AF to sinus rhythm or atrial tachycardia, as well as long-term freedom from arrhythmia recurrence. The analysis revealed that sites exhibiting high RDF and low WBR were significantly associated with successful ablation termination, supporting the hypothesis that these regions represent functional drivers of AF.

### 1A. Regional Dominant Frequency and Wave Break Rate

The calculation of electrode dominant frequency (EDF) begins with preprocessing of individual bipolar IEGMs using a morphological transformation that replaces each activation complex with a standardized Gaussian pulse, thereby normalizing amplitude and shape variations. The mean amplitude is removed to eliminate DC offset. For instantaneous EDF (iEDF) estimation, the preprocessed signal undergoes STFT with a 1-second Hanning window and 95% overlap; the power spectrum is computed via fast Fourier transform (FFT), and iEDF is defined as the frequency corresponding to peak power at each time point. In contrast, regional dominant frequency (RDF) computation integrates information across all electrodes: after individual preprocessing, the signals are averaged to produce a composite waveform, which is then filtered using a two-sided exponential FIR filter of length 180 (hₙ = exp(−|−1 + (n−1)/90|) for n=1,…,180). This filter enhances signal coherence by attenuating high-frequency noise from fragmented activations. The filtered signal is demeaned, and its STFT is computed identically to iEDF. The instantaneous RDF (iRDF) is the peak frequency of the regional power spectrum at each time, and the RDF is defined as the upper quartile of iRDF over the segment duration T. A time window T of 1 second is selected to balance temporal resolution against frequency precision, enabling detection of transient wave breaks that would be obscured by longer windows. Wave break (WB) is formally defined as an iRDF drop exceeding 3 Hz below the RDF (or below 0.5 Hz) for more than 100 ms. The wave break rate (WBR) is the count of such events per second, serving as a direct metric of wavefront disorganization and a key feature for characterizing propagation quality.

### 1B. Example of RDF-Based Wave Break Identification

An illustrative example demonstrates RDF-based wave break identification using IEGMs recorded from the left atrial roof in a persistent AF patient. During periods of coherent wavefront propagation, preprocessed electrode signals exhibit near-synchronous peaks, yielding a large-amplitude composite signal after averaging. Application of the low-pass filter preserves this structure, resulting in a stable iRDF. Conversely, during wave break, activation times diverge across electrodes, causing the averaged signal to fragment into multiple small peaks. The low-pass filter attenuates these high-frequency components, producing a pronounced drop in iRDF. In the depicted segment, two such drops occur at approximately 12 and 24 seconds, corresponding to three discrete wave break events and yielding a WBR of 0.1 WB/s. Notably, the iRDF trace differs significantly from the average of individual iEDFs, underscoring the unique sensitivity of the regional approach to spatiotemporal heterogeneity.

### 1C. Minimum Required Segment Duration for Accurate RDF Estimation

To determine the minimum segment duration required for reliable RDF estimation, the study compared RDF values computed from progressively shorter segments against a 30-second "gold standard." Using 201 recording sites from 15 patients, Pearson correlation analysis showed that segments longer than 4 seconds achieved a correlation coefficient exceeding 85% (specifically, 90%) with the 30-second reference. This establishes 4 seconds as sufficient for robust RDF estimation in clinical practice, enabling efficient mapping without compromising accuracy.

### 1D. Minimum Required Segment Duration for Accurate WBR Estimation

For WBR estimation, a similar analysis was conducted using 37 segments longer than 50 seconds from two patients. Correlation between WBR values from shorter segments and the 50-second reference indicated that segments exceeding 25 seconds were necessary to achieve reliable WBR measurement. This longer requirement reflects the stochastic nature of wave break events, necessitating adequate observation time to capture representative dynamics.

### 1E. Statistics

Statistical analysis employed the Anderson-Darling test to assess normality. Non-parametric comparisons between paroxysmal and persistent AF groups utilized the Mann-Whitney U test. Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient evaluated associations between RDF and WBR, with p < 0.05 deemed statistically significant. Data are reported as mean ± standard deviation unless otherwise specified.

### 1F. Implementation

Embodiments of the invention are implemented in software executed on a data processing system comprising a central processing unit (CPU), memory, display device, input device (e.g., keyboard, mouse), and interface device for receiving IEGM data from electroanatomic mapping systems. The system includes network connections for data transfer and a database for storing patient records and processed results. Computer-executable programmed instructions perform preprocessing, feature extraction, and visualization. A graphical user interface (GUI) displays color-coded RDF and WBR maps overlaid on atrial geometries, allowing clinicians to interactively explore regional electrophysiological properties and identify ablation targets.

### 1G. Results

Five of twenty initially enrolled patients were excluded due to poor data quality or inadequate left atrial coverage (<60%). The remaining fifteen patients (5 paroxysmal, 10 persistent; mean age 61.3 ± 9.2 years; 13 males) underwent mapping with a mean procedural duration of 4:39 ± 0:54 hours. Recordings averaged 29.9 ± 9.8 seconds per site across 24.4 ± 7 locations per patient. Ibutilide was administered to 8/10 persistent patients. Stable 25-second segments (n=279) yielded mean RDF of 5.5 ± 0.82 Hz and WBR of 0.16 ± 0.13 WB/s. Paroxysmal patients exhibited significantly higher RDF (5.99 ± 0.8 Hz vs. 5.32 ± 0.75 Hz; p<0.001) and WBR (0.24 ± 0.14 vs. 0.14 ± 0.11; p<0.001) than persistent patients. Sites with high RDF and low WBR (↑RDF,↓WBR)—defined per-patient as upper RDF quartile and lower WBR quartile—were identified in 14/15 patients (2.6 ± 1.2 sites/patient). Ablation at these sites correlated strongly with AF termination: 8/9 patients with acute termination had ↑RDF,↓WBR sites ablated, including all three who converted to atrial tachycardia. At 24.5 ± 6.3 months follow-up, no recurrences occurred in the six patients with successful sinus rhythm conversion post-ablation, whereas four of six cardioverted patients experienced recurrence.

### 1H. Discussion

The invention introduces regional dominant frequency (RDF) and wave break rate (WBR) as novel metrics for investigating atrial fibrillation mechanisms. RDF captures the dominant oscillatory rate of a region by integrating signals from multiple electrodes, avoiding the pitfalls of single-electrode DF analysis. WBR quantifies wavefront instability through transient iRDF drops, providing an objective measure of disorganization. Together, they enable identification of ↑RDF,↓WBR sites—regions of rapid yet stable activity hypothesized to represent AF drivers. This contrasts with traditional CFE mapping, which targets disorganized signals that may be epiphenomena rather than sources. The spatial distribution of WBR and RDF reveals heterogeneity across the atria, with termination sites consistently colocalizing with ↑RDF,↓WBR regions. This supports WBR as a valuable feature for selecting ablation targets beyond empirical strategies. Data collection via sequential high-density mapping offers superior endocardial resolution compared to panoramic basket catheters, albeit without global synchrony. Limitations include regional averaging over ~2.5 cm diameter and exclusive left atrial sampling. Future work will validate these metrics prospectively and integrate them into real-time mapping platforms.

## 2. Computer Modelling of Spiral Rotor and Associated Wave Break Analysis

Computer simulations using a modified FitzHugh-Nagumo model of cardiac tissue were employed to generate spiral rotor activity, a canonical mechanism of AF maintenance. Unipolar and bipolar electrograms were synthesized from the simulated transmembrane potentials at virtual electrode positions mimicking a high-density catheter array. Application of the RDF/WBR algorithm to these simulated electrograms successfully identified iRDF drops corresponding to wavefront collisions at the rotor core periphery. This computational validation confirms that the method accurately detects wave break associated with reentrant sources, reinforcing its biological plausibility.

## 3. Clinical Example of an Identified Rotor During Wave Break

A clinical propagation map illustrates rotational activity observed during wave break in a persistent AF patient. The map, generated from sequential IEGM recordings, shows a focal region of high RDF surrounded by a zone of elevated WBR, consistent with a rotor emitting spiral waves that collide with surrounding tissue. Ablation at the high-RDF, low-WBR core resulted in immediate AF termination, providing direct evidence of the method’s ability to localize functional drivers.

## EQUIVALENTS

The scope of the invention encompasses all equivalent methods, systems, and implementations that utilize regional signal fusion and time-frequency analysis of intracardiac electrograms to compute dominant frequency and wave break rate for the purpose of identifying atrial fibrillation sources. This includes variations in preprocessing algorithms, filter designs, time-frequency transforms (e.g., wavelet transforms), definitions of wave break thresholds, and visualization techniques, provided they achieve the same functional result of characterizing wavefront stability and instability to guide ablation therapy.
