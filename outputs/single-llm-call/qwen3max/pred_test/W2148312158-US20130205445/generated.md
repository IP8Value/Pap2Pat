# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for enhancing seed yield and growth in plants through the modulation of sugar transport mechanisms within plant cells. More specifically, the invention pertains to the overexpression or regulated expression of the Tonoplast Monosaccharide Transporter (TMT) protein, particularly the TMT1 isoform, in plant tissues to improve agronomic traits such as seed weight, oil content, protein content, and overall biomass production. The TMT protein functions as a proton-coupled antiporter localized in the tonoplast—the membrane surrounding the plant vacuole—and facilitates the import of monosaccharides, primarily glucose and fructose, from the cytosol into the vacuolar lumen. By increasing the activity or expression level of TMT proteins, especially TMT1, the invention enables more efficient sequestration of sugars into the vacuole, thereby altering subcellular sugar partitioning, reducing cytosolic sugar concentrations, and modulating sugar-sensing pathways that influence plant development, respiration, photosynthesis, and carbon allocation. This approach provides a novel strategy for improving crop productivity without necessarily increasing total cellular sugar levels, but rather by optimizing their intracellular distribution and metabolic utilization.

## BACKGROUND ART

Plants serve as the foundational source of food, feed, fiber, and bioenergy for human and animal populations worldwide. Among economically significant crops, rapeseed (Brassica napus) is particularly valued for its high-quality edible oil, which is rich in unsaturated fatty acids and widely used in cooking, industrial applications, and biodiesel production. Enhancing the yield and nutritional quality of such oilseed crops remains a central objective in modern agricultural biotechnology. To facilitate genetic and physiological studies relevant to crop improvement, the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana has been extensively employed due to its small genome, short life cycle, ease of transformation, and high degree of genetic conservation with major crop species. Over the past several decades, numerous efforts have been directed toward increasing plant yield through manipulation of photosynthetic efficiency, hormone signaling, nutrient uptake, and carbohydrate metabolism. One notable example is the YEP (Yield Enhancement Protein), which has been shown to improve harvest index in certain transgenic lines, though its mechanism remains incompletely understood.

A critical aspect of plant productivity is the regulation of sugar transport and storage. Sugars produced during photosynthesis in source leaves must be efficiently transported to sink tissues—such as developing seeds, roots, or fruits—for growth, storage, or energy production. A significant portion of soluble sugars, particularly glucose and fructose, is stored in the large central vacuole of plant cells. This compartmentalization is mediated by specific transport proteins embedded in the tonoplast. Among these, the Tonoplast Monosaccharide Transporter (TMT) family plays a pivotal role. In Arabidopsis thaliana, three TMT isoforms—TMT1 (encoded by At1g20840), TMT2 (At4g35300), and TMT3 (At3g51490)—have been identified. These proteins share structural homology and are predicted to possess multiple transmembrane domains characteristic of major facilitator superfamily (MFS) transporters. Functional studies using gene knockout mutants have demonstrated that loss of TMT1 and TMT2 results in reduced accumulation of glucose and fructose in vacuoles, particularly under cold stress conditions, confirming their role in vacuolar monosaccharide import. The cDNA sequence of TMT1 (GenBank accession Z50752) encodes a polypeptide of approximately 50 kDa, and its expression is normally low at standard growth temperatures but strongly induced by cold exposure. Despite the identification of multiple monosaccharide transporters in Arabidopsis—including STP, pGlcT, and ERD6-like proteins—the TMT family remains uniquely associated with tonoplast-localized, pH-dependent sugar uptake. Prior art has not disclosed or suggested that overexpression of TMT1 could lead to enhanced seed yield, improved oil and protein content, or accelerated plant growth, representing a significant gap addressed by the present invention.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a method for increasing seed yield, seed weight, oil content, protein content, and overall plant growth rate through the targeted enhancement of TMT protein activity in plant cells. This is achieved by overexpressing the TMT1 protein or functionally equivalent homologues or analogues thereof in transgenic or isogenic plant lines. Overexpression of TMT1 leads to increased import of glucose and fructose into the vacuole, resulting in reduced cytosolic monosaccharide concentrations. This altered subcellular sugar partitioning attenuates sugar-mediated repression of photosynthesis-related genes (e.g., CAB1) and suppresses induction of sugar-responsive genes involved in nitrogen metabolism (e.g., NR1), thereby reprogramming carbon allocation toward anabolic processes. Consequently, transgenic plants exhibit enhanced photosynthetic gene expression, reduced respiratory carbon loss, lower nocturnal CO₂ release, and increased phloem export capacity due to upregulated expression of the sucrose transporter SUC2. These physiological changes collectively result in significantly higher thousand-kernel weights, elevated lipid and protein contents per seed, and greater total seed biomass per plant.

The invention encompasses the use of any TMT protein that shares functional equivalence with Arabidopsis TMT1, including homologues and analogues. As used herein, a “homologue” refers to a protein encoded by a nucleic acid sequence that exhibits at least 70% amino acid sequence identity to the Arabidopsis TMT1 protein (SEQ ID NO:1) and retains the ability to transport monosaccharides across the tonoplast in a proton-gradient-dependent manner. An “analogue” denotes a structurally or functionally modified variant of TMT1—such as those containing epitope tags (e.g., c-Myc) or conservative amino acid substitutions—that maintains substantial biological activity in enhancing vacuolar sugar sequestration and improving yield traits. The preferred embodiment utilizes the native or modified TMT1 protein from Arabidopsis thaliana, though the principle is transferable to other sugar transport proteins with similar tonoplast localization and transport kinetics.

Gene expression may be driven by constitutive promoters (e.g., cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter) or inducible, tissue-specific, or developmentally regulated promoters to achieve spatially or temporally controlled overexpression. Standard plant transformation techniques—including Agrobacterium-mediated floral dip, biolistic delivery, or protoplast transfection—are employed to introduce expression cassettes into plant genomes. These cassettes typically include the TMT coding sequence operably linked to a promoter, a terminator, and optionally a selectable marker gene (e.g., kanamycin resistance) for the identification of transgenic lines. Stable integration into the nuclear genome ensures heritability of the trait. The invention further contemplates targeted regulation of TMT expression in specific tissues—such as mesophyll cells, phloem companion cells, or developing seeds—to maximize yield benefits while minimizing potential pleiotropic effects.

The economic significance of this invention lies in its applicability to major oilseed and grain crops, including but not limited to rapeseed, soybean, maize, wheat, rice, and sunflower. By enhancing oil or protein production per unit area, the method enables greater harvest efficiency, potentially allowing for more frequent cropping cycles on the same land. The invention thus provides a scalable, genetically stable platform for sustainable intensification of agricultural production.

## BEST MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Examples

In a preferred embodiment, a plasmid construct for TMT1 overexpression was generated by first introducing a c-Myc epitope tag (EQKLISEEDL) into the Arabidopsis TMT1 cDNA (Attmt1) via site-directed mutagenesis, replacing amino acids 376–383 with the c-Myc sequence. This modified cDNA was then cloned into the binary vector pART27 under the control of the constitutive cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter to create an expression cassette. The complete expression cassette, comprising the 35S promoter, c-Myc-tagged Attmt1 coding sequence, and nopaline synthase terminator, was excised using NotI restriction enzyme digestion and ligated into the T-DNA region of pART27. The final construct, designated pSR6, was verified by full-length sequencing to ensure fidelity.

Transgenic Arabidopsis lines were produced using the floral dip method, wherein homozygous tmt1-2::tDNA double mutant plants (lacking functional TMT1 and TMT2 isoforms) were infiltrated with Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101 harboring pSR6. Transformed seeds were selected on kanamycin-containing media, and resistant lines were propagated. Northern blot analysis confirmed high-level TMT1 mRNA accumulation in independent lines 1, 4, and 10, with line 4 showing the strongest expression.

Plants were cultivated under standard soil conditions (21°C, 10-h photoperiod) or in liquid culture supplemented with or without 5% glucose. Seed analyses revealed that TMT1-overexpressing lines exhibited significantly increased thousand-kernel weights—up to 28% higher than wild-type controls. Lipid quantification by gravimetric analysis after isopropanol extraction showed 12% higher oil content per seed in line 4, while protein content, measured via bicinchoninic acid assay, was elevated by 14%. The number of seeds per silique remained unchanged (~42 seeds), but total seed mass per plant increased dramatically: line 1 produced 218 mg/plant versus 125 mg in wild type.

Physiological assays demonstrated that TMT1 overexpressors exhibited reduced respiration of newly imported [¹⁴C]glucose (13–14% respired vs. 18% in wild type) and lower nocturnal CO₂ release. Phloem exudate analysis showed 33–43% higher sugar export rates from source leaves, correlating with 1.5-fold increased SUC2 transcript levels. Growth monitoring revealed accelerated development at 15 days post-germination, confirming enhanced early vigor. These combined traits—increased seed weight, oil, protein, carbon-use efficiency, and phloem transport—collectively validate the efficacy of TMT1 overexpression as a robust method for yield enhancement in plants.
