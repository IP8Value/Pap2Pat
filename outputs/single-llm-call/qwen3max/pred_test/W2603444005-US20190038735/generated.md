## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to immunogenic compositions comprising virus-like particles (VLPs) displaying Staphylococcus aureus epitopic peptides, particularly autoinducing peptides (AIPs) involved in quorum-sensing mediated virulence regulation. More specifically, the invention provides VLP-based vaccines for the prophylactic and therapeutic management of infections caused by Staphylococcus aureus, including methicillin-resistant strains (MRSA), through immune-mediated inhibition of agr-dependent virulence signaling pathways.

## BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW OF THE INVENTION

Staphylococcus aureus is a leading cause of skin and soft tissue infections (SSTIs) in the United States and globally, with a significant proportion of clinical isolates exhibiting resistance to multiple antibiotics, including methicillin (MRSA). Between 2000 and 2012, the incidence of SSTIs in the U.S. increased by approximately 40%, with associated healthcare expenditures rising from $4.4 billion to $13.8 billion. MRSA infections are particularly problematic due to high rates of treatment failure, recurrent infection, and the absence of any licensed vaccine against S. aureus to date. Conventional antibiotic therapies are increasingly compromised by the emergence and spread of multidrug-resistant strains, necessitating alternative strategies that do not rely solely on microbial killing.

A critical aspect of S. aureus pathogenesis is its ability to regulate the expression of over 200 virulence factors through the accessory gene regulator (agr) operon, a quorum-sensing system that coordinates bacterial behavior in response to population density. The agr system functions via the secretion and detection of small, cyclic autoinducing peptides (AIPs). Accumulation of AIPs above a threshold concentration activates the membrane-bound histidine kinase AgrC, triggering a phosphorylation cascade that culminates in the upregulation of RNAIII, the primary effector molecule that modulates virulence gene expression. S. aureus strains are classified into four agr groups (I–IV), each producing a distinct AIP (AIP1–AIP4) and expressing a cognate AgrC receptor. Notably, agr type I isolates, particularly the USA300 clone, are strongly associated with invasive community- and healthcare-associated MRSA infections.

Traditional antibiotic approaches exert strong selective pressure that accelerates resistance development. In contrast, targeting virulence mechanisms—such as agr signaling—offers a promising anti-infective strategy that may reduce pathogenicity without directly killing bacteria, thereby minimizing resistance selection. However, AIPs are inherently poor immunogens due to their small size (typically 7–9 amino acids) and conformational lability, especially the thiolactone bond essential for biological activity in AIP1 (YSTCDFIM). This bond is chemically unstable, prone to hydrolysis, and difficult to preserve during vaccine formulation. Consequently, conventional conjugation of linear or cyclic AIPs to carrier proteins has failed to elicit protective immunity in preclinical models.

To overcome these limitations, the present invention leverages a bacteriophage virus-like particle (VLP) platform that enables multivalent, conformationally constrained display of modified AIP sequences. VLPs are non-replicating, self-assembling nanostructures derived from viral structural proteins that mimic native virion architecture but lack genetic material. Their repetitive surface geometry potently stimulates B-cell receptors, leading to robust, high-affinity antibody responses even against weakly immunogenic epitopes. By genetically fusing a stabilized AIP mimetic—such as AIP1S (YSTSDFIM), in which cysteine-4 is substituted with serine to prevent disulfide scrambling while preserving structural mimicry of the cyclic form—into an exposed loop of a bacteriophage coat protein dimer, the invention achieves high-density, ordered presentation that effectively breaks immune tolerance to the native AIP.

This approach hypothesizes that antibodies induced against the conformationally restricted AIP1S displayed on VLPs will cross-react with native, cyclic AIP1, thereby neutralizing its ability to activate AgrC and suppress downstream virulence factor production. The resulting attenuation of pathogenesis enhances host innate immune clearance and reduces disease severity, as demonstrated in murine models of SSTI. The VLP platform is further adaptable to other AIP variants (AIP2–AIP4) and additional S. aureus virulence epitopes, enabling the development of multivalent or hybrid vaccines capable of broad protection across agr types.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides virus-like particles (VLPs) for the prevention and treatment of Staphylococcus aureus infections, particularly those mediated by agr type I quorum-sensing systems. In one embodiment, the VLPs comprise bacteriophage coat polypeptide dimers genetically fused to Staphylococcus aureus epitopic peptides, including modified autoinducing peptides (AIPs) such as AIP1S (YSTSDFIM), AIP2S, AIP3S, and AIP4S, wherein the native thiolactone-forming cysteine is substituted (e.g., with serine) to enhance stability while preserving conformational mimicry of the native cyclic AIP. These AIP-VLPs are designed for prophylactic vaccination to induce high-titer, neutralizing antibodies that bind soluble AIP1 and inhibit agr signaling in vivo.

In another embodiment, the invention encompasses VLPs displaying S. aureus toxin epitopes, such as alpha-hemolysin (Hla), either alone or in combination with AIP epitopes to create hybrid VLPs that simultaneously target virulence regulation and effector toxins. Such hybrid constructs offer synergistic protection by blocking both upstream signaling and downstream cytotoxic damage.

The VLPs are assembled from single-chain dimers of bacteriophage coat proteins, preferably derived from RNA bacteriophages such as PP7, MS2, or Qβ. The epitopic peptide is inserted into a surface-exposed loop region—such as the AB-loop—of the coat protein, ensuring high valency (up to 90 copies per VLP) and optimal immunogenic presentation. The absence of the labile thiolactone group in the displayed peptides (e.g., AIP1S instead of AIP1) improves manufacturability and stability without compromising immunological recognition of the native AIP.

Also provided are nucleic acid constructs encoding the engineered coat polypeptide dimers. These constructs include a bacterial or bacteriophage promoter (e.g., T7 promoter), a coding sequence for the single-chain coat polypeptide dimer with an inserted heterologous epitopic sequence, flanked by restriction sites (e.g., KpnI and BamHI) for modular cloning, an antibiotic resistance gene (e.g., kanamycin or ampicillin resistance) for selection, a replication origin compatible with E. coli (e.g., p15A or ColE1), and transcription termination sequences. In some embodiments, the construct includes a second antibiotic resistance gene and a helper phage packaging signal to facilitate VLP production in co-infected cells. PCR primers are designed to amplify the insert or verify correct cloning, and repressor elements may be included to tightly control expression until induction.

The VLP compositions are formulated for parenteral administration and are useful both prophylactically—to prevent S. aureus colonization and infection—and therapeutically—to mitigate disease progression in established infections by suppressing agr-mediated virulence. The platform’s flexibility allows rapid adaptation to emerging agr variants or other bacterial pathogens utilizing similar quorum-sensing peptides.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The following description employs standard molecular biology techniques well known in the art, as described in references such as Sambrook et al., Molecular Cloning: A Laboratory Manual, and Ausubel et al., Current Protocols in Molecular Biology. Numerical ranges (e.g., “about 10 µg”) encompass variations inherent in biological systems and assay precision. Technical terms are defined as follows for clarity and consistency.

A “patient” or “subject” refers to a human or non-human mammal, including but not limited to mice, rats, rabbits, dogs, cats, livestock, and non-human primates, intended to receive prophylactic or therapeutic intervention. An “effective amount” is a quantity sufficient to elicit a measurable immune response or clinical benefit without undue toxicity.

A “polynucleotide” or “nucleic acid” is a polymer of nucleotides, whether DNA or RNA, natural or synthetic. A “polypeptide” is a chain of amino acids linked by peptide bonds. A “single-chain dimer” refers to two coat protein monomers connected by a flexible peptide linker, enabling covalent dimerization and proper VLP assembly.

The “AB-loop” of a bacteriophage coat protein is a surface-exposed, structurally flexible region between beta-strands A and B, commonly used for epitope insertion due to its solvent accessibility and tolerance to modification. “Amino acid residues” refer to the individual building blocks of polypeptides, including natural and non-natural variants. “Valency” denotes the number of epitope copies displayed per VLP, typically ranging from 60 to 180 depending on capsid symmetry.

A “coding sequence” is a nucleotide sequence that directs the synthesis of a polypeptide. A “heterologous region” is a segment of DNA or protein not naturally associated with the host molecule. An “origin of replication” enables autonomous plasmid duplication in a host cell. A “promoter sequence” initiates transcription, and “transcription termination sequences” signal its end. Together with ribosome binding sites and other elements, these constitute “expression control sequences.”

An “antibiotic resistance gene” confers survival in the presence of specific antibiotics, facilitating plasmid maintenance. “PCR” (polymerase chain reaction) amplifies DNA using “PCR primers”—short oligonucleotides complementary to target flanking regions. Example primers for AIP1S cloning include 5′-GGCGGTACCTACAGTACCTCTGACTTCATCATGGAGGCTACTCGCACTCTGACTGAG-3′ (forward) and 5′-CGGGCTTTGTTAGCAGCCGG-3′ (reverse).

A “transformed cell” harbors exogenous DNA. A “signal sequence” directs protein localization. “Degenerate nucleic acid sequences” encode the same polypeptide using different codons. An “epitope” is an antigenic determinant recognized by antibodies or T cells.

“Coat protein” and “coat polypeptide” refer to structural subunits of bacteriophages that self-assemble into capsids. An “immune response” includes humoral (antibody) and cellular reactions. A composition is “immunogenic” if it provokes such a response. A “vaccine” is a preparation intended to establish immunity.

A “virus-like particle of a bacteriophage” is a non-infectious, self-assembled structure composed of bacteriophage coat proteins, optionally encapsidating RNA, but lacking genomic nucleic acid required for replication. Preferred VLPs derive from RNA bacteriophages such as PP7, whose coat proteins form T=3 icosahedral capsids. Components are “operatively linked” when positioned to enable functional interaction (e.g., promoter driving gene expression).

“SA-induced disorders” include SSTIs, pneumonia, bacteremia, and endocarditis caused by S. aureus. “Immunogenicity” measures the ability to induce immunity; “prophylactic efficacy” reflects protection against subsequent infection. An “SA epitopic peptide” is a fragment of an S. aureus antigen, such as AIP1S or Hla-derived sequences, capable of eliciting specific antibodies.

### Production of Virus-Like Particles

Virus-like phage particles are produced either in vitro by purified protein refolding or, more commonly, in vivo by recombinant expression in bacterial hosts such as E. coli. Bacteriophages like PP7, MS2, and Qβ are RNA viruses that infect Gram-negative bacteria. Their genomes encode a few proteins, including the major coat protein, which self-assembles around the genomic RNA during replication.

The PP7 genome is organized with overlapping genes, and its virion comprises 180 coat protein monomers arranged as 90 dimers forming an icosahedral shell. Recombinant expression of the PP7 coat protein—especially as a single-chain dimer—leads to spontaneous VLP assembly in E. coli cytoplasm, often encapsidating host RNA.

PP7 coat polypeptides share structural homology with other RNA phage coat proteins, featuring a conserved beta-barrel fold. Structural similarity can be assessed by X-ray crystallography, NMR, or computational modeling (e.g., using GalaxyWeb). An “active coat polypeptide” retains the ability to self-assemble into VLPs.

SA epitopic peptides, such as AIP1S (YSTSDFIM), are inserted into the AB-loop of the coat protein. This loop tolerates insertions of 5–20 amino acids without disrupting folding. Truncated or optimized variants may be used to enhance stability or immunogenicity. Sequence alignment tools (e.g., Clustal Omega) guide design.

Transcription units for VLP production include a promoter (e.g., T7), the engineered coat gene, and a terminator. These are cloned into plasmids containing an origin of replication and antibiotic resistance marker. Upon transformation into E. coli and induction (e.g., with IPTG), VLPs assemble and are purified via chromatography (e.g., size exclusion on Sephacryl S-400). The resulting particles are homogeneous, as confirmed by dynamic light scattering, and suitable for vaccination.

This rational design overcomes the poor immunogenicity of free AIPs by presenting them in a highly ordered, repetitive array that strongly cross-links B-cell receptors, driving potent antibody responses against conformationally relevant epitopes—thereby fulfilling the long-felt need for an effective anti-virulence vaccine against MRSA.

## EXAMPLES

VLP-AIP conjugates serve as promising vaccine candidates against Staphylococcus aureus infections. Specifically, PP7-AIP1S VLPs were constructed by inserting the modified AIP1 sequence YSTSDFIM into the AB-loop of the PP7 single-chain coat protein dimer. This construct was expressed in E. coli C41 cells, and VLPs were purified to homogeneity using size exclusion chromatography. Immunization of BALB/c mice with PP7-AIP1S induced high-titer antibodies that specifically recognized native, cyclic AIP1 in competitive ELISA assays, demonstrating successful mimicry of the conformational epitope despite the absence of the thiolactone bond. These antibodies did not cross-react with AIP2, confirming specificity for agr type I signaling. When challenged with the MRSA USA300 strain (agr type I), vaccinated mice exhibited significantly reduced dermonecrosis, abscess formation, and bacterial burden compared to controls immunized with wild-type PP7 VLPs or PBS. These results validate VLP-AIPs as effective immunogens capable of inducing functional, neutralizing antibodies that disrupt quorum-sensing and attenuate virulence in vivo.

### ADDITIONAL EXAMPLES

The agr operon in S. aureus governs a quorum-sensing circuit essential for virulence. Among the four agr types, agr-I is predominant in invasive infections, making AIP1 a high-priority vaccine target. However, native AIP1’s small size and unstable thiolactone render it non-immunogenic. To address this, we developed a VLP-based platform using the PP7 bacteriophage scaffold. The AIP1S variant (C4S mutation) was inserted into the AB-loop of a PP7 single-chain dimer, yielding PP7-AIP1S VLPs displaying 90 copies of the epitope. Vaccination induced AIP1-specific antibodies that inhibited agr activation in vitro and in vivo. In a murine SSTI model, PP7-AIP1S vaccination significantly reduced skin lesion severity and enhanced bacterial clearance. Molecular analysis confirmed suppressed RNAIII transcription and reduced alpha-hemolysin production at infection sites, directly linking vaccine efficacy to agr inhibition.

This platform extends beyond S. aureus. Listeria monocytogenes employs an agr-like system regulating over 650 virulence genes. Enterococcus faecalis uses the fsr locus and GBAP peptide for biofilm regulation. Clostridium difficile’s agr system controls toxin A/B production. VLP vaccines targeting these peptides could similarly disrupt virulence. Given the FDA approval of VLP vaccines for HPV and hepatitis B, this approach offers a clinically translatable strategy. In an era of escalating antibiotic resistance, VLP-based anti-virulence vaccines represent a vital component of a multi-pronged defense against bacterial pathogens.

## DISCUSSION

Conventional antibiotics face diminishing returns due to widespread resistance, necessitating non-traditional strategies like virulence inhibition. The S. aureus agr operon is a master regulator of pathogenesis, making it an ideal target. AIP1, secreted by agr-I strains responsible for most invasive MRSA cases, is a compelling antigen, yet its physicochemical properties have thwarted prior vaccine efforts. The present invention overcomes these barriers through conformationally constrained, multivalent display on VLPs.

Our data demonstrate that PP7-AIP1S vaccination elicits antibodies that neutralize native AIP1, suppress agr signaling, and protect against SSTI. This validates the hypothesis that structural mimicry—achieved via loop insertion—can substitute for the labile thiolactone. The approach is extensible to other AIP types, enabling a pan-agr vaccine. Moreover, agr-like systems exist in Listeria, Enterococcus, and Clostridium, suggesting broad applicability.

VLPs are inherently safe, non-replicating, and highly immunogenic, with proven success in human vaccines. Their modular design allows rapid iteration against evolving pathogens. While concerns about agr inhibition promoting biofilm formation or selecting mutants exist, our data show enhanced bacterial clearance, likely due to restored phagocyte function in the absence of agr-mediated immune evasion. Future studies will assess long-term ecological impacts, but the immediate benefit—reducing acute virulence—is clear.

Combating antibiotic resistance demands diversified tactics: new antibiotics, antivirulence agents, immunotherapies, and vaccines. VLP-based anti-quorum-sensing vaccines exemplify this integrative approach, offering a path to sustainable infection control.

## SUMMARY

Experimental results confirm that PP7-AIP1S VLPs induce high-affinity antibodies specific for native AIP1, inhibit agr signaling in vivo, and provide significant protection in a murine model of MRSA skin infection. Vaccinated mice showed reduced dermonecrosis, lower bacterial loads, and diminished virulence factor expression compared to controls. These findings establish proof-of-concept for VLP-displayed AIP mimetics as effective anti-virulence vaccines against S. aureus and related pathogens.

### Methods

All animal procedures were approved by the University of New Mexico IACUC (Assurance D16-00228) and complied with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. The CA-MRSA strain USA300 LAC was grown to early exponential phase in TSB, diluted in saline to 4×10⁷ CFU/50 µL, and verified by plating.

VLPs were produced by cloning AIP1S into pDSP7K via KpnI/BamHI sites using specified primers. E. coli C41 cells were transformed, induced with 1 mM IPTG, lysed, and VLPs purified by size exclusion chromatography. Concentration was determined by SDS-PAGE against lysozyme standards.

BALB/cJ mice (4 weeks old, female) received 10 µg VLP or PBS intramuscularly at weeks 0 and 4. Serum was collected at 2, 4, and 8 weeks post-boost. For ELISA, plates were coated with PP7 or PP7-AIP1S; sera were depleted of anti-PP7/E. coli antibodies by pre-incubation with PP7 and C41 cells, then filtered. Binding was detected with HRP-conjugated anti-mouse IgG. Competition ELISAs used pre-incubation with AIP1 or AIP2.

For infection, mice were depilated, infected subcutaneously with LAC, and monitored for weight, abscess size (ImageJ), and dermonecrosis. On day 6, skin biopsies were homogenized; bacterial burden was quantified by plating, cytokines by BioPlex multiplex assay.

RNA was isolated from day-1 skin samples using QIAzol, reverse-transcribed, and qPCR performed for RNAIII and 16S rRNA using TaqMan probes. Hla levels in day-6 homogenates were assessed by Western blot with anti-Hla antibody and quantified by densitometry.

Statistical analysis used GraphPad Prism 5.04; significance was set at p<0.05 using ANOVA or Kruskal-Wallis tests with appropriate post-hoc corrections.
