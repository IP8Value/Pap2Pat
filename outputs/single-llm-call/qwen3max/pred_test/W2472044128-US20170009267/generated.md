## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of genetic engineering and microbial biotechnology, and more specifically to engineered strains of *Bacillus subtilis* capable of overproducing N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) under industrially relevant fermentation conditions. GlcNAc is a naturally occurring amino sugar that serves as a fundamental building block for numerous biologically important macromolecules, including chitin, peptidoglycan, and glycoproteins. Due to its roles in joint health, immune modulation, and gut integrity, GlcNAc has gained significant commercial interest as a nutraceutical and pharmaceutical ingredient. Traditionally, GlcNAc has been produced through chemical hydrolysis of chitin derived from crustacean shells; however, this method suffers from several drawbacks, including environmental pollution, inconsistent product quality, and reliance on non-renewable marine resources. Consequently, there has been a growing demand for sustainable, high-yield microbial production platforms that can synthesize GlcNAc from renewable carbon sources such as glucose.

Current microbial approaches to GlcNAc production typically involve the heterologous or enhanced expression of key biosynthetic enzymes—namely glucosamine-6-phosphate synthase (GlmS) and glucosamine-6-phosphate N-acetyltransferase (Gna1)—in host organisms such as *Escherichia coli* or *Bacillus subtilis*. In particular, *B. subtilis* has emerged as an attractive industrial chassis due to its Generally Recognized As Safe (GRAS) status, robust growth characteristics, and well-established genetic tools. However, despite these advantages, engineered *B. subtilis* strains often exhibit severely impaired growth and low volumetric productivity when cultivated in minimal media containing glucose as the sole carbon source—a condition essential for cost-effective large-scale fermentation. This limitation stems not from insufficient precursor supply but from unforeseen metabolic imbalances introduced by the synthetic pathway itself. Specifically, prior art strains accumulate abnormally high intracellular concentrations of GlcNAc-6-phosphate (GlcNAc6P), which correlates with reduced cell growth, diminished energy charge, and suboptimal GlcNAc yields. The root cause of this bottleneck has remained elusive, hindering rational strain optimization.

The limitations of current GlcNAc production methods underscore a critical need for novel genetic interventions that decouple product synthesis from cellular stress responses. Existing strategies focused on enhancing precursor flux—such as overexpression of phosphofructokinase variants or glutamine synthase—have failed to resolve the core issue, indicating that the problem lies downstream in the pathway. Without a mechanistic understanding of the metabolic obstruction, efforts to improve titer, yield, and productivity remain largely empirical and inefficient. Therefore, there exists a pressing need for a genetically engineered *B. subtilis* strain in which the primary bottleneck in GlcNAc biosynthesis has been identified and eliminated, thereby enabling robust growth and high-level production in minimal glucose medium. The present invention fulfills this need by disclosing the unexpected discovery of a futile phosphorylation–dephosphorylation cycle involving GlcNAc and GlcNAc6P, mediated by the native glucokinase GlcK, and provides a targeted solution through precise gene knockout.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The goal of the present invention is to provide a recombinant *Bacillus subtilis* strain capable of efficiently producing GlcNAc in minimal glucose medium without suffering from growth inhibition or metabolic stress. This objective is achieved by identifying and eliminating a previously unrecognized ATP-dissipating futile cycle that occurs between GlcNAc and GlcNAc-6-phosphate, which arises due to the promiscuous activity of the endogenous glucokinase encoded by the *glcK* gene. The invention thus provides a genetically modified microorganism wherein the *glcK* gene has been disrupted or deleted, resulting in dramatically improved cellular physiology, energy homeostasis, and GlcNAc productivity.

The method of *glcK* knockout is performed using established markerless genome editing techniques suitable for *B. subtilis*, such as the MazF counterselection system or homologous recombination with a disruption cassette. Specifically, a DNA fragment containing a selectable marker flanked by homology arms corresponding to sequences upstream and downstream of the *glcK* open reading frame is introduced into the host strain via natural competence or electroporation. Following integration and subsequent excision of the marker, a clean deletion of the *glcK* coding sequence is obtained, yielding a strain devoid of functional glucokinase activity. This knockout prevents the undesired re-phosphorylation of exogenously synthesized or intracellularly accumulated GlcNAc back to GlcNAc6P, thereby breaking the futile cycle that consumes ATP without net product formation.

The recombinant *B. subtilis* strain of the invention, designated BSGN6-PxylA-glmS-P43-GNA1-Δ*glcK* (abbreviated BSGNK), is constructed upon a foundational GlcNAc-overproducing background strain referred to herein as BSGN. The BSGN strain itself is engineered to block all known catabolic routes for GlcNAc by deleting the genes *nagP*, *gamP*, *nagA*, *nagB*, and *gamA*, which encode transporters and enzymes involved in GlcNAc degradation. Additionally, BSGN harbors two key biosynthetic genes: *glmS*, encoding glucosamine-6-phosphate synthase under the control of the xylose-inducible promoter PxylA, and *GNA1*, encoding glucosamine-6-phosphate N-acetyltransferase under the constitutive promoter P43. These modifications establish a unidirectional synthetic pathway from fructose-6-phosphate to GlcNAc. The further deletion of *glcK* in this background yields the superior-producing BSGNK strain.

The method for producing GlcNAc according to the invention comprises cultivating the recombinant *B. subtilis* strain BSGNK in a synthetic minimal medium containing glucose as the sole carbon source, under aerobic conditions at a temperature of approximately 37°C and with adequate agitation to ensure oxygen transfer. The synthetic medium is formulated to support robust growth while minimizing complex additives, typically comprising mineral salts such as ammonium chloride, sodium phosphate, magnesium sulfate, calcium chloride, and trace elements including cobalt, copper, manganese, molybdenum, and zinc. Notably, the medium does not require supplementation with amino acids, nucleotides, or other organic growth factors, rendering the process economically viable for industrial scale-up.

The benefits of the invention are manifold. First, the Δ*glcK* modification reduces intracellular GlcNAc6P concentration from over 30 mM in the parental BSGN strain to near-wild-type levels (~0.06 mM), thereby alleviating phosphosugar stress that impairs central metabolism and RNA stability. Second, the specific growth rate of BSGNK is more than doubled compared to BSGN, leading to higher biomass accumulation and, consequently, greater volumetric GlcNAc productivity. Third, the energy charge of the cell increases significantly—from 0.68 to 0.81—indicating restored ATP homeostasis due to elimination of the ATP-wasting futile cycle. Finally, the GlcNAc yield on glucose reaches 147.5 mg per gram of glucose consumed, representing a 2.3-fold improvement over the parent strain, while maintaining a high specific production rate per unit of dry cell weight. These combined advantages make the invention a transformative advancement in the microbial production of GlcNAc.

### EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate the materials, methods, and results supporting the invention, without limiting its scope. All strains were derived from *Bacillus subtilis* 168. The seed medium used for initial strain propagation consisted of Luria-Bertani (LB) broth containing 10 g/L tryptone, 5 g/L yeast extract, and 10 g/L NaCl, incubated at 37°C with shaking at 300 rpm. For physiological and production studies, a defined fermentation medium based on M9 mineral salts was employed, containing 2 g/L glucose, 1 g/L NH₄Cl, 0.5 g/L NaCl, 8.5 g/L Na₂HPO₄·H₂O, appropriate amounts of KH₂PO₄ to maintain pH, 1 mL/L of 1 M MgSO₄, 1 mL/L of 0.1 M CaCl₂, 1 mL/L of 0.05 M FeCl₃, and 10 mL/L of a trace element solution comprising CoCl₂·6H₂O (60 mg/L), CuCl₂·2H₂O (43 mg/L), MnCl₂·4H₂O (100 mg/L), Na₂MoO₄·2H₂O (60 mg/L), and ZnCl₂ (170 mg/L). Cultivation was carried out in shake flasks at 37°C and 300 rpm unless otherwise specified.

The knockout of the *glcK* gene was executed by transforming the BSGN strain with a linear DNA disruption cassette amplified from a *B. subtilis* Δ*glcK* donor strain using primers GlcK-F and GlcK-R. Transformants were selected on LB agar plates supplemented with appropriate antibiotics, and correct integration was verified by PCR and sequencing. The resulting strain, BSGNK, was confirmed to lack glucokinase activity and exhibited no growth defect on alternative carbon sources such as xylose or glycerol.

Fermentation of GlcNAc was conducted by inoculating BSGNK into the aforementioned M9-based fermentation medium with 2 g/L glucose. Cells were grown to mid-exponential phase (OD₆₀₀ ≈ 0.5), and GlcNAc production was monitored over time using ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography coupled to tandem mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS/MS). The results demonstrated that BSGNK achieved a volumetric productivity of 9.20 mg/L/h, more than double that of BSGN (4.2 mg/L/h). Furthermore, steady-state metabolomics revealed a dramatic reduction in GlcNAc6P accumulation, while dynamic ¹³C-labeling experiments confirmed the absence of the futile cycle: upon addition of [U-¹³C]glucose, labeled GlcNAc6P (M+8) appeared rapidly without concurrent rise in unlabeled (M+0) species, indicating de novo synthesis rather than re-phosphorylation of pre-existing GlcNAc. These findings collectively validate that *glcK* deletion effectively eliminates the metabolic bottleneck and enables efficient, scalable GlcNAc production.
