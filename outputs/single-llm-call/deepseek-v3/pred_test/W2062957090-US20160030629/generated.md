# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel peptide-albumin hydrogels suitable for three-dimensional cell culture applications. Traditional two-dimensional cell culture systems fail to accurately mimic the complex extracellular matrix environment where cells naturally reside. While natural hydrogels derived from extracellular matrix components provide biocompatibility, they suffer from inconsistent biochemical presentations and uncontrollable material properties. Synthetic polymer hydrogels offer reproducibility but lack the structural fidelity to properly emulate natural cellular environments.  

Existing peptide hydrogels require undesirable pH or temperature adjustments to initiate gelation, creating harsh conditions that compromise cell viability during encapsulation. Furthermore, current hydrogel systems lack convenient methods for cell isolation, often requiring extreme environmental changes or undesirable buffers that threaten cell survival. There remains an unmet need for hydrogels that enable mild encapsulation conditions while permitting safe and efficient cell recovery for downstream analysis.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses peptide-albumin hydrogels formed through self-assembly of designed peptides in cell culture media containing serum albumin. The hydrogels form under physiological conditions without requiring pH or temperature adjustments, enabling direct cell encapsulation in nutrient-rich media. The hydrogel matrix exhibits unique shear-thinning and self-healing properties that allow reversible gel-sol transitions through mechanical disruption and dilution. This enables gentle cell isolation through simple pipetting and centrifugation procedures.  

Methods of forming the hydrogels involve mixing peptide solutions with cell culture media containing albumin at neutral pH and physiological temperatures. The resulting hydrogels provide tunable mechanical properties, uniform nanofiber networks, and excellent biocompatibility for three-dimensional cell culture applications including drug testing and tissue engineering.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention provides self-assembled hydrogels comprising peptide nanofibers and serum albumin that form three-dimensional scaffold networks under physiological conditions. The hydrogel matrix features a reversible network structure capable of repeated disassembly and reassembly while maintaining structural integrity.  

The hydrogel matrix exhibits uniform internetwork morphology with an average cell size between 50-200 μm and pore sizes ranging from 1-20 μm. The scaffold comprises peptide nanofibers having an average diameter of 10-30 nm and lengths between 100-500 nm. These dimensions closely mimic natural extracellular matrix architecture.  

The hydrogels demonstrate tunable mechanical strength with storage modulus (G') values adjustable from 50-1000 Pa by varying peptide concentration. Remarkably, the hydrogels maintain over 80% recovery of original stiffness after repeated shear-thinning cycles. The reversible properties enable complete hydrogel dissolution through dilution while permitting rapid reassembly upon concentration.  

The hydrogels exhibit excellent water solubility and temperature stability, maintaining structural integrity from 4°C to 50°C. The preparation method involves dissolving synthetic peptides in aqueous buffer at concentrations between 1-10 mM and mixing with cell culture media containing 1-10% serum albumin. Hydrogel formation occurs within 5-30 minutes at room temperature or physiological conditions.  

Key advantages of the hydrogel formation method include:  
1) Neutral pH conditions compatible with cell viability  
2) No requirement for temperature adjustments or specialized buffers  
3) Direct encapsulation in nutrient-rich culture media  
4) Gentle isolation through mechanical disruption rather than chemical degradation  

The hydrogels find particular utility in three-dimensional cell culture applications. Cells remain viable and proliferative within the matrix while developing natural three-dimensional morphologies. The reversible properties enable convenient cell recovery through simple dilution and centrifugation procedures without compromising cell viability.  

The peptides comprise linear self-assembling sequences containing hydrophobic regions, hydrophilic regions, and turning regions. Preferred embodiments feature alternating hydrophobic and hydrophilic residues with β-turn promoting sequences. The peptides typically range from 8-20 amino acids in length with molecular weights between 800-2000 Da.  

Peptide synthesis follows standard Fmoc solid-phase methods with purification by HPLC. Lyophilized peptides remain stable for extended periods when stored desiccated at -20°C. 4) Excellent biocompatibility for long-term cell culture

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope:  

**Example I: Peptide Hydrogel Formation and Characterization**  

A peptide designated h9e was synthesized via Fmoc solid-phase synthesis and purified by HPLC. Lyophilized peptide was dissolved in 100 mM sodium bicarbonate at 10 mM concentration. Hydrogels formed by mixing 100 μL peptide solution with 900 μL Minimum Essential Medium (MEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum, yielding final peptide concentrations of 1-3 mM.  

Transmission electron microscopy revealed uniform nanofiber networks with fiber diameters of 20±5 nm. Rheological testing demonstrated storage moduli of 100-700 Pa depending on peptide concentration, with complete gel formation within 15 minutes. The hydrogels exhibited 85-100% recovery after repeated shear-thinning cycles.  

**Example II: Three-Dimensional Cell Culture Applications**  

MCF-7 breast cancer cells were encapsulated in h9e-MEM hydrogels by direct mixing. Cells formed proliferative three-dimensional clusters resembling tumor morphology. Cisplatin testing demonstrated effective drug diffusion through the hydrogel matrix, with dose-dependent cytotoxicity observed. Cells maintained 95% viability after isolation through gentle pipetting and centrifugation.  

**Example III: Stem Cell Culture Compatibility**  

The h9e peptide formed hydrogels in mesenchymal stem cell (MSC) medium and 2i medium. Rheological tests confirmed maintained shear-thinning and self-healing properties across different media formulations. Encapsulated stem cells exhibited normal morphology and proliferation with 90% recovery viability after isolation.  

Stability testing showed the h9e peptide solution remained functional after 30 days storage at 4°C. The h9e-2i hydrogel maintained structural integrity for over 7 days under culture conditions. These results demonstrate the invention's versatility across diverse cell culture applications while preserving excellent cell viability and recovery.
