# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to therapeutics for treating post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) and associated psychiatric conditions. More specifically, the invention pertains to epigenetic interventions targeting DNA methylation profiles in the nucleus accumbens (NAc) to modulate PTSD susceptibility and resilience. The invention further discloses novel therapeutic agents, including S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) and retinoic acid, capable of reversing PTSD-like behaviors through modulation of DNA methylation pathways.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is a debilitating psychiatric condition characterized by persistent re-experiencing of traumatic events, hyperarousal, and avoidance behaviors. Current treatments, including pharmacotherapy and cognitive behavioral therapy, exhibit limited efficacy, underscoring the need for novel therapeutic strategies.  

The nucleus accumbens (NAc), a key component of the brain's reward circuitry, has been implicated in PTSD pathogenesis. Dysregulation of NAc function contributes to maladaptive fear responses and emotional dysregulation observed in PTSD patients. Recent studies have identified alterations in NAc synaptic plasticity and gene expression profiles in PTSD models, suggesting a role for epigenetic mechanisms in disease progression.  

Epigenetic modifications, particularly DNA methylation, play a critical role in memory formation and stress response. DNA methylation involves the covalent addition of a methyl group to cytosine residues, predominantly at CpG dinucleotides, leading to transcriptional repression or activation depending on genomic context. Aberrant DNA methylation patterns have been associated with PTSD in human and animal studies, implicating epigenetic dysregulation in disease etiology.  

Prior research has demonstrated that DNA methylation in the NAc modulates fear memory consolidation and extinction. However, the specific methylation signatures distinguishing PTSD susceptibility from resilience remain poorly characterized. Furthermore, existing PTSD models lack construct validity, as they fail to replicate the progressive incubation of fear observed in human PTSD.  

The present invention addresses these gaps by introducing a novel rat model of PTSD that recapitulates key clinical features, including individual variability in trauma response. Utilizing this model, the inventors have identified distinct DNA methylation profiles associated with PTSD susceptibility and resilience, paving the way for targeted epigenetic interventions.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive framework for understanding and treating PTSD through epigenetic modulation. A rat model of PTSD was developed, featuring exposure to traumatic stress followed by recurring reminders, which reliably induces PTSD-like behaviors in susceptible individuals while sparing resilient counterparts.  

Genome-wide DNA methylation analysis of the NAc revealed distinct epigenetic signatures distinguishing susceptible and resilient animals. Susceptible rats exhibited hypermethylation of genes involved in neuronal signaling and stress response pathways, whereas resilient animals maintained methylation patterns akin to unstressed controls. These differential methylation profiles were correlated with behavioral phenotypes, including exploration deficits, social withdrawal, and hyperarousal.  

The invention further discloses the therapeutic efficacy of SAM, a universal methyl donor, in reversing PTSD-like behaviors. SAM treatment normalized exploration and social interaction deficits in susceptible animals while concomitantly restoring DNA methylation patterns at specific genomic loci. Pathway analysis identified retinoic acid signaling as a key upstream regulator of differentially methylated genes, prompting the development of a novel combination therapy comprising SAM and retinoic acid. This combination therapy demonstrated superior efficacy in reversing all three core PTSD-like behaviors, offering a promising avenue for clinical translation.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTIONS OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention encompasses methods for diagnosing, preventing, and treating PTSD through modulation of DNA methylation profiles. The invention is further directed to pharmaceutical compositions comprising SAM, retinoic acid, or combinations thereof, for use in PTSD therapy.  

### Embodiment 1. DNA Methylation Profiles of PTSD Susceptibility and Resilience in a Rat Model of PTSD  

The invention provides a rat model of PTSD featuring exposure to traumatic stress followed by recurring reminders, which reliably induces PTSD-like behaviors in a subset of animals while others remain resilient. Male Sprague-Dawley rats were housed under standard conditions with ad libitum access to food and water. Following habituation to handling and behavioral testing apparatus, baseline measurements of exploration, social interaction, and hyperarousal were recorded.  

Animals were subjected to a traumatic stress protocol comprising exposure to predator scent combined with foot shock. PTSD-like behaviors were assessed following two stress reminders (Re1 and Re2) administered at one-week intervals. Behavioral measurements included open field exploration, social interaction with a conspecific, and acoustic startle response. Rats were classified as susceptible or resilient based on quantitative thresholds for each behavioral parameter, with susceptible animals exhibiting significant deficits in exploration and social interaction coupled with enhanced hyperarousal.  

Nucleus accumbens tissue was collected for DNA methylation analysis using capture bisulfite sequencing. Genomic DNA was extracted, bisulfite converted, and subjected to high-throughput sequencing. Bioinformatics analysis included quality control of sequencing reads, alignment to the rat reference genome, and identification of differentially methylated CpG sites.  

Comparative analysis revealed 1,247 differentially methylated CpG sites between susceptible and resilient groups, with susceptible animals exhibiting hypermethylation at genes involved in synaptic plasticity and glucocorticoid signaling. Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA) identified corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) signaling and reelin pathways as significantly enriched among differentially methylated genes. These epigenetic signatures provide biomarkers for PTSD susceptibility and targets for therapeutic intervention.  

### Embodiment 2. Reversal of Two PTSD Behaviors, Exploration and Social Interaction by SAM Treatment  

This embodiment demonstrates the therapeutic efficacy of S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) in reversing PTSD-like behaviors through modulation of DNA methylation. Susceptible rats received daily intraperitoneal injections of SAM (50 mg/kg) or saline for 14 days, commencing one week after the second stress reminder (Re2).  

Behavioral testing revealed significant improvement in exploration and social interaction in SAM-treated animals, with effect sizes exceeding 50% relative to saline controls. Hyperarousal responses were partially attenuated, though not to statistically significant levels.  

Whole-genome bisulfite sequencing of NAc tissue from SAM-treated animals identified reversal of trauma-induced hypermethylation at 387 CpG sites, including genes encoding glutamate receptors and neurotrophic factors. Correlation analysis demonstrated significant associations between methylation levels at specific loci and behavioral recovery, supporting a causal role for DNA methylation in PTSD pathogenesis.  

### Embodiment 3. Discovery of "Upstream Regulators" of Genes that are Differentially Methylated in Susceptible and Resilient Traumatized Animals and Reversed by SAM Treatment  

Comparative pathway analysis identified beta-estradiol and retinoic acid as upstream regulators of genes exhibiting differential methylation in susceptible animals. Retinoic acid biosynthesis and degradation pathways were significantly enriched among genes whose methylation status was normalized by SAM treatment.  

These findings implicate retinoid signaling as a key modulator of PTSD-related epigenetic changes. The invention accordingly provides methods for targeting retinoic acid pathways as a therapeutic strategy for PTSD.  

### Embodiment 4. Reversal of All Three Characteristic Pathological Behaviors of PTSD with a Novel Combination of Retinoic Acid and SAM  

Building on the discovery of retinoic acid pathway involvement, the invention discloses a combination therapy comprising SAM (50 mg/kg/day) and all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA; 5 mg/kg/day). This regimen produced synergistic effects, completely normalizing exploration deficits, social withdrawal, and hyperarousal in susceptible animals.  

The combination therapy restored DNA methylation patterns at 92% of trauma-altered CpG sites, significantly outperforming either agent alone. These results demonstrate the utility of multi-target epigenetic interventions for PTSD treatment.  

## APPLICATIONS OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The invention has broad applications in PTSD therapeutics and drug discovery. Key applications include:  

1. **Diagnostic Tools**: DNA methylation profiles in the NAc serve as biomarkers for PTSD susceptibility, enabling early identification of high-risk individuals.  

2. **Therapeutic Agents**: SAM and retinoic acid, alone or in combination, represent novel pharmacotherapies for PTSD with demonstrated efficacy in reversing core symptoms.  

3. **Drug Development Platforms**: The disclosed rat model provides a validated system for screening novel epigenetic modulators for PTSD treatment.  

4. **Personalized Medicine**: Correlation of individual methylation patterns with behavioral phenotypes enables tailored therapeutic strategies based on patients' epigenetic signatures.
