### FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel triple negative breast cancer cell line designated DKAT, which exhibits phenotypic plasticity between epithelial and mesenchymal states. The invention further provides methods for using the DKAT cell line to study epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET), and to identify therapeutic agents effective against aggressive triple negative breast cancers.

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer represents a heterogeneous group of diseases with varying clinical behaviors and molecular profiles. Gene expression profiling has identified distinct molecular subtypes including luminal A, luminal B, HER2-enriched, normal-like, and basal-like breast cancers. Triple negative breast cancers, defined by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and HER2 amplification, frequently exhibit basal-like characteristics and are associated with poor clinical outcomes. 

The molecular heterogeneity observed in breast cancer extends to cellular phenotypes, where tumor cells may exhibit epithelial, mesenchymal, or intermediate characteristics. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) describes the process by which epithelial cells lose cell-cell adhesion and polarity while acquiring mesenchymal features including increased migratory capacity. EMT plays critical roles in embryonic development and wound healing, and has been implicated in cancer progression and metastasis. The reverse process, mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET), may facilitate metastatic colonization at distant sites.

Triple negative breast cancers frequently display markers of EMT and contain populations of cells with stem-like properties. The CD44high/CD24low immunophenotype has been associated with both EMT and tumor-initiating capacity. However, current models for studying EMT in breast cancer are limited by the inability of most cell lines to reversibly transition between epithelial and mesenchymal states. Existing triple negative breast cancer cell lines either maintain fixed epithelial or mesenchymal phenotypes, or undergo irreversible EMT in culture.

There remains an unmet need for accurate experimental models that recapitulate the phenotypic plasticity observed in aggressive human breast cancers. Such models would enable investigation of the molecular mechanisms underlying EMT/MET and facilitate development of therapeutics targeting this plasticity. The present invention addresses these needs by providing a novel triple negative breast cancer cell line capable of reversible EMT/MET transitions.

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel human triple negative breast cancer cell line designated DKAT, deposited under ATCC Accession No. PTA-10322. The DKAT cell line was established from a malignant pleural effusion of a patient with aggressive, treatment-resistant metastatic breast cancer. The cell line exhibits phenotypic plasticity, capable of reversible transitions between epithelial and mesenchymal states in response to altered culture conditions.

The DKAT cell line possesses a characteristic marker profile including expression of basal cytokeratins (CK5, CK17), luminal cytokeratin (CK18), membranous and cytoplasmic E-cadherin, and contains a high percentage of CD44high/CD24low cells. The cell line harbors a p53 mutation at codon 273 (CGT>CAT) and demonstrates constitutive Akt phosphorylation at Serine 473. When cultured in serum-free mammary epithelial growth medium (MEGM), DKAT cells maintain an epithelial phenotype. Transition to serum-containing stromal cell growth medium (SCGM) induces EMT, characterized by increased vimentin expression, decreased E-cadherin, and enhanced migratory capacity. This transition is reversible, as cells returned to MEGM reacquire epithelial characteristics.

The invention further provides methods of using the DKAT cell line to study breast cancer pathogenesis. The cell line forms tumors in immunocompromised mice at very low inoculation doses (as few as 10 cells), demonstrating high tumorigenicity. Xenograft tumors recapitulate the heterogeneity of the original human tumor, containing regions with both epithelial and mesenchymal characteristics. The DKAT model enables identification of genes and proteins differentially expressed during invasion, metastasis, EMT and MET.

Additionally, the invention provides methods for identifying chemotherapeutic agents with therapeutic effects against aggressive triple negative breast cancers. The DKAT cell line serves as a platform for screening candidate compounds that modulate EMT/MET or target cancer stem-like cells. The phenotypic plasticity of DKAT cells makes them particularly valuable for studying therapeutic resistance mechanisms in breast cancer.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel human triple negative breast cancer cell line designated DKAT that addresses critical limitations in existing breast cancer models. Current triple negative breast cancer cell lines either maintain fixed epithelial (e.g., SUM-149) or mesenchymal (e.g., MDA-MB-231) phenotypes, or undergo unidirectional EMT in culture. In contrast, the DKAT cell line exhibits reversible phenotypic plasticity between epithelial and mesenchymal states, more accurately modeling the dynamic heterogeneity observed in human tumors.

The DKAT cell line was established from a malignant pleural effusion of a 35-year-old patient with rapidly progressing, treatment-resistant metastatic breast cancer. The primary tumor displayed remarkable heterogeneity, with different tumor foci exhibiting distinct epithelial and mesenchymal characteristics. This phenotypic diversity suggested inherent plasticity that was subsequently confirmed in the derived cell line.

Under standard culture conditions in serum-free MEGM, DKAT cells maintain an epithelial phenotype characterized by cobblestone morphology, membranous E-cadherin localization, and expression of basal (CK5, CK17) and luminal (CK18) cytokeratins. Transition to serum-containing SCGM induces EMT within hours, evidenced by spindle-shaped morphology, vimentin upregulation, E-cadherin downregulation, and increased migratory capacity. This transition is mediated at least in part by the transcription factor Zeb1. Importantly, the process is reversible, as cells returned to MEGM reacquire epithelial characteristics.

The DKAT cell line contains a high percentage of CD44high/CD24low cells, a phenotype associated with tumor-initiating capacity and EMT. Single DKAT cells can generate tumorspheres containing both epithelial and mesenchymal populations, demonstrating multilineage differentiation potential. In xenograft models, DKAT cells form heterogeneous tumors with regions of epithelial and mesenchymal differentiation, mirroring the original human tumor.

The DKAT model provides unique opportunities to study molecular mechanisms underlying phenotypic plasticity in aggressive breast cancers. The cell line enables identification of genes and proteins differentially expressed during EMT/MET transitions, invasion, and metastasis. Furthermore, the DKAT system serves as a platform for screening therapeutic agents that target cancer stem-like cells or modulate EMT/MET processes relevant to metastatic progression and therapeutic resistance.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Establishment of DKAT Culture

The DKAT cell line was established from a malignant pleural effusion obtained from a 35-year-old Caucasian woman with aggressive triple negative breast cancer. The patient initially presented with a 4 cm primary tumor that was ER/PR negative and HER2 normal by immunohistochemistry. Despite treatment with cyclophosphamide, methotrexate, and 5-fluorouracil, the patient developed rapid disease progression with metastases to lung, pleura, liver, and bone, succumbing to the disease within twelve months of diagnosis.

Pleural fluid was centrifuged to pellet cells, which were resuspended in Mammary Epithelial Growth Medium (MEGM) supplemented with bovine pituitary extract, insulin, human recombinant epidermal growth factor, and hydrocortisone. Within 48 hours, cells began proliferating with a doubling time of approximately 24 hours. The cell line has been maintained continuously in culture for over 70 passages while retaining stable characteristics.

#### Example 2: Maintenance of Other Cell Lines

For comparative studies, additional breast cancer cell lines were maintained under standard conditions. MDA-MB-231 and MCF-7 cells (ATCC) were cultured in αMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). SUM-190 cells (ATCC) were maintained in RPMI with 10% FBS. Human mammary epithelial cells (HMECs) immortalized with hTERT were cultured in supplemented MEGM. All cell lines were routinely tested for mycoplasma contamination.

#### Example 3: Cytogenetic Analysis

Spectral karyotyping (SKY) analysis of 25 metaphase DKAT cells at passage 3 revealed a modal chromosome number of 56. Two predominant clones were identified - a stemline and sideline differing by the presence of one double minute chromosome. Additional sidelines included near-tetraploid cells and cells with higher chromosome numbers. These cytogenetic abnormalities are consistent with the malignant origin of the cell line.

#### Example 4: Immunocytochemistry

Immunocytochemical analysis demonstrated that DKAT cells express markers consistent with basal-like breast cancer, including cytokeratins 5 and 17, p63, and EGFR. The cells were negative for ER and PR by immunostaining. Fluorescence in situ hybridization confirmed normal HER2 copy number. E-cadherin showed both membranous and cytoplasmic localization, while vimentin expression was low under standard culture conditions.

#### Example 5: Immunofluorescence

Immunofluorescence studies using antibodies against epithelial and mesenchymal markers revealed the phenotypic plasticity of DKAT cells. In MEGM, cells showed strong staining for E-cadherin, cytokeratins 5, 17, and 18, with weak vimentin signal. Transition to SCGM resulted in decreased E-cadherin, increased vimentin, and morphological changes to spindle-shaped phenotype within hours. These changes were reversible upon return to MEGM.

#### Example 6: Western Blotting

Western blot analysis demonstrated constitutive phosphorylation of Akt at Serine 473 in DKAT cells, a characteristic feature of aggressive breast cancers. The cells showed low expression of PTEN, which may contribute to Akt activation. Zeb1 protein levels increased during EMT induced by SCGM culture, while Snail1 and Twist expression remained unchanged. Claudin-1 and cytokeratin 17 decreased during EMT, while N-cadherin increased - changes that reversed during subsequent MET.

#### Example 7: Sequencing of p53 Gene

Sequencing of exons 5-9 of the TP53 gene in DKAT cells identified a single point mutation in exon 8 at codon 273 (CGT>CAT), resulting in an arginine to histidine substitution. This missense mutation is identical to that found in MDA-MB-468 breast cancer cells and is known to impair p53 function. No mutations were detected in exons 9 or 20 of PIK3CA, another gene frequently mutated in breast cancer.

#### Example 8: Gene Expression Analysis

Microarray analysis comparing DKAT cells grown in MEGM versus SCGM revealed significant changes in gene expression during EMT. Epithelial-specific genes including cytokeratins (KRT5, KRT6C, KRT17) and claudin-1 (CLDN1) were downregulated, while mesenchymal markers (CDH2/N-cadherin) and EMT-associated factors (TGFBI, LOX, ALDH1A3) were upregulated. These expression changes correlated with protein level alterations detected by western blotting.

#### Example 9: Functional Assays

Invasion assays demonstrated that DKAT cells cultured in SCGM migrated more rapidly to close scratch wounds compared to cells in MEGM (100% closure at 12 hours vs 60%). This enhanced migratory capacity was dependent on Zeb1, as shRNA knockdown of Zeb1 prevented the increased migration during EMT. Flow cytometry revealed a high percentage (>90%) of CD44high/CD24low cells in the DKAT population, a phenotype associated with stem-like properties.

In xenograft studies, DKAT cells showed remarkable tumorigenicity, forming tumors in immunocompromised mice at inoculation doses as low as 10 cells. Tumors recapitulated the heterogeneity of the original human cancer, containing regions with epithelial and mesenchymal characteristics depending on microenvironment. Tumors formed without Matrigel showed infiltrative growth patterns, while Matrigel-containing implants developed nodules with both luminal and myoepithelial differentiation.

Tumorsphere assays demonstrated that single DKAT cells could generate multicellular spheres containing both epithelial (E-cadherin+/vimentin-) and mesenchymal (E-cadherin-/vimentin+) populations, confirming the intrinsic plasticity of the cell line. This ability to generate heterogeneous progeny from single cells supports the stem-like properties of DKAT cells and their value for studying cellular differentiation in breast cancer.
