# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and genetic diagnostics, specifically to modified DNA polymerases with enhanced selectivity for applications in personalized medicine. DNA polymerases are essential enzymes that catalyze the synthesis of DNA molecules from nucleoside triphosphates using a DNA template. These enzymes find widespread application in various molecular biology techniques including polymerase chain reaction (PCR), DNA sequencing, and genetic testing. The invention particularly concerns thermostable DNA polymerases derived from Thermus aquaticus (Taq) that have been modified to exhibit improved discrimination between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes during nucleic acid amplification. Such enhanced selectivity enables more accurate detection of single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and DNA methylation patterns, which are crucial for disease diagnosis, pharmacogenomics, and personalized treatment strategies.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

Personalized medicine represents a transformative approach to healthcare that tailors medical treatment to individual patient characteristics, including genetic predisposition. This paradigm shift from a "one-size-fits-all" model to customized therapies relies heavily on the analysis of genetic variations, particularly single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). SNPs are the most abundant type of genetic variation between individuals, with over 38 million identified in the human genome. While many SNPs occur in non-coding regions without phenotypic consequences, those located in coding sequences can significantly alter protein structure and function, influencing disease susceptibility and drug response.  

The detection and analysis of SNPs present significant technical challenges. Current methods for SNP analysis include selective primer extension techniques such as minisequencing, pyrosequencing, and allele-specific amplification (ASA). ASA, particularly when combined with real-time PCR, offers an efficient approach for SNP detection by combining amplification and detection in a single reaction. This method relies on the polymerase's ability to discriminate between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes, where the matched case features a correct Watson-Crick base pair at the 3' primer end while the mismatch case contains a non-canonical base pair.  

Another critical area in molecular diagnostics is the analysis of DNA methylation patterns, particularly 5-methylcytosine (5mC), which serves as an important epigenetic marker involved in gene regulation. Aberrant methylation patterns are associated with various diseases, particularly cancer, making methylation-specific PCR (MSP) a valuable tool for early cancer detection and monitoring. MSP typically involves bisulfite treatment of DNA followed by amplification with primers specific to methylated or unmethylated sequences.  

Both ASA and MSP depend critically on the selectivity of the DNA polymerase in discriminating between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes. While several DNA polymerase variants with increased fidelity are known, there remains a need for polymerases with enhanced mismatch discrimination capabilities without compromising amplification efficiency. Previous attempts to improve polymerase selectivity have focused on mutations in conserved motifs, particularly substitutions of polar amino acids with hydrophobic residues. However, these modifications often result in reduced enzymatic activity or stability.  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing novel DNA polymerase mutants with substitutions at specific basic amino acid positions that interact with the primer strand backbone. These modifications result in polymerases that exhibit significantly improved mismatch discrimination while maintaining high amplification efficiency and stability. The invention particularly focuses on mutations in the Klenow fragment of Taq DNA polymerase (KlenTaq), which offers advantages over full-length Taq polymerase including greater thermostability and lower error rates.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The present invention provides modified DNA polymerases with enhanced selectivity for discriminating between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes. These polymerases are particularly useful for applications requiring high specificity, such as allele-specific amplification and methylation-specific PCR. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that substitution of specific basic amino acids in the KlenTaq DNA polymerase can significantly improve mismatch discrimination while maintaining or even enhancing enzymatic activity.  

The DNA polymerase of the invention comprises an amino acid sequence having at least 90% identity to SEQ ID NO: 1 (full-length Taq polymerase) or SEQ ID NO: 2 (KlenTaq fragment), wherein the sequence includes at least one amino acid substitution at a position corresponding to R487, K508, R536, R587, or R660 of SEQ ID NO: 2. In preferred embodiments, the substitution replaces the basic amino acid with a hydrophobic or polar uncharged amino acid. Particularly advantageous substitutions include R487V, K508Y, R536K, R587I, and R660V, with R660V showing the most pronounced improvement in selectivity while maintaining wild-type levels of activity.  

The invention encompasses DNA polymerases with single amino acid substitutions as well as combinations of multiple substitutions. The polymerase may contain 2, 3, 4, 5, or more amino acid substitutions at the specified positions. In all cases, the modified polymerases retain the ability to catalyze DNA synthesis while exhibiting enhanced discrimination against mismatched primer-template complexes. This property is quantitatively demonstrated by an increased ratio of catalytic efficiency for matched versus mismatched substrates (kA/kG) compared to the wild-type enzyme.  

The modified DNA polymerases of the invention are particularly useful in methods for detecting single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs). Such methods typically involve:  
(a) contacting a nucleic acid sample with a primer that is complementary to a target sequence containing a suspected SNP;  
(b) extending the primer with a modified DNA polymerase of the invention in the presence of nucleoside triphosphates; and  
(c) detecting the presence or absence of primer extension product, wherein efficient extension indicates the presence of the matched sequence while inefficient extension indicates the presence of the mismatched sequence.  

Similarly, the polymerases can be employed in methylation-specific PCR (MSP) for detecting DNA methylation patterns. In these applications, the DNA is first treated with bisulfite to convert unmethylated cytosines to uracil, followed by amplification with primers specific to either methylated or unmethylated sequences. The enhanced selectivity of the modified polymerases improves discrimination between these sequences, enabling more accurate detection of methylation status.  

The invention further provides nucleic acid molecules encoding the modified DNA polymerases, expression vectors containing these nucleic acids, and host cells transformed with such vectors. Methods for producing the modified polymerases include culturing host cells under conditions suitable for expression of the polymerase and recovering the polypeptide from the culture.  

Kits comprising the modified DNA polymerase along with necessary reagents and instructions for performing SNP detection or methylation analysis are also provided. Such kits may include primers, nucleoside triphosphates, buffers, and detection reagents suitable for real-time PCR applications.  

The modified DNA polymerases exhibit several advantages over existing enzymes for molecular diagnostics. These include:  
1. Enhanced discrimination between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes, enabling more accurate SNP detection and methylation analysis;  
2. Maintenance of high catalytic activity, allowing efficient amplification even with challenging templates;  
3. Improved thermostability compared to wild-type Taq polymerase;  
4. Compatibility with direct amplification from whole blood samples without extensive DNA purification;  
5. Suitability for multiplex assays where multiple targets are amplified simultaneously.  

These properties make the modified polymerases particularly valuable for clinical diagnostics, pharmacogenomic testing, and research applications requiring high specificity in nucleic acid amplification.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

This example describes the identification and screening of KlenTaq mutants with improved mismatch discrimination. Saturation mutagenesis was performed at positions R487, K508, R536, R587, and R660 using degenerate primers to create libraries covering all possible amino acid substitutions at each position. The libraries were screened in a high-throughput format using 384-well plates and SYBR Green I detection. Each mutant was tested for thermostability, specificity, and 3' end mismatch discrimination by comparing amplification efficiency between matched and mismatched primer-template complexes.  

From the screening, several mutants showing increased selectivity were identified for each position. These included R487V, K508Y, R536K, R587I, and R660V. The selectivity of these mutants was confirmed by purifying the enzymes and repeating the allele-specific amplification assays. Notably, the R660V mutant showed particularly strong discrimination while maintaining wild-type levels of activity.  

### Example 2  

Primer extension assays were performed to directly compare the activity and mismatch extension selectivity of selected mutants. A 20-nucleotide radioactively labeled primer was annealed to either matched or mismatched 33-nucleotide templates. Reactions were initiated by addition of dNTPs and analyzed by denaturing PAGE after 10 minutes at 55°C.  

While the wild-type KlenTaq showed full-length product formation for both matched and mismatched cases, all mutants showed no product formation for the mismatch case after 10 minutes. For the match case, mutants R587K and R660V showed clean full-length product formation comparable to wild-type, while R487V and K508Y exhibited reduced activity. These results confirmed the enhanced selectivity of the mutants while revealing differences in their catalytic efficiency.  

### Example 3  

The kinetic properties of selected mutants were characterized under pre-steady state conditions to quantify their mismatch discrimination. Single nucleotide incorporation turnover rates were determined at saturating enzyme and dNTP concentrations. The ratio of catalytic efficiencies for matched versus mismatched substrates (kA/kG) was significantly increased in all mutants compared to wild-type, with R487V showing a 36-fold improvement in discrimination.  

### Example 4  

The performance of selected mutants was evaluated in real-time ASA using human genomic DNA containing the Factor II prothrombin SNP. All mutants showed increased Δc(t) values compared to wild-type KlenTaq, indicating improved discrimination. The R660V mutant showed the highest discrimination (18 cycles difference between matched and mismatched cases) while maintaining high activity (c(t) = 30 for matched case vs. 28 for wild-type).  

### Example 5  

The R660V mutant was compared to commercially available polymerases (Platinum Taq and Taq 2X Master Mix) using homozygous (A/A) human genomic DNA standards. The mutant showed nearly double the Δc(t) values of the commercial enzymes, demonstrating superior discrimination. Similar results were obtained with heterozygous (G/A) and wild-type (G/G) templates, confirming the mutant's suitability for reliable SNP genotyping.  

### Example 6  

A multiplex ASA assay was developed by incorporating a 30-nucleotide overhang into one allele-specific primer, enabling simultaneous detection of both alleles in the same reaction based on melting temperature differences. The assay successfully genotyped all three possible SNP configurations (wild-type, homozygous, and heterozygous) when using the R660V mutant.  

### Example 7  

The R660V mutant was shown to perform ASA directly from whole blood samples without DNA purification. Despite fluorescence quenching by blood components, successful genotyping was achieved by increasing SYBR Green I concentration 30-fold and polymerase concentration 5-fold compared to standard reactions with purified DNA.  

### Example 8  

The R660V mutant was evaluated for HLA typing by sequence-specific primed PCR (PCR-SSP). Compared to AmpliTaq Gold DNA polymerase, the mutant showed equivalent sensitivity but superior specificity, with no spurious amplification of non-target alleles.  

### Example 9  

The error rate of R660V was determined to be 2.8×10^-5 mutations per base per duplication, representing an improvement over wild-type KlenTaq (8.8×10^-5). This improvement primarily resulted from reduced transition mutations while transversion rates remained unchanged.  

### Example 10  

The R660V mutant was successfully applied to methylation-specific PCR (MSP) for detection of hypermethylation in the Septin 9 gene, a colon cancer marker. The mutant showed clear discrimination between methylated and unmethylated templates and outperformed a commercial MSP kit in both activity and specificity.
