# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Lysosomal enzymes are essential proteins responsible for breaking down various macromolecules within the lysosomes of eukaryotic cells. These enzymes are synthesized in the endoplasmic reticulum and undergo post-translational modifications, including glycosylation, before being trafficked to lysosomes via the mannose 6-phosphate receptor pathway. Deficiencies in specific lysosomal enzymes lead to a group of disorders known as lysosomal storage diseases, characterized by the accumulation of undegraded substrates within lysosomes. Enzyme replacement therapy has emerged as a treatment strategy for certain lysosomal storage diseases, wherein deficient enzymes are administered intravenously to patients. The therapeutic enzymes are taken up by cells through receptor-mediated endocytosis, primarily via the cation-independent mannose 6-phosphate receptor (CI-MPR).  

Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting (GILT) technology represents an innovative approach to improve lysosomal enzyme delivery. The GILT system utilizes peptide tags derived from insulin-like growth factor II (IGF-II) that bind CI-MPR independently of mannose 6-phosphate modifications. This technology enables efficient lysosomal targeting of therapeutic enzymes regardless of their glycosylation state, potentially overcoming limitations associated with traditional enzyme replacement therapies that rely on proper glycosylation for effective delivery.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides improved compositions and methods for lysosomal targeting of therapeutic proteins. The invention specifically discloses furin-resistant lysosomal targeting peptides that maintain binding affinity for the cation-independent mannose 6-phosphate receptor while exhibiting reduced susceptibility to proteolytic cleavage. These peptides demonstrate unexpectedly reduced binding affinity for the insulin receptor, which represents a significant advancement in the field.  

The invention further describes novel IGF-II muteins that are resistant to furin cleavage while retaining the ability to bind CI-MPR. These muteins exhibit reduced binding affinity for both the insulin receptor and the IGF-I receptor, providing superior targeting specificity compared to native IGF-II peptides. The muteins incorporate specific amino acid substitutions, deletions, or replacements in key regions while maintaining essential binding surfaces for CI-MPR interaction.  

The invention encompasses targeted therapeutic fusion proteins comprising these improved targeting peptides linked to lysosomal enzymes. The fusion proteins may include spacer elements between the targeting moiety and the therapeutic enzyme. Nucleic acids encoding these IGF-II muteins and fusion proteins are also disclosed, along with pharmaceutical compositions containing them and methods for treating lysosomal storage disorders.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides significant improvements in Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting (GILT) technology for therapeutic enzyme delivery. The disclosed compositions and methods address critical limitations of current enzyme replacement therapies by providing more stable and specific targeting molecules.  

### Lysosomal Enzymes  

Lysosomal enzymes constitute a diverse group of acid hydrolases responsible for breaking down various cellular components. Suitable lysosomal enzymes for use in the invention include those deficient in lysosomal storage disorders, particularly acid alpha-glucosidase (GAA) for Pompe disease, alpha-galactosidase A for Fabry disease, and beta-glucocerebrosidase for Gaucher disease. These enzymes share at least 70% polypeptide sequence identity with their native human counterparts when aligned using standard algorithms such as BLAST with default parameters.  

### Pompe Disease  

Pompe disease serves as a primary example of a lysosomal storage disorder treatable by the current invention. This autosomal recessive condition results from deficiency of acid alpha-glucosidase, leading to glycogen accumulation in lysosomes. The disease manifests along a spectrum from severe infantile-onset forms with cardiomyopathy to milder late-onset variants primarily affecting skeletal muscle. Residual GAA activity correlates inversely with disease severity, with infantile-onset cases typically having less than 1% of normal activity. Without treatment, classic infantile Pompe disease leads to death from cardiorespiratory failure within the first year of life.  

### Enzyme Replacement Therapy  

Enzyme replacement therapy has emerged as the standard treatment for several lysosomal storage disorders. Current approaches rely on intravenous administration of recombinant enzymes that are taken up by cells via CI-MPR-mediated endocytosis. However, these therapies face limitations related to inefficient targeting of certain tissues, particularly skeletal muscle, and variability in enzyme glycosylation patterns that affect uptake efficiency.  

### Glycosylation Independent Lysosomal Targeting  

The GILT technology overcomes glycosylation-dependent limitations by utilizing IGF-II-derived peptides that bind CI-MPR independently of mannose 6-phosphate modifications. The GILT tag comprises key regions of IGF-II necessary for receptor interaction, fused to therapeutic enzymes via flexible linkers. This approach enables consistent targeting regardless of the enzyme's glycosylation state.  

### Furin-Resistant GILT Tag  

A critical advancement of the current invention involves engineering furin-resistant GILT tags. The mature human IGF-II peptide contains potential furin cleavage sites that can compromise targeting efficiency. The invention identifies these vulnerable sequences and provides specific mutations to abolish furin cleavage while preserving CI-MPR binding. Preferred substitutions include lysine or alanine replacements at critical positions, with no more than 30% of residues altered to maintain structural integrity.  

The modified IGF-II polypeptides exhibit dissociation constants (Kd) for CI-MPR between 1-50 nM, comparable to wild-type IGF-II. Simultaneously, these muteins demonstrate significantly reduced affinity for the IGF-I receptor, with binding affinities at least 10-fold lower than wild-type IGF-II. This specificity is achieved through strategic substitutions in regions critical for IGF-I receptor interaction while preserving surfaces required for CI-MPR binding.  

### Binding Affinity for the Insulin Receptor  

The invention further provides IGF-II muteins with reduced binding to the insulin receptor, measured by competitive binding assays showing at least 5-fold lower affinity compared to wild-type IGF-II. This reduction minimizes potential metabolic side effects while maintaining therapeutic efficacy.  

### Mutagenesis  

The invention encompasses various mutagenesis strategies to create improved targeting peptides, including nucleotide changes leading to conservative and non-conservative amino acid substitutions, insertions, deletions, and truncations. Scanning amino acid analysis identifies critical residues for modification while preserving essential functions.  

### Spacer  

The invention includes optimized spacer sequences between targeting peptides and therapeutic enzymes. These spacers typically range from 5-30 amino acids and may incorporate flexible glycine-serine repeats or structured domains that promote proper folding of both moieties.  

### Cells

Expression may also occur in non-mammalian systems including insect cells, plant cells, yeast, prokaryotes, or fungi.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

The ZC-701 protein, comprising wild-type IGF-II fused to GAA, was analyzed for furin susceptibility. N-terminal sequencing identified cleavage at a canonical furin site, with SDS-PAGE analysis showing approximately 40% of the protein was cleaved during production. This heterogeneity motivated development of furin-resistant variants.  

### Example 2  

Multiple mutant versions of ZC-701 were constructed and analyzed. Constructs 1459, 1460, 1461, 1463, 1479, and 1487 incorporated various point mutations to disrupt furin cleavage sites while maintaining CI-MPR binding. Subsequent constructs (1726, 1749, 1746, 1747) introduced additional modifications to reduce insulin receptor affinity. Deletion mutants (1758, 1750, 1748, 1751-1756, 1763) further optimized targeting specificity.  

### Example 3  

Furin-resistant GILT-tagged GAA was expressed in CHO cells and purified. Enzyme activity assays confirmed the fusion protein maintained full catalytic activity comparable to untagged GAA, demonstrating the modifications did not compromise enzymatic function.  

### Example 4  

Competitive receptor binding assays quantified the improved specificity of the engineered muteins. The IC50 for CI-MPR binding remained below 20 nM for all optimized constructs, while insulin receptor binding affinity increased to above 500 nM, representing at least a 25-fold improvement in specificity compared to wild-type IGF-II.  

### Example 5  

Cell uptake assays demonstrated the furin-resistant GILT-tagged GAA maintained efficient lysosomal delivery, with tissue culture studies showing complete colocalization with lysosomal markers. The modified protein showed no detectable cleavage products and exhibited uniform molecular weight by SDS-PAGE analysis.
