## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel metal hydride battery systems utilizing ionic liquid-based electrolytes. The invention discloses a proton-conducting battery system comprising a metal hydride anode, a nickel hydroxide cathode, and an electrolyte composition containing ionic liquids mixed with protic acids. The electrolyte composition enables proton transport through both vehicle and Grotthuss mechanisms while providing a wider electrochemical stability window compared to conventional aqueous electrolytes.  

The invention specifically discloses electrolyte compositions comprising aprotic ionic liquids combined with organic acids such as acetic acid or phosphoric acid. These compositions demonstrate ionic conductivities exceeding 10 mS/cm while maintaining electrochemical stability up to 2.5V. The battery embodiments utilize various ABx-type hydrogen storage alloys including AB5, AB2, and A2B7 type structures with specific compositions optimized for proton conduction in non-aqueous electrolytes.

## EXAMPLES  

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Acetic Acid  

Example 1 demonstrates preparation of electrolyte compositions containing 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium acetate mixed with acetic acid in concentrations from 0.1M to 6M. The compositions show ionic conductivity increasing with acetic acid concentration, reaching 14.3 mS/cm for 6M acetic acid in [DEMA][TfO].  

### Electrolyte Composition Comprising Ionic Liquid and Solvent  

Example 2 discloses preparation of electrolyte containing 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium acetate mixed with propylene carbonate in 1:1 weight ratio. The composition demonstrates ionic conductivity of 8.7 mS/cm at 25°C.  

### Electrolyte Compositions Comprising Ionic Liquid and Protic Acid  

Example 3 provides preparation of electrolytes containing 1-butyl-3-methylimidazolium triflate mixed with phosphoric acid. The compositions show proton conductivity through both vehicle and Grotthuss mechanisms.  

### Electrolyte Composition Containing 2 M Acetic Acid in EMIM Ac  

Example 4 demonstrates charge/discharge testing of a metal hydride battery with AB5 alloy anode and nickel hydroxide cathode using 2M acetic acid in 1-ethyl-3-methylimidazolium acetate electrolyte. The battery shows stable cycling with discharge capacity of 161.9 mAh/g at 4 mA/g current density.  

### Example 5  

Example 5 discloses cyclic voltammetry analysis of hydrogen adsorption peaks in 2M acetic acid/[EMIM][Ac] electrolyte. The results show a 0.6V voltage gap between hydrogen cathodic peak and electrolyte reduction edge, indicating stable operation window.  

Additional examples demonstrate preparation of ternary electrolyte compositions containing ionic liquids, acetic acid, and propylene carbonate. The compositions show optimized ionic conductivity and battery performance characteristics.
