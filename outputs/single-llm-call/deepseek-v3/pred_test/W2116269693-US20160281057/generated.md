# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to agents for improving sperm motility, particularly through the use of hydrogen molecules (H2). More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for enhancing sperm motility in vitro, including the treatment of cryopreserved sperm and experimentally damaged sperm suspensions. The invention has significant applications in assisted reproductive technologies, particularly for treating male infertility associated with asthenozoospermia. The technical field encompasses compositions containing hydrogen gas or hydrogen-dissolved liquids, methods for administering such compositions to sperm samples, and the resulting improvements in sperm motility parameters.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Male infertility is a widespread medical condition affecting a substantial proportion of couples attempting conception. Among the various causes of male infertility, impaired sperm motility (asthenozoospermia) represents a significant contributing factor. Male infertility may result from multiple etiologies including genetic defects, systemic disorders, environmental factors, and oxidative stress. Azoospermia, the complete absence of sperm in the ejaculate, represents a particularly severe form of male infertility. Non-obstructive azoospermia, resulting from testicular failure, presents significant challenges for fertility treatment.  

The hypo-osmotic swelling (HOS) test has been used as a diagnostic tool for assessing sperm membrane integrity. Micro-insemination techniques, including intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), have been developed to overcome severe male factor infertility. Cryopreservation of sperm has become an essential component of fertility preservation, though the freezing process itself can adversely affect sperm motility and viability. The damaging effects of freezing on sperm include structural alterations to the plasma membrane, acrosome, and mitochondria, as well as depletion of intracellular ATP stores.  

Sperm motility depends fundamentally on flagellar movement, which is powered by ATP production in mitochondria. Mitochondria serve as both the primary source of ATP for sperm motility and the main generators of reactive oxygen species (ROS). While low levels of ROS play essential roles in sperm function regulation, excessive ROS production leads to oxidative damage, lipid peroxidation, and motility impairment. The balance between ROS production and antioxidant defenses is particularly precarious in sperm due to their high content of polyunsaturated fatty acids and limited DNA repair capacity.  

Various antioxidants have been investigated for their potential to improve sperm motility, including zinc, aspartic acid, coenzyme Q10, myoinositol, and xanthine derivatives. The hydrogen molecule (H2) has emerged as a novel therapeutic agent due to its unique properties as a selective antioxidant. H2 can rapidly diffuse across biological membranes and selectively neutralize cytotoxic hydroxyl radicals while sparing physiologically important reactive oxygen species. Previous studies have demonstrated the protective effects of H2 against oxidative stress-induced injury in various organs and tissues.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Object to be Solved by the Invention  

The present invention addresses the problem of immotile or poorly motile sperm in male infertility treatment. Current approaches for managing asthenozoospermia remain limited in efficacy, particularly for severe cases and cryopreserved sperm samples. There exists a need for improved methods to enhance sperm motility that are safe, effective, and compatible with assisted reproductive technologies. The invention specifically aims to overcome the motility impairments caused by oxidative stress and cryopreservation damage in human sperm.  

### Means to Solve the Object  

The invention is based on the discovery that hydrogen molecules (H2) can effectively restore motility in damaged sperm samples. Through extensive research, it was found that treatment with hydrogen-containing gases or hydrogen-dissolved liquids significantly improves sperm motility parameters. The invention provides a culture solution containing hydrogen molecules for treating sperm suspensions. The method is particularly effective for cryopreserved sperm, demonstrating improved motility after thawing when treated with hydrogen-containing solutions.  

Key findings supporting the invention include:  
1. Hydrogen treatment increases the percentage of sperm with forward motility in experimentally damaged samples from 3.6% to 16.8%.  
2. The motility-enhancing effect is maintained for at least 2.5 hours after hydrogen treatment.  
3. Hydrogen treatment enhances mitochondrial membrane potential in sperm, suggesting improved ATP production.  
4. Frozen-thawed sperm samples show significant motility improvement after hydrogen treatment, particularly in asthenozoospermic samples.  

### Effect of the Invention  

The present invention provides several important benefits:  
1. Significant improvement in sperm motility parameters, particularly forward progressive motility.  
2. Maintenance of improved motility for clinically relevant time periods.  
3. Compatibility with standard assisted reproductive techniques and cryopreservation protocols.  
4. Safety advantages as hydrogen is a naturally occurring molecule with minimal side effects.  
5. Particular effectiveness for difficult-to-treat cases including cryopreserved sperm and severe asthenozoospermia.  

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The invention provides an agent for improving sperm motility comprising hydrogen molecules (H2) in gaseous or dissolved form. The composition of the agent may include hydrogen gas mixed with other gases such as carbon dioxide (5%), oxygen (20%), and nitrogen (balance). Alternatively, the agent may comprise a liquid medium saturated with hydrogen molecules, such as physiological saline or culture solution.  

The method of measuring sperm motility involves placing a sperm suspension in a counting chamber and visually assessing concentration, forward motility rate, non-forward motility rate, and immobility rate. Computer-assisted sperm analysis (CASA) may be employed for more precise velocity measurements. The percentage of sperm with motility is calculated by dividing the number of motile sperm by the total sperm count.  

Sperm collection and preparation involves obtaining semen samples by masturbation after 3-5 days of abstinence. Samples are washed in cleavage medium supplemented with plasma protein fraction and centrifuged through a Percoll density gradient. For cryopreservation, sperm are mixed with TEST-yolk buffer, exposed to nitrogen vapor, and stored in liquid nitrogen. Thawing involves warming cryotubes at 37°C for 5 minutes.  

The gaseous agent for improving sperm motility comprises a mixed gas containing 5% CO2, 20% O2, and 50% H2, with the balance being N2. The liquid agent contains hydrogen molecules dissolved in physiological saline or culture medium at concentrations ranging from 50% to 100% of saturation. The liquid agent may further include buffer components to maintain physiological pH.  

Hydrogen impermeable materials for storage include glass and certain plastics, while hydrogen permeable materials include specific gas-permeable polymers suitable for exposure chambers. The method of enhancing sperm motility involves placing sperm suspensions in an exposure chamber with the hydrogen-containing gas mixture for 30-60 minutes, or incubating with hydrogen-dissolved liquid medium for 5 minutes. The treatment may be repeated to maintain improved motility.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1: Sperm Collection and Adjustment**  
Semen samples were collected from patients after 3-5 days of abstinence. Samples were washed in cleavage medium with 10% plasma protein fraction, centrifuged through Percoll density gradient, and resuspended in cleavage medium.  

**Example 2: Treatment Using Gaseous Agent**  
Damaged sperm suspensions were placed in an exposure chamber with H2-mixed gas (5% CO2, 20% O2, 50% H2, 25% N2) or N2-mixed gas (control) for 30-60 minutes. The H2 treatment group showed significantly increased forward motility (16.8%) compared to control (3.6%).  

**Example 3: Measurement of Sperm Motility**  
Sperm concentration and motility were measured using a counting chamber. Forward motility increased from 3.6% to 16.8% after H2 treatment. Time-lapse microscopy and CASA analysis confirmed motility improvement without significant velocity changes.  

**Example 4: Mitochondrial Membrane Potential**  
Sperm stained with TMRM showed increased fluorescence after H2 treatment, indicating enhanced mitochondrial membrane potential. This suggests improved ATP production as a mechanism for motility enhancement.  

**Example 5: Treatment of Frozen-Thawed Sperm**  
Cryopreserved sperm samples were thawed and treated with H2-containing washing medium. Asthenozoospermic samples (<50% motility) showed significant motility improvement from 27.5% to 37.1% after treatment.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The invention has wide applicability in the field of assisted reproductive technology. The sperm-motility enhancing agent can be utilized in fertility clinics for improving outcomes in IVF and ICSI procedures. The technology is particularly valuable for treating male infertility cases involving poor sperm motility, including cryopreserved sperm samples. The invention may be commercialized as a medical device or therapeutic agent for use in reproductive medicine.
