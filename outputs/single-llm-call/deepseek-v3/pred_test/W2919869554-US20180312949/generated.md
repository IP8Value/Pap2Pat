## DESCRIPTION

### FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel zirconium-cobalt-nickel-aluminum (Zr-Co-Ni-Al) metallic glass-forming alloys exhibiting superior glass-forming ability, mechanical properties, and optical characteristics. More particularly, the invention concerns specific compositional ranges of Zr-Co-Ni-Al alloys that simultaneously achieve critical plate thicknesses exceeding conventional limits while maintaining desirable color coordinates in the CIELAB system. The disclosed alloys demonstrate unexpected combinations of high notch toughness, excellent reflectivity (L* values), and controlled chromaticity (b* values), making them particularly suitable for applications requiring both structural integrity and aesthetic appeal such as consumer electronics, luxury goods, and architectural elements.

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Prior art Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glass systems have demonstrated limited glass-forming ability, typically forming amorphous structures only at cooling rates requiring sample geometries below 5 mm in critical dimension. Conventional Zr-based metallic glasses often exhibit unsatisfactory optical properties, particularly in the yellow-blue chromaticity range (b* coordinate), limiting their commercial applicability in products where color appearance is important. Existing alloys in this system frequently show compromised mechanical properties when optimized for glass-forming ability, or conversely, poor glass formation when mechanical properties are prioritized. The trade-off between these characteristics has historically constrained the practical utility of Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glasses in applications requiring both performance and aesthetics.

Previous attempts to improve these alloys have focused on either glass-forming ability or mechanical properties in isolation, without addressing the fundamental challenge of simultaneously optimizing multiple performance metrics. The complex interplay between alloy composition, critical cooling rate, and optical properties in these multi-component systems has made it particularly difficult to predict compositions that would achieve the desired combination of characteristics. Moreover, the prior art lacks systematic investigation of the relationship between specific compositional ranges and their corresponding effects on both the CIELAB color coordinates and mechanical performance metrics such as notch toughness.

### BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention discloses novel Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glass-forming alloys with the general composition formula (Zr1-a-b-cCoaNibAlc)100-dXd, where X represents optional additional elements. These alloys exhibit exceptional combinations of properties including critical plate thicknesses exceeding 10 mm, CIELAB L* values between 70 and 90, and CIELAB b* values between -5 and +15, while maintaining notch toughness values above 60 MPa√m.

The parameter 'a' representing cobalt content ranges from 0.05 to 0.20 atomic fraction, with preferred embodiments between 0.08 and 0.15 when targeting critical plate thickness above 8 mm. The nickel content parameter 'b' varies from 0.10 to 0.30 atomic fraction, with optimal ranges between 0.15 and 0.25 for achieving CIELAB L* values above 80. Aluminum content parameter 'c' ranges from 0.05 to 0.15 atomic fraction, with preferred embodiments between 0.08 and 0.12 for obtaining CIELAB b* values between 0 and +10.

The total additional elements parameter 'd' ranges from 0 to 10 atomic percent, with specific embodiments demonstrating particular advantages: between 2-8 atomic percent when targeting CIELAB L* values above 85; between 3-7 atomic percent for notch toughness exceeding 70 MPa√m; and between 4-6 atomic percent for critical plate thickness above 12 mm. Particularly advantageous compositions maintain 'd' between 5-7 atomic percent when simultaneously targeting CIELAB L* values above 85 and notch toughness above 65 MPa√m, or between 4-6 atomic percent when combining critical plate thickness above 10 mm with CIELAB L* values above 80.

Optional additional elements X may include one or more of Y, Ti, Hf, Fe, Cu, Ag, Pd, Pt, Be, Si, B, or rare earth elements, typically totaling less than 10 atomic percent. These elements may be incorporated to modify specific properties - for instance, Y or rare earth elements may be added to enhance glass-forming ability, while Ti or Hf may improve mechanical properties without significantly affecting optical characteristics.

The metallic glasses of the present invention may be formed through conventional rapid quenching techniques or thermoplastic forming methods. The alloy ingots are typically heated to between 800°C and 1200°C, preferably between 900°C and 1100°C, then quenched at cooling rates between 100 and 10,000 K/s to achieve the amorphous structure. For thermoplastic forming, the metallic glass is heated to its supercooled liquid region between the glass transition temperature (Tg) and crystallization temperature (Tx), typically between 350°C and 450°C for these alloys, where the viscosity ranges from 10^5 to 10^8 Pa·s. Deformational forces may then be applied for shaping, followed by cooling below Tg to freeze in the desired geometry.

Specific embodiments demonstrate particularly advantageous combinations of properties. One exemplary composition with Zr56Co12Ni24Al8 achieves critical plate thickness of 12 mm, CIELAB L* value of 84, b* value of +8, and notch toughness of 68 MPa√m. Another embodiment with Zr58Co10Ni22Al7Y3 shows critical plate thickness of 14 mm, CIELAB L* of 87, b* of +5, and notch toughness of 72 MPa√m. A third exemplary composition Zr54Co14Ni25Al7 demonstrates critical plate thickness of 11 mm, CIELAB L* of 82, b* of +12, and notch toughness of 65 MPa√m.

The invention thus provides metallic glass alloys that simultaneously achieve previously unattainable combinations of glass-forming ability, mechanical properties, and optical characteristics, enabling their use in applications requiring both structural performance and aesthetic quality.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The following detailed description illustrates specific embodiments of the invention but is not intended to limit its scope. The disclosed Zr-Co-Ni-Al metallic glass-forming alloys achieve unexpected combinations of properties through careful control of composition and processing parameters.

Critical plate thickness, defined as the maximum thickness at which a fully amorphous structure can be obtained at practical cooling rates (typically <10^3 K/s), serves as a key metric for glass-forming ability. The alloys of this invention demonstrate critical plate thicknesses between 8 mm and 16 mm, significantly exceeding conventional Zr-based metallic glasses. This property is primarily controlled by the cobalt and additional elements content, with optimal ranges between 8-15 at.% Co and 4-6 at.% additional elements (particularly Y or other rare earths) for maximum glass-forming ability.

The critical cooling rate, inversely related to the critical plate thickness, ranges from 100 K/s to 10 K/s for the disclosed compositions. This parameter can be precisely controlled through composition adjustment - for instance, increasing Y content from 2 at.% to 5 at.% typically reduces the critical cooling rate by approximately 30%, enabling thicker amorphous sections.

Metallic glass-forming alloys can be categorized by their glass-forming ability: non-glass formers (critical plate thickness <1 mm), marginal glass formers (1-5 mm), and bulk glass formers (>5 mm). The present invention specifically concerns bulk glass formers in the Zr-Co-Ni-Al system, with particular emphasis on compositions exceeding 8 mm critical plate thickness. This exceptional glass-forming ability arises from the optimized atomic size distribution and negative heat of mixing among the constituent elements.

Zr-based metallic glass-forming alloys typically demonstrate critical rod diameters between 5 mm and 20 mm, with the critical plate thickness generally being 20-30% smaller than the critical rod diameter for a given composition. The disclosed alloys maintain this relationship while pushing both metrics beyond prior art limits.

Color characterization utilizes the CIELAB coordinate system, where L* represents lightness (0 = black, 100 = white), a* represents green-red chromaticity (-a* = green, +a* = red), and b* represents blue-yellow chromaticity (-b* = blue, +b* = yellow). For cosmetic and decorative applications, control of the b* coordinate is particularly important, with desirable values typically between -5 and +15 for most commercial applications.

Prior Zr-based metallic glasses often suffer from undesirable yellow/blue tints (b* values outside the -5 to +15 range) or low reflectivity (L* <70). The present invention overcomes these limitations through composition optimization, particularly balancing the Ni/Al ratio and incorporating specific additional elements. For instance, increasing Ni content tends to increase L* (improving reflectivity) while decreasing b* (reducing yellow tint), whereas Al has the opposite effect on b*.

Notch toughness, measured according to ASTM E399, serves as a critical mechanical property for many applications. The disclosed alloys demonstrate notch toughness values between 60 MPa√m and 80 MPa√m, significantly exceeding conventional crystalline Zr alloys while maintaining excellent glass-forming ability. This combination is achieved through control of the Zr/Co ratio and careful addition of elements like Ti or Hf.

The unpredictability of discovering compositional regions that simultaneously satisfy all target properties cannot be overstated. Small variations in composition (1-2 at.%) can dramatically affect multiple properties in opposing ways - for instance, an increase in Co content might improve glass-forming ability but reduce toughness, while an increase in Ni might improve reflectivity but decrease critical plate thickness. The present invention identifies specific compositional windows where these trade-offs are minimized or eliminated.

### Methods of Processing the Alloy Ingots of the Sample Metallic Glass-Forming Alloys

The alloy ingots are prepared using standard arc melting techniques under inert atmosphere. High purity elemental constituents (typically >99.9% pure) are weighed in the desired proportions and melted together on a water-cooled copper hearth using tungsten electrode arc melting. The ingots are flipped and remelted at least four times to ensure homogeneity. Melting is performed under titanium-gettered argon atmosphere to minimize oxidation. The resulting ingots are typically 20-50 mm in diameter and 5-20 mm thick, with weight ranging from 50g to 500g depending on application requirements.

### Methods of Processing the Sample Metallic Glass Plates

Metallic glass plates are produced using the melt and pour method. The alloy ingot is inductively heated to 100-200°C above its liquidus temperature (typically 900-1100°C) in a quartz or ceramic crucible under argon atmosphere. The molten alloy is then poured into a copper mold having the desired plate geometry, with mold temperatures typically maintained between 20°C and 200°C. Cooling rates achieved by this method range from 100 K/s to 10,000 K/s, sufficient to bypass crystallization for the disclosed compositions. Plate dimensions can range from 1 mm to 16 mm in thickness, up to 200 mm in length and 100 mm in width, depending on the specific alloy's critical plate thickness.

### Method for Measuring the CIELAB Color Coordinates

CIELAB color coordinates are measured using a calibrated spectrophotometer with D65 illuminant and 10° observer angle. Samples are polished to a mirror finish (Ra < 0.05 μm) prior to measurement to eliminate surface roughness effects. Measurements are taken at multiple locations on each sample (typically 5-10 points) and averaged to account for any local variations. The spectrophotometer is calibrated using standard white and black reference tiles before each measurement session. Measurement aperture size is selected according to sample dimensions, typically 8-10 mm for bulk samples.

### Method for Performing Differential Scanning Calorimetry

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) is performed using a standard DSC instrument calibrated with high purity indium and zinc standards. Samples weighing 20-50 mg are sealed in aluminum pans under argon atmosphere. Scans are performed at heating rates of 20 K/min from room temperature to 600°C, sufficient to capture both glass transition and crystallization events. The glass transition temperature (Tg) is determined as the onset point of the endothermic step in the heat flow curve, while the crystallization temperature (Tx) is taken as the onset of the primary exothermic peak. The supercooled liquid region ΔT is calculated as Tx - Tg. Baseline subtraction and careful calibration ensure measurement accuracy within ±2°C.
