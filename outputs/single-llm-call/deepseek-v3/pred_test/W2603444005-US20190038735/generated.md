## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to immunogenic compositions comprising virus-like particles (VLPs) for use in vaccines against Staphylococcus aureus infections, particularly methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA). More specifically, the invention provides VLP-based vaccines displaying autoinducing peptides (AIPs) or epitopic peptides derived from S. aureus toxins, which elicit protective immune responses against S. aureus virulence factors regulated by quorum sensing.  

## BACKGROUND AND OVERVIEW OF THE INVENTION  

Staphylococcus aureus infections pose a significant public health burden, with skin and soft tissue infections (SSTIs) increasing by 40% between 2000 and 2012 in the United States. Over half of these infections are caused by methicillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA), which is associated with higher treatment failure rates and increased medical costs compared to antibiotic-susceptible strains. The rise of antibiotic resistance underscores the urgent need for alternative therapeutic strategies, including vaccines targeting S. aureus virulence mechanisms.  

S. aureus virulence is largely regulated by the accessory gene regulator (agr) operon through a quorum-sensing system. This system relies on autoinducing peptides (AIPs), which activate the membrane-bound receptor AgrC to induce expression of virulence factors. Four agr allelic variants (agr-I to agr-IV) exist, each producing a distinct AIP (AIP1-AIP4). Among these, agr type I isolates are most frequently associated with invasive infections, making them a critical target for therapeutic intervention.  

Current antibiotic therapies face limitations due to resistance development and recurrent infections. Vaccination against AIPs presents a promising alternative by neutralizing agr signaling, thereby suppressing virulence factor production. However, AIPs are small, conformationally constrained peptides that are inherently non-immunogenic, complicating vaccine development. The present invention addresses these challenges by utilizing bacteriophage-derived virus-like particles (VLPs) to present AIP-derived peptides in a highly immunogenic multivalent format.  

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides VLP-based vaccines for prophylactic and therapeutic use against S. aureus infections. The VLPs comprise a bacteriophage single-chain coat polypeptide dimer displaying epitopic peptides derived from S. aureus AIPs or toxins. In one embodiment, the VLPs present a modified AIP1 sequence (AIP1S; YSTSDFIM) inserted into the AB-loop of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa RNA bacteriophage PP7 coat protein. This modification eliminates the labile thiolactone bond of native AIP1 while preserving structural mimicry to elicit antibodies that recognize cyclic AIP1.  

The invention further encompasses hybrid VLPs displaying multiple S. aureus epitopes, including AIP2, AIP2S, AIP3, AIP3S, AIP4, and AIP4S peptides, as well as toxin-derived epitopes. Nucleic acid constructs encoding these VLPs are also provided, comprising a bacterial or bacteriophage promoter, a coat polypeptide dimer coding sequence, restriction sites for epitope insertion, an antibiotic resistance gene, and a replication origin. Additional components may include PCR primers, repressor sequences, helper phage genes, and transcription terminators.  

The VLPs are produced recombinantly in E. coli and self-assemble into icosahedral particles displaying 90 copies of the epitopic peptide. Vaccination with these VLPs induces high-titer antibodies that neutralize AIP-mediated agr signaling, thereby reducing virulence factor production and enhancing bacterial clearance in vivo. The invention thus provides a versatile platform for targeting quorum-sensing systems in S. aureus and other bacterial pathogens.

### Production of Virus-Like Particles  

VLPs are produced in vitro or in vivo using recombinant expression systems. The invention utilizes RNA bacteriophage coat proteins, such as those from PP7, which self-assemble into icosahedral capsids. The PP7 coat protein is expressed as a single-chain dimer, with epitopic peptides inserted into the AB-loop for surface display.  

Examples of suitable RNA bacteriophages include PP7, Qβ, and AP205. These phages share structural similarities, including a highly conserved AB-loop region ideal for epitope presentation. The AB-loop forms a constrained β-turn that maintains inserted peptides in a stable conformation, enhancing immunogenicity.  

The invention further provides methods for determining structural similarity between coat polypeptides, such as sequence alignment and X-ray crystallography. Active coat polypeptides retain the ability to self-assemble into VLPs while displaying heterologous epitopes. Preferred epitopic peptides include AIP1S (YSTSDFIM) and truncations thereof, as well as peptides derived from S. aureus toxins like alpha-hemolysin (Hla).  

Transcription units for VLP production comprise a promoter, coat protein coding sequence, epitope insertion site, and transcription terminator. Suitable promoters include T7, lac, and tac promoters, which drive high-level expression in E. coli. VLPs are purified by size exclusion chromatography and characterized by SDS-PAGE, dynamic light scattering, and electron microscopy.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: VLP-AIPs as Vaccine Candidates  

VLPs displaying AIP1S (PP7-AIP1S) were produced in E. coli and purified to homogeneity. Vaccination of mice with PP7-AIP1S elicited high-titer antibodies recognizing native AIP1, as demonstrated by competitive ELISA. These antibodies specifically inhibited AIP1 binding to AgrC, suppressing agr signaling in vitro.  

### Additional Examples  

#### Example 2: Efficacy in a Murine SSTI Model  

Mice vaccinated with PP7-AIP1S were challenged with the MRSA strain USA300 LAC (agr type I). Compared to controls, vaccinated mice exhibited reduced abscess formation, dermonecrosis, and bacterial burden at the infection site. Cytokine analysis revealed lower levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNFα, IL-1α, IL-17), consistent with attenuated agr activity.  

#### Example 3: Inhibition of agr Signaling In Vivo  

Quantitative PCR and Western blot analysis confirmed reduced RNAIII transcription and Hla protein levels in PP7-AIP1S-vaccinated mice, demonstrating immune suppression of agr-regulated virulence.  

#### Example 4: Broad Applicability of VLP Platform  

The invention extends to other pathogens utilizing agr-like quorum-sensing systems, including Listeria monocytogenes, Enterococcus faecalis, and Clostridium difficile. VLPs displaying epitopes from these systems can be constructed analogously to PP7-AIP1S, offering a versatile vaccine platform.  

## DISCUSSION  

The invention addresses the critical need for non-antibiotic therapies against MRSA by targeting the agr quorum-sensing system. VLP-based vaccines elicit robust antibody responses against AIPs, neutralizing virulence regulation and enhancing host defense. This approach is particularly effective against agr type I isolates, which are predominant in invasive infections.  

The modular design of the VLP platform allows for incorporation of multiple epitopes, enabling broad-spectrum protection against diverse S. aureus strains. Furthermore, the proven safety and efficacy of VLP vaccines in humans (e.g., HPV vaccines) support the clinical potential of this invention.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides VLP-based vaccines that induce immune control of S. aureus virulence by targeting AIPs. Vaccination with PP7-AIP1S VLPs elicits antibodies that neutralize AIP1, suppresses agr signaling, and reduces infection severity in vivo. The platform is adaptable to other bacterial pathogens, offering a promising strategy to combat antibiotic resistance.  

### Methods  

#### Ethics Statement  

Animal studies were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) of the University of New Mexico Health Sciences Center (Animal Welfare Assurance number D16-00228) and conducted in accordance with the Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.  

#### Bacterial Strains and Growth Conditions  

The MRSA strain USA300 LAC was cultured in tryptic soy broth and prepared for infection studies as described.  

#### VLP Construction and Purification  

PP7-AIP1S VLPs were produced in E. coli C41 using the pDSP7K plasmid. VLPs were purified by size exclusion chromatography and characterized for purity and homogeneity.  

#### Mouse Immunizations  

BALB/c mice were immunized intramuscularly with PP7-AIP1S or control VLPs (10 µg/dose) at 0 and 4 weeks. Serum was collected at 2, 4, and 8 weeks post-boost for antibody analysis.  

#### ELISA  

Antibody binding to AIP1S was measured by ELISA using PP7-AIP1S-coated plates. Competitive ELISA confirmed specificity for native AIP1.  

#### Mouse Skin Infection Model  

Mice were challenged subcutaneously with 4 × 10^7 CFU USA300 LAC. Abscess area, dermonecrosis, weight loss, and bacterial burden were monitored for 6 days.  

#### Cytokine Analysis  

Cytokine levels in abscess homogenates were quantified by multiplex assay.  

#### RNA Isolation and qPCR  

RNAIII transcription was measured by qPCR using primers specific to S. aureus RNAIII and 16S rRNA.  

#### Hla Quantification  

Hla protein levels were assessed by Western blot using anti-Hla antibodies.  

#### Statistical Analysis  

Data were analyzed using GraphPad Prism with one-way ANOVA and appropriate post-hoc tests. Significance was set at p < 0.05.
