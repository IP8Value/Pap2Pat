# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for modulating cellular proliferation through regulation of cyclin D1 degradation. Cyclin D1 is a critical regulator of cell cycle progression that controls the G1 to S phase transition by forming complexes with cyclin-dependent kinases CDK4 and CDK6. Dysregulation of cyclin D1 expression and degradation is implicated in numerous epithelial cancers, making it an important therapeutic target.  

Cyclin D1 protein levels are tightly controlled through multiple mechanisms including transcriptional regulation, protein stability, and subcellular localization. The Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling cascade stimulates cyclin D1 transcription in response to mitogenic signals. Following synthesis, cyclin D1 associates with CDK4/6 and regulatory proteins p21 Cip1 or p27 Kip1 to form active kinase complexes that promote G1 progression.  

The mechanism of cyclin D1 ubiquitination and subsequent degradation has remained incompletely characterized. It is known that cyclin D1 undergoes polyubiquitination followed by proteasomal degradation, requiring phosphorylation at threonine residue 286 (Thr286) near its C-terminus. The cyclin D1 T286A mutant is resistant to ubiquitination and exhibits increased stability. While glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK3β) has been implicated in Thr286 phosphorylation, recent evidence questions its role in cyclin D1 stability regulation.  

Ubiquitin-mediated protein degradation involves three key enzymatic components: E1 ubiquitin activating enzymes, E2 ubiquitin conjugating enzymes, and E3 ubiquitin ligases that confer substrate specificity. The SCF (SKP1-CUL1-F-box protein) family of E3 ligases represents the best characterized group of ubiquitin ligases that regulate substrate ubiquitination in a phosphorylation-dependent manner. These complexes contain SKP1 as an adaptor protein, CUL1 as a scaffold, an F-box protein that recognizes phosphorylated substrates, and RBX1 that recruits E2 enzymes.  

A critical gap in understanding cyclin D1 regulation has been the identity of the specific E3 ligase responsible for its ubiquitination. While SKP2 has been proposed as a potential cyclin D1 E3 ligase, evidence suggests its role may be indirect or context-dependent. Furthermore, cyclin D1 does not accumulate in SKP2 knockout cells. The present invention addresses this unmet need by identifying FBXW8 as the specific E3 ligase that recognizes phosphorylated cyclin D1 and mediates its ubiquitination and degradation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for modulating cellular proliferation through regulation of cyclin D1 degradation. In particular aspects, the invention relates to detection of agents that modulate phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at Thr286 or its subsequent ubiquitination by FBXW8-containing E3 ligase complexes.  

The invention encompasses methods for controlling cell proliferation by modulating FBXW8 activity through various mechanisms including: altering transcription of FBXW8-encoding nucleic acids; modifying translation of FBXW8 mRNA; regulating activation of E3 ligase complexes containing FBXW8; controlling degradation of FBXW8 protein; or interfering with FBXW8 binding to phosphorylated cyclin D1.  

The invention further provides screening methods to identify test agents that affect cyclin D1 regulation. These include assays to detect effects on: interaction between FBXW8 and phosphorylated cyclin D1; binding affinity of FBXW8 for phosphorylated cyclin D1; ubiquitination of phosphorylated cyclin D1; total levels of phosphorylated cyclin D1; overall cyclin D1 protein levels; or amounts of ubiquitinated cyclin D1.  

Additional aspects of the invention include isolated polypeptide complexes comprising FBXW8, SKP1, CUL1 or CUL7, and cyclin D1, as well as reaction mixtures containing these components for use in screening assays. The invention also provides methods for treating proliferative disorders by modulating the FBXW8-cyclin D1 interaction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides compositions and methods for regulating cell proliferation through control of cyclin D1 degradation. The detailed description that follows describes various components of the invention, their production and use, and representative embodiments.  

### FBXW8 Protein  

FBXW8 is an F-box protein containing WD40 repeat domains that confer substrate specificity to SCF-type ubiquitin ligase complexes. The full-length human FBXW8 protein (GenBank Accession No. NM_153003) comprises 567 amino acids with characteristic F-box and WD40 repeat domains. The F-box domain mediates interaction with SKP1, while WD40 repeats recognize phosphorylated substrates.  

FBXW8 forms functional E3 ligase complexes by associating with either CUL1 (forming SCF^FBXW8) or CUL7 (forming SCF-like^FBXW8 complexes). These complexes specifically recognize cyclin D1 phosphorylated at Thr286, promoting its ubiquitination and degradation.  

### MAPK Protein  

Mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs), particularly ERK1 and ERK2, phosphorylate cyclin D1 at Thr286 through interaction with a conserved D-domain (amino acids 179-193) in cyclin D1. This phosphorylation creates a recognition motif for FBXW8 binding.  

### Cyclin D1  

Cyclin D1 (GenBank Accession No. NM_053056) is a 295 amino acid protein that regulates G1 phase progression. The invention particularly concerns cyclin D1 phosphorylated at Thr286, which undergoes FBXW8-mediated ubiquitination. Key embodiments include detection methods for phosphorylated cyclin D1 and reagents such as phosphorylation-specific antibodies.  

### Nucleic Acid Molecules and Expression Systems  

The invention provides nucleic acids encoding FBXW8, cyclin D1, and associated proteins for recombinant expression. Expression vectors incorporate regulatory elements such as promoters (CMV, EF1α), enhancers, and selection markers. Both viral (lentiviral, adenoviral) and non-viral delivery systems are encompassed.  

### Screening Assays  

Representative screening assays include:  
1) In vitro ubiquitination assays with purified components (E1, E2, E3, ubiquitin)  
2) Cell-based assays monitoring cyclin D1 phosphorylation/ubiquitination  
3) Binding assays measuring FBXW8-cyclin D1 interaction  
4) High-throughput screens using fluorescent or luminescent reporters  

### Therapeutic Applications  

The invention provides methods for treating cancers characterized by cyclin D1 dysregulation by:  
1) Inhibiting FBXW8 to stabilize cyclin D1 and block proliferation  
2) Enhancing FBXW8 activity to promote cyclin D1 degradation  
3) Disrupting MAPK-mediated cyclin D1 phosphorylation  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Cyclin D1 Destabilization in S Phase  

Synchronized HCT116 colon cancer cells showed dramatic cyclin D1 reduction during S phase (FIG. 2B), while normal cells maintained stable levels (FIG. 2A). Pulse-chase analysis confirmed cyclin D1 half-life decreased from 27.5 min in G1 to 11.8 min in S phase in cancer cells (FIG. 1D). Proteasome inhibition stabilized cyclin D1 specifically in S phase (FIG. 1E), demonstrating ubiquitin-mediated degradation.  

### Example 2: MAPK Phosphorylation of Cyclin D1  

In vitro kinase assays showed ERK2 phosphorylated wild-type cyclin D1 but not T286A mutant (FIG. 2B). Deletion of the D-domain (ΔD) abolished phosphorylation (FIG. 2C), confirming MAPK requires this docking site. Immunoprecipitation verified ERK2 binds cyclin D1 via the D-domain (FIG. 2D).  

### Example 3: FBXW8-Mediated Ubiquitination  

FBXW8 specifically associated with phosphorylated cyclin D1 (FIG. 3D). In vitro ubiquitination assays demonstrated FBXW8-SCF complexes polyubiquitinated cyclin D1 in a Thr286 phosphorylation-dependent manner (FIGS. 3E-G). Knockdown of FBXW8, CUL1 or CUL7 stabilized cyclin D1 (FIGS. 4D-F), confirming their role in degradation.  

### Example 4: Biological Significance  

FBXW8 knockdown reduced cell proliferation (FIG. 5A) by causing cytoplasmic accumulation of phosphorylated cyclin D1 and CDK4 sequestration (FIG. 5B). Expression of nuclear-localized T286A cyclin D1 rescued proliferation (FIGS. 5D-E), demonstrating the importance of FBXW8-mediated degradation.
