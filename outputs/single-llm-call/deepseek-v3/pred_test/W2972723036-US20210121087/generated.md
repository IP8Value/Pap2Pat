# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of medical diagnostics and monitoring, and more specifically to systems and methods for noninvasive estimation of intracranial pressure (ICP) using computational modeling of cerebral hemodynamics. The technology employs physiological signal processing and statistical modeling techniques to derive accurate ICP estimates from arterial blood pressure (ABP) and cerebral blood flow velocity (CBFV) measurements without requiring invasive intracranial monitoring procedures.  

## BACKGROUND  

Intracranial pressure (ICP) is a critical physiological parameter that reflects the pressure exerted by cerebrospinal fluid within the skull cavity. Elevated ICP presents serious health risks including cerebral ischemia, brain herniation, and permanent neurological damage if left untreated. Current clinical practice for ICP measurement predominantly relies on invasive techniques that require neurosurgical intervention, such as external ventricular drains or intraparenchymal microtransducer probes. These invasive methods carry substantial risks including infection rates between 5-10%, hemorrhage, and technical complications from device placement.  

Existing noninvasive ICP estimation approaches suffer from significant limitations in accuracy and clinical utility. Techniques based on transcranial Doppler ultrasound, optic nerve sheath diameter measurements, or tympanic membrane displacement lack the precision required for continuous monitoring in critical care settings. Model-based methods attempting to derive ICP from ABP and CBFV measurements have been hampered by physiological variability between patients and temporal misalignment of input waveforms. The inability to account for patient-specific cerebrovascular dynamics and unknown time offsets between ABP and CBFV signals has resulted in estimation errors exceeding clinically acceptable thresholds.  

There exists an unmet clinical need for a robust, accurate noninvasive ICP monitoring system capable of providing continuous measurements with precision comparable to invasive methods. Such a system would enable widespread ICP monitoring in patient populations where invasive procedures are contraindicated or unavailable, while reducing healthcare costs and complications associated with current invasive monitoring techniques.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a computer-implemented system for noninvasive intracranial pressure estimation comprising: a data acquisition subsystem configured to receive arterial blood pressure (ABP) and cerebral blood flow velocity (CBFV) measurements from a patient; a processing subsystem implementing a statistical model of cerebral hemodynamics; and an output subsystem configured to display estimated ICP values. The system operates through a two-stage computational process that first establishes a baseline ICP estimate and subsequently tracks dynamic changes in ICP.  

In the system embodiment, data acquisition involves obtaining continuous ABP measurements from a radial arterial line and CBFV measurements via transcranial Doppler ultrasound. The processing subsystem executes a model-based estimation routine that: computes an initial ICP value by applying a finite impulse response (FIR) filter model to the ABP and CBFV waveforms; determines time offsets between the ABP and CBFV signals to compensate for physiological delays; generates a likelihood distribution of ICP values based on prediction errors; and combines this likelihood with a prior probability distribution to compute a posterior ICP estimate. The system dynamically updates ICP estimates by implementing an autoregressive tracking model that filters changes between successive estimates while compensating for patient-specific biases.  

A software embodiment comprises processor-executable instructions that implement: signal conditioning modules for preprocessing ABP and CBFV waveforms; a parameter estimation module that solves the cerebral hemodynamics model across ranges of candidate ICP values and time offsets; a Bayesian statistical module that computes likelihood distributions and performs probabilistic fusion with prior distributions; and a tracking module that implements Kalman-filter like updating of ICP estimates. The software processes data in discrete time windows, typically 20 cardiac cycles in duration, to generate continuous ICP estimates with refresh rates sufficient for clinical monitoring applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

Conventional noninvasive ICP estimation techniques exhibit significant inaccuracies due to their inability to account for physiological variability between patients and temporal misalignment of input waveforms. Existing methods that attempt to map ABP and CBFV measurements to ICP values typically employ fixed time relationships between the signals, failing to accommodate patient-specific cerebrovascular transit times. This limitation introduces systematic errors in ICP estimates that render such methods unsuitable for clinical decision-making.  

The present invention overcomes these limitations through development of novel computational techniques that explicitly model physiological variability while maintaining computational efficiency for real-time implementation. A statistical model for ICP estimation forms the core innovation, comprising: a two-element Windkessel model of cerebrovascular dynamics; a parameter estimation routine that solves the model across ranges of candidate ICP values and time offsets; and a Bayesian statistical framework that combines model predictions with prior physiological knowledge.  

The statistical model computes initial ICP values by treating CBFV as the output of a two-tap FIR filter with cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP) as input, where CPP equals ABP minus ICP. The model parameters (filter taps α and β) are estimated for each candidate ICP value by minimizing CBFV prediction error in a least-squares sense across multiple time offsets between ABP and CBFV waveforms. These time offsets are constrained physiologically such that CBFV peaks lead corresponding ABP systolic peaks while diastolic points remain approximately aligned. The resulting prediction errors are transformed into a likelihood distribution L(I,d) over ICP values and time offsets, with high likelihood assigned to parameter pairs yielding small prediction errors.  

To compensate for patient-specific biases, the invention employs a two-stage estimation process. The baseline stage combines the likelihood distribution with a preset prior probability distribution Pr(I) representing typical ICP values encountered clinically. This prior distribution is formulated as a mixture of truncated Gaussians that models both normal and pathologically elevated ICP ranges. The posterior distribution Pr(I|p_a,q_v) is computed by multiplying the likelihood and prior distributions, with its median taken as the baseline ICP estimate.  

Subsequent tracking stages use uniform prior distributions to reduce dependence on initial assumptions, instead relying on an autoregressive (AR) model of ICP dynamics to filter changes between estimates. The AR model predicts ICP changes between time windows as ΔI[m] = γΔI[m-1] + v[m], where γ represents autoregulatory state and v[m] is white noise. Model-predicted changes are combined with observed changes through a Kalman-filter like process that weights each component by its inverse variance, yielding robust tracking of dynamic ICP variations.  

A critical innovation involves handling waveform misalignment through explicit modeling of time offsets as parameters in the statistical framework. For each estimation window, the invention scans time offsets within physiologically plausible ranges, typically ±25 ms around an initial coarse alignment. This compensates for unknown physiological delays between ABP and CBFV measurements while maintaining computational tractability through constrained parameter spaces.  

The technology further provides for ICP pulse pressure estimation by applying the model parameters to reconstruct ICP waveforms in the frequency domain. Mean-subtracted ABP and CBFV wavelets are decomposed via discrete Fourier transform, with model-derived transfer functions used to estimate ICP harmonic components. This enables noninvasive assessment of ICP pulsatility without requiring pre-trained models or additional instrumentation.  

Embodiments of the technology include a computer system comprising: one or more processors; memory storing executable instructions; input interfaces for ABP and CBFV data acquisition; and output interfaces for displaying ICP estimates. The system implements a data processing pipeline comprising: signal conditioning stages for time alignment, resampling, and noise removal; model parameter estimation routines; Bayesian statistical modules; and tracking filters. Results from clinical validation studies demonstrate estimation accuracy with mean error 0.6 mmHg and limits of agreement -6.6 to 7.7 mmHg relative to invasive measurements, comparable to variability between different invasive monitoring modalities.  

The computer-readable storage medium embodiment comprises instructions that, when executed: receive ABP and CBFV time series data; partition the data into analysis windows; solve the cerebral hemodynamics model across parameter spaces; compute likelihood distributions; perform Bayesian updating with prior distributions; implement the AR tracking model; and output continuous ICP estimates. The medium may further store calibration parameters, physiological constraints, and user interface components for clinical deployment.  

In operation, the method involves: acquiring ABP and CBFV signals from a patient; preprocessing the signals to remove noise and artifacts; estimating model parameters for ranges of candidate ICP values and time offsets; computing likelihood distributions from prediction errors; determining posterior distributions through Bayesian updating; establishing baseline ICP estimates by averaging multiple window estimates; and tracking ICP changes through the AR model filter. The complete processing pipeline executes with sufficient speed to provide real-time monitoring capabilities.  

The technology's advantages over prior approaches include: explicit handling of physiological time delays through parameterized offset modeling; incorporation of prior physiological knowledge via interpretable probability distributions; computational efficiency enabling real-time implementation; and robust tracking of dynamic ICP changes through autoregressive filtering. Clinical validation demonstrates superior accuracy compared to existing noninvasive methods, with performance approaching that of invasive gold-standard techniques while eliminating associated risks.  

Potential applications extend beyond critical care monitoring to include: screening for intracranial hypertension in emergency departments; intraoperative cerebral perfusion monitoring during neurosurgical procedures; and management of hydrocephalus in outpatient settings. The technology's noninvasive nature enables widespread deployment across healthcare settings where invasive monitoring is impractical or unavailable, potentially improving outcomes for diverse patient populations at risk of intracranial hypertension.
