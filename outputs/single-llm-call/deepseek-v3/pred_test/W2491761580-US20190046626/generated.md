# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to recombinant expression of Chlamydia trachomatis major outer membrane protein (MOMP) antigens. More particularly, the invention provides methods for producing recombinant MOMP (rMOMP) through optimized expression in Escherichia coli outer membranes, compositions comprising such recombinant antigens, and their use in vaccines for the treatment and prevention of Chlamydia infections. The invention further encompasses codon-harmonized nucleic acid sequences encoding MOMP and derivatives thereof, expression vectors containing these sequences, and methods of using the recombinant antigens to elicit protective immune responses against Chlamydia infections.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Chlamydia trachomatis is an obligate intracellular Gram-negative bacterium responsible for significant human pathologies. Different strains of C. trachomatis are classified into multiple serovars based on serological differences in the major outer membrane protein (MOMP). Serovars A, B, Ba, and C cause ocular trachoma leading to conjunctivitis, conjunctival scarring, and corneal scarring. Serovars L1, L2 and L3 cause lymphogranuloma venereum characterized by submucosa and lymph-node invasion with necrotizing granulomas and fibrosis. Serovars D through K cause oculogenital diseases including cervicitis, urethritis, endometritis, pelvic inflammatory disease, tubal infertility, ectopic pregnancy, neonatal conjunctivitis and infant pneumonia.  

Chlamydia infection represents one of the most prevalent sexually transmitted diseases worldwide, imposing substantial medical and economic burdens. Current treatment relies on antibiotics, but prevention through vaccination remains an unmet medical need. Early vaccine candidates using formalin-inactivated whole Chlamydia provided only short-lived protection. The subunit vaccine approach has emerged as a promising alternative strategy.  

MOMP constitutes approximately 60% of the outer membrane mass of Chlamydia elementary bodies (EBs), the infectious form of the bacterium. Antibodies against MOMP can neutralize EB infectivity, and T cell epitopes have been identified within MOMP. Native MOMP purified from EBs has demonstrated protective efficacy in animal models. However, native antigen purification presents substantial challenges for commercial-scale manufacturing.  

Recombinant expression of MOMP offers potential advantages but has faced significant technical hurdles. Cytoplasmic expression typically results in inclusion body formation, requiring complex denaturation and refolding procedures that often yield improperly folded protein with reduced immunogenicity. Previous attempts at outer membrane expression in E. coli have encountered problems with low yields and host cell toxicity. There remains a critical need for improved methods to produce properly folded, immunogenic recombinant MOMP suitable for vaccine development.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods for recombinant expression of Chlamydia MOMP in E. coli outer membranes through a combination of molecular biology and fermentation optimizations. The methods employ codon harmonization to match codon usage frequencies between the native Chlamydia host and E. coli expression system. This approach preserves natural translation kinetics to facilitate proper protein folding and membrane insertion.  

The invention utilizes expression vectors with moderate-strength promoters and low copy numbers to balance protein production with host cell viability. Various leader sequences are evaluated for optimal targeting of recombinant MOMP to the outer membrane. Cell culture conditions including medium composition, induction temperature, induction cell density, and induction duration are systematically optimized to maximize surface expression while minimizing toxicity.  

Recombinant MOMP produced by these methods maintains native-like β-strand secondary structure and elicits antibody responses comparable to native MOMP in animal models. The antigens demonstrate protective efficacy against Chlamydia challenge, reducing bacterial burden similarly to native antigen controls. The methods enable scalable production of properly folded, immunogenic MOMP vaccine candidates.  

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the recombinant MOMP antigens, optionally combined with adjuvants such as saponin-based or aluminum-based formulations. These compositions are useful for preventing or treating Chlamydia infections through various administration routes. Nucleic acid molecules encoding codon-harmonized MOMP sequences and derivatives are also provided, along with expression vectors and transformed host cells for their production.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides improved methods for recombinant expression of Chlamydia major outer membrane protein (MOMP) in E. coli outer membranes. MOMP represents an attractive vaccine target due to its surface exposure, abundance in elementary bodies, and presence of neutralizing antibody epitopes and T cell recognition sites. However, previous attempts at recombinant production have faced challenges with improper folding, low yields, and host cell toxicity.  

Native purification of MOMP from Chlamydia-infected cells presents substantial manufacturing challenges. The invention addresses these limitations through optimized heterologous expression in E. coli. Key innovations include codon harmonization to preserve natural translation kinetics, selection of expression systems that balance protein production with cell viability, and fermentation conditions that maximize surface localization of properly folded antigen.  

### Codon Optimization Strategies  

The invention employs two complementary approaches to optimize MOMP coding sequences for recombinant expression: codon harmonization and codon optimization. Codon harmonization matches codon usage frequencies between the native Chlamydia host and E. coli expression system, preserving rare codons that may facilitate proper folding. This contrasts with conventional codon optimization which typically eliminates rare codons in favor of host-preferred sequences.  

For codon harmonization, the frequency of each codon in Chlamydia is compared to corresponding frequencies in E. coli. Rare codons used in the native host are maintained where they may serve important functions in translation pausing. For remaining residues, the E. coli codon with closest frequency to the native sequence is selected. This strategy yields nucleic acid sequences that approximate native translation kinetics while remaining compatible with the expression host's translational machinery.  

The invention further provides fully codon-optimized sequences where every codon is replaced with the most frequently used E. coli counterpart. Comparative evaluation demonstrates that codon-harmonized sequences provide superior expression levels and proper membrane localization compared to fully optimized sequences.  

### Expression System Optimization  

The invention provides a panel of expression vectors varying in promoter strength and copy number to identify optimal conditions for MOMP surface expression. Strong promoters and high-copy vectors lead to excessive transcription that overwhelms the host's membrane protein processing capacity. The invention identifies that moderate promoters combined with low-copy vectors provide the best balance of expression level and proper localization.  

Various leader sequences are evaluated for efficient targeting of recombinant MOMP to the outer membrane. Native Chlamydia leader sequences prove less effective than heterologous leaders from other Gram-negative bacteria. Particularly effective leaders include those from E. coli OmpA and OmpP, Shigella flexneri SopA, Salmonella enterica PgtE, Yersinia pestis Pla, and Erwinia carotovora PelB. The PelB leader demonstrates particularly robust performance across multiple MOMP serovars.  

### Fermentation Conditions  

The invention systematically optimizes culture conditions to maximize recombinant MOMP surface expression while maintaining cell viability. Key parameters include:  

Medium composition - Chemically defined and rich media are evaluated, with Cinnabar medium providing superior results. Autoinduction media prove less effective than IPTG-induced systems for this application.  

Induction temperature - Temperatures between 16°C and 37°C are tested, with 30°C providing optimal balance of expression level and cell health. Lower temperatures reduce expression while higher temperatures increase cell fragility.  

Induction cell density - Induction at mid-log phase (OD590 ~0.5) yields highest expression levels. Induction at higher densities results in dramatically reduced surface expression.  

Induction duration - Short inductions (4-6 hours) provide optimal results, while longer inductions (16 hours) lead to complete loss of surface expression. This suggests cumulative toxicity from membrane protein overexpression.  

IPTG concentration - Concentrations between 0.1 mM and 1 mM provide comparable expression levels, with 0.4 mM selected as standard condition.  

### Protein Purification  

The invention provides methods for purifying recombinant MOMP from E. coli outer membranes. Cell disruption is performed by microfluidization followed by differential centrifugation to isolate membrane fractions. Sequential detergent treatments selectively extract recombinant MOMP while removing contaminating host proteins:  

1) Triton X-100 removes inner membrane components  
2) β-octyl-glucoside removes non-MOMP outer membrane proteins  
3) Sarkosyl with DTT efficiently extracts recombinant MOMP  

Further purification is achieved through size exclusion chromatography and ion exchange chromatography. The final product demonstrates >70% purity by mass spectrometry with undetectable endotoxin levels.  

### Structural and Immunological Characterization  

Circular dichroism spectroscopy confirms that recombinant MOMP maintains native-like β-strand secondary structure. The antigen migrates similarly to native MOMP by SDS-PAGE, with some SDS-resistant oligomeric forms.  

In mouse immunization studies, recombinant MOMP elicits antibody titers against native elementary bodies comparable to native MOMP controls. The antibody subclass profile indicates balanced Th1/Th2 responses. Most importantly, vaccination with recombinant MOMP provides significant protection against intravaginal Chlamydia challenge, reducing bacterial burden to levels similar to native antigen controls.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions  

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising recombinant MOMP antigens. These compositions may include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers such as sterile water, buffers (pH 7-8), saline, or other standard vaccine formulations. The antigens may be formulated as sterile injectable solutions, dispersions, or lyophilized powders for reconstitution.  

The compositions may include additional Chlamydia antigens, either MOMP variants from different serovars or other immunogenic proteins. Adjuvants may be incorporated to enhance immune responses, including saponin-based formulations (e.g., ISCOMs), aluminum salts (e.g., aluminum hydroxide), or combinations thereof.  

Dosage forms are designed to provide immunologically effective amounts of antigen, typically in the range of 1-100 μg per dose for human administration. Exact dosing is determined through standard toxicity and efficacy studies to establish the therapeutic index.  

### Methods of Use  

The recombinant MOMP antigens are useful for preventing or treating Chlamydia infections. Administration may occur via various routes including intramuscular, subcutaneous, intradermal, or mucosal delivery. The antigens may be incorporated into medical devices such as microneedle arrays or controlled-release formulations.  

Vaccination schedules may include prime-boost regimens, with initial immunization followed by one or more boosts to enhance and maintain protective immunity. The compositions are particularly suited for prevention of sexually transmitted Chlamydia infections but may also find application in ocular or respiratory forms of disease.  

### Codon-Harmonized MOMP Nucleotide Sequences  

The invention provides codon-harmonized nucleic acid molecules encoding Chlamydia MOMP and derivatives. These sequences include:  

1) Full-length MOMP from C. trachomatis serovars D and E  
2) Full-length MOMP from C. muridarum  
3) MOMP derivatives with amino acid substitutions, deletions or additions  
4) Fusion proteins combining MOMP with additional polypeptides  

The sequences incorporate optimized leader peptides for efficient outer membrane targeting. Various promoter options are provided to allow tuning of expression levels. The nucleic acid molecules may be incorporated into expression cassettes or vectors for recombinant production.  

## EXAMPLE 1  

### Expression of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP  

This example demonstrates the optimization of recombinant MOMP expression in E. coli outer membranes.  

Codon harmonization was performed by comparing codon usage frequencies between Chlamydia trachomatis and E. coli. For each amino acid, the E. coli codon with closest frequency to the native Chlamydia usage was selected, preserving rare codons where they occurred in the native sequence. This harmonized sequence provided approximately 2-fold higher surface expression compared to fully codon-optimized sequences when evaluated by whole-cell flow cytometry.  

A panel of expression vectors was evaluated, varying in promoter strength and copy number. The pAVE029 vector with λPL moderate-strength promoter and low copy number provided optimal results, yielding high surface expression without excessive cellular toxicity. Strong promoters (e.g., T7) or high-copy vectors led to reduced surface localization.  

Various leader sequences were tested for efficient outer membrane targeting. The PelB leader from Erwinia carotovora demonstrated superior performance across multiple MOMP serovars, providing robust surface expression as measured by antibody binding assays. Native Chlamydia leader sequences proved less effective for heterologous expression.  

Culture conditions were systematically optimized. Induction at 30°C in Cinnabar medium when cells reached mid-log phase (OD590 ~0.5) provided highest expression levels. Induction for 4 hours with 0.4 mM IPTG balanced protein production with cell viability. These optimized conditions enabled successful outer membrane expression of MOMP from C. trachomatis serovars D and E, as well as C. muridarum.  

## EXAMPLE 2  

### Purification of Recombinant Chlamydia MOMP  

This example describes the purification of recombinant MOMP from E. coli outer membranes.  

Cells expressing recombinant MOMP were harvested by centrifugation and disrupted by microfluidization in 50 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0 buffer with protease inhibitors. Undisrupted cells were removed by low-speed centrifugation (9,700 × g, 15 min), and membranes were pelleted by high-speed centrifugation (23,800 × g, 90 min).  

The membrane fraction was washed with high-salt buffer (1 M NaCl, 0.05% Tween 20) to remove peripheral proteins, followed by treatment with 1% Triton X-100 to solubilize inner membranes. Subsequent extraction with 3% β-octyl-glucoside removed non-MOMP outer membrane proteins.  

Recombinant MOMP was finally extracted using 1% sarkosyl with 5 mM DTT. The solubilized protein was purified by size exclusion chromatography (Sephacryl S300) in 10 mM HEPES pH 7.3, 150 mM NaCl, 0.1% zwittergent 3-14, followed by anion exchange chromatography (Q Sepharose).  

The final product demonstrated >70% purity by mass spectrometry, with yield of approximately 6 mg per liter of culture. Circular dichroism spectroscopy confirmed β-strand secondary structure characteristic of native MOMP. The purified protein was free of detectable endotoxin contamination.  

## EXAMPLE 3  

### Mouse Immunogenicity and Challenge Study  

This example evaluates the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of recombinant MOMP in a mouse model.  

Female C57BL/6 mice were immunized subcutaneously three times at 2-week intervals with 10 μg of either recombinant or native MOMP, formulated with CpG and Montanide ISA 720 adjuvants. Control groups received adjuvant alone or live elementary bodies.  

Serum samples collected two weeks after final immunization were analyzed by ELISA using C. trachomatis serovar D EBs as coating antigen. Recombinant MOMP elicited IgG titers comparable to native antigen (no significant difference by ANOVA). Antibody subclass analysis revealed balanced IgG1/IgG2c ratios indicating mixed Th1/Th2 responses.  

Mice were challenged intravaginally with 10^5 C. trachomatis serovar D EBs. Vaginal swabs collected over 21 days post-challenge were analyzed by quantitative PCR. Both recombinant and native MOMP immunization significantly reduced bacterial burden compared to adjuvant controls (p<0.01), with no significant difference between the two antigen groups.  

These results demonstrate that outer membrane-expressed recombinant MOMP maintains the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of native antigen, validating the expression and purification methods of the invention.
