# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics and genetic screening, specifically to methods, compositions, and kits for detecting deletions in the Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) gene. The invention provides an improved multiplex polymerase-based nucleic acid amplification assay for identifying DMD gene exons associated with exon-skipping therapies.  

## BACKGROUND  

Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD) is a severe X-linked recessive disorder caused by mutations in the DMD gene, leading to the absence of functional dystrophin protein. The disease primarily affects males, resulting in progressive muscle degeneration and early mortality. Approximately 60–70% of DMD cases are caused by deletions of one or more exons in the DMD gene.  

Current treatments for DMD include corticosteroids, physical therapy, and emerging exon-skipping therapies such as eteplirsen (Exondys 51) and golodirsen (Vyondys 53). Exon-skipping therapies aim to restore the reading frame of the DMD gene by skipping specific exons, thereby producing a truncated but partially functional dystrophin protein. However, existing screening methods for DMD, such as creatine kinase (CK) assays, suffer from low specificity and sensitivity, leading to delayed diagnosis and missed therapeutic opportunities.  

Gene therapy approaches, including microdystrophin delivery via adeno-associated virus (AAV) vectors, are under investigation but face challenges in dosing and long-term efficacy. Early diagnosis is critical for maximizing the benefits of exon-skipping therapies, yet current newborn screening (NBS) programs lack reliable genetic assays for detecting treatable DMD mutations.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention addresses the limitations of existing DMD screening methods by providing a highly sensitive and specific multiplex polymerase-based nucleic acid amplification assay for detecting deletions in key DMD exons (44, 46, 50, 52, and 54). These exons are strategically selected because their deletions are amenable to exon-skipping therapies targeting exons 45, 51, and 53.  

The invention includes amplification primer pairs and oligonucleotide probes designed for real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR), enabling simultaneous detection of multiple exons in a single reaction. The method is optimized for use with dried blood spots (DBS), facilitating high-throughput newborn screening.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
- High sensitivity and specificity (100% in validation studies).  
- Early identification of treatable DMD cases, enabling timely intervention.  
- Cost-effective and scalable for population-wide screening.  
- Compatibility with existing NBS infrastructure.  

The invention further encompasses compositions, kits, and methods for diagnosing and treating DMD using exon-skipping therapies based on the detected mutations.

### Technical Background  

The DMD gene spans approximately 2.4 Mb and consists of 79 exons. Mutations leading to DMD typically disrupt the reading frame, resulting in non-functional dystrophin. Exon-skipping therapies restore the reading frame by excluding specific exons during mRNA splicing.  

### Methods for Analyzing the DMD Gene  

The invention provides a method for analyzing the DMD gene in a subject, comprising:  
1. Obtaining a biological sample (e.g., dried blood spot).  
2. Extracting nucleic acids.  
3. Performing multiplex PCR using primer pairs specific to exons 44, 46, 50, 52, and 54.  
4. Detecting amplification products using fluorescent probes.
