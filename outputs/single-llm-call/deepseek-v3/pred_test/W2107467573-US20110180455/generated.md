# DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to an improved process for producing middle distillates from Fischer-Tropsch synthesis products using a novel hydrocracking and hydroisomerisation catalyst system. The invention provides a method for efficiently converting heavy Fischer-Tropsch fractions into high-quality middle distillate products with improved yield and selectivity.  

## PRIOR ART  

The Fischer-Tropsch process is a well-known method for producing hydrocarbons from synthesis gas. While this process can generate a range of hydrocarbon products, the direct production of middle distillates through Fischer-Tropsch synthesis alone is limited by the Anderson-Flory-Schultz polymerization kinetics, typically yielding no more than about 40% middle distillates.  

Existing methods for upgrading Fischer-Tropsch products to middle distillates involve catalytic hydrocracking of the heavier fractions. Conventional hydrocracking catalysts typically comprise a hydrogenation/dehydrogenation function provided by metals or metal sulfides combined with an acidic function of the Brönsted type. These systems often suffer from limitations including excessive secondary cracking, poor isomerization selectivity, and catalyst deactivation.  

Various feedstocks have been used for Fischer-Tropsch synthesis, including those derived from renewable sources, oils and fats, and coal. The processing of these feedstocks presents unique challenges due to variations in composition and the presence of oxygenated compounds that can affect catalyst performance.  

Hydroisomerisation catalytic reactions are central to the upgrading of Fischer-Tropsch products. These reactions typically employ bifunctional catalysts containing both metal sites for hydrogenation/dehydrogenation and acid sites for isomerization. The balance between the acid function and hydrogenating function is critical for optimal performance, with an imbalance leading to either insufficient conversion or excessive cracking.  

Conventional catalysts often use silica-alumina-based catalytic systems, which while effective for some applications, suffer from limitations in acidity control and pore structure. Zeolite-based catalytic systems have been developed to address some of these limitations, offering more uniform pore structures and controllable acidity. However, these systems can still exhibit excessive cracking and poor isomerization selectivity for certain feedstocks.  

## Subject-Matter of the Invention  

The present invention provides a novel process for producing middle distillates from Fischer-Tropsch synthesis products using a specialized hydrocracking/hydroisomerisation catalyst. The catalyst comprises a unique combination of hydro-dehydrogenating metals supported on a substrate containing an IZM-2-type crystallised solid within an oxide-type amorphous or poorly crystallised porous inorganic binder.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention relates to a process for converting heavy Fischer-Tropsch fractions into middle distillates through hydrocracking and hydroisomerisation reactions. The process operates under specific conditions including temperatures between 200-400°C, pressures of 20-100 bar, and hydrogen to hydrocarbon ratios of 200-2000 Nl/l.  

The chemical composition of the catalyst can be represented by the general formula:  
(XO₂)a:(Y₂O₃)b:(M₂/nO)c:(R)d:(H₂O)e  
where X is a tetravalent element, Y is a trivalent element, M is an alkali or alkaline earth metal, R is an organic structuring agent, and a, b, c, d, e represent molar ratios with specific ranges as described below.  

### The Hydrocracking and Hydroisomerisation Catalyst  

The hydrocracking and hydroisomerisation catalyst of the invention comprises several key components providing distinct functions. The hydro-dehydrogenating function is provided by metals selected from Group VIII and/or Group VIB of the periodic table. Preferred Group VIII metals include noble metals such as platinum and palladium, as well as non-noble metals including nickel and cobalt. Preferred Group VIB metals include molybdenum and tungsten.  

The metal content ranges from 0.1-10 wt% for noble metals and 1-30 wt% for non-noble metals, calculated as oxide equivalents. Particularly effective combinations include platinum with palladium or nickel with molybdenum. The metals are deposited on a substrate comprising an IZM-2-type crystallised solid mixed with an oxide-type amorphous or poorly crystallised porous inorganic binder.  

The IZM-2 solid content ranges from 10-90 wt% of the catalyst composition. This crystallised solid has a unique X-ray diffraction pattern characterized by specific dhkl values and relative intensities. The crystalline structure exhibits a three-dimensional pore system with pore sizes between 0.5-0.7 nm.  

The chemical composition of the IZM-2 solid in its crude synthesis form includes XO₂ (where X is preferably silicon), Y₂O₃ (where Y is preferably aluminium), M₂/nO (where M is preferably sodium), organic structuring agent R, and water. The molar ratios are: a=1, b=0.001-0.5, c=0.001-0.5, d=0.001-1, e=0-20.  

The preparation of the IZM-2 solid involves forming a reaction mixture containing sources of X, Y, and M elements, hydroxide ions, and the organic structuring agent. Hydrothermal treatment is conducted at 100-200°C for 1-10 days, optionally using seeds to promote crystallization. The solid is then filtered, washed, dried, and calcined at 400-600°C.  

The catalyst substrate is prepared by mixing the IZM-2 solid with an alumina source, typically fine or ultrafine boehmites. The mixture is shaped by extrusion and calcined. Post-synthesis treatments may include hydrothermal treatment, impregnation, or autoclaving to modify the properties.  

The hydro-dehydrogenating function is introduced by impregnating the shaped substrate with solutions containing the metal precursors. Group VIB metals are typically introduced first, followed by Group VIII metals. Promoter elements such as boron, silicon, or phosphorus may be added to modify the catalyst properties.  

### The Embodiments According to the Invention  

The invention encompasses several embodiments for producing middle distillates from Fischer-Tropsch synthesis products.  

In a first embodiment, the process comprises:  
(a) fractionating the Fischer-Tropsch effluent to separate light and heavy fractions;  
(b) hydrotreating the heavy fraction using a catalyst comprising Group VIB and VIII metals on an alumina support;  
(c) fractionating the hydrotreated effluent into light, intermediate, and heavy fractions;  
(d) passing the intermediate fraction over a hydroisomerising catalyst containing a noble metal on a zeolite support;  
(e) passing the heavy fraction over the hydrocracking/hydroisomerisation catalyst of the invention;  
(f) distilling the products to separate middle distillates and recycling the residual fraction.  

The hydrotreatment in step (b) is conducted at 300-400°C, 30-100 bar, with a hydrogen flow rate of 300-1000 Nl/l. The hydroisomerisation in step (d) uses temperatures of 250-350°C and pressures of 20-80 bar. The hydrocracking/hydroisomerisation in step (e) operates at 300-380°C and 30-90 bar.  

A second embodiment involves:  
(a) fractionating the Fischer-Tropsch feedstock;  
(b) hydrotreating the entire feedstock;  
(c) removing water from the hydrotreated product;  
(d) hydroisomerising and hydrocracking using the catalyst of the invention;  
(e) distilling to recover middle distillates and recycling the heavy fraction.  

Additional embodiments allow for variations in fractionation schemes, operating conditions, and recycling strategies to optimize product yields and properties. The process demonstrates particular flexibility in handling different Fischer-Tropsch feedstocks while maintaining high middle distillate yields.  

## The Products Obtained  

The process yields middle distillate products with excellent cold flow properties, high cetane numbers, and low sulfur content. The products typically include kerosene and diesel fractions with pour points below -10°C and cetane numbers above 70.  

### Example 1  

Catalyst C1 was prepared by mixing IZM-2 solid (60 wt%) with alumina binder. The mixture was extruded, dried at 120°C, and calcined at 550°C. Platinum (0.5 wt%) and palladium (0.3 wt%) were deposited by impregnation using aqueous solutions of tetraammineplatinum nitrate and palladium nitrate. The catalyst was dried and calcined at 400°C.  

### Example 2  

1,6-bis(methylpiperidinium)hexane dibromide was prepared as the organic structuring agent. IZM-2 solid was synthesized by hydrothermal treatment of a gel containing this agent, sodium aluminate, and colloidal silica. The solid was ion-exchanged to ammonium form and calcined. The substrate was prepared by mixing 70 wt% IZM-2 with alumina, extruding, and calcining. Platinum (0.6 wt%) was deposited by impregnation. The final catalyst showed excellent metal dispersion with a distribution coefficient above 0.9.  

### Example 3  

A Fischer-Tropsch feedstock was separated into light and heavy fractions. The heavy fraction was hydrotreated using catalyst C1 at 340°C and 60 bar. After water removal, the 370°C+ fraction was processed over hydrocracking catalyst C4 (containing 0.5 wt% Pt on IZM-2/alumina) at 360°C and 70 bar. The products were distilled to yield 78% middle distillates with excellent cold flow properties.  

The invention provides significant advantages over prior art processes, including higher middle distillate yields, improved product quality, and longer catalyst life. The unique catalyst composition enables efficient conversion of Fischer-Tropsch products while minimizing undesirable side reactions.
