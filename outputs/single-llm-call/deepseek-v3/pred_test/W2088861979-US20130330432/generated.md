# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of metabolic disorder diseases, particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM), a chronic condition characterized by hyperglycemia resulting from defects in insulin secretion and insulin resistance. In T2DM, peripheral tissues such as liver, muscle, and adipose tissues exhibit reduced sensitivity to insulin, leading to symptoms such as increased thirst, frequent urination, and elevated ketone levels. Prolonged insulin resistance can result in chronic hyperglycemia and hyperlipidemia, which may contribute to severe complications including cardiovascular disease, nephropathy, retinopathy, and neuropathy.  

Current treatments for T2DM include pharmacological agents such as sulfonylureas, biguanides, thiazolidinediones, meglitinides, and α-glucosidase inhibitors. However, these therapies often exhibit adverse side effects, including hypoglycemia, weight gain, gastrointestinal disturbances, and hepatotoxicity. Additionally, many antidiabetic drugs have limited mechanisms of action, necessitating combination therapies to achieve optimal glycemic control. Despite the availability of these treatments, the global prevalence of T2DM continues to rise, highlighting the need for safer and more effective therapeutic options.  

Ethnobotanical compounds have emerged as promising candidates for antidiabetic therapy due to their diverse bioactive properties and reduced side effects. Among these, Curculigo latifolia (C. latifolia), a plant native to Malaysia, has demonstrated significant potential in modulating glucose and lipid metabolism. The present invention focuses on the therapeutic application of C. latifolia extracts in the management of metabolic disorders, particularly T2DM.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for treating metabolic disorders, particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus, using extracts derived from Curculigo latifolia (C. latifolia). The invention encompasses the preparation and administration of C. latifolia fruit, leaf, and root extracts, which have been shown to ameliorate hyperglycemia, enhance insulin sensitivity, and improve lipid profiles in diabetic models. The extracts exhibit multiple mechanisms of action, including the upregulation of genes involved in glucose uptake (e.g., GLUT4, IRS-1, IGF-1) and lipid metabolism (e.g., PPARα, PPARγ, LPL). Additionally, the invention includes methods for assessing the efficacy of C. latifolia extracts through in vitro and in vivo studies, demonstrating their potential as a safe and effective alternative to conventional antidiabetic therapies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The invention provides a method for ameliorating blood glucose levels in subjects with metabolic disorders, particularly type 2 diabetes mellitus, through the administration of Curculigo latifolia (C. latifolia) extracts. The extracts may be derived from the fruit, leaves, or roots of the plant and are prepared using aqueous extraction followed by lyophilization. The preferred method of administration includes oral delivery in doses ranging from 50 mg/kg to 200 mg/kg body weight, with optimal efficacy observed at higher concentrations.  

In vitro studies have demonstrated the cytotoxicity and glucose-modulating effects of C. latifolia extracts on adipocyte (3T3-L1) and myotube (L6) cell lines. The extracts enhance insulin secretion and glucose uptake activity, suggesting insulin-sensitizing and insulin-mimetic properties. Furthermore, the extracts increase adiponectin secretion, a key adipokine involved in glucose and lipid metabolism.  

In vivo studies utilizing a high-fat diet (HFD) and streptozotocin (STZ)-induced diabetic rat model have confirmed the antidiabetic and hypolipidemic effects of C. latifolia extracts. Treatment with the extracts significantly reduced fasting blood glucose levels, improved oral glucose tolerance, and normalized lipid profiles, including reductions in total cholesterol, triglycerides, and LDL, as well as increases in HDL. These effects were accompanied by upregulation of genes involved in glucose metabolism (IRS-1, IGF-1, GLUT4) and lipid metabolism (PPARα, PPARγ, LPL, adiponectin receptors).  

The invention further encompasses methods for monitoring therapeutic efficacy, including biochemical assays for glucose, insulin, adiponectin, and lipid parameters, as well as gene expression analyses to assess metabolic pathway modulation.  

### Example 1  

The preparation of Curculigo latifolia extracts involves the collection and processing of fruit, leaves, and roots. Fresh fruits are mashed and extracted with distilled water, while dried roots are ground into powder and similarly extracted. The aqueous extracts are filtered, lyophilized, and stored at -80°C until use. For therapeutic applications, fruit and root extracts are combined at a 1:1 ratio to enhance bioactive potency.  

### Example 2  

In vitro cytotoxicity and glucose-modulating effects were assessed using 3T3-L1 adipocytes and L6 myotubes. Cell viability was determined via MTT assay, confirming non-toxicity at therapeutic concentrations. C. latifolia extracts significantly increased glucose uptake in both cell lines under basal and insulin-stimulated conditions, indicating dual mechanisms of action involving insulin sensitization and direct glucose uptake stimulation.  

### Example 3  

Insulin secretion assays were conducted using pancreatic β-cell lines (INS-1). C. latifolia extracts enhanced insulin secretion in a dose-dependent manner, suggesting β-cell protective and stimulatory effects. This activity was correlated with reduced oxidative stress, as evidenced by decreased reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation in STZ-treated cells.  

### Example 4  

Glucose uptake activity was quantified using radiolabeled 2-deoxyglucose in differentiated 3T3-L1 adipocytes and L6 myotubes. C. latifolia extracts significantly increased glucose uptake, with maximal effects observed at 200 µg/mL. The extracts also upregulated GLUT4 translocation to the plasma membrane, as confirmed by immunofluorescence microscopy.  

### Example 5  

Adiponectin secretion was measured in 3T3-L1 adipocytes using ELISA. C. latifolia extracts increased adiponectin levels by up to 56%, correlating with improved insulin sensitivity and lipid metabolism in diabetic models.  

### Example 6  

In vivo efficacy was evaluated in Sprague-Dawley rats rendered diabetic via HFD and low-dose STZ. Diabetic rats were treated with C. latifolia extracts (50, 100, or 200 mg/kg) or glibenclamide (10 mg/kg) for four weeks. The extracts significantly reduced fasting blood glucose (64.5% reduction at 200 mg/kg), improved lipid profiles, and upregulated metabolic genes (PPARγ, GLUT4, AdipoR1/2) in adipose and muscle tissues. Histopathological analysis confirmed the absence of hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity, supporting the safety profile of the extracts.
