# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present disclosure relates generally to the field of cancer diagnosis, and more particularly to methods and systems for detecting cancerous involvement in lymph nodes through real-time pH measurement of lymphatic fluid. The invention provides a rapid, non-invasive diagnostic approach that overcomes limitations of conventional lymph node assessment techniques such as frozen section pathology. By leveraging metabolic changes in cancer cells that alter the pH microenvironment of affected lymph nodes, the disclosed method enables accurate intraoperative detection of metastatic lymph nodes with high sensitivity and specificity.  

## BACKGROUND  

The lymphatic system comprises a network of vessels and nodes that transport lymphatic fluid throughout the body. Lymph nodes serve as critical filtration points where immune cells identify and eliminate pathogens and abnormal cells. Sentinel lymph nodes (SLNs) represent the first drainage sites for lymphatic fluid from tumors and are therefore the most likely locations for early metastatic spread in cancers such as breast carcinoma.  

Current methods for detecting cancer involvement in lymph nodes rely primarily on sentinel lymph node biopsy followed by frozen section pathological examination. This conventional approach suffers from several limitations including dependence on pathologist expertise, time constraints leading to incomplete sampling, and potential false negatives when cancer cells are mistaken for histiocytes. Alternative methods such as fluorescent labeling with anti-EGFR or anti-HER2 substances, microwave wave detection, and tissue impedance imaging have been explored but still lack real-time capability, simplicity, or sufficient reliability for routine clinical use.  

There exists a significant unmet need for a real-time, non-invasive method to accurately detect cancerous involvement in lymph nodes during surgical procedures. Such a method would prevent unnecessary lymph node dissections while ensuring complete removal of metastatic nodes, thereby optimizing patient outcomes and minimizing complications such as lymphedema.  

## SUMMARY  

The present disclosure provides a novel method for detecting cancer-involved lymph nodes by measuring the pH value of lymphatic fluid. The invention is based on the discovery that metastatic cancer cells alter their metabolism to fatty acid oxidation when colonizing lymph nodes, creating an acidic microenvironment detectable through pH measurement.  

The method involves interacting lymphatic fluid with a buffer solution and measuring the resulting pH value using pH-sensing paper. A lymph node is identified as cancer-involved if the measured pH is less than 7.0, with values below 6.0 providing particularly strong indication of metastasis. In preferred embodiments, the method utilizes a specialized diagnostic instrument comprising a hypodermic needle containing pH-sensing paper and a reservoir of biocompatible buffer solution such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).  

Key aspects of the invention include: injecting a buffer solution into the lymph node to standardize the measurement; placing pH-sensing paper in contact with the lymphatic fluid; detecting color changes in the pH-sensing paper corresponding to acidic conditions; and correlating these pH changes with metastatic involvement. The system provides real-time results in less than 30 seconds, enabling immediate surgical decision-making without the delays associated with conventional pathological techniques.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following detailed description provides a thorough understanding of the present teachings by describing well-known methods and components at a high level while setting forth specific nomenclature for complete comprehension. The disclosure presents exemplary embodiments of the invention and explains their application in clinical settings.  

Early detection of cancer-involved lymph nodes is critical for proper staging and treatment of malignancies such as breast cancer. The disclosed method leverages metabolic changes in metastatic cancer cells that create a characteristic acidic environment in affected lymph nodes. When cancer cells metastasize to lymph nodes, they shift their metabolism from glycolysis to fatty acid oxidation (FAO), a process mediated by activation of Yes-Associated Protein (YAP) and upregulation of FAO-related genes.  

The fatty acid oxidation process generates carbon dioxide that dissolves in lymphatic fluid, forming bicarbonate and hydrogen ions through the reversible reaction CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. This biochemical pathway creates an acidic microenvironment in metastatic lymph nodes, with pH values typically below 6.0 compared to the neutral-to-alkaline pH (7.0-10.0) of healthy lymph nodes.  

The diagnostic instrument and method of the present disclosure enable real-time detection of this pH difference. The system is calibrated using pathological assay results to establish definitive pH thresholds distinguishing normal from metastatic lymph nodes. This calibration preserves cancer-free lymph nodes for the patient while ensuring complete removal of involved nodes.  

For breast cancer diagnosis, the method comprises measuring the pH value of lymphatic fluid from a lymph node and detecting cancerous involvement based on acidic pH values. The measurement can be performed either in vitro on resected lymph nodes or in vivo through minimally invasive procedures.  

FIG. 1 illustrates the basic process comprising: measuring the pH value of lymphatic fluid from a lymph node (step 102) and detecting the lymph node as cancer-involved if the pH is below a predetermined threshold (step 104). The in vitro procedure involves resecting a lymph node and directly measuring its lymphatic fluid pH, while the in vivo approach measures pH through needle insertion without requiring node removal.  

The method further includes putting lymphatic fluid in interaction with a buffer solution to standardize the measurement conditions. The pH value of the interacted lymphatic fluid is then measured and classified as acidic (indicating cancer involvement), basic/alkali, or neutral (both indicating likely absence of metastasis).

This version emphasizes the importance of buffer solution interaction for consistent measurements.  

Another embodiment involves injecting buffer solution directly into the lymph node and inserting pH-sensing paper to detect acidic conditions. FIG. 3 details this approach: placing pH-sensing paper inside a syringe needle (step 302); filling the syringe with buffer solution (step 304); putting the pH-sensing paper in contact with lymphatic fluid (step 306); allowing interaction between lymphatic fluid and buffer solution (step 308); and detecting cancer involvement based on the resulting color change (step 310).

### Example 1: Real-Time Diagnosis of Cancer Involved Lymph Nodes  

This example describes an exemplary instrument and method for real-time diagnosis of metastatic lymph nodes. The system utilizes a pH sensing mechanism comprising injectable PBS and litmus paper housed within a modified syringe apparatus.  

The procedure begins with preparation of fresh lymph node samples either through resection or needle aspiration. The pH sensing mechanism is then deployed by inserting the needle into the lymph node, injecting PBS to standardize the measurement environment, and reading the color change of the litmus paper. A calibration pattern distinguishes normal lymph nodes (pH 7.0-10.0) from metastatic lymph nodes (pH <6.0) with high accuracy.  

Pathological validation is performed through H&E staining following standard protocols: deparaffinization and hydration of tissue samples; staining with hematoxylin and eosin; washing and dehydration; and microscopic examination. Comparative testing between the pH sensing method and frozen section diagnostics demonstrates superior sensitivity (92% vs 83%) and selectivity (90% vs 83%) for the disclosed approach.  

FIG. 6 illustrates the MMLD (Metabolism based Metastatic Lymph Diagnoser) instrument used for pH sensing of secreted fluid from SLNs. The device incorporates a micro-syringe (18 gauge needle) filled with sterile PBS and embedded litmus paper for immediate pH readout. H&E images of normal and cancerous lymph nodes confirm the correlation between cancer cell percentage and pH values, with metastatic nodes consistently showing acidic conditions.
