# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The field of gene and cell-based therapies has emerged as a promising approach for treating various diseases by introducing therapeutic genes or cells into patients. These therapies hold potential for addressing conditions ranging from genetic disorders to cancers. However, significant limitations exist in monitoring the distribution, persistence, and activity of therapeutic genes and cells following administration. Current approaches rely heavily on invasive biopsy techniques, which carry risks of complications and sampling errors while providing limited spatial and temporal information about therapeutic cell kinetics.  

Positron emission tomography (PET) reporter gene imaging represents a non-invasive solution for tracking therapeutic cells in vivo. This imaging modality utilizes reporter genes that encode proteins capable of selectively accumulating positron-emitting reporter probes. The most widely used PET reporter gene systems employ viral-derived enzymes such as the Herpes Simplex Virus type 1 thymidine kinase (HSV1-tk) and its optimized mutant sr39tk. These enzymes phosphorylate specific radiolabeled probes including 9-[4-18F-3-(hydroxymethyl)butyl]guanine (18F-FHBG) and 2'-deoxy-2'-18F-5-ethyl-1-β-D-arabinofuranosyluracil (18F-FEAU), enabling detection of reporter gene-expressing cells by PET imaging.  

A critical limitation of viral-derived reporter genes like HSV1-tk is their immunogenicity, which can trigger immune-mediated elimination of therapeutic cells in patients. This immunogenicity stems from the foreign nature of viral proteins in humans, leading to presentation of viral epitopes by host MHC class I molecules. Clinical trials have documented immune responses against HSV1-tk-expressing cells, compromising therapeutic efficacy.  

To address immunogenicity concerns, human-derived reporter genes have been explored as alternatives. Candidate human reporter genes include modified versions of deoxycytidine kinase (dCK) and truncated mitochondrial thymidine kinase 2 (TK2). While these human enzymes show reduced immunogenicity, they exhibit limitations in sensitivity compared to viral counterparts or retain significant activity toward endogenous nucleosides, potentially perturbing cellular nucleotide metabolism.  

There remains an unmet need for PET reporter gene systems that combine the sensitivity of viral-derived systems with the immunological compatibility of human proteins while minimizing interference with endogenous metabolic pathways. The human mitochondrial thymidine kinase 2 (htk2) represents a promising candidate for development as an improved reporter gene due to its natural nucleoside kinase activity and human origin. Engineered versions of htk2 could provide the basis for novel PET reporter gene/reporter probe systems with optimal characteristics for clinical translation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides systems, materials, and methods for non-invasive imaging of therapeutic cells using positron emission tomography. The invention discloses a highly sensitive, non-immunogenic reporter gene based on a mutated form of human thymidine kinase 2 (htk2) along with novel radiolabeled probes for whole-body molecular imaging applications.  

Embodiments of the invention find particular utility in monitoring cell and gene therapies during clinical trials, enabling detection of adverse physiological phenomena such as unintended cell migration or premature cell death. The invention includes PET reporter gene imaging kits comprising nucleic acid constructs encoding the htk2 reporter gene and corresponding radiolabeled probes.  

Key aspects of the invention include the use of human-derived genes to minimize immunogenicity while maintaining high imaging sensitivity. The disclosed htk2 reporter gene exhibits reduced activity toward endogenous nucleosides compared to wild-type enzymes, minimizing potential perturbations of cellular nucleotide pools. An enhanced version of human mitochondrial thymidine kinase 2 (htk2) has been developed through structure-guided mutagenesis, creating the TK2-N93D/L109F double mutant with optimized properties for PET reporter gene applications.  

The invention further provides novel PET/SPECT radiolabeled L- and D-enantiomer analogs of thymidine as reporter probes, including 2-deoxy-2-18F-5-methyl-1-β-L-arabinofuranosyluracil (L-18F-FMAU). Methods for generating htk2 mutants through targeted amino acid substitutions are disclosed, along with nucleic acid molecules encoding the htk2 polypeptide, vectors for delivering these nucleic acids, and host cells transfected with such vectors.  

Complete systems for imaging mammalian cells using PET or SPECT are described, comprising the htk2 reporter gene and compatible radiolabeled probes. The invention enables quantitative monitoring of therapeutic cell distribution and persistence in vivo, addressing critical needs in the development and clinical implementation of cell-based therapies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

Gene therapy refers to the introduction of genetic material into cells to compensate for abnormal genes or to make beneficial proteins. Therapeutic genes (TG) encode products intended to treat or prevent disease, while therapeutic cells (TC) are cells administered to patients with therapeutic intent, often after genetic modification. Monitoring gene expression and cell kinetics represents a fundamental challenge in developing these therapies, as the distribution, persistence, and activity of therapeutic elements directly impact treatment outcomes.  

Conventional treatments for monitoring therapeutic cells, such as serial biopsies, provide limited spatial information and carry risks of complications. PET reporter gene imaging overcomes these limitations by enabling whole-body, non-invasive tracking of cells engineered to express PET reporter genes. These imaging approaches rely on PRG-PRP systems where the reporter gene product interacts specifically with an administered radiolabeled probe.  

The present invention discloses the TK2-N93D/L109F mutant as an improved reporter gene, derived from human thymidine kinase 2 through targeted mutagenesis. This mutant demonstrates efficient phosphorylation of L-18F-FMAU while showing reduced activity toward endogenous nucleosides thymidine and deoxycytidine compared to wild-type TK2. The N93D mutation reduces susceptibility to thymidine triphosphate-mediated feedback inhibition, while the L109F mutation enhances catalytic activity.  

The invention describes mechanisms for trapping PET/SPECT radiolabeled nucleoside analogs through phosphorylation by the engineered htk2 reporter gene. Safety features are incorporated through the use of human-derived sequences unlikely to trigger immune responses, and through minimized interference with endogenous nucleotide metabolism. Methods for generating htk2 mutants include deletion of the first 50 amino acids comprising the mitochondrial targeting sequence and introduction of specific amino acid substitutions at positions 93 and 109.  

The human thymidine kinase 2 polypeptide of the invention includes variants with amino acid substitutions that confer desired enzymatic properties while maintaining human sequence to prevent immunogenicity. Nucleic acid molecules encoding these polypeptides can be incorporated into various vectors, including viral and non-viral delivery systems, for introduction into host cells.  

Compositions of matter comprising non-naturally occurring analogs of thymidine, particularly L-enantiomers such as L-18F-FMAU, are disclosed as reporter probes. These probes exhibit favorable biodistribution and are selectively phosphorylated by the engineered htk2 reporter gene. The invention provides complete systems for imaging mammalian cells using PET or SPECT, comprising both the reporter gene and compatible reporter probe components.  

Kits for imaging mammalian cells incorporate the htk2 reporter gene construct, appropriate delivery vectors, and instructions for using with corresponding radiolabeled probes. Methods of imaging involve introducing the reporter gene into mammalian cells, administering the reporter probe, and detecting probe accumulation using PET or SPECT imaging. The thymidine kinase polypeptides of the invention may be fused to heterologous amino acid sequences for various applications.  

Four novel PET probes are disclosed, with detailed characterization of their biodistribution in mice and radiation dosimetry properties. Applications of the invention span preclinical research through clinical translation, including monitoring of adoptive cell therapies, stem cell engraftment, and gene therapy vector distribution.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

PET reporter probe systems were evaluated to identify optimal PRG-PRP combinations. L-[18F]FMAU and L-[18F]FEAU were investigated as candidate PRPs through in vitro assays, cell culture experiments, and in vivo imaging studies. The potential immunogenicity of hTK2-N93D PRG was analyzed through epitope mapping, leading to design of variants with reduced immunogenicity.  

Human nucleoside kinases were systematically evaluated as potential PRGs, with TK2 emerging as a promising candidate. The mitochondrial signal peptide of TK2 was truncated to alter subcellular localization, and point mutation N93D was introduced to reduce feedback inhibition. Kinetic analyses compared wild-type and TK2-N93D variants, demonstrating the mutant's improved characteristics for L-[18F]FMAU phosphorylation while maintaining low activity toward endogenous nucleosides.  

In vivo comparisons between TK2-N93D, wild-type TK2, and HSV1-sr39tk demonstrated the superior performance of the mutant reporter gene. An adenoviral liver model was developed for evaluating PRG-PRP systems, incorporating reference and experimental viruses for direct comparison. Potential immunogenic epitopes presented by host MHC class I molecules were identified, informing future engineering strategies to abolish epitope binding while maintaining enzymatic function.  

### Example 2  

Preclinical studies evaluated the invention in hepatic colorectal cancer and melanoma models. Gene therapy approaches for hepatic metastases and T-cell immunotherapy for melanoma were investigated using the TK2-based reporter system. Human LS174T colorectal cancer cells were injected into immunocompromised mice, with tumor burden monitored by optical imaging.  

Ad vector delivery to hepatic metastases was combined with PRP administration and microPET/CT imaging. Reporter gene enzyme activity assays and PCR analyses confirmed system performance. The Pmel-1/B16 melanoma model incorporating adoptive T cell transfer demonstrated utility of the reporter system for monitoring cell-based immunotherapies.  

### Example 3  

Products for long-term cell tracking PET studies were developed, including commercializable kits for preclinical research. An automated, modular radiochemistry platform (ELIXYS) was employed for reproducible probe production. Lentiviral and non-viral PRG delivery vectors were engineered, with ΦC31 integrase-based systems enabling stable genomic integration.  

Studies established conversion factors to quantify therapeutic cells in vivo through standardized PET signal calibration. Mouse CD8+ T cells and human mesenchymal stem cells were directly injected into tumor xenografts, with reporter gene expression per cell determined and correlated with PET signal intensity following L-[18F]FMAU administration.  

### Example 4  

An exploratory Investigational New Drug (eIND) submission was prepared, incorporating biodistribution and dosimetry studies. Preclinical safety/toxicity evaluations established the suitability of the system for human applications. Chemistry, manufacturing, and controls documentation supported regulatory filings.  

Biodistribution of L-[18F]FMAU was characterized in detail, with comparative evaluation of alternative probes including L-[18F]FEAU. Contingency plans addressed potential limitations identified during development. Whole-body PET/CT imaging in healthy human volunteers confirmed favorable probe characteristics and provided preliminary human dosimetry data.  

### Example 5  

Gene and cell therapy applications motivated development of novel PET reporter probes. Methods for evaluating biodistribution of PET tracers were established and applied to studies in mice and humans. Rational design of mutations in human thymidine kinase 2 produced variants with optimized properties for reporter gene applications.  

Conclusions supported the suitability of 18F-L-FMAU as a PRP when paired with engineered TK2 variants. Comparative studies demonstrated advantages over existing viral-based systems, particularly regarding immunogenicity and metabolic interference.  

### Example 6  

PET reporter gene imaging addressed limitations of current cell-based therapies by enabling non-invasive monitoring. The TK2-N93D/L109F PRG-PRP system demonstrated sensitivity comparable to HSV1-tk while avoiding immunogenicity concerns. Biodistribution studies of L-18F-FMAU in mice and humans informed clinical translation strategies.  

Structure-guided enzyme engineering produced the TK2 double mutant through iterative optimization. Kinetic analyses, cell uptake assays, and in vivo imaging characterized system performance. Human PET/CT studies established biodistribution parameters and radiation dosimetry for clinical applications.  

### Example 7  

Detailed radiochemical synthesis methods were developed for L-2-O-[(Trifluoromethyl)sulfonyl]-1,3,5-tri-O-benzoyl precursor and its 18F-fluoro analog. The production process incorporated no-carrier-added [18F]-fluoride ion trapping and release, reaction with triflate under controlled heating, purification, and final product formulation.  

### Example 8  

Illustrative PET reporter gene probes were disclosed and evaluated against ideal probe criteria. Substrate specificity for TK1 and efficient F-18 labeling were confirmed. Biodistribution studies and metabolic stability assessments supported selection of lead compounds for human studies. Comparative analyses identified optimal probe characteristics for various applications.
