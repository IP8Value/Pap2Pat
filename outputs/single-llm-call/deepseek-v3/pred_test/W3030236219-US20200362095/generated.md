# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Bottlebrush polymers represent an important class of macromolecular architectures characterized by a linear polymeric backbone with densely grafted polymeric sidechains. These unique structural features impart bottlebrush polymers with distinctive physical and chemical properties compared to their linear counterparts, including reduced chain entanglement, increased stiffness, and enhanced solubility. The three-dimensional brush-like morphology of bottlebrush polymers arises from steric repulsion between adjacent sidechains, forcing the backbone into an extended conformation. This architecture enables precise control over nanoscale assembly and material properties through modulation of backbone length, sidechain composition, and grafting density.  

Recent advances in polymer synthesis have enabled the preparation of bottlebrush polymers with increasingly complex architectures, including block copolymers where distinct segments of the backbone bear different sidechain compositions. Among these, triblock bottlebrush copolymers (TBCs) containing three contiguous blocks of dissimilar sidechain polymers are particularly interesting due to their potential for multi-compartment self-assembly. The sequential arrangement of hydrophobic, hydrophilic, and stimuli-responsive blocks in ABC-type TBCs can drive hierarchical organization into nanostructures with well-defined domains. Such materials exhibit tunable responsiveness to environmental cues while maintaining structural integrity, making them attractive candidates for biomedical applications.  

Extended release technologies have emerged as critical tools for improving drug efficacy and patient compliance by maintaining therapeutic concentrations over prolonged periods. Polymeric carriers play a central role in these systems, with injectable hydrogels representing a particularly promising platform for localized and sustained drug delivery. Hydrogels formed from physically crosslinked polymers offer advantages including mild preparation conditions, reversible gelation, and compatibility with sensitive biologics. Thermoresponsive polymers that undergo sol-gel transitions near physiological temperature are especially valuable for minimally invasive administration as flowing solutions that transform into depots at the target site.  

Current limitations in extended release formulations include premature drug leakage, insufficient depot longevity, and lack of multifunctionality. There exists a need for new polymeric materials that can simultaneously encapsulate diverse therapeutics while providing precise control over release kinetics. Bottlebrush copolymers with tailored block sequences and responsive elements may address these challenges through their unique self-assembly behavior and tunable erosion profiles. In particular, ABC-type triblock bottlebrush architectures combining hydrophobic, hydrophilic, and thermoresponsive domains could enable independent control over drug loading and gelation behavior for optimized therapeutic performance.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides thermally-responsive nanomaterials based on novel triblock bottlebrush copolymers (TBCs) with precisely controlled architecture and composition. These ABC-type TBCs comprise three distinct polymeric blocks arranged in sequence along a common backbone, where each block bears sidechains with tailored chemical functionality. The invention encompasses the molecular design, synthesis methods, self-assembled nanostructures, and therapeutic applications of these advanced polymeric materials.  

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers of the invention feature a central hydrophilic segment flanked by dissimilar terminal blocks exhibiting lower critical solution temperature (LCST) behavior and hydrophobicity, respectively. This ABC architecture enables hierarchical self-assembly into well-defined micellar structures at ambient temperature that further organize into physically crosslinked networks upon heating above the LCST transition. The resulting materials function as injectable depots for sustained release of encapsulated therapeutic agents.  

Key embodiments of the invention include ABC TBCs where:  
- The A block comprises polyester sidechains providing hydrophobic character  
- The B block comprises polyether sidechains conferring hydrophilicity  
- The C block comprises poly(N-alkylacrylamide) sidechains exhibiting LCST behavior  

Particularly preferred embodiments utilize polylactic acid (PLA) as the A block polyester, polyethylene glycol (PEG) as the B block polyether, and poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM) as the C block polyacrylamide. This combination produces materials that self-assemble into stable micelles at room temperature and undergo rapid gelation at physiological temperature (37°C).  

The invention further provides methods for preparing the triblock bottlebrush copolymers through sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers. This graft-through approach enables precise control over block lengths and compositions while maintaining high backbone functionality. The resulting TBCs exhibit narrow molecular weight distributions and well-defined architecture as confirmed by size exclusion chromatography and nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy.  

Self-assembly of the ABC TBCs produces uniform nanoparticles (∼100 nm diameter) capable of encapsulating both small molecule drugs and biologics. These nanostructures further organize into three-dimensional networks upon heating above the LCST of the C block, forming mechanically robust hydrogels. The invention encompasses pharmaceutical formulations comprising the TBCs and therapeutic agents, methods of administration, and kits for therapeutic use.  

The thermoresponsive properties of the ABC TBCs enable unique applications in controlled drug delivery. Injectable formulations remain as free-flowing solutions at room temperature, facilitating administration, then rapidly form localized depots at body temperature. This behavior allows for minimally invasive implantation of drug-loaded hydrogels that provide extended release over days to weeks. Demonstrated applications include cancer immunotherapy combinations, ocular therapeutics, and treatment of inflammatory diseases.  

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers are represented by Formula (I):  

```
[Block A]-[Block B]-[Block C] (I)
```  

Where:  
- Block A comprises polyester sidechains  
- Block B comprises polyether sidechains  
- Block C comprises poly(N-alkylacrylamide) sidechains  

In preferred embodiments, the sidechains are selected such that Block A and Block C comprise different polymers exhibiting distinct thermal and solubility properties. The molecular weights of each block are controlled to achieve optimal self-assembly and drug release characteristics.  

The invention further provides specific embodiments of Formula (I) including Formulas (I-a) through (I-e) with defined linker groups, sidechain compositions, and block ratios. These structural variations enable tuning of material properties for specific therapeutic applications. Methods for preparing the TBCs, forming nanoparticles and hydrogels, and loading therapeutic agents are also described in detail.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS  

### Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers  

The present invention provides triblock bottlebrush copolymers (TBCs) with precisely controlled architecture and tailored functionality. These ABC-type TBCs comprise three distinct polymeric blocks arranged in sequence along a common backbone, where each block bears sidechains with defined chemical composition. The ABC architecture enables hierarchical self-assembly into complex nanostructures with temperature-responsive behavior.  

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers feature a central hydrophilic B block flanked by dissimilar terminal A and C blocks. The A block typically comprises hydrophobic polyester sidechains that drive micelle core formation. Preferred polyester sidechains include polylactic acid (PLA), polyglycolic acid (PGA), and polycaprolactone (PCL) with molecular weights ranging from 1-20 kDa. The B block comprises polyether sidechains, preferably polyethylene glycol (PEG) with molecular weights of 2-10 kDa, that provide hydrophilicity and steric stabilization. The C block contains poly(N-alkylacrylamide) sidechains exhibiting lower critical solution temperature (LCST) behavior near physiological temperature. Particularly preferred C block sidechains comprise poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM) with molecular weights of 3-8 kDa.  

The ABC TBCs are represented by Formula (I):  

```
[Block A]-[Block B]-[Block C] (I)
```  

Where:  
- Block A comprises at least 10 repeating units of polyester sidechains  
- Block B comprises at least 15 repeating units of polyether sidechains  
- Block C comprises at least 10 repeating units of poly(N-alkylacrylamide) sidechains  

In preferred embodiments, the sidechain molecular weights are selected such that the A block constitutes 20-40% by weight, the B block 30-50% by weight, and the C block 20-40% by weight of the total copolymer. This balance ensures optimal self-assembly into stable micelles at ambient temperature that transform into mechanically robust hydrogels upon heating.  

The invention encompasses specific embodiments of Formula (I) including Formulas (I-a) through (I-e) with defined linker groups and sidechain compositions. For example, Formula (I-d) represents TBCs with norbornenyl backbone units connected to sidechains through ester linkages:  

```
[Nb-PLA]m-[Nb-PEG]n-[Nb-PNIPAM]p (I-d)
```  

Where:  
- Nb-PLA denotes norbornenyl-polylactide macromonomer units  
- Nb-PEG denotes norbornenyl-polyethylene glycol macromonomer units  
- Nb-PNIPAM denotes norbornenyl-poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) macromonomer units  
- m, n, and p represent the degree of polymerization for each block  

The molecular weights of each block are controlled to achieve desired properties, with typical values of m=10-30, n=15-40, and p=10-30. The total degree of polymerization ranges from 50-100 repeating units, producing TBCs with molecular weights between 100-500 kDa.  

The ABC architecture provides unique advantages over conventional ABA or BAB bottlebrush copolymers. The dissimilar end blocks (A and C) prevent formation of mechanically weak primary loops during network formation, enhancing hydrogel integrity. Additionally, the sequence allows independent control over micellization (governed by A block) and gelation (controlled by C block), enabling precise tuning of drug loading and release characteristics.  

### Methods for Preparing Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers  

The triblock bottlebrush copolymers are synthesized via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-functionalized macromonomers. This graft-through approach provides excellent control over block lengths and compositions while maintaining high backbone functionality.  

The synthesis involves three main steps:  
1) Preparation of norbornene-terminated macromonomers (Nb-PLA, Nb-PEG, Nb-PNIPAM)  
2) Sequential ROMP of macromonomers to form ABC triblock architecture  
3) Purification and characterization of final TBC product  

Norbornene-PLA (Nb-PLA) macromonomer is prepared by ring-opening polymerization of lactide initiated by norbornenyl alcohol, followed by end-capping with norbornene dicarboxylic anhydride. Norbornene-PEG (Nb-PEG) is synthesized by coupling amine-terminated PEG to norbornene-N-hydroxysuccinimide ester. Norbornene-PNIPAM (Nb-PNIPAM) is prepared similarly by conjugating amine-terminated PNIPAM to the norbornene active ester.  

ROMP is initiated using Grubbs 3rd generation catalyst (G3) in anhydrous dichloromethane at room temperature. The Nb-PLA macromonomer is polymerized first, followed by sequential addition of Nb-PEG and Nb-PNIPAM after complete consumption of each preceding monomer. The reaction is quenched with ethyl vinyl ether, and the product purified by dialysis against water followed by lyophilization.  

The resulting ABC TBCs exhibit narrow molecular weight distributions (Ð < 1.2) as confirmed by size exclusion chromatography. Block ratios are verified by 1H NMR spectroscopy through integration of characteristic proton signals from each polymer segment. Thermal analysis by differential scanning calorimetry confirms the LCST behavior of PNIPAM-containing blocks.  

### Particles and Hydrogels  

The ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymers self-assemble into well-defined nanoparticles in aqueous solution at ambient temperature. These nanostructures feature a hydrophobic PLA core surrounded by a hydrophilic PEG corona, with PNIPAM segments positioned at the interface. The micelles exhibit narrow size distributions (90-120 nm diameter) as measured by dynamic light scattering and transmission electron microscopy.  

At concentrations above 5 wt%, the TBC solutions undergo thermoreversible gelation upon heating above the LCST of the PNIPAM block (~32°C). This transition involves collapse of PNIPAM segments and subsequent intermicellar crosslinking, forming a three-dimensional hydrogel network. Rheological analysis demonstrates rapid development of elastic modulus (G' > 1 kPa) upon heating to 37°C, with complete reversibility upon cooling.  

The hydrogel pore structure is characterized by small angle X-ray scattering (SAXS), revealing an inter-domain spacing of ~60 nm at 10 wt% concentration. This nanostructured architecture persists through multiple heating-cooling cycles, indicating excellent thermodynamic stability. Cryogenic TEM confirms maintenance of micellar integrity throughout the gelation process.  

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical formulations comprising the ABC TBCs and therapeutic agents. Small molecule drugs are encapsulated by nanoprecipitation - dissolving both polymer and drug in organic solvent followed by rapid dilution into aqueous buffer. Proteins and biologics are incorporated by mixing with pre-formed micelles below the LCST. Drug loading efficiencies range from 30-90% depending on compound hydrophobicity.  

### Formulations, Kits, and Administration  

The invention provides pharmaceutical formulations comprising the ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymers and at least one therapeutic agent. These formulations may be provided as:

Typical formulations contain 5-20 wt% TBC polymer and 0.1-10 wt% therapeutic agent in physiologically acceptable buffers. ### Methods of Treatment and Use  

The ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymers are useful for treating various diseases through controlled drug delivery. Key therapeutic applications include:  

**Cancer Therapy:** Intratumoral injection of PTX/R848-loaded hydrogels provides localized chemo-immunotherapy with reduced systemic toxicity. The sustained release profile synchronizes chemotherapy-induced antigen release with prolonged immune activation.  

**Ocular Diseases:** Subconjunctival injection forms depots for extended delivery of anti-angiogenics (e.g., aflibercept) or anti-inflammatories (e.g., dexamethasone) to treat macular degeneration or uveitis.  

**Chronic Inflammation:** Subcutaneous implantation delivers anti-TNF biologics (e.g., adalimumab) for rheumatoid arthritis with reduced dosing frequency.  

**Vaccine Adjuvants:** Micellar formulations encapsulating TLR agonists (e.g., resiquimod) enhance humoral and cellular immune responses to co-administered antigens.  

The thermoresponsive properties enable minimally invasive administration as low-viscosity solutions that transform into solid depots at body temperature. Drug release occurs over 1-4 weeks through a combination of diffusion and hydrogel erosion mechanisms.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Thermoresponsive ABC Bottlebrush Copolymer Amphiphiles  

ABC triblock bottlebrush copolymers were synthesized comprising PLA (5 kDa), PEG (5 kDa), and PNIPAM (5.5 kDa) sidechains. The TBCs self-assembled into monodisperse micelles (113 nm diameter by DLS) that encapsulated hydrophobic drugs (paclitaxel, resiquimod) with >80% efficiency. Upon heating to 37°C, 10 wt% solutions formed opaque hydrogels with storage modulus G' = 1.2 kPa. Drug release profiles showed sustained delivery over 7 days in vitro.  

### General Methods and Instruments  

Polymers were characterized by 1H NMR (400 MHz, CDCl3), SEC (THF, PS standards), and DLS (25°C, 1 mg/mL in PBS). Hydrogel rheology was measured using parallel plate geometry (1 Hz, 0.1% strain). SAXS experiments were conducted at the Advanced Photon Source (Argonne National Laboratory).  

### Synthesis of Norbornene-N-Hydroxysuccinimidyl (NHS)-Ester  

5-Norbornene-2-exo,3-exo-dicarboxylic anhydride (5.0 g) and N-hydroxysuccinimide (3.8 g) were dissolved in THF (50 mL) with triethylamine (5.6 mL). After stirring at 25°C for 12 h, the product was isolated by precipitation into cold hexanes (85% yield).  

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PEG Macromonomer (Nor-PEG)  

Amine-terminated PEG (5 kDa, 2.0 g) was reacted with norbornene-NHS ester (0.5 g) in DMF (10 mL) containing DIEA (0.5 mL). After 24 h, the product was dialyzed (3.5 kDa MWCO) and lyophilized (90% yield).  

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PNIPAM Macromonomer (Nor-PNIPAM)  

Using similar procedure as Nor-PEG, amine-terminated PNIPAM (5.5 kDa) was conjugated to norbornene-NHS ester (85% yield).  

### Ring-Opening Metathesis Polymerization  

Nb-PLA (0.5 g) was dissolved in anhydrous DCM (5 mL) and treated with G3 catalyst (0.5 mol%) under N2. After 1 h, Nb-PEG (0.5 g) was added, followed by Nb-PNIPAM (0.5 g) after another hour. The reaction was quenched with EVE, dialyzed (50 kDa MWCO), and lyophilized to give ABC TBC (1.2 g, 80% yield).
