## BACKGROUND  

The field of additive manufacturing has undergone significant advancement in recent years, particularly in the area of composite material fabrication. Traditional manufacturing techniques for carbon fiber reinforced composites have relied on labor-intensive layup processes that limit design flexibility and geometric complexity. While additive manufacturing methods such as fused deposition modeling have enabled greater design freedom, these approaches have been largely restricted to thermoplastic matrices with inferior mechanical properties compared to thermoset systems.  

U.S. Pat. No. 6,299,810 describes a conventional lathe-based system for manufacturing carbon fiber composite structures. This apparatus utilizes a rotating mandrel upon which resin-impregnated fibers are wound under tension. The system includes heating elements to cure the resin and requires application of release agents to facilitate part removal. While effective for producing axisymmetric components, this method cannot create complex three-dimensional geometries and suffers from limitations in fiber alignment control.  

More recent developments in fiber reinforced additive manufacturing are represented in U.S. Published Patent Application No. 20140361460, which discloses a three-dimensional printing system for composite materials. This system supplies unmelted composite filament to a heated print head where the material is melted and deposited onto a build platen. The apparatus applies ironing forces to compact deposited layers and includes controller systems to coordinate the printing process. While representing an advancement in composite additive manufacturing, this approach remains limited to thermoplastic matrices and does not achieve the high levels of fiber alignment necessary for optimal mechanical performance.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides novel apparatus, systems, and methods for the additive manufacturing of high-performance carbon fiber/epoxy composites. Unlike prior art systems limited to thermoplastic matrices or random fiber distributions, the invention enables precise control over fiber alignment within thermoset resin systems through an advanced direct ink writing process.  

The inventive system comprises a specialized print head and resin delivery system capable of processing high-viscosity, fiber-loaded thermoset inks. The apparatus includes a computer-controlled multi-axis deposition stage that enables precise placement of aligned fiber bundles in three-dimensional space. The system overcomes limitations of conventional additive manufacturing by incorporating a unique nozzle geometry that promotes shear-induced fiber alignment while preventing clogging.  

The method of additive manufacturing involves extrusion of a specially formulated carbon fiber/epoxy ink through the print head nozzle under controlled pressure conditions. As the ink passes through the nozzle, high shear forces induce alignment of the discontinuous fibers in the extrusion direction. The aligned fiber bundles are precisely deposited according to programmed paths to build up complex three-dimensional structures layer by layer. Following deposition, the printed structures undergo thermal curing to achieve full crosslinking of the epoxy matrix.  

Industrial applications of the invention span aerospace, automotive, and defense sectors where high-performance composites with tailored mechanical properties are required. The ability to precisely control fiber orientation at microscopic scales enables fabrication of components with unprecedented orthotropic properties. The scope of the invention encompasses not only the apparatus and methods for manufacturing, but also the resulting composite materials with their unique microstructure and performance characteristics.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF SPECIFIC EMBODIMENTS  

High performance Carbon Fiber/Epoxy composites represent a critical class of structural materials, yet current manufacturing processes impose significant limitations on part geometry and fiber alignment. While additive manufacturing and 3D printing technologies offer potential solutions, existing systems for additively manufacturing continuous carbon fiber epoxy composites suffer from nozzle clogging, poor resolution, and inadequate fiber alignment. Commercial epoxy resin systems further complicate the process due to their limited pot life and challenging rheological properties.  

The MarkForged 3D system represents one commercial approach to composite additive manufacturing, but remains limited to thermoplastic matrices with inferior thermal and mechanical properties compared to thermoset systems. The present invention overcomes these limitations through novel apparatus, systems, and methods specifically designed for high-performance carbon fiber/epoxy composites.  

Embodiment 100 of the invention is illustrated in FIG. 1, showing the essential components of the 3D printing system. The extruded material 102 consists of a bisphenol-F epoxy resin modified with colloidal silica and dispersed high aspect ratio carbon fibers. The print head 106 receives this material and processes it through a precisely engineered nozzle system. A computer controller 110 coordinates the movement of the print head relative to the build platform to create complex three-dimensional structures.  

The product creation process involves several critical stages. First, the carbon fiber loaded ink is prepared by mixing oligomerized bisphenol-F diglycidyl ether with fumed silica and chopped carbon fibers. This mixture undergoes centrifugal mixing and vacuum degassing to achieve a homogeneous, bubble-free ink. The prepared ink is loaded into the print head system where it is extruded through the nozzle under controlled pressure conditions.  

FIG. 2 provides a detailed view of print head 106 with nozzle 108. The nozzle incorporates a fiber core shaper 122 that promotes alignment of the discontinuous fibers during extrusion. Resin chamber 120 maintains the ink at optimal viscosity prior to extrusion. The nozzle geometry is specifically designed to create shear conditions that align fibers without causing clogging or aggregation.  

FIG. 3 illustrates the complete system 100 in operation, showing the seven separate stages that characterize the invention. The first stage involves preimpregnation of the carbon fiber core within the resin matrix. In the second stage, the print head body remains open to atmospheric pressure to prevent undesirable pressure gradients. The third stage employs a computer controlled precision 3-6 axis DIW stage for precise deposition of material.  

The fourth stage focuses on the print head nozzle 108 where the critical fiber alignment occurs. The fifth stage incorporates a fiber feed reel 123 that supplies continuous fiber reinforcement when desired. The sixth stage shows the drawn fiber 102 path through the system, while the seventh stage includes a satellite head 124 for multi-material deposition.  

System 100 functionality extends beyond simple deposition to include advanced process control features. The resin bath and vacuum process ensures optimal material properties throughout printing. Computer control systems monitor and adjust parameters such as extrusion pressure, deposition speed, and nozzle temperature in real time to maintain consistent print quality.  

The scope of the application encompasses not only the apparatus and methods described, but also the unique composite materials produced. These materials exhibit unprecedented orthotropic properties due to the controlled alignment of high aspect ratio carbon fibers within a high-performance thermoset matrix. The invention represents a significant advancement in additive manufacturing technology with broad applications across multiple industries.
