# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of molecular medicine and therapeutics, specifically to methods and compositions for treating muscular dystrophy through pharmacological upregulation of utrophin. The invention encompasses small molecule compounds capable of activating the utrophin promoter, increasing utrophin expression or activity, and thereby compensating for dystrophin deficiency in muscular dystrophy patients. The therapeutic methods involve administering these compounds orally or parenterally to patients suffering from various forms of muscular dystrophy, including but not limited to Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), Becker muscular dystrophy, limb-girdle muscular dystrophy, congenital muscular dystrophy, and other dystrophinopathies.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Muscular dystrophy represents a group of genetic disorders characterized by progressive muscle weakness and degeneration. Among these, Duchenne muscular dystrophy stands as the most common and severe form, caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene that lead to absence or dysfunction of the dystrophin protein. Dystrophin serves a crucial structural role in muscle fibers by linking the cytoskeleton to the extracellular matrix through the dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC). In the absence of functional dystrophin, muscle fibers become susceptible to damage during contraction, leading to cycles of degeneration and regeneration that ultimately result in replacement of muscle tissue with fibrotic and fatty deposits.  

Utrophin, an autosomal homolog of dystrophin, shares significant structural and functional similarities with dystrophin, including the ability to bind components of the DAPC. While utrophin is expressed in fetal muscle, its expression in adult muscle is normally restricted to neuromuscular and myotendinous junctions. Research has demonstrated that upregulation of utrophin in dystrophin-deficient muscle can compensate for the lack of dystrophin and ameliorate the dystrophic phenotype. This compensatory mechanism presents a promising therapeutic approach for muscular dystrophy, as utrophin upregulation would be applicable to all patients regardless of their specific dystrophin mutation.  

Current therapeutic strategies for muscular dystrophy face several limitations, including mutation-specific applicability, delivery challenges, and potential immune responses. Small molecule drugs that can upregulate utrophin expression offer distinct advantages in terms of delivery, safety profile, and broad applicability across all muscular dystrophy patients. The present invention addresses these needs by providing specific small molecule compounds and methods for their use in utrophin upregulation therapy.

Aromatic moieties are planar cyclic systems with conjugated π-electrons that satisfy Hückel's rule (4n+2 π-electrons). Heteroaromatic moieties similarly satisfy Hückel's rule but contain at least one heteroatom in the aromatic ring. Aryl groups specifically refer to aromatic hydrocarbon groups, while heteroaryl groups refer to aromatic groups containing at least one heteroatom.

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope:  

**Example 1: Screening of Utrophin Promoter Activators**  
A stable C2C12 muscle cell line containing the human utrophin A promoter linked to a luciferase reporter was established. This cell line was used to screen a library of 1120 compounds for utrophin promoter activation. Initial screening identified 20 hits showing at least 20% upregulation of promoter activity. Dose-response testing confirmed 14 compounds as dose-dependent activators of the utrophin promoter, with maximum fold-changes ranging from 1.2 to 1.9 in the primary screen and up to 3.5 in secondary assays.  

**Example 2: Validation of Nabumetone Activity**  
C2C12 muscle cells were treated with 25 μM nabumetone for 24 hours, resulting in a 1.8-fold increase in endogenous utrophin A mRNA as measured by quantitative real-time PCR. Extended treatment for 4 days led to a 1.2-fold increase in utrophin protein levels as determined by Western blot analysis. These results demonstrate that nabumetone can upregulate both utrophin mRNA and protein in muscle cells.  

**Example 3: In Vivo Efficacy Studies**  
Mdx mice (a model for Duchenne muscular dystrophy) were treated with nabumetone at 50 mg/kg/day orally for 8 weeks. Treated animals showed increased utrophin expression at the sarcolemma, improved muscle histology with reduced necrosis and fibrosis, and enhanced muscle function in grip strength and treadmill endurance tests compared to untreated controls. These results demonstrate the therapeutic potential of utrophin upregulation for muscular dystrophy.  

The examples demonstrate that small molecule compounds, particularly nabumetone, can effectively upregulate utrophin expression and ameliorate the dystrophic phenotype in cellular and animal models of muscular dystrophy. These findings support the therapeutic methods and compositions described in the present invention.
