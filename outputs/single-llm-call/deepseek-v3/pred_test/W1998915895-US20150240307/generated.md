# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to methods and reagents for diagnosing rheumatoid arthritis (RA) through the detection and analysis of microRNA (miRNA) expression levels in biological samples. More particularly, the invention provides novel plasma miRNA biomarkers that enable highly specific and sensitive diagnosis of RA, including methods for measuring said biomarkers and diagnostic reagents incorporating the same. The invention further encompasses the use of composite miRNA expression values to improve diagnostic accuracy and correlation with disease pathology.

## BACKGROUND ART  

Rheumatoid arthritis is a systemic autoimmune disorder characterized by chronic inflammation of synovial joints, leading to progressive cartilage destruction, bone erosion, and eventual joint deformity. The disease exhibits significant heterogeneity in clinical presentation and progression, with untreated cases resulting in substantial disability. While therapeutic advances have improved outcomes, early and accurate diagnosis remains critical for optimal clinical management.  

Current diagnostic approaches rely primarily on clinical evaluation combined with serological markers including rheumatoid factor (RF) and anti-citrullinated protein antibodies (ACPA). However, approximately 30% of RA patients test negative for these markers, complicating diagnosis in early disease stages when treatment would be most effective. Furthermore, existing biomarkers lack specificity, as they may be elevated in other autoimmune conditions such as systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) or non-autoimmune inflammatory disorders.  

MicroRNAs have emerged as promising diagnostic molecules due to their remarkable stability in biological fluids and tissue-specific expression patterns. These small non-coding RNAs regulate gene expression through post-transcriptional mechanisms and have been implicated in various pathological processes. Prior investigations have identified altered miRNA expression in RA synovial tissue and peripheral blood cells, but no plasma-based miRNA signature with both high sensitivity and specificity for RA diagnosis has been established. The present invention addresses this unmet need through the discovery of novel plasma miRNA biomarkers that differentiate RA from healthy controls and other arthritic conditions with superior accuracy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention  

Conventional diagnostic methods for rheumatoid arthritis suffer from several limitations. Serological markers such as RF and ACPA demonstrate inadequate sensitivity in early disease stages and fail to identify approximately 30% of RA patients. These markers also lack specificity, as they may be elevated in other inflammatory conditions. Clinical diagnosis based on joint examination becomes reliable only after significant disease progression, delaying critical therapeutic interventions. There exists a pressing need for biomarkers capable of detecting RA with high accuracy during early disease phases, particularly in seronegative patients, while maintaining specificity against other arthritic and autoimmune conditions.  

### Means of Solving the Problems  

The present invention solves these diagnostic challenges through the identification and utilization of specific plasma miRNAs that exhibit differential expression in RA patients. Comprehensive miRNA profiling revealed that miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p demonstrate significantly elevated levels in RA plasma compared to healthy controls and patients with osteoarthritis or systemic lupus erythematosus. These miRNAs achieve diagnostic sensitivities of 63.7-64.7% with specificities of 89.5% at optimized cutoff values.  

The invention further provides a composite biomarker value termed ePRAM (estimated Probability of RA by plasma MiRNAs), calculated through multivariate logistic regression analysis of miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p expression levels. This composite marker demonstrates superior diagnostic performance with 78.4% sensitivity and 92.3% specificity, significantly outperforming individual miRNA markers and conventional serological tests.  

The diagnostic method comprises measuring expression levels of the specified miRNAs in plasma samples using techniques including but not limited to quantitative PCR, microarray analysis, or Northern blotting. The invention includes protocols for sample preparation, RNA isolation, normalization using synthetic controls, and data analysis to determine disease probability. Diagnostic reagents incorporating nucleic acid probes or primers specific for the target miRNAs form another essential component of the invention.  

### Effect of the Invention  

The present invention provides multiple advantages over existing RA diagnostic methods. The identified miRNA biomarkers offer substantially improved specificity compared to conventional serological markers, effectively differentiating RA from osteoarthritis and systemic lupus erythematosus. The composite ePRAM value enables diagnosis with higher accuracy than individual biomarkers, particularly in seronegative patients where current methods often fail.  

The invention facilitates early diagnosis through detection of molecular changes preceding clinical manifestations, enabling timely therapeutic intervention. The method requires only standard blood collection and can be performed using common laboratory equipment, ensuring practical implementation in clinical settings. Furthermore, the correlation between miRNA expression levels and disease activity indices provides potential utility for monitoring treatment response and disease progression.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

### 1. Test Method of a Sample Derived From the Blood of a Test Subject for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis  

The invention provides a comprehensive method for diagnosing rheumatoid arthritis through analysis of plasma miRNA expression patterns. The test method begins with collection of a blood sample from a test subject using standard venipuncture techniques with EDTA anticoagulant. Following centrifugation to separate cellular components, the plasma fraction is stored at -80°C until analysis to preserve miRNA integrity.  

Total RNA extraction is performed using phenol-based reagents with inclusion of synthetic C. elegans miRNA (cel-miR-39) as an external normalization control. The protocol incorporates modifications to enhance miRNA recovery, including sample dilution with RNase-free water and double extraction with organic solvents. Column purification ensures removal of contaminants that could inhibit downstream enzymatic reactions.  

For miRNA quantification, the invention employs multiple complementary techniques. Reverse transcription converts isolated miRNAs to cDNA using miRNA-specific stem-loop primers. Quantitative real-time PCR analysis is then performed with SYBR Green chemistry or TaqMan probes targeting miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p. Alternative detection methods including miRNA microarray analysis and Northern blotting are equally applicable.  

Data analysis involves normalization of target miRNA Ct values against the spiked-in cel-miR-39 control to account for variations in RNA isolation efficiency. Absolute miRNA concentrations are calculated using standard curves generated from synthetic oligonucleotides. The invention specifies particular primer sequences that ensure specific amplification of the target miRNAs without cross-reactivity to related sequences.  

Diagnostic determination incorporates several analytical approaches. Individual miRNA concentrations are compared against predetermined cutoff values (1.46 pM for miR-24, 8.17×10-2 pM for miR-125a-5p) established through receiver operating characteristic analysis. The composite ePRAM value is calculated using the logistic regression formula: ePRAM = exp(-x)/(1+exp(-x)), where x represents the linear combination of normalized miRNA expression values.  

The method demonstrates particular utility for diagnosis of ACPA-negative RA cases, where it achieves 73% positivity compared to conventional serological markers. Additional embodiments include correlation of miRNA levels with clinical disease activity indices (DAS28) to assess disease severity and monitoring therapeutic response. The invention further encompasses variations in sample processing, including alternative RNA isolation methods, different normalization strategies, and adaptation to high-throughput analysis platforms.  

### 2. Diagnostic Reagent for Determining the Pathology of Rheumatoid Arthritis  

The invention provides specialized diagnostic reagents for detection of rheumatoid arthritis-associated miRNAs. The core component comprises nucleic acid probes or primers specifically hybridizing to miR-24, miR-125a-5p, and miR-30a-5p. These oligonucleotides are designed with optimal length (18-25 bases) and melting temperature (55-65°C) to ensure specific binding under standardized hybridization conditions.  

Preferred embodiments include TaqMan probes with 5' fluorescent reporter and 3' quencher dyes for real-time PCR detection. The invention specifies particular probe sequences complementary to mature miRNA targets while avoiding cross-reactivity with precursor forms or related miRNA family members. Stem-loop reverse transcription primers enable efficient cDNA synthesis from the small miRNA templates.  

The diagnostic reagent may be provided as a kit format incorporating multiple components:  
1) Specific nucleic acid probes and primers for target miRNA detection  
2) Control synthetic miRNAs for standardization  
3) Reverse transcription and PCR enzyme mixes  
4) Optimized reaction buffers  
5) Internal reference miRNAs for data normalization  

Alternative embodiments include microarray platforms with immobilized probes for simultaneous detection of multiple RA-associated miRNAs. The invention also encompasses variations in probe chemistry, including locked nucleic acid (LNA) modifications to enhance binding affinity and specificity. Additional kit components may include reagents for anti-CCP antibody measurement to enable combined diagnostic analysis.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Method  

Validation studies were conducted using plasma samples from 102 RA patients and 104 healthy controls. Blood samples were collected in EDTA tubes and centrifuged at 1400g for 7 minutes to obtain platelet-poor plasma. Total RNA was extracted using a phenol-chloroform method with spiked-in cel-miR-39 for normalization.  

For miRNA profiling, the inventors employed TaqMan miRNA arrays screening 768 human miRNAs. Candidate miRNAs showing differential expression were validated by quantitative RT-PCR using SYBR Green chemistry. Absolute miRNA concentrations were determined using standard curves generated from synthetic oligonucleotides.  

Statistical analysis included receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to determine optimal diagnostic cutoff values. Multivariate logistic regression identified the most predictive miRNA combinations. Clinical correlations were assessed using Pearson's correlation coefficients against standard disease activity measures.  

### Results  

Initial microarray screening identified 26 candidate miRNAs differentially expressed in RA plasma. Subsequent validation narrowed these to 11 significant markers, with miR-24, miR-125a-5p and miR-26a showing the strongest discrimination (AUC 0.80-0.85). The composite ePRAM score combining miR-24, miR-125a-5p and miR-30a-5p achieved superior diagnostic performance (AUC 0.89).  

The method demonstrated 78.4% sensitivity and 92.3% specificity at the optimal ePRAM cutoff of 0.50. Importantly, the test maintained diagnostic accuracy in ACPA-negative patients (73% positive) and showed minimal overlap with osteoarthritis or SLE cases (75-91% specificity). miRNA levels correlated with clinical disease activity indices, suggesting potential utility for monitoring therapeutic response.  

## COMPARATIVE EXAMPLE  

Analysis of miR-24 and miR-125a-5p expression in osteoarthritis (n=24) and systemic lupus erythematosus (n=11) patients confirmed the specificity of these markers for RA. Both miRNAs showed significantly lower levels in non-RA conditions compared to RA patients (p<0.01), with 79-91% of samples correctly classified as RA-negative. These results demonstrate the superior specificity of the invention compared to conventional serological markers which often show cross-reactivity.
