## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present disclosure relates to the field of molecular biology and genomics, specifically to methods for genome-wide detection and mapping of chromosomal fragile sites (CFSs) and double-strand breaks (DSBs). More particularly, the invention provides a novel approach for directly labeling single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) and DSBs within chromosomal DNA to predict and identify sites of chromosome fragility, particularly under conditions of replication stress.  

## BACKGROUND  

Chromosomal breakage syndromes encompass a group of genetic disorders characterized by increased susceptibility to chromosomal instability and breakage. These syndromes, including Ataxia-telangiectasia, Bloom syndrome, Xeroderma pigmentosum, Nijmegen breakage syndrome, and Fanconi anemia, are often associated with defects in DNA repair and replication mechanisms. Chromosomal breakage arises due to various endogenous and exogenous factors, including replication stress, oxidative damage, and exposure to clastogenic agents.  

Chromosome fragile sites are specific genomic regions prone to breakage under conditions that impair DNA replication or repair. These sites are often associated with genomic rearrangements observed in cancers and other genetic disorders. The molecular mechanisms underlying fragility at these sites remain incompletely understood, though replication fork stalling and collapse are implicated as key contributors.  

Replication fork progression is tightly regulated to ensure accurate genome duplication. Under replication stress, such as nucleotide depletion induced by hydroxyurea (HU), forks stall and may collapse, leading to the formation of ssDNA gaps and DSBs. The Mec1/ATR checkpoint pathway plays a critical role in stabilizing stalled forks and preventing breakage. In checkpoint-deficient cells, such as mec1 mutants, replication stress leads to extensive ssDNA accumulation and subsequent chromosome breakage.  

Current methods for mapping chromosomal breakage and fragile sites rely on indirect approaches, such as immunoprecipitation of DNA repair proteins (e.g., γ-H2AX) or enrichment of ssDNA. These methods suffer from limitations, including false positives due to non-specific binding or inability to distinguish between true DSBs and internal ssDNA gaps. There remains a need for a direct, high-resolution method to map chromosomal fragility and predict breakage sites based on ssDNA formation.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a genome-wide method for detecting and mapping chromosomal fragile sites by directly labeling ssDNA and DSBs in chromosomal DNA. The method involves stabilizing cells in a matrix to preserve genomic integrity, isolating chromosomal DNA, and labeling ssDNA and DSBs using template-dependent polymerases or end-repair enzymes. The labeled DNA is then isolated, sequenced, and analyzed to identify sites of fragility and breakage.  

Key steps of the method include:  
1. Embedding cells in a matrix to stabilize chromosomal DNA during processing.  
2. Preparing genomic DNA by removing cell walls, membranes, and proteins.  
3. Labeling ssDNA using a template-dependent polymerase (e.g., Klenow fragment) and labeled nucleotides.  
4. Labeling DSBs using end-repair enzymes (e.g., T4 DNA polymerase) and labeled nucleotides.  
5. Isolating labeled DNA via affinity purification (e.g., streptavidin beads for biotinylated labels).  
6. Processing labeled DNA for high-throughput sequencing.  
7. Mapping breakage sites using sequencing data and correlating with replication fork progression.  

The method is applicable to various cell types, including yeast and mammalian cells, and can be used to study chromosomal fragility in genetic disorders, cancer, and exposure to clastogenic agents. Additionally, the invention provides kits and systems for high-throughput screening of fragile sites and monitoring treatment efficacy in diseases associated with chromosomal instability.

### Predictive Nature of ssDNA on DSBs  
The invention is based on the discovery that ssDNA formation precedes and predicts DSBs at chromosomal fragile sites. By directly labeling ssDNA, the method identifies regions of replication stress that are prone to breakage.  

### Direct Labeling of Chromosomal DNA  
Cells are embedded in a matrix (e.g., agarose) to stabilize chromosomal DNA during processing. Genomic DNA is prepared by removing cell walls/membranes and proteins. ssDNA is labeled using a template-dependent polymerase (e.g., Klenow) and labeled nucleotides (e.g., Cy-dUTP). DSBs are labeled using end-repair enzymes (e.g., T4 DNA polymerase) and labeled nucleotides.  

### Isolation and Sequencing of Labeled DNA  
Labeled DNA is isolated using affinity ligands (e.g., streptavidin beads) and processed for high-throughput sequencing. Sequencing methods include pyrosequencing, reversible terminator sequencing, and real-time single-molecule sequencing.  

### Applications  
The method is applicable to yeast and mammalian cells. It can identify fragile sites in chromosomal breakage disorders, screen for clastogenic agents, and monitor treatment efficacy in cancer.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: In-Matrix Labeling to Detect ssDNA and DSBs in Yeast Chromosomes  

#### Yeast Strains and Growth Conditions  
Strains HM14-3a and WFA34 were grown in synthetic complete medium at 30°C. Cells were synchronized in G1 using alpha factor and released into S phase with HU (200 mM).  

#### In-Gel ssDNA Labeling  
Cells were embedded in agarose plugs, spheroplasted, and labeled with Cy-dUTP using Klenow fragment. Labeled DNA was eluted, sonicated, and co-hybridized to microarrays.  

#### DSB Labeling  
DSBs were labeled using the End-It DNA End-Repair Kit and Cy-dUTP. DNA was eluted, sonicated, and analyzed by microarray.  

#### Results  
ssDNA and DSB profiles correlated with replication fork progression. Breakage sites were enriched near late-firing origins, confirming that ssDNA predicts DSBs.  

### Example 2: Application to Mammalian Cells  

#### Cell Culture and Treatment  
Human lymphoblastoid cells were treated with aphidicolin to induce replication stress.  

#### In-Gel Labeling  
Cells were embedded in agarose, lysed, and labeled for ssDNA and DSBs as in Example 1.  

#### Sequencing and Analysis  
Labeled DNA was sequenced using Illumina platforms. Breakage sites were mapped and correlated with replication timing data.  

#### Results  
The method successfully identified fragile sites in human chromosomes, demonstrating its applicability to mammalian genomes.
