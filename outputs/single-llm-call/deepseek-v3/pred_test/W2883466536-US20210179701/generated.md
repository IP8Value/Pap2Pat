# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to isolated antibodies that specifically bind to platelet-derived growth factor C (PDGF-C). More particularly, the invention provides monoclonal antibodies that recognize and neutralize human PDGF-CC, along with methods of using such antibodies for therapeutic and diagnostic purposes. The antibodies of the invention are useful for modulating PDGF-CC activity in diseases and conditions associated with PDGF-CC signaling, including cancer, fibrosis, and central nervous system disorders involving blood-brain barrier dysfunction.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Platelet-derived growth factors (PDGFs) constitute a family of secreted signaling proteins that play critical roles in embryonic development, tissue homeostasis, and pathological conditions. The PDGF family consists of four polypeptide chains (PDGF-A, -B, -C, and -D) that form five dimeric isoforms (PDGF-AA, -AB, -BB, -CC, and -DD). These ligands signal through two receptor tyrosine kinases, PDGFR-α and PDGFR-β, which dimerize upon ligand binding to initiate downstream signaling cascades.  

PDGF-C is synthesized as a latent homodimer that requires proteolytic cleavage to release the active growth factor domain (GFD). Unlike other PDGF family members, PDGF-CC activation is mediated by tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) in the central nervous system (CNS) and by other proteases in peripheral tissues. The activated PDGF-CC dimer binds specifically to PDGFR-α, inducing receptor dimerization, autophosphorylation, and subsequent activation of intracellular signaling pathways that regulate cell proliferation, migration, and survival.  

PDGF-CC signaling has been implicated in various pathological processes, including tumor growth, angiogenesis, and fibrosis. In cancer biology, PDGF-CC promotes tumor progression through autocrine stimulation of cancer cells and paracrine recruitment of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs). Additionally, PDGF-CC induces angiogenesis by stimulating endothelial cells and upregulating pro-angiogenic factors such as osteopontin. Dysregulation of PDGF-CC signaling has been observed in gliomas, sarcomas, leukemias, and epithelial cancers, where it contributes to malignant transformation and treatment resistance.  

In the CNS, PDGF-CC plays a unique role in regulating blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity. Studies have demonstrated that PDGF-CC induces BBB opening through PDGFR-α activation, making it a key mediator in neurological disorders such as stroke, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, and epilepsy. Inhibition of PDGF-CC signaling has shown therapeutic potential in preclinical models of these conditions by preserving BBB function and reducing neuronal damage.  

Despite the established role of PDGF-CC in disease pathogenesis, molecular tools for specifically targeting PDGF-CC signaling have been lacking. Small molecule inhibitors of PDGFRs, such as imatinib, lack specificity for PDGF-CC and may cause off-target effects. There remains an unmet need for high-affinity monoclonal antibodies that selectively neutralize PDGF-CC activity for therapeutic and diagnostic applications.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides isolated antibodies that specifically bind to human PDGF-CC, including monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) that neutralize PDGF-CC activity. The antibodies recognize an epitope within the core active domain of PDGF-CC and inhibit PDGF-CC-induced PDGFR-α activation.  

In one embodiment, the invention provides monoclonal antibodies selected from the group consisting of 6B3, ch6B3, 11F5, 12F5, 19C7, and hu6B3. These antibodies comprise complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) with specific amino acid sequences that confer high-affinity binding to PDGF-CC. For example, antibody 6B3 comprises light chain CDRs with the sequences KASQDVGTSVA (CDR-L1), WTSTRHT (CDR-L2), and QQYNSYPLT (CDR-L3), and heavy chain CDRs with the sequences GYTFTNYWMH (CDR-H1), RINPNSGGTNYNEKFKS (CDR-H2), and EGGFGSSYWYFDV (CDR-H3).  

The invention further provides nucleic acid sequences encoding the antibody variable regions, expression vectors comprising these sequences, and host cells engineered to produce the antibodies. The antibodies can be produced as full-length immunoglobulins, Fab fragments, single-chain variable fragments (scFvs), or other antigen-binding derivatives.  

Methods of using the anti-PDGF-C antibodies include modulating PDGF-CC activity in a cell, inhibiting PDGF-CC autocrine or paracrine signaling, reducing angiogenesis, decreasing stromal support, reducing cell viability, and decreasing tumor size. The antibodies are particularly useful for modulating PDGF-CC activity in the blood-brain barrier, including inhibiting PDGF-CC-mediated opening of the barrier and reducing barrier disruption caused by CNS pathologies.  

The invention also provides diagnostic assays for detecting PDGF-C peptides, drug screening assays for identifying PDGF-CC inhibitors, and kits containing anti-PDGF-C antibodies for research or clinical use. Additionally, the invention encompasses transgenic animals expressing human PDGF-C protein for studying PDGF-CC signaling and testing therapeutic antibodies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

PDGF-C has emerged as a molecular hallmark for carcinomas due to its overexpression in various cancer types and its role in promoting tumor angiogenesis and stromal recruitment. The present invention provides isolated antibodies that specifically interact with PDGF-C, enabling modulation of PDGF-CC activity for therapeutic purposes.  

The anti-PDGF-C antibodies of the invention bind to an epitope within the core active domain of human PDGF-C protein (SEQ ID NO: 1). The antibodies may also bind variants of PDGF-C that retain biological activity, including naturally occurring isoforms, proteolytic fragments, and engineered mutants with at least 80% sequence identity to the wild-type protein.  

The epitope recognized by the antibodies is conformational and includes residues within the growth factor domain of PDGF-CC. For example, antibody 6B3 binds to a peptide sequence within the PDGF-CC core domain (residues 230-245), as demonstrated by biosensor analysis and competitive binding assays. This epitope is distinct from the receptor-binding interface, allowing the antibodies to neutralize PDGF-CC activity without interfering with PDGFR-α structure.  

The antibodies comprise two heavy chains and two light chains, each containing variable and constant domains. The variable domains include framework regions (FRs) and complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) that collectively form the antigen-binding site. The antibodies may be of any isotype, including IgA, IgD, IgE, IgG, and IgM, with IgG1 being preferred for therapeutic applications due to its effector functions.  

The invention encompasses antibodies with modified Fc regions to enhance or reduce effector functions such as complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC). For example, mutations in the CH2 domain can reduce Fcγ receptor binding while preserving antigen recognition. Alternatively, the antibodies may be engineered as Fab fragments or scFvs to eliminate Fc-mediated effects entirely.  

Nucleic acid molecules encoding the antibody heavy and light chains are provided, along with expression vectors for producing the antibodies in host cells. The vectors may include promoters (e.g., CMV, EF-1α), selection markers (e.g., glutamine synthetase, neomycin resistance), and secretion signals to facilitate recombinant protein production. Suitable host cells include mammalian cell lines (e.g., CHO, HEK293), bacterial cells (e.g., E. coli), yeast (e.g., Pichia), and insect cells (e.g., Sf9).  

The antibodies can be humanized to reduce immunogenicity by grafting murine CDRs onto human framework regions. Additional modifications include affinity maturation through random or site-directed mutagenesis, conjugation to polymers (e.g., PEG) to improve pharmacokinetics, and formulation with stabilizers (e.g., trehalose) for long-term storage.  

### Treatment Methods and Pharmaceutical Formulations  

The anti-PDGF-C antibodies are useful for treating diseases associated with PDGF-CC signaling, including cancers (e.g., gliomas, breast cancer, prostate cancer), fibrotic disorders (e.g., liver fibrosis, lung fibrosis), and CNS conditions involving BBB dysfunction (e.g., stroke, traumatic brain injury).  

Administration routes include intravenous, subcutaneous, intrathecal, intranasal, and direct CNS delivery (e.g., intracerebroventricular). For BBB modulation, the antibodies may be administered prophylactically before a predicted barrier disruption (e.g., prior to stroke) or therapeutically after injury onset.  

The antibodies can be combined with other therapies, such as chemotherapeutic agents for cancer or tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) for stroke. Pharmaceutical formulations may include buffers (e.g., PBS), surfactants (e.g., polysorbate 80), cryoprotectants (e.g., glycerol), and carriers (e.g., human serum albumin) to maintain antibody stability.  

Dosage regimens depend on disease severity, with typical ranges of 0.1-20 mg/kg administered weekly or biweekly. For CNS delivery, lower doses (0.01-1 mg/kg) may be effective due to the concentrated biodistribution.  

### PDGF-C/PDGF-CC Detection/Diagnosis Assays  

The antibodies enable sensitive detection of PDGF-C in biological samples using immunoassays. In a two-site sandwich assay, a capture antibody binds PDGF-C, which is then detected by a labeled secondary antibody. Alternatively, competitive assays measure PDGF-C levels by inhibiting antibody binding to immobilized antigen.  

Detection methods include enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), Western blotting, immunohistochemistry, and flow cytometry. Reporter molecules such as horseradish peroxidase (HRP), alkaline phosphatase (AP), or fluorescent dyes (e.g., FITC) can be conjugated to the antibodies for signal amplification.  

### Kits  

Diagnostic kits comprise anti-PDGF-C antibodies, detection reagents (e.g., enzyme substrates), wash buffers, and control samples (e.g., recombinant PDGF-C). The kits may include software for quantitative analysis and instructions for performing assays in clinical or research settings.  

### Drug Screening Assay  

The antibodies facilitate high-throughput screening for PDGF-CC inhibitors. In one assay format, test compounds compete with labeled antibody for binding to immobilized PDGF-CC, with reduced signal indicating potential inhibitors. Alternatively, cell-based assays measure PDGFR-α phosphorylation in the presence of candidate drugs.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1—Generation of Monoclonal Antibodies to Human PDGF-CC  

Mice were immunized with recombinant human PDGF-CC core domain protein, and hybridomas were generated by fusing splenocytes with myeloma cells. Supernatants from 16 hybridomas showed strong reactivity to PDGF-CC in ELISA and Western blot. Six clones (6B3, 9A5, 11F5, 19A1, 19B1, 19C7) were selected for further characterization based on IgG production and specificity.  

Purified IgGs from these clones recognized human PDGF-CC but not mouse PDGF-CC in immunoblots. Biosensor analysis revealed dissociation constants (KD) in the low nanomolar range, indicating high-affinity binding. Cross-competition studies showed that clones 6B3 and 19C7 bind overlapping epitopes, while 11F5 recognizes a distinct site.  

### Example 2—Amino Acid Sequence of Antibodies  

The variable heavy and light chain sequences of clones 6B3, 11F5, and 19C7 were determined. Clones 6B3 and 19C7 shared identical CDRs, explaining their similar epitope specificity.  

### Example 3—Generation of Chimeric Anti-PDGF-CC Antibody 6B3 (ch6B3)  

The murine variable regions of 6B3 were cloned into vectors containing human IgG1 constant regions. The chimeric antibody (ch6B3) retained PDGF-CC binding affinity and neutralizing activity comparable to the parental murine antibody.  

### Example 4—Identification of the 6B3 Binding Epitope on PDGF-C  

Biosensor analysis mapped the 6B3 epitope to a peptide within the PDGF-CC core domain (residues 230-245). Preincubation with this peptide blocked 6B3 binding to PDGF-CC in a dose-dependent manner.  

### Example 5—Biosensor Confirmation of Human PDGF-CC Specificity  

The antibodies showed no cross-reactivity with PDGF-DD or other PDGF family members. Affinity measurements confirmed sub-nanomolar binding to human PDGF-CC.  

### Example 6—PDGF-Ligand Induced Receptor Activation and Cell Proliferation  

Antibody 6B3 neutralized PDGF-CC-induced PDGFR-α phosphorylation in porcine aorta endothelial (PAE) cells. It also inhibited PDGF-CC-stimulated PAE cell proliferation with an IC50 of 2 nM.  

### Example 7—Immunohistochemistry—Tissue Screens  

Antibody 6B3 detected PDGF-CC expression in normal and tumor tissues, with significantly higher levels in cancers of the bladder, brain, breast, colon, kidney, pancreas, and prostate (p<0.05).  

### Example 8—In Vivo Therapy Studies  

In xenograft models, 6B3 treatment reduced tumor volume by 30% and decreased microvessel density. Orthotopic breast cancer models showed enhanced sensitivity to endocrine therapy after 6B3 administration.  

### Example 9—Modulation of PDGF-CC Mediated Opening of the Blood-Brain Barrier by Anti-PDGF-C Antibody 6B3  

Intraperitoneal injection of ch6B3 prevented PDGF-CC-induced blood-retinal barrier leakage in mice. The antibody crossed the compromised barrier and neutralized PDGF-CC activity in the CNS.  

In stroke models, ch6B3 reduced infarct volume by 40% when administered prophylactically and by 25% as a therapeutic intervention. Hemorrhage scores were significantly lower in treated animals compared to controls (p<0.01).  

These examples demonstrate the utility of anti-PDGF-C antibodies for treating PDGF-CC-associated diseases, particularly those involving BBB dysfunction. The antibodies provide a targeted approach to modulate PDGF-CC signaling with high specificity and minimal off-target effects.
