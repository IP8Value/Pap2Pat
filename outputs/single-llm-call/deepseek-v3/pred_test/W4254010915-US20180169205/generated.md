# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to the field of immunology and vaccine development. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel compositions and methods for inducing T cell-independent (TI) immune responses using engineered hybrid flagellin proteins. The invention provides polymeric vaccine scaffolds capable of stimulating robust antibody production even in T cell-deficient settings, thereby addressing critical limitations in current vaccination strategies.  

### Related Art  

The adaptive immune response traditionally relies on interactions between T helper cells and B cells to generate pathogen-specific antibodies. However, in T cell-deficiency settings such as AIDS, chemotherapy, or congenital immunodeficiencies, this T cell-dependent (TD) mode of antibody production becomes ineffective. Certain innate-like B cell populations, including marginal zone B cells and B-1 B cells, can become activated through B cell receptor (BCR) clustering induced by repetitive antigenic epitopes, independent of T cell help. Efficient BCR clustering requires antigens capable of crosslinking 10-20 BCRs spaced approximately 35 nm apart, necessitating TI antigens of at least 500 nm length with epitopes spaced 5-10 nm apart.  

Current TI antigens have limitations in their ability to consistently induce robust immune responses. Bacterial flagellin (FliC), a major structural protein of bacterial flagella, possesses inherent properties that make it an attractive candidate for vaccine development. FliC from Salmonella typhimurium forms filaments up to 15 μm in length with an 11-fold symmetry, meeting the spatial requirements for effective BCR crosslinking. The FliC protein comprises four domains (D0-D3), with the D3 domain being solvent-exposed in polymerized filaments while other domains remain buried within the filament core. Structural studies have demonstrated that the D3 domain is dispensable for filament stability, making it an ideal site for insertion of heterologous antigenic domains.  

While bacterial flagellin has been explored for its adjuvant properties through Toll-like receptor 5 (TLR5) activation, its potential as a scaffold for TI vaccines remains underexplored. Previous attempts to utilize flagellin as a vaccine component have focused primarily on its TD immunostimulatory properties, neglecting its inherent structural advantages for TI responses. There exists an unmet need for vaccine platforms capable of eliciting protective immune responses in immunocompromised individuals and other settings where conventional TD vaccines are ineffective.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention addresses critical limitations in current vaccine strategies by providing novel methods and compositions for inducing both T cell-dependent and T cell-independent immune responses. The invention is based on the unexpected discovery that engineered hybrid flagellin proteins can serve as potent scaffolds for vaccine development, particularly in immunocompromised hosts.  

The invention provides a method of inducing an immune response comprising administering to a subject a composition containing a hybrid flagellin protein. This hybrid protein comprises a flagellin backbone modified to incorporate at least one heterologous antigenic domain, wherein the hybrid protein retains the ability to polymerize into filaments while presenting the heterologous antigen in a repetitive array. The polymeric nature of these filaments enables effective BCR crosslinking and subsequent TI B cell activation.  

In specific embodiments, the hybrid flagellin protein contains a replacement of the native D3 domain with a heterologous antigen, such as the envelope glycoprotein from Dengue virus serotype 2 (DENV2 E). The invention encompasses various aspects including the design of hybrid proteins, methods of production and purification, formulations for vaccine compositions, and administration protocols. The vaccine composition may further include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, adjuvants, or nucleic acids encoding the hybrid flagellin proteins.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for vaccine development based on engineered hybrid flagellin proteins. The hybrid proteins combine the structural advantages of flagellin filaments with the antigenic properties of heterologous pathogen-derived proteins, creating an innovative platform for both TD and TI vaccination strategies.  

The hybrid protein composition comprises a flagellin backbone modified to incorporate at least one heterologous antigen while maintaining the ability to polymerize into filaments. In preferred embodiments, the heterologous antigen replaces the D3 domain of flagellin, allowing surface exposure of the antigen in polymerized filaments. The antigen may be derived from various infectious agents or pathogens, including but not limited to viral envelope proteins, bacterial surface antigens, or parasitic proteins.  

The FliC flagellin protein serves as the structural scaffold, providing domains necessary for polymerization (D0 and D1) while accommodating antigenic insertions in the D3 domain. The invention contemplates multiple hybrid flagellin proteins incorporating different antigenic domains, allowing for multiplexed vaccine formulations targeting multiple pathogens or serotypes.  

Administration of the hybrid flagellin proteins induces a robust immune response characterized by production of IgM, IgA, and various IgG isotypes. The polymeric form of the hybrid protein demonstrates superior immunogenicity compared to monomeric forms, likely due to enhanced BCR crosslinking and antigen presentation. The invention provides methods for subject administration through various routes including intranasal, intraperitoneal, intramuscular, or subcutaneous delivery.  

Vaccine compositions according to the invention may include adjuvants to further enhance immune responses, though the polymeric flagellin itself exhibits inherent adjuvant properties through TLR5 activation. Pharmaceutically acceptable carriers suitable for vaccine formulations are also provided, including buffers, stabilizers, and excipients compatible with the hybrid proteins.  

The invention further encompasses nucleic acid preparations encoding the hybrid flagellin proteins, allowing for alternative vaccine strategies involving genetic immunization. Expression systems for producing the hybrid proteins include baculovirus, bacterial, or mammalian cell systems optimized for high yield production of properly folded proteins.  

### Example 1  

The design of the hFliC protein involved replacing the D3 domain of native FliC (residues 185-285) with the extracellular domain of DENV2 E protein, connected via flexible glycine-serine linkers. PCR amplification was used to generate flagellin fragments corresponding to residues 1-184 and 286-494, which were then ligated to the DENV2 E sequence. The final gene construct was cloned into the pENTR plasmid for baculovirus expression.  

Protein production utilized a baculovirus expression system in SF9 insect cells cultured in serum-free media. Secreted protein was purified from culture supernatant using ammonium sulfate precipitation followed by cobalt affinity chromatography. Protein purity and molecular weight (~85 kDa) were verified by Coomassie staining and Western blot using anti-HisTag antibodies.  

Circular dichroism spectroscopy confirmed that the hFliC protein maintained secondary structural features of both parent proteins, exhibiting characteristic α-helical regions from flagellin and β-sheet regions from DENV2 E. Molecular modeling of hFliC filaments predicted an increased diameter of 35 nm compared to 20 nm for wild-type FliC filaments, with DENV2 E domains exposed on the filament surface.  

Polymerization of hFliC was achieved in high molar ammonium sulfate or sodium citrate solutions, similar to conditions for wild-type FliC. Negative stain transmission electron microscopy confirmed filament formation and the predicted increase in diameter. ELISA assays demonstrated that polymerized hFliC retained antigenicity, showing specific binding to DENV2 E-specific monoclonal IgA antibodies.  

Immunogold labeling using DENV2-specific antibodies confirmed surface exposure of the DENV2 E domains on hFliC filaments. B cell crosslinking experiments with 4G2 B cell hybridomas demonstrated that hFliC filaments could cluster BCRs, a critical requirement for TI activation.  

An immunization protocol was developed involving priming doses (intranasal or intraperitoneal) followed by three intranasal booster doses at weekly intervals. Serum antibody titers were quantified by ELISA at various time points. Wild-type C57BL/6J mice receiving polymeric hFliC showed robust IgM, IgG, and IgA responses, with route-dependent variations in isotype profiles.  

### Example 2  

Further characterization of hFliC focused on its performance in T cell-restricted models. The D3 domain replacement strategy was validated by demonstrating that the inserted DENV2 E domain maintained independent conformational integrity while allowing filament polymerization. Protein expression and purification were optimized to yield preparations with >85% purity as assessed by Coomassie staining and Western blot.  

Circular dichroism spectra of hFliC showed a hybrid profile combining α-helical characteristics from flagellin and β-sheet features from DENV2 E. Negative stain TEM confirmed filament formation with increased diameter (35 nm) compared to wild-type FliC (20 nm). Immunogold labeling localized the DENV2 E domains to the filament surface.  

In wild-type C57BL/6J mice, polymeric hFliC induced stronger humoral responses than monomeric forms, particularly when administered intraperitoneally. Serum analysis revealed robust IgM, IgG (including IgG1, IgG2a, IgG2b), and IgA responses depending on administration route.  

Most significantly, hFliC filaments elicited substantial antibody responses in 6.5-TCR transgenic mice with restricted T cell repertoires. These T cell-deficient mice mounted significant IgM and IgG3 responses to intraperitoneal hFliC administration, demonstrating the TI activation capacity of the vaccine platform. The IgG3 isotype is particularly indicative of TI responses, supporting the utility of this approach in immunocompromised hosts.  

In vitro studies confirmed that hFliC filaments could crosslink BCRs on antigen-specific B cells, providing a mechanistic basis for the observed TI responses. The polymeric nature of the filaments, with repetitive surface display of antigenic domains, enables the critical BCR clustering required for TI activation.  

## CONCLUSIONS  

The present invention provides a novel vaccine platform based on engineered hybrid flagellin proteins that can induce robust immune responses even in T cell-deficient settings. By replacing the solvent-exposed D3 domain of flagellin with heterologous antigens, the invention creates polymeric vaccine scaffolds that combine the structural advantages of flagellin filaments with the immunogenic properties of pathogen-derived proteins.  

Key advantages of this platform include: (1) the ability to present antigens in highly repetitive arrays optimal for BCR crosslinking; (2) intrinsic adjuvant properties through TLR5 activation; (3) flexibility to incorporate diverse antigenic domains; and (4) proven efficacy in both TD and TI immune activation. The demonstration of significant antibody responses in T cell-restricted mouse models supports the potential of this approach for vaccinating immunocompromised populations.  

The invention has particular relevance for developing vaccines against challenging pathogens like Dengue virus, where conventional approaches have shown limited success. The modular design allows for incorporation of antigens from multiple serotypes, potentially overcoming issues of imbalanced immune responses seen with other vaccine strategies. Furthermore, the platform's ability to stimulate mucosal IgA responses suggests potential for protection against pathogens entering through mucosal surfaces.  

This hybrid flagellin platform represents a significant advance in vaccine technology, particularly for populations with compromised T cell function. The invention provides both compositions and methods enabling new vaccination strategies against diverse pathogens while addressing critical limitations in current approaches.
