# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for sustained inhibition of gene expression in eukaryotic cells. More particularly, the invention provides a novel hybrid mRNA/DNA system that enables long-term cytoplasmic expression of short hairpin RNA (shRNA) without requiring nuclear entry of genetic material. The invention further relates to delivery vehicles and methods for administering the composition to target tissues, particularly for therapeutic applications in cancer treatment and other diseases requiring prolonged gene silencing.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

RNA interference (RNAi) represents a powerful biological mechanism for sequence-specific silencing of gene expression through the action of small interfering RNAs (siRNAs). These siRNAs, typically 21-23 base pairs in length, guide the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC) to degrade complementary mRNA targets. While siRNA technology holds tremendous therapeutic potential, its clinical application has been limited by the transient nature of siRNA-mediated gene silencing, which typically lasts only 2-4 days in cell culture systems.  

Small interfering RNA applications have expanded significantly since their discovery, with numerous delivery systems developed including nanoparticles, liposomes, and viral vectors. However, conventional siRNA delivery methods face substantial challenges in achieving sustained gene silencing effects. Chemically synthesized siRNAs provide only temporary suppression of target genes due to their rapid degradation and dilution during cell division.  

Current limitations of cationic transporters for siRNA delivery include nonspecific accumulation in tissues, cytotoxicity, and inefficient release of oligonucleotide payloads within cells. Viral vectors such as adenoviruses and lentiviruses can achieve longer-term gene silencing through stable integration and expression of shRNA constructs, but raise significant safety concerns including immunogenicity and insertional mutagenesis. Furthermore, viral vector production presents manufacturing challenges that limit clinical scalability.  

Plasmid DNA-based shRNA expression systems that rely on nuclear RNA polymerase III promoters (such as U6 and H1) face critical limitations due to inefficient nuclear entry of exogenous DNA. Only approximately 1% of cytoplasmic plasmid DNA reaches the nucleus, with particularly poor nuclear transport in post-mitotic or non-dividing cells. This nuclear dependence severely restricts both the efficiency and duration of shRNA expression from plasmid systems.  

Existing cytoplasmic shRNA expression systems that utilize bacteriophage T7 RNA polymerase still require initial nuclear entry of plasmid DNA to drive T7 polymerase expression. These systems fail to achieve sustained shRNA expression and do not overcome the fundamental limitation of nuclear dependence. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a truly nucleus-independent system for prolonged cytoplasmic shRNA expression and subsequent gene silencing.  

## DISCLOSURE  

### Technical Problem  

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the transient nature and nuclear dependence of current RNA interference technologies. Existing siRNA and shRNA delivery systems cannot achieve sustained gene silencing without repeated administration or nuclear entry of genetic material. There exists an unmet need for compositions and methods that enable prolonged, nucleus-independent inhibition of gene expression for therapeutic applications.  

### Technical Solution  

The invention provides a composition for sustained inhibition of gene expression comprising three key components: 1) a 5'-capped mRNA fragment encoding T7 RNA polymerase, 2) a plasmid DNA construct for self-amplification of T7 RNA polymerase, and 3) a DNA fragment encoding a gene expression inhibitor under control of a T7 promoter. This hybrid mRNA/DNA system enables cytoplasmic expression of shRNA without requiring nuclear entry of any genetic material.  

T7 RNA polymerase is a DNA-dependent RNA polymerase from bacteriophage T7 that recognizes specific T7 promoter sequences and can transcribe RNA in the cytoplasm without requiring host cell cofactors. The self-amplification mechanism involves initial translation of delivered T7 polymerase mRNA, followed by iterative transcription and translation of T7 polymerase from the autogene plasmid through an autocatalytic positive feedback loop.  

The plasmid DNA for T7 polymerase self-amplification contains several essential elements: an internal ribosomal entry site (IRES) domain to enhance translation efficiency, a gene encoding T7 RNA polymerase, a polyadenylation (polyA) tail for mRNA stability, and a T7 termination sequence. The IRES element, derived from encephalomyocarditis virus (EMCV), enables cap-independent translation of T7 polymerase transcripts in the cytoplasm.  

The DNA fragment encoding the gene expression inhibitor contains a T7 promoter sequence followed by sequences that produce shRNA when transcribed. The shRNA folds into a stem-loop structure that is processed by cellular machinery into mature siRNA. The system can alternatively express microRNAs or aptamers as gene expression inhibitors.  

A key advantage of the nucleus-independent T7 polymerase system is prolonged shRNA expression lasting over 13 days, compared to conventional nuclear shRNA expression systems that typically last only 3-4 days. The composition further includes a transporter, preferably liposomes composed of 1,2-dioleoyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphatidylcholine (DOPC), for efficient delivery to target tissues.  

The liposomal transporter demonstrates high loading efficiency for oligonucleotide components, with optimal complexation at a 1:10 weight ratio of oligonucleotides to DOPC. The transporter exhibits favorable biophysical properties including a hydrodynamic diameter of approximately 31 nm and minimal cytotoxicity. In vivo studies demonstrate preferential accumulation of the composition in tumor tissue through enhanced permeability and retention effects.  

The invention provides methods for preparing the composition by synthesizing the mRNA fragment, plasmid DNA, and DNA fragment encoding the gene expression inhibitor; mixing these components with DOPC lipids; and removing uncomplexed oligonucleotides by filtration. The ratio of oligonucleotides to DOPC is critical for optimal formulation, with a 1:10 weight ratio providing complete complexation.  

### Advantageous Effects of the Invention  

The invention provides several significant advantages over existing RNA interference technologies:  

1) Prolonged gene silencing effects lasting over 13 days from a single administration, compared to 2-4 days for conventional siRNA approaches  
2) Complete independence from nuclear entry requirements, enabling efficient gene silencing in both dividing and non-dividing cells  
3) High specificity and potency of gene silencing through sustained cytoplasmic production of shRNA  
4) Reduced immunogenicity compared to viral delivery systems  
5) Scalable manufacturing using synthetic biology and formulation techniques  
6) Tumor-selective accumulation through optimized liposomal delivery  
7) Minimal activation of innate immune responses as demonstrated by lack of TNF-α and INF-α induction  
8) Compatibility with systemic administration routes  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is further described with reference to the following examples, which illustrate specific embodiments without limiting the scope of the invention.  

### Example 1. Design and Preparation of pT7/T7 Polymerase System Capable of Sustained Expression of shRNA in Cytoplasm  

The auto_T7pol plasmid was designed for sustained cytoplasmic self-amplification of T7 polymerase. The plasmid contains the T7 polymerase gene downstream of a T7 promoter and EMCV IRES element, followed by a polyA tail and T7 termination sequence. The pT7/shRNA DNA template was designed with a T7 promoter driving expression of a 64-base pair hairpin structure targeting red fluorescent protein (RFP) mRNA.  

5'-capped T7pol mRNA was synthesized by in vitro transcription using anti-reverse cap analog (ARCA) to ensure proper translation initiation. As a control, pSuper_shRFP plasmid was prepared using an H1 promoter for nuclear expression of shRNA. The auto_T7pol plasmid demonstrated efficient cytoplasmic expression of T7 polymerase, while the pT7/shRNA template produced functional shRNA when transcribed by T7 polymerase.  

### Example 2. Preparation of DOPC Liposome Transporter Loaded with Sustained Cytoplasmic Expression System for shRNA  

Lipoplexes (auto_shRFP@LS) were prepared by mixing DOPC lipids with the three nucleic acid components (pT7/shRNA DNA fragment, T7pol mRNA, and T7 autogene plasmid) at a 1:10 weight ratio of oligonucleotides to lipid. The mixture was lyophilized and reconstituted in saline, with free oligonucleotides removed by ultrafiltration. The formulation achieved >90% loading efficiency of oligonucleotides into liposomes.  

### Example 3. Examination of Biophysicochemical Properties of DOPC Liposome Transporter  

The auto_shRFP@LS lipoplexes exhibited a hydrodynamic diameter of 31.4 ± 1.2 nm and near-neutral zeta potential (1.1 mV). Cytotoxicity assays demonstrated excellent biocompatibility, with >90% cell viability at concentrations up to 60 μg/mL. Confocal microscopy confirmed cytoplasmic localization of delivered nucleic acids, with no nuclear accumulation. The lipoplexes showed minimal activation of TNF-α or INF-α in human peripheral blood mononuclear cells, indicating low immunogenicity.  

### Example 4. Experiment on Sustained Expression of T7 Polymerase  

Western blot analysis demonstrated sustained T7 polymerase expression for over 9 days in cells transfected with the hybrid mRNA/DNA system. The system showed 4-fold higher T7 polymerase expression compared to conventional CMV promoter-driven systems requiring nuclear entry. The IRES element and 5'-capped mRNA were essential for efficient translation and sustained expression.  

### Example 5. Examination of Long-Term Cytoplasmic Expression of shRNAs  

Quantitative TaqMan assays showed continuous production of mature RFP siRNA for over 13 days in cells transfected with auto_shRFP@LS. siRNA levels peaked at 10 days post-transfection and remained significantly elevated at 13 days. Control systems lacking the T7 autogene plasmid showed markedly reduced siRNA production.  

### Example 6. Examination of In Vitro Gene Silencing  

The auto_shRFP@LS system achieved 54% reduction in RFP mRNA levels by day 7, with silencing effects persisting for 13 days. Western blot and flow cytometry confirmed corresponding reductions in RFP protein levels and fluorescence. The system outperformed both synthetic siRNA (75% reduction at day 3, complete recovery by day 7) and nuclear shRNA expression systems.  

### Example 7. Examination of Cancer Accumulation Property  

In tumor-bearing mice, Cy5.5-labeled auto_shRFP@LS showed preferential accumulation in tumor tissue compared to free siRNA, with 2.7-fold higher fluorescence intensity in tumors versus kidneys. The 31 nm particle size enabled effective extravasation through the enhanced permeability and retention effect.  

### Example 8. Examination of Gene Silencing in Mouse Xenograft Model  

A single intravenous injection of auto_shRFP@LS (40 μg) in RFP-expressing tumor-bearing mice produced significant RFP silencing for 6 days, compared to complete recovery by day 3 in mice treated with synthetic siRNA. Immunohistochemistry and qPCR confirmed sustained reduction of RFP protein and mRNA in tumor tissue. The system showed no significant induction of TNF-α or INF-α in vivo.  

The invention provides a versatile platform for prolonged gene silencing with applications in cancer therapy, genetic disorders, and other diseases requiring sustained modulation of gene expression. While the examples focus on RFP as a model target, the system can be adapted to silence any target gene by modifying the shRNA sequence in the pT7/shRNA DNA fragment.
