# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to wheelchairs equipped with inertial sensors for detecting destabilizing events and providing functional neuromuscular stimulation to enhance trunk stability. More particularly, the invention pertains to a wheelchair system comprising motion sensors, a controller, and neural stimulating electrodes that activate paralyzed trunk and hip muscles in response to measured linear or angular motion parameters indicative of collisions or sharp turns. The system improves postural stability for wheelchair users with spinal cord injuries by automatically applying stimulation when destabilizing events are detected, thereby reducing the risk of falls and injuries.  

## BACKGROUND  

Wheelchair users, particularly those with spinal cord injuries (SCI), face significant challenges in maintaining trunk stability during daily mobility. Approximately two-thirds of the 285,000 individuals in the United States living with SCI rely on wheelchairs, and trunk instability is among their top functional concerns. Loss of trunk control leads to postural sway, destabilization, and an increased risk of falls, which account for over 100,000 wheelchair-related injuries annually. These injuries often result in fractures, lacerations, and other complications requiring extensive rehabilitation, with treatment costs ranging from $25,000 to $75,000 per incident.  

Current solutions for maintaining trunk stability, such as seat belts, specialized cushions, or powered wheelchair seating systems, impose significant limitations on voluntary movement and functional reach. These methods are often poorly adopted due to discomfort, restricted mobility, and psychological reluctance to use assistive devices perceived as indicative of greater disability. Functional neuromuscular stimulation (FNS) has shown promise in restoring trunk stability by activating paralyzed muscles, but existing systems lack real-time responsiveness to destabilizing events such as collisions or sharp turns.  

There remains an unmet need for a wheelchair system that automatically detects destabilizing events and provides timely neuromuscular stimulation to enhance stability without restricting voluntary movement. The present invention addresses this need by integrating inertial sensors with neural stimulation to improve safety and independence for wheelchair users.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention introduces a wheelchair system that enhances trunk stability through real-time detection of destabilizing events and automated functional neuromuscular stimulation. The system comprises a motion sensor, such as an inertial measurement unit (IMU), mounted on the wheelchair to measure linear and angular motion parameters. These parameters include linear acceleration and angular velocity, which are indicative of destabilizing events such as collisions or sharp turns.  

The system further includes neural stimulating electrodes attached to the user's trunk and hip muscles, such as the erector spinae, gluteus maximus, and quadratus lumborum. A controller, which may be mounted on the wheelchair or the user, receives signals from the motion sensor and evaluates them to determine whether a destabilizing event has occurred. Upon detecting an event, the controller activates the appropriate muscles via the neural stimulating electrodes to counteract the destabilizing motion.  

The motion sensor preferably includes a gyroscope and accelerometer for measuring angular and linear motion, respectively. A radio frequency transmitter relays sensor data to the controller, which comprises a radio frequency receiver and a microprocessor for processing the signals. The system may also include additional safety features such as a motor-controlled seat belt or an actuator-controlled brake to further enhance stability during destabilizing events. A distress indicator may also be incorporated to alert caregivers in case of an emergency.  

The invention further provides a method of providing neural stimulation in response to destabilizing events. The method involves measuring a motion parameter of the wheelchair, generating a signal indicative of the motion parameter, evaluating the signal to determine whether a destabilizing event has occurred, and activating at least one muscle in response to the signal. The muscles are selected based on the type of event detected, such as trunk extensors for collisions or lateral stabilizers for sharp turns.  

For collision detection, the method monitors linear acceleration and calculates a moving root mean square (RMS) value. This value is compared against predetermined threshold values, and neuromuscular stimulation is applied if the threshold is exceeded. Similarly, for sharp turns, the method monitors angular velocity and applies stimulation when the measured velocity exceeds a turn-specific threshold.  

The system also includes a method of applying restraint, such as tightening a seat belt or activating a brake, in response to detected destabilizing events. This multi-faceted approach ensures comprehensive stability support for wheelchair users, reducing the risk of falls and associated injuries.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The wheelchair system 10 of the present invention is designed to enhance trunk stability through real-time detection of destabilizing events and automated neuromuscular stimulation. The system comprises a motion sensor 14, which is preferably an inertial measurement unit (IMU) mounted on the rear crossbar of the wheelchair. The sensor 14 measures linear acceleration and angular velocity, providing critical data for detecting destabilizing events such as collisions or sharp turns.  

The controller 18 receives signals from the motion sensor 14 and processes them to determine whether a destabilizing event has occurred. The controller 18 includes a microprocessor 24 that evaluates the signals using software algorithms. For example, the microprocessor 24 may calculate a moving RMS of linear acceleration to detect collisions or compare angular velocity against predefined thresholds to identify sharp turns.  

Neural stimulating electrodes 26 are attached to the user's trunk and hip muscles, such as the erector spinae, gluteus maximus, and quadratus lumborum. The electrodes 26 may be implanted or surface-mounted, depending on the user's specific needs. The system 10 is designed to activate the appropriate muscles in response to destabilizing events, thereby enhancing postural stability.  

The primary goal of the system 10 is to prevent falls by providing timely neuromuscular stimulation during destabilizing events. For example, during a collision event, the system detects elevated linear acceleration and activates the trunk and hip extensor muscles to counteract forward flexion. Similarly, during a sharp turn, the system detects elevated angular velocity and activates the lateral stabilizers to prevent excessive leaning.
