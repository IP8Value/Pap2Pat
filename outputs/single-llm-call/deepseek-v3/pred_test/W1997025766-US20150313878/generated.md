# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Prostate cancer represents one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting male populations worldwide, with significant morbidity and mortality rates associated with advanced disease states. Current diagnostic methodologies rely heavily on prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing, which suffers from limited specificity leading to frequent false positives and unnecessary invasive procedures. The development of more accurate diagnostic markers remains an unmet clinical need. Existing therapeutic interventions for prostate cancer, including androgen deprivation therapy and chemotherapy, often encounter resistance mechanisms that limit their long-term efficacy.  

The limitations inherent in PSA testing underscore the necessity for novel diagnostic approaches capable of distinguishing indolent from aggressive prostate cancer phenotypes with higher precision. Furthermore, the emergence of castration-resistant prostate cancer highlights the urgent demand for alternative therapeutic strategies targeting molecular pathways independent of androgen receptor signaling. Current diagnostic and treatment paradigms fail to adequately address the heterogeneity of prostate cancer at the molecular level, necessitating the development of biomarkers and therapeutic agents that account for this biological complexity.  

The present invention addresses these critical gaps in prostate cancer management by identifying soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) as a novel biomarker and therapeutic target. The discovery of sAC overexpression in prostate carcinoma relative to benign tissue, particularly at tumor invasion fronts, provides both diagnostic utility and a molecular basis for targeted intervention. This invention fulfills the pressing need for non-invasive diagnostic methods while simultaneously offering a therapeutic approach that operates through previously unexplored cAMP signaling pathways in prostate cancer biology.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods for inhibiting the proliferation of prostate cancer cells through modulation of soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) activity. The invention encompasses both diagnostic and therapeutic applications targeting sAC-dependent signaling pathways in prostate carcinoma.  

The therapeutic aspect of the invention involves administering compounds that selectively inhibit sAC protein activity, including small molecule inhibitors such as KH7 and nucleic acid-based inhibitors such as siRNA molecules targeting sAC mRNA. These inhibitors effectively suppress prostate cancer cell proliferation by inducing cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase transition through modulation of EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf signaling cascades.  

Diagnostically, the invention provides methods for detecting prostate cancer by assaying sAC expression levels in biological samples. The methods include immunohistochemical analysis of prostate tissue samples using sAC-specific antibodies, with overexpression of sAC protein serving as an indicator of malignant transformation. The diagnostic approach further incorporates quantitative assessment of sAC expression patterns, particularly at tumor invasion fronts, to distinguish malignant from benign prostatic tissue.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

Soluble adenylyl cyclase (sAC) represents a distinct class of cAMP-producing enzymes that localize to intracellular compartments rather than plasma membranes. Unlike transmembrane adenylyl cyclases (tmACs), sAC activity is regulated by bicarbonate ions and calcium rather than G protein-coupled receptors. The invention demonstrates that sAC-generated cAMP pools exert unique control over cellular proliferation through mechanisms distinct from traditional cAMP signaling pathways.  

Nuclear translocation of sAC in prostate cancer cells establishes a spatially restricted cAMP signaling microenvironment that regulates transcription factor activity. The invention reveals that sAC migrates from cytoplasmic compartments to nuclear regions during malignant transformation, where it modulates CREB phosphorylation and subsequent gene expression programs driving cell cycle progression. This nuclear localization pattern distinguishes sAC activity in cancer cells from its physiological functions in normal tissues.  

The method of inhibiting prostate cancer cell proliferation comprises administering an effective amount of a substance that specifically reduces sAC enzymatic activity. Prostate cancer cells for purposes of this invention include both androgen-sensitive (e.g., LNCaP) and androgen-insensitive (e.g., PC3) cell lines, as well as primary tumor cells obtained from prostate cancer patients. The invention encompasses inhibition of all sAC isoforms, with particular emphasis on the somatic isoform which predominates in prostate carcinoma cells.  

Small molecule inhibitors of sAC constitute one embodiment of the invention. The compound KH7 (4-(N-(3-aminopropyl)-N-(n-butyl)amino)-2,7-difluorofluorescein diacetate) serves as a prototype sAC inhibitor, demonstrating selective inhibition of sAC activity at concentrations between 10-30 μM without affecting tmAC or soluble guanylyl cyclase activity. Structural analogs of KH7 maintaining sAC inhibitory capacity while potentially improving pharmacological properties are included within the scope of the invention.  

RNA interference technology provides another embodiment for sAC inhibition. The invention encompasses siRNA molecules targeting conserved regions of sAC mRNA, including sequences corresponding to nucleotides 353-371, 1024-1042, 1356-1374, and 1478-1496 of human sAC (NM_001167749). These siRNA constructs achieve ≥80% reduction in sAC protein expression when delivered to prostate cancer cells using lipid-based transfection reagents at concentrations of 40 nM.  

The invention further includes shRNA expression vectors for sustained sAC knockdown. An adenoviral delivery system containing the murine U6 promoter driving expression of anti-sAC shRNA (sequence: 5'-GGGGTACCAAAAAAGTGGAAAGTGGAACGAAAGCATCT-CTTGAATGCTTTCGTTCCACTTTCCACAAACAAGGC-TTTTCTCCAAG-3') effectively suppresses sAC expression in prostate cancer cells. This viral vector demonstrates cross-reactivity between rat and human sAC sequences despite incomplete homology.  

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise sAC inhibitors formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers for administration to human subjects. These compositions may be adapted for various delivery routes including intravenous, intraprostatic, or oral administration. The formulations maintain inhibitor stability while ensuring adequate bioavailability to target tissues.  

Therapeutic administration involves delivering a therapeutically effective amount of sAC inhibitor sufficient to reduce prostate cancer cell proliferation by at least 50% compared to untreated controls. Effective dosing ranges from 0.1-100 mg/kg body weight depending on the specific inhibitor compound and administration route. Treatment regimens may involve single or multiple doses adjusted according to therapeutic response and tolerability.  

The invention provides combination therapies incorporating sAC inhibitors with existing prostate cancer treatments. Such combinations may include concurrent administration with androgen deprivation therapy, chemotherapy agents, or radiation therapy. The complementary mechanisms of action between sAC inhibition and conventional treatments may produce synergistic anti-tumor effects while potentially reducing individual drug toxicity.  

Diagnostic methods of the invention involve detecting sAC overexpression as a marker of prostate cancer. The process begins with obtaining a cellular sample from a patient's prostate tissue through biopsy or surgical resection. The sample undergoes analysis for sAC gene expression or protein production using techniques including quantitative PCR, Western blotting, or immunohistochemistry.  

Immunohistochemical detection utilizes sAC-specific antibodies such as monoclonal antibody R21 (recognizing both somatic and testicular isoforms) or R40 (specific for the testicular isoform). Staining protocols incorporate heat-induced epitope retrieval, signal amplification with alkaline phosphatase, and visualization with 3,3'-diaminobenzidine. The invention provides standardized scoring systems for evaluating sAC expression levels, including an H-score calculation that incorporates both staining intensity and percentage of positive cells.  

The diagnostic approach includes comparing sAC expression levels in test samples to predetermined control values derived from benign prostatic tissue. Overexpression is defined as sAC levels exceeding control values by at least 30% as measured by H-score analysis. Particular attention is given to staining patterns at tumor invasion fronts, where sAC upregulation is most pronounced in malignant specimens.  

The invention encompasses detection of sAC splice variants, particularly the full-length (sACfl) and truncated (sACt) isoforms. Antibodies or nucleic acid probes specific for each isoform enable differential analysis of their expression patterns in prostate tissue. The somatic isoform (sACfl) demonstrates particular utility as a cancer biomarker due to its predominant expression in malignant cells.  

Antibody generation methods form part of the invention, including protocols for producing polyclonal and monoclonal antibodies against sAC epitopes. Immunogens may comprise full-length sAC protein, specific peptide fragments, or recombinant sAC domains. The invention includes antibodies raised against amino acid sequences unique to sACfl or shared between sAC isoforms.  

Hybridoma technology enables production of monoclonal antibodies such as R21 and R40, which recognize distinct sAC epitopes. The invention further encompasses engineered antibody variants including chimeric antibodies, humanized antibodies, and single-chain antibody fragments (scFv) with optimized binding characteristics for diagnostic applications.  

Antibody detection methodologies include direct and indirect labeling techniques using enzymatic, fluorescent, or radioactive tags. The invention provides standardized protocols for quantifying antibody binding signals and correlating them with sAC expression levels. These methods facilitate reproducible interpretation of immunohistochemical results across different laboratory settings.  

The invention includes methods for correlating sAC expression levels with disease prognosis. Analysis of sAC overexpression patterns combined with traditional histopathological grading (Gleason score) enhances predictive accuracy for tumor aggressiveness. The integration of sAC biomarker data with clinical parameters enables more informed treatment selection for individual patients.  

Prognostic evaluation involves comparing sAC expression in tumor samples to standardized control values and assigning a risk stratification based on the degree of overexpression. This analysis complements existing staging systems by providing molecular-level information about tumor biology. Patients exhibiting high sAC overexpression may be directed toward more aggressive treatment regimens.  

### Example 1  

This example demonstrates sAC protein overexpression in human prostate carcinoma tissues compared to benign prostatic tissue. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded prostate tissue sections were deparaffinized and subjected to heat-induced epitope retrieval using the BOND-III Autostainer system. Mouse anti-sAC monoclonal antibody R21 was applied at a 1:750 dilution, followed by alkaline phosphatase-mediated signal amplification and 3,3'-diaminobenzidine chromogen development.  

A urologic pathologist evaluated staining patterns using a standardized intensity scale (1+ weak, 2+ moderate, 3+ strong). The H-score system quantified sAC expression by incorporating both staining intensity and percentage of positive cells, calculated as (%1+ x1) + (%2+ x2) + (%3+ x3). Malignant glands exhibited significantly higher H-scores (189.2) compared to benign glands (144.3), with p<0.01.  

Subcellular localization analysis revealed cytoplasmic sAC staining in all samples, with additional nuclear staining in 38% of cases and apical/luminal staining in 22% of cases. No significant difference in localization patterns emerged between benign and malignant tissues. However, malignant specimens showed preferential sAC upregulation at tumor invasion fronts near the prostatic capsule, while benign tissues maintained consistent expression throughout the gland.  

### Example 2  

This example compares sAC isoform expression patterns in prostate cancer using multiple antibody reagents. Tissue sections were processed as in Example 1 and stained with three distinct anti-sAC antibodies: R21 (recognizing both somatic and testicular isoforms), R40 (specific for testicular isoform), and R52 (directed against a unique somatic isoform epitope).  

Quantitative analysis revealed that R21 staining intensity correlated strongly with malignancy status, while R40 showed minimal reactivity in both benign and malignant tissues. R52 demonstrated specific binding to malignant glands with minimal background in benign tissue. These findings indicate that the somatic isoform of sAC predominates in prostate carcinoma, while the testicular isoform shows limited expression.  

Comparative analysis of different tumor subtypes revealed consistent sAC overexpression across adenocarcinoma variants. High-grade tumors (Gleason score ≥8) showed more intense and widespread sAC staining compared to intermediate-grade tumors (Gleason score 6-7). The R52 antibody proved particularly effective for distinguishing malignant foci in mixed benign/malignant specimens.  

### Example 3  

This example demonstrates sAC inhibition in prostate cancer cell lines. Androgen-sensitive LNCaP and androgen-insensitive PC3 cells were cultured in DMEM with 2% fetal calf serum and treated with 20 μM KH7 or its inactive analog KH7.15 for 48 hours. Western blot analysis confirmed sAC expression in both cell lines at levels exceeding normal prostate epithelial cells (PNT2).  

KH7 treatment reduced intracellular cAMP content by 65% and decreased cell proliferation by 58% in LNCaP cells and 62% in PC3 cells. The antiproliferative effect correlated with increased lactate dehydrogenase release (indicating cytotoxicity) and caspase-3 cleavage (indicating apoptosis). Cell cycle analysis revealed G2/M phase arrest, with G2 population increasing from 18% to 42% in KH7-treated LNCaP cells.  

Parallel experiments using sAC-specific siRNA achieved ≥80% sAC knockdown and produced comparable effects on proliferation (49% reduction), cell death markers, and cell cycle distribution. These consistent findings across pharmacological and genetic inhibition methods confirm the specificity of sAC's role in prostate cancer cell proliferation.  

### Example 4  

This example elucidates the mechanism of sAC-dependent proliferation control. LNCaP cells treated with KH7 showed reduced expression of cyclin B1 (66% decrease) and CDK1 (64% decrease), key regulators of G2/M transition. Phospho-CDK1 levels decreased by 78%, indicating impaired activation of the cyclin B1-CDK1 complex.  

EPAC/Rap1 signaling emerged as the primary pathway mediating sAC's proliferative effects. KH7 treatment reduced GTP-bound Rap1 by 72% and phospho-B-Raf by 65%. The EPAC-specific activator 8-pCPT-cAMP reversed these effects and restored normal cell proliferation rates despite sAC inhibition. In contrast, PKA modulators (activator N6-benzoyl-cAMP and inhibitors H-89/Rp-cAMP-S) showed no significant effects on proliferation parameters.  

These results establish that sAC controls prostate cancer cell proliferation through EPAC/Rap1/B-Raf signaling rather than traditional PKA pathways. The invention accordingly provides methods for targeting this specific signaling axis in prostate cancer treatment.
