# DESCRIPTION  

## 1. FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of renal cell carcinoma (RCC) diagnosis through the identification and utilization of novel humoral biomarkers. Specifically, the invention discloses matrix metalloproteinase-7 (MMP-7) as a diagnostic biomarker for RCC, providing methods for detecting MMP-7 in biological samples to aid in the diagnosis, prognosis, and monitoring of RCC.  

A humoral biomarker, as defined herein, refers to a protein or peptide present in bodily fluids such as serum or plasma that can be measured to indicate the presence, progression, or regression of a disease state. The invention particularly focuses on the detection of MMP-7 and its proenzyme form (pro-MMP-7) in serum samples as indicators of RCC. The methods described herein encompass various immunoassay techniques for quantifying MMP-7 levels, including but not limited to enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) and homogeneous time-resolved fluorescence immunoassays.  

## 2. BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Renal cell carcinoma presents with nonspecific symptoms that often lead to late-stage diagnosis, including hematuria, flank pain, and palpable abdominal mass. Current diagnostic methods rely heavily on imaging techniques such as computed tomography (CT) scans and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), which are expensive and may not detect early-stage tumors.  

Existing efforts to characterize RCC have identified several potential molecular markers, including interleukin-6 and fibroblast growth factor (FGF), though none have achieved sufficient sensitivity and specificity for clinical use. Plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 has been investigated as a prognostic factor, while alterations in E-cadherin expression have been observed in RCC tissues.  

Prior art described in WO 02/082076 discloses various tumor markers but fails to provide a reliable serum biomarker for RCC. The medical community continues to face a significant unmet need for additional tumor markers that can facilitate early detection, accurate prognosis, and effective treatment monitoring of RCC.  

## 3. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention introduces MMP-7 as a novel humoral biomarker for RCC diagnosis and management. The diagnostic methods utilize quantitative measurement of MMP-7 levels in biological samples, where "level" refers to the concentration of MMP-7 protein, including both the active form and its proenzyme precursor.  

MMP-7, also known as matrilysin, is a member of the matrix metalloproteinase family that plays crucial roles in extracellular matrix remodeling. MMPs are zinc-dependent endopeptidases involved in tissue remodeling, wound healing, and pathological processes including cancer invasion and metastasis.  

While prior art has described MMP-7 overexpression in various cancers at the tissue level, the present invention represents the first disclosure of MMP-7 as a circulating biomarker specifically for RCC. The invention encompasses methods for using MMP-7 detection in RCC diagnosis, disease monitoring, treatment efficacy assessment, and recurrence detection.  

## MMP-7  

MMP-7 is a 28-29 kDa protein consisting of a signal peptide, propeptide, and catalytic domain. Activation of MMP-7 occurs through proteolytic cleavage of the propeptide, resulting in the mature 19 kDa active enzyme. The enzyme demonstrates broad substrate specificity, capable of degrading various extracellular matrix components including fibronectin, laminin, and type IV collagen.  

Under normal physiological conditions, MMP-7 expression occurs primarily in epithelial cells of various organs. However, numerous studies have documented MMP-7 overexpression in multiple cancer types, including colorectal, gastric, and breast cancers. The inventors have discovered significantly increased MMP-7 expression specifically in RCC, as demonstrated through comprehensive proteomic analysis.  

While mRNA overexpression analysis has limitations in clinical application due to tissue accessibility requirements, the inventors' discovery of MMP-7 as a circulating biomarker overcomes these limitations. The identification method combined two-dimensional electrophoresis with immunoblotting using patient sera, followed by mass spectrometric protein identification.  

The invention describes multiple applications of MMP-7 detection in RCC management. Diagnostic applications include distinguishing RCC patients from healthy individuals and patients with benign renal conditions. The method also enables monitoring of RCC treatment efficacy by tracking changes in MMP-7 levels during therapeutic interventions.  

Alternative detection methods include immunoassays for MMP-7 autoantibodies, which may serve as complementary diagnostic markers. The invention further provides specific ligand assay techniques for detecting both MMP-7 and pro-MMP-7, as well as autoantibodies against these forms.  

A diagnostic kit embodiment includes all necessary reagents for carrying out the method, such as specific antibodies, calibration standards, and detection reagents. The kit may be configured for various assay formats including but not limited to ELISA and homogeneous immunoassays.  

## 4. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION AND EXAMPLES  

Serum samples were obtained from 30 RCC patients and 30 healthy donors under approved ethical protocols. Two immunoassay formats were developed and validated: a conventional enzyme-linked immunoassay and a homogeneous immunoassay based on time-resolved amplified cryptate emission (TRACE) technology.  

The enzyme-linked immunoassay protocol involved coating microtiter plates with capture antibody, followed by sample incubation, detection antibody addition, and enzymatic signal development. A standard curve was generated using recombinant MMP-7, demonstrating linearity across the clinically relevant concentration range.  

Measurement of MMP-7 levels in RCC patient sera revealed significantly elevated concentrations (median 7.56 μg/L) compared to healthy controls (median 2.13 μg/L), with P<0.0001 by Mann-Whitney test. The TRACE-based homogeneous immunoassay showed comparable performance, with intra-assay coefficient of variation <5% and excellent recovery rates (89-109%).  

Statistical analysis included receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve generation, which demonstrated 93% clinical sensitivity at 75% specificity for RCC detection. These results establish MMP-7 as a clinically valuable serum biomarker for RCC diagnosis and management.  

The complete experimental data support the utility of MMP-7 measurement in various clinical scenarios, including initial diagnosis, treatment monitoring, and recurrence detection. The methods described herein provide clinicians with a powerful new tool for managing RCC patients throughout the disease course.
