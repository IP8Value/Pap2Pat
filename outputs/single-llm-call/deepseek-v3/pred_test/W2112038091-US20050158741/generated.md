# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The development of ribozyme-based systems for controlling gene expression represents a significant advancement in biomedical research and therapeutic applications. Traditional gene regulation technologies have primarily relied on transcriptional control mechanisms, which, while effective, present limitations in precision, efficiency, and versatility. The discovery of RNA interference and small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) expanded the toolbox for gene regulation by enabling targeted RNA degradation. However, these methods still face challenges related to specificity, delivery, and off-target effects.  

There remains a critical need for alternative gene regulation systems that operate independently of transcriptional control, offering greater flexibility and reduced interference with endogenous cellular processes. Ribozymes, or catalytic RNA molecules, present a promising solution due to their ability to self-cleave and thereby modulate mRNA stability and translation. The development of ribozyme-based systems capable of efficient self-cleavage in mammalian cells, coupled with small-molecule inhibitors to regulate this activity, opens new avenues for precise and tunable control of gene expression.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides ribozyme-based gene regulation systems that enable exogenous control of gene expression through modulation of RNA self-cleavage. The invention centers on the use of self-cleaving ribozymes, particularly modified versions derived from *Schistosoma mansoni* (Sm1), which exhibit high cleavage efficiency in mammalian cells. These ribozymes, termed N79, are engineered to include specific mutations and structural modifications that enhance their functionality and responsiveness to inhibitory compounds.  

A self-cleaving ribozyme is defined as an RNA molecule capable of catalyzing its own cleavage at a specific site without requiring protein cofactors. The invention utilizes schistosome RNA mutant motifs, which have been optimized for stability and catalytic activity in mammalian cellular environments. Key modifications include alterations to loop III, a structural element critical for ribozyme function. The loop III sequence has been modified to include four, six, or eight nucleotides, with specific substitutions at positions 5, 7, and 14 of the conserved core to further enhance cleavage activity.  

The invention further incorporates aptamer sequences grafted onto the ribozyme structure, enabling control of cleavage activity through binding of effector molecules. These aptamers are selected for their ability to bind small molecules, such as antibiotics or synthetic compounds, which modulate ribozyme activity upon binding. The grafting of aptamer sequences is achieved through standard molecular biology techniques, ensuring seamless integration into the ribozyme's secondary structure.  

DNA constructs encoding the ribozyme-based system are provided, comprising a promoter operably linked to a nucleic acid sequence encoding the ribozyme and a target gene product. Upon transcription, the mRNA undergoes self-cleavage unless inhibited by the presence of specific effectors. This mechanism allows for precise regulation of gene expression, with induction levels controllable by the concentration and type of effector administered.  

Specific embodiments of the invention include DNA constructs wherein the ribozyme is positioned within the 5' untranslated region (UTR), the coding sequence, or the 3' UTR of the target mRNA. Additional embodiments feature multiple ribozyme motifs joined in tandem to amplify the regulatory effect. Host cells, including mammalian cell lines and primary cells, are engineered to stably express these constructs, enabling scalable production and application.  

Packaging cell lines are also provided for the production of viral vectors, such as adeno-associated virus (AAV), which deliver the ribozyme system to target tissues *in vivo*. Methods for modulating gene expression in host cells include the administration of small-molecule inhibitors, such as toyocamycin or 5-fluorouridine (FUR), which block ribozyme cleavage and restore target gene expression. These methods are applicable to both *ex vivo* and *in vivo* settings, offering broad utility in research and therapy.  

The invention further encompasses methods for regulating endogenous gene expression by introducing ribozyme-containing constructs that target specific mRNAs. Antisense oligonucleotides are employed to inhibit ribozyme activity, providing an alternative strategy for gene induction. Modified antisense oligonucleotides, such as morpholinos, exhibit enhanced stability and binding affinity, improving their efficacy in cellular environments.  

High-throughput screening methods are disclosed for identifying novel inhibitors of ribozyme activity. These assays utilize reporter cell lines expressing ribozyme-regulated luciferase, enabling rapid evaluation of compound libraries. Screening efforts have identified adenosine and uridine analogs, such as toyocamycin and FUR, as potent ribozyme inhibitors. These compounds act via covalent incorporation into the ribozyme-containing mRNA, disrupting its catalytic activity.  

The invention also provides kits for regulating gene expression, comprising ribozyme-encoding DNA constructs, inhibitors, and delivery reagents. Methods for determining inhibitor levels in biological samples are included, facilitating pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic studies.  

In summary, the invention offers a versatile and efficient platform for controlling gene expression through RNA self-cleavage modulation, with applications spanning basic research, drug discovery, and therapeutic development.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a ribozyme-based system for controlling gene expression through modulation of RNA self-cleavage. This system leverages the catalytic activity of self-cleaving ribozymes, which are engineered for high efficiency and responsiveness to small-molecule inhibitors in mammalian cells.  

A functional self-cleaving ribozyme is defined as an RNA molecule that catalyzes its own cleavage at a specific phosphodiester bond in the absence of protein factors. Examples include hammerhead, hairpin, and hepatitis delta virus ribozymes. The invention focuses on hammerhead ribozymes derived from *Schistosoma mansoni* (Sm1), which have been optimized for use in mammalian systems.  

### Self-Cleaving RNA Mutant Motifs  

Self-cleaving RNA mutant motifs are synthetic or engineered variants of natural ribozymes that exhibit enhanced stability, activity, or regulatory properties. These motifs are incorporated into mRNA transcripts to enable exogenous control of gene expression. The hammerhead ribozyme structure comprises three helical stems (I, II, and III) and a conserved catalytic core. Key modifications include alterations to loop III, which connects stems II and III, and substitutions at positions 5, 7, and 14 of the core.  

The advantages of self-cleaving RNA mutant motifs include their small size, high specificity, and compatibility with standard molecular biology techniques. Modifications such as the addition of stem or loop structures, aptamers, or hairpins further enhance their utility. For example, a schistosome RNA motif with a four-nucleotide loop III and a substitution at position 7 exhibits robust activity in mammalian cells.  

### Expression Vectors and Cell Lines  

The invention provides DNA constructs encoding self-cleaving ribozymes under the control of constitutive or inducible promoters. These constructs are delivered to host cells via transfection, viral vectors, or other gene delivery methods. Stable cell lines expressing ribozyme-regulated reporters are used for high-throughput screening of inhibitors.  

### Methods of Delivering Nucleic Acids  

Nucleic acids are delivered to cells using transformation, transfection, electroporation, or viral vectors (e.g., AAV, lentivirus). Viral vectors are engineered to target specific cell types via surface receptor recognition.  

### Methods of Identifying Ribozyme Regulators  

High-throughput screens utilize reporter cell lines to identify small molecules that inhibit ribozyme activity. Positive hits are validated in secondary assays to determine mechanism and potency.  

### Methods of Treatment and Administration  

The invention includes methods for therapeutic gene regulation, such as *ex vivo* modification of patient cells or *in vivo* delivery of ribozyme constructs. Pharmaceutical compositions comprise ribozyme-encoding nucleic acids and inhibitors formulated for specific routes of administration.  

### Exemplification  

The ribozyme system is exemplified by its use to regulate luciferase expression in HEK293 cells. Toyocamycin and FUR are shown to induce gene expression by inhibiting ribozyme cleavage. *In vivo* studies demonstrate regulation of luciferase in mouse models.  

### Methods  

Detailed protocols are provided for transfection, ribozyme activity assays, and inhibitor screening.
