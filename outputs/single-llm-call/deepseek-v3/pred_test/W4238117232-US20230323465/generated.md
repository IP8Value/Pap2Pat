# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cancer diagnostics, and more specifically to improved molecular testing methods for distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid tumors. The invention provides novel diagnostic gene panels comprising specific splice variants that demonstrate high sensitivity and specificity in classifying thyroid lesions, including the recently reclassified noninvasive follicular thyroid neoplasm with papillary-like nuclear features (NIFTP).  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Thyroid cancer incidence has increased dramatically worldwide, with papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) representing the most common subtype. While fine needle aspiration (FNA) cytopathology remains the standard preoperative diagnostic method, approximately 30% of FNA samples yield indeterminate results. This diagnostic uncertainty has been further complicated by the recent reclassification of follicular variant of PTC (FVPTC) into malignant and non-malignant categories, with the latter now designated as NIFTP.  

Current molecular tests for thyroid nodules, including ThyroSeq, Afirma, and ThyraMIR, suffer from significant limitations. These tests demonstrate reduced specificity following the introduction of the NIFTP classification, as they cannot reliably distinguish NIFTP from malignant tumors. DNA-based tests are additionally limited by the frequent occurrence of assessed mutations in benign lesions.  

There exists a critical need for improved diagnostic tools that can accurately differentiate between benign and malignant thyroid tumors, particularly in cases of indeterminate cytology. An ideal test would provide high specificity while maintaining sensitivity, properly classify NIFTP lesions, and account for variable cellular composition in FNA samples.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides an improved molecular test for thyroid cancer diagnosis based on detection of specific splice variants demonstrating differential expression between benign and malignant thyroid tumors. The test utilizes a carefully selected panel of five gene isoforms (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1) that collectively achieve high diagnostic accuracy.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. A splice-variant-specific diagnostic gene panel demonstrating superior performance in distinguishing benign from malignant thyroid tumors, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.86 in receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis.  
2. Methods for identifying thyroid tumors as benign or malignant based on quantitative measurement of expression levels of the biomarker panel.  
3. Incorporation of thyroid peroxidase isoform 1 (TPO1) as a thyrocyte-specific load control to account for variable cellular composition in FNA samples.  
4. Sample preparation protocols optimized for fine needle aspiration specimens, including RNA isolation and quality control measures.  
5. Reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) methods employing specific primer sets for each target isoform.  
6. The ability to accurately distinguish NIFTP from malignant FVPTC, addressing a critical limitation of existing molecular tests.  
7. Treatment guidance protocols based on test results, including surgical and pharmacological interventions for malignant tumors.  

The invention further provides compositions and kits for implementing the diagnostic methods, including primer sets, probes, and all necessary reagents for sample processing and analysis.

### II. Sample Preparation for Detection of Expression  

The invention provides optimized methods for processing thyroid FNA samples to enable accurate molecular analysis. Sample preparation comprises:  

1. Collection of fine needle aspiration specimens using standard clinical procedures, with immediate preservation in 95% ethanol at -20°C or RNALater at -80°C.  
2. Total RNA isolation using specialized kits for low cellularity samples, such as the GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit.  
3. Quality control assessment based on detection of the thyrocyte-specific marker TPO1, with samples showing cycle threshold (Ct) values >30 being excluded from analysis.  
4. Reverse transcription of the entire RNA eluate (typically 11 μl) using SuperScript III reverse transcriptase to maximize cDNA yield from limited starting material.  
5. Optional pre-analytical processing steps including centrifugation, filtration, or magnetic bead-based selection to enrich for thyroid epithelial cells when necessary.  

### III. Detection of Target Nucleic Acid  

Expression levels of the diagnostic biomarkers are quantified using sensitive and specific molecular techniques:  

1. Real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR) performed using Power SYBR Green Master Mix on a thermal cycler for 40 amplification cycles.  
2. Custom primer sets designed to specifically amplify the selected splice variants of CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, and PLAG1 (sequences provided in the specification).  
3. Multiplex amplification formats enabling simultaneous detection of multiple targets.  
4. Data normalization using the thyrocyte-specific reference gene TPO1 to account for variable thyroid cell content in FNA samples.  
5. Quality control measures including duplicate reactions and standard curve analysis to ensure assay reproducibility.  

The invention encompasses alternative amplification methods including but not limited to: reverse transcription followed by real-time quantitative PCR (qRT-PCR), nucleic acid sequence-based amplification (NASBA), ligase chain reaction, multiplex ligatable probe amplification, invader technology, rolling circle amplification, in vitro transcription (IVT), strand displacement amplification, transcription-mediated amplification (TMA), and RNA (Eberwine) amplification.  

### IV. Detection of Target Protein(s)

### V. Treatment of Patients Guided by Target Biomarker Panel  

The diagnostic results inform appropriate clinical management:  

1. For malignant tumors (FVPTC, PTC):  
   - Total or partial thyroidectomy  
   - Radioactive iodine ablation when indicated  
   - Targeted therapies including TERT inhibitors, BRAF inhibitors, or MEK inhibitors for advanced cases  
   - External beam radiation therapy for locally invasive disease  
   - Thyroid hormone replacement therapy post-operatively  

2. For benign tumors (AN, FA, HA) and NIFTP:  
   - Conservative management with observation in selected cases  
   - Limited surgical intervention when clinically indicated  
   - Avoidance of unnecessary aggressive treatment  

The invention provides specific treatment protocols based on the molecular classification of thyroid nodules, enabling personalized therapeutic approaches.

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope.  

### Materials and Methods  

Clinical samples were collected under IRB approval from patients undergoing thyroid surgery. Intraoperative FNAs were obtained with 25-gauge needles and preserved in 95% ethanol or RNALater. Total RNA was isolated using Trizol and RNeasy Mini Kit or GenElute Single Cell RNA Purification Kit. cDNA was synthesized using SuperScript III reverse transcriptase.  

PCR assays were performed with Platinum Taq DNA polymerase and analyzed by agarose gel electrophoresis. Real-time qPCR used Power SYBR Green Master Mix on a Bio-Rad iQ5 thermal cycler. Data analysis included semi-quantitative densitometry, z-transformation of expression levels, and ROC analysis.  

### Results  

Evaluation of 22 transcript isoforms identified five (CDH3, FNDC4, HMGA2, KLK7, PLAG1) with optimal differential expression between benign and malignant thyroid tumors. TPO1 was selected as the optimal thyrocyte-specific load control.  

Testing on 159 FNA samples demonstrated:  
- 86.2% success rate meeting TPO1 quality threshold  
- AUC of 0.86 for distinguishing benign from malignant tumors  
- 91% specificity at 75% sensitivity  
- 91% negative predictive value and 74% positive predictive value  
- Significant separation of NIFTP from malignant tumors (p=0.0002)  

### Discussion  

The 5-isoform panel addresses critical limitations of current thyroid molecular tests by:  
1. Providing high specificity (91%) while maintaining sensitivity (75%)  
2. Accurately classifying NIFTP lesions  
3. Incorporating thyrocyte-specific quality control  
4. Working effectively with single-pass FNA samples  

The invention enables more precise preoperative diagnosis, particularly for indeterminate cytology cases, guiding appropriate clinical management.  

### Conclusions  

The invention provides a novel molecular test for thyroid cancer diagnosis based on specific splice variants that demonstrates superior performance in distinguishing benign from malignant tumors, including accurate classification of NIFTP. The test offers significant advantages over existing commercial assays and has important implications for clinical decision-making.
