# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to human antibodies and their use in medicine. More specifically, the invention provides fully human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to the extracellular domain of influenza A virus M2 protein, pharmaceutical compositions containing such antibodies, and methods for their use in the prevention and treatment of influenza A virus infection. The antibodies described herein were isolated from human B cells using a novel mammalian cell display technology and demonstrate high affinity binding to conserved epitopes on the M2 protein. These natural human antibodies exhibit potent prophylactic and therapeutic activity against influenza A virus infection in animal models, making them particularly suitable for passive immunization strategies in humans.

## RELATED ART

Influenza A virus remains a significant global health concern, causing millions of cases of severe illness and hundreds of thousands of deaths annually. Current influenza vaccines primarily induce antibodies against the viral surface proteins hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA), with neutralizing antibodies against HA providing the main protective effect. However, the effectiveness of these vaccines is limited by the phenomenon of antigenic drift, where mutations in HA lead to new viral variants requiring constant vaccine updates. Additionally, antigenic shift through genome reassortment can abruptly introduce new HA or NA subtypes against which the population has little immunity.

While passive immunization with monoclonal antibodies targeting HA has shown efficacy, this approach suffers from the same limitations as current vaccines due to HA's variability. The matrix protein 2 (M2) of influenza A virus has emerged as an attractive alternative target for immunization strategies due to its remarkable conservation across human influenza A virus isolates over the past century. M2 is a tetrameric ion channel involved in viral uncoating and maturation processes. Its 23-amino acid extracellular domain (M2e) shows minimal variation between strains, making it an ideal target for broadly protective antibodies.

Current seasonal influenza vaccines induce only weak humoral responses against M2, as the protein is sparsely represented in viral membranes. Interestingly, M2-specific antibodies appear to mediate protection primarily through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) against infected cells rather than through direct viral neutralization. Several M2e-specific monoclonal antibodies have demonstrated protective effects in mouse models when administered prophylactically. These include fully human antibodies derived from transchromosomic mice, but no natural human M2e-specific antibodies have been reported previously.

Passive immunization with monoclonal antibodies offers several advantages over vaccination, particularly for individuals who respond poorly to vaccines such as the elderly, young children, or immunocompromised patients. It also provides rapid protection crucial for post-exposure treatment or prophylaxis. Natural human antibodies are particularly desirable for therapeutic applications as they combine minimal immunogenicity with low off-target reactivity, having undergone natural affinity maturation processes that yield high-affinity binders.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel approach for isolating natural human antibodies against influenza A virus M2 protein using a mammalian cell display technology. Through library-based screening of human B cells, we have identified and isolated single-chain variable fragments (scFvs) that specifically bind to the M2e domain. These scFv antibodies were subsequently converted into fully human IgG1 antibodies demonstrating high affinity binding and potent protective activity against influenza infection.

The antibodies of the invention exhibit several advantages over existing approaches. As natural human antibodies, they avoid potential immunogenicity issues associated with humanized or transgenic mouse-derived antibodies. The antibodies show high affinity binding to the M2e antigen, with dissociation constants in the low nanomolar range. Detailed epitope mapping reveals that the antibodies recognize conserved regions of M2e, enabling cross-reactivity with diverse influenza A strains including recent H5N1 isolates.

The invention encompasses antibodies comprising specific complementarity-determining region (CDR) sequences within their light chain variable regions (LCVRs) and heavy chain variable regions (HCVRs). Through comprehensive analysis of antibody variable regions, we have identified particularly effective combinations of LCVRs and HCVRs that confer high-affinity binding to M2e. The antibody designated D005 represents a particularly preferred embodiment, demonstrating exceptional binding characteristics and protective efficacy.

The antibodies of the invention bind effectively to both recombinant M2e peptides and native, cell-associated M2 protein. Importantly, they also recognize M2 protein incorporated into viral particles. In mouse models of influenza A infection, these antibodies show remarkable prophylactic activity, protecting against lethal viral challenge at remarkably low doses. Furthermore, they demonstrate significant therapeutic potential, with single-dose administration providing protection even when given days after infection.

The invention provides monoclonal antibodies comprising specific LCVR and HCVR sequences that form antigen binding sites with high specificity for influenza A M2 protein. Pharmaceutical compositions containing these antibodies are described, along with methods for their use in passive immunization against influenza A virus infection. The antibodies are particularly suited for both prophylactic and therapeutic applications in humans, offering protection against diverse influenza A strains through targeting of the conserved M2 protein.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The term "antibody" as used herein refers to an immunoglobulin molecule capable of specific binding to a target, such as a carbohydrate, polynucleotide, lipid, polypeptide, etc., through at least one antigen recognition site located in the variable region of the immunoglobulin molecule. Antibodies can exist in various forms including full-length immunoglobulins, antibody fragments, and engineered variants. The basic structural unit of an antibody consists of two identical heavy chains and two identical light chains, with each chain containing constant and variable regions.

Antibody structure typically includes several domains: the variable region (Fv) containing the antigen-binding site, and the constant region that mediates effector functions. The Fv region is composed of the variable domains from both heavy (VH) and light (VL) chains. A "single chain antibody" refers to an engineered antibody fragment containing the VH and VL domains connected by a peptide linker, forming a single polypeptide chain (scFv). The linker is typically about 10-25 amino acids in length and provides flexibility for the VH and VL domains to associate properly.

A "diabody" is another form of antibody fragment where the VH and VL domains are connected by a shorter linker (typically 5-10 amino acids) that prevents intramolecular pairing of domains on the same chain, instead promoting intermolecular pairing between two chains to form a bivalent molecule. The term "human antibody" refers to antibodies having variable and constant regions derived from human immunoglobulin sequences. Such antibodies may include sequences that are naturally occurring in humans or that have been modified while retaining substantially human characteristics.

"Fully human antibody" describes antibodies entirely derived from human sequences, including both variable and constant regions, without any non-human components. These antibodies offer particular advantages for therapeutic use due to their minimal immunogenicity in human patients. The constant regions of human antibodies can be of any isotype, including IgG (with subclasses IgG1, IgG2, IgG3, IgG4), IgA, IgD, IgE, or IgM. IgG1 is particularly preferred for therapeutic applications due to its ability to effectively mediate effector functions such as ADCC.

The variable regions of antibodies contain framework regions that maintain the overall structure and complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) that primarily mediate antigen binding. CDRs are the hypervariable loops within the variable domains that make direct contact with antigen. There are three CDRs in each of the VH and VL domains (CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3), with CDR3 typically being the most variable and often contributing most significantly to antigen binding specificity.

Light chain variable regions (LCVRs) can be of either kappa or lambda type, with kappa being more common in humans. The invention includes antibodies with both types of LCVRs, though particularly preferred embodiments utilize kappa light chains. Heavy chain variable regions (HCVRs) show greater diversity and are responsible for much of the binding affinity and specificity. The combination of specific LCVRs and HCVRs creates unique antigen binding sites with defined specificities.

The antigen targeted by the antibodies of the invention is the extracellular domain of influenza A virus M2 protein (M2e). This conserved 23-amino acid region has remained remarkably stable across human influenza A isolates. The invention encompasses antibodies that bind not only to the consensus M2e sequence but also to variants found in different influenza strains, including recent H5N1 isolates. The antibodies may recognize M2e in various forms including free peptides, conjugates with carrier proteins, fusion proteins, or as presented on virus particles or infected cells.

"Specifically binding" refers to the antibody's ability to preferentially bind to the M2e antigen with high affinity while showing minimal binding to unrelated molecules. Binding affinity can be quantified by various methods including Friguet ELISA, standard ELISA, or surface plasmon resonance (Biacore analysis). The dissociation constant (Kd) provides a measure of binding strength, with lower values indicating higher affinity. The antibodies of the invention typically show Kd values in the low nanomolar range for M2e binding.

The term "effective amount" refers to a quantity of antibody sufficient to achieve a desired prophylactic or therapeutic effect, such as preventing or treating influenza A infection in a subject. For the antibodies described herein, remarkably small amounts (as low as 6 μg in mouse models) have shown protective effects. Administration may be through various routes including intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intranasal.

The invention includes antibodies conjugated to various tags for purification or detection purposes. Such tags include but are not limited to haemagglutinin tags, myc tags, poly-histidine tags, protein A, glutathione S-transferase, Glu-Glu affinity tags, substance P, FLAG peptides, and streptavidin binding peptides. These tags facilitate antibody purification and detection without significantly affecting binding properties.

The antibodies were isolated using a novel library-based screening method involving mammalian cell display. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells from donors with high M2e titers were used to generate scFv libraries displayed on cell surfaces via Sindbis virus infection. M2e-specific B cells were isolated by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) using M2e conjugates as bait. This method efficiently identifies natural antibody pairs consisting of matched heavy and light chains that have undergone physiological affinity maturation.

The monoclonal antibodies of the invention are preferably fully human IgG1 molecules comprising specific LCVR and HCVR sequences that form high-affinity antigen binding sites for M2e. Particularly preferred embodiments include the antibody designated D005, which demonstrates exceptional binding characteristics and protective efficacy. The LCVR and HCVR sequences contain specific CDR combinations that confer optimal binding properties.

The invention encompasses numerous combinations of LCVRs and HCVRs that bind M2e with high affinity. Sequence analysis has identified particularly preferred LCVR groups that, when paired with specific HCVRs, create superior antigen binding sites. Certain CDR combinations within these variable regions appear most frequently and represent highly optimized binding configurations. Cloning artifacts have been removed from preferred sequences to ensure biological relevance.

For HCVRs, the most abundant CDR combinations have been identified and preferred embodiments include specific amino acid sequences at key positions. Similarly, preferred LCVR embodiments contain defined CDR sequences that contribute to high-affinity binding when paired with complementary HCVRs. The invention provides the specific amino acid sequences of these preferred variable regions and their component CDRs.

The monoclonal antibodies can be produced recombinantly in various formats including full-length IgG, Fab fragments, scFv fragments, or other engineered variants. Preferred production methods use mammalian expression systems such as HEK-293T cells with episomal vectors that enable high-yield expression. The antibodies are typically purified by affinity chromatography, maintaining their structural integrity and binding properties.

Binding characteristics of the antibodies have been thoroughly characterized. They show high affinity for M2e with dissociation constants typically below 10 nM, and often in the 4-5 nM range. The antibodies recognize minimal epitopes within M2e and maintain binding to variant sequences from different influenza strains. They effectively bind cell-associated M2 protein and can detect M2 in viral particles.

Epitope mapping has defined the minimal binding regions required for antibody recognition. The antibodies maintain binding to various M2e variants while showing specificity for the target sequence. This broad reactivity combined with specificity makes them particularly valuable for targeting diverse influenza strains through a conserved epitope.

The monoclonal antibodies have demonstrated remarkable prophylactic and therapeutic activity in mouse models of influenza A infection. Single-dose administration provides complete protection against lethal challenge when given prior to infection. Importantly, therapeutic administration up to two days post-infection still provides significant protection, suggesting clinical utility for both prevention and treatment.

Pharmaceutical compositions containing the antibodies are prepared with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or stabilizers suitable for human administration. These compositions may be formulated for various routes of delivery including parenteral administration. The antibodies are particularly suited for passive immunization strategies in populations where conventional vaccination is ineffective or when rapid protection is required.

Methods of using the antibodies include prophylactic administration to prevent influenza A infection in at-risk individuals, and therapeutic administration to treat established infections. The antibodies may also be used diagnostically to detect influenza A infection through recognition of M2 protein. Combination therapies with other antiviral agents or vaccines are also contemplated.

The invention further provides nucleic acid molecules encoding the antibody HCVRs and LCVRs, expression vectors for recombinant production, and host cells containing these genetic elements. Various production systems can be employed including mammalian, bacterial, yeast, or insect cell expression systems, with mammalian cells being preferred for proper glycosylation and folding.

## EXAMPLE 1

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) were isolated from heparinized blood samples using Ficoll gradient centrifugation. The PBMC were stained with M2e peptide conjugated to Qβ virus-like particles along with Alexa Fluor 647-labeled Qβ and human gamma globulin to block nonspecific binding. Additional staining was performed using a mouse monoclonal antibody specific for M2 in combination with a FITC-labeled secondary antibody, as well as fluorescently labeled antibodies against human IgM, IgD, CD14, CD3, and CD19 to identify antigen-specific B cells. Using fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS), 334 M2e-specific B cells were isolated based on their specific staining pattern.

RNA was extracted from the sorted cells and used as template for RT-PCR amplification of immunoglobulin variable region genes. The heavy chain and light chain variable regions were amplified separately and assembled into single-chain Fv (scFv) coding sequences. Two distinct libraries were constructed: one containing scFv with kappa light chains and another with lambda light chains. These libraries were cloned into a Sindbis virus-based cell surface display vector, resulting in approximately 1.0 × 10^6 independent transformants for the kappa library and 1.1 × 10^6 for the lambda library. Given that the libraries were derived from 334 B cells, this ensured comprehensive coverage of all natural heavy and light chain pairings present in the original cell population.

Recombinant Sindbis virus particles were generated from these libraries with titers of 3.4 × 10^7 pfu/ml for the kappa library and 5.7 × 10^7 pfu/ml for the lambda library. Baby hamster kidney (BHK) cells were infected with these viral libraries at low multiplicity of infection to ensure that most infected cells expressed only one antibody species. The infected cells were then stained for surface expression of M2e-specific scFv using RNase-M2e conjugates in combination with RNase-specific antibodies and fluorescent secondary reagents. While the lambda library showed no detectable M2e-reactive cells, approximately 0.3% of cells infected with the kappa library demonstrated specific binding to M2e.

Through three rounds of FACS sorting using either Qβ-M2e or RNase-M2e conjugates as bait, 255 BHK cells displaying M2e-reactive antibodies were isolated. These cells were individually sorted into wells containing BHK feeder cells and cultured for 2-3 days to allow viral amplification. Of these, 201 wells showed evidence of viral infection and were subsequently reanalyzed for M2e binding using RNase-M2e conjugates. This secondary screening identified 130 wells containing cells expressing scFv antibodies with varying degrees of M2e reactivity. Supernatants from wells showing the strongest M2e binding were used to recover the corresponding scFv coding sequences by RT-PCR, yielding a diverse panel of M2e-specific human antibodies.

## EXAMPLE 2

Reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was performed on BHK cells displaying M2e-specific antibodies to amplify the scFv coding regions. The amplified products were digested with SfiI restriction enzyme and cloned into an Epstein-Barr virus-based episomal expression vector designed to produce scFv-Fc fusion proteins. This vector, derived from pCEP4, contained the EBV origin of replication and nuclear antigen (EBNA-1) for extrachromosomal maintenance, with the hygromycin resistance gene replaced by a puromycin selection marker. The construct drove expression of scFv antibodies fused to the Fc region of mouse IgG2c (msFcγ2c) under control of a CMV promoter.

HEK-293T cells were transfected with these scFv-msFcγ2c expression constructs using Lipofectamine Plus reagent. Transfected cells were selected with puromycin to establish stable pools of protein-expressing cells. These cells were maintained in serum-free medium on poly-L-lysine coated surfaces for large-scale protein production. Culture supernatants were harvested twice weekly, filtered through 0.22 μm membranes, and the scFv-msFcγ2c fusion proteins purified by affinity chromatography using protein A-Sepharose.

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) was performed to analyze M2e-specific binding of the antibodies. Microtiter plates were coated with either RNase-M2e consensus peptide or RNase-M2e from an H5N1 strain (A/VN/1203/2004) at 4 μg/ml in carbonate buffer. After blocking with 3% BSA, plates were incubated with serial dilutions of scFv-msFcγ2c antibodies in PBS containing 1% BSA. Bound antibodies were detected with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-mouse Fcγ-specific antibody and developed with OPD substrate. Absorbance at 450 nm was measured to quantify binding.

Expression levels of the scFv-msFcγ2c fusion proteins were monitored by ELISA and western blot. Three antibodies designated D005, E040, and F052 were selected for further characterization based on their strong M2e binding and high expression levels, typically yielding 20-40 mg per liter of culture supernatant. Nucleotide sequencing of these clones confirmed their human origin and identified their unique variable region sequences.

## EXAMPLE 3

The scFv coding regions from clones D005, E040, and F052 were subcloned into an expression vector designed to produce scFv-msFcγ2c fusion proteins for large-scale purification. HEK-293T cells were transfected with these constructs and stable pools selected with puromycin. For protein production, cells were grown in serum-free medium in roller bottles at 37°C with 5% CO2. Culture supernatants were harvested twice weekly over a three-week period and clarified by filtration.

The scFv-msFcγ2c fusion proteins were purified by affinity chromatography using protein A-Sepharose columns equilibrated with PBS. After loading clarified supernatant, columns were washed extensively with PBS until the absorbance at 280 nm returned to baseline. Bound proteins were eluted with 0.1 M glycine-HCl, pH 2.7, and immediately neutralized with 1 M Tris-HCl, pH 9.0. Protein-containing fractions were pooled and dialyzed against PBS for buffer exchange.

Purified proteins were analyzed by SDS-PAGE under reducing and non-reducing conditions to assess purity and integrity. Coomassie staining revealed single bands of approximately 55 kDa under non-reducing conditions and two bands of approximately 27 kDa (scFv) and 28 kDa (msFcγ2c) under reducing conditions, confirming proper assembly of the fusion proteins. Protein concentrations were determined by absorbance at 280 nm using calculated extinction coefficients.

## EXAMPLE 4

The binding characteristics of purified scFv-msFcγ2c antibodies were analyzed by ELISA. Plates were coated with either RNase-M2e consensus peptide or RNase-M2e from the H5N1 strain at 4 μg/ml in carbonate buffer. After blocking, serial three-fold dilutions of antibodies starting from 1 μg/ml were applied in PBS/1% BSA. Following incubation and washing, bound antibodies were detected with HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse Fcγ antibody and OPD substrate.

All three antibodies showed strong binding to both M2e variants with half-maximal effective concentrations (EC50) between 5-10 ng/ml (45-90 pM), demonstrating their ability to cross-react with different influenza strains. Competition ELISA was performed by pre-incubating antibodies with increasing concentrations of free M2e peptides before applying to M2e-coated plates. This confirmed the specificity of binding and allowed estimation of relative affinities for different peptide variants.

Epitope mapping was conducted using a series of overlapping and truncated M2e peptides to identify the minimal binding region required for antibody recognition. The antibodies showed similar binding patterns, recognizing a central epitope within M2e that is conserved across influenza A strains. This explains their ability to cross-react with both the consensus sequence and the H5N1 variant.

## EXAMPLE 5

The ability of scFv-msFcγ2c antibodies to bind native M2 protein expressed on cell surfaces was analyzed using flow cytometry. L929 cells stably transfected with full-length M2 from influenza A/PR/8/34 (L929-M2#E9) were stained with increasing concentrations of D005, E040, or F052 antibodies. After washing, bound antibodies were detected with Cy5-labeled goat anti-mouse IgG and analyzed by flow cytometry.

All three antibodies showed concentration-dependent staining of M2-expressing cells but not untransfected controls, demonstrating their ability to recognize native M2 protein in its natural membrane context. The antibodies exhibited similar binding curves with half-maximal staining achieved at approximately 10-20 nM, consistent with their solution-phase binding affinities. This confirmed that the antibodies recognize properly folded, cell-surface expressed M2 protein rather than just linear peptide epitopes.

## EXAMPLE 6

The prophylactic activity of scFv-msFcγ2c antibodies was evaluated in a mouse model of influenza A infection. Groups of six C57BL/6 mice were injected intraperitoneally with 500 μg of D005, E040, or F052 antibody, while control mice received equivalent amounts of mouse IgG. Two days later, all animals were infected intranasally with 4×LD50 of mouse-adapted influenza A/PR/8/34 virus.

Mice were monitored twice daily for weight loss and body temperature as indicators of disease progression. Control animals showed rapid weight loss exceeding 20% of initial body weight and severe hypothermia (<30°C) within 7-8 days post-infection, requiring euthanasia. In contrast, all mice treated with M2e-specific antibodies maintained normal body temperature and showed only transient weight loss of less than 10%, with full recovery by day 14 post-infection. No mortality was observed in antibody-treated groups during the 21-day observation period.

To determine the minimal effective dose, additional groups of mice received decreasing amounts of D005 antibody (200 μg, 60 μg, 20 μg, or 6 μg) prior to viral challenge. Remarkably, all doses down to 20 μg provided complete protection, while even the lowest dose of 6 μg protected 50% of animals from lethal infection. This demonstrates the exceptional potency of these antibodies in preventing influenza A infection.

## EXAMPLE 7

The therapeutic potential of scFv-msFcγ2c antibodies was evaluated by administering D005 at various time points after viral infection. Groups of mice were infected with 4×LD50 of influenza A/PR/8/34 and then treated with a single 200 μg dose of D005 at 1, 2, or 3 days post-infection. Control groups received D005 two days before infection (prophylactic) or mouse IgG at the same time points.

All prophylactically treated mice survived without significant symptoms, while all IgG-treated controls succumbed to infection. Importantly, mice treated with D005 at 1 or 2 days post-infection showed survival rates equivalent to prophylactic treatment, with only transient mild symptoms. Even when treatment was delayed until 3 days post-infection, the antibody significantly prolonged survival compared to controls, though full protection was not achieved. These results demonstrate that M2e-specific antibodies can provide therapeutic benefit even after establishment of infection.

## EXAMPLE 8

To produce fully human IgG1 versions of the antibodies for potential clinical use, the variable regions from scFv D005 were cloned into expression vectors encoding human IgG1 heavy chain and kappa light chain constant regions. These constructs were transfected into HEK-293T cells using the EBV-based episomal expression system. Stable pools of expressing cells were selected with puromycin and expanded for large-scale production in serum-free medium.

The fully human IgG1-D005 antibody was purified from culture supernatants by protein A affinity chromatography, yielding approximately 70 mg per liter of culture. SDS-PAGE analysis under reducing conditions showed the expected bands at approximately 50 kDa (heavy chain) and 25 kDa (light chain), while non-reducing conditions revealed the intact IgG at approximately 150 kDa. Size exclusion chromatography confirmed the antibody was predominantly monomeric with minimal aggregation.

## EXAMPLE 9

The binding characteristics of fully human IgG1-D005 were analyzed using 293T cells transiently transfected with either full-length M2 protein or an M2-VN/PR fusion protein containing extracellular domains from both H5N1 (VN) and H1N1 (PR) strains. Cells were stained with increasing concentrations of IgG1-D005, and bound antibody was detected with fluorescent anti-human IgG and analyzed by flow cytometry.

IgG1-D005 showed concentration-dependent binding to cells expressing either M2 construct but not to untransfected controls. The antibody recognized both M2 variants with similar affinity, having EC50 values of approximately 5 nM for M2-PR and 7 nM for M2-VN/PR. This confirmed that the fully human antibody maintains the broad reactivity of its mouse Fc-fused predecessor while being more suitable for human therapeutic applications.

## EXAMPLE 10

The prophylactic activity of fully human IgG1-D005 was compared to its scFv-msFcγ2c counterpart in the mouse influenza model. Groups of mice received equimolar amounts (approximately 200 μg) of either IgG1-D005, scFv-D005-msFcγ2c, or control human IgG two days before lethal viral challenge. An additional group received scFv-D005 fused to human Fcγ mutated (hFcm) to abrogate Fc receptor binding, testing the role of ADCC in protection.

All mice treated with either form of D005 antibody survived infection with minimal symptoms, while control animals succumbed by day 11. The hFcm variant showed slightly reduced protection, suggesting Fc-mediated effector functions contribute to but are not absolutely required for protection. These results demonstrate that conversion to fully human IgG1 maintains the potent prophylactic activity of the original antibody while being more suitable for human use.

## EXAMPLE 11

The binding affinities of antibodies D005, E040, and F052 for M2e were precisely quantified using Friguet ELISA. Antibodies at a fixed concentration (10 ng/ml) were incubated with increasing concentrations of free M2e peptide (3-fold dilutions from 10 nM to 0.17 pM) in solution for 2 hours at room temperature. Residual free antibody was then measured by ELISA on M2e-coated plates.

The dissociation constants (Kd) were determined as the concentration of free M2e peptide that reduced antibody binding to plates by 50%. All three antibodies showed high affinity for M2e, with Kd values of 4.1 nM for D005, 4.8 nM for E040, and 5.2 nM for F052. Repeat assays using M2e from different influenza strains confirmed these antibodies maintain high-affinity binding across diverse variants, explaining their broad protective efficacy.
