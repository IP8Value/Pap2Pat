# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of environmental biotechnology, specifically to microbial bioremediation of atrazine-contaminated soils. Atrazine, a widely used herbicide, persists in agricultural soils and poses significant environmental and health risks due to its slow degradation and accumulation. Prior art methods for atrazine remediation include chemical treatments and microbial degradation using soil bacteria. However, existing microbial solutions suffer from limitations such as low efficiency, poor adaptability to field conditions, and the need for repeated high-dose inoculations. Additionally, many known atrazine-degrading bacteria lack the ability to competitively colonize plant roots, limiting their effectiveness in rhizosphere environments where herbicide residues are most concentrated.  

Current approaches rely on bulk soil application of degradative bacteria, which often fail to establish stable populations due to competition with indigenous microorganisms and insufficient nutrient availability. Furthermore, conventional methods do not address the need for sustained degradation activity over extended periods, particularly in soils with fluctuating herbicide concentrations. There remains an unmet need for a robust, rhizosphere-competent bacterial strain capable of efficient atrazine degradation while maintaining high population densities under field conditions.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the limitations of prior art by providing a novel atrazine-degrading bacterium, *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 (deposited as CGMCC 9667), which exhibits superior root colonization and herbicide degradation capabilities. This strain was isolated from the rhizosphere of maize grown in atrazine-exposed agricultural soils, demonstrating natural adaptation to herbicide-rich environments.  

*Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 possesses unique genetic and physiological traits, including the presence of *trzN* and *atzBC* genes, which enable complete degradation of atrazine and its intermediate metabolites. The strain exhibits high motility and chemotactic behavior, facilitating rapid colonization of plant roots. Unlike prior art strains, *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 maintains robust populations in the rhizosphere even at low initial inoculum densities, particularly in the presence of atrazine, which acts as both a carbon and nitrogen source.  

The invention further provides methods for isolating, culturing, and applying *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 as a biological agent for soil remediation. The bacterium can be delivered via seed inoculation, ensuring targeted establishment in the rhizosphere where herbicide degradation is most critical. Experimental results demonstrate that root-associated populations of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 degrade up to 99.8% of atrazine within 30 days, significantly reducing phytotoxicity and preventing accumulation of harmful metabolites.  

The strain has been deposited at the China General Microbiological Culture Collection Center (CGMCC) under accession number CGMCC 9667, ensuring public availability for patent purposes. Taxonomic identification was confirmed via 16S rRNA gene sequencing and phylogenetic analysis, revealing close homology to known *Arthrobacter* species but with distinct degradative and colonizing capabilities.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS  

The following embodiments illustrate the isolation, characterization, and application of *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* Liulou 1 for atrazine degradation. Examples include laboratory and field-scale experiments demonstrating the strain’s efficacy in diverse soil and plant systems.  

### Isolation of Strain Liulou 1  

Strain Liulou 1 was isolated from the rhizosphere of maize grown in atrazine-exposed soils of Shandong Province, China. Soil samples were serially diluted and plated on SM agar supplemented with 50 mg/L atrazine as the sole nitrogen source. After incubation at 28°C for 5 days, colonies exhibiting clear atrazine degradation halos were selected and purified by repeated streaking. The strain was subcultured in SMY medium (containing yeast extract) for long-term maintenance.  

### Phylogenetic Identification of Strain Liulou 1  

Genomic DNA was extracted from pure cultures using a commercial kit. The 16S rRNA gene was amplified via PCR using universal primers 27F and 1492R. Sequencing was performed on an ABI 3730xl system, and the resulting 1,450-bp sequence was compared to the NCBI database using BLASTn. Phylogenetic trees were constructed using MEGA5 software, confirming assignment to *Arthrobacter ureafaciens* with 99.7% sequence similarity to the type strain CGMCC 1.1897T.  

### Taxonomic and Individual Identification by Rep-PCR  

Repetitive element sequence-based PCR (Rep-PCR) was performed using BOXA1R and ERIC2 primers to generate strain-specific fingerprints. Amplification products were resolved by agarose gel electrophoresis, revealing unique banding patterns distinguishing Liulou 1 from other *Arthrobacter* strains.  

### Identification of Atrazine Degradation Genes  

Multiplex PCR confirmed the presence of *trzN* (encoding atrazine chlorohydrolase) and *atzBC* (encoding hydroxyatrazine ethylaminohydrolase and N-isopropylammelide isopropylaminohydrolase). These genes enable complete mineralization of atrazine to cyanuric acid, a non-toxic intermediate.  

### Example 5: Degradation and Application Experiments  

1. **Atrazine Degradation in Liquid Culture**: Strain Liulou 1 degraded 2 mg/L atrazine within 48 hours in SM medium, with hydroxyatrazine (HA) as the primary intermediate.  
2. **Motility Assays**: The strain exhibited swimming motility in semisolid SA agar, migrating 3 cm in 14 days.  
3. **Root Colonization**: Inoculated wheat seeds achieved root populations of 6 log CFU/g within 28 days, with atrazine enhancing colonization 100-fold.  
4. **Soil Remediation**: Pot experiments showed 97.9% atrazine degradation in planted soils, compared to 46% in uninoculated controls.
