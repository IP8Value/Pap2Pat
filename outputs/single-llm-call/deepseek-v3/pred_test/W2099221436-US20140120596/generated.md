# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to a process for pretreating lignocellulosic material to enhance its susceptibility to enzymatic hydrolysis. More particularly, the invention pertains to an alkaline twin-screw extrusion (ATSE) pretreatment method that combines mechanical shear forces with alkaline chemical treatment at relatively low temperatures and high solids loading to effectively disrupt the lignin-carbohydrate complex in lignocellulosic biomass. The pretreatment process enables efficient conversion of cellulose and hemicellulose to fermentable sugars for subsequent production of biofuels and biochemicals.  

## BACKGROUND  

Renewable energy sources have become increasingly important due to the gradual depletion of petroleum reserves and growing environmental concerns. Biofuels derived from lignocellulosic biomass offer a sustainable alternative to fossil fuels while supporting a circular economy model. Lignocellulosic materials such as agricultural and forestry wastes (e.g., corn stover, rice straw, wheat straw, bagasse, sawdust) represent abundant and low-cost feedstocks for biofuel production.  

The conversion of lignocellulosic biomass to biofuels typically involves three main steps: pretreatment to disrupt the recalcitrant structure, enzymatic hydrolysis to release fermentable sugars, and fermentation to produce ethanol or other biofuels. Among these steps, pretreatment is critical as it directly impacts the efficiency of downstream processes. The natural resistance of lignocellulosic biomass to enzymatic degradation stems from its complex structure where cellulose microfibrils are embedded in a matrix of hemicellulose and lignin. Key structural factors affecting enzymatic hydrolysis include lignin content, hemicellulose content, acetyl group content, cellulose crystallinity, degree of polymerization, and accessible surface area.  

Various pretreatment methods have been developed, including chemical (acid, alkali), mechanical (milling, grinding), thermo-chemical (steam explosion, ammonia fiber explosion), and biological approaches. While each method has shown varying degrees of success, current pretreatment technologies face challenges such as long processing times, chemical recovery issues, high energy consumption, and high operational costs that hinder commercial viability.  

Alkaline pretreatments have demonstrated particular effectiveness in delignification and improving enzymatic digestibility. Conventional alkaline methods include radio-frequency-assisted alkali pretreatment, high solids alkaline pretreatment, and cold sodium hydroxide/urea pretreatment. However, these methods often require severe operating conditions, large amounts of chemicals, or extended processing times.  

Extrusion pretreatment has emerged as a promising physical-chemical method that applies heat, compression, and shear forces to disrupt biomass structure. Various extrusion configurations have been studied, including single-screw and twin-screw extruders, often combined with chemical treatments. Prior art includes Chinese Patent Application CN101121175A describing a biomass pretreatment method using extrusion and Chinese Patent Application CN101255479 disclosing a continuous extrusion pretreatment process. However, existing extrusion methods suffer from limitations such as low treatment rates, low biomass-to-liquid ratios, or high temperature requirements.  

There remains a need for an improved pretreatment process that combines the advantages of alkaline treatment and mechanical extrusion while overcoming the limitations of current methods. The present invention addresses this need by providing an alkaline twin-screw extrusion process that operates at relatively low temperatures with high biomass-to-liquid ratios, achieving effective delignification and enhanced enzymatic digestibility without requiring external heating.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel process for pretreating lignocellulosic material to facilitate its conversion to biofuels and biochemicals. The process involves subjecting lignocellulosic biomass to alkaline twin-screw extrusion (ATSE) pretreatment, which combines mechanical shear forces with alkaline chemical treatment under optimized conditions.  

In one embodiment, the pretreatment process comprises: (a) providing lignocellulosic material; (b) mixing the material with an alkaline aqueous solution to form a mixture having a specific solid-to-liquid ratio; (c) subjecting the mixture to shear mixing in a twin-screw extruder to mechanically disrupt the biomass structure and promote chemical penetration; and (d) optionally heat-treating the resulting aqueous slurry to further enhance delignification.  

Key advantages of the invention include: operation at relatively low temperatures (approximately 99°C) achieved through friction heat without external heating; high solids loading (biomass-to-liquid ratio up to 1:2); short residence time (approximately 30 seconds in the extruder); effective delignification (up to 83% lignin removal); and high sugar yields (up to 80% total sugar recovery). The process is particularly suitable for pretreatment of agricultural residues such as corn stover, achieving about 90% preservation of original polysaccharides and 71% lignin reduction under optimal conditions.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive process for pretreating lignocellulosic material to enhance its enzymatic digestibility for subsequent conversion to biofuels and biochemicals. The following detailed description sets forth various embodiments and aspects of the invention.  

**Lignocellulosic Material**  
The lignocellulosic material suitable for pretreatment includes any biomass containing cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin. Preferred sources include agricultural residues (e.g., corn stover, wheat straw, rice straw), forestry wastes (e.g., sawdust, wood chips), energy crops (e.g., switchgrass, miscanthus), and other plant-derived materials. The lignocellulosic material typically contains about 30-50% cellulose, 15-35% hemicellulose, and 10-25% lignin by dry weight.  

Prior to pretreatment, the lignocellulosic material may be prepared by cutting or chopping to an appropriate size, typically about 2-5 cm in length. Unlike conventional extrusion methods that require fine grinding, the present invention can process larger biomass particles, reducing energy consumption in size reduction.  

**Alkaline Aqueous Solution**  
The alkaline aqueous solution used in the pretreatment contains one or more alkaline agents selected from sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide, calcium hydroxide, ammonia, or combinations thereof. Sodium hydroxide is particularly preferred due to its effectiveness in delignification. The alkaline solution has a pH typically ranging from about 11 to 14, preferably about 12 to 13.5. The concentration of sodium hydroxide in the solution ranges from about 2% to 20% by weight, preferably about 4% to 10%.  

**Mixing Process**  
The lignocellulosic material is mixed with the alkaline aqueous solution to form a pretreatment mixture. The solid-to-liquid weight ratio of the feed mixture ranges from about 1:1 to 1:5, preferably about 1:2. The mixing can be performed using various types of mixers, with shear mixers being particularly effective.  

A key aspect of the invention involves subjecting the mixture to shear mixing in a specially designed twin-screw extruder. The extruder comprises co-rotating intermeshing screws containing alternating transport screw elements (TSE) and reversed screw elements (RSE). This configuration generates substantial shear energy, typically in the range of about 200-400 kWh/ton biomass, which mechanically disrupts the biomass structure while promoting chemical penetration.  

The mechanical action of the extruder generates friction heat, increasing the temperature to about 90-110°C, preferably about 99°C, without requiring external heating. The residence time of the material in the extruder is relatively short, typically about 20-60 seconds, preferably about 30 seconds. During extrusion, the biomass undergoes physical and chemical modifications including fiber separation, size reduction, and partial delignification.  

**Optional Heat Treatment**  
Following extrusion, the pretreated material may undergo an optional heat treatment step to further enhance delignification. The heat treatment is typically performed at about 70-100°C for about 1-10 hours, preferably about 1-3 hours. This step can be conducted in sealed containers or continuous conveyors to maintain process continuity.  

**Resulting Slurry**  
The pretreatment produces an aqueous slurry having a dry matter content of about 20-40% by weight and a pH of about 10-13. The slurry contains partially delignified biomass with increased surface area and porosity, making it highly susceptible to enzymatic hydrolysis.  

**Process Variations**  
The invention encompasses both one-step and two-step process embodiments. In the one-step process, mixing and heat-treating occur simultaneously in the extruder. In the preferred two-step process, mixing occurs in the extruder followed by separate heat treatment. The two-step process allows better control of pretreatment intensity and achieves superior delignification and sugar yields.  

**Downstream Processing**  
Following pretreatment, the aqueous slurry can be processed through various downstream steps including:  
1. Washing and neutralization to remove residual alkali and dissolved lignin  
2. Enzymatic hydrolysis using cellulases and hemicellulases to convert polysaccharides to fermentable sugars  
3. Fermentation of sugars to produce ethanol or other biofuels  
4. Distillation to recover purified ethanol  
5. Lignin recovery from spent liquor for value-added applications  

**System Configuration**  
The pretreatment system may include multiple components such as:  
- Feed preparation equipment (choppers, washers)  
- Twin-screw extruder with specific screw configuration  
- Heat preservation unit (continuous conveyor or holding tanks)  
- Washing and separation equipment  
- Chemical recovery system (evaporators, causticizers)  

**Process Advantages**  
Compared to conventional pretreatment methods, the invention offers several advantages:  
- High sugar yields (up to 80% total sugar recovery)  
- Effective delignification (up to 83% lignin removal)  
- No need for severe size reduction prior to pretreatment  
- Relatively low operating temperature (about 99°C)  
- No external heating required  
- Short processing time (about 30 seconds in extruder)  
- High solids loading (biomass-to-liquid ratio up to 1:2)  
- Compatibility with existing pulp mill infrastructure  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Corn stover was prepared by cutting to 2-5 cm pieces. A sodium hydroxide solution (6% by weight) was added to achieve an alkali loading of 0.06 g NaOH/g biomass at a solid-to-liquid ratio of 1:2. The mixture was processed through a twin-screw extruder operating at 325 rpm with a residence time of 30 seconds. The extruder generated sufficient friction heat to raise the temperature to 99°C. The pretreated material was heat-treated for 1 hour at ambient temperature, then washed and neutralized. Composition analysis showed 71% lignin removal and 90% polysaccharide preservation. Enzymatic hydrolysis yielded 83% glucan conversion and 89% xylan conversion, with a total sugar yield of 77.6%.  

### Example 2  

Corn stover was pretreated as in Example 1 but without the heat treatment step. The pretreated material showed 50-75% lignin removal depending on alkali loading (0.06-0.1 g NaOH/g biomass). Enzymatic hydrolysis yielded lower sugar conversions compared to heat-treated samples, demonstrating the benefit of the optional heat treatment step.  

### Example 3  

Poplar wood pieces were pretreated using the same ATSE process with 0.08 g NaOH/g biomass and 3 hours heat treatment. The pretreated material showed 80% lignin removal and 85% total sugar yield after enzymatic hydrolysis, demonstrating the process effectiveness for woody biomass.  

The examples illustrate that the ATSE pretreatment achieves effective delignification and high sugar yields across different lignocellulosic materials under relatively mild conditions. The optimal pretreatment conditions were determined to be 0.06 g NaOH/g biomass with 1 hour heat treatment, balancing sugar yield, chemical consumption, and processing time.
