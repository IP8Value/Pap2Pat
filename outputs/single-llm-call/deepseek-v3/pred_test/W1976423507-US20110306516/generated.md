## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to methods for producing induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from adult fibroblasts. More specifically, the invention provides an improved method for reprogramming somatic cells into iPSCs using a defined surface marker profile for fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) to isolate fully reprogrammed cells.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Stem cells are undifferentiated cells capable of self-renewal and differentiation into specialized cell types. Embryonic stem cells (ESCs) are pluripotent cells derived from the inner cell mass of blastocysts and have the potential to differentiate into any cell type in the body. However, the use of ESCs raises ethical concerns due to the destruction of embryos.  

Adult stem cells, such as hematopoietic or mesenchymal stem cells, are multipotent and can differentiate into a limited range of cell types. While they avoid ethical issues associated with ESCs, their restricted differentiation potential limits their utility in regenerative medicine.  

The discovery of induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) revolutionized the field by enabling the reprogramming of somatic cells into a pluripotent state using defined transcription factors (Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and c-Myc). iPSCs offer significant advantages, including patient-specific cell generation for disease modeling, drug screening, and therapeutic applications.  

Despite their potential, current iPSC production methods suffer from inefficiencies, with reprogramming rates often below 0.01%. Existing techniques rely on manual colony picking, which is labor-intensive and subject to variability. Additionally, partially reprogrammed cells and residual fibroblasts often contaminate iPSC cultures, complicating downstream applications.  

Current methods for identifying iPSCs rely on morphological assessment or positive selection for pluripotency markers such as SSEA4 and TRA-1-60. However, these approaches fail to exclude partially reprogrammed cells or residual fibroblasts expressing markers such as CD13.  

There remains a need for improved methods to isolate fully reprogrammed iPSCs efficiently and reproducibly, particularly for clinical applications requiring high purity and consistency.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The method involves contacting adult fibroblasts with a reprogramming composition comprising Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and c-Myc, culturing the cells under conditions conducive to reprogramming, and sorting the cells based on the absence of CD13 and the presence of SSEA4 and TRA-1-60.  

The reprogramming composition may include viral vectors (retroviral or Sendai viral) encoding the transcription factors, along with small molecule inhibitors such as ALK5 inhibitor SB431542, MEK inhibitor PD0325901, and ROCK inhibitor Thiazovivin to enhance reprogramming efficiency.

The sorted cells are then expanded and characterized using techniques such as flow cytometry, immunofluorescence microscopy, quantitative real-time PCR, Southern blotting, teratoma formation, and embryoid body differentiation assays.  

The invention further provides methods for confirming iPSC identity, including genetic analysis, detection of enzymatic activity, and gene expression profiling. Additionally, the invention encompasses compositions comprising purified iPSCs and their use in therapeutic applications, disease modeling, and biomedical research.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to improved methods for generating induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) from adult fibroblasts. The method involves reprogramming somatic cells using defined transcription factors followed by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) to isolate fully reprogrammed iPSCs based on a specific surface marker profile.  

### Reprogramming Methods  

Adult fibroblasts are obtained from skin biopsies and cultured in fibroblast medium (DMEM supplemented with fetal bovine serum). The fibroblasts are then infected with retroviral or Sendai viral vectors encoding Oct4, Klf4, Sox2, and c-Myc. Alternatively, non-integrating methods such as episomal vectors or mRNA transfection may be employed.  

Following infection, the cells are cultured in human embryonic stem cell medium (HUESM) supplemented with small molecule inhibitors (SB431542, PD0325901, and Thiazovivin) to enhance reprogramming efficiency. The culture medium is exchanged regularly to maintain optimal conditions.  

### FACS Sorting and iPSC Isolation  

At 7-14 days post-infection, the cells are harvested and stained with fluorescently labeled antibodies against CD13, SSEA4, and TRA-1-60. The cells are then sorted using FACS to isolate the CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, and TRA-1-60-positive population, which represents fully reprogrammed iPSCs.  

The sorted cells are plated onto murine embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs) or Matrigel-coated plates in the presence of ROCK inhibitor to enhance survival. Colonies are allowed to expand and are subsequently characterized for pluripotency.  

### Characterization of iPSCs  

iPSC colonies are analyzed for expression of pluripotency markers (Nanog, Oct4, Sox2) using immunofluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry. Genetic analysis is performed using quantitative real-time PCR and Southern blotting to confirm silencing of viral transgenes and activation of endogenous pluripotency genes.  

Functional pluripotency is assessed by embryoid body formation and teratoma assays. Embryoid bodies are generated by culturing iPSCs in suspension and analyzed for differentiation into the three germ layers (endoderm, mesoderm, ectoderm) using lineage-specific markers. Teratoma formation is evaluated by injecting iPSCs into immunocompromised mice and examining the resulting tumors for multi-lineage differentiation.  

### Applications of iPSCs  

The purified iPSCs may be used for disease modeling, drug screening, and regenerative medicine. Patient-specific iPSCs can be generated for personalized therapies, including gene correction and cell replacement strategies. Additionally, iPSCs provide a platform for studying developmental biology and disease mechanisms.  

### Rapid Production of iPS Cells and Colonies  

Fibroblast lines are derived from skin biopsies and expanded in culture. The fibroblasts are reprogrammed using retroviral or Sendai viral vectors and cultured in HUESM supplemented with small molecule inhibitors. At 7-14 days post-infection, the cells are sorted by FACS to isolate the CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, and TRA-1-60-positive population.  

The sorted cells are plated onto MEFs or Matrigel and allowed to form colonies. iPSC colonies are characterized using the Lyoplate assay to confirm surface marker expression and validated by flow cytometry.  

### Immunofluorescence and Microscopy  

Antibodies against pluripotency markers (Nanog, Oct4, SSEA4, TRA-1-60) are used for immunofluorescence staining. RNA is isolated using the RNeasy kit, and cDNA is synthesized using the SuperScript III First-Strand system. Quantitative real-time PCR is performed using the LightCycler SYBR Green Master kit to assess gene expression.  

For teratoma assays, iPSCs are dissociated using Dispase, resuspended in HUESM and Matrigel, and injected into NOD-SCID Il2rg-null mice. Teratomas are harvested after 6-8 weeks, fixed, and analyzed by histology and H&E staining.  

### Primers  

Southern blot primers are designed to detect human Oct4, Sox2, and Klf4. Probes are generated using the DIG probe synthesis kit, and genomic DNA is isolated from iPSCs, fibroblasts, and ESCs. DNA is digested with BglII, resolved on agarose gels, and transferred to nylon membranes.  

The membranes are hybridized with DIG-labeled probes, washed, and detected using anti-DIG antibodies and CDP-Star reagent. The blots are stripped and re-probed as needed to confirm transgene silencing.  

### Comparison of Retrovirus and Sendai Virus Vectors  

Sendai virus vectors are tested as a non-integrating alternative to retroviral reprogramming. The efficiency of Sendai virus-mediated reprogramming is compared to retroviral methods by assessing the proportion of CD13-negative, SSEA4-positive, and TRA-1-60-positive cells at various time points.  

Sendai virus-infected cells show delayed downregulation of CD13 but ultimately yield iPSCs with similar pluripotency and differentiation potential as retrovirally reprogrammed cells.
