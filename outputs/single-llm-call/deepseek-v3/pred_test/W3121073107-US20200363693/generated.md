## DESCRIPTION  

### TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to optoelectronic integrated devices, particularly to large-scale silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulator arrays. More specifically, the invention pertains to heterogeneously integrated photonic circuits combining silicon waveguides with thin-film lithium niobate for high-performance electro-optic modulation. The disclosed technology enables ultra-compact photonic integrated circuits with superior modulation bandwidth and efficiency while overcoming limitations of conventional doped silicon modulators and bulk lithium niobate devices.  

### BACKGROUND ART  

Electro-optic modulators serve as fundamental components in photonic integrated circuits, converting electrical signals into optical domain modulation. Conventional approaches utilizing doped silicon suffer from inherent limitations including low modulation bandwidth, significant nonlinearity, and high optical loss. While silicon-on-insulator platforms enable integration of p-n junction modulators, their performance remains constrained by the absence of linear electro-optic effect in silicon.  

Lithium niobate has emerged as a promising alternative light guiding medium due to its strong Pockels effect. However, traditional bulk lithium niobate modulators exhibit poor optical mode confinement, resulting in large device footprints and low modulation efficiency. These characteristics make bulk lithium niobate devices incompatible with complex, high-density photonic integrated circuits requiring numerous compact modulators.  

Recent advances in thin-film lithium niobate technology have enabled new modulator configurations. Existing approaches include hybrid waveguides employing lithium niobate on silicon-on-insulator (LN-SOI) or silicon-on-insulator on lithium niobate (SOI-LN) configurations. The LN-SOI approach typically involves deposition, waveguide patterning, and bonding of lithium niobate pieces, achieving modulation bandwidths up to 106 GHz. The SOI-LN configuration fabricates silicon or silicon nitride waveguides atop thin-film lithium niobate wafers, eliminating lithium niobate etching but requiring large bend radii up to 300 μm that limit integration density.  

Current bonding methods for integrating lithium niobate with silicon suffer from scalability limitations and process incompatibilities with large-scale photonic circuit fabrication. There exists an unmet need for wafer-level integration techniques enabling high-density arrays of electro-optic modulators combining the high refractive index of silicon with the superior electro-optic properties of lithium niobate. The present invention addresses these limitations through novel structural configurations and fabrication methods enabling large-scale integration of lithium niobate film electro-optic modulators.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention has as its primary object the provision of a large-scale silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulator array overcoming limitations of prior art devices. The invention achieves this through an innovative integration method and optimized structural configuration enabling both ultra-compact light routing and high-performance electro-optic modulation.  

The disclosed modulator array comprises multiple silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulators monolithically integrated on a silicon crystal substrate. The layered structure includes a silicon dioxide film layer serving as an optical isolation layer, silicon waveguide layers for light routing and confinement, an adhesive layer for bonding, and a lithium niobate film layer providing the electro-optic modulation medium. Direct-current bias and radio-frequency electrode layers complete the structure, enabling efficient electrical-to-optical signal conversion.  

Key components of the array include optimized silicon waveguides with bilevel taper structures for mode transition between routing and modulation sections, multimode interference couplers for light splitting and combining, and coplanar waveguide electrodes for high-frequency signal propagation. The fabrication process involves wafer-level processing including silicon oxidation, polysilicon deposition, ion implantation for PN junction formation, lithium niobate ridge patterning, precision alignment bonding, and metallization.  

Individual modulators in the array function through coordinated operation of their constituent layers. The silicon crystal substrate provides mechanical support and thermal dissipation. The silicon dioxide film layer optically isolates waveguides from the substrate. Silicon waveguide layers confine and route optical signals with minimal loss. The adhesive layer enables robust bonding between silicon and lithium niobate components. The lithium niobate film layer facilitates efficient electro-optic modulation through the Pockels effect. Direct-current bias electrodes establish operating points while radio-frequency electrodes apply modulation signals.  

Working principles involve splitting input light via optical splitters, biasing through direct-current electrodes, applying radio-frequency signals via traveling-wave electrodes, and combining modulated signals through optical couplers. The invention achieves unprecedented performance metrics including modulation efficiency of 1.76 V·cm, bandwidth exceeding 350 GHz, bend radii as small as 10 μm, and waveguide separations down to 0.7 μm. These characteristics enable ultra-compact, high-density photonic integrated circuits for applications including microwave photonics and optical neural networks.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The integration method for the silicon-based lithium niobate film electro-optic modulator array begins with thermal oxidation of a silicon crystal substrate to form a uniform silicon dioxide film layer approximately 1-3 μm thick. This layer provides optical isolation between the substrate and subsequent waveguide layers. A polycrystalline silicon layer is then deposited via chemical vapor deposition to form the silicon waveguide layers, with thicknesses optimized for different functional sections - approximately 220 nm for light routing waveguides and 90 nm for light modulating waveguides.  

Optical splitters and couplers are patterned into the silicon waveguide layers using lithography and reactive ion etching. These include multimode interference structures with widths of 2.7 μm and lengths of 6.6 μm for efficient light distribution. Ion implantation forms PN junctions across silicon waveguides to enable carrier-based modulation where needed. Metal connection lines are deposited above PN junctions to facilitate electrical contact.  

The lithium niobate component fabrication involves etching a lithium niobate wafer to form periodic ridge structures with precise dimensional control. These ridges are aligned with optical couplers in the silicon waveguide layers using high-precision placement systems. The lithium niobate film layer is then bonded to the silicon waveguide layers using an intermediate adhesive layer, typically comprising a low-index dielectric material. Following bonding, a metal layer is deposited on the lithium niobate film surface and patterned to form radio-frequency electrodes through lift-off or etching processes.  

Each modulator in the array features a seven-layer structure optimized for specific functions. The silicon crystal substrate layer provides mechanical stability and thermal management. The silicon dioxide film layer prevents optical leakage into the substrate. Silicon waveguide layers guide light with dimensions tailored for either tight confinement in routing sections (600 nm wide × 220 nm tall) or strong overlap with lithium niobate in modulation sections (480 nm wide × 90 nm tall). The adhesive layer ensures robust bonding while minimizing optical loss. The lithium niobate film layer, approximately 700 nm thick, enables efficient electro-optic modulation through the linear Pockels effect.  

Direct-current bias electrode layers establish operating points for the modulators, while radio-frequency electrode layers apply high-speed modulation signals. The radio-frequency electrodes employ coplanar waveguide designs with ground-signal-ground configurations, optimized for impedance matching and velocity matching with optical signals. Electrode dimensions are carefully selected, with signal electrodes typically 7 μm wide and 400 nm thick, separated by 4.5 μm gaps from ground electrodes.  

The modulator's operation involves several coordinated processes. Light splitting occurs through multimode interference couplers dividing input signals between modulator arms. Direct-current biasing establishes the operating point through applied voltages to the bias electrodes. Radio-frequency signal application utilizes traveling-wave electrodes designed for minimal microwave loss (5-25 dB/cm) and impedance matching (50-57 Ω). Mode transition between routing and modulation sections is facilitated by bilevel taper structures achieving over 98% coupling efficiency.  

Wafer-level design considerations include large-scale silicon waveguide layer patterning with optimized modulator arrangement and interconnection structures. Optical splitters and couplers are distributed to enable complex signal routing while maintaining compact dimensions. The direct-current bias electrode layer is designed for uniform voltage distribution across the array. The lithium niobate film layer maintains consistent thickness (±5% variation) across the wafer for uniform modulation characteristics. Radio-frequency electrode layers incorporate impedance matching networks to ensure consistent high-frequency performance.  

A key advantage of the invention lies in its compatibility with standardized silicon-based integration technologies. The fabrication process leverages existing semiconductor manufacturing equipment and techniques, enabling high-volume production. The use of full-wafer lithium niobate bonding eliminates piecewise assembly requirements of prior approaches. The monolithic integration scheme ensures precise alignment between silicon and lithium niobate components while maintaining scalability to large array sizes. These features collectively enable practical realization of ultra-compact, high-performance photonic integrated circuits incorporating hundreds or thousands of electro-optic modulators on a single chip.
