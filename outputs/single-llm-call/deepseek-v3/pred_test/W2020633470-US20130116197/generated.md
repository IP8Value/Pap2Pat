# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to modified recombinant binding proteins capable of specifically binding vascular endothelial growth factor A (VEGF-A) isoforms. More particularly, the invention provides engineered binding proteins comprising designed ankyrin repeat domains (DARPins) that exhibit high affinity binding to VEGF-Axxx isoforms while demonstrating improved stability and pharmacokinetic properties compared to existing VEGF inhibitors. The binding proteins of the invention are useful for inhibiting angiogenesis and vascular leakage in various pathological conditions, particularly in ophthalmic diseases such as age-related macular degeneration and diabetic retinopathy.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Angiogenesis, the formation of new blood vessels from pre-existing vasculature, plays critical roles in both physiological and pathological processes. While essential for normal development and wound healing, uncontrolled angiogenesis contributes to numerous disease states including cancer, inflammatory disorders, and ocular diseases. The vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) family, particularly VEGF-A, has been identified as a master regulator of angiogenesis.  

VEGF-A exerts its biological effects primarily through interaction with VEGF receptor 2 (VEGFR-2), a type III receptor tyrosine kinase that initiates intracellular signaling cascades promoting endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and survival. VEGF-A also binds neuropilin co-receptors which modulate VEGFR signaling. Alternative splicing of the VEGF-A gene produces multiple isoforms differing in their receptor binding characteristics and extracellular matrix affinity. The pro-angiogenic VEGF-Axxx isoforms (including VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121) represent the most biologically relevant variants driving pathological angiogenesis.  

Current approaches to inhibit VEGF-A signaling include monoclonal antibodies (e.g., bevacizumab), antibody fragments (e.g., ranibizumab), and aptamers (e.g., pegaptanib). While these agents have demonstrated clinical efficacy in conditions such as wet age-related macular degeneration, they suffer from limitations including short intravitreal half-life requiring frequent injections, potential immunogenicity, and manufacturing challenges. The need for monthly or bimonthly intravitreal injections creates substantial treatment burden for patients and increases risks of injection-related complications.  

Designed repeat proteins, particularly ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins), offer several advantages as therapeutic binding proteins. These engineered proteins can be selected for high affinity and specificity from combinatorial libraries using display technologies such as ribosome display. DARPins exhibit exceptional biophysical properties including high thermal stability, solubility, and expression yields in microbial systems. Their small size and lack of disulfide bonds facilitate production and formulation while enabling tissue penetration advantages over antibodies.  

Prior art (WO 02/20565) describes general methods for generating DARPins but does not teach specific VEGF-A binding variants with the combination of properties exhibited by the present invention. There remains an unmet need for improved anti-angiogenic agents that combine high potency with extended duration of action and potential for alternative delivery routes such as topical administration.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides recombinant binding proteins comprising designed ankyrin repeat domains that specifically bind VEGF-Axxx isoforms with picomolar affinity. These binding proteins potently inhibit VEGF-A biological activity including VEGFR-2 activation and endothelial cell sprouting. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing these binding proteins and methods for their use in treating angiogenesis-related disorders.  

Key advantages of the inventive binding proteins include:  
1) High affinity (KD ≤ 25 pM) for VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121  
2) Specificity for VEGF-Axxx isoforms over other VEGF family members  
3) Exceptional stability (midpoint denaturation temperature >65°C)  
4) High solubility enabling concentrated formulations  
5) Small size facilitating tissue penetration  
6) Robust expression in bacterial systems  
7) Compatibility with polymer conjugation (e.g., PEGylation) to modulate pharmacokinetics  

The binding proteins are particularly useful for treating ocular diseases characterized by pathological angiogenesis or vascular leakage, including age-related macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, and diabetic macular edema. Both intravitreal and topical administration routes are enabled by the favorable properties of these engineered proteins.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The following detailed description provides specific embodiments and applications of the invention. Unless defined otherwise, all technical and scientific terms used herein have the same meaning as commonly understood by those skilled in the art.  

**Definitions**  

"VEGF-Axxx isoforms" refers to pro-angiogenic splice variants of VEGF-A including VEGF-A165 and VEGF-A121 that bind VEGFR-2 and stimulate angiogenesis. These isoforms are distinguished from anti-angiogenic VEGF-Axxxb variants.  

A "binding domain" in the context of this invention refers to a polypeptide region of the recombinant binding protein that specifically interacts with VEGF-Axxx isoforms. The binding domain comprises a designed ankyrin repeat protein scaffold.  

The term "protein" encompasses polypeptides of any length, including full-length proteins, protein fragments, and peptide domains. For purposes of this invention, polypeptides are at least 30 amino acids in length.  

"Recombinant" refers to proteins produced through recombinant DNA technology, wherein DNA encoding the protein is manipulated in vitro and introduced into a host organism for expression.  

A "polypeptide tag" is an amino acid sequence fused to the binding protein to facilitate purification, detection, or stabilization. Examples include histidine tags (e.g., hexa-His), FLAG tags, myc tags, and GST fusion tags.  

"Polypeptide linkers" are flexible amino acid sequences connecting protein domains. Suitable linkers include glycine-serine repeats (e.g., (GGGGS)n where n=1-4) and other sequences known in the art to provide flexibility while maintaining domain separation.  

A "proteinaceous polymer moiety" refers to a polypeptide-based polymer conjugated to the binding protein to modulate its properties. Examples include elastin-like polypeptides or albumin-binding domains. The molecular weight of such moieties typically ranges from 5-40 kDa.  

"Non-proteinaceous polymer moieties" include synthetic polymers such as polyethylene glycol (PEG), polyoxazoline, or dextran. PEGylation (covalent attachment of PEG chains) is particularly useful for increasing hydrodynamic radius and plasma half-life. PEG moieties may range from 5-40 kDa in molecular weight.  

A "binding protein" according to the invention comprises at least:  
1) A VEGF-Axxx binding domain comprising a designed ankyrin repeat protein  
2) Optionally, a polypeptide tag for purification or detection  
3) Optionally, a polymer moiety (proteinaceous or non-proteinaceous)  

The molecular weight of the complete binding protein typically ranges from 14-150 kDa, with monomeric binding domains being approximately 14-18 kDa.  

The "binding domain" specifically recognizes VEGF-Axxx isoforms with high affinity (KD ≤ 100 pM, preferably ≤25 pM) while showing minimal cross-reactivity with VEGF-B, VEGF-C, VEGF-D, placental growth factor (PIGF), or platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF). Binding specificity is determined by surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis.  

The protein scaffold of the binding domain is based on designed ankyrin repeat proteins (DARPins), which comprise stacked repeat units forming a continuous binding surface. Alternative scaffolds such as fibronectin domains, affibodies, or anticalins are specifically excluded from the scope of the invention.  

A "predetermined property" refers to a biophysical or functional characteristic engineered into the binding protein, such as thermal stability, solubility, or inhibition potency. These properties are achieved through library screening and selection methods including ribosome display, phage display, or yeast surface display.  

The invention specifically excludes antibodies and antibody fragments (e.g., Fabs, scFvs) as well as any proteins comprising immunoglobulin folds or immunoglobulin-like domains.  

**Anti-Angiogenic Effects**  

The binding proteins exhibit potent anti-angiogenic activity as demonstrated in human umbilical vein endothelial cell (HUVEC) spheroid sprouting assays. Preferred embodiments inhibit VEGF-A-induced sprouting with IC50 values ≤1 nM, more preferably ≤100 pM in this assay.  

The binding domains are engineered to contain no free cysteine residues that could lead to unwanted disulfide bond formation or aggregation. Typical binding domains comprise 2-5 ankyrin repeat units with total lengths of 90-160 amino acids.  

**Repeat Proteins**  

Designed repeat proteins are non-immunoglobulin scaffolds comprising multiple tandem repeats of a structural unit. Each "repeat unit" consists of a conserved framework region and variable positions that can be randomized to create binding diversity.  

The structural motif of ankyrin repeat proteins features a β-turn followed by two antiparallel α-helices. This creates an elongated protein with a continuous binding surface formed by the variable residues. The repeat units stack via hydrophobic interfaces to form stable, modular domains.  

A "capping module" refers to structural elements flanking the internal repeat units that shield hydrophobic core residues from solvent. N- and C-terminal capping modules are essential for proper folding and stability of the repeat protein.  

**Binding Characteristics**  

The binding domains specifically recognize VEGF-Axxx isoforms while showing minimal interaction with VEGFR-2 (KD for VEGF-Axxx/VEGFR-2 interaction >1 μM). Dissociation constants (KD) are determined by surface plasmon resonance using immobilized VEGF-A165 at 25°C in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).  

Preferred binding domains exhibit:  
1) KD ≤100 pM for VEGF-A165  
2) ≥100-fold selectivity over VEGF-B, VEGF-C, VEGF-D, PIGF, and PDGF  
3) Midpoint denaturation temperature (Tm) ≥65°C in PBS  
4) No detectable aggregation after 7 days at 37°C in PBS  

**Pharmaceutical Applications**  

The binding proteins are useful for treating conditions mediated by VEGF-A-induced angiogenesis or vascular leakage, including:  

- **Edema**: Abnormal fluid accumulation resulting from vascular leakage, particularly in retinal tissues (e.g., diabetic macular edema)  
- **Pathological angiogenesis**: Aberrant blood vessel growth occurring in tumors, ocular diseases, and inflammatory conditions  
- **Retinal ischemic diseases**: Conditions characterized by retinal hypoxia and subsequent neovascularization including diabetic retinopathy and retinal vein occlusion  
- **Macular degeneration**: Age-related macular degeneration (wet form) characterized by choroidal neovascularization  
- **Pulmonary hypertension**: Vascular remodeling in pulmonary arteries driven by VEGF-mediated signaling  
- **Inflammation**: VEGF contributes to inflammatory processes by promoting vascular permeability  
- **Tumor formation**: Solid tumors require VEGF-mediated angiogenesis for growth beyond 1-2 mm3  

Pharmaceutical compositions contain the binding protein in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or stabilizers. For ocular applications, preferred formulations include:  
- Solutions for intravitreal injection (10-100 mg/mL)  
- Eye drop formulations (1-20 mg/mL)  
- Sustained-release preparations (e.g., intraocular implants)  

Administration may be by:  
- Intravitreal injection (single doses of 0.1-2 mg)  
- Topical application (1-10 mg/mL, 1-8 times daily)  
- Continuous intravitreal infusion  

Combination therapies with other anti-angiogenic agents (e.g., PIGF inhibitors) or conventional therapies (e.g., laser photocoagulation) are also contemplated.  

**Production Methods**  

The recombinant binding proteins are produced by:  
1) Generating libraries of ankyrin repeat proteins with randomized binding surfaces  
2) Selecting VEGF-Axxx-specific DARPins using ribosome display  
3) Screening clones for high-affinity binding by ELISA  
4) Expressing selected DARPins in E. coli  
5) Purifying proteins from soluble fractions  

The modular architecture of DARPins enables efficient optimization through iterative rounds of selection and screening. Following identification of lead candidates, binding domains may be further engineered through:  
- Site-directed mutagenesis  
- Affinity maturation  
- Polymer conjugation (e.g., PEGylation)  

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope.  

**Materials and Methods**  

Standard molecular biology techniques were used for DNA manipulation. DARPin libraries were constructed as previously described (Binz et al., 2004) and selected against immobilized VEGF-A165. ELISA screening used crude bacterial extracts expressing individual DARPin clones. Selected binders were expressed in E. coli XL1-Blue and purified by immobilized metal affinity chromatography. Protein purity (>95%) was verified by SDS-PAGE and mass spectrometry.  

### Example 1: Selection and Characterization of VEGF-A-Binding DARPins  

Ribosome display selections were performed over seven rounds (three standard selections followed by three off-rate selections and one final collection round) using naïve N2C and N3C DARPin libraries. VEGF-A165-specific clones were identified by crude extract ELISA, with 10 clones showing >50% inhibition in a VEGF-A Quantikine ELISA at 250 pM inhibitor concentration.  

Selected DARPins were expressed in E. coli with yields >200 mg/L in shake flask cultures. Purified proteins exhibited high solubility (>50 mg/mL in PBS) and thermal stability (Tm >65°C by differential scanning calorimetry). SPR analysis confirmed sub-nanomolar affinities for VEGF-A165 (KD = 10-100 pM) with >100-fold selectivity over other VEGF family members.  

### Example 2: Functional Inhibition Assays  

The anti-angiogenic activity of selected DARPins was evaluated in two cellular assays:  

1) **VEGFR-2 Phosphorylation**: DARPins (1.4 nM) completely inhibited VEGF-A-induced (1.4 nM) VEGFR-2 phosphorylation in HUVECs, comparable to ranibizumab controls.  

2) **HUVEC Spheroid Sprouting**: In collagen-embedded HUVEC spheroids stimulated with 160 pM VEGF-A, DARPins #2 and #4 (1 nM) reduced total sprout length by >80% (p<0.001 vs PBS control), exceeding the inhibition by equimolar ranibizumab.  

### Example 3: Target Specificity by SPR  

Surface plasmon resonance analysis of DARPin #27 confirmed high affinity for VEGF-A165 (KD = 25 pM) with no detectable binding to VEGF-C at 1 μM concentration. The binding kinetics showed very slow off-rate (koff = 2×10^-5 s^-1) contributing to the exceptional functional potency.  

### Example 4: In Vivo Efficacy  

PEGylated DARPin #30 (40 kDa PEG) was evaluated in a rabbit model of VEGF-A-induced vascular leakage:  

- Intravitreal injection of 50 μg DARPin #30 reduced fluorescein leakage by >90% compared to PBS controls (p=0.025)  
- Terminal half-life in vitreous was >7 days, compared to <3 days for ranibizumab  
- No ocular inflammation or systemic toxicity was observed  

### Example 5: PEGylated DARPin Production  

DARPin #4 was conjugated to 20 kDa maleimide-PEG via a C-terminal cysteine residue. The reaction proceeded to >90% completion within 2 hours at 4°C. Size exclusion chromatography confirmed monodisperse PEGylated product with hydrodynamic radius increased by ~2-fold compared to unconjugated DARPin. The PEGylated DARPin retained full VEGF-A binding activity by ELISA.
