# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of liver cancer detection, specifically to methods and compositions for diagnosing and managing intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC). The invention provides novel glycan biomarkers and detection systems that enable early and accurate identification of ICC, particularly through the use of Wisteria floribunda agglutinin (WFA)-binding glycoproteins. The disclosed methods are applicable to clinical samples such as bile, blood, and tissue sections, offering improved sensitivity and specificity compared to existing diagnostic techniques.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Liver cancer remains a significant global health challenge, with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICC) being the most prevalent forms. While HCC has been extensively studied, ICC presents unique diagnostic difficulties due to its anatomical location and molecular heterogeneity. Current detection methods for ICC rely on imaging techniques, cytological analysis, and serological markers such as CA19-9, but these approaches suffer from limited sensitivity and specificity.  

Hepatocellular carcinoma is typically diagnosed using tumor markers like alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) and des-gamma-carboxy prothrombin (DCP), but these markers are ineffective for ICC. Intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, in contrast, lacks reliable serum biomarkers, often leading to late-stage diagnoses and poor patient outcomes. Existing tumor markers for cholangiocarcinoma, including CA125, MUC5AC, and Mac-2-binding protein, exhibit suboptimal performance in clinical settings.  

Glycan biomarkers have emerged as promising diagnostic tools due to their association with malignant transformation. Lectins, which bind specific carbohydrate structures, have been employed in glycan profiling to identify cancer-associated glycosylation changes. However, prior art methods have not successfully translated into clinically viable tests for ICC.  

Current limitations in ICC detection include the inability to distinguish ICC from HCC in combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma (HCC-ICC) cases, low sensitivity of biliary cytology, and the lack of reliable serum biomarkers. Tissue-based markers such as cytokeratin and EpCAM are useful in immunohistochemistry but are not applicable to liquid biopsies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Object to be Solved by the Invention  

The primary object of the invention is to provide a reliable method for early detection of intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, particularly in cases where conventional diagnostic techniques yield ambiguous results. A further object is to distinguish ICC from HCC in combined HCC-ICC tumors, which is critical for determining appropriate treatment strategies.  

### Means to Solve the Object  

The invention addresses these challenges by utilizing WFA-binding glycoproteins as specific biomarkers for ICC. The method involves:  
1. **Lectin Microarray Analysis:** Differential glycan profiling identifies WFA as a highly specific lectin for ICC.  
2. **Sandwich Detection Systems:** A combination of WFA and antibodies (e.g., MY.1E12) enables sensitive detection of ICC-associated glycoproteins in bile, blood, and tissue samples.  
3. **Immunoassay Formats:** ELISA, immunochromatography, and fluorescence-based assays are optimized for clinical use.  

Key components include:  
- **Immobilized WFA:** Captures ICC-specific glycoproteins from clinical samples.  
- **Antibody Probes:** Detect enriched glycoproteins (e.g., sialylated MUC1).  
- **Signal Amplification:** Enzymatic, fluorescent, or chemiluminescent labeling enhances sensitivity.  

### Effect of the Invention  

The invention provides:  
- **Early Detection:** Identifies ICC at stages when surgical intervention is still viable.  
- **High Precision:** Achieves 90% sensitivity and 76% specificity in bile-based assays.  
- **Clinical Utility:** Simplifies diagnostic workflows compared to cytology or imaging.  
- **Versatility:** Applicable to multiple sample types (bile, serum, tissue).  

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The invention is implemented through the following steps:  

1. **Sample Preparation:** Bile, blood, or tissue specimens are collected and processed to isolate glycoproteins.  
2. **WFA Immobilization:** Lectin is conjugated to solid supports (e.g., microplates, beads).  
3. **Glycoprotein Capture:** Clinical samples are incubated with immobilized WFA to enrich ICC-specific markers.  
4. **Detection:** Captured glycoproteins are probed with antibodies (e.g., MY.1E12) and detected via ELISA or fluorescence.  

Example protocols include:  
- **Sandwich ELISA:** WFA-coated plates + MY.1E12-HRP conjugate.  
- **Immunohistochemistry:** WFA staining of tissue sections.  
- **Mass Spectrometry:** Identification of WFA-binding proteins.  

## EXAMPLE 1  

Lectin microarray analysis was performed on 45 ICC tissue samples and 38 normal bile duct epithelia (BDE). WFA showed the highest signal intensity ratio (T/N = 4.6, *P* < 0.0001) and ROC AUC of 0.93, confirming its specificity for ICC.  

## EXAMPLE 2  

Tissue staining with biotinylated WFA demonstrated strong reactivity in 92.2% of ICC cases (83/90) versus 32% in normal BDE. HCC tissues were entirely WFA-negative, proving discriminative power.  

## EXAMPLE 3  

Bile samples from ICC patients were analyzed using WFA-MY.1E12 sandwich ELISA, yielding 90% sensitivity and 76.3% specificity (AUC = 0.86), outperforming biliary cytology (55.6% sensitivity).  

## EXAMPLE 4  

Mass spectrometry identified sialylated MUC1 as a dominant WFA-binding glycoprotein in ICC bile, validated by Western blot.  

## EXAMPLE 5  

Immunohistochemistry categorized candidate glycoproteins (e.g., MUC1, N-CAM-L1) based on WFA co-staining patterns.  

## EXAMPLE 6  

An optimized sandwich ELISA using WFA-coated plates and MY.1E12 detection achieved superior ROC performance (AUC = 0.89) compared to CA19-9 (AUC = 0.56).  

## EXAMPLE 7  

Improved ELISA protocols reduced background noise, enhancing specificity to 82% without compromising sensitivity.  

## EXAMPLE 8  

Serum testing with WFA-based assays detected ICC with 85% sensitivity, enabling non-invasive screening.
