# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to peptide siderophores and their use in iron acquisition and transport. More particularly, the invention discloses novel peptide siderophores containing N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands, their production by bacterial cells, and applications in agriculture and medicine.  

The invention further describes bacterial cells capable of producing these peptide siderophores, particularly Gram-negative bacteria belonging to the family Enterobacteriaceae. These bacterial cells synthesize unique cyclic peptide siderophores with iron-chelating properties superior to conventional hydroxamate or catecholate-type siderophores.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Iron serves as an essential micronutrient for nearly all living organisms, playing critical roles in oxygen transport, electron transfer, and enzymatic catalysis. Despite being the fourth most abundant element in the Earth's crust, iron bioavailability remains limited due to the insolubility of ferric iron in aerobic environments at physiological pH.  

Current iron transporters face significant limitations in efficiency and specificity. Hydroxamate-type siderophores demonstrate moderate iron affinity but suffer from susceptibility to hydrolysis. Catecholate siderophores exhibit higher iron affinity but are prone to oxidation. Existing synthetic chelators often lack the biological compatibility necessary for practical applications in agriculture or medicine.  

The need exists for novel means of iron provision that combine high affinity metal binding with biological stability and multifunctionality. Particularly needed are siderophores capable of dual functions - both iron acquisition and other beneficial biological activities that could enhance plant growth or provide therapeutic benefits.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The technical problem addressed by this invention involves overcoming the limitations of existing iron chelators by providing a novel class of peptide siderophores with superior iron-binding properties and additional biological functionalities.  

The solution comprises peptide siderophores containing N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands, which demonstrate unexpected advantages in iron chelation and transport. A preferred embodiment called gramibactin exhibits dual functionality as both an efficient iron chelator and a nitric oxide donor, providing unique benefits for plant-microbe interactions.  

The invention encompasses cyclic peptide siderophores characterized by the presence of N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands. These compounds show remarkable stability and metal-binding capacity, as demonstrated through PET-CT tracer studies and supplementation experiments. In vitro assays confirm their iron-chelating ability, while in vivo fluorescence imaging reveals efficient iron transport in biological systems.  

Ecological and agricultural implications are significant, as these siderophores promote plant growth through improved iron uptake and additional mechanisms. The chelating properties of N-nitroso-hydroxylamines surpass conventional ligands, with formation constants indicating exceptional metal affinity.  

Preferred embodiments include peptide siderophores with two side chains, where at least one side chain contains an N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligand. Another embodiment features a peptide siderophore with a hydroxy carboxylic acid ligand in addition to the N-nitroso-hydroxylamine group.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

Iron plays indispensable roles in fundamental life processes across all domains of life. As a redox-active metal, it participates in electron transfer chains, oxygen binding, and enzymatic catalysis. However, iron's biological utility depends on specialized molecules that solubilize and transport it under physiological conditions.  

Siderophores represent one such class of molecules - low molecular weight compounds that chelate iron with high affinity and specificity. Their iron-chelating function relies on specialized ligands that form coordination complexes with ferric iron. Common ligand types include hydroxamates, catecholates, and α-hydroxy carboxylates, each with distinct chemical properties and biological implications.  

Metal-ligand bonding in siderophores typically involves hexadentate coordination, where six ligand atoms (usually oxygen) surround the central iron ion. The invention's N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands provide superior coordination geometry and binding energy compared to conventional siderophore ligands.  

Production of these peptide siderophores occurs through two primary methods: microbial culturing of siderophore-producing bacteria and chemical synthesis. The bacterial route offers advantages for large-scale production, while chemical synthesis allows precise structural modifications.  

Peptide siderophores according to the invention feature a cyclic structure that confers stability against proteolytic degradation. Their peptide backbone serves as a scaffold for presenting the N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands in optimal spatial arrangement for iron coordination.

## Methods of Administration  

Application methods include foliar spraying, soil drenching, seed coating, and hydroponic system supplementation. Treatment may target whole plants, specific plant parts (roots, leaves), or growth media (soil, hydroponic solutions).  

The invention facilitates beneficial plant-bacterial interactions by enhancing the iron-scavenging capacity of plant growth-promoting rhizobacteria (PGPR). These interactions improve plant nutrient uptake while suppressing pathogenic microbes through iron competition.  

## EXAMPLES  

Rhizosphere bacteria were isolated and screened for siderophore production using chrome azurol S (CAS) assays. A Burkholderia graminis strain demonstrated exceptional siderophore biosynthesis capability, confirmed through NRPS gene cluster analysis.  

Structure prediction based on gene cluster analysis suggested a novel siderophore architecture. Production was optimized in iron-limited MM9 medium, followed by purification via HPLC. NMR structural elucidation revealed the cyclic peptide structure with N-nitroso-hydroxylamine ligands.  

Absolute configuration determination via Marfey's analysis confirmed the L-configuration of amino acid residues. Metal complexation studies showed exceptional stability of the Fe(III)-gramibactin complex, with a formation constant exceeding conventional siderophores.  

PET/CT experiments using 68Ga-labeled gramibactin demonstrated efficient metal uptake in plant systems. Comparative studies showed significantly improved iron utilization in gramibactin-treated plants, evidenced by increased chlorophyll content.  

Unexpectedly, gramibactin exhibited nitric oxide donor functionality, confirmed through Griess tests and chemiluminescence detection. In planta studies localized NO release to root tissues, suggesting a role in iron homeostasis signaling.  

These findings reveal gramibactin's bifunctionality as both siderophore and NO donor, with profound implications for agricultural applications and plant-microbe interactions.  

## Materials and Methods  

### Bacterial Strains and General Culture Conditions  

B. graminis strain DSM 11318 was obtained from the DSMZ culture collection. Cultures were maintained in LB medium at 30°C. Knockout strains were generated via homologous recombination using pEX18Tc-derived vectors.  

### MM9 Media Preparation  

MM9 medium contained (per liter): 1× salt solution (Na2HPO4 6 g, KH2PO4 3 g, NaCl 0.5 g, NH4Cl 1 g), 2 mM MgSO4, 0.1 mM CaCl2, 0.4% glucose, and micronutrients. Iron limitation was achieved by adding 100 μM 2,2'-dipyridyl.  

### CAS Agar Preparation  

CAS agar was prepared according to Schwyn and Neilands (1987), containing hexadecyltrimethylammonium bromide, CAS dye, and iron(III) solution.  

### Siderophore Production in Liquid Media  

B. graminis was cultured in MM9 medium for 72 h at 30°C with shaking (180 rpm). Culture supernatant was filtered (0.22 μm) and concentrated by rotary evaporation.  

### General Analytical Procedures  

NMR spectra were recorded at 298 K on a 600 MHz spectrometer. LC-MS analysis used a C18 column with 0.1% formic acid/acetonitrile gradient.  

### Isolation of Gramibactin  

The crude extract was fractionated by silica gel chromatography, followed by preparative HPLC (C18 column, water/acetonitrile). Pure gramibactin was obtained as a reddish-brown powder.  

### Structure Elucidation of Gramibactin  

Acid hydrolysis (6N HCl, 110°C, 24 h) yielded constituent amino acids for Marfey's analysis. 2D NMR (COSY, TOCSY, HSQC, HMBC) established the complete structure.  

### 15N Labeling of Gramibactin  

(NH4)2SO4 in MM9 was replaced with (15NH4)2SO4 (99% 15N). The isotope pattern in high-resolution MS confirmed incorporation.
