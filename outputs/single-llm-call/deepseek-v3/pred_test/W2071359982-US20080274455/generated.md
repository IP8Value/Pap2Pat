# DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to a novel method for diagnosing schizophrenia by assessing the expression levels of Kir2.3 and/or DRD2 genes or proteins in biological samples obtained from subjects. The invention provides biochemical assays capable of detecting differential expression patterns of these molecular markers in peripheral blood lymphocytes (PBL) of schizophrenic patients compared to healthy controls. Diagnostic kits and hybridization probes for implementing the method are also disclosed.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Schizophrenia represents a severe neuropsychiatric disorder characterized by disturbances in cognitive, emotional and motor processes. The dysfunction of dopaminergic systems has been extensively studied as a primary factor in the pathogenesis of this disease. Previous research has established that neurotransmitters, neuropeptides and dopamine receptor genes are expressed in immune cells, particularly in peripheral blood lymphocytes. These peripheral expressions may reflect the status of corresponding receptors in the brain.  

Genetic linkage studies have identified several candidate markers associated with schizophrenia. The D3 subtype of dopamine receptor genes (DRD3) was found to be upregulated in schizophrenic PBL and proposed as a potential peripheral marker. Fuchs's research group reported decreased mRNA levels of the α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (α7AChR) gene in PBL of schizophrenic patients. These findings were based on either dopaminergic ligand binding assays or quantitative RT-PCR analysis of RNA levels.  

Prior art includes Johnston-Wilson et al. (WO 0144269) describing genetic markers for schizophrenia, Cochran et al. (EP 1 348963 A1) disclosing methods for detecting neurotransmitter-related disorders, and WO02400661 presenting a Kir2.3 animal model. Horn et al. (U.S. Pat. No. 4,931,270) described dopamine receptor dysfunction in psychiatric conditions. The expression patterns of various neurotransmitters and dopamine receptors have been extensively characterized, with DRD3 emerging as a notable peripheral marker. The α7AChR gene expression has also been implicated in schizophrenia pathology.  

Despite these advances, the search for more reliable and specific peripheral markers continues. The present invention addresses this need by identifying novel molecular markers - Kir2.3 and DRD2 - that show significant differential expression in schizophrenic patients. These markers provide improved diagnostic capabilities when used alone or in combination with existing markers.  

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for diagnosing schizophrenia comprising assessing the expression level of Kir2.3 and/or DRD2 in a biological sample obtained from a subject. The method involves comparing the detected expression level to a predetermined baseline expression level, where an elevated expression indicates a positive diagnosis.  

In preferred embodiments, the assessment focuses on Kir2.3 gene expression using hybridization probes specifically binding to Kir2.3 nucleic acids. Another preferred embodiment assesses DRD2 gene expression through similar hybridization techniques. The invention includes specific hybridization probes designed for Kir2.3 and DRD2 detection, optimized for microarray analysis and PCR methods.  

Biological samples suitable for analysis include peripheral blood lymphocytes, with PBL being the preferred sample source. The invention further provides a method for diagnosing schizophrenia at the protein level by assessing Kir2.3 and/or DRD2 protein concentrations in biological samples and comparing them to baseline protein level values.  

The invention encompasses the use of specific binding agents for diagnosing schizophrenia, including antibodies against Kir2.3 and DRD2 proteins. A diagnostic kit is provided containing necessary reagents for implementing the method, such as hybridization probes, primers, antibodies, and detection reagents.  

## DEFINITIONS  

For purposes of this invention:  
"Subject" refers to a human being suspected of having or at risk of developing schizophrenia.  
"Biological sample" means any tissue or fluid sample containing nucleic acids or proteins, including but not limited to blood, serum, plasma, or isolated peripheral blood lymphocytes.  
"Nucleic acid obtained from biological sample" includes DNA, RNA, cDNA, or any nucleic acid derivative isolated from the biological sample.  
"Immune cell" refers to any cell of the immune system, particularly peripheral blood lymphocytes.  
"Isolation" means the process of separating desired nucleic acids or proteins from other cellular components.  
"Probe" denotes a nucleic acid sequence capable of specifically hybridizing to Kir2.3 or DRD2 nucleic acids.  
"Expression level" refers to the quantity of Kir2.3 or DRD2 nucleic acids or proteins present in a sample.  
"Specific hybridization" indicates binding between complementary nucleic acid sequences under stringent conditions.  
"DRD2" means the dopamine D2 receptor gene or its protein product.  
"Kir2.3" refers to the inwardly rectifying potassium channel gene or its protein product.  
"Part of a protein or nucleic acid" includes any fragment retaining biological activity or binding specificity.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides biochemical methods for diagnosing schizophrenia through analysis of genes encoding dopamine D2 receptor (DRD2) and inwardly rectifying potassium channel (Kir2.3). These molecules play crucial roles in electrical signaling within the nervous system. The association between dopamine receptors and inwardly rectifying potassium channels forms the biological basis for their combined use as diagnostic markers.  

One embodiment involves assessing gene expression levels in biological samples through nucleic acid detection techniques. The method compares measured expression levels to predetermined baseline values established from healthy controls. Another embodiment assesses protein levels of the markers using immunological techniques, similarly comparing to baseline protein level values.  

The invention emphasizes the combined use of these novel markers with known markers to improve diagnostic accuracy. Baseline expression levels are defined as the average expression in healthy control populations, with thresholds established through statistical analysis of control samples.  

Quantitative detection methods include gene expression assays performed in situ, real-time quantitative PCR (RT-PCR), and microarray analysis. For protein level assessment, the invention describes production of specific antibodies against Kir2.3 and DRD2, with normalization procedures for antibody binding quantification.  

The method includes isolation of receptor proteins or potassium channel proteins from biological samples. Activity measurements of these marker proteins provide additional diagnostic parameters. Threshold values for diagnosis are determined through optimization studies using samples from confirmed schizophrenic patients.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate implementation of the invention:  

Patient recruitment involved schizophrenic individuals from inpatient psychiatric units, with informed consent obtained after study explanation. Each patient underwent comprehensive medical examination including physical, neurological, and laboratory tests. Diagnoses followed DSM-IV criteria using standardized psychiatric assessment tools.  

Peripheral blood lymphocytes were isolated from blood samples using red blood cell lysis buffer. RNA extraction was performed using commercial kits, with quality verified by electrophoresis and spectrophotometry. Microarray analysis utilized human cDNA arrays hybridized with fluorescent-labeled probes prepared from patient RNA samples.  

Real-time PCR was conducted using both SybrGreen and TaqMan probe protocols. Reverse transcription of total RNA preceded quantitative PCR amplification with gene-specific primers. Primer and probe designs were optimized using specialized software, with quality verified by mass spectrometry.  

Statistical analysis employed unpaired Student's t-tests for significance determination. Agglomerative hierarchical clustering classified gene expression patterns into diagnostic groups. Results demonstrated significant overexpression of DRD2 and Kir2.3 in schizophrenic patients compared to controls, with consistent findings across different detection methods.  

The examples confirm the utility of DRD2 and Kir2.3 as peripheral markers for schizophrenia diagnosis. The methods show high sensitivity and specificity, particularly when using TaqMan probe protocols. The invention provides reliable tools for early and accurate schizophrenia diagnosis through molecular analysis of accessible biological samples.
