## DESCRIPTION  

### FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of clinical diagnostics, specifically to methods for determining insulin resistance and assessing therapeutic response in patients through measurement of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT) activity and its metabolic product 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA). The invention provides novel diagnostic approaches for identifying individuals who would benefit from NNMT inhibitor treatment, monitoring treatment efficacy, and adjusting therapeutic dosing regimens based on biochemical markers of NNMT activity.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Abdominal adiposity and visceral adipose tissue expansion have been identified as significant risk factors for developing insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D), and cardiovascular disease. The mechanisms linking visceral adiposity to metabolic dysfunction remain incompletely understood but may involve altered adipokine secretion, chronic low-grade inflammation, and dysregulated lipid metabolism.  

Nicotinamide, the amide form of vitamin B3, serves as a precursor for NAD+ synthesis and participates in critical cellular processes including energy metabolism and redox regulation. Chronic administration of nicotinamide or its derivatives has been associated with reduced insulin sensitivity in both healthy individuals and those with impaired glucose tolerance. Clinical studies have demonstrated that prolonged high-dose nicotinamide treatment can induce reversible insulin resistance, while combination therapies containing extended-release niacin have been shown to worsen glycemic control in patients with established diabetes.  

Nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT) represents the primary enzyme responsible for nicotinamide metabolism, catalyzing the methylation of nicotinamide to form 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA) while consuming S-adenosylmethionine (SAM) as a methyl donor. NNMT exhibits broad tissue distribution with particularly high expression in liver and adipose tissue. Previous research in animal models has revealed increased NNMT activity in adipose tissue of mice fed high-fat diets, with obesity-prone strains showing elevated NNMT expression compared to obesity-resistant counterparts. Genetic knockdown of NNMT in mice has been shown to protect against diet-induced obesity and insulin resistance.  

Prior investigations have identified elevated urinary MNA excretion in rodent models of diabetes and in human patients with T2D. However, the relationship between plasma MNA concentrations, adipose tissue NNMT expression, and insulin resistance in humans remains poorly characterized. Current diagnostic methods for insulin resistance, such as hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp studies, are labor-intensive and impractical for routine clinical use. There exists a need for reliable biomarkers that can identify individuals at risk for insulin resistance, predict response to therapeutic interventions, and facilitate personalized treatment approaches.  

#### Technical Problems Underlying the Present Invention  

Current clinical practice faces several limitations in diagnosing and managing insulin resistance. Existing methods for assessing insulin sensitivity, while accurate, are technically demanding and unsuitable for widespread screening. Conventional biomarkers lack specificity for the underlying metabolic disturbances associated with adipose tissue dysfunction. Furthermore, no established methods exist for predicting which patients would benefit from NNMT-targeted therapies or for monitoring treatment response in real-time.  

The present invention addresses these deficiencies by providing quantitative methods that correlate NNMT activity with insulin resistance through measurement of its enzymatic product MNA. The invention overcomes prior art limitations by establishing specific threshold values for MNA concentrations that predict therapeutic benefit from NNMT inhibition and by developing protocols for dose adjustment based on changes in MNA levels following treatment initiation.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods for assessing and managing insulin resistance through measurement of NNMT activity biomarkers. In a first aspect, the invention discloses a method for determining whether a patient will benefit from treatment with an NNMT inhibitor by comparing the patient's plasma MNA level to a reference value, where an elevated MNA level indicates potential therapeutic benefit.  

In a second aspect, the invention provides a method for determining whether a patient responds to NNMT inhibitor treatment by measuring changes in MNA concentration relative to pre-treatment levels, where a significant decrease in MNA indicates positive treatment response. A third aspect describes a method for adjusting therapeutic dosing by monitoring MNA levels and modifying drug administration based on the degree of MNA reduction achieved.  

A fourth aspect introduces a quantitative method for determining the degree of insulin resistance in a patient by measuring plasma MNA concentration and comparing it to established reference ranges. The invention specifically disclaims all other embodiments not explicitly described herein, including diagnostic applications unrelated to NNMT activity assessment or therapeutic monitoring of NNMT inhibitors.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

#### Definitions  

For purposes of this invention, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

The term "comprise" or variations such as "comprises" or "comprising" shall be interpreted as inclusive rather than exclusive, meaning that the specified elements may coexist with additional unspecified elements.  

The term "about" when referring to numerical values indicates an approximate range that accounts for typical measurement variability, generally within ±10% of the stated value.  

"Body mass index" or "BMI" refers to a value calculated as an individual's weight in kilograms divided by the square of their height in meters (kg/m²).  

An "inhibitor of nicotinamide-N-methyltransferase (NNMT)" refers to any compound that reduces NNMT enzymatic activity by at least 20% at physiologically relevant concentrations, including small molecules, peptides, nucleic acids, or antibodies that specifically bind and inhibit NNMT.  

The "level of 1-methylnicotinamide (MNA)" refers to the molar concentration of MNA measured in a biological sample, typically expressed in nmol/L for plasma or serum samples.  

A "small molecule" denotes an organic compound with molecular weight less than 1000 daltons. A "peptide" refers to a polymer of amino acids less than 50 residues in length. A "nucleic acid" includes both DNA and RNA molecules of any length. An "antibody" encompasses intact immunoglobulins as well as antigen-binding fragments thereof.  

A "reference value" represents a predetermined MNA concentration derived from healthy control populations, typically in the range of 50-100 nmol/L for fasting plasma samples. A "pre-treatment value" refers to the MNA level measured in a patient prior to initiation of NNMT inhibitor therapy.  

"Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D)" is defined according to American Diabetes Association criteria as fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L or 2-hour plasma glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L during oral glucose tolerance testing. "Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT)" indicates 2-hour glucose values of 7.8-11.0 mmol/L, while "normal glucose tolerance (NGT)" refers to 2-hour glucose <7.8 mmol/L.  

"White adipose tissue (WAT)" denotes energy-storing adipose tissue depots, with "visceral WAT" referring to intra-abdominal depots and "omental WAT" specifically indicating the adipose tissue associated with the greater omentum.  

A "lack of decrease of MNA level" indicates less than 10% reduction in plasma MNA concentration following NNMT inhibitor administration.  

A "patient" refers to a human subject diagnosed with or at risk for insulin resistance or T2D, while a "healthy subject" describes an individual with NGT, BMI <25 kg/m², and no evidence of metabolic disease.  

A "therapeutically effective amount" means a dosage sufficient to produce a measurable decrease in plasma MNA levels or improvement in insulin sensitivity indices when administered to a patient in need thereof.  

#### EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for determining whether a patient will benefit from treatment with an NNMT inhibitor comprising obtaining a blood sample from the patient, measuring the MNA level in said sample, comparing the measured MNA level to a reference level derived from healthy subjects, and identifying the patient as likely to benefit from treatment when the measured MNA level exceeds the reference level by at least 20%.  

In one embodiment, the reference level is established from a population of healthy subjects with NGT and BMI <25 kg/m², with fasting plasma MNA concentrations typically below 100 nmol/L. Patients exhibiting MNA levels above 120 nmol/L are considered strong candidates for NNMT inhibitor therapy.  

The invention further provides a method for determining whether a patient responds to NNMT inhibitor treatment comprising obtaining serial blood samples before and after treatment initiation, measuring MNA levels in each sample, comparing post-treatment levels to baseline values, and identifying the patient as responsive to treatment when MNA levels decrease by at least 10% from pre-treatment values. In preferred embodiments, a decrease of at least 20% indicates robust therapeutic response.  

Another embodiment describes a method for adjusting the dose of an NNMT inhibitor comprising monitoring plasma MNA levels at regular intervals following treatment initiation and increasing the dose if MNA levels fail to decrease by at least 10% within 4 weeks, or decreasing the dose if MNA levels fall below 50% of baseline values. Dose adjustments are typically made in 25-50% increments based on the magnitude of MNA change observed.  

#### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific applications of the invention without limiting its scope:  

**Example 1: Study Population Selection**  
Clinical studies were conducted in three independent cohorts totaling 314 participants. Inclusion criteria required absence of acute inflammation (CRP <952 nmol/L), normal blood pressure (<140/90 mmHg), and no history of cardiovascular disease. Exclusion criteria included thyroid dysfunction, substance abuse, and pregnancy. All participants provided written informed consent, and study protocols were approved by the appropriate institutional review board.  

**Example 2: Measurement of Metabolic Parameters**  
Body composition was assessed by dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry (DEXA). Insulin sensitivity was determined using hyperinsulinemic-euglycemic clamp studies, with glucose infusion rates <50 μmol/kg/min defining insulin resistance. Plasma MNA concentrations were quantified by LC-MS/MS with inter-assay variability <10%.  

**Example 3: Correlation of NNMT Expression with Metabolic Status**  
In a cross-sectional cohort of 199 surgical patients, omental adipose tissue NNMT expression was 3.5-fold higher in insulin-resistant individuals (p<0.001) and strongly correlated with plasma MNA levels (r=0.66, p<0.001). MNA concentrations >120 nmol/L identified insulin-resistant patients with 82% specificity and 76% sensitivity.  

**Example 4: Intervention Studies**  
A 12-week exercise program in 60 participants reduced subcutaneous adipose tissue NNMT expression by 16-21% in patients with IGT or T2D (p<0.001), accompanied by improved glucose infusion rates. Bariatric surgery in 55 obese patients decreased omental NNMT expression by 41% and plasma MNA by 24% (p<0.001).  

**Example 5: Therapeutic Monitoring**  
In a pilot study of NNMT inhibitor therapy, patients achieving >20% reduction in plasma MNA within 4 weeks showed significant improvements in HOMA-IR scores compared to non-responders (p=0.01). Dose escalation in partial responders produced additional MNA reductions correlating with further insulin sensitivity gains.  

The examples demonstrate that plasma MNA serves as a quantitative biomarker for adipose tissue NNMT activity and insulin resistance severity. The methods described enable identification of patients likely to benefit from NNMT-targeted therapies and provide objective measures for monitoring treatment response and guiding dose adjustments.
