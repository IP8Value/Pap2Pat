# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods for predicting in vivo toxicity of oligonucleotides, particularly antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs). More specifically, the invention provides in vitro assays using primary mammalian hepatocytes to assess hepatotoxicity biomarkers that correlate with in vivo toxicity profiles. The invention further relates to methods for screening libraries of oligonucleotide variants to identify compounds with reduced toxicity while maintaining therapeutic efficacy.

## BACKGROUND

Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) represent a promising therapeutic modality for modulating gene expression through Watson-Crick hybridization to target RNA sequences. Despite three decades of development, clinical success has been limited with only two FDA-approved ASO drugs, largely due to unpredictable toxicity profiles. The complexity of oligonucleotide development is compounded by the difficulty in predicting in vivo toxicity from in vitro models. Current in vitro toxicity assays fail to recapitulate the hepatotoxicity and immunotoxicity observed in vivo, necessitating extensive animal testing early in development.

The mechanisms underlying oligonucleotide toxicity remain poorly understood and may involve both hybridization-dependent off-target effects and hybridization-independent protein interactions. Major adverse effects include liver and kidney toxicity as well as inflammatory reactions. While whole body autoradiography shows oligonucleotides primarily accumulate in liver, kidney and spleen after intravenous administration, not all accumulating compounds cause toxicity, indicating complex structure-toxicity relationships.

Traditional approaches to assess oligonucleotide safety rely heavily on rodent in vivo tests measuring plasma aminotransferase levels and histopathological changes. These methods require large animal cohorts and have high attrition rates, posing ethical, cost and time challenges. Although certain sequence motifs have been associated with hepatotoxicity risk, no reliable in vitro assay exists that can predict in vivo effects. This limitation significantly hinders oligonucleotide drug development and highlights the need for predictive in vitro assays that can reduce reliance on animal testing while improving translation to human safety profiles.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides in vitro toxicity assays using primary mammalian hepatocytes to predict in vivo oligonucleotide toxicity. The methods involve administering oligonucleotides to hepatocytes under gymnotic (transfection reagent-free) conditions, culturing the cells, and measuring biomarkers of toxicity that correlate with in vivo toxicity profiles. Key biomarkers include lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release, cellular ATP levels, microRNA-122 (miR-122), cytokines (particularly IL-1α and MIP-1α), glutathione (GSH) levels, and caspase activation.

The invention provides methods for predicting in vivo toxicity by administering an oligonucleotide to primary hepatocytes, culturing the cells in vitro, measuring biomarkers of toxicity, and comparing the results to control data. The method enables selection of oligonucleotides with reduced toxicity for in vivo administration by screening libraries of oligonucleotide variants. The screening process involves obtaining a library of oligonucleotides, administering each to hepatocytes, culturing the cells, measuring toxicity biomarkers, and selecting compounds with favorable toxicity profiles.

The invention specifically utilizes gymnosis (gymnotic delivery) for oligonucleotide administration to primary hepatocytes, typically using concentrations between 1-30 μM with a short culturing period of 2-3 days. Biomarker measurements include LDH release as an indicator of cell membrane integrity, ATP levels as a measure of cell viability, miR-122 as a hepatocyte-specific damage marker, cytokines as indicators of inflammatory response, and glutathione levels as markers of oxidative stress. The invention provides various embodiments using different combinations of these biomarkers for comprehensive toxicity profiling.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Prediction of In Vivo Toxicity

The invention enables prediction of in vivo toxicity by assessing comparative toxicity profiles of oligonucleotides in primary hepatocyte cultures. The method identifies non-toxic oligonucleotides by comparing biomarker responses across test compounds, allowing selection of candidates with wide safety margins. The therapeutic index can be calculated by comparing the concentration causing toxicity biomarkers to the concentration required for target modulation. This approach facilitates identification of oligonucleotides with optimal efficacy-toxicity ratios before in vivo testing.

### Primary Mammalian Hepatocyte Cells

The invention utilizes primary mammalian hepatocyte cells as the preferred cellular model for toxicity prediction. Hepatocytes may be obtained through liver perfusion methods or as cryopreserved primary hepatocytes. Alternative cell sources include induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cell-derived hepatocytes. The cells may be cultured as monocultures or co-cultured with non-parenchymal liver cells to assess inflammatory responses. Predictive biomarkers measured in these cultures include ATP levels, LDH release, miR-122, cytokine secretion (in co-culture), and intracellular GSH levels. The inclusion of non-parenchymal cells enables assessment of immune-mediated toxicity mechanisms.

### Administering the Oligonucleotide—Gymnotic Delivery (Gymnosis)

The invention employs gymnotic delivery (gymnosis) for oligonucleotide administration to primary hepatocytes without transfection reagents. This method involves adding oligonucleotides directly to culture media at concentrations typically between 1-30 μM, followed by a short culturing period of 2-3 days under standard cell culture conditions. Key measurements include LDH and ATP levels compared to reference values, caspase activation as an apoptosis marker, and cytokine profiles. Gymnotic delivery provides advantages by mimicking physiological oligonucleotide uptake mechanisms observed in vivo, unlike artificial transfection methods that can alter cellular responses.

### Control Data/Reference Values

The invention utilizes control data for comparative assessment of oligonucleotide toxicity. Test compound results are compared to negative controls (vehicle-treated cells) and positive controls (known toxic oligonucleotides). Biomarker alterations indicative of toxicity include decreased cellular ATP levels, elevated LDH release, increased miR-122 secretion, altered cytokine profiles (particularly IL-1α and MIP-1α), and reduced GSH levels. The invention further provides methods for establishing control data from libraries of reference oligonucleotides with known in vivo toxicity profiles.

### (Determination of) Hepatotoxicity

Hepatotoxicity is determined through comprehensive biomarker profiling in primary hepatocyte cultures. The invention introduces specific liver toxicity biomarkers including LDH, ATP, miR-122, cytokines and GSH for in vitro oligonucleotide hepatotoxicity assessment. The method involves screening libraries of oligonucleotide variants by administering compounds to primary hepatocytes, culturing the cells, measuring toxicity biomarkers, and selecting candidates with reduced toxicity. Selected oligonucleotides may then be advanced to in vivo testing.

The invention is particularly useful for screening libraries of oligonucleotide variants derived from a parent compound, including stereodefined phosphorothioate oligonucleotides. Libraries may contain variants with different nucleobase sequences or chemical modifications, particularly gapmer designs with modified wings and central DNA regions. The method enables identification of stereodefined oligonucleotides with reduced in vivo toxicity while maintaining target engagement.

### Parent Oligonucleotide

The parent oligonucleotide may be any therapeutic oligonucleotide requiring toxicity optimization, particularly non-stereodefined phosphorothioate oligonucleotides. The parent compound serves as the starting point for generating a library of variants with improved toxicity profiles while retaining therapeutic activity.

### Library of Variants

The invention involves creating libraries of oligonucleotide variants from a parent compound. Each library member contains stereodefined phosphorothioate internucleoside linkages, with at least two and potentially all linkages being stereodefined. Variants are created by inserting stereodefined phosphorothioate linkages into gap regions, wing regions, or both, while maintaining the parent compound's pattern of modified and unmodified nucleosides. Child oligonucleotides may contain 1-20 independently stereodefined phosphorothioate linkages, with preferred embodiments having all phosphorothioate linkages stereodefined.

### Stereodefined Oligonucleotide Embodiments:

The invention provides methods for creating stereodefined oligonucleotides with reduced toxicity. The process involves specifying a parent oligonucleotide type, creating a library of stereodefined variants, screening for in vitro toxicity, and identifying variants with improved safety profiles. Key features include retaining the parent compound's pattern of modified and unmodified nucleosides while introducing stereodefined phosphorothioate linkages. The method further includes determining in vitro or in vivo potency of selected variants to ensure maintained therapeutic efficacy.

### Primary Hepatocytes Assay Example:

An exemplary assay protocol involves seeding primary hepatocytes at 0.25-0.4 x 10^5 cells/well in collagen-coated 96-well plates. Cells are precultured for 3-4 hours before oligonucleotide treatment in serum-free media. Culture conditions typically use William's Medium E at 37°C with 5% CO2. Biomarkers are measured after 2-3 days of treatment, with LDH and ATP serving as primary endpoints. The assay may be adapted for human hepatocytes or co-culture systems with non-parenchymal cells.

## Definitions

### Oligonucleotide

An oligonucleotide is a short nucleic acid polymer typically comprising 8-50 nucleotide units, more preferably 12-30 nucleotides. Oligonucleotides may contain natural or modified nucleotides, including 2'-modified sugars, locked nucleic acids (LNA), and phosphorothioate backbone modifications. They are synthesized using solid-phase phosphoramidite chemistry and purified by methods such as HPLC or PAGE. Oligonucleotides have various applications including as research tools, diagnostics, and therapeutics.

### Antisense Oligonucleotides (ASOs)

Antisense oligonucleotides are single-stranded oligonucleotides that hybridize to target RNA through Watson-Crick base pairing, modulating gene expression. ASOs may function through RNase H-mediated degradation of target RNA, steric blocking of translation, or modulation of splicing. Types include gapmers (with central DNA regions flanked by modified nucleotides), mixmers (alternating modified and unmodified nucleotides), and fully modified oligonucleotides. ASOs have therapeutic applications in various diseases including genetic disorders, cancers, and infectious diseases.

### Modified Internucleoside Linkages

Modified internucleoside linkages include phosphorothioate (PS) linkages where a sulfur atom replaces one non-bridging oxygen in the phosphate group. Other modifications include methylphosphonates, boranophosphates, and neutral linkages such as amide or methylene(methylimino) (MMI) groups. Nuclease-resistant linkages are particularly important for therapeutic oligonucleotides. Some embodiments may include phosphodiester linkages in combination with modified linkages.

### Modulation of Expression

Modulation of expression refers to altering target RNA or protein levels, including inhibition of expression (knockdown), restoration of normal expression, or alteration of splicing patterns. Determination of target modulation involves measuring RNA or protein levels by methods such as qPCR, Northern blot, Western blot, or immunoassays following oligonucleotide treatment.

### Modified Oligonucleotides

Modified oligonucleotides include chemical alterations to enhance properties such as nuclease resistance, binding affinity, or cellular uptake. Common modifications include phosphorothioate internucleoside linkages and high-affinity sugar modifications such as 2'-O-methyl, 2'-fluoro, or locked nucleic acid (LNA) nucleotides. LNA nucleotides contain a methylene bridge connecting the 2'-oxygen and 4'-carbon of the ribose ring, conferring enhanced binding affinity and nuclease resistance.

### RNase H Activity and Recruitment

RNase H activity refers to the enzymatic cleavage of RNA in RNA-DNA heteroduplexes. Oligonucleotides designed to recruit RNase H typically contain central DNA regions flanked by modified nucleotides that protect against nuclease degradation while allowing RNase H recognition and activity.

### Efficacy

Efficacy refers to the ability of an oligonucleotide to produce the desired therapeutic effect, typically measured as target RNA or protein reduction. Efficacy is determined through in vitro and in vivo testing, with key parameters including EC50 values (concentration producing 50% effect) and therapeutic index (ratio of toxic to effective dose). Potency retention is crucial when modifying oligonucleotides to reduce toxicity.

### Conjugate Moieties

Oligonucleotides may be conjugated to various moieties to enhance properties such as delivery, targeting, or pharmacokinetics. Conjugates include carbohydrates (e.g., GalNAc for liver targeting), proteins, lipids, small molecules, and polymers. These are typically attached to the 5' or 3' end of the oligonucleotide through linker chemistry.

### Stereodefined Oligonucleotides

Stereodefined oligonucleotides have controlled stereochemistry at phosphorothioate linkages, with defined Rp or Sp configurations at specific positions. Stereoselectivity refers to preferential formation of one stereoisomer, while stereospecificity describes the biological consequences of specific stereochemical configurations.

## Immunotoxicity Biomarker Assay:

The invention further provides methods for predicting immunotoxicity of oligonucleotides by measuring cytokine release profiles in hepatocyte-NPC co-cultures. Key biomarkers include IL-1α and MIP-1α, which show time-dependent increases with toxic oligonucleotides. The assay involves treating co-cultures with test compounds, collecting supernatants at 24, 48 and 64 hours, and analyzing cytokine levels by multiplex ELISA.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

Mouse liver perfusion and hepatocyte isolation were performed by retrograde two-step collagenase perfusion. Following euthanasia, the liver was perfused via the vena cava with calcium-free EGTA-supplemented Hank's solution, followed by collagenase-containing Hank's solution. Hepatocytes were isolated by mechanical dissociation, filtration through nylon mesh, and Percoll centrifugation. Cells were seeded at 0.25 x 10^5 cells/well in collagen-coated 96-well plates in William's Medium E with 10% FCS. After 3-4 hours, medium was replaced with serum-free WME and oligonucleotides added at 1-30 μM concentrations. RNA was isolated using RNeasy 96 Kit with DNase treatment. cDNA synthesis used iScript kit and qRT-PCR was performed with SYBR Green on a LightCycler 480. LDH was measured using Cytotoxicity Detection Kit, albumin by ELISA, GSH by monochlorobimane fluorescence, and ATP by CellTiter-Glo assay. Apoptosis was assessed using Caspase-Glo 3/7 assay, cytokines by Bio-Plex multiplex ELISA, and miR-122 by TaqMan miRNA assay following isolation with miRNeasy Mini Kit.

### Example 2

The in vitro hepatotoxicity assay was validated using 34 ASOs with known in vivo hepatotoxicity profiles. Primary mouse hepatocytes were treated with ASOs at 10 μM for 3 days, followed by measurement of LDH release and cellular ATP levels. The assay correctly predicted in vivo toxicity for 32 of 34 ASOs, demonstrating high predictive value. Two ASOs showing in vivo toxicity were not detected in vitro, possibly due to species-specific mechanisms or assay sensitivity limits.

### Example 3

Effects of oligonucleotides on hepatocyte function and viability were assessed by LDH and albumin secretion assays, along with intracellular GSH and ATP measurements. Toxic ASOs showed significant increases in LDH release and decreases in ATP and GSH levels after 3 days treatment, while safe ASOs showed minimal changes. Albumin secretion was less sensitive for detecting toxicity in this system.

### Example 4

ASO-mediated effects on apoptosis were evaluated by caspase-3/7 activation at 24, 48 and 64 hours. Toxic ASOs induced significant caspase activation by 48 hours, preceding changes in viability markers. Safe ASOs showed no caspase activation, correlating with their in vivo safety profiles.

### Example 5

Effects of oligonucleotides on hepatocyte-NPC co-cultures showed similar toxicity patterns to hepatocyte monocultures, with toxic ASOs increasing LDH and decreasing ATP and GSH. Co-cultures additionally showed cytokine release (IL-1α and MIP-1α) with toxic ASOs, providing additional biomarkers for immunotoxicity assessment.

### Example 6

Changes in secreted cytokines and miR-122 were evaluated in hepatocyte-NPC co-cultures. Toxic ASOs induced time-dependent increases in MIP-1α (by 24 hours) and IL-1α (by 48 hours), along with elevated miR-122 levels. These biomarkers provided early indicators of toxicity preceding cell viability changes.

### Example 7

miR-122 release was measured in hepatocyte monocultures and co-cultures treated with ASOs. Toxic ASOs increased miR-122 secretion 4-10-fold in both systems by 48 hours, indicating hepatocyte damage independent of NPC interactions.

### Example 8

Thirty-four ASOs with diverse in vivo hepatotoxicity profiles were tested in the in vitro assay. LDH and ATP measurements after 3 days treatment correctly predicted toxicity for 32 ASOs, demonstrating the assay's reliability for screening large compound sets.

### Example 9

Human primary hepatocytes were tested using similar protocols to mouse hepatocytes. Cryopreserved human hepatocytes were seeded at 0.4 x 10^5 cells/well and treated with ASOs for 3 days. The assay detected toxicity for ASOs with known clinical hepatotoxicity, though with generally weaker responses than in mouse cells. The human system correctly identified two clinical-stage ASOs with documented liver toxicity in human trials.
