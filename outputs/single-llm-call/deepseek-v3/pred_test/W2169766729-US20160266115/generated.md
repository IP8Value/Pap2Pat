# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The immune system is a complex network of cells, tissues, and organs that work collectively to defend the body against pathogens and maintain homeostasis. Key components of the immune system include leukocytes such as granulocytes, lymphocytes, and monocytes, each playing distinct roles in immune surveillance and response. Lymphocytes, including T cells, B cells, and natural killer (NK) cells, mediate adaptive and innate immunity, while monocytes and granulocytes contribute to inflammatory responses and pathogen clearance.  

The interplay between these immune components is critical for mounting effective immune responses. However, conventional methods for assessing immune status often focus on isolated markers or relative proportions of cell populations, which may not capture the systemic nature of immune function. A comprehensive approach to characterizing immune phenotypes is needed to better understand immune dysregulation in diseases such as cancer, autoimmune disorders, and acute inflammatory conditions.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a method for generating immune system profiles by quantifying leukocyte subtypes in peripheral blood using flow cytometry. The method involves measuring the absolute cell counts of defined immune markers, such as granulocytes, lymphocytes, monocytes, T cells, B cells, NK cells, regulatory T cells, and immunosuppressive monocytes, per unit volume of blood. These measurements are compiled into a database, which is then analyzed using clustering algorithms to identify common immune phenotypes among individuals.  

The invention further includes normalizing immune marker values relative to healthy controls and applying hierarchical clustering to group individuals with similar immune profiles. These profiles are characterized by distinct patterns of leukocyte composition and are associated with specific medical outcomes. For example, immune profiles resembling those of healthy volunteers correlate with improved survival, whereas aberrant profiles are linked to poor prognosis in diseases such as glioblastoma, renal cell carcinoma, and non-Hodgkin lymphoma.  

Additionally, the invention provides methods for diagnosing pathological immune subtypes by comparing an individual's immune phenotype to the database of immune profiles. The immune profiles can also predict responses to immune-modulating therapies, enabling personalized treatment strategies. Specific embodiments include methods for treating glioblastoma, renal cell carcinoma, and non-Hodgkin lymphoma by identifying a patient's immune profile and administering targeted therapy.  

The invention further encompasses a method for assessing the likelihood of medical outcomes based on immune profiles and for accelerating the testing of immune-modulating drugs by stratifying patients according to their immune status.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention employs flow cytometry to quantify leukocyte subsets in peripheral blood, providing a comprehensive assessment of immune system composition. Whole blood is stained with fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies targeting specific immune markers, and cell counts are determined using standardized gating strategies. The absolute numbers of cells per microliter of blood are recorded for each marker, enabling direct comparison across individuals.  

The data generated from flow cytometry are incorporated into a database, where immune marker values are normalized to the mean values of healthy controls. Clustering algorithms, such as hierarchical clustering and principal component analysis, are applied to identify groups of individuals with similar immune phenotypes. These groups, termed immune profiles, represent stable states of immune system composition and function.  

Five primary immune profiles have been identified, each defined by unique patterns of leukocyte counts. Profile 1 is characterized by reduced lymphocytes and elevated immunosuppressive monocytes, while Profile 2 exhibits decreased monocytes and immunosuppressive monocytes. Profile 3 features elevated granulocytes, monocytes, lymphocytes, regulatory T cells, and immunosuppressive monocytes. Profile 4 shows increased granulocytes and monocytes but decreased lymphocytes, including T, B, and NK cells. Profile 5 is marked by abnormally low monocytes and lymphocytes.  

The immune profiles are associated with distinct medical outcomes. For instance, patients with Profiles 1 or 2, which resemble healthy immune systems, exhibit significantly longer survival compared to those with aberrant profiles. The invention also includes methods for treating diseases by identifying a patient's immune profile and administering appropriate therapy. For example, glioblastoma patients with elevated immunosuppressive monocytes may benefit from therapies targeting these cells.  

The invention further provides reagents and protocols for performing flow cytometry, including antibody panels and data analysis software. Alternative methods for quantifying immune markers, such as PCR or microarray technologies, are also encompassed. The immune profiles can be used to predict responses to immune-modulating drugs and to identify novel biomarkers for disease prognosis.  

### EXAMPLES  

A study was conducted involving healthy volunteers and patients with glioblastoma (GBM), non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL), renal cell carcinoma (RCC), ovarian cancer (OVA), and acute lung injury (ALI). Peripheral blood samples were collected and analyzed using flow cytometry to quantify immune markers. Hierarchical clustering of the data revealed distinct immune profiles within and across disease groups.  

For example, GBM patients clustered into three profiles, with one profile consisting predominantly of dexamethasone-treated patients exhibiting severe immune suppression. ALI patients segregated into three profiles, one of which was associated with a 71% mortality rate. Across all diseases, five major immune profiles were identified, each with unique leukocyte compositions.  

A key finding was the inverse relationship between CD4+ T cells and CD14+HLA-DRlo/neg immunosuppressive monocytes. The ratio of CD4+ cells to immunosuppressive monocytes was found to be a prognostic biomarker, with a ratio below 2.0 correlating with significantly shorter survival in cancer patients.  

The study demonstrated that comprehensive immune profiling can identify shared immune dysregulation across diseases and predict clinical outcomes. This approach has broad applications in diagnostics, prognostics, and personalized medicine.
