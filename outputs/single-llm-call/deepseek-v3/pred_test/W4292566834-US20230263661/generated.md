# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNOLOGICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of ophthalmology, specifically concerning the diagnosis and treatment of ocular disorders involving particulate matter within the eye. More particularly, the invention provides systems and methods for noninvasively manipulating intraocular particles using acoustic radiation forces to mitigate visual disturbances and prevent secondary complications such as open-angle glaucoma. The disclosed technology encompasses acoustic devices capable of generating controlled acoustic fields to direct particle movement, aggregate particulate matter, and facilitate therapeutic interventions without surgical intervention.  

## BACKGROUND  

Ophthalmic disorders involving particulate matter in the aqueous humor or vitreous chamber present significant clinical challenges. Conditions such as hyphema, pseudoexfoliation syndrome, and asteroid hyalosis introduce particles that obstruct the visual axis, impair drainage pathways, or deposit on sensitive ocular tissues. Current treatment paradigms are largely reactive, addressing complications such as elevated intraocular pressure or corneal staining only after they manifest. Medical management often involves pharmacologic agents to reduce intraocular pressure, while surgical options include trabeculectomy or vitrectomy, both of which carry inherent risks.  

A critical limitation of existing therapies is their inability to prevent particle accumulation in the trabecular meshwork or corneal endothelium. The natural outflow of aqueous humor through Schlemm’s canal may become obstructed by particulate matter, leading to secondary open-angle glaucoma. Furthermore, visual disturbances caused by floating particles persist until spontaneous clearance occurs, which may take weeks or months. The lack of preventive or proactive treatment modalities underscores the need for innovative approaches to control intraocular particle dynamics.  

## GENERAL DESCRIPTION  

The invention addresses the unmet need for noninvasive particle moderation by leveraging acoustic radiation forces to manipulate particulate matter within the eye. Acoustic fields are generated by transducers configured to produce standing or traveling waves, which exert forces on particles based on their acoustic contrast relative to the surrounding medium. The acoustic contrast factor, defined by differences in density and compressibility between particles and aqueous humor, determines the direction and magnitude of particle displacement.  

Key aspects of the invention include the controlled aggregation of particles at nodal regions of standing waves, where acoustic forces are minimized. By strategically positioning these nodes, particles can be directed away from critical structures such as the trabecular meshwork or visual axis. The system further enables the hastening of particle clearance by acoustic streaming, wherein unidirectional wave propagation induces bulk fluid motion to expedite particle drift toward drainage pathways.  

The acoustic device comprises one or more transducers operably coupled to a controller that modulates frequency, amplitude, and phase to achieve desired particle trajectories. The transducer may be configured as a phased array to dynamically adjust acoustic radiation patterns, or as a single-element emitter with a collimating lens to focus energy. A coupling layer ensures impedance matching between the transducer and ocular tissues, minimizing energy loss and thermal effects.  

Notably, the invention provides solutions for both diagnostic and therapeutic applications. In diagnostic settings, controlled particle movement aids in differentiating particle types based on their acoustic responsiveness, facilitating targeted treatment plans. Therapeutically, the system prevents secondary complications by clearing the visual axis and reducing particle deposition in the trabecular meshwork. The technology is compatible with existing ophthalmic imaging systems, allowing real-time monitoring of particle manipulation.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The scope of the invention encompasses various embodiments tailored to specific anatomical and pathological contexts. The human eyeball comprises an anterior chamber filled with aqueous humor and a posterior vitreous chamber, separated by the iris and lens. The trabecular meshwork and Schlemm’s canal form the primary drainage pathway for aqueous humor, which may become obstructed by particulate matter.  

An exemplary embodiment includes an ocular acoustic device with a cylindrical piezoelectric transducer resonating at 1.1 MHz, though frequencies between 0.5–5 MHz are suitable depending on particle size. The transducer is mounted on a motion stage to adjust its position relative to the eye, enabling dynamic redirection of acoustic nodes. A controller regulates excitation parameters, including voltage (10–20 Vpp) and waveform (continuous sine or pulsed), to optimize particle trapping efficiency.  

In operation, the transducer generates standing waves that establish nodal planes spaced approximately 0.75 mm apart. Particles with positive acoustic contrast, such as triamcinolone acetonide (11.5 µm) or pigment aggregates, migrate to these nodes within seconds. The controller may switch to traveling wave mode to induce acoustic streaming, displacing particles toward the peripheral angle.  

The device may incorporate an acoustic hologram or phased array to sculpt complex pressure fields, enabling multi-focal particle aggregation. For example, particles can be concentrated in the inferior angle to preserve superior trabecular meshwork function. A coupling gel with acoustic impedance matching that of the cornea ensures efficient energy transfer while minimizing thermal rise (<2.5°C).  

The invention further includes methods for controlling particle movement, comprising: generating acoustic waves via a transducer; producing an acoustic field with defined nodal regions; and displacing particulate matter toward predetermined ocular locations. The method may be implemented in a networked computing environment, where imaging data guides real-time adjustments to acoustic parameters.  

In summary, the invention provides a versatile platform for noninvasive intraocular particle management, with applications spanning diagnostics, preventive care, and targeted therapy. The scope extends to all configurations of acoustic devices and methods that exploit acoustic contrast for particle manipulation in ophthalmic settings.
