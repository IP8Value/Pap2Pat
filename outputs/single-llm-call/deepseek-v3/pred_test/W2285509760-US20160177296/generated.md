# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for treating avian influenza virus (AIV) infections in poultry. Avian influenza virus poses significant threats to both animal and human health, with highly pathogenic strains causing severe economic losses in the poultry industry worldwide. Current estimates indicate that AIV outbreaks result in annual losses exceeding billions of dollars due to mass culling of infected flocks, trade restrictions, and control measures. The virus demonstrates remarkable genetic variability through antigenic drift and shift, making long-term vaccine strategies particularly challenging.

Conventional vaccination approaches against AIV suffer from several critical limitations. Most commercially available vaccines require subcutaneous or intramuscular administration, which presents logistical challenges for mass immunization of poultry flocks. The protective immune response typically requires two to three weeks to develop following vaccination, leaving birds vulnerable during this window period. Furthermore, vaccine efficacy depends on precise matching between the vaccine strain and circulating field strains, necessitating frequent reformulation as the virus evolves. Improper storage and handling of vaccines further contribute to inconsistent protection in field conditions.

The antigenic evolution of AIV represents a persistent obstacle to effective vaccine development. The virus's surface glycoproteins, particularly hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA), undergo continuous genetic changes that enable immune evasion. This antigenic variability necessitates constant surveillance and regular updates to vaccine compositions, creating substantial delays between virus emergence and vaccine availability. Additionally, current vaccination practices interfere with disease surveillance programs since vaccinated animals produce antibodies indistinguishable from natural infection, complicating differentiation between infected and vaccinated animals (DIVA testing).

Growing concerns regarding antiviral resistance further highlight the need for alternative prophylactic strategies. The emergence of AIV strains resistant to neuraminidase inhibitors such as oseltamivir underscores the limitations of existing pharmaceutical interventions. Moreover, the economic impact of AIV outbreaks extends beyond direct losses in poultry production to include trade restrictions, market disruptions, and costs associated with containment measures. The virus's ability to persist in aquatic environments and wild bird reservoirs creates persistent challenges for eradication efforts.

Current vaccination strategies fail to adequately address the critical issue of virus shedding, which drives transmission within and between flocks. Even vaccinated birds may continue to shed virus, facilitating silent spread within populations. The typical shedding period ranges from several days to weeks depending on virus strain and host factors. This prolonged shedding window necessitates preemptive culling policies that result in substantial animal welfare concerns and economic losses.

The limitations of current vaccination strategies create an urgent need for novel approaches that can provide rapid, broad-spectrum protection against diverse AIV strains while overcoming the logistical challenges of mass administration in poultry populations. An ideal solution would combine immediate antiviral activity with the ability to target conserved viral elements across multiple subtypes, thereby reducing dependence on strain-specific matching. Furthermore, such technology should be compatible with mass administration methods suitable for large-scale poultry operations while minimizing interference with disease surveillance programs.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for treating and preventing avian influenza virus infections through RNA interference (RNAi) technology. The invention utilizes small interfering RNA (siRNA) compositions specifically designed to target conserved regions of essential AIV genes, delivered via a proprietary transkingdom RNA interference (tkRNAi) platform. This innovative approach combines the specificity of RNAi with an efficient bacterial delivery system to create a versatile antiviral solution for poultry.

The tkRNAi platform employs nonpathogenic Escherichia coli bacteria genetically engineered to express short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) targeting AIV nucleoprotein (NP), polymerase acidic (PA), polymerase basic 1 (PB1), or polymerase basic 2 (PB2) mRNA sequences. These bacterial vectors incorporate two critical genetic elements: the invasion (inv) gene encoding invasin protein for cellular entry and the hlyA gene encoding listeriolysin O (LLO) for endosomal escape. The system takes advantage of conserved sequences within AIV genomes to provide broad protection across multiple subtypes and strains.

The invention specifically discloses siRNA sequences targeting NP mRNA (5'-GGA TCT TAT TTC TTC GGA G-3') and PA mRNA (5'-GCA ATT GAG GAG TGC CTG A-3'), which demonstrate 100% sequence conservation across numerous AIV isolates. These sequences form the basis of individual therapeutic vectors (anti-AIV/NP and anti-AIV/PA) as well as a combination "cocktail" vector (anti-AIV/cocktail) providing simultaneous targeting of multiple viral genes. The shRNA expression is driven by T7, T3, or SP6 promoters within the pmbv43 plasmid vector system.

The invention provides methods for treating or reducing the risk of AIV infection in avian subjects through administration of these therapeutic compositions. Preferred administration routes include intranasal delivery, oral administration, or aerosol inhalation, allowing for practical large-scale application in poultry operations. The pharmaceutical composition may comprise a single E. coli bacterium expressing one shRNA or a combination of two distinct E. coli bacteria each expressing different shRNAs targeting separate viral genes.

The tkRNAi delivery platform offers several advantages over conventional RNAi approaches. The bacterial vectors specifically target mucosal epithelial cells through β(1) integrin receptor-mediated endocytosis, providing direct delivery to respiratory tissues where AIV replication occurs. Following cellular uptake, listeriolysin O facilitates endosomal escape and cytoplasmic release of shRNAs, which are processed by the host RNAi machinery into functional siRNAs. These siRNAs then guide sequence-specific silencing of complementary viral mRNA targets.

The invention demonstrates significant reductions in infectious viral titers in avian cell models, with log10 reductions ranging from 1.9 to 4.0 compared to untreated controls. The cocktail vector approach shows particular promise, achieving up to 10,000-fold reductions in viral shedding. Importantly, the technology maintains efficacy against multiple AIV subtypes including H6N2 and H8N4, suggesting broad applicability across diverse field strains.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

The present invention addresses critical limitations in current avian influenza control strategies through development of an innovative RNA interference-based antiviral platform. The technology combines the specificity of RNAi with an efficient bacterial delivery system to create a versatile prophylactic and therapeutic solution for AIV in poultry. ### Motivation for Anti-Influenza Technology

Traditional vaccination approaches against AIV face inherent limitations due to the virus's antigenic variability and the logistical challenges of poultry immunization. The present invention circumvents these limitations by targeting conserved viral sequences essential for replication, rather than variable surface antigens. RNA interference provides an ideal mechanism for such targeting, offering sequence-specific silencing of viral genes with minimal off-target effects. However, the clinical application of RNAi has been hindered by delivery challenges, particularly in mucosal tissues where AIV primarily replicates.

The tkRNAi platform overcomes these delivery obstacles through use of engineered bacteria that naturally target epithelial cells. This approach leverages the natural tropism of invasive bacteria for mucosal surfaces while incorporating molecular components to facilitate cytoplasmic delivery of therapeutic shRNAs. The system has been optimized for avian applications through careful selection of target sequences and delivery parameters specific to poultry respiratory tissues.

### Limitations of Current Vaccination Strategies

Conventional AIV vaccines predominantly induce humoral immunity against HA and NA surface glycoproteins. While effective against matched strains, this approach fails to provide universal protection due to antigenic variation. Furthermore, intramuscular administration fails to induce robust mucosal immunity at primary sites of viral replication. The present invention addresses these shortcomings by inducing intracellular antiviral activity directly in respiratory epithelial cells, independent of viral subtype.

### Limitations of RNA Interference Delivery

Previous attempts to develop RNAi-based antivirals have been hampered by delivery challenges. Viral vectors raise safety concerns including insertional mutagenesis and immunogenicity, while synthetic carriers suffer from low efficiency and toxicity at effective doses. The tkRNAi platform provides a safer alternative using nonpathogenic, non-replicating bacteria that deliver shRNAs without genomic integration. The system has demonstrated an excellent safety profile in clinical applications while offering scalable production advantages.

### Transkingdom RNAi Delivery Platform

The tkRNAi system comprises genetically modified E. coli (strain CEQ221) carrying the pmbv43 expression plasmid. This plasmid encodes three critical functional elements: 1) shRNA sequences under control of bacteriophage promoters (T7, T3, or SP6), 2) the inv gene for cellular invasion, and 3) the hlyA gene for endosomal escape. The bacterial vector is auxotrophic for diaminopimelic acid (Dap), ensuring limited replication in host tissues.

The invasion protein mediates β(1) integrin-dependent endocytosis into epithelial cells, while listeriolysin O forms pores in endosomal membranes to release shRNAs into the cytoplasm. Once liberated, shRNAs are processed by Dicer into 21-23nt siRNAs that guide RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC)-mediated cleavage of complementary viral mRNAs.

### Application to Anti-AIV Vectors

The invention specifically applies the tkRNAi platform to AIV through development of vectors expressing shRNAs targeting conserved regions of NP and PA genes. These targets were selected based on their essential roles in viral replication and high sequence conservation across subtypes. NP forms the viral ribonucleoprotein complex required for genome packaging, while PA contributes to the RNA-dependent RNA polymerase activity.

The anti-AIV/NP vector expresses shRNA targeting nucleotides 1453-1471 of H6N2 NP (GenBank KM244053) or equivalent conserved regions in other subtypes. The anti-AIV/PA vector targets nucleotides 2065-2083 of H6N2 PA (GenBank KM244051). These sequences show 100% conservation across >10,000 influenza A isolates, including recent H5, H7, and H9 subtypes.

### Avian Tissue Model Development

The invention establishes an avian tissue model for evaluating anti-AIV vector efficacy using chicken hepatocellular carcinoma (LMH) cells. This model validates β(1) integrin expression on avian epithelial cells and demonstrates efficient vector uptake. LMH cells infected with AIV strains H6N2 and H8N4 provide a reproducible system for quantifying antiviral effects through TCID50 assays.

Vector invasion efficiency in LMH cells exceeds 75%, with intracellular shRNA expression confirmed by fluorescent microscopy and flow cytometry. Optimal vector concentration was determined to be 7.8 × 10^5 CFU/mL, balancing efficacy with absence of cytotoxicity as assessed by cell viability assays.

### Verification of Vector Uptake and Invasion

The invention demonstrates successful tkRNAi vector invasion into avian epithelial cells through multiple experimental approaches. β(1) integrin receptor expression was confirmed in LMH cells by RT-PCR under both uninfected and AIV-infected conditions. Red fluorescent protein (RFP)-tagged vectors allowed visualization of intracellular bacteria by fluorescence microscopy, while flow cytometry quantified invasion efficiencies.

These studies established that vector uptake occurs within 2 hours post-invasion, with sustained shRNA expression detectable for at least 36 hours. The presence of listeriolysin O facilitated efficient endosomal escape, as evidenced by cytoplasmic RFP distribution rather than punctate endosomal patterns.

### Evaluation of Anti-AIV Vector Efficacy

The invention provides comprehensive data demonstrating anti-AIV vector efficacy in reducing infectious viral titers. Multivariable linear regression analysis of TCID50 results showed statistically significant reductions in viral shedding across multiple experimental replicates.

For H8N4 virus, the anti-AIV/cocktail vector achieved a 4.0 log10 reduction (10,000-fold) in viral titer compared to untreated controls (p<0.001). Individual vectors targeting NP or PA showed 3.8 and 2.5 log10 reductions respectively. Similar patterns were observed for H6N2, with the cocktail vector producing a 1.9 log10 reduction (79.4-fold, p=0.013).

### Roles of NP and PA Proteins

The selection of NP and PA as therapeutic targets reflects their essential conserved functions in the viral lifecycle. NP forms oligomers that encapsulate viral RNA, protecting it from host defenses while facilitating replication and packaging. PA contributes to the viral polymerase complex along with PB1 and PB2, playing critical roles in transcription and genome replication.

Targeting these highly conserved proteins provides several advantages over HA/NA-focused approaches. First, sequence conservation reduces the likelihood of escape mutations. Second, disrupting core replication machinery affects multiple stages of the viral lifecycle. Third, these targets remain consistent across subtypes, enabling broad-spectrum activity.

### Design of siRNAs Targeting Conserved Regions

The invention discloses specific siRNA sequences demonstrating exceptional conservation across AIV strains. BLAST analysis confirmed 100% identity with corresponding regions in over 10,000 influenza A isolates, including avian, swine, and human strains. The sequences show no significant homology to chicken genes, minimizing off-target effects.

The 19-nucleotide target sequences were incorporated into shRNA constructs featuring a 9-nt hairpin loop (5'-TTC AAG AGA-3') and poly-T termination signal. These designs optimize Dicer processing and RISC loading while preventing interferon responses associated with longer dsRNA fragments.

### Design of siRNAs and Use of Viral Specific siRNAs to Inhibit Avian Influenza in an Avian Tissue Model

The invention provides detailed methodologies for designing and implementing siRNA-based antivirals against AIV in avian cell models. The technology establishes an avian tissue transfection and infection model using chicken LMH cells to evaluate siRNA efficacy against two LPAI subtypes (H8N4 and H6N2). This system enables quantitative assessment of antiviral effects through TCID50 assays and statistical analysis using multivariable linear regression.

Key experimental components include characterization of chicken β(1) integrin expression, optimization of vector invasion conditions, and verification of intracellular shRNA delivery. The model demonstrates consistent antiviral activity across multiple experimental replicates, with statistically significant reductions in viral shedding titers observed for both individual and cocktail siRNA treatments.

The Materials and Methods section details specific protocols for cell culture maintenance, viral propagation, siRNA design, vector construction, and TCID50 quantification. Chicken hepatocellular carcinoma epithelial cells (LMH) serve as the primary avian tissue model, maintained in Waymouth's MB 752/1 medium under standardized conditions. Madin-Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells provide the substrate for infectious titer determination through TCID50 assay.

The invention utilizes well-characterized LPAI strains (H6N2 and H8N4) propagated in LMH cells to establish infection models. Viral stocks are quantified by TCID50 assay, with working stocks maintained at -80°C. Optimal multiplicity of infection (MOI) is empirically determined based on cytopathic effect development.

The siRNA design process incorporates published NP and PA targeting sequences verified for 100% complementarity to H6N2 and H8N4 target genes through BLAST analysis. The 19-nt sequences demonstrate no significant homology to chicken genes, minimizing off-target effects. shRNA constructs are cloned into the pmbv43 plasmid featuring T7 promoter, inv gene, and hlyA gene.

Transfection efficiency is optimized using fluorescent controls and viability assays to establish non-cytotoxic vector concentrations. The invention determines 7.8 × 10^5 CFU/mL as the optimal anti-AIV vector dose for LMH cell invasion, balancing efficacy with absence of cytotoxicity.

For antiviral evaluation, LMH cells are pre-treated with anti-AIV vectors (NP, PA, cocktail, or scramble) followed by infection with H6N2 or H8N4 at MOI 0.01. Culture supernatants are harvested at 48 hours post-infection for TCID50 quantification on MDCK cells. The Reed and Muench method calculates viral titers, with statistical analysis using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests and multivariable linear regression to compare treatment groups while controlling for experimental day variability.
