## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to virology and infectious diseases. More specifically, the invention provides novel systems and methods for producing infectious hepatitis C virus (HCV) particles in mammalian cells, particularly for genotypes that are difficult to culture in conventional systems. The invention further provides methods for studying HCV entry, assembly, and release mechanisms, as well as systems for screening potential antiviral compounds.  

## BACKGROUND  

Hepatitis C virus (HCV) infects approximately 2-3% of the world's population, with the majority of infected individuals failing to clear the virus and developing serious liver complications. HCV belongs to the genus Hepacivirus within the Flaviviridae family and exists as at least six distinct genotypes, with genotypes 1a and 1b accounting for over two-thirds of global infections. Despite significant medical need, there remains no approved vaccine for HCV, and available treatments show reduced effectiveness against genotype 1 compared to other genotypes.  

Current limitations in HCV research stem from several technical challenges. The primary animal model for HCV, chimpanzees, has limited experimental availability. Small animal models fail to accurately reproduce true HCV infection. The structure of intact HCV virions remains unknown, and the mechanisms by which the viral RNA genome circulates in infected patients are poorly understood. While hepatocytes are the natural target cells for HCV, most human hepatic cell lines poorly propagate patient-derived HCV isolates in vitro.  

Previous breakthroughs in HCV research include the development of subgenomic replicons (lacking structural genes) of subtypes 1b and 1a in selected subclones of human hepatic Huh-7 cell lines. Additionally, a full infectious cycle was reproduced in cell culture using the JFH-1 strain (genotype 2a) or J6/JFH-1 chimeras, producing particles termed HCVcc. However, these models are complicated by the high error rate of HCV RNA replicase, leading to the emergence of adaptive variants with altered infectivity profiles. Cell culture-adapted HCV often displays reduced infectivity or impaired fitness in vivo, while consensus sequence genomes that are infectious in chimpanzees frequently fail to infect cultured cells. These limitations are particularly pronounced for genotype 1 strains, where cell-adaptive mutations typically result in low yields of HCVcc with impaired infectivity.  

Flaviviridae members share common replication features, including cytoplasmic replication and induction of endoplasmic reticulum-derived membrane rearrangements. Previous work demonstrated that flavivirus virions could be produced by providing their structural genes in trans. Building on these observations, the current invention provides novel systems for producing infectious HCV particles that overcome the limitations of existing models by uncoupling HCV particle production from HCV RNA replication.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a mammalian cell system capable of producing replication-competent HCV particles. The system comprises a plasmid encoding HCV genes under control of a bacteriophage promoter, coupled with a cytoplasmic RNA amplification system. This configuration allows production of HCV structural proteins and assembly of infectious particles without requiring active HCV RNA replication, thereby avoiding the accumulation of cell-adaptive mutations that compromise viral fitness.  

In one embodiment, the invention provides a plasmid encoding HCV polynucleotide sequences flanked by a bacteriophage T7 promoter at the 5' end and a hepatitis delta virus antisense ribozyme at the 3' end. The invention further provides a first host cell line, preferably baby hamster kidney (BHK) cells carrying a West Nile virus (WNV) subgenomic replicon (BHK-WNV cells), which creates a permissive environment for HCV particle assembly and release.  

The system further includes a second plasmid encoding a reporter gene, such as firefly luciferase or enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP), and a second host cell line for assaying HCV entry. The reporter system allows quantitative measurement of HCV entry by detecting reporter gene expression following viral infection.  

The invention provides methods for identifying cellular proteins necessary for HCV entry. These methods involve transfecting the second host cell line with small interfering RNA (siRNA) targeting candidate cellular proteins and measuring changes in reporter gene expression. Alternatively, the second host cell may be transduced with lentivirus expressing candidate proteins to assess their role in viral entry.  

Additional embodiments provide methods for identifying HCV glycoproteins essential for entry by incubating HCV particles with antibodies targeting specific viral glycoproteins and measuring subsequent changes in infectivity. The system also enables identification of test compounds that effectively block HCV entry or viral genome uncoating by measuring reductions in reporter gene expression following compound administration.  

The invention further provides methods for identifying cellular factors involved in HCV release. These methods involve isolating clones of the first host cell line that fail to produce infectious HCV particles, followed by subtractive hybridization to identify host cell-specific genes critical for viral production.  

Additional applications include methods for identifying test compounds that inhibit HCV assembly by incubating the first host cell line with candidate compounds, collecting resulting HCV particles, and measuring their infectivity in the second host cell line. The system also enables study of HCV spreading through use of biarsenical dyes to track viral particles in infected cells and tissues.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATIVE EMBODIMENTS  

The following terms shall have the meanings set forth below unless otherwise specified. The singular forms "a," "an," and "the" include plural referents unless the context clearly dictates otherwise. The term "about" when referring to a numerical value indicates an approximate range that is plus or minus 10% of the stated value.  

A "promoter" refers to a DNA sequence recognized by RNA polymerase to initiate transcription of a downstream gene. In preferred embodiments, the promoter is a bacteriophage T7 promoter. The term "operably linked" means that genetic elements are joined in a way that permits them to function together for their intended purpose, such as a promoter positioned to direct transcription of a coding sequence.  

A "reporter gene" refers to a gene whose expression product can be readily detected, including but not limited to genes encoding luciferase, fluorescent proteins, or enzymes producing detectable products. A "transformed cell" refers to a cell containing introduced nucleic acid molecules, while "transformation" refers to the process of introducing such nucleic acids.  

A "replicon" refers to a nucleic acid molecule capable of autonomous replication in a host cell. A "sub-genomic flavivirus replicon" refers to a replicon derived from flavivirus that lacks one or more viral genes required for particle formation but retains sequences necessary for RNA replication.  

A "test compound" refers to any chemical or biological molecule being evaluated for its effects on HCV infection, including small molecules, peptides, antibodies, and nucleic acids. "Subtractive hybridization" refers to techniques for identifying differences in gene expression between two cell populations by selectively removing common sequences.  

The invention encompasses various embodiments of transformed mammalian cells. In one embodiment, the cell is transformed with a plasmid encoding HCV polynucleotide sequences under control of a bacteriophage promoter. In another embodiment, the cell is a recombinant mammalian cell line, preferably BHK cells, containing a flavivirus subgenomic replicon.  

Methods for producing HCV structural proteins involve transfecting appropriate host cells with plasmids encoding HCV sequences and culturing the cells under conditions allowing protein expression. The invention provides isolated replication-competent HCV particles produced by these methods, as well as systems for assaying HCV particle entry into target cells.  

The HCV particle entry assay involves incubating target cells with viral particles and measuring reporter gene expression. To enhance specificity, actinomycin D may be added to block RNA polymerase II-dependent transcription. Preferred reporter genes include firefly luciferase and EGFP.  

Methods for identifying cellular proteins necessary for HCV entry include transfecting target cells with siRNA targeting candidate proteins and measuring subsequent changes in viral entry. Membrane proteins particularly implicated in HCV entry include SR-BI, CD81, ASGP-R subunit 1, ASGP-R subunit 2, and claudin-1. Alternative approaches involve transducing cells with plasmids or lentivirus expressing candidate proteins and measuring increases in viral entry.  

To identify HCV glycoproteins essential for entry, the invention provides methods involving incubation of HCV particles with antibodies targeting specific viral glycoproteins and measurement of resulting decreases in infectivity. Screening methods for compounds that block HCV entry involve incubating target cells with test compounds prior to viral infection and measuring reductions in reporter gene expression.  

The invention further provides methods for studying HCV spreading and dissemination using biarsenical dyes to track viral particles in infected cells, tissues, or whole animals. These methods enable investigation of viral spreading dynamics and evaluation of antiviral compounds in physiologically relevant contexts.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Materials and Methods  

**Cell Cultures:** BHK-21 cells were grown in E-MEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and GlutaMax-I. BHK cells harboring WNV lineage II subgenomic replicon encoding Renilla luciferase (BHK-WNV cells) were propagated in D-MEM supplemented with 10% FBS, GlutaMax-I and 5 µg/ml blasticidin. Huh-7.5 cells and derived clones were maintained as described previously.  

**Plasmid Constructs:** A novel plasmid system (p2B) was designed to amplify cytoplasmic transcription of genes under control of bacteriophage promoters. The system comprises two plasmids: pCR-T7p/SP6pol encoding SP6 RNA polymerase under T7 promoter control, and pSL-SP6p/T7pol encoding T7 RNA polymerase under SP6 promoter control. HCV coding plasmids were constructed with T7 promoter, HCV sequence, and hepatitis delta virus ribozyme at the 3' end.  

**Antibodies:** Anti-HCV antibodies included monoclonal antibodies against E1 (A4), E2 (ALP98, AP33), and core (C7-50), plus rabbit polyclonal antibody against NS5A. Antibodies against candidate receptors included anti-CD81 (JS-81), anti-SR-BI, anti-ASGPR-1, and anti-claudin.  

**Production of HCV Particles:** BHK-WNV cells were transfected with HCV-coding plasmids using Lipofectamine LTX. Culture supernatants were harvested, clarified by centrifugation, and concentrated by ultracentrifugation. Particles were purified on sucrose gradients and analyzed by electron microscopy.  

**Infectivity Assays:** Huh-7.5 cells were incubated with HCV particles and analyzed for NS5A expression by immunofluorescence or for viral RNA by RT-qPCR. Reporter assays used cells transfected with plasmids encoding luciferase or EGFP under bacteriophage promoter control.  

### Example 2: HCV-like Particles Production  

BHK-WNV cells produced HCV-like particles when transfected with plasmids encoding HCV structural genes. Flavivirus subgenomic replicons enhanced particle release compared to parental BHK-21 cells. Analysis of released particles demonstrated no trans-encapsidation of WNV or dengue virus subgenomic replicons.  

### Example 3: Glycosylation Requirements  

Treatment of BHK cells with glycosylation inhibitors affected secretion of HCV envelope and core proteins. Western blot analysis demonstrated that N-glycosylation is required for proper processing of HCV structural proteins.  

### Example 4: HCVbp Production and Characterization  

BHK-WNV cells transfected with HCVbp-coding plasmid produced enveloped particles visible by transmission electron microscopy. Sucrose gradient analysis showed particles with buoyant densities of 1.08-1.13 g/cm3 were infectious for Huh-7.5 cells, as demonstrated by NS5A expression and bromo-uridine incorporation into replicating RNA. CD81 was required for efficient particle entry.  

### Example 5: Authentic Wild-type HCV Production  

BHK-WNV cells produced authentic wild-type HCV particles visible by negative stain electron microscopy as 50-60 nm spherical particles. These particles infected human liver slices, as shown by specific staining with anti-HCV antibodies and biarsenical dyes in periportal spaces.  

### Example 6: CD81 Requirement for Entry  

HepG2 cells transduced with lentivirus encoding human CD81 became permissive for HCV particle entry, demonstrating CD81's essential role in viral entry.  

### Example 7: Cellular Factors in Particle Release  

Comparative analysis identified cellular factors induced by WNV subgenomic replicon that enhance HCV particle release. siRNA knockdown studies implicated specific host proteins in viral assembly and egress.  

### Example 8: Antiviral Drug Effects  

Treatment of BHK-WNV cells with antiviral drugs inhibited WNV subgenomic replicon activity but did not affect HCV particle production. Electron microscopy revealed changes in intracellular trafficking in drug-treated cells.
