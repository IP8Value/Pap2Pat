# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to electrical machines, particularly to improved thermal management systems for high-power electric motors used in transportation applications. More specifically, the invention concerns novel stator constructions incorporating interleaved ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic materials to enhance heat transfer while maintaining electromagnetic performance. The disclosed technology is particularly advantageous for electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft propulsion systems where compact, high-reliability motors must handle extreme transient power demands.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Modern electric transportation systems impose stringent power requirements on their propulsion components. In electric aircraft applications, particularly eVTOL configurations, the electric propulsion system must deliver 3-10 times more power during takeoff and landing compared to cruise conditions. These transient power surges create substantial thermal challenges as increased current density leads to rapid temperature rise in the motor windings. Conventional active cooling systems often prove inadequate for managing these thermal transients while maintaining the required power density and reliability.  

Existing thermal management approaches for electric motors face several limitations. Traditional iron-core stator designs exhibit poor thermal conductivity perpendicular to the lamination plane, creating thermal bottlenecks. Active liquid cooling systems add complexity, weight, and potential failure modes undesirable in aerospace applications. Air cooling methods using simple radial fin heatsinks often cannot provide sufficient heat transfer capacity for the extreme transient conditions encountered in eVTOL operations.  

Phase change materials have been explored for thermal energy storage in some applications, but they introduce new challenges including limited cycle life, temperature regulation difficulties, and added mass. What is needed is a passive thermal management approach that enhances the inherent heat transfer capabilities of the motor structure itself without compromising electromagnetic performance or adding excessive weight. The present invention addresses these needs through novel material integration in the stator construction.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a revolutionary approach to motor thermal management through the strategic interleaving of ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic materials in the stator construction. The disclosed architecture maintains excellent magnetic performance while dramatically improving thermal conductivity to the heatsink.  

At the core of the invention is a stator body constructed from laminated layers combining both ferromagnetic and nonferromagnetic materials. The ferromagnetic layers, typically electrical steel laminations, provide the necessary magnetic flux paths, while the interleaved nonferromagnetic layers, preferably high-conductivity copper, create thermal shunts to rapidly conduct heat away from the windings. This dual-material lamination approach achieves a remarkable improvement in thermal conductivity without compromising the magnetic circuit integrity.  

The nonferrous material incorporated in the laminations exhibits high thermal conductivity, typically exceeding 200 W/m·K, with copper being the preferred embodiment. The volume ratio of nonferrous to ferrous material is carefully optimized to balance thermal and electromagnetic performance, with a preferred range between 15-25% nonferrous content. This ratio provides substantial thermal benefit while limiting torque reduction to less than 1% in typical implementations.  

The lamination orientation is specifically arranged to maximize both magnetic and thermal performance. The ferromagnetic layers are aligned to optimize flux conduction in the radial direction while the nonferromagnetic layers provide axial thermal conduction paths. The axial thickness of individual laminations is precisely controlled, typically between 0.005-0.015 inches, to minimize eddy current losses while maintaining structural integrity.  

The invention further incorporates a slotless motor design that eliminates traditional stator teeth, allowing increased winding area and reduced magnetic saturation. A specialized coil carrier constructed from high thermal conductivity ceramic material provides both electrical insulation and enhanced heat transfer paths. The ceramic carrier, preferably aluminum nitride (AlN) or boron nitride (BN), is machined to precise dimensions that optimize both thermal performance and electromagnetic airgap characteristics.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The invention finds particular utility in aircraft applications, especially electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) configurations. In such implementations, the electric motor works in conjunction with a primary propulsion system to provide thrust during critical flight phases. The motor drive system interfaces with an aircraft controller that manages power distribution based on flight requirements.  

A preferred embodiment utilizes a slotless-winding, outer-rotor, air-cooled permanent magnet synchronous machine (PMSM) configuration. The rotor comprises a Halbach permanent magnet array mounted to an outer shell, while the innovative stator construction forms the core of the thermal management system. The stator incorporates the interleaved lamination structure described above, combined with the high-performance ceramic coil carrier.  

The coil form represents a critical component of the thermal management system, constructed from ceramic material with thermal conductivity exceeding 100 W/m·K. The coil construction utilizes form-wound Litz wire positioned within precisely machined ceramic channels that provide both electrical insulation and thermal conduction paths. The ceramic geometry is optimized to balance thermal performance against electromagnetic considerations, with typical wall thicknesses between 0.015-0.030 inches.  

Magnetic flux generation follows conventional PMSM principles, but with enhanced control enabled by the novel flux conductor design incorporating the interleaved laminations. The flux conductor comprises alternating layers of ferromagnetic steel and nonferromagnetic copper, each precisely dimensioned to maintain magnetic performance while enhancing thermal transfer. The steel laminations typically use high-grade electrical steel such as HF-10 C5 insulated laminations, while the copper layers preferably utilize Copper 110 alloy foil.  

The thermal conductivity of the composite lamination structure demonstrates marked improvement over conventional designs. While typical electrical steel exhibits thermal conductivity around 30 W/m·K in the plane of the laminations and only 2 W/m·K perpendicular to the laminations, the interleaved copper layers create effective thermal conductivity exceeding 150 W/m·K in the axial direction. This enables heat to rapidly conduct from the windings to the heatsink structure.  

The nonferromagnetic laminations are distributed axially throughout the stator stack with precise periodicity to maintain balanced performance. In a preferred embodiment, one copper lamination is inserted for every two steel laminations, achieving approximately 20% copper content by volume. This distribution provides optimal thermal performance while limiting eddy current losses to negligible levels (typically <2W in a 300kW motor).  

The heatsink incorporates radial fins optimized for air cooling in aerospace applications. Airflow through the heatsink follows axial paths between the fins, with the high thermal conductivity stator structure efficiently delivering heat to the fin surfaces. Heat dissipation occurs through combined convection and radiation mechanisms, with the improved thermal paths allowing lower operating temperatures at given power levels.  

Manufacturing the innovative stator structure involves specialized processes to ensure proper material integration. The coil winding process utilizes precision placement within the ceramic carrier channels. Potting forms and resin impregnation techniques are adapted to accommodate the ceramic components while maintaining high dielectric strength. An alternative construction method employs additive manufacturing for the heatsink structure to further optimize cooling performance.  

The motor drive system incorporates advanced monitoring capabilities to track power consumption and predict thermal behavior. A thermal model continuously estimates temperature rise based on operating conditions, allowing proactive management before reaching critical thresholds. When approaching temperature limits, the system provides warnings and can throttle power output to maintain safe operation. Predictive algorithms calculate future temperature trajectories based on current trends and planned power profiles.  

A user interface displays thermal margin information to operators, showing available power headroom before thermal limits would require derating. This information proves particularly valuable in eVTOL applications where power demand varies dramatically between flight phases. The display may present information numerically, graphically, or through simplified status indicators depending on application requirements.
