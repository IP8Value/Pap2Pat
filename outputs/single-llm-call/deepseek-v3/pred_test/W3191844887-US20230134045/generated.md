# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### 1. Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates to a novel method and kit for evaluating prognosis in patients with breast cancer. More specifically, the invention provides a means to assess the risk of recurrence and disease progression in patients diagnosed with luminal breast cancer that is hormone receptor (HR) positive and human epidermal growth factor receptor type 2 (HER2) negative. The method utilizes the detection of pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) expression levels as prognostic biomarkers.  

### 2. The Prior Art  

Current methods for evaluating prognosis in breast cancer patients rely primarily on clinicopathological parameters such as tumor size, nodal status, and histological grade. While these factors provide useful prognostic information, they have significant limitations in predicting long-term outcomes, particularly in HR+/HER2- luminal breast cancer. Existing molecular diagnostic tools such as OncotypeDx, MammaPrint, and Prosigna offer improved risk stratification but are costly and complex to implement in routine clinical practice. These gene-expression-based assays require specialized laboratory processing and analysis, limiting their accessibility. There remains an unmet need for simple, cost-effective prognostic methods that can accurately predict recurrence risk and guide treatment decisions for patients with HR+/HER2- luminal breast cancer.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method for evaluating prognosis in patients with breast cancer, particularly luminal breast cancer that is HR positive and HER2 negative. The method comprises detecting expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in a biological sample obtained from the patient. High expression levels of both PKM2 and O-GlcNAc are indicative of poor prognosis, including increased risk of recurrence and decreased disease-free survival.  

The method involves obtaining a tissue sample from the patient, performing immunohistochemical analysis to determine PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression levels, and correlating these expression levels with clinical outcome data. A quantitative scoring system is employed to assess the intensity and distribution of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc staining. The method may further incorporate clinical parameters such as tumor stage or the clinical treatment score post-5 years (CTS5) to enhance prognostic accuracy.  

The invention also provides a kit for evaluating prognosis in breast cancer patients. The kit includes reagents for detecting PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression, such as specific antibodies against these markers, along with appropriate controls and instructions for use. The kit enables standardized assessment of these biomarkers in clinical specimens.  

The method and kit of the present invention offer several advantages over existing prognostic tools. They provide a simple, cost-effective approach that can be implemented using routine immunohistochemistry, making them widely accessible in clinical settings. The combination of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc biomarkers offers superior prognostic accuracy compared to single markers or clinical parameters alone. Furthermore, these metabolic biomarkers provide complementary information to existing clinicopathological and genomic assays, enabling more comprehensive risk assessment.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT  

The following detailed description provides specific embodiments of the method and kit for evaluating prognosis in breast cancer patients. While the invention is described in relation to luminal HR+/HER2- breast cancer, it may have applications in other breast cancer subtypes as well.  

### Definition  

For purposes of this invention, "data representation" refers to the quantitative assessment of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression levels through immunohistochemical analysis. Expression levels are represented as a Q score calculated by multiplying the staining intensity score (0-3) by the percentage of immunopositive cells (0-100), resulting in a range of 0-300.  

"High expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc" are defined as Q scores above the mean value observed in a reference population of breast cancer patients. The exact cutoff values may be adjusted based on validation studies in different patient cohorts.  

The "clinical treatment score post-5 years (CTS5)" is a known prognostic tool that integrates tumor size, tumor grade, patient age, and number of positive lymph nodes to estimate the risk of late recurrence after 5 years of endocrine therapy.  

### High Expression of Pyruvate Kinase Isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) is Associated With Poor Clinical Outcome in Luminal Breast Cancer  

The association between PKM2 expression and clinical outcomes was investigated through analysis of the METABRIC dataset, which includes gene expression and clinical data from a large cohort of breast cancer patients. The study protocol was approved by appropriate institutional review boards, and all patients provided informed consent.  

Patients with luminal breast cancer were selected based on PAM50 classification. PKM2 expression levels were divided into quartiles, with survival analysis comparing the top 25% (high expression) and bottom 25% (low expression) groups. Patients with high PKM2 expression showed significantly worse 10-year overall survival (p = 0.005) and relapse-free survival (p = 0.045) compared to those with low PKM2 expression.  

These findings demonstrate that high PKM2 expression is strongly associated with poor clinical outcomes in luminal breast cancer. The results were consistent across different analytical methods and validate PKM2 as a prognostic biomarker in this patient population.  

### High Expression of O-Linked β-N-Acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) and PKM2 Can Predict Poor Prognosis of HR+/HER2- Breast Cancer  

A retrospective cohort study was conducted on 162 HR+/HER2- breast cancer patients who received treatment and were followed for a median of 123.4 months. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Chang-Gung Memorial Hospital (IRB#201700716A3).  

Immunohistochemical assessment was performed on paraffin-embedded tumor sections using specific antibodies against O-GlcNAc and PKM2. Staining intensity and distribution were evaluated by two blinded pathologists, and Q scores were calculated for each marker.  

Patient characteristics including age, tumor size, grade, and stage were recorded. Spearman's correlation analysis showed minimal correlation between O-GlcNAc or PKM2 expression and standard clinical parameters, suggesting these biomarkers provide independent prognostic information.  

Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed that high expression of either O-GlcNAc (p = 0.038) or PKM2 (p = 0.032) was significantly associated with worse 10-year disease-free survival. The combination of high expression of both markers showed even greater significance (p = 0.004), indicating synergistic prognostic value.  

Subtype analysis demonstrated that the prognostic significance of these markers was maintained in both luminal A and luminal B breast cancers. The association between double-marker status and recurrence risk was particularly strong, with high expression of both O-GlcNAc and PKM2 predicting the poorest outcomes.  

Disease-free survival analysis showed clear separation of survival curves based on O-GlcNAc and PKM2 expression levels. The combined double-marker status provided the most robust discrimination between high-risk and low-risk patients. These findings support the use of O-GlcNAc and PKM2 as complementary prognostic biomarkers in HR+/HER2- breast cancer.  

### Logistic Regression and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve Analysis  

Logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the contribution of O-GlcNAc, PKM2, and clinical parameters to prognosis. The analysis demonstrated that each marker significantly contributed to predicting disease-free survival outcomes.  

ROC curve analysis was conducted to compare different prognostic models. The model combining O-GlcNAc, PKM2, and tumor stage showed the best performance (AUC = 0.722), outperforming models using clinical parameters alone or single biomarkers. This triple-marker model provided superior prognostic accuracy compared to existing tools.  

Multivariate Cox regression analysis confirmed that O-GlcNAc expression was consistently associated with disease-free survival across different models. PKM2 expression showed virtually significant association in most analyses. The combination of these metabolic biomarkers with clinical parameters such as tumor stage or CTS5 score provided the most comprehensive prognostic information.  

These statistical analyses demonstrate that the expression levels of O-GlcNAc and PKM2 provide valuable prognostic information that complements existing clinical parameters. The combination of these biomarkers with standard clinicopathological factors offers improved risk stratification for HR+/HER2- breast cancer patients.
