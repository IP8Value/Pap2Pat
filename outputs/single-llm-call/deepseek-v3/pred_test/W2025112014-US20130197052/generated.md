# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid compounds and their use in the treatment and prevention of non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)-induced gastropathy. More particularly, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising these hybrid compounds that exhibit superior gastroprotective effects through mitochondrial oxidative stress prevention, iron chelation, and antioxidant properties.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) are widely used medications for pain relief, anti-inflammatory purposes, and fever reduction. While effective for these indications, NSAID use is associated with significant gastrointestinal side effects, particularly gastropathy characterized by gastric mucosal injury. The current understanding of NSAID-induced gastropathy involves both cyclooxygenase (COX)-dependent and COX-independent pathways, with mitochondrial oxidative stress playing a central role in the latter mechanism.

Gastropathy resulting from NSAID administration manifests as gastric mucosal lesions, bleeding, and ulceration. The pathogenesis involves complex interactions between oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and subsequent cellular apoptosis. Mitochondria in gastric mucosal cells are particularly vulnerable to NSAID-induced damage, leading to electron transport chain disruption, reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation, and iron-mediated oxidative stress. This mitochondrial oxidative stress (MOS) triggers apoptotic pathways that ultimately result in mucosal injury.

Current gastroprotective strategies primarily focus on acid suppression through proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) and histamine H2 receptor antagonists. While effective in reducing acid-related damage, these approaches fail to address the mitochondrial pathology underlying NSAID-induced gastropathy. Moreover, long-term use of conventional gastroprotective agents is associated with adverse effects including increased risk of bacterial infections, collagenous colitis, and other complications. The limitations of existing therapies highlight the need for novel compounds that specifically target the mitochondrial pathology in NSAID-induced gastropathy.

Oxidative stress plays a pivotal role in NSAID-induced gastric damage. Reactive oxygen species generated in mitochondria attack cellular components, leading to lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and DNA damage. The iron-mediated Fenton reaction further exacerbates oxidative damage by converting hydrogen peroxide into highly reactive hydroxyl radicals. This oxidative cascade ultimately results in mitochondrial dysfunction, characterized by impaired respiration, loss of membrane potential, and activation of apoptotic pathways.

Serotonin (5-hydroxytryptamine, 5HT) has been investigated for its potential role in gastric protection due to its mitochondrial localization properties. However, serotonin itself exhibits toxicity at higher concentrations and lacks sufficient antioxidant capacity to counteract NSAID-induced oxidative stress. The conjugation of serotonin with potent antioxidants presents a promising strategy to combine mitochondrial targeting with enhanced antioxidant activity.

Gallic acid, a natural polyphenol, possesses significant antioxidant properties and iron-chelating capacity. However, its therapeutic potential is limited by poor bioavailability and rapid metabolism. The combination of gallic acid's antioxidant properties with serotonin's mitochondrial localization capability through chemical conjugation represents an innovative approach to developing effective gastroprotective agents.

Existing gastroprotective compounds suffer from several limitations. Proton pump inhibitors, while effective in acid suppression, do not address the underlying oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction. Furthermore, prolonged PPI use is associated with adverse effects including increased infection risk and potential nutrient malabsorption. Antioxidant compounds such as vitamin E and quercetin show limited efficacy due to poor bioavailability and insufficient mitochondrial targeting. Iron chelators like desferrioxamine have demonstrated some protective effects but are limited by their pharmacokinetic properties and side effects.

The development of novel compounds that simultaneously address multiple aspects of NSAID-induced gastropathy—including mitochondrial oxidative stress, iron-mediated damage, and apoptotic signaling—represents an unmet medical need. Such compounds should ideally combine mitochondrial targeting capability with potent antioxidant and iron-chelating properties while maintaining favorable safety profiles. The present invention addresses these needs through the development of novel tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid compounds with superior gastroprotective properties.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide novel compounds of general formula I that exhibit superior gastroprotective effects against NSAID-induced gastropathy. These compounds are designed to combine the mitochondrial targeting properties of tryptamine derivatives with the antioxidant and iron-chelating capabilities of gallic acid and related phenolic compounds.

Another objective of the invention is to provide a new chemical scaffold for the treatment of NSAID-induced gastropathy that addresses the underlying mitochondrial oxidative stress rather than merely suppressing gastric acid secretion. The compounds of the invention are specifically designed to penetrate mitochondria, scavenge reactive oxygen species, chelate free iron, and prevent mitochondrial dysfunction and subsequent apoptosis in gastric mucosal cells.

A further objective is to provide pharmaceutical compositions comprising these novel compounds that demonstrate superior efficacy compared to existing gastroprotective agents such as proton pump inhibitors and histamine H2 receptor antagonists. The compositions are designed to offer protection against NSAID-induced gastric damage while minimizing adverse effects associated with current therapies.

An additional objective is to provide methods for synthesizing these novel tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid compounds with high purity and yield. The synthetic methods are designed to be scalable and reproducible for industrial production of the active pharmaceutical ingredients.

Yet another objective is to provide methods for treating and preventing NSAID-induced gastropathy using the novel compounds of the invention. These methods aim to protect gastric mucosa by preventing mitochondrial oxidative stress, chelating free iron, and inhibiting apoptotic pathways activated by NSAID treatment.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid compounds represented by general formula I that exhibit potent gastroprotective effects against NSAID-induced damage. These compounds combine the structural features of tryptamine derivatives with phenolic antioxidants to create molecules with unique mitochondrial targeting, antioxidant, and iron-chelating properties.

Representative compounds of the invention include SEGA (3a), a serotonin-gallic acid amide conjugate that demonstrates superior antioxidant activity and gastroprotective effects. Other representative compounds include TRGA (3b), MEGA (3c), GASA (4d), and various 5-hydroxy derivatives and dimeric serotonin derivatives that exhibit varying degrees of protective activity against NSAID-induced gastropathy.

The compounds of the invention exhibit remarkable antioxidant properties as demonstrated by ferric reducing antioxidant power (FRAP) assays and 2,2-diphenyl-1-picrylhydrazyl (DPPH) radical scavenging assays. In particular, SEGA (3a) shows concentration-dependent antioxidant activity that surpasses that of gallic acid alone or unconjugated serotonin. The compounds also demonstrate significant iron-chelating capacity, with SEGA (3a) effectively binding free iron and preventing iron-mediated oxidative damage.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising these compounds in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents. These compositions may be formulated for various administration routes including oral, parenteral, or topical delivery, with oral formulations being particularly preferred for gastroprotective applications.

Methods for treating NSAID-induced gastropathy using effective amounts of the compounds are also provided. The compounds demonstrate dose-dependent protection against indomethacin-induced gastric damage, with SEGA (3a) showing particularly high efficacy (ED50 = 6.9 mg/kg body weight). Administration routes include intraperitoneal, intravenous, or oral delivery, with intraperitoneal administration showing excellent bioavailability and mitochondrial uptake.

The compounds of the invention exhibit multiple protective mechanisms including scavenging of reactive oxygen species, chelation of intramitochondrial iron, prevention of mitochondrial oxidative stress, restoration of mitochondrial function, and inhibition of apoptotic pathways. SEGA (3a) has been shown to prevent indomethacin-induced collapse of mitochondrial membrane potential, restore respiratory control ratios, and maintain mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity in gastric mucosal cells.

Additionally, the compounds accelerate healing of existing NSAID-induced gastric damage by promoting restoration of normal mucosal architecture and function. SEGA (3a) treatment significantly reduces the time required for complete healing of indomethacin-damaged gastric mucosa compared to natural healing processes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid compounds synthesized through conjugation of tryptamine derivatives with phenolic antioxidants. The synthesis involves coupling reactions between appropriately protected gallic acid derivatives and various tryptamine compounds, followed by deprotection to yield the final products.

SEGA (3a), the lead compound of the invention, is synthesized through amide bond formation between 5-hydroxytryptamine (serotonin) and gallic acid. The synthesis begins with protection of gallic acid hydroxyl groups as benzyl ethers, followed by activation of the carboxylic acid and coupling with serotonin hydrochloride in the presence of coupling agents such as EDC (1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide) and HOBt (hydroxybenzotriazole). Subsequent hydrogenolytic removal of the benzyl protecting groups yields SEGA (3a) in high purity.

TRGA (3b) and MEGA (3c) are synthesized through similar amide coupling reactions using tryptamine and 5-methoxytryptamine, respectively, in place of serotonin. These compounds demonstrate the importance of the 5-hydroxy group on the indole ring for optimal gastroprotective activity, as its replacement with hydrogen or methoxy results in reduced efficacy.

GASA (4d) represents an ester-linked conjugate of gallic acid with serotonin, synthesized through esterification rather than amide bond formation. While demonstrating some activity, this compound is less effective than the amide-linked SEGA (3a), highlighting the importance of the amide linkage for optimal pharmacological properties.

The synthesis of 5-hydroxy derivatives involves selective protection and deprotection strategies to introduce additional hydroxyl groups at strategic positions on the aromatic rings. These modifications enhance the antioxidant capacity and iron-chelating properties of the resulting compounds.

Dimeric serotonin derivatives are synthesized through oxidative coupling or linker-based dimerization strategies. These compounds explore the potential synergistic effects of combining two serotonin moieties with antioxidant phenolic structures.

The antioxidant activity of these derivatives is systematically evaluated using FRAP and DPPH assays. The FRAP assay measures the reducing power of the compounds by their ability to convert Fe(III) to Fe(II), while the DPPH assay assesses free radical scavenging capacity. SEGA (3a) demonstrates superior activity in both assays compared to other derivatives and parent compounds.

The gastroprotective effects of the compounds are evaluated in indomethacin-induced gastropathy models in rats. SEGA (3a) shows dose-dependent protection against gastric mucosal injury with an ED50 of 6.9 mg/kg body weight. Histological studies confirm the preservation of normal gastric architecture in SEGA (3a)-treated animals compared to significant mucosal damage in untreated controls.

### Biological Effects

The biological effects of the tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid compounds are extensively characterized through in vitro and in vivo studies. The FRAP assay demonstrates that SEGA (3a) possesses significant ferric ion reducing capacity, indicating potent antioxidant activity. At a concentration of 10 μM, SEGA (3a) shows a FRAP value comparable to ascorbic acid, significantly higher than gallic acid alone or unconjugated serotonin.

In the DPPH radical scavenging assay, SEGA (3a) demonstrates concentration-dependent free radical scavenging activity, with complete scavenging observed at 100 μM concentration. This activity is superior to that of other derivatives and parent compounds, confirming the enhanced antioxidant properties achieved through the hybrid structure.

SEGA (3a) exhibits remarkable gastroprotective effects in indomethacin-treated rats. Pretreatment with SEGA (3a) at 50 mg/kg body weight completely prevents the development of gastric lesions, while lower doses show partial protection in a dose-dependent manner. Histological studies reveal preserved gastric mucosal structure with normal glandular architecture in SEGA (3a)-treated animals, compared to extensive epithelial damage and hemorrhage in untreated controls.

The mechanism of protection involves multiple effects on mitochondrial function. SEGA (3a) prevents indomethacin-induced mitochondrial oxidative stress as evidenced by reduced protein carbonyl formation, decreased lipid peroxidation, and preserved thiol content in gastric mucosal mitochondria. These effects are mediated through both direct ROS scavenging and iron chelation.

SEGA (3a) effectively rescues NSAID-induced damage to gastric mucosa by restoring mitochondrial function. Treatment with SEGA (3a) maintains normal respiratory control ratios in gastric mucosal mitochondria, preventing the indomethacin-induced decline in state 3 respiration. The compound also preserves mitochondrial membrane potential, as measured by JC-1 fluorescence ratios, and maintains mitochondrial dehydrogenase activity in gastric mucosal cells.

The prevention of oxidative stress-induced apoptosis is another key mechanism of SEGA (3a)'s protective effects. The compound significantly reduces activation of caspase-9 and caspase-3 in indomethacin-treated gastric mucosa, indicating inhibition of the mitochondrial apoptotic pathway. This is further supported by modulation of Bcl-2 family proteins, with SEGA (3a) preventing the indomethacin-induced downregulation of antiapoptotic Bcl-2 and upregulation of proapoptotic Bax.

SEGA (3a) demonstrates significant iron-chelating capacity in mitochondrial compartments. Using Phen Green SK fluorescence, the compound is shown to reduce intramitochondrial free iron levels in indomethacin-treated gastric mucosal cells. This iron chelation contributes to the prevention of iron-mediated oxidative damage and subsequent mitochondrial dysfunction.

The restoration of mitochondrial function by SEGA (3a) is accompanied by accelerated healing of existing gastric damage. In healing studies, SEGA (3a)-treated animals show complete restoration of gastric mucosa within 20 hours, compared to persistent damage in untreated controls. This healing effect is attributed to the compound's ability to maintain mitochondrial integrity and prevent ongoing oxidative damage in injured tissue.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

The general EDC coupling procedure for formation of esters or amides involves activation of the carboxylic acid component with EDC hydrochloride in the presence of HOBt or DMAP as appropriate for amide or ester formation, respectively. To a solution of the carboxylic acid (1 equivalent) and amine or alcohol (1.2 equivalents) in anhydrous DMF at 0°C is added EDC hydrochloride (1.2 equivalents). The reaction mixture is stirred at room temperature overnight, then quenched with ice water and extracted with ethyl acetate. The organic phase is washed with brine, dried over sodium sulfate, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product is purified by silica gel column chromatography using appropriate eluent systems.

### Example 2

The general procedure for debenzylation (hydrogenolysis) involves catalytic hydrogenation of benzyl-protected intermediates. A mixture of the benzylated compound and 10% palladium on carbon (catalytic amount) in methanol/chloroform (5:1) is subjected to hydrogenation at 40 psi for 2 hours. The reaction mixture is filtered through Celite to remove the catalyst, and the filtrate is evaporated under reduced pressure to yield the deprotected product.

### Example 3

Benzyl 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoate (1a) is prepared by benzylation of gallic acid using benzyl bromide and potassium carbonate in DMF, followed by selective esterification of the carboxylic acid with benzyl alcohol under EDC/DMAP conditions. 3,4,5-Tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid (1b) is obtained by hydrolysis of the benzyl ester under basic conditions. Coupling of 1b with 2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethylamine using EDC/HOBt yields 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)-N-(2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)benzamide, which is characterized by NMR and mass spectrometry. Hydrogenolytic removal of the benzyl protecting groups affords 3,4,5-trihydroxy-N-(2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)benzamide (SEGA, 3a), which is purified by recrystallization and characterized by analytical methods.

### Example 4

(E)-N-(2-(5-hydroxy-1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)-3-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acrylamide is prepared by EDC-mediated coupling of 4-hydroxycinnamic acid with serotonin hydrochloride in the presence of HOBt and triethylamine. The product is purified by column chromatography and characterized by NMR and mass spectrometry.

### Example 5

3-((2-((1H-Indol-3-yl)methylamino)ethyl)-1H-indol-5-ol is prepared by reductive amination of 5-hydroxytryptamine with indole-3-carboxaldehyde followed by sodium borohydride reduction. The product is purified by silica gel chromatography and characterized by spectroscopic methods.

### Example 6

N-(2-(1H-Indol-3-yl)ethyl)-3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzamide is synthesized by EDC/HOBt-mediated coupling of 3,4,5-tris(benzyloxy)benzoic acid with tryptamine in DMF. The product is characterized by NMR before hydrogenolytic deprotection to yield N-(2-(1H-indol-3-yl)ethyl)-3,4,5-trihydroxybenzamide (TRGA, 3b), which is characterized by analytical methods.

### Example 7

(E)-N-(2-(1H-Indol-3-yl)ethyl)-3-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acrylamide is prepared analogously to Example 4 using tryptamine instead of serotonin. The product is purified and characterized by standard methods.

### Example 8

N-((1H-Indol-3-yl)methyl)-2-(1H-indol-3-yl)ethanamine is synthesized by reductive amination of tryptamine with indole-3-carboxaldehyde followed by sodium borohydride reduction. The product is purified by chromatography and characterized.

### Example 9

Compound 2g is synthesized by coupling of 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoic acid with 5-methoxytryptamine using EDC/HOBt. Compound 3c is prepared by similar coupling with appropriate protected intermediates. Compounds 2h and 2i are synthesized through analogous procedures using different tryptamine derivatives. Each compound is characterized by NMR and mass spectrometry.

Compound 4d (GASA) is prepared by esterification of gallic acid with serotonin using EDC/DMAP conditions. Compounds 4b and 4c are intermediate protected derivatives. Compound 5a is a 5-hydroxy derivative prepared by selective protection/deprotection strategies. Dimeric compounds 6a, 6b, and 6c are synthesized through oxidative coupling or linker-based strategies.

NMR data for all compounds are acquired in appropriate deuterated solvents using standard pulse sequences. Chemical shifts are reported in ppm relative to tetramethylsilane or residual solvent peaks. Mass spectra are obtained using electrospray ionization or other appropriate ionization methods.

The FRAP assay is performed by mixing compound solutions with FRAP reagent (acetate buffer containing TPTZ and ferric chloride) and monitoring absorbance at 595 nm. FRAP values are calculated relative to ferrous sulfate standards. The DPPH assay involves mixing compound solutions with DPPH in methanol and measuring absorbance decrease at 517 nm after 30 minutes.

Animal models involve Sprague-Dawley rats (180-220 g) fasted for 24 hours before indomethacin administration (48 mg/kg body weight) to induce gastric damage. Test compounds are administered intraperitoneally 30 minutes before indomethacin. After 4 hours, stomachs are examined for lesions, which are scored on a standardized injury index scale. Histological studies involve fixation of stomach tissue in formalin, paraffin embedding, sectioning, and hematoxylin-eosin staining for microscopic evaluation.

Mitochondrial studies involve isolation of gastric mucosal mitochondria using differential centrifugation and Percoll density gradients. Mitochondrial oxidative stress is assessed by measuring protein carbonyls, lipid peroxidation products, and thiol content. Respiratory function is measured using a Clark-type oxygen electrode to determine state 3 and state 4 respiration rates. Membrane potential is assessed using JC-1 fluorescence ratios.

Caspase activities are measured using colorimetric assays with specific substrates (LEHD-pNA for caspase-9, DEVD-pNA for caspase-3). Apoptosis is evaluated by TUNEL assay and active caspase-3 immunohistochemistry. Intramitochondrial superoxide is detected using MitoSOX Red fluorescence, while free iron is visualized using Phen Green SK fluorescence.

## ADVANTAGES OF THE INVENTION

The present invention offers several significant advantages over existing therapies for NSAID-induced gastropathy. First, the tryptamine-gallic acid hybrid compounds provide targeted protection against mitochondrial oxidative stress, addressing the root cause of NSAID-induced damage rather than merely suppressing symptoms. This represents a paradigm shift from acid suppression to mitochondrial protection in gastroprotective therapy.

Second, the compounds demonstrate multifunctional activity combining antioxidant, iron-chelating, and antiapoptotic properties in a single molecular entity. This integrated approach provides more comprehensive protection than single-mechanism agents, potentially reducing the need for combination therapies.

Third, the lead compound SEGA (3a) shows superior efficacy compared to conventional antioxidants and iron chelators, with an ED50 of 6.9 mg/kg body weight against indomethacin-induced gastropathy. This potency is comparable to proton pump inhibitors but without their associated adverse effects on gastric acid secretion and microbiome balance.

Fourth, the compounds accelerate healing of existing gastric damage, a property not shared by most current gastroprotective agents. This healing effect results from the compounds' ability to maintain mitochondrial function and prevent ongoing oxidative damage in injured tissue.

Fifth, the synthetic methods are scalable and efficient, allowing for cost-effective production of the active pharmaceutical ingredients. The compounds demonstrate good stability in physiological conditions, suggesting favorable pharmacokinetic properties for therapeutic use.

Sixth, the compounds show excellent mitochondrial targeting, with radiolabeling studies demonstrating specific accumulation in gastric mucosal mitochondria. This targeted delivery enhances therapeutic efficacy while potentially reducing systemic side effects.

Seventh, the invention provides a versatile chemical platform for further optimization. Structure-activity relationships have been established, guiding future development of even more potent derivatives with improved pharmacological properties.

Eighth, the compounds may have applications beyond NSAID-induced gastropathy, potentially including other conditions involving mitochondrial oxidative stress such as ischemia-reperfusion injury, neurodegenerative diseases, and inflammatory conditions. This broad potential expands the therapeutic scope of the invention.

Ninth, the compounds demonstrate favorable safety profiles in preliminary studies, with no significant toxicity observed at therapeutic doses. This safety advantage is particularly important for chronic use in patients requiring long-term NSAID therapy.

Tenth, the invention provides the first examples of tryptamine-antioxidant hybrids specifically designed for mitochondrial targeting in gastroprotection. This innovative approach opens new avenues for drug development in gastrointestinal disorders and beyond.
