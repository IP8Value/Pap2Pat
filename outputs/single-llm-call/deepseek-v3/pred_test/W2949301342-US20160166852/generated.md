# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION  

The field of optogenetics has revolutionized neuroscience by enabling precise control over neuronal activity using light-sensitive ion channels and pumps. Traditional optogenetic techniques rely on visible light to modulate membrane potentials in genetically modified neurons, providing critical insights into neurological disorders such as Parkinson's disease, epilepsy, and depression. However, existing optogenetic methods suffer from significant limitations, including invasiveness due to surgical implantation of light-delivery fibers and restricted penetration depth of visible light into biological tissues. These constraints hinder broader clinical applications, particularly in deep-brain stimulation.  

Current light sources, such as lasers and light-emitting diodes (LEDs), are either prohibitively expensive or lack precise targeting capabilities. Near-infrared (IR) light has been explored as an alternative due to its deeper tissue penetration, but IR wavelengths still exhibit substantial attenuation through the skull and brain tissue, limiting their effectiveness. Furthermore, conventional optogenetics operates on a macroscopic scale, flooding large tissue regions with light rather than targeting individual neurons. There exists a pressing need for improved optogenetic techniques that overcome these limitations by enabling non-invasive, deep-tissue stimulation with microscopic or nanoscopic precision.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present invention addresses the limitations of conventional optogenetics by introducing two novel approaches: X-ray optogenetics (X-optogenetics) and ultrasound optogenetics (U-optogenetics). These methods eliminate the need for invasive surgical implantation of light sources while enabling deeper and more precise neuronal modulation.  

X-optogenetics utilizes X-ray excitable nanophosphors that convert X-ray energy into visible light, which then activates light-sensitive ion channels or pumps (rhodopsins) in targeted neurons. This approach leverages the deep-penetrating capability of X-rays and the precise focusing achievable with advanced X-ray optics, such as polycapillary lenses or Fresnel zone plates. The system includes functionalized nanophosphors that bind specifically to rhodopsins, ensuring localized light emission near the target proteins.  

U-optogenetics employs ultrasonic waves to induce sonoluminescence in chemiluminescent agents, such as fluoresceinyl Cypridina luminescent analog (FCLA), which emit visible light upon exposure to ultrasound. This method avoids ionizing radiation and provides an alternative means of non-invasive neuronal stimulation, albeit with slightly reduced penetration depth compared to X-optogenetics.  

Both techniques represent significant advancements over existing optogenetic methods by enabling non-invasive, deep-tissue stimulation with high spatial and temporal precision. The invention further includes optimized nanoparticle formulations, targeting strategies, and radiation dose calculations to ensure safety and efficacy.  

## DETAILED DISCLOSURE  

### Introduction to Optogenetics  

Optogenetics is a transformative technology that uses light-sensitive proteins, such as channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2), halorhodopsin (NphR), and archeorhodopsin (Arch), to modulate neuronal activity. These proteins, collectively termed rhodopsins, are genetically expressed in target neurons and respond to specific wavelengths of light by altering membrane potentials. Traditional optogenetics relies on implanted optical fibers to deliver light from external sources, such as lasers or LEDs, which limits its applicability due to invasiveness and shallow penetration depth.  

### Rhodopsins and Their Mechanisms  

Rhodopsins are transmembrane proteins that regulate ion flow in response to light. Channelrhodopsins, such as ChR2, are cation channels that depolarize neurons upon blue light exposure, while halorhodopsins and archeorhodopsins are ion pumps that hyperpolarize neurons in response to yellow or green light. These proteins are derived from microbial sources and have been engineered for optimal performance in mammalian neurons.  

### Limitations of Lasers and LEDs  

Conventional light sources used in optogenetics, such as lasers and LEDs, suffer from several drawbacks. Lasers are costly and require precise alignment, while LEDs produce diffuse light that lacks spatial precision. Both sources exhibit limited tissue penetration, restricting their use to superficial brain regions. Additionally, surgical implantation of light-delivery fibers introduces risks of infection and tissue damage, further limiting clinical translation.  

### Introduction to X-Ray Optogenetics  

X-optogenetics overcomes these limitations by using X-rays to excite nanophosphors, which then emit visible light to activate rhodopsins. X-rays penetrate deeply into tissues and can be focused with high precision using advanced optics. The system comprises three key components: (1) X-ray excitable nanophosphors, (2) targeted delivery of nanophosphors to rhodopsins, and (3) focused X-ray sources for spatial and temporal control.  

### X-Ray Optogenetic System  

The X-ray optogenetic system includes a carbon nanotube (CNT) X-ray source capable of pulsed emission for precise temporal control. X-rays are focused using polycapillary lenses or Fresnel zone plates to minimize off-target radiation exposure. The nanophosphors absorb X-rays and emit visible light, which activates nearby rhodopsins. Functionalization of nanophosphors with targeting moieties, such as antibodies or peptides, ensures proximity to rhodopsins, enhancing stimulation efficiency.  

### Light-Emitting Particles  

Nanophosphors suitable for X-optogenetics include gadolinium oxysulfide (Gd2O2S) and lithium gallate (LiGa5O8) doped with rare-earth ions such as europium (Eu3+) or terbium (Tb3+). These particles exhibit high X-ray-to-visible-light conversion efficiency and tunable emission spectra. Particle size, coating, and solubility are optimized for biocompatibility and blood-brain barrier penetration.  

### Light-Sensitive Ion Channels  

The invention leverages well-characterized rhodopsins, such as ChR2, NphR, and Arch, which respond to specific wavelengths emitted by the nanophosphors. Targeting strategies ensure that nanophosphors are localized near rhodopsins, minimizing light attenuation through tissue.  

### Ultrasound Optogenetic Technique  

U-optogenetics employs ultrasonic waves to induce sonoluminescence in chemiluminescent agents, such as FCLA. Ultrasonic waves generate cavitation bubbles that collapse, producing light bursts that activate rhodopsins. FCLA enhances sonoluminescence by reacting with reactive oxygen species, emitting light at wavelengths suitable for rhodopsin activation.  

### Ultrasound Optogenetic System  

The ultrasound system includes a transducer operating at frequencies between 100 kHz and 1 MHz, optimized for tissue penetration and sonoluminescence efficiency. Chemiluminescent agents are targeted to rhodopsins using similar strategies as in X-optogenetics.  

### Chemiluminescent Agents  

FCLA is a preferred chemiluminescent agent due to its peak emission at 532 nm, which aligns with the activation spectra of many rhodopsins. Other agents with tunable emission spectra may also be employed.  

### X-Ray Optogenetic Systems and Methods  

The invention provides detailed methods for X-ray optogenetics, including nanoparticle synthesis, functionalization, and in vivo delivery. Radiation dose calculations ensure safety, with a maximum permissible dose of 10 mSv per procedure.

### Nanophosphor Properties  

Key properties include solubility, conversion efficiency, size distribution, and coating. Solubility is achieved through hydrophilic coatings, such as PEG or polyacrylic acid (PAA). Conversion efficiency is optimized by doping with rare-earth ions.  

### Importance of Solubility  

Water-soluble nanophosphors ensure uniform distribution and minimize aggregation in biological fluids.

High conversion efficiency reduces the required X-ray dose, enhancing safety. For example, Gd2O2S:Eu exhibits a conversion efficiency of 60,000 photons per MeV.

### Importance of Biocompatibility  

Biocompatible coatings minimize toxicity and inflammatory responses, ensuring safe in vivo use.
