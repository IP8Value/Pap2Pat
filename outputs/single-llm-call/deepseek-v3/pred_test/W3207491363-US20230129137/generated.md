# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Heart disease remains a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, with myocardial infarction (MI) resulting in irreversible loss of functional cardiomyocytes due to the limited regenerative capacity of adult mammalian hearts. Current therapeutic strategies, including pharmacological interventions and surgical procedures, often fail to restore cardiac function fully, leaving patients with progressive heart failure. Apical resection surgery in neonatal mice and pigs has demonstrated that cardiomyocyte proliferation can drive complete cardiac recovery post-MI, suggesting that targeting cardiomyocyte cell cycle regulation may offer a viable therapeutic approach.  

The Hippo signaling pathway plays a critical role in cardiac development and regeneration by regulating downstream effectors such as YES-associated protein (YAP) and transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ). These proteins influence cardiomyocyte proliferation and survival when activated. The small molecule TT-10, a fluorine-substituted derivative of TAZ-12, has shown promise in promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation and reducing infarct size in murine models. However, systemic administration of TT-10 has limitations, including transient therapeutic effects and the need for high doses, which may increase the risk of adverse events.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides nanoparticle compositions comprising poly-lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) polymers encapsulating TT-10, designed to enhance cardiac repair following myocardial injury. These compositions facilitate sustained release of TT-10, improving its bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy at lower doses compared to conventional delivery methods. The invention further describes methods for administering these nanoparticle compositions to subjects with cardiac injury, wherein intramyocardial injection of TT-10-loaded PLGA nanoparticles (TT-10-NPs) enhances cardiomyocyte proliferation, reduces apoptosis, and promotes angiogenesis in the peri-infarct region.  

Key advantages of the invention include prolonged drug release kinetics, localized delivery to the infarcted myocardium, and improved cardiac functional parameters such as left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) and fractional shortening (LVFS). Additionally, the nanoparticle-mediated delivery system minimizes systemic exposure, reducing potential side effects while maintaining therapeutic potency.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Nanoparticle Compositions  

The nanoparticle compositions of the invention comprise biodegradable PLGA polymers, which are formulated to encapsulate TT-10. PLGA is a copolymer of lactic acid and glycolic acid, with a molecular weight ranging between 10,000 and 100,000 Daltons. The ratio of lactide to glycolide in the PLGA copolymer may vary, with preferred embodiments comprising a 50:50 ratio to optimize degradation kinetics and drug release profiles.  

The nanoparticles have an average diameter between 100 and 200 nanometers, ensuring efficient cellular uptake and tissue penetration. Encapsulation efficiency of TT-10 within the PLGA matrix is approximately 2.5 micrograms per milligram of nanoparticles, with in vitro release studies demonstrating an initial burst release of 47% within the first 24 hours, followed by sustained release of up to 90% over nine days.  

### Methods for Improving Cardiac Function  

The invention further provides methods for administering the nanoparticle compositions to subjects with myocardial infarction. The nanoparticles are delivered via intramyocardial injection at the infarct border zone, with a total dosage of 67.2 nanograms of TT-10 per treatment. This localized administration ensures prolonged retention of the therapeutic agent at the injury site.  

Treatment with TT-10-NPs significantly improves cardiac functional parameters, including increases in LVEF and LVFS, as well as reductions in end-systolic and end-diastolic volumes. Histological analyses reveal decreased infarct size and enhanced vascularity in the peri-infarct region, attributable to TT-10-mediated activation of YAP/TAZ signaling. The therapeutic effects persist for at least four weeks post-administration, with cardiomyocyte proliferation peaking at one week and declining thereafter as the nanoparticles degrade.  

### TT-10 and Its Role in Myocardial Repair  

TT-10 is a small molecule inhibitor of the Hippo pathway, which promotes nuclear translocation of YAP and subsequent activation of genes involved in cell cycle progression and survival. In cardiomyocytes, TT-10 treatment upregulates markers of proliferation (Ki67, phosphorylated histone H3) while reducing apoptosis (TUNEL-positive cells). The nanoparticle formulation enhances these effects by maintaining optimal intracellular concentrations of TT-10 over an extended period.
