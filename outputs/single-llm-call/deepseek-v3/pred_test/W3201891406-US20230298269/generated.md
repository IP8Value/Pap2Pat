# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present disclosure relates generally to computer graphics and machine learning, and more particularly to differentiable rendering techniques that enable efficient computation of derivatives for surface representations. The disclosed methods and systems provide improved differentiable rendering capabilities for both explicit surface representations (such as triangle meshes) and implicit surface representations (such as isosurfaces), while maintaining computational efficiency and accuracy at occlusion boundaries. The technology finds particular utility in computer vision applications, three-dimensional reconstruction, and training of machine-learned models where accurate derivatives of rendered images with respect to scene parameters are required.

## BACKGROUND

Contemporary solutions for differentiable rendering face several significant limitations that hinder their practical application and computational efficiency. Traditional mesh-based rendering approaches produce undefined derivatives at occlusion boundaries and when changing topology, making them unsuitable for many optimization tasks. While volumetric representations have emerged as an alternative that provides natural differentiability, they suffer from excessive computational costs due to the need for ray marching operations. Existing surface splatting techniques, while differentiable, operate on point cloud representations that lack the structural integrity of true surface representations and can suffer from sampling artifacts during optimization.

Current approaches to differentiable mesh rendering typically fall into four categories, each with substantial drawbacks. Gradient replacement methods employ heuristic derivatives that often mismatch the actual rendering process, leading to convergence problems. Edge sampling techniques explicitly process mesh edges to compute derivatives, resulting in computational costs that scale poorly with mesh complexity. Reparameterization methods are limited to path tracing contexts and cannot support simple rasterization. Forward rendering approaches with smooth gradients require costly closest-point queries or extensive mesh processing. For implicit surface representations, existing methods either require user-provided occlusion masks that cannot handle self-occlusion, or rely on expensive volume rendering techniques.

These limitations create substantial barriers to practical applications of differentiable rendering in computer vision and graphics. There exists a pressing need for a rendering technique that combines the efficiency of surface representations with the differentiability of volumetric approaches, while properly handling occlusion boundaries and supporting diverse surface representations. The present invention addresses these needs through a novel rasterize-then-splat methodology that decouples surface sampling from derivative computation while maintaining accuracy and computational efficiency.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a novel method for differentiable rendering that combines conventional rasterization with depth-aware splatting operations. The method begins by obtaining a three-dimensional mesh representing the surface to be rendered. This mesh may be generated through various means, including as output from a machine-learned model or through traditional modeling techniques. The three-dimensional mesh is then rasterized using conventional, non-differentiable rasterization techniques to produce a two-dimensional raster representation. During this rasterization process, initial color values are determined for each pixel based on the surface properties and any applied shading or texturing schemes.

Following rasterization, the method constructs splats for each pixel in the rasterized image. These splats represent localized surface samples and are designed to preserve derivatives for the vertices of the original mesh. The splat construction involves computing splat center points through perspective division and viewport transformation of the three-dimensional coordinates. Each splat is associated with smooth falloff characteristics that enable proper derivative computation at occlusion boundaries.

The method then determines updated color values for each pixel by weighting contributions from nearby splats based on their proximity to the pixel location. This weighting process accounts for depth relationships between surfaces to properly handle occlusion cases. The result is a differentiable two-dimensional rendering that maintains accurate derivatives with respect to the original three-dimensional surface parameters, even at occlusion boundaries. The rendering can be effectively utilized for training machine-learned models or optimizing scene parameters due to its smooth derivative properties.

The invention further encompasses a computing system configured to implement the differentiable rendering method. The system includes one or more processors and memory storing instructions that, when executed, cause the system to obtain a three-dimensional mesh, rasterize the mesh to produce initial color values, construct splats for each pixel, and determine updated color values through splat weighting operations. The system may interface with machine-learned models that generate or modify three-dimensional meshes based on the differentiable renderings.

Additionally, the invention includes non-transitory computer-readable media storing instructions for performing the differentiable rendering method. These media may contain software implementations of the rasterization, splat construction, and color updating processes, enabling deployment on various computing platforms. The media may further include pre-trained machine-learned models configured to work with the differentiable rendering outputs for applications such as pose estimation or three-dimensional reconstruction.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The disclosed invention provides a comprehensive solution for differentiable rendering that addresses the fundamental challenges of computing accurate derivatives for surface representations. At its core, the method employs a splat-based forward rendering approach that combines the efficiency of conventional rasterization with the differentiability of carefully constructed splat operations. The process begins with a three-dimensional mesh representing the surface to be rendered. This mesh may take various forms, including traditional triangle meshes, parametric surfaces, or implicit surface representations extracted from volumetric data.

Rasterization of the three-dimensional mesh follows conventional computer graphics techniques, generating screen-space coordinates for pixels and determining initial color values based on surface properties. Unlike prior differentiable rendering approaches, this rasterization step need not be differentiable itself, allowing for highly optimized implementations. The key innovation lies in the subsequent processing where splats are constructed for each pixel to preserve derivatives with respect to the original mesh vertices.

For each pixel, the method computes splat center points by applying perspective division and viewport transformation to the three-dimensional coordinates. These splats incorporate smooth falloff characteristics that enable proper derivative computation across the rendered image. The updated color value for each pixel is determined by weighting contributions from nearby splats based on their proximity to the pixel location, with special handling for occlusion cases through depth-aware accumulation buffers.

The resulting two-dimensional rendering maintains accurate derivatives throughout the image, including at occlusion boundaries where traditional methods fail. This differentiability enables the use of the rendering in training machine-learned models, where smooth derivatives are essential for effective optimization. The rendering can be processed by various machine-learned models, including pose estimation models and three-dimensional reconstruction models, with derivatives flowing backward through the rendering process to adjust model parameters.

The three-dimensional mesh used as input may be generated by a machine-learned model trained specifically for mesh generation. The rasterization scheme accommodates various surface representations while maintaining computational efficiency. Initial color values are determined through conventional shading and texturing approaches, with the understanding that any differentiable shading model can be applied in this stage.

Splat construction involves creating localized surface representations with smooth falloff characteristics that preserve derivative information. Each splat's weight is computed based on its distance to neighboring pixels, enabling proper blending during the color updating process. The final color value for each pixel combines contributions from multiple splats, weighted by their proximity and depth relationships, to produce a visually plausible rendering with accurate derivatives.

The method significantly advances the field by enabling effective training of machine-learned models through differentiable rendering. The loss function used during training can be evaluated based on differences between the rendered output and target images, with derivatives flowing backward through the rendering process to adjust model parameters. This capability proves particularly valuable for pose estimation models, where the rendering provides the necessary bridge between three-dimensional representations and two-dimensional observations.

Compared to point-based rendering approaches, the disclosed method maintains the structural integrity of surface representations while providing proper handling of occlusion boundaries. The technique overcomes fundamental limitations of pure rasterization approaches by augmenting them with splat-based derivative preservation. The technical effects and benefits include enabling generation of truly differentiable renderings, reducing computational resources compared to volumetric approaches, and providing accurate derivatives throughout the rendered image, including at challenging occlusion boundaries.

### Example Methods

The disclosed method for differentiable rendering involves several key steps executed in sequence. First, the system obtains a three-dimensional mesh, which may be generated through machine-learning techniques or traditional modeling approaches. This mesh undergoes rasterization to produce a two-dimensional raster representation with initial color values determined through shading and texture application.

For each pixel in the raster, the method constructs a splat centered at the projected position of the corresponding surface point. The splat construction involves computing center points through perspective division and viewport transformation of three-dimensional coordinates. These splats incorporate smooth falloff characteristics that enable proper derivative computation during subsequent processing.

The method determines updated color values for each pixel by weighting contributions from nearby splats based on their proximity. This weighting process selects a subset of splats for each pixel and computes their contributions based on distance measures. The weights are normalized to ensure consistent blending, and the final color value combines these weighted contributions to produce the rendered output.

The system generates derivatives at occlusion boundaries by properly accounting for depth relationships between surfaces. These derivatives enable effective training of machine-learned models through backwards propagation of errors. The method processes two-dimensional differentiable renderings with these models to generate outputs such as pose estimates or refined three-dimensional structures.

Training machine-learned models involves evaluating loss functions that measure differences between rendered outputs and target images. Based on these evaluations, the system adjusts model parameters to improve accuracy. Specific models that benefit from this approach include pose estimation models and three-dimensional mesh generation models, both of which rely on accurate derivatives flowing through the rendering process.

The method supports generation of image data from alternative viewpoints by rendering the three-dimensional mesh with different pose or orientation parameters. It can also generate secondary three-dimensional meshes for comparison or refinement purposes. The entire process may be implemented across various computing systems, including servers, databases, and software applications communicating over networks.
