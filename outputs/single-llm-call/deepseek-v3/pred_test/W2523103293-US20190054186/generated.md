### FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions comprising magnetic nanoparticles and methods for their use in therapeutic applications. More particularly, the invention relates to polymer-coated magnetic nanoparticle complexes (MNPCs) that can be remotely actuated by low frequency alternating current (AC) magnetic fields for selective treatment of cancer cells through cytoskeletal disruption.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Magnetic hyperthermia has emerged as a promising approach for cancer treatment by using magnetic nanoparticles (MNPs) to generate localized heat when exposed to alternating magnetic fields. This method aims to selectively elevate tumor temperature to cytotoxic levels while minimizing damage to surrounding healthy tissues. However, conventional magnetic hyperthermia faces significant limitations including the requirement for high frequency magnetic fields, substantial power inputs, and challenges in achieving sufficient heat generation at clinically relevant MNP concentrations.  

Recent studies have explored surface heating effects where heat generation occurs at the nanoparticle surface rather than bulk heating. While this approach shows potential for reducing power requirements, existing surface heating methods still suffer from poor specificity between cancerous and normal cells, limited intracellular uptake of nanoparticles, and potential systemic toxicity at therapeutic doses.  

A critical gap in the art remains the inability to selectively target cancer cells while sparing healthy cells, particularly at low magnetic field frequencies that would be clinically practical. Current systems lack mechanisms to exploit fundamental differences between cancerous and non-cancerous cells, such as their mechanical properties and cytoskeletal architecture. There exists an unmet need for nanoparticle systems that can leverage these biological differences to achieve selective cancer cell destruction without relying on thermal effects.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides magnetic nanoparticle polymer complexes (MNPCs) comprising magnetic nanoparticles coated with amphiphilic block copolymers that enable enhanced cellular uptake and selective cancer cell destruction when actuated by low frequency AC magnetic fields. The MNPCs of the invention comprise a magnetic nanoparticle core surrounded by a polymeric coating, wherein the coating comprises an amphiphilic block copolymer having a hydrophilic block and a hydrophobic block.  

The MNPCs are actuated by application of low frequency AC magnetic fields in the range of 30-3000 Hz, preferably about 50 Hz, with field intensities between 10-100 mT. Remarkably, this actuation selectively disrupts the cytoskeleton of cancer cells while leaving non-cancerous cells unaffected, despite similar intracellular uptake of the MNPCs. The selective effect is achieved through differential mechanical responses of cancerous versus non-cancerous cells to the forces generated by the actuated MNPCs.  

The polymeric coating of the MNPCs preferably comprises a pentablock copolymer structure with polyacrylic acid (PAA) blocks and Pluronic P85 blocks arranged in a PAA-b-P85-b-PAA configuration. This architecture provides both anchoring groups for attachment to the magnetic nanoparticle surface and functional groups that enhance cellular uptake. The MNPCs spontaneously form micellar structures in aqueous environments with hydrodynamic diameters in the range of 30-70 nm.  

The invention further provides methods of treating cancer by administering the MNPCs to a subject and applying low frequency AC magnetic fields to the target tissue. The methods achieve selective cancer cell killing through mechanical disruption of the cancer cell cytoskeleton without significant thermal effects or damage to surrounding healthy cells. Additionally, the MNPCs can be used for biomedical imaging by incorporating contrast agents or fluorescent labels into the polymeric coating.
