# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of corneal endothelial cell regeneration and transplantation. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for identifying, producing, and utilizing cell populations suitable for corneal transplantation. The invention particularly relates to the use of specific gene expression markers to determine the suitability of cell populations for corneal endothelial regeneration.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Corneal endothelial cells (CECs) play a critical role in maintaining corneal transparency by regulating fluid and ion transport. However, human CECs have limited proliferative capacity, and significant cell loss due to disease or trauma can lead to corneal edema, opacification, and vision impairment. Current treatments primarily rely on allogeneic corneal transplantation, which is limited by donor scarcity.  

Existing approaches to culture human CECs face significant challenges, as cultured CECs often lose their characteristic phenotype and undergo endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition. While recent advances have explored alternative sources such as stem cells, the lack of reliable markers for CEC differentiation and maturation has hindered progress in developing effective regenerative therapies. Current methods fail to consistently produce functional CECs suitable for transplantation, highlighting the need for improved techniques to identify and generate transplantable cell populations.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

A major obstacle in corneal regenerative medicine is the identification of cell populations that exhibit the functional characteristics of mature corneal endothelial cells and are suitable for transplantation. Existing methods lack reliable markers to assess the differentiation status and functional maturity of cultured CECs or stem cell-derived CEC-like cells. This limitation prevents the consistent production of transplant-ready cell populations and complicates quality control in therapeutic development.  

### Solution to Problem  

The present invention provides solutions to these challenges through several key innovations:  

The invention provides means for identifying cells suitable for corneal transplantation by establishing specific gene expression markers that correlate with CEC differentiation and function. These markers include POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B, which demonstrate specific expression patterns in mature CECs.  

The invention further provides means for producing cells suitable for corneal transplantation through optimized culture conditions that promote differentiation toward the CEC lineage. This includes methods for culturing stem cells under defined conditions until their gene expression profile matches reference values indicative of transplant-ready cells.  

A method for producing a transplant-ready cell population is disclosed, comprising the steps of culturing stem cells under conditions suitable for inducing differentiation into corneal endothelial cells, measuring expression of specific marker genes, and continuing culture until the expression levels reach predetermined reference values. The method further includes preparing the cell population for transplantation by forming cell suspensions, sheets, or spheres suitable for administration.  

The invention defines specific genes for determining cell population suitability, with particular emphasis on POU6F2 as a marker of CEC maturation. Quantitative measurement of gene expression provides an objective standard for assessing differentiation status and transplantation readiness.  

Additionally, the invention provides means for efficiently producing transplant-ready cell populations by optimizing culture media composition, duration, and environmental conditions. These methods enable the reliable generation of functional CECs from various stem cell sources.  

### Advantageous Effects of Invention  

The present invention offers several significant advantages over existing technologies:  

The identification of specific gene expression markers, particularly POU6F2, provides an objective standard for assessing CEC differentiation and maturation, enabling quality control in cell production. The invention's methods allow for the reliable generation of transplant-ready cell populations from various stem cell sources, addressing the critical shortage of donor tissue.  

By establishing defined culture conditions and quantitative assessment criteria, the invention enables consistent production of functional CECs, overcoming the variability and phenotypic instability of current culture methods. The disclosed techniques facilitate the development of standardized protocols for corneal regenerative medicine, supporting clinical translation and therapeutic applications.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention encompasses various embodiments for producing and characterizing cell populations suitable for corneal endothelial regeneration:  

The cell population of interest comprises cells cultured under conditions that induce differentiation toward the corneal endothelial lineage. These conditions include specific media formulations optimized for CEC differentiation, preferably serum-free media supplemented with appropriate growth factors and differentiation promoters.  

Preferred media compositions exclude differentiation inducers while maintaining components that support CEC development. The media pH is typically maintained between 7.2-7.4, with culture temperature at approximately 37°C. Culture duration may vary from several days to weeks, depending on the starting cell population and differentiation progress.  

Cells may be cultured in various containers, including tissue culture plates or flasks, optionally coated with extracellular matrix components to support cell attachment and differentiation. The starting cell population may include induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), embryonic stem cells (ESCs), or other suitable progenitor cells.  

Key aspects of the invention involve measuring expression of specific genes to assess differentiation status. Preferred genes for measurement include POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B, with POU6F2 being particularly indicative of CEC maturation. Quantitative measurement of gene expression, such as by qRT-PCR or RNA sequencing, provides objective criteria for determining transplantation suitability.  

The cell population may be prepared for transplantation in various forms, including suspensions, sheets, or spheres, optionally combined with scaffold materials or pharmaceutical carriers. The preparation is suitable for administration to patients requiring corneal transplantation, particularly those with endothelial dysfunction.  

An alternative method (Method B) for determining transplantation suitability involves similar principles but may employ different combinations of marker genes or modified culture conditions. This method similarly relies on quantitative gene expression analysis to assess differentiation status and determine readiness for therapeutic use.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific implementations of the invention:  

Example 1 describes obtaining human corneal endothelial tissue from donor corneoscleral rims. Descemet's membrane is carefully removed, and endothelial cells are collected for RNA extraction. Total RNA is used to prepare sequencing libraries for gene expression analysis.  

Example 2 details the isolation and culture of corneal endothelial cells. Cells are enzymatically detached and cultured in optimized media containing specific growth factors. RNA is extracted at various time points to monitor differentiation markers.  

Example 3 involves sequencing analysis of gene expression. RNA samples are processed to create sequencing libraries, which are then analyzed to quantify expression levels of specific genes. Data is mapped to reference genomes and compared across samples to identify differentiation markers.  

Example 4 focuses on identifying and validating specific markers. Expression levels of POU6F2, LMX1B, and TFAP2B are quantified in various tissues, confirming their specificity to corneal endothelium. Immunofluorescence and Western blot analyses further validate protein expression patterns.  

Example 5 demonstrates the application of these markers in assessing stem cell differentiation. Induced pluripotent stem cells are cultured under CEC differentiation conditions, with gene expression monitored over time. Cells reaching predetermined marker expression levels are deemed suitable for transplantation.  

These examples collectively demonstrate the utility of the disclosed methods for producing and characterizing cell populations for corneal endothelial regeneration. The invention provides a comprehensive framework for developing reliable therapies to address corneal endothelial dysfunction.
