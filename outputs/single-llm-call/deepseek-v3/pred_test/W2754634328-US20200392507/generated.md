# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and methods for reducing hypertrophic scarring through targeted gene silencing. More specifically, the invention provides compositions comprising small interfering RNA (siRNA) molecules directed against transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) genes, formulated with a histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) carrier to form nanoparticles for effective delivery. The invention further discloses methods for using these compositions to treat fibrotic conditions by simultaneously silencing TGF-β1 and COX-2 expression, activating fibroblast apoptosis, and reducing scar tissue formation.  

## BACKGROUND  

Hypertrophic scar reduction and management present significant therapeutic challenges due to limited understanding of underlying mechanisms and few validated treatment options. Fibrosis, characterized by excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) accumulation in damaged tissue, can lead to permanent scarring when the balance between ECM deposition and degradation is disrupted during wound healing. Hypertrophic scars (HS) result from prolonged inflammatory responses to injury, causing increased vascularization, hypercellularity, and excessive collagen deposition from local fibroblasts.  

Fibroblasts, the most abundant cells in connective tissue, play a key role in wound healing and can differentiate into myofibroblasts that increase ECM synthesis and tissue contraction. HS fibroblasts demonstrate reduced collagenase expression but elevated TGF-β1 expression, leading to excessive collagen deposition. HS myofibroblasts exhibit high α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) expression and reduced sensitivity to apoptotic signals. While TGF-β1's central role in fibrosis is well-established, the role of prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) synthesized by COX-2 remains controversial. Current treatments for excessive scarring, including surgical excision, radiation, corticosteroid injections, and various topical therapies, have shown limited efficacy.  

There exists an unmet need for novel therapeutics that can effectively target the molecular pathways driving fibrosis while overcoming delivery barriers in scar tissue. The present invention addresses this need by providing a dual-targeted siRNA approach that simultaneously silences TGF-β1 and COX-2 using HKP-packaged nanoparticles for enhanced delivery and efficacy.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising siRNA molecules targeting TGF-β1 and COX-2 genes, formulated with a histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) carrier. The siRNA molecules are designed to specifically hybridize with and silence expression of TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNA sequences. Each siRNA molecule comprises a double-stranded RNA structure of approximately 19-25 base pairs with 1-2 nucleotide overhangs at each 3' end. The siRNA molecules may include chemical modifications such as 2'-O-methyl modifications, phosphorothioate linkages, or cholesterol conjugations to enhance stability and cellular uptake.  

The composition contains a mixture of TGF-β1-specific siRNA and COX-2-specific siRNA molecules in approximately equal molar ratios. These siRNA molecules are combined with the HKP carrier at a weight ratio of about 1:4 (siRNA:HKP) to form nanoparticles through electrostatic and non-ionic interactions. The HKP carrier comprises a lysine core with multiple branches containing histidine and lysine repeats, which facilitates endosomal escape and intracellular delivery of the siRNA payload. The resulting nanoparticles have an average diameter of approximately 150 nm and a zeta potential of about 40 mV, providing stability and efficient cellular uptake.  

A method is provided for identifying effective siRNA sequences targeting TGF-β1 and COX-2. The method involves designing candidate siRNA sequences in silico, testing their silencing efficiency in cell transfection studies using quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR), and selecting sequences demonstrating potent target gene knockdown with minimal off-target effects. The selected siRNA sequences are then evaluated in animal wound models to assess their therapeutic potential. Preferred animal models include human hypertrophic scar tissue implanted onto nude mice and human skin grafts on nude mice, which closely mimic human fibrotic conditions.  

The pharmaceutical composition may contain multiple siRNA molecules targeting different regions of the TGF-β1 and COX-2 transcripts, creating a synergistic effect that enhances gene silencing and therapeutic outcomes. This combination approach demonstrates greater efficacy than targeting either gene alone, as evidenced by enhanced fibroblast apoptosis and scar reduction.  

The invention further provides methods for down-regulating pro-fibrotic factors in mammalian tissue by administering the composition to affected areas. The composition is delivered via intradermal or intrascar injection, allowing direct access to fibroblasts and myofibroblasts within scar tissue. Upon administration, the siRNA nanoparticles silence TGF-β1 and COX-2 expression, disrupting fibrotic pathways and reducing production of ECM components including collagen types I and III, α-SMA, and hydroxyproline.  

A method for activating fibroblast and myofibroblast apoptosis is disclosed, comprising administering the composition to fibrotic tissue and measuring apoptosis induction through terminal deoxynucleotidyl transferase dUTP nick end labeling (TUNEL) assays or flow cytometry. The simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 creates a pro-apoptotic environment that reduces fibroblast proliferation and promotes scar resolution.  

The invention also provides a method for reducing hypertrophic scar size by administering the composition directly to scar tissue in multiple injections over a treatment period. The composition is typically administered in doses of about 20 μg siRNA per 50 μl formulation per cm³ scar tissue, delivered in aliquots to different regions of the scar to ensure even distribution. Treatment results in significant reduction of scar size, typically by about 40-45% compared to untreated controls, through combined effects of reduced ECM production and increased fibroblast apoptosis.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions  

siRNA sequences targeting conserved regions of human TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNAs were designed and screened in human PC3 cells. Cells were transfected with Lipofectamine 2000-packaged siRNA complexes, and target gene silencing was analyzed by qRT-PCR after 48 hours. The most potent siRNA sequences demonstrating significant knockdown of TGF-β1 and COX-2 expression were selected for further testing. These sequences showed minimal off-target effects and good toxicity profiles in cell culture.  

### Example 2: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions Resulted in Activation of Apoptosis of Fibroblasts/Myofibroblasts  

Human fibroblasts isolated from hypertrophic scar tissue were transfected with TGF-β1siRNA, COX-2siRNA, or their combination. Electron microscopy revealed apoptotic morphology only in cells treated with the combination. Flow cytometry analysis confirmed significantly increased apoptosis in the combination treatment group compared to single siRNA treatments. Immunofluorescence showed reduced α-SMA expression in combination-treated fibroblasts, indicating decreased myofibroblast differentiation.  

### Example 3: HKP Enhances siRNA Delivery Into Human Hypertrophic Scar  

HKP and siRNA were mixed at a 4:1 weight ratio in aqueous solution to form nanoparticles. Lyophilized nanoparticles were reconstituted and showed an average size of 150 nm diameter with 40 mV zeta potential. In human hypertrophic scar tissue implanted on nude mice, HKP-packaged fluorescent-labeled siRNA showed sustained tissue retention for 48 hours compared to rapid clearance of naked siRNA. qRT-PCR demonstrated dose-dependent knockdown of TGF-β1 and COX-2 in treated scars.  

### Example 4: HKP-Packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA Reduces Size of Human Hypertrophic Scar  

Human hypertrophic scar tissue implanted on nude mice was treated with HKP(TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA) via intrascar injections every 5 days for 15 days. Treated scars showed approximately 45% size reduction compared to controls by day 28. qRT-PCR analysis revealed significant silencing of TGF-β1, COX-2, α-SMA, and Col1A1 in treated tissue. Histological examination showed reduced collagen deposition and improved tissue architecture.  

### Example 5: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Reduces Size of Human Skin Grafts  

Human skin grafts on nude mice were treated with the composition as in Example 4. Similar results were observed, with approximately 40% reduction in graft size and significant downregulation of fibrotic markers. This confirmed the therapeutic potential for both established scars and developing fibrotic tissue.  

### Example 6: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Demonstrates a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Mechanism of Action  

Hydroxyproline acid levels, indicative of collagen content, were significantly reduced in treated scars and skin grafts compared to controls. Histological staining showed marked differences in tissue structure and reduced expression of pro-fibrotic factors including α-SMA, VEGF and CD3 in treated samples.  

### Example 7: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Induced Apoptotic Activities of the Fibroblast/Myofibroblasts In Vivo  

TUNEL assays demonstrated significantly increased apoptosis in fibroblasts from treated scar tissue compared to controls. Quantitative analysis confirmed higher numbers of apoptotic cells in treated samples, supporting the pro-apoptotic mechanism of the combination therapy.  

### Example 8: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Serves as a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Therapeutic Agent  

The composition demonstrated potent anti-fibrotic activity at microgram doses, significantly lower than other oligonucleotide-based therapies in development. The dual-targeted approach with HKP delivery provides a clinically viable strategy for hypertrophic scar treatment with a novel mechanism of action.  

## Discussion  

The dermal wound healing process involves complex interactions between inflammatory, proliferative, and remodeling phases. Fibroblasts play central roles in ECM production and tissue contraction during healing. The invention's simultaneous targeting of TGF-β1 and COX-2 disrupts key fibrotic pathways while promoting fibroblast apoptosis, addressing multiple aspects of hypertrophic scar formation.  

The HKP-based nanoparticle formulation overcomes delivery challenges by protecting siRNA and facilitating cellular uptake. Therapeutic doses are significantly lower than other oligonucleotide therapies due to this efficient delivery and the synergistic effects of dual gene targeting. The invention represents a novel approach to scar reduction by combining precise molecular targeting with advanced delivery technology.  

## Materials and Methods  

### siRNA Sequence Design and Selection  

25-mer blunt-ended siRNA duplexes targeting human TGF-β1 and COX-2 mRNAs were designed and screened in PC3 cells. The most potent sequences were selected based on qRT-PCR analysis of target gene knockdown after Lipofectamine 2000-mediated transfection.  

### Growth Inhibition In Vitro  

Human hypertrophic scar fibroblasts were treated with HKP-siRNA formulations and analyzed for growth inhibition using Annexin V/PI staining and flow cytometry. Cell morphology was assessed by electron microscopy, and fibrotic marker expression was evaluated by immunofluorescence and Western blot.  

### Nanoparticle Preparation  

HKP and siRNA were mixed at 4:1 weight ratio in aqueous solution to form nanoparticles. Particle size and zeta potential were measured by dynamic light scattering. Nanoparticles were lyophilized and reconstituted for in vivo use.  

### Mouse  

8-week-old male nude mice (nu/nu Balb/c) were used for all in vivo studies under approved protocols.  

### Human Hypertrophic Scar and Skin Tissue Implant Models  

Human hypertrophic scar tissue or skin grafts were implanted subcutaneously in nude mice. After 4 weeks, HKP-siRNA nanoparticles were administered via intrascar injections every 5 days for 15 days.  

### Therapeutic Evaluation with the Human Tissue Implant Models  

Treated tissues were analyzed for size reduction, gene expression (qRT-PCR), protein levels (Western blot, immunohistochemistry), collagen content (hydroxyproline assay), and apoptosis (TUNEL assay).  

### Statistical Analysis  

Data are presented as mean ± SD (in vitro) or mean ± SE (in vivo). Student's t-test determined significance between groups (p<0.05 considered significant). IBM SPSS Statistics v20 was used for analysis.
