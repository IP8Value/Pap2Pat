## DESCRIPTION  

### FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of immunopharmacology and cancer therapeutics. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of naltrexone and its analogues as antagonists of Toll-like receptor 9 (TLR9) signaling for the treatment of cancers and inflammatory disorders characterized by TLR9 overactivity. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing naltrexone and methods for treating subjects with TLR9-mediated disorders.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Naltrexone is a well-characterized opioid receptor antagonist that has been clinically approved for the treatment of alcohol and opioid dependence. Chemically, naltrexone hydrochloride is known as (5α)-17-(cyclopropylmethyl)-4,5-epoxy-3,14-dihydroxymorphinan-6-one hydrochloride, with a molecular weight of 377.9 g/mol. Beyond its canonical opioid receptor antagonism, naltrexone has demonstrated efficacy in off-label applications for various inflammatory conditions including Crohn's disease, multiple sclerosis, and fibromyalgia when administered at low doses (0.5-4.5 mg/day).  

Recent research in cancer therapeutics has revealed promising applications of low-dose naltrexone (LDN) in treating malignancies such as B-cell lymphoma and pancreatic cancer. The molecular mechanisms underlying these therapeutic effects extend beyond opioid receptor blockade, implicating immunomodulatory pathways. Notably, naltrexone and its structural analogue naloxone have been shown to antagonize Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) signaling, reversing neuropathic pain in animal models.  

The role of TLRs in cancer pathogenesis has become increasingly apparent, with TLR activation linked to tumor proliferation, angiogenesis, and immune evasion. In particular, TLR9 activation within the tumor microenvironment promotes cancer progression through chronic inflammation and suppression of antitumor immunity. Current cancer treatment strategies aim to modulate TLR signaling pathways, though specific TLR9 antagonists with clinical applicability remain limited. The discovery that naltrexone can effectively inhibit TLR9-mediated cytokine production provides a novel therapeutic approach for cancers and inflammatory disorders driven by TLR9 overactivity.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention establishes naltrexone as a potent antagonist of TLR9 signaling, distinct from its known activity against TLR4. The invention demonstrates that naltrexone specifically inhibits cytokine production induced by TLR9 activation in immune cells, particularly plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs), without affecting cell viability. This TLR9 antagonism occurs through mechanisms independent of the MyD88 signaling pathway shared by other TLR family members.  

The invention encompasses novel therapeutic applications of naltrexone and its pharmaceutical salts for treating disorders characterized by TLR9 overexpression or overactivity, including but not limited to melanoma, prostate cancer, pancreatic cancer, and B-cell malignancies. The invention further provides methods for determining TLR9 expression and signaling activity in subjects, as well as optimized pharmaceutical compositions containing naltrexone in therapeutic doses ranging from 0.5 mg to 200 mg.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention identifies naltrexone as a selective antagonist of TLR9 signaling, inhibiting cytokine production following TLR9 activation while sparing other TLR pathways. TLR9 is an intracellular pattern recognition receptor localized within endosomal compartments, where it recognizes unmethylated CpG DNA motifs characteristic of bacterial and viral genomes. Upon ligand binding, TLR9 initiates a signaling cascade through MyD88 adaptor protein, ultimately activating NF-κB and interferon regulatory factors that drive proinflammatory cytokine production.  

In the context of immune responses, plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs) serve as the primary cell type expressing TLR9, producing copious amounts of type I interferons and TNF-α upon activation. The invention demonstrates that naltrexone specifically inhibits TNF-α production in pDCs following TLR9 stimulation with CpG oligonucleotides, while having no effect on TLR4-mediated cytokine production in monocytes. This selective antagonism occurs without inducing apoptosis or compromising cell viability, as confirmed by annexin V/7AAD staining and trypan blue exclusion assays.  

TLR9 overactivity and overexpression contribute to pathological conditions through several mechanisms. In autoimmune disorders, TLR9 activation by self-DNA complexes breaks immune tolerance, while in cancer, ectopic TLR9 expression promotes tumor invasion and metastasis. The invention provides methods to quantify TLR9 expression and signaling activity in subjects using techniques including flow cytometry, intracellular cytokine staining, and ELISA-based cytokine detection.  

For therapeutic applications, the invention defines "treating" or "treatment" as the administration of naltrexone to ameliorate, stabilize, or eliminate symptoms of TLR9-mediated disorders. The term "subject" refers to human patients or animal models exhibiting TLR9 overactivity, particularly those diagnosed with cancers or inflammatory conditions. The invention encompasses both monotherapies and combination therapies where naltrexone is administered with standard cancer treatments or immunomodulators.  

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention contain naltrexone hydrochloride or its pharmaceutically acceptable salts in doses ranging from 0.5 mg to 200 mg. Preferred compositions utilize low-dose naltrexone (1.0-4.5 mg) for chronic administration or higher doses (50-200 mg) for acute TLR9 inhibition. The compositions may include excipients, stabilizers, or bioavailability enhancers, formulated for oral, sublingual, transdermal, or parenteral administration.  

The invention provides methods for treating cancer patients by administering naltrexone in doses sufficient to inhibit TLR9-mediated cytokine production while avoiding opioid receptor blockade. In one embodiment, LDN (3.0 mg/day) is combined with vitamin D3 for melanoma treatment. Another embodiment combines LDN with Mycobacterium vaccae immunotherapy for prostate cancer.  

As a vaccine adjuvant, naltrexone enhances cytotoxic T cell responses by modulating TLR9 activity in antigen-presenting cells. Vaccine compositions of the invention incorporate naltrexone in concentrations from 1 μM to 200 μM to optimize immune stimulation while preventing excessive TLR9 activation.  

**Example 1** demonstrates naltrexone's TLR9 antagonism through in vitro assays using human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs). Stimulation with CpG oligonucleotides (TLR9 ligand) in the presence of 200 μM naltrexone significantly reduced TNF-α production in pDCs compared to controls (p<0.05), while LPS-induced cytokine production remained unaffected.  

**Example 2** details the treatment of melanoma patients with LDN (3.0 mg/day) and vitamin D3 (5000 IU/day). Patients exhibited reduced inflammatory markers and stabilized disease progression over six months of therapy.  

**Example 3** describes prostate cancer treatment using LDN (4.5 mg/day) combined with heat-killed Mycobacterium vaccae. The combination therapy enhanced tumor-specific T cell responses while suppressing TLR9-mediated immunosuppression in the tumor microenvironment.  

The invention concludes that naltrexone's TLR9 antagonism represents a novel mechanism of action with broad applications in cancer immunotherapy and treatment of inflammatory disorders. By selectively inhibiting pathogenic TLR9 signaling without compromising protective immune functions, naltrexone provides a unique therapeutic approach for conditions driven by TLR9 overactivity.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1—Identification of Naltrexone as an Antagonist of TLR9  

Agonist and antagonist assays were performed on TLR-transfected cell lines and primary human PBMCs. PBMCs isolated from healthy donors were stimulated with 1 μM CpG ODN 2395 (TLR9 ligand) in the presence or absence of naltrexone (1-200 μM). After 24 hours, IL-6 and TNF-α production was quantified by ELISA. Naltrexone dose-dependently inhibited cytokine production, with 200 μM naltrexone reducing TNF-α levels by 68±7% in pDCs (p<0.05). Parallel experiments using LPS (TLR4 ligand) showed no significant cytokine inhibition, confirming TLR9-specific antagonism.  

### Example 2—LDN in the Treatment of Melanoma  

Ten stage III melanoma patients received daily LDN (3.0 mg) and vitamin D3 (5000 IU). Peripheral blood analyses at baseline and 3-month intervals revealed decreased TLR9 expression on circulating pDCs (42% reduction, p<0.01) and normalized serum IL-6 levels. Five patients demonstrated stable disease by RECIST criteria at 6 months, with no significant treatment-related adverse events.  

### Example 3—LDN in the Treatment of Prostate Cancer  

Eight metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer patients were treated with LDN (4.5 mg/day) and intradermal Mycobacterium vaccae every 4 weeks. Flow cytometry of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes showed increased CD8+ T cell frequencies (2.1-fold, p<0.05) and decreased PD-1 expression (35% reduction, p<0.01) after 12 weeks. PSA stabilization occurred in 5/8 patients, with median progression-free survival of 28 weeks.
