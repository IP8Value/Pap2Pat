# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of flexible energy storage devices, and more particularly to stretchable lithium-ion batteries (LIBs) utilizing kirigami-inspired structural designs. The invention provides novel battery architectures capable of substantial mechanical deformation while maintaining stable electrochemical performance, addressing critical limitations in wearable electronics and other applications requiring conformable power sources.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The development of flexible electronic devices has created an urgent need for energy storage solutions that can withstand repeated mechanical deformation. Conventional lithium-ion batteries employ rigid packaging and planar electrode configurations that fundamentally limit their stretchability. Prior attempts to create flexible batteries have included thin-film designs, wavy interconnect structures, and elastomeric encapsulation. However, these approaches typically sacrifice energy density, manufacturing scalability, or cycle life.  

Thin-film batteries demonstrate limited bendability but cannot achieve substantial stretching without structural failure. Wavy interconnect designs allow some stretchability but suffer from inconsistent contact between components during deformation. Elastomeric encapsulation methods often introduce undesirable interfacial resistance and packaging challenges. Existing solutions also frequently require specialized manufacturing processes incompatible with conventional battery production lines.  

There remains an unmet need for batteries that combine high stretchability (>50% strain) with: (1) energy densities comparable to conventional LIBs (>80 Wh/kg), (2) robust cycling performance under mechanical deformation (>100 cycles), (3) compatibility with standard battery manufacturing processes, and (4) the ability to power commercial electronic devices. The present invention addresses these limitations through innovative kirigami-based architectures that transform conventional battery materials into stretchable configurations.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a kirigami-based lithium-ion battery comprising patterned cuts and folds that enable substantial stretchability while maintaining electrochemical functionality. The battery structure undergoes controlled out-of-plane deformation or rotational displacement at cut locations during stretching, preventing damage to active materials and current collectors.  

Key aspects include:  
1) A folding and cutting process that creates specific kirigami patterns (zigzag-cut, cut-N-twist, or cut-N-shear) in multilayer battery structures  
2) A stress release mechanism where applied strain is accommodated through buckling or rotational motion at patterned cuts rather than material fracture  
3) Stretchability exceeding 150% while maintaining >85% capacity retention over 100 charge/discharge cycles  
4) Battery assembly methods preserving electrode alignment and interfacial contact throughout deformation  

The manufacturing method involves:  
a) Preparing conventional battery materials (graphite anode, LiCoO₂ cathode, Cu/Al current collectors)  
b) Precisely cutting these materials into kirigami patterns using customized tooling  
c) Assembling components with perfect layer-to-layer registration  
d) Sealing the structure while maintaining cut pattern integrity  

The resulting batteries demonstrate stable operation while powering commercial devices like smartwatches, with energy densities up to 87 Wh/kg and maximum output power of 4.1W. The invention enables stretchable energy storage using standard battery materials and manufacturing processes.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The kirigami patterns of the invention include three primary embodiments:  

1) **Zigzag-cut pattern**: Created by asymmetrically cutting a folded stack between creases, forming zigzag cuts that enable stretchability through out-of-plane buckling. The buckling amplitude determines the achievable strain, with longer cuts permitting greater deformation. This pattern maintains planar electrode surfaces between cuts.  

2) **Cut-N-twist pattern**: Formed by symmetrically cutting at all creases followed by end twisting, analogous to a telephone cord. This "locked" structure constrains out-of-plane deformation, accommodating strain through rotation at cuts. The packing density is defined by face width (typically 3-10mm).  

3) **Cut-N-shear pattern**: Achieves doubled packing density versus cut-N-twist by incorporating folding after symmetric cutting, followed by shear displacement. Strain is accommodated through cut rotation without buckling, similar to cut-N-twist but with improved volumetric efficiency.  

**Battery Construction**:  
Conventional LIB materials are adapted to kirigami structures:  
- Anode: Graphite slurry coated on 20μm Cu foil  
- Cathode: LiCoO₂ slurry coated on 10μm Al foil  
- Separator: Polypropylene membrane (Celgard 2500)  
- Electrolyte: 1M LiPF₆ in EC:DMC:DEC (1:1:1)  
- Packaging: Aluminized polyethylene laminate  

All components are precision-cut to identical kirigami patterns before assembly. Critical to performance is maintaining perfect alignment (<100μm tolerance) between layers during stacking and sealing. A customized punching system ensures uniform cuts across all layers.  

**Electrochemical Performance**:  
Kirigami LIBs demonstrate:  
- Stable charge/discharge plateaus at ~3.7V under stretching  
- 85% capacity retention after 100 stretch/release cycles  
- 99.8% Coulombic efficiency during mixed compact/stretched cycling  
- Rate capability from C/3 to 1C with 73% capacity retention  
- Minimal impedance change after mechanical deformation  

**Mechanical Characteristics**:  
- Steady 3.83V output during 90% stretching  
- 4.1W maximum power output maintained over 3000 stretch cycles  
- 13.3MPa tensile strength (cut-N-twist pattern)  
- SEM confirms no crack propagation in current collectors  

**Deformation Mechanics**:  
Finite element analysis reveals two competing deformation modes:  
1) Crack growth: Governed by Griffith's criterion where critical moment M_crack = (2γEH²/3a)¹/²  
2) Plastic rolling: Governed by dissipated energy where critical moment M_roll = βH²  

For typical parameters (γ=0.868N/m, E=69GPa, β=1MPa, H=3mm, a=0.03mm), plastic rolling dominates, creating a "safe zone" that prevents fracture. This explains the observed mechanical robustness.  

**Device Integration**:  
A cut-N-twist kirigami LIB (80mAh) successfully powered a Samsung Gear 2 smartwatch through:  
- 24.5 hours standby time (2.9mA discharge)  
- 90 minutes calling time (48mA discharge)  
- Functionality maintained during 30% wrist-to-arm stretching  
- No performance degradation during elbow flexion  

Scaling the design to full watchband dimensions (25cm × 3cm × 0.3cm) would provide ~700mAh capacity at 160Wh/kg - exceeding conventional smartwatch batteries while adding stretchability.  

### EXAMPLES  

**Example 1**: LIBs with zigzag-cut patterns demonstrated 100% stretchability and 85 Wh/kg energy density. Electrochemical cycling showed stable performance at both compact and stretched states, with 99.8% Coulombic efficiency over 100 cycles. Rate testing revealed 73% capacity retention when increasing from C/3 to 1C discharge rates.  

**Example 2**: Cut-N-shear pattern LIBs achieved 150% stretchability with 87 Wh/kg energy density. The doubled packing density versus cut-N-twist provided higher capacity per unit length while maintaining similar electrochemical and mechanical characteristics.  

**Example 3**: Multilayer stacking fabrication used graphite/LiCoO₂ active materials. Cathode slurry (LCO:PVDF:Carbon black = 8:1:1) was coated on Al foil and dried at 130°C. Anode slurry (graphite:CMC:SBR = 95:1.25:1.25) was coated on Cu foil and dried at 120°C. Condensed electrodes were cut to kirigami patterns before assembly.  

**Example 4**: Electrochemical characterization included cyclic galvanostatic testing (2.5-4.2V cutoff), EIS analysis (0.1Hz-100kHz), and output power measurement. The cut-N-twist LIB showed <5% impedance change after 100 stretch cycles, confirming interface stability.  

**Example 5**: Finite element simulations calculated critical moments for crack growth (M_crack) and plastic rolling (M_roll). For β=1MPa (typical bending diameter 500-800μm), M_roll remained below M_crack across all angles θ, ensuring safe deformation.  

**Example 6**: SEM analysis of anode (Cu) and cathode (Al) current collectors after 100 stretch cycles showed no crack propagation at cut locations, consistent with plastic rolling dominance predicted by modeling.  

**Example 7**: Smartwatch integration tests measured 3.6V stopping voltage during both standby (C/30 rate) and calling (C/2 rate) modes. Discharge curves matched direct device testing, validating the kirigami LIB's ability to power commercial electronics.
