## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cancer diagnostics and therapeutics. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for diagnosing pancreatic cancer through detection of specific biomarker proteins in biological samples, as well as therapeutic antibodies targeting these biomarkers for treatment of pancreatic cancer.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Pancreatic cancer remains one of the most lethal malignancies, with a five-year survival rate of less than 5%. The poor prognosis is largely attributable to late diagnosis, when the disease has already metastasized. Current diagnostic methods, including imaging techniques and the CA19-9 blood test, lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity for early detection. There exists an urgent need for improved diagnostic methods capable of identifying pancreatic cancer at earlier, more treatable stages.  

The molecular pathogenesis of pancreatic cancer involves characteristic genetic alterations, including activating mutations in KRAS and inactivation of tumor suppressor genes such as p16INK4a and TP53. These genetic changes drive the progression from pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) to invasive pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC). Early diagnosis is critical, as surgical resection of localized tumors can significantly improve patient outcomes. However, the identification of reliable biomarkers for early detection has proven challenging due to the complexity of the plasma proteome and the low abundance of tumor-derived proteins in circulation.  

Genetically engineered mouse (GEM) models that recapitulate human pancreatic cancer provide a valuable platform for biomarker discovery. These models offer defined stages of tumor development, controlled genetic backgrounds, and standardized sampling conditions that reduce biological variability. Blood-based biomarkers identified in such models may have direct relevance to human disease, as mouse and human pancreatic cancers share conserved molecular features. The present invention addresses these challenges by employing advanced proteomic technologies to identify and validate novel biomarker proteins for pancreatic cancer diagnosis and treatment.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods for diagnosing pancreatic cancer by detecting altered levels of specific biomarker proteins in biological samples. The method involves obtaining a biological sample from a subject, determining the level of at least one marker protein selected from the group consisting of ALCAM, TIMP1, ICAM1, LCN2, TNFRSF1A, REG1A, REG3, WFDC2, IGFBP4, and PTPRG, and comparing the level to a reference range, wherein an altered level indicates pancreatic cancer.  

The invention further provides methods for treating pancreatic cancer by administering a therapeutic antibody that specifically binds to one or more of the biomarker proteins. The therapeutic antibody may be conjugated to a cytotoxic agent, radionuclide, or chemotherapeutic drug for targeted delivery to pancreatic cancer cells.  

Additionally, the invention encompasses diagnostic kits containing reagents for detecting the biomarker proteins, including antibodies, standards, and instructions for use. The kits may be configured for panel testing of multiple biomarkers to improve diagnostic accuracy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The term "altered level" as used herein refers to a statistically significant increase or decrease in the concentration of a biomarker protein in a test sample compared to a reference range established from control samples. For the purposes of this invention, an increased level typically indicates the presence of pancreatic cancer.  

A "biological sample" includes any fluid or tissue sample that may contain the biomarker proteins, with blood samples being particularly preferred. Blood samples may be processed to obtain plasma or serum for analysis. The term "diagnosing" encompasses detecting pancreatic cancer, determining prognosis, monitoring disease progression, and assessing therapeutic response.  

The "marker proteins" of the invention include ALCAM (CD166), tissue inhibitor of metalloproteinase 1 (TIMP1), intercellular adhesion molecule 1 (ICAM1), neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (LCN2), tumor necrosis factor receptor superfamily member 1A (TNFRSF1A), regenerating islet-derived proteins (REG1A and REG3), whey-acidic protein four-disulfide core domain 2 (WFDC2), insulin-like growth factor binding protein 4 (IGFBP4), and protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type gamma (PTPRG). These proteins may be used individually or in combination with CA19.9, the current clinical standard for pancreatic cancer detection.  

A "panel test" refers to the simultaneous measurement of multiple biomarkers to improve diagnostic accuracy. The invention provides specific combinations of biomarkers that show superior performance compared to single markers, particularly for distinguishing pancreatic cancer from chronic pancreatitis and for early detection.  

The term "subjects" includes human patients suspected of having or at risk for developing pancreatic cancer. Risk factors include family history, genetic predisposition (e.g., BRCA mutations), chronic pancreatitis, smoking, obesity, and diabetes mellitus. Other diagnostic methods such as imaging (CT, MRI, endoscopic ultrasound) may be used in conjunction with the biomarker tests described herein.  

Assay procedures for detecting the biomarker proteins may include mass spectrometry, chromatography, electrophoresis, sedimentation, isoelectric focusing, antibody-based assays, or two-dimensional electrophoresis. Altered levels may be determined by comparing test results to established reference ranges derived from healthy controls.  

The invention encompasses both homogeneous and heterogeneous immunoassay formats, including ELISA, radioimmunoassay, immunoblotting, and immunoprecipitation. Antibodies specific for the biomarker proteins may be monoclonal or polyclonal and can be produced using standard techniques.  

Diagnostic kits according to the invention may contain antibodies, calibration standards, buffers, and instructions for performing the assays. The kits may include biochemical materials and reagents necessary for sample processing and analysis. Components may include microplates, detection reagents, and data analysis software.  

Therapeutic antibodies of the invention may be full-length immunoglobulins, Fab fragments, single-chain antibodies, or other antigen-binding variants. The antibodies may be conjugated to radionuclides (e.g., 90Y, 131I), chemotherapeutic agents (e.g., doxorubicin, paclitaxel), or cytotoxic agents (e.g., ricin, diphtheria toxin) for targeted therapy.  

For treatment and prevention, a "therapeutically effective amount" refers to a dosage sufficient to achieve a desired clinical effect, such as tumor regression or stabilization of disease. The antibodies may be formulated with pharmaceutical carriers such as saline, albumin, or liposomes. Administration routes include intravenous, intraperitoneal, or intratumoral injection. Dosage considerations depend on factors such as patient weight, disease stage, and combination therapies.  

### Materials and Methods  

The invention employs genetically engineered mouse models of pancreatic cancer for biomarker discovery. Mice are bred to carry specific oncogenic mutations (e.g., Pdx1-Cre Kras G12D Ink4a/Arf lox/lox) and euthanized at defined stages of tumor development. Plasma is collected by cardiac puncture and processed to remove abundant proteins (e.g., albumin, IgG) through immunodepletion.  

Proteins in the plasma samples are isotopically labeled using light (D0) and heavy (D3) forms of acrylamide for quantitative comparison. Fractionation is performed using anion-exchange chromatography followed by reversed-phase chromatography. The resulting fractions are analyzed by liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) for protein identification and quantification.  

### Example 2  

A mouse model of pancreatic cancer was used to identify tumor-associated proteins in plasma. Mice developed pancreatic intraepithelial neoplasia (PanIN) at 5.5 weeks and progressed to pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) by 7 weeks. Plasma pools from tumor-bearing mice and controls were differentially labeled with acrylamide isotopes, mixed, and extensively fractionated.  

LC-MS/MS analysis identified 1,442 proteins across seven orders of magnitude of abundance. Quantitative analysis revealed 165 proteins significantly upregulated in tumor-bearing mice. Cross-species validation showed concordance between mouse and human pancreatic cancer for many of these proteins.  

### Example 3  

Acrylamide isotopic labeling provided relative quantitative information about protein levels in cancer versus control samples. Proteins upregulated in cancer samples were prioritized as biomarker candidates. Liver-derived proteins and acute-phase reactants were excluded from further consideration.  

Immunohistochemistry validated the expression of candidate proteins (ALCAM, PTPRG, TIMP1, TNC) in mouse pancreatic tumor tissue. ELISA measurements confirmed elevated levels of ALCAM, ICAM1, and TIMP1 in plasma from tumor-bearing mice.  

### Example 4  

Biomarker candidates were validated in human samples. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated expression of PTPRG, TNFRSF1A, and TNC in human pancreatic cancer tissue. ELISA measurements of ALCAM, ICAM1, LCN2, TNFRSF1A, TIMP1, REG1A, REG3, WFDC2, and IGFBP4 in human sera showed significant elevation in pancreatic cancer patients compared to controls.  

A blinded analysis using prediagnostic sera from the CARET study demonstrated that a panel of five proteins (LCN2, REG1A, REG3, TIMP1, IGFBP4) could discriminate pancreatic cancer cases from controls 7-13 months before clinical diagnosis. The panel showed superior performance compared to CA19-9 alone, particularly when combined with CA19-9 measurements.  

The invention thus provides novel methods and compositions for early detection and targeted treatment of pancreatic cancer, addressing a critical unmet need in oncology. The biomarker panels and therapeutic antibodies described herein offer significant improvements over current diagnostic and therapeutic approaches.
