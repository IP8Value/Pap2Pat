# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to molecular recording of protein expression in living cells. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for recording temporal patterns of protein expression through self-assembling protein chains that preserve chronological information along their linear extent. The invention enables retrospective analysis of cellular physiological states by encoding expression histories into protein-based structures that can be read out through standard immunostaining techniques after cell fixation.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Reading biological signals and processes that occur over time in living cells is fundamental to advancing biological research. Current methods for monitoring cellular activities rely primarily on live imaging of genetically encoded fluorescent reporters, which requires continuous microscopy over extended periods. While powerful, live imaging is resource-intensive, limiting scalability across large cell populations. Fixed-tissue imaging methods offer greater scalability through techniques like tissue clearing and expansion microscopy, but only provide snapshot views rather than continuous recording.  

Existing nucleic acid-based recording systems require cell lysis and sequencing, destroying native cellular context. There remains an unmet need for technologies that combine continuous recording of physiological signals with scalable readout in intact cells. The present invention addresses these limitations by developing protein-based recording systems that preserve temporal information in living cells while enabling high-throughput analysis after fixation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides expression-recording islands (XRIs) comprising self-assembling filament-forming monomers that polymerize into linear protein assemblies within cells. Each monomer contains at least one detectable epitope tag and a protein spacer that regulates assembly geometry. The XRI composition grows bidirectionally over time, with new monomers added continuously to the elongating ends. The relative abundance of different epitope-tagged monomers at any given time determines their local density along the growing chain, encoding temporal expression patterns.  

The invention further provides nucleic acid vectors encoding XRI compositions, including adeno-associated viral (AAV) vectors for delivery to mammalian cells. Cells containing XRI vectors express the self-assembling monomers, which form stable linear assemblies that record expression histories without disrupting normal physiology.  

Methods are provided for identifying expression history records by detecting epitope tag patterns along XRIs after cell fixation. The fractional cumulative expression of reference tags serves as a calibratable temporal axis for interpreting recorded signals. Comparative analysis of expression records enables investigation of cellular responses to stimuli across populations.  

The XRI system allows determination of the amount, pattern, and identity of expressed epitope tags along the protein assembly. This temporal history of expression can be analyzed in various cell types, with the number of detectable labels and protein spacers being adjustable based on recording needs.  

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN OF THE SEQUENCES  

The XRI compositions utilize engineered protein motifs including the 1POK(E239Y) filament-forming domain derived from E. coli isoaspartyl dipeptidase. Insulator components such as maltose-binding protein (MBP) are fused to the filament-forming domain to prevent lateral aggregation. Epitope tags including HA, FLAG, and V5 enable immunodetection of recorded signals. The compositions may further include trafficking sequences, cleavage sites, and other functional domains as needed for specific applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a protein-based system for recording cellular physiological information over time. The expression-recording island (XRI) strategy employs linear protein assemblies that grow continuously within living cells, preserving the temporal order of incorporated monomers. As gene expression changes, different epitope-tagged monomers become available for incorporation, creating a molecular record along the elongating chain. After fixation, immunostaining reveals the sequence of expression events encoded in the XRI's epitope pattern.  

### Compositions and Components  

The XRI composition comprises self-assembling protein monomers that polymerize into stable linear structures. Each monomer includes a filament-forming domain, at least one detectable epitope tag, and optional spacer domains that regulate assembly properties. Nucleic acid sequences encoding XRI components can be delivered to cells via various vectors, with expression driven by constitutive or inducible promoters as needed.  

Non-limiting examples of suitable filament-forming proteins include engineered variants of 1POK and DHF40. Detectable tags may include HA, FLAG, V5, or other immunodetectable sequences. Spacer domains such as MBP or mEGFP can be incorporated to optimize linear growth and reduce lateral aggregation. The composition may further include trafficking signals, degradation tags, or other functional elements to tune performance in different cellular contexts.  

### Expression and Use of XRI Components  

The XRI system provides a platform for recording cellular physiological histories without requiring continuous live imaging. In practice, cells are engineered to express XRI components under appropriate regulatory control. Baseline expression characteristics are established for control conditions, after which test cells are exposed to candidate stimuli. Changes in expression patterns are recorded through differential incorporation of epitope-tagged monomers into the growing XRI.  

The system enables screening of therapeutic agents by comparing expression records between treated and untreated cells. It further supports studies of normal development by capturing temporal changes in gene expression profiles. Recording duration can span days to weeks, with the number of distinguishable time points being adjustable through the choice of epitope tags and detection methods.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Animal procedures were conducted in accordance with approved protocols. Hippocampal neuron cultures were prepared from postnatal day 0-1 mice, plated on Matrigel-coated coverslips in defined medium. Molecular cloning produced mammalian-codon-optimized XRI constructs in AAV backbones under UBC or c-fos promoters.  

DNA transfection used calcium phosphate precipitation, while AAV transduction employed serotype 9 vectors. Chemical treatments included 4-hydroxytamoxifen (4-OHT) for inducible Cre activation and KCl for neuronal depolarization. Electrophysiology confirmed normal neuronal function in XRI-expressing cells.  

Animal surgeries involved stereotaxic AAV injection into hippocampal CA1, followed by 4-OHT administration via intraperitoneal injection. Histological processing included perfusion fixation, sectioning, and immunostaining with antibodies against HA, FLAG, V5 and neuronal markers.  

Immunofluorescence imaging used confocal microscopy with standardized parameters across experimental groups. Image analysis quantified epitope tag distributions along XRIs, with line profiles converted to temporal records through calibration algorithms. RNA sequencing confirmed minimal transcriptional changes in XRI-expressing neurons.  

Statistical analysis employed appropriate tests for each dataset, with details provided in supplementary materials. The results demonstrate reliable recording of gene expression timecourses over several days, with single-cell resolution preserved in both cultured neurons and intact brain tissue.
