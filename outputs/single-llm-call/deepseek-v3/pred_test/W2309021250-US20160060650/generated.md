## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of plant biotechnology and specifically to novel potato breeding strategies involving cellulose binding domain proteins.  

Cellulose binding domains (CBDs) represent a specialized class of carbohydrate binding modules that mediate adherence to carbohydrate substrates. While typically associated with saprophyte-encoded cellulolytic enzymes, CBDs are notably absent from most plant pathogen-encoded and plant-encoded cellulolytic enzymes. The primary function of these domains involves mediating adherence to carbohydrate substrates, with some CBDs demonstrating additional capacity to directly dissociate cellulose microfibrils.  

Current potato breeding faces significant limitations, particularly regarding the genetic improvement of established cultivars like Bintje. The potato industry struggles with introducing desirable traits such as disease resistance while maintaining the superior agronomic qualities of popular cultivars. Conventional breeding approaches are hampered by the sterility of many commercial potato varieties, preventing genetic recombination and trait introgression.  

This background motivates the need for innovative strategies to overcome reproductive barriers in potato breeding. The present invention addresses this need through the novel application of Phytophthora infestans CBD1 protein expression in potato plants.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel strategy for expressing the CBD1 gene from Phytophthora infestans in potato plants, particularly in the cultivar Bintje. This expression results in transformed potato plants exhibiting significant morphological changes, including enhanced growth characteristics and, most notably, restoration of ovule development capacity in normally sterile cultivars.  

A key object of the invention is to provide transformed potato plants capable of producing seed berries when pollinated with viable pollen from other potato varieties. These transformed plants demonstrate altered ovule development patterns compared to non-transformed controls, enabling previously impossible breeding combinations.  

The invention further outlines methods for obtaining improved potato plants through crosses between CBD1-expressing Bintje plants and donor varieties containing desirable traits such as late blight resistance or enhanced tuber qualities. The resulting progeny can be selected for desired characteristics while eliminating the CBD1 transgene, providing improved cultivars without permanent genetic modification.  

Additional objects of the invention include providing a model system for studying ovary development in Solanaceous crops and enabling the transfer of multigenic traits into sterile potato cultivars through conventional breeding approaches made possible by the invention.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention involves the transformation of potato plants, particularly the cultivar Bintje, with the CBD1 gene from Phytophthora infestans. This transformation overcomes significant limitations in potato production by enabling genetic recombination in otherwise sterile cultivars.  

Current potato production faces challenges in improving established cultivars like Bintje, which despite its excellent agronomic qualities lacks resistance to major pathogens. The invention utilizes the unique properties of the Phytophthora CBD1 protein to modify plant development and enable breeding strategies previously impossible with sterile cultivars.  

In the transgenic Bintje plants of the invention, the CBD1 gene induces ovum development leading to seed ball formation when pollinated with viable pollen from other potato varieties. This represents a dramatic departure from normal Bintje development, where flowers typically abscise after bloom without ovary formation.  

The CBD1 gene discovery stems from its identification in Phytophthora infestans, where it functions as a cellulose-binding protein associated with the pathogen's hyphal cell wall. The protein's unique characteristics suggested potential applications in modifying plant-pathogen interactions, but its unexpected effects on plant reproductive development form the basis of this invention.  

Testing CBD1's influence on plant growth revealed variable effects depending on developmental stage, with some transgenic lines showing increased height during later growth phases. Most remarkably, all transgenic lines exhibited the capacity for ovary development, an unprecedented characteristic in non-transformed Bintje plants.  

The invention demonstrates an unusual feature of Phytophthora CBD1 transgenic Bintje plants: their capacity for parthenocarpic fruit development. This development occurs without fertilization, though pollination with viable donor pollen produces larger seed berries containing viable seeds. This phenomenon suggests CBD1's involvement in cell wall modification processes typically regulated by auxin during normal fruit development.  

The role of auxin in cell wall loosening and cell expansion is well-established, typically mediated through plant-encoded cell wall carbohydrate-modifying enzymes. The invention's demonstration that CBD1 expression can bypass normal auxin-mediated pathways represents a significant advance in understanding plant reproductive development.  

Expression of CBD1 provides a powerful tool for developing the Bintje gene pool, which has remained genetically static since the cultivar's introduction in 1904. The invention overcomes key limitations of the current Bintje cultivar by enabling the integration of valuable traits such as late blight resistance and improved tuber qualities through conventional breeding approaches.  

The invention demonstrates successful integration of single-gene late blight resistance from donor varieties into the Bintje genetic background. Additionally, the technology facilitates introduction of color variations and other multigenic traits into Bintje progeny while allowing selection for plants lacking the CBD1 transgene itself.  

Transformation according to the invention may be accomplished through various methods including Agrobacterium-mediated transformation or particle-accelerated ("gene gun") transformation technology. Additional transformation methods suitable for use with the invention include but are not limited to electroporation, silicon carbide fiber-mediated transformation, and polyethylene glycol-mediated transformation.  

The invention involves incorporation of isolated polynucleotides encoding CBD1 into recombinant constructs suitable for plant transformation. These constructs typically include plant expression vectors containing regulatory sequences such as promoters operably linked to the CBD1 coding sequence.

## EXAMPLES  

### Plant Transformation and Selection  

The plant transformation procedure involved using 4-week-old tissue culture-grown Bintje potato plantlets. Leaves were excised and immersed in an acetosyringone-induced culture of Agrobacterium tumefaciens containing the CBD1 gene in binary vector pBI121. Following standard regeneration procedures, plantlets were maintained on MS media and screened by PCR using 35S-specific primers.  

Integration sites were determined using genome walking techniques, revealing unique insertion patterns for different transgenic events. Immuno-localization studies confirmed abundant CBD1 protein expression throughout developing seed berry tissues in all transgenic lines.  

### Example 2  

A breeding study demonstrated successful transfer of late blight resistance from the cultivar Sarpo Mira into Bintje progeny. Reciprocal crosses using CBD1 transgenic Bintje as the female parent and Sarpo Mira as the pollen donor produced viable seed. Progeny screening revealed successful inheritance of late blight resistance in multiple lines, with some showing complete resistance matching the donor parent.  

### Example 3  

Additional breeding studies demonstrated transfer of multigenic traits for tuber color, shape and nutritive value. Crosses between CBD1 transgenic Bintje and the purple-skinned, yellow-fleshed cultivar Peter Wilcox produced progeny exhibiting a range of tuber characteristics. These results confirm the invention's capacity to introduce complex trait combinations into the Bintje genetic background through conventional breeding approaches enabled by CBD1 expression.
