# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and recombinant protein production, specifically to the use of promoters derived from the Hspa5 gene to enhance the expression of foreign genes, particularly monoclonal antibodies (mAbs), in mammalian cell cultures. The invention is particularly applicable to Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell-based production systems, which are widely used in biopharmaceutical manufacturing.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Protein-based pharmaceutical products, including monoclonal antibodies, have become increasingly important in modern medicine due to their high specificity and therapeutic efficacy. Over 120 monoclonal antibody products have been approved for clinical use since the first approval of Muromonab-CD3 in 1986. The production of these biologics relies heavily on recombinant protein expression systems, with mammalian cells being the preferred hosts due to their ability to perform complex post-translational modifications essential for protein functionality.  

Microorganisms and plants have been explored as alternative hosts for protein production, but they often fail to achieve the necessary post-translational modifications, such as glycosylation, required for therapeutic efficacy. Cultured mammalian cells, particularly CHO cells, have emerged as the dominant platform due to their robust growth, scalability, and established regulatory compliance. However, despite these advantages, challenges remain in achieving consistently high productivity, especially during prolonged fed-batch cultures.  

Prior approaches to increasing productivity have focused on optimizing genomic integration sites, increasing gene copy numbers, and enhancing transcriptional efficiency through promoter engineering. Commonly used promoters include viral-derived promoters such as human cytomegalovirus (hCMV), simian virus 40 early (SV40E), and Rous sarcoma virus (RSV), as well as endogenous promoters like human elongation factor-1α (hEF1α) and Chinese hamster elongation factor-1α (CHEF1α). While these promoters have demonstrated utility, they often exhibit declining activity in the latter stages of culture, limiting overall yield.  

Existing promoters also suffer from limitations such as variability in expression levels across different clones and culture conditions, as well as insufficient long-term stability. There remains a need for promoters that maintain high activity throughout the entire culture period, particularly in fed-batch production systems.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

The primary technical problem addressed by the present invention is the decline in foreign gene expression during the latter stages of fed-batch culture in mammalian cell systems, particularly in CHO cells. This decline reduces the overall yield of recombinant proteins, such as monoclonal antibodies, and necessitates the development of promoters capable of sustaining high expression levels throughout the entire culture duration.  

### Solution to Problem  

The invention provides a solution by utilizing the promoter region of the Hspa5 gene (heat shock protein family A member 5), which exhibits sustained high activity during prolonged culture. The Hspa5 promoter (Hspa5p) is derived from the upstream regulatory region of the Hspa5 gene and includes polynucleotides with sequences corresponding to SEQ ID NO: 1, SEQ ID NO: 5, SEQ ID NO: 6, SEQ ID NO: 7, SEQ ID NO: 8, and SEQ ID NO: 9. These sequences represent variations of the Hspa5 promoter from different species, including human, mouse, and rat, as well as optimized truncations and mutants thereof.  

Key features of the Hspa5 promoter include:  
- A highly conserved region approximately 0.6 kb upstream of the transcription start site, containing core promoter elements such as TATA-box, CpG island, CCAAT-box, and GC-box.  
- ER stress-responsive elements (ERSE) that contribute to the promoter's sustained activity under conditions of endoplasmic reticulum stress, which is common during high-level recombinant protein production.  
- The ability to drive high-level expression of foreign genes, including monoclonal antibodies, throughout the entire fed-batch culture period.  

### Advantageous Effects of Invention  

The Hspa5 promoter offers several advantageous effects, including:  
1. **Sustained High Activity**: Unlike conventional promoters, Hspa5p maintains high transcriptional activity during the latter stages of fed-batch culture, leading to increased overall yield.  
2. **Broad Applicability**: The promoter is effective for various recombinant proteins, including different subclasses of monoclonal antibodies (IgG1, IgG2, IgG4Pro).  
3. **Species Compatibility**: Hspa5p from different species (human, mouse, rat) exhibits comparable performance, allowing flexibility in host cell selection.  
4. **Enhanced Productivity**: Cells expressing recombinant proteins under the control of Hspa5p show improved specific production rates (SPR) and higher titers compared to those using traditional promoters like hEF1α.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

### Definitions  

- **Gene**: A DNA sequence encoding a functional RNA or protein product.  
- **Polynucleotide**: A linear polymer of nucleotides, including DNA and RNA.  
- **Polypeptide**: A linear polymer of amino acids, including proteins and peptides.  
- **Gene Expression**: The process by which information from a gene is used to synthesize a functional gene product.  
- **Foreign Gene**: A gene introduced into a host cell that is not naturally present in the host genome.  
- **Foreign Protein**: A protein encoded by a foreign gene and expressed in a host cell.  
- **Gene Expression Unit**: A DNA construct comprising a promoter, coding sequence, and regulatory elements required for transcription and translation.  
- **Promoter**: A DNA sequence that initiates transcription of a gene.  
- **Promoter Activity**: The ability of a promoter to drive transcription of a downstream gene.  
- **DNA Element**: A functional sequence within a promoter or enhancer that regulates gene expression.  

### Hspa5 Promoter  

The Hspa5 promoter is derived from the upstream region of the Hspa5 gene and includes sequences from various species, such as human (SEQ ID NO: 1), mouse (SEQ ID NO: 5), and rat (SEQ ID NO: 6). The promoter may also include mutant variants (SEQ ID NO: 7, SEQ ID NO: 8, SEQ ID NO: 9) with optimized activity.  

### Methods for Enhancing Foreign Gene Expression  

The invention provides methods for enhancing foreign gene expression by:  
1. **Cloning the Hspa5 Promoter**: The promoter region is isolated from genomic DNA or synthesized chemically and inserted into an expression vector upstream of the foreign gene.  
2. **Constructing Expression Vectors**: The Hspa5 promoter is combined with other regulatory elements, such as enhancers or insulators, to create a gene expression unit.  
3. **Transforming Host Cells**: The expression vector is introduced into host cells (e.g., CHO cells) using standard transfection techniques.  
4. **Culturing Transformed Cells**: The cells are cultured under conditions suitable for high-level protein production, such as fed-batch culture.  

### Production of Recombinant Proteins  

The invention further provides methods for producing recombinant proteins, including monoclonal antibodies, by:  
1. **Expressing Heavy and Light Chains**: Separate vectors or a single vector encoding both heavy and light chains of an antibody are introduced into host cells under the control of the Hspa5 promoter.  
2. **Purifying the Product**: The recombinant protein is harvested from the culture supernatant and purified using standard techniques such as protein A affinity chromatography.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Identification of the Hspa5 Promoter  

Transcriptome analysis of CHO-K1 cells cultured under fed-batch conditions revealed that the Hspa5 gene exhibited increasing expression levels during the latter stages of culture. The 3-kb upstream region of the Hspa5 gene was cloned and tested for promoter activity using a luciferase reporter assay. The Hspa5 promoter demonstrated higher activity compared to the hEF1α promoter, particularly in the late culture phase.  

### Example 2: Optimization of the Hspa5 Promoter  

Truncation analysis identified a 0.6-kb region upstream of the Hspa5 gene as the minimal functional promoter. This region contained conserved elements such as TATA-box, CpG island, and ERSE. Further optimization included testing promoters from human, mouse, and rat, all of which showed comparable activity.  

### Example 3: Production of Monoclonal Antibodies  

CHO cells expressing IgG1, IgG2, and IgG4Pro under the control of the Hspa5 promoter were cultured in fed-batch mode. The cells exhibited sustained high levels of antibody production throughout the culture period, with titers significantly higher than those achieved using the hEF1α promoter.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The invention is industrially applicable for the large-scale production of recombinant proteins, particularly monoclonal antibodies, in mammalian cell cultures. The Hspa5 promoter enables higher yields and more consistent production, reducing manufacturing costs and improving the availability of biologic therapeutics.
