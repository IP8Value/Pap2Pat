# DESCRIPTION

Alzheimer's disease (AD) represents a progressive neurodegenerative disorder characterized by cognitive decline and memory loss. The pathological hallmarks of AD include extracellular amyloid plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles. Amyloid-β (Aβ) peptides play a central role in AD pathogenesis, with accumulating evidence suggesting that soluble Aβ oligomers rather than fibrillar deposits constitute the primary neurotoxic species. Among various Aβ isoforms, N-truncated pyroglutamate-modified Aβ peptides (AβpE3) demonstrate enhanced aggregation propensity and increased neurotoxicity compared to full-length Aβ1-42. The present invention relates to novel antibodies specifically recognizing oligomeric forms of AβpE3 and their use in diagnosis and treatment of Alzheimer's disease.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Alzheimer's disease constitutes the most common form of dementia, affecting millions worldwide with increasing prevalence in aging populations. The disease manifests through progressive memory impairment, cognitive dysfunction, and ultimately complete loss of independence. Neuropathologically, AD is characterized by two hallmark lesions: extracellular amyloid plaques composed primarily of Aβ peptides and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles containing hyperphosphorylated tau protein.

Aβ peptides derive from sequential proteolytic processing of the amyloid precursor protein (APP) by β- and γ-secretases. These peptides exhibit considerable heterogeneity, with Aβ1-40 and Aβ1-42 being the most abundant isoforms. However, post-translational modifications generate additional variants, including N-truncated forms that frequently undergo pyroglutamate modification at position 3 (AβpE3). These modified peptides demonstrate increased stability, enhanced aggregation propensity, and greater neurotoxicity compared to their full-length counterparts.

The discovery of AβpE3 in AD brains revealed its significant accumulation in amyloid plaques. Subsequent studies demonstrated that AβpE3 peptides exhibit faster oligomerization kinetics and form more stable aggregates than Aβ1-42. Glutaminyl cyclase catalyzes the conversion of N-terminal glutamate to pyroglutamate, and inhibition of this enzyme reduces AβpE3 formation in animal models. Transgenic mice overexpressing human APP develop AD-like pathology, with APP/PS1KI mice showing particularly robust neuron loss and behavioral deficits. These models demonstrate age-dependent accumulation of intraneuronal Aβ, supporting the hypothesis that intracellular accumulation precedes extracellular deposition.

Current therapeutic approaches targeting Aβ in AD include passive immunization with anti-Aβ antibodies and small molecule inhibitors of Aβ production or aggregation. However, these strategies have shown limited clinical efficacy, possibly due to their inability to specifically target the most toxic Aβ species. The identification of soluble oligomers and β-sheet containing amyloid fibrils as particularly toxic forms of Aβ has shifted therapeutic focus toward these assemblies. Despite progress in understanding Aβ toxicity, existing treatments fail to address the specific pathological role of AβpE3 oligomers, highlighting the need for novel therapeutic strategies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses the technical problem of specifically detecting and neutralizing toxic AβpE3 oligomers in Alzheimer's disease. The solution comprises antibody molecules capable of selectively recognizing oligomeric forms of AβpE3 without cross-reacting with full-length Aβ1-42 or monomeric AβpE3. These antibodies enable neuropathological differentiation between healthy controls and AD cases through distinct staining patterns in brain tissue.

The invention provides new diagnostic and pharmaceutical tools based on antibodies directed against oligomeric AβpE3. Exemplary antibody molecules include 9D5H6 and 8C4D2, which demonstrate specific binding to low-n oligomers (4-10mers) of AβpE3-42. These antibodies exhibit characteristic staining patterns in AD cases, with abundant intraneuronal and vascular immunoreactivity contrasting with negligible staining in healthy controls.

The binding molecules of the invention serve multiple purposes: diagnosing subjects suspected of suffering from amyloid-related disorders, monitoring treatment efficacy, and predicting therapeutic response. Passive immunization of 5XFAD mice with antibody 9D5 demonstrated significant reduction of AβpE3 levels and plaque load, accompanied by normalization of behavioral deficits. These findings identify AβpE3 oligomers as key pathogenic components in AD and provide therapeutic methods for reducing their levels.

Compared to glutaminyl cyclase inhibitors, the present invention offers superior specificity by directly targeting toxic oligomers rather than preventing their formation. The binding molecules not only recognize but also inhibit Aβ oligomerization, providing dual therapeutic benefits. Pharmaceutical compositions containing these binding molecules represent novel treatments for amyloid-related disorders, with particular relevance to Alzheimer's disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The antibodies of the invention were characterized through extensive immunohistochemical analysis in APP/PS1KI and 5xFAD transgenic mice. These studies revealed abundant intraneuronal staining in transgenic models, mirroring the strong immunostaining observed in human AD cases, particularly in blood vessels. Healthy control specimens showed negligible staining, confirming the specificity for pathological AβpE3 oligomers.

The antibodies recognize AβpE3-42 oligomers in SDS-Western blot and Dot Blot assays without cross-reacting with Aβ1-42. This specificity makes them particularly useful in diagnostic assays, including sandwich ELISA formats where antibody 9D5 serves as the capturing antibody. Comparative analysis of plasma samples from AD patients and healthy controls demonstrated differential levels of AβpE3 oligomers, enabling neuropathological differentiation.

Diagnostic applications include methods for diagnosing subjects suspected of suffering from amyloid-related disorders by determining Aβ oligomer levels in cell or tissue samples and comparing them to reference amounts. The comparison may involve healthy controls or established threshold values. Similarly, methods for monitoring treatment efficacy involve serial measurement of Aβ oligomer levels, with decreasing amounts indicating therapeutic success.

The binding molecules of the invention include antibodies, polypeptides, peptides, peptidomimetics, or small molecules capable of specifically recognizing and inhibiting Aβ oligomerization. Antibody molecules may be monoclonal, polyclonal, or fragments thereof, with preferred embodiments including the 9D5H6 and 8C4D2 antibodies. These antibodies recognize an epitope present in oligomeric but not monomeric AβpE3, as demonstrated through immunoblotting, immunostaining, and immunoisolation experiments.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the binding molecules formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers and excipients. Administration may be systemic or local, with preferred routes including parenteral delivery. Dosage regimens are tailored to the specific amyloid-related disorder and patient characteristics, with monitoring of therapeutic response through the diagnostic methods described.

The invention further provides methods for identifying agents useful in treating amyloid-related disorders through thioflavin T aggregation assays measuring the rate of AβpE3 oligomerization. Candidate agents showing significant reduction in aggregation rate represent potential therapeutics. Nucleic acid molecules encoding the antibody molecules, expression vectors, and host cells for recombinant production are also encompassed.

Diagnostic kits may contain the antibody molecules, detection reagents, and instructions for use. These kits enable diagnosis of diseases associated with amyloidogenesis, including familial and sporadic Alzheimer's disease, through in vitro or in vivo methods. The kits may also facilitate monitoring of treatment efficacy and prediction of therapeutic response.

### EXAMPLES

Antibodies 9D5H6 and 8C4D2 were generated by immunizing Balb/c mice with AβpE3-38 peptide. Hybridoma supernatants were screened by ELISA, and clones showing specific reactivity with AβpE3-38 and AβpE3-42 but not AβpE1-42 were selected for further characterization. Comparative studies using control antibodies (4G8, W0-2, G2-10, G2-11, NT78, and 2-48) established the unique specificity of 9D5H6 and 8C4D2 for oligomeric AβpE3.

Size-exclusion chromatography followed by dot blot analysis demonstrated that 9D5H6 specifically recognizes low-n oligomers (4-10mers) of AβpE3-42, while showing no reactivity with Aβ1-42 fractions. Western blot analysis under denaturing conditions confirmed detection of low molecular weight AβpE3-42 without cross-reactivity to Aβ1-42. Thioflavin T aggregation assays revealed that 9D5H6 inhibits the formation of higher Aβ aggregates by binding to low-n oligomers.

Immunohistochemical analysis of 5XFAD mice showed age-dependent accumulation of AβpE3, with strong intraneuronal staining appearing by 6 months and increasing dramatically by 12 months. Double-staining experiments demonstrated colocalization with microglia marker Iba-1 but not astrocytic marker GFAP, suggesting microglial uptake of AβpE3 oligomers.

Passive immunization of 5XFAD mice with 9D5H6 significantly reduced Aβ plaque load in hippocampus and cortex, accompanied by stabilization of behavioral performance in elevated plus maze tests. ELISA analysis confirmed reduction of AβpE3 levels in both TBS and SDS fractions of brain lysates from treated animals.

A pilot study using sandwich ELISA with 9D5H6 as capture antibody demonstrated significantly lower levels of AβpE3 oligomers in plasma from AD patients compared to healthy controls. This finding supports the utility of 9D5H6 as a diagnostic tool for Alzheimer's disease.

The examples demonstrate that AβpE3 oligomers represent a novel pathogenic entity in Alzheimer's disease, detectable and targetable by the antibodies of the invention. These findings provide both diagnostic tools and therapeutic strategies for amyloid-related disorders, with particular relevance to Alzheimer's disease.
