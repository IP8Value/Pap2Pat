# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates to cell-based therapeutic treatments for diabetes. More specifically, the invention provides methods for differentiating bone marrow-derived stem cells (BMSCs) into pancreatic precursor cells and insulin-producing β-cells, as well as methods for treating diabetes by administering such differentiated cells or BMSCs genetically modified to express pancreatic developmental factors. The invention further relates to compositions and kits for producing pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells from BMSCs.  

## BACKGROUND  

Understanding β-cell homeostasis and regenerative repair mechanisms is critical for developing effective treatments for diabetes. While islet cell transplantation has demonstrated physiological advantages over insulin administration, this approach faces significant limitations including donor scarcity, immune rejection, and poor long-term graft survival. In adulthood, β-cell mass maintenance occurs through replication of existing β-cells and neogenesis from progenitor cells, though this regenerative capacity is insufficient to compensate for autoimmune destruction or metabolic stress in diabetes.  

Pancreatic progenitor cells residing in ductal epithelium and islets have been identified as potential sources for β-cell regeneration. Notably, nestin, a marker of neuroepithelial stem cells, has been found expressed during pancreas development and in adult islets and pancreatic ducts. Interestingly, suppression of nestin expression in embryonic stem cells inhibits pancreatic differentiation, suggesting its importance in pancreatic development.  

Several markers have been identified that are common to precursor stem cells for both neuronal and islet cell types, indicating a possible shared developmental pathway. Bone marrow-derived stem cells have demonstrated remarkable plasticity, capable of differentiating into neural phenotypes and, more relevantly, reversing hyperglycemic phenotypes in diabetic animal models.  

Current methods of exogenous insulin replacement fail to achieve euglycemia consistently and do not address the underlying β-cell deficiency. There exists a pressing need for cell therapy approaches that can restore functional β-cell mass, either through differentiation of transplanted cells or stimulation of endogenous regeneration. The present invention addresses these unmet needs by providing novel methods for deriving insulin-producing cells from BMSCs and utilizing them to treat diabetes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for differentiating bone marrow-derived stem cells (BMSCs) into pancreatic precursor cells through a multi-step culture process. The method comprises culturing BMSCs in a first culture medium containing chromatin remodeling agents to prepare the cells for differentiation. Subsequently, the cells are cultured in a second culture medium containing induction factors to initiate pancreatic differentiation, followed by culture in a third differentiation medium containing factors that promote pancreatic lineage specification. Finally, the cells are matured in a fourth culture medium containing factors that promote functional maturation into insulin-producing cells.  

In another aspect, the invention provides methods for transfecting BMSCs with viral vectors encoding pancreatic developmental factors such as PDX1 and/or VEGF. These genetically modified BMSCs can be administered to treat diabetes by promoting endogenous β-cell regeneration.  

The invention further includes methods for obtaining BMSCs from bone marrow aspirate and differentiating them into pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing β-cells. These differentiated cells can then be administered to subjects to treat diabetes. In preferred embodiments, the BMSCs are obtained from the subject being treated (autologous transplantation) to avoid immune rejection.  

Additional aspects of the invention include pharmaceutical compositions comprising the differentiated pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells, as well as kits containing the necessary components for producing these cells from BMSCs according to the methods of the invention.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on the discovery that bone marrow-derived stem cells can be directed to differentiate into pancreatic lineage cells through a carefully controlled sequence of epigenetic modification and differentiation signals that recapitulate normal pancreatic development. The culture system of the invention achieves this by first inhibiting DNA methylation and enhancing chromatin remodeling to loosen chromatin structure and permit differentiation. This epigenetic priming is followed by timed exposure to factors that regulate gene expression and modify the timing of pancreatic cell fate determination.  

Key to the invention is the sequential expression of transcription factors that follow normal mammalian pancreatic development, including PDX1, Ngn3, and Pax6. These factors induce the development of both ductal and endocrine pancreatic cell lineages. Retinoic acid (RA) is used at specific stages to induce differentiation, as it is required for normal pancreas development and can control multiple steps in pancreatic cell differentiation.  

The invention promotes generation of PDX1-positive pancreatic progenitor cells through a two-step induction process. In the first step, chromatin remodeling prepares the cells for differentiation. In the second step, PDX1 activation initiates pancreatic differentiation programs that activate both endocrine and non-endocrine pathways. The invention is based on the recognition that BMSCs contain multipotent precursor stem cells that can be epigenetically manipulated to express pancreatic transcription factors and differentiate into mature insulin-producing cells.  

The invention addresses critical unmet needs in diabetes cell therapy by providing methods to induce sustained recovery from diabetes through both de novo β-cell differentiation and endogenous β-cell regeneration. The methods achieve efficient engraftment of VEGF-expressing BMSCs, which support regeneration and functional recovery of endogenous β-cells through mechanisms involving improved intra-islet vascularization and activation of insulin/IGF signaling pathways.  

The induction method of the invention specifies precise components for each culture medium. The second culture medium contains ITS (insulin-transferrin-selenium) and retinoic acid to induce expression of pancreatic transcription factors. The third differentiation medium contains bFGF and EGF to promote expression of endocrine markers. The fourth maturation medium contains nicotinamide to promote functional maturation of insulin-producing cells. Medium changes are performed at specific intervals to optimize differentiation efficiency.  

The invention provides methods for producing β-islet cells and insulin-producing β-cells from whole bone marrow, eliminating the need for prior stem cell purification. Additionally, the invention describes methods for producing VEGF and PDX1 expressing bone marrow stem cells through viral vector transfection. These genetically modified cells can be administered to treat diabetes by promoting endogenous β-cell regeneration.  

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise the differentiated pancreatic precursor cells or insulin-producing cells formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients. The compositions may be formulated for various delivery routes including intravenous, intraportal, or intramuscular administration. The invention also provides kits containing the necessary components for practicing the methods, including culture media, differentiation factors, and instructions for use.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Cell Isolation and Culture  

Adult Fisher rats were used to isolate whole bone marrow, which was cultured to establish nestin-positive sphere-forming cell populations. These cell populations were maintained under defined conditions to preserve their stem cell characteristics while allowing for expansion.  

### Cell Culture and Differentiation  

A complex differentiation protocol was established comprising four basic steps: (1) chromatin remodeling with 5-azacytidine (5-AZA) and trichostatin A (TSA), (2) induction of pancreatic commitment with serum-free medium containing ITS and RA, (3) endocrine differentiation with bFGF and EGF on poly-L-ornithine-coated dishes, and (4) functional maturation with nicotinamide. Control experiments confirmed the necessity of each step for efficient differentiation.  

### Quantitative Real-Time PCR  

Gene expression analysis using qRT-PCR demonstrated sequential activation of pancreatic transcription factors including PDX1, Ngn3, and insulin following the differentiation protocol. Markers of mature β-cells appeared in the final stages of differentiation.  

### Immunocytochemistry  

Differentiated spheroids were fixed, blocked, and stained for pancreatic markers. Immunofluorescence imaging confirmed expression of PDX1 in early stages and insulin in late stages of differentiation. Co-staining for multiple markers demonstrated the progressive acquisition of β-cell characteristics.  

### Example 5  

Dithizone (DTZ) staining was used to identify insulin-producing cells. A DTZ stock solution was prepared and working solution was applied to differentiated cultures. After incubation and washing, distinct red-stained islet-like clusters were observed, indicating the presence of insulin-producing cells.  

An insulin assay was performed to assess functionality. Differentiated spheroids were incubated in DMEM, then preincubated in KRB buffer before glucose stimulation. The buffer was collected and insulin content measured by ELISA, demonstrating glucose-responsive insulin secretion.  

### Example 8  

Cells were exposed to 5-AZA and TSA for chromatin remodeling, then cultured in serum-free medium with ITS and RA to induce PDX1 expression. PDX1 expression was confirmed by immunohistochemistry and qRT-PCR. Subsequent culture with bFGF and EGF induced expression of endocrine markers, analyzed by qRT-PCR. The final differentiation step with nicotinamide produced cells expressing mature β-cell markers, as shown by immunofluorescent staining.  

Glucose-induced insulin secretion was tested, with insulin measured by ELISA. The differentiated cells showed appropriate glucose responsiveness, confirming their functionality. Images were captured by fluorescent microscope and confocal microscopy for detailed analysis.  

RNA was extracted from pancreatic tissues and converted to cDNA for quantitative gene expression analysis using PCR arrays focused on insulin signaling pathways. Data were analyzed using specialized online tools to identify significant changes in gene expression patterns.  

Genomic DNA was extracted from mouse organs and human BMSCs for real-time PCR analysis. Human-specific primers targeting β-actin were used to detect engraftment of human cells in mouse tissues. Statistical analysis was performed with appropriate tests including Student's t-test, ANOVA, and Kaplan-Meier survival analysis.  

Human BMSCs were characterized and used to treat STZ-induced diabetic NOD/SCID mice. Injection of hBMSCs-VEGF into the circulation of diabetic mice resulted in reversion of hyperglycemia. Histological examination showed improved pancreatic islet morphology, with human cells engrafted in the pancreas.  

Similar experiments with hBMSCs-PDX1 showed transient improvement in hyperglycemia, with some differentiation into insulin-producing cells but less vascular engraftment compared to VEGF-expressing cells. Control experiments with unmodified hBMSCs showed no significant therapeutic effect.  

Analysis of serum insulin levels demonstrated that sustained recovery was associated with increased mouse insulin, suggesting endogenous β-cell regeneration rather than replacement by transplanted cells. PCR array data showed activation of insulin/IGF signaling pathways in rescued mice, with upregulation of genes involved in β-cell function and survival.  

Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed increased expression of AKT, PDX1, and decreased apoptosis markers in pancreatic islets of mice treated with hBMSCs-VEGF, supporting the mechanism of enhanced β-cell survival and proliferation through activation of insulin signaling pathways.
