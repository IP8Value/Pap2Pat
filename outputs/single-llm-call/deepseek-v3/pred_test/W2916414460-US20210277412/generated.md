# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) represents a significant pathogen within the genus Circovirus, family Circoviridae, being among the smallest known autonomously replicating DNA viruses capable of infecting mammalian hosts. The viral particle comprises a circular single-stranded DNA genome approximately 2 kilobases in length, encapsulated within a non-enveloped icosahedral capsid structure measuring 17 nm in diameter and composed of a single coat protein subunit. First identified in 1991 from Canadian piglets exhibiting postweaning multisystemic wasting syndrome (PMWS), PCV-2 has since been recognized as the primary causative agent behind this condition, which remains the most economically devastating disease affecting global swine production. Subsequent research has established PCV-2 as an etiological agent for multiple additional syndromes collectively termed porcine circovirus associated diseases (PCAD).  

Current vaccination strategies against PCV-2, while effective, present several limitations that necessitate development of improved vaccine platforms. Five commercial vaccines are presently available, comprising two whole-inactivated virus formulations and three subunit-based products. All existing vaccines utilize the ORF2-encoded capsid protein as the immunogenic component, as this structural protein contains the principal neutralizing epitopes capable of inducing protective humoral immunity. While these vaccines demonstrate efficacy in controlling clinical disease, they rely on production systems that impose significant manufacturing constraints. Insect cell, bacterial, and yeast expression platforms for recombinant capsid protein production require expensive growth media, specialized bioreactor equipment, and carry inherent risks of contamination with animal pathogens.  

Virus-like particles (VLPs) represent an attractive alternative vaccine platform that combines the immunogenic advantages of whole virus particles with the safety profile of subunit vaccines. The PCV-2 capsid protein possesses inherent self-assembly properties allowing formation of VLPs that are morphologically indistinguishable from native virions. However, current VLP production methods continue to rely on traditional expression systems that impose substantial cost and scalability limitations. Pseudovirions (PsVs), comprising VLPs packaged with heterologous nucleic acids, offer additional advantages as both vaccine candidates and research tools, particularly for neutralization assays.  

Plant-based transient expression systems present a promising solution to these production challenges. Plant cells provide a eukaryotic environment capable of proper post-translational modifications while eliminating risks of contamination with mammalian pathogens. The upstream requirements for transient expression in plants are substantially reduced compared to traditional bioreactor-based systems, requiring only basic laboratory facilities and greenhouse space. Furthermore, plant production platforms offer significant advantages in scalability and cost-effectiveness for large-scale manufacturing. Despite these benefits, the production of PCV-2 VLPs in plant systems has not previously been demonstrated, representing an unmet need in the field of veterinary vaccinology.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel method for producing porcine circovirus type 2 (PCV-2) pseudovirions (PsVs) in plant cells through transient expression of viral structural components. The method comprises introducing into plant cells a first polynucleotide encoding a PCV capsid polypeptide and a second polynucleotide comprising a replicating vector capable of directing replication and encapsidation of heterologous genetic material. The capsid polypeptide self-assembles into virus-like particles that package the replicating vector or derivatives thereof, forming pseudovirion structures.  

The invention further encompasses the plant-produced PCV pseudovirions per se, comprising an assembled PCV capsid protein encapsidating non-native genetic material. These pseudovirions demonstrate utility both as vaccine candidates and as tools for neutralization assays. The vaccine composition comprises an immunologically effective amount of plant-produced PCV pseudovirions in combination with a pharmaceutically acceptable adjuvant.  

A key aspect of the invention relates to the production method wherein plant cells are co-infiltrated with Agrobacterium tumefaciens strains containing: (i) an expression cassette encoding the PCV-2 capsid protein, and (ii) a replicating vector comprising a viral replication origin and a gene of interest. The replicating vector preferably derives from a geminivirus backbone and includes regulatory elements for rolling circle replication in plant cells. The method yields high quantities of properly assembled pseudovirions that can be purified through density gradient centrifugation techniques.  

The invention further provides a neutralization assay utilizing the plant-produced PCV pseudovirions. The assay method comprises combining the pseudovirions with a biological sample suspected of containing PCV-specific antibodies, contacting the mixture with susceptible mammalian cells, and assaying for expression of a reporter polypeptide encoded within the pseudovirion genome. Reduction in reporter signal indicates presence of neutralizing antibodies in the test sample.  

Additional embodiments include pseudovirions incorporating heterologous polypeptides within the capsid structure or encapsidating modified genetic elements. The viral replication regulatory proteins Rep and RepA from bean yellow dwarf virus represent particularly useful components for facilitating replication of the vector system in plant cells. The invention demonstrates that plant production systems can generate PCV pseudovirions with proper morphology and immunogenic properties while offering substantial advantages in production scalability and cost-effectiveness compared to traditional expression platforms.

The term "pseudovirion" (PsV) refers to a virus-like particle that packages non-native genetic material. Pseudovirions typically comprise viral structural proteins that self-assemble into capsid structures resembling native virions but contain heterologous nucleic acids rather than the complete viral genome. The term "virus-like particle" (VLP) refers to a capsid structure composed of viral structural proteins that lacks any nucleic acid component.  

The PCV-2 particles produced according to the invention elicit immune responses comparable to natural infection while eliminating risks associated with live virus preparations. The plant-produced pseudovirions present conformational epitopes in their native configuration, enabling induction of neutralizing antibodies against authentic viral particles. This represents a significant advantage over subunit vaccines comprising isolated capsid proteins that may not maintain proper quaternary structure.  

Production of pseudovirions in plants offers multiple advantages over traditional expression systems. Plant cells provide appropriate post-translational modification machinery while eliminating risks of contamination with mammalian pathogens. The transient expression system allows rapid production scaling without need for stable transformation. The invention demonstrates that plant cells can properly assemble PCV-2 capsid proteins into higher-order structures capable of packaging nucleic acid components, representing a significant advance over prior art describing soluble capsid protein expression.  

The method for producing PCV pseudovirions in plant cells comprises introducing into plant cells a first polynucleotide encoding a PCV capsid polypeptide and a second polynucleotide comprising a replicating vector. The first polynucleotide preferably encodes the full-length PCV-2 ORF2 product, though truncated or modified versions retaining self-assembly capability may be employed. The polynucleotide sequence is preferably codon-optimized for expression in the host plant species and may include regulatory elements such as plant-optimized promoters, enhancers, and transcriptional terminators.  

The second polynucleotide comprises a replicating vector capable of directing its own replication in plant cells and subsequent encapsidation into pseudovirions. The vector preferably includes a viral origin of replication, such as that derived from geminiviruses, and regulatory elements for rolling circle replication. The vector further incorporates a gene of interest, which may comprise a reporter gene (e.g., yellow fluorescent protein) for use in neutralization assays or an immunomodulatory gene for vaccine applications.  

The replicating vector may further include sequences encoding viral replication regulatory proteins such as the Rep and RepA proteins from bean yellow dwarf virus. These proteins facilitate replication of the vector through interaction with the viral origin sequence. In alternative embodiments, the replication proteins may be provided in trans via separate expression cassettes.  

The method involves introducing the polynucleotides into plant cells, preferably through Agrobacterium-mediated transient expression. The Agrobacterium strains containing the expression constructs are grown to appropriate density and infiltrated into plant leaves, either through vacuum infiltration or syringe injection. Following an incubation period of typically 3-7 days, the plant tissue is harvested and processed to recover the pseudovirions.  

Purification of pseudovirions from plant tissue involves homogenization of infected leaves in appropriate buffer, clarification by centrifugation, and subsequent density gradient centrifugation. Sucrose gradient centrifugation effectively separates pseudovirions from soluble plant proteins, with the pseudovirions pelleting through the gradient. Further purification may be achieved through cesium chloride density gradient centrifugation, yielding highly pure pseudovirion preparations.  

The plant-produced PCV pseudovirions demonstrate proper morphology when examined by transmission electron microscopy, appearing as icosahedral particles approximately 17 nm in diameter. The particles package the replicating vector or derivatives thereof, as confirmed by PCR analysis of protected nucleic acids following nuclease treatment. The pseudovirions maintain infectivity as demonstrated by delivery of reporter genes to susceptible mammalian cells.  

The invention further provides a pseudovirion neutralization assay utilizing the plant-produced particles. The assay involves incubating pseudovirions with test serum samples, followed by inoculation onto permissive cell lines. Neutralizing antibodies present in the serum prevent pseudovirion entry and subsequent reporter gene expression, allowing quantitation of neutralizing antibody titers. This assay format provides significant advantages over traditional neutralization tests requiring live virus cultivation.  

### Example 1  

A PCV-2 genome-like plant expression vector was designed incorporating elements from bean yellow dwarf virus. The vector module included replication elements comprising the viral origin and a cassette for expression of the yellow fluorescent protein (YFP) reporter gene. The bean yellow dwarf virus Rep/RepA genes were cloned into a separate expression cassette to provide replication proteins in trans. The entire replicating module was subsequently cloned into the pEAQ-HT expression vector for high-level transient expression in plants.  

Competent Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain LBA4404 was transformed with the DNA constructs through electroporation. Transformants were selected on kanamycin-containing media and screened by colony PCR to confirm presence of the expression cassettes. Positive clones were inoculated into liquid LB broth containing appropriate antibiotics and grown to logarithmic phase for plant infiltration.  

Nicotiana benthamiana plants at 6-8 weeks of age were vacuum infiltrated with the Agrobacterium suspensions at optical density 600 nm of 1.0. Infiltrated plants were maintained under controlled environmental conditions with 16-hour light periods for 4 days post infiltration before harvest.  

Harvested leaf tissue was homogenized in extraction buffer (150 mM NaCl, 1 mM CaCl2, 0.001% Triton X-100, 0.25 mM L-Arginine, 10% glycerol, 10 mM Tris/HCl pH 6.5) supplemented with protease inhibitors. The homogenate was clarified by centrifugation at 8000 × g for 20 minutes, followed by filtration through Miracloth. The clarified supernatant was subjected to sucrose density gradient centrifugation by underlaying with 45% and 65% sucrose solutions in DB150 buffer and centrifuging at 120,000 × g for 4 hours at 4°C.  

Gradient fractions were collected and analyzed by SDS-PAGE and immunoblotting using anti-PCV-2 capsid protein antibodies. The pseudovirions pelleted through the gradient and were resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline. Further purification was achieved through cesium chloride density gradient centrifugation, yielding a distinct band at density 1.564 g/cm3 containing assembled pseudovirions.  

Transmission electron microscopy of purified fractions revealed icosahedral particles approximately 17 nm in diameter, consistent with native PCV-2 virions. Negative staining showed properly assembled capsid structures with minimal irregular particles. The particle size distribution was determined through image analysis of multiple micrographs, showing a mean diameter of 16 nm.  

The packaged nucleic acid component was analyzed by rolling circle amplification of protected DNA following nuclease treatment. Restriction digestion patterns confirmed the presence of the expected replicating vector sequences. Quantitative PCR demonstrated high copy numbers of the packaged DNA, indicating efficient encapsidation.  

The functionality of pseudovirions was tested through inoculation of mammalian cell cultures. Cells transfected with plant-produced pseudovirions showed strong YFP expression, confirming delivery of the packaged genetic material. The reporter signal was effectively neutralized by pre-incubation with PCV-2-specific antisera, demonstrating utility for neutralization assays.  

### Discussion  

The geminivirus-based vector system enabled high-level replication of the expression cassette in plant cells through rolling circle replication. This replication mechanism generated sufficient copies of the vector for efficient encapsidation into pseudovirions. The bean yellow dwarf virus Rep and RepA proteins provided in trans facilitated proper replication initiation and termination.  

Molecular analysis confirmed formation of the expected amplicons through rolling circle replication in plant cells. The YFP reporter gene inserted into the expression cassette maintained functionality throughout the replication and encapsidation process, as demonstrated by fluorescence in mammalian cells following pseudovirion infection.  

Co-infiltration of plants with the PCV-2 capsid protein gene and the replicating vector resulted in efficient formation of virus-like particles that packaged the replicating DNA. The self-assembly properties of the capsid protein were maintained in the plant expression system, generating particles morphologically similar to native PCV-2 virions.  

The plant-produced pseudovirions demonstrated proper infectivity in mammalian cell cultures, delivering the packaged reporter gene with high efficiency. This infectivity could be specifically neutralized by anti-PCV-2 antibodies, confirming maintenance of authentic viral entry mechanisms. The system therefore provides a safe and effective platform for PCV-2 neutralization assays without requiring handling of live virus.  

The invention demonstrates that plant expression systems can produce complex viral structures like pseudovirions with proper morphology and functionality. The methods described herein provide significant advantages in safety, scalability and cost-effectiveness compared to traditional pseudovirion production platforms. The plant-produced PCV-2 pseudovirions have direct applications in vaccine development and serological testing for porcine circovirus associated diseases.
