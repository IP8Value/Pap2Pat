## BACKGROUND

The field of artificial intelligence has long sought to develop machines capable of human-like thought processes. This pursuit traces back to Alan Turing, who in 1950 proposed what became known as the Turing Test as a measure of machine intelligence. Turing suggested that a machine could be considered intelligent if its conversational behavior was indistinguishable from that of a human. While this behavioral test provided an operational definition of intelligence, it did not address the fundamental differences between machine cognition and human cognition.

Modern computing devices demonstrate machine-like intelligence characterized by objective knowledge processing, but lack the subjective, self-referential knowledge that defines human cognition. Human-like artificial intelligence requires six fundamental capabilities that distinguish it from machine intelligence. First, the system must relate, correlate, prioritize and remember sensory input data in a manner analogous to human cognitive processes. Second, it requires a self-location and identification coordinate frame that provides proprioceptive self-knowledge comparable to human spatial awareness. Third, the system must demonstrate experiential intelligence, learning from and remembering experiences over time. Fourth, all sensory data must be contextualized relative to the system's self-knowledge framework. Fifth, human-like intelligence can only emerge from human-like sensors that provide equivalent sensory inputs to those experienced by humans. Sixth, the robotic embodiment must physically resemble human form and movement capabilities to properly interact with human environments.

The present invention discloses a Relational Robotic Controller (RRC) system that satisfies these six requirements through an integrated architecture combining humanoid robotic hardware with specialized cognitive processing modules. The RRC serves as the central hub of intelligence for humanoid robots, providing capabilities for identification, recognition, visualization and comprehension of sensory data. Unlike conventional AI systems that process data objectively, the RRC internalizes all sensory input relative to its programmed self-knowledge framework, achieving truly subjective, human-like artificial intelligence.

Key innovations of the RRC system include its Nodal Map Module that establishes the robotic self-knowledge coordinate frame, its Task Selector Modules that implement experiential learning through behavioral programming, and its interface circuits that transform raw sensor data into formats compatible with human-like cognitive processing. The system integrates visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory and gustatory sensors in a humanoid robotic platform, enabling comprehensive environmental interaction comparable to human experience.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The Relational Robotic Controller (RRC) represents a breakthrough in artificial intelligence by enabling programming of human-like intelligence levels through its unique modular architecture. The RRC operates by relating and correlating all input sensory data both with other sensory inputs and with output control signals generated by the system. When sensory signals are related to the self-location and identification Nodal Map Module, they become "internalized" to the robotic self, enabling contextual knowledge formation analogous to human subjective experience.

The primary Nodal Map Module serves as the RRC's central hub of intelligence, maintaining the proprioceptive self-knowledge coordinate frame essential for human-like cognition. RRC-recording monitors provide human-like sensory input, while specialized interface circuits transform this raw data into formats compatible with RRC processing. The human-like interface requirements ensure sensory data preserves the essential characteristics of human perceptual experience.

Mechanical human-like sensors include tactile arrays simulating skin mechanoreceptors, binocular camera systems emulating human vision, microphone arrays matching auditory sensitivity, and chemical sensors for olfactory and gustatory perception. Vestibular sensors provide balance and spatial orientation data. Each sensory modality undergoes specific interface processing to extract collective modalities that form the basis for human-like perception.

Internalization of sensory data represents a critical innovation, enabling the system to relate all perceptions to its self-knowledge framework. The search engine mode of operation allows systematic exploration of sensory input patterns within the context of accumulated experience. Visual and tactile search engines operate similarly, identifying Task Initiating Triggers that activate appropriate responses from the Hierarchical Task Diagram repertoire.

The Hierarchical Task Diagram (HTD) organizes the robot's behavioral capabilities into nested task structures. Task Selector Modules (TSMs) associated with each HTD level recognize patterns and initiate appropriate responses. The Nodal Map Module maintains spatial relationships while Sequence Stepper Modules coordinate complex action sequences. Specialized circuits perform identification, recognition and comprehension of three-dimensional photometric images, enabling human-like visual perception.

Procedural memory systems within the RRC support skill acquisition and motor control, while declarative memory systems enable linguistic and conceptual knowledge. Learning occurs through behavioral programming techniques that reinforce successful task completion, creating experiential knowledge analogous to human learning processes.

### Section B—the Optical Apparatus Stage (Steps 1 and 2)

The optical apparatus stage begins the process of three-dimensional photometric image formation. Step 1 establishes image planes located at each convergence point along the midline line of sight (MLOS). These image planes serve as reference structures for binocular visual data integration. Step 2 obtains image pixel signals from left and right camera CCD arrays, extracting raw visual data for subsequent processing.

Image plane correspondence and alignment are critical for accurate depth perception. The system maintains precise geometric relationships between corresponding points on left and right image planes, enabling calculation of binocular disparity cues. This stage outputs aligned stereoscopic image data ready for interface circuit processing.

### Section C—the Interface Circuit Stage (Steps 3, 4, 5, 6)

The interface circuit stage transforms raw stereoscopic image data into formats supporting human-like visual perception. Step 3 deconstructs image planes into separate CCD array data streams for specialized processing. Step 4 further decomposes each data stream into collective modalities representing distinct visual information channels, such as color, luminance, and motion detection.

These collective modalities are carefully designed to parallel human visual processing pathways. Bimodal CCD arrays separate color and luminance information, while additional modalities extract high-resolution spatial data and transient response characteristics. The system combines advantages of different modalities while mitigating their individual limitations.

Step 5 forms the Cyclopean eye by projecting collective modalities onto a single overlay while maintaining binocular correspondence. This integrated representation combines the strengths of multiple processing pathways. Step 6 calibrates and projects the three-dimensional photometric image onto the robot's coordinate frame, using convergence angle calculations to determine depth relationships.

The interface circuit applies multiple visual cues to resolve depth ambiguity, including binocular disparity, relative size, texture gradient, and motion parallax. A constraint satisfaction process integrates these cues to generate a coherent three-dimensional representation. The resulting photometric image within the coordinate frame provides the foundation for human-like visual perception and comprehension.

### Section D—the RRC Processing Stage: Training the System to Respond to the 3D-Photometric Image (Steps 7 and 8)

The RRC processing stage develops the robot's ability to interpret and respond to photometric images through behavioral programming. Step 7 generates recognizable object representations within the coordinate frame, applying environmental constraints to refine identification. Objects are categorized by functional significance and prioritized based on spatial relationships and potential interactions.

Step 8 implements task programming in response to recognized images. The robot learns to associate specific actions with visual patterns through supervised training, developing appropriate responses to objects and situations. Head and body orientation behaviors are programmed to maintain optimal visual field coverage, with convergence adjustment spanning near to far focus distances.

Task Selector Modules are trained to perform pattern recognition and identification, prioritizing Task Initiating Triggers based on situational relevance. This stage culminates in human-like visual perception capabilities, where the robot demonstrates appropriate behavioral responses to three-dimensional visual scenes.
