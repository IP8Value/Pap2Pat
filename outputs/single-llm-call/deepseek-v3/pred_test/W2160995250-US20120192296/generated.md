## DESCRIPTION  

### FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of ectodermal dysplasias, a group of genetic disorders affecting the development of ectodermal tissues including skin, hair, teeth, and sweat glands. More specifically, the invention provides novel agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies capable of activating the EDAR signaling pathway, along with methods for their production and therapeutic applications in treating X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED).  

The invention particularly relates to monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to and agonize the ectodysplasin A receptor (EDAR), thereby compensating for deficiencies in the natural ligand ectodysplasin A (EDA). These antibodies demonstrate cross-species reactivity and therapeutic efficacy in animal models of XLHED, including correction of dental abnormalities, sweat gland formation, and hair follicle development.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Ectodermal dysplasias comprise a heterogeneous group of genetic disorders characterized by abnormal development of two or more ectodermal structures, including hair, teeth, nails, sweat glands, and other epidermal appendages. Among these, X-linked hypohidrotic ectodermal dysplasia (XLHED) represents the most common form, caused by mutations in the EDA gene encoding ectodysplasin A. XLHED manifests clinically through triad of symptoms: hypotrichosis (sparse hair), hypodontia (missing teeth), and hypohidrosis (reduced sweating capacity).  

The molecular basis of XLHED involves defects in the ectodysplasin signaling pathway. EDA exists in two splice variants, EDA1 and EDA2, which bind respectively to EDAR and XEDAR receptors. EDA1, the longer isoform containing a collagen-like domain, signals through EDAR to activate NF-κB and other downstream pathways critical for ectodermal development. Studies in Tabby mice (Eda mutant mice) have demonstrated that recombinant EDA1 protein can rescue developmental defects when administered during critical gestational periods.  

Prior art includes U.S. Pat. No. 6,355,782 and U.S. Pat. No. 7,115,555 describing DNA and amino acid sequences related to EDA/EDAR signaling, and US 2003/0023991 disclosing therapeutic applications of EDA proteins. Commercial polyclonal anti-EDAR antibodies have shown limitations in specificity and agonist activity. The CH11 monoclonal antibody against Fas receptor and APO-1 monoclonal antibody have demonstrated that certain antibody isotypes can exhibit agonist activity, but similar approaches have not been successfully applied to EDAR signaling.  

Significant challenges remain in developing effective XLHED treatments. EDA1-dependent biological assays are technically difficult, and existing EDAR antibodies lack consistent agonist activity. There exists a clear need for stable, potent EDAR agonists that can compensate for EDA deficiency and restore normal ectodermal development. The present invention addresses this unmet need by providing monoclonal antibodies that specifically activate EDAR signaling with high potency and cross-species reactivity.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method for producing agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies comprising: a) producing an EDAR antigen or antigenic fragment thereof; b) immunizing EDAR-deficient mice with said antigen; c) detecting anti-EDAR antibodies in serum of immunized mice; d) producing hybridomas from antibody-producing cells; e) identifying hybridomas producing agonist anti-EDAR antibodies; f) selecting hybridoma lines based on agonist activity; g) cloning and sub-cloning selected hybridoma lines; and h) purifying agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies from selected clones.  

The invention further provides isolated agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies produced by this method, wherein said antibodies specifically bind to EDAR and activate EDAR signaling pathways. These antibodies demonstrate cross-reactivity with both human and mouse EDAR proteins and exhibit therapeutic effects in animal models of XLHED.  

Pharmaceutical compositions containing the agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies are provided for treatment of ectodermal dysplasias. The compositions may be administered to subjects in need thereof to promote development of ectodermal structures including hair follicles, sweat glands, and teeth. The antibodies show particular utility in treating XLHED when administered during critical developmental windows, either prenatally or neonatally.

An "agonist" activates receptor signaling. A "monoclonal antibody" derives from a single clone. "Antigen-binding portion" includes Fab, F(ab')2, scFv and other fragments retaining binding specificity.  

The method for producing agonist anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies comprises:  

a) Producing EDAR antigen comprising at least one cysteine-rich domain (CRD) of EDAR extracellular domain, preferably as an EDAR-Fc fusion protein;  

b) Immunizing EDAR-deficient mice, preferably OVE1B strain, with the antigen in suitable adjuvant;  

c) Detecting serum anti-EDAR antibodies by ELISA using EDAR-coated plates;  

d) Fusing antibody-producing cells with myeloma cells to generate hybridomas;  

e) Screening hybridoma supernatants for EDAR binding and agonist activity;  

f) Selecting clones demonstrating both EDAR binding and agonist activity;  

g) Sub-cloning positive hybridomas by limiting dilution;  

h) Purifying monoclonal antibodies from selected clones using protein G affinity chromatography.  

Preferred embodiments utilize human EDAR-Fc or mouse EDAR-Fc fusion proteins as immunogens. Agonist activity may be assessed by in vitro apoptosis assays using EDAR-Fas reporter cells or by in vivo tail hair formation assays in Tabby mice.  

The antibodies demonstrate unexpected properties including:  

1) Cross-species reactivity with human, mouse, dog, rat and chicken EDAR;  
2) Recognition of conformational epitopes involving multiple CRDs;  
3) Divalent-dependent agonist activity requiring F(ab')2 but not Fab fragments;  
4) Therapeutic efficacy in both prenatal and postnatal treatment windows;  
5) Long serum half-life (10-11 days in mice) enabling sustained effects.  

Antibody sequences may be modified through isotype switching, humanization, or affinity maturation while retaining agonist properties. CDR regions may be grafted into alternative scaffold proteins to create non-antibody EDAR agonists.  

Pharmaceutical compositions contain the antibodies in physiologically acceptable carriers, optionally with stabilizers, buffers, or adjuvants. Preferred administration routes include intravenous, subcutaneous, and intraperitoneal injection. Dosage regimens are tailored to patient age and developmental stage, with typical doses ranging from 0.1-10 mg/kg.  

The antibodies find use in:  

1) Treating XLHED by promoting ectodermal development;  
2) Modifying hair morphology in cosmetic applications;  
3) Veterinary applications for ectodermal disorders;  
4) Research tools for studying EDAR signaling.  

### EXAMPLES  

#### Example 1  

An EDAR-Fc expression vector was constructed encoding the extracellular domain of human EDAR fused to human IgG1 Fc. The vector was transfected into 293 cells for stable expression, and EDAR-Fc protein was purified from serum-free culture supernatants.  

EDAR-deficient OVE1B mice were immunized subcutaneously with 150 μg EDAR-Fc in PBS/STIMUNE adjuvant. Mice showing serum anti-EDAR reactivity by ELISA received booster immunizations at days 10-14 and 40. Three days after the final boost, lymph node cells were fused with myeloma cells and cultured in HAT selection medium.  

Hybridoma supernatants were screened by ELISA for EDAR binding. Positive clones were sub-cloned by limiting dilution and adapted to serum-free medium. Monoclonal antibodies were purified by protein G affinity chromatography and characterized by SDS-PAGE.  

Fab fragments were generated by ficin digestion and tested for EDAR binding affinity using surface plasmon resonance. EDAR-Fas-expressing Jurkat cells were used to assess agonist activity through apoptosis induction.  

Newborn Tabby mice were injected intraperitoneally with hybridoma supernatants or purified antibodies. Tail hair formation was assessed at 3-6 weeks post-injection using standardized scoring criteria.  

#### Example 2  

Nine monoclonal antibodies (mAbEDAR1-9) demonstrated binding to both human and mouse EDAR by ELISA and flow cytometry. All antibodies recognized EDAR-Fc fusion proteins but not control proteins, confirming specificity.  

#### Example 3  

Epitope mapping using EDAR-Fc truncation mutants revealed three distinct binding patterns:  

1) Antibodies recognizing CRD1 alone  
2) Antibodies requiring both CRD1 and CRD2  
3) One antibody requiring full extracellular domain (CRD1-3)  

#### Example 4  

Competition ELISA demonstrated that anti-EDAR monoclonal antibodies bind distinct epitopes from commercial polyclonal anti-EDAR antibodies (APO200), indicating novel binding sites.  

#### Example 5  

Polyclonal anti-EDAR antibodies failed to induce apoptosis in EDAR-Fas reporter cells or tail hair formation in Tabby mice, confirming the unique agonist properties of the monoclonal antibodies.  

#### Example 6  

Pharmacokinetic studies in Tabby mice showed serum half-lives of 10.5-11 days for lead antibodies, significantly longer than recombinant EDA1 protein.  

#### Example 7  

Dose-response studies established EC50 values of 0.1-0.7 mg/kg for tail hair induction, comparable to recombinant Fc-EDA1. Higher doses (10 mg/kg) restored sweat gland function.  

#### Example 8  

Pregnant Tabby mice treated with anti-EDAR antibody at gestational days 13 and 20 produced offspring with:  

1) Normal tail hair density  
2) Functional sweat glands  
3) Corrected molar tooth morphology  
4) Normalized tracheal gland development  

#### Example 9  

Neonatal wild-type mice treated with anti-EDAR antibody exhibited transient hair morphology changes including:  

1) Ruffled, greasy fur appearance  
2) Thinning of zig-zag and awl hairs  
3) Increased hair pigmentation  

These effects resolved spontaneously by 8 weeks post-treatment, demonstrating reversible modulation of hair follicle development.
