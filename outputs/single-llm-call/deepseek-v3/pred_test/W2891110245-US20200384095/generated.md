# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of vaccinology and immunology, specifically to a novel vaccine consortium comprising bivalent outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) derived from Salmonella enterica serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A. The invention provides a composition capable of inducing broad-spectrum protective immunity against both typhoid and paratyphoid fever through simultaneous activation of humoral and cell-mediated immune responses. This vaccine formulation represents a significant advancement over existing monovalent vaccines by providing cross-protection against multiple serovars of enteric fever-causing pathogens while maintaining cost-effectiveness suitable for global distribution.  

## BACKGROUND AND PRIOR ART OF THE INVENTION  

Enteric fever remains a substantial global health burden, with Salmonella Typhi causing approximately 21.7 million infections and 200,000 deaths annually. Recent epidemiological trends indicate an alarming rise in Salmonella Paratyphi A infections, particularly in Asia and among travelers from endemic regions. The changing disease patterns suggest that Paratyphi A may soon become the predominant cause of enteric fever morbidity and mortality. Both pathogens share similar transmission routes through contaminated food and water, invading intestinal epithelial cells before disseminating to systemic organs.  

Current licensed vaccines against typhoid fever include the live-attenuated oral Ty21a vaccine, Vi polysaccharide vaccine, and more recently, a tetanus toxoid-conjugated Vi vaccine. However, these vaccines demonstrate critical limitations: the Vi polysaccharide vaccine is ineffective against Paratyphi A infections due to the absence of Vi antigen in these strains, while the oral Ty21a vaccine provides only partial cross-protection. This incomplete protection spectrum creates an urgent need for a bivalent vaccine capable of protecting against both major etiological agents of enteric fever.  

Outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) have emerged as promising next-generation acellular vaccine candidates. These naturally secreted nanoparticles contain pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) including lipopolysaccharides (LPS), outer membrane proteins, and other immunostimulatory components that activate both innate and adaptive immunity without requiring additional adjuvants. The only currently licensed OMV-based vaccine (MeNZB against Neisseria meningitidis serogroup B) has demonstrated approximately 80% protective efficacy, validating the OMV platform. However, no OMV-based vaccine exists for enteric fever pathogens.  

The present invention addresses these unmet needs by developing a bivalent OMV formulation combining vesicles from both S. Typhi and S. Paratyphi A. This approach overcomes the limitations of current vaccines by providing: (1) broad-spectrum protection against both pathogens, (2) natural adjuvanticity through inherent PAMPs, (3) activation of both mucosal and systemic immunity, and (4) cost-effective production suitable for global distribution. The vaccine induces long-term protection through simultaneous stimulation of humoral responses (anti-LPS, anti-Vi, and anti-outer membrane protein antibodies) and cell-mediated immunity (Th1 and Th17 responses), providing comprehensive defense against both intestinal invasion and systemic infection.  

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION  

The primary objective of this invention is to provide a bivalent OMV-based vaccine consortium capable of inducing protective immunity against both Salmonella Typhi and Salmonella Paratyphi A infections. This formulation aims to overcome the limitations of current monovalent vaccines by generating cross-protective responses through inclusion of antigenic components from both pathogens.  

Additional objectives include:  
1. Development of an OMV preparation method that preserves immunogenic components while ensuring safety through removal of viable bacteria  
2. Creation of a vaccine formulation that activates both mucosal and systemic immune compartments  
3. Induction of long-term immunity through simultaneous stimulation of humoral and cell-mediated responses  
4. Provision of a cost-effective production process suitable for large-scale manufacturing  
5. Demonstration of protective efficacy against heterologous challenge strains  
6. Establishment of immune correlates of protection including antibody profiles and cytokine responses  
7. Optimization of immunization schedules and dosage regimens for maximal protection  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel bivalent vaccine formulation comprising outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) isolated from Salmonella Typhi and Salmonella Paratyphi A in a 1:1 ratio. This composition represents a significant advancement over existing enteric fever vaccines by simultaneously targeting both major causative pathogens through a single immunization regimen.  

The OMVs are naturally secreted nanoparticles ranging from 130-300 nm in diameter, containing immunogenic components including LPS, outer membrane proteins, periplasmic proteins, and other pathogen-associated molecular patterns. The vaccine formulation preserves these native antigenic structures while ensuring safety through rigorous purification processes that remove viable bacteria. Each OMV batch contains approximately 400-440 μg/ml of protein and 250 μg/ml of O-antigen, with protein profiles spanning 20-90 kDa as determined by SDS-PAGE analysis.  

Key advantages of this bivalent formulation include:  
1. **Broad antigenic coverage**: The combination of Typhi and Paratyphi A OMVs presents a comprehensive array of protective antigens, including Vi polysaccharide (Typhi-specific) and shared LPS epitopes, ensuring protection against both pathogens.  
2. **Dual immune activation**: The formulation simultaneously stimulates humoral immunity (IgG, IgA, IgM antibodies against OMPs, LPS, and Vi antigen) and cell-mediated immunity (Th1 and Th17 responses), providing comprehensive protection against both extracellular and intracellular infection stages.  
3. **Built-in adjuvanticity**: The OMVs' natural composition includes multiple PAMPs that activate Toll-like receptors and other pattern recognition receptors, eliminating the need for external adjuvants.  
4. **Mucosal protection**: Oral immunization induces secretory IgA responses that prevent intestinal invasion, complementing systemic IgG responses that protect against disseminated infection.  
5. **Cost-effective production**: The natural secretion of OMVs during bacterial growth enables scalable manufacturing without complex purification or conjugation steps required for polysaccharide vaccines.  

Immunization with this bivalent formulation induces robust antibody responses against multiple antigenic targets, including:  
- Vi polysaccharide (Typhi-specific)  
- O-antigen components of LPS (shared and serovar-specific epitopes)  
- Outer membrane proteins (OmpC, OmpF, and other immunodominant proteins)  
- Periplasmic and membrane-associated proteins  

The vaccine demonstrates particular strength in generating IgG3-dominant responses, indicative of Th1-biased immunity crucial for intracellular pathogen clearance. This contrasts with conventional polysaccharide vaccines that typically induce Th2-skewed IgG1 responses.  

Protective efficacy studies in murine models demonstrate 80-90% survival against lethal challenge with heterologous strains, compared to 100% mortality in non-immunized controls. The vaccine significantly reduces bacterial burden in systemic organs (spleen and liver) and prevents intestinal colonization, demonstrating comprehensive protection against both invasion and dissemination.  

### Bacterial Strains and Culture Conditions  

The vaccine utilizes OMVs derived from clinically relevant strains of Salmonella Typhi (C-6953) and Salmonella Paratyphi A (C-6915), authenticated through PCR-based genotyping and serological characterization. Strains are maintained in 20% glycerol stocks at -80°C and cultured in Tryptic Soy Broth at 37°C with shaking (100 rpm) to exponential phase (OD600 ≈ 0.8) for OMV production. Culture conditions are optimized for maximal OMV secretion while maintaining antigenic integrity.  

### Preparation of OMVs  

OMVs are purified through a multi-step process:  
1. Exponential-phase cultures are centrifuged (8,000 rpm, 40 min, 4°C) to remove bacterial cells  
2. Supernatants are filtered through 0.22 μm membranes to eliminate residual bacteria  
3. OMVs are pelleted by ultracentrifugation (140,000 × g, 4 h, 4°C)  
4. Vesicles are resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4)  
5. Protein and LPS content are quantified by modified Lowry and Dubois methods respectively  

This process yields OMVs with consistent protein (400-440 μg/ml) and O-antigen (250 μg/ml) content across production batches. Electron microscopy confirms vesicle integrity and size distribution (130-300 nm).  

### Negative Staining of OMVs and OMV-Secreting Bacteria  

OMV morphology and bacterial secretion are verified by negative stain electron microscopy:  
1. Samples are adsorbed onto carbon-coated grids for 1 minute  
2. Excess fluid is blotted and replaced with 2% uranyl acetate stain  
3. Grids are examined under transmission electron microscope (80 kV)  

This confirms vesicle formation and absence of contaminating bacterial cells in the final product.  

### Animals  

Vaccine immunogenicity and efficacy are evaluated in 7-week-old female BALB/c mice maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions. Animals are housed in temperature-controlled (25°C) facilities with 75% humidity and fed sterile food/water ad libitum. All procedures follow institutional animal care guidelines.  

### Collection of Serum and Stool  

Immune responses are monitored through serial sampling:  
1. Blood is collected from the lateral tail vein at days 0, 14, 21, 28, 35, 78, and 90 post-immunization  
2. Serum is separated by centrifugation (1,000 rpm, 10 min, 4°C)  
3. Stool samples are homogenized in PBS with protease inhibitors and clarified by centrifugation (10,000 × g, 10 min)  

Samples are stored at -20°C until analysis for antibody and cytokine content.  

### SDS-PAGE and Immunoblot  

OMV protein profiles are characterized by:  
1. Separation on 12% SDS-PAGE gels (100 V, 1 h)  
2. Staining with Coomassie or silver stain  
3. Immunoblotting using nitrocellulose membranes  
4. Probing with mouse anti-OMV sera  
5. Detection with alkaline phosphatase or chemiluminescent substrates  

This confirms presence of immunogenic proteins (20-90 kDa) and LPS components.  

### Dot Blot Assay  

LPS immunogenicity is assessed by:  
1. Direct application of LPS samples to nitrocellulose membranes  
2. Blocking with TBS-Tween  
3. Incubation with anti-OMV sera  
4. Detection with anti-mouse IgG-HRP and chemiluminescence  

This demonstrates antibody recognition of O-antigen components.  

### ELISA  

Antibody responses are quantified by:  
1. Coating microtiter wells with OMVs (5 μg/well) or Vi polysaccharide  
2. Blocking with BSA  
3. Incubating with serially diluted serum samples  
4. Detecting bound antibodies with HRP-conjugated anti-mouse immunoglobulins  
5. Measuring absorbance at 492 nm after OPD substrate development  

This quantifies IgG, IgA, IgM and subtypes (IgG1, IgG2a, IgG3) against vaccine antigens.  

### Ex Vivo Studies on Isolated Dendritic Cells  

Cell-mediated immunity is evaluated through:  
1. Isolation of bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs)  
2. Stimulation with bivalent OMVs (10 μg/ml, 24 h)  
3. Measurement of cytokines (IL-6, IL-12p70, IL-23) by ELISA  
4. Co-culture with splenic CD4+ T cells (4:1 ratio)  
5. Assessment of Th1/Th17 cytokines (IFN-γ, IL-17)  

These studies demonstrate induction of protective T cell responses.  

Immunization protocols involve oral administration of 25 μg bivalent OMVs (1:1 ratio) on days 0, 14, and 28. This regimen induces peak antibody responses by day 28-35 that persist for at least 90 days, demonstrating durable immunity.  

Protective efficacy is confirmed through:  
1. Intraperitoneal challenge with lethal doses (1 × 10^8 CFU) of heterologous strains  
2. Monitoring survival for 9 days post-challenge  
3. Quantifying bacterial burden in spleen/liver at 3 days post-infection  
4. Assessing intestinal colonization  

Vaccinated mice show 80-90% survival versus 100% mortality in controls, with 3-4 log reductions in bacterial load.  

Mechanistic studies reveal multiple protective mechanisms:  
1. Serum agglutination of bacteria  
2. Inhibition of bacterial motility and mucin penetration  
3. Enhanced opsonophagocytosis by macrophages  
4. Activation of complement-mediated killing  

These effects are mediated by OMV-induced antibodies against LPS, OMPs, and Vi antigen.
