# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and methods for classifying cancer patients based on molecular profiling of tumor-associated stroma. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for predicting clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients through analysis of stromal gene expression signatures. The invention further relates to prognostic predictors derived from tumor microenvironment analysis and their application in personalized cancer treatment strategies.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Breast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women worldwide, with approximately 26% of patients with early-stage disease developing metastatic recurrence despite current treatment modalities. Existing prognostic indicators, including tumor size, lymph node status, and hormone receptor status, demonstrate limited predictive accuracy for disease recurrence and patient outcomes. While genomic characterization of tumors has advanced significantly through microarray technologies, current prognostic signatures derived from whole tumor tissue analysis fail to account for the critical role of tumor microenvironment in cancer progression.  

Recent advances in cancer biology have revealed that tumor-associated stroma plays an active role in cancer initiation, progression, and metastasis. The stromal compartment, comprising fibroblasts, immune cells, endothelial cells, and extracellular matrix components, undergoes significant molecular alterations during tumorigenesis. However, current diagnostic methods predominantly focus on epithelial tumor cells while neglecting the prognostic information contained within the tumor microenvironment. This oversight represents a significant limitation in cancer prognosis and treatment selection.  

A critical need exists for novel methods that accurately predict clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients by incorporating stromal molecular signatures. Current challenges in breast cancer management include the inability to reliably identify patients who will develop metastatic disease, the lack of biomarkers for monitoring treatment efficacy, and the absence of molecular tools for personalized therapy selection. The present invention addresses these unmet needs by providing a stromal-derived prognostic predictor (SDPP) that significantly improves outcome prediction accuracy when compared to existing clinical and molecular markers.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive system for predicting clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients through analysis of tumor-associated stromal gene expression patterns. The method involves laser capture microdissection (LCM) to isolate pure populations of tumor-associated stroma and matched normal stromal tissue, followed by microarray analysis to identify prognostic gene expression signatures.  

Key aspects of the invention include the development of a multivariate stromal-derived prognostic predictor (SDPP) comprising specific gene sets that are differentially expressed in tumor stroma associated with favorable versus poor clinical outcomes. The SDPP gene sets include pro-angiogenic factors, hypoxia-related genes, and T-cell markers that collectively provide superior prognostic information compared to existing predictors.  

The invention further provides methods for predicting clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients by detecting expression levels of SDPP gene sets. These methods enable determination of prognosis, prediction of disease outcome, identification of tumor subtypes, and assessment of recurrence or metastasis probability. The predictive accuracy of the SDPP is enhanced through integration with other known prognostic signatures and clinical parameters.  

Compositions of the invention include isolated nucleic acids corresponding to SDPP gene sets, polypeptide products of these genes, and binding agents such as antibodies specific for stromal gene products. The invention further provides diagnostic kits and microarrays for detecting SDPP gene expression levels, as well as computer systems and software for implementing the prognostic algorithms.  

Clinical applications of the invention include methods for assigning or optimizing cancer treatment based on SDPP analysis, monitoring treatment efficacy through serial stromal profiling, and identifying patients suitable for clinical trials. The SDPP enables personalized medicine approaches by correlating specific stromal gene expression patterns with response to particular therapeutic modalities.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel breast cancer outcome prediction system based on molecular profiling of tumor-associated stroma. While conventional prognostic methods focus on epithelial tumor cells, the invention recognizes the critical prognostic information contained within the tumor microenvironment. The stroma-derived prognostic predictor (SDPP) of the invention demonstrates superior accuracy in predicting clinical outcomes compared to existing predictors.  

The SDPP is constructed through comprehensive analysis of gene expression patterns in microdissected stromal tissue from breast cancer patients. Laser capture microdissection enables isolation of pure stromal populations free from epithelial contamination, followed by microarray analysis to identify differentially expressed genes associated with clinical outcomes. The SDPP gene sets are selected through rigorous statistical analysis of expression data correlated with patient outcomes.  

Exemplary SDPP gene sets include genes involved in angiogenesis (e.g., VEGF pathway components), hypoxia response (e.g., HIF-1α targets), immune regulation (e.g., T-cell markers), and extracellular matrix remodeling. The expression levels of these gene sets form a reference profile used to classify new patient samples. Class prediction algorithms, including Bayesian classifiers, are trained on known outcome data to generate accurate prognostic models.  

Clinical outcomes predicted by the SDPP include disease-free survival, overall survival, likelihood of metastasis, and probability of recurrence. The invention provides methods for detecting SDPP gene expression levels through various techniques including quantitative PCR, microarray analysis, and protein detection methods. Sample types suitable for analysis include fresh frozen tissue, formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded specimens, and liquid biopsies containing stromal components.  

### Identifying Classes and Genes for Predicting Clinical Outcome  

The invention employs class discovery methods to identify distinct stromal subtypes associated with differential clinical outcomes. Microarray experiments on microdissected stroma reveal the top 200 most variable genes that effectively cluster tumor stroma into biologically significant groups. Cluster significance is assessed through bootstrap analysis, with subsequent correlation of clusters with clinical outcome data.  

Class distinction methods identify genes differentially expressed between stromal subtypes with known clinical outcomes. Pairwise comparisons using linear models for microarray analysis (LIMMA) and significance analysis of microarrays (SAM) yield gene sets with strong prognostic associations. These genes form the basis for constructing multivariate predictors through machine learning approaches.  

Class prediction utilizes the identified gene sets to develop accurate prognostic models. The SDPP incorporates gene weighting based on prognostic strength and employs Bayesian classifiers trained on outcome-annotated samples. Cross-validation demonstrates the predictive accuracy of the SDPP across independent patient cohorts.  

### Cancers  

While initially developed for breast cancer, the SDPP methodology is applicable to various cancer types where tumor stroma influences disease progression. The invention encompasses application of stromal profiling to cancers including but not limited to prostate, lung, colorectal, and pancreatic malignancies. In breast cancer, the SDPP provides prognostic information across all major molecular subtypes (luminal A/B, HER2-enriched, basal-like).

DNA microarrays of the invention comprise probes for comprehensive SDPP gene set analysis. ### Methods of Diagnosis  

The invention provides methods for diagnosing breast cancer subtypes and predicting outcomes based on SDPP gene expression. These methods enable prognosis determination, recurrence prediction, and metastasis risk assessment through stromal profiling. Detection techniques include quantitative PCR, microarray analysis, and protein-based assays.  

The diagnostic methods integrate SDPP analysis with other prognostic factors to enhance prediction accuracy. Combined analysis of stromal and epithelial markers provides comprehensive molecular profiling for personalized cancer management.  

### Methods of Assigning or Selecting Treatment  

The SDPP enables personalized treatment selection based on stromal molecular profiles. The invention provides methods for assigning targeted therapies according to SDPP-predicted outcomes and biological pathways activated in individual patients. Treatment strategies include modulation of Th1/Th2 immune responses, angiogenesis inhibition, and hypoxia-targeting approaches.  

The invention further provides methods for monitoring treatment efficacy through serial SDPP analysis. Changes in stromal gene expression during therapy serve as early indicators of treatment response. Additionally, the SDPP facilitates identification of novel therapeutic agents that modulate prognostic stromal pathways.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

A cohort of 44 patients (34 with invasive ductal carcinoma and 10 reduction mammoplasty controls) was analyzed using laser capture microdissection to isolate pure stromal populations. RNA from microdissected samples underwent T7-based amplification and hybridization to whole genome microarrays.  

Unsupervised clustering of stromal gene expression revealed two significant clusters associated with clinical outcomes. Differentially expressed genes between clusters were identified through LIMMA analysis (FDR <0.01, fold change >1.9). A multivariate predictor was constructed using logistic regression and validated through cross-validation.  

Gene ontology analysis of the poor-outcome cluster revealed enrichment for angiogenesis, hypoxia response, and extracellular matrix remodeling genes. Immunohistochemical validation confirmed elevated expression of endothelial markers (CD31) and matrix metalloproteinases in poor-prognosis stroma. The SDPP demonstrated superior prognostic accuracy compared to standard clinical parameters in multivariate analysis.  

### Example 2  

Integration of the SDPP with existing prognostic signatures was performed using Bayesian classifiers. The combined predictor showed enhanced accuracy in outcome prediction compared to individual signatures. Analysis revealed complementary prognostic information from stromal and epithelial markers, supporting a comprehensive tumor microenvironment approach to cancer prognosis.  

### Example 3  

An independent validation cohort confirmed the prognostic value of the SDPP gene set. Stromal samples processed through identical LCM and microarray protocols demonstrated consistent clustering patterns associated with clinical outcomes. Quantitative RT-PCR validation of selected genes confirmed microarray findings, with excellent correlation between platforms.  

### Example 4  

Analysis of stromal versus epithelial gene expression in matched samples revealed distinct prognostic information from each compartment. The SDPP maintained predictive accuracy in whole tissue samples, demonstrating clinical utility beyond microdissected specimens. Immunohistochemical analysis of protein products confirmed the biological relevance of SDPP gene expression patterns.
