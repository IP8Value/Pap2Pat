# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to a novel method for identifying biomarker genes associated with oocyte competence, particularly through analysis of cumulus cell (CC) gene expression patterns. The invention provides an in vitro method for evaluating oocyte competence by detecting and quantifying specific biomarker gene expression levels in cumulus cells surrounding the oocyte. This method enables improved embryo selection in assisted reproductive technology (ART) procedures, particularly in the context of single embryo transfer (SET). The invention further encompasses predictive models utilizing combinations of biomarker genes, including but not limited to EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3, to assess pregnancy potential with high accuracy.  

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION  

Single embryo transfer has become the preferred treatment in ART to limit multiple pregnancies while maintaining acceptable live birth rates. The critical challenge in SET is selecting the most viable embryo for transfer in the first cycle. Current embryo selection criteria rely primarily on morphological assessment, which has inherent limitations in predictive accuracy. Alternative methods under investigation include quantitative gene expression analysis in cumulus cells, which exhibit close biological interaction with the oocyte during growth and maturation.  

Existing studies have attempted to correlate CC gene expression with various endpoints including embryo development, oocyte nuclear maturity stage, aneuploidy status, and pregnancy outcome. However, these studies have shown limited reproducibility, with few genes consistently identified across different investigations. Factors contributing to this variability include differences in experimental design, endpoints studied, ovarian stimulation protocols, and culture media used across different IVF laboratories.  

The current invention addresses these limitations through a systematic experimental design incorporating both inter-patient and intra-patient analyses. The study validates a previously developed pregnancy prediction model and builds new multiparametric models with improved predictive power. A unique aspect of the invention is the intra-patient comparison of CC from oocytes that resulted in pregnancy after frozen embryo transfer versus their sibling oocytes that did not result in pregnancy in the fresh transfer cycle - an analysis not previously reported in literature.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for detecting biomarker gene expression in cumulus cells as an indicator of oocyte competence. The method utilizes live birth or embryo development as selection endpoints, with pregnancy outcome being the most clinically relevant parameter. The invention employs exon-level analysis of gene expression and incorporates intra-patient based comparisons to enhance predictive accuracy. Statistical significance is determined using p-values <0.05, with more stringent thresholds (p<0.0042) applied after Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons.  

Key embodiments of the invention include methods for detecting individual biomarker genes and combinations thereof. The invention particularly identifies EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3 as significant predictors of pregnancy outcome. The method involves assessing correlation between biomarker gene expression combinations through stepwise multiparametric regression analysis, with overall model p-values determining predictive validity.  

The in vitro method for evaluating oocyte competence comprises determining the level of biomarker gene expression in cumulus cells and evaluating oocyte competence based on the expression level. The method includes normalizing expression levels using housekeeping genes such as beta-2-microglobulin (B2M) and ubiquitin C (UBC). Preferred combinations of biomarkers include various groupings of EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3, with specific embodiments detailed below.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

Oocyte competence refers to the developmental potential of an oocyte to result in a successful pregnancy. Oocytes may be obtained through standard ovarian stimulation protocols followed by transvaginal ultrasound-guided retrieval. Granulosa cells and cumulus cells are defined as the somatic cells surrounding the oocyte, with cumulus cells being specifically those remaining attached after oocyte denudation.  

Marker gene expression is determined through quantitative methods including real-time PCR, with detection methods encompassing RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and amplification using gene-specific primers. Expression levels are normalized to reference genes and may be analyzed for specific splice variants of HSPH1, CAMK1D, NCOA7, SASH1, MROH9, SPTBN5, GALNTL6, and DNAH3.  

The invention specifies numerous combinations of splice variants and gene combinations for optimal prediction of oocyte competence. Predictive models incorporate equations calculating probability of pregnancy based on gene expression levels, with specific models provided for different gene sets.  

Clinical diagnostic kits are described, comprising reagents for mRNA or protein detection of the biomarker genes, along with instructions for use. Applications of the method include identifying women with abnormal oocyte production, assessing fertility treatment efficacy, and selecting oocytes for fertilization and implantation.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

A retrospective study analyzed 47 ICSI patients undergoing single embryo transfer. Cumulus cells were collected from individual oocytes and analyzed for expression of 12 genes. Statistical analysis included inter-patient comparison using two-tailed t-tests and stepwise multiple regression modeling. Results validated previous findings of CAMK1D, EFNB2 and STC1 as predictive genes and identified new predictive combinations with improved accuracy (up to 93% with AUC 0.95).  

### Example 2  

Exon-level microarray analysis was performed to identify predictive genes. The study employed intra-patient comparisons and stepwise regression to build predictive models. An "11 gene list" was identified along with alternative models showing significant predictive value.  

### Example 3  

A retrospective study analyzed cumulus cell gene expression using QPCR. Both one-parametric and multiparametric analyses were performed, demonstrating the superiority of gene combination models. Results were stratified by stimulation protocol (rFSH vs HP-hMG).  

### Example 4  

A prospective study evaluated clinical application of the method. Cumulus cell gene expression scores were calculated and showed significant correlation with pregnancy rates, demonstrating the clinical utility of the invention.
