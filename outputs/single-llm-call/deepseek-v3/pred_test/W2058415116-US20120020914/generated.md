# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of virology and oncology, specifically to compositions and methods for inhibiting human papillomavirus (HPV) transcription and treating HPV-associated diseases. More particularly, the invention provides therapeutic applications of leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) for suppressing HPV oncogene expression in cervical cancer cells and other HPV-transformed cells.  

## BACKGROUND  

Human papillomavirus infection represents a significant global health burden as the causative agent of cervical cancer and other anogenital malignancies. Current treatment modalities for HPV-associated dysplasia and carcinoma remain limited to surgical excision, cytotoxic chemotherapy, and radiation therapy, all of which carry substantial morbidity. While prophylactic vaccines have demonstrated efficacy against certain HPV types, no effective pharmaceutical intervention exists for established HPV infections or HPV-induced neoplasia. The critical role of viral oncoproteins E6 and E7 in maintaining the malignant phenotype of HPV-transformed cells makes these proteins attractive therapeutic targets. The present invention addresses this unmet medical need by identifying LIF as a potent inhibitor of HPV transcription through its action on the viral long control region (LCR).  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides methods and compositions for treating HPV-associated diseases by administering leukemia inhibitory factor (LIF) to inhibit HPV oncogene expression.  

In a first embodiment, the invention provides a method for treating cervical dysplasia comprising administering a therapeutically effective amount of LIF to a patient in need thereof.  

In a second embodiment, the invention provides a method for treating cervical cancer comprising administering LIF in combination with at least one chemotherapeutic agent.  

In a third embodiment, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising LIF and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier for use in inhibiting HPV LCR-driven transcription.  

In a fourth embodiment, the invention provides a method for reducing expression of HPV E6 and E7 oncogenes in transformed cells by contacting said cells with LIF.  

In a fifth embodiment, the invention provides a method for inhibiting proliferation of HPV-transformed cells by administering LIF.  

In a sixth embodiment, the invention provides a method for screening compounds that mimic LIF's inhibitory effect on HPV transcription.  

In a seventh embodiment, the invention provides use of LIF for the manufacture of a medicament for treating HPV-associated diseases.  

In an eighth embodiment, the invention provides a kit comprising LIF and instructions for treating HPV-associated conditions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Introduction to Papillomaviruses and Cervical Cancer  

Papillomaviruses represent a diverse family of small DNA viruses that infect epithelial cells. The association between certain HPV types and cervical cancer has been firmly established through extensive epidemiological and molecular studies. HPV infection typically progresses through stages of dysplasia before developing into invasive carcinoma, with viral oncoproteins E6 and E7 playing essential roles in both initiation and maintenance of the malignant phenotype.  

### Classification of Papillomaviruses  

The Papillomaviridae family comprises multiple genera including alpha, beta, gamma, mu and nu papillomaviruses, classified based on nucleotide sequence identity of the L1 open reading frame. High-risk HPV types, particularly HPV-16 and HPV-18 from the alpha genus, demonstrate strong association with cervical cancer. The HPV genome contains early (E) and late (L) genes regulated by the long control region, which contains binding sites for cellular transcription factors including SP1 and TATA-binding proteins.  

### Papillomaviruses and Cancer  

The molecular pathogenesis of HPV-induced carcinogenesis involves integration of viral DNA into the host genome, leading to constitutive expression of E6 and E7 proteins. These oncoproteins inactivate tumor suppressor proteins p53 and retinoblastoma protein (pRb), respectively, disrupting normal cell cycle control. Persistent expression of E6 and E7 is required for maintenance of the transformed phenotype, making their transcriptional regulation an attractive therapeutic target.  

### Diagnosis and Treatment of HPV  

Current diagnostic methods for HPV include nucleic acid hybridization tests, PCR-based assays, and cytological examination (Pap smear). Treatment options remain limited to surgical excision for premalignant lesions and combination chemoradiation for invasive cancer. The lack of effective antiviral therapies against HPV highlights the need for novel treatment approaches targeting viral gene expression.  

### Leukemia Inhibitory Factor (LIF) and its Utility in Treating HPV-Transformed Cells  

The present invention demonstrates that LIF potently inhibits HPV-16 LCR-driven transcription in cervical cancer cell lines, leading to reduced expression of E6 and E7 oncogenes. This inhibition occurs through mechanisms involving STAT3 activation and results in decreased proliferation of HPV-transformed cells. As a cytokine with established safety profile in clinical trials, LIF represents a promising therapeutic candidate for HPV-associated dysplasia and carcinoma.  

### Experimental Procedures  

The invention encompasses methods for culturing HPV-transformed cells, treating cells with LIF, measuring viral oncogene expression using quantitative PCR, assessing transcriptional activity through luciferase reporter assays, and analyzing cell proliferation via MTT assays. These techniques provide means for evaluating LIF's therapeutic effects and screening related compounds.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Inhibition of HPV-16 LCR-Driven Transcription by LIF  

SiHa cervical cancer cells containing an HPV-16 LCR-driven luciferase reporter construct were treated with varying concentrations of LIF. Quantitative analysis demonstrated dose-dependent inhibition of LCR activity, with 10 ng/mL LIF reducing transcription by approximately 60%. Parallel experiments measuring endogenous E6 and E7 mRNA levels by real-time PCR confirmed significant reduction in oncogene expression following LIF treatment.  

### Example 2: STAT3 Activation by LIF in Cervical Cancer Cells  

Flow cytometric analysis of phospho-STAT3 levels revealed transient tyrosine phosphorylation following LIF treatment. Reporter assays using STAT-responsive constructs confirmed functional activation of STAT3 signaling pathways in LIF-treated cells, suggesting potential mechanisms for LIF-mediated inhibition of HPV transcription.  

### Example 3: Anti-Proliferative Effects of LIF on HPV-Transformed Cells  

MTT proliferation assays demonstrated that LIF treatment reduced growth of CaSki cervical cancer cells by approximately 50% compared to untreated controls. This anti-proliferative effect correlated with decreased E6/E7 expression, supporting the therapeutic potential of LIF for HPV-associated neoplasia.
