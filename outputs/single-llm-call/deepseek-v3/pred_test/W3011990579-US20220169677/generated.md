# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE  

The present disclosure relates to novel vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) inhibitors. More specifically, the disclosure provides compounds that inhibit the interaction between VEGF-A165 and neuropilin-1 (NRP-1), along with methods of their preparation and therapeutic uses.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE  

Neuropilin-1 (NRP-1) serves as a cell surface receptor involved in both physiological and pathological angiogenesis processes, including cancer progression. NRP-1 functions as a co-receptor for various growth factors, including VEGF-A165, and enhances their signaling through receptor tyrosine kinases. VEGF-A165 represents the most potent modulator of angiogenesis, and its binding to NRP-1 significantly enhances interaction with VEGFR-2.  

Current anti-angiogenic strategies targeting the VEGF pathway face limitations such as drug resistance and insufficient efficacy. While several peptide-based NRP-1 inhibitors have been identified, including those containing the C-end rule (CendR) motif (R/KXXR/K), these compounds often exhibit suboptimal binding affinities. Prior art peptides such as A7R (ATWLPPR) and tuftsin (TKPR) demonstrate IC50 values in the micromolar range, limiting their therapeutic potential.  

A critical need exists for improved VEGF-A165/NRP-1 inhibitors with enhanced binding affinity and stability. The present disclosure addresses this need by providing novel compounds incorporating cysteine residues that dramatically improve binding to NRP-1 while maintaining favorable pharmacokinetic properties.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example  

The synthesis of peptide VEGF-A165/NRP-1 inhibitors was accomplished via solid phase peptide synthesis (SPPS). The procedure employed Fmoc chemistry with appropriate protecting groups for functional moieties. Wang resin served as the solid support for peptide assembly.  

Coupling reactions utilized HATU as the coupling reagent with DIPEA as base. Peptides were cleaved from the resin using a TFA-based cocktail and purified by preparative HPLC. Final products were converted to pharmaceutically acceptable salts for biological testing.  

Specific synthesis of compound H-Cys-Lys(hArg)-Dab-ΔPro-Arg-OH began with Fmoc-L-Arg(Pbf) Wang resin. Sequential steps included: Fmoc deprotection monitored by Kaiser test; coupling of Fmoc-ΔPro-OH verified by acetaldehyde/chloranil test; coupling of Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH; further Fmoc deprotection; coupling of Fmoc-Lys(Alloc)-OH; Alloc deprotection; and final coupling of Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH.  

Another example detailed synthesis of compound H-Lys(Cys-hArg)-Dab-ΔPro-Arg-OH using similar SPPS methodology. This involved sequential coupling of Fmoc-ΔPro-OH, Fmoc-Dab(Boc)-OH, Boc-Lys(Fmoc)-OH, and Fmoc-hArg(Pbf)-OH to the resin-bound Fmoc-L-Arg(Pbf). Final products were purified by HPLC and characterized by mass spectrometry.  

The examples demonstrate reproducible synthesis of compounds exhibiting nanomolar affinity for NRP-1. These compounds represent significant improvements over prior art peptides in both binding affinity and enzymatic stability.
