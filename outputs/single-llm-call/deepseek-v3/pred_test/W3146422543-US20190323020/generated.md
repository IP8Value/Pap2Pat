## FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of metabolic engineering and industrial biotechnology. More specifically, the invention pertains to recombinant Aspergillus fungi genetically modified for enhanced production of aconitic acid and 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3-HP), and methods of using such fungi for bioproduction of these valuable chemical building blocks.  

## BACKGROUND  

Itaconic acid production in filamentous fungi, particularly Aspergillus species, has been extensively studied for industrial applications. The biosynthesis of itaconic acid occurs through a metabolic pathway where cis-aconitic acid serves as a key intermediate. The enzyme cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cadA) catalyzes the conversion of cis-aconitic acid to itaconic acid, representing a critical control point in this biosynthetic pathway.  

Regulation of the itaconic acid (IA) gene cluster in Aspergillus species involves complex genetic and biochemical mechanisms. The cluster typically includes genes encoding cadA along with associated transporters and regulatory elements. Recent studies have demonstrated that manipulation of this gene cluster, particularly through genetic modification of the cadA gene, can redirect metabolic flux and alter production profiles of organic acids in these fungi.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides recombinant Aspergillus fungi with genetic modifications that enable enhanced production of aconitic acid and 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3-HP). Central to this invention is the identification and characterization of the cadA gene's role in Aspergillus metabolism, particularly in strains of Aspergillus pseudoterreus.  

Through expressed sequence tag (EST) analysis, the inventors have characterized gene expression patterns within the itaconic acid biosynthetic cluster. Gene deletion analysis has revealed that disruption of the cadA gene results in complete elimination of itaconic acid production while enabling accumulation of aconitic acid.  

The invention specifically discloses a ΔcadA Aspergillus strain that exhibits several advantageous characteristics for industrial bioproduction. This strain demonstrates complete absence of itaconic acid production while accumulating both cis-aconitic acid and trans-aconitic acid. The ΔcadA strain further shows metabolic flexibility that allows for introduction of exogenous nucleic acid molecules encoding enzymes for alternative biosynthetic pathways.  

Key exogenous genes introduced in various embodiments include panD (aspartate 1-decarboxylase), BAPAT (β-alanine-pyruvate aminotransferase), and HPDH (3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase). The invention provides specific vectors and promoters suitable for expression of these genes in Aspergillus hosts. Genetic inactivation of the cadA gene is achieved through various methods including complete gene deletion, insertional mutagenesis, and antisense technology.  

The invention further encompasses compositions and kits containing the isolated ΔcadA Aspergillus fungi, along with methods for making aconitic acid and 3-HP using these recombinant strains. The methods include specific culturing conditions, media formulations, and product isolation techniques optimized for maximum production yields.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

The term "3-hydroxypropionate dehydrogenase (HPDH)" refers to an enzyme that catalyzes the conversion of malonate semialdehyde to 3-hydroxypropionic acid using NADPH as a cofactor. HPDH enzymes suitable for use in the present invention include those derived from Escherichia coli (EcHPDH) as well as functional variants thereof having at least 80% sequence identity to wild-type EcHPDH while retaining enzymatic activity.  

The term "aconitic acid" encompasses both cis-aconitic acid and trans-aconitic acid isomers, which are intermediates in the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle. In the context of this invention, aconitic acid refers to either or both isomers produced by recombinant Aspergillus strains.  

The term "aspartate 1-decarboxylase (panD)" refers to an enzyme that catalyzes the decarboxylation of L-aspartate to produce β-alanine. Functional panD enzymes include those derived from Tribolium castaneum (TcPAND) and homologous enzymes from other species that maintain at least 75% sequence identity while retaining decarboxylase activity.  

The term "β-alanine-pyruvate aminotransferase (BAPAT)" refers to an enzyme that catalyzes the transfer of an amino group between β-alanine and pyruvate, producing malonate semialdehyde and L-alanine. The invention utilizes BAPAT enzymes such as those from Bacillus cereus (BcBAPAT) and functional variants thereof.  

The term "cadA (cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase)" refers to the enzyme that converts cis-aconitic acid to itaconic acid in wild-type Aspergillus strains. The invention encompasses both wild-type cadA sequences and modified versions where the gene has been inactivated through various genetic manipulations.  

The term "detectable" when referring to production levels means that the compound can be identified and quantified using standard analytical techniques including but not limited to HPLC, GC-MS, or spectrophotometric assays with a detection limit of at least 0.01 g/L.  

The term "exogenous" in reference to nucleic acid molecules indicates that the genetic material originates from a source other than the host Aspergillus strain, including synthetic or codon-optimized sequences, or genes derived from different species.  

The term "genetic enhancement or up-regulation" refers to any modification that increases expression of a target gene, including but not limited to introduction of strong promoters, gene copy number amplification, or modification of regulatory elements.  

The term "genetic inactivation or down-regulation" encompasses any genetic modification that reduces or eliminates expression or activity of a target gene, including complete gene deletion, insertional inactivation, promoter modification, antisense RNA techniques, and RNA interference.  

The term "isolated" when referring to biological components means that the component has been separated from its natural environment and is substantially free of other biological materials, with purity of at least 90% as determined by standard analytical methods.  

The term "promoter" refers to a DNA sequence that initiates transcription of an associated gene. The invention utilizes various promoters including constitutive promoters such as gpdA and eno1 from Aspergillus species, as well as inducible and tissue-specific promoters.  

The term "recombinant" indicates that the organism or nucleic acid contains genetic material assembled through molecular biological techniques, including combinations of DNA from different sources or modifications not found in nature.  

The term "sequence identity" refers to the percentage of identical residues between two aligned sequences, calculated using standard algorithms such as BLAST with default parameters. For purposes of this invention, sequence identity comparisons are performed over the full-length of the reference sequence.  

The term "transformed" indicates that an organism has been genetically modified through introduction of exogenous nucleic acid by any suitable method including protoplast transformation, electroporation, or Agrobacterium-mediated transformation.  

The term "vector" refers to a nucleic acid construct capable of delivering and maintaining inserted DNA within a host cell. The invention utilizes various vectors including plasmid vectors, integrating vectors, and expression vectors containing regulatory elements for gene expression in Aspergillus.  

### Overview  

The invention centers on the metabolic engineering of Aspergillus pseudoterreus for production of valuable organic acids. Wild-type A. pseudoterreus naturally produces itaconic acid through a biosynthetic pathway where glucose is metabolized through glycolysis to produce citric acid, which is converted to cis-aconitic acid in the TCA cycle. The enzyme cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase (cadA) then converts cis-aconitic acid to itaconic acid, with additional transporters (mttA and mfsA) facilitating secretion of the final product.  

Through targeted genetic modification, the inventors have developed a ΔcadA strain that completely lacks itaconic acid production capability. This strain instead accumulates both cis-aconitic acid and trans-aconitic acid, representing a significant diversion of carbon flux from the wild-type metabolic pathway. Metabolic analysis reveals that the ΔcadA strain redirects carbon to production of various organic acids while maintaining robust growth characteristics.  

The ΔcadA strain serves as an exceptional biocatalyst for aconitic acid production, offering advantages over traditional chemical synthesis methods. Aconitic acid has been identified as one of the top 30 potential building blocks for chemical synthesis by the U.S. Department of Energy, with applications in polymer production and other industrial processes. The biological production method disclosed herein provides a sustainable alternative to petroleum-based synthesis routes.  

Detailed analysis of EST data revealed expression patterns of genes within the itaconic acid biosynthetic cluster. These studies demonstrated strong correlations between expression levels of cluster genes (including cadA, mttA, and mfsA) and itaconic acid production capacity. Construction and analysis of various deletion strains confirmed that elimination of cadA specifically blocks itaconic acid production while enabling aconitic acid accumulation.  

The invention further encompasses recombinant Aspergillus fungi where the ΔcadA strain has been genetically enhanced through introduction of exogenous genes encoding enzymes for 3-HP biosynthesis. This metabolic engineering creates a novel pathway in which aspartate is decarboxylated to β-alanine, which is subsequently converted to malonate semialdehyde and then to 3-HP. The resulting strains represent a unique platform for biological production of 3-HP under acidic conditions favorable for industrial scale processes.  

### Recombinant ΔcadA Fungi  

The invention provides isolated recombinant Aspergillus fungi characterized by genetic inactivation of the cadA gene. These ΔcadA fungal strains, particularly derived from A. pseudoterreus, exhibit complete elimination of itaconic acid production while demonstrating enhanced accumulation of aconitic acid isomers.  

Genetic inactivation of the cadA gene is achieved through various methods including complete gene deletion, insertional mutagenesis, and replacement of coding sequences with selectable marker genes. In preferred embodiments, the cadA gene is functionally deleted through replacement of its coding sequence with a pyrithiamine resistance (ptrA) marker using homologous recombination techniques. The resulting strains show undetectable levels of cadA mRNA and protein, along with absence of itaconic acid production.  

The ΔcadA strains of the invention demonstrate several advantageous characteristics for industrial biocatalysis. These include: maintenance of robust growth on glucose-containing media, ability to grow at low pH (2.8-3.4), and redirection of carbon flux toward aconitic acid production. Metabolic profiling reveals accumulation of both cis- and trans-aconitic acid isomers at levels significantly exceeding those found in wild-type strains.  

In various embodiments, the ΔcadA strains are further modified to express exogenous nucleic acid molecules encoding enzymes for alternative metabolic pathways. Particularly valuable are constructs expressing panD, BAPAT, and HPDH genes, which enable production of 3-HP through a β-alanine intermediate pathway. These genes are operably linked to strong fungal promoters such as gpdA and eno1 to ensure high-level expression in the Aspergillus host.  

The invention encompasses specific methods for genetic modification of Aspergillus strains. Protoplast transformation represents a preferred method, involving preparation of fungal protoplasts through enzymatic digestion of cell walls, followed by polyethylene glycol-mediated DNA uptake. Transformation efficiency is enhanced through use of selectable markers such as ptrA and optimization of regeneration conditions.  

Gene inactivation is verified through various analytical techniques. PCR and Southern blot analysis confirm genomic modifications at the cadA locus. Quantitative RT-PCR demonstrates absence of cadA mRNA transcripts. Biochemical assays using cell extracts verify elimination of cis-aconitic acid decarboxylase activity. Production of aconitic acid is quantified through HPLC analysis of culture supernatants, with typical ΔcadA strains producing 5-20 g/L aconitic acid under optimized conditions.  

The invention further provides specific nucleic acid sequences for cadA and its functional variants. Wild-type cadA sequences from A. pseudoterreus are disclosed, along with modified versions containing conservative amino acid substitutions that maintain enzymatic activity. Similarly, the invention encompasses sequences for panD, BAPAT, and HPDH enzymes, including codon-optimized versions for expression in Aspergillus.  

In additional embodiments, the ΔcadA strains are further modified through deletion of competing metabolic pathways. Particularly valuable is deletion of the malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase gene (Apald6), which significantly improves 3-HP yields by reducing metabolic diversion to acetyl-CoA. This modification, combined with expression of the 3-HP biosynthetic pathway, enables production of 3-HP at titers exceeding 0.8 g/L in bioreactor cultures.  

### Methods of Producing Aconitic Acid  

The invention provides methods for producing aconitic acid using recombinant ΔcadA Aspergillus fungi. These methods involve culturing the modified fungi under conditions that promote accumulation and secretion of aconitic acid, followed by isolation and purification of the product.  

Preferred culture conditions utilize minimal media containing 80-120 g/L glucose as carbon source, with ammonium sulfate as nitrogen source. The media is adjusted to pH 3.0-3.4 prior to inoculation to favor organic acid production. Cultures are typically maintained at 28-32°C with agitation rates of 150-250 rpm for shake flasks or 400-600 rpm for bioreactors.  

Inoculation is performed using spore suspensions or vegetative mycelia, with optimal inoculation density being 1-5×10^6 spores per 50 mL medium. The culture progresses through several phases: an initial growth phase (0-48 hours) where biomass accumulates, followed by a production phase (48-168 hours) where aconitic acid secretion occurs. Monitoring of culture parameters including pH, dissolved oxygen, and glucose consumption guides process optimization.  

Aconitic acid production is enhanced through controlled pH maintenance. In bioreactor systems, pH is initially allowed to drop to 2.8 through natural acid production, then maintained at this level through controlled addition of base (typically 5M KOH). This pH control strategy maximizes both fungal growth and acid production.  

Product isolation involves separation of fungal biomass from culture broth through filtration or centrifugation. The clarified broth containing aconitic acid is then processed through various purification steps including activated carbon treatment, ion exchange chromatography, and crystallization. Final product purity exceeds 95% as determined by HPLC analysis.  

Typical production yields range from 5-20 g/L aconitic acid in batch cultures, with productivity of 0.05-0.15 g/L/hour during peak production phases. These yields represent significant improvement over wild-type strains, which produce minimal aconitic acid due to its rapid conversion to itaconic acid by cadA.  

### Methods of Producing 3-HP  

The invention further provides methods for producing 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3-HP) using recombinant Aspergillus fungi. These methods utilize ΔcadA strains further modified to express the β-alanine pathway enzymes (panD, BAPAT, and HPDH), creating a novel metabolic route from aspartate to 3-HP.  

Culture conditions for 3-HP production are optimized to balance fungal growth with pathway activity. Preferred media contains 80-120 g/L glucose, with ammonium sulfate concentration reduced to 1-2 g/L to limit nitrogen availability and favor carbon flux toward 3-HP. The media is initially adjusted to pH 3.0-3.4 to maintain fungal acid tolerance.  

Inoculation and growth conditions mirror those for aconitic acid production, with temperature maintained at 28-32°C and adequate aeration provided through agitation or sparging. A key distinction is the monitoring of both biomass and 3-HP levels, as production is growth-associated in the engineered strains.  

The production phase typically begins during active growth (24-48 hours post-inoculation) and continues for 5-7 days. During this period, 3-HP accumulates extracellularly while intracellular levels remain relatively constant, indicating efficient transport mechanisms. Culture pH is maintained at 2.8-3.0 to keep 3-HP predominantly in its protonated form, facilitating subsequent separation.  

Product isolation involves removal of fungal biomass followed by concentration of the clarified broth through evaporation or reverse osmosis. Further purification steps may include solvent extraction, activated carbon treatment, and ion exchange chromatography. Final product purity exceeds 90% with recovery yields of 70-85% of total produced 3-HP.  

In optimized strains (particularly those with additional Apald6 deletion), 3-HP production reaches 0.8-1.2 g/L in small-scale bioreactors, with volumetric productivities of 0.02-0.05 g/L/hour. These levels represent significant improvement over initial engineered strains and demonstrate the feasibility of fungal 3-HP production under highly acidic conditions.

### Example 1  

#### Materials and Methods  

**Strains and Vector Construction**  
The parental strain Aspergillus pseudoterreus ATCC® 32359TM was obtained from the American Type Culture Collection. The cadA deletion vector was constructed by assembling PCR-amplified fragments using Gibson Assembly. The 5' and 3' flanking regions of the cadA gene (approximately 1 kb each) were amplified from genomic DNA using high-fidelity PCR. These fragments were combined with a pyrithiamine resistance (ptrA) marker cassette and cloned into the pBlueScript SK(-) vector.  

**Growth Conditions**  
Strains were maintained on Complete Medium (CM) agar plates containing 2% malt extract, 0.1% yeast extract, 0.1% peptone, and 2% glucose, with 1.5% agar. For liquid cultures, Minimal Medium (MM) was used, containing 100 g/L glucose, 2.36 g/L (NH4)2SO4, 0.11 g/L KH2PO4, 2.08 g/L MgSO4·7H2O, 0.13 g/L CaCl2·2H2O, and trace elements, adjusted to pH 3.4 with 5M KOH.  

**Protoplast Transformation**  
Protoplasts were prepared from young mycelia (16-18 hours growth) using lysing enzymes from Trichoderma harzianum (Sigma-Aldrich). After washing and conditioning in osmotic stabilizer (1.2 M MgSO4), protoplasts were transformed with 10 μg linearized DNA using polyethylene glycol (PEG) mediated transformation. Transformants were selected on CM agar containing 0.1 μg/mL pyrithiamine.  

**Deletion Mutant Construction**  
The cadA deletion mutant was constructed by replacing the cadA coding sequence with the ptrA marker via homologous recombination. Correct integration was verified by diagnostic PCR using primer pairs spanning the 5' and 3' junctions of the integration site. Southern blot analysis confirmed single-copy integration and absence of the wild-type cadA allele.  

**Analytical Methods**  
Dry cell weight was determined by harvesting mycelia via vacuum filtration, washing with distilled water, and lyophilizing to constant weight. Organic acid analysis was performed by HPLC using an Aminex HPX-87H column with 0.0045 M H2SO4 as mobile phase. RNA isolation and transcript analysis was conducted using quantitative real-time RT-PCR with cadA-specific primers.  

### Example 2  

#### Expression Profile of Itaconic Acid Gene Cluster  

Analysis of Expressed Sequence Tag (EST) data revealed the expression patterns of genes within the itaconic acid biosynthetic cluster during various growth phases. The cluster genes (tf, cadA, mttA, and mfsA) showed coordinated expression that correlated with itaconic acid production.  

During the growth phase (0-24 hours), transcript levels of all cluster genes remained low. As cultures entered production phase (24-48 hours), a sharp increase in cadA expression was observed, coinciding with the onset of itaconic acid secretion. The transporter genes mttA and mfsA showed similar but delayed expression patterns, consistent with their role in product secretion.  

The transcription factor gene tf exhibited expression preceding that of other cluster genes, suggesting its regulatory role in activating the biosynthetic pathway. These expression patterns provided critical insights for metabolic engineering strategies, demonstrating that cadA expression is tightly linked to itaconic acid production capacity.  

### Example 3  

#### Transformation System Development  

A reliable transformation system was developed for A. pseudoterreus to enable construction of recombinant knockout strains. Key parameters optimized included:  

1) Protoplast preparation: Young mycelia (16-18 hours growth) yielded highest protoplast counts (10^7-10^8 protoplasts/mL) with minimal viability loss.  
2) Transformation conditions: PEG concentration of 25% combined with 10 μg linear DNA gave optimal transformation efficiency (50-100 transformants/μg DNA).  
3) Regeneration: Osmotically stabilized top agar (0.8% agar in 1.2 M sorbitol) supported highest protoplast regeneration rates (30-40%).  

Using this system, knockout strains were generated for each gene in the itaconic acid cluster (tf, cadA, mttA, and mfsA). Deletion of cadA specifically eliminated itaconic acid production while enabling aconitic acid accumulation, confirming its essential role in the biosynthetic pathway.  

### Example 4  

#### Production Kinetics Analysis  

The production kinetics of itaconic acid (IA) were compared between Δtf and wild-type strains in bioreactor cultures. Both strains showed similar growth patterns, reaching stationary phase by 48 hours. However, IA production differed significantly:  

Wild-type strain:  
- IA production initiated at 24 hours  
- Maximum production rate: 0.15 g/L/hour (24-72 hours)  
- Final titer: 25.3 ± 2.1 g/L at 120 hours  

Δtf strain:  
- IA production delayed until 48 hours  
- Maximum production rate: 0.08 g/L/hour (48-96 hours)  
- Final titer: 12.7 ± 1.5 g/L at 120 hours  

These results demonstrate that tf deletion reduces but does not eliminate IA production, suggesting partial redundancy in pathway regulation. The ΔcadA strain, in contrast, showed complete absence of IA production while accumulating 8.2 ± 0.9 g/L aconitic acid under identical conditions.  

### Example 5  

#### Transcription Factor (tf) Gene Deletion Effects  

Deletion of the tf gene resulted in significant changes to gene expression and metabolic profiles. Quantitative RT-PCR analysis revealed:  

- 80-90% reduction in cadA, mttA, and mfsA transcript levels  
- 50% reduction in glycolytic gene expression  
- 2-fold increase in TCA cycle gene expression  

Metabolite analysis showed:  
- 50% reduction in itaconic acid accumulation  
- 2-fold increase in citrate and malate production  
- No detectable aconitic acid accumulation  

These findings indicate that tf plays a major regulatory role in the itaconic acid biosynthetic cluster, but its deletion does not completely abolish pathway activity. The metabolic shift toward TCA cycle intermediates suggests complex regulatory interactions between primary and secondary metabolism.  

### Example 6  

#### cadA Gene Deletion and Aconitic Acid Production  

Deletion of the cadA gene resulted in complete elimination of itaconic acid production and significant accumulation of aconitic acid. Metabolic analysis revealed:  

- Undetectable itaconic acid in culture supernatants  
- Accumulation of both cis- and trans-aconitic acid isomers (total 8.2 ± 0.9 g/L)  
- Increased citrate (5.1 ± 0.6 g/L) and malate (3.3 ± 0.4 g/L) production  
- No significant change in growth rate or biomass yield  

HPLC analysis showed that aconitic acid accumulation began during late growth phase (36-48 hours) and continued through stationary phase, reaching maximum levels by 120 hours. The ratio of cis- to trans-aconitic acid remained constant at approximately 3:1 throughout the production period.  

These results demonstrate that cadA deletion effectively blocks the itaconic acid pathway at the cis-aconitic acid step, causing accumulation of this intermediate and its isomer. The ΔcadA strain thus provides a novel platform for biological aconitic acid production.  

### Example 7  

#### Construction of 3-HP Producing Strain  

A 3-hydroxypropionic acid (3-HP) producing strain was constructed by introducing the β-alanine pathway into the ΔcadA background. Key steps included:  

1) Codon optimization and synthesis of TcPAND, BcBAPAT, and EcHPDH genes for Aspergillus expression  
2) Assembly of expression cassettes containing:  
   - gpdA promoter-TcPAND-eno1 terminator  
   - eno1 promoter-BcBAPAT-elf3 terminator  
   - gpdA promoter-EcHPDH-trpC terminator  
3) Protoplast transformation and selection using ptrA marker  

The resulting strain (ABF_004528_6) showed:  
- Functional expression of all three heterologous enzymes  
- Production of 0.42 ± 0.05 g/L 3-HP in shake flask cultures  
- Complete absence of itaconic acid production  
- Reduced but detectable aconitic acid accumulation (1.2 ± 0.3 g/L)  

Further improvement was achieved by deleting the competing malonate semialdehyde dehydrogenase (Apald6), increasing 3-HP production to 0.88 ± 0.11 g/L in bioreactor cultures. This demonstrates successful pathway engineering in A. pseudoterreus for 3-HP production under acidic conditions.
