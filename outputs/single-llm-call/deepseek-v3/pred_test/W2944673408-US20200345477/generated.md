# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to the field of medical device testing, and more specifically to methods for evaluating the mechanical and geometric properties of elastomeric implants such as breast implants under compressive loading conditions. The invention provides a novel methodology for determining implant shape stability, strain characteristics, and tangent moduli through controlled compression testing between parallel plates.  

### Description of Related Art  

Current methods for testing breast implants rely primarily on conventional mechanical testing techniques including tensile strength measurements, ultimate elongation tests, tear resistance evaluations, joint testing, cyclic fatigue testing, and ultimate strength assessments. These standardized tests, while providing valuable baseline data for comparing different implant designs, fail to adequately characterize the multi-dimensional strain behavior and shape change characteristics of implants under compressive loading conditions that simulate in vivo mechanical stresses.  

Existing mechanical testing protocols suffer from several limitations. First, they do not provide comprehensive data regarding how an implant's geometry changes under compressive loads, which directly impacts its interaction with surrounding tissue. Second, current methods cannot accurately quantify the relationship between compressive forces and resulting diametric strain, projection strain, and surface area strain - all critical factors influencing implant durability and clinical performance. Third, conventional testing does not account for the effects of surface friction between the implant and surrounding tissues, which varies significantly between dry and lubricated conditions in vivo.  

The current state of mechanical testing for breast implants relies heavily on ASTM and ISO standards that focus primarily on material properties rather than total implant behavior under load. While these standards provide important data regarding shell strength and durability, they fail to capture the complex geometric changes that occur when an implant is subjected to compressive forces similar to those experienced during normal patient activities such as walking, running, or sleeping in certain positions.  

There exists a significant need for a simplified yet comprehensive testing method that can accurately determine both the geometric and mechanical properties of breast implants under compressive loading. Such a method should provide quantitative data regarding shape stability, multi-dimensional strain characteristics, and tangent moduli while accounting for variable surface friction conditions. The ability to predict implant behavior under compressive loads would allow for better design optimization and improved clinical outcomes.  

## OBJECTS OF THE INVENTION  

It is an object of the present invention to provide a simplified means to determine breast implant geometries under compressive loading conditions through direct measurement of plate spacing and applied force.  

Another object of the invention is to provide a method for determining engineering mechanical properties of breast implants including but not limited to diametric strain, projection strain, surface area strain, and tangent moduli.  

A further object of the invention is to provide a validated quasi-equilibrium assumption that allows for accurate determination of implant geometric and mechanical properties using dynamic compression testing data.  

An additional object of the invention is to provide means for assessing the impact of design changes on implant performance by comparing multi-dimensional strain characteristics and tangent moduli between different implant configurations.  

Yet another object of the invention is to provide a comprehensive evaluation of breast implant properties that accounts for both dry and lubricated interface conditions, better simulating the range of in vivo environments.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel methodology for evaluating the mechanical and geometric properties of breast implants and other elastomeric devices under compressive loading conditions. The method utilizes a load frame apparatus to compress implants between parallel plates while measuring plate spacing and applied force.  

A key aspect of the invention involves the application of a quasi-equilibrium assumption that allows dynamic compression data to be used for calculating implant geometric properties. This assumption has been experimentally validated through comparison with static compression measurements.  

The invention includes calculation methods for determining critical implant geometric properties including diameter, surface area, and contact area with the compression plates. These calculations are based on a composite geometric model that treats the compressed implant as a combination of a central flattened cylinder and outer toroidal sections.  

The methodology further provides for calculation of engineering mechanical properties including multi-dimensional strains (diametric, projection, and surface area) and tangent moduli. These properties mathematically describe the shape stability of implants under compressive loading.  

The invention has particular application to breast implants, where shape stability has been shown to correlate with clinical outcomes such as capsular contracture rates and rupture rates. The method can also be applied to other elastomeric medical devices where understanding compressive behavior is important for performance evaluation.  

Benefits of the invention include improved accuracy in predicting implant behavior under compressive loads, simplified testing procedures compared to conventional methods, and the ability to quantitatively compare different implant designs. The methodology has been validated to provide geometric measurements within ±2% accuracy for loads above 5N, with reproducibility characterized by a relative standard deviation of approximately 0.6%.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention relates to breast implants undergoing dynamic compression between parallel plates, where the geometric and mechanical properties of the implant are determined through measurement of plate spacing and applied load. The methodology is particularly applicable to both saline-filled and silicone gel-filled breast implants with smooth or textured surfaces.  

Breast implant composition varies according to design, but typically includes an outer silicone elastomer shell filled with either saline solution or silicone gel. Some advanced designs incorporate multiple lumens or internal baffle structures to modify mechanical properties. The invention accounts for these variations through the comprehensive geometric model and property calculations.  

The load frame apparatus consists of two parallel plates (platens) mounted in a mechanical testing machine capable of applying controlled compressive forces while precisely measuring plate spacing. The preferred embodiment utilizes polished stainless steel plates with a surface roughness of approximately 0.18 micrometers, though other materials and surface finishes may be employed depending on specific testing requirements.  

Plate spacing and displacement are measured continuously during testing using precision displacement transducers integrated with the load frame. The initial plate spacing (H_o) is established at a minimal load (typically 0.445N) to ensure consistent starting conditions for all tests.  

The dynamic load program consists of controlled compression at constant crosshead speeds, typically 25.4 cm/min, though other rates may be used to simulate specific loading conditions. The load cell measures applied force with precision sufficient to detect changes of 0.1N or less throughout the testing range (typically 0.445N to 534N).  

The process steps of the test method include:  
1. Calibration of the load frame to ensure accurate force and displacement measurements  
2. Manufacture and loading of the breast implant into the test apparatus  
3. Recording of initial breast implant properties including volume and shell thickness  
4. Adjustment of plate spacing to establish the initial test condition  
5. Optional lubrication of the plate-implant interface with silicone oil or other suitable lubricants  
6. Implementation of the dynamic load program  
7. Continuous recording of plate spacing and applied load throughout the test  
8. Computation of breast implant geometry and engineering properties using the acquired data  

The composite geometric model forms the mathematical foundation for calculating implant properties. The model represents the compressed implant as a central flattened cylinder combined with the outer half of a torus. This configuration allows derivation of equations for critical dimensions including the implant-platen contact diameter (d):  

d = ½[- (πH)/2 + √((16V)/(πH) + (π²/4 - 8/3)H²)]  

The implant diameter (D) is then calculated from the plate spacing (H) and contact diameter (d):  

D = H + d  

These equations are simplified for computational efficiency while maintaining accuracy within ±2% of empirical measurements for loads above 5N.  

The surface area (A) of the compressed implant is calculated using:  

A = (πd²)/2 + (π²dH)/2 + πH²  

This formulation accounts for both the flattened central portion and toroidal periphery of the compressed implant.  

Engineering stresses are computed by normalizing the applied load (F) by the initial planform area (A_po = πD_o²/4):  

S = F/A_po  

Engineering strains are calculated as percentage changes in key dimensions:  

Projection strain: ε_H = - (H - H_o)/H_o × 100%  
Diametric strain: ε_D = (D - D_o)/D_o × 100%  
Areal strain: ε_A = (A - A_o)/A_o × 100%  

Engineering moduli are determined as the local slopes of the stress-strain curves:  

Projection tangent modulus: E_SH = ΔS/Δε_H  
Diametric tangent modulus: E_SD = ΔS/Δε_D  
Areal tangent modulus: E_SA = ΔS/Δε_A  

These moduli provide quantitative measures of implant shape stability under compressive loading.  

Validation of the quasi-equilibrium assumption is demonstrated through close agreement between static and dynamic test results, with average differences in projection measurements of less than 0.6% for comparable loading conditions. The shape stability of breast implants is characterized by the relative values of these strains and moduli under both dry and lubricated interface conditions.  

### First Invention Embodiment  

The first embodiment illustrates load-displacement measurements for three types of breast implants: an IDEAL IMPLANT saline-filled structured implant, an Allergan Natrelle INSPIRA cohesive gel implant, and an Allergan Natrelle INSPIRA standard gel implant.  

Validation of the quasi-equilibrium assumption is demonstrated for Breast Implant #1 (IDEAL IMPLANT) through comparison of static and dynamic compression tests. The average difference between dynamic and static projection measurements at constant load was 0.59%, confirming the validity of the assumption.  

For Breast Implant #2 (Allergan cohesive gel), the quasi-equilibrium assumption was similarly validated with an average difference of 0.06% between static and dynamic measurements. The close agreement between these measurement methods confirms the accuracy of using dynamic testing data for determining implant geometric and mechanical properties.  

### Second Invention Embodiment  

The second embodiment demonstrates calculation of breast implant diameter from plate spacing measurements using the composite geometric model equations. For Breast Implant #1 (IDEAL IMPLANT), diameters calculated from dynamic H measurements showed an average error of 1.7% relative to static measurements for loads between 22.2N and 534N.  

Validation of the quasi-equilibrium assumption was further confirmed for Breast Implant #2 (Allergan gel), where calculated diameters from dynamic testing showed an average error of -0.003% relative to static measurements over the same load range. These results demonstrate the robustness of the methodology across different implant types.  

### Third Invention Embodiment  

The third embodiment focuses on calculation of diametric strain for three implant types. Breast Implant #1 (IDEAL IMPLANT) exhibited diametric strains 6-19% lower than Breast Implant #2 (Allergan gel) and 24-42% lower than Breast Implant #3 (Allergan cohesive gel) under lubricated conditions for loads between 100N and 500N.  

These differences in diametric strain demonstrate the superior shape stability of the structured saline implant compared to both standard and cohesive gel implants, particularly under conditions simulating lubricated in vivo environments.  

### Fourth Invention Embodiment  

The fourth embodiment involves computation of planform stress by normalizing applied load by initial planform area. This stress calculation provides a standardized basis for comparing implants of similar size but different internal structures and filler materials.  

The planform stress analysis reveals that at comparable stress levels, the IDEAL IMPLANT maintains better shape retention (lower multi-dimensional strains) than either gel implant type, demonstrating its enhanced mechanical stability under compressive loading.  

### Fifth Invention Embodiment  

The fifth embodiment determines engineering moduli for three implant types. Breast Implant #1 (IDEAL IMPLANT) showed projection tangent moduli 80-100% higher than Breast Implant #2 (Allergan gel) and Breast Implant #3 (Allergan cohesive gel) at 40% projection strain under lubricated conditions.  

Similarly, the IDEAL IMPLANT exhibited areal tangent moduli 130% higher than the gel implants at 40% areal strain under lubricated conditions. These substantial differences in tangent moduli quantitatively demonstrate the superior shape stability of the structured saline implant design.  

### Additional Embodiments  

The invention contemplates application to other elastomeric devices beyond breast implants, including multi-lumen structures and anatomically shaped implants. The methodology is particularly suited for use in cyclic fatigue testing, with recommended load frequencies between 0.5-2 Hz and crosshead speeds of 5-50 cm/min.  

The data acquisition system should be capable of recording force and displacement measurements at minimum 10 Hz sampling rates to adequately capture implant response characteristics. The force range may be adjusted from 0.1N to 10kN depending on implant size and testing requirements.  

Additional embodiments include testing with contoured plate surfaces to better simulate anatomical loading conditions, integration with specialized software programs for automated data analysis, and comparative testing with both lubricated and unlubricated interfaces to evaluate frictional effects.  

The methodology can characterize anatomically shaped breast implants by modifying the geometric model to account for asymmetric compression behavior. Platen materials may vary from stainless steel to polymeric composites, with surface finishes ranging from 0.1-10 micrometers roughness depending on specific testing objectives.  

The invention applies to elastomeric devices with various geometries including spherical, elliptical, and custom shapes, constructed from materials including but not limited to silicone elastomers, polyurethanes, and other medical-grade polymers.  

The complete methodology provides a comprehensive framework for evaluating implant mechanical properties that surpasses conventional testing approaches in both scope and clinical relevance, while maintaining the precision and reproducibility required for regulatory evaluation and quality control.
