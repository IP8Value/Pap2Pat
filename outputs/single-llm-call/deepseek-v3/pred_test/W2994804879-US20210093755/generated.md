# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of medical devices, specifically to an improved apparatus and method for nasobiliary drainage tube repositioning during endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) procedures. More particularly, the invention provides a specialized device designed to facilitate safe and efficient transfer of an endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) catheter from the oral cavity to the nasal cavity without requiring direct manipulation of the patient's mouth or reliance on traditional grasping techniques that pose risks to both patient and physician.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) serves as a critical intervention for biliary diseases, where endoscopic nasobiliary drainage (ENBD) is often required for effective bile drainage in cases of cholangitis or biliary obstruction. ENBD demonstrates superior efficacy in reducing hyperbilirubinemia and presents lower risks of catheter obstruction compared to alternative drainage methods. Additionally, ENBD procedures are frequently necessary for obtaining bile samples for cytological or bacteriological analysis.  

The conventional technique for ENBD catheter repositioning involves transferring the drainage catheter from the mouth to the nostril through a multistep process. This typically requires inserting a nasal plastic tube, manually capturing its tip within the oral cavity, connecting it to the ENBD catheter, and then extracting the assembly through the nasal passage. However, this method presents several significant limitations. The procedure necessitates removal of the mouthpiece from sedated patients, often requiring extended waiting periods for patient arousal. Furthermore, physicians frequently sustain finger injuries from accidental biting by uncooperative patients. The use of forceps introduces additional risks of oral cavity trauma during tube grasping maneuvers.  

Previous attempts to modify ENBD repositioning techniques have proven inadequate. One proposed solution involved creating a wire loop using crossed guidewires, but this approach suffered from frequent slippage during operation and lacked standardized loop dimensions. Other techniques required supplementary fluoroscopic or endoscopic monitoring, adding complexity to the procedure. A particular challenge arises in patients with high Mallampati scores, where poor oropharyngeal visualization significantly hinders successful catheter transfer. These persistent shortcomings in existing methods underscore the need for an improved device that addresses safety concerns while enhancing procedural efficiency across diverse patient anatomies.  

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

The present invention addresses multiple deficiencies in current ENBD repositioning methods. Primary limitations include the excessive time required for conventional techniques, significant risks of oral trauma to patients, frequent finger injuries to medical staff, dependence on patient cooperation, and inconsistent success rates particularly in patients with challenging oropharyngeal anatomy. The invention specifically targets these issues by providing a standardized, anatomy-independent solution that eliminates direct oral manipulation while reducing procedural complexity.  

### Technical Solution  

The invention comprises a specialized device system featuring three primary components: a ring-type module, a dedicated nasal tube, and an integrated adjuster mechanism. The ring-type module consists of a curved loop structure attached to an ergonomic handle, designed for secure insertion and stable positioning within the oral cavity. The nasal tube incorporates multiple depth markers that enable precise insertion control without requiring visual confirmation. The adjuster mechanism allows for customized positioning based on individual patient anatomy, ensuring universal applicability.  

The novel method of using this device involves sequential steps that significantly streamline the ENBD repositioning process. The procedure begins with insertion of the ring-type module into the oral cavity, followed by guided placement of the marked nasal tube through the nasal passage. The integrated design allows automatic capture and alignment of the nasal tube within the ring module, eliminating manual grasping requirements. Subsequent connection to the ENBD catheter and controlled extraction complete the transfer process with minimal manipulation and maximal safety.  

### Advantageous Effects  

The invention provides numerous improvements over conventional techniques. Safety enhancements include complete elimination of finger insertion into the oral cavity, thereby preventing bite injuries to medical staff and reducing risks of oral mucosa damage to patients. The standardized design ensures consistent performance regardless of patient anatomy or Mallampati score. Efficiency gains are achieved through dramatically reduced procedure times, with clinical studies demonstrating a 77% reduction compared to conventional methods.  

Additional advantages include enhanced adjustability through the multistep marker system, allowing precise depth control tailored to individual patient needs. The economic benefits stem from reduced equipment requirements, elimination of disposable forceps, and decreased procedure time. The design also accommodates use by less experienced physicians while maintaining high success rates, making the technology accessible across various healthcare settings.  

## MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The ring-type module forms the core component of the invention, comprising a specifically curved ring part and an elongated ring handle. The ring part features a smooth, atraumatic surface contour designed to safely engage with the nasal tube without causing tissue damage. The ring handle incorporates ergonomic grips and orientation indicators to ensure proper positioning during insertion.  

The nasal tube component demonstrates several innovative features including multistep depth markers arranged at precise intervals. These markers provide visual and tactile feedback during insertion, enabling accurate placement without requiring endoscopic visualization. The tube material combines flexibility for patient comfort with sufficient rigidity to maintain structural integrity during the repositioning process.  

The adjuster mechanism includes a sliding groove system that permits controlled movement of the nasal tube relative to the ring module. Integrated fixing means secure the components during critical phases of the procedure, preventing accidental disengagement. Material selection for the ring part prioritizes biocompatibility and durability, with preferred embodiments utilizing medical-grade polymers that balance flexibility with structural stability.  

Clinical implementation follows a standardized protocol beginning with insertion of the ring part into the oral cavity at a predetermined depth. The nasal tube is then advanced through the nasal passage until appropriate marker alignment is achieved. Subsequent steps involve controlled withdrawal of the assembly to establish proper tube positioning, connection with the ENBD catheter, and final extraction to complete the nasobiliary drainage setup.  

Clinical results demonstrate the invention's superiority over conventional methods. Procedure times average 44 seconds compared to 194 seconds for traditional techniques, representing a 77% reduction. Success rates reach 97.3% across diverse patient populations, with no significant variation based on Mallampati scores. Complete elimination of oral cavity damage and physician injuries has been documented in all cases using the new device.  

The invention accommodates wide variability in patient physical characteristics through adjustable insertion depths and size modifications. The ring handle design incorporates multiple orientation aids including a multistep first marker for depth control and a multistep second marker for alignment verification. The sliding groove mechanism works in conjunction with the fixing means to ensure secure tube capture throughout the procedure.
