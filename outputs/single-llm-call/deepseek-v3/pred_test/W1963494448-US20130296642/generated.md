## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Field of Invention  
The present invention relates generally to the field of molecular diagnostics and personalized medicine, specifically to methods and compositions for assessing risk of incisional hernia formation based on gene expression profiling.  

### Describe Related Art  
Current approaches for assessing risk of incisional hernia formation rely primarily on clinical factors such as wound infection, abdominal distention, pulmonary complications, patient demographics, and comorbidities. While these factors provide some predictive value, they lack specificity and fail to account for underlying molecular mechanisms that may predispose individuals to hernia formation. Previous studies have examined collagen composition in hernia patients, particularly the ratio of collagen I to collagen III, but these investigations have been limited to post-hoc analysis of hernia tissue rather than predictive assessment.  

### Limitations of Incisional Hernia Repair  
Despite advances in surgical techniques and materials, incisional hernia repair remains problematic with recurrence rates between 10-50%. Current methods fail to identify patients at high risk for hernia formation prior to initial surgery, missing opportunities for preventative interventions.  

### Identify Risk Factors  
Known clinical risk factors for incisional hernia formation include wound infection, abdominal distention, pulmonary complications, male gender, advanced age, obesity, diabetes, and previous abdominal surgeries. However, these factors alone provide insufficient predictive accuracy for individual patient risk assessment.  

### Need for Improved Methods  
There exists a significant unmet need for objective, molecular-based methods to accurately predict incisional hernia risk prior to initial surgery. Such methods would enable personalized surgical planning and preventative strategies to reduce hernia incidence and recurrence rates.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods and compositions for determining risk of incisional hernia formation through analysis of specific gene expression patterns.  

### Introduce Method for Determining Risk Level  
The invention encompasses a method for determining a subject's risk level for developing incisional hernia following abdominal surgery. This method involves measuring expression levels of specific biomarker genes in a tissue sample obtained from the subject.  

### Measure Gene Expression  
Gene expression is measured for selected biomarkers including GREM1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and IL10. These measurements may be performed using various molecular techniques capable of quantifying gene expression levels.  

### Compare to Control Sample  
The measured expression levels are compared to control values derived from individuals with known clinical outcomes regarding hernia formation.  

### Determine Risk Level  
Based on the comparison, a risk level for incisional hernia formation is determined. The risk level may be classified as low, moderate, or high probability of hernia development.  

### Treat Based on Risk Level  
The determined risk level informs treatment decisions, allowing for personalized surgical approaches and preventative measures tailored to the patient's specific risk profile.  

### Specify Genes Used  
Key biomarker genes include GREM1 (Gremlin 1), COL1A2 (Collagen type I alpha 2 chain), COL3A1 (Collagen type III alpha 1 chain), and IL10 (Interleukin 10). Additional biomarkers may include COL1A1, FBN1, TIMP1, and other genes identified through gene expression profiling.  

### Specify Tissue Samples Used  
Tissue samples may be obtained from skin or fascia at potential surgical sites. Samples are preferably collected prior to any surgical incision to avoid confounding effects of wound healing processes.  

### Specify Measurement Methods  
Gene expression may be measured using microarray analysis, quantitative PCR (qPCR), PCR arrays, RNA sequencing, or other nucleic acid detection methods. Protein expression of encoded gene products may be assessed through immunohistochemistry or immunoassay techniques.  

### Specify Treatment Options  
Based on determined risk level, treatment options may include: selection of specific surgical mesh materials, modified surgical techniques, prophylactic mesh placement, or targeted pharmacological interventions to modify wound healing processes.  

### Introduce Diagnostic Method  
The invention further provides a diagnostic method for assessing incisional hernia risk through analysis of gene expression patterns in tissue samples.  

### Introduce Product  
The invention encompasses products for implementing the disclosed methods, including biomarker panels, diagnostic kits, and biochip arrays configured for incisional hernia risk assessment.

## EXAMPLES  

### Patient Samples and Tissue Acquisition  

Patient samples were collected under IRB-approved protocols from individuals undergoing either recurrent incisional hernia repair or laparoscopic cholecystectomy (controls). Tissue samples of approximately 1 cm² were obtained from trocar placement sites, remote from hernia locations or previous incisions. Samples were divided and preserved in RNALater™ RNA stabilization reagent or 10% buffered formalin for subsequent analysis.  

### RNA Isolation and RNA Amplification  

Total RNA was isolated from skin and fascia specimens using the RNeasy® Lipid Tissue Mini Kit with on-column DNase treatment. RNA quality was assessed by spectrophotometry and electrophoresis. Total RNA was amplified using the WT-Ovation™ Pico RNA Amplification System to generate sufficient material for microarray analysis.  

### Immunohistochemistry  

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded tissue sections were processed for immunohistochemistry using automated staining systems. Primary antibodies against collagen I, collagen III, and other protein biomarkers were applied with appropriate controls. Staining intensity and distribution were evaluated by trained pathologists.  

### Statistical Analysis of Microarray Data  

Microarray data were analyzed using R statistical software. Low-expression genes were filtered out to reduce false positives. Differential gene expression was assessed using moderated t-statistics with log2-transformed normalized intensities. Multiple testing correction was applied using false discovery rate methods with a significance cutoff of q < 30%.  

### Demographics  

Patient demographics were comparable between recurrent hernia and control groups, with the exception of diabetes prevalence and previous surgery history, which were expected differences between the populations. The majority of analyzed samples were from female patients to control for gender effects.  

### Gene Ontology Analysis of Differentially Expressed Genes  

Gene ontology analysis revealed significant enrichment of differentially expressed genes in biological processes including wound healing response, immune regulation, and extracellular matrix organization. Molecular functions such as collagen binding and extracellular matrix structural components were overrepresented among significant genes.  

### COL1/COL3 Ratio by Microarray, PCR Array, and Immunohistochemistry  

The collagen I to collagen III ratio showed consistent patterns across multiple analytical platforms. Microarray data indicated decreased COL1A2/COL3A1 ratio in recurrent hernia patients compared to controls. PCR array results confirmed this finding with improved statistical power. Immunohistochemistry demonstrated corresponding protein-level differences in collagen composition.
