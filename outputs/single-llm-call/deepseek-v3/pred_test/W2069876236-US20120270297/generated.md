## DESCRIPTION  

### BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates to improved methods for cultivating thermophilic bacteria of the genus Thermotoga and genetic manipulation systems for these organisms. Thermotoga species represent one of only two bacterial genera capable of growth at temperatures up to 90°C, along with Aquifex. These strictly anaerobic microorganisms have been isolated from diverse geothermal environments including heated sea floors, continental hot springs, and oil reservoirs. Phylogenetic analysis based on 16S rRNA sequences places Thermotoga at a deep branch of the evolutionary tree, suggesting these organisms emerged during early Earth history when surface temperatures were substantially higher and atmospheric oxygen levels were negligible.  

Study of Thermotoga molecular genetics provides unique insights into fundamental questions regarding the origin of life and mechanisms of macromolecular thermostability under extreme conditions. Of particular industrial relevance, Thermotoga species demonstrate the ability to hydrolyze various polysaccharides through fermentative catabolism, with hydrogen gas production as a metabolic end product. This metabolic capability has generated significant interest in utilizing these organisms for biomass-based clean energy production, particularly through metabolic engineering approaches. However, investigation of Thermotoga has been largely limited to biochemical, genomic, and fermentation studies due to the lack of reliable genetic tools, a limitation shared with most hyperthermophilic microorganisms.  

Traditional methods for cultivating Thermotoga present substantial technical challenges. Conventional anaerobic glove box techniques require specialized equipment and present operational difficulties when manipulating cultures. The Hungate technique, while avoiding the need for glove boxes, introduces limitations due to the narrow openings of culture vessels that increase contamination risks. An overlay method has been described but still presents technical hurdles for routine laboratory use. There exists a clear need for improved cultivation methods that maintain culture viability while simplifying operational requirements.  

Restriction-modification (R-M) systems present another significant barrier to genetic manipulation of Thermotoga. These bacterial defense mechanisms typically consist of paired restriction endonucleases that cleave foreign DNA at specific recognition sequences and corresponding methyltransferases that protect host DNA through site-specific methylation. Type II restriction endonucleases are particularly well-characterized and recognize short palindromic DNA sequences. The diversity of R-M systems among bacterial species necessitates careful consideration when introducing exogenous DNA into new host organisms.  

Several cryptic mini-plasmids have been identified in Thermotoga species, including pRQ7 in T. sp. RQ7, pMC24 in T. maritima, and pRKU1 in T. petrophila RKU-1. These extremely small (846 bp) plasmids contain a single open reading frame presumed to encode a replication protein. Studies indicate pRQ7 replicates via a rolling circle mechanism while maintaining negative supercoiling. Based on pRQ7, two Thermotoga-Escherichia coli shuttle vectors (pJY1 with chloramphenicol resistance and pJY2 with kanamycin resistance) were constructed for expression in T. neapolitana and T. maritima. While these vectors conferred transient antibiotic resistance in liquid media through liposome-mediated transformation, no stable transformants could be isolated on solid media. The development of reliable gene transfer systems for Thermotoga remains an unmet need in the field.  

### SUMMARY  

The present invention provides novel solutions to the technical challenges of Thermotoga cultivation and genetic manipulation. An improved embedded growth method enables high-efficiency plating of Thermotoga cultures without requiring anaerobic chambers. This method combines Thermotoga cells with a solidifying matrix under aerobic conditions, achieving plating efficiencies approaching 50%. The invention further describes a soft culture medium formulation that facilitates transfer between solid and liquid cultures while maintaining cell viability.  

The invention also provides novel DNA sequences and vectors for genetic manipulation of Thermotoga species. A Thermotoga-E. coli shuttle vector designated pDH10 incorporates elements from plasmid pRQ7 along with a thermostable kanamycin resistance marker. This vector demonstrates stable maintenance in both Thermotoga and E. coli hosts. The invention further describes methods for introducing exogenous DNA into Thermotoga through both liposome-mediated transformation and electroporation, representing the first demonstration of stable transformation in these organisms.

#### Improved Method for Cultivation of Thermotoga  

The present invention provides an improved method for cultivating Thermotoga species that overcomes limitations of prior techniques. Traditional approaches requiring anaerobic chambers present significant practical challenges for routine laboratory work. The disclosed embedded growth method enables high-efficiency plating under aerobic conditions by suspending cells within a solid matrix that limits oxygen exposure.  

Key aspects of the method involve preparation of SVO medium according to established formulations, with modifications to support embedded growth. For plating applications, double-strength liquid SVO is autoclaved separately from solidifying agents such as agar or Gelrite, then combined while hot. Cell cultures are mixed with the medium prior to pouring, allowing cells to become embedded within the solid matrix. This approach achieves plating efficiencies approaching 50%, representing a dramatic improvement over surface plating methods that typically yield efficiencies several orders of magnitude lower.  

The invention further describes a soft SVO medium formulation incorporating 0.075% Gelrite that facilitates culture transfer between solid and liquid phases. This intermediate viscosity medium creates localized anaerobic conditions that maintain cell viability during transfer operations. Cultures grown in soft SVO medium demonstrate excellent viability even after extended storage periods at room temperature.  

The scope of the invention encompasses various solidifying agents beyond those specifically exemplified, including but not limited to agar, agarose, Gelrite, and other polysaccharide gelling agents. Similarly, the invention applies to diverse culture media formulations optimized for Thermotoga growth and various culture vessel configurations suitable for aerobic handling.  

#### Strains and Cultivation Conditions  

The invention utilizes specific bacterial strains and cultivation conditions to enable genetic manipulation of Thermotoga. Exemplary strains include Thermotoga neapolitana ATCC 49049 (equivalent to T. neapolitana DSM 4359), T. sp. RQ7, and T. maritima MSB8. These organisms are cultivated at 77°C in SVO medium under anaerobic conditions established by nitrogen sparging and sealed serum bottle configurations.  

For E. coli host strains such as DH5α used in vector construction, standard LB medium with 1.5% agar for solid media supports growth at 37°C. Antibiotic selection typically employs ampicillin at 100 μg/ml for E. coli and kanamycin at 150 μg/ml for liquid cultures or 250 μg/ml for solid media when working with Thermotoga transformants.  

Vector construction involves PCR amplification of the pRQ7 plasmid sequence using primers designed to linearize the plasmid immediately downstream of the replication protein stop codon. The resulting fragment is cloned into pUC19 to generate intermediate construct pDH1, which is subsequently joined with pKT1 carrying a thermostable kanamycin resistance gene to yield the shuttle vector pDH10.  

The scope of the invention encompasses various primer designs, vector backbones, and selectable markers beyond those specifically exemplified. Epitope tags and other protein fusion partners may be incorporated to facilitate detection or purification of expressed gene products. The invention further includes all DNA sequences encoding functional derivatives of the disclosed vector components.  

#### Purification and Analyses of CTN 0339 and CTN 0340 Gene Products  

The invention provides methods for purifying and analyzing gene products from Thermotoga, exemplified by proteins CTN 0339 and CTN 0340. Cell lysis is achieved through standard methods followed by centrifugation to remove debris. Heat treatment at 80°C for 20 minutes serves as an effective purification step for thermostable proteins, precipitating many host proteins while leaving the target proteins in solution.  

Protein analysis employs SDS-PAGE for size determination and purity assessment, complemented by Western blotting for specific detection when appropriate epitope tags are present. Restriction activity assays evaluate sequence-specific DNA cleavage, while modification assays assess methyltransferase activity through protection against cognate restriction enzymes.  

The scope of the invention includes various epitope tags and detection strategies beyond those specifically described. DNA sequences encoding functional derivatives of the exemplified proteins are also encompassed, including fragments retaining catalytic activity, sequence variants with altered properties, and fusion proteins combining multiple functional domains.  

#### Determination of the Cleavage Site of the REase  

The invention provides methods for determining restriction enzyme cleavage specificities, exemplified by analysis of CTN 0339. PCR amplification generates substrate DNA containing potential recognition sequences. Digestion products are analyzed by sequencing to identify cleavage positions. Vector construction strategies facilitate production of substrate DNA with defined sequences for systematic analysis.  

Sequencing analysis confirms blunt-end cleavage activity for the exemplified enzyme. These methods are broadly applicable to characterization of novel restriction enzymes from diverse sources. The scope of the invention includes all variations of these analytical methods and the resulting sequence specificity determinations.  

#### Restriction and Modification Assays of the Thermotoga Proteins  

The invention provides assay methods for characterizing restriction and modification activities of Thermotoga proteins. Restriction activity of CTN 0339 is demonstrated through digestion of pUC19 DNA substrate. Protection assays show that CTN 0340 modifies DNA to prevent cleavage by its cognate restriction enzyme, confirming their functional pairing as a restriction-modification system.  

These assays establish that CTN 0339 functions as a restriction endonuclease while CTN 0340 serves as its cognate methyltransferase. The invention encompasses all variations of these assay methods and their applications for characterizing restriction-modification systems from diverse sources.  

#### Overexpression of R.TneDI  

The invention provides methods for overexpression of Thermotoga restriction enzymes in heterologous hosts such as E. coli. The R.TneDI enzyme (CTN 0339) is expressed from a pET-24a(+) vector in BL21(DE3) cells following induction with IPTG. Expressed protein is confirmed by SDS-PAGE and characterized for restriction activity.  

This overexpression system enables production of thermostable restriction enzymes for various applications. The invention includes all variations of these expression methods and the resulting recombinant proteins.  

#### Determination of the Cleavage Site of R.TneDI  

The invention provides methods for determining the recognition sequence of R.TneDI. A specifically designed PCR product containing potential recognition sites serves as substrate for digestion. Sequencing of the smaller digestion product confirms cleavage at the CGCG recognition sequence, establishing R.TneDI as a blunt-end cutter targeting this tetranucleotide.  

Comparative analysis shows R.TneDI represents a novel addition to the collection of Type II restriction enzymes recognizing CGCG sequences. The invention encompasses all methods for determining restriction enzyme specificities and the resulting sequence characterizations.  

#### Demonstration of Genes Encoding TneDI Type II R-M System  

The invention demonstrates that genes CTN 0339 and CTN 0340 encode a functional Type II restriction-modification system designated TneDI. This system protects host chromosomal DNA while cleaving unmethylated foreign DNA at CGCG sites. The invention proposes R.TneDI as a valuable tool for specific DNA cleavage in biotechnological applications.  

The invention includes all applications of the TneDI system and related restriction-modification systems from Thermotoga species. Alternative approaches such as in vitro transcription and translation may be employed to characterize these systems.  

#### Cloning and Expression of R.TneDI  

The invention describes cloning R.TneDI into E. coli host XL1-Blue MRF' followed by transfer to expression host BL21(DE3). Modification activity requires S-adenosylmethionine (AdoMet) as essential cofactor. Analysis of AdoMet stability under reaction conditions informs assay design.  

While the exact cytosine modification position remains undetermined, comparative analysis reveals conserved catalytic motifs shared with characterized methyltransferases such as M.PvuII. The Rossman fold serves as AdoMet binding domain in these enzymes.  

The invention encompasses all variations of these cloning and expression methods and their applications for producing thermostable restriction-modification system components.  

#### Identification of Orthologs of TneDI System  

The invention identifies orthologous restriction-modification systems in related Thermotogaceae genomes through sequence analysis. Conserved gene neighborhoods suggest recent horizontal gene transfer events distributing these systems among thermophilic bacteria.  

These findings inform understanding of restriction-modification system evolution and distribution. The invention includes all applications of this evolutionary analysis for identifying and characterizing related enzyme systems.  

#### Antibiotic Sensitivity Tests  

The invention provides methods for determining antibiotic sensitivity in Thermotoga species. Filter paper discs loaded with varying kanamycin amounts are placed on culture-embedded SVO plates. Inhibition zone formation after 48 hours incubation indicates sensitivity.  

These assays establish that T. sp. RQ7 and T. maritima demonstrate suitable sensitivity to kanamycin for use as selectable markers, while T. neapolitana shows relative resistance. Optimal kanamycin concentrations are determined as 150 μg/ml for liquid media and 250 μg/ml for solid media.  

The invention encompasses all variations of these antibiotic sensitivity testing methods and their applications for developing genetic selection systems in thermophilic bacteria.  

#### Extraction of DNA from Thermotoga  

The invention provides methods for extracting plasmid and genomic DNA from Thermotoga species. Plasmid DNA is obtained using standard alkaline lysis protocols adapted for thermophilic organisms. Genomic DNA extraction employs phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol treatment followed by isopropanol precipitation.  

These methods yield DNA suitable for downstream applications including PCR, cloning, and sequencing. The invention includes all variations of these DNA extraction protocols and their applications for genetic analysis of thermophilic microorganisms.  

#### Construction of pDH10  

The invention describes construction of Thermotoga-E. coli shuttle vector pDH10. This vector combines elements from pRQ7 providing replication function in Thermotoga with pKT1 supplying a thermostable kanamycin resistance marker. The kan gene is expressed from a Thermus thermophilus promoter expected to function in Thermotoga based on conserved promoter sequences.  

Vector construction involves PCR amplification of pRQ7 followed by sequential cloning into pUC19 and pKT1 backbones. The resulting pDH10 vector carries ColE1 origin and β-lactamase gene for E. coli maintenance, along with pRQ7 origin and kanamycin resistance for Thermotoga selection.  

The invention encompasses all variations of this vector design including alternative selectable markers, promoter elements, and replication origins. DNA sequences encoding functional derivatives of the vector components are also included.  

#### Transformation and Selection Methods  

The invention provides methods for transforming Thermotoga species with exogenous DNA. Liposome-mediated transformation employs DOTAP liposomal reagent to deliver plasmid DNA, with all operations conducted aerobically. Electroporation methods utilize specially prepared electrocompetent cells suspended in 10% glycerol, 0.85 M sucrose solution.  

Optimal electroporation parameters include 25 μF capacitance, 200 Ω resistance, and 1.8-2.0 kV pulse strength. Recovery involves incubation in SVO medium at 77°C for 3 hours followed by plating in selective medium using the embedded growth method.  

The invention includes all variations of these transformation methods and their applications for genetic manipulation of thermophilic microorganisms.  

#### Stability Assays of the Transformed DNA in Thermotoga and E. coli  

The invention provides methods for assessing plasmid stability in Thermotoga and E. coli hosts. Serial passage in nonselective media monitors plasmid retention over multiple generations. Colony forming unit comparisons on selective versus nonselective plates quantify plasmid-bearing populations.  

Surprisingly, pDH10 demonstrates nearly 100% stability in Thermotoga even without selective pressure. In E. coli, pDH10 shows significantly improved stability compared to parent vector pKT1, despite similar copy numbers. This enhanced stability appears attributable to the incorporated pRQ7 sequence.  

The invention encompasses all applications of these stability assay methods and their use in evaluating vector performance in diverse host organisms.  

#### Incorporation of pRQ7 Increased the Stability of pUC19 Derivatives in E. coli  

The invention demonstrates that incorporation of pRQ7 sequences enhances stability of pUC19-derived vectors in E. coli. Comparative analysis shows pDH10 maintains presence in ~90% of cells after three nonselective transfers and ~32% after six transfers, while pKT1 is completely lost by three transfers.  

Copy number analysis confirms this stability improvement is not due to increased plasmid copy number. The invention includes all applications of this stability-enhancing sequence element in diverse vector systems.  

#### Transformation and Expression of kan Gene  

The invention demonstrates functional expression of a heterologous kanamycin resistance gene in Thermotoga species. The kan gene is stably maintained and expressed in both T. sp. RQ7 and T. maritima transformants, as confirmed by PCR analysis and antibiotic resistance phenotypes.  

While intact pDH10 plasmid is not detectable in Thermotoga by standard methods, PCR evidence suggests the vector maintains autonomy rather than integrating into the chromosome. The extremely low copy number in Thermotoga may account for difficulties in plasmid detection.  

The invention includes all applications of this heterologous gene expression system and its use for genetic manipulation of thermophilic microorganisms.
