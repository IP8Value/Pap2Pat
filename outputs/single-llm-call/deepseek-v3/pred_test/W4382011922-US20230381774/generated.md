# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cellular reprogramming and gene editing. More specifically, the invention provides novel methods and systems for enhancing cellular reprogramming efficiency through modulation of mechanical forces and epigenetic states. The disclosed technology encompasses microfluidic devices and biophysical approaches that facilitate direct cell conversion with improved efficiency and reliability.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cell reprogramming has emerged as a powerful tool for generating specialized cell types for regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and drug discovery. Traditional approaches to cell reprogramming rely primarily on biochemical factors such as transcription factors and small molecules to induce changes in cell fate. However, these methods often suffer from low efficiency and incomplete conversion, limiting their therapeutic potential.  

A critical barrier to efficient reprogramming is the epigenetic state of the cell, particularly the organization of chromatin into heterochromatin and euchromatin regions. The epigenetic landscape acts as a molecular memory that maintains cell identity and resists conversion to alternative fates. While biochemical factors can modulate epigenetic marks, they often fail to fully overcome this barrier, resulting in partial or unstable reprogramming.  

Recent studies have highlighted the importance of biophysical factors in regulating cellular function and fate. Mechanical forces transmitted through the cytoskeleton and extracellular matrix can influence gene expression by altering nuclear architecture and chromatin organization. However, the specific mechanisms by which mechanical cues regulate epigenetic states during reprogramming remain poorly understood.  

There exists a pressing need for improved methods of cell reprogramming that can more effectively overcome epigenetic barriers and achieve high-efficiency conversion. The present invention addresses this need by integrating mechanical manipulation with biochemical reprogramming factors to synergistically enhance the conversion process.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides innovative microfluidic devices and methods for enhancing cellular reprogramming through mechanical deformation of cells. The disclosed technology utilizes precisely controlled microfluidic systems to apply transient mechanical forces that prime the epigenetic state of cells, making them more amenable to reprogramming.  

In one embodiment, the invention comprises a microfluidic cell culture and processing system designed to induce controlled cellular and nuclear deformation. The system includes microchannels of specific dimensions that temporarily squeeze cells as they pass through, causing measurable changes in nuclear morphology and chromatin organization. This mechanical perturbation has been shown to reduce heterochromatin marks such as H3K9me3 while increasing euchromatin marks associated with gene activation.  

The microfluidic systems of the invention are designed with optimized channel widths and heights to achieve desired levels of deformation without compromising cell viability. Key parameters include the aspect ratio (AR) and cross-section area ratio (CR) of the microchannels, which determine the degree and duration of mechanical stress applied to cells. Through systematic optimization, the invention provides specific design guidelines for microfluidic devices that maximize reprogramming efficiency while maintaining cell health.  

The methods of the invention enable high-efficiency conversion of somatic cells to various target cell types, including neurons, cardiomyocytes, and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). In particular embodiments, the invention enhances direct induced neuronal (iN) reprogramming by combining mechanical deformation with transcription factor delivery. The mechanical priming step significantly boosts the efficiency of subsequent biochemical reprogramming, often by 4-5 fold compared to conventional methods.  

Additional embodiments utilize the microfluidic systems for guided stem cell differentiation, gene therapy applications, and cell rejuvenation. The mechanical deformation techniques can be combined with CRISPR-based gene editing to facilitate targeted gene activation or silencing. The invention also provides methods for reorganizing chromatin structure to reverse age-related epigenetic changes and restore cellular function.

The present invention provides comprehensive solutions to the challenges of low efficiency in cell reprogramming by integrating mechanical manipulation with established biochemical approaches. The detailed description that follows outlines the various components, methods, and applications of the technology.  

Cell reprogramming faces significant limitations due to the stability of epigenetic barriers that maintain cellular identity. The invention addresses this challenge by employing biophysical factors to transiently modify the epigenetic state prior to or during reprogramming. Mechanical forces applied through microfluidic deformation have been shown to reduce heterochromatin marks and increase chromatin accessibility at key loci, creating a more permissive environment for fate conversion.  

The microfluidic cell processing and culturing systems of the invention comprise precisely fabricated channels that induce controlled cellular and nuclear deformation. As cells pass through these channels, they experience transient squeezing that alters nuclear morphology and chromatin organization. This mechanical perturbation is carefully calibrated to achieve optimal epigenetic changes without causing permanent damage to cellular structures.  

Three-dimensional biomaterials engineering represents another key aspect of the invention. By modulating cell adhesion through engineered substrates, the technology can further enhance reprogramming efficiency. Specific embodiments utilize polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS)-based microfluidic lab-on-chip devices with precisely controlled surface topographies that influence cell spreading and intracellular tension.  

The invention also incorporates genetic modification techniques for mammalian cells, including delivery of reprogramming factors via lentiviral vectors. In preferred embodiments, the CRISPR ribonucleoprotein complex is employed for precise gene editing in combination with mechanical deformation. The CRISPR-associated endonuclease activity can be targeted to specific genomic loci whose accessibility is enhanced by the mechanical priming step.  

Microfluidic channel design represents a critical innovation of the present technology. The aspect ratio (AR) and cross-section area ratio (CR) of the channels are optimized to achieve desired levels of deformation for different cell types. Experimental results demonstrate that channels with AR between 0.8-1.2 and CR between 0.6-0.8 provide optimal reprogramming enhancement while maintaining high cell viability.  

The methods of making and using the microfluidic devices include fabrication techniques such as soft lithography and bonding processes that ensure consistent channel dimensions. The invention provides detailed protocols for cell loading, flow rate control, and post-deformation culture conditions that maximize reprogramming outcomes.  

Applications of the technology extend to personalized medicine, where patient-specific cells can be efficiently reprogrammed for autologous therapies. The microfluidic cell culture system enables manipulation of cells under precisely controlled culture conditions, with the ability to introduce agents that modulate cell physiology at specific time points.  

Nuclear deformation and subsequent chromatin changes represent the mechanistic foundation of the invention's effectiveness. Mechanical squeezing induces transient disruption of the nuclear lamina and associated chromatin domains, leading to global and locus-specific changes in epigenetic marks. These changes persist long enough to facilitate reprogramming factor binding and gene activation, but eventually resolve as cells establish their new identity.  

The invention further provides methods for culturing mammalian cells under conditions that promote mechanical deformation-induced reprogramming. This includes optimization of microchannel design for specific cell types, from fibroblasts to blood cells, and integration with small molecule compounds that enhance the process. Specific inhibitors of intracellular structures, such as blebbistatin for non-muscle myosin II, can be combined with mechanical deformation to further boost efficiency.  

Reprogramming experiments with various inhibitors demonstrate that reducing intracellular tension through multiple mechanisms synergistically enhances iN reprogramming efficiency. Three-dimensional spheroid cultures in microfluidic systems provide another embodiment that increases reprogramming efficiency by modulating cell-cell interactions and spatial organization.  

Spatial distribution of reprogrammed cells within microfluidic devices reveals patterns of conversion efficiency that inform further optimization. Inhibition of specific signaling pathways, particularly TGF-β/Activin and BMP pathways, removes spatial heterogeneity and promotes more uniform reprogramming outcomes.  

The technology has been reduced to practice using human neonatal dermal fibroblasts, demonstrating robust expression of neuron-specific β-tubulin III (Tuj1) following mechanical and biochemical treatment. Relative reprogramming efficiency measurements show consistent improvement over conventional methods, with particular enhancement when combining mechanical deformation with inhibition of TGF-β/Activin/Nodal and BMP pathways.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Transient Nuclear Deformation Primes Epigenetic State and Promotes Cell Reprogramming  

Cell reprogramming technologies have advanced significantly, yet direct reprogramming approaches often suffer from low efficiency due to persistent epigenetic barriers. The heterochromatin state of somatic cells presents a particular challenge for transcription factors attempting to access target genes. While biochemical factors can modify some epigenetic marks, they frequently fail to fully overcome this barrier.  

The present example demonstrates how mechanical perturbation of the cell nucleus through microfluidic deformation can prime the epigenetic state for more efficient reprogramming. A custom microfluidic device was fabricated to study nuclear deformation effects on mouse fibroblasts and their nuclei. The device incorporated channels of varying widths to apply controlled mechanical stress while monitoring cell viability and nuclear morphology.  

Cell viability assays confirmed that optimal channel widths and flow rates could be identified that induced significant nuclear deformation without causing membrane leakage or DNA damage. Elastic modulus measurements revealed transient changes in cell stiffness following deformation, accompanied by persistent alterations in nuclear shape. These physical changes correlated with reduced levels of the heterochromatin mark H3K9me3, as measured by FRET biosensors and immunostaining.  

The reprogramming experimental procedure involved timing mechanical squeezing prior to or during transcription factor delivery. Results showed that deformation applied within the first 3 days of reprogramming produced the greatest enhancement in efficiency, as measured by expression of neuronal markers. Quantitative PCR analysis of Tubb3 expression revealed up to 4.5-fold increases in conversion rates compared to non-deformed controls.  

Epigenetic changes following nuclear deformation were characterized through multiple approaches. Immunostaining showed reductions in H3K9me3 and increases in euchromatin marks such as H3K4me3. DNA methylation assays demonstrated decreased 5-methylcytosine levels, particularly at promoter regions of neuronal genes. The role of H3K9me3 in iN conversion was further tested using specific histone methyltransferase and demethylase inhibitors, confirming its importance as a barrier to reprogramming.  

Mechanistic studies investigated ion channel involvement in mechanotransduction and observed deformation effects on lamin A/C phosphorylation and lamin B1 behavior. Changes in LADs and nuclear lamina interactions were quantified, with siRNA knockdown of lamin A/C shown to mimic mechanical squeezing effects. The technology was validated across different cell types and scaled up using a higher-throughput microfluidic device design supported by fluid flow simulations.  

### Example 2: Reduction of Intracellular Tension and Cell Adhesion Promotes Open Chromatin Structure and Enhances Cell Reprogramming  

Cell reprogramming holds immense potential for regenerative medicine but faces limitations from inefficient direct conversion strategies. This example explores how modulating mechanotransduction pathways through intracellular tension reduction can enhance reprogramming efficiency by promoting a more open chromatin structure.  

The role of focal adhesions and the cytoskeleton in direct reprogramming was investigated using atomic force microscopy (AFM) to measure changes in cell stiffness during the process. RNA sequencing analysis of Ascl1-transduced fibroblasts revealed upregulation of genes related to actin cytoskeleton organization and cell contraction, suggesting mechanical phenotype changes are integral to early reprogramming stages.  

Reduction of cytoskeletal tension using blebbistatin, a non-muscle myosin II inhibitor, enhanced iN reprogramming in a dose-dependent manner. Optimal concentrations increased conversion efficiency by approximately 4.5-fold while maintaining cell viability. Complementary experiments with Rho-kinase inhibitors showed similar effects, while microtubule disruptors decreased efficiency, indicating actin-specific mechanisms.  

Time-course studies determined that tension reduction was most effective when applied during early reprogramming phases (days 1-5). Electrophysiological characterization confirmed that derived iN cells were functionally mature, with spontaneous action potentials and synaptic activity comparable to controls.  

Cytoskeletal disruption modulated expression of both fibroblast and neuronal markers. Blebbistatin treatment reduced mesenchymal marker expression (calponin, α-SMA) while upregulating neuronal genes (Ascl1, Brn2, Myt1l). Single-cell RNA sequencing revealed increased percentages of cells expressing neuronal markers and downregulation of heterochromatin-related genes in tension-reduced conditions.  

Global chromatin changes were observed through immunofluorescence and enzyme activity assays. Tension reduction increased histone acetylation (AcH3) and H3K4 methylation while decreasing heterochromatin marks (H3K27me3, H3K9me3). These changes correlated with increased HAT and H3K4-specific HMT activity, decreased HDAC and HDM activity, and reduced DNA methylation.  

ATAC-seq analysis demonstrated increased chromatin accessibility at neuronal gene promoters (Ascl1, Myt1l, Tubb3) following tension reduction. ChIP-qPCR confirmed localized increases in active marks at these loci. Parallel experiments with FAK inhibition showed similar epigenetic changes and reprogramming enhancement, suggesting common mechanotransduction pathways.  

Biomaterial approaches to reduce cell adhesion, including microgrooved PDMS and binary colloidal crystals, replicated the effects of chemical tension reduction. These engineered substrates decreased phosphorylated FAK expression and increased reprogramming efficiency, demonstrating the potential for scaffold-based enhancement of cell conversion.  

### Example 3: 3D Spheroid Culture to Increase Cell Reprogramming Efficiency  

Three-dimensional spheroid culture represents an alternative approach to enhance cell reprogramming efficiency. This example details methods for forming fibroblast spheroids in microfluidic systems and analyzing their conversion patterns.  

The spheroid culture method involves seeding cells in non-adherent conditions within specially designed microchambers. Over 24-48 hours, cells self-assemble into compact spheroids with defined cell-cell contacts. Immunostaining for Tuj1 reveals enhanced neuronal marker expression in spheroid cultures compared to monolayer controls.  

Reprogramming efficiency evaluation shows more uniform conversion within spheroids, with reduced heterogeneity in marker expression. Spatial analysis demonstrates concentric patterns of Tuj1 positivity, suggesting microenvironmental influences on conversion rates.  

Inhibition of TGF-β and BMP signaling pathways synergizes with spheroid culture to further enhance reprogramming. Small molecule inhibitors added during spheroid formation reduce expression of mesenchymal markers while promoting neuronal gene activation. The combined approach of 3D culture and pathway inhibition yields the highest conversion efficiencies in the system.  

### Example 4: Mechano-Epigenetic Mapping for Guided Gene Activation and Silencing  

This example describes a mechano-epigenetic mapping method that combines mechanical deformation with CRISPR-mediated gene regulation. The approach enables targeted activation or silencing of specific genes based on their mechanical responsiveness.  

The method involves first characterizing chromatin accessibility changes induced by mechanical deformation through ATAC-seq analysis. This creates a map of genomic regions that show increased accessibility following deformation. CRISPR components are then designed to target these mechanically sensitive regions for precise gene editing.  

Illustrative experiments demonstrate successful activation of neuronal genes (Ascl1, NeuroD1) and silencing of fibroblast genes (α-SMA, calponin) using this approach. The combination of mechanical priming and CRISPR targeting achieves more potent and persistent effects than either method alone, with reduced off-target activity compared to conventional gene editing.  

### Example 5: Matrix Stiffness Regulates Epigenetic State and Cell Reprogramming  

The influence of extracellular matrix stiffness on reprogramming efficiency is examined in this example. Polyacrylamide (PAAm) gels of varying stiffness are fabricated and characterized for mechanical properties.  

Fibroblasts cultured on soft (0.5-1 kPa) substrates show reduced cell spreading and nuclear volume compared to stiff (10-20 kPa) substrates. Cell cycle analysis reveals stiffness-dependent progression patterns, with soft substrates promoting G1 arrest that may facilitate reprogramming.  

Reprogramming efficiency follows a biphasic relationship with substrate stiffness, peaking at intermediate values (5-8 kPa). This optimal range maximizes Ascl1 expression while maintaining cell viability. Derived iN cells exhibit mature neuronal morphology and express characteristic markers (Tuj1, MAP2, synapsin).  

Histone acetylation and chromatin accessibility analyses demonstrate stiffness-dependent epigenetic changes. Softer substrates increase H3 and H4 acetylation while decreasing DNA methylation. ChIP-qPCR shows stiffness-mediated changes in active marks at neuronal gene promoters.  

HAT activity assays reveal increased enzyme activity on optimal stiffness substrates, correlating with reprogramming efficiency. The findings establish substrate stiffness as a critical parameter for designing biomaterials that enhance cell conversion through mechano-epigenetic regulation.  

### Example 6: Viscoelasticity of Matrix Regulates Epigenetic State and Cell Reprogramming  

This example extends the mechanical regulation concept to viscoelastic matrix properties. Hydrogels with controlled viscoelasticity are fabricated by varying crosslinking density and polymer composition.  

Rheological characterization confirms distinct viscoelastic profiles for the hydrogel series. Reprogramming efficiency shows strong dependence on loss modulus, with optimal values promoting highest conversion rates. Cells on viscoelastic substrates exhibit distinct nuclear morphology and chromatin organization compared to elastic controls.  

Epigenetic analyses reveal viscoelasticity-dependent changes in histone marks and chromatin accessibility. HDAC activity shows inverse correlation with loss modulus, suggesting viscous dissipation mechanisms in epigenetic regulation.  

Mechanistic studies identify cytoskeletal reorganization and nuclear deformation as key mediators of viscoelasticity effects. The results provide guidelines for designing viscoelastic matrices that enhance reprogramming through controlled mechanical signaling.
