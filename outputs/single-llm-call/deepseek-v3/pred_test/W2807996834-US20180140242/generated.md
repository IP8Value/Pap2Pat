# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to methods and systems for identifying patients with schizophrenia who are at risk of psychotic relapse. More particularly, the invention provides a novel approach combining targeted symptom monitoring through a subset of Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) items with biomarker analysis to enable early detection of impending relapse. The invention further encompasses administration of pharmaceutical interventions upon detection of relapse risk factors, with the goal of preventing full psychotic episodes and improving long-term patient outcomes. This technological solution addresses critical limitations in current clinical practice by enabling frequent, remote monitoring of specific symptoms most predictive of relapse through electronic assessment tools.

## BACKGROUND

Schizophrenia is a chronic and severe mental disorder affecting approximately 1% of the population worldwide. The disease is characterized by distortions in thinking, perception, emotions, language, sense of self and behavior. Common symptoms include hallucinations, delusions, thought disorders, movement disorders, flat affect, reduced feelings of pleasure, cognitive difficulties and lack of motivation. While antipsychotic medications can control symptoms for many patients, the disease course is frequently marked by periods of relapse where symptoms worsen significantly.

The consequences of psychotic relapse in schizophrenia are profound and multifaceted. Repeated relapses contribute to treatment resistance, progressive cognitive and functional impairment, diminished quality of life, and increased economic burden on healthcare systems. Most alarmingly, schizophrenia carries a high risk of suicide, with approximately 4.9% of patients eventually taking their own lives. These risks underscore the critical importance of relapse prevention as a cornerstone of schizophrenia management.

Current clinical practice faces significant challenges in detecting early signs of relapse. Standard care typically involves brief assessments during infrequent outpatient visits, often spaced weeks or months apart. While comprehensive scales like the PANSS exist to assess the full spectrum of schizophrenia symptoms, their time-consuming nature prevents frequent administration in routine practice. More commonly, clinicians rely on global assessment scores that fail to track individual symptom changes systematically. This approach is inadequate for detecting the subtle but important symptom changes that precede relapse.

A growing body of research has identified potential early warning signs of relapse, though findings have been inconsistent across studies. Prospective studies have identified psychotic symptoms like hallucinations and suspiciousness as common precursors, while retrospective reports emphasize non-psychotic symptoms such as sleep disturbances and difficulty concentrating. The inconsistency in findings may stem from differences in monitored symptoms, assessment frequency, and study methodologies. Furthermore, the abrupt onset of relapse relative to standard assessment intervals means many early signs go undetected until full relapse occurs.

Recent advances in mobile health technologies present new opportunities to overcome these limitations. Smartphones, tablets, and wearable devices can collect both passive behavioral data and active self-reports on symptoms. However, practical implementation requires careful selection of symptoms to monitor, as cognitive impairments and fatigue make lengthy assessments burdensome for patients. There remains an unmet need for a systematic, technology-enabled approach that focuses monitoring on the most predictive symptoms while maintaining feasibility for frequent administration.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel method for identifying schizophrenia patients at risk of psychotic relapse through targeted monitoring of specific symptoms and biomarkers. The method utilizes a carefully selected subset of Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) items that have been shown to exhibit the greatest changes prior to relapse, enabling more efficient and focused symptom tracking. These items include P1 (delusions), P2 (conceptual disorganization), P3 (hallucinations), P4 (excitement), P6 (suspiciousness) from the positive symptoms subscale, and G2 (anxiety) and G4 (tension) from the general psychopathology subscale.

The invention encompasses a technology-enabled remote assessment system that facilitates frequent monitoring of these key symptoms through electronic devices. The system employs specialized algorithms to analyze symptom trajectories and detect patterns indicative of impending relapse. When such patterns are identified, the system generates alerts to healthcare providers and may trigger administration of preventive pharmaceutical interventions.

A critical aspect of the invention is the identification of specific temporal patterns in symptom worsening. Through advanced modeling of longitudinal data, the invention demonstrates that the selected PANSS items typically begin increasing approximately 7-10 days before relapse, reaching clinically significant levels about 0.3-1.2 days prior to relapse. This precise understanding of symptom trajectories enables optimally timed interventions.

The invention further incorporates biomarker analysis to enhance predictive accuracy. Specific biomarkers associated with increased relapse risk are monitored alongside symptom changes, creating a multidimensional assessment of relapse risk. The combined analysis of symptom trajectories and biomarker levels provides superior predictive power compared to either measure alone.

Upon detection of elevated relapse risk, the invention provides for administration of pharmaceutical agents such as antipsychotic medications. The timing and dosage of these interventions are optimized based on the severity and progression rate of symptom changes. The ultimate goal of the invention is to decrease the likelihood of full relapse through early, targeted interventions informed by comprehensive monitoring data.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive method for identifying schizophrenia patients at risk of psychotic relapse through systematic monitoring of specific symptoms and biomarkers. The patient population for this method includes individuals with a diagnosis of schizophrenia who have achieved clinical stability following an acute episode. The method is particularly suited for patients in maintenance treatment who are at risk of future relapse.

Central to the invention is the measurement of symptom severity using a targeted subset of PANSS items. The full PANSS assessment, while comprehensive, is impractical for frequent administration due to its length and complexity. The invention solves this problem by focusing on seven specific PANSS items that demonstrate the greatest predictive value for impending relapse. These items were identified through rigorous analysis of longitudinal data from clinical trials, showing consistent and substantial increases prior to relapse events.

The invention incorporates remote detection of symptom severity through electronic devices such as smartphones, tablets, or specialized medical monitoring systems. These devices present patients with simplified versions of the selected PANSS items, adapted for self-assessment while maintaining clinical validity. The frequency of assessments is optimized based on the known trajectories of symptom worsening, typically ranging from daily to weekly depending on individual risk factors.

Data collected through electronic assessments are transmitted securely to healthcare providers for analysis. Sophisticated algorithms compare current symptom levels with baseline measurements and previous assessments, identifying statistically significant increases that may signal impending relapse. The system generates automated alerts when predefined risk thresholds are exceeded, prompting clinical evaluation and potential intervention.

The invention further encompasses administration of pharmaceutical agents when relapse risk is detected. The specific medication, dosage, and route of administration are determined based on the severity and progression of symptom changes, as well as individual patient factors. Options include oral antipsychotics, long-acting injectable formulations, or adjunctive medications targeting specific symptoms like anxiety or insomnia.

### PANSS

The Positive and Negative Syndrome Scale (PANSS) is a well-established instrument for measuring symptom severity in schizophrenia patients. The full PANSS consists of 30 items divided into three subscales: seven items for positive symptoms, seven for negative symptoms, and sixteen for general psychopathology. Each item is scored from 1 (absent) to 7 (extreme), with higher scores indicating greater symptom severity. Administration typically involves a clinical interview lasting 30-45 minutes, supplemented by information from family members or caregivers.

While the PANSS provides comprehensive assessment, its length limits feasibility for frequent administration in clinical practice. The present invention addresses this limitation by utilizing a subset of PANSS items that show the strongest association with impending relapse. Analysis of clinical trial data revealed that seven specific items - P1, P2, P3, P4, P6, G2, and G4 - consistently demonstrated the greatest increases prior to relapse events.

The predictive value of these items stems from their sensitivity to early psychotic decompensation. Five items (P1, P2, P3, P4, P6) belong to the positive symptoms subscale, reflecting core psychotic features that typically worsen during relapse. The remaining two items (G2, G4) assess anxiety and tension, representing affective components that frequently precede psychotic exacerbation. This combination captures both the psychotic and non-psychotic dimensions of relapse precursors.

The invention overcomes limitations of traditional PANSS administration by enabling remote detection of symptom severity through electronic devices. Adapted versions of the selected PANSS items are presented via smartphone applications or web-based interfaces, allowing patients to complete brief assessments more frequently than possible with in-person evaluations. The electronic format maintains the clinical relevance of the original PANSS while improving feasibility for routine monitoring.

Electronic questionnaires for remote detection are designed to maximize compliance and accuracy. Items are presented with simplified language and visual analog scales when appropriate, reducing cognitive burden while preserving measurement validity. The system may incorporate multimedia elements such as voice recordings or video clips to enhance understanding of symptom ratings.

Data collected through electronic assessments are transmitted securely to centralized servers for analysis. Advanced algorithms compare current scores with individual baselines and previous measurements, identifying statistically significant increases that may signal impending relapse. The system accounts for normal symptom fluctuations while detecting clinically meaningful changes.

When significant symptom worsening is detected, the system generates automated alerts to designated healthcare providers. These alerts include quantitative data on symptom progression and risk estimates based on predictive models. Providers can then initiate appropriate interventions, potentially preventing full relapse through timely medication adjustments or other clinical actions.

The invention specifically utilizes PANSS items P01-P04, P06, G02, and G04 as core indicators of relapse risk. In some embodiments, additional items P07 and G09 may be included to enhance sensitivity. Increases in these items, particularly when occurring in combination, provide strong evidence of impending relapse. The system tracks both absolute score increases and rate of change, as rapid symptom worsening may indicate higher urgency for intervention.

Remote detection offers several advantages over traditional clinic-based assessment. The ability to monitor symptoms frequently (even daily) enables detection of subtle changes that might be missed during monthly clinic visits. Electronic data collection also reduces recall bias and provides objective documentation of symptom progression over time. Furthermore, the system can incorporate passive data from wearable devices (e.g., sleep patterns, activity levels) to complement self-reported symptoms.

The invention includes methods for increasing assessment frequency during periods of elevated risk. When early signs of worsening are detected, the system may automatically increase the frequency of assessments to monitor progression more closely. This adaptive approach balances comprehensive monitoring with patient burden, optimizing the trade-off between detection sensitivity and feasibility.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention and demonstrate its practical application in clinical settings. These examples are provided for illustrative purposes only and do not limit the scope of the invention.

### PANSS: Methods

Data from three large-scale relapse prevention studies were pooled to analyze symptom changes preceding psychotic relapse. The studies shared similar designs involving open-label stabilization phases followed by randomized, double-blind withdrawal periods. Key differences included the specific antipsychotic formulations studied (oral extended-release paliperidone, 1-month injectable paliperidone palmitate, and 3-month injectable paliperidone palmitate) and some procedural details of the stabilization phases.

All studies employed frequent PANSS assessments (at least every 4 weeks, with more frequent assessments in early phases) to monitor symptom changes. This design feature enabled detailed analysis of symptom trajectories leading up to relapse events. Patients entering the double-blind phases had demonstrated at least 8 weeks of clinical stability, creating an appropriate population for studying early relapse signs.

Previous findings from these studies had established the efficacy of paliperidone formulations in delaying time to relapse compared to placebo. The current analysis focused specifically on identifying which PANSS items showed the greatest changes prior to relapse, regardless of treatment assignment. This approach allowed identification of symptom patterns predictive of relapse across different therapeutic contexts.

### PANSS: Definition of Relapse

The primary efficacy variable across all studies was time to first relapse during the double-blind phase. Relapse was defined by any of the following criteria: (1) psychiatric hospitalization for symptom decompensation; (2) clinically significant suicidal/homicidal ideation or aggressive behavior; (3) significant increase in PANSS total score (≥25% increase from baseline for patients with baseline score >40, or ≥10-point increase for those with baseline score ≤40) on two consecutive assessments within 7 days; or (4) clinically meaningful worsening in specific PANSS items (P1, P2, P3, P6, P7, G8) as defined by predetermined threshold increases.

For analysis purposes, patients meeting any of these criteria were considered relapsed, while those completing the studies without meeting these criteria were considered non-relapsed. Patients with ambiguous relapse status (e.g., meeting some but not all criteria) were excluded to maintain analytical rigor. This standardized definition ensured consistency in identifying relapse events across the pooled dataset.

### PANSS: Statistical Analysis

Individual PANSS items were analyzed to identify those showing the greatest changes at relapse. Items were ranked by mean change scores from baseline to relapse, with separate analyses conducted for different relapse definitions and treatment groups. This approach revealed consistent patterns across subgroups, supporting the generalizability of findings.

Linear mixed effect models and non-linear mixed effect models were applied to characterize symptom trajectories leading to relapse. Patient observations were aligned by days relative to relapse rather than study visit schedules, enabling precise modeling of temporal patterns. The linear models used polynomial functions of varying orders (selected by information criteria) to fit symptom progression curves, while non-linear models employed exponential functions with interpretable parameters.

Model parameters included subject-level random effects to account for within-patient correlations across repeated measurements. For the non-linear models, key parameters included: (a) the rate of symptom increase (a), (b) the time before relapse when symptoms reached clinically significant levels (b), and (c) baseline symptom levels (c). These parameters provided clinically meaningful metrics for understanding relapse precursors.

### PANSS: Demographics and Characteristics of Patients Experienced a Relapse During Double-Blind Phase

Among 907 randomized patients included in the analysis, 267 experienced relapse during the double-blind phases. Relapse reasons were distributed as follows: 63 patients (23.6%) had psychiatric events (hospitalization, suicidal/homicidal ideation, or aggressive behavior), 65 (24.3%) met PANSS item worsening criteria, and 139 (52.1%) met PANSS total score criteria without item-level worsening.

Demographic and clinical characteristics were similar between relapsed and non-relapsed patients, with the exception of slightly higher baseline PANSS scores in the relapsed group (mean 54.9 vs. 52.5, p=0.002). This suggests that while baseline symptom severity may influence relapse risk, the identified symptom changes represent true precursors rather than artifacts of initial severity differences.

### Example 5

Analysis of PANSS item changes revealed seven items with consistently greater increases at relapse: P1 (delusions), P6 (suspiciousness), P3 (hallucinations), G2 (anxiety), P4 (excitement), G4 (tension), and P2 (conceptual disorganization). These items showed mean increases >1 point at relapse, significantly greater than changes in other items (p<0.001 for all comparisons).

Trajectory modeling demonstrated that these items began increasing approximately 7-10 days before relapse, reaching clinically significant levels (1-point increase) 0.3-1.2 days prior to relapse. The exponential model parameter estimates were: P1 (b=-1.17), P6 (b=-0.82), P3 (b=-0.74), G2 (b=-0.63), G4 (b=-0.44), P4 (b=-0.33), and P2 (b=-0.33). Negative b values indicate symptoms exceeded 1-point increases before relapse.

Changes were most pronounced in the final days before relapse, with limited increases observed at the last pre-relapse visit (typically 2-4 weeks prior). This pattern was consistent across different relapse definitions and treatment groups, supporting the robustness of findings. The results have important implications for early detection, suggesting that weekly or more frequent monitoring is needed to capture emerging relapse signs.
