## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of peptide therapeutics and specifically to growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) receptor antagonists and their use in treating pulmonary fibrosis, reducing lung inflammation, and inhibiting tumor growth. More particularly, the invention provides novel GHRH antagonist peptides, pharmaceutical compositions containing them, and methods for their therapeutic application.  

## BACKGROUND  

Growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH) is a hypothalamic peptide that stimulates the synthesis and release of growth hormone (GH) from the pituitary gland. Beyond its endocrine functions, GHRH acts as a local growth factor in various tissues through autocrine and paracrine mechanisms. The GHRH receptor (GHRH-R) is expressed not only in the pituitary but also in peripheral tissues including the lungs, where it plays roles in cellular proliferation, inflammation, and tissue remodeling.  

GHRH and its receptor have been implicated in the pathophysiology of cancer. Many tumors produce GHRH locally, which stimulates tumor growth through autocrine/paracrine mechanisms. GHRH receptor antagonists have shown potent antitumor effects in various cancer models by blocking these growth-promoting signals.  

Emerging evidence indicates that GHRH signaling also participates in pulmonary fibrosis and lung inflammation. Pulmonary fibrosis is a progressive, often fatal condition characterized by excessive deposition of extracellular matrix and scarring of lung tissue. Current treatments are limited, highlighting the need for novel therapeutic approaches. The present invention demonstrates that GHRH receptor antagonists can effectively reduce lung inflammation and fibrosis while also inhibiting tumor growth, providing multifunctional therapeutic agents for these conditions.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses novel GHRH receptor antagonist peptides that exhibit potent biological activities. These antagonists effectively treat pulmonary fibrosis by reducing lung inflammation and scarring. The peptides also demonstrate significant antitumor activity against various cancers, particularly lung cancers.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1) GHRH antagonist peptides, particularly MIA-602 and related analogs, that bind to GHRH receptors with high affinity;  
2) Methods for treating pulmonary fibrosis by administering these antagonists to reduce inflammation and fibrotic tissue formation;  
3) Methods for reducing lung inflammation through GHRH receptor blockade;  
4) Methods for inhibiting tumor growth, especially in lung cancers, using these peptide antagonists.  

The GHRH antagonists of the invention modulate multiple signaling pathways involved in fibrosis and cancer, including PI3K/Akt, ERK, and STAT3 pathways. They also affect mitochondrial function and oxidative stress, contributing to their therapeutic effects. Pharmaceutical compositions containing these peptides and methods for their use in treating pulmonary and oncologic conditions form important embodiments of the invention.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Synthesis of GHRH Antagonists  

GHRH antagonists were synthesized using Fmoc-chemistry solid phase peptide synthesis. The C-terminus was modified as methylamide or ethylamide. Side chains were protected with appropriate groups. Coupling reactions used HBTU/HOBt activation. After chain assembly, peptides were deprotected and cleaved from resin, then purified by reverse-phase HPLC. Analytical HPLC confirmed purity >95%. Mass spectrometry verified molecular weights.  

Specific analogs synthesized included:  
AVR-235: PhAc-Ada-Tyr-DArg-Asp-Ala-Ile-5FPhe-Thr-Ala-Har-Tyr(Me)-His-Orn-Val-Leu-Abu-Gln-Leu-Ser-Ala-His-Orn-Leu-Leu-Gln-Asp-Ile-Nle-D-Arg-Ha-NH2  
AVR-354: Similar sequence with variations at positions 6, 10, and 15  
AVR-333, AVR-352, and AVR-353 were prepared with analogous methods  

### Example 2: Evaluation of Inhibitory Effects of GHRH Antagonists In Vitro  

Human cancer cell lines (lung, prostate, colorectal) were cultured and treated with GHRH antagonists at 1-10 μM concentrations. Cell viability was assessed after 72 hours using MTT assay. MIA-602 inhibited proliferation by 40-80% across cell lines. Receptor binding assays showed high affinity (IC50 <1 nM) for GHRH-R.  

### Example 3: Evaluation of Tumor Inhibitory Activity of GHRH Antagonists In Vivo  

Nude mice bearing human lung cancer xenografts received daily subcutaneous MIA-602 (5 μg). Tumor volumes were measured weekly. After 4 weeks, MIA-602 treated mice showed 60-70% reduction in tumor growth compared to controls (p<0.01).  

### Example 4: Binding Affinities of AVR GHRH Antagonists  

Competitive binding assays using 125I-GHRH showed AVR peptides displaced labeled GHRH with IC50 values of 0.2-0.8 nM, indicating high receptor affinity.  

### Example 5: Growth Hormone-Releasing Hormone Receptor Antagonist Modulates Lung Inflammation and Fibrosis Due to Bleomycin  

C57BL/6 mice received intratracheal bleomycin to induce pulmonary fibrosis. MIA-602 (10 μg/day) or vehicle was administered subcutaneously. After 28 days:  
- Micro-CT showed reduced fibrosis in treated mice  
- Hydroxyproline content (collagen marker) was 40% lower with MIA-602  
- Histopathology revealed less inflammation and scarring  
- Lung fibroblasts showed increased apoptosis  
- RNA-seq demonstrated downregulation of fibrotic and inflammatory genes  

These data establish that GHRH receptor antagonists like MIA-602 effectively treat pulmonary fibrosis and lung inflammation while also inhibiting tumor growth.
