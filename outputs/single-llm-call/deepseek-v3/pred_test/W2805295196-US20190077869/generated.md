# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of immunotherapy for hematologic malignancies, particularly through the use of immune-checkpoint inhibitors targeting novel regulatory pathways in blood-borne cancers. Specifically, the invention provides immunomodulating compounds that disrupt inhibitory interactions between poliovirus receptor (PVR/CD155), poliovirus receptor-related 2 (PVRL2/CD112), and their cognate receptor T cell immunoreceptor with Ig and ITIM domains (TIGIT), as well as inhibitors targeting Galectin-9 and TIM-3 pathways. These therapeutic agents demonstrate enhanced efficacy when combined with bispecific T-cell engagers or chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells, offering improved treatment options for acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and other hematologic malignancies.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cancer immunotherapy has emerged as a transformative approach for treating malignancies by harnessing the power of the immune system. Immune-checkpoint blockade represents a paradigm shift in oncology, with antibodies targeting CTLA-4 and PD-1/PD-L1 pathways demonstrating remarkable clinical success across multiple tumor types. However, the majority of patients with hematologic malignancies, particularly acute myeloid leukemia, fail to respond adequately to current checkpoint inhibitors, highlighting the need for novel therapeutic targets.  

The activation of T cells requires a carefully orchestrated balance between co-stimulatory and co-inhibitory signals. The B7 family members CD80 and CD86 provide critical co-stimulation through CD28, while simultaneously delivering inhibitory signals via CTLA-4. Similarly, the TNF receptor superfamily members including OX40, 4-1BB, and CD27 regulate T cell responses through complex ligand-receptor interactions. Cytokine signals further modulate immune responses, with IL-2, IFN-γ, and TGF-β playing pivotal roles in shaping anti-tumor immunity.  

Despite these advances, tumors develop multiple mechanisms of immune resistance, including upregulation of alternative checkpoint molecules. The TIGIT pathway has recently emerged as a critical regulator of immune exhaustion, with its ligands PVR and PVRL2 being overexpressed in various malignancies. Similarly, the Galectin-9/TIM-3 axis contributes to immune suppression in the tumor microenvironment. Current cancer immunotherapy approaches often fail to address these redundant inhibitory pathways, necessitating the development of novel combination strategies.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides immunomodulating compounds that specifically target the immune-checkpoint molecules CD112 (PVRL2), CD155 (PVR), TIGIT, Galectin-9, and TIM-3 for the treatment of blood-borne cancers. These inhibitors include antibody constructs, soluble receptors, small molecules, RNA interference agents, and gene editing systems that disrupt the immunosuppressive signals mediated by these pathways.  

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions combining these inhibitors with CAR T cells or bispecific antibody constructs. Particularly, CAR T cells targeting CD33, CD19, or Flt3 demonstrate synergistic activity when administered with inhibitors of the CD112/CD155/TIGIT or Galectin-9/TIM-3 axes. The antibody constructs of the invention include bispecific molecules engaging both tumor antigens and CD3 on T cells, with optimized linkers and binding domains for enhanced efficacy.  

Specific embodiments include monoclonal antibodies or antibody fragments that bind to CD112, CD155, TIGIT, Galectin-9, or TIM-3 with high affinity and block their immunosuppressive interactions. The invention also provides small molecule inhibitors, RNAi agents, and CRISPR/Cas9 systems designed to reduce expression of these checkpoint molecules. Immunostimulants that counteract the inhibitory signals of these pathways are additionally disclosed.  

The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention may modulate intracellular signaling pathways downstream of these immune checkpoints, thereby reversing T cell exhaustion and restoring anti-tumor immunity. The inhibitors are formulated for optimal delivery and pharmacokinetics, with specific dosing regimens developed for hematologic malignancies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following detailed description sets forth embodiments of the invention, but it should be understood that the invention is not limited to these specific embodiments. Prior art attempts to target single checkpoint pathways have shown limited efficacy in blood cancers, whereas the current invention addresses multiple redundant inhibitory mechanisms simultaneously.  

The invention is based on the discovery that CD112 and CD155 serve as critical immune checkpoints in acute myeloid leukemia, with their blockade significantly enhancing T cell-mediated killing of AML cells. Similarly, modulation of the Galectin-9/TIM-3 axis provides complementary therapeutic benefits. These targets demonstrate particularly high expression on AML cell lines and primary blasts compared to normal hematopoietic cells, providing a therapeutic window for targeted intervention.  

Protein expression analysis reveals that CD112 and CD155 are present on the majority of CD33+ AML blasts, including leukemia-initiating cells characterized by CD34+/CD38- phenotype. Their expression correlates with negative prognosis in AML patients, independent of other clinical factors. Blockade of these pathways, either through antibody-mediated inhibition of the ligands or receptor, or through genetic knockout using CRISPR/Cas9 technology, significantly enhances T cell cytotoxicity against AML cells.  

The term "inhibit" or "inhibiting" as used herein refers to the partial or complete blockade of the interaction between immune-checkpoint ligands and their receptors, or the reduction in expression or signaling activity of these molecules. "Blood-borne cancer" encompasses hematologic malignancies including acute myeloid leukemia, acute lymphoblastic leukemia, myelodysplastic syndromes, and non-Hodgkin lymphomas, though the invention may also have application in solid tumors.  

Acute myeloid leukemia as described herein includes both de novo and secondary AML, as well as refractory and relapsed forms of the disease. The immune-checkpoint inhibitors of the invention may be administered as monotherapy or in combination with standard treatments including chemotherapy, radiation therapy, or hematopoietic stem cell transplantation.  

Antibody constructs according to the invention include full-length immunoglobulins, Fab fragments, F(ab')2 fragments, Fv fragments, single-chain Fv (scFv), and bispecific antibody formats. These may be produced through recombinant DNA technology in mammalian cells or bacteria, with optimization of binding affinity and effector functions. Humanized variants of non-human antibodies and affinity-matured mutants are particularly preferred embodiments.  

The invention further provides soluble proteins that act as decoy receptors for CD112, CD155, Galectin-9 or TIM-3, as well as polypeptide fragments designed through computational modeling to disrupt these interactions. Small molecule inhibitors with appropriate pharmacokinetic properties for hematologic malignancies are additionally disclosed.  

RNA interference agents including siRNA and shRNA designed to reduce expression of these checkpoint molecules represent another embodiment. CRISPR/Cas9 systems for genetic knockout of CD112, CD155, Galectin-9 or TIM-3 genes in tumor cells are also provided, with specific guide RNA sequences optimized for efficient editing.  

CAR T cells of the invention incorporate antigen-binding domains targeting CD33, CD19, or Flt3, combined with co-stimulatory domains that enhance persistence and activity. These may be administered concurrently with checkpoint inhibitors to overcome tumor-mediated immune suppression.  

Bispecific T cell engagers (BiTEs) according to the invention include constructs with CD3-binding domains paired with tumor antigen-binding domains, connected by optimized peptide linkers. Particular embodiments combine CD33/CD3 or CD19/CD3 targeting with checkpoint blockade for enhanced efficacy.  

The therapeutic methods involve determining an effective dose that provides clinical benefit without excessive toxicity, with consideration of pharmacokinetic parameters including half-life, volume of distribution, and serum binding. Administration may be parenteral, including intravenous infusion of sterile formulations containing appropriate carriers, buffers, and stabilizers.  

Pharmaceutical compositions may include lyophilized preparations for reconstitution, sustained-release formulations, or depot injections. Combination therapies with conventional AML treatments are contemplated, with specific dosing schedules developed to maximize synergistic effects while minimizing adverse events.  

### Examples  

Specific embodiments of the invention were validated through extensive experimentation. Patient samples were analyzed for expression of immune checkpoint molecules, revealing that high expression of CD112 and CD155 independently predicted poor prognosis in AML. In vitro killing assays demonstrated that blockade of these pathways significantly enhanced T cell-mediated lysis of AML cell lines and primary blasts.  

CRISPR/Cas9-generated knockout cells confirmed the specificity of the approach, with CD112/CD155-deficient AML cells showing increased susceptibility to immune attack. These effects were independent of antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity mechanisms. The therapeutic potential was further validated in xenograft models, where knockout of CD112/CD155 prolonged survival of mice receiving human T cells.  

Protein expression analysis revealed that CD112 and CD155 were present on the majority of AML blasts, including leukemia-initiating cells. Their blockade enhanced the activity of CD33/CD3 BiTE antibody constructs, with increased granzyme B release confirming T cell activation. These findings support the development of combination therapies targeting these novel checkpoints for improved AML treatment.  

The prognostic impact of these molecules was established through analysis of two independent patient cohorts, confirming CD112 and CD155 as independent negative prognostic markers in multivariate analysis. This clinical correlation strengthens the therapeutic rationale for targeting these pathways in AML immunotherapy.
