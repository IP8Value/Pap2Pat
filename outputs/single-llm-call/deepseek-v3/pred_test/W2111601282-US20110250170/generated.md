## DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to novel methods and compositions for the treatment of tumours, particularly glioblastoma, through the selective delivery of bioactive agents using monoclonal antibodies. Conventional cancer therapies, including chemotherapy, radiation, and surgery, often suffer from systemic toxicity, limited efficacy, and tumour recurrence. The disclosed invention addresses these limitations by employing antibody-based strategies for targeted delivery to tumour-specific antigens.  

A key strategy involves the use of monoclonal antibodies specific to tumour-associated antigens, such as tenascin-C, for selective delivery of therapeutic agents. Tenascin-C is an extracellular matrix protein overexpressed in gliomas and other malignancies, making it an ideal target for antibody-mediated therapy. The invention utilizes monoclonal antibodies, particularly human monoclonal antibody fragments, that bind to the A1 domain of tenascin-C (e.g., F16 antibody) to deliver immunocytokines or other bioactive agents to the tumour microenvironment.  

The invention further describes derivatives of monoclonal antibodies specific to splice isoforms of tenascin-C, enhancing tumour specificity. Clinical development of immunocytokines (e.g., F16-IL2) and radioimmunoconjugates is detailed, demonstrating their efficacy in preclinical models. The L19 antibody, which binds the ED-B domain of fibronectin, is also discussed as an alternative targeting moiety. The ED-B domain, like tenascin-C, is overexpressed in high-grade astrocytomas, providing another viable target for antibody-based therapy.  

Immunocytokines comprising L19 conjugated with IL-12 or F16-IL2 are disclosed for cancer treatment, particularly for central nervous system tumours such as glioblastoma. The standard of care for glioblastoma, including alkylating agents like temozolomide, is limited by resistance and recurrence. The invention overcomes these challenges by combining temozolomide with immunocytokines (e.g., F16-IL2), resulting in synergistic tumour eradication.  

Microvascular proliferation in glioblastoma is a hallmark of tumour aggressiveness, and the invention exploits this by targeting endothelial markers (e.g., tenascin-C or ED-B) with monoclonal antibodies. Radioimmunotherapy and combination therapy with alkylating agents and antibody-IL2 conjugates are shown to enhance therapeutic outcomes. The first development of the invention involves a method of treating tumours using a chemotherapeutic agent (e.g., temozolomide) and an immunocytokine (e.g., F16-IL2). Subsequent developments refine targeting strategies, dosing regimens, and antibody formats to optimize efficacy and safety.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

Chemotherapeutic agents suitable for the invention include alkylating agents such as temozolomide, cyclophosphamide, and dacarbazine. Alkylating agents function by disrupting DNA replication, inducing apoptosis in rapidly dividing tumour cells. Prodrugs, such as temozolomide, are preferred due to their favourable pharmacokinetics and reduced systemic toxicity. Temozolomide, a DNA-methylating agent, is particularly effective against glioblastoma and other solid tumours.  

The antibody moiety of the invention targets tumour-associated antigens, including tenascin-C and fibronectin ED-B. Preferred antibodies include F16 (specific to tenascin-C A1 domain) and L19 (specific to fibronectin ED-B). The antibody 4A1-F16 is a particularly effective human monoclonal antibody fragment for targeting glioblastoma. Variants of these antibodies, including single-chain variable fragments (scFv) and small immunoproteins (SIP), are also disclosed.  

The VL CDR3 sequence (SEQ ID NO: 10) is critical for antigen binding. The VH and VL domains of the antibody comprise complementary determining regions (CDRs) that confer specificity. The VH CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3, along with the VL CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3, are detailed to define the binding epitope. Variants of these domains, including amino acid substitutions or deletions, retain binding affinity while optimizing stability.  

The antibody molecule may be formatted as an scFv, SIP, or homodimer. The scFv format links VH and VL domains via a peptide linker, while SIP formats include CH4 domains for enhanced serum half-life. The immunocytokine component comprises IL2 or other cytokines fused to the antibody via a peptide linker. Human IL2 (SEQ ID NO: X) is preferred, though variants with modified activity or stability are also claimed.  

Administration of the immunocytokine involves intravenous or intraperitoneal delivery, with dosing schedules tailored to tumour type and patient response. The invention is applicable to individuals diagnosed with glioblastoma or other tenascin-C-positive malignancies. Treatment regimens combine immunocytokines with alkylating agents, demonstrating synergistic tumour regression in preclinical models.  

## TERMINOLOGY  

### Antibody Molecule  

An antibody molecule refers to an immunoglobulin or functional fragment thereof, including Fab, F(ab')2, scFv, diabody, or domain antibodies (dAbs). Antibody fragments may be generated by enzymatic digestion (e.g., papain cleavage) or recombinant expression. Humanized or fully human antibodies minimize immunogenicity in clinical applications.  

Chimeric molecules, such as immunocytokines, fuse antibody fragments to cytokines (e.g., IL2) for targeted immune activation. Bispecific antibodies, including BiTE (bispecific T-cell engager) constructs, are also encompassed. Camelid VH dAbs and other engineered binding domains are included within the scope of "antibody molecule."  

### Antigen-Binding Site  

The antigen-binding site comprises the VH and VL CDRs that confer specificity to tenascin-C or ED-B. Engineering of antigen-binding sites may involve affinity maturation or stability enhancements. Multispecific binding sites (e.g., biparatopic antibodies) are also disclosed.  

## EXPERIMENTS  

### Summary  

The therapeutic properties of temozolomide combined with F16-IL2 were evaluated in glioblastoma xenografts. Combination therapy resulted in complete tumour regression and prolonged survival compared to monotherapy.  

### Immunohistochemistry on Human Glioblastoma Samples and on Glioblastoma Xenografts  

F16 antibody demonstrated strong staining of tenascin-C A1 domain in human glioblastoma specimens and U87MG xenografts, comparable to L19 (ED-B-specific antibody). Immunohistochemical procedures confirmed selective antigen expression in tumour vasculature.  

### Cell Lines and Animals  

U87MG glioblastoma cells were cultured and implanted subcutaneously or intracranially in nude mice. Animal studies were conducted under approved protocols.  

### Antibodies and Therapeutic Agents  

F16-IL2 immunocytokine and temozolomide were administered in combination, showing enhanced efficacy without added toxicity.  

### Biodistribution Experiments  

Radiolabelled F16-IL2 exhibited preferential tumour accumulation (2.3% ID/g) with high tumour-to-blood ratios (11.5).  

### Subcutaneous Glioma Mouse Models  

Combination therapy (F16-IL2 + temozolomide) eradicated subcutaneous tumours, with no recurrence observed for >160 days.  

### Immunofluorescence Assessment of Tumour-Infiltrating Cells and of Microvascular Density  

F16-IL2 increased infiltration of NK cells and macrophages into tumours, with no off-target inflammation in normal organs.  

### Statistical Analysis  

Kaplan-Meier survival curves confirmed superior outcomes for combination therapy (log-rank p < 0.002). Tumour growth retardation and apoptosis were significantly enhanced.
