# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of targeted protein degradation therapeutics, specifically Proteolysis Targeting Chimeras (PROTACs). PROTACs are heterobifunctional molecules designed to induce the degradation of specific target proteins through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. The invention particularly discloses a novel double-target PROTAC capable of simultaneously degrading Smad3 protein while stabilizing hypoxia-inducible factor 2α (HIF-2α), thereby providing a dual therapeutic effect for chronic kidney disease (CKD) and associated conditions.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) represents a significant global health burden, characterized by progressive loss of renal function and eventual renal fibrosis. Current treatment strategies for CKD primarily focus on managing symptoms and slowing disease progression through angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers, and dialysis. However, these approaches fail to address the underlying molecular mechanisms driving fibrosis, particularly the dysregulation of transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β)/Smad3 signaling, which promotes extracellular matrix deposition and epithelial-mesenchymal transition.  

PROTACs have emerged as a promising therapeutic modality by hijacking the ubiquitin-proteasome system to selectively degrade disease-causing proteins. Unlike traditional small-molecule inhibitors that merely block protein activity, PROTACs catalytically eliminate target proteins, offering prolonged efficacy at lower doses. Despite their potential, conventional PROTACs suffer from limitations, including off-target effects due to unintended stabilization of endogenous substrates of the recruited E3 ligase.  

The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing a double-target PROTAC that not only degrades Smad3 but also stabilizes HIF-2α, a protective factor in CKD. By selectively recruiting the von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) E3 ligase, the PROTAC simultaneously reduces fibrotic signaling while enhancing renal repair mechanisms, offering a novel approach to CKD treatment.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a double-target PROTAC comprising:  
1. A Smad3-binding small molecule compound (SMC) as the protein-of-interest (POI) ligand.  
2. A VHL-binding SMC as the E3 ligase-recruiting moiety.  
3. A chemical linker connecting the two SMCs, optimized for maximal ubiquitination efficiency.  

The PROTAC is constructed through a method comprising:  
- Selecting Smad3 as the target protein due to its central role in renal fibrosis.  
- Selecting VHL as the E3 ligase for its kidney-specific expression and role in HIF-α regulation.  
- Screening and identifying high-affinity SMCs for Smad3 and VHL.  
- Optimizing linker chemistry, with succinic acid ((CH2COOH)2) identified as the most effective.  
- Synthesizing the PROTAC in an S-chiral configuration for enhanced potency.  

The double-target PROTAC exhibits a unique dual mechanism:  
1. Degradation of Smad3 via ubiquitination, attenuating fibrotic signaling.  
2. Stabilization of HIF-2α by occupying VHL, promoting renal angiogenesis and erythropoiesis.  

Applications include treatment of CKD, renal fibrosis, and anemia, with demonstrated efficacy in murine models of unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) and 5/6 nephrectomy (5/6Nx). Benefits over existing therapies include:  
- Catalytic, substoichiometric activity reducing dosing requirements.  
- Tissue specificity due to VHL’s renal predominance.  
- Synergistic effects from combined Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization.  

## EMBODIMENTS  

The invention is based on the hypothesis that a VHL-recruiting, Smad3-targeting PROTAC would concurrently mitigate fibrosis and enhance renal repair. Key embodiments include:  

### Construction of Smad3/HIF-α Double-Target PROTAC  
1. A Smad3-binding SMC was identified via virtual screening and surface plasmon resonance.  
2. A VHL-binding SMC was selected to hijack HIF-α ubiquitination.  
3. Two linker variants—polyethylene glycol (PEG) and succinic acid—were synthesized, with the latter showing superior Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization.  
4. The PROTAC was synthesized in R and S chiral forms, with the S-configuration exhibiting higher potency.  

### Benefits of Smad3/HIF-α Double-Target PROTAC  
- In vitro: Dose-dependent Smad3 degradation (EC50 = 25 nM) and HIF-2α stabilization in renal fibroblasts.  
- In vivo: Attenuation of fibrosis in UUO mice, improved renal function in 5/6Nx mice, and correction of anemia via elevated erythropoietin and iron mobilization.  
- Safety: No toxicity observed in murine models at therapeutic doses.  

### Example 1  

#### Screening and Optimization  
1. Small molecules binding Smad3 were screened, with a selective SMC identified for the POI ligand.  
2. The binding site of the VHL-recruiting SMC was determined to ensure efficient ubiquitin transfer.  
3. PROTACs with PEG and succinic acid linkers were synthesized and structurally verified via HPLC and mass spectrometry.  

#### Activity Validation  
1. Western blotting confirmed Smad3 degradation and HIF-2α stabilization in NRK-49F cells, with succinic acid linker PROTAC showing superior efficacy.  
2. In vitro ubiquitination assays demonstrated proteasome-dependent Smad3 degradation.  
3. In vivo studies in UUO and 5/6Nx models showed reduced collagen deposition, improved capillary density, and ameliorated anemia.  

#### Summary  
The double-target PROTAC exemplifies a novel strategy to concurrently address fibrosis and hypoxia in CKD, with translational potential for clinical development.
