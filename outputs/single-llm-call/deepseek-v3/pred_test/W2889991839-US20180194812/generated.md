## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to immunogenic compositions and methods for preventing or treating bacterial infections. More specifically, the invention provides conjugate vaccines comprising Klebsiella pneumoniae surface polysaccharide antigens conjugated to Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins, compositions containing such conjugates, and methods for their use in inducing protective immune responses against infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa are Gram-negative bacterial pathogens that account for a significant proportion of nosocomial infections worldwide. Both pathogens have been identified by health organizations as being of highest concern due to their accelerating acquisition and spread of antibiotic resistance. Klebsiella pneumoniae exhibits considerable virulence through multiple mechanisms including capsule production, lipopolysaccharide (LPS) endotoxin activity, and various adhesion factors. The emergence of multi-drug resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae strains, including those resistant to last-resort antibiotics like colistin, represents a critical threat to public health.

The virulence of Klebsiella pneumoniae is mediated in part by its surface polysaccharides, including both capsular polysaccharides (CPS or K antigens) and O polysaccharides (OPS) associated with lipopolysaccharide. While over 80 distinct K antigen serotypes have been identified, only 9 OPS structures are known, with types O1, O2, O3 and O5 accounting for over 80% of clinical isolates globally. The O polysaccharide component of LPS has been shown to be accessible to antibodies despite the presence of capsule, and antibodies against OPS have demonstrated protective efficacy in animal models through opsonophagocytic killing mechanisms.

Pseudomonas aeruginosa expresses a single polar flagellum composed primarily of flagellin protein multimers, with two known serotypes termed FlaA (including subforms A1 and A2) and FlaB. Flagella serve as essential virulence factors, mediating motility, attachment, and colonization. Antibodies against flagellin proteins have been shown to inhibit motility and protect against fatal Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection in animal models when induced by active immunization or passively transferred. Immunity against flagellin has been found to be serotype-specific, necessitating coverage for both FlaA and FlaB in a broadly protective vaccine.

Current vaccine approaches for these pathogens face several limitations. Capsule-based Klebsiella vaccines would require coverage for numerous serotypes due to geographical disparities in prevalent types. Whole-cell killed organisms and purified LPS vaccines are limited by endotoxin activity and manufacturing challenges. While polysaccharide-protein conjugate vaccines represent a promising approach, existing conjugate vaccines using traditional carrier proteins like tetanus toxoid may suffer from reduced immunogenicity due to pre-existing anti-carrier immunity.

There remains an unmet need for effective immunoprophylactic measures against these antibiotic-resistant pathogens, particularly a broad-spectrum vaccine that could target both Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa simultaneously given their similar clinico-epidemiological patterns in nosocomial infections.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel immunogenic compositions and methods for preventing or treating bacterial infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. In one embodiment, the invention provides a conjugate vaccine comprising at least one Klebsiella pneumoniae surface polysaccharide antigen covalently linked to at least one Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein. The surface polysaccharide antigen may be selected from O1, O2, O3 or O5 O polysaccharides of Klebsiella pneumoniae, while the flagellin protein may be selected from type A or type B flagellins of Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a quadrivalent conjugate vaccine composition comprising: (a) a first conjugate comprising Klebsiella pneumoniae O1 O polysaccharide conjugated to Pseudomonas aeruginosa type B flagellin; (b) a second conjugate comprising Klebsiella pneumoniae O2 O polysaccharide conjugated to Pseudomonas aeruginosa type A flagellin; (c) a third conjugate comprising Klebsiella pneumoniae O3 O polysaccharide conjugated to Pseudomonas aeruginosa type B flagellin; and (d) a fourth conjugate comprising Klebsiella pneumoniae O5 O polysaccharide conjugated to Pseudomonas aeruginosa type A flagellin.

The invention further provides methods for preparing the conjugate vaccines, including: (a) methods for producing recombinant Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins in heterologous expression systems; (b) methods for isolating Klebsiella pneumoniae O polysaccharides from bacterial cultures; and (c) methods for chemically conjugating the polysaccharide antigens to flagellin carrier proteins using various conjugation chemistries.

Additionally, the invention provides methods for inducing an immune response in a subject comprising administering an immunologically effective amount of the conjugate vaccine composition. The immune response may be protective against infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The invention also provides methods for passive immunization comprising administering antibodies raised against the conjugate vaccine compositions.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel immunogenic compositions and methods for preventing or treating bacterial infections, particularly those caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The invention is based on the surprising discovery that conjugation of Klebsiella pneumoniae surface polysaccharide antigens to Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins results in highly immunogenic compositions capable of inducing protective immune responses against both pathogens.

The conjugate vaccines of the invention address several limitations of current vaccine approaches by: (1) targeting the more conserved O polysaccharide antigens rather than the highly variable capsule antigens of Klebsiella pneumoniae; (2) utilizing Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins as both immunogenic carrier proteins and protective antigens; and (3) providing broad coverage against both pathogens in a single vaccine formulation. The compositions and methods described herein represent a significant advance in the field of bacterial vaccinology, particularly for combating antibiotic-resistant nosocomial infections.

### Conjugate

The conjugate of the invention comprises at least one Klebsiella pneumoniae surface polysaccharide antigen covalently linked to at least one Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein. The Klebsiella pneumoniae surface polysaccharide antigen is preferably an O polysaccharide (OPS) selected from the group consisting of O1, O2, O3 and O5 serotypes. These OPS types are associated with the vast majority (>80%) of clinical Klebsiella pneumoniae isolates worldwide. The O polysaccharide may be obtained from Klebsiella pneumoniae strains including but not limited to B5055 (O1:K2), 7380 (O2:K-), 390 (O3:K11), and 4425/51 (O5:K57).

The Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein may be selected from type A flagellin (FlaA) or type B flagellin (FlaB). These proteins may be obtained from Pseudomonas aeruginosa strains including but not limited to PAK (FlaA1) and PAO1 (FlaB). The flagellin proteins may be produced recombinantly in heterologous expression systems such as Escherichia coli, with or without post-translational modifications.

The conjugate is formed by covalent linkage between the Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS and Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein. In preferred embodiments, the OPS is linked to the flagellin protein through its reducing end 2'5'-anhydromannose aldehyde to lysine residues on the flagellin protein using thioether-aminooxy chemistry. The ratio of surface polysaccharide to flagellin protein in the conjugate may range from about 0.5:1 to about 2:1 by weight. The conjugate may include fragments or derivatives of the Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein, wherein the derivative maintains at least 80% sequence identity to the native flagellin protein and retains immunogenic properties.

### Compositions

The invention provides vaccine compositions comprising one or more of the conjugates described above. The compositions may further include unconjugated Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein to enhance the immune response. The unconjugated flagellin may be selected to match or differ from the flagellin type used in the conjugate(s).

In a preferred embodiment, the composition is a multivalent conjugate vaccine comprising: (a) a first conjugate of Klebsiella pneumoniae O1 OPS linked to Pseudomonas aeruginosa FlaB; (b) a second conjugate of Klebsiella pneumoniae O2 OPS linked to Pseudomonas aeruginosa FlaA; (c) a third conjugate of Klebsiella pneumoniae O3 OPS linked to Pseudomonas aeruginosa FlaB; and (d) a fourth conjugate of Klebsiella pneumoniae O5 OPS linked to Pseudomonas aeruginosa FlaA. This quadrivalent formulation provides coverage against the predominant OPS types of Klebsiella pneumoniae and both flagellin types of Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

The vaccine compositions may further comprise pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or adjuvants. Suitable adjuvants include but are not limited to aluminum salts, oil-in-water emulsions, TLR agonists, and saponins. Notably, the Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein itself may serve as an adjuvant through its TLR5 agonist activity when present in unconjugated form.

The compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration including nasal, parenteral, oral, transdermal, mucosal, or pulmonary delivery. The compositions may be prepared as liquid dosage forms, freeze-dried products, liposome solutions, isotonic aqueous solutions, suspensions, gels, or particulate forms. For pulmonary delivery, the compositions may be formulated as nebulizer solutions, metered-dose inhalers, or powder inhalers.

The invention also provides kits comprising the vaccine compositions along with instructions for administration, delivery devices, and storage instructions. The kits may include multiple vials or containers for reconstitution prior to administration.

### Methods of Treatment

The invention provides methods of inducing an immune response in a subject comprising administering an immunologically effective amount of the conjugate vaccine composition. The immune response may be characterized by production of antibodies against both the Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS antigen and the Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein. The antibodies may demonstrate functional activity including opsonophagocytic killing of Klebsiella pneumoniae and motility inhibition of Pseudomonas aeruginosa.

The subject may be a human patient at risk of infection by Klebsiella pneumoniae or Pseudomonas aeruginosa, such as hospitalized patients, immunocompromised individuals, or patients undergoing surgical procedures. Administration may occur prior to, during, or after periods of increased infection risk.

The method may involve administration of the vaccine composition alone or in sequential doses. A prime/boost regimen may be employed, such as oral administration of live or killed attenuated Salmonella expressing the antigens followed by parenteral boosting with the conjugate vaccine composition. The immunologically-effective amount may range from about 1 μg to about 50 μg of each conjugate component per dose, with one to four doses administered at intervals of about 2 to 8 weeks.

The induced immune response may provide protection against infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa, including but not limited to surgical site infections, wound infections, pneumonias, intravenous catheter-based infections, urinary tract infections, and septicemia. The protection may extend to antibiotic-resistant strains of these pathogens.

### Methods of Making the Conjugate

The invention provides methods for producing the conjugate vaccines. The method for making a conjugate comprising a Klebsiella pneumoniae surface polysaccharide antigen covalently linked to a Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein may comprise:

(a) Culturing a Klebsiella pneumoniae strain to produce O polysaccharide;
(b) Isolating and purifying the O polysaccharide from the bacterial culture;
(c) Expressing recombinant Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein in a heterologous Gram-negative bacterial system;
(d) Purifying the recombinant flagellin protein; and
(e) Chemically conjugating the O polysaccharide to the flagellin protein.

The Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS may be isolated using methods including mild acid hydrolysis, nitrous acid deamination, or hydrazine treatment. The OPS may be purified by techniques such as tangential flow filtration, ion exchange chromatography, size exclusion chromatography, and ammonium sulfate precipitation.

The Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin protein may be expressed in Escherichia coli or other Gram-negative bacteria using recombinant DNA technology. The flagellin may be purified by mechanical shearing, low pH treatment, heating, or chromatographic methods including immobilized metal affinity chromatography, cation exchange chromatography, and anion exchange chromatography.

Conjugation of the OPS to flagellin may be achieved through various chemistries including: (a) coupling through available functional groups on the polysaccharide and protein; (b) incorporation of functional groups into the polysaccharide and protein followed by coupling; or (c) use of crosslinking agents such as homobifunctional or heterobifunctional crosslinkers. Specific conjugation methods may include 1-cyano-4-dimethylaminopyridinium tetrafluoroborate (CDAP) chemistry or use of linkers such as adipic acid dihydrazide (ADH).

The conjugate product may be purified by techniques such as size-exclusion chromatography and ion-exchange chromatography to remove unconjugated components and reaction reagents. The purified conjugate may be characterized for polysaccharide to protein ratio, molecular size distribution, and removal of endotoxin.

### Genetically Engineered Strains

The invention provides genetically engineered bacterial strains useful for producing the vaccine components. These include:

(a) Modified Klebsiella pneumoniae strains containing attenuating mutations in the guaBA locus (guanine biosynthesis genes) and/or deletions of capsule biosynthesis genes (wza-wzc locus). These mutations render the strains avirulent while maintaining OPS production. Exemplary strains include CVD 3001 (ΔguaBA Δwzabc derivative of B5055) and CVD 3002 (Δwzabc derivative of 4425/51).

(b) Modified Gram-negative bacteria containing attenuating mutations in guaBA, clpP, clpX, or fliC genes, engineered for expression of Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins. These strains may include codon-optimized Pseudomonas fliC genes and excretion signals for flagellin hyper-secretion.

(c) Attenuated Salmonella enterica strains containing inactivating mutations in fliC and expressing heterologous Pseudomonas aeruginosa fliC genes. These strains may further contain deletions in guaBA and clpPX loci to enhance safety and flagellin production.

The genetically engineered strains allow for safe and economical production of large quantities of Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS and Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins for vaccine manufacture.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

This example describes the construction and characterization of monovalent and quadrivalent conjugate vaccines comprising Klebsiella pneumoniae O polysaccharides linked to Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins.

Reagent strains were created to purify heterologous flagellins and OPS antigens. For Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS production, strain B5055 (O1:K2) was engineered to create CVD 3001 by deleting guaBA and wzabc genes, resulting in an attenuated, non-encapsulated strain that maintained O1 OPS production. Similar approaches were used to create reagent strains for O2, O3 and O5 OPS production.

Pseudomonas aeruginosa flagellin proteins were produced recombinantly in Escherichia coli BL21 using codon-optimized flaA and flaB genes cloned into pTrc-His-TOPO. Fermentation cultures yielded soluble flagellin that was purified by nickel affinity chromatography followed by tangential flow filtration and ion exchange chromatography. The purified flagellins showed single bands by SDS-PAGE at the predicted molecular weights with low endotoxin levels (<0.1 EU/μg).

Klebsiella pneumoniae OPS was isolated from fermentation cultures of reagent strains. Cultures were treated with acetic acid/sodium nitrite to release OPS, which was then purified by tangential flow filtration, anion exchange chromatography, and hydrophobic interaction chromatography. The purified OPS showed homogeneous size distribution by HPLC-SEC and contained <1% protein and <0.01 EU/μg endotoxin.

Conjugates were synthesized by chemically linking KP OPS to PA flagellin using thioether-aminooxy chemistry. Briefly, OPS was derivatized with O-(3-mercaptopropyl)-hydroxylamine followed by sodium cyanoborohydride reduction. Flagellin was activated with N-γ-maleimidobutyryl-oxysuccinimide ester. The derivatized components were then conjugated through thioether bond formation. The resulting conjugates were purified by size-exclusion chromatography and characterized for polysaccharide:protein ratio, molecular size, and endotoxin content.

A quadrivalent conjugate vaccine formulation was prepared comprising: (1) O1 OPS-FlaB; (2) O2 OPS-FlaA; (3) O3 OPS-FlaB; and (4) O5 OPS-FlaA conjugates. Each conjugate component was present at 5 μg polysaccharide per human dose. The formulation was adjuvanted with aluminum phosphate and contained 5 μg each of unconjugated FlaA and FlaB.

Immunogenicity of the quadrivalent conjugate vaccine was assessed in rabbits. Animals received four doses of vaccine at 0, 2, 4 and 6 weeks, with sera collected pre-immunization and after the third and fourth doses. ELISA measurements demonstrated robust IgG responses against all four OPS types and both flagellin proteins. Anti-OPS titers were significantly higher in conjugate-vaccinated animals compared to those receiving unconjugated OPS mixed with flagellin.

Functional activity of the immune sera was demonstrated through: (1) opsonophagocytic killing assays showing enhanced uptake of Klebsiella pneumoniae by J774 macrophages when opsonized with conjugate-induced sera; (2) motility inhibition assays showing reduced motility of Pseudomonas aeruginosa in the presence of anti-flagellin antibodies; and (3) passive protection studies demonstrating reduced bacterial burden and improved survival in mouse models of Klebsiella pneumoniae septicemia and Pseudomonas aeruginosa burn wound infection.

These results demonstrate that the conjugate vaccine compositions of the invention induce functional antibodies against both Klebsiella pneumoniae and Pseudomonas aeruginosa, providing protection against infections caused by these important nosocomial pathogens. The quadrivalent formulation offers broad coverage against the predominant OPS types of Klebsiella pneumoniae and both flagellin types of Pseudomonas aeruginosa in a single vaccine preparation.
