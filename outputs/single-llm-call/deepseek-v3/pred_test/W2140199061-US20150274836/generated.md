# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind mesothelin, a cell surface antigen overexpressed in various human cancers. More particularly, the invention provides novel human single-domain antibodies targeting the C-terminal region of mesothelin, including the SD1 antibody that demonstrates potent complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) against mesothelin-expressing tumor cells. The invention further encompasses antibody fragments, immunoconjugates, pharmaceutical compositions, and methods of using these antibodies for cancer diagnosis and treatment.  

## BACKGROUND  

Mesothelin has emerged as an important therapeutic target due to its overexpression in malignant mesotheliomas and other solid tumors including cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and breast cancer. The mesothelin gene encodes a 71 kDa precursor protein that is processed into a 31 kDa N-terminal secreted protein and a 40 kDa C-terminal membrane-bound mature mesothelin. Several anti-mesothelin monoclonal antibodies have been developed, including the immunotoxin SS1P and the chimeric antibody MORAb-009 (amatuximab), both currently in clinical trials.  

Existing anti-mesothelin antibodies primarily recognize Region I at the N-terminal end of cell-surface mesothelin, which is distant from the cell membrane. These antibodies demonstrate ADCC activity but lack CDC, an important mechanism for cancer therapeutic antibodies. The first approved monoclonal antibody for cancer therapy, rituximab, relies partially on CDC for its antitumor activity. Studies suggest that CDC occurs more effectively when the antibody-binding site is close to the cell membrane, as demonstrated by ofatumumab which binds nearer to the cell membrane than rituximab and shows superior CDC activity.  

Current limitations of anti-mesothelin antibodies include their inability to induce CDC and their targeting of immunodominant but therapeutically suboptimal epitopes. The C-terminal region of mesothelin near the cell surface represents an attractive but challenging target due to its poor immunogenicity. Conventional hybridoma technology has failed to produce antibodies against this region, with over 96% of generated clones binding only to Region I. There remains a significant unmet need for anti-mesothelin antibodies capable of inducing both CDC and ADCC by targeting novel epitopes closer to the cell surface.  

Complement-dependent cytotoxicity is mediated through the classical complement pathway initiated by binding of C1q to antibody-antigen complexes on the cell surface. This triggers a cascade leading to formation of membrane attack complexes that lyse target cells. The proximity of antibody binding to the cell membrane enhances CDC efficacy by facilitating complement activation. The present invention addresses these limitations by providing novel human single-domain antibodies that specifically bind the C-terminal region of mesothelin and demonstrate potent CDC activity in addition to ADCC.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind mesothelin, particularly the SD1 and SD2 human single-domain antibodies. The SD1 antibody binds an epitope in the C-terminal region of mesothelin (residues 539-588) and demonstrates strong CDC activity against mesothelin-expressing tumor cells. When converted to an SD1-human Fc fusion protein (SD1-hFc), the antibody shows potent antitumor activity in vitro and inhibits xenograft tumor growth in vivo.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. The SD1 antibody which specifically binds the C-terminal region of mesothelin and demonstrates complement-dependent cytotoxicity  
2. The SD1-hFc fusion protein showing strong antitumor activity through both CDC and ADCC mechanisms  
3. Isolated monoclonal antibodies comprising the complementarity determining regions (CDRs) of SD1 or SD2  
4. Nucleic acid molecules encoding the antibodies or antibody fragments  
5. Immunoconjugates comprising the antibodies linked to therapeutic agents  
6. Pharmaceutical compositions containing the antibodies  
7. Methods for treating mesothelin-expressing cancers using the antibodies  

The SD1 antibody binds native mesothelin on cancer cells with high affinity (KD = 13.59 nM for full-length mesothelin and 16.08 nM for the C-terminal peptide) and specificity. Unlike existing anti-mesothelin antibodies that recognize Region I, SD1 binds a conformational epitope near the cell surface and effectively recruits C1q to initiate complement-mediated cytotoxicity. SD1-hFc demonstrates significant tumor growth inhibition in mouse xenograft models, reducing tumor volume by approximately 70% compared to controls.  

The invention further encompasses antibody fragments including Fab, Fab', F(ab')2, Fv, and single-chain antibody fragments derived from SD1 or SD2. Also provided are immunoconjugates where the antibodies are linked to therapeutic agents such as toxins, chemotherapeutic drugs, or radionuclides. Pharmaceutical compositions contain the antibodies in formulations suitable for intravenous administration, including lyophilized forms and controlled-release formulations.  

Methods of use include treating mesothelin-expressing cancers such as mesothelioma, ovarian cancer, cholangiocarcinoma, and lung adenocarcinoma by administering a therapeutically effective amount of the antibodies alone or in combination with other anticancer agents. Diagnostic methods involve detecting mesothelin expression in biological samples using the antibodies, which may be directly or indirectly labeled with detectable moieties.

### II. Terms and Methods  

An "antibody" refers to an immunoglobulin molecule capable of specific binding to a target antigen. The term encompasses intact immunoglobulins and variants thereof, including antibody fragments that retain antigen-binding capability.  

"Complementarity determining regions" (CDRs) are the hypervariable regions within antibody variable domains that primarily determine antigen binding specificity. The three CDRs of each heavy chain (VH CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3) and light chain (VL CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3) form the antigen-binding site.  

A "monoclonal antibody" refers to an antibody obtained from a population of substantially homogeneous antibodies, derived from a single clone. Monoclonal antibodies may be produced by hybridoma technology or recombinant methods.  

"Humanized immunoglobulin" refers to an antibody containing minimal non-human sequences, typically comprising human framework regions with non-human CDRs grafted into them. Humanization reduces immunogenicity while maintaining antigen binding affinity.  

"Binding affinity" refers to the strength of interaction between an antibody and its antigen, typically measured by equilibrium dissociation constant (KD). Lower KD values indicate higher affinity. Affinity may be determined by surface plasmon resonance, ELISA, or other binding assays.  

"Breast cancer" encompasses malignant neoplasms originating in breast tissue, including triple negative breast cancer which lacks expression of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2/neu.

"Conservative variants" of antibodies include amino acid substitutions that do not substantially alter antigen binding. Conservative substitutions typically involve replacing amino acids with others having similar properties (e.g., hydrophobic for hydrophobic, acidic for acidic).  

A "diagnostic" refers to methods and compositions for detecting disease, characterized by sensitivity (true positive rate) and specificity (true negative rate).  

An "effector molecule" is a therapeutic or diagnostic agent linked to an antibody, including toxins, drugs, radionuclides, enzymes, or detectable labels.  

An "epitope" is the specific portion of an antigen recognized by an antibody. Conformational epitopes depend on the three-dimensional structure of the antigen, while linear epitopes depend on primary sequence.  

"Framework regions" are the relatively conserved portions of antibody variable domains that provide structural support for the CDRs.

### III. Introduction  

The present invention provides novel monoclonal antibodies targeting mesothelin, particularly the SD1 and SD2 human single-domain antibodies. Current anti-mesothelin therapeutics such as SS1P and MORAb-009 target the N-terminal region of mesothelin and demonstrate limited mechanisms of action, primarily ADCC. These antibodies fail to induce complement-dependent cytotoxicity, an important antitumor mechanism exhibited by successful therapeutic antibodies like rituximab.  

The invention overcomes these limitations by providing antibodies that bind the C-terminal region of mesothelin near the cell surface. Using phage display technology with a human single-domain antibody library, we isolated the SD1 antibody that specifically recognizes a 50-amino acid peptide (residues 539-588) at the C-terminus of mesothelin. This region has not been targeted by previous antibodies due to its poor immunogenicity and the technical challenges of generating antibodies against membrane-proximal epitopes.  

SD1 demonstrates unique biological properties compared to existing anti-mesothelin antibodies. When converted to an SD1-human Fc fusion protein (SD1-hFc), the antibody shows potent CDC activity against mesothelin-expressing tumor cells by effectively recruiting C1q to the cell surface. SD1-hFc also demonstrates strong ADCC activity and significant tumor growth inhibition in mouse xenograft models. These properties make SD1 a promising candidate for improved mesothelin-targeted cancer therapy.  

### IV. Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies  

The invention provides isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind mesothelin, particularly the SD1 and SD2 human single-domain antibodies. SD1 was isolated by phage panning against a C-terminal mesothelin peptide (residues 539-588) using an engineered human VH antibody domain library. The antibody binds both the peptide and full-length mesothelin protein with high affinity (KD = 13.59 nM for mesothelin and 16.08 nM for the peptide).  

SD1 recognizes a conformational epitope in native mesothelin that is distinct from epitopes targeted by existing antibodies like SS1P and MORAb-009. This is demonstrated by competition ELISA showing that the C-terminal peptide blocks SD1 binding to full-length mesothelin but does not affect binding of SS1P or HN1 antibodies. SD1 does not inhibit mesothelin binding to MUC16/CA125, further confirming its non-overlapping epitope specificity.  

The SD1-hFc fusion protein demonstrates potent complement-dependent cytotoxicity against mesothelin-expressing tumor cells, killing approximately 40% of A431/H9 cells and 30% of NCI-H226 mesothelioma cells in vitro. This CDC activity is specific to SD1's C-terminal epitope, as antibodies targeting the N-terminal region (HN1) show no CDC activity. SD1-hFc effectively recruits C1q to cancer cells in a dose-dependent manner, initiating the complement cascade.  

In addition to CDC, SD1-hFc shows strong ADCC activity, killing over 40% of A431/H9 cells and 30% of NCI-H226 cells when combined with human peripheral blood mononuclear cells or purified natural killer cells. These dual mechanisms of action contribute to SD1-hFc's significant antitumor activity in vivo, reducing A431/H9 xenograft tumor growth by approximately 70% compared to controls in nude mice.  

The invention provides the nucleotide and amino acid sequences of SD1 and SD2 antibodies, enabling production of these antibodies and their variants. Isolated monoclonal antibodies may comprise the complementarity determining regions of SD1 or SD2, incorporated into various antibody formats including full-length IgG, Fab fragments, or single-chain variable fragments.  

Antibodies of the invention may be labeled with detectable moieties for diagnostic applications or conjugated to therapeutic agents for cancer treatment. The antibodies may be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers for administration to subjects with mesothelin-expressing cancers.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

#### Production and Characterization of SD1-hFc  

The SD1 human single-domain antibody was isolated by phage display using a C-terminal mesothelin peptide (residues 539-588) as target. The VH domain was fused to human IgG1 Fc and expressed in HEK-293F cells, yielding SD1-hFc fusion protein at >70 mg/L in culture supernatant.  

Binding specificity was confirmed by:  
1. Western blot showing SD1-hFc pulls down endogenous mesothelin from cancer cell lysates  
2. ELISA demonstrating specific binding to mesothelin protein and peptide (KD = 13.59 nM and 16.08 nM respectively)  
3. Flow cytometry showing cell surface binding to mesothelin-expressing lines (A431/H9, NCI-H226, KMBC)  

Functional assays revealed:  
1. CDC activity killing 40% of A431/H9 and 30% of NCI-H226 cells  
2. ADCC activity with >40% killing using human PBMCs  
3. Effective C1q recruitment to cancer cell surfaces  
4. Tumor growth inhibition in mouse xenografts (70% reduction vs controls)  

### Example 2  

#### Epitope Mapping and Mechanism of Action  

Competition ELISA demonstrated that SD1 binds a distinct epitope from existing antibodies:  
1. C-terminal peptide blocked SD1 binding to full-length mesothelin  
2. Peptide did not affect SS1P or HN1 binding  
3. SD1 did not inhibit mesothelin-MUC16 interaction  

Mechanistic studies showed:  
1. SD1-hFc recruits C1q to cancer cells in dose-dependent manner  
2. HN1 (N-terminal binder) shows no C1q recruitment or CDC  
3. CDC activity depends on SD1's C-terminal epitope  

In vivo efficacy was demonstrated in nude mice bearing A431/H9 xenografts:  
1. SD1-hFc (50 mg/kg) significantly reduced tumor growth  
2. Both CDC and ADCC contribute to antitumor activity  
3. Mouse complement is less effective than human complement  

### Example 3  

#### Diagnostic Use of SD1 Antibody  

An ELISA method was developed to detect soluble mesothelin in blood samples:  
1. SD1 antibody coated on microtiter plates captures mesothelin  
2. Detection with labeled secondary antibody  
3. Quantitative measurement compared to standards  

This assay can:  
1. Detect mesothelin in serum from cancer patients  
2. Monitor disease progression or treatment response  
3. Confirm diagnosis of mesothelin-expressing cancers  

### Example 4  

#### Treatment of Mesothelin-Positive Cancers  

A treatment regimen for mesothelioma was developed:  
1. Administer SD1-hFc at 50 mg/kg twice weekly  
2. Monitor tumor size and clinical symptoms  
3. Combine with cisplatin/pemetrexed chemotherapy  

Alternative approaches include:  
1. SD1 immunoconjugate with Pseudomonas exotoxin  
2. Combination with PD-1/PD-L1 checkpoint inhibitors  
3. Adjuvant therapy after surgical resection
