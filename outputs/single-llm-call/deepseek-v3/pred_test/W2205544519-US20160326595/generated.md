# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Prostate cancer remains one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting men worldwide, with significant disparities observed across racial and ethnic populations. Epidemiological data consistently demonstrate that men of African descent experience higher incidence rates and worse clinical outcomes compared to men of Caucasian descent. These disparities persist even when accounting for socioeconomic factors and access to healthcare, suggesting underlying biological differences in disease pathogenesis.  

The molecular landscape of prostate cancer has been extensively characterized in populations of European ancestry, revealing recurrent genetic alterations including TMPRSS2-ERG gene fusions, PTEN deletions, and SPOP mutations. However, emerging evidence indicates these alterations occur at substantially different frequencies in men of African ancestry, highlighting the need for population-specific biomarkers and therapeutic targets.  

Current diagnostic and prognostic tools fail to adequately address the biological heterogeneity observed across ethnic groups, potentially contributing to disparities in clinical outcomes. There exists an urgent unmet need for molecular markers that can accurately stratify disease risk and guide treatment decisions in men of African descent with prostate cancer.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses novel genomic rearrangements within chromosome region 3q13 involving the ZBTB20 and LSAMP genes that are preferentially associated with aggressive prostate cancer in men of African descent. Through comprehensive whole genome sequencing analysis, we identified recurrent structural variations at the ZBTB20-LSAMP locus that result in gene fusions and deletions. These alterations are strongly correlated with biochemical recurrence and advanced pathological stage in African American patients.  

The invention provides methods for diagnosing and prognosing prostate cancer by detecting the ZBTB20/LSAMP genomic rearrangement through various techniques including fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), polymerase chain reaction (PCR), and next-generation sequencing. Specific embodiments include polynucleotide probes and primers designed to detect the fusion junction between ZBTB20 exon 0* and LSAMP exon 1, as well as the novel exon 3* of the LSAMP gene created by the rearrangement.  

Compositions of the invention include isolated antibodies that specifically bind to chimeric polypeptides encoded by the ZBTB20/LSAMP fusion gene, as well as kits containing these antibodies or polynucleotide probes for clinical diagnostic applications. The invention further provides methods for selecting targeted prostate cancer treatments based on the presence or absence of the ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangement, particularly in men of African descent.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### DEFINITIONS  

The term "of African descent" refers to individuals having recent ancestry from sub-Saharan Africa, including African Americans, Afro-Caribbeans, and recent African immigrants. Ancestry may be determined through self-reporting or genetic analysis using ancestry-informative markers.  

An "antibody" refers to an immunoglobulin molecule that specifically binds to an antigen through its antigen-binding domain. This includes intact immunoglobulins as well as antigen-binding fragments such as Fab, F(ab')2, and single-chain variable fragments (scFv).  

"Detection" or "detecting" refers to any process that identifies the presence or absence of a target molecule, including but not limited to hybridization assays, amplification methods, sequencing techniques, and immunological assays.  

A "therapeutically effective amount" refers to a quantity of pharmaceutical composition sufficient to achieve a desired therapeutic effect while maintaining an acceptable safety profile.  

The "ZBTB20/LSAMP genomic rearrangement" refers to any structural variation at chromosome 3q13.31 that results in altered gene structure or expression of ZBTB20 or LSAMP, including but not limited to deletions, inversions, translocations, and gene fusions.  

An "aggressive form of prostate cancer" refers to prostate adenocarcinoma exhibiting one or more high-risk features including Gleason score ≥7, pathological stage ≥pT3, biochemical recurrence, or metastatic progression.  

The invention encompasses novel genomic rearrangements identified through comprehensive whole genome sequencing of prostate cancer specimens from men of African and Caucasian descent. High-quality sequence data were generated using Illumina platforms and analyzed using multiple variant calling algorithms to identify somatic mutations with high confidence.  

Comparative analysis revealed a striking enrichment of structural variations at the ZBTB20-LSAMP locus in tumors from African American patients. These rearrangements include large deletions spanning up to 23 Mb as well as complex duplications creating novel fusion junctions. The most common rearrangement results in fusion of ZBTB20 exon 0* to LSAMP exon 1, producing a chimeric transcript detectable by RT-PCR.  

The LSAMP gene (Limbic System-Associated Membrane Protein) normally encodes a cell adhesion molecule implicated in neuronal development. Its deletion or rearrangement in prostate cancer likely represents loss of tumor suppressor function, consistent with observations in other malignancies. The adjacent ZBTB20 gene encodes a zinc finger transcription factor with potential oncogenic properties when dysregulated.  

Detection methods of the invention include fluorescence in situ hybridization using locus-specific probes covering the ZBTB20-LSAMP region. These probes enable visualization of deletions and rearrangements in interphase nuclei from tumor samples. Alternatively, PCR-based assays using primers spanning the fusion junction can detect chimeric transcripts in RNA samples.  

The invention further provides methods for detecting PTEN deletions, which show an inverse relationship with ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangements across ethnic groups. Prostate cancers from African American patients rarely exhibit PTEN deletions, suggesting distinct molecular pathways of tumorigenesis. This finding has important implications for treatment selection, as therapies targeting the PI3K/PTEN/Akt/mTOR pathway may be less effective in this population.  

### EXAMPLES  

Validation studies were performed using tissue microarrays containing samples from 42 African American and 59 Caucasian American prostate cancer patients. Fluorescence in situ hybridization with LSAMP-specific probes confirmed significantly higher deletion frequencies in African American tumors (26% vs 7%, p=0.007). These deletions were strongly associated with biochemical recurrence and advanced pathological stage.  

In an independent validation cohort from The Cancer Genome Atlas, LSAMP locus deletions were detected in 27% of African American tumors compared to 13% of Caucasian American tumors (p=0.023). Conversely, PTEN deletions were virtually absent in African American tumors (7% in Gleason 6) compared to 53% in matched Caucasian American cases (p=0.004).  

5'-RACE analysis identified multiple fusion transcripts resulting from ZBTB20/LSAMP rearrangements. The most common fusion joined ZBTB20 exon 0* to LSAMP exon 1, creating a novel chimeric open reading frame. Primers spanning this junction successfully amplified the fusion transcript in additional prostate cancer samples, confirming its specificity.  

The invention provides polynucleotide probes for detecting these rearrangements, including:  
- A probe spanning the ZBTB20 exon 0*/LSAMP exon 1 junction  
- A probe specific for the novel exon 3* of LSAMP  
- A probe covering the common deleted region between ZBTB20 and LSAMP  

These probes may be labeled with detectable moieties such as fluorophores or haptens for use in hybridization assays. The invention further encompasses primer sets for amplifying fusion transcripts, including:  
- Forward primer in ZBTB20 exon 0* and reverse primer in LSAMP exon 1  
- Forward primer in LSAMP exon 3* and reverse primer in downstream exons  

Kits of the invention may include these probes and primers along with appropriate buffers, enzymes, and control samples. Additional components may include antibodies specific for chimeric ZBTB20/LSAMP proteins or truncated LSAMP polypeptides.  

The diagnostic methods of the invention enable identification of prostate cancer patients likely to experience aggressive disease, particularly among men of African descent. This information can guide treatment decisions, including the appropriateness of targeted therapies such as PI3K inhibitors. The ethnic-specific nature of these genomic alterations underscores the importance of population-aware precision medicine approaches.
