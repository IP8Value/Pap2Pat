# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of cancer imaging and treatment. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and systems utilizing phosphatidylserine (PS) as a biomarker for targeted cancer therapies and imaging modalities. The invention further encompasses the application of electric fields to modulate PS exposure on cancer cell surfaces, thereby enhancing the efficacy of PS-targeting treatments.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cancer remains one of the leading causes of death worldwide, with an estimated 1.8 million new cases diagnosed annually. Despite advances in conventional treatments such as chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immunotherapy, the mortality rate remains high due to drug resistance, toxicity, and limited efficacy in advanced stages. Current therapeutic approaches often lack specificity, leading to significant off-target effects on healthy tissues.  

A critical limitation of existing therapies is their inability to address the heterogeneity of cancer cells, particularly variations in cell surface biomarkers that influence treatment susceptibility. For instance, certain cancer cells exhibit low surface phosphatidylserine (PS) exposure, making them more responsive to radiation or chemotherapy, while others with high PS exposure are more susceptible to PS-targeting agents. This heterogeneity necessitates the development of novel strategies that can dynamically modulate biomarker expression to improve treatment outcomes.  

Furthermore, existing imaging techniques for cancer detection and monitoring often lack the specificity required to distinguish between malignant and benign tissues accurately. While biomarkers such as EGFR and HER2 have been utilized, their applicability is limited to specific cancer types. There is an urgent need for universal biomarkers that can be exploited across diverse cancers for both therapeutic and diagnostic purposes.  

The present invention addresses these limitations by leveraging PS as a dual-purpose biomarker for targeted therapy and imaging. Additionally, the invention introduces the use of electric fields to modulate PS exposure, thereby enhancing the efficacy of PS-targeting modalities. This approach offers a promising avenue for personalized cancer treatment with minimal side effects.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a novel method for treating cancer by applying direct current electric fields (DC-EF) to modulate phosphatidylserine (PS) exposure on cancer cell surfaces. The method involves the use of a non-contact electric field device to induce changes in PS distribution, thereby sensitizing cancer cells to PS-targeting therapies such as SapC-DOPS, bavituximab, and other PS-specific agents.  

In one embodiment, the invention describes the application of low-DC electric fields (75–150 V/cm) to increase PS exposure on cancer cells, enhancing their susceptibility to PS-targeting drugs. In another embodiment, high-DC electric fields are employed to reduce PS exposure, rendering cancer cells more responsive to conventional therapies like chemotherapy and radiotherapy. The electric field parameters, including magnitude, duration, and frequency, are optimized to ensure minimal impact on normal cells while maximizing therapeutic efficacy.  

The invention further encompasses the use of PS as a biomarker for cancer imaging. Specific embodiments include the formulation of PS-targeting nanovesicles, such as SapC-DOPS, loaded with contrast agents for optical, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and positron emission tomography (PET) applications. These imaging modalities enable precise tumor localization and real-time monitoring of treatment response.  

Additionally, the invention details the combination of electric field therapy with other treatment modalities, including chemotherapy agents (e.g., temozolomide, gemcitabine), radiotherapy agents, and immunotherapy. The synergistic effects of these combinations are demonstrated through in vitro and in vivo studies, showing enhanced cancer cell killing and reduced off-target toxicity.  

The invention also introduces a system for delivering electric fields to target tissues, comprising a parallel-plate capacitor device that generates controlled electric fields without direct contact with cells or medium. This system ensures safe and reproducible application of electric fields in clinical settings.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present patent application discloses a comprehensive method for imaging and treating cancer by targeting phosphatidylserine (PS) and modulating its exposure on cell surfaces using electric fields. The following sections provide a detailed explanation of the invention, including definitions, mechanisms, and embodiments.  

### Definitions and Terminology  

As used herein, the term "phosphatidylserine (PS)" refers to a phospholipid component of cell membranes that is asymmetrically distributed in normal cells but exposed on the outer leaflet in cancer cells. The term "electric field (EF)" denotes an applied voltage gradient that induces changes in cell membrane potential and ion flux. "SapC-DOPS" refers to a nanovesicle composed of saposin C and dioleoylphosphatidylserine, which selectively targets PS on cancer cells.  

The singular forms "a," "an," and "the" include plural referents unless the context clearly dictates otherwise. Ranges expressed herein (e.g., 75–150 V/cm) are inclusive of their endpoints and may be adjusted based on specific applications.  

### Membrane Lipid Therapy and PS as a Biomarker  

The invention is rooted in the concept of membrane lipid therapy, which exploits the dysregulation of lipid asymmetry in cancer cells. Unlike normal cells, cancer cells exhibit externalized PS due to disrupted flippase activity, making PS a universal biomarker for malignancy. The level of PS exposure correlates with tumor aggressiveness and treatment susceptibility, providing a rationale for PS-targeted interventions.  

### Electric Fields in Cancer Treatment  

The application of electric fields to modulate PS exposure is a cornerstone of the invention. Low-DC electric fields (75–150 V/cm) are shown to increase PS exposure by activating voltage-gated calcium channels, leading to elevated intracellular calcium levels. This inhibits flippases and promotes PS externalization. Conversely, high-DC electric fields reduce PS exposure, sensitizing cells to conventional therapies.  

The electric field device comprises insulated electrodes arranged in a parallel-plate configuration, ensuring uniform field distribution without direct contact with tissues. This design minimizes thermal effects and collateral damage to normal cells.  

### Combined Biomarker-Driven Therapy  

The invention demonstrates that combining electric field-induced PS modulation with PS-targeting agents (e.g., SapC-DOPS, bavituximab) yields synergistic effects. For instance, pre-treatment with low-DC electric fields enhances SapC-DOPS uptake and cytotoxicity in glioblastoma cells. Similarly, high-DC electric fields potentiate the effects of temozolomide by reducing PS-mediated immunosuppression.  

### Mechanisms of PS Regulation  

The invention elucidates the role of calcium-dependent pathways in PS regulation. Electric fields alter transmembrane potential, activating voltage-gated calcium channels and increasing intracellular calcium. This, in turn, inhibits flippases and scramblases, leading to PS externalization. The invention further explores the impact of electric fields on apoptosis, cell growth, and mitochondrial membrane integrity.  

### Phosphatidylserine  

Phosphatidylserine (PS) is a phospholipid with a glycerol backbone, two fatty acid chains, and a serine headgroup. In normal cells, PS is predominantly localized to the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane, where it participates in signaling pathways, including protein kinase C activation and synaptic vesicle trafficking.  

Cancer cells lose PS asymmetry due to oxidative stress, metabolic alterations, and flippase dysfunction. This results in surface PS exposure, which serves as an "eat me" signal for phagocytes and a marker for immune evasion. The invention leverages this phenomenon for both therapeutic and diagnostic purposes.  

### PS-Targeting Drugs  

The invention details several PS-targeting agents, including:  
1. **SapC-DOPS**: A nanovesicle that binds PS at acidic pH, selectively delivering cytotoxic payloads to cancer cells.  
2. **Bavituximab**: A monoclonal antibody targeting PS-β2GP1 complexes, which blocks immunosuppressive signaling and activates T-cell responses.  
3. **PPS1D1**: A peptide-peptoid hybrid that induces apoptosis in PS-rich cancer cells.  

Preclinical studies demonstrate the efficacy of these agents in glioblastoma, pancreatic cancer, and lung cancer models.  

### Altering Surface PS to Enhance Therapy  

The invention shows that modulating PS exposure via electric fields enhances the efficacy of PS-targeting modalities. For example, low-DC electric fields increase SapC-DOPS uptake, while high-DC fields improve radiation sensitivity. This approach is particularly effective in combination with chemotherapies like temozolomide.  

### Phosphatidylserine as a Tumor Imaging Target  

PS serves as a robust imaging target due to its ubiquitous expression in tumors. The invention describes SapC-DOPS-based imaging using:  
1. **Optical Imaging**: Far-red fluorophores (e.g., CellVue Maroon) enable real-time tumor visualization.  
2. **MRI**: Iron oxide or gadolinium-loaded SapC-DOPS vesicles provide high-resolution tumor contrast.  
3. **PET/SPECT**: Radiolabeled PS-targeting agents (e.g., 89Zr-PGN635) offer sensitive tumor detection.  

### Electric Field System Device  

The invention includes a schematic representation of the electric field delivery system, comprising:  
- Insulated electrodes  
- A power supply with adjustable voltage and frequency  
- A control unit for monitoring field parameters  

The device is designed for clinical use, with customizable settings for different cancer types.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Synthesis of SapC-DOPS Nanovesicles  

SapC-DOPS nanovesicles were synthesized by mixing saposin C and dioleoylphosphatidylserine in a 1:10 molar ratio. The vesicles were extruded to a uniform size of 200 nm and loaded with iron oxide particles for MRI applications.  

### Example 2: Tumor Imaging via MRI  

USPIO-labeled SapC-DOPS vesicles were injected into glioblastoma-bearing mice. T2*-weighted MRI revealed a significant drop in signal intensity within tumors, confirming selective vesicle accumulation.  

### Example 3: Gadolinium-Loaded Vesicles for Glioblastoma Imaging  

Gd-DTPA-BSA/SapC-DOPS vesicles were administered to mice with orthotopic glioblastoma. MRI showed a 9% reduction in T1 relaxation time in tumors, with no change in normal brain tissue.  

### Example 4: Electric Field Modulation of PS  

U87 glioblastoma cells were exposed to 100 V/cm DC-EF for 1 hour. Flow cytometry revealed a 2-fold increase in surface PS, correlating with enhanced SapC-DOPS uptake.  

### Example 5: Combination Therapy with Hydroxyurea  

Cells treated with hydroxyurea and high-DC EF showed delayed cell cycle progression and increased apoptosis, suggesting synergistic effects with PS-targeting agents.
