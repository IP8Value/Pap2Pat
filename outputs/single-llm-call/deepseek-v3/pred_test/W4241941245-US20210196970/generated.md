# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates to systems and methods for treating cancer, particularly cholangiocarcinoma, using cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) in combination with chemotherapy. More specifically, the invention provides a synergistic treatment approach combining FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy with CAP to enhance therapeutic efficacy while reducing toxicity.  

### Brief Description of the Related Art  

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) is a rare and aggressive malignancy arising in the intrahepatic or extrahepatic biliary tract. The disease is often diagnosed at advanced stages, resulting in a poor prognosis with a five-year survival rate below 20%. Surgical resection or liver transplantation at an early stage remains the best option for curative treatment. However, CCA exhibits a high recurrence rate post-resection, and chemoresistance presents a significant challenge in administering adjuvant chemotherapy, leading to poor clinical outcomes.  

For recurrent CCA, gemcitabine and fluorouracil (5-FU) have been standard treatment options, either as monotherapy or in combination. The FOLFIRINOX protocol, comprising fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin, has shown promise in increasing overall survival for metastatic pancreatic cancer compared to gemcitabine. However, FOLFIRINOX is poorly tolerated, with high incidences of thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, and febrile neutropenia. To mitigate toxicity, modified dosages such as FOLFOX-4, FOLFOX-5, and FOLFOX-7 have been explored for pancreatic, colorectal, and bladder cancers.  

Cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) has emerged as a novel cancer treatment, demonstrating selective anti-cancer effects in vitro. CAP induces apoptosis and DNA damage in tumor cells while sparing normal tissue at low doses. Indirect CAP treatment has shown efficacy against CCA in vitro, selectively killing cancer cells over normal hepatocytes. Furthermore, CAP exhibits synergistic effects when combined with anti-neoplastic agents, including gemcitabine in pancreatic cancer and temozolomide in glioblastoma.  

The Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS) paired with the Canady Helios Cold Plasma Scalpel has demonstrated anti-cancer effects in clinical trials, reducing tumor cell viability without thermal damage to normal tissue. Given the poor prognosis and chemoresistance of CCA, CAP presents a promising adjuvant therapy. This invention explores the synergistic effects of combining FOLFIRINOX with CAP to enhance treatment efficacy while minimizing toxicity.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the challenges of treating cholangiocarcinoma by providing a synergistic combination therapy of FOLFIRINOX and cold atmospheric plasma (CAP). CCA is characterized by poor prognosis, chemoresistance, and high recurrence rates, necessitating novel therapeutic approaches. While FOLFIRINOX has shown efficacy in metastatic pancreatic cancer, its clinical utility is limited by severe toxicity.  

The invention introduces CAP as an adjuvant therapy to enhance the anti-cancer effects of FOLFIRINOX while reducing the required chemotherapy dosage. CAP selectively targets tumor cells, inducing apoptosis and DNA damage without harming normal tissue. When combined with FOLFIRINOX, CAP exhibits a synergistic effect, significantly reducing CCA cell viability and proliferation compared to either treatment alone.  

A method for treating cholangiocarcinoma is disclosed, comprising administering a low-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen followed by CAP application. The preferred embodiment includes surgical tumor resection followed by CAP treatment at the surgical margins to target residual cancer cells. The invention further describes a CAP-enabled generator system for precise plasma delivery, ensuring controlled and effective treatment.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The invention provides a method for treating cholangiocarcinoma using a combination of FOLFIRINOX chemotherapy and cold atmospheric plasma (CAP). Cholangiocarcinomas are aggressive malignancies with limited treatment options, and CAP offers a selective and non-thermal approach to target cancer cells.  

FOLFIRINOX, comprising fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin, is an effective combination therapy for aggressive cancers. However, its clinical use is hindered by toxicity. The invention outlines a method wherein a reduced-dose FOLFIRINOX regimen is administered, followed by CAP treatment to enhance efficacy.  

In one embodiment, the method involves surgical removal of the cancerous tumor, followed by CAP application to the surgical margins to eliminate residual cancer cells. The CAP treatment is delivered using a plasma generator system comprising a housing, removable cover, touchscreen display, and connectors. The system includes a power supply, CPU, memory, gas control module, and power module to generate and regulate plasma.  

The CAP applicator is connected to the generator and delivers cold atmospheric plasma below 35°C, ensuring minimal thermal damage to surrounding tissue. The plasma is generated using a helium flow rate of 3 L/min at a power setting of 120p (28.7 W). The invention also details alternative arrangements for carrier gas and electrical energy delivery to optimize plasma generation.  

The combined treatment of FOLFIRINOX and CAP induces cell cycle arrest and reduces cell viability in cholangiocarcinoma cells. The synergistic effect allows for lower chemotherapy doses, minimizing toxicity while maintaining therapeutic efficacy.  

### Experiments  

The invention was validated using the human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma cell line KKU-055. Cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) and treated with FOLFIRINOX at varying concentrations, followed by CAP exposure.  

The CAP device utilized was the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System, operating at 28.7 W with a helium flow rate of 3 L/min. Treatment durations ranged from 1 to 7 minutes, and cell viability was assessed 48 hours post-treatment using an MTT assay.  

FOLFIRINOX dosages were calculated based on clinical recommendations, with serial dilutions to establish toxicity thresholds. Cell proliferation was analyzed using confocal microscopy and Ki-67 staining, while cell cycle distribution was monitored using the IncuCyte Live-Cell Analysis System. Statistical analysis included two-way ANOVA and post hoc Fisher exact tests to evaluate synergistic effects.  

### Results  

FOLFIRINOX treatment significantly reduced KKU-055 cell viability at doses ≥3.4 µM 5-FU, with the highest dose (53.8 µM 5-FU) achieving a 19% viability reduction. CAP treatment alone reduced viability to 3% at 7 minutes.  

Combination therapy demonstrated synergistic effects, with FOLFIRINOX (6.7 µM 5-FU) and 5-minute CAP reducing viability by 91%. Statistical analysis confirmed significant synergy (p < 0.05).  

Cell proliferation assays revealed decreased Ki-67 positivity in combination-treated cells, indicating reduced proliferation. Cell cycle analysis showed CAP-induced arrest, with combination treatment further inhibiting cell cycle progression.  

### Discussion  

The study highlights the potential of CAP as an adjuvant therapy for cholangiocarcinoma. FOLFIRINOX, while effective, is limited by toxicity, and CAP enhances its efficacy at lower doses. The synergistic effect observed in vitro supports clinical exploration of this combination.  

CAP's selectivity and minimal side effects make it an ideal candidate for combination therapy. Future studies should investigate the underlying mechanisms and optimize dosing regimens for clinical translation.  

### Conclusion  

The invention demonstrates the synergistic efficacy of combining FOLFIRINOX with CAP for cholangiocarcinoma treatment. This approach reduces chemotherapy burden while enhancing anti-cancer effects, offering a promising therapeutic strategy. Further clinical studies are warranted to validate these findings and optimize treatment protocols.
