# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods utilizing pine cone extracts, particularly polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complexes (PPC) derived from Pinus sylvestris, for modulating immune responses. More specifically, the invention pertains to the use of PPC as an adjuvant to enhance T cell proliferation during primary immune responses while demonstrating effects on reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels and apoptosis-related gene expression without substantially altering activation-induced cell death mechanisms. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations, vaccination systems, and therapeutic methods employing PPC to potentiate immune responses against infections, cancers, and other pathological conditions requiring enhanced cellular immunity.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The medicinal properties of pine cone extracts have been recognized across diverse cultures and historical periods. Ancient Greek physician Pedanius Dioscorides (ca. 40-90 AD), in his seminal work De Materia Medica, documented the therapeutic use of pinecone extracts for treating kidney disorders, digestive ailments, and skin conditions resembling modern psoriasis. This five-volume pharmacopeia, written circa 65 AD, represents one of the most influential herbal compendiums in medical history, preserving empirical knowledge of botanical medicines that preceded modern pharmacological science by nearly two millennia.  

Parallel traditions emerged in Japanese folk medicine, particularly among inhabitants of Kyushu island, who utilized aqueous pinecone extracts as broad-spectrum remedies for infectious diseases and malignancies. The empirical observation that pinecone preparations could address such diverse pathologies suggested an immunomodulatory mechanism underlying their therapeutic effects. Contemporary scientific investigation has confirmed that polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complexes (PPC) derived from Pinus sylvestris cones exhibit significant immunopharmacological activity.  

Previous studies have established that orally administered PPC biases immune responses toward T helper type 1 (Th1) polarization when co-administered with DNA, protein, or dendritic cell vaccines. This Th1 bias manifests as enhanced interferon-gamma (IFNγ) production coupled with suppressed interleukin-4 (IL-4) secretion. Notably, PPC administration approximately doubles the quantity of antigen-specific CD8+ cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) detectable during secondary immune responses. In vitro experiments demonstrate that PPC exposure induces rapid differentiation of dendritic-like cells from both human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and murine bone marrow precursors.  

Despite these established immunomodulatory effects, the precise mechanisms by which PPC enhances cellular immunity remain incompletely characterized. In particular, the temporal aspects of PPC activity—whether it primarily affects the expansion phase, contraction phase, or memory formation during immune responses—require clarification. The present invention addresses this knowledge gap by systematically investigating PPC's effects throughout the primary immune response cascade, employing the well-characterized Staphylococcal enterotoxin B (SEB) superantigen model. This model permits precise tracking of Vβ8+ T cell populations through expansion, contraction, and apoptosis phases, providing mechanistic insights into PPC's immunopotentiating properties.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods utilizing pine cone-derived polyphenylpropanoid polysaccharide complexes (PPC) to modulate immune responses. Primary objects of the invention include: (1) providing PPC formulations that enhance primary T cell responses; (2) establishing methods for potentiating vaccine efficacy through PPC co-administration; and (3) developing therapeutic protocols for conditions benefiting from enhanced cellular immunity.  

The invention demonstrates that oral PPC administration significantly amplifies the expansion phase of primary T cell responses to superantigens, approximately doubling the number of activated CD4+ and CD8+ Vβ8+ T cells detectable in lymph nodes. This proliferative enhancement occurs without substantially altering the subsequent contraction phase or activation-induced cell death (AICD) mechanisms. Surprisingly, while PPC reduces reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels in activated T cells—a modification expected to inhibit apoptosis—it does not significantly affect expression patterns of key apoptosis-regulating genes (Bcl-2, Bim) or ultimately alter AICD kinetics.  

The invention further establishes that PPC administration modifies cytokine profiles during immune responses, reducing interleukin-2 (IL-2) while enhancing interferon-gamma (IFNγ) production. This Th1-biased cytokine shift occurs without significant alteration of interleukin-12p70 (IL-12p70) levels, suggesting distinct mechanisms from classical dendritic cell-mediated Th1 polarization.  

Therapeutic applications of the invention encompass vaccine adjuvant systems, immunotherapeutic protocols for chronic infections, and combinatorial approaches for cancer immunotherapy. Pharmaceutical formulations include oral dosage forms, injectable preparations, and combination products integrating PPC with other immunomodulators. The invention's embodiments demonstrate superior immunopotentiation compared to conventional adjuvants, particularly in generating sustained CD8+ T cell responses critical for antiviral and antitumor immunity.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT  

Building upon previous investigations of PPC's immunomodulatory properties, the present invention systematically characterizes its effects throughout the primary immune response cascade. Using the SEB superantigen model, which specifically activates Vβ8+ T cell populations, the invention demonstrates that oral PPC administration enhances T cell proliferation without substantially altering subsequent contraction or apoptosis phases.  

Experimental embodiments employ intraperitoneal SEB injection (100 μg) in Balb/c mice, with concurrent oral PPC administration (200 μg/mL solution, 100 μL gavage three times daily on days 0-1). Lymph node cell analysis at 48 hours (proliferation peak) reveals that PPC co-administration doubles the number of CD3+/Vβ8+ T cells compared to SEB alone (p<0.05), with equivalent enhancement of both CD4+/Vβ8+ (p<0.01) and CD8+/Vβ8+ (p<0.01) subsets. This proliferative amplification occurs without PPC alone inducing nonspecific T cell activation, as naive mice receiving PPC without SEB show no significant changes in Vβ8+ T cell numbers.  

At 96 hours post-SEB administration (contraction phase), the invention demonstrates differential survival of T cell subsets. While CD4+/Vβ8+ populations contract to baseline levels in both SEB and SEB+PPC groups, CD8+/Vβ8+ cells remain significantly elevated in PPC-treated mice (p<0.05 versus SEB alone). This selective CD8+ persistence suggests that PPC's enhancement of secondary responses observed in prior studies primarily stems from amplified primary expansion rather than contraction phase modulation.  

Cytokine profiling reveals that PPC administration significantly reduces serum IL-2 (p<0.05) while enhancing IFNγ levels (p<0.005) at 48 hours, without altering IL-12p70 concentrations. This distinctive cytokine signature implies that PPC modulates T cell responses through mechanisms distinct from classical dendritic cell-mediated Th1 polarization. The elevated IFNγ may contribute to the observed preferential CD4+ contraction, consistent with literature reports of IFNγ-induced CD4+ apoptosis.  

Mechanistic investigations demonstrate that PPC reduces intracellular ROS levels in activated T cells (p<0.01), suggesting potential antioxidant activity. However, contrary to expectations from ROS reduction, PPC does not significantly alter Bcl-2 protein expression per cell (though absolute Bcl-2+ cell numbers increase proportionally with expanded T cell populations). Similarly, PPC does not modify SEB-induced suppression of Bcl-2 mRNA or elevation of pro-apoptotic Bim expression. These gene expression patterns correlate with Annexin V staining results showing equivalent apoptosis induction in SEB and SEB+PPC groups (p>0.05), confirming that PPC's immunopotentiation primarily operates during the expansion rather than contraction phase.  

Unexpectedly, PPC administration to naive mice (without SEB) induces a fourfold increase in Bcl-3 mRNA—an NFκB activator associated with T cell survival—suggesting context-dependent immunomodulatory mechanisms. This Bcl-3 induction does not occur in SEB-activated T cells, indicating that PPC's effects differ between resting and activated immune states.  

The invention further encompasses dendritic cell maturation studies, demonstrating that PPC enhances CD11c+ cell accumulation in lymph nodes. This observation suggests that PPC's T cell proliferative enhancement may involve dendritic cell-mediated mechanisms, potentially through increased antigen presentation capacity or lymph node homing.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example I: Preparation of PPC  

Pine cones from Pinus sylvestris were washed extensively in deionized water and mechanically shredded. The shredded material underwent aqueous extraction at pH 12.0 and 121°C for 30 minutes in stainless steel reactors. The crude extract was standardized by ultraviolet spectroscopy at 280 nm using an extinction coefficient of 22.8 L·gram^-1·cm^-1 for polyphenylpropanoids. Contaminant testing confirmed absence of pesticides, heavy metals, endotoxins, and microorganisms. The purified extract was spray-dried to yield a stable brown powder, which was suspended in sterile water (25 mg/mL), centrifuged (10,000 × g, 20 minutes), and filtered (0.2 μm) before use. Working solutions (200 μg/mL) were prepared weekly in sterile water.  

### Example II: In Vivo Immune Stimulation  

Balb/c mice (6-7 weeks old) received intraperitoneal injections of 100 μg SEB in 100 μL PBS or PBS alone (naive controls). The SEB+PPC group received concurrent oral PPC (200 μg/mL, 100 μL gavage) three times daily on days 0-1. Lymph nodes and spleens were harvested at 48 or 96 hours for analysis.  

### Example III: Flow Cytometric Analysis  

Single-cell suspensions from lymph nodes were stained with fluorescent antibodies against CD3 (APC), CD4 (FITC), CD8 (PE), and Vβ8 (PerCP-eFluor710). Flow cytometry was performed using a FACSCalibur instrument, with data analysis using FlowJo software.  

### Example IV: ROS Detection  

Lymph node cells were incubated with CM-H2DCFDA (5 μM) and anti-CD3/Vβ8 antibodies for 15 minutes at 37°C. ROS levels were quantified by flow cytometry as fluorescence intensity in CD3+ populations.  

### Example V: Gene Expression Analysis  

Total RNA was extracted from lymph node cells using RNeasy kits. Reverse transcription produced cDNA for quantitative PCR analysis of Bcl-2, Bcl-3, Bcl-xL, Bax, and Bim mRNA using SYBR Green chemistry. Expression levels were normalized to β-actin and analyzed by the ΔΔCt method.  

### Example VI: Results Summary  

1. Oral PPC doubled CD4+/Vβ8+ and CD8+/Vβ8+ T cell numbers at 48 hours (p<0.01)  
2. CD8+/Vβ8+ cells remained elevated at 96 hours in PPC-treated mice (p<0.05)  
3. PPC reduced IL-2 (p<0.05) and enhanced IFNγ (p<0.005) at 48 hours  
4. ROS levels decreased in PPC-treated T cells (p<0.01) without altering apoptosis  
5. Bcl-3 mRNA increased fourfold in naive mice receiving PPC alone  

These examples demonstrate PPC's unique immunomodulatory profile, establishing its utility as a vaccine adjuvant and immunotherapeutic agent.
