## FIELD  

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for magnetic field patterning of nickel nanofibers. More particularly, the invention pertains to the formulation of nickel metal-organic decomposition (MOD) precursor inks, their printing onto substrates, and subsequent thermal reduction under a magnetic field to produce aligned nickel nanowires with enhanced electrical and magnetic properties.  

## BACKGROUND  

The formulation of nickel MOD precursor inks presents unique challenges due to the tendency of nickel to oxidize upon reduction from its complexes. Traditional nanoparticle inks suffer from issues such as agglomeration and solvent evaporation, which adversely affect print quality and stability. In contrast, MOD precursor inks offer advantages such as homogeneity, stability, and the absence of particulate instability. However, prior formulations of nickel MOD inks have been limited by low metal content and the need for extreme reduction conditions.  

Various digital printing techniques, including aerosol jet printing, inkjet printing, and microextrusion, have been employed for depositing functional metal inks. Nickel, as a ferromagnetic metal, exhibits desirable properties such as corrosion resistance, high electrical conductivity, and significant magnetic permeability, making it suitable for applications in printed electronics and magnetic devices. Despite these advantages, prior methods for fabricating nickel nanowires have relied on multi-step processes involving templates, inert atmospheres, and harsh reduction conditions.  

Limitations of nanoparticle inks include agglomeration and inconsistent rheological properties during printing. MOD precursor inks overcome these challenges by providing a homogeneous solution that can be precisely printed and thermally reduced to form metallic structures. The present invention addresses the need for a simplified, template-free method to produce aligned nickel nanowires with controlled morphology and enhanced functional properties.  

Prior art methods for fabricating nanowires have involved hydrothermal processing, chemical reduction, and electrochemical deposition, often requiring extensive post-processing. The present invention introduces a novel approach utilizing magnetic field alignment during thermal reduction, enabling the direct formation of aligned nanowires on substrates without additional purification steps.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a method for producing aligned nickel nanowires through the printing, curing, and magnetic alignment of nickel MOD precursor inks. The method comprises formulating a homogeneous nickel precursor ink, printing the ink onto a substrate, and thermally reducing the printed ink in the presence of a magnetic field to yield aligned nanowires.  

A key aspect of the invention is the ability to sequentially print, cure, and magnetically align two or more geometric patterns, enabling the fabrication of interlayered nanofibers with tailored electrical and magnetic properties. The resulting structures exhibit enhanced isotropic electrical conductivity and magnetic anisotropy, making them suitable for applications such as printed electronics, magnetic sensors, and optical devices.  

The invention further encompasses the formulation of nickel MOD precursor inks comprising nickel formate complexed with ethylenediamine, which undergoes thermal reduction at relatively low temperatures. The reduction process occurs in ambient conditions, producing pure nickel nanowires without the need for inert atmospheres or post-processing.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention involves printing a nickel or other ferromagnetic metal precursor ink onto a substrate using techniques such as aerosol jet printing, inkjet printing, or screen printing. The printed ink is then sintered under controlled thermal conditions to reduce the metal precursor to its elemental state. A magnetic field is applied during sintering to induce alignment of the resulting nanowires along the field lines.  

Fabrication of aligned interlayered nanofibers is achieved by sequentially printing multiple layers, each cured under a magnetic field oriented in a desired direction. This method enhances the isotropic electrical properties of the printed structures, enabling applications requiring directional conductivity or magnetic response.  

The technology is further illustrated by the following specific examples, which demonstrate the formulation of the nickel MOD ink, its printing and curing, and the characterization of the resulting nanowires.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**Materials Used**  
The nickel MOD precursor ink was formulated using nickel formate (Alfa Aesar) and ethylenediamine (Fisher Scientific) as the primary components. Distilled water served as the solvent, and additives such as polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP) and BYK 333 surfactant were incorporated to modify ink properties.  

**Ink Formulation**  
Ethylenediamine (0.72 mL, 10.77 mmol) was dissolved in 4 mL of distilled water, followed by the addition of nickel formate (0.8 g, 5.38 mmol). The mixture was stirred for 15 minutes at room temperature and filtered through a 0.2 µm syringe filter. PVP (1% w/w) and BYK 333 surfactant (20 µL of a 10% v/v solution) were added to enhance ink stability and wettability.  

**Characterization of Surface Tension and Viscosity**  
The surface tension of the ink was measured as 44.01 mN/m using a pendant drop method, while the viscosity was determined to be 3.2 cP using a microVISC viscometer. These properties were optimized for compatibility with aerosol jet printing.  

**Substrate Treatment**  
Glass substrates were cleaned with isopropyl alcohol and treated with atmospheric plasma to improve ink adhesion and wetting.  

**Thermal Analysis**  
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) and differential thermal analysis (DTA) revealed a two-stage decomposition process. Solvent evaporation occurred below 100°C, and nickel reduction was observed at 235°C. The final nickel content in the ink was 5.7% w/w.  

**Surface Morphology and Elemental Analysis**  
Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS) confirmed the formation of pure nickel nanowires with minimal carbon and oxygen contamination. The EDS spectrum showed characteristic nickel peaks at 0.851 and 7.471 keV.  

**Thickness of Printed Films**  
Optical profilometry indicated a wet film thickness of ~4 µm for a single printed layer, which decreased upon drying and curing due to solvent evaporation.  

**Electrical Conductivity**  
Four-point probe measurements demonstrated anisotropic resistivity, with lower values observed along the axis of aligned nanowires. The lowest resistivity achieved was 560 µΩ·cm for nanowire grid structures.  

**Magnetic Properties**  
Vibrating sample magnetometry (VSM) revealed enhanced remanent magnetization (20 emu·g⁻¹) along the alignment direction, compared to 7 emu·g⁻¹ perpendicular to the nanowires. Saturation magnetization was consistent with bulk nickel at 30 emu·g⁻¹.  

**Aerosol Jet Printing**  
A solid 1 cm × 1 cm square pattern was printed using a 25-gauge dispensing tip at a standoff distance of 5 mm and a translational speed of 2 mm/s.  

**Curing and Magnetic Alignment**  
Printed films were cured at 240°C for 15 minutes under a 250 Oe magnetic field to produce aligned nanowires. Alternating field orientations between layers yielded interconnected nanowire grids.  

**Morphology and Structure**  
SEM images showed nanowires composed of fused nanoparticles (100–250 nm), resembling a "string of pearls" morphology. Fast Fourier transform (FFT) analysis quantified alignment, with peaks corresponding to the magnetic field direction.  

**Electrical Resistivity**  
Resistivity decreased with additional printed layers, reaching 560 µΩ·cm for nanowire grids. This represents an 80-fold improvement over bulk nickel.  

**Hysteresis Loops**  
Magnetic hysteresis loops confirmed shape anisotropy, with higher remanence along the nanowire axis. Grid structures exhibited balanced properties in orthogonal directions.  

**Flexible Substrate Printing**  
The method was demonstrated on flexible substrates, confirming feasibility for wearable electronics and other applications requiring conformal deposition.  

The foregoing examples illustrate the invention’s utility in producing aligned nickel nanowires with tailored functional properties. Variations in ink composition, printing parameters, and magnetic field conditions may be employed without departing from the scope of the invention.
