# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to novel Helicobacter pylori strains engineered for use as delivery vehicles for biologically active agents. More particularly, the invention pertains to isolated H. pylori strains exhibiting low pathogenicity while retaining the ability to chronically colonize mammalian gastric mucosa, making them suitable for therapeutic and diagnostic applications. The disclosed bacterial strains overcome limitations of existing attenuated bacterial delivery systems by providing stable, persistent colonization without requiring host adaptation.  

## BACKGROUND  

Live bacteria have been explored as delivery vehicles for vaccines, therapeutics, and diagnostic agents due to their ability to target specific tissues and stimulate immune responses. However, current bacterial delivery systems suffer from critical limitations including poor colonization efficiency, transient persistence, and safety concerns associated with pathogenicity. Attenuated strains of pathogenic bacteria often lose essential colonization factors during attenuation, rendering them ineffective as delivery vehicles. Conversely, insufficiently attenuated strains may retain unacceptable virulence.  

Helicobacter pylori presents a unique solution to these challenges. As a natural colonizer of the human gastric mucosa, H. pylori has evolved mechanisms for persistent infection without systemic invasion. The invention leverages this biology by identifying and engineering specific H. pylori strains that maintain colonization capability while exhibiting reduced pathogenicity. These strains serve as superior platforms for delivering biologically active agents to the gastrointestinal tract.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides isolated Helicobacter pylori strains characterized by:  
1) Low pathogenicity in mammalian hosts  
2) Ability to establish chronic gastric infection  
3) Natural transformability allowing genetic modification  
4) Stable colonization without requiring host adaptation  

Specific embodiments include the H. pylori strains designated OND737, OND738, OND739, OND740, OND248, and OND256. These strains were selected through a rigorous screening process evaluating colonization efficiency, persistence, immunogenicity, and safety profiles in multiple animal models.  

A second aspect of the invention provides recombinant derivatives of these H. pylori strains engineered to deliver biologically active agents. The strains may be modified to express therapeutic proteins, vaccines, or diagnostic markers while maintaining their colonization capabilities.  

Strain OND737 demonstrates particularly robust colonization across multiple mouse models, including C57BL/6, C3H, and DBA/2 strains. OND738 shows superior persistence in long-term infections, maintaining stable colonization for over 10 months. OND739 and OND740 exhibit enhanced immunogenicity while maintaining low pathogenicity. Strains OND248 and OND256 have been optimized for efficient natural transformation, facilitating genetic modification.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

The following detailed description illustrates the invention's embodiments and enables their practice. The scope encompasses the described strains, their derivatives, and methods of use, while incorporating conventional techniques known in microbiology and genetic engineering.  

Key terms are defined as:  
- "Low pathogenicity": Causing minimal or no clinical symptoms while establishing infection  
- "Chronic infection": Persistent colonization lasting at least 3 months  
- "Natural transformation": Ability to uptake and incorporate exogenous DNA without artificial manipulation  
- "Mammalian subject": Includes humans and animal models  

The invention's H. pylori strains exhibit several critical characteristics:  
1) They infect mice without requiring host adaptation  
2) They maintain stable urease activity essential for gastric colonization  
3) They demonstrate predictable colonization patterns across genetic backgrounds  

Clinical isolates were obtained from human biopsies and screened for desirable properties. Mutants or derivatives may be created through insertion of heterologous nucleic acids, deletion of virulence factors, or other modifications that maintain colonization ability.  

Biologically active agents that may be delivered include:  
- Vaccine antigens  
- Therapeutic proteins  
- Diagnostic markers  
- RNA interference agents  

Pharmaceutical applications include:  
- Mucosal vaccination  
- Localized drug delivery  
- Diagnostic imaging  
- Gut microbiome modulation  

Methods for identifying suitable strains involve:  
1) Screening clinical isolates for natural transformability  
2) Testing colonization efficiency in animal models  
3) Assessing persistence over extended periods  
4) Evaluating immunogenicity and safety profiles  

### Isolation of Clinical Strains  

Clinical isolates were obtained from gastric biopsy specimens and resurrected from glycerol stocks. Isolates were grown on selective blood agar plates under microaerobic conditions. Genotyping confirmed H. pylori identity through 16S rRNA sequencing and species-specific PCR. Natural transformation competence was assessed using standardized protocols with selectable markers.  

### Example 2  

Candidate strains were tested in a C57BL/6 mouse model. Mice were challenged with 10^9 CFU via orogastric inoculation. Colonization levels were quantified 4 weeks post-infection by viable plate counts from homogenized gastric tissue. Strains showing >10^4 CFU/g tissue were selected for further evaluation.  

### Example 3  

Robust colonizers underwent additional testing to confirm stable colonization. Mice were sacrificed at 3-month intervals to monitor bacterial persistence. Strains maintaining >10^3 CFU/g tissue over 6 months were designated true colonizers.  

### Example 4  

Long-term colonization was assessed over 10 months. Gastric biopsies and serum samples were collected periodically. Strains establishing chronic infection without causing pathological changes were selected for development.  

### Example 5  

Immunogenicity was evaluated in DBA/2J mice. H. pylori-specific IgG antibodies were measured by ELISA at 2-month intervals. Strains OND737 and OND739 induced strong antibody responses without causing excessive inflammation.  

### Example 6  

Additional screening in C57BL/6 mice identified OND248 and OND256 as efficient colonizers. These strains showed consistent colonization across multiple experiments with low variability between animals.  

### Example 7  

Colonization efficiency was optimized using casein-rich diets. Mice fed 20% casein showed 2-3 fold higher bacterial loads compared to standard chow, without adverse effects. This diet was adopted for subsequent studies.  

### Example 8  

Broad-spectrum colonization was tested in C3H, FVB/n, CBA, DBA/2, 129/s and Swiss mouse strains. Strains OND737, OND738, and OND740 colonized all tested models at >10^4 CFU/g tissue, while comparator strain X47 showed inconsistent results. These data demonstrate the invention's utility across diverse genetic backgrounds.
