# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The complement system represents an essential component of innate immunity, providing a first line of defense against pathogens through opsonization, inflammation, and direct lysis of target cells. Central to this system is the C3 protein, which serves as the convergence point for all complement activation pathways. Upon activation, C3 undergoes proteolytic cleavage to generate C3a and C3b fragments. The C3b fragment plays a pivotal role in complement cascade amplification by forming the catalytic subunit of both C3 and C5 convertases in the alternative pathway.  

The C3b fragment interacts with multiple complement regulators and receptors, including complement receptor type 1 (CR1) and factor H (fH). These interactions are critical for controlling complement activation and preventing host tissue damage. The identification of CRIg (complement receptor of the immunoglobulin family) provided further insight into complement regulation, as this receptor specifically binds to C3b and its degradation products. Structural studies of the C3b:CRIg complex revealed key interactions between these molecules, highlighting regions critical for complement regulation.  

Recent crystallographic analyses have elucidated the three-dimensional structures of C3b in complex with various binding partners. These studies demonstrated substantial conformational changes in C3b compared to its precursor C3, particularly in the CUB and TED domains. Such structural rearrangements create novel binding surfaces that are recognized by complement regulators and receptors. The alternative pathway of complement activation represents a particularly important therapeutic target, as its dysregulation contributes to numerous inflammatory and autoimmune disorders. Current strategies for complement inhibition face challenges including broad pathway suppression and rapid clearance of inhibitors from circulation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides the crystal structure of a human CRIg polypeptide in both its unbound form and in complex with C3b and C3c fragments. The crystalline form of CRIg belongs to space group P212121 with unit cell dimensions of a = 54.8 Å, b = 78.3 Å, c = 108.5 Å, and diffracts X-rays to a resolution of at least 2.5 Å. The invention further discloses the crystal structure of the CRIg:C3b complex, which crystallizes in space group C2 with unit cell parameters a = 216.4 Å, b = 180.4 Å, c = 154.6 Å, β = 115.73°, and provides structural data to 3.1 Å resolution.  

The structural coordinates reveal critical binding interfaces between CRIg and C3b, particularly involving the β-chain of C3b. The invention identifies specific residues within the immunoglobulin variable (IgV) domain of CRIg that mediate high-affinity binding to C3b and C3c. These structural insights enable the rational design of CRIg agonists and antagonists for therapeutic modulation of complement activity. The crystalline complexes demonstrate that CRIg binding occurs on the face of C3b opposite the TED domain, creating steric hindrance that prevents formation of alternative pathway convertases.  

The invention further provides methods for producing and purifying these crystalline complexes, including conditions for obtaining diffraction-quality crystals. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising these crystalline structures are disclosed for use in treating complement-associated disorders. The structural coordinates deposited with the Protein Data Bank (accession codes 2ICE and 2ICF) form an integral part of this invention and enable structure-based drug design targeting the CRIg:C3b interaction interface.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT  

### Definitions  

For purposes of this invention, "CRIg" refers to complement receptor of the immunoglobulin family, also known as VSIG4 or Z39Ig. The term encompasses native sequence CRIg polypeptides including the 241 amino acid isoform (SEQ ID NO:1) and the 279 amino acid isoform (SEQ ID NO:2), as well as functional fragments comprising at least the extracellular IgV domain.  

A "native sequence CRIg polypeptide" refers to a polypeptide having the same amino acid sequence as CRIg derived from nature, including naturally occurring variants such as allelic variants or splice variants. The term "CRIg variant" refers to polypeptides having at least 80% sequence identity to native CRIg while retaining C3b binding activity, with preferred embodiments having at least 90% or 95% identity.  

The "extracellular domain" (ECD) of CRIg comprises amino acids 1-193 of the mature protein and includes the IgV domain responsible for C3b binding. Percent sequence identity is calculated using the Smith-Waterman algorithm with default parameters, where the percentage is based on the number of identical residues in the optimal alignment.  

"Complement-associated diseases" encompass disorders mediated by excessive or dysregulated complement activation, including but not limited to age-related macular degeneration (AMD), paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH), atypical hemolytic uremic syndrome (aHUS), and various inflammatory conditions. The invention specifically provides crystalline structures enabling development of therapeutics for these indications.  

### Modes of Carrying Out the Invention  

The invention employs x-ray crystallography to determine the three-dimensional structures of CRIg and its complexes with C3b and C3c. High-quality crystals were obtained using hanging drop vapor diffusion at 19°C with reservoir solutions containing 10% PEG 4000, 0.2 M MgCl2, and 100 mM Na-HEPES (pH 7.0). Crystals grew within one week to dimensions exceeding 100 μm in each direction, suitable for high-resolution data collection.  

Diffraction data were collected at synchrotron beamlines using cryogenic conditions (100 K). The CRIg structure was solved by molecular replacement using known IgV domain structures as search models. The C3b:CRIg complex structure was determined using the refined CRIg structure and C3b coordinates (PDB 2I07) as molecular replacement models. Iterative cycles of refinement and model building yielded final structures with Rwork/Rfree values of 21.7%/28.3%.  

The crystalline structures reveal that CRIg binds primarily to the β-chain of C3b, with a buried surface area of approximately 1930 Å². Key interactions involve residues in the MG3 and MG4 domains of C3b contacting the CDR-like loops of the CRIg IgV domain. The binding interface includes both hydrophobic contacts and specific hydrogen bonds, particularly involving His897 of C3b which forms critical interactions with CRIg backbone atoms.  

These structural insights enable several therapeutic applications:  
1) Design of CRIg agonists that mimic its complement inhibitory activity  
2) Development of small molecule inhibitors targeting the CRIg:C3b interface  
3) Engineering of improved CRIg-Fc fusion proteins for therapeutic use  
4) Structure-guided optimization of antibody inhibitors like S77  

The crystalline forms provide unparalleled purity and stability for pharmaceutical applications. The invention further provides methods for producing these crystals at scale, including purification protocols yielding >95% homogeneous protein suitable for crystallization.  

### Example 1  

Crystals of human CRIg were grown as described above and diffracted to 2.5 Å resolution. Data collection statistics are summarized in Table 1 (not shown). The structure revealed the characteristic IgV fold with nine β-strands forming two β-sheets in a Greek key topology. A conserved disulfide bond between Cys23 and Cys88 stabilizes the domain structure.  

The C3b:CRIg complex crystals diffracted to 3.1 Å resolution in space group C2. Structural analysis showed CRIg binding to the MG3/MG4 domains of C3b, with the CRIg CDR-like loops inserting into a groove on the C3b surface. Comparison with unbound C3b revealed conformational changes in the CUB and TED domains upon CRIg binding, suggesting allosteric effects on convertase formation.  

The binding interface involves residues 80-100 of CRIg contacting C3b residues 890-920. Key interactions include hydrogen bonds between CRIg Ser31 and C3b His897, and hydrophobic packing of CRIg Leu97 against C3b Phe898. These specific contacts explain the species selectivity observed for human versus rhesus C3b inhibition.  

### CRIg Variants  

Based on the crystal structure, several CRIg variants were designed and tested for C3b binding affinity. Mutations at the binding interface (e.g., S31A, L97A) significantly reduced binding, while conservative substitutions (S31T, L97I) maintained activity. Variants with enhanced glycosylation showed improved pharmacokinetic properties without affecting C3b binding.  

### Example 3  

The crystalline structures enabled design of CRIg-based inhibitors that selectively block the alternative complement pathway. In functional assays, CRIg-Fc inhibited AP-mediated hemolysis (IC50 = 10 nM) without affecting classical pathway activity. Structural analysis showed this specificity results from CRIg's unique binding site on C3b that is only accessible in the alternative pathway convertase.  

### Example 4  

In a collagen-induced arthritis model, CRIg-Fc (10 mg/kg twice weekly) significantly reduced joint inflammation and bone erosion compared to controls (p<0.01). Histological analysis showed reduced complement deposition in treated joints. The crystalline structures enabled optimization of dosing regimens by revealing the CRIg:C3b binding kinetics.  

### Example 5  

Decay acceleration assays demonstrated that CRIg binding to C3b prevents formation of C3/C5 convertases without destabilizing existing complexes. Crystallographic analysis showed this results from steric blocking of factor B binding sites on C3b. The structures further revealed how CRIg binding allosterically affects C5 binding sites located distal to the primary interface.  

The complete structural coordinates have been deposited in the Protein Data Bank under accession codes 2ICE (CRIg) and 2ICF (C3b:CRIg complex). These coordinates enable structure-based design of improved complement inhibitors for therapeutic use.  

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions containing these crystalline proteins, methods for their production, and therapeutic applications targeting complement-associated diseases. The detailed structural information represents a significant advance in complement modulation with broad clinical applicability.
