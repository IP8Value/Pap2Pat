# DESCRIPTION  

Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by Plasmodium parasites transmitted through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. Malaria remains a serious global health problem, particularly in tropical and subtropical regions, with hundreds of millions of clinical cases and hundreds of thousands of deaths reported annually. Preventative medicines such as chemoprophylaxis have been used for malaria prevention, but these approaches have significant limitations including drug resistance, side effects, and the need for continuous administration. Vaccination programs have sought to develop effective malaria vaccines, but current options remain limited in their protective efficacy.  

The RTS,S vaccine targeting the circumsporozoite protein (CSP) and the ChAd63-MVA ME-TRAP vaccine have shown partial efficacy in clinical trials, but neither provides complete protection against malaria infection. Irradiated sporozoites have been considered the "gold standard" for malaria vaccination, inducing sterile protection in animal models and humans, but this approach faces practical challenges for large-scale implementation. There remains a critical need to identify better target antigens that can induce robust and durable protective immunity against malaria.  

A major obstacle in malaria vaccine development has been the identification of suitable antigens that can induce protective immune responses. The lack of an appropriate small animal model for Plasmodium falciparum infection has further complicated preclinical evaluation of candidate vaccines. Liver-stage antigen 1 (LSA-1) was identified as a promising liver-stage protein expressed during the hepatic phase of parasite development. LSA-1 plays important roles in liver-stage development and has been associated with protection in natural infection and irradiated sporozoite immunization studies. However, clinical trials of LSA-1-based vaccines have failed to demonstrate significant protection, highlighting the need for alternative antigens and improved delivery methods.  

The present invention provides novel antigenic compositions and vaccines targeting Plasmodium liver-stage antigens with superior protective efficacy. The invention identifies key candidate antigens including PfLSA1, PfLSAP2, PfUIS3, PfI0580c, and PfSPECT-1 as targets for protective immune responses. Through systematic evaluation using viral vector delivery systems, the invention demonstrates that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 induce particularly robust protection against malaria infection.  

PfLSA1 is a Plasmodium falciparum protein expressed during the liver stage of parasite development. PfLSAP2 is another Plasmodium falciparum protein associated with liver-stage development. PfUIS3 is a Plasmodium falciparum protein upregulated in infective sporozoites. PfI0580c is a Plasmodium falciparum protein of unknown function expressed during liver-stage development. PfSPECT-1 is a Plasmodium falciparum protein involved in sporozoite development.  

The method of identifying these key candidate antigens involved comprehensive screening of liver-stage expressed proteins using viral vector delivery systems in animal models. The results demonstrate that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 induce particularly strong protective efficacy, while PfUIS3 and PfI0580c show moderate protective efficacy. A protective immune response in this context is characterized by the induction of antigen-specific CD8+ T cells producing interferon-gamma (IFNγ) and other effector cytokines, along with the development of antigen-specific antibodies.  

The viral vectors used in the invention may be modified to enhance immunogenicity, such as through codon optimization using redundant codons to improve antigen expression. The composition may utilize two or more different viral vectors in a prime-boost vaccination regimen to enhance immune responses. The pharmaceutical composition may include adjuvants selected from the group consisting of aluminum salts, oil-in-water emulsions, saponins, toll-like receptor agonists, and other immunostimulatory compounds.  

The invention provides nucleic acid sequences encoding viral proteins and Plasmodium proteins, including nucleic acid encoding viral proteins and PfLSA1, nucleic acid encoding viral proteins and PfLSAP2, nucleic acid encoding viral proteins and PfUIS3, nucleic acid encoding viral proteins and PfI0580c, and nucleic acid encoding viral proteins and PfSPECT-1. The invention further encompasses nucleic acid encoding viral proteins and additional Plasmodium proteins beyond those specifically enumerated.  

The invention includes virus particles comprising the described nucleic acid sequences, as well as virus particles comprising Plasmodium proteins. Host cells comprising the nucleic acid sequences are also provided. Methods of eliciting an immune response against malaria are described, including methods for prevention or treatment of malaria infection.  

Vaccination regimens may involve a single dose administration or a prime-boost strategy using different viral vectors. The pharmaceutical composition is suitable for use in prevention or treatment of malaria. The invention further provides kits for vaccination against malaria, including kits specifically comprising PfLSA1, PfLSAP2, PfUIS3, PfI0580c, or PfSPECT-1 antigens. Methods of manufacturing the immunogenic compositions and vaccines are also described.

## 2 ASSESSING IMMUNOGENICITY AND EFFICACY OF EIGHT CANDIDATE P. FALCIPARUM VACCINES IN MICE  

Liver-stage malaria vaccines aim to prevent parasite development in hepatocytes. The ex vivo IFNγ ELISpot assay measures T cell responses but has limitations in assessing multifunctional responses. Multi-parameter flow cytometry provides more comprehensive immune profiling by assessing multiple cytokines (IFNγ, TNFα, IL-2) and markers (CD107a). Antibody levels are measured to assess humoral immunity, though functional antibody assessment presents technical challenges.  

Transgenic P. berghei parasites expressing P. falciparum antigens enable efficacy testing in mice. These chimeric parasites are generated using the P. berghei UIS4 promoter for liver-stage expression. The vaccine development strategy involves systematic evaluation of multiple antigens using viral vectors in different mouse strains.  

Ex vivo IFNγ ELISpot results demonstrate antigen-specific T cell responses in Balb/c and C57BL/6 mice. Intracellular cytokine staining reveals distinct cytokine profiles between mouse strains. Antibody responses measured by LIPS assay show antigen-specific IgG production. Efficacy assessment uses P. berghei sporozoite challenge models.  

The ChAd63-MVA PfCe1TOS vaccine induces cellular immunity and provides partial protection. PFI0580c vaccination shows moderate efficacy in Balb/c mice. PfUIS3 vaccination demonstrates significant protection in certain models. Transgenic parasites expressing P. falciparum antigens enable direct efficacy assessment.  

Comparative analysis reveals that PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 show superior protective efficacy compared to other candidates. ELISpot and ICS results correlate with protection. CD8+ T cell responses appear particularly important for protection. Humoral responses show more variability between antigens and mouse strains.  

The results demonstrate proof-of-concept for identifying better target antigens. PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 emerge as particularly promising candidates based on immunogenicity and efficacy data. The findings support further development of these antigens for human vaccination.  

## 3 ASSESSMENT OF PfUIS3, PfLSA1 AND PfLSAP2 AS CANDIDATES FOR A LIVER-STAGE MALARIA VACCINE  

PfUIS3, PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 demonstrate promising characteristics as vaccine candidates. Inbred mouse studies have limitations due to MHC restriction, necessitating evaluation in outbred models.  

PfUIS3 shows protection in Balb/c mice mediated by CD8+ T cells. Protection in C57BL/6 mice is less pronounced. CD-1 outbred mice show variable responses to PfUIS3 vaccination.  

PfLSA1 induces sterile protection in Balb/c mice through CD8+ T cell-dependent mechanisms. Protection extends to C57BL/6 and CD-1 mice, demonstrating broad efficacy.  

PfLSAP2 vaccination provides robust protection across mouse strains. The immune mechanisms involve strong CD8+ T cell responses and antibody production.  

Immunogenicity assessment reveals consistent responses to these antigens. Combination vaccines maintain immunogenicity without significant interference. Comparative analysis ranks the protective efficacy of these candidates.  

## 4. PROTECTIVE EFFICACY OF THE CANDIDATES VACCINES IN CD-1 OUTBRED MICE  

PfLSA1 demonstrates high protective efficacy in CD-1 mice. PfLSAP2 shows similarly strong protection in this model. PfUIS3 provides more variable protection in outbred mice.  

Control antigens PfCSP and PfTRAP show limited efficacy. Other candidates demonstrate varying levels of protection. The results validate PfLSA1 and PfLSAP2 as lead candidates.  

## 5. SUMMARY  

The results support PfLSA1, PfUIS3, PfLSAP2, and PfI0580c as promising vaccine candidates. Transgenic parasite models provide valuable tools for antigen evaluation.  

## 5. SPECT-1  

PfSPECT-1 represents another potential vaccine target. Its expression pattern and immunogenicity profile warrant further investigation.

PfSPECT-1 vaccine design involves ChAd63 and MVA vectors. Transgenic parasites confirm antigen expression. Immunization studies demonstrate measurable protection. Comparative analysis positions PfSPECT-1 among other candidates.
