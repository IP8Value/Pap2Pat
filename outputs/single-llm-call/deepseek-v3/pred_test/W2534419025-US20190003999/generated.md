## BACKGROUND  

Single particle cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) has emerged as a powerful technique for high-resolution structural analysis of biological macromolecules. This method involves imaging frozen hydrated specimens at cryogenic temperatures to preserve native structure while minimizing radiation damage. Traditional sample preparation techniques for cryo-EM rely on the adsorption of purified protein samples onto amorphous carbon or holey carbon grids prior to vitrification. These conventional approaches suffer from several limitations including poor sample distribution, preferential orientation issues, and the requirement for highly purified protein samples at relatively high concentrations.  

Efforts to improve grid performance have focused on enhancing grid rigidity and developing on-grid purification methods. The introduction of low-dose imaging techniques and advanced single particle analysis algorithms has enabled structure determination at near-atomic resolution. However, these advances have highlighted the need for improved sample preparation methods that can maintain specimen integrity while providing optimal particle distribution and orientation.  

Lipid monolayer affinity grids have been developed to address some of these challenges by providing specific binding sites for target molecules. While these grids offer advantages in particle orientation control, they suffer from limitations including poor mechanical stability, uneven distribution of capture ligands, and non-specific binding to lipid surfaces. The development of new grid materials with improved mechanical properties and specific binding characteristics would represent a significant advance in the field of cryo-EM sample preparation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel electron microscopy grids comprising graphene oxide sheets functionalized with nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) capture agents and deactivating agents. These grids combine the mechanical advantages of graphene-based materials with the specific binding properties of affinity capture ligands. The invention further provides methods for preparing such grids and their use in the structural analysis of biological macromolecules.  

The functionalized grids of the invention exhibit optimal elasticity and rigidity for cryo-EM applications while providing high-density, uniformly distributed capture sites for target molecules. The incorporation of deactivating agents minimizes non-specific binding, enabling direct on-grid purification from complex biological samples such as cell lysates. This integrated approach to sample preparation significantly simplifies workflow while improving the quality of structural data obtained by cryo-EM.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention provides novel electron microscopy grids comprising graphene-based materials modified with specific capture agents and deactivating agents. These grids exhibit desired elasticity and rigidity properties that make them particularly suitable for cryo-EM applications. The operational principle of these grids involves the specific capture of target molecules through affinity interactions while minimizing non-specific adsorption of contaminants.  

The grid coatings of the invention are modified with capture agents that provide specific binding sites for target molecules. In preferred embodiments, these capture agents comprise nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) derivatives capable of coordinating transition metal ions such as nickel, which in turn bind to histidine-tagged proteins. The density and distribution of these capture agents on the grid surface can be precisely controlled to optimize particle distribution for structural studies.  

Deactivating agents are incorporated into the grid coatings to minimize non-specific binding. These agents function by blocking reactive functional groups on the graphene oxide surface that might otherwise participate in unwanted interactions with sample components. Preferred deactivating agents include small molecules such as 4-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and proteins such as bovine serum albumin (BSA). The combination of multiple deactivating agents provides superior blocking performance compared to single agent systems.  

The grid coatings of the invention may comprise various forms of graphene-based materials. Graphene oxide (GO) represents a particularly useful substrate due to its amphiphilic properties and the presence of functional groups that facilitate chemical modification. Graphene oxide sheets can be prepared with controlled dimensions and oxidation states to optimize their performance as grid coatings. The thickness of these coatings is carefully controlled to provide adequate mechanical support while minimizing background scattering in electron microscopy applications.  

Capture agents are attached to the grid coatings through covalent or non-covalent interactions. In preferred embodiments, NTA derivatives are conjugated to graphene oxide sheets through lysine linkers. The density of these capture agents can be adjusted by controlling the reaction conditions during grid preparation. The resulting functionalized grids provide high-affinity binding sites that are uniformly distributed across the grid surface.  

Deactivating agents are applied to the grids either during or after the attachment of capture agents. These agents may include small molecules that react with residual functional groups on the graphene oxide surface, as well as proteins that adsorb to non-specific binding sites. The sequential application of different deactivating agents provides comprehensive blocking of non-specific interactions while preserving the activity of the intended capture sites.  

The targets of the present disclosure include any biological macromolecules that can be specifically captured by the functionalized grids. Particularly suitable targets include histidine-tagged proteins, nucleic acids, protein complexes, and viral particles. The invention enables the direct capture of these targets from complex biological mixtures such as cell lysates, eliminating the need for extensive pre-purification steps.  

The functionalized grids of the invention are particularly useful for high-resolution structure analysis by cryo-EM. The grids facilitate optimal particle distribution and orientation while minimizing background noise from non-specifically bound material. Proteins captured on these grids can be imaged at resolutions sufficient for atomic model building, particularly when combined with advanced image processing algorithms.  

Preparation of proteins for capture on the functionalized grids may involve the incorporation of affinity tags. Histidine tags represent particularly useful affinity handles due to their small size and high binding affinity for NTA-metal complexes. Other affinity tags may also be employed, provided they are compatible with the selected capture agents on the grid surface.  

Imaging of target proteins captured on the functionalized grids can be performed using standard cryo-EM techniques. The grids enable the acquisition of high-quality micrographs with improved signal-to-noise ratios compared to conventional support films. Structural analysis of the captured proteins can be performed using single particle reconstruction methods, with resolutions routinely achievable in the 3-10 Å range.  

The invention encompasses the use of graphene or graphene oxide sheets as the primary grid coating material. These materials provide superior mechanical properties and electron scattering characteristics compared to traditional carbon supports. The thickness of these coatings can be precisely controlled to optimize performance for specific applications.  

Modification of graphene or graphene oxide with capture agents involves chemical conjugation through available functional groups. Carboxylic acid groups on graphene oxide sheets can be activated for reaction with amine-containing capture agents such as NTA-lysine derivatives. The degree of functionalization can be controlled to achieve optimal binding capacity while maintaining grid stability.  

Lipid monolayers may be incorporated into the grid design to provide additional functionality. These monolayers can be deposited onto graphene-bearing grids to create hybrid systems combining the advantages of both material types. The lipid composition can be tailored to provide specific interactions with target molecules or to control particle orientation.  

Single wall carbon nanotubes represent another component that may be incorporated into the grid design. These materials can provide structural reinforcement or additional functional groups for capture agent attachment. The combination of graphene oxide sheets with carbon nanotubes creates composite materials with tunable mechanical and chemical properties.  

Graphene oxide-modified grids are prepared using Langmuir-Schaefer transfer techniques. This method enables the deposition of uniform monolayers with controlled orientation and packing density. The transfer process can be optimized by adjusting parameters such as surface pressure, subphase composition, and transfer speed.  

Metal ions play a critical role in the function of NTA-modified grids. Nickel ions are particularly useful for mediating the interaction between NTA groups and histidine tags. The grids are typically activated by treatment with nickel sulfate solutions prior to sample application. The metal ion concentration can be adjusted to optimize binding specificity and capacity.  

Activation of NTA groups with metal ions creates high-affinity binding sites for histidine-tagged proteins. This activation step is typically performed immediately before sample application to minimize oxidation or displacement of the metal ions. The activated grids can be stored for limited periods under appropriate conditions.  

Blocking of non-specific reaction sites is performed using combinations of chemical and biological agents. Small molecules such as PABA react with residual epoxy and aldehyde groups on the graphene oxide surface. Proteins such as BSA provide additional blocking by adsorbing to hydrophobic patches and other potential non-specific binding sites.  

Incubation of clarified lysate with the functionalized grids enables direct on-grid purification of target molecules. The incubation time and temperature can be adjusted to optimize capture efficiency while minimizing non-specific binding. Typical incubation conditions involve room temperature incubation for 2-5 minutes with gentle agitation.  

Washing and cryo-fixation of the grids are performed to remove unbound material and preserve the captured particles in vitreous ice. Washing steps typically involve brief rinses with appropriate buffers to remove loosely bound contaminants. Cryo-fixation is performed using standard plunge-freezing techniques with optimized blotting conditions to achieve ideal ice thickness.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Graphene oxide sheets were functionalized with nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) groups through a multi-step synthetic procedure. The carboxylic acid groups on graphene oxide were first activated by treatment with thionyl chloride in dimethylformamide at 70°C for three days. The activated graphene oxide was then reacted with tris(O-t-butyl)-Nα,Nα-dicarboxymethyllysine ester in acetonitrile with triethylamine as a base. The resulting t-butyl-protected intermediate was deprotected using trifluoroacetic acid to yield GO-NTA. The functionalization was characterized using Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy, which showed the expected absorption bands for NTA functional groups. UV-visible spectroscopy confirmed the successful modification through characteristic shifts in the absorption maxima.  

### Example 2  

Graphene oxide was synthesized using an improved Hummers' method from graphite precursor. The oxidation reaction was carried out using a mixture of sulfuric acid, phosphoric acid, and potassium permanganate. The resulting graphene oxide was purified by repeated washing and centrifugation. The product was characterized using UV-visible spectroscopy, which showed the characteristic absorption peak at approximately 240 nm for dry graphene oxide and a shift to 270 nm when dispersed in aqueous solution. The degree of oxidation was controlled by adjusting reaction time and temperature to optimize the density of carboxylic acid groups available for subsequent functionalization.  

### Example 3  

GO-NTA was synthesized using thionyl chloride activation followed by reaction with tris(O-t-butyl)-Nα,Nα-dicarboxymethyllysine ester. The t-butyl protecting groups were removed by treatment with trifluoroacetic acid in tetrahydrofuran. The reaction progress was monitored by thin layer chromatography and the product was purified by repeated washing with tetrahydrofuran and water. The final product was obtained as a dark brown powder that could be readily dispersed in aqueous and organic solvents. The success of each synthetic step was confirmed by spectroscopic analysis and functional testing of the resulting material.  

### Example 4  

GO-NTA sheets were exfoliated using probe sonication in a methanol-water mixture. The sonication was performed in multiple short bursts to minimize damage to the graphene oxide structure while achieving sufficient exfoliation. The resulting dispersion was centrifuged to remove any non-exfoliated material, yielding a stable suspension of single and few-layer GO-NTA sheets. The exfoliation efficiency was monitored by measuring the concentration of dispersed material using UV-visible spectroscopy and by examining the dispersion stability over time.  

### Example 5  

Exfoliated GO-NTA was deposited at the air-water interface using a Langmuir trough setup. The GO-NTA dispersion was slowly introduced at the interface using a syringe pump until the surface pressure reached 15 mN/m. The film was allowed to equilibrate before compression to the desired surface pressure. Isopropanol was added to the subphase to lower the surface tension and facilitate monolayer formation. The surface pressure-area isotherm showed characteristic features indicating the formation of a coherent monolayer that could be transferred to solid substrates.  

### Example 6  

A GO-NTA monolayer was formed at the air-water interface and characterized using surface pressure-area isotherm measurements. The isotherm showed distinct regions corresponding to different phases of monolayer organization, including a gaseous phase at low pressures, a liquid-expanded phase at intermediate pressures, and a condensed phase at high pressures. The collapse pressure of the monolayer was approximately 15 mN/m, beyond which the film underwent irreversible reorganization. The monolayer could be transferred to solid substrates at various surface pressures to yield films with different packing densities and mechanical properties.  

### Example 7  

GO-NTA was modified with 4-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) to deactivate residual reactive groups on the graphene oxide surface. The modification was performed by reacting GO-NTA with excess PABA in aqueous solution under sonication. The reaction mixture was stirred for 24 hours to ensure complete modification of available reactive sites. The product was purified by repeated washing and centrifugation to remove unreacted PABA. The success of the modification was confirmed by spectroscopic analysis and by testing the reduction in non-specific binding capacity of the modified material.  

### Example 8  

PABA-GO-NTA was further modified with fluorescein for visualization purposes. Aminofluorescein was reacted with the PABA-modified material in aqueous solution under dark conditions. The reaction proceeded through the formation of amide bonds between the fluorescein amine groups and carboxylic acid groups on the PABA-modified surface. The product was extensively washed to remove unreacted fluorescein and characterized by fluorescence spectroscopy and microscopy. The fluorescein labeling enabled direct visualization of the grid coating distribution and quality by fluorescence microscopy techniques.  

### Example 9  

Bovine serum albumin (BSA) was used to further modify the grid surface to minimize non-specific binding. Following Langmuir-Schaefer transfer of GO-NTA or PABA-GO-NTA onto EM grids, the grids were incubated with BSA solution for 5 minutes. The grids were then washed with deionized water to remove unbound protein and stored in a desiccator until use. The BSA modification provided additional blocking of non-specific binding sites while preserving the activity of the NTA capture sites. The effectiveness of the blocking was assessed by testing the grids with both target proteins and non-specific control proteins.
