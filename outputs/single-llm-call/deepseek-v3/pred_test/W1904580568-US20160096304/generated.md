# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of electrospinning, specifically to methods and apparatuses for producing microscale or nanoscale fibers with core-sheath architectures. Core-sheath fibers represent an important class of materials that enable particle encapsulation, bicomponent functionality, hollow structures, and processing of materials that are otherwise unelectrospinnable. Conventional manufacturing methods for producing such fibers, particularly coaxial needle electrospinning systems, suffer from significant limitations in throughput, typically producing polymer mass rates on the order of 0.01-0.1 g/h.  

Electrospinning has emerged as a promising alternative to traditional fiber production methods due to its ability to create fibers with diameters ranging from nanometers to micrometers. Coaxial electrospinning, where two polymer solutions are ejected in a core-sheath architecture from concentrically positioned needles, has expanded the capabilities of electrospinning. However, this approach remains limited by low production rates and frequent clogging issues.  

Recent developments in needleless electrospinning technologies, such as the Nanospider technology by Elmarco and Arsenal Electrospinning Technology, have attempted to address throughput limitations. These systems utilize rotating electrodes or open bath designs to generate multiple electrospinning jets from free liquid surfaces. While these technologies represent improvements over needle-based systems, they still fail to provide commercially viable solutions for high-throughput production of core-sheath fibers.  

The present invention introduces a novel apparatus and method that overcomes these limitations through the implementation of coaxial nozzle arrays in a slit-surface configuration. This design enables the formation of multiple Taylor cones and corresponding electrospinning jets along an extended interface between core and sheath solutions. The invention provides significant advantages over existing technologies, including throughput increases of over 1,000-fold compared to traditional methods, while maintaining the versatility to process a wide range of material systems.  

Despite these advances, there remains a need for further improvements in electrospinning technology to achieve truly industrial-scale production of core-sheath fibers. The present invention addresses this need by optimizing shear stresses at fluid boundaries, controlling flow dynamics through precise slit geometries, and enabling continuous operation with minimal maintenance requirements.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for high-throughput production of electrospun core-sheath fibers. The invention achieves this through a novel slit-surface electrospinning approach that optimizes shear stresses at the interface between core and sheath polymer solutions.  

The method involves forming electrospun core-sheath fibers by simultaneously flowing core and sheath polymer solutions through precisely designed slit fixtures. The apparatus comprises first and second vessels for containing the core and sheath polymers respectively, with flow control mechanisms to regulate the delivery of each solution to the electrospinning interface. An electrical potential is applied between the slit fixture and a collector to initiate the formation of multiple electrospinning jets.  

A key innovation of the present invention is the optimization of shear stress at the interface between core and sheath solutions to achieve desired core-to-sheath ratios in the resulting fibers. This is accomplished through careful control of flow rates, slit geometries, and solution viscosities. The invention enables the formation of stable core-sheath cone-jets along an extended interface, with each jet producing continuous core-sheath fibers at significantly higher rates than conventional coaxial needle systems.  

Various embodiments of the invention are disclosed, including systems with adjustable slit widths for both core and sheath channels, variable slit heights, and configurations allowing for continuous operation. The invention maintains the versatility of traditional coaxial electrospinning while overcoming its throughput limitations, enabling commercial-scale production of core-sheath fibers for applications ranging from drug delivery to advanced textiles.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention employs a needleless electrospinning process that generates multiple core-sheath fiber jets simultaneously from an extended slit interface. The formation of core-sheath fibers occurs through the spontaneous organization of two polymer solutions at the slit exit when subjected to an appropriate electric field.  

The process begins with the formation of Taylor cones along the length of the slit interface. Initially, these cones are composed primarily of sheath material, but within milliseconds transition to distinct core-sheath configurations as the core solution becomes entrained in the electrospinning jet. This phenomenon is analogous to the fluid dynamics principle of selective withdrawal, where sufficient shear stress at the interface between two fluids causes the lower fluid to be drawn into the flow of the upper fluid.  

Control of the electrospinning process is achieved through precise management of flow rates and slit geometries. The flow velocity of each solution at the slit exit is determined by both the volumetric flow rate and the cross-sectional area of the respective slit. These parameters can be adjusted to optimize the ratio of sheath to core flow velocities, which directly impacts the quality and structure of the resulting core-sheath fibers.  

The effects of varying sheath flow rate while maintaining constant core flow rate have been systematically investigated. Increasing the sheath flow rate relative to the core flow rate results in improved encapsulation of the core material, as evidenced by narrower core streams within the electrospinning jets. Conversely, reducing the sheath flow rate below a critical threshold relative to the core flow rate leads to loss of distinct core-sheath structure in the resulting fibers.  

Similarly, variations in core flow rate while keeping sheath flow rate constant demonstrate the importance of maintaining an appropriate velocity ratio between the two solutions. Optimal core-sheath fiber formation occurs when the sheath solution flow velocity exceeds the core solution flow velocity by a factor of at least 1.5, with superior results achieved at ratios of 2.0 or higher.  

### Core Flow Rate  

The core flow rate is a critical parameter that can be varied while maintaining a constant sheath flow rate to control fiber morphology. Experimental results demonstrate that distinct core-sheath cone-jets form reliably when the core flow rate is maintained at 20-40 mL/h with a sheath flow rate of 200 mL/h. At higher core flow rates (e.g., 60 mL/h with 200 mL/h sheath flow), the core-sheath structure becomes less distinct due to insufficient shear stress at the interface.  

### Slit Fixture Geometry Core Slit Width  

The core slit width significantly impacts flow velocities and Taylor cone formation. When the core slit width is varied while maintaining constant sheath slit width, several effects are observed. Narrower core slits increase the core solution flow velocity at the exit, which can improve core encapsulation when balanced with appropriate sheath flow conditions.  

The effects of varying core slit width on flow velocities follow predictable fluid dynamics principles, with velocity inversely proportional to cross-sectional area for a given flow rate. More importantly, the core slit width affects Taylor cone formation by altering the interface dynamics between core and sheath solutions. Optimal core slit widths have been found to range between 0.5-2.0 mm, depending on solution properties and desired fiber characteristics.  

### Sheath Slit Width  

Variation of sheath slit width while keeping core slit width constant produces distinct effects on the electrospinning process. Narrower sheath slits (e.g., 1.5 mm) produce higher sheath flow velocities at equivalent flow rates compared to wider slits (e.g., 3.0 mm). This increased velocity enhances the shear stress at the interface, promoting more effective entrainment of the core solution.  

The effects of sheath slit width on Taylor cone formation are particularly significant. Experimental results demonstrate that distinct core-sheath cone-jets form reliably with sheath slit widths of 1.5-2.2 mm, while wider slits (3.0 mm) require adjustment of other parameters (such as reduced core flow rate) to achieve comparable results.  

### Sheath Slit Width and Core Slit Width  

Simultaneous variation of both sheath and core slit widths allows for precise control over fiber quality and production characteristics. When both slit widths are reduced proportionally, the increased flow velocities can lead to more stable jet formation but may require higher pumping pressures.  

The effects of varying both slit widths on flow velocities follow predictable patterns, with the velocity ratio between sheath and core solutions being particularly important for maintaining proper core encapsulation. Optimal fiber quality is achieved when the sheath-to-core velocity ratio exceeds 2.0, which can be accomplished through various combinations of slit widths and flow rates.  

Taylor cone formation is most stable when the slit width ratio (sheath:core) is maintained between 1.5:1 and 3:1. Outside this range, either the core encapsulation becomes insufficient (at lower ratios) or the core flow becomes unstable (at higher ratios).  

### Core Slit Height  

The core slit height represents another adjustable parameter that affects Taylor cone formation. Increased slit height provides greater solution volume at the electrospinning interface, which can enhance process stability but may require higher voltages to maintain proper electric field strength.  

Experimental results indicate that optimal core slit heights range from 5-15 mm, depending on solution properties and flow rates. Within this range, the slit height has minimal effect on core encapsulation quality but significantly impacts process stability and continuous operation capability.  

### Viscosities of the Solutions  

Solution viscosity plays a crucial role in core-sheath fiber formation. The viscosity of the sheath solution must exceed a critical threshold relative to the core solution viscosity to generate sufficient shear stress for proper core entrainment.  

Varying the viscosity of the sheath solution demonstrates clear effects on Taylor cone formation. Higher viscosity sheath solutions (e.g., 760 cP) produce more distinct core-sheath cone-jets compared to lower viscosity solutions (e.g., 280 cP) when processing a core solution of intermediate viscosity (500 cP).  

The effects of viscosity on fiber morphology are particularly significant. Higher viscosity solutions generally produce fibers with larger diameters and more uniform core-sheath structures, while lower viscosity solutions tend toward smaller diameters but with greater variability in core positioning.  

### Core Sheath Fiber Applications  

The invention enables production of core-sheath electrospun fibers with diverse applications. Bicomponent fibers can be created by selecting core and sheath materials with complementary properties, such as combining polycaprolactone (PCL) with collagen for tissue engineering applications.  

Particle encapsulation represents another important application, where small molecule chemicals, proteins, fluorophores, or metals are incorporated into the fiber core. The invention's high throughput capability makes it particularly suitable for producing drug-loaded fibers for controlled release applications.  

The technology also enables creation of fibers from materials that are normally unelectrospinnable, such as polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS), by providing an electrospinnable sheath that can be optionally removed after processing. Hollow fibers can be produced by using a water-soluble core material (e.g., poly(ethylene oxide)) that is subsequently dissolved, leaving behind a hollow sheath structure.  

Additional applications include creating fibers with piezoelectric properties by appropriate material selection, generating non-solid core fibers for specialized delivery systems, and producing fibers where core and sheath components can react to form novel composite materials. The technology also offers cost advantages by enabling economical production of fibers where expensive materials are used only in the core while inexpensive materials form the sheath.  

### Advantages of the Invention as it Relates to High Throughput Open-Bath Monofiber Fabrication Systems  

The present invention provides several advantages over conventional electrospinning systems. The open-bath design avoids solvent evaporation issues common in enclosed systems, allowing for stable operation over extended periods. The system can electrospin polymer suspensions as well as solutions, expanding the range of processable materials.  

Material waste is minimized as nearly all supplied polymer is converted into fibers, with higher efficiency of solution consumption compared to needle-based systems. The open design allows for electrospinning in any spatial orientation, including downward collection which simplifies fiber handling.  

The system produces fewer but longer fibers compared to multi-needle arrays, resulting in improved handleability and reduced fiber breakage. Fiber diameter can be precisely controlled through adjustment of process parameters, and the closed fluid path maintains sterility for biomedical applications.
