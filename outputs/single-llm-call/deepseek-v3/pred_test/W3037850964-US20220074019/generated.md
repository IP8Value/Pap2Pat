# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to a process for extracting rare earth elements (REEs) from REE-bearing materials, particularly clay-rich sedimentary deposits and coal-related waste materials. More specifically, the invention provides an organic acid-based solution (OAS) system capable of selectively leaching REEs under ambient temperature and pH conditions while minimizing environmental impact compared to conventional inorganic acid extraction methods. The disclosed process utilizes environmentally benign organic acid formulations combined with monovalent salts to achieve efficient recovery of REEs from various geological matrices without requiring high temperatures, extreme pH conditions, or generating hazardous waste streams.  

## BACKGROUND  

The global demand for rare earth elements continues to escalate due to their critical role in renewable energy technologies, advanced electronics, and defense applications. Currently, China dominates REE production, accounting for approximately 132,000 tons annually compared to only 26,000 tons produced in the United States. This supply imbalance creates strategic vulnerabilities for nations dependent on these critical materials.  

Conventional REE extraction methods face significant limitations including environmental concerns associated with inorganic acid use, high energy requirements for processing, and societal opposition to traditional mining operations. Existing techniques often employ harsh mineral acids such as hydrochloric or sulfuric acid, which generate toxic byproducts and require extensive waste management. Chinese elution-deposit operations utilize in-situ solution mining approaches, but these still rely on inorganic lixiviants with similar environmental drawbacks.  

Recent investigations into alternative extraction methods have explored biological approaches including fungi-based REE recovery. While promising, these biological systems suffer from slow reaction kinetics, inconsistent yields, and challenges in scaling to industrial levels. The current market conditions and technological limitations clearly demonstrate the need for an improved REE extraction process that combines environmental sustainability with commercial viability.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel organic acid-based solution (OAS) system for extracting rare earth elements from various REE-bearing materials under mild environmental conditions. The process involves contacting REE-bearing materials with an OAS composition comprising organic acids, monovalent salts, and optional additives including surfactants and biocides. The system operates effectively at ambient temperatures between 15-30°C and within a pH range of 3-6, significantly reducing energy requirements compared to conventional methods.  

Key components of the OAS include organic acids selected from the group consisting of citric acid, acetic acid, and indole-3-acetic acid, preferably at concentrations between 0.05-0.5M. The solution further incorporates monovalent salts such as sodium chloride or ammonium sulfate at concentrations ranging from 0.1-1.0M. This combination facilitates multiple extraction mechanisms including ion exchange, chelation, and colloidal dispersion to maximize REE recovery.  

The process demonstrates particular efficacy for clay-rich materials such as coal seam underclays, where it achieves REE recoveries exceeding 30% of total REE content while maintaining selectivity against gangue elements. Additional advantages include minimal leaching of radioactive elements (Th, U), reduced environmental impact through biodegradable lixiviants, and compatibility with both ex-situ heap recovery and in-situ extraction methodologies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following detailed description illustrates specific embodiments of the invention but does not limit its scope. Those skilled in the art will recognize that various modifications and equivalents may be made without departing from the spirit of the invention.  

The REE extraction process builds upon natural geochemical processes involving the weathering of REE-rich host rocks and subsequent formation of aluminosilicate-rich clay deposits. Over geologic time, REE ions mobilize from primary igneous minerals and accumulate in secondary clay matrices through adsorption and ion exchange mechanisms. The invention mimics and enhances these natural processes through controlled application of optimized organic acid solutions.  

Clay-rich ore deposits, particularly those associated with coal formations, represent ideal feedstocks as they typically contain 55-90% clay minerals with REEs distributed across exchangeable, colloidal, and mineral-bound phases. The organic acid system preferentially targets exchangeable and weakly-bound REE fractions through multiple mechanisms: (1) maintaining balanced clay surface charges to enhance cation exchange capacity, (2) selectively dissolving matrix components to increase fluid accessibility, and (3) solubilizing phosphate-bound REE through chelation.  

A preferred embodiment utilizes citrate-based solutions buffered with sodium citrate to maintain stable pH conditions between 3-6. This pH range optimizes organic acid dissociation while minimizing dissolution of gangue minerals. The citrate anion provides seven potential oxygen donor sites that form stable complexes with REE ions through monodentate bonding configurations.  

The extraction process flowchart comprises three primary stages: (1) contacting REE-bearing material with OAS under controlled liquid-solid ratios (typically 10:1), (2) separating REE-enriched leachate from residual solids through filtration or centrifugation, and (3) concentrating REEs from the pregnant leach solution using sorbent capture or precipitation methods.  

### Experimental Studies  

Laboratory experiments demonstrate the efficacy of the OAS system under various conditions. Testing with Appalachian Basin underclay samples (particle size <0.002mm) using 0.1M citrate + 0.5M NaCl at pH=5 and 22°C for 24 hours yielded REE recoveries comparable to conventional ammonium sulfate and hydrochloric acid treatments. Notably, the organic acid system achieved higher selectivity with REE-to-gangue element ratios of 2.5 compared to 0.2-0.3 for inorganic acids.  

Flow-through experiments simulating in-situ recovery conditions (25°C, ~2000psi pore pressure) demonstrated rapid REE release within initial 20-40 minutes of contact time, with optimal recovery following 5-day shut-in periods. Particle size analysis confirmed enhanced extraction efficiency for clay fractions (<2μm) due to increased surface area availability.  

Mineralogical characterization of feed materials identified predominant REE hosts including rhabdophane, monazite, and crandallite within clay matrices composed mainly of illite, kaolinite, and smectite. Sequential digestion revealed that citrate-based solutions access not only exchangeable fractions but also portions of HCl-extractable REEs typically inaccessible to simple salt solutions.  

Two operational examples illustrate process versatility:  

**Ex-Situ Heap Recovery (ESHR):** Coal waste materials serve as feedstock in an ion exchange process using dilute organic acids and monovalent salts. The system operates at ambient temperatures with pH buffering to maintain optimal conditions. Key parameters include acid-to-dry coal ratios between 5:1-10:1 and contact times of 4-48 hours depending on material characteristics.  

**In-Situ Recovery (ISR):** The process facilitates extraction from open pit or underground mines through injection of OAS into drilled wells. The solution flows through the ore body, dissolving REEs before being pumped to surface processing facilities. The system incorporates filtering and drying/calcining stages to produce REY concentrates while allowing lixiviant recycling.  

Comparative studies show the organic acid system reduces Th leaching by >80% compared to inorganic acids while maintaining high REE recovery rates. This selectivity significantly decreases downstream processing costs and environmental liabilities associated with radioactive element management.
