# DESCRIPTION

The present invention introduces a novel method for diagnosing endometrial cancer through molecular characterization of tumor samples. This diagnostic approach provides significant improvements over current histopathological methods by enabling more accurate classification of tumor aggressiveness and prediction of clinical outcomes. The invention encompasses multiple aspects including diagnostic markers, prognostic indicators, therapeutic stratification tools, and monitoring methods for endometrial carcinoma management.

## PRIOR ART

Endometrial carcinoma represents the most common gynecologic malignancy in developed countries, with approximately 75% of cases being diagnosed at early stages. Despite this favorable early detection rate, 15-20% of these tumors eventually relapse, and metastatic cases demonstrate poor response to current chemotherapy or radiation treatments, resulting in generally fatal outcomes. Current treatment approaches rely heavily on histopathological classification into type I (endometrioid) and type II (non-endometrioid) categories, with type II tumors showing more aggressive behavior. However, these classifications fail to adequately predict clinical outcomes or guide optimal treatment selection for individual patients.

The molecular basis of endometrial cancer has been increasingly understood, with type I tumors typically showing microsatellite instability and mutations in PTEN, PIK3CA, KRAS, and CTNNB1 genes, while type II tumors frequently exhibit TP53 mutations and chromosomal instability. Recent studies have demonstrated prognostic value in molecular alterations such as PIK3CA mutations and PTEN loss, but these markers alone lack sufficient predictive power for clinical decision-making. The gene expression signature of PTEN tumor suppressor pathway activity has emerged as a potential biomarker, though its clinical utility remains limited by variable expression patterns across tumor subtypes.

Current understanding suggests that aggressive endometrial tumors likely harbor underlying genetic alterations that drive their malignant behavior, but the specific molecular drivers have remained incompletely characterized. The 8q24 chromosomal region, containing the MYC oncogene, represents the most frequent focal amplification in endometrial cancer and has been associated with poor prognosis in multiple cancer types. However, the functional consequences of this amplification in endometrial cancer and its potential as a therapeutic target have not been thoroughly investigated.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention provides methods for diagnosing endometrial carcinoma through analysis of specific chromosomal regions and gene expression profiles. Particular embodiments focus on determining alterations in chromosomal regions 3q26.32 and 12p12.1, along with evaluation of gene expression patterns that differentiate tumor subtypes. The invention enables evaluation of patient survival probability through molecular characterization of tumor samples.

A key aspect of the invention involves stratifying therapy based on STMN1 gene or protein expression levels, which have been identified as significant prognostic indicators. The methods further allow for monitoring therapeutic efficacy through serial assessment of STMN1 expression patterns during treatment. Additionally, the invention provides means for monitoring cancer progression through longitudinal evaluation of STMN1 expression changes.

The invention encompasses diagnostic kits for endometrial carcinoma evaluation, incorporating reagents for detecting chromosomal alterations and gene expression patterns. These kits facilitate comprehensive tumor characterization for diagnostic, prognostic, and therapeutic monitoring purposes. Particular embodiments enable prediction of patient response to taxane-based chemotherapy through assessment of STMN1 expression levels.

Further therapeutic stratification methods are provided based on evaluation of PI3K pathway activity, including identification of patients likely to benefit from PI3K inhibitors, Akt inhibitors, mTOR inhibitors, or PTEN activators. These approaches enable personalized treatment selection tailored to the molecular characteristics of individual tumors.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The invention relates to methods for diagnosing and differentiating endometrial carcinoma through molecular characterization of tumor samples. Specific embodiments involve determining genetic alterations in chromosomal regions 3q26.32 and 12p12.1, which have been identified as significant markers of tumor aggressiveness. These chromosomal alterations are evaluated in combination with gene expression profiling of specific genes associated with endometrial cancer progression.

Through comprehensive molecular analysis, the invention enables identification of two distinct clusters of endometrial tumors exhibiting different clinical and histopathologic characteristics. Cluster 2 tumors demonstrate more aggressive behavior and poorer recurrence-free survival outcomes compared to Cluster 1 tumors. This classification system provides superior prognostic information compared to traditional histopathological grading.

A critical aspect of the invention involves determining PI3K pathway activity in patients with aggressive endometrial carcinoma. This evaluation includes assessment of STMN1 gene or protein expression levels, which serve as key indicators of tumor aggressiveness and treatment response. The diagnostic methods enable differentiation of endometrial carcinoma subtypes based on STMN1 expression patterns, providing clinically actionable information for patient management.

The invention provides methods for stratifying endometrial tumors and guiding therapeutic decisions based on STMN1 expression levels. These approaches facilitate personalized treatment selection and monitoring of therapeutic efficacy through serial assessment of STMN1 expression changes. Additionally, the methods enable evaluation of patient survival probability based on combined analysis of PI3K pathway activity and STMN1 expression status.

Comprehensive molecular profiling includes determination of specific chromosomal amplifications and deletions in combination with STMN1 expression analysis. This integrated approach allows for accurate prognosis estimation and identification of endometrial cancers with high-grade aggressive phenotypes. The methods further enable stratification of therapeutic regimens for individual patients based on molecular tumor characteristics.

The invention encompasses predictive methods for determining clinical outcomes and optimal treatment courses for endometrial cancer patients. These include monitoring disease progression and evaluating therapeutic efficacy through molecular markers. Particular embodiments provide kits for endometrial carcinoma diagnosis, therapy stratification, treatment monitoring, and survival probability assessment.

Specific therapeutic applications include prediction of response to taxane chemotherapy in metastatic endometrial cancer based on STMN1 expression levels. The invention also provides methods for stratifying therapy using PI3K pathway inhibitors, including PI3K inhibitors, Akt inhibitors, mTOR inhibitors, or PTEN activators, based on molecular tumor profiling.

### Patient Series

The primary investigation series included 82 endometrial cancers collected from patients treated at a single institution, with corresponding genome-wide copy-number data available for 70 tumors. Validation series included an internal set of 40 primary and 19 metastatic endometrial cancers, and an external set of 111 previously characterized tumors. Additional validation was performed using a qPCR series of 162 samples and a FISH series of 399 samples. All patients provided informed consent and were followed from primary surgery until death or study conclusion.

### RNA Analysis

RNA was extracted from tumor biopsies using standard protocols and hybridized to Agilent microarrays according to manufacturer specifications. Signal intensities were evaluated using BRB-ArrayTools with batch median normalization. Quality control measures included assessment of RNA integrity and hybridization efficiency. Expression data was processed to identify differentially expressed genes between tumor subgroups.

### DNA Analysis

Genomic DNA was extracted from tumor samples using established protocols. Targeted sequencing of PIK3CA, KRAS, and PTEN genes was performed using next-generation sequencing platforms. Copy number variations were assessed using SNP arrays, with particular focus on chromosomal regions 3q26.32 and 12p12.1. Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) was employed for validation of specific amplifications in formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded samples.

### Statistics

Statistical analysis was performed to relate molecular data to clinical phenotypes using appropriate tests including Pearson's χ2 and Student's t-tests. Multivariate linear regression models were developed to predict gene expression levels based on molecular characteristics. Survival analyses were conducted using log-rank and Mantel-Cox methods, with adjustment for relevant clinical covariates. All tests were two-sided with significance threshold of p<0.05.

### Results and Discussion

Comprehensive analysis of expression and clinical data distinguished two major groups of endometrial tumors with distinct molecular profiles. Cluster 2 tumors exhibited significantly more aggressive clinical behavior and poorer recurrence-free survival compared to Cluster 1 tumors. Differential expression analysis identified STMN1 as a key gene distinguishing these clusters, with validation by quantitative RT-PCR confirming its prognostic significance.

Clinical and histopathologic characterization revealed that Cluster 2 tumors were associated with high-grade, non-endometrioid histology and other features of aggressive disease. Survival analysis demonstrated significantly worse outcomes for patients with Cluster 2 tumors, independent of traditional prognostic factors. Genomic analysis identified regions of significant amplification, deletion, and loss of heterozygosity associated with poor prognosis.

Application of GISTIC analysis distinguished significant copy-number changes, with peak regions selected for further investigation. Amplification of 3q26.32 emerged as particularly associated with poor survival outcomes. Analysis of KRAS mutations revealed distinct patterns between tumor clusters, while investigation of PIK3CA activation demonstrated significant differences in pathway activity.

A PI3K activation score was developed and validated, showing significant association with 3q26.32 amplification. Connectivity Map analysis identified potential therapeutic agents targeting the PI3K pathway, with validation of PIK3CA amplification and PI3K score correlation. Immunohistochemical analysis confirmed STMN1 protein expression as an independent prognostic indicator, correlating with PI3K pathway activation scores.

Multivariate analysis established STMN1 expression as a robust predictor of poor survival, independent of other clinical factors. Investigation of PTEN expression and mutations revealed complex interactions with PI3K pathway activation. Analysis of PIK3CA mutations demonstrated additive effects with amplification in driving pathway activation.

The prognostic value of STMN1 expression was validated in independent cohorts, confirming its utility for identifying high-risk patients. Immunohistochemical staining protocols were optimized for clinical application, demonstrating strong association between 3q26.32 amplification and tumor recurrence. Ploidy analysis provided additional prognostic information, though with less predictive power than molecular markers.

These findings support the clinical utility of molecular profiling for endometrial cancer management, with particular emphasis on STMN1 expression and PI3K pathway activation as key determinants of tumor behavior and treatment response. The integrated analysis approach enables more accurate risk stratification and personalized therapeutic decision-making for endometrial cancer patients.
