# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to a recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) vector engineered to express heterologous polynucleotides encoding antigens of avian pathogens. The invention further provides vaccine compositions comprising the recombinant GaHV3 vector, methods for producing the vector, and methods of vaccinating avian species against infectious diseases, including Marek’s disease (MD) and infectious bursal disease (IBD).  

## BACKGROUND  

Marek’s disease (MD) is a lymphoproliferative disorder of poultry caused by Gallid herpesvirus 2 (GaHV2), also known as Marek’s disease virus-1 (MDV-1). The disease is characterized by rapid-onset lymphomatous tumors and paralytic symptoms due to neuronal infiltration of lymphocytes. MDV-1 replicates in feather follicle epithelial cells, facilitating transmission via inhalation of infected dust and dander. Mortality rates vary between 10–30% but can reach 100% depending on viral strain, host susceptibility, and vaccination status.  

Live attenuated vaccines have controlled MD for over four decades. Licensed vaccine strains include the naturally attenuated MDV-1 strain Rispens (CVI988), GaHV3 (MDV-2) strain SB-1, and herpesvirus of turkeys (HVT) strain FC126. Beyond their efficacy against MD, avian herpesvirus vaccines serve as recombinant vectors for protecting against other avian diseases. HVT-based recombinant vaccines, for example, have demonstrated efficacy against multiple viral pathogens, even in the presence of maternal antibodies.  

Despite their success, limitations exist with HVT vectors. Interference occurs when multiple recombinant HVT vaccines are administered simultaneously, reducing efficacy. Consequently, manufacturers discourage combining HVT-based vaccines. Additionally, generating multivalent HVT vectors expressing multiple foreign antigens remains technically challenging. These constraints highlight the need for alternative vector platforms that complement rather than interfere with existing vaccines.  

The SB-1 strain of GaHV3, licensed in the mid-1980s, is widely used in bivalent vaccines with HVT to enhance protection against virulent MDV pathotypes. Commercial SB-1/HVT vaccines are employed globally, including in the USA, South America, and Asia. The complementary effects of SB-1 and HVT in inducing immunity against MD suggest that SB-1 could serve as an effective recombinant vector alongside HVT without inhibitory interactions.  

The 166-kb genome of SB-1 shares organizational similarities with MDV-1, including homologous genes and unique microRNAs. The construction of a full-length infectious SB-1 genome in a bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) enables genetic manipulation, facilitating its development as a recombinant vector. Previous studies confirm that BAC-derived SB-1 confers robust protection against virulent MDV challenge, validating its utility as a vaccine platform.  

Infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV) poses additional challenges in poultry health. Control is complicated by maternal antibodies, emerging antigenic variants, and production costs. Bivalent MDV/IBDV vaccines offer simultaneous protection, reducing costs and enabling in ovo administration—unlike live-attenuated IBDV vaccines, which can cause subclinical disease or embryo mortality. While HVT-vectored IBDV vaccines are commercially successful, interference limits their combination with other HVT-based vaccines. Thus, alternative vectors like SB-1 are needed to complement existing platforms.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a recombinant Gallid herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) vector comprising one or more heterologous polynucleotides inserted into nonessential intergenic loci, wherein the polynucleotides encode antigens of avian pathogens. Preferred insertion sites include the UL3/UL4, UL10/UL11, and UL21/UL22 loci. The heterologous polynucleotide may encode the VP2 capsid protein of IBDV, glycoproteins of infectious laryngotracheitis virus (ILTV), fusion protein or hemagglutinin-neuraminidase of Newcastle disease virus (NDV), hemagglutinin of avian influenza virus (AIV), or glycoproteins of avian infectious bronchitis virus (IBV).  

The recombinant GaHV3 vector further comprises an expression cassette with a promoter operably linked to the heterologous polynucleotide, such as the murine cytomegalovirus immediate early (mCMV IE2) promoter, and a transcription termination sequence. The vector may include multiple heterologous polynucleotides inserted into distinct loci, enabling multivalent antigen expression.  

Vaccine compositions comprise the recombinant GaHV3 vector and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, adjuvant, or excipient. The vaccine may further include a recombinant Marek’s disease virus (MDV) vector, such as HVT, to enhance immunogenicity. Administration routes include subcutaneous, intramuscular, or in ovo inoculation.  

The invention also provides isolated DNA encoding the recombinant GaHV3 vector, optionally cloned into a bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) for stable propagation. Methods for producing the vector involve homologous recombination, site-specific recombinase technology, or BAC-based mutagenesis. The vaccine is administered to avian species, including chickens, turkeys, and ducks, to induce protection against MD, IBD, and other avian diseases.  

A marker system may be incorporated into the recombinant GaHV3 vector for selection, such as the galactokinase (galK) gene, which is subsequently replaced by the heterologous polynucleotide. The invention further encompasses immunogenic fragments, allelic variants, and codon-optimized sequences of the antigens to enhance expression or immunogenicity.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Definitions  

The following terms are defined for clarity in interpreting the invention:  

**Animal**: Refers to avian species, including but not limited to chickens, turkeys, ducks, and quails.  

**Avian species**: Encompasses all bird species susceptible to avian pathogens, with poultry being the preferred group.  

**Nucleic acid**: Includes DNA, RNA, and synthetic analogs, such as polynucleotides or oligonucleotides, whether single- or double-stranded.  

**Heterologous polynucleotide**: A nucleic acid sequence foreign to the GaHV3 genome, such as a gene encoding an avian pathogen antigen.  

**Recombinant viral vector**: A genetically modified virus engineered to express heterologous genes, such as the recombinant GaHV3 vector of the invention.  

**Protein**: A polypeptide of any length, including full-length proteins, fragments, or peptides, which may be immunogenic.  

**Antigen**: A molecule capable of inducing an immune response, including proteins, glycoproteins, or epitopes derived from avian pathogens.  

**Immunological response**: The activation of humoral or cellular immunity in a vaccinated host, measurable by antibody titers or T-cell proliferation.  

**Vaccine**: A composition comprising the recombinant GaHV3 vector and additional components, such as adjuvants or carriers, to enhance immunogenicity.  

### Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) Vector  

The recombinant GaHV3 vector is derived from the SB-1 strain, modified to carry heterologous polynucleotides in intergenic loci, including UL3/UL4, UL10/UL11, and UL21/UL22. These sites are nonessential for viral replication, ensuring stable antigen expression without compromising vector viability.  

A preferred antigen is the VP2 protein of IBDV, which induces neutralizing antibodies and confers protection against very virulent strains. The VP2 sequence may share at least 90% identity with SEQ ID NO: 10 or SEQ ID NO: 6. Immunogenic fragments of VP2, including conformational or linear epitopes, are also encompassed.  

For protection against ILTV, the vector may express glycoproteins E, I, or B (SEQ ID NO: 31–33). NDV antigens include the fusion protein (SEQ ID NO: 34) or hemagglutinin-neuraminidase (SEQ ID NO: 35). AIV antigens comprise hemagglutinin from serotypes H5 (SEQ ID NO: 36), H7 (SEQ ID NO: 37), or H9 (SEQ ID NO: 38). IBV antigens include the S1 or S2 glycoprotein (SEQ ID NO: 40).  

Codon optimization may be employed to enhance antigen expression in avian hosts. The vector may also encode multiple antigens, such as VP2 and AIV hemagglutinin, inserted into separate loci under independent promoters.  

### Methods for Producing the Recombinant GaHV3 Vector  

The vector is produced via homologous recombination in chicken embryo fibroblasts (CEFs) or BAC-based recombineering. A galK selection system facilitates cassette exchange, wherein the galK gene is replaced by the heterologous polynucleotide. Alternatively, site-specific recombinases (e.g., Cre-loxP) enable precise integration.  

Recombinant viruses are propagated in CEFs, purified, and titrated by plaque assay. BAC-derived vectors are transfected into CEFs, and reconstituted viruses are amplified for vaccine production.  

### Vaccines  

The vaccine comprises the recombinant GaHV3 vector and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, such as PBS or stabilizers. Adjuvants (e.g., aluminum hydroxide) may be included to enhance immunity. The vaccine may be combined with a recombinant MDV vector (e.g., HVT-expressing IBDV VP2) to exploit synergistic effects.  

### Use of the Vaccine  

The vaccine is administered to 1-day-old chicks via subcutaneous injection or in ovo. A prime-boost regimen may be employed for prolonged immunity. Vaccinated birds develop neutralizing antibodies within two weeks and are protected against lethal challenge with virulent strains.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Construction of Recombinant SB-1-UL3/4VP2  

The VP2 expression cassette, driven by the mCMV IE2 promoter, was inserted into the UL3/UL4 locus of SB-1 BAC via galK recombineering. Recombinant viruses were plaque-purified and VP2 expression confirmed by immunofluorescence.  

### Example 2: In Vitro Growth Kinetics  

Recombinant SB-1-UL3/4VP2 exhibited delayed replication compared to wild-type SB-1 but retained viability. Viral titers were quantified by real-time PCR, demonstrating stable VP2 expression.  

### Example 3: Immunogenicity Study  

Chicks vaccinated with SB-1-UL3/4VP2 developed neutralizing antibodies by week 2 post-vaccination. All birds survived challenge with virulent IBDV UK661, whereas unvaccinated controls succumbed within 55 hours.  

### Example 4: Challenge Study  

Birds receiving SB-1-UL3/4VP2 (1000 PFU) were fully protected against IBDV, with no clinical signs. In contrast, controls vaccinated with parental SB-1 showed severe disease progression.  

### Example 5: Interference Study  

Co-administration of SB-1-UL3/4VP2 and HVT-vectored IBDV vaccine did not reduce antibody responses, confirming the absence of interference.
