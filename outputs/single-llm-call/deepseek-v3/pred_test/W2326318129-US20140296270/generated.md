## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates generally to the fields of molecular biology, oncology, and medicinal chemistry. More specifically, the invention provides novel small molecule inhibitors of Signal Transducer and Activator of Transcription 3 (STAT3), methods for their identification and development, pharmaceutical compositions containing these inhibitors, and methods for their use in treating diseases associated with aberrant STAT3 activity, particularly cancer.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) is a member of the STAT family of transcription factors that plays a critical role in mediating cellular responses to cytokines and growth factors. STAT3 becomes activated through phosphorylation on tyrosine 705 (Y705) by receptor-associated kinases such as JAK kinases or receptor tyrosine kinases, leading to dimerization via reciprocal phosphotyrosine-SH2 domain interactions, nuclear translocation, and DNA binding to regulate gene expression.

Constitutive activation of STAT3 has been observed in approximately 50% of all human cancers, including head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC), breast cancer, prostate cancer, and hematological malignancies. Persistent STAT3 activation contributes to oncogenesis by promoting cell proliferation, survival, angiogenesis, immune evasion, and metastasis. STAT3 also plays a crucial role in maintaining cancer stem cells, which are implicated in tumor initiation, therapy resistance, and relapse.

Current approaches to target STAT3 include peptide-based inhibitors, oligonucleotide decoys, and small molecules. However, these inhibitors suffer from limitations such as poor pharmacokinetic properties, lack of selectivity, and insufficient potency. There remains an unmet need for potent, selective, and drug-like small molecule inhibitors of STAT3 that can effectively target STAT3-dependent cancers while sparing normal tissues.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel small molecule inhibitors of STAT3 identified through a virtual ligand screening approach targeting the phosphotyrosine (pY)-peptide binding pocket of the STAT3 SH2 domain. The screening process involved docking approximately 920,000 compounds from commercial chemical libraries into the STAT3 SH2 domain structure, followed by experimental validation of top hits in biochemical and cellular assays.

Initial screening identified lead compounds C3, C30, and C188, with C188 showing particularly promising activity in inhibiting STAT3-dependent processes. Subsequent structure-activity relationship (SAR) studies and pharmacophore-based optimization led to the development of second-generation inhibitors, including the highly potent compound C188-9. This compound demonstrates improved binding affinity (KD = 4.7 nM) and inhibitory activity (IC50 = 3.7 μM in inhibiting G-CSF-induced STAT3 phosphorylation) compared to the parent compound C188.

The inhibitors of the invention show remarkable selectivity for STAT3 over the closely related STAT1 protein, despite the high degree of similarity between their SH2 domains. This selectivity is achieved through specific interactions with key residues in the STAT3 SH2 domain that differ from those in STAT1. Molecular modeling and structural analysis revealed that the inhibitors bind to a hydrophobic pocket adjacent to the pY-binding site, making critical contacts with residues that form the selectivity filter between STAT3 and STAT1.

The compounds of the invention effectively inhibit multiple aspects of STAT3 signaling, including:
1) Blocking STAT3 binding to phosphopeptide ligands
2) Inhibiting cytokine-induced STAT3 phosphorylation
3) Preventing STAT3 nuclear translocation
4) Disrupting STAT3-DNA binding
5) Inducing apoptosis in cancer cells dependent on STAT3 activity

Notably, the lead compound C188-9 demonstrates potent anti-tumor activity in preclinical models of HNSCC, significantly inhibiting tumor growth in xenograft models at well-tolerated doses. The compound shows favorable pharmacokinetic properties, including oral bioavailability and tumor accumulation exceeding plasma levels.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions containing the STAT3 inhibitors, methods for their use in treating STAT3-dependent cancers either as monotherapy or in combination with other therapeutic modalities (e.g., chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy), and kits for administering the inhibitors. The compounds are particularly useful for targeting cancer stem cells and overcoming therapy resistance in various malignancies.

### III. EMBODIMENTS FOR TARGETING STAT3

The STAT family of proteins consists of seven members (STAT1, STAT2, STAT3, STAT4, STAT5A, STAT5B, and STAT6) that share conserved structural domains including an N-terminal domain, coiled-coil domain, DNA-binding domain, linker domain, SH2 domain, and transactivation domain. Among these, STAT3 exists as two major isoforms - STAT3α (full-length) and STAT3β (lacking the C-terminal transactivation domain) - generated by alternative splicing.

STAT3α is the predominant isoform implicated in oncogenic transformation and cancer progression. In contrast, STAT3β lacks transcriptional activation capacity and can antagonize STAT3α-mediated transformation. The invention takes advantage of these differential properties by specifically targeting STAT3α while sparing STAT1, despite their high sequence homology in the SH2 domain (approximately 50% identity).

The targeting strategy involves exploiting subtle but critical differences in the SH2 domain binding pockets between STAT3 and STAT1. Key residues that differ between STAT3 and STAT1 and contribute to the selectivity of the inhibitors include:

1) STAT3 Ser611 versus STAT1 Asn567
2) STAT3 Ser613 versus STAT1 Thr569
3) STAT3 Lys591 versus STAT1 Glu547
4) STAT3 Glu638 versus STAT1 Gln594

The virtual ligand screening approach involved the following steps:
1) Preparation of the STAT3 SH2 domain structure (residues 127-722) in a format compatible with molecular modeling software
2) Definition of the binding pocket based on the phosphopeptide interaction site
3) Screening of commercial chemical databases containing approximately 920,000 compounds
4) Flexible docking calculations to account for protein and ligand flexibility
5) Selection of top-scoring compounds for experimental validation

This approach identified first-generation STAT3 inhibitors (C3, C30, C188), which served as scaffolds for developing second-generation (e.g., C188-9) and third-generation inhibitors with improved potency, selectivity, and pharmacokinetic properties.

### IV. TARGETING CANCER STEM CELLS

Cancer stem cells (CSCs) represent a subpopulation of tumor cells with self-renewal capacity and the ability to generate heterogeneous lineages of cancer cells that comprise the tumor. CSCs are implicated in tumor initiation, progression, metastasis, therapy resistance, and relapse.

STAT3 plays a critical role in maintaining CSC populations across multiple cancer types, including breast cancer, leukemia, and solid tumors. The STAT3 inhibitors of the invention effectively target CSCs by disrupting STAT3-mediated self-renewal pathways while sparing normal stem cells.

The CSC-targeting strategy involves:
1) Identifying STAT3-dependent CSC populations through surface marker expression (e.g., CD44+/CD24- in breast cancer)
2) Assessing STAT3 activation status in CSCs versus bulk tumor cells
3) Evaluating inhibitor effects on CSC self-renewal using mammosphere formation assays
4) Testing inhibitor efficacy in xenograft models with CSC enrichment

The lead compound C188-9 demonstrates potent activity against breast CSCs, significantly reducing mammosphere formation efficiency (MSFE) at sub-micromolar concentrations. In xenograft models, C188-9 treatment reduces tumor-initiating cell frequency and prevents tumor recurrence after therapy withdrawal.

### V. COMBINATION THERAPY

The STAT3 inhibitors of the invention may be administered as monotherapy or in combination with other therapeutic agents to enhance efficacy and overcome resistance. Combination strategies include:

1) STAT3 inhibitor + chemotherapy: The inhibitors may be combined with conventional chemotherapeutic agents such as cisplatin, carboplatin, taxanes (paclitaxel, docetaxel), anthracyclines (doxorubicin), antimetabolites (5-fluorouracil, gemcitabine), or topoisomerase inhibitors.

2) STAT3 inhibitor + targeted therapy: Combinations with molecularly targeted agents such as EGFR inhibitors (erlotinib, gefitinib), HER2 inhibitors (trastuzumab, lapatinib), or angiogenesis inhibitors (bevacizumab) may provide synergistic effects.

3) STAT3 inhibitor + radiation therapy: Given the role of STAT3 in radiation resistance, combining STAT3 inhibition with radiotherapy may enhance tumor response, particularly in radioresistant cancers like HNSCC.

4) STAT3 inhibitor + immunotherapy: As STAT3 contributes to immune evasion, combining STAT3 inhibitors with immune checkpoint inhibitors (anti-PD-1, anti-PD-L1, anti-CTLA-4) may enhance anti-tumor immune responses.

Administration protocols may involve concurrent or sequential dosing schedules, with the STAT3 inhibitor given before, during, or after the combination agent depending on the mechanistic rationale and pharmacokinetic considerations.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention but are not intended to limit its scope.

### Exemplary Materials and Methods

1. Protein Preparation:
The STAT3 SH2 domain (residues 127-722) was expressed and purified for structural studies. Coordinates were converted to an ICM-compatible format for virtual screening.

2. Virtual Screening:
Commercial chemical databases (Life Chemicals, ChemDiv, etc.) containing approximately 920,000 compounds were screened against the STAT3 SH2 domain structure. The binding pocket was defined based on phosphopeptide interaction sites.

3. Biochemical Assays:
a) STAT3 SH2/pY-peptide binding assay using surface plasmon resonance (SPR)
b) Electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA) for STAT3-DNA binding
c) Immunoblot assays for STAT3 phosphorylation and downstream targets

4. Cellular Assays:
a) Confocal microscopy for STAT3 nuclear translocation
b) High-throughput fluorescence microscopy (HTFM) for STAT3 activation
c) Apoptosis assays in breast cancer cell lines (MDA-MB-468, MCF-7)

5. In Vivo Studies:
a) Xenograft models using HNSCC cell lines (UM-SCC-17B)
b) Pharmacokinetic studies in mice and rats
c) Toxicity and maximum tolerated dose determinations

### Identification by VLS of Compounds that Blocked Stat3 Binding to its Phosphopeptide Ligand and Inhibited IL-6-Mediated Phosphorylation of STAT3

Virtual ligand screening identified three lead compounds (C3, C30, C188) that inhibited STAT3 binding to its phosphopeptide ligand with IC50 values ranging from 2-20 μM. These compounds also inhibited IL-6-mediated STAT3 phosphorylation in cellular assays, with C188 showing the greatest potency (IC50 = 16.8 μM).

Follow-up similarity screening and pharmacophore analysis using C188 as the scaffold identified second-generation inhibitors with improved activity, including C188-9 (IC50 = 3.7 μM). Molecular modeling revealed that C188-9 makes additional favorable contacts with the STAT3 SH2 domain compared to C188, particularly through its extended 3-substituent group.

### Compound-Mediated Inhibition of Ligand-Stimulated Phosphorylation of STAT3 is Specific for Stat3 Vs. Stat1

Despite the high sequence similarity between STAT3 and STAT1 SH2 domains, C188-9 showed approximately 5-fold greater selectivity for STAT3 (IC50 = 3.7 μM for STAT3 vs. 19.1 μM for STAT1 in UM-SCC-17B cells). This selectivity was maintained across multiple cell lines and assay formats.

### Sequence Analysis and Molecular Modeling of the Interaction of Each Compound with the Stat3 Vs. Stat1 SH2 Domain

Comparative analysis of STAT3 and STAT1 SH2 domain sequences revealed key differences at positions corresponding to STAT3 Ser611, Ser613, Lys591, and Glu638, which form the basis for compound selectivity. Molecular modeling showed that C188-9 makes optimal van der Waals contacts with STAT3-specific residues, while experiencing steric or electrostatic clashes with corresponding STAT1 residues.

### Example 5

In UM-SCC-17B xenograft models, C188-9 (50 mg/kg/day) significantly inhibited tumor growth compared to vehicle control (p = 0.027) and reduced tumor pSTAT3 levels by 57% (p = 0.017). RNA-seq analysis of xenograft tumors revealed that C188-9 modulated expression of 76 known STAT3 target genes involved in oncogenesis, apoptosis resistance, and therapy resistance.
