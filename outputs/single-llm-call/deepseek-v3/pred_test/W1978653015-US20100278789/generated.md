# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD AND BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of beta-cell replacement therapy for diabetes. Beta-cell replacement through transplantation has emerged as a promising therapeutic approach for insulin-dependent diabetes mellitus. However, this treatment faces significant limitations due to the scarcity of donor pancreata and the limited proliferative capacity of adult beta cells in vitro.  

Current methods for expanding beta cells in culture often result in cellular dedifferentiation, characterized by loss of insulin expression and acquisition of proliferative capacity. While dedifferentiated beta cells can be expanded substantially, their therapeutic utility requires effective redifferentiation into functional insulin-producing cells. The art lacks reliable methods for achieving this critical redifferentiation step while maintaining cellular viability and function.  

The NOTCH signaling pathway plays a fundamental role in pancreatic development and cell fate determination. During embryogenesis, NOTCH signaling maintains pancreatic progenitor cells in an undifferentiated state through lateral inhibition mechanisms. In the adult pancreas, reactivation of NOTCH pathway components has been observed in cultured islet cells, suggesting a potential role in the dedifferentiation process. Prior art has demonstrated NOTCH pathway involvement in various cellular processes but has failed to establish methods for exploiting this pathway to generate functional beta cells for therapeutic applications.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a novel method for generating adult islet beta cells comprising contacting dedifferentiated beta cells with a NOTCH pathway inhibitor under serum-free conditions. The method involves incubating the cells in a specialized culturing medium that promotes redifferentiation while maintaining cell viability. This approach yields functional insulin-producing cells suitable for therapeutic applications.  

A further aspect of the invention provides a method for treating diabetes comprising contacting dedifferentiated beta cells with a NOTCH pathway inhibitor, followed by transplantation of the redifferentiated cells into a patient in need thereof. The transplanted cells demonstrate restored insulin production and glycemic control in diabetic models.  

The invention additionally provides a method for purifying populations of dedifferentiated beta cells through permanent genetic tagging of primary beta cells, followed by culturing and isolation of the tagged dedifferentiated progeny. These purified cell populations serve as ideal starting material for redifferentiation protocols.  

Another embodiment provides isolated populations of dedifferentiated beta cells characterized by specific molecular markers and responsiveness to NOTCH pathway modulation. These cell populations exhibit unique plasticity toward redifferentiation when treated according to the methods disclosed herein.  

The invention further provides methods for identifying agents that affect beta cell proliferation or redifferentiation through screening candidate compounds against the disclosed cell populations and monitoring NOTCH pathway activity and insulin expression.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The present invention provides redifferentiated populations of expanded adult islet beta cells suitable for cell replacement therapy in insulin-dependent diabetes. These cells overcome the limitations of prior art by combining extensive expansion capacity with reliable redifferentiation potential, addressing the critical shortage of transplantable beta cells.  

The principles underlying the invention involve establishing novel culture conditions that promote increased insulin content in dedifferentiated expanded beta cells. This is achieved through precise modulation of the NOTCH signaling pathway during the redifferentiation process. The invention represents a significant advancement over prior methods that relied exclusively on human pancreas donors or yielded incompletely differentiated cells.  

A key aspect involves purification of dedifferentiated beta cells from cultured islets using genetic recombination approaches for cell-specific labeling. This enables isolation of pure populations of dedifferentiated beta cells through selective expression of marker genes in insulin-producing cells and their progeny.  

The invention demonstrates that downregulation of NOTCH pathway components, particularly Hairy and Enhancer of Split 1 (HES1), induces redifferentiation of cultured human islet cells. Experimental data show upregulation of NOTCH1, NOTCH2, and HES1 during beta cell dedifferentiation, while inhibition of these components promotes restoration of beta cell characteristics.  

Genetic approaches using HES1-specific short hairpin RNA (shRNA) effectively demonstrate differentiating effects in dedifferentiated human islet cells. These findings establish that agents capable of downregulating NOTCH pathway components can be utilized for beta cell redifferentiation, representing a novel therapeutic strategy.  

The invention provides a comprehensive method for ex vivo expansion and redifferentiation of adult islet beta cells. Adult islet beta cells are defined as insulin-producing cells isolated from post-natal pancreatic islets of Langerhans. Isolation methods include standard collagenase digestion and density gradient purification techniques followed by optional fluorescence-activated cell sorting.  

The process involves dispersing adult islet beta cells into single cell suspensions derived from either autologous or non-autologous mammalian donors. Expanded adult islet beta cells are obtained through culturing under conditions that maintain growth and viability while preventing apoptosis. The culture medium may be supplemented with various constituents to support cellular expansion.  

Redifferentiation of expanded adult islet beta cells is achieved through controlled downregulation of NOTCH pathway components. The NOTCH signaling pathway comprises multiple receptors, ligands, and downstream effectors that can be targeted for inhibition. Downregulation may be accomplished through various agents including antibodies, antibody fragments, RNA silencing constructs, or small molecule inhibitors.  

RNA silencing approaches include RNA interference (RNAi), post-transcriptional gene silencing, and related mechanisms mediated by the Dicer enzyme and RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC). The invention contemplates use of double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) constructs designed to avoid induction of interferon and PKR pathways while effectively silencing target genes.  

Specific embodiments utilize small interfering RNA (siRNA) or short hairpin RNA (shRNA) targeting HES1 or other NOTCH pathway components. These constructs demonstrate particular potency when designed with specific characteristics including optimized target sequences, chemical modifications, or conjugation to cell-penetrating peptides.  

Alternative silencing agents include microRNAs (miRNAs), DNAzymes (particularly the "10-23" model), antisense oligonucleotides, ribozymes, and triplex-forming oligonucleotides (TFOs). Each of these modalities can be optimized for NOTCH pathway component downregulation through established design principles and delivery strategies.  

The invention further encompasses polypeptide and polynucleotide agents that promote beta cell differentiation, delivered via various gene delivery vehicles including viral vectors (retroviral, lentiviral, adenoviral), lipid-based systems, and non-viral expression constructs. These delivery systems incorporate appropriate regulatory elements such as promoters, enhancers, and polyadenylation signals to ensure effective transgene expression.  

Gamma secretase inhibitors represent another class of NOTCH pathway inhibitors suitable for use in the disclosed methods. These small molecules prevent NOTCH receptor cleavage and subsequent signaling, thereby promoting beta cell redifferentiation.  

The invention provides methods for measuring insulin content in redifferentiated cells and optimized media formulations for incubating adult islet beta cells during expansion and redifferentiation phases. Genetic modification of the cells may be employed to enhance therapeutic properties or enable tracking of cellular progeny.  

For therapeutic applications, the invention describes methods for treating diabetes by transplanting redifferentiated adult islet beta cells. Diabetes treatment encompasses amelioration of hyperglycemia and associated complications through restoration of endogenous insulin production. Transplantation approaches include strategies to reduce rejection of non-autologous cells through encapsulation techniques or co-administration of immunosuppressive agents.  

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising redifferentiated beta cells are formulated with physiologically acceptable carriers and excipients for various administration routes including injection, transmucosal delivery, oral administration, or buccal delivery. Dosage considerations account for therapeutic efficacy while minimizing toxicity, with compositions presented in forms suitable for clinical use.  

The invention further provides methods for purifying populations of dedifferentiated beta cells through permanent genetic tagging of primary beta cells followed by isolation of tagged dedifferentiated progeny. Tagging methods employ expression constructs with cell-specific promoters driving selectable or detectable markers.  

Purified populations of dedifferentiated beta cells serve as valuable tools for screening candidate agents that affect proliferation or redifferentiation. These screening methods enable identification of novel compounds that modulate NOTCH pathway activity or enhance beta cell function.  

### Examples  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope:  

Example 1 describes islet cell culture conditions and lineage tracing methods using lentiviral vectors expressing Cre recombinase and reporter constructs. Cells are analyzed for HES1 inhibition effects through RNA analyses, immunofluorescence, and immunoblotting techniques. Statistical analyses demonstrate significant upregulation of HES1 in cultured beta cells and corresponding changes in NOTCH pathway components and cell cycle inhibitors.  

Results show that HES1 inhibition prevents induction of beta-cell replication while reducing dedifferentiation. Detailed analyses reveal effects on p57 transcripts, NOTCH transcripts, insulin expression, and key transcription factors including PDX1 and NEUROD1. Immunostaining demonstrates increased fractions of insulin-positive cells following HES1 inhibition.  

Example 2 presents materials and methods for analyzing NOTCH pathway activation in islet cell cultures. Results demonstrate upregulation of NOTCH1-3 and HES1 transcripts with concurrent downregulation of NOTCH4 and ligands. These changes correlate with cellular dedifferentiation and show negative correlation between HES1 and insulin expression.  

Example 3 details lentiviral vector construction and cell lineage tracing experiments. Methods include islet cell culture, viral infection, cell sorting, and immunofluorescence analyses. Results demonstrate specific labeling of beta cell progeny and their subsequent redifferentiation following HES1 inhibition.  

Further examples describe additional experimental procedures and results supporting the efficacy of NOTCH pathway modulation in beta cell redifferentiation. These include analyses of cell replication, apoptosis, hormone co-expression, and in vivo functionality of redifferentiated cells in diabetic mouse models.
