# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates to interleukin-1 receptor antagonist proteins and peptides derived therefrom. More particularly, the invention provides novel truncated forms of the interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein (IL1RA) that retain biological activity while exhibiting improved pharmacological properties compared to full-length IL1RA and commercially available recombinant versions such as anakinra. The invention further relates to pharmaceutical compositions containing these peptides and their use in treating inflammatory disorders, neurodegenerative diseases, and other conditions mediated by interleukin-1 signaling.  

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION  

Interleukin-1 (IL-1) is a potent proinflammatory cytokine that plays a central role in acute and chronic inflammatory responses. IL-1 exists in two major isoforms, IL-1α and IL-1β, which exert their biological effects through binding to the interleukin-1 receptor type I (IL-1R1). Upon ligand binding, IL-1R1 recruits the interleukin-1 receptor accessory protein (IL-1RAcP), forming a signaling complex that activates downstream inflammatory pathways including nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) and mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) signaling cascades.  

The interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein (IL-1Ra) is a naturally occurring competitive inhibitor of IL-1 signaling that binds to IL-1R1 without recruiting IL-1RAcP, thereby blocking IL-1-mediated inflammatory responses. Anakinra, a recombinant version of IL-1Ra, has been approved for treatment of rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory conditions. However, therapeutic use of full-length IL-1Ra proteins is limited by several factors including poor penetration of the blood-brain barrier, short plasma half-life, and the need for frequent high-dose administration.  

Previous attempts to develop smaller peptide fragments of IL-1Ra with antagonist activity have been described in the patent literature, including WO05086695A2 which discloses peptide fragments derived from IL-1Ra. While these fragments show promise, there remains a need for improved IL-1 antagonists with enhanced pharmacological properties. Short peptide antagonists offer several potential advantages over full-length proteins including increased solubility, improved stability, lower production costs, and better tissue penetration, particularly across the blood-brain barrier.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel truncated forms of IL1RA that retain the ability to bind IL-1R1 and inhibit IL-1 downstream signaling while exhibiting improved pharmacological properties. These peptide antagonists, derived from the N-terminal domain of IL-1Ra involved in receptor interactions, effectively compete with IL-1β for binding to IL-1R1.  

The peptides of the invention demonstrate significant inhibitory effects on IL-1-induced activation of NF-κB and secretion of tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α) by macrophages. Surprisingly, these peptides show enhanced neuroprotective effects compared to full-length IL-1Ra, including stimulation of neurite outgrowth and promotion of neuronal cell survival. The peptides also exhibit improved blood-brain barrier penetration, making them particularly suitable for treatment of neuroinflammatory and neurodegenerative disorders.  

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions containing these peptide antagonists and methods for their use in treating various conditions including inflammatory disorders such as rheumatoid arthritis, neurodegenerative diseases such as Alzheimer's disease and Parkinson's disease, and other conditions mediated by IL-1 signaling.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Inflammation  

Inflammation is a complex biological response to harmful stimuli such as pathogens, damaged cells, or irritants. The inflammatory response involves immune cells, blood vessels, and molecular mediators with the purpose of eliminating the initial cause of cell injury, clearing out necrotic cells and tissues damaged from the original insult, and initiating tissue repair. Inflammation can be classified as either acute or chronic. Acute inflammation is the initial response of the body to harmful stimuli and is characterized by the movement of plasma and leukocytes from blood into injured tissues. Chronic inflammation refers to prolonged inflammation that can lead to a progressive shift in the types of cells present at the site of inflammation and is characterized by simultaneous destruction and healing of tissue.  

### Interleukins  

Interleukins are a group of cytokines that play essential roles in the activation and differentiation of immune cells. These molecules are primarily produced by leukocytes and act on leukocytes, though their effects are not limited to this cell type. The interleukin-1 (IL-1) family consists of several related cytokines that play crucial roles in inflammation and immune responses. IL-1α and IL-1β are the two major agonist isoforms that signal through IL-1R1. IL-1α is primarily membrane-associated and functions as an alarmin, while IL-1β is secreted and acts as a key mediator of systemic inflammation.  

IL-1α is synthesized as a 31 kDa precursor that can be processed to a 17 kDa mature form. The structure of IL-1α includes a beta-trefoil fold characteristic of the IL-1 family. IL-1α is produced by various cell types including epithelial cells, endothelial cells, and macrophages. Its production is tightly regulated at transcriptional and post-translational levels.  

IL-1β is produced primarily by activated macrophages as an inactive precursor that is cleaved by caspase-1 to generate the mature 17 kDa active form. The interleukin-1 receptor antagonist protein (IL-1Ra) is a naturally occurring inhibitor that binds to IL-1R1 with similar affinity as IL-1β but does not recruit IL-1RAcP, thereby blocking signal transduction.  

The IL-1 receptors include IL-1R1 (the signaling receptor), IL-1R2 (a decoy receptor), and IL-1RAcP (the accessory protein required for signaling). IL-1R1 is a type I transmembrane protein containing three extracellular immunoglobulin-like domains, a single transmembrane region, and a cytoplasmic Toll/IL-1 receptor (TIR) domain essential for downstream signaling.  

### Peptide Fragments of the Present Invention  

The present invention provides truncated forms of IL1RA that exhibit improved pharmacological properties compared to full-length IL1RA and anakinra. These peptides, derived from the N-terminal domain of IL-1Ra involved in receptor interactions, demonstrate increased solubility, enhanced stability, and lower production costs while retaining the ability to bind IL-1R1 and inhibit IL-1 signaling.  

A preferred embodiment of the invention is a peptide comprising the sequence SGRKSSKMQA (SEQ ID NO:1), which corresponds to residues 14-23 of human IL-1Ra. This peptide binds IL-1R1 with high affinity (Kd ≈ 2.75 × 10^-8 M) comparable to full-length IL-1Ra (Kd ≈ 4.8 × 10^-8 M) and significantly higher than IL-1β (Kd ≈ 1.71 × 10^-7 M). The peptide effectively competes with IL-1β for binding to IL-1R1 and inhibits downstream signaling.  

Additional peptides of the invention include those comprising the sequences RIWDVNQKT (SEQ ID NO:29), TAMEADQPVS (SEQ ID NO:35), or GPNA, all derived from different regions of IL-1Ra. These peptides and their functional fragments or variants exhibit similar receptor binding and antagonistic properties.  

Functional variants of the peptides include those with at least 70% sequence identity to the reference sequences, preferably at least 80%, more preferably at least 90%. Sequence identity is calculated using standard alignment algorithms such as BLAST with default parameters. Conservative substitutions that maintain biological activity are particularly contemplated, including substitutions within the following groups: aliphatic (A, V, L, I), hydroxyl (S, T), acidic (D, E), amide (N, Q), basic (K, R, H), aromatic (F, Y, W), and sulfur-containing (M, C) amino acids.  

The peptides may be modified at their N- or C-termini, including acetylation, amidation, or addition of stabilizing groups. Post-translational modifications such as phosphorylation, glycosylation, or lipidation may be included to enhance stability or bioavailability. The peptides may contain natural or unnatural amino acids and typically comprise between 5 and 50 amino acid residues, preferably between 8 and 20 residues.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

The Ilantide sequence motif (SGRKSSKMQA; SEQ ID NO:1) was identified within the N-terminal domain of human IL-1Ra using structural modeling based on the IL-1Ra–IL-1RI complex (PDB:1IRA). This region corresponds to residues involved in interactions with the Ig3 module of IL-1RI.  

### Example 2  

Surface plasmon resonance analysis demonstrated that Ilantide binds to the ectodomain of IL-1RI with an apparent Kd of 2.75 × 10^-8 M, comparable to IL-1Ra (4.8 × 10^-8 M) and higher affinity than IL-1β (1.71 × 10^-7 M). Competitive binding assays showed Ilantide dose-dependently inhibits IL-1β binding to IL-1RI.  

### Example 3  

Ilantide inhibited IL-1β-induced macrophage activation in a sequence-specific manner. HEK-Blue IL-1β cells treated with Ilantide showed dose-dependent inhibition of NF-κB activation (IC50 ≈ 10^-7 M), while scrambled control peptides had no effect.  

### Example 4  

Ilantide inhibited IL-1β-induced TNF-α secretion by AMJ2-C8 macrophages in a dose-dependent manner (maximum inhibition >90% at 10^-5 M). The dendrimeric form (Ilantide-t) showed greater efficacy than the monomeric form (Ilantide-m).  

### Example 5  

Ilantide induced neurite outgrowth in primary cerebellar granule neurons with maximum effect at 10^-7 M, comparable to full-length IL-1Ra. The peptide also blocked IL-1β-induced inhibition of neuritogenesis.  

### Example 6  

Ilantide promoted survival of cerebellar granule neurons in culture, reducing potassium withdrawal-induced cell death by up to 60% at 10^-6 M. The neuroprotective effect was comparable to insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) positive controls.  

### Example 7  

In a rat collagen-induced arthritis model, daily subcutaneous administration of Ilantide-t (10 mg/kg) significantly attenuated clinical manifestations compared to vehicle controls (P < 0.05). Treatment reduced joint swelling, erythema, and mobility impairment.  

### Example 8  

Following subcutaneous administration (10 mg/kg) in rats, Ilantide-t was detected in plasma (5,816 ± 44 ng/ml at 20 min) and cerebrospinal fluid (125 ± 13 ng/ml at 30 min), demonstrating blood-brain barrier penetration with a plasma/CSF ratio of 55.  

### Example 9  

Ilantide reversed neuronal cytotoxicity induced by kainic acid in hippocampal neuron cultures. Treatment with Ilantide-t (10^-7 M) increased cell viability by 45% compared to untreated controls (P < 0.01) and was more effective than full-length IL-1RA at equimolar concentrations.
