# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of bioluminescence, particularly to engineered luciferase enzymes and novel luciferin analogs capable of producing near-infrared (NIR) bioluminescence. The invention encompasses click beetle red luciferase (CBR) variant polypeptides with enhanced luminescence, altered emission spectra, and modified substrate specificity, as well as polynucleotides encoding these variants, vectors, host cells, transgenic animals, and bioluminescent systems incorporating these components. The invention further provides methods for detecting enzymatic activity, measuring bioluminescence, and imaging biological samples in vivo.  

## BACKGROUND  

Bioluminescence imaging (BLI) has become a cornerstone technique in molecular biology, enabling non-invasive visualization of gene expression, cellular processes, and disease progression in living organisms. Traditional bioluminescence systems, such as firefly luciferase (Luc2) paired with D-luciferin (D-LH2), emit light peaking near 600 nm, which is sufficient for shallow tissue imaging but suffers from significant absorption in deeper tissues due to hemoglobin, melanin, and other chromophores.  

Current limitations of conventional bioluminescence systems include:  
1. **Poor tissue penetration** due to absorption of visible light (500–650 nm).  
2. **Insufficient biodistribution** of substrates, particularly in challenging tissues like the brain.  
3. **Low signal intensity** when using red-shifted substrates, limiting sensitivity.  

Previous efforts to improve deep-tissue imaging have focused on mutagenesis to red-shift luciferase emission or synthesizing luciferin analogs with extended π-conjugation. However, these approaches have yielded only marginal improvements, as the inherent photon-emitting species (oxyluciferin) imposes spectral constraints. Additionally, existing NIR-emitting substrates, such as AkaLumine-HCl, exhibit limited signal enhancement over conventional systems at certain concentrations.  

There remains a critical need for improved bioluminescent systems that combine high photon flux, deep-tissue penetration, and stable enzyme-substrate interactions. The present invention addresses these challenges by providing engineered CBR variants with superior properties, including enhanced NIR emission, improved substrate specificity, and increased stability in biological systems.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides novel click beetle red luciferase (CBR) variant polypeptides exhibiting enhanced luminescence, altered emission spectra, and modified substrate specificity compared to wild-type CBR. These variants are engineered through rational mutagenesis, particularly at residues R334 and G351, which influence hydrogen bonding and substrate interactions.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
- **CBR variant polypeptides** with enhanced luminescence, such as the R334S+G351R mutant (CBR2), which demonstrates increased stability and brightness in mammalian cells.  
- **Altered emission spectra**, including a significant red-shift to ~730 nm when paired with naphthyl-luciferin analogs (e.g., NH2-NpLH2).  
- **Modified substrate specificity**, with improved affinity for novel luciferin derivatives (e.g., NH2-NpLH2 and OH-NpLH2) while retaining activity with D-LH2.  
- **Codon-optimized polynucleotides** (e.g., CBR2opt) encoding CBR variants for enhanced expression in mammalian cells.  
- **Vectors and host cells** comprising these polynucleotides, enabling recombinant production of the variants.  
- **Non-human transgenic animals** incorporating cells or polynucleotides encoding the CBR variants.  
- **Near-infrared bioluminescence systems** combining CBR variants with NIR-emitting luciferin analogs for deep-tissue imaging.  
- **Methods of use**, including in vivo imaging, transcriptional reporting, protein-protein interaction assays (e.g., BRET), and enzymatic activity detection.  
- **Kits** containing polynucleotides, vectors, or purified CBR variant polypeptides for research and diagnostic applications.  

The CBR variants exhibit superior performance in vivo, producing brighter and more stable signals than conventional systems (e.g., Luc2/D-LH2) while enabling high-resolution tomography in deep tissues such as the brain.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### 1. DEFINITIONS  

For clarity, the following terms are defined as used in this application:  

- **Bioluminescence/Luminescence**: Light emission resulting from enzymatic oxidation of a luciferin substrate.  
- **Enhanced**: Increased luminescence intensity, stability, or catalytic efficiency compared to a reference enzyme (e.g., wild-type CBR).  
- **Luciferin substrate**: A molecule oxidized by luciferase to produce light, including naturally occurring luciferins (e.g., D-LH2) and synthetic derivatives (e.g., NH2-NpLH2).  
- **Luciferase reaction/detection mixture**: A solution containing luciferase, substrate, ATP, and cofactors necessary for light emission.  
- **Relative substrate specificity**: The preference of a luciferase for one substrate over another, quantified by kinetic parameters (Km, Vmax).  
- **Fusion polypeptide/protein**: A chimeric protein comprising a CBR variant linked to a heterologous sequence (e.g., HALOTAG, fluorescent protein).  
- **Variant**: A modified version of a parental polypeptide or polynucleotide sequence, including substitutions, deletions, or insertions.  
- **Conservative substitution**: Replacement of an amino acid with another of similar hydrophobicity, charge, or size (e.g., Ser for Thr).  
- **Vector**: A nucleic acid molecule (e.g., plasmid, viral genome) capable of delivering and expressing a polynucleotide in a host cell.  
- **Wild-type/Native**: The unmodified, naturally occurring form of a polypeptide or polynucleotide.  

### 2. CBR VARIANTS  

The invention encompasses engineered CBR variants with improved properties, generated via site-directed mutagenesis at residues critical for substrate binding and catalysis. Key mutations include:  

- **R334S and G351R**: These substitutions restructure the hydrogen-bonding network in the luciferin binding pocket, enhancing activity with naphthyl-luciferin analogs.  
- **Additional substitutions**: Libraries of mutations at positions 334 (E, Q, D, N, H, S, T, C, Y) and 351 (R, K, E) were screened to identify optimal combinations.  

**Characteristics of CBR Variants**:  
- **Enhanced luminescence**: CBR2 (R334S+G351R) exhibits ~2–3-fold higher signal than Luc2 in mammalian cells.  
- **Altered emission spectra**: Peak emission shifts from 614 nm (D-LH2) to 730 nm (NH2-NpLH2).  
- **Modified substrate specificity**:  
  - Km for PBI-4813: 0.3 µM (vs. 1.0 µM for wild-type CBR).  
  - Km for PBI-4739: 0.5 µM (vs. 1.3 µM for wild-type CBR).  
  - Relative Vmax for NH2-NpLH2: 8 × 10⁴ RLU/µM (vs. 6 × 10⁵ for wild-type CBR).  

**Fusion Proteins and Applications**:  
- CBR variants may be fused to reporter proteins (e.g., HALOTAG), signal peptides, or membrane anchors for targeted imaging.  
- Codon-optimized sequences (e.g., CBR2opt) enhance expression in mammalian hosts.  

### 3. NEAR-INFRARED BIOLUMINESCENCE SYSTEMS  

The invention provides NIR bioluminescence systems combining CBR variants with novel luciferin analogs, including:  

- **NH2-NpLH2**: Amino-naphthyl naphtho[2,1]thiazole luciferin (emission peak ~730 nm).  
- **OH-NpLH2**: Hydroxy-naphtha[2,1]thiazole luciferin (emission peak ~743 nm).  

These substrates exhibit:  
- High aqueous solubility (>46 mM).  
- Improved cell permeability compared to D-LH2.  
- Low cytotoxicity and stability in physiological conditions.  

### 4. METHODS OF USING THE CBR VARIANTS AND/OR BIOLUMINESCENT SYSTEMS  

Applications include:  
- **In vivo imaging**: Subcutaneous or intracranial cell tracking in animal models.  
- **Transcriptional reporting**: Multiplexing with other luciferases (e.g., aequorin).  
- **Protein-protein interaction assays**: BRET using CBR variants as energy donors.  
- **Enzyme activity detection**: Pro-substrate cleavage by proteases, kinases, or other enzymes.  

### 5. SAMPLE  

Samples suitable for analysis include mammalian cells (e.g., HEK-293, MCF-7), tissue lysates, and bodily fluids.
