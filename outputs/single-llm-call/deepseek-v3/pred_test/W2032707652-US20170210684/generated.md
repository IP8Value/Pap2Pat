## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of zeolite catalysts and their application in hydrocarbon processing. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel zeolite structures identified through predictive materials modeling for use in hydroisomerization processes, particularly in the dewaxing of lubricant base oils and other hydrocarbon feedstocks.

## BACKGROUND

Hydrocarbon feedstocks derived from crude oil serve as the primary source for transportation fuels and chemical feedstocks. The processing of these feedstocks requires efficient catalytic systems to transform raw materials into valuable products. Hydrotreating and hydrocracking processes have been developed to improve the quality of hydrocarbon products, but these techniques face limitations when dealing with waxy components in lubricant base oils. Traditional dewaxing techniques often struggle to achieve the desired balance between reducing pour points and maintaining viscosity indices in lubricant oils.

Zeolites have emerged as important catalysts in hydrocarbon processing due to their unique pore structures and shape-selective properties. These crystalline aluminosilicates contain sub-2 nm internal pores that provide size and shape selectivity for catalytic reactions. In particular, zeolites have shown promise in catalyzing the transformation of linear long-chain alkanes to slightly branched alkanes of similar molecular weight, which is crucial for improving lubricant oil properties.

Prior art in zeolite catalysts has focused on known framework types such as MFI, FER, and MTW for various hydrocarbon processing applications. However, these conventional zeolites often exhibit limitations in selectivity and activity for specific transformations. The prior art in dewaxing techniques has demonstrated that an optimal zeolite catalyst must possess a delicate balance between framework confinement and molecular size to effectively promote hydroisomerization while minimizing unwanted cracking reactions.

Existing approaches to zeolite catalyst development have relied heavily on experimental trial-and-error methods, which are time-consuming and resource-intensive. While computational screening methods have been attempted for simpler systems, the complexity of hydrocarbon mixtures and the need to account for multiple performance parameters have made comprehensive screening impractical until now. There remains a significant need for new zeolite catalysts with improved performance characteristics and systematic methods for their discovery.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses novel mildly acidic zeolite catalysts with exceptional performance characteristics for hydrocarbon processing applications, particularly hydroisomerization dewaxing processes. These zeolites provide significant benefits over conventional catalysts, including higher selectivity for linear alkanes, improved adsorption characteristics, and enhanced catalytic activity for desired transformations.

The invention describes a dewaxing process utilizing these zeolite catalysts to convert linear long-chain alkanes in hydrocarbon feedstocks to slightly branched isomers while minimizing cracking reactions. The process achieves superior product properties including reduced pour points and increased viscosity indices in lubricant base oils.

A key aspect of the invention is the method for discovering optimal zeolite structures through predictive materials modeling. This computational approach enables the systematic evaluation of hundreds of thousands of potential zeolite framework types to identify those with the most favorable characteristics for specific hydrocarbon processing applications. The method combines high-throughput screening calculations with advanced simulation techniques to predict adsorption and catalytic performance metrics.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The invention relates to hydrocarbon feedstocks suitable for producing lubricant base oils, particularly those containing linear long-chain alkanes with 18-30 carbon atoms. These feedstocks often exhibit undesirable properties such as high pour points due to the presence of linear paraffins. The limitations of linear long-chain alkanes in lubricant applications stem from their tendency to crystallize at elevated temperatures, leading to poor low-temperature performance.

Zeolites serve as effective catalysts for the hydroisomerization of these feedstocks due to their shape-selective pore structures. The invention addresses the challenges of experimentally testing zeolites by providing a computational framework for evaluating potential catalyst performance. The predictive modeling approach enables the assessment of zeolite characteristics without requiring physical synthesis of each candidate material.

The high-throughput screening workflow of the invention involves multiple stages of evaluation to identify promising zeolite structures. The process begins with assembling a comprehensive group of candidate zeolite structures from existing databases and hypothetical frameworks. Each candidate is evaluated based on key performance quantities including adsorption affinity, selectivity for linear versus branched alkanes, and chain length dependence.

The invention describes a bi-functional catalyst system for dewaxing processes, combining zeolites with Brønsted acid functionality and hydrogenation components. The zeolite component provides shape-selective properties while the hydrogenation component, typically comprising Group VIII metals, facilitates the desired hydroisomerization reactions. The operating conditions for the hydroisomerization process are optimized to balance conversion efficiency with product quality.

The systematic discovery method involves forming a composite score for each candidate zeolite structure based on multiple performance indicators. This score incorporates factors such as Henry's constant for linear alkane adsorption, selectivity between linear and branched isomers, and chain length dependence. Candidate structures are progressively pruned using a fast screening procedure followed by more detailed evaluation of surviving candidates at representative reaction conditions.

The invention has successfully identified both rediscoveries of previously known zeolite framework types and novel structures with superior performance characteristics. These findings validate the predictive capability of the computational screening approach and demonstrate its utility for materials discovery in hydrocarbon processing applications.

### Example

An exemplary application of the invention involves the use of mildly acidic zeolites for hydroisomerization of long-chain alkanes. The screening process begins with evaluation of adsorption characteristics using siliceous forms of various zeolite framework types. In one implementation, the method screened over 330,000 zeolite structures from multiple databases to identify optimal candidates for processing a complex hydrocarbon feed mixture.

The evaluation considers three primary factors contributing to catalytic performance: affinity for linear alkanes, selectivity between linear and branched isomers, and chain length dependence. The first screening step utilizes short simulations to rapidly assess these characteristics across the entire candidate pool. Top-performing structures from established zeolite databases are identified and compared with novel candidates from hypothetical framework collections.

The invention reveals important relationships between key performance indicators, such as the correlation between Henry's constant and adsorption enthalpy. These relationships help explain the superior performance of certain zeolite structures and guide the selection of optimal candidates. The concept of pore bumpiness emerges as a significant factor influencing selectivity, with smoother channels generally favoring linear alkane adsorption.

Comparative analysis of adsorption characteristics at different conditions demonstrates the importance of evaluating performance across a range of relevant operating parameters. The arrangement of adsorbed hydrocarbons in top-performing structures provides insights into the molecular-level mechanisms underlying their superior catalytic properties.

### Methods

The invention utilizes comprehensive databases of zeolite structures, including the IZA-SC database of experimentally characterized frameworks and the PCOD database of predicted zeolite-like materials. Simulation methods employ the TraPPE force field to accurately model molecular interactions within these porous materials.

Configurational-bias Monte Carlo simulations form the core of the computational screening approach, enabling efficient sampling of molecular configurations in complex pore environments. The method incorporates energy grid tabulation techniques to accelerate calculations while maintaining accuracy. CB-GCMC simulations provide detailed information about adsorption equilibria under various conditions.

The high-throughput screening calculations are performed on advanced computing architectures, leveraging parallel processing capabilities to evaluate hundreds of thousands of candidate structures. The two-level parallel execution hierarchy optimizes computational efficiency while maintaining the rigor of molecular-level simulations.

### Exemplary Embodiments

Embodiment 1 describes a catalyst system comprising a zeolite with one-dimensional channel system having pore diameters between 0.45 nm and 0.75 nm, combined with a Group VIII metal hydrogenation component.

Embodiment 2 discloses a catalyst system incorporating a zeolite framework type selected from ATO, MRE, or AFO, with optimized Brønsted acid site density for hydroisomerization applications.

Embodiment 3 presents novel zeolite structures identified through the screening process, including materials with PCOD identifiers 8113534, 8113542, and 8113550, which exhibit exceptional selectivity for linear alkanes.

Embodiment 4 describes a catalyst system combining zeolites with three-dimensional channel systems and platinum or palladium hydrogenation components, optimized for dewaxing of lubricant base oils.

Embodiment 5 specifies a catalyst system where the zeolite component has a pore bumpiness parameter (Δd) less than 0.1 nm, ensuring high selectivity for linear versus branched alkanes.

Embodiment 6 discloses a catalyst system with zeolites having elliptical channel cross-sections, where the semiminor axis length ranges from 0.45 nm to 0.55 nm for optimal linear alkane confinement.

Embodiment 7 describes a bi-functional catalyst system where the zeolite component has a free channel diameter between 0.5 nm and 0.6 nm and the hydrogenation component comprises 0.1-1.0 wt% platinum.

Embodiment 8 specifies a catalyst system incorporating zeolites with one-dimensional 12-ring channel architectures, demonstrating superior performance in hydroisomerization applications.

Embodiment 9 discloses optional combinations of zeolite frameworks and hydrogenation components optimized for specific hydrocarbon feedstocks and product requirements.

Embodiment 10 describes catalyst systems with tailored acid site distributions and metal loadings to balance isomerization activity and cracking selectivity.

Additional embodiments specify particular zeolite framework types, channel system dimensionalities, pore characteristics, and hydrogenation components that collectively define the scope of the invention. These include embodiments optimized for specific alkane chain lengths, operating conditions, and product property targets.

The invention further encompasses methods for evaluating candidate zeolite structures based on adsorption affinities and selectivities, with particular attention to performance metrics relevant to industrial hydroisomerization processes. Specific embodiments describe optimization criteria for maximizing the product of key performance indicators under representative reaction conditions.

The scope of the invention includes catalyst systems derived from both experimentally synthesized zeolites and predicted framework types, recognizing that the computational screening method can identify promising candidates regardless of their current synthetic accessibility. This forward-looking approach enables the development of next-generation zeolite catalysts as synthetic methods advance.
