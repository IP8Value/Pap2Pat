## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to a microviscometer device and method for detecting analytes, particularly nucleic acids such as DNA, through measurement of solution viscosity changes. More specifically, the invention utilizes asynchronous magnetic bead rotation (AMBR) to monitor viscosity changes associated with chemical or physical state changes of analytes in solution.  

## BACKGROUND  

Sensitive and specific detection of nucleic acids is crucial for applications ranging from medical diagnostics to forensic analysis. Current DNA detection methods predominantly rely on fluorescence-based techniques such as quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), which require fluorescent labeling of target molecules. While fluorescence provides high sensitivity, it involves complex sample preparation steps and expensive reagents. Alternative detection methods have been explored but none achieve comparable sensitivity without significant cost increases.  

Viscosity-based detection offers a promising alternative approach. The viscosity of a DNA solution depends on both the concentration and length of DNA strands, meaning viscosity changes can indicate DNA polymerization or digestion events. However, conventional viscometers require large sample volumes and lack the sensitivity needed for diagnostic applications. Existing microviscometer technologies face limitations in measurement precision, sample volume requirements, and real-time monitoring capabilities.  

The present invention addresses these limitations through an AMBR-based microviscometer that detects viscosity changes via the rotational dynamics of magnetic beads in solution. This approach enables sensitive, label-free detection of nucleic acid reactions using small sample volumes, overcoming key limitations of prior art methods.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method and system for detecting analytes through viscosity measurement using asynchronous magnetic bead rotation (AMBR). The AMBR method involves suspending magnetic beads in a sample solution and applying a rotating magnetic field. When the field rotation rate exceeds a critical frequency, the beads rotate asynchronously at a rate dependent on solution viscosity. By monitoring bead rotation periods, the system detects viscosity changes associated with analyte state changes.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
- Detection of physical state changes such as polymerization or fragmentation of nucleic acids  
- Detection of chemical state changes such as enzymatic modifications  
- Real-time monitoring of reaction progression through continuous viscosity measurement  
- Applications including nucleic acid synthesis monitoring, protein synthesis monitoring, and hydrolysis reaction monitoring  

The AMBR system achieves superior sensitivity compared to conventional viscometers while requiring minimal sample volumes, enabling new applications in diagnostic testing and reaction monitoring.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a microviscometer system based on asynchronous magnetic bead rotation (AMBR) for detecting analytes through solution viscosity changes. The system monitors the rotational dynamics of magnetic beads in a rotating magnetic field to determine solution viscosity, which changes in response to analyte state modifications.  

### Asynchronous Magnetic Bead Rotation (AMBR)  

The AMBR method utilizes the asynchronous rotation of magnetic beads to measure solution viscosity. When placed in a rotating magnetic field below a critical frequency, beads rotate synchronously with the field. Above this frequency, viscous drag prevents synchronous rotation, causing beads to rotate asynchronously at a slower, viscosity-dependent rate.  

The system operates in both synchronous and asynchronous modes, with the latter providing viscosity measurements. Various paramagnetic bead types can be used, with diameters ranging from 7.6 to 45 μm. Bead rotation is monitored optically or via magnetic sensors, with rotation periods calculated through image analysis or signal processing.  

Key advantages of AMBR include:  
- Ability to measure viscosity changes associated with nanomolar analyte concentrations  
- Operation with sample volumes below 10 μL, extending to picoliter volumes in microfluidic implementations  
- Real-time, continuous monitoring capability  
- Label-free detection requiring no chemical modification of analytes  

The system detects viscosity changes across a wide range (0.89 to 2.8 cP in demonstrated embodiments), suitable for monitoring most biochemical reactions. Applications include nucleic acid synthesis monitoring (e.g., PCR), protein synthesis monitoring, and hydrolysis reaction monitoring.  

### Analytes  

The invention detects any analyte whose physical or chemical state change affects solution viscosity. Such changes include:  
- Polymerization or fragmentation of nucleic acids  
- Protein synthesis or degradation  
- Carbohydrate polymerization  
- Lipid metabolism  

Nucleic acid analytes include DNA and RNA oligomers from 50 to 50,000 base pairs. Protein analytes include natural and synthetic proteins, peptides, and amino acid polymers. The system also detects cells, carbohydrates, lipids, and their derivatives.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Reagents included glycerol/water solutions and DNA solutions with magnetic beads (7.6-45 μm diameter). Samples were placed between glass slides with a 210 μm gap, sealed to prevent evaporation. A rotating magnetic field (2.7 mT) was applied while recording bead motion at 10 fps. Rotation periods were determined via image analysis and Fourier transform, showing linear correlation with Ubbelohde viscometer measurements.  

### Calibration of AMBR Viscometer  

Calibration experiments established a linear relationship between rotation period and viscosity (R² > 0.99). Theoretical analysis confirmed this relationship through force balance equations accounting for magnetic torque and viscous drag. Experimental results matched theoretical predictions across the 0.89-2.8 cP range.  

### Example 3  

Bead-to-bead variation analysis showed rotation period standard deviation of 10%, primarily due to magnetic property variations rather than size differences. Single-bead measurements over time showed only 1% variation, confirming measurement precision.  

### Preparation of Digestion Reaction Samples  

DNA digestion with EcoRI produced measurable viscosity decreases corresponding to fragment size changes. PCR reactions showed viscosity increases proportional to product concentration. Gel electrophoresis confirmed AMBR results with 5-cycle delay. The system detected DNA concentration changes as low as 0.42 g/L.
