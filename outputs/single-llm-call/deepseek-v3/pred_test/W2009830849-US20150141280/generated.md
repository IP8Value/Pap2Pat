# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of breast cancer diagnosis, specifically to novel methods and compositions for detecting Luminal A subtype breast cancer through identification of circulating microRNA (miRNA) biomarkers. More particularly, the invention provides diagnostic kits and methods utilizing a panel of specific miRNAs that are differentially expressed in the blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. The invention addresses the critical need for minimally invasive, subtype-specific diagnostic tools that can complement existing breast cancer screening methods such as mammography.  

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION  

Breast cancer represents a significant global health burden, being the most common female malignancy in nearly all European countries and North America. With over 1,300,000 new cases diagnosed annually worldwide, breast cancer remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality among women. The disease exhibits substantial heterogeneity, with distinct tumor phenotypes reflecting diverse molecular alterations and initiating events. Current classification systems divide breast tumors into subtypes primarily based on expression patterns of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/neu).  

The Luminal A subtype constitutes the most prevalent form of breast cancer, accounting for approximately 70% of cases. Phenotypically, Luminal A-like tumors are characterized as hormone receptor positive (ER+ and/or PR+) and HER2/neu negative. These tumors are often detected through screening, typically node-negative, and generally associated with favorable prognoses. Despite these relatively positive characteristics, hematogenous spread of malignant cells leading to metastatic disease remains the primary cause of breast cancer mortality, underscoring the need for improved diagnostic and prognostic tools.  

Current breast cancer diagnosis relies heavily on mammographic screening followed by confirmatory tissue biopsy, an invasive procedure that carries inherent risks and discomfort for patients. While mammography remains the gold standard screening tool, it has well-documented limitations including reduced sensitivity in dense breast tissue and the inability to distinguish between tumor subtypes. The medical community recognizes an urgent need for complementary, minimally invasive diagnostic methods that can facilitate early detection and subtype classification.  

MicroRNAs have emerged as promising candidates for cancer biomarkers due to their stability in circulation and their intimate involvement in carcinogenic processes. These small non-coding RNA molecules regulate gene expression at the post-transcriptional level and participate in tumorigenesis both as causative agents (functioning as oncogenes or tumor suppressors) and as products of the carcinogenic process. Importantly, tumor-specific miRNAs have been detected in circulation, suggesting their potential utility as non-invasive diagnostic markers.  

Prior attempts to identify circulating biomarkers for breast cancer have yielded limited success. Existing markers such as Carcinoma Antigen 15-3 (CA 15-3) and Carcinoembryonic Antigen (CEA) demonstrate poor sensitivity in early-stage disease, rendering them unsuitable for diagnostic applications. While several studies have reported altered miRNA expression patterns in breast cancer tissue, the translation of these findings into clinically useful circulating biomarkers has proven challenging. Previous research has failed to identify miRNA profiles with sufficient sensitivity and specificity for routine diagnostic use, particularly for specific molecular subtypes like Luminal A.  

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION  

The primary object of the present invention is to identify and validate novel circulating miRNA biomarkers specific to Luminal A-like breast cancer. A further object is to provide a diagnostic kit and method capable of detecting these biomarkers with high sensitivity and specificity. The invention aims to overcome limitations of current diagnostic approaches by offering a minimally invasive test that can complement existing screening methods and facilitate early, accurate diagnosis of Luminal A subtype breast cancer.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a diagnostic kit for detecting Luminal A-like breast cancer comprising reagents for identifying and quantifying specific miRNAs in biological samples. The kit includes primers and probes for detecting miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652, which collectively form a diagnostic panel with superior sensitivity and specificity compared to existing biomarkers.  

The diagnostic kit components include specific oligonucleotide primers for reverse transcription and quantitative PCR amplification of the target miRNAs, fluorescent probes for real-time detection, appropriate controls, and detailed instructions for use. The kit may be configured for use with various sample types including whole blood, plasma, or serum, with particular advantages observed when using EDTA-whole blood samples due to miRNA stability in this medium.  

The invention further provides methods for using the diagnostic kit to aid in breast cancer diagnosis and management. The methods involve obtaining a biological sample from a patient, extracting RNA, performing reverse transcription and quantitative PCR to measure expression levels of the target miRNAs, and comparing these levels to established reference ranges. The method generates a diagnostic output indicating the likelihood of Luminal A-like breast cancer presence based on the characteristic down-regulation of miR-181a and miR-652 and altered expression of miR-29a observed in affected patients.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on comprehensive studies involving microarray profiling and validation of circulating miRNA expression patterns in patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. The study design incorporated rigorous analytical methods and statistical validation to ensure reliable identification of clinically relevant biomarkers.  

Blood samples were prospectively collected from 110 women, including 54 consecutive patients with newly diagnosed Luminal A-like breast cancer (ER positive, PR positive, HER2/neu negative) and 56 healthy control participants. Total RNA was extracted from 1 ml of blood using TRI Reagent BD, with RNA concentration and integrity verified by spectrophotometry and bioanalyzer analysis.  

Initial microarray profiling was performed using TaqMan human miRNA arrays covering 667 miRNAs. Data analysis employed a nonlinear artificial neural network (ANN) algorithm incorporating Monte Carlo Cross validation to minimize false discovery and ensure robust predictive performance. This approach identified 76 miRNAs with altered expression in Luminal A-like breast cancer patients compared to controls.  

Validation studies using RT-qPCR confirmed significant dysregulation of specific miRNAs in an independent cohort of 44 cancer patients and 46 controls. The validation process revealed consistent down-regulation of miR-181a and miR-652 in the circulation of women with Luminal A-like breast tumors (p = 0.004 and p = 0.009 respectively). These miRNAs were also under-expressed in tumor tissue compared to tumor-associated normal tissue. MiR-29a showed altered expression patterns in circulation, though its tissue expression profile differed from the other markers.  

### Validation Study Statistical Analysis  

Statistical analysis was performed using Minitab version 16.0. Data distribution was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test, with non-normal data log-transformed for analysis. Significance of circulating miRNA level differences was determined using Mann-Whitney U tests, t-tests, ANOVA, and appropriate correlation measures (Spearman's Rho or Pearson). Binary logistic regression analysis generated receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves to evaluate diagnostic performance.  

### Results  

The miRNA expression results demonstrated that miR-181a and miR-652 were consistently down-regulated in the blood of Luminal A-like breast cancer patients across all disease stages. MiR-29a showed distinct expression patterns that contributed to diagnostic accuracy when combined with the other markers.  

ROC curve analysis revealed that the combination of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 produced an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.80, with sensitivity and specificity of 77% and 74% respectively. This performance exceeds that of currently available circulating biomarkers for breast cancer. Notably, miR-181a expression showed a negative correlation with invasive tumor size (Pearson r = -0.429, p = 0.059), suggesting potential utility in disease monitoring.  

The invention thus provides a clinically valuable tool for Luminal A-like breast cancer detection, offering significant advantages over existing diagnostic methods through its minimally invasive nature, subtype specificity, and superior performance characteristics. The identified miRNA panel represents a substantial advance in breast cancer diagnostics with potential applications in early detection, disease monitoring, and therapeutic decision-making.
