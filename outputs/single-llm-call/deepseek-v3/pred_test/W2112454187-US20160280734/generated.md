# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of radiochemistry and more specifically to automated systems for synthesizing radiolabeled compounds used in positron emission tomography (PET) imaging. The disclosed apparatus and methods provide an improved automated radiosynthesizer capable of handling diverse chemical reactions under high pressure and temperature conditions while maintaining operational flexibility and reliability. The system incorporates novel features including movable reactor assemblies, disposable cassettes containing all fluid paths, and an integrated reagent and gas handling robot to enable synthesis of a wide range of PET tracers without hardware modifications between productions.  

## BACKGROUND  

Positron emission tomography has emerged as a powerful diagnostic tool in modern medicine, enabling non-invasive imaging of biological processes in vivo. The technique relies on positron-emitting radionuclides incorporated into biologically active molecules, known as PET tracers. Among these, 2-[18F]fluoro-2-deoxy-d-glucose ([18F]FDG) has become the most widely used PET tracer due to its relatively straightforward synthesis and broad clinical applications. The increasing demand for [18F]FDG has driven the development of automated radiosynthesizers to standardize production, reduce costs, and minimize radiation exposure to operators.  

While automated synthesis of [18F]FDG represents a significant advancement, numerous other 18F-labeled PET tracers with important clinical and research applications remain difficult to produce automatically. Many of these compounds require complex chemical reactions involving high pressures, elevated temperatures, or corrosive reagents that exceed the capabilities of conventional automated synthesizers. For example, nucleoside analogs used for imaging cell proliferation and gene expression often necessitate high-temperature reactions in volatile solvents. Previous attempts to automate synthesis of such tracers have required modification of the original chemistry to accommodate equipment limitations, potentially compromising tracer quality or yield.  

Current automated radiosynthesizers face several technical limitations that restrict their versatility. Permanent tubing and valve connections to reaction vessels create pressure constraints, as these components often cannot withstand the conditions required for many syntheses. Additionally, the need to clean and reconfigure systems between productions of different tracers introduces complexity and potential sources of error. There exists a need for an automated radiosynthesis platform that can accommodate diverse chemical processes while maintaining simplicity of operation and reliability.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides an automated radiosynthesizer system that overcomes limitations of conventional devices through innovative mechanical and operational designs. The system comprises three primary components working in concert: movable reactor assemblies, disposable cassettes containing all fluid paths, and an integrated reagent and gas handling robot. This configuration enables dynamic reconfiguration of fluid paths during synthesis while maintaining sealed conditions for high-pressure reactions.  

The reactor assemblies each contain a glass reaction vessel held within a temperature-controlled chuck. Each reactor can be precisely positioned beneath a cassette and raised to seal against a gasket, creating isolated reaction environments as needed. This movable configuration eliminates permanent tubing connections that typically limit reaction pressures. The reactors incorporate active heating and cooling systems capable of rapid temperature changes, with maximum operating temperatures exceeding 180°C.  

Disposable cassettes form another key innovation, containing all wetted components including reagent vials, fluid paths, valves, and purification cartridges. Each cassette slides into the synthesizer and locks into position, with alignment features ensuring proper engagement with both reactor assemblies and the handling robot. The cassette design incorporates multiple functional positions for different synthesis operations including evaporation, sealed reactions, reagent addition, and product transfer. By containing all disposable components, the cassettes eliminate cleaning requirements between syntheses and facilitate rapid transition between different tracer productions.  

The reagent and gas handling robot provides coordinated control of both liquid reagents and gas flows throughout the synthesis process. Mounted on a three-axis motion system, the robot includes a pneumatically actuated vial gripper for transferring reagent vials between storage and addition positions, along with a gas supply system for delivering inert gas and vacuum to the cassettes. This integrated approach reduces the number of valves and connections compared to conventional systems, improving reliability.  

The synthesizer's control system orchestrates all components through a modular software architecture that allows users to construct synthesis protocols from predefined unit operations. This software paradigm enables creation of diverse synthesis protocols without requiring low-level programming, making the system accessible to chemists without specialized engineering knowledge. The system supports remote monitoring and control through client devices connected via network interfaces.  

In operation, the automated synthesizer performs radiosynthesis through a sequence of coordinated steps. Radioisotope handling begins with trapping on a preconditioned cartridge, followed by elution into the reaction vessel. Reagent handling involves precise transfer of chemicals from sealed vials using the robotic system. Reactions proceed under controlled temperature and pressure conditions, with the movable reactor assemblies enabling sealed operation when needed. Evaporations are performed with simultaneous heating and vapor removal through vacuum ports. Product transfer and purification utilize built-in cartridges and fluid paths within the cassettes.  

Alternative embodiments of the invention include variations in the reactor configuration, cassette design, and robotic handling systems. The platform can be adapted for production of multiple PET tracers in sequence by exchanging cassettes and software protocols without hardware modifications. The system's flexibility extends to handling different radioisotopes and supporting diverse chemical processes including nucleophilic substitutions, condensations, and hydrolysis reactions.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF ILLUSTRATED EMBODIMENTS  

The automated radiosynthesizer is illustrated through several embodiments that demonstrate its components and operation. The synthesizer comprises a main synthesis module housing three reactor assemblies positioned beneath corresponding cassettes, with the reagent and gas handling robot mounted adjacent to these components. A separate control system enclosure contains electronics, pneumatics, and cooling equipment connected to the synthesis module through appropriate interfaces.  

Key synthesizer components include the reactor assemblies, each containing a glass reaction vessel held within a three-segment spring-loaded chuck that ensures optimal thermal contact. Each segment incorporates a cartridge heater and thermocouple for precise temperature control, with active liquid cooling provided through integrated channels. The reactors are mounted on linear actuators that enable precise positioning beneath the cassettes, with pneumatic cylinders providing vertical movement to seal the reaction vessels against cassette gaskets. Hall effect sensors provide feedback on reactor position and sealing status.  

The control system interface presents users with an intuitive software environment for creating and executing synthesis protocols. Client devices connect to the system through network interfaces, allowing remote monitoring and control. The software implements a paradigm shift from low-level programming to unit operation-based protocol construction, where predefined chemistry operations serve as building blocks for complete syntheses. Examples of unit operations include EVAPORATE, REACT, ADD, and TRANSFER, each representing a discrete chemical process.  

The user interface displays system status through several views, including an overview of all active unit operations, a detailed sequencer showing the current operation's progress, and real-time parameters for the executing step. An abort button provides immediate termination capability for safety. The software architecture follows a client-server model with specialized applications handling different functions including web services, core synthesis control, database operations, and video streaming from reactor cameras.  

Each disposable cassette features a molded polyurethane body containing stainless steel needles, tubing, and chemically inert valves. A custom PTFE-coated silicone gasket on the cassette's bottom surface provides sealing surfaces for the reaction vessels. The cassette design incorporates eleven reagent vial storage positions in an inverted configuration, plus three addition positions with dual upward-pointing needles for fluid delivery. Internal fluid paths connect these components to various functional positions including two sealed reaction locations, an evaporation port, and transfer pathways.  

The cassette's bottom surface illustrates the gasket pattern with distinct sealing zones for different operations. The top view reveals vial storage positions, reagent addition ports, gas inlets, and locations for waste and recovery vials. Side profile illustrations demonstrate gas and vacuum flow paths through the cassette during operations. The cassette incorporates multiple stopcock valves that reconfigure fluid paths for operations like cartridge trapping and product purification.  

The reagent and gas handling robot comprises a head portion mounted on a three-axis motion system. X- and y-axis linear actuators provide planar movement, while z-axis pneumatic cylinders enable vertical positioning. The robot's head integrates a vial gripper for reagent manipulation and a gas supply system with separate ports for inert gas and vacuum. A gas manifold distributes flows at controlled pressures, with in-line check valves preventing backflow. Hall effect sensors provide feedback on robot position and vial presence.  

The control system incorporates multiple subsystems coordinated by a central server. An embedded computer or programmable logic controller (PLC) manages motor controllers, temperature regulation, pneumatics, and sensor inputs through specialized interface modules. The system includes analog pressure regulators for gas supply, solenoid valve banks for pneumatic control, and dedicated controllers for stirring, heating, radiation detection, and HPLC operation.  

In operation, the automated synthesizer performs radiosynthesis through sequential unit operations. Radioisotope handling begins with trapping on a preconditioned cartridge, such as a QMA column for [18F]fluoride, followed by elution into the reaction vessel. Reagent handling involves the robotic transfer of chemicals from sealed vials to addition positions, with precise control of delivery volumes and timing. Reactions proceed under controlled temperature and pressure conditions, with the movable reactor assemblies enabling sealed operation when needed.  

Evaporations are performed with simultaneous heating and vapor removal through vacuum ports, while inert gas flow assists in solvent removal. Product transfer and purification utilize built-in cartridges and fluid paths within the cassettes, with the system capable of multiple transfer steps between reactors. The synthesizer supports semi-preparative HPLC for final purification and analytical HPLC for quality control.  

Results from synthesis validation demonstrate the system's capabilities. Production of tracers such as d-[18F]FAC and l-[18F]FMAU achieved radiochemical yields and synthesis times comparable to manual methods, with radiochemical purity exceeding 99%. The system successfully handled high initial radioactivity levels (up to 35 GBq) while maintaining operational reliability. Imaging studies confirmed equivalent biological distribution of automated versus manually produced tracers.  

The automated synthesizer offers several advantages over conventional systems. Its movable reactor design enables high-pressure reactions by eliminating fixed tubing connections. Disposable cassettes simplify setup and eliminate cleaning between syntheses. The integrated robotic system reduces valve counts and improves reliability. The unit operation-based software makes the system accessible to chemists without requiring specialized programming skills.
