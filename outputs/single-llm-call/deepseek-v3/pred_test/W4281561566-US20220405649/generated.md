# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Decision support systems are widely employed across industries to assist in complex decision-making processes by leveraging heuristic approaches and models trained on historical data. Such systems are utilized in applications including credit risk assessment, fraud detection, and medical diagnosis, where accurate predictive models are essential. These models aim to classify input data efficiently while extracting meaningful patterns from available features. Traditional machine learning models often achieve improved performance through ensemble methods that combine multiple simpler learners, thereby enhancing predictive accuracy and generalization capabilities.  

Quantum machine learning models have emerged as a promising alternative, exploiting quantum mechanical principles to process information in ways unattainable by classical computing. These models utilize quantum kernels to map data into high-dimensional Hilbert spaces, enabling enhanced feature representation through quantum entanglement and interference. Quantum support vector machines (QSVMs), for example, leverage quantum feature maps to construct decision boundaries that may outperform classical counterparts in certain scenarios. However, existing quantum machine learning models face limitations, including suboptimal performance on practical datasets, reliance on manually selected feature maps, and lack of automated model optimization.  

Current implementations of quantum machine learning models often require significant expertise in quantum physics to design effective feature maps, limiting accessibility for practitioners without specialized knowledge. Additionally, while quantum kernels offer theoretical advantages, their practical performance has yet to consistently surpass classical machine learning methods. There remains a need for improved quantum machine learning frameworks that automate model selection, enhance predictive accuracy, and broaden applicability across diverse machine learning tasks.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention introduces an ensemble generation system for quantum kernel-based machine learning, which improves model performance through an automated boosting procedure. The system comprises a server configured to generate an ensemble of quantum kernel-based learners by iteratively selecting top-performing quantum kernels, adjusting sample weights, and combining learners to form a robust predictive model. This approach enhances feature space discovery and optimizes hyperparameters without requiring manual intervention, making quantum machine learning more accessible and effective.  

A key advantage of the system embodiment is its ability to explore a wide range of quantum feature maps and kernel configurations automatically. By employing a boosting procedure that excludes previously selected feature maps in subsequent iterations, the system ensures diverse exploration of Hilbert spaces, leading to improved decision boundaries. The system further incorporates performance metrics to evaluate quantum kernel-based learners, dynamically adjusting weights to emphasize misclassified samples and optimize ensemble composition.  

The invention also encompasses a computer-implemented method for generating an ensemble of quantum kernel-based learners. The method involves modifying a plurality of quantum kernels through a boosting procedure, wherein each iteration selects a top-performing quantum kernel, assigns weights to learners, and adjusts sample distributions to refine model accuracy. This iterative process continues until termination conditions are met, resulting in a weighted combination of high-performing quantum kernel-based learners. The method provides technical improvements over conventional quantum machine learning by automating feature map selection and enhancing model generalization.  

A computer program product is further disclosed, comprising computer-readable instructions that, when executed by a processor, perform the ensemble generation process. The program product facilitates training and testing of quantum kernel-based learners, calculates performance metrics, and implements the boosting procedure to construct an optimized ensemble. The advantage of this program product lies in its ability to integrate seamlessly with existing quantum computing infrastructure while delivering superior predictive performance compared to single-learner quantum models.  

The training and performance metrics utilized in the invention ensure robust model evaluation. By employing validation datasets for hyperparameter tuning and testing datasets for final performance assessment, the system mitigates overfitting and enhances generalization. The modified plurality of quantum kernels, selected through the boosting procedure, demonstrates improved accuracy over individual quantum kernels, particularly in complex classification and regression tasks.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present patent application discloses a system and method for generating ensembles of quantum kernel-based learners to enhance machine learning performance. The following detailed description outlines the structure, components, and operational principles of the invention, providing sufficient detail to enable a person skilled in the art to practice the disclosed embodiments.  

The scope of the embodiments described herein encompasses systems, methods, apparatus, and computer program products for implementing quantum machine learning ensembles. While certain implementations may utilize superconducting qubits or trapped ion technologies, the invention is not limited to specific quantum computing architectures and may be adapted to various qubit technologies and quantum processing systems.  

Conventional feature space discovery implementations in quantum machine learning suffer from several drawbacks. Many existing approaches rely on predefined feature maps derived from physical models, such as Ising-type Hamiltonians, which may not optimally represent the input data for a given task. Additionally, manual selection of feature maps and hyperparameters restricts the exploration of alternative model architectures, potentially limiting performance. The disclosed invention addresses these limitations by introducing an automated boosting procedure that systematically explores diverse feature maps and kernel configurations.  

The boosting procedure is central to the invention's operation. It begins by assigning uniform weights to all training samples and iteratively selects quantum kernel-based learners based on their performance metrics. In each iteration, the procedure identifies the top-performing quantum kernel from a predefined set, calculates its error rate, and determines a weight for the associated learner. The selected feature map is then excluded from subsequent iterations to encourage exploration of alternative Hilbert spaces. Sample weights are adjusted to emphasize previously misclassified data points, ensuring subsequent learners focus on challenging regions of the feature space.  

A significant advantage of the boosting procedure is its ability to dynamically adapt the ensemble composition. By combining learners with weighted votes, the system constructs a final model that leverages the strengths of multiple quantum kernels. This approach contrasts with traditional boosting methods that rely on weak learners, as the disclosed system utilizes stronger quantum kernel-based classifiers, enabling faster convergence and improved accuracy.  

The ensemble of quantum kernel-based learners generated by the system can be applied to various machine learning tasks, including binary classification, multiclass classification, and regression. The quantum kernels may implement different feature maps, such as Pauli feature maps with varying rotation factors, to capture diverse aspects of the input data. The ensemble's flexibility allows it to handle complex datasets where single learners may underperform, making it particularly suitable for applications requiring high predictive accuracy.  

The invention further encompasses computer processing systems, methods, apparatus, and program products for implementing the ensemble generation process. These embodiments provide technical improvements over conventional quantum machine learning models by automating feature map selection, optimizing hyperparameters, and enhancing generalization through ensemble methods. The system's ability to explore a broad range of quantum kernels without manual intervention reduces the expertise required to deploy effective quantum machine learning models, broadening their applicability.

The system defines boosting procedure parameters, such as the maximum number of iterations and early stopping conditions, and stores these parameters alongside the datasets in memory 116.  

The training component 202 handles the training of quantum kernel-based learners, executing quantum circuits to calculate kernel matrices and evaluate learner performance. It explores permutations of feature maps and Pauli rotation factors, identifying configurations that optimize predictive accuracy. The testing component 204 assesses learner performance on validation and testing datasets, generating performance metrics that guide the boosting procedure.  

The boosting procedure operates as follows: First, the system tests the performance of quantum kernel-based learners on the weighted training dataset, generating a performance metric for each learner. The top-performing quantum kernel and its associated learner are selected, and the selected feature map is excluded from subsequent iterations. Sample weights are adjusted to increase the emphasis on misclassified data points, and a weight value is assigned to the learner based on its error rate. This process repeats until termination conditions are met, such as achieving perfect classification, exceeding a maximum iteration count, or observing diminishing performance improvements.  

Upon termination, the top-performing learners are combined into a final ensemble, with each learner's contribution weighted according to its assigned weight value. The ensemble is then employed in quantum machine learning models to generate predictions, detect overfitting, and combine with other models as needed. The system generates reports detailing the ensemble composition, performance metrics, and feature map selections, providing transparency into the model's decision-making process.
