# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the fields of medicine, cell biology, molecular biology and genetics. More particularly, the invention pertains to methods and compositions for the diagnosis, prognosis, prevention and treatment of dengue virus infection through modulation of host response pathways, including the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, interferon-mediated pathways, and NF-κB-mediated cytokine/chemokine responses. The invention further provides novel biomarkers for dengue infection and therapeutic agents targeting specific components of these host response pathways.

## BACKGROUND

Dengue virus infection remains a significant global health challenge, with an estimated 50-100 million cases of dengue fever and 250,000-500,000 cases of severe dengue hemorrhagic fever/dengue shock syndrome occurring annually worldwide. Dengue virus, a member of the Flaviviridae family, exists as four distinct serotypes (DENV1-4) and is primarily transmitted by Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus mosquitoes. While infection with one serotype confers lifelong immunity to that specific serotype, it does not protect against infection by other serotypes, and secondary infections often result in more severe disease manifestations.

The current understanding of dengue pathogenesis remains incomplete, particularly regarding the molecular mechanisms underlying the progression from mild dengue fever to severe dengue hemorrhagic fever/dengue shock syndrome. The virus primarily infects Langerhans cells initially, subsequently spreading to other tissues including liver, spleen, kidney and blood through the lymphatic system. In these tissues, monocytes, macrophages and endothelial cells serve as major sites of viral replication.

Diagnosis of dengue infection currently relies on clinical presentation combined with laboratory tests including viral isolation, RT-PCR, and serological detection of IgM and IgG antibodies. However, these methods have significant limitations in terms of sensitivity, specificity, and ability to predict disease severity. Treatment options remain largely supportive, with no specific antiviral therapies currently available. The development of effective therapeutics has been hampered by the lack of suitable animal models and incomplete understanding of host-virus interactions.

The need for improved diagnostic methods capable of early detection and accurate prognosis of disease severity is particularly acute, as timely intervention can significantly reduce mortality in severe cases. Similarly, the development of targeted therapeutic agents that can modulate host response pathways to inhibit viral replication or mitigate pathological immune responses represents an urgent unmet medical need in dengue management.

### Dengue

Dengue refers to a spectrum of clinical manifestations caused by infection with dengue virus, ranging from mild dengue fever (DF) to severe dengue hemorrhagic fever/dengue shock syndrome (DHF/DSS). Dengue fever typically presents with high fever, headache, retro-orbital pain, myalgia, arthralgia, nausea, vomiting, and rash, lasting 5-7 days. Dengue hemorrhagic fever is characterized by thrombocytopenia, hemoconcentration, and plasma leakage, which may progress to hypovolemic shock in dengue shock syndrome.

Diagnosis of dengue infection currently relies on clinical criteria combined with laboratory confirmation through viral isolation, nucleic acid detection, or serological tests. However, these methods have limitations in early diagnosis and prediction of disease severity. Treatment remains supportive, focusing on fluid management and symptom relief, as no specific antiviral therapies are currently available.

Prevention of dengue primarily involves vector control measures targeting Aedes mosquitoes. Vaccine development has been challenging due to the need for tetravalent protection against all four serotypes and the risk of antibody-dependent enhancement in subsequent heterologous infections. Personal prevention methods include use of insect repellents, protective clothing, and mosquito nets.

### Ubiquitin-Proteasome Pathway

The ubiquitin-proteasome pathway is a critical cellular process for protein degradation and regulation. Ubiquitin, a 76-amino acid protein, is conjugated to target proteins through a cascade involving E1 ubiquitin-activating enzymes, E2 ubiquitin-conjugating enzymes, and E3 ubiquitin ligases. Polyubiquitinated proteins are recognized and degraded by the 26S proteasome complex.

The proteasome is a large multi-subunit protease complex comprising a 20S catalytic core and regulatory 19S particles. Proteasome inhibitors such as MG-132 and ALLN (N-acetyl-Leu-Leu-norleucinal) block proteolytic activity, preventing degradation of ubiquitinated proteins. These inhibitors belong to classes including tripeptide aldehydes, vinyl sulfones, and lactacystin derivatives.

The invention demonstrates that dengue virus infection upregulates specific components of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, including HERC5, HERC6, UBE2L6, and USP15. Inhibition of this pathway with MG-132 or ALLN significantly reduces viral replication, suggesting that the pathway plays a crucial role in the dengue life cycle. This discovery provides a novel target for antiviral intervention.

Interferon-related proteins such as viperin also interact with the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, forming part of the host antiviral response. The invention shows that modulation of this interaction can affect viral replication, providing additional targets for therapeutic development.

### Examples

The invention is illustrated by examples demonstrating inhibition of dengue replication through modulation of the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway. In one example, HepG2 cells infected with dengue virus showed significantly reduced viral replication when treated with MG-132 or ALLN, as measured by plaque assay and PCR.

Microarray analysis of infected HepG2 cells revealed upregulation of ubiquitin-proteasome pathway genes, with confirmation by TaqMan PCR in both HepG2 and A549 cell lines. Patient samples from dengue fever cases showed similar gene expression patterns, validating the cell culture findings.

Concentration-dependent effects of proteasome inhibitors were demonstrated, with MG-132 and ALLN showing significant antiviral activity at non-cytotoxic concentrations. Cytotoxicity was assessed using FDA staining, with results showing selective inhibition of viral replication at optimal doses.

Analysis of ubiquitin-proteasome pathway genes in dengue-infected patients confirmed pathway activation in clinical cases. Microarray approaches identified candidate genes whose activation was verified by TaqMan PCR. Cell culture and virus culture methods were optimized to establish reproducible infection systems.

Additional methods included selection of in vitro infection systems, generation of A549 cells stably expressing viperin, chemokine assays, flow cytometry, and analysis of patient samples. Fluorescence labeling, RNA reverse transcription, cDNA hybridization, and array scanning with image analysis provided comprehensive gene expression data.

Quality checks and lowess normalization ensured data reliability, with technical repeats showing high correlation. Differentially expressed genes were selected using Significance Analysis of Microarrays (SAM), incorporating permutation tests to calculate false discovery rates.

TaqMan low density array analysis validated microarray findings, with cell and strain selection based on viral titer production. Highest yielding human cell lines were selected for detailed expression analysis using FACS and PCR/microarray techniques.

Pathway detection using Panther software identified relevant biological processes, with validation through MetaCore program mapping. Up-regulated genes common to all three systems (two cell lines and patients) were identified as particularly relevant targets.

Analysis of cytokines/mediators focused on IP-10 and I-TAC as key biomarkers. Measurement of their production demonstrated correlation with disease parameters. Studies of NF-κB effects on virus replication and interferon/viperin interactions provided mechanistic insights.

The ubiquitination process and effects of MG-132 and ALLN inhibitors were characterized, showing dose-dependent inhibition of viral replication with minimal cytotoxicity at effective concentrations. These findings support the therapeutic potential of proteasome inhibition in dengue infection.
