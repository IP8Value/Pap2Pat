# DESCRIPTION  

The present invention introduces novel retro-inverso (RI) peptides designed for the treatment of cancer. These peptides are engineered to inhibit key processes involved in tumor metastasis, including cell migration, invasion, and angiogenesis. The RI peptides exhibit enhanced stability in biological fluids and demonstrate potent inhibitory effects on the interaction between the urokinase receptor (uPAR) and Formyl Peptide Receptor 1 (FPR1), a critical pathway in cancer progression.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, with metastatic spread being the primary contributor to patient death. Current therapeutic strategies often fail to address the complex mechanisms underlying metastasis, particularly the deregulation of cell motility. Cell migration is essential for physiological processes such as tissue repair and immune responses, but its dysregulation drives tumor dissemination.  

The urokinase receptor (uPAR) is a well-established prognostic marker in cancer, associated with poor outcomes due to its role in promoting cell-associated proteolysis and intracellular signaling. uPAR interacts with multiple co-receptors, including the G protein-coupled Formyl Peptide Receptors (FPRs), particularly FPR1, which is implicated in tumor progression. Inhibition of the uPAR-FPR1 interaction represents a promising therapeutic strategy, but prior peptide-based inhibitors have faced limitations such as enzymatic instability and off-target effects.  

The retro-inverso (RI) modification, wherein the peptide sequence is inverted and composed of D-amino acids, offers a solution to these challenges. RI peptides retain the side-chain topology of their parent L-peptides while exhibiting resistance to proteolysis. However, the success of this approach depends on the preservation of biological activity despite the inversion of the peptide backbone.  

Prior art peptides, including linear and cyclized variants, have demonstrated partial efficacy but suffer from rapid degradation or toxicity. There remains an unmet need for stable, potent inhibitors of the uPAR-FPR1 interaction capable of preventing metastasis without adverse effects. The present invention addresses this need by providing RI peptides with optimized structural and functional properties.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention encompasses retro-inverso peptides capable of inhibiting the uPAR-FPR1 interaction, thereby preventing cell migration, invasion, and angiogenesis. These peptides are defined by the general formula:  

**Ac-(D)-X1-(D)-X2-(D)-X3-(D)-X4-NH2**  

where X1 is an aromatic residue (e.g., Tyr), X2 and X4 are basic residues (e.g., Arg), and X3 is a polar or constrained residue (e.g., Glu, Ser, or Aib). Preferred embodiments include RI-1 (Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Glu-(D)-Arg-NH2), RI-2 (Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Ser-(D)-Arg-NH2), and RI-3 (Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-Aib-(D)-Arg-NH2).  

The peptides are synthesized using solid-phase Fmoc chemistry, purified by reversed-phase HPLC, and characterized by mass spectrometry. They exhibit nanomolar potency in inhibiting FPR1 activation, cell migration, and matrigel invasion. Notably, RI-3 demonstrates exceptional stability in human serum and lacks cytotoxicity, making it suitable for therapeutic applications.  

The peptides function by competitively blocking agonist binding to FPR1, preventing receptor internalization, and disrupting downstream signaling pathways such as PI3K/AKT and MAPK. Additionally, they inhibit endothelial tube formation, a critical step in tumor angiogenesis, and reduce trans-endothelial migration of cancer cells.  

Therapeutic applications include the treatment of solid tumors (e.g., sarcoma, melanoma, ovarian carcinoma) and disorders involving pathological angiogenesis (e.g., diabetic retinopathy). The peptides may also serve as theranostic agents, enabling simultaneous diagnosis and treatment of metastatic disease.  

## EXAMPLE 1. SYNTHESIS OF THE RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES RI-1, RI-2, RI-3, RI-4, RI-5  

**RI-1 Synthesis**: The peptide Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Glu-(D)-Arg-NH2 was assembled on Rink amide resin using Fmoc-protected D-amino acids. After chain assembly, the peptide was cleaved with TFA, purified by HPLC (C18 column, water/acetonitrile gradient), and confirmed by mass spectrometry (MH+ = 664.4 Da).  

**RI-2 Synthesis**: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-(D)-Ser-(D)-Arg-NH2 was synthesized analogously, with final purification yielding a product with MH+ = 620.4 Da.  

**RI-3 Synthesis**: Ac-(D)-Tyr-(D)-Arg-Aib-(D)-Arg-NH2 incorporated the non-natural amino acid Aib (α-aminoisobutyric acid) to stabilize a β-turn conformation. The product (MH+ = 622.3 Da) exhibited superior conformational stability in molecular dynamics simulations.  

**RI-4 and RI-5 Synthesis**: These tripeptides (Phenyl-Acetyl-D-Arg-Aib-D-Arg-NH2 and 3-Phenyl-Propionyl-D-Arg-Aib-D-Arg-NH2) were designed to eliminate a labile amide bond. Purified products showed MH+ = 533.5 Da and 547.5 Da, respectively.  

## EXAMPLE 2. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES DO NOT PROMOTE CELL MIGRATION  

HT1080 fibrosarcoma cells were exposed to 10 nM fMLF (positive control) or RI peptides in an xCELLigence migration assay. While fMLF induced significant migration (cell index increase of 2.5 ± 0.3 over 18 hr), RI-1 to RI-5 elicited no response (cell index change < 0.1), confirming their lack of agonist activity.  

## EXAMPLE 3. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 INTERFERES WITH FPR1 BIOLOGICAL ACTIVITY  

RBL-2H3/ETFR cells (FPR1-expressing) were pre-treated with 100 nM RI-3, then exposed to FITC-fMLF. RI-3 reduced FITC-fMLF binding by 85% (vs. 90% for unlabeled fMLF), demonstrating competitive inhibition. Confocal microscopy confirmed RI-3 prevented FPR1 internalization induced by fMLF.  

## EXAMPLE 4. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES INHIBIT CELL MIGRATION IN A DOSE-DEPENDENT MANNER  

HT1080 cells migrating toward 10% FBS were inhibited by RI-3 with an IC50 of 100 fM. Maximal inhibition (57%) occurred at 10 nM, comparable to the parent peptide RERF.  

## EXAMPLE 5. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDES INHIBIT CELL INVASION IN A DOSE-DEPENDENT MANNER  

Matrigel invasion assays revealed RI-3 inhibited HT1080 cell invasion with an IC50 of 10 pM (51% inhibition at 10 nM). RI-1 and RI-2 showed weaker effects (27% and 23% inhibition, respectively).  

## EXAMPLE 6. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 DOES NOT AFFECT CELL PROLIFERATION  

HT1080 cells cultured with 10 µM RI-3 for 96 hr showed no difference in proliferation vs. controls (absorbance at 490 nm: 1.2 ± 0.1 vs. 1.3 ± 0.1, p > 0.05).  

## EXAMPLE 7. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 IS STABLE IN HUMAN SERUM  

RI-3 pre-incubated in human serum (18 hr, 37°C) retained 81% inhibitory activity in matrigel invasion assays, confirming protease resistance.  

### EXAMPLE 8. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 PREVENTS MATRIGEL INVASION BY HUMAN SARCOMA, MELANOMA, AND EPITHELIAL OVARIAN CARCINOMA CELLS  

RI-3 (10 nM) reduced invasion by HT1080 fibrosarcoma, Saos-2 osteosarcoma, and Sarc chondrosarcoma cells by 64%, 24%, and 47%, respectively. Efficacy correlated with uPAR/FPR1 expression levels.  

### EXAMPLE 9. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 PREVENTS IN VITRO TRANS-ENDOTHELIAL MIGRATION  

RI-3 reduced trans-endothelial migration of Sarc cells by 24% (p < 0.01) in real-time impedance assays.  

### EXAMPLE 10. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 INHIBITS ENDOTHELIAL TUBE FORMATION  

HUVECs treated with RI-3 (10 nM) showed 61% and 75% reduction in FBS- and VEGF-induced tube formation, respectively.  

### EXAMPLE 11. RETRO-INVERSO PEPTIDE RI-3 EXERTS ANTI-METASTATIC EFFECT IN VIVO  

Nude mice injected with Sarc cells and treated with RI-3 (6 mg/kg/day, 10 days) exhibited:  
- **Tumor volume**: 185 ± 82 mm³ vs. 330 ± 163 mm³ in controls (p < 0.05).  
- **Microvessel density**: 12 ± 3 vessels/field vs. 25 ± 5 in controls (p < 0.001).  
- **Circulating tumor cells**: 2.6 ± 2.7 CTCs/mL vs. 10.04 ± 3.7 in controls (p < 0.01).  

Lung metastases were absent in RI-3-treated mice but present in 3/5 controls.
