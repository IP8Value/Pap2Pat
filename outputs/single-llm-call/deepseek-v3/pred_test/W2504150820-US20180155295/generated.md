## FIELD  

The present invention relates to novel small molecule modulators of the CXCR4 chemokine receptor. More particularly, the invention provides compounds that act as agonists or antagonists of CXCR4 receptor activity, pharmaceutical compositions containing these compounds, and methods of using these compounds for therapeutic purposes.  

## BACKGROUND  

CXCR4 is a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) that binds the chemokine CXCL12 (stromal cell-derived factor-1, SDF-1). CXCR4 plays crucial physiological roles in hematopoiesis, immune function, and embryonic development. The CXCR4/CXCL12 axis mediates stem cell homing to bone marrow and regulates lymphocyte trafficking.  

Currently, only one CXCR4-targeting drug (AMD3100, plerixafor) is approved for clinical use. AMD3100 is a small molecule antagonist used to mobilize hematopoietic stem cells for transplantation. However, there remains an unmet need for additional CXCR4-acting agents with improved pharmacological properties and novel mechanisms of action.  

Chemokine signaling through CXCR4 involves complex downstream pathways including calcium mobilization, ERK phosphorylation, and receptor internalization. CXCR4 plays important roles in cancer metastasis, inflammation, and HIV infection, making it an attractive therapeutic target. Previous approaches to CXCR4 antagonist discovery have focused on peptide mimetics and small molecules targeting the orthosteric binding site.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides novel small molecule modulators of the CXCR4 chemokine receptor. While CXCR4 is an important therapeutic target, developing small molecule agonists has proven particularly challenging due to the receptor's large binding pocket and complex activation mechanisms.  

Through extensive mutagenesis and crystal structure studies, key residues involved in CXCR4 ligand binding have been identified. The inventors employed a dual in silico screening strategy combining ligand-based pharmacophore modeling with structure-based virtual screening to identify novel CXCR4 modulators.  

Initial screening identified several compounds with CXCR4 modulating activity. Calcium imaging assays revealed both agonist (e.g., NUCC-390) and antagonist (e.g., NUCC-388) compounds. The agonist NUCC-390 demonstrated biological effects similar to the native ligand SDF-1, including ERK activation, receptor internalization, and induction of chemotaxis.

### EXPERIMENTAL  

### Annotated Database Creation  

An annotated database was created from a GPCR-focused library containing approximately 13,000 compounds. Low energy conformers were generated using computational methods with an energy cutoff of 10 kcal/mol.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

CXCR4 antagonists were selected from chemical databases and used to build a common-feature pharmacophore model. This model was used to screen the annotated compound database, identifying 26 potential hits with high fit values.  

### EXAMPLE 3  

CXCR4 crystal structures were analyzed and prepared for docking studies. Structure-based virtual screening was performed using two different docking algorithms, identifying 52 potential hits from the GPCR-focused library.  

### EXAMPLE 4  

Known CXCR4 antagonists were docked to validate the virtual screening approach. Analysis of docked poses identified critical interacting residues. Subsequent docking of the GPCR library identified 22 compounds with favorable binding characteristics.  

### EXAMPLE 5  

Compounds were tested in calcium imaging assays to assess biological activity. Several compounds showed significant CXCR4 modulating activity, with NUCC-390 demonstrating agonist effects and NUCC-388 showing antagonist activity.  

### EXAMPLE 6  

The agonist NUCC-390 was shown to activate ERK phosphorylation, a key downstream signaling pathway of CXCR4 activation.  

### EXAMPLE 7  

NUCC-390 induced internalization of CXCR4 receptors, similar to the effects of SDF-1. Both NUCC-390 and SDF-1 stimulated chemotaxis in cell migration assays.
