# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cancer immunotherapy and specifically to methods of modulating macrophage polarization through targeting of integrin CD11b signaling pathways. More particularly, the invention provides compositions and methods for treating cancer by agonizing CD11b to promote pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization, inhibit immune suppression, modulate tumor vascularization, and enhance anti-tumor immune responses.  

### Background Information  

Myeloid cells, including macrophages, monocytes, and neutrophils, play critical roles in both acute/chronic inflammation and tumor progression. While pro-inflammatory myeloid cells stimulate cytotoxic T cell responses that suppress tumor growth, immune suppressive myeloid cells promote tumor progression through angiogenesis and immune suppression. Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are particularly abundant in the tumor microenvironment and exhibit primarily immunosuppressive phenotypes that facilitate cancer progression.  

Macrophage polarization is regulated by several signaling pathways, including those mediated by integrins, CSF1R, PI3Kγ, and BTK. While integrin α4β1 has been shown to promote myeloid cell trafficking into tumors and subsequent tumor progression, the role of integrin CD11b/CD18 (αMβ2) in tumor immunity has remained unclear. CD11b/CD18 is known to mediate macrophage adhesion, migration, and accumulation during inflammation through interactions with ligands such as ICAM-1 and fibrinogen.  

Current cancer treatments targeting myeloid cells focus primarily on inhibitors of pathways like CSF1R, PI3Kγ, and BTK. However, there remains an unmet need for novel therapeutic approaches that can effectively repolarize immunosuppressive TAMs toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes while simultaneously modulating tumor vascularization and enhancing anti-tumor immunity. The present invention addresses this need by identifying CD11b as a critical regulator of macrophage polarization through control of miRNA Let7a and c-Myc expression, providing new methods for treating cancer through CD11b agonism.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for treating cancer by modulating CD11b signaling in myeloid cells. Activation of CD11b signaling inhibits immune suppressive macrophage polarization, promotes pro-inflammatory transcriptional programs, enhances CD8+ T cell recruitment to tumors, and suppresses tumor growth. Loss of CD11b expression or function promotes immune suppressive gene expression in macrophages both in vitro and in vivo, increases regulatory T cell infiltration, decreases CD8+ T cell recruitment, and accelerates tumor progression.  

The invention specifically discloses that CD11b ligation promotes miRNA Let7a expression while inhibiting c-Myc expression, thereby controlling macrophage polarization and tumor immune responses. Decreased CD11b signaling downregulates Let7a, leading to elevated IL-6 levels and increased STAT3 activation that drives immune suppressive cytokine production. The small molecule CD11b agonist Leukadherin-1 (LA1) has been shown to repolarize TAMs, increase CD8+ T cell recruitment, and potently inhibit tumor progression in multiple cancer models.  

The methods of treatment encompass administering effective amounts of CD11b agonists, including but not limited to LA1, alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents such as chemotherapeutics, radiation therapy, or immunotherapies. The invention further provides methods for screening potential therapeutic agents that modulate CD11b signaling and macrophage polarization.  

The compositions and methods are effective against various cancer types, including but not limited to lung carcinoma, melanoma, and breast cancer. The invention also provides transgenic non-human mammals with constitutively active CD11b (I332G mutation) that exhibit reduced tumor growth, serving as valuable models for studying CD11b-mediated anti-tumor effects.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention is based on the discovery that integrin CD11b serves as a critical regulator of macrophage polarization and tumor immunity through control of miRNA Let7a and c-Myc expression. CD11b activation promotes pro-inflammatory macrophage phenotypes while inhibiting immune suppressive polarization, thereby creating a tumor microenvironment more conducive to anti-tumor immune responses.  

CD11b signaling in myeloid cells inhibits immune suppression by restraining IL-6 production and STAT3 activation. The mechanism involves CD11b-mediated promotion of miRNA Let7a expression, which in turn suppresses c-Myc. Loss of CD11b signaling leads to decreased Let7a, increased c-Myc expression, elevated IL-6 production, and consequent immune suppressive macrophage polarization. This pathway represents a novel target for cancer immunotherapy.  

The invention further demonstrates that CD11b modulates tumor neovascularization by controlling PDGF-BB expression through the Let7a pathway. CD11b-deficient macrophages exhibit increased PDGF-BB production, leading to more mature, pericyte-coated blood vessels in tumors. While this vascular normalization promotes tumor growth, it also increases susceptibility to chemotherapy due to improved drug delivery.  

The small molecule CD11b agonist LA1 has been shown to effectively repolarize TAMs toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes, increase CD8+ T cell infiltration, and inhibit tumor growth in multiple models including lung carcinoma, melanoma, and breast cancer. LA1 treatment reduces expression of immune suppressive markers (Arg1, S100A8, MMP9) while increasing MHC-II expression in TAMs. Importantly, LA1's anti-tumor effects are CD11b-dependent, as they are abolished in Itgam-/- mice.  

The invention provides methods of treating cancer comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of a CD11b agonist, optionally in combination with other therapeutic agents. The CD11b agonist may be LA1 or other small molecules, antibodies, or biologics that activate CD11b signaling. Combination therapies may include chemotherapeutic agents (e.g., gemcitabine, taxol), radiation therapy, or immunotherapies (e.g., checkpoint inhibitors).  

The invention also encompasses methods of screening for therapeutic agents that modulate CD11b-mediated macrophage polarization. Such methods may involve assessing candidate compounds for their ability to increase Let7a expression, decrease c-Myc expression, or alter macrophage cytokine profiles toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes.  

Transgenic non-human mammals with constitutively active CD11b (I332G mutation) are provided as part of the invention. These animals exhibit reduced tumor growth and serve as valuable models for studying CD11b-mediated anti-tumor effects. Methods of making and using such transgenic animals are included in the invention.  

The compositions and methods are applicable to various cancer types where modulation of macrophage polarization and tumor immunity would be beneficial. Specific embodiments include treatment of lung carcinoma, melanoma, breast cancer, and other solid tumors characterized by significant myeloid cell infiltration.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

Cell lines including C57BL/6 LLC, B16 melanoma, CL66 breast and MDA-MB-231 breast tumor cells were obtained from ATCC and cultured in appropriate media. Authentication was performed by tumor histology and RNA/DNA sequencing.  

Wild-type C57BL/6 and CD11b-deficient (Itgam-/-) mice were used for tumor studies. The ITGAM I332G knock-in mouse was generated by replacing exon 9 of the ITGAM gene to substitute Ile332 with Gly. Heterozygous mutant mice were bred to obtain homozygous I332G mice that were phenotypically normal but exhibited constitutively active CD11b.  

For tumor studies, 5×10^5 LLC or B16 cells were injected subcutaneously into syngeneic WT or Itgam-/- mice. Tumor volumes were measured regularly and calculated using the formula v = (l^2 × w)/2. CL66-luc cells were inoculated orthotopically in mammary fat pads for breast cancer models.  

LA1 was administered intraperitoneally at 2-6 mg/kg daily, alone or in combination with taxol (2.5 mg/kg every other day) or radiation therapy (20 Gy). Tumor growth was monitored until endpoint, at which point tumors were harvested for histological and flow cytometric analysis.  

Immunohistochemistry was performed on tumor sections using antibodies against F4/80, CD4, CD8, CD31, desmin, and smooth muscle actin. Immunofluorescence images were analyzed using Metamorph software. Flow cytometry was used to analyze immune cell populations in tumors and blood using antibodies against various surface markers.  

Bone marrow-derived macrophages were cultured and polarized with cytokines (IFNγ/LPS or IL-4) with or without inhibitors of Stat3 or cMyc. Gene expression was analyzed by qPCR, and protein expression by immunoblotting. CD11b ligation experiments were performed using ICAM-1 or VCAM-1 coated plates with or without anti-CD11b antibodies.  

For adoptive transfer experiments, F4/80+ cells from tumors or bone marrow were isolated, treated with LA1 or vehicle, and co-injected with tumor cells into recipient mice. Tumor growth was monitored for 14 days post-inoculation.  

Statistical analysis was performed using appropriate tests (t-tests, ANOVA) with significance set at p<0.05. Sample sizes were chosen to provide 80% power to detect meaningful differences between groups. All mouse studies were randomized and blinded.  

The results demonstrated that LA1 treatment significantly inhibited tumor growth in multiple models, with effects dependent on intact CD11b expression. LA1 repolarized TAMs toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes, increased CD8+ T cell infiltration, and altered tumor vascularization. These findings support the use of CD11b agonists as novel cancer therapeutics.
