# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of biomedicine and artificial intelligence. More specifically, the invention pertains to a novel deep learning-based system for rapid and accurate high-throughput screening of nanoformulated drugs. The system integrates advanced computer vision techniques with convolutional neural networks to analyze single-cell images obtained from flow cytometry, enabling precise evaluation of drug efficacy without the limitations of traditional screening methods. By leveraging deep learning algorithms, the invention provides a transformative approach to pharmaceutical research that overcomes longstanding challenges in nanomedicine development, including interference from nanoparticle physicochemical properties and subjective manual analysis.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The development of new pharmaceutical compounds remains a critical challenge in modern medicine, particularly for cancer treatment where therapeutic efficacy must be balanced against toxicity. Traditional drug development approaches rely heavily on high-throughput screening technologies that test thousands of compounds against biological targets. These conventional screening methods, while valuable, face significant limitations when applied to nanoformulated drugs - pharmaceutical compounds encapsulated within nanoscale carriers that improve drug delivery and efficacy.  

High-throughput screening technology typically employs assays such as 3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) or lactate dehydrogenase release to measure cellular responses. However, these colorimetric and absorbance-based methods suffer from substantial interference when testing nanoformulated drugs due to nanoparticles' intrinsic optical properties, high surface energy, and unique physicochemical characteristics. The interference manifests as false signals that obscure true drug efficacy measurements, rendering traditional screening methods unreliable for nanomedicine evaluation.  

The application of computer technology to drug screening has emerged as a promising alternative, particularly through virtual screening systems that predict drug properties computationally. These systems typically employ machine learning algorithms trained on molecular structure databases to prioritize compounds for further testing. While virtual screening reduces experimental costs, current implementations remain limited by their dependence on manually curated structural features and finite chemical databases. These constraints prevent accurate evaluation of nanoformulated drugs where carrier-drug interactions create complex behaviors not captured by existing datasets.  

Machine learning approaches in drug discovery have demonstrated potential to identify subtle cellular changes undetectable by human observation. Recent studies show these algorithms can predict cellular responses from microscopic images by recognizing patterns imperceptible to conventional analysis. However, traditional machine learning methods require extensive feature engineering and struggle with the high-dimensional data from biological imaging. The need for a new drug screening system capable of handling nanoformulated compounds while maintaining high accuracy and throughput has become increasingly urgent.  

Deep learning represents a significant advancement in machine learning, particularly for image analysis tasks. Unlike conventional algorithms, deep neural networks automatically learn hierarchical feature representations from raw data, eliminating the need for manual feature engineering. Chinese Patent Application No. 2017101273955 describes a deep learning-based system for virtual drug screening, but focuses primarily on molecular structure analysis rather than cellular response evaluation. The present invention addresses this gap by developing a specialized deep learning architecture for processing single-cell images to assess nanoformulated drug efficacy directly from biological samples.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention introduces a deep learning-based drug screening system called DeepScreen that revolutionizes nanoformulated drug evaluation. The system comprises several integrated modules that transform raw single-cell images into precise drug efficacy classifications. A picture preprocessing module initially processes flow cytometry images to prepare them for analysis. This module performs critical operations including noise reduction, contrast enhancement, and artifact removal to optimize image quality for subsequent processing.  

Following preprocessing, a channel merging module combines multiple fluorescence channels from the original images into unified multi-channel representations. This module intelligently fuses bright-field, Annexin V-APC, and optional marker channels to create comprehensive cellular profiles. The merged channels undergo normalization in a picture standardization module that ensures consistent intensity ranges and dimensional uniformity across all samples. This standardization enables reliable comparison between different experimental batches and conditions.  

The core of the invention resides in its neural network module, which implements a sophisticated convolutional neural network architecture specifically designed for single-cell image analysis. The network structure incorporates 46 layers arranged in an optimized "network-in-network" configuration with parallel processing branches. This architecture combines the advantages of deep feature extraction with efficient parameter utilization, enabling high accuracy while minimizing overfitting. The network includes specialized layers for spatial feature detection, nonlinear activation, and hierarchical pattern recognition tailored to cellular morphology changes induced by drug treatments.  

Training of the neural network employs advanced methodologies including balanced mini-batch sampling, adaptive moment estimation optimization, and comprehensive regularization strategies. The system trains on labeled datasets comprising thousands of single-cell images classified by drug efficacy levels. During operation, the trained model processes new cell images through its picture preprocessing module and neural network module to generate efficacy predictions with demonstrated accuracy exceeding 0.9 in validation testing.  

The method of deep learning-based drug screening implemented by the invention offers multiple advantages over conventional approaches. The picture preprocessing module enables robust analysis despite variations in image quality, while the neural network module's depth and architecture provide unprecedented sensitivity to subtle cellular changes. Compared to traditional screening requiring days of incubation, the invention achieves reliable results within hours by detecting early cellular responses. Furthermore, the system maintains high accuracy across different nanoparticle formulations and cell types, demonstrating remarkable generalization capability.  

In summary, the invention represents a significant advancement in pharmaceutical screening technology by combining state-of-the-art deep learning with innovative image processing to solve longstanding challenges in nanoformulated drug evaluation. The system's speed, accuracy, and reliability position it as a transformative tool for accelerating nanomedicine development and personalized therapy optimization.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT  

The construction process of the drug screening system model involves several carefully designed stages to ensure optimal performance. Initially, the system acquires single-cell images from flow cytometry experiments where cells have been treated with various nanoformulated drugs and control compounds. These images capture multiple fluorescence channels reflecting different cellular components and states, providing comprehensive data for the deep learning analysis.  

### Example 1 A Deep Learning-Based Quick and Precise High-Throughput Drug Screening System  

The construction process begins with preparation of training data through controlled experiments using established cancer cell lines. A549 human pulmonary adenocarcinoma cells and HEpG2 human hepatocellular carcinoma cells serve as model systems, treated with concentrations of nanoformulated drugs corresponding to known efficacy levels. The picture preprocessing module then processes these raw images through sequential operations including background subtraction, intensity normalization, and geometric alignment.  

The channel merging module subsequently combines the preprocessed single-channel images into multi-channel composites. For basic operation, the system merges bright-field and Annexin V-APC channels, while optional configurations can incorporate additional markers like anti-EGFR-FITC for enhanced specificity. The picture standardization module then resizes all images to uniform 70×70 pixel dimensions using bicubic interpolation and applies z-score normalization to intensity values across the dataset.  

The neural network module implements a 46-layer convolutional architecture inspired by Google Inception networks but specifically optimized for single-cell analysis. The network structure features parallel processing paths with varied convolution kernel sizes (1×1, 3×3, 5×5) to capture features at multiple scales. Between convolutional blocks, the architecture incorporates batch normalization layers and rectified linear unit activations to maintain stable gradient flow during training.  

Training methodology employs the Adam optimizer with carefully tuned hyperparameters including a learning rate of 0.001 and mini-batch size of 64. The system implements balanced sampling to ensure equal representation of all efficacy classes during training, coupled with aggressive regularization including dropout (rate=0.5) and L2 weight decay (λ=0.01). Training proceeds for approximately 50 epochs until validation accuracy stabilizes.  

The classification model organizes drug responses into four efficacy categories: ineffective (IE), low efficacy (LE), medium efficacy (ME), and high efficacy (HE). Training data consists of 103,568 A549 cell images and 115,968 HEpG2 cell images collected from independent experiments, with additional 25,892 and 28,992 images respectively reserved for testing. The model testing process evaluates performance on completely unseen data using standard metrics including accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score.  

Model accuracy reaches 0.851 for mixed cell populations, 0.864 for HEpG2 cells, and 0.908 for A549 cells in basic two-channel configuration. Incorporating optional anti-EGFR-FITC channel boosts accuracy to 0.966 for A549 cells, while simplified single-channel operation maintains acceptable performance at 0.705 accuracy. These results demonstrate the DeepScreen model's flexibility to accommodate different experimental setups while maintaining robust performance.  

The advantages of the DeepScreen model include its rapid analysis capability, achieving reliable results within hours compared to days required by traditional methods. The system shows particular strength in evaluating early-stage drug effects, detecting subtle cellular changes missed by conventional assays. Furthermore, the model demonstrates remarkable resistance to interference from nanoparticle properties and drug fluorescence, maintaining consistent accuracy across diverse nanoformulations. These characteristics position DeepScreen as a transformative tool for accelerating nanomedicine development and personalized therapy optimization.
