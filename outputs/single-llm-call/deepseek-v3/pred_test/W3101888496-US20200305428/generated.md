# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to agricultural pathogen control, specifically to novel compounds and methods for enhancing plant immunity against pathogenic organisms including bacteria, fungi, and nematodes. More particularly, the invention provides compositions and methods for inhibiting cinnamate-4-hydroxylase (C4H) activity in plants to induce defense responses against plant parasitic nematodes and other pathogens. The invention further relates to formulations containing these compounds and their application methods for protecting crops from infection and disease.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Plant pathogens cause significant economic losses in agriculture worldwide. These pathogens include bacteria, fungi, and nematodes that infect various plant species, leading to reduced yields and crop quality. Bacterial pathogens such as Pseudomonas syringae and Ralstonia solanacearum cause devastating diseases in many crops. Fungal pathogens like Fusarium, Verticillium, and Pythium species are responsible for wilts, rots, and other destructive conditions. Nematodes, particularly plant parasitic nematodes, represent a major threat to global food security.

Plant parasitic nematodes are microscopic roundworms that feed on plant roots and other tissues. These nematodes include sedentary endoparasites such as root-knot nematodes (Meloidogyne spp.) and cyst nematodes (Heterodera and Globodera spp.), as well as migratory endoparasites like Pratylenchus species. These pathogens establish complex feeding relationships with host plants, forming specialized feeding sites that divert plant nutrients and disrupt normal physiological processes.

The life cycles of plant parasitic nematodes typically involve multiple developmental stages. For root-knot nematodes, the infective second-stage juvenile (J2) penetrates plant roots and establishes permanent feeding sites called giant cells. The nematode then develops through subsequent juvenile stages to adulthood while inducing gall formation on roots. Cyst nematodes similarly form syncytial feeding sites, while migratory endoparasites move through root tissues causing extensive damage.

Plant disease symptoms caused by nematodes include stunting, yellowing, wilting, and reduced yield. Above-ground symptoms often reflect the root damage occurring below ground. Nematode infection also predisposes plants to secondary infections by other pathogens, compounding yield losses.

Current control strategies for plant pathogens include chemical pesticides, cultural practices, biological control agents, and resistant cultivars. However, these methods have significant limitations. Chemical nematicides often have broad-spectrum activity that harms beneficial soil organisms and raises environmental concerns. Many traditional nematicides have been banned or restricted due to toxicity issues. Cultural control methods like crop rotation are limited by the wide host ranges of many pathogens. Biological control agents typically provide inconsistent results under field conditions. While resistant cultivars represent an important tool, pathogen populations can evolve to overcome resistance, and resistant varieties are not available for all crops.

There remains an urgent need for novel approaches to control plant pathogens that are effective, environmentally sustainable, and compatible with integrated pest management systems. The present invention addresses these needs by providing compounds that enhance plant innate immunity against multiple classes of pathogens.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compounds and methods for enhancing plant immunity against pathogenic organisms. The invention is based on the discovery that inhibition of cinnamate-4-hydroxylase (C4H), a key enzyme in the phenylpropanoid pathway, induces defense responses in plants that confer resistance to nematodes and other pathogens.

The invention encompasses small molecule inhibitors of C4H activity, including aromatic carboxylic acids and benzoic acid derivatives. These compounds include benzodioxole structures comprising carboxylic acid groups that effectively inhibit C4H enzyme function. The compounds are represented by general formulas I, II, and III as described herein, including stereoisomers, tautomers, hydrates, salts, and solvates thereof.

The compounds of the invention are useful for preventing infection by plant pathogens and increasing plant resistance to established infections. When applied exogenously to plants, these compounds induce accumulation of trans-cinnamic acid conjugates and compensatory upregulation of C4H gene expression, leading to enhanced defense responses.

The invention further provides compositions and formulations containing the active compounds, including agrochemical acceptable salts and esters. These compositions may include surfactants, adjuvants, and other agriculturally acceptable additives to facilitate application and enhance efficacy.

Methods of using the compounds to control plant pathogens are also provided. The compounds exhibit activity against plant parasitic nematodes, phytopathogenic fungi, and bacteria. Application methods include foliar sprays, soil drenches, seed treatments, and other standard agricultural application techniques.

The invention provides significant advantages over existing pathogen control methods. The compounds act by enhancing plant innate immunity rather than through direct toxicity to pathogens, reducing environmental impact. The compounds are effective against multiple pathogen classes, providing broad-spectrum protection. Furthermore, the mode of action makes development of pathogen resistance less likely compared to conventional pesticides.

Plant defense and immune responses involve complex biochemical pathways that recognize pathogens and mount protective reactions. The phenylpropanoid pathway plays a central role in plant defense by producing phenolic compounds involved in resistance mechanisms. The present invention modulates this pathway through targeted inhibition of cinnamate-4-hydroxylase (C4H), leading to enhanced immunity.

The compounds of the invention affect plant growth and development in specific ways. While not phytotoxic at recommended application rates, they may cause temporary inhibition of primary root elongation and impair lignification processes during early treatment periods. These effects are transient and followed by compensatory growth responses.

The compounds exhibit direct and indirect effects on plant pathogens. While some compounds show modest direct toxicity to nematodes and fungi at high concentrations, their primary mode of action involves induction of plant defense responses that inhibit pathogen establishment and reproduction.

Cinnamate-4-hydroxylase (C4H) is a cytochrome P450 monooxygenase that catalyzes the conversion of trans-cinnamic acid to p-coumaric acid in the phenylpropanoid pathway. This enzyme occupies a key position at the branch point between primary and secondary metabolism in plants. Inhibition of C4H leads to accumulation of trans-cinnamic acid and its conjugates while reducing flux through downstream branches of the pathway.

The role of C4H in plant defense is complex. While complete genetic knockout of C4H is lethal due to its essential role in lignin biosynthesis, partial inhibition induces defense gene expression and metabolite accumulation. The unexpected effects of C4H inhibition include upregulation of pathogenesis-related proteins and activation of salicylic acid-mediated defense pathways.

The invention provides a method of enhancing plant immunity through controlled inhibition of C4H activity. A C4H inhibitor, as defined herein, is a compound that reduces the enzymatic conversion of trans-cinnamic acid to p-coumaric acid by at least 20% when applied at concentrations between 1 μM and 1 mM.

Techniques for measuring C4H activity are well established in the art. These include in vitro assays using microsomal preparations and recombinant enzyme, as well as in planta measurements of trans-cinnamic acid conversion rates. The invention employs these techniques to quantify inhibitor efficacy and optimize application protocols.

Inhibition of C4H enzyme activity leads to measurable biochemical changes in treated plants. These include accumulation of trans-cinnamic acid conjugates such as cinnamoyl glucose and compensatory upregulation of C4H gene expression. These changes are associated with enhanced disease resistance.

The effects of C4H inhibition can be assessed using phenotypic assays. A primary root elongation assay measures the transient growth inhibition caused by effective C4H inhibitors. A lignification impairment assay evaluates the temporary reduction in lignin deposition following treatment. Both assays provide quantitative measures of inhibitor activity in planta.

In planta assays demonstrate the protective effects of C4H inhibitors against pathogens. These include nematode infection assays, fungal pathogenicity tests, and bacterial challenge experiments. The compounds reduce disease symptoms and pathogen reproduction across multiple test systems.

The invention includes the use of C4H inhibitor precursors that are converted to active compounds in planta. These precursors may include esters, glycosides, or other derivatives that improve stability, uptake, or translocation characteristics.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope. All percentages are by weight unless otherwise indicated.

### Plant Material

Rice cultivars used in the experiments included Oryza sativa japonica varieties Nipponbare and Zhonghua 11. Sugar beet cultivar used was Beta vulgaris cv. Monohil. Wheat cultivars included Triticum aestivum cv. Chinese Spring and Bobwhite. Tomato cultivar used was Solanum lycopersicum cv. Moneymaker. Carrot cultivar used was Daucus carota cv. Nantes.

Seeds were surface sterilized and germinated on moist filter paper in Petri dishes. After germination, seedlings were transferred to sterile sand-filled SAP tubes (5 cm diameter × 15 cm height) and grown in a controlled environment chamber at 25°C with a 16/8 h light/dark cycle. Seedlings were watered with half-strength Hoagland solution every 2 days.

### Nematode Cultures

Meloidogyne graminicola was maintained on rice plants and extracted from infected roots using a modified Baermann funnel technique. Nematodes were counted in suspension under a stereomicroscope and adjusted to 500 J2s/mL for inoculation.

Meloidogyne incognita was maintained on tomato plants and extracted similarly. Heterodera schachtii was maintained on sugar beet and extracted from infected roots by sieving and sucrose centrifugation. Cysts were crushed to release eggs, which were hatched in sterile water. Pratylenchus penetrans was maintained on carrot disk cultures and extracted by washing disks with water.

For inoculation, plants at the 2-3 leaf stage were carefully removed from SAP tubes, roots were dipped in nematode suspension for 30 seconds, and plants were replanted. Control plants were dipped in water only.

### Fungal Cultures

Fungal isolates were obtained from culture collections and maintained on potato dextrose agar (PDA). Spores were harvested from 7-day-old cultures by flooding plates with sterile water containing 0.01% Tween 20. Spore concentrations were adjusted to 10^5 spores/mL for inoculation.

### Chemical Treatments

Foliar spray solutions were prepared by dissolving test compounds in water with 0.01% Tween 20 as surfactant. Plants were sprayed to runoff using a handheld sprayer at 14 and 7 days before nematode inoculation. Seed coatings were applied by mixing compounds with a polymer binder and applying to seeds at 1 g/kg seed.

### Nematode Infection Experiments

Inoculated plants were maintained for 4 weeks post-inoculation, then harvested and phenotyped. Roots were washed free of soil and stained with acid fuchsin to visualize nematodes. Galls and nematodes were counted under a stereomicroscope. Data were analyzed by ANOVA with Tukey's HSD test (p<0.05).

### Fungal Infection Experiments

Plants were inoculated by spraying with spore suspensions or placing agar plugs on leaves. Disease symptoms were scored after 7-14 days. Lesion numbers and sizes were measured and analyzed statistically.

### Nematode Toxicity Assays

J2 nematodes were incubated in compound solutions (10-1000 μM) for 24-72 hours. Motility was scored every 24 hours by counting moving nematodes under a microscope. Nematodes were considered dead if immobile after transfer to water.

### Fungal Toxicity Assay

Fungal spores were spotted on PDA plates containing test compounds (10-1000 μM). Colony diameters were measured after 3-7 days. Percentage growth inhibition was calculated relative to controls.

### Pseudomonas syringae Infection Experiment

Tomato plants were sprayed with test compounds 3 days before inoculation with P. syringae pv. tomato DC3000 (10^8 CFU/mL). Disease symptoms were scored after 5 days. Bacterial populations were quantified by plating leaf homogenates.

### Effect of C4H Inhibition on Growth and Development in Uninfected Crops

Phytotoxic effects of C4H inhibitors were evaluated on uninfected plants. Rice and wheat seedlings were treated with compounds at 10-1000 μM and monitored for growth parameters. No significant phytotoxicity was observed below 500 μM. Transient root elongation inhibition occurred at 100-500 μM but recovered within 7 days.

### C4H Inhibitors have No Effect on Yield and Fertility in Wheat

An experiment was designed to test C4H inhibition effects on wheat yield and fertility. Wheat plants were treated with 100 μM piperonylic acid at tillering and booting stages. At maturity, seed size, number, and germination ability were measured. No significant differences were found between treated and control plants for any parameter, demonstrating that C4H inhibition at effective doses does not compromise yield or fertility.

### The Early Phenylpropanoid Pathway and Rice Defense

An experiment tested MDCA and PA on rice defense against M. graminicola. Rice plants were treated with 100 μM compounds 7 days before nematode inoculation. Gall counts were reduced by 65-75% compared to controls. Nematode development was also impaired, with fewer mature females in treated plants. These results indicate enhanced defense responses including possible hypersensitive reactions in treated plants.

### Second Infection Experiment in Rice

A second experiment tested MDCA and PA on rice defense with detailed growth measurements. Plant height and developmental stage were monitored weekly. Treated plants showed temporary growth retardation (10-15% shorter at 2 weeks) but recovered fully by 4 weeks. Phenotypic parameters including shoot height and root length showed similar patterns. Gall counts and nematode numbers were reduced by 70-80%, demonstrating strong inhibition of nematode invasion and gall establishment.

### Example 5

Testing C4H inhibition in wheat against root-knot nematodes showed 60% reduction in gall formation. Additional C4H inhibitors tested against M. graminicola in rice showed varying efficacy, with CF3-BA showing effects similar to IBA. In tomato, C4H inhibitors reduced M. incognita disease pressure by 50-70%, including in NahG-expressing and DIECA-treated plants, indicating action independent of salicylic acid and jasmonic acid pathways.

qPCR analysis showed upregulation of OsPAL4 and OsC4H genes after treatment, along with downregulation of OsGNS5. Testing against Pseudomonas syringae showed reduced disease pressure without direct antibacterial effects. Pythium splendens infection was reduced by seed coating treatments without affecting germination.

The mechanism of action involves induction of plant defense pathways rather than direct toxicity. Results demonstrate protective effects against diverse diseases in multiple crops, supporting the utility of C4H inhibitors as a novel crop protection strategy.
