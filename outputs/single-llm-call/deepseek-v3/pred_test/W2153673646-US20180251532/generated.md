## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Apoptosis, or programmed cell death, plays a critical role in maintaining tissue homeostasis and eliminating damaged or infected cells. Dysregulation of apoptosis contributes to numerous diseases, including cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, and autoimmune conditions. The BCL-2 protein family serves as central regulators of apoptosis, with pro-apoptotic members such as BAX initiating mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP), a pivotal step in the intrinsic apoptosis pathway.  

The BCL-2 protein family comprises both anti-apoptotic and pro-apoptotic members that regulate MOMP through complex protein-protein interactions. BAX exists as an inactive monomer in the cytosol until activated by BH3-only proteins, triggering conformational changes that enable mitochondrial translocation, oligomerization, and pore formation. The activation pathway of BAX involves exposure of its N-terminal domain, insertion of the C-terminal helix α9 into the mitochondrial membrane, and oligomerization to form pores.  

Structural studies have revealed that BAX undergoes dramatic conformational changes during activation. The inactive monomer maintains a compact structure with the C-terminal transmembrane helix α9 tucked into a hydrophobic groove. Activation involves displacement of helices α1-α2 and exposure of the N-terminal trigger site, followed by membrane insertion and oligomerization.  

Diseases associated with aberrant BAX activation include cancer, where insufficient apoptosis allows tumor survival, and neurodegenerative disorders, where excessive apoptosis leads to neuronal loss. Current therapies targeting the BCL-2 family have limitations, creating a need for novel approaches to modulate BAX activity with high specificity.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides synthetic Fab antibodies that specifically bind to BAX and methods for their use. The Fabs exhibit high affinity binding to monomeric BAX and inhibit BAX activation by blocking the N-terminal trigger site. The invention includes compositions comprising these Fabs, methods for inhibiting apoptotic cell death, and screening methods for identifying small molecule BAX inhibitors.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides synthetic Fab antibodies that bind specifically to BAX with high affinity. These Fabs were identified through phage display screening against monomeric BAX, yielding 14 distinct clones with nanomolar binding affinities. The Fabs bind to overlapping epitopes on the BAX surface, with particularly strong inhibition of BAX activation demonstrated by clone 3G11.  

The synthetic Fabs bind to key residues on the BAX surface including Lys-21 in helix α1 and Arg-134 in helix α6, which form part of the N-terminal activation site. Binding affinities range from 2.3 nM to 250 nM as measured by ELISA and biolayer interferometry. The CDR regions responsible for BAX binding include CDRL3, CDRH1, CDRH2, and CDRH3, with specific sequences provided in SEQ ID NOs 1-14.  

The Fabs can be produced recombinantly in E. coli or mammalian expression systems. Production involves periplasmic expression in E. coli BL21(DE3), nickel affinity purification, and protein A chromatography. The purified Fabs maintain stable binding to BAX and inhibit its apoptotic function.  

Methods of inhibiting apoptotic cell death comprise administering an effective amount of the synthetic Fab antibodies to prevent BAX activation. The Fabs can be conjugated to therapeutic agents such as chemotherapeutic drugs or imaging moieties. Administration routes include intravenous, intramuscular, or direct tumor injection for cancer therapy.  

The invention provides methods for measuring inactive monomeric BAX levels using the Fabs in diagnostic assays. Screening methods employ competitive ELISA assays to identify small molecules that displace Fab binding to BAX. Such molecules share characteristics including molecular weights <500 Da and binding to the N-terminal activation site.  

Additional methods include predicting cancer therapy efficacy by monitoring BAX inhibition and labeling synthetic Fabs for imaging applications. The Fabs can be fluorescently labeled or conjugated to radioisotopes for tracking BAX activation states in vivo.  

### Introduction  

Synthetic antibody technology enables discovery of high-affinity binders through in vitro selection methods. The invention utilized phage display screening to identify conformation-specific Fabs against monomeric BAX. This approach overcame challenges in raising antibodies against transient protein states, as the N-terminal activation site is only exposed during BAX activation.  

The phage display library (Library F) contained designed diversity in CDR regions, particularly CDR-H3 and CDR-L3. Screening against freshly purified monomeric BAX yielded Fabs that stabilize the inactive conformation. Rationale for in vitro selection included the need for probes that distinguish between BAX activation states and inhibit specific conformational changes.  

### Materials and Methods  

Recombinant BAX was produced in E. coli BL21 CodonPlus (DE3)-RIPL cells using the pTYB1 vector. Expression was induced with 1 mM IPTG at 30°C for 4 hours. Cells were lysed by microfluidization, and BAX was purified by chitin affinity chromatography followed by gel filtration to isolate monomeric fractions.  

Fab proteins were expressed periplasmically in E. coli BL21(DE3) using low-phosphate media. Cells were lysed with BugBuster reagent, and Fabs were purified by nickel affinity chromatography and protein A purification. Fab concentrations were determined by A280 measurements using calculated extinction coefficients.  

For binding assays, direct ELISAs immobilized 1 μg/well BAX on 96-well plates. Competitive ELISAs used biotinylated 3G11 (b3G11) to measure displacement by other Fabs. Binding kinetics were determined by biolayer interferometry using Ni-NTA biosensors and BAX concentrations from 30-200 nM.  

Functional assays included liposome permeabilization using ANTS/DPX-loaded vesicles, mitochondrial translocation assays with Bak-/- liver mitochondria, and cytochrome c release measurements. Structural characterization employed NMR spectroscopy, hydrogen-deuterium exchange mass spectrometry (HXMS), and computational docking with HADDOCK.  

### Results  

Screening of Library F against monomeric BAX yielded 14 specific Fabs with diverse CDR sequences. EC50 values ranged from 2.3 nM (2B1) to 250 nM (2A5) by ELISA. Biolayer interferometry confirmed high-affinity binding, with 3G11 showing particularly slow dissociation kinetics. Competitive ELISAs demonstrated overlapping epitopes among the Fabs.  

Liposome assays showed that high-affinity Fabs (3G11, 3E8, 2B1) completely inhibited tBID-triggered BAX activation at 2 μM, while the weaker binder 2A5 had no effect. Mitochondrial assays confirmed that 3G11 dose-dependently prevented BAX translocation and cytochrome c release.  

Structural studies revealed that 3G11 forms a stable 1:1 complex with BAX. HXMS showed protection of helices α1 and α6 upon 3G11 binding, indicating interaction with the N-terminal activation site. Mutagenesis confirmed critical roles for Lys-21 and Arg-134 in Fab binding and inhibition.  

### Discussion  

The BCL-2 protein family regulates apoptosis through complex interactions, with BAX serving as the executioner of mitochondrial dysfunction. The synthetic Fabs described herein provide novel tools for probing BAX conformation and function. By binding the N-terminal activation site, these Fabs inhibit BAX through a distinct mechanism compared to known peptide inhibitors.  

Therapeutic applications include cancer treatment by preventing apoptosis resistance and neuroprotection by blocking excessive cell death. The Fabs also enable screening for small molecule BAX inhibitors that mimic their binding mode. Future directions include developing these Fabs as diagnostic tools and therapeutic leads for diseases of dysregulated apoptosis.
