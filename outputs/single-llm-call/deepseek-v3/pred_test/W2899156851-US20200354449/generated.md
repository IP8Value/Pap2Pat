# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapeutics and diagnostics, specifically to fully human monoclonal antibodies that bind to receptor tyrosine kinase-like orphan receptor 2 (ROR2). The invention further encompasses antibody-based binding proteins, antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), antibody effector conjugates (AECs), chimeric antigen receptors (CARs), and related compositions and methods for treating neoplastic diseases characterized by ROR2 expression.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cancer remains a leading cause of mortality worldwide, with current treatment modalities often limited by toxicity and lack of specificity. Genetic alterations underlie malignant transformation, with receptor tyrosine kinases playing pivotal roles in oncogenic signaling pathways. Among these, the ROR family of receptors, comprising ROR1 and ROR2, have emerged as promising therapeutic targets due to their restricted expression in normal adult tissues compared to widespread overexpression in various malignancies.  

ROR2 is a type I transmembrane protein that regulates critical cellular processes including proliferation, differentiation, migration, and survival through interactions with Wnt ligands. While ROR2 expression is prominent during embryogenesis, its presence in healthy adult tissues is minimal. In contrast, ROR2 is frequently overexpressed in numerous cancers such as renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, breast cancer, colorectal cancer, and pancreatic cancer, where its expression often correlates with aggressive disease and poor prognosis.  

Structurally, ROR2 contains an extracellular portion with immunoglobulin-like, Frizzled, and Kringle domains, followed by a transmembrane domain and intracellular tyrosine kinase-like domains. This complex architecture presents challenges for developing high-quality anti-ROR2 antibodies capable of selective tumor targeting while avoiding cross-reactivity with normal tissues.  

Current limitations in cancer treatment highlight the need for targeted therapies that combine tumor specificity with potent cytotoxic activity. Antibody-drug conjugates represent an emerging class of therapeutics that address this need by linking tumor-selective antibodies to highly cytotoxic payloads. However, the development of effective ADCs requires antibodies with optimal binding characteristics, efficient internalization upon target engagement, and favorable pharmacokinetic properties.  

The normal physiological functions of human ROR2 remain incompletely understood, though its role in developmental processes suggests potential safety concerns if broadly inhibited. This underscores the importance of developing highly specific anti-ROR2 antibodies that can discriminate between physiological and pathological ROR2 expression patterns.  

There remains an unmet need for high-quality anti-ROR2 antibodies suitable for therapeutic applications, particularly those capable of serving as components of ADCs, AECs, or CAR-T cell therapies. The present invention addresses this need through the development of novel fully human monoclonal antibodies with superior binding characteristics and functional properties.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides fully human antibodies that specifically bind to ROR2 with high affinity. These antibodies are particularly useful for the treatment and diagnosis of neoplastic diseases characterized by ROR2 expression. The invention encompasses not only the antibodies themselves but also various derivatives and conjugates thereof, including but not limited to antibody-drug conjugates, antibody effector conjugates, chimeric antigen receptors, and bispecific or multispecific antibodies.  

The antibodies of the invention demonstrate favorable binding kinetics, selective internalization properties, and potent cytotoxic activity when configured as ADCs. Particular embodiments exhibit cross-reactivity with ROR2 from relevant toxicology species, facilitating preclinical development. The invention further provides polynucleotides encoding the antibodies, expression vectors comprising these polynucleotides, host cells transformed with such vectors, and methods for producing the antibodies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel fully human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind human ROR2. These antibodies were identified through an innovative screening approach combining immunization of transgenic mice with functional assessment of antibody internalization and cytotoxic potential.  

In non-limiting embodiments, the invention provides anti-ROR2 antibodies comprising specific complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) as detailed herein. The antibodies may be formulated as naked antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates, antibody effector conjugates, or other modified formats. The antibody drug conjugates of the invention typically comprise the anti-ROR2 antibody covalently linked to a cytotoxic or cytostatic agent through a cleavable or non-cleavable linker.  

The antibody selection process involved immunization of transgenic mice, enrichment of antigen-specific B cells, and functional screening using a secondary ADC assay that simultaneously assessed antigen binding and cytotoxic potential. This approach enabled identification of antibodies with optimal characteristics for ADC development without requiring prior sequence recovery and recombinant expression.  

Antibody effector conjugates according to the invention may comprise the anti-ROR2 antibody linked to an effector molecule such as a cytokine, enzyme, or other immune-modulating agent. Chimeric antigen receptors of the invention incorporate the antigen-binding domains of the anti-ROR2 antibodies fused to transmembrane and intracellular signaling domains for expression in immune cells.  

The invention further provides bi- or multispecific antibodies capable of simultaneously binding ROR2 and another target antigen. Such constructs may enhance therapeutic efficacy through multiple mechanisms of action.  

Isolated polynucleotides encoding the heavy and light chains of the anti-ROR2 antibodies are provided, along with expression vectors containing these polynucleotides operably linked to regulatory elements. Host cells transformed with such vectors can be used for large-scale antibody production.  

The antibodies and conjugates of the invention are useful for treating various neoplastic diseases characterized by ROR2 expression, including but not limited to breast cancer, renal cell carcinoma, osteosarcoma, melanoma, and pancreatic cancer. Treatment methods typically involve administering a therapeutically effective amount of an anti-ROR2 antibody or conjugate to a subject in need thereof.  

The invention also provides methods for detecting neoplastic disease by contacting a biological sample with an anti-ROR2 antibody and determining the level of ROR2 expression compared to control samples. Such diagnostic methods may facilitate early detection, prognosis assessment, and treatment monitoring.  

### Definitions  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:  

A "human antibody" refers to an antibody having variable and constant regions derived from human immunoglobulin sequences. The human antibodies of the invention may include naturally occurring amino acid sequence variations due to somatic mutation or conservative substitutions.  

An "antibody-based binding protein" encompasses not only full-length antibodies but also antigen-binding fragments, single-chain antibodies, and other engineered variants that retain specific binding to ROR2.  

A "modified antibody format" includes antibodies engineered for specific properties, such as Fc modifications to alter effector function or half-life, conjugation to therapeutic or diagnostic moieties, or formatting as bispecific or multispecific constructs.  

An "antibody derivative or fragment" refers to portions of an antibody that retain antigen-binding capability, including but not limited to Fab, F(ab')2, Fv, scFv, and single domain antibody fragments.  

The structure of an "intact antibody" typically comprises two heavy chains and two light chains, with each heavy chain paired with a light chain. Each chain includes variable and constant regions, with the variable regions containing complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) responsible for antigen binding.  

Immunoglobulin genes undergo V(D)J recombination during B cell development to generate antibody diversity. The heavy chain genes include variable (V), diversity (D), and joining (J) segments, while light chain genes contain V and J segments.  

Heavy and light chains are classified based on their constant regions. Heavy chains may be of the α, δ, ε, γ, or μ isotypes, determining the antibody class (IgA, IgD, IgE, IgG, or IgM, respectively). Light chains may be of the κ or λ isotype.  

The heavy chain constant region determines antibody class and subclass, while the light chain constant region is relatively invariant. The variable regions of both heavy and light chains contain three CDRs (CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3) flanked by framework regions (FRs) that provide structural support.  

A "single chain antibody" or "single chain variable region fragment" (scFv) comprises the variable regions of heavy and light chains connected by a peptide linker. "Disulfide stabilized Fvs" (dsFvs) are stabilized by interchain disulfide bonds.  

"Antigen-binding fragments" include Fab, F(ab')2, Fd, and Fv fragments that retain antigen-binding capability. "Single domain antibodies" are derived from heavy chain-only antibodies or engineered from conventional antibodies.  

"Isolated complementarity determining regions" refer to CDR peptides that maintain antigen-binding capacity when properly presented, such as in scaffold proteins.  

Methods for generating antibodies include immunization approaches, phage display, yeast display, and mammalian cell display systems. The invention particularly encompasses methods for generating scFv antibodies, Fv fragments, dsFvs, single domain antibodies (dAbs), and camelid single domain antibodies.  

A "conservatively modified variant" refers to an amino acid sequence containing substitutions with biochemically similar amino acids (e.g., substitution of a leucine with an isoleucine). "Silent variations" are nucleotide sequence changes that do not alter the encoded amino acid sequence.  

"Percent identity" refers to the percentage of identical residues between two aligned sequences, typically calculated using standard algorithms such as BLAST with appropriate parameters.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention provides human anti-ROR2 antibodies and antibody-based binding proteins with specific binding characteristics. These molecules are particularly useful when configured as AECs, ADCs, or CARs for targeted cancer therapy.  

The antibodies of the invention bind to specific epitopes on ROR2, with particular embodiments recognizing conformational epitopes involving multiple extracellular domains. The antibodies may be full-length immunoglobulins, derivatives, modified formats, or fragments as described herein.  

Sequence listings provided as SEQ ID NO:2-25 represent particularly preferred embodiments of the invention. Binding competition assays can assess whether different antibodies bind overlapping or distinct epitopes on ROR2. Methods for determining binding epitopes include alanine scanning, peptide mapping, and structural analysis.  

A preferred embodiment comprises fully human anti-ROR2 antibodies with binding affinities (KD) in the nanomolar range, particularly below 10 nM. The antibodies exhibit favorable association (kon) and dissociation (koff) rates, with some embodiments showing particularly slow off-rates indicative of stable antigen binding. Binding affinity may be determined by surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry, or other biophysical methods.  

The variable heavy (VH) chains of the antibodies comprise three CDRs (CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3) with specific amino acid sequences as provided herein. Particular VH CDR1 sequences include those listed in Table 3 of the specification. Similarly, specific VH CDR2 and CDR3 sequences are provided, with preferred triplets identified for each CDR.  

The variable light (VL) chains likewise contain three CDRs with specific sequences. VL CDR1, CDR2, and CDR3 sequences are provided, including preferred embodiments listed in Table 3. Combinations of specific VH and VL CDRs define particularly effective antigen-binding sites.  

Certain antibodies of the invention exhibit cross-reactivity with ROR2 from other species, particularly cynomolgus monkey and mouse ROR2. This cross-reactivity facilitates preclinical safety and efficacy testing. Preferred CDR sets demonstrate this cross-reactivity while maintaining high affinity for human ROR2.  

The antibody-based binding proteins of the invention may comprise specific heavy and light chain sequence pairs that together confer optimal binding characteristics. Bispecific or multispecific antibodies may incorporate these sequences along with binding domains targeting effector antigens such as CD3 or immune checkpoint molecules.  

Preferred heavy chain/light chain pairs are those demonstrating high affinity, favorable kinetics, and efficient internalization. Sequences that are substantially identical to these preferred pairs (e.g., ≥90% identity) are also encompassed, as are conservatively modified variants that retain binding function.  

The antibodies may be of various isotypes, with IgG1 being particularly preferred for ADC applications due to its favorable effector functions and pharmacokinetics. Bivalent antibodies are generally preferred for optimal target engagement.  

Particular embodiments of anti-ROR2 antibodies bind human ROR2 and cynomolgus ROR2, human ROR2 and mouse ROR2, or all three orthologs. This cross-reactivity profile is valuable for translational research. Some antibodies bind mouse ROR2 weakly while maintaining strong binding to human ROR2.  

The invention provides polynucleotides encoding the anti-ROR2 antibodies, including cDNA and genomic DNA sequences. Host cells transformed with these polynucleotides can be used for antibody production. Expression vectors for producing anti-ROR2 antibodies typically include promoters (e.g., CMV), enhancers, secretion signals, and other regulatory elements optimized for mammalian cell expression.  

Methods for introducing expression vectors into host cells include electroporation, lipofection, and viral transduction. Stable expression may be achieved through selectable markers and gene amplification techniques.  

Antibody effector conjugates (AECs) of the invention comprise anti-ROR2 antibodies linked to effector molecules such as cytokines, enzymes, or other immune-modulating agents. Antibody drug conjugates (ADCs) incorporate cytotoxic or cytostatic agents linked through various linker chemistries.  

Linkers for ADCs and AECs may be cleavable (e.g., protease-sensitive) or non-cleavable, with peptide, hydrazine, thiourea, or other chemistries. Specific linkers include valine-citruline-PAB, LPXTGn-, LPXAGn-, and NPQTGn- motifs. Conjugation may be site-specific (e.g., via engineered cysteine residues or sortase recognition motifs) or non-site-specific.  

Cytotoxic agents for ADCs include auristatins, maytansinoids, calicheamicins, duocarmycins, and PNU derivatives. Therapeutic agents may be radioisotopes, immunomodulators, or other bioactive molecules. A preferred ADC embodiment incorporates a PNU derivative toxin linked through a cleavable peptide spacer.  

Conjugation methods include maleimide chemistry, SMAC-technology, and sortase-mediated coupling. Stoichiometric ratios typically range from 2-8 drugs per antibody, with conjugation to heavy chains often preferred. Multiple toxins or labels may be incorporated per antibody molecule.  

The invention encompasses ROR2-targeted chimeric antigen receptors (CARs) comprising anti-ROR2 binding domains fused to transmembrane and intracellular signaling domains. Cells (particularly T cells) expressing these CARs are useful for adoptive immunotherapy.  

Methods of treating neoplastic diseases involve administering anti-ROR2 antibodies or conjugates to patients with ROR2-expressing cancers. Treatment may be monotherapy or combined with other agents. Dosages are tailored based on disease characteristics and patient factors.  

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the antibodies or conjugates with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, buffers, and stabilizers. Administration routes include intravenous, subcutaneous, and intratumoral injection. Sustained-release formulations may be employed.  

Diagnostic methods utilize anti-ROR2 antibodies to detect altered ROR2 levels in biological samples. Kits package antibodies with reagents for standardized detection. Monitoring methods track disease progression or treatment response by serial ROR2 measurements.  

Screening methods identify patients likely to respond to anti-ROR2 therapy by assessing tumor ROR2 expression. Imaging methods employ labeled antibodies to visualize ROR2-positive lesions in vivo.  

The invention includes eukaryotic and non-eukaryotic cells engineered to produce anti-ROR2 antibodies, as well as immune cells engineered to express ROR2-targeted CARs. Examples demonstrate antibody library generation, screening, and characterization.  

Antibody production involves expression in mammalian cells (e.g., HEK293T), purification by affinity chromatography, and quality control assessments. Binding is quantified by ELISA and surface plasmon resonance.  

ADC cytotoxicity is demonstrated in vitro using ROR2-expressing cell lines, with IC50 values determined by dose-response assays. Specific examples show potent activity against breast cancer models while sparing ROR2-negative cells.  

The invention provides broadly applicable methods for developing targeted cancer therapeutics based on ROR2-specific antibodies. The fully human nature of these antibodies reduces immunogenicity risk while maintaining high affinity and functional efficacy.
