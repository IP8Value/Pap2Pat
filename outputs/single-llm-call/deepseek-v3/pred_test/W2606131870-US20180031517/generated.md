## DESCRIPTION  

### TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of ion sensing technology, and more specifically to novel ion-selective electrodes incorporating metal-organic supercontainer (MOSC) molecules as size-selective ionophores. The invention particularly concerns potentiometric sensors capable of detecting molecular ions with high selectivity and near-Nernstian response characteristics. The disclosed technology finds application in environmental monitoring, biomedical diagnostics, food quality control, and industrial process monitoring where precise ion detection is required.  

### BACKGROUND  

Ion sensing technology has become increasingly important across multiple scientific and industrial domains. Conventional ion-selective electrodes (ISEs) typically employ ionophores that facilitate the selective recognition of target ions through specific chemical interactions. However, existing ionophores suffer from several limitations including poor selectivity for molecular ions, limited stability in complex matrices, and suboptimal response characteristics. Traditional ionophores designed for small elemental ions demonstrate inadequate performance when applied to larger molecular ions due to fundamental differences in size and binding mechanisms.  

Current state-of-the-art ionophores, particularly those based on calixarene derivatives, show preferential binding to small metal ions through coordination chemistry at their lower rim structures. These systems fail to effectively recognize larger molecular ions due to size mismatches and the absence of appropriate binding domains. Furthermore, conventional ionophores often exhibit significant cross-sensitivity to interfering ions, limiting their utility in real-world applications where complex sample matrices are common. There exists a pressing need for ion sensing technology capable of selectively detecting molecular ions with improved sensitivity, stability, and response characteristics.  

### BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present invention addresses these limitations through the development of a novel ion sensing platform based on metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs). These structurally defined molecular containers possess precisely engineered cavities that enable size-selective recognition of target molecular ions. The disclosed technology utilizes MOSCs as ionophores in both conventional liquid-contact ISEs and solid-contact ISE configurations, demonstrating superior performance characteristics compared to prior art systems.  

Key advantages of the MOSC-based ion sensing technology include near-Nernstian response to target molecular ions, exceptional size selectivity, and remarkable stability in various environmental conditions. The MOSC architecture features multiple well-defined binding domains including endo-cavities and exo-cavities that can be chemically tuned for specific ion recognition. This multi-cavity structure enables synergistic binding interactions that enhance both sensitivity and selectivity.  

The invention encompasses apparatus and methods for detecting molecular ions through potentiometric measurements. In exemplary embodiments, MOSCs are incorporated into mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) or directly attached to solid sensing surfaces (SSSs) to form functional ion-selective interfaces. The technology demonstrates particular utility in detecting cationic molecular species such as methylene blue (MB+) and tetrabutylammonium (TBA+), showing detection limits in the nanomolar range and response times on the order of seconds.  

Notably, the MOSC-based sensors exhibit minimal response to smaller elemental ions, confirming their size-selective nature. The invention further demonstrates stability against leaching and maintains performance in complex matrices such as simulated wastewater. These characteristics represent significant improvements over conventional ionophores that often suffer from interference and instability in real-world applications.  

Example embodiments of the invention include:  

Example 1 provides a proof-of-concept study demonstrating MOSC-based detection of MB+ and TBA+ with near-Nernstian response characteristics. The example details fabrication methods for both conventional ISEs and solid-contact ISEs incorporating MOSC ionophores, along with comprehensive performance characterization.  

Example 2 presents leeching test results confirming the exceptional stability of MOSC-incorporated membranes. Comparative studies demonstrate the critical role of MOSCs in preventing ionophore leaching and maintaining sensor performance over extended periods.  

Example 3 illustrates application of the technology in complex matrices through testing with simulated wastewater samples. The results confirm maintained sensor performance despite challenging environmental conditions.  

Example 4 expands the application scope to molecular ion sensing using silicon nanowire ion-sensitive field-effect transistors (SiNW-ISFETs). This embodiment demonstrates the compatibility of MOSC technology with advanced semiconductor-based sensing platforms.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention utilizes metal-organic supercontainers (MOSCs) as the foundational component for advanced ion sensing applications. MOSCs represent a class of structurally well-defined molecular containers characterized by multiple binding cavities with precisely controlled dimensions and chemical functionalities.  

Referring to FIG. 1, an exemplary MOSC structure (designated as 1-Co) is illustrated. The molecule features an edge-directed octahedral geometry with an outer diameter of approximately 3.3 nm and an inner diameter of 1.7 nm, creating an internal volume of 1.2 nm³. The architecture comprises seven distinct binding domains: one central endo-cavity and six peripheral exo-cavities. This multi-pore structure, depicted in FIG. 2 and FIG. 3, enables simultaneous and potentially synergistic interactions with target molecular ions.  

The chemically tunable nature of MOSCs allows for customization of both cavity dimensions and surface properties to optimize recognition of specific molecular ions. The endo-cavity, with its diameter of approximately 1.7 nm, is particularly suited for accommodating larger molecular ions through a combination of size matching and cation-π interactions. The six exo-cavities, each with diameters around 0.74 nm, provide additional binding sites that can contribute to enhanced ion recognition.  

MOSCs function as highly effective host systems for molecular ions due to their ability to concentrate target species within the defined cavities. When employed as ionophores in ion-selective electrodes, MOSCs facilitate the establishment of a phase-boundary potential at the membrane|analyte interface that responds selectively to the target ion concentration.  

FIG. 4A-B illustrates the incorporation of MOSCs into substrate materials to form functional sensing interfaces. Two primary scaffold configurations are disclosed: MOSC-MMM (mixed-matrix membrane) and MOSC-SSS (solid sensing surface). In the MMM configuration, MOSCs are dispersed within a polymer matrix along with appropriate plasticizers and ionic additives. The SSS configuration involves direct chemical attachment of MOSCs to solid substrates through covalent or coordination bonds.  

The tunability of MOSC cavities enables optimization for specific ion detection applications. For molecular ion sensing, the cavity dimensions can be engineered to match the steric requirements of the target species while incorporating functional groups that promote favorable interactions. This design approach contrasts with conventional ionophores that typically rely on single binding sites with limited size discrimination capability.  

In the MMM scaffold configuration, the invention provides an ion sensing device comprising a polymeric membrane containing dispersed MOSCs, a substrate supporting the membrane, and appropriate reference electrodes for potential measurement. The MOSC-MMM system demonstrates particular advantages in conventional ISE configurations where liquid inner contacts are employed.  

The SSS scaffold configuration, illustrated in FIG. 8 and FIG. 9, enables direct integration of MOSCs with solid-state electronic devices. In this embodiment, MOSCs are chemically bonded to the surface of conductive or semiconductive substrates to form compact sensing interfaces. This configuration is particularly suited for miniaturized sensors and integration with semiconductor fabrication processes.  

Electrical response measurement methodologies are depicted in FIG. 5, FIG. 6, and FIG. 7. The invention encompasses both potentiometric and field-effect measurement approaches for detecting changes in interfacial potential induced by MOSC-ion interactions. In potentiometric mode, the potential difference between the MOSC-containing sensing electrode and a reference electrode is measured as a function of target ion concentration. In field-effect configurations, changes in drain current or threshold voltage of semiconductor devices functionalized with MOSCs serve as the detection signal.  

The attachment of MOSCs to SSSs through chemical bonds provides enhanced stability and prevents leaching of the active sensing component. Various bonding strategies can be employed including coordination to surface metal centers, covalent attachment through functional groups, or electrostatic interactions with charged surfaces.  

Key advantages of the MOSC-based ion sensors include:  
1) Near-Nernstian response to target molecular ions  
2) Exceptional size selectivity against smaller interfering ions  
3) Rapid response times on the order of seconds  
4) Excellent long-term stability with minimal potential drift  
5) Compatibility with both conventional and solid-contact sensor configurations  
6) Tunability for specific applications through chemical modification of MOSC structures  

### EXAMPLE 1: PROOF-OF-CONCEPT STUDY WITH MB+ AND TBA+  

Example 1 demonstrates the fundamental sensing capabilities of MOSC-based ion sensors using MOSC-II-Co as the exemplary ionophore. The study focuses on detection of methylene blue (MB+) and tetrabutylammonium (TBA+) as model molecular ions.  

The MOSC-II-Co molecule was synthesized according to established procedures and characterized structurally. The materials for device fabrication included high molecular weight poly(vinylchloride) (PVC) as the polymer matrix, bis(2-ethylhexyl)sebacate (DOS) as plasticizer, and potassium tetrakis(4-chlorophenyl) borate (KTpClPB) as ionic additive.  

Ion sensing devices were fabricated in both conventional ISE and solid-contact ISE (SC-ISE) configurations. Conventional ISEs were prepared by dip-coating pipette tips with MOSC-containing mixed-matrix membrane (MMM) solutions, followed by insertion of Ag/AgCl reference elements. SC-ISEs were fabricated by spin-coating and pyrolyzing SU8 layers on silicon substrates, followed by MMM deposition.  

Potential response measurements, illustrated in FIG. 10, demonstrated near-Nernstian behavior with slopes of 56.8 ± 2.5 mV/decade for MB+ and 60.0 ± 1.5 mV/decade for TBA+. Detection limits reached approximately 300 nM for MB+ and 1 μM for TBA+, representing significant improvements over conventional molecular ion sensors.  

Impedance spectroscopy results, shown in FIG. 11, revealed a two-fold reduction in charge-transfer resistance for MOSC-containing membranes compared to control membranes without MOSCs. This finding confirms the role of MOSCs in facilitating ion transfer across the membrane|analyte interface.  

Potentiometric response characteristics, detailed in FIG. 12, demonstrated excellent size selectivity. While showing strong response to MB+ and TBA+, the sensors exhibited minimal sensitivity to smaller ions including K+ (10.4 ± 2.1 mV/decade), Ag+ (5.0 ± 7.3 mV/decade), and NH4+ (-6.4 ± 1.4 mV/decade). This size-selective behavior, illustrated in FIGS. 13-16, stems from the dimensional matching between target ions and MOSC cavities.  

Response time and potential stability measurements, presented in FIG. 15, showed rapid response (1 s for concentrations above 30 μM) and excellent stability with drift less than a few mV/h. These characteristics make the technology suitable for real-time monitoring applications.  

### EXAMPLE 2: LEECHING STUDY  

Example 2 evaluates the stability of MOSC-incorporated membranes against leaching of active components. Comparative studies were conducted between MMMs containing MOSC-II-Co and control membranes without MOSCs.  

The leaching test involved storing fabricated membranes in deionized water for extended periods (over one week). Visual inspection and UV-vis spectroscopy confirmed that MOSC-containing membranes retained their active components, while control membranes showed significant leaching of molecular ions. Results are presented in FIG. 17.  

This exceptional stability stems from the strong encapsulation of target ions within MOSC cavities and the robust integration of MOSCs within the polymer matrix. The findings have important implications for long-term sensor deployment in field applications.  

### EXAMPLE 3: SIMULATED WASTE WATER SAMPLE  

Example 3 demonstrates the performance of MOSC-based sensors in complex matrices using simulated wastewater prepared with Fyris River water. The study evaluated the sensor's response to MB+ additions in this challenging environment.  

As shown in FIG. 18, the sensors maintained near-Nernstian response characteristics despite the complex matrix. A small shift in detection limit was observed and attributed to partial reduction of MB+ by microorganisms present in the river water. Control experiments using UV-vis spectroscopy confirmed this interpretation.  

The results confirm the technology's robustness and potential for environmental monitoring applications where complex sample matrices are common.  

### EXAMPLE 4: MOLECULAR ION SENSING  

Example 4 expands the application of MOSC technology to silicon nanowire ion-sensitive field-effect transistors (SiNW-ISFETs) for molecular ion sensing. This embodiment demonstrates the compatibility of MOSCs with advanced semiconductor-based sensing platforms.  

The example details preparation of MOSC-containing mixed-matrix membranes (MMMs) and their integration with SiNW-ISFET devices. Electrical measurement setups and procedures are described, including threshold voltage (VTH) monitoring as the primary detection mechanism.  

Surface functionalization of SiNW-ISFETs with MMMs, illustrated in FIG. 19, enabled sensitive detection of molecular ions through MOSC-mediated recognition. The potential diagram of the SiO2/electrolyte interface and MMM/electrolyte interface are analyzed to explain the sensing mechanism.  

Key findings include:  
- Negligible change in subthreshold slope (SS) upon MMM functionalization  
- Minimal gate leakage current  
- Significant VTH shifts in response to target ion concentration changes  

For MB+ detection using MB+-MMM1 functionalized devices:  
- Demonstration of size-selective response based on MOSC cavity dimensions  
- Determination of binding constant for MOSC-II-Co with MB+ (1.42 × 10⁴ M⁻¹)  
- Observation of ΔVTH proportional to MB+ activity (αMB)  
- Near-Nernstian response with 56.8 mV/decade slope  
- Lower detection limit of approximately 300 nM  

Comparative studies with conventional ISEs confirmed superior performance of the MOSC-based approach. Investigation of interfering elemental ions demonstrated exceptional selectivity against common interferents.  

For elemental ion sensing using Na+-MMM functionalized devices:  
- Demonstration of near-Nernstian response to αNa  
- Determination of selectivity coefficients (KNa, KPot)  
- Evaluation of cross-sensitivity towards molecular ions  

The example concludes with demonstration of multiplexed analysis capability, showing simultaneous detection of molecular and elemental ions in complex samples. This highlights the technology's potential for comprehensive ion analysis in real-world applications.
