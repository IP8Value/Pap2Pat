# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to doping engineered hole transport layers (HTLs) for optoelectronic devices, particularly perovskite-based solar cells. More specifically, the invention pertains to a vacuum-evaporated triple-layer HTL structure comprising n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped organic semiconductor materials, wherein the doping concentration is optimized to enhance charge carrier extraction and device stability. The invention further encompasses methods for fabricating such HTLs and their integration into perovskite solar cells to achieve improved energy conversion efficiency and operational longevity.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Solar cells are semiconductor devices that convert sunlight into electrical energy through the photovoltaic effect. Traditional solar cells rely on p-n junctions formed within crystalline silicon or other inorganic semiconductors, where absorbed photons generate electron-hole pairs that are separated by an internal electric field. However, the energy conversion efficiencies of conventional solar cells are constrained by material properties and fabrication costs.  

Organometal halide perovskites have emerged as promising light-absorbing materials due to their high absorption coefficients, tunable bandgaps, and solution-processability. Perovskite solar cells typically consist of a perovskite active layer sandwiched between electron transport layers (ETLs) and hole transport layers (HTLs). The HTL plays a critical role in extracting and transporting photogenerated holes to the electrode while blocking electrons.  

Despite their potential, perovskite solar cells face limitations in stability and performance due to defects in the HTL. Solution-processed HTLs, such as those based on spiro-OMeTAD, often suffer from pinholes that allow environmental degradation of the perovskite layer. Additionally, conventional doping methods for HTLs lack precise control over doping concentrations, leading to suboptimal energy level alignment with the perovskite valence band.  

The present invention addresses these challenges by introducing a vacuum-evaporated, doping-engineered HTL structure that eliminates pinholes and optimizes energy level matching for enhanced device performance and stability.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides an optoelectronic device structure comprising a perovskite active layer and a triple-layer hole transport layer (HTL). The HTL includes an n-type doped sublayer, an intrinsic sublayer, and a p-type doped sublayer, each formed by vacuum evaporation. The n-type doped sublayer is engineered to match its highest occupied molecular orbital (HOMO) level with the valence band maximum (VBM) of the perovskite, facilitating efficient hole extraction. The intrinsic sublayer minimizes dopant interdiffusion, while the p-type doped sublayer ensures effective hole transport to the electrode. The invention further includes methods for fabricating the HTL and integrating it into perovskite solar cells, resulting in devices with improved efficiency and air stability.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention utilizes organometal halide perovskite films as the light-absorbing layer in optoelectronic devices. Exemplary perovskite materials include methylammonium lead iodide-chloride (MAPbI3-XClX), which exhibits a bandgap suitable for solar energy conversion. The perovskite film is typically deposited on a substrate coated with a compact electron transport layer, such as titanium dioxide (TiO2).  

The hole transport layer (HTL) in photovoltaic devices is critical for efficient charge carrier extraction. Conventional HTLs, such as solution-processed spiro-OMeTAD films, often contain pinholes that degrade device performance by allowing environmental contaminants to penetrate the perovskite layer. The invention overcomes this limitation by employing a vacuum evaporation method to deposit pinhole-free HTLs.  

Vacuum-evaporated spiro-OMeTAD films exhibit superior uniformity and density compared to solution-processed films. The invention further enhances HTL performance through doping engineering, wherein the electronic properties of the HTL are modulated by incorporating n-type and p-type dopants at controlled concentrations.  

The triple-layer HTL structure comprises an n-type doped sublayer, an intrinsic sublayer, and a p-type doped sublayer. The n-type doped sublayer is formed by co-evaporating spiro-OMeTAD with an n-dopant, such as dimercaptoethane (DMC), at a vapor pressure optimized to align the HOMO level with the perovskite VBM. The intrinsic sublayer consists of undoped spiro-OMeTAD and serves as a buffer to prevent dopant interdiffusion. The p-type doped sublayer is formed by co-evaporating spiro-OMeTAD with a p-dopant, such as tetrafluorotetracyanoquinodimethane (F4-TCNQ), to facilitate hole transport to the electrode.  

Exemplary n-dopants include DMC, while p-dopants include F4-TCNQ and molybdenum trioxide (MoO3). Alternative hole transport materials (HTMs) may also be employed, provided they exhibit suitable energy levels and compatibility with vacuum evaporation.  

The energy diagram of the perovskite-based device illustrates the alignment of the HOMO levels across the n-type, intrinsic, and p-type sublayers with the perovskite VBM. This alignment creates a built-in potential that enhances hole extraction efficiency. The optimal n-type doping concentration is determined by varying the dopant vapor pressure and measuring the resulting work functions and HOMO levels using ultraviolet photoelectron spectroscopy (UPS).  

Current density versus voltage plots of perovskite solar cells incorporating the triple-layer HTL demonstrate improved fill factor (FF) and open-circuit voltage (Voc) compared to devices with conventional HTLs. Performance metrics, including power conversion efficiency (PCE), are enhanced due to the optimized energy level alignment and reduced charge recombination.  

The fabrication process of the perovskite-based device involves sequential deposition of the perovskite layer and the triple-layer HTL. The perovskite layer is formed by spin-coating a precursor solution followed by thermal annealing. The n-type doped HTL is deposited by vacuum evaporation at a controlled DMC vapor pressure, typically 1.0 × 10–5 Torr, to achieve the desired doping concentration. The intrinsic and p-type doped sublayers are subsequently deposited by co-evaporation of spiro-OMeTAD with the respective dopants.  

The vacuum evaporation system comprises a chamber with a pump unit, a substrate stage with temperature control, and evaporation units for generating dopant and HTM vapors. A deposition rate monitor ensures precise control over layer thicknesses, while a valve regulates vapor flow to maintain optimal doping concentrations. The system is particularly advantageous for volatile dopants, such as DMC, as it prevents oxidation and ensures reproducible doping levels.  

An alternative system configuration includes multiple evaporation units with individual shutters to enable sequential deposition of the n-type, intrinsic, and p-type sublayers without breaking vacuum. This configuration enhances deposition uniformity and minimizes contamination.  

The invention further includes methods for matching the HOMO level of the n-type doped sublayer with the perovskite VBM by adjusting the dopant vapor pressure. UPS measurements are employed to determine the work functions and HOMO levels of the deposited layers, enabling precise energy level alignment.  

For non-volatile dopants, an alternative fabrication method involves pre-mixing the dopant with the HTM prior to evaporation. The doping concentration is controlled by adjusting the dopant-to-HTM ratio in the source material.  

The process for fabricating an n-i-p structured HTL on a perovskite active layer includes preparing a patterned fluorine-doped tin oxide (FTO) glass substrate, depositing a compact TiO2 layer, and spin-coating the perovskite solution. The n-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layer is then deposited by vacuum evaporation at the optimal DMC vapor pressure, followed by the intrinsic and p-type doped layers. Gold contacts are finally deposited by thermal evaporation to complete the device.  

X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements confirm the crystalline structure of the perovskite layer, while UPS measurements verify the energy level alignment of the HTL sublayers. The thicknesses of the n-type doped, intrinsic, and p-type doped spiro-OMeTAD layers are typically 30 nm, 20 nm, and 30 nm, respectively, as determined by atomic force microscopy (AFM).  

The stability of solar cell devices incorporating the triple-layer HTL is evaluated by monitoring performance metrics over time. Devices with the vacuum-evaporated HTL exhibit superior air stability compared to reference samples with solution-processed HTLs, attributed to the absence of pinholes and optimized doping engineering.  

Dopant diffusion experiments demonstrate that the intrinsic sublayer effectively minimizes interdiffusion between the n-type and p-type dopants, preserving the energy level alignment. The use of MoO3 as a p-dopant further enhances device stability by forming a stable interface with the electrode.  

The invention also encompasses the application of the n-i-p doped HTL structure to other perovskite-based optoelectronic devices, such as light-emitting diodes and photodetectors. Doping engineering is presented as a versatile technique for modulating the electronic properties of organic semiconductors in various device architectures.  

Prior art references on doping-engineered structures in organic light-emitting devices are acknowledged, but the novelty of the present invention lies in the specific combination of n-type doping with predetermined concentration levels and vacuum-evaporated triple-layer HTLs for perovskite solar cells. This combination achieves unprecedented device stability and performance, as demonstrated by experimental results.
