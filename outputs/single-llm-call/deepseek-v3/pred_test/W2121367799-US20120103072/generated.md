## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of clinical diagnostics and prognostic medicine. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods for assessing mortality risk in patients undergoing maintenance hemodialysis (MHD) through analysis of lipoprotein particle characteristics, particularly low-density lipoprotein (LDL) particle size and subfraction concentrations.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

End stage renal disease (ESRD) represents a significant and growing public health concern, with over 400,000 patients currently receiving maintenance dialysis treatment in the United States alone. The prognosis for these patients remains poor, with approximately two-thirds dying within five years of initiating dialysis treatment - a mortality rate that exceeds many forms of cancer. Cardiovascular disease (CVD) accounts for nearly half of these deaths, making it the leading cause of mortality in this patient population.  

Hemodialysis treatment, while life-sustaining for ESRD patients, presents numerous challenges in clinical management. The conventional serum lipid markers that reliably predict cardiovascular risk in the general population - including LDL cholesterol (LDL-C) and HDL cholesterol (HDL-C) - demonstrate paradoxical associations in dialysis patients. This phenomenon, known as "reverse epidemiology" or the "lipid paradox," shows that lower rather than higher levels of these traditional biomarkers are associated with increased mortality risk.  

This paradox has created an urgent need for alternative cardiovascular biomarkers that can more accurately risk-stratify dialysis patients. Lipoproteins, which transport lipids through the bloodstream, exist as heterogeneous populations of particles that vary in size, density, and composition. While conventional lipid measurements assess cholesterol content within these particles, emerging evidence suggests that the physical characteristics of the lipoprotein particles themselves - particularly their size distribution and concentration profiles - may provide more clinically relevant prognostic information in chronic disease states.  

Lipoprotein classes can be broadly categorized based on density: very low-density lipoproteins (VLDL), intermediate-density lipoproteins (IDL), low-density lipoproteins (LDL), and high-density lipoproteins (HDL). Each class comprises a spectrum of particles with distinct metabolic and atherogenic properties. Recent technological advances now enable precise measurement of lipoprotein particle size distributions and subfraction concentrations, offering new opportunities for risk assessment in special populations like dialysis patients where conventional lipid measures fail.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods for predicting overall survival in maintenance hemodialysis patients through analysis of lipoprotein particle characteristics. In one embodiment, the method comprises measuring LDL particle size in a biological sample obtained from a hemodialysis patient and comparing the measured particle size to a reference level, wherein a smaller particle size indicates increased mortality risk.  

In another embodiment, the method involves measuring the concentration of very small LDL (vs-LDL) subfractions and comparing this concentration to a reference level, wherein an elevated vs-LDL concentration identifies patients with increased mortality risk. Conversely, measurement of large LDL (l-LDL) subfraction concentrations compared to reference levels can identify patients with reduced mortality risk, as higher l-LDL concentrations are associated with improved survival.  

The invention further encompasses methods that combine measurement of both vs-LDL and l-LDL subfraction concentrations, providing comprehensive risk stratification by comparing both concentrations to their respective reference levels. Additional embodiments include methods that measure either LDL particle size or HDL concentration as prognostic indicators.  

These methods utilize advanced analytical techniques, particularly ion mobility analysis, to precisely characterize lipoprotein particle distributions. The resulting data enable clinicians to more accurately predict mortality risk in hemodialysis patients compared to conventional lipid measurements, facilitating personalized treatment strategies and improved clinical outcomes.

### Sample Collection, Preparation, and Purification  

The methods of the invention begin with collection of biological samples from maintenance hemodialysis patients. Suitable samples include peripheral blood specimens collected in the fasting state, typically during mid-week dialysis sessions to minimize variability associated with the dialysis cycle. Blood samples are preferably collected in tubes containing appropriate anticoagulants such as EDTA to maintain sample integrity.  

Following collection, samples undergo preparation to isolate the lipoprotein fraction of interest. This typically involves centrifugation to separate plasma or serum from cellular components. For LDL-specific analyses, additional purification steps may include density gradient ultracentrifugation to isolate the LDL fraction (density range 1.019-1.063 g/mL). The centrifugation conditions typically involve forces between 100,000-200,000 × g for durations ranging from 2-24 hours at temperatures between 4-20°C.  

Sample processing may include dialysis steps to remove interfering substances such as salts or small molecules that could affect subsequent analyses. Appropriate buffers maintain sample pH and ionic strength during processing. Samples may be diluted with compatible buffers to achieve optimal concentrations for analytical measurements while maintaining lipoprotein particle integrity.  

Lipoprotein purification focuses on isolating LDL particles while minimizing contamination from other lipoprotein classes or plasma proteins. The purified LDL fraction can then be characterized for particle size distribution and subfraction concentrations. Very small LDL particles are defined as those with diameters between 180.0-208.2 Å, small LDL between 208.2-214.1 Å, medium LDL between 214.1-220.0 Å, and large LDL between 220.0-233.3 Å.  

Ion mobility analysis serves as the preferred analytical method for characterizing LDL particle distributions. This technique involves creating charged droplets containing lipoprotein particles, which are then introduced into a drift tube under the influence of an electric field. Particles separate based on their collision cross-section, which correlates with particle size, allowing high-resolution measurement of the LDL particle size distribution and quantification of specific subfractions.  

### Determining Prognosis  

The prognostic methods of the invention involve correlating specific LDL particle characteristics with mortality risk in maintenance hemodialysis patients. Prognosis can be expressed in various forms including absolute mortality risk, relative risk compared to reference populations, or categorical risk stratification (e.g., low, intermediate, high risk).  

The prognostic value of LDL particle characteristics remains significant after adjustment for clinical factors including age, gender, race/ethnicity, diabetes status, dialysis vintage, comorbidity burden, and dialysis dose. Statistical methods establish the independent predictive value of LDL particle measurements beyond conventional risk factors.  

Key measurements for prognosis include LDL particle size (typically expressed as mean or median diameter) and concentrations of specific LDL subfractions, particularly very small LDL (vs-LDL) and large LDL (l-LDL). Temporal changes in these parameters may provide additional prognostic information, with decreasing particle size or increasing vs-LDL concentration indicating worsening prognosis.  

Clinical interpretation involves comparing measured LDL characteristics to established reference ranges derived from control populations. Reference ranges may be expressed as percentiles, with values below the 25th percentile for LDL particle size or above the 75th percentile for vs-LDL concentration indicating increased mortality risk. Conversely, values above the 75th percentile for l-LDL concentration suggest reduced risk.  

Statistical analyses establish the relationship between lipoprotein measurements and clinical outcomes. Appropriate methods include chi-squared tests for categorical variables, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis for time-to-event data, and Cox proportional hazards regression models for multivariate adjustment. These analyses typically demonstrate that smaller LDL particle size and higher vs-LDL concentration independently predict increased mortality, while higher l-LDL concentration predicts improved survival.  

The prognostic algorithms may be implemented through computer software programs that integrate multiple risk factors. Visual Basic for Applications (VBA) programs or similar platforms can process lipoprotein measurements along with clinical data to generate comprehensive risk assessments. Such programs may incorporate machine learning approaches to refine predictive models as additional patient data become available.  

Clinical application involves obtaining a biological sample from a maintenance hemodialysis patient, measuring LDL particle size and/or subfraction concentrations by ion mobility analysis, comparing these measurements to reference levels, and determining overall survival or mortality risk based on the comparison. Results are typically reported in standardized analysis reports that interpret findings in the context of established risk categories.  

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope. These examples demonstrate the application of lipoprotein particle analysis for mortality prediction in maintenance hemodialysis patients.  

### Example 1  

A prospective cohort study enrolled 235 maintenance hemodialysis patients from eight outpatient dialysis facilities. The patient population had a mean age of 54±14 years, with 45% women and racial/ethnic diversity representative of the dialysis population. Baseline demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteristics were collected, including conventional lipid profiles (total cholesterol 143±42 mg/dL, LDL-C 76±29 mg/dL, HDL-C 37±12 mg/dL) and comprehensive inflammatory and nutritional markers.  

### Example 2  

Lipoprotein subfraction analysis revealed significant correlations between conventional lipid measures and LDL particle characteristics. Serum LDL-C showed moderate correlation with large LDL particle concentration (r=0.34, p<0.01), while HDL-C correlated positively with large HDL particles (r=0.23, p<0.01) and negatively with small LDL particles. LDL particle diameter demonstrated positive correlations with very small and large LDL subfractions, highlighting the complex relationships between particle size and concentration profiles.  

### Example 3  

Survival analysis over six years of follow-up (31% mortality) demonstrated that conventional lipid measures (LDL-C, HDL-C) showed no significant association with mortality. In contrast, analysis of LDL subfractions revealed that the highest quartile of very small LDL concentration had a death hazard ratio of 2.43 (95% CI 1.03-5.72) compared to the lowest quartile, while the highest quartile of large LDL concentration had a protective hazard ratio of 0.38 (95% CI 0.15-0.96). Similarly, increasing quartiles of LDL particle diameter showed progressively lower mortality risk, with hazard ratios of 1.00 (reference), 0.93 (0.46-1.87), 0.43 (0.21-0.89), and 0.45 (0.31-1.00) across quartiles.  

### Example 4  

Combination analyses demonstrated enhanced prognostic value when integrating LDL particle characteristics with conventional measures. Patients with both above-median LDL-C (>73 mg/dL) and above-median LDL particle diameter (>216.5 Å) showed the lowest mortality risk. Similar patterns emerged when combining total LDL particle concentration with particle diameter, with the most favorable prognosis observed in patients having both above-median total LDL particles (>216 nmol/L) and above-median particle diameter.  

## SUMMARY OF RESULTS AND DISCUSSION  

The present invention demonstrates that novel measurements of LDL particle characteristics - particularly particle size and subfraction concentrations - provide superior mortality prediction in maintenance hemodialysis patients compared to conventional lipid profiles. These findings address the critical limitation of traditional lipid measures in this population, where the "lipid paradox" renders LDL-C and HDL-C ineffective as prognostic markers.  

Key findings include the strong association between decreased LDL particle diameter (indicating smaller particle size) and increased mortality risk, independent of conventional risk factors. The concentration of specific LDL subfractions showed particularly striking prognostic value, with very small LDL particles predicting higher mortality and large LDL particles predicting improved survival. These relationships persisted after comprehensive adjustment for demographics, comorbidities, nutritional status, and inflammation.  

The invention highlights important differences in lipoprotein particle composition in dialysis patients compared to the general population. While larger LDL particles typically contain more cholesterol in healthy individuals, this relationship appears altered in chronic kidney disease, explaining why conventional LDL-C measurements fail to predict outcomes. The direct measurement of particle characteristics through ion mobility analysis bypasses these limitations, providing clinically relevant prognostic information.  

Comparative analyses demonstrated the superiority of LDL particle diameter and subfraction concentrations over conventional lipid measurements for mortality prediction. The ability to combine these novel measures with traditional lipid profiles further enhanced risk stratification, suggesting complementary rather than redundant information. These findings have important implications for clinical management of dialysis patients, enabling more accurate identification of high-risk individuals who may benefit from intensified interventions.  

While the invention focuses on LDL particle characteristics, the methods may be extended to include other lipoprotein classes (HDL, VLDL) or combinations of measurements that provide comprehensive risk assessment. The scope of the invention encompasses all variations and modifications that utilize lipoprotein particle analysis for prognosis in chronic kidney disease patients.  

It should be noted that while specific ranges and values are provided in the examples, these are illustrative rather than limiting. The invention encompasses all effective ranges and combinations of lipoprotein measurements that provide prognostic value in the described patient population. The methods may be adapted to incorporate future refinements in analytical techniques or evolving understanding of lipoprotein biology in chronic disease states.
