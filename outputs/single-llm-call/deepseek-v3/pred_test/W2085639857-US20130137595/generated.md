# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of biomarkers for lymphoma, particularly Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL), in individuals with human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection. The invention provides novel plasma protein biomarkers and methods for their detection, enabling early and non-invasive diagnosis of HL in HIV-infected populations. The disclosed biomarkers are associated with inflammatory and cancer-related pathways, offering a reliable means to identify HL before symptomatic presentation.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Lymphoma, particularly Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL), represents a significant health concern among individuals with HIV infection. Despite advances in highly active antiretroviral therapy (HAART), which has prolonged the lifespan of HIV-infected individuals, the incidence of non-AIDS-defining cancers, including HL, has risen. HIV-infected individuals face a 5.6- to 14.7-fold increased risk of developing HL compared to the general population. HL is characterized by a unique tumor composition, wherein cancerous B lymphocytes constitute less than 2% of the tumor mass, while the remainder consists of inflammatory cells recruited by cytokine secretion. This inflammatory microenvironment underscores HL as an uncontrolled inflammatory disorder, often linked to Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) in immunosuppressed individuals.  

Current methods for detecting HL rely on symptomatic presentation, often delaying diagnosis until advanced stages. Early detection is critical, as treatment regimens for early-stage HL are less intensive and associated with reduced morbidity. However, no routine, non-invasive screening tool exists for HL in HIV-infected individuals. Existing diagnostic approaches, such as biopsy and imaging, are invasive, costly, and impractical for widespread screening. Blood-based biomarkers offer a promising alternative, yet prior efforts have been limited to isolated proteins like C-reactive protein (CRP) and beta-2-microglobulin, which lack specificity for HL in the context of HIV.  

There remains an unmet need for a comprehensive panel of biomarkers that can reliably detect HL in HIV-infected individuals before symptom onset. Such a panel should account for the inflammatory and cancer-related pathways implicated in HL while minimizing confounding effects from HIV-related immune dysregulation. The present invention addresses this need by identifying a novel set of plasma proteins that differentiate HL-positive from HL-negative HIV-infected individuals, enabling early and accurate diagnosis.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method for detecting Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL) in HIV-infected individuals by measuring the levels of specific plasma protein biomarkers. These biomarkers, identified through high-throughput proteomic analysis, are associated with inflammatory responses and cancer pathways, making them highly relevant to HL pathogenesis. The method involves analyzing plasma samples using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) to quantify the abundance of these biomarkers, which collectively distinguish HL-positive from HL-negative individuals.  

Key biomarkers disclosed herein include proteins such as C-reactive protein, beta-2-microglobulin, lactate dehydrogenase, and 57 additional proteins identified for the first time as potential HL indicators. The invention further encompasses a diagnostic test kit comprising reagents for detecting these biomarkers, such as antibodies or mass spectrometry probes, and a system for analyzing the results. The diagnostic system integrates statistical algorithms to normalize data, compare biomarker levels, and generate a diagnostic output.  

The invention’s utility lies in its ability to provide a non-invasive, blood-based screening tool for HL in HIV-infected individuals, facilitating early intervention and improved treatment outcomes. By leveraging proteomic profiling, the invention overcomes the limitations of single-marker assays and offers a comprehensive approach to HL detection in high-risk populations.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The invention provides methods and systems for detecting Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL) in HIV-infected individuals using a panel of plasma protein biomarkers. The following sections describe the materials, methods, and analytical techniques employed to identify and validate these biomarkers.  

### Materials and Methods  

**Ethics Statement**  
The study protocol was approved by the George Washington University Medical Center Institutional Review Board, with written informed consent obtained from all participants. Additional approval was secured from the Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL) Institutional Review Board prior to sample transfer.  

**Human Subjects and Sample Processing**  
Plasma samples were obtained from HIV-infected individuals with and without HL, matched by age and gender. Samples were collected from HL patients who had not received chemotherapy within 30 days prior to collection. Plasma proteins were enriched by immunodepleting the 12 most abundant proteins, followed by trichloroacetic acid precipitation and tryptic digestion. Peptides were purified using C18 solid-phase extraction and stored for LC-MS analysis.  

**Liquid Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry**  
Peptides were analyzed using a high-pressure nanocapillary LC system coupled to an LTQ-Orbitrap mass spectrometer. Separation was achieved with a C18 column and a gradient elution. Mass spectra were acquired at high resolution (100,000) and matched against a mass tag database for peptide identification.  

**Data Analysis**  
Peptide abundances were normalized and subjected to statistical testing, including the non-parametric Mann-Whitney test for intensity differences and a modified Chi-square test for detection incidence. Protein inference was performed using RRollup and Protein Prophet to aggregate peptide-level data into protein abundances.  

**Ingenuity Pathway Analysis**  
Differentially regulated proteins were analyzed for biological functions using Ingenuity Pathway Analysis (IPA). Proteins with at least a 1.5-fold change between groups were evaluated for associations with inflammatory response and cancer pathways.  

### Results and Discussion  

Proteomic analysis identified 60 plasma proteins significantly elevated in HIV-infected individuals with HL compared to controls. These proteins included known markers like CRP and beta-2-microglobulin, as well as novel candidates linked to inflammation and cancer. Pathway analysis revealed enrichment for immune response and cancer-related functions, supporting their biological relevance to HL.  

Three previously reported HL biomarkers were validated in this study, confirming their utility in HIV-infected populations. The remaining 57 proteins represent novel candidates for HL detection, with potential roles in tumor-associated inflammation. The systemic nature of these biomarkers suggests they may originate from infiltrating leukocytes or the liver, rather than the rare cancerous lymphocytes.  

Confounding factors, such as HIV disease progression, were addressed by controlling for CD4 counts. The consistency of known biomarkers across HIV-positive and general populations underscores the robustness of the findings. Further validation is required to confirm the specificity and sensitivity of these biomarkers for HL diagnosis.  

In conclusion, the invention provides a comprehensive panel of plasma protein biomarkers for early HL detection in HIV-infected individuals. By integrating proteomic profiling with advanced statistical and pathway analyses, the invention offers a transformative approach to lymphoma diagnosis and management.
