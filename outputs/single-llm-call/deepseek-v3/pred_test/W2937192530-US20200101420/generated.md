# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of solar-powered water desalination and purification. More specifically, the invention pertains to a solar membrane distillation apparatus utilizing a photothermal superhydrophobic porous carbon membrane for efficient vapor generation and separation. The apparatus integrates solar thermal energy harvesting with membrane distillation technology to enable sustainable and energy-efficient desalination of saline or contaminated water sources.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Access to clean freshwater remains a critical global challenge, with over two billion people lacking reliable access to safe drinking water. Conventional desalination technologies such as reverse osmosis and thermal distillation require substantial energy inputs, often derived from fossil fuels, making them economically and environmentally unsustainable for widespread deployment. Solar desalination presents an attractive alternative by harnessing abundant solar energy, but traditional direct solar evaporation methods suffer from low efficiency due to heat losses and limited vapor generation rates.  

Recent advances have explored the use of porous carbon nanomaterials for solar evaporation, leveraging their excellent light absorption and thermal localization properties. While these nano-structured materials demonstrate improved evaporation rates compared to bulk water heating, they remain limited by salt accumulation, poor vapor transport, and inability to separate pure vapor from the saline feedwater. Membrane distillation has emerged as a promising approach combining thermal distillation with membrane separation, but conventional systems require external heating sources and suffer from membrane wetting and fouling issues.  

The present invention addresses these limitations through a novel solar membrane distillation apparatus incorporating a photothermal superhydrophobic porous carbon membrane. This integrated system combines the benefits of solar thermal evaporation with membrane-based vapor separation while overcoming key challenges of existing technologies.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a solar membrane distillation apparatus comprising a solar distillation membrane, water chamber, vapor chamber, and condenser. The solar distillation membrane consists of a graphitic foam structure coated with a hydrophobic composition to create a superhydrophobic surface. The membrane may be configured in planar, tubular, or vessel-shaped embodiments to optimize light absorption and vapor transport.  

The apparatus includes a housing with a light-transmitting wall to admit solar radiation to the photothermal membrane. The water chamber contains feedwater in contact with one side of the membrane, while the vapor chamber collects pure water vapor permeating through the membrane. A condenser converts the vapor into liquid freshwater for collection.  

Key innovations include the use of micro/nano particles embedded in the graphitic foam surface to enhance light absorption and vapor nucleation, combined with a covalently bonded hydrophobic coating to prevent membrane wetting. The system may incorporate a pre-heating chamber to improve thermal efficiency and various configurations of the distillation membrane to optimize performance for different applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The solar membrane distillation apparatus of the present invention comprises several key components that synergistically enable efficient solar-powered desalination. The central element is the solar distillation membrane, which serves dual functions as a photothermal absorber and vapor separation barrier. The membrane is fabricated from graphitic foam, a porous carbon material with high thermal conductivity and solar absorptivity.  

The graphitic foam structure features interconnected pores ranging from 10-500 micrometers in diameter, with a bulk porosity between 60-90%. The foam surface is modified with micro-carbon particles (1-100 μm) and graphite nanoparticles (10-500 nm) to create a hierarchical porous structure that enhances light absorption through multiple scattering. These particles are bonded to the foam surface using a phenolic resin matrix that is thermally cured at 300°C for one hour.  

A superhydrophobic surface is created by covalent bonding of fluorosilane molecules, specifically (heptadecafluoro-1,1,2,2-tetrahydrodecyl) trichlorosilane, to the graphitic foam and particle surfaces. The hydrophobic coating is applied via solution immersion in 0.1 wt% hexane solution followed by oven drying at 100°C. This creates a water contact angle exceeding 150°, preventing membrane wetting while allowing vapor transmission.  

The apparatus housing includes a light-transmitting wall (typically glass or transparent polymer) that admits solar radiation to the membrane surface. The water chamber contains saline feedwater in direct contact with the membrane's lower surface, while the vapor chamber collects pure water vapor that permeates through the membrane. A condenser, typically comprising cooled metal plates or tubes, converts the vapor into liquid freshwater.  

Several membrane configurations are disclosed. In the planar embodiment (FIG. 1), the membrane forms a flat panel separating the water and vapor chambers. The tubular embodiment (FIG. 7) features a cylindrical membrane with feedwater flowing through the interior and vapor collecting externally. The vessel-shaped embodiment (FIG. 8) provides an open distillation chamber for batch processing.  

The system may incorporate a pre-heating chamber to improve thermal efficiency. In one embodiment, a solar water heater or metal pipe pre-heater raises the feedwater temperature before entering the distillation chamber. The condenser may include a heat exchange conduit to recover thermal energy from the distilled water.  

Operation of the apparatus begins with solar irradiation passing through the light-transmitting wall and being absorbed by the photothermal membrane. The localized heating creates a temperature gradient across the membrane, driving vapor generation at the feedwater interface. The superhydrophobic surface prevents liquid penetration while allowing vapor transmission to the condenser.  

Key process parameters include controlling the particle size distribution (10 nm - 100 μm), pore size (0.1-10 μm), and hydrophobic coating thickness (10-100 nm) to optimize light absorption and vapor transport. The graphitic foam's porosity and thermal conductivity are tuned to balance mechanical strength and thermal performance.  

Experimental results demonstrate the apparatus achieves evaporation rates exceeding 1.5 kg m^-2 h^-1 under 1 sun illumination (1000 W m^-2), with salt rejection >99.9%. The system maintains stable performance over extended operation without membrane fouling or wetting. Temperature profiles show effective thermal localization at the membrane-water interface, with bulk feedwater temperatures remaining below 40°C.  

The invention further discloses methods for fabricating the solar distillation membrane. The process involves machining graphitic foam into the desired shape, coating with carbon particle slurry, thermal curing, and applying the hydrophobic silane coating. Quality control includes measuring water contact angles, light absorption spectra, and pore size distribution to ensure consistent performance.  

Alternative embodiments incorporate different hydrophobic coating materials, membrane geometries, and system configurations tailored for specific applications ranging from small-scale portable units to large industrial desalination plants. The apparatus may also be adapted for wastewater treatment, chemical separation, and other membrane distillation applications.

### Example  

An exemplary fabrication process begins with preparing a graphitic foam baseline support machined to 5 mm thickness. The foam exhibits a bulk porosity of 85% and average pore size of 50 μm. An intermediate porous surface layer is created by coating with a slurry of graphite particles (225-275 m^2 g^-1) and phenolic resin at 2:1 weight ratio. After drying overnight, the coated foam is thermally cured at 300°C for one hour.  

The membrane is made superhydrophobic by immersing in 0.1 wt% fluorosilane/hexane solution for 12 hours, followed by oven drying at 100°C. The finished membrane shows water contact angles of 158° and solar absorption efficiency of 95% across the visible spectrum.  

A tubular solar distillation membrane is constructed by rolling the treated graphitic foam into a cylinder with 5 cm diameter. The membrane demonstrates vapor permeation flux of 1.8 kg m^-2 h^-1 under 1 sun illumination, producing freshwater with <100 ppm salinity from 3.5% NaCl feedwater. Temperature measurements show the membrane surface reaches 85°C while the bulk feedwater remains at 35°C, confirming effective thermal localization.  

Performance testing under concentrated solar-thermal irradiation (5 suns equivalent) shows linear scaling of evaporation rate with incident power, reaching 8.5 kg m^-2 h^-1 while maintaining >99.9% salt rejection. Long-term operation over 100 hours shows no degradation in membrane performance or wetting.  

The invention thus provides a robust, scalable solution for solar-powered desalination that overcomes key limitations of existing technologies through innovative integration of photothermal materials and membrane distillation principles.
