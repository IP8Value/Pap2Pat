## BACKGROUND  

### 1. Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to the field of therapeutic antibodies and specifically to monoclonal antibodies that selectively inhibit connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels without affecting gap junction function. More particularly, the invention provides methods for treating spinal cord injuries by administering antibodies that specifically bind to Cx43 extracellular domains to prevent pathological opening of hemichannels while preserving gap junction communication between cells.  

### 2. Description of Related Art  

Traumatic spinal cord injury (SCI) represents a significant medical challenge, with approximately 13,000 new cases annually in the United States and between 250,000-500,000 cases worldwide each year. Following the initial mechanical trauma, secondary damage occurs through processes including edema, metabolic crisis, and spreading depolarizations that expand the area of spinal cord damage. This secondary injury creates a cytotoxic environment characterized by elevated levels of extracellular glutamate, lactate, potassium ions, nitric oxide, arachidonate, reactive oxygen species, and ammonia.  

Current treatments for SCI remain limited in their ability to prevent secondary damage or promote functional recovery. Existing therapeutic approaches primarily focus on acute management through surgical decompression, methylprednisolone administration, and rehabilitation therapies. However, these interventions fail to address the underlying pathological mechanisms driving secondary injury progression.  

Recent research has identified connexin proteins, particularly Cx43, as playing a significant role in SCI pathology. Cx43 forms both gap junctions that connect adjacent cells and hemichannels that open to the extracellular space. While gap junctions facilitate intercellular communication, pathological opening of hemichannels contributes to secondary injury by releasing pro-inflammatory and excitotoxic factors. Previous studies using Cx43-deficient mice or antisense oligonucleotides have shown reduced inflammation and improved recovery after SCI, but these approaches indiscriminately affect both gap junctions and hemichannels.  

There remains an unmet need for targeted therapies that specifically inhibit pathological Cx43 hemichannel opening while preserving beneficial gap junction function. The development of selective hemichannel-blocking agents would represent a significant advancement in SCI treatment by addressing a key mechanism of secondary injury while maintaining essential intercellular communication.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods for treating spinal cord injury by administering an antibody that specifically binds to connexin 43 (Cx43) and selectively inhibits hemichannel opening without affecting gap junction function. The antibody, designated MHC1, binds to extracellular domains of Cx43 and prevents pathological hemichannel opening induced by spinal cord trauma or inflammatory factors.  

The invention encompasses methods of treating spinal cord injury comprising administering to a subject in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of an anti-Cx43 antibody that specifically inhibits hemichannel activity. The antibody is preferably administered within 30 minutes after injury, though therapeutic effects may be observed when administered within 30 days post-injury. Administration routes include intraperitoneal, intravenous, or intracerebroventricular delivery.  

The MHC1 antibody demonstrates high binding affinity for Cx43 extracellular domains, with an EC50 of approximately 7.63 × 10^-2 nM as measured by ELISA and a KD of 421 nM as measured by bio-layer interferometry. The antibody specifically inhibits hemichannel opening induced by low extracellular calcium/magnesium conditions, mechanical stress, or inflammatory cytokines such as IL-1β, while having no detectable effect on gap junction communication or pannexin channel function.  

In various embodiments, the invention provides:  
1) A method of treating spinal cord injury comprising administering an anti-Cx43 antibody that selectively inhibits hemichannel opening;  
2) Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the anti-Cx43 antibody and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier;  
3) Methods for reducing secondary injury after spinal cord trauma by blocking pathological hemichannel opening;  
4) Methods for preserving neuronal tissue and improving functional recovery after spinal cord injury;  
5) Humanized or chimeric versions of the MHC1 antibody for therapeutic use;  
6) Nucleic acid molecules encoding the antibody heavy and light chain variable regions;  
7) Methods for producing the antibody through recombinant expression systems;  
8) Diagnostic methods for detecting active hemichannels in spinal cord tissue;  
9) Combination therapies pairing the antibody with other SCI treatments.  

Therapeutic benefits observed in animal models include significant improvements in hind limb motor function, reduced glial scar formation, preservation of white and gray matter, decreased lesion size, and protection of neuronal viability. These effects are particularly pronounced when the antibody is administered during the acute phase following injury.  

## DESCRIPTION  

Connexin proteins form intercellular channels that play critical roles in cellular communication and tissue homeostasis. The connexin family comprises 21 members in humans, with Cx43 being the most widely expressed isoform in various tissues including the central nervous system. Cx43 assembles into two distinct types of channels: gap junctions that directly connect cytoplasm of adjacent cells, and hemichannels that open to the extracellular space under specific conditions.  

Gap junctions facilitate direct transfer of ions, metabolites, and signaling molecules between coupled cells, playing essential roles in tissue coordination and homeostasis. In contrast, hemichannels normally remain closed but can open pathologically in response to cellular stress, mechanical trauma, or inflammatory stimuli. Pathological hemichannel opening contributes to disease processes by allowing uncontrolled flux of ions and release of signaling molecules such as ATP and glutamate.  

In the spinal cord, Cx43 is predominantly expressed by astrocytes where it mediates both gap junction coupling and hemichannel activity. Following spinal cord injury, Cx43 expression becomes upregulated and hemichannel opening contributes to secondary injury through release of cytotoxic factors that promote inflammation, glial scar formation, and neuronal death. While complete inhibition of Cx43 (affecting both gap junctions and hemichannels) shows some therapeutic benefit, this approach also eliminates important gap junction-mediated communication between astrocytes.  

The present invention specifically targets pathological hemichannel opening while preserving gap junction function through use of monoclonal antibodies that bind extracellular domains of Cx43. The MHC1 antibody was developed through immunization with Cx43 extracellular domain peptides and subsequent hybridoma generation. The antibody demonstrates exquisite specificity for Cx43, showing no cross-reactivity with pannexin channels or other connexin isoforms.  

### I. ANTIBODIES  

The invention provides isolated antibodies that specifically bind to connexin 43 (Cx43) and selectively inhibit hemichannel opening without affecting gap junction function. The term "antibody" encompasses intact immunoglobulins as well as antigen-binding fragments including Fab, F(ab')2, Fv, and single-chain Fv fragments. The antibodies comprise complementarity determining regions (CDRs) that mediate binding to Cx43 extracellular domains, particularly the second extracellular loop (E2 domain).  

The MHC1 antibody contains heavy and light chain variable regions with CDRs that confer specific binding to Cx43. The antibody may be produced as a mouse monoclonal, chimeric antibody with mouse variable regions and human constant regions, or fully humanized antibody. Production methods include hybridoma technology, recombinant expression in mammalian cells (e.g., HEK293), or phage display techniques.  

Antibody variants with modified CDRs are also contemplated, including substitutions, insertions or deletions that maintain or enhance binding affinity for Cx43. The antibodies may be conjugated to detectable labels (e.g., fluorescent dyes) or therapeutic agents (e.g., anti-inflammatory drugs) for combined diagnostic and therapeutic applications.  

### II. TREATMENT OF DISEASES  

The invention provides methods for treating spinal cord injury by administering a therapeutically effective amount of an anti-Cx43 antibody that selectively inhibits hemichannel opening. Treatment is preferably initiated during the acute phase following injury (within 30 minutes to 30 days post-injury) when pathological hemichannel activity is most pronounced.  

Pharmaceutical compositions comprise the antibody formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers such as saline, buffers, or stabilizers. The compositions may be administered via intraperitoneal, intravenous, or intracerebroventricular routes. Dosage regimens are adjusted based on injury severity, with typical doses ranging from 10-50 mg/kg body weight.  

Therapeutic effects include:  
- Reduction of secondary injury expansion  
- Preservation of neuronal viability  
- Attenuation of glial scar formation  
- Improvement in motor function recovery  
- Maintenance of white and gray matter integrity  

Combination therapies pairing the antibody with other SCI treatments (e.g., methylprednisolone, rehabilitation therapies) are also provided. The antibody may be co-administered with agents that enhance blood-spinal cord barrier permeability to improve delivery to injury sites.  

### III. KITS AND DIAGNOSTICS  

The invention provides kits containing the anti-Cx43 antibody along with instructions for therapeutic use in spinal cord injury. Diagnostic kits include labeled antibodies for detecting Cx43 hemichannel activity in tissue samples using techniques such as immunohistochemistry or dye uptake assays.  

### IV. EXAMPLES  

#### Example 1: Antibody Generation and Characterization  
The MHC1 monoclonal antibody was generated by immunizing mice with Cx43 extracellular domain peptides. Hybridomas were screened for Cx43 specificity and hemichannel-blocking activity. The antibody demonstrated high affinity binding to Cx43 (EC50 = 7.63 × 10^-2 nM) and selectively inhibited hemichannel opening induced by low calcium/magnesium conditions or mechanical stress.  

#### Example 2: Therapeutic Efficacy in Spinal Cord Injury Models  
In mouse models of spinal cord contusion injury, MHC1 administration (25 mg/kg, i.p.) within 30 minutes post-injury:  
- Reduced Evans blue dye uptake by 67% compared to controls  
- Improved Basso Mouse Scale scores by 2.5 points at 8 weeks  
- Decreased glial scar formation by 45%  
- Preserved 38% more neuronal tissue in lesion areas  

#### Example 3: Mechanism of Action Studies  
MHC1 specifically blocked Cx43 hemichannel opening induced by IL-1β or mechanical stress in primary astrocytes, while having no effect on gap junction communication as measured by dye coupling assays. The antibody showed no activity against pannexin channels in control experiments.  

#### Example 4: Pharmacokinetics and Biodistribution  
Following intraperitoneal administration in injured mice, MHC1 penetrated the blood-spinal cord barrier and localized to injury sites within 4 hours. Antibody levels remained detectable for 7 days post-administration.  

#### Example 5: Humanized Antibody Development  
The mouse MHC1 variable regions were grafted onto human IgG frameworks to create a humanized version with comparable binding affinity and hemichannel-blocking activity. The humanized antibody showed reduced immunogenicity in primate models.
