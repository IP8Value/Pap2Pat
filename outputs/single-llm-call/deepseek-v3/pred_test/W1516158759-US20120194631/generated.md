## BACKGROUND

Videoconferencing technology has evolved significantly in recent decades, enabling remote participants to engage in real-time audio and visual communication across geographical distances. Traditional videoconferencing systems typically comprise display screens, cameras, microphones, and speakers arranged to facilitate bidirectional communication between two or more locations. These systems attempt to recreate face-to-face meeting experiences by transmitting visual and auditory information between participants situated in different physical spaces.

Despite technological advancements, conventional videoconferencing systems exhibit several inherent limitations that impair natural communication dynamics. A primary limitation involves the difficulty in accurately conveying and interpreting directional cues between participants. In physical face-to-face interactions, humans rely extensively on subtle spatial cues including gaze direction, body orientation, and pointing gestures to establish mutual awareness of attention focus. Current videoconferencing implementations fail to faithfully reproduce these spatial characteristics, resulting in disrupted attention projection and awareness between participants.

The challenges in determining directionality become particularly pronounced in hub-and-satellite meeting configurations where multiple participants are collocated at a hub location while one or more participants join remotely from satellite locations. In such arrangements, the satellite participant's display typically shows all hub participants simultaneously, while the hub participants view the satellite participant through a single display proxy. This asymmetrical configuration creates ambiguity regarding the satellite participant's focus of attention, as the hub participants cannot precisely discern whom the satellite is addressing or referencing at any given moment.

Other environments exhibit similar difficulties in maintaining accurate directional awareness during mediated communication. These include telepresence robotics systems, virtual meeting spaces, and hybrid collaboration environments combining physical and virtual participants. Across these varied implementations, the common challenge remains the accurate projection and interpretation of attention focus between participants separated by physical distance and technological mediation.

## SUMMARY

The present invention discloses a novel communication system designed to improve attention awareness in videoconferencing environments, particularly in hub-and-satellite meeting configurations. The system comprises multiple interconnected modules that collectively process both explicit and implicit control inputs to generate appropriate directional outputs indicating a participant's focus of attention.

The communication system receives explicit control inputs through deliberate participant actions such as mouse movements, touchscreen interactions, or dedicated control device manipulations. These inputs directly specify desired attention directions according to the participant's conscious intent. The system simultaneously processes implicit control inputs derived from natural participant behaviors including head position, eye gaze direction, and body orientation. These inputs reflect subconscious attention cues that participants naturally exhibit during communication.

Processing modules within the system analyze both explicit and implicit control inputs according to predetermined algorithms to generate corresponding output signals. A mode selection module applies configurable selection criteria to determine the most appropriate output representation based on the available inputs. The system dynamically adjusts the position of an attention indicator based on the selected output, providing continuous feedback regarding the participant's current focus of attention.

Explicit input mechanisms include graphical user interface elements, physical control devices, and gesture recognition systems that translate deliberate participant actions into directional control signals. Implicit input mechanisms incorporate sensor arrays, computer vision algorithms, and motion tracking technologies to detect and interpret natural participant behaviors indicative of attention focus.

Output mechanisms encompass various implementations including mechanical vane systems, solid-state display elements, and augmented reality overlays. These mechanisms physically or virtually indicate attention direction through movement, illumination patterns, or graphical representations. The system supports multiple output modalities simultaneously, allowing for flexible deployment across different videoconferencing environments and hardware configurations.
