# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present application claims benefit of prior filed patent application No. [INSERT APPLICATION NUMBER], filed [INSERT DATE], the contents of which are incorporated herein by reference in their entirety.  

Bipolar disorder (BD) is a major psychiatric disease characterized by episodes of depression and mania or hypomania interspaced by periods of euthymia. With a typical age of onset in late adolescence or early adulthood, BD is a major health problem that requires continuous monitoring and often lifelong treatment. BD places a substantial economic burden on health-care systems and society. A recent Swedish resource use study estimated the average annual cost at 28,011 euro/year/patient, while a 2009 US study estimated the direct and indirect costs of BD at 151 billion dollars annually.  

Although family and twin studies demonstrate the importance of genetic factors affecting susceptibility to BD, the precise pathogenesis remains poorly understood. Emerging evidence suggests BD may be a neuroinflammatory disorder where relapses exhibit toxicity, highlighting the importance of early detection to prevent negative prognosis. Accumulating data implicates mitochondrial dysfunction as playing a key role in BD pathogenesis, supported by findings including calcium dysregulation, disturbed energy metabolism, oxidative phosphorylation abnormalities, and abnormalities in cellular resilience and synaptic plasticity from microarray studies, biochemical analyses, neuroimaging, and post-mortem brain examinations.  

Metabolomics, the profiling of small-molecule metabolites, provides potential to characterize specific metabolic phenotypes associated with disease. Metabolomics offers advantages over other 'omics' techniques by directly sampling metabolic changes and integrating information from gene, transcript, and protein level alterations along with posttranslational modifications. Previous metabolomic analyses of post-mortem BD brain samples revealed altered levels of myo-inositol, creatine, glutamate, lactate and phosphocholine, suggesting mitochondrial dysfunction. However, post-mortem brain analyses are limited by rapid post-mortem metabolic changes affecting small molecule concentrations. Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) represents the most relevant sampling substrate for in vivo brain disorder studies as it reflects brain metabolic status and biochemistry.  

Capillary electrophoresis time-of-flight mass spectrometry (CE-TOFMS) constitutes a state-of-the-art metabolome analysis platform offering extremely high resolution, versatility, and ability to simultaneously quantify virtually all charged low-molecular-weight compounds in a sample. While CE-TOFMS has demonstrated metabolite alterations in plasma from autism spectrum disorder and schizophrenia patients, no reports exist applying this technique to CSF samples from BD patients. There remains an urgent unmet need for improved methods of diagnosing and treating BD, particularly through identification of reliable biomarkers and novel therapeutic targets addressing the underlying mitochondrial dysfunction.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel methods for diagnosing bipolar disorder by measuring biomarker levels in biological samples. In one embodiment, the method comprises measuring isocitrate levels in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples from a subject and comparing them to control levels, where increased isocitrate indicates BD. Another embodiment measures biomarker levels in serum samples. The invention further provides methods for confirming BD diagnosis, determining predisposition to BD, and monitoring treatment efficacy by serial biomarker measurements.  

The invention also provides methods for identifying compounds useful for preventing or treating BD. One embodiment involves contacting eukaryotic cells with test compounds and measuring expression levels of isocitrate dehydrogenase 3 (IDH3) genes, where compounds normalizing IDH3 expression represent candidate therapeutics. Another embodiment administers test compounds to animal models and measures biomarker levels in CSF or serum, with normalization indicating therapeutic potential.  

Additional embodiments measure combinations of biomarkers including isocitrate, serine, and other citric acid cycle intermediates. The methods may utilize existing sample data or newly collected specimens, with control samples representing healthy subjects. Sample collection timing accounts for euthymic states and medication effects. Statistical analyses establish diagnostic thresholds based on percentage changes from control levels.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION  

The invention provides biomarkers for BD diagnosis comprising metabolites in the citric acid cycle pathway, particularly isocitrate. In CSF samples, increased isocitrate levels compared to controls indicate BD. Serum biomarkers may include altered levels of serine and other amino acids. Diagnostic thresholds are established by comparing patient metabolite amounts to control ranges using statistical analyses accounting for age, sex, and other variables.  

Sample collection methods include lumbar puncture for CSF and venipuncture for serum, preferably during euthymic periods. Samples undergo processing including centrifugation, aliquoting, and storage at -80°C. Control samples represent age- and sex-matched healthy subjects without psychiatric history. Multiple measurements may be averaged to account for biological variation.  

The invention encompasses measuring biomarkers in combination, with preferred embodiments analyzing at least two and up to five markers simultaneously. Specific combinations include isocitrate with serine, citrate, or cis-aconitate. Diagnostic accuracy improves when combining CSF and serum biomarkers.  

Clinical assessments accompany biomarker testing, including standardized psychiatric interviews and rating scales. The methods apply to BD types I, II, and NOS, with severity rated using CGI and GAF scales. Diagnostic validity persists regardless of therapeutic regimens, though medication history is recorded.  

For compound identification, eukaryotic cells (e.g., neurons, glia) are contacted with test compounds and IDH3 gene expression measured by qPCR. Compounds increasing IDH3A and IDH3B expression represent candidates. Animal models receive test compounds and CSF/serum biomarkers are monitored. Compounds normalizing isocitrate levels indicate therapeutic potential.  

Test compounds include small molecules, biologics, and natural products. Formulations comprise pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. Administration routes include oral, parenteral, and intrathecal. Kits contain reagents for biomarker measurement and instructions for use.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

A study recruited 54 male BD patients and 40 male healthy controls. CSF sampling occurred during euthymic periods after overnight fast. CE-TOFMS analysis measured 116 metabolites, detecting 72 in CSF. Univariate analysis identified 13 significantly altered compounds including citric acid cycle intermediates. Multivariate logistic regression revealed isocitrate as independently associated with BD (OR=4.402, 95% CI 2.249-7.266).  

Post-mortem brain analyses showed decreased IDH3A and IDH3B mRNA in BD dorsolateral prefrontal cortex. Protein levels of IDH3A were reduced in BD cerebellum. Genetic analyses found no significant associations between IDH/ACO genes and BD. Chronic lithium or valproate treatment in rats did not alter CSF isocitrate levels or brain IDH3 expression.  

These results demonstrate CSF isocitrate as a BD biomarker unrelated to medication effects. Decreased brain IDH3 expression suggests mitochondrial dysfunction in BD pathogenesis.
