# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates generally to semiconductor materials and more specifically to improved methods for fabricating light-emitting diode (LED) structures based on indium gallium nitride (InGaN) and gallium nitride (GaN) materials. Group III-V nitride semiconductor materials have become increasingly important for optoelectronic devices, particularly for LEDs emitting in the blue and green spectral regions. These materials offer wide bandgap tunability, high thermal stability, and excellent electrical properties that make them suitable for high-performance lighting applications.  

Conventional GaN-based LEDs typically utilize multi-quantum well (MQW) structures comprising alternating layers of InGaN potential wells and GaN potential barriers. These structures are commonly grown on sapphire or silicon carbide (SiC) substrates due to the lack of commercially viable GaN single-crystal substrates. However, these substrate materials present several limitations, including high cost, mechanical hardness, and challenges in device fabrication processes. Silicon substrates have emerged as an alternative due to their low cost, availability in large sizes, high surface quality, and compatibility with established processing techniques.  

A significant challenge in the fabrication of InGaN/GaN MQW structures is achieving high crystal quality in the InGaN potential wells, particularly for green LEDs which require high indium content. The growth of these structures is complicated by the tendency of InGaN layers to decompose during subsequent high-temperature processing steps, leading to non-uniform indium incorporation and poor interface quality between wells and barriers. Conventional fabrication methods often result in heterogeneous indium distribution, degraded optical performance, and reduced LED efficiency.  

There remains an unmet need for improved methods of fabricating the active regions of GaN-based LEDs that can overcome these limitations. Specifically, methods are needed that can stabilize the InGaN potential wells during high-temperature processing, maintain abrupt interfaces between wells and barriers, and achieve uniform indium distribution throughout the quantum well structure. The present invention addresses these challenges through novel processing techniques that significantly improve the quality and performance of InGaN/GaN MQW structures.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a method for fabricating an active region of a GaN-based light-emitting diode (LED) that overcomes the limitations of conventional approaches. The method involves growing a single InGaN/GaN quantum well structure with improved stability and interface quality through controlled processing steps.  

The method comprises fabricating an InGaN potential well layer on a substrate, followed by depositing a GaN cap layer at the same temperature as the potential well growth. This cap layer serves to protect the InGaN layer during subsequent high-temperature processing. The structure is then subjected to an annealing process that stabilizes the potential well while maintaining uniform indium distribution. Following this stabilization, a GaN potential barrier layer is grown at an elevated temperature to complete the quantum well structure.  

By implementing this sequence of fabrication steps, the invention achieves several advantages over conventional methods. The GaN cap layer prevents decomposition of the InGaN potential well during temperature ramping, maintaining homogeneous indium incorporation. The annealing process further stabilizes the well structure and improves crystal quality. The resulting quantum well structure exhibits abrupt interfaces between wells and barriers, uniform fluorescence characteristics, and improved optical performance compared to conventionally fabricated structures.  

This method is particularly advantageous for producing green-emitting LEDs, where high indium content InGaN layers are required. The invention enables fabrication of high-quality InGaN/GaN MQW structures on silicon substrates, providing a cost-effective solution for solid-state lighting applications while overcoming the limitations of conventional fabrication techniques.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Overview  

The present invention provides a comprehensive process for growing high-quality InGaN/GaN multi-quantum well (MQW) structures suitable for light-emitting diode applications. The method addresses key challenges in conventional fabrication approaches, particularly the decomposition of InGaN potential wells during high-temperature processing steps.  

The process begins with the growth of a single InGaN/GaN quantum well structure on a suitable substrate, preferably silicon (111). The structure comprises an InGaN potential well layer grown under optimized conditions to achieve the desired indium content for specific emission wavelengths. Following the well growth, a critical innovation of the present method involves depositing a GaN cap layer at the same temperature as the potential well growth. This cap layer, with a carefully controlled thickness, serves as a protective barrier during subsequent temperature ramping.  

The temperature versus time profile of the process is carefully designed to ensure proper stabilization of the quantum well structure. After cap layer deposition, the structure undergoes an annealing process that further stabilizes the potential well while maintaining uniform indium distribution. This step is crucial for preventing indium segregation and maintaining crystal quality.  

Following stabilization, the GaN potential barrier layer is grown at an elevated temperature, typically about 120°C higher than the well growth temperature. The complete process enables fabrication of quantum well structures with abrupt interfaces, uniform indium distribution, and excellent optical properties.  

The conventional process for fabricating active regions of GaN-based LEDs typically involves growing the InGaN potential well followed immediately by temperature ramping to grow the GaN barrier layer. This approach often leads to decomposition of the InGaN layer during temperature increase, resulting in non-uniform indium distribution and poor interface quality. The present invention overcomes these limitations through the introduction of the protective cap layer and optimized thermal processing sequence.  

The advantages of the new method include improved crystal quality of the InGaN potential wells, more abrupt interfaces between wells and barriers, more uniform indium distribution, and consequently better optical performance of the resulting LED structures. These improvements are particularly significant for green-emitting LEDs where high indium content InGaN layers are required.  

## EXAMPLE  

An exemplary embodiment of the invention involves fabricating an active region for a green-emitting GaN-based LED on a silicon (111) substrate. The process begins with substrate preparation, including degreasing and etching to remove surface contamination and oxide layers. Following in-situ heating in hydrogen at 1100°C, a thin aluminum film is deposited on the silicon surface to prevent reaction between silicon and subsequent gallium-containing layers.  

A stress-releasing layer structure is then grown, comprising 0.3 μm of AlN/AlGaN, followed by a 3 μm thick silicon-doped GaN layer. An InGaN strain pre-relief layer of 200 nm thickness is grown before commencing the multi-quantum well structure.  

The active region fabrication proceeds with growth of six periods of InGaN/GaN multi-quantum wells. Each period consists of three distinct layers: an InGaN potential well layer, a GaN cap layer grown at the same temperature as the well, and a GaN barrier layer grown at a temperature 120°C higher than the well temperature.  

In this exemplary embodiment, three different cap layer thicknesses were investigated: 8 Å (Sample A), 15 Å (Sample B), and 30 Å (Sample C). The samples were characterized using double crystal X-ray diffractometry (DCXRD), fluorescence microscopy (FLM), and photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy.  

XRD analysis revealed that samples with 15 Å and 30 Å cap layers exhibited clear satellite peaks, indicating well-defined quantum well/barrier periodic structures with abrupt interfaces. Higher-order satellite peaks were observable for these samples, confirming excellent interface quality. In contrast, the sample with only 8 Å cap layer showed no discernible satellite peaks, indicating degradation of the quantum well structure during temperature ramping.  

Fluorescence microscopy images demonstrated that increasing cap layer thickness resulted in more uniform fluorescence patterns, indicating more homogeneous indium distribution in the quantum wells. The sample with 30 Å cap layer showed the most uniform fluorescence, while the 8 Å sample exhibited bright spots indicative of indium segregation.  

Photoluminescence measurements confirmed the optical advantages of the invention, with samples having thicker cap layers showing increased PL intensity and narrower full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) values. The peak emission wavelength red-shifted with increasing cap thickness, from 500 nm for the 8 Å sample to 530 nm for the 30 Å sample, demonstrating improved indium incorporation and quantum well quality.  

This exemplary embodiment demonstrates that the GaN cap layer is a critical parameter for achieving high-quality InGaN/GaN MQW structures. The cap layer thickness of 30 Å provided optimal protection for the InGaN potential well during subsequent high-temperature processing, resulting in the best structural and optical properties. The invention thus enables fabrication of high-efficiency green LEDs with superior performance characteristics compared to conventionally fabricated devices.
