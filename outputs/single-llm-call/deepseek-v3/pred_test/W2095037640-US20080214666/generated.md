## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Fatty acids (FAs) represent a physiologically important class of molecules involved in energy storage, membrane structure, and various signaling pathways. While long-chain even-numbered saturated and unsaturated FAs have been extensively studied, monomethyl branched-chain fatty acids (mmBCFAs) remain less characterized despite their ubiquity in nature. mmBCFAs, such as C15ISO and C17ISO, are saturated tetradecanoic and hexadecanoic FAs with a single methyl group appended on the carbon next to the terminal carbon. These molecules are present in significant quantities in various bacterial genera, where they contribute to membrane function by regulating fluidity and proton permeability.  

Although comprehensive reports on mmBCFAs in eukaryotes are limited, sporadic data indicate their presence in fungi, plants, and animals. In mammals, mmBCFAs have been detected in tissues such as skin, Vernix caseosa, harderian and sebaceous glands, hair, brain, blood, and cancer cells. The conservation of mmBCFAs across diverse organisms suggests important and potentially unique physiological functions. For example, C21anteISO is the major covalently bound FA in mammalian hair fibers, and its removal results in loss of hydrophobicity. Additionally, C17anteISO esterified to cholesterol has been shown to bind and activate enzymes of protein biosynthesis. mmBCFAs have also been implicated in disease conditions such as brain deficiency and cancer, with C15ISO acting as a selective apoptosis inducer in human cancer cells.  

Despite their physiological significance, the biosynthesis and regulation of mmBCFAs in eukaryotes remain poorly understood. In bacteria, mmBCFA biosynthesis involves branched-chain α-keto-acids derived from leucine, isoleucine, and valine, which serve as primers for FA elongation. Key enzymes in this pathway include branched-chain aminotransferase (BCAT) and the branched-chain α-keto-acid dehydrogenase (BCKAD) complex. However, analogous enzymes have not been systematically characterized in eukaryotic systems.  

Given the potential importance of mmBCFAs in health and disease, there is a need to elucidate their biosynthetic pathways, regulatory mechanisms, and physiological roles in eukaryotes. The present invention addresses these gaps by providing novel methods and compositions for studying and manipulating mmBCFA-related processes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### mmBCFA in Eukaryotes  

The invention demonstrates that mmBCFAs are essential for growth and development in eukaryotic organisms. Using the nematode *Caenorhabditis elegans* as a model system, the invention reveals that mmBCFAs, particularly C15ISO and C17ISO, are synthesized de novo and play critical roles in larval development and adult physiology. Disruption of mmBCFA biosynthesis leads to severe developmental defects, including larval arrest, which can be rescued by dietary supplementation with specific mmBCFAs.  

### Biosynthesis of mmBCFA in *C. elegans*  

The invention identifies two fatty acid elongation enzymes, ELO-5 and ELO-6, as key players in mmBCFA biosynthesis in *C. elegans*. ELO-5 is involved in the production of C15ISO and possibly C17ISO, while ELO-6 elongates C15ISO to C17ISO. These enzymes are expressed in the gut and other tissues, highlighting the systemic importance of mmBCFAs. The invention further shows that mmBCFA biosynthesis in *C. elegans* utilizes branched-chain α-keto-acids derived from amino acids, similar to bacterial pathways, and involves the BCKAD complex for primer synthesis.  

### Essentiality of mmBCFA for Growth and Development  

The invention establishes that mmBCFAs are indispensable for *C. elegans* growth and development. RNAi-mediated suppression of *elo-5* or *elo-6* results in mmBCFA deficiency, leading to larval arrest or progressive developmental defects. These phenotypes are specifically rescued by supplementation with C17ISO or C17anteISO, indicating the unique physiological role of these mmBCFAs. The invention also demonstrates that mmBCFA levels are developmentally regulated, with C17ISO accumulating during oogenesis and early larval stages.  

### ACS-1 Requirement for Embryogenesis  

The invention identifies ACS-1, a mmBCFA-specific acetyl-CoA synthetase, as essential for embryogenesis in *C. elegans*. Suppression of *acs-1* causes embryonic lethality, which is linked to defects in eggshell and cuticle formation. The invention proposes a model wherein ACS-1 products interact with ZEN-4, a kinesin-like protein, to mediate proper embryonic development.  

### Genetic Interaction Between *zen-4* and *acs-1*  

The invention reveals a genetic interaction between *zen-4* and *acs-1*, suggesting that mmBCFAs or their derivatives are required for ZEN-4 function during embryogenesis. This interaction underscores the importance of mmBCFAs in cytoskeletal and cellular processes critical for development.  

### Effect of ACS-1 on Eggshell and Adult Cuticle Formation  

The invention shows that ACS-1 activity is crucial for the formation of protective layers such as the eggshell and adult cuticle. Disruption of *acs-1* leads to osmotic sensitivity in embryos, resembling phenotypes observed in mutants with defective eggshell structures. This finding highlights the structural role of mmBCFAs in maintaining cellular integrity.  

### Relationship Between mmBCFA and DAF Pathway  

The invention demonstrates a connection between mmBCFA levels and the DAF-2/DAF-16 insulin-signaling pathway in *C. elegans*. mmBCFA deficiency activates DAF-16 nuclear translocation and upregulates genes such as *pnk-1* and *sod-3*, which are associated with stress response and longevity. This genetic interaction suggests that mmBCFAs modulate insulin signaling to coordinate growth and metabolic homeostasis.  

### Link Between mmBCFA Biosynthesis and Protein Uptake  

The invention identifies a link between mmBCFA biosynthesis and protein uptake, mediated by the oligopeptide transporter PEP-2. RNAi of *pep-2* results in reduced mmBCFA levels and slow postembryonic development, indicating that mmBCFAs may regulate nutrient sensing and utilization.  

### Transcriptional Suppression of *elo-5* by TOR Pathway  

The invention shows that *elo-5* is a downstream target of the TOR pathway, which integrates nutrient availability with growth and metabolism. Inhibition of the TOR pathway suppresses *elo-5* expression, suggesting that mmBCFA biosynthesis is tightly coupled to cellular energy status.  

### Effect of Exogenous mmBCFA on *pnk-1* Expression  

The invention reveals that high levels of exogenous mmBCFAs downregulate *pnk-1*, a pantothenate kinase involved in coenzyme A metabolism. This feedback regulation suggests a mechanism by which mmBCFAs modulate their own biosynthesis and metabolic integration.  

### Role of mmBCFA in Coordination of Food Signaling and Energy Expenditure  

The invention proposes that mmBCFAs act as signaling molecules to coordinate food sensing and energy expenditure. mmBCFA levels influence the expression of genes involved in lipid metabolism and insulin signaling, positioning mmBCFAs as key regulators of metabolic homeostasis.

The invention demonstrates that mammalian cells can elongate exogenous short-chain mmBCFAs, such as C13ISO, into longer-chain forms like C15ISO and C17ISO.
