# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to methods for analyzing glycans in biological samples, particularly for detecting glycan changes associated with tumors. Glycans, which are carbohydrate chains attached to proteins through posttranslational modifications, play critical roles in various biological processes including cell signaling, immune responses, and cancer pathogenesis. N-linked glycans, specifically attached to asparagine residues, are abundant in membrane and secreted proteins and have been detected in human serum proteins such as immunoglobulins. Alterations in glycosylation patterns have been observed in sera from cancer patients, suggesting that glycans could serve as valuable biomarkers for disease diagnosis.  

Current cancer screening methods, such as those relying on glycoprotein markers like CA-125 or CEA, have limitations in sensitivity and specificity, particularly for early-stage cancer detection. While mass spectrometry techniques, including matrix-assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-TOF) mass spectrometry, have been employed for glycomic analysis, challenges remain in achieving reproducible and quantitative measurements. Conventional MALDI-TOF methods often suffer from variability in glycan extraction, loading, and ionization efficiency, leading to inconsistent results.  

The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing novel processes for glycan extraction and loading that enhance reproducibility in MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry. Specifically, the invention provides improved methods for sample preparation, glycan release, and solid-phase extraction, which minimize the coefficient of variation in peak intensity measurements. These advancements enable more reliable detection of tumor-specific glycan biomarkers, particularly for ovarian cancer screening.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides an analytical method for profiling glycans in biological samples with enhanced reproducibility in MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry. The method comprises a series of optimized steps including sample preparation, glycan extraction, glycan loading onto MALDI target plates, and mass spectrometric analysis. A key aspect of the invention is the reduction of the coefficient of variation in peak intensity measurements to below 15%, ensuring consistent and reliable quantitative data.  

The sample preparation step involves denaturing proteins in a human serum sample through controlled heating and cooling cycles. Glycans are subsequently released from denatured proteins using a glycan-releasing enzyme, such as peptide-N-glycosidase F (PNGase F), under optimized reaction conditions including microwave irradiation. The released glycans are then separated from proteins via ethanol precipitation and solid-phase extraction using graphitized carbon cartridges.  

The glycan loading step employs specialized MALDI target plates, referred to as Plate #1 and Plate #2. Plate #1 serves as a micro-reactor for initial sample processing, while Plate #2 is a sample-loading plate with pre-spotted matrix for enhanced ionization efficiency. The surface materials and preparation of Plate #2, including the use of a micro-focus self-condensing design, contribute to improved signal reproducibility.  

Preferred embodiments of the invention include specific protocols for denaturing proteins, treating samples with PNGase F, and separating glycans. The invention also details optimal conditions for the glycan-releasing enzyme reaction, matrix composition, and Plate #2 preparation. Additionally, the invention provides methods for selecting tumor-specific glycan markers and establishing cut-off values for cancer screening applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The present invention utilizes MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry for high-throughput analysis of glycans extracted from human serum samples. A critical innovation is the microwave irradiation-assisted enzyme digestion reaction, which significantly enhances the efficiency and reproducibility of glycan release. Following digestion, the glycans are analyzed using Plate #2, a specially designed MALDI target plate that ensures uniform sample deposition and ionization.  

The analytical method begins with injecting serum samples into Plate #1, a micro-reactor plate designed for efficient protein denaturation and glycan release. Proteins are denatured through alternating cycles of boiling and cooling, followed by treatment with PNGase F under microwave irradiation. The released glycans are then separated from proteins via ethanol precipitation and solid-phase extraction.  

Plate #2, the sample-loading plate, is prepared by coating with a matrix solution, such as 2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid (DHB), using a controlled spraying process. The glycan solution is loaded onto Plate #2, mixed with a solvent containing acetonitrile and sodium chloride, and dried under reduced pressure. The dried samples are then subjected to MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry, where glycan profiles are acquired in the positive-ion reflectron mode.  

The invention further provides methods for applying this analytical technique to cancer screening. By comparing mass spectra between healthy controls and cancer patients, tumor-specific glycan peaks are identified and used as biomarkers. Cut-off values for these biomarkers are established through statistical analysis, enabling the classification of samples as positive or negative for cancer. The method demonstrates superior sensitivity and specificity compared to conventional tumor markers like CA-125, particularly for early-stage ovarian cancer detection.  

### Glycan Analysis from Human Serum  

Mass spectra of glycans extracted from human serum reveal distinct peaks corresponding to various glycan structures. Experimental procedures include denaturing serum proteins, releasing glycans with PNGase F, and purifying glycans via solid-phase extraction. The resulting glycan peaks are characterized by their mass-to-charge ratios (m/z) and intensities, which are normalized to total ion abundance for quantitative analysis.  

### Example 2  

Mass spectra of glycans from ovarian tumor patients exhibit specific peaks that serve as biomarkers for cancer detection. These biomarkers are selected based on their differential intensities between patients and healthy controls. The molecular structures and mass values of these glycans are annotated using glycan databases, though some peaks remain unassigned yet still contribute to diagnostic accuracy.  

### Example 3  

Traditional methods of glycan analysis often suffer from poor reproducibility due to variability in sample preparation and ionization. In contrast, the present invention demonstrates significantly improved reproducibility, as evidenced by lower coefficients of variation in peak intensities. This advancement enables more reliable biomarker discovery and validation.  

### Example 4  

Validation studies confirm the reproducibility of the invented method across multiple sample batches. Age-matched patient and control groups are analyzed, with mean relative standard deviation values for peak intensities consistently below 15%. This reproducibility is critical for clinical applications requiring high precision.  

### Example 5  

Glycan profiling analysis divides patients into training and validation sets to establish and test biomarker panels. Cut-off values for marker peaks are determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, and the method's differentiation power is evaluated through cross-validation. The results demonstrate superior screening performance compared to CA-125, highlighting the invention's potential for early cancer detection.  

The invention also describes commercial applications, including kits for glycan extraction and analysis, as well as methods for modifying the technique to analyze other disease-specific glycans. The versatility and robustness of the invented method make it suitable for widespread use in clinical diagnostics.
