# DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to novel methods for counteracting cognitive impairments caused by sleep deprivation through administration of AMPA receptor modulators. More specifically, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions containing positive allosteric modulators of AMPA receptors, such as CX717, and methods for their use in enhancing cognitive performance in sleep-deprived subjects. The invention demonstrates that AMPA receptor potentiators can completely restore task performance deficits induced by prolonged wakefulness while simultaneously normalizing patterns of cerebral metabolic activity in key brain regions involved in memory and cognition.  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The invention pertains to the fields of neuropharmacology and sleep medicine, specifically relating to methods of using compounds and pharmaceutical compositions that modulate α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid (AMPA) receptors. The disclosed methods are particularly directed toward ameliorating cognitive deficits resulting from sleep deprivation in human and non-human subjects. The invention encompasses therapeutic applications for individuals experiencing circadian rhythm disruptions, caregivers requiring extended wakefulness, patients with sleep-disrupting medical conditions, and persons voluntarily extending waking periods for occupational or recreational purposes.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Electrophysiology of Sleep and Sleep-Deprivation  

Normal sleep architecture in mammals is characterized by cyclical transitions between distinct neurophysiological states, each exhibiting unique electrophysiological signatures. The waking state demonstrates electroencephalographic (EEG) patterns dominated by beta (15-25 Hz) and gamma (30-80 Hz) frequency oscillations, reflecting cortical activation and information processing. Non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep stages exhibit progressively slower oscillations including sleep spindles (12-15 Hz) and delta waves (0.5-4 Hz), while rapid eye movement (REM) sleep displays theta (4-8 Hz) activity resembling wakefulness combined with muscle atonia.  

Sleep deprivation induces profound alterations in these electrophysiological patterns. Extended wakefulness leads to increasing intrusion of slow-wave delta activity into waking EEG recordings, particularly in prefrontal cortical regions. Quantitative analysis of power spectral density reveals progressive elevation of delta (0.5-5 Hz) and beta (18-25 Hz) band power during prolonged wakefulness, reflecting both homeostatic sleep pressure and microsleep episodes. Event-related potentials demonstrate attenuated amplitudes and prolonged latencies following sleep loss, particularly for P300 components associated with attention and working memory. Nonlinear analysis of EEG dynamics reveals reduced complexity and disrupted temporal organization of brain electrical activity during sleep deprivation.  

These electrophysiological changes show remarkable conservation across mammalian species, with analogous patterns observed in rodents, nonhuman primates, and humans. The consistency of these biomarkers supports their utility in assessing sleep deprivation severity and evaluating potential countermeasures.  

### Neuroanatomical Substrates of Sleep Deprivation  

Functional neuroimaging studies utilizing positron emission tomography (PET) and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) have delineated the distributed neural networks affected by sleep deprivation. PET investigations of cerebral glucose metabolism demonstrate regionally specific decreases in thalamic, basal ganglia, and cerebellar activity following sleep loss, accompanied by compensatory increases in prefrontal and parietal cortical regions required for task performance.  

fMRI studies reveal dynamic reorganization of functional connectivity patterns during sleep deprivation. Distinct neural networks subserving different cognitive domains show differential vulnerability to sleep loss. Working memory tasks exhibit reduced prefrontal cortex activation, while attention tasks show diminished thalamic engagement. Notably, sleep-deprived subjects frequently recruit additional brain regions not normally involved in task performance, suggesting compensatory mechanisms.  

The prefrontal cortex appears particularly sensitive to sleep deprivation, showing both metabolic reductions and altered functional connectivity. This vulnerability correlates with the well-documented impairment of executive functions during sleep loss. Concurrent changes in dopaminergic neurotransmission within fronto-striatal circuits may underlie observed alterations in mood and motivation.  

Animal models, particularly nonhuman primates, have proven invaluable for isolating the specific effects of sleep deprivation from confounding factors present in human studies. Primate models demonstrate remarkable homology with human neuroanatomical and functional responses to sleep loss, supporting their predictive validity for therapeutic development.  

### Role of Stress in Sleep Deprivation and Cognitive Performance  

Chronic stress produces well-characterized effects on hippocampal structure and function, including dendritic atrophy, impaired long-term potentiation, and deficits in hippocampus-dependent memory tasks. Growing evidence suggests extended sleep deprivation shares neurobiological features with chronic stress. Both conditions elevate circulating glucocorticoids, reduce hippocampal neurogenesis, and impair spatial memory performance.  

Electrophysiological studies demonstrate that sleep deprivation, like chronic stress, alters hippocampal excitability and synaptic plasticity. Anatomical investigations reveal similar patterns of dendritic remodeling in prefrontal and hippocampal regions following either chronic stress or prolonged wakefulness. These parallels support the conceptualization of extended sleep deprivation as a form of neural stress with particular impact on limbic system structures.  

### Effects of Sleep-Deprivation on Memory  

Sleep deprivation produces robust impairments across multiple memory systems and phases. Consolidation of declarative memories is particularly vulnerable, with studies demonstrating the importance of slow-wave sleep for hippocampal reactivation and memory stabilization. Spatial memory tasks including water maze and radial maze performance show marked deterioration following sleep loss.  

Neurochemical studies reveal that sleep deprivation alters multiple neurotransmitter systems involved in memory processes. Serotonin metabolism shows region-specific changes, while norepinephrine levels demonstrate circadian disruption. Prostaglandin synthesis is upregulated, potentially contributing to inflammatory aspects of sleep loss. These neurochemical alterations converge on hippocampal circuitry, impairing synaptic plasticity mechanisms essential for memory formation.  

The hippocampus plays a central role in relational memory, representing contextual and spatial relationships between stimuli. Lesion studies in humans and animals demonstrate that hippocampal damage produces profound deficits in spatial working memory and associative learning. Electrophysiological recordings reveal sleep deprivation-induced alterations in hippocampal place cell activity and theta rhythm coherence, providing mechanistic insights into spatial memory impairments.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Definitions  

For purposes of this invention, the following terms shall have the meanings specified:  

"Subjects" or "patients" refers to human or non-human animals receiving treatment according to the disclosed methods, particularly individuals experiencing cognitive impairment due to sleep deprivation.  

"AMPA receptor modulators" encompass compounds that allosterically potentiate ion flow through AMPA-type glutamate receptors, including but not limited to CX717 and related ampakines.  

"Treat" or "treatment" denotes therapeutic intervention to ameliorate cognitive deficits associated with sleep deprivation, including prevention of anticipated impairment.  

"Acute" administration refers to single or short-term dosing to address temporary sleep deprivation, while "chronic" administration indicates prolonged or repeated dosing for persistent sleep disruption.  

"Concurrent administration" describes simultaneous or sequential delivery of AMPA receptor modulators with other therapeutic agents.  

"Effective amount" signifies a quantity sufficient to produce measurable improvement in cognitive performance without causing unacceptable adverse effects.  

### Method of Using AMPA Receptor Potentiator Compounds  

The invention provides methods for enhancing cognitive performance during sleep deprivation through administration of positive allosteric modulators of AMPA receptors. The disclosed approach represents a significant advance over conventional stimulants by specifically targeting glutamatergic synaptic plasticity rather than inducing generalized arousal.  

Key advantages include the use of non-stimulant compounds that enhance cognition without disrupting sensory processing or causing hyperactivity. The invention further provides novel applications for AMPA receptor modulators in sleep deprivation contexts, demonstrating complete reversal of performance deficits rather than mere attenuation.  

Beneficiaries of the invention include multiple populations vulnerable to sleep deprivation effects:  

1) Individuals with circadian rhythm disruptions such as shift workers and jet lag sufferers  
2) Caregivers of newborns, invalids, or critically ill patients requiring extended wakefulness  
3) Patients with medical conditions that disrupt normal sleep architecture  
4) Persons voluntarily extending waking periods for occupational, academic, or recreational purposes  

### Pharmaceutical Composition  

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising AMPA receptor potentiators formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. Preferred embodiments include unit-dose formulations for intravenous, oral, buccal, topical, parenteral, or transdermal administration.  

Compositions may incorporate isotonic preparations, aqueous or non-aqueous suspensions, lyophilized formulations, or emulsified delivery systems. Specific formulations include rectal suppositories, ointments, creams, lotions, pastes, transdermal patches, and liposomal preparations designed for controlled release.  

### Biological Activity Assessment  

AMPA receptor modulation is quantified using whole-cell patch-clamp electrophysiology in acute hippocampal slices. Test compounds are applied while recording evoked excitatory postsynaptic potentials (EPSPs) following Schaffer collateral fiber stimulation. Enhancement of AMPA receptor function is evidenced by increased EPSP amplitude and duration without alteration of presynaptic release probability.  

Field potential recordings provide complementary assessment of network-level effects. Bipolar nichrome stimulating electrodes activate afferent pathways while extracellular recordings monitor population responses. Indirect measures of AMPA receptor function include analysis of current-voltage relationships and sensitivity to selective antagonists.  

### Non-Human Primate Model  

The invention employs a validated nonhuman primate model for assessing sleep deprivation effects on cognitive performance. Subjects perform a computerized delayed-match-to-sample (DMTS) task requiring visual recognition memory and working memory.  

The DMTS paradigm involves:  
1) Sample phase presentation of a target image  
2) Variable delay periods (1-30 seconds)  
3) Match phase requiring identification of the sample among distractors  
4) Liquid reward for correct responses  

Sleep deprivation protocols maintain wakefulness for 30-36 hours through environmental enrichment and gentle stimulation. Test compounds are administered intravenously prior to behavioral assessment, with simultaneous monitoring of neural activity and cerebral metabolism.  

### Salts of Compounds  

The invention includes pharmaceutically acceptable salts of AMPA receptor modulators, including acid addition salts formed with inorganic or organic acids, and salts derived from bases. Preferred salt forms enhance solubility, stability, or bioavailability while maintaining therapeutic efficacy.  

### Pharmaceutical Formulations  

Representative formulations incorporate active compounds with appropriate carriers for specific administration routes:  

Intravenous preparations utilize sterile isotonic solutions, preferably with cyclodextrin solubilization. Oral formulations include tablets, capsules, or liquids with enteric coatings as needed. Buccal administration employs mucoadhesive films or lozenges for rapid absorption.  

Topical applications feature penetration enhancers for transdermal delivery. Parenteral options include intramuscular or subcutaneous depot formulations. Specialized delivery systems include liposomal encapsulation and nanoparticle carriers for targeted CNS delivery.  

### Dosage  

Effective dosages vary according to administration route and subject characteristics. Intravenous administration typically employs 0.3-1.5 mg/kg, while oral dosing may require higher amounts (2-10 mg/kg) to achieve equivalent exposure.  

Dosing regimens are tailored to sleep deprivation duration and cognitive demands. Acute sleep loss may warrant single-dose administration, while chronic sleep disruption benefits from scheduled repeated dosing. Therapeutic monitoring guides individualized dose optimization.
