# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The development of effective vaccines represents one of the most significant achievements in modern medicine, providing protection against numerous infectious diseases. Traditional vaccination strategies typically rely on parenteral administration, most commonly through intramuscular or subcutaneous injection. While these methods have proven successful for many vaccines, they present several limitations including poor patient compliance due to needle phobia, requirement for trained medical personnel for administration, and suboptimal induction of mucosal immunity at common sites of pathogen entry such as the gastrointestinal and respiratory tracts.  

Current oral vaccine delivery methods face substantial challenges in achieving effective immunization. The harsh acidic environment of the stomach can degrade vaccine components before they reach intestinal sites capable of inducing immune responses. Furthermore, the intestinal epithelium presents a formidable barrier to antigen uptake, and current delivery systems often fail to target the specific regions of the intestine most conducive to immune induction. These limitations result in reduced vaccine efficacy, necessitating higher doses and multiple administrations to achieve protective immunity. There exists a pressing need for improved vaccine delivery systems that can overcome these barriers while inducing robust systemic and mucosal immune responses.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel immunogenic compositions and methods for their targeted delivery to the ileum to induce enhanced immune responses. The immunogenic compositions comprise biological agents capable of eliciting an immune response, including but not limited to polypeptides, carbohydrates, lipids, and polynucleotides. In preferred embodiments, the biological agent is delivered via an expression vector, particularly recombinant adenoviral vectors containing heterologous nucleic acid sequences encoding immunogens of interest.  

The expression vectors of the invention incorporate transcriptional control sequences including viral promoters operably linked to the heterologous nucleic acid sequences. Particularly advantageous embodiments utilize double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) as an adjuvant to enhance the immune response. The compositions may further include additional adjuvants selected from lipid adjuvants, non-lipid adjuvants, and Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR-3) agonists.  

A critical aspect of the invention involves the targeted delivery of immunogenic compositions to the ileum using specialized delivery systems. In one embodiment, the composition is formulated with an enteric coating that disintegrates at the pH characteristic of the ileum, typically between pH 7.0 and 8.0. The enteric coating comprises pH-sensitive polymers selected from cellulose acetate phthalate, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate, polyvinyl acetate phthalate, and methacrylic acid copolymers. The thickness of the enteric coating is carefully controlled between 50-200 μm to ensure precise release timing.  

Alternative delivery systems include radio-controlled capsules that release their contents upon receiving an external signal when positioned in the ileum, as confirmed by gamma scintigraphy. The compositions may be formulated as tablets, capsules, or microparticles, with the tablet form being particularly advantageous for large-scale distribution and administration.  

The invention further provides methods of immunization comprising delivering the immunogenic composition to the ileum of a subject. This targeted delivery results in significantly enhanced immune responses compared to non-directed delivery, including increased numbers of antibody-secreting cells, higher antibody titers, and stronger mucosal immunity. The method induces both humoral and cell-mediated immune responses, with particular enhancement of IgA production at mucosal surfaces.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for inducing enhanced immune responses through targeted delivery of immunogenic biological agents to the ileum. The following sections provide detailed descriptions of the various components and aspects of the invention.  

### I. Definitions  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms shall have the meanings set forth below:  

"Immunogenic" refers to the capacity of a substance to provoke an immune response, including both humoral and cell-mediated components. An immunogenic substance stimulates the production of antibodies and/or activates T cells specific for the substance.  

"Biological agent" means any substance derived from or modeled after biological molecules, including polypeptides, polynucleotides, carbohydrates, lipids, or combinations thereof, that can elicit an immune response when administered to a subject.  

"Immunogenic biological agent" refers to a biological agent that has been modified or formulated to enhance its ability to stimulate an immune response, either alone or in combination with adjuvants or delivery systems.  

"Antigen" means any substance that can be specifically bound by an antibody or T cell receptor. An antigen may or may not be immunogenic on its own.  

"Immunogenically effective dose" refers to the minimum amount of an immunogenic biological agent required to elicit a detectable immune response in a subject.  

"Humoral immune responses" are antibody-mediated immune responses involving the production of immunoglobulins by B cells.  

"Cell mediated immune responses" are T cell-mediated immune responses that do not primarily involve antibody production, including cytotoxic T lymphocyte responses and helper T cell responses.  

"Ileum" refers to the final section of the small intestine, extending from the jejunum to the ileocecal valve, characterized by abundant Peyer's patches and specialized immune inductive sites.  

"Enteric coating" means a protective barrier applied to oral dosage forms that prevents release of contents in the stomach but allows release in the small intestine.  

"Disintegrate" means the breakdown of an enteric coating or dosage form to release its contents, typically through dissolution, erosion, or mechanical disruption.  

"Chimeric or recombinant" refers to nucleic acid molecules or proteins created by combining genetic material from different sources.  

"Expression vector" means a DNA construct containing regulatory elements necessary for expression of an inserted coding sequence in a host cell.  

"Promoter" means a DNA sequence that directs transcription of an operably linked nucleic acid sequence.  

"Operably linked" refers to the functional joining of nucleic acid sequences such that the regulatory sequences control expression of the coding sequences.  

"Heterologous" means derived from a different species or source than the reference entity.  

"Nucleic acid" refers to polymers of nucleotides including DNA, RNA, and synthetic analogs thereof.  

"Nucleotide" means the basic structural unit of nucleic acids, consisting of a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar, and a phosphate group.  

"Conservatively modified variants" refers to amino acid or nucleotide substitutions that maintain the essential properties of the original sequence.  

"Therapeutic dose" means an amount of a pharmaceutical composition sufficient to treat or ameliorate a disease or condition.  

"Antibody" refers to immunoglobulin molecules and immunologically active portions thereof that specifically bind antigens.  

"T cells" are lymphocytes that play central roles in cell-mediated immunity, including helper T cells, cytotoxic T cells, and regulatory T cells.  

"Adaptive immune response" means an antigen-specific immune response characterized by immunological memory.  

"Antigen presenting cells" are specialized cells that process and present antigens to T cells, including dendritic cells, macrophages, and B cells.  

"Adjuvant" means a substance that enhances the immune response to an antigen.  

"Polypeptide," "peptide," and "protein" refer to polymers of amino acids of varying lengths.  

"Amino acid" refers to the basic building blocks of proteins, including the 20 naturally occurring amino acids and synthetic analogs.  

"Amino acid analogs" are synthetic compounds structurally similar to natural amino acids but containing modifications.  

"Amino acid mimetics" are compounds that mimic the structure and function of amino acids or peptides.  

"Selectively hybridize to" means the specific binding of a nucleic acid probe to a complementary target sequence under stringent conditions.  

"Polynucleotide variants" are sequences that differ from a reference sequence but maintain substantial identity and function.  

"Identical or percent identity" refers to the degree of sequence similarity between two nucleic acid or amino acid sequences.  

"Control" means a reference standard used for comparison in experimental studies.  

"Diagnosis" refers to the identification of a disease or condition based on its signs, symptoms, and test results.  

"Prognosis" means the likely course and outcome of a disease or condition.  

"Therapy" and "treatment" refer to medical interventions intended to relieve or cure diseases or symptoms.  

"Amelioration" means the improvement of disease symptoms or reduction in their severity.  

"Subject," "patient," and "individual" refer to human or animal recipients of the compositions and methods described herein.

### II. Immunogenic Biological Agents  

The immunogenic biological agents of the invention encompass a broad range of molecules capable of eliciting immune responses. Polypeptide immunogens include viral antigens such as influenza hemagglutinin, bacterial antigens such as tetanus toxoid, parasite antigens such as malaria circumsporozoite protein, fungal antigens such as candida cell wall proteins, and cancer antigens such as HER2/neu. Carbohydrate immunogens include bacterial polysaccharides from Streptococcus pneumoniae and Neisseria meningitidis. Lipid immunogens include mycobacterial cell wall components and synthetic lipid adjuvants. Polynucleotide immunogens include DNA plasmids encoding antigenic proteins and RNA molecules capable of stimulating innate immune responses.  

The immunogenic biological agents may induce immune responses directly or indirectly. Direct immune responses occur when the agent itself stimulates immune cells, while indirect responses involve processing and presentation of the agent by antigen-presenting cells. Administration options include oral, nasal, and rectal routes, with oral administration being particularly preferred for intestinal targeting.  

Expression vectors represent a preferred embodiment for delivering polynucleotide immunogens. Viral vectors, particularly replication-incompetent adenoviral vectors, provide efficient gene transfer and strong immunogenicity. The vectors incorporate transcriptional control sequences including viral promoters such as the cytomegalovirus immediate early promoter, which drive high-level expression of heterologous immunogens.  

Adjuvants enhance the immunogenicity of the biological agents. Lipid adjuvants include monophosphoryl lipid A and squalene emulsions. Non-lipid adjuvants include aluminum salts and calcium phosphate. TLR-3 agonists such as poly(I:C) provide potent stimulation of innate immunity. Additional adjuvants may be selected from cytokines, chemokines, and immune potentiators that modulate the quality and magnitude of the immune response.  

### III. Targeted Delivery Systems  

The invention employs specialized delivery systems to target immunogenic compositions to the ileum. Enteric coatings comprise pH-sensitive polymers that remain intact in the acidic stomach environment but dissolve in the neutral-to-alkaline conditions of the small intestine. Suitable polymers include cellulose acetate phthalate, hydroxypropyl methylcellulose phthalate, polyvinyl acetate phthalate, and methacrylic acid copolymers such as Eudragit S100.  

Biodegradable polymers provide alternative coating materials that degrade through enzymatic action or hydrolysis in the intestinal environment. Osmotic-controlled release oral delivery systems utilize semipermeable membranes to control the rate of drug release. Combination polymers incorporating materials with varying pH, water, and enzymatic sensitivities allow precise targeting to specific intestinal regions.  

The thickness of the enteric coating critically determines the release profile, with coatings between 50-200 μm providing optimal ileum-specific delivery. The coating may form a matrix with the therapeutic composition or surround a core containing the active ingredients.  

Radio-controlled capsules represent an advanced delivery technology that enables precise spatial and temporal release. Early models utilized external radio signals to trigger capsule opening, while updated designs incorporate real-time tracking via radiolabeling to confirm ileum positioning before release. The capsules may contain liquid or solid formulations and provide confirmation of content release through incorporated tracers.  

Pharmaceutical compositions may be formulated as tablets, capsules, or microparticles for prophylactic or therapeutic purposes. Tablet formulations offer advantages in stability, dosing accuracy, and patient acceptability. Microparticle systems provide high surface area for rapid dissolution and absorption in the intestinal lumen.  

### IV. Immune Responses and Vaccines  

The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention elicit robust immune responses when delivered to the ileum. The compositions function as vaccines against infectious diseases including influenza, HIV, hepatitis, and rotavirus, as well as non-infectious conditions such as cancer and autoimmune disorders.  

Active immunotherapy involves administration of immune response-modifying agents to stimulate protective immunity. Dosing frequency and amount are optimized based on the immunogen, adjuvant, and target population, with typical regimens involving one to three administrations. Immune responses are monitored through standard assays measuring antibody titers, cellular responses, and mucosal immunity.  

The effectiveness of therapy may be evaluated through detection of immunocomplexes, measurement of neutralizing antibodies, and assessment of antigen-specific T cell responses. Diagnostic markers including serum antibody levels and mucosal IgA concentrations provide indicators of vaccine efficacy and durability.  

### V. Examples  

Example 1 demonstrates the advantages of ileum-targeted vaccine delivery using radio-controlled capsules. A recombinant adenoviral vector encoding influenza hemagglutinin (rAd-HA) was formulated with dsRNA adjuvant and delivered to human subjects. Gamma scintigraphy confirmed capsule release in the ileum, resulting in significantly higher antibody-secreting cell (ASC) responses compared to jejunum delivery.  

ASC assays revealed median counts of 200 IgG ASCs and 54 IgA ASCs per million PBMCs in the ileum group, with all subjects responding. Hemagglutination inhibition (HAI) titers showed seroconversion in 67% of ileum-treated subjects, while microneutralization (MN) assays demonstrated greater than 5-fold increases in geometric mean titers.  

Example 2 describes tablet formulations with enteric coatings targeting ileum release. Dissolution testing confirmed pH-dependent release profiles with complete disintegration at pH ≥7.0. Clinical studies demonstrated comparable immune responses to radio-controlled capsule delivery, validating the tablet approach.  

Example 3 details a Phase 1 clinical study evaluating GMP-grade rAd-HA-dsRNA tablets. Manufacturing processes ensured consistent coating thickness and drug content. Results showed durable antibody responses persisting for at least 6 months post-vaccination, with geometric mean titers exceeding protective thresholds.  

Example 4 examines in vitro dissolution characteristics under varying pH conditions. Coatings comprising Eudragit S100 showed minimal release (<5%) at pH ≤6.8 but rapid disintegration (>95% in 30 minutes) at pH 7.0-7.4, confirming ileum-specific release properties.  

Example 5 presents comprehensive immune monitoring data from clinical trials. Flow cytometric analysis revealed induction of α4β7+CCR9+ B cells with gut-homing potential in over 90% of ileum-treated subjects. Mucosal IgA responses were detected in nasal and fecal samples, demonstrating the induction of compartmentalized immunity.  

The examples collectively establish that targeted ileum delivery enhances both systemic and mucosal immune responses compared to conventional approaches. The technology has particular utility for vaccines against pathogens entering through mucosal surfaces, offering improved protection while eliminating the need for needle-based administration.
