# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of bacterial capsular saccharides and their use in vaccine formulations. More particularly, the invention pertains to immunogenic conjugates comprising capsular polysaccharides from Neisseria meningitidis serogroup X (MenX) coupled to carrier proteins, pharmaceutical compositions containing such conjugates, and methods for their preparation and use in eliciting protective immune responses against meningococcal infections. The invention further provides stable aqueous formulations of these conjugates and improved analytical methods for characterizing the capsular polysaccharides and their conjugates.

## BACKGROUND ART  

Capsular saccharides have long been recognized as important components in vaccine formulations against bacterial pathogens. These surface-exposed polysaccharides play crucial roles in bacterial virulence and represent attractive targets for immune recognition. However, plain polysaccharide vaccines exhibit significant limitations, particularly their inability to induce immunological memory and their poor immunogenicity in infants and young children.  

The conjugation of capsular polysaccharides to carrier proteins has revolutionized vaccine development by converting T-cell independent antigens into T-cell dependent ones. This approach was first successfully implemented with the Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib) conjugate vaccine, which demonstrated the ability to induce protective immunity in vulnerable populations. For Neisseria meningitidis, a leading cause of bacterial meningitis, vaccines have been developed against several serogroups including A, C, W, and Y, with tetravalent formulations now available.  

Despite these advances, polysaccharide-based vaccines face stability challenges, particularly for phosphodiester-containing capsules like those of MenA and MenX. The natural hydrolytic instability of these polysaccharides necessitates special storage conditions and limits shelf life. Serogroup X meningococcal disease, while historically less prevalent, has emerged as an important cause of epidemics in sub-Saharan Africa, creating an urgent need for effective vaccines.  

The MenX capsular polysaccharide consists of (1→4)-linked N-acetyl-D-glucosamine-1-phosphate repeating units, presenting unique challenges for vaccine development. Unlike other meningococcal serogroups, no licensed MenX vaccine currently exists, and the polysaccharide's structural features complicate conjugation chemistry and formulation stability.  

Current meningococcal conjugate vaccines often employ lyophilized formulations to overcome stability issues, but this approach introduces complexities in distribution and administration. There remains a significant unmet need for stable, fully liquid formulations of meningococcal conjugate vaccines, particularly for serogroup X. The present invention addresses these challenges through novel conjugation strategies and formulation approaches that maintain polysaccharide integrity while preserving immunogenicity.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides immunogenic conjugates comprising Neisseria meningitidis serogroup X capsular polysaccharide covalently linked to carrier proteins, along with methods for their preparation and use in vaccine formulations. In a first aspect, the invention provides a conjugate comprising MenX capsular polysaccharide coupled to a carrier molecule through stable covalent linkages.  

The conjugates demonstrate superior immunogenicity, eliciting protective antibody responses against MenX infection. Surprisingly, the MenX polysaccharide maintains excellent stability in aqueous formulations when properly conjugated, overcoming historical challenges with phosphodiester-containing polysaccharides. The invention further provides combination vaccines incorporating the MenX conjugate together with other meningococcal serogroup conjugates.  

A second aspect of the invention provides processes for preparing the conjugates. In a preferred embodiment, the process involves controlled oxidation of the polysaccharide's primary hydroxyl groups followed by coupling to the carrier protein. The oxidation step preferably uses TEMPO (2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidine-1-oxyl) as a catalyst, which selectively oxidizes primary alcohols to aldehydes while preserving the polysaccharide's structural integrity. The generated aldehydes then react with amino groups on the carrier protein through reductive amination, forming stable secondary amine linkages.  

Alternative conjugation methods are also provided, including:  
1) Direct coupling through reductive amination without prior oxidation  
2) Sequential reduction, oxidative cleavage, and reductive amination  
3) Reduction, oxidative cleavage, and direct coupling  

Each method offers distinct advantages depending on the desired polysaccharide size, conjugation density, and stability profile. The processes consistently yield conjugates with optimal polysaccharide-to-protein ratios between 0.5:1 and 5:1 (w/w), ensuring both immunogenicity and stability.  

A third aspect provides pharmaceutical compositions containing the MenX conjugates. The compositions may include pharmaceutically acceptable excipients, adjuvants such as aluminum salts, and optionally conjugates from other meningococcal serogroups. The formulations demonstrate remarkable stability in aqueous solutions across a pH range of 5.0-7.5 and at temperatures from 2-8°C for extended periods.  

The invention further provides improved analytical methods for characterizing MenX polysaccharides and their conjugates. A key advancement involves hydrolyzing the polysaccharide and quantifying the resulting N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate by high-performance anion-exchange chromatography with pulsed amperometric detection (HPAEC-PAD). This method enables precise monitoring of polysaccharide content throughout purification and conjugation processes.  

Additional aspects include:  
- Intermediate compounds in the conjugation process  
- Methods for assaying MenX polysaccharide in various samples  
- Use of the conjugates in medicaments for preventing meningococcal disease  
- Kit formulations for vaccine administration  

The conjugates are particularly effective when the carrier protein is selected from CRM197, tetanus toxoid, diphtheria toxoid, or recombinant proteins containing CD4+ T cell epitopes. Specific embodiments utilize hybrid carrier proteins combining immunogenic domains from multiple pathogens to enhance T cell help.  

The invention further provides methods for treating or preventing MenX infection by administering immunologically effective amounts of the conjugates. The compositions may be formulated for intramuscular, subcutaneous, or intradermal administration, with dosage regimens tailored to target populations.  

## PARTICULAR EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

In specific embodiments, the invention provides:  

1. A conjugate comprising Neisseria meningitidis serogroup X capsular polysaccharide covalently linked to a carrier protein, wherein:  
   - The polysaccharide has an average degree of polymerization between 5 and 30 repeating units  
   - The polysaccharide is oxidized at primary hydroxyl groups to an extent of 10-50%  
   - The carrier protein is CRM197, tetanus toxoid, or diphtheria toxoid  
   - The polysaccharide-to-protein ratio is between 0.5:1 and 5:1 (w/w)  

2. A process for preparing the conjugate comprising:  
   a) Oxidizing primary hydroxyl groups of MenX capsular polysaccharide using TEMPO-mediated oxidation  
   b) Reacting the oxidized polysaccharide with a carrier protein under conditions suitable for reductive amination  
   c) Purifying the resulting conjugate  

3. A pharmaceutical composition comprising:  
   - The MenX conjugate  
   - An aluminum salt adjuvant  
   - A pharmaceutically acceptable buffer  
   - Optionally, conjugates from meningococcal serogroups A, C, W, and/or Y  

4. A method for determining MenX polysaccharide content in a sample comprising:  
   a) Hydrolyzing the polysaccharide to release N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate  
   b) Separating the hydrolysate by HPAEC  
   c) Detecting and quantifying the N-acetylglucosamine-4-phosphate by PAD  

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope:  

**Example 1: Polysaccharide Purification**  
MenX polysaccharide was purified from bacterial cultures grown in optimized media. Bacterial growth was monitored by proton NMR spectroscopy, and culture supernatants were analyzed by NMR to assess polysaccharide production. The polysaccharide was precipitated with cetavlon, purified by ethanol precipitation, and sized by ultrafiltration to obtain material with an average degree of polymerization of 15-20 repeating units.  

**Example 2: Conjugate Preparation**  
Purified MenX polysaccharide was oxidized using TEMPO/NaOCl/NaBr system in pH 9.0 buffer at 4°C for 2 hours. The reaction was quenched with ethanol, and the oxidized polysaccharide was purified by gel filtration chromatography. The activated polysaccharide was then mixed with CRM197 carrier protein in 0.2 M phosphate buffer pH 7.2 at a 3:1 (w/w) ratio, followed by addition of sodium cyanoborohydride. After 48-hour incubation at 25°C, the conjugate was purified by sequential ultrafiltration and sterile filtered.  

**Example 3: Analytical Characterization**  
The conjugate was analyzed by HPAEC-PAD to quantify free and conjugated polysaccharide. SDS-PAGE showed a shift in carrier protein mobility confirming conjugation. MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry determined a polysaccharide-to-protein ratio of 2.8:1 (w/w). NMR confirmed preservation of the polysaccharide's structural features post-conjugation.  

**Example 4: Stability Studies**  
The conjugate formulation in 10 mM histidine buffer pH 6.5 containing 150 mM NaCl and 0.01% polysorbate 80 showed no significant degradation after 24 months at 2-8°C as assessed by HPAEC-PAD, size exclusion chromatography, and NMR. The level of free polysaccharide remained below 5% throughout the study period.  

**Example 5: Immunogenicity Testing**  
Mice immunized with the conjugate (2 μg polysaccharide dose with aluminum phosphate adjuvant) developed high titers of anti-MenX antibodies as measured by ELISA. Serum bactericidal antibody titers exceeded 1:1024 against MenX strains. The immune response was boosterable, demonstrating T-cell dependent characteristics.  

**Example 6: Combination Vaccine Formulation**  
The MenX conjugate was combined with licensed MenACWY conjugate vaccines in various ratios. The combinations showed no interference in immunogenicity against any serogroup and maintained stability profiles comparable to individual conjugates.  

The invention thus provides novel MenX conjugate vaccines with improved stability and immunogenicity, addressing an unmet medical need in meningococcal disease prevention. The formulations and methods described enable reliable production and characterization of these important vaccine components.
