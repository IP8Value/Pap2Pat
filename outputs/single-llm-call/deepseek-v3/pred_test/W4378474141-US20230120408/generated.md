# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### 1. Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to the field of superconducting materials and thin film fabrication. More specifically, the invention pertains to wafer-scale production of superconducting magnesium diboride (MgB2) thin films exhibiting high transition temperatures, uniform thickness, and controlled resistivity. The invention further relates to methods of synthesizing such films through optimized deposition and annealing techniques, including the use of capping layers to prevent magnesium evaporation during thermal processing.  

### 2. Description of the Related Art  

Conventional techniques for fabricating superconducting MgB2 thin films face several limitations that hinder their practical application in large-scale device manufacturing. In-situ sputtering of MgB2 films typically results in low critical transition temperatures (Tc) due to oxidation, small grain size, and off-stoichiometry caused by the high vapor pressure and low sticking coefficient of magnesium at elevated temperatures. Post-annealing of these films may improve Tc but often at the expense of increased surface roughness exceeding 10 nm root mean square (rms).  

Room temperature deposition of magnesium-boron composite films requires subsequent annealing, which when performed in vacuum leads to magnesium evaporation, surface roughening, and limited transition temperatures around 6 K. Previous attempts to prevent magnesium evaporation through capping layers have encountered challenges, as materials like tantalum crack at temperatures above 700°C and require removal for superconducting property measurements.  

Existing methods for controlling film resistivity and related properties typically involve adding impurities or altering stoichiometry, but these approaches prove difficult to implement consistently in reactively sputtered materials due to gas nonuniformity and hysteretic parameter curves. Furthermore, conventional substrate materials like sapphire present issues with oxygen diffusion into the MgB2 layer, particularly detrimental for films under 100 nm thickness.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a wafer with a superconducting MgB2 film characterized by exceptional uniformity and performance metrics. The film demonstrates sheet resistance uniformity across the wafer with thickness variations maintained within tight tolerances. Surface roughness is minimized to values as low as 0.34-0.45 nm rms through optimized deposition parameters. The resistivity of the film can be precisely tuned within a range spanning from 25 to over 100 μΩ·cm through controlled adjustment of the magnesium to boron ratio.  

In an alternative embodiment, the invention provides a wafer with superconducting MgB2 film exhibiting critical temperatures exceeding 30 K, with demonstrated values reaching up to 37.2 K in optimized conditions. The film incorporates a specialized capping layer, preferably composed of boron, which forms a viscous, crack-free oxide layer during annealing to prevent magnesium evaporation while maintaining film integrity.  

The invention further encompasses a method for making high-quality MgB2 films through precise control of deposition and annealing parameters. The method involves tuning the Mg to B ratio during deposition to achieve desired resistivity and superconducting transition temperature characteristics. Specific deposition and annealing conditions are selected to optimize film properties, including critical temperature, surface roughness, and uniformity.  

Various deposition methods may be employed, including co-sputtering of magnesium and boron targets or sequential deposition of alternating boron and magnesium layers. Annealing conditions are carefully controlled, with temperatures maintained below 650°C to prevent excessive magnesium evaporation while ensuring complete reaction between magnesium and boron components.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides an advanced method for synthesizing high-performance MgB2 films suitable for wafer-scale device fabrication. The process begins with the deposition of magnesium and boron precursors on a prepared substrate, followed by capping layer application and controlled thermal annealing to form the superconducting phase.  

### Technical Description  

The deposition process employs a co-sputtering method using dedicated magnesium and boron targets in a specialized apparatus. The amount of magnesium and boron deposited is carefully controlled to achieve the desired stoichiometric ratio for subsequent reaction. The Mg/B ratio is precisely tuned to obtain specific resistivity values and superconducting transition temperatures in the final film. In alternative embodiments, boron and magnesium may be deposited as alternating layers with controlled thicknesses to influence final film properties.  

A critical aspect of the invention involves depositing a capping layer on the as-deposited film prior to annealing. The capping layer serves multiple purposes: preventing magnesium evaporation during high-temperature processing, maintaining film stoichiometry, and controlling surface morphology. Preferred capping layer materials include boron, which forms a viscous oxide layer at annealing temperatures, creating an effective diffusion barrier without cracking. The thickness of the capping layer is optimized to provide sufficient protection while minimizing its impact on subsequent device fabrication steps.  

Thermal annealing of the capped film is performed under controlled conditions to facilitate the reaction between magnesium and boron while minimizing undesirable effects. The annealing temperature is carefully selected, typically between 600-650°C, to balance complete reaction formation with prevention of excessive magnesium loss. Hold times at the peak temperature are optimized based on film thickness and desired grain structure, typically ranging from 1-10 minutes. The annealing conditions significantly influence final film properties including critical temperature, grain size, and surface roughness.  

Following annealing, the capped film undergoes controlled cooling to room temperature. The resulting composition of matter consists primarily of superconducting MgB2 phase with minimal secondary phases. The microstructure and composition are illustrated in FIGS. 2A-2B, showing the uniform formation of MgB2 grains and interface characteristics with the substrate and capping layer.  

The invention produces MgB2 films with exceptional properties suitable for various electronic applications. The effect of capping layer and thermal annealing on superconductive properties is demonstrated through consistent high transition temperatures exceeding 30 K in optimized films. The uniformity of film thickness and sheet resistance across wafer-scale areas enables reliable device fabrication. The tunability of resistivity through Mg:B ratio adjustment provides flexibility for different application requirements, with achievable values spanning from 25 to over 100 μΩ·cm. Surface roughness is maintained at exceptionally low levels (<1 nm rms) through deposition parameter optimization. The superconductive properties, including critical current density and kinetic inductance, meet or exceed requirements for advanced electronic devices.  

Several exemplary film compositions demonstrate the range of achievable properties. Example 1 comprises a film with uniform sheet resistance and thickness across a 100 mm diameter wafer, exhibiting less than 5% variation in electrical properties. Example 2 provides a film with resistivity above 100 μΩ·cm, achieved through precise reduction of magnesium content during deposition. Example 3 demonstrates a film with critical temperature above 15K, suitable for operation in liquid hydrogen temperatures. Example 4 describes a film with resistivity between 25-50 μΩ·cm, optimized for low-loss microwave applications. Example 5 achieves exceptional performance with critical temperature above 30 K, making it suitable for cryocooler-based systems.  

The method of making these films involves several key steps: depositing magnesium and boron precursors under controlled conditions, applying an optimized capping layer, performing thermal annealing with precise temperature profiles, and cooling the processed film. Additional process controls include tuning the Mg:B ratio through sputtering power adjustments, selecting appropriate film thickness and surface area parameters, optimizing capping layer thickness and material selection, and controlling annealing temperature and hold time parameters.  

Alternative deposition approaches include co-depositing boron and magnesium simultaneously or depositing alternating layers of boron and magnesium with controlled thickness ratios. The hold time at peak annealing temperature is carefully controlled to ensure complete reaction while minimizing grain growth that could increase surface roughness. Following annealing, the capping layer may be removed through appropriate etching processes when required for device fabrication.  

The resulting MgB2 films can be incorporated into various superconducting devices through standard micro fabrication techniques. The invention enables manufacturing of devices including but not limited to superconducting photon detectors, transmission lines, parametric amplifiers, frequency multipliers, and kinetic inductance-based quantum computing elements. The combination of high critical temperature, controllable resistivity, and wafer-scale uniformity makes these films particularly valuable for applications requiring consistent performance across large areas or multiple devices.  

## CONCLUSION  

The preferred embodiment of the invention provides a comprehensive solution for wafer-scale production of high-performance superconducting MgB2 thin films. By addressing the key challenges of magnesium evaporation, surface roughness, and stoichiometry control, the invention enables reliable fabrication of devices with exceptional superconducting properties. The use of optimized capping layers, precisely controlled deposition parameters, and tailored annealing conditions results in films with high transition temperatures, uniform electrical characteristics, and smooth surfaces suitable for nanoscale patterning.  

The invention represents a significant advancement over conventional MgB2 film fabrication methods by providing reproducible, scalable processes compatible with standard semiconductor manufacturing infrastructure. The demonstrated control over film resistivity and kinetic inductance through magnesium to boron ratio adjustment offers unprecedented flexibility for device design and optimization. These advancements position MgB2 as a practical superconducting material for both conventional and emerging applications in electronics, quantum computing, and sensing technologies.
