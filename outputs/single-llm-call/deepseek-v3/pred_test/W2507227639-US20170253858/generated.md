# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of regenerative medicine and cellular therapeutics, specifically to methods for generating mature cardiomyocytes from pluripotent stem cells. More particularly, the invention provides compositions and methods for inducing cardiomyocyte maturation using three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffolds. The invention further encompasses applications of mature cardiomyocytes in therapeutic transplantation, drug screening, disease modeling, and cardiotoxicity testing.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE  

Cardiovascular disease remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, with myocardial infarction representing a particularly devastating manifestation that results in irreversible loss of cardiomyocytes. The limited regenerative capacity of adult cardiac tissue creates an urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies to replace lost myocardium. While stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes offer potential for cardiac regeneration, current differentiation protocols yield immature cells exhibiting fetal-like characteristics including disorganized sarcomeres, weak contractility, improper calcium handling, and altered drug responses. These limitations significantly impair the therapeutic utility and predictive value of stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes.  

Existing approaches to cardiomyocyte maturation have demonstrated only partial success. Current methods relying on hormonal stimulation, mechanical stress, or two-dimensional culture systems fail to recapitulate the complex microenvironment necessary for complete functional maturation. Furthermore, conventional two-dimensional culture systems lack the biomechanical cues and three-dimensional architecture present in native cardiac tissue. The absence of proper maturation signals results in cardiomyocytes with persistent fetal-like properties that are unsuitable for clinical applications or accurate drug screening.  

A critical barrier in the field remains the lack of methods to efficiently generate cardiomyocytes that exhibit adult-like functional properties. Immature cardiomyocytes demonstrate automaticity, increased arrhythmic potential, and inadequate force generation, making them poor candidates for cell replacement therapies. Additionally, the altered electrophysiology and drug sensitivity of immature cells limit their utility for pharmacological testing. There exists an unmet need for robust methods to induce complete functional maturation of stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes that closely mimic native adult cardiomyocytes in both phenotype and function.  

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE  

The present invention provides a novel method for inducing cardiomyocyte maturation through culture in three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffolds. The method comprises preparing a decellularized cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold from adult cardiac tissue, seeding the scaffold with immature cardiomyocytes derived from pluripotent stem cells, and culturing the cells under conditions that promote maturation. The three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold recapitulates critical aspects of the native cardiac microenvironment including biochemical composition, biomechanical properties, and three-dimensional architecture.  

The invention encompasses both in vitro and in vivo maturation approaches. In vitro maturation involves culturing immature cardiomyocytes within the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold for sufficient duration to induce adult-like characteristics. In vivo maturation may be achieved by implanting the cell-scaffold construct into an appropriate animal model to allow further maturation in a physiological environment. The method further includes the use of endothelial cells and stromal cells to enhance the maturation process through paracrine signaling and cell-cell interactions.  

Key maturation markers induced by the invention include increased expression of calcium handling proteins (Junctin, L-type calcium channels, SERCA2a), structural proteins (myosin light chain 2, connexin 43), and ion channels (Kir2.1, HCN4). The mature cardiomyocytes produced exhibit improved calcium handling kinetics, reduced automaticity, enhanced contractile force, and appropriate drug responses compared to immature cells.  

The invention provides methods for harvesting the mature cardiomyocytes from the three-dimensional scaffold for therapeutic use. The preparation of the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold involves decellularization of cardiac tissue, lyophilization, milling into powder form, and enzymatic digestion to create a hydrogel scaffold. The scaffold composition preserves critical extracellular matrix components including collagens, elastin, fibrinogen, periostin, fibrillin, and fibronectin while removing cellular material.  

Applications of the invention include cell replacement therapy for myocardial infarction and heart failure, drug screening platforms, disease modeling systems, and cardiotoxicity testing. The mature cardiomyocytes produced by the method exhibit superior therapeutic potential and predictive accuracy compared to current immature cardiomyocyte preparations.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following detailed description provides specific embodiments of the present invention with reference to the accompanying text. It will be apparent to those skilled in the art that various modifications and variations can be made without departing from the scope of the invention.  

For purposes of this disclosure, "immature cardiomyocytes" refers to cardiomyocytes derived from pluripotent stem cells that exhibit fetal-like characteristics including disorganized sarcomeres, spontaneous beating, improper calcium handling, and altered drug responses. "Mature cardiomyocytes" refers to cardiomyocytes exhibiting adult-like characteristics including organized sarcomeres, proper calcium handling, appropriate drug responses, and dependence on pacemaker cells for rhythmic contraction.  

The "cardiac microenvironment" encompasses the three-dimensional extracellular matrix architecture, biochemical composition, biomechanical properties, and cellular interactions present in native cardiac tissue. The "three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold" refers to a decellularized cardiac matrix preparation that maintains the native three-dimensional architecture and biochemical composition of cardiac extracellular matrix.  

The method of making mature cardiomyocytes comprises several key steps. First, immature cardiomyocytes are derived from pluripotent stem cells using established differentiation protocols. These immature cells are then seeded into the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold at appropriate densities. The cell-scaffold construct is cultured under conditions that promote maturation, typically for at least 7 days, though longer culture periods may enhance maturation further.  

Parameters of the cardiac microenvironment critical for maturation include scaffold stiffness, fiber architecture, and biochemical composition. The adult cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold exhibits approximately 10-fold greater stiffness than fetal matrix scaffolds, with storage modulus values around 67.5 Pa compared to 7.2 Pa for fetal matrix. The adult matrix also displays more organized collagen fiber bundles and higher collagen density compared to fetal matrix.  

The method of inducing cardiomyocyte maturation involves culturing cells in the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold under standard cell culture conditions (37°C, 5% CO2). The scaffold provides both biochemical cues from preserved extracellular matrix proteins and biomechanical cues from its three-dimensional architecture and stiffness. These combined signals activate maturation pathways in the cardiomyocytes.  

Preparation of the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold involves decellularization of adult cardiac tissue using detergent solutions (e.g., SDS, Triton X-100), followed by lyophilization and milling to create a powder. The powder is digested with pepsin to create a hydrogel that can polymerize at physiological temperature and pH. The resulting scaffold maintains key extracellular matrix components including collagens, elastin, fibrinogen, and other structural proteins.  

Demonstration of cardiomyocyte maturation involves assessment of multiple markers. At the gene expression level, mature cardiomyocytes show increased expression of calcium handling genes (JCN, CACNA1C, ATP2A2), structural genes (MYL2, GJA1), and ion channel genes (KCNJ2, HCN4). At the protein level, mature cells exhibit increased expression of CaV1.2, myosin light chain 2, and connexin 43. Functional maturation is demonstrated by improved calcium handling kinetics, including increased calcium transient amplitude, faster upslope and downslope, and shorter decay times.  

The composition comprising the three-dimensional extracellular matrix scaffold and cardiomyocytes represents a novel therapeutic product. This composition can be used directly for implantation or the mature cardiomyocytes can be harvested from the scaffold for other applications. The scaffold composition promotes cardiomyocyte survival, function, and integration upon implantation.  

The invention provides methods for treating cardiovascular disease in mammals by administering the mature cardiomyocytes or cell-scaffold construct to damaged cardiac tissue. The cells may be delivered by direct injection, scaffold implantation, or other appropriate methods. The mature cardiomyocytes integrate with host tissue and improve cardiac function through replacement of lost myocardium.  

The mature cardiomyocytes serve as a superior platform for drug screening compared to immature cells. Their adult-like drug responses enable more accurate prediction of pharmacological effects and cardiotoxicity. Screening methods involve exposing the mature cardiomyocytes to test compounds and monitoring functional responses such as beat rate, calcium handling, and electrophysiology.  

Methods for generating cardiomyocytes in vitro using the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold enable large-scale production of mature cells for therapeutic and research applications. The three-dimensional environment promotes efficient maturation at scale while maintaining cell viability and function.  

The three-dimensional scaffold composition comprises decellularized cardiac extracellular matrix that has been processed into a hydrogel form. The scaffold may be supplemented with additional components such as growth factors, cytokines, or other extracellular matrix proteins to enhance its maturation-promoting properties.  

The invention promotes cardiomyocyte maturation through multiple mechanisms. The three-dimensional architecture provides proper biomechanical cues, while the preserved extracellular matrix proteins deliver appropriate biochemical signals. The combination of these factors activates maturation pathways more effectively than two-dimensional culture systems or single-component scaffolds.  

Methods of treating cardiovascular disease using the mature cardiomyocytes involve determining an appropriate therapeutic dose based on the extent of tissue damage and administering the cells via suitable delivery methods. The cells may be administered alone or in combination with the extracellular matrix scaffold to enhance engraftment and function.  

Methods for prognosing cardiovascular disease involve using the mature cardiomyocytes derived from patient-specific induced pluripotent stem cells to model disease progression and predict outcomes. The mature cells more accurately reflect native cardiomyocyte behavior than immature cells, improving prognostic accuracy.  

Diagnostic methods utilize the mature cardiomyocytes to identify cardiac abnormalities by comparing patient-derived cells to healthy controls. The mature phenotype enables detection of subtle functional defects that may not be apparent in immature cells.  

Screening methods for treatment of cardiovascular disease employ the mature cardiomyocytes to test potential therapeutic compounds. The adult-like responses of the cells allow identification of compounds with the greatest therapeutic potential and lowest risk of adverse effects.  

Methods of inducing cell maturation generally involve culturing immature cells in the three-dimensional cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold under conditions that promote differentiation. While particularly effective for cardiomyocytes, the approach may be adapted for other cell types requiring maturation signals from extracellular matrix.  

The composition comprising the three-dimensional extracellular matrix scaffold and cardiomyocytes may be formulated as a pharmaceutical composition with additional excipients to enhance stability, delivery, or efficacy. The composition may be cryopreserved for storage and transport while maintaining cell viability and function.  

Pharmaceutically acceptable excipients include buffers, cryoprotectants, viscosity modifiers, and other components that maintain cell viability and scaffold integrity without interfering with therapeutic function. The excipients are selected based on the intended route of administration and storage conditions.  

Routes of administration for therapeutic applications include intramyocardial injection, intracoronary infusion, epicardial application, or other methods that deliver the cells to cardiac tissue. The route is selected based on the nature and location of the tissue damage.  

Pharmaceutical compositions may be formulated as injectable solutions, gels, patches, or other forms suitable for cardiac delivery. The formulation preserves cell viability and scaffold properties while enabling precise delivery to target tissues.  

A therapeutically effective amount of mature cardiomyocytes is determined based on the extent of tissue damage, typically ranging from millions to billions of cells depending on the size of the infarct or damaged area. Dosage may be adjusted based on patient factors and treatment response.  

Typical dosages range from about 1×10^6 to 1×10^9 cells per treatment, administered in single or multiple doses. The exact dosage is determined by the treating physician based on the patient's condition and response to therapy.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention but are not intended to limit its scope.  

**Example 1: iPSC Maintenance and Cardiac Differentiation**  

The WTC-11 GCaMP iPSC line was maintained on Growth Factor-Reduced Matrigel in mTeSR1 medium. For cardiac differentiation, iPSCs were seeded as single cells into Matrigel-coated plates with Y-27632. Cardiac induction was initiated at 75-90% confluency using CHIR99021 in RPMI/B27 without insulin. After 24 hours, medium was changed to fresh RPMI/B27 without insulin. On day 3, IWP2 was added in RPMI/B27 without insulin. At day 5, cells were fed fresh RPMI/B27 without insulin, followed by RPMI/B27 with insulin every 3 days starting at day 7. Spontaneous beating typically began by day 12.  

**Example 2: Cardiac Tissue Decellularization and ECM Generation**  

Bovine adult and fetal hearts were processed by removing excess fat and stored at -80°C. Tissue was cut into 3 mm^3 pieces and subjected to sequential treatments with ddH2O, PBS, SDS (1% for adult, 0.5% for fetal), and Triton X-100, followed by extensive washing. Decellularized tissue was lyophilized and milled into powder. The ECM powder was digested with pepsin (2 mg/mL in 0.01 M HCl) for 48-72 hours with continuous stirring. Digested ECM was aliquoted and stored at -80°C.  

**Example 3: Multiphoton Imaging and Fiber Analysis**  

Second-harmonic generation (SHG) microscopy at 740 nm and autofluorescence imaging at 860 nm were performed to visualize collagen and elastin fibers. Images were captured every 5 μm through the scaffolds. SHG signal was collected using a 320-390 nm bandpass filter; autofluorescence used a 500 nm long-pass filter. Images were analyzed using ctFIRE MATLAB to determine fiber width and number.  

**Example 4: Statistical Analysis**  

Split-Unit ANOVA was used for qRT-PCR data analysis. Student's t-test was performed on calcium transient data. Error bars represent standard error of the mean with n=3 unless otherwise stated. Significance levels: *p<0.05, **p<0.01, ***p<0.001.  

**Example 5: iPSC-derived Cardiomyocyte Maturation**  

iPSC-derived CMs were cultured in 2D or 3D fetal and adult cardiac ECMs. After 7 days, cells in 3D adult ECM showed significantly increased expression of maturation markers (JCN, CACNA1C, KCNJ2, ATP2A2) compared to 2D cultures. Protein analysis confirmed increased CaV1.2, MYL2, and Cx43 expression. Calcium imaging revealed improved handling kinetics in 3D cultures.  

**Example 6: Pharmacological Responses**  

iPSC-derived CMs in 3D adult ECM responded appropriately to isoproterenol (increased beat rate) and propranolol (return to baseline). Caffeine exposure induced faster calcium release in 3D cultures compared to 2D, demonstrating enhanced maturation.  

**Example 7: Mechanical Properties**  

Rheology measurements showed adult cardiac ECM hydrogels had storage modulus of 67.5 ± 12.6 Pa, approximately 10-fold stiffer than fetal ECM (7.2 ± 3.2 Pa). This stiffness difference contributes to the enhanced maturation observed in adult ECM.  

**Example 8: Long-term Culture Effects**  

Extended culture (up to 5 months) of iPSC-derived CMs in 3D adult ECM resulted in progressive maturation, with continued improvements in calcium handling and structural organization, suggesting prolonged culture may further enhance maturity.  

**Example 9: Endothelial Cell Co-culture**  

Co-culture with endothelial cells or exposure to endothelial cell-conditioned medium further enhanced maturation marker expression in iPSC-derived CMs, indicating synergistic effects of matrix and paracrine signals.  

**Example 10: Therapeutic Application**  

iPSC-derived CMs matured in 3D adult ECM were transplanted into infarcted rat hearts. The cells showed improved engraftment and functional integration compared to immature cells, with significant improvement in cardiac function parameters.  

**Example 11: Drug Screening Platform**  

Mature iPSC-derived CMs in 3D ECM accurately predicted cardiotoxic effects of known drugs, demonstrating superior performance compared to immature cells in pharmaceutical screening applications.  

**Example 12: Patient-specific Disease Modeling**  

iPSC-derived CMs from patients with inherited cardiac conditions were matured in 3D ECM, revealing disease-specific abnormalities not detectable in immature cells, enabling personalized medicine approaches.  

The examples demonstrate that the three-dimensional adult cardiac extracellular matrix scaffold promotes robust maturation of iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes, resulting in cells with adult-like characteristics suitable for therapeutic applications and drug screening. The method represents a significant advance over current two-dimensional culture systems and provides a platform for generating clinically relevant quantities of mature cardiomyocytes.
