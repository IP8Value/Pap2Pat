# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to methods for disrupting biological membranes using engineered proteins. More particularly, the invention provides novel small globular proteins (SGPs) capable of forming pores in lipid bilayers, thereby inducing cell lysis. The invention further encompasses therapeutic applications of these membrane-disrupting proteins, including methods for treating tumors by selectively disrupting cancer cell membranes while minimizing damage to surrounding healthy tissues.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Protein design has emerged as a powerful approach for creating novel biomolecules with tailored functions. Among designed protein architectures, coiled-coil proteins represent one of the most extensively studied structural motifs, characterized by two or more α-helices winding around each other like strands of a rope. Helix bundle proteins constitute another important class, where multiple α-helices assemble into compact tertiary structures. In contrast, β-structural proteins rely on β-sheets as their dominant secondary structure elements.  

De novo design of proteins has enabled the creation of artificial polypeptides with predetermined structures and functions not found in nature. One such designed protein is the small globular protein (SGP), which was engineered to adopt a stable tertiary structure. However, prior implementations of SGP have shown limitations in their biological activity and therapeutic potential. Specifically, conventional SGPs lacked the ability to effectively disrupt cellular membranes or demonstrate selective cytotoxicity against pathological cells.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention advances the field of protein engineering by providing novel SGPs with potent membrane-disrupting properties. These engineered proteins function as pore-forming agents capable of compromising the integrity of biological membranes. The membrane-disrupting activity of SGP manifests through its ability to physically alter membrane architecture, leading to cell lysis.  

Remarkably, SGP demonstrates significant anti-tumor activity both in vitro and in vivo. The proposed mechanism of action involves selective interaction with cell membranes, where SGP inserts itself into lipid bilayers and forms pores that compromise membrane integrity. While SGP shows broad membrane-disrupting capability in vitro, its in vivo activity appears more selective, particularly in tumor environments.  

Compared to other pore-forming peptides and proteins, SGP offers distinct advantages. Unlike bacterial toxins or naturally occurring antimicrobial peptides, SGP represents an entirely artificial protein designed specifically for membrane disruption. The invention encompasses various therapeutic applications of SGP, including methods for treating solid tumors, inhibiting metastatic growth, and managing drug-resistant cancers.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The term "small globular protein" or "SGP" refers to an engineered protein of approximately 75 amino acids with a calculated molecular weight of about 7555 Daltons. The structure of SGP comprises three distinct helical domains arranged in a compact tertiary fold.  

Functional derivatives of SGP include modified versions that retain membrane-disrupting activity. Such derivatives may contain amino acid substitutions, deletions, or additions that do not substantially alter the protein's core structure or function. The term "functional derivative" encompasses polypeptides with at least 70% sequence identity to native SGP that maintain comparable membrane-disrupting capability.  

Conservative variations of SGP include substitutions with amino acids of similar physicochemical properties. Conservative substitutions may involve replacement of one hydrophobic residue with another (e.g., leucine for isoleucine), one polar residue for another (e.g., glutamine for asparagine), or one charged residue for a similarly charged residue (e.g., arginine for lysine).  

Substantially identical or highly conserved polypeptides refer to SGP variants sharing significant sequence homology while maintaining biological activity. Sequence identity is determined by comparing aligned sequences over a defined comparison window, typically at least 20 contiguous amino acids.  

Methods for determining sequence homology include computational algorithms such as BLAST (Basic Local Alignment Search Tool). The BLAST algorithm identifies high-scoring segment pairs using scoring matrices like BLOSUM62. Statistical significance is assessed through the smallest sum probability (P(N)), with values below 0.001 indicating significant homology. Five specific BLAST programs are particularly relevant: blastp for protein-protein comparisons, blastn for nucleotide-nucleotide comparisons, blastx for translated nucleotide queries against protein databases, tblastn for protein queries against translated nucleotide databases, and tblastx for translated nucleotide queries against translated nucleotide databases.  

An "effective amount" of SGP refers to a quantity sufficient to disrupt target cell membranes without causing undue toxicity to the subject. For therapeutic applications, this typically ranges from about 1 μM to 10 mM in local administration.  

The term "biological membrane" encompasses any lipid bilayer structure, including plasma membranes, organelle membranes, and synthetic lipid vesicles. "Membrane architecture" refers to the structural organization of lipids, proteins, and carbohydrates comprising a cellular membrane.  

Methods for disrupting membrane architecture include contacting target cells with SGP under conditions allowing pore formation. "Cell lysis" denotes the breakdown of cellular integrity resulting from membrane disruption. Selective membrane disruption refers to preferential targeting of pathological cells (e.g., tumor cells) over healthy cells in vivo.  

Methods for treating tumors comprise administering SGP to a subject in need thereof. Treatable tumors include carcinomas, sarcomas, melanomas, and other solid malignancies. Administration may occur through various routes including intratumoral injection, interstitial infusion, or targeted delivery systems.  

Pharmaceutical formulations of SGP may include non-toxic, pharmaceutically acceptable carriers such as buffers, salts, or stabilizers. Targeted delivery systems include liposomal formulations, where SGP is encapsulated within lipid vesicles. Liposomes may be formed from phospholipids and cholesterol, with sizes ranging from 50-200 nm.  

Alternative delivery approaches include intrathecal injections for central nervous system tumors, surgical implants for localized malignancies, and interstitial infusion for diffuse lesions. Omega-3 fatty acid derivatization may enhance SGP absorption, while chemical modifications like peptide transport vectors can improve tissue penetration.  

Site-specific macromolecular carriers enable covalent bonding of SGP to targeting moieties. Cationic macromolecular carriers facilitate complex formation with negatively charged cell surfaces. The invention further encompasses combination therapies with conventional anti-cancer drugs.  

A "therapeutically effective amount" refers to an SGP dose sufficient to inhibit tumor growth or metastasis without causing unacceptable side effects. The invention includes methods for inducing non-necrotic, non-apoptotic cell death in target cell populations.  

Apoptotic pathways may also be activated through caspase-dependent mechanisms. The invention demonstrates primary tumor growth inhibition and metastatic tumor growth inhibition across multiple cancer types. Formulations may be optimized for stability, solubility, and controlled release.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

SGP, along with its variants SGP-L and SGP-E, were synthesized using Fmoc solid-phase peptide synthesis. The peptides were cleaved from the resin using trifluoroacetic acid and purified by reverse-phase HPLC. Amino acid analysis confirmed the expected composition: Gly (6), Ala (10), Leu (25), Asp (3), Pro (3), Tyr (3), and Lys (18). Fast atom bombardment mass spectrometry verified the molecular weight as 7555.1 Daltons, matching the theoretical value. Peptide concentrations were determined spectrophotometrically based on tryptophan and tyrosine absorbance at 280 nm.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

A computer-generated model of SGP was constructed using molecular modeling software. The three-dimensional structure revealed a compact globular fold with three helical domains arranged in a specific spatial orientation. This model provided insights into potential membrane interaction surfaces and guided rational design of functional derivatives.  

### EXAMPLE 3  

Multiple human cell lines were cultured to evaluate SGP effects, including Kaposi's sarcoma (KS1767), breast carcinoma (MDA-MB-435), prostate cancer (PC-3), and lung carcinoma (H358) lines. Cells were maintained in appropriate media supplemented with fetal bovine serum and antibiotics under standard culture conditions.  

### EXAMPLE 4  

Cell viability assays demonstrated SGP's membrane-disrupting activity. At concentrations >10 μM, SGP induced rapid (within 60 seconds) non-necrotic, non-apoptotic cell death characteristic of membrane disruption. Intermediate concentrations (5-10 μM) caused necrosis over 60 minutes, while lower concentrations (<5 μM) induced apoptosis within 24 hours. Control peptides showed no effect, while SGP-L and SGP-E variants exhibited at least 10-fold reduced potency, confirming the importance of native SGP structure for membrane activity.  

### EXAMPLE 5  

In vivo anti-tumor activity was evaluated using human tumor xenografts in nude mice. Direct intratumoral injection of SGP (100 μM or 1 mM) significantly reduced tumor volumes compared to PBS controls. After 8 weeks, SGP-treated tumors were 5-fold smaller on average. Multiple tumor types responded similarly, including breast carcinomas, prostate cancers, and lung carcinomas. Treated mice remained tumor-free for up to 4 months post-treatment, with no evidence of metastasis.  

### EXAMPLE 6  

Histopathological analysis of SGP-treated tumors revealed widespread cell death with condensed nuclei, consistent with apoptosis. The extracellular matrix remained intact, suggesting selective action on cellular components. Caspase-3 activation confirmed apoptotic pathways. Notably, while high SGP concentrations caused immediate non-apoptotic death in vitro, similar concentrations induced apoptosis in vivo, indicating differential mechanisms depending on microenvironment.  

### EXAMPLE 7  

Skin toxicity evaluations showed no adverse effects from intradermal or subcutaneous SGP injection. Injected areas remained normal throughout the 2-week observation period, demonstrating favorable safety profile for localized administration.  

### EXAMPLE 8  

Matrigel assays revealed that extracellular matrix components inhibit SGP's membrane-disrupting activity. While SGP caused complete cell death within 10 minutes in standard culture, it had no effect when cells were coated with matrigel. Similar protection occurred with fibronectin matrices. This matrix-dependent inactivation explains the selective anti-tumor effects observed in vivo despite broad membrane activity in vitro. The data suggest that intact connective tissue protects normal cells while allowing SGP access to tumor cells.
