# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present disclosure relates to recombinant production of immunoglobulin A (IgA) proteases. More particularly, the invention provides methods for producing soluble and active recombinant IgA proteases in bacterial host cells, wherein the expressed polypeptides lack the α protein domain and β-core domain present in naturally occurring IgA protease precursors. The disclosure further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising the recombinant IgA proteases and methods of using such compositions for treating IgA deposition disorders.  

## BACKGROUND  

Immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1) proteases constitute a specialized class of bacterial enzymes that specifically cleave the hinge region of human IgA1 antibodies. These proteases are produced by various pathogenic bacteria including Neisseria meningitidis, Neisseria gonorrhoeae, Haemophilus influenzae, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and other bacterial species. IgA1 proteases exhibit remarkable substrate specificity, cleaving only human IgA1 and certain ape IgA molecules while showing no activity against human IgA2, IgG, IgM, or IgA from other mammalian species.  

Naturally occurring IgA1 proteases function as virulence factors that enable bacterial pathogens to evade the host immune response by cleaving secretory IgA antibodies. Structurally, serine-type IgA1 proteases are initially synthesized as precursor proteins comprising four domains: (i) an N-terminal signal peptide for secretion; (ii) the protease domain containing the catalytic machinery; (iii) an α protein domain of unknown function; and (iv) a C-terminal β-core domain responsible for outer membrane translocation. During maturation, the precursor undergoes autoproteolytic processing to release the active protease domain.  

IgA deposition disorders, particularly IgA nephropathy (IgAN), represent a significant unmet medical need. These conditions are characterized by pathological accumulation of IgA1-containing immune complexes in tissues, notably the kidney glomeruli. Current treatment options are limited and often involve nonspecific immunosuppression. The potential therapeutic application of IgA1 proteases for cleaving pathogenic IgA1 deposits has been demonstrated in animal models, where administration of H. influenzae IgA1 protease reduced glomerular IgA1 immune complex deposition.  

Previous attempts to produce therapeutic quantities of IgA1 proteases have faced substantial challenges. Native production in pathogenic bacteria yields extremely low quantities (typically <0.1 mg/L) of protease, making this approach impractical for large-scale manufacturing. Recombinant expression in alternative bacterial hosts has been hampered by poor solubility, low activity, and inefficient processing of the full-length precursor protein. The requirement for hemin in H. influenzae culture further complicates production. While some success has been reported with Streptococcus-derived metalloproteases expressed in E. coli, yields remain insufficient for therapeutic applications.  

There exists a pressing need for improved methods of producing soluble, active IgA proteases in quantities sufficient for therapeutic use. Such methods should avoid reliance on pathogenic bacterial strains while achieving high yields of properly folded, functional enzyme. The present disclosure addresses these needs through novel recombinant expression strategies that overcome previous limitations.  

## SUMMARY  

The present disclosure provides methods for producing recombinant IgA proteases in bacterial host cells. The methods involve expressing IgA protease polypeptides that lack the α protein domain and β-core domain present in naturally occurring IgA protease precursors. Surprisingly, these truncated polypeptides can be produced as soluble, active enzymes in high yield when expressed under optimized conditions.  

In one embodiment, the method comprises transforming a bacterial host cell with an expression vector encoding an IgA protease polypeptide comprising the protease domain but lacking the α protein domain and β-core domain. Suitable host cells include E. coli strains such as C41(DE3), C43(DE3), and derivatives thereof. The expression vector may be IPTG-inducible and include a strong promoter such as the T7 promoter. Following transformation, the host cells are cultured under conditions that promote expression of the IgA protease polypeptide.  

The expressed IgA protease polypeptides form inclusion bodies within the host cells. These inclusion bodies are isolated, solubilized using chaotropic agents such as urea or guanidine hydrochloride, and subsequently refolded in appropriate buffers to yield soluble, active IgA protease. Refolding buffers may comprise Tris, CHES, MES, or PBS, optionally supplemented with arginine. The refolding process is preferably conducted at controlled temperature and protein concentration to maximize recovery of active enzyme.  

The methods enable production of IgA protease at significantly higher yields than previously achievable. In particular embodiments, the methods produce at least 100 mg/L of soluble IgA protease in culture, with total recovery of 20-40 mg/L of purified active enzyme. This represents a greater than 1000-fold improvement over prior methods of IgA protease production. The recombinant IgA proteases exhibit specific activity comparable to or exceeding that of native enzymes.  

The disclosure further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the recombinant IgA proteases. These compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration including intravenous, subcutaneous, or intramuscular injection. The compositions find particular utility in treating IgA deposition disorders such as IgA nephropathy, where they may be administered in amounts effective to cleave pathogenic IgA1 deposits.

### IgA Protease Polypeptides  

The IgA protease polypeptides of the disclosure comprise the protease domain of naturally occurring IgA proteases but lack the α protein domain and β-core domain. These polypeptides are expressed in recombinant host cells, preferably bacterial cells such as E. coli.  

Serine-type IgA proteases are classified as type 1 or type 2 based on their cleavage specificity. Type 1 proteases cleave between proline and serine residues in the IgA1 hinge region, while type 2 proteases cleave between proline and threonine. Representative IgA protease sequences include those from H. influenzae (GenBank X59800), N. meningitidis (GenBank M29293), and N. gonorrhoeae (GenBank M29292).  

The disclosure provides methods for producing these proteases in yields substantially higher than previously achievable. Key to this improvement is the expression of truncated polypeptides lacking domains required for natural secretion but unnecessary for catalytic activity. In preferred embodiments, the expressed polypeptides consist essentially of the mature protease domain (approximately 100 kDa) with an optional N-terminal signal peptide or C-terminal affinity tag.  

Expression vectors for producing the IgA protease polypeptides may utilize strong promoters such as T7, tac, or cold shock promoters. The vectors preferably include selectable markers (e.g., antibiotic resistance genes) and origins of replication compatible with the host cell. Particularly suitable vectors include pET and pCold series plasmids.  

Host cells are transformed with the expression vectors and cultured under conditions inducing IgA protease expression. Optimal conditions may include induction with 0.2-1 mM IPTG at 12-30°C for 3-24 hours. Preferred host strains include E. coli C41(DE3) and C43(DE3), which exhibit superior yields of soluble, active protease.  

Following expression, the IgA protease polypeptides are typically recovered from inclusion bodies. The inclusion bodies are isolated by cell lysis and centrifugation, then solubilized using chaotropic agents. Refolding is accomplished by gradual removal of denaturant in appropriate buffers, yielding soluble, active enzyme. The refolded protease may be further purified using techniques such as immobilized metal affinity chromatography, ion exchange chromatography, and size exclusion chromatography.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions  

The disclosure provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the recombinant IgA proteases formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. Such compositions may be administered to subjects suffering from IgA deposition disorders to cleave pathogenic IgA1 deposits.  

The compositions may include excipients such as buffers (e.g., phosphate, citrate), tonicity agents (e.g., NaCl, glycerol), stabilizers (e.g., sugars, amino acids), and preservatives (e.g., benzyl alcohol). The formulations may be lyophilized for long-term storage or provided as ready-to-use solutions.  

Administration routes include intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous injection. Dosages will vary depending on factors such as disease severity but typically range from 0.1-100 mg/kg. Treatment regimens may involve single or repeated administrations as needed to maintain therapeutic effect.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

This example describes construction of expression vectors encoding truncated IgA1 protease polypeptides.  

DNA fragments encoding the H. influenzae IgA1 protease domain (with or without signal peptide) were amplified by PCR from plasmid pFG26. The fragments were cloned into pET21a and pColdIV vectors using NdeI and BamHI restriction sites, generating four constructs:  

1. pET-S-IGAN: encodes His-tagged protease domain with signal peptide  
2. pET-IGAN: encodes His-tagged protease domain without signal peptide  
3. pCold-S-IGAN: cold shock vector encoding His-tagged protease with signal peptide  
4. pCold-IGAN: cold shock vector encoding His-tagged protease without signal peptide  

The constructs were transformed into various E. coli strains including BL21(DE3), ArcticExpress(DE3), and C41(DE3). Transformants were cultured in LB medium containing carbenicillin at 37°C until OD600 reached 0.6. Protein expression was induced with 0.2-1 mM IPTG at 12-30°C for 3-24 hours.  

Cells were harvested by centrifugation and lysed using B-PER II reagent. Total cell lysates and soluble fractions were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blot using anti-His antibodies. The pET-IGAN construct in C41(DE3) cells induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at 20°C for 24 hours yielded the highest levels of soluble, active IgA1 protease (approximately 100 mg/L).  

### Example 2  

This example describes purification of soluble IgA1 protease from E. coli C41(DE3).  

Cells expressing IgA1 protease from the pET-IGAN construct were harvested by centrifugation and resuspended in TBS buffer. The suspension was homogenized using a high-pressure homogenizer and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 30 minutes at 4°C. The supernatant was filtered through a 0.2 μm membrane.  

The filtrate was applied to a nickel-chelating sepharose column equilibrated with TBS. Bound proteins were eluted with an imidazole gradient. Eluted fractions containing IgA1 protease were pooled, diluted 10-fold with 25 mM Tris (pH 8.0), and loaded onto a Q sepharose column. The flow-through fraction was concentrated by tangential flow filtration and further purified by size exclusion chromatography on an S300 column.  

The final product was >95% pure by SDS-PAGE analysis, with yields of 20-40 mg/L culture. Activity assays demonstrated specific activity of approximately 46 ng/μL/min per ng of protease, exceeding that of native H. influenzae IgA1 protease.  

### Example 3  

This example describes production of IgA1 protease from inclusion bodies.  

E. coli C41(DE3) cells expressing IgA1 protease were lysed, and inclusion bodies were isolated by centrifugation. The inclusion bodies were washed with detergent and solubilized in 8 M urea. The solubilized protein was refolded by gradual dilution into refolding buffer (50 mM Tris, 1 M arginine, 5 mM reduced glutathione, 0.5 mM oxidized glutathione, pH 8.0) at 4°C.  

The refolded protein was concentrated and purified by nickel affinity chromatography followed by anion exchange chromatography. The final product exhibited equivalent activity to protease purified from soluble fractions, with yields of 1-2 g/L of refolded active protease.  

### Example 4  

This example describes large-scale production of IgA1 protease.  

A 10 L fermentation of E. coli C41(DE3)/pET-IGAN was performed in defined medium. Expression was induced with 0.4 mM IPTG at 20°C for 24 hours. Cells were harvested by continuous centrifugation and stored at -80°C.  

For purification, frozen cell paste was thawed and homogenized in TBS. Inclusion bodies were isolated by centrifugation and washed with 2% Triton X-100. The washed inclusion bodies were solubilized in 8 M urea and refolded by continuous dialysis against 50 mM Tris, 0.5 M arginine, pH 8.0.  

The refolded protein was concentrated and purified by sequential chromatography on nickel, Q sepharose, and S300 columns. The process yielded approximately 200 mg of >95% pure, active IgA1 protease per liter of fermentation culture.

The methods and compositions described herein represent a significant advance in the treatment of IgA deposition disorders.
