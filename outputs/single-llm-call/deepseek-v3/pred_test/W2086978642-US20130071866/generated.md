# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to methods for measuring adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels in biological samples and utilizing such measurements for evaluating illness severity in real-time. More specifically, the invention provides an improved ATP extraction technique using phenol-based solutions combined with measurement of intermediate metabolites to establish novel biomarkers for critical illness assessment. The technical field encompasses diagnostic methods applicable in intensive care settings where rapid evaluation of patient deterioration is essential for clinical decision-making.

## BACKGROUND ART  

Adenosine triphosphate serves as the fundamental energy currency in biological systems, playing central roles in cellular metabolism and viability. Conventional ATP measurement techniques face substantial limitations due to inefficient extraction methods that fail to recover ATP from protein-rich samples. Existing chaotropic reagents such as trichloroacetic acid and perchloric acid demonstrate poor performance with high-protein specimens, as ATP becomes co-precipitated with insoluble proteins during processing.  

Current illness severity scoring systems including the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE) II and Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) scores suffer from multiple drawbacks. These systems require time-consuming manual calculations, depend on subjective clinical assessments, and demonstrate inter-examiner variability. Furthermore, they fail to provide real-time monitoring capabilities, instead offering only periodic snapshots of patient condition.  

The invention introduces a revolutionary ATP extraction methodology utilizing phenol compound solutions that overcome previous technical barriers. This advancement enables accurate ATP quantification even in protein-dense biological matrices. When combined with measurements of metabolic intermediates such as lactic acid and ketone bodies, the extracted ATP levels form the basis for novel composite biomarkers that address the critical need for dynamic illness evaluation tools in intensive care settings.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Object to be Solved by the Invention  

The present invention addresses the unmet clinical need for real-time biomarkers capable of accurately evaluating illness severity and predicting patient outcomes. Conventional scoring systems fail to provide continuous monitoring and require complex calculations that limit their utility in time-sensitive critical care situations. The invention solves these problems by establishing quantitative relationships between extracted ATP levels, intermediate metabolites, and clinical outcomes to create novel prognostic indicators.

### Means to Solve the Object  

The invention provides multiple technical solutions to achieve reliable illness severity evaluation. First, it measures ATP levels in biological samples using an optimized extraction process. The method extracts ATP by treating samples with a phenol compound solution having a specified pH range, preferably between 6.0-8.0. The solution preferably contains phenol as the active phenol compound and incorporates protein denaturants to enhance ATP recovery.  

The measurement employs luciferin-luciferase luminescence reactions to quantify extracted ATP with high sensitivity. Additionally, the method measures lactic acid levels and ketone body levels in the same samples to create composite biomarkers. These measurements enable calculation of the ATP-Lactate Energy Score (A-LES) and ATP-Ketone Energy Score (A-KES), which correlate strongly with illness severity and clinical outcomes.

### Effect of the Invention  

The invention produces multiple technical effects that advance the field of critical care monitoring. It enables accurate real-time evaluation of illness severity through ATP level quantification, overcoming previous limitations in extraction efficiency. The composite biomarkers (A-LES and A-KES) provide superior prognostic capability compared to conventional scoring systems, with particular advantages in monitoring disease progression and treatment response.  

By combining ATP measurements with intermediate metabolite levels, the invention captures comprehensive metabolic dysfunction patterns associated with critical illness. This multi-parameter approach improves upon single-marker systems by reflecting the complex pathophysiology of organ failure and energy metabolism disturbances. The method demonstrates particular utility in intensive care settings where rapid assessment of patient deterioration is essential for timely intervention.

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

The invention provides detailed methodologies for implementing the ATP-based evaluation system. Illness severity is defined through quantitative thresholds for ATP levels and composite scores. Sample collection procedures specify venous or arterial blood collection into appropriate anticoagulant-containing vessels, with immediate transfer to phenol-based extraction reagents.  

The invention encompasses measurement of ATP levels in various sample types including whole blood, plasma, serum, and tissue homogenates. Applicable illnesses include but are not limited to septic shock, multiple organ failure, post-surgical complications, and severe infections. The ATP measurement protocol involves phenol compound treatment at controlled pH (6.0-8.0), phase separation by centrifugation, and luminescence-based quantification using luciferin-luciferase reactions.  

Healthy individuals demonstrate characteristic ATP levels ranging from 0.43-0.81 mM, with slight variations based on sex and age. The invention defines specific criteria for severity evaluation: mild abnormality corresponds to ATP levels 0.30-0.42 mM, severe abnormality to 0.20-0.29 mM, and high mortality risk to levels below 0.20 mM.  

For the A-LES biomarker (lactate/ATP ratio), normal ranges are below 2.83, mild abnormality corresponds to 2.84-8.00, severe abnormality to 8.01-20.00, and values above 20.00 indicate critical condition with high mortality risk. The A-KES biomarker (ketone bodies/ATP ratio) follows similar thresholds adjusted for ketone body physiology.  

The invention provides detailed protocols for calculating these composite scores and interpreting their clinical significance. Time-dependent monitoring of ATP levels and derived biomarkers enables dynamic assessment of treatment efficacy and disease progression not possible with conventional scoring systems.

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples demonstrate practical applications of the invention without limiting its scope:

### Example 1  

Clinical validation involved measuring ATP and lactic acid levels in 42 intensive care unit patients. A-LES values were calculated and correlated with clinical outcomes. Time-series analysis revealed decreasing ATP levels and increasing A-LES values predicted mortality with 89.7% accuracy. The data demonstrated superior sensitivity of A-LES compared to single parameter measurements, with values above 20.00 consistently associated with fatal outcomes.  

Serial measurements showed A-LES changes preceded clinical deterioration by 12-48 hours, enabling early intervention. The results established strong correlation between A-LES trends and patient outcomes (r=0.98, p<0.001), confirming the biomarker's prognostic value.

### Example 2  

Comparative analysis between APACHE II scores and A-LES values in 28 critically ill patients demonstrated equivalent predictive accuracy (AUC 0.83 vs 0.85 respectively). However, A-LES provided advantages in measurement frequency and objectivity. The study revealed strong correlation between admission A-LES and APACHE II scores (r=0.91, p<0.001), validating the composite biomarker's clinical relevance.  

Notably, A-LES showed greater dynamic range than APACHE II in monitoring disease progression, with changes of 300-400% observed in deteriorating patients compared to maximum 50% changes in APACHE II scores.

### Example 3  

Animal studies measured ATP, lactic acid, and ketone body levels in murine sepsis models. Both A-LES and A-KES values showed progressive increases correlating with disease severity (r=0.94 and 0.89 respectively). The results confirmed the biomarkers' ability to reflect mitochondrial dysfunction and metabolic derangements.  

Time-course analysis revealed A-LES increases preceded clinical symptoms by 6-12 hours, demonstrating early warning capability. The study established species-specific reference ranges and validated cross-species applicability of the measurement principles.

The invention has broad applicability across medical diagnostics and critical care monitoring. Hospitals and intensive care units can implement the testing method for real-time patient assessment, with particular value in sepsis monitoring, postoperative care, and trauma management.
