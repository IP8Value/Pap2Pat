## BACKGROUND  

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) presents a significant challenge for therapeutic delivery due to its highly selective permeability that prevents most macromolecules from crossing into the central nervous system. Receptor-mediated transport systems have emerged as promising strategies to overcome this limitation by exploiting endogenous cellular uptake mechanisms.  

pH-induced changes in protein binding affinity represent a fundamental biological mechanism that can be harnessed for therapeutic purposes. Histidine plays a central role in pH sensitivity due to its side-chain pKa of 5.5-6.5 within protein contexts, making it particularly responsive to the pH gradients encountered during receptor-mediated endocytosis.  

The transferrin receptor (TfR) serves as an ideal therapeutic target due to its ubiquitous expression and natural role in iron transport. Transferrin, the natural ligand for TfR, demonstrates pH-dependent binding behavior - maintaining tight association at blood pH (7.2-7.4) while releasing iron and increasing affinity at endosomal pH (5.0-6.0). This natural pH sensitivity has inspired the development of cytotoxins based on transferrin conjugates, though these approaches remain limited by the natural trafficking pathways of transferrin.  

There exists a significant need for engineered antibodies that can specifically exploit pH-dependent mechanisms to enhance therapeutic delivery while overcoming the limitations of natural ligand pathways. Such pH-dependent antibodies would ideally maintain high target affinity at physiological pH while demonstrating rapid dissociation under acidic conditions to facilitate enhanced intracellular accumulation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides pH-dependent antibodies specifically engineered for enhanced therapeutic delivery through the transferrin receptor (TfR) pathway. These antibodies demonstrate pH-sensitive binding characteristics achieved through strategic histidine mutagenesis of complementarity-determining regions (CDRs), particularly CDRH1.  

In preferred embodiments, the invention comprises single-chain variable fragments (scFvs) containing multiple histidine substitutions in CDRH1 that confer rapid dissociation from TfR at endosomal pH (5.0-6.0) while maintaining high affinity binding at physiological pH (7.2-7.4). The antibodies may be administered as monomers or artificially dimerized constructs.  

The invention further provides methods for delivering therapeutic agents by conjugating them to the pH-dependent antibodies, allowing targeted delivery to cells expressing TfR. Various embodiments include pharmaceutical compositions containing the pH-dependent antibodies alone or conjugated to therapeutic payloads, formulated for administration to subjects in need thereof.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to engineered antibodies and antibody fragments that demonstrate pH-dependent binding to the transferrin receptor (TfR). The scope of the invention encompasses pH-sensitive antibodies, methods for their production, pharmaceutical compositions containing them, and methods for their therapeutic use.  

As used herein, "administering" or "administration" refers to the introduction of the pharmaceutical composition into a subject by any suitable route, including but not limited to intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, or direct CNS delivery. "Contacting" or "exposing" refers to bringing the antibody into physical proximity with target cells or tissues under conditions allowing binding interaction.  

The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise an effective amount of the pH-dependent antibody or antibody conjugate in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. The effective amount refers to a quantity sufficient to achieve the desired therapeutic effect without causing unacceptable toxicity. Suitable carriers include aqueous solutions, buffers, stabilizers, and preservatives, as well as controlled or sustained release formulations designed to maintain therapeutic levels over extended periods.  

The antibodies of the invention include full-length immunoglobulin molecules as well as functional fragments thereof. The term "antibody" encompasses monoclonal antibodies, human monoclonal antibodies, chimeric antibodies, humanized antibodies, fully human antibodies, and recombinant human antibodies. Functional fragments include Fab, Fab', F(ab')2, single-chain variable fragments (scFv), Fv, dsFv, Fd, domain antibodies (dAbs), and tandem diabodies (TandAbs).  

The antibody fragments comprise a light chain variable region (VL) and heavy chain variable region (VH), which may be derived from any immunoglobulin isotype. The constant regions may include heavy chain constant domains and either kappa or lambda light chain constant regions. The complementarity determining regions (CDRs), particularly CDRH1, CDRH2, and CDRH3, contain the antigen-binding determinants responsible for pH-dependent TfR recognition.  

A "pH-sensitive antibody" as used herein refers to an antibody or antibody fragment that demonstrates pH-dependent binding to TfR, characterized by maintained high affinity at neutral pH (7.2-7.4) and rapid dissociation at acidic pH (5.0-6.0). This property is achieved through strategic placement of histidine residues in CDRs, particularly CDRH1, where protonation under acidic conditions disrupts antigen binding.  

The transferrin receptor (TfR) targeted by the invention is a cell surface glycoprotein involved in iron homeostasis through endocytosis of iron-bound transferrin. The invention exploits the natural endocytosis and recycling pathway of TfR to deliver therapeutic payloads intracellularly.  

The pH-dependent antibodies are produced through methods including yeast surface display combined with histidine saturation mutagenesis of CDRH1. This approach allows screening of large libraries to identify clones with optimal pH-sensitive binding profiles. Preferred embodiments include scFv M16, which contains multiple histidine substitutions in CDRH1 that confer enhanced dissociation at pH 5.5 while maintaining sub-nanomolar affinity at pH 7.4.  

Therapeutic agents that may be delivered using the invention include small molecule drugs, proteins, nucleic acids, toxins, and diagnostic agents. These may be covalently or non-covalently linked to the pH-dependent antibody through various conjugation strategies. The invention further provides expression vectors encoding the pH-dependent antibodies, host cells containing such vectors, and liposome or nanoparticle formulations decorated with the targeting antibodies.  

Administration of the pH-dependent antibodies may be accomplished through various routes depending on the therapeutic indication. The invention also provides kits containing the pH-dependent antibodies along with instructions for use, as well as therapeutic devices incorporating the technology for controlled delivery.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope:  

Example 1 demonstrates pH-dependent mechanisms in biological systems and motivates the engineering approach. Histidine saturation mutagenesis was performed on the anti-TfR scFv H7 CDRH1 region, creating a diverse library for screening under pH-dependent selection pressure.  

Example 2 details yeast surface display methodology combined with fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) to identify scFvs with increased dissociation from TfR at pH 5.5. Clone M16 was identified as having pH-sensitivity resulting primarily from histidine residues in CDRH1.  

Example 3 characterizes the internalization and intracellular distribution of pH-sensitive scFv M16 compared to wild-type H7 and pH-insensitive mutant N5. M16 demonstrated significantly greater intracellular accumulation and distinct trafficking patterns, attributed to its pH-dependent dissociation properties.  

Example 4 provides materials and methods for cell culture, yeast culture, plasmid construction, and library generation. Histidine-saturation mutagenesis of scFv H7 CDRH1 was accomplished through creation of a recombinant histidine-saturated CDRH1 library by homologous recombination.  

Example 5 describes FACS-based screening and selection procedures, including yeast library preparation, sorting protocols, pH shift procedures, and binding affinity quantification. The screening strategy successfully isolated pH-sensitive clones like M16 from the CDRH1his library.  

Example 6 details scFv secretion and purification methods, binding assays, immunocytochemistry procedures, and image analysis techniques. Quantitative analysis demonstrated M16's enhanced internalization and reduced co-localization with endosomal/lysosomal markers compared to controls.  

Example 7 presents statistical analysis of the results, confirming the significant differences in pH-dependent behavior between engineered clones. The data support the conclusion that pH-sensitive TfR binding can be engineered through strategic histidine mutagenesis to enhance intracellular antibody accumulation.  

Example 8 discusses the therapeutic relevance of pH-dependent antibodies for TfR-targeted therapy, particularly their potential to overcome limitations of current delivery approaches. The engineered antibodies represent a significant advance in receptor-mediated delivery technology with applications in treating neurological disorders and cancers.
