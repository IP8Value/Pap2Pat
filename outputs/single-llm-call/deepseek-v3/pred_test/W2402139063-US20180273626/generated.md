# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and allergy therapeutics, specifically to compositions and methods for treating IgE-mediated allergic diseases using engineered IgE variants resistant to existing anti-IgE therapies such as omalizumab. The invention encompasses novel IgE polypeptides containing defined mutations that prevent binding by therapeutic anti-IgE antibodies while retaining functional interactions with IgE receptors (FcεRI and CD23). These compositions enable innovative treatment strategies combining conventional anti-IgE therapy with administration of resistant IgE variants to modulate allergic responses through receptor occupancy maintenance and synergistic inhibition of effector cell activation.  

## BACKGROUND  

Allergic diseases represent a significant global health burden, affecting approximately 30% of the population in developed countries. These disorders are mediated by immunoglobulin E (IgE) antibodies, which bind allergens and trigger inflammatory responses through high-affinity interactions with FcεRI receptors on mast cells and basophils. IgE also interacts with low-affinity CD23 receptors on B cells and other immune cells, regulating IgE production and antigen presentation.  

Therapeutic strategies targeting IgE have proven effective in managing allergic asthma, chronic urticaria, and other IgE-mediated conditions. Omalizumab, a humanized monoclonal anti-IgE antibody, neutralizes free IgE and reduces FcεRI expression on effector cells by disrupting IgE homeostasis. Omalizumab binds the Cε3 domain of IgE, competitively inhibiting interactions with both FcεRI and CD23 through steric hindrance. However, current anti-IgE therapies have significant limitations. Omalizumab requires high doses and prolonged treatment to achieve clinical effects due to its inability to efficiently displace IgE from preformed IgE:FcεRI complexes on mast cells and basophils.  

Recent advances in protein engineering have yielded Designed Ankyrin Repeat Proteins (DARPins) that disrupt IgE:FcεRI complexes more effectively than omalizumab. However, all current anti-IgE agents deplete both pathological allergen-specific IgE and benign IgE species indiscriminately. This depletion triggers homeostatic regulatory pathways that may counteract therapeutic benefits, including increased basophil sensitivity per remaining IgE molecule and upregulated IgE production through CD23-mediated signaling.  

There remains an unmet need for improved anti-IgE therapies that overcome these limitations while maintaining the safety profile of existing treatments. The present invention addresses this need through engineered IgE variants resistant to omalizumab neutralization that can be administered concurrently with conventional anti-IgE therapy to modulate allergic responses through novel mechanisms.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides omalizumab-resistant IgE variants containing specific mutations in the Cε3 domain that prevent omalizumab binding while preserving FcεRI and CD23 interactions. In particular embodiments, the invention encompasses IgE polypeptides with an arginine-to-asparagine substitution at position 419 (R419N), which introduces an N-linked glycosylation site that sterically blocks omalizumab binding without substantially perturbing receptor interactions.  

The compositions of the invention include pharmaceutical formulations containing these omalizumab-resistant IgE variants alone or in combination with conventional anti-IgE agents. The IgE variants may comprise full-length IgE or Fc3-4 fragments containing the mutated Cε3 domain. The invention further provides methods of treating IgE-mediated disorders by administering these compositions to subjects, either as monotherapy or in combination with omalizumab or other anti-IgE therapies.  

A key advantage of the invention is the ability to perform "IgE exchange" therapy, where native IgE on effector cells is replaced with omalizumab-resistant variants during anti-IgE treatment. This approach maintains receptor occupancy while removing allergen-specific IgE, potentially preventing homeostatic counter-regulation observed with conventional IgE depletion strategies. Experimental data demonstrate that the IgE-R419N-Fc3-4 variant exchanges effectively with native IgE on human basophils in the presence of omalizumab and shows synergistic inhibition of basophil activation when combined with omalizumab at low doses.  

The invention further encompasses kits containing the omalizumab-resistant IgE variants and instructions for use in combination therapy. These kits may include separate or combined formulations of the IgE variant and anti-IgE agent, along with ancillary reagents for administration. The compositions are suitable for various administration routes including subcutaneous, intravenous, or mucosal delivery.
