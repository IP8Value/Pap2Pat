# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cell therapies represent a promising approach for treating various diseases and injuries by harnessing the regenerative potential of living cells. However, significant regulatory constraints exist regarding the isolation and manipulation of cells intended for therapeutic use. Current exogenous cell therapies require extensive in vitro processing under good manufacturing practice guidelines, leading to high costs and lengthy approval processes. Alternative approaches such as cytokine-based cell mobilization present drawbacks including multiple hospital visits and potential side effects for patients.

Intraoperative cell therapies offer a compelling solution by enabling cell harvesting and administration during the same surgical session. This approach eliminates the need for ex vivo expansion while reducing costs and treatment time. Bone healing applications particularly benefit from such strategies, given the high incidence of fractures and associated impaired healing cases. Current clinical approaches using CD34+ or CD133+ cells demonstrate the potential of cell-based therapies, but rely on magnetic bead separation methods that leave beads attached to transplanted cells.

Existing cell isolation techniques face limitations regarding regulatory classification as advanced therapy medicinal products when using antibody-labeled beads that remain with the cells. There exists a need for minimally manipulative isolation procedures that can produce cell populations free of isolation materials. The present invention addresses these limitations through a novel approach to cell enrichment that enables reversible binding without residual isolation components.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a device and method for enriching cells expressing specific surface markers from mixed cell populations. The system utilizes aptamer-coupled beads that reversibly bind target cells through specific interactions with cell surface markers such as CD31. The device comprises a column containing the aptamer-coupled beads and a filter with pore sizes designed to retain beads while allowing passage of cells.

The method involves contacting a mixed cell population with aptamer-coupled beads, allowing binding of target cells expressing the surface marker, washing away unbound cells, and releasing bound cells through mechanical forces. The released cells form an enriched population substantially free of beads and aptamers. The invention specifically enables enrichment of CD31+ cells, which demonstrate therapeutic potential for applications including bone healing, angiogenesis, and immunomodulation.

Key aspects of the invention include the use of aptamers specific for CD31 coupled to solid supports such as agarose beads. The beads have diameters between 45-165 μm and are packed in columns containing filters with 20 μm pores. Aptamer concentrations between 5-10 μg/ml provide optimal binding characteristics. The system configuration allows for portable, closed-system operation suitable for intraoperative use.

The method steps include incubating cells with aptamer-coupled beads, washing with buffer, applying mechanical force to release cells, and collecting the enriched population by centrifugation. The resulting CD31+ enriched cell population maintains viability and therapeutic potential while being substantially free of isolation components. The invention further provides kits containing the device components for convenient cell enrichment procedures.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a comprehensive system for enriching cells expressing specific surface markers from mixed cell populations. The system utilizes aptamer-based reversible binding to isolate target cells while maintaining cell viability and therapeutic potential. This detailed description outlines the components, methods, and applications of the invention.

### I. CD31+ Cells

CD31+ cells, also known as Platelet/Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule 1 (PECAM-1) expressing cells, represent a valuable cell population with demonstrated therapeutic potential. These cells exhibit angiogenic, osteogenic, and immunosuppressive properties that make them particularly suitable for regenerative applications. CD31 is widely expressed among viable lymphocytes and monocytes, with expression levels independent of gender and preserved with aging.

Traditional methods for purifying CD31+ cells rely on antibody-based approaches such as fluorescence-activated cell sorting or magnetic bead separation. These methods present limitations including potential cell damage, residual isolation components, and regulatory challenges. The present invention overcomes these limitations through an aptamer-based purification method that enables reversible binding and clean separation of CD31+ cells.

### II. Aptamers

Aptamers represent oligonucleotide molecules capable of specific binding to target molecules with high affinity. These nucleic acid ligands offer several advantages over antibodies for cell isolation applications, including stability during storage, ease of chemical synthesis, and minimal toxicity. The invention utilizes aptamers specific for CD31 that demonstrate high sensitivity and specificity for target cells.

The Systematic Evolution of Ligands by Exponential Enrichment (SELEX) process enables generation of aptamers with desired binding characteristics. Counter-SELEX methods further improve aptamer specificity by eliminating sequences that bind undesired targets. The CD31-specific aptamers used in the invention exhibit binding affinities suitable for reversible cell capture while maintaining cell viability.

Aptamer immobilization on solid supports employs tag and probe components that facilitate stable coupling. Biotin-neutravidin interactions provide particularly effective coupling due to their extremely high binding affinity. The invention utilizes aptamer concentrations between 5-10 μg/ml for optimal cell capture and release characteristics.

### III. Devices for Enrichment of Cells

The cell enrichment device comprises several key components arranged to facilitate efficient cell isolation. A column containing aptamer-coupled beads serves as the primary capture element, with beads sized between 45-165 μm in diameter. A filter with 20 μm pores prevents bead passage while allowing cell transit. The system includes syringe fittings for controlled fluid flow and centrifuge tube housings for cell collection.

Device configurations may incorporate mixtures of aptamer-coupled and aptamer-free beads at ratios optimizing cell capture and system performance. The invention provides portable, closed-system designs suitable for intraoperative use, including prepackaged sterile configurations. Various embodiments enable cell release through mechanical forces, temperature changes, or complementary nucleic acids.

The device enables production of cell populations enriched for cells expressing specific surface markers while remaining substantially free of isolation components. CD31 represents a particularly suitable target marker, though the system accommodates other surface markers through appropriate aptamer selection.

### IV. Methods of Enriching Cells from a Mixed Cell Population

The invention provides methods for enriching cells from mixed populations through aptamer-mediated reversible binding. The process involves contacting aptamer-coupled beads with the cell population under conditions promoting target cell binding. Incubation times between 10-30 minutes at temperatures between 4-37°C provide optimal binding characteristics.

Following binding, washing steps remove unbound cells while mechanical forces release bound cells from the aptamer-coupled beads. The invention enables cell release through fluid shear forces generated by resuspension, avoiding the need for enzymatic or chemical treatments. Released cells pass through filters that retain beads, resulting in populations substantially free of isolation components.

The methods specifically enable enrichment of CD31+ cells from various sources including whole blood, peripheral blood mononuclear cells, bone marrow, and tissue samples. The rapid enrichment process, requiring less than one hour, makes the method particularly suitable for intraoperative applications.

### V. Kits

The invention provides kits containing components for convenient cell enrichment procedures. Portable column configurations offer various volume options to accommodate different sample sizes. Filters with defined pore sizes ensure proper bead retention while allowing cell passage. The kits include aptamer-coupled beads at optimized ratios with aptamer-free beads, along with appropriate resuspension buffers.

Kit components enable complete cell enrichment procedures from sample loading through enriched cell collection. The invention contemplates various kit configurations tailored to specific applications, including bone healing, angiogenesis promotion, and immunomodulation therapies.

### VI. Methods of Treatment

The invention provides cell-based therapies utilizing enriched CD31+ cell populations. Administration methods include direct injection at surgical sites or affected tissues. The enriched cells demonstrate therapeutic potential for promoting angiogenesis, osteogenesis, and immunomodulation in various disease states.

Specific applications include treatment of bone fractures, ischemic diseases, and conditions benefiting from immunomodulation. The intraoperative nature of the cell enrichment process enables immediate therapeutic application during surgical procedures. The invention further encompasses combination therapies incorporating enriched cells with biodegradable scaffolds or other regenerative approaches.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and methods

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were prepared using density centrifugation from apheresis leukoreduction filters. A commercially available biotinylated aptamer specific for CD31 was validated for specificity using flow cytometry comparing aptamer binding to antibody staining. Magnetic bead studies benchmarked aptamer-based isolation against commercial antibody approaches.

The cell enrichment system was assembled using microcentrifuge tubes modified with 20 μm filters. Neutravidin agarose beads were packed within the tubes after aptamer coupling. PBMCs were processed through the system at controlled flow rates, with CD31+ cells released by mechanical resuspension and collected by centrifugation.

Conditioned medium studies assessed the angiogenic potential of enriched cells using tube formation assays with human umbilical vein endothelial cells. Osteogenic potential was evaluated through Alizarin red staining of mesenchymal stromal cells cultured with conditioned medium from enriched cells.

### Aptamer Selection and Validation

Flow cytometry analysis confirmed the CD31 aptamer's high sensitivity (99.0±1.3%) and specificity (96.9±4.1%) at optimal concentrations. Magnetic isolation studies demonstrated equivalent enrichment between aptamer-based and antibody-based approaches. The aptamer's capacity for reversible binding without residual cell attachment was validated through biophysical release methods.

### Development of a CD31+ Cell Enrichment System

System optimization studies identified 5 μg/ml as the optimal aptamer concentration, balancing yield (25.1±0.9%), purity (87±3% CD31+), and viability (97±1%). A 50:50 ratio of aptamer-coupled to aptamer-free beads prevented system clogging while maintaining performance. Bead volumes of 800 μl (400 μl coated, 400 μl uncoated) provided saturation of cell capture capacity.

### CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations

The optimized system significantly increased CD31+ cell percentages from 64±9% to 87±3% while maintaining high viability. Enriched populations showed increased monocytes (CD31+CD14+) and naive T cells (CD3+CD31+). Microscopy confirmed absence of residual beads in isolated cells.

### Angiogenic and Osteogenic Potential of CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations

Conditioned medium from enriched cells increased capillary-like tube formation 1.9±0.2 fold in angiogenesis assays. Osteogenic potential was demonstrated by 1.8±0.3 fold increased calcification in mesenchymal stromal cells. These results confirm retention of therapeutic potential following the enrichment process.
