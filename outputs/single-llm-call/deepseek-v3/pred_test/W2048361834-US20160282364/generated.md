# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) is a zinc metalloprotease that plays a critical role in insulin metabolism by catalyzing the degradation of insulin. IDE exhibits broad substrate specificity and has been implicated in the regulation of multiple peptide hormones including glucagon and amylin. The enzyme's ability to modulate insulin levels has made it an attractive target for therapeutic intervention in diabetes and related metabolic disorders.  

Current IDE inhibitors suffer from several limitations including lack of selectivity, poor pharmacokinetic properties, and insufficient potency to achieve meaningful biological effects. Many known inhibitors exhibit off-target activity against other metalloproteases or demonstrate inadequate bioavailability. Furthermore, most existing inhibitors do not discriminate between IDE's various substrates, potentially leading to unintended physiological consequences.  

The treatment of diabetes remains a significant unmet medical need despite advances in therapeutic options. Current approaches often fail to adequately control postprandial glucose excursions or address underlying defects in insulin signaling. There exists a pressing need for novel IDE inhibitors that can selectively modulate IDE activity with appropriate temporal control to improve glucose homeostasis without disrupting other physiological processes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses novel macrocyclic compounds that potently and selectively inhibit insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE). These inhibitors were identified through an in vitro selection process using a DNA-templated macrocycle library against mouse IDE. The most potent inhibitor, designated 6bK, demonstrates nanomolar potency against both human and mouse IDE while showing excellent selectivity over related proteases.  

A co-crystal structure of IDE bound to inhibitor 6b reveals that the macrocycle binds at a unique site spanning the interface between IDE's two major domains. This binding mode differs from conventional substrate-mimetic inhibitors and explains the compound's exceptional selectivity. Competitive binding assays demonstrate that 6bK does not compete with known active-site directed inhibitors, confirming its distinct binding mechanism.  

The inhibitors exhibit remarkable effects on glucose metabolism when administered in vivo. Acute IDE inhibition with 6bK significantly improves oral glucose tolerance in both lean and diet-induced obese (DIO) mice. This effect results from the compound's ability to transiently modulate multiple IDE substrates including insulin, glucagon, and amylin. The inhibitors demonstrate favorable pharmacokinetic properties with rapid clearance and minimal accumulation in tissues.  

The invention provides methods for identifying IDE-binding compounds using fluorescence polarization assays. These homogeneous assays employ macrocyclic IDE-binding molecules conjugated to detectable labels such as fluorophores. The methods enable measurement of fluorescence anisotropy to quantify binding interactions and determine inhibitor potency. The assays can be performed under physiological conditions and adapted for high-throughput screening of compound libraries.  

The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing the IDE inhibitors and methods for their use in treating metabolic disorders. The inhibitors are particularly effective when administered in temporal proximity to meals to modulate postprandial glucose excursions. The compounds may be formulated as injectable preparations or oral dosage forms with appropriate excipients.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides potent and selective macrocyclic inhibitors of insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) for modulating glucose homeostasis. These compounds offer significant advantages over existing IDE inhibitors by combining high potency with excellent selectivity and favorable pharmacokinetic properties.  

### Methods for Identifying IDE-Binding Compounds  

The invention encompasses robust methods for identifying compounds that bind IDE with high affinity. These methods involve contacting IDE with a detectable probe that specifically binds the enzyme, then measuring changes in probe binding in the presence of candidate compounds.  

In preferred embodiments, the probe comprises a macrocyclic IDE-binding molecule conjugated to a fluorophore such as fluorescein. Binding is quantified by fluorescence polarization assays that measure the anisotropy of emitted light. Vertically polarized excitation light induces fluorophore excitation, and emission intensities are measured in both vertical and horizontal planes. The degree of polarization reflects rotational freedom of the probe, which decreases upon IDE binding.  

The assays may be performed across a dynamic range of IDE concentrations to determine binding affinities. Typical IDE concentrations range from 0.1 nM to 1 µM, with probe concentrations near or below the Kd value. Assays are preferably conducted under physiological conditions (pH 7.4, 37°C) in aqueous buffers containing appropriate salts.  

Alternative detection methods include surface plasmon resonance, FRET-based assays, or physical separation of bound and free probe using precipitation or binding agents. These methods enable screening of diverse compound libraries to identify novel IDE inhibitors with desired pharmacological properties.

### Clinical Applications of Macrocyclic IDE Inhibitors  

The IDE inhibitors demonstrate remarkable efficacy in improving glucose tolerance when administered to animal models. In oral glucose tolerance tests, 6bK treatment (80 mg/kg) reduced blood glucose excursions by 40% compared to vehicle controls. This effect results from coordinated modulation of multiple IDE substrates including insulin, glucagon and amylin.  

The inhibitors exhibit an optimal pharmacokinetic profile for transient IDE inhibition. Plasma half-life of approximately 90 minutes ensures rapid clearance while providing sufficient duration of action during postprandial periods. Tissue distribution studies show minimal accumulation in non-target organs and no detectable brain penetration.  

Administration protocols involve dosing in temporal proximity to meals, typically 30 minutes before food intake. This timing aligns IDE inhibition with periods of greatest insulin demand. Dosing may be adjusted based on meal frequency, with omission of doses when meals are skipped.  

The compounds may be formulated as injectable solutions or solid oral dosage forms. Injectable preparations preferably use sterile water or saline with appropriate solubilizing agents. Oral formulations incorporate standard excipients such as microcrystalline cellulose, lactose, or starch.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

In vitro selection against mouse IDE identified macrocycle 6b as a potent inhibitor (IC50 = 37 nM). Structural optimization yielded 6bK with improved potency (IC50 = 12 nM) and selectivity (>1000-fold over related proteases).  

Co-crystallization of 6b with IDE revealed binding at a novel site bridging the enzyme's two major domains. The benzophenone group inserts into a hydrophobic pocket while the cyclohexylalanine side chain contacts a distinct subpocket. Mutagenesis studies confirmed critical binding interactions with residues A479 and G362.  

In vivo studies demonstrated 6bK's ability to improve glucose tolerance in both lean and DIO mice. Following oral glucose challenge (3 g/kg), 6bK-treated animals showed significantly lower blood glucose levels compared to controls (p<0.01). The effect correlated with increased plasma insulin and reduced glucagon levels during the test period.  

### Example 2  

IDE inhibition modulated calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) signaling in vivo. 6bK treatment augmented CGRP-induced hypotension in a dose-dependent manner, confirming IDE's role in CGRP degradation. The effect was transient, with blood pressure returning to baseline within 2 hours post-administration.
