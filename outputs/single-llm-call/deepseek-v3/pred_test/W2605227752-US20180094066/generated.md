# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to antibodies and antigen-binding fragments that specifically bind to the human prolactin receptor (PRLR). More particularly, the invention provides monoclonal antibodies, antibody-drug conjugates, and related compositions that bind with high affinity to PRLR and are useful for the treatment of cancers expressing PRLR, particularly breast cancers. The antibodies and conjugates of the invention demonstrate potent antitumor activity through targeted delivery of cytotoxic agents to PRLR-expressing tumor cells.

## BACKGROUND

The prolactin receptor (PRLR) is a single-pass transmembrane receptor belonging to the type 1 cytokine receptor superfamily. PRLR mediates signaling through multiple intracellular pathways including Jak2/Stat5, MAPK, and PI3K upon binding its ligand prolactin (PRL). While PRL-PRLR signaling plays important physiological roles in lactation and reproduction, emerging evidence suggests this pathway may contribute to tumor development and progression in certain cancers.

PRLR is overexpressed in malignant breast epithelium compared to normal breast tissue and is expressed across molecular subtypes of human breast cancers. However, the clinical utility of PRLR-targeting agents has been limited to date. Small molecule PRL antagonists suffer from short serum half-life and potential immunogenicity. Anti-PRLR antibodies that solely block PRLR signaling have shown limited antitumor efficacy in clinical trials, suggesting that PRLR signaling blockade alone may be insufficient for robust clinical activity.

There remains an unmet need for effective therapies targeting PRLR-positive cancers, particularly for advanced breast cancers that have progressed following standard therapies. The present invention addresses this need by providing anti-PRLR antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) that combine PRLR targeting with potent cytotoxic payload delivery.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides isolated antibodies and antigen-binding fragments that specifically bind human prolactin receptor (PRLR). These antibodies and fragments demonstrate high affinity binding to both monomeric and dimeric forms of human PRLR. The antibodies may be full-length immunoglobulin molecules or antigen-binding portions thereof, and may be modified to affect functionality such as binding affinity, serum half-life, or effector function.

Exemplary anti-PRLR antibodies of the invention include those designated REGN2878, which comprises heavy and light chain variable regions (HCVR and LCVR) with specific complementarity determining regions (CDRs). The HCVR amino acid sequence comprises HCDR1, HCDR2, and HCDR3 regions, while the LCVR amino acid sequence comprises LCDR1, LCDR2, and LCDR3 regions. Antibodies may comprise the complete set of six CDRs or subsets thereof sufficient for PRLR binding.

The invention further provides nucleic acid molecules encoding the HCVR, LCVR, and individual CDR regions of the anti-PRLR antibodies. Recombinant expression vectors containing these nucleic acids and host cells transformed with such vectors are also provided, along with methods for producing the antibodies.

Antibodies of the invention may be modified, such as through altered glycosylation patterns or Fc region mutations to enhance or diminish binding to Fc receptors. The antibodies may be conjugated to therapeutic moieties such as cytotoxic agents or radioisotopes to form antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs). Exemplary cytotoxic agents include maytansinoids (e.g., DM1, DM4) and auristatins (e.g., MMAE), while exemplary linkers include cleavable and non-cleavable varieties.

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the anti-PRLR antibodies or ADCs are provided for therapeutic use, particularly in treating PRLR-expressing cancers such as breast cancer. The antibodies and ADCs demonstrate potent cytotoxicity against tumor cells expressing even low levels of PRLR, with IC50 values below 20 nM in certain embodiments. Combination therapies with other anticancer agents such as hormone therapies are also contemplated.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The following detailed description sets forth the scope and terminology of the invention. It is to be understood that both the foregoing general description and the following detailed description are exemplary and explanatory only and are not restrictive of the invention.

### Definitions

As used herein, "prolactin receptor" or "PRLR" refers to the human prolactin receptor protein in any of its naturally occurring forms, including monomeric and dimeric states. The term encompasses full-length PRLR as well as fragments containing the extracellular domain. "Monomeric human PRLR" refers to the single-chain form of the receptor, while "dimeric human PRLR" refers to the ligand-bound or constitutively dimerized form.

An "anti-PRLR antibody" is an antibody that specifically binds to human PRLR. The term "antibody" encompasses intact immunoglobulin molecules as well as antigen-binding fragments thereof. Immunoglobulin molecules are typically composed of two heavy chains and two light chains, each containing variable regions (VH and VL) that contribute to antigen binding.

The "heavy chain variable region" (HCVR) and "light chain variable region" (LCVR) contain framework regions interspersed with three "complementarity determining regions" (CDRs) that primarily mediate antigen contact. The term "antigen-binding portion" refers to a fragment of an antibody that retains specific binding to the target antigen, including but not limited to Fab, Fab', F(ab')2, Fv, and single-chain Fv fragments.

"Engineered molecules" include antibodies modified through techniques such as chimerization, humanization, or CDR grafting. The invention encompasses various antibody formats including single domain antibodies, domain-deleted antibodies, diabodies, triabodies, tetrabodies, minibodies, and nanobodies.

"Isolated antibody" refers to an antibody that has been subjected to purification or isolation steps to separate it from other components. Anti-PRLR antibodies may contain mutations such as germline mutations or conservative amino acid substitutions that do not substantially affect binding properties.

An "epitope" is the specific region of PRLR to which an antibody binds, which may be conformational or linear. Antibodies are considered to bind the "same epitope" if they compete for binding in a cross-blocking assay. "Substantial identity" between sequences indicates at least 90% nucleotide sequence identity, while "substantial similarity" refers to amino acid sequences with conservative substitutions that preserve function.

The antibodies may exhibit pH-dependent binding characteristics, with reduced or enhanced binding at acidic pH (below 6.0) compared to neutral pH (7.0-7.4). Such properties can be engineered through techniques such as histidine residue substitutions.

### Anti-PRLR Antibodies Comprising Fc Variants

The Fc region of anti-PRLR antibodies may be modified to alter binding to Fc receptors such as FcRn. Mutations enhancing FcRn binding can increase serum half-life, while mutations diminishing binding may be employed when rapid clearance is desired. Exemplary modifications include amino acid substitutions at positions known to affect Fc-FcRn interactions.

### Biological Characteristics of the Antibodies

Anti-PRLR antibodies of the invention bind monomeric human PRLR with high affinity, typically exhibiting KD values less than 4 nM and dissociative half-lives greater than 5 minutes. Binding to dimeric human PRLR is even stronger, with KD values below 250 pM and dissociative half-lives exceeding 55 minutes in certain embodiments.

The antibodies may block prolactin-mediated signaling in cells expressing human PRLR, with IC50 values below 1.3 nM in inhibition assays. Alternatively, some antibodies may bind PRLR without blocking signaling (IC50 >10 nM), which may be preferable for certain ADC applications. Antibodies may possess one or more of these biological characteristics in various combinations.

### Antibody-Drug Conjugates (ADCs)

The invention provides ADCs comprising anti-PRLR antibodies conjugated to therapeutic moieties such as cytotoxic agents or radioisotopes. Cytotoxic agents include microtubule inhibitors (e.g., maytansinoids, auristatins), DNA-damaging agents, and topoisomerase inhibitors. Radionuclides suitable for conjugation include α-emitters, β-emitters, and positron emitters.

Linkers may be cleavable (e.g., peptide-based, disulfide-containing) or non-cleavable (e.g., thioether). Conjugation typically occurs through cysteine or lysine residues, with drug-to-antibody ratios ranging from 2-4 in preferred embodiments. The ADCs demonstrate potent cytotoxicity against PRLR-expressing cells while sparing PRLR-negative cells.

### Targeting ADCs to Cells Expressing Low Levels of PRLR

Remarkably, the anti-PRLR ADCs of the invention effectively target and kill cells expressing low levels of PRLR (less than 30-fold above background or below 1 million copies per cell). Such cells are killed with IC50 values below 20 nM, demonstrating the surprising potency of these conjugates even at low target density.

In vivo, the ADCs inhibit tumor growth and reduce tumor size in PRLR+ tumor-bearing animals. Complete tumor regression to undetectable levels can be achieved in xenograft models of PRLR+ breast cancer within 52 days post-administration or sooner, depending on dosing regimen.

### Class I Cytokine Receptor Targeting

PRLR belongs to the class I cytokine receptor family, which includes receptors that form homodimers (e.g., PRLR, growth hormone receptor) or heterodimers (e.g., IL-4R, IL-6R). The ADC targeting strategy described herein is broadly applicable to this receptor class, as demonstrated by effective killing of cells expressing low levels of various class I cytokine receptors when targeted with appropriate ADCs.

### Epitope Mapping and Related Technologies

The epitopes recognized by anti-PRLR antibodies can be determined through various techniques including alanine scanning mutagenesis, peptide blot analysis, hydrogen/deuterium exchange mass spectrometry, and cross-blocking assays. Antibodies binding the same epitope or competing for binding can be identified through these methods, facilitating antibody characterization and selection.

### Preparation of Human Antibodies

Fully human anti-PRLR antibodies can be generated using VELOCIMMUNE® technology, which employs genetically engineered mice containing human immunoglobulin gene segments. Immunization with human PRLR extracellular domain followed by hybridoma generation or direct B cell isolation yields diverse, high-affinity human antibodies suitable for therapeutic development.

### Bioequivalents

The invention encompasses bioequivalent variants of the anti-PRLR antibodies, including those with amino acid or nucleotide sequence modifications that preserve binding and functional properties. Such variants may include conservative substitutions, terminal or internal deletions, or glycosylation pattern alterations.

Pharmaceutical equivalents demonstrate comparable rates and extents of absorption, with any intentional differences being medically insignificant. Bioequivalence can be established through in vivo tests in humans or animal models, in vitro tests correlated with in vivo data, or well-controlled clinical trials.

Multispecific antibodies (e.g., bispecific) combining PRLR binding with additional specificities are also contemplated. Therapeutic formulations may comprise the antibodies or ADCs in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers and optionally other active agents such as chemotherapeutics or hormone therapies.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention but are not intended to limit its scope.

### Example 1. Generation of Anti-PRLR Antibodies

Anti-PRLR antibodies were generated by immunizing VELOCIMMUNE® mice with recombinant human PRLR extracellular domain. Splenocytes were harvested and used to generate hybridomas or for direct isolation of antigen-binding B cells. From over 300 antigen-binding clones, REGN2878 was selected as a lead antibody based on specificity, affinity, internalization properties, and ability to block ligand-mediated signaling.

The complete HCVR and LCVR amino acid sequences and corresponding nucleic acid sequences are provided. The antibody is of human IgG1 isotype with typical hinge region structure.

### Control Constructs Used in the Following Examples

Control antibodies included an irrelevant human IgG1 and corresponding ADC conjugate. These controls were used in all experiments to assess specificity of anti-PRLR antibody effects.

### Example 3. Surface Plasmon Resonance Derived Binding Affinities and Kinetic Constants of Human Monoclonal Anti-PRLR Antibodies

Surface plasmon resonance (Biacore) analysis demonstrated REGN2878 binds human monomeric PRLR with KD of 1.05 nM and monkey PRLR with KD of 1.64 nM. No binding to rodent PRLR was detected. The conjugated form REGN2878-DM1 maintained high affinity binding (KD = 1.24 nM for human PRLR).

### Example 4A. Anti-PRLR Antibodies Bind to Endogenous and Overexpressed PRLR Cell Lines

Flow cytometry analysis showed REGN2878 specifically bound to PRLR-expressing cell lines including MCF7 (moderate PRLR), T47D (high endogenous PRLR), and engineered MCF7/PRLR (very high PRLR). Binding correlated with PRLR expression levels as quantified by FACS and cell surface copy number determination.

### Example 4B. Anti-PRLR Antibodies are Internalized by PRLR-Expressing Cells In Vitro

Fluorescently labeled REGN2878 showed rapid internalization in PRLR-expressing cells, with intracellular localization apparent within 1 hour at 37°C. This efficient internalization is critical for ADC activity.

### Example 5. Anti-PRLR Antibodies Inhibit PRL-Mediated Receptor Activation in Cells Expressing Human PRLR

In a luciferase reporter assay using HEK293/PRLR/STAT5-Luc cells, REGN2878 and REGN2878-DM1 blocked PRL-induced signaling with IC50 values of 0.4 nM. Control antibodies showed no inhibition, demonstrating specificity.

### Example 6. Preparation and Characterization of Anti-PRLR Antibody Drug Conjugates

REGN2878 was conjugated to DM1 via a non-cleavable SMCC linker with average drug-to-antibody ratio of 3.4. The conjugate maintained high purity (>95% monomeric) and binding affinity. Similar conjugates were prepared with MMAE and DM4 using standard methods.

### Example 7. Anti-PRLR Antibody-Drug Conjugates Effectively Kill Cells with Low-to-Moderate PRLR Expression Levels as Well as Cells with High PRLR Expression Levels

REGN2878-DM1 demonstrated potent cytotoxicity against PRLR-expressing cells:
- MCF7/PRLR (high PRLR): IC50 = 0.06 nM  
- T47D (moderate PRLR): IC50 = 0.97 nM
- MCF7 (low PRLR): IC50 >100 nM (no activity)

Activity was maintained with different cytotoxins (MMAE IC50 = 0.5 nM; DM4 IC50 = 0.8 nM in T47D cells). In vivo, REGN2878-DM1 showed dose-dependent antitumor activity in xenograft models, with complete regressions achieved in MCF7/PRLR and T47D models at optimal doses. Combination with fulvestrant enhanced efficacy in ER+ models.
