## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to methods and compositions for mitochondrial uncoupling. More specifically, the invention provides methods for extending lifespan, decreasing cellular proliferation, restoring intestinal homeostasis, slowing tumor growth, and preventing or treating cancer through activation of mitochondrial uncoupling pathways. The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions and kits for practicing these methods.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Circadian rhythms are endogenous biological oscillations that regulate numerous physiological processes with approximately 24-hour periodicity. These rhythms are controlled by molecular clocks composed of transcriptional-translational feedback loops. In mammals and Drosophila, the core clock mechanism involves transcriptional activators CLOCK and CYCLE (or its mammalian ortholog BMAL1) that drive expression of repressor proteins including PERIOD (PER) and TIMELESS (TIM). These repressors subsequently inhibit CLOCK/CYCLE activity, completing the feedback loop.  

The circadian system regulates diverse cellular functions including metabolism, cell cycle progression, and oxidative stress responses. Disruption of circadian rhythms has been linked to various pathologies including metabolic disorders, neurodegenerative diseases, and cancer. Previous studies have primarily focused on the effects of disrupting circadian transcriptional activators (CLOCK/CYCLE), which generally leads to metabolic dysfunction and shortened lifespan.  

The relationship between circadian regulation and lifespan extension remains incompletely understood. While loss of circadian transcriptional repressors PER and TIM has been associated with both detrimental and beneficial metabolic effects, the mechanisms underlying these observations have not been elucidated. In particular, the tissue-specific effects of circadian disruption on mitochondrial function and their relationship to aging processes have not been characterized.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for activating mitochondrial uncoupling to achieve beneficial physiological effects. The invention is based on the discovery that loss of the circadian repressor PER leads to constitutive activation of mitochondrial uncoupling proteins, particularly UCP4C, resulting in extended lifespan and improved intestinal homeostasis.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  

A method of extending lifespan in a subject comprising administering an effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling activator. The method produces delayed aging and extended lifespan through increased mitochondrial uncoupling activity.  

A method of decreasing cellular proliferation in a subject comprising administering an effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling activator. The decreased proliferation is particularly evident in intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts.  

A method of restoring intestinal homeostasis in a subject comprising administering an effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling activator. The method maintains intestinal barrier function and reduces age-related intestinal dysplasia.  

A method of slowing tumor growth in a subject comprising administering an effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling activator. The method is particularly effective against intestinal stem cell-derived tumors.  

A method of preventing or treating cancer in a subject comprising administering an effective amount of a mitochondrial uncoupling activator. The method reduces reactive oxygen species (ROS) production and associated proliferative signaling.  

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising mitochondrial uncoupling activators and methods of administration. Suitable activators include small molecule uncouplers (e.g., 2,4-dinitrophenol, niclosamide, BAM15), fatty acids (e.g., stearic acid), and nucleic acids encoding uncoupling proteins (e.g., UCP4C).  

The invention also provides kits for practicing the methods, comprising mitochondrial uncoupling activators and instructions for use. The kits may further include additional therapeutic agents, delivery vehicles, or diagnostic tools.

### Activation of Mitochondrial Uncoupling has Beneficial Effects  

The present invention demonstrates that activation of mitochondrial uncoupling produces multiple beneficial physiological effects. These effects include delayed aging and extended lifespan through mechanisms independent of dietary restriction or insulin signaling pathways.  

Increased mitochondrial uncoupling leads to improved intestinal homeostasis by maintaining intestinal barrier function and reducing age-related dysplasia. The uncoupled state decreases cellular proliferation rates, particularly in intestinal stem cells and enteroblasts, through reduction of reactive oxygen species (ROS) production.  

Mitochondrial uncoupling also slows tumor growth by creating a metabolic state that inhibits cellular proliferation. This effect is particularly evident in intestinal stem cell-derived tumors. The reduction in ROS production associated with uncoupling prevents oxidative damage and associated proliferative signaling.  

Collectively, these beneficial effects make mitochondrial uncoupling an attractive therapeutic strategy for extending healthspan, treating age-related disorders, and preventing or treating cancer.  

### Methods and Compositions for Activating Mitochondrial Uncoupling  

The invention provides various methods and compositions for activating mitochondrial uncoupling. Mitochondrial uncoupling refers to the dissipation of the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, uncoupling electron transport from ATP synthesis. This process increases respiration and generates heat while reducing ROS production.  

Small molecule mitochondrial uncouplers represent one class of activators. Exemplary compounds include:  

Mifepristone (RU486), which has been shown to induce uncoupling at certain concentrations.  

Stearic acid, a long-chain fatty acid that activates endogenous uncoupling proteins.  

2,4-dinitrophenol (DNP), a protonophore that transports protons across mitochondrial membranes.  

Butylated hydroxytoluene (BHT), an antioxidant that also exhibits uncoupling activity.  

Structural requirements for uncoupling activity include appropriate hydrophobicity for membrane penetration, acidity for proton transport, and stability under physiological conditions. Substituted phenols represent one class of compounds meeting these requirements.  

Salicylanilide derivatives constitute another important class of uncouplers. These compounds demonstrate potent uncoupling activity with favorable structure-activity relationships. Niclosamide, a salicylanilide derivative, exhibits particularly favorable uncoupling activity with an established safety profile. Related compounds such as nitazoxanide also show therapeutic potential.  

Carbonyl cyanide phenylhydrazones represent another structural class of uncouplers. BAM15 is a particularly promising member of this class with demonstrated in vivo efficacy.  

Other types of agents can activate uncoupling through different mechanisms. Membrane-active peptides such as alamethicin and mastoparan can disrupt mitochondrial membrane integrity, creating uncoupled states. Signal peptides can induce uncoupling through specific interactions with mitochondrial proteins.  

Nucleic acids encoding one or more uncoupling proteins represent another approach. Delivery of UCP4C or related genes via viral or non-viral vectors can establish long-term uncoupling activity. Various delivery methods including lipid nanoparticles, electroporation, and viral vectors can be employed for this purpose.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

#### Fly Strains  

The following Drosophila melanogaster strains were used:  
cycle (cyc01), period (per01), and timeless (tim01) mutants outcrossed to Canton-S background; UAS-CRISPR lines targeting period gene; UAS-Ucp4C overexpression lines; various tissue-specific GAL4 drivers including intestinal (esg-GAL4) and neuronal (elav-GAL4) drivers.  

#### UAS-CRISPR Line Construction  

CRISPR lines targeting the period gene were constructed using pCFD6 vector. Guide RNAs were designed to target multiple sites in the period coding sequence. Constructs were microinjected into embryos for genomic integration at the attP5 site.  

#### Fly Media  

Flies were reared on standard cornmeal-yeast medium. Adult flies were maintained on media containing varying yeast extract concentrations (0.01-10%) for dietary restriction studies.  

#### Drug Supplementation  

Mitochondrial uncouplers (2,4-DNP, BHT) were dissolved in ethanol and added to cooled food at final concentrations of 0.01-0.1 mM (DNP) or 0.1-1 mM (BHT). RU486 was used at 5-100 μg/mL for GeneSwitch induction.  

#### Lifespan Analysis  

Newly eclosed flies were collected, allowed to mate for 48 hours, then separated by sex. Males were maintained at 25°C, 12:12 light:dark cycle at density of 30-35 flies/vial. Deaths were recorded every 1-2 days with transfer to fresh food every 2-3 days. Statistical significance was determined by log-rank test.  

#### Western Blot Analysis  

Whole fly lysates were separated by SDS-PAGE and probed with antibodies against phospho-S6K, phospho-AKT, and other signaling molecules. HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies were used for chemiluminescent detection.  

#### Quantitative Real-Time PCR  

Total RNA was extracted from whole flies or dissected intestines using TRIzol. cDNA was synthesized using reverse transcriptase. Gene expression was quantified using SYBR Green chemistry and normalized to actin5C.  

#### Cold Shock Recovery Assay  

Flies were placed at 4°C for 1 hour then returned to 25°C. Recovery was monitored by activity monitors, with recovery defined as three consecutive beam breaks.  

#### Triglyceride Quantification  

Lipids were extracted in chloroform:ethanol (2:1), separated by TLC, and quantified by densitometry after Amido Black staining. Values were normalized to fly mass.  

#### Intestinal Barrier Dysfunction Assay  

Aged flies were fed FD&C Blue No. 1 dye. Intestinal leakage was assessed by whole-body dye penetration outside the digestive tract.  

#### Feeding Assay  

Food intake was measured using capillary feeders (CAFE assay) containing dyed food solution. Consumption was measured by liquid displacement over 24 hours.  

#### CO2 Respiration Assay  

Groups of 10 flies were placed in sealed respirometers with soda lime. CO2 production was measured by liquid displacement in calibrated capillaries over 2 hours.  

#### Mitochondrial Purification  

Flies were homogenized in mitochondrial isolation medium (250 mM sucrose, 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 0.15 mM MgCl2). Nuclei and debris were removed by low-speed centrifugation. Mitochondria were pelleted at 4000 × g.  

#### Oxygen Consumption Measurements  

Purified mitochondria were analyzed using high-resolution respirometry (Oxygraph-2k). Substrates (glutamate/malate, succinate) and inhibitors (rotenone, antimycin A) were added sequentially to assess different respiratory states.  

#### Membrane Potential Assay  

JC-1 dye was used to assess mitochondrial membrane potential. Fluorescence ratios (590/530 nm) were measured under various conditions (state 3 respiration, oligomycin-inhibited, CCCP-uncoupled).  

#### Blue-Native PAGE  

Mitochondrial complexes were separated on 3-12% Bis-Tris NativePAGE gels. Complex I activity was visualized by NADH-linked tetrazolium reduction.  

#### Circadian Locomotor Activity  

Individual flies were monitored in Drosophila Activity Monitors (Trikinetics) for 3 days LD then 6 days DD. Rhythmicity was analyzed using ClockLab software.  

#### Intestinal Staining  

Dissected intestines were stained with TMRE (membrane potential), MitoSOX (mitochondrial ROS), or antibodies (pHH3, GFP). Confocal microscopy was used for imaging and quantification.  

### Loss of the Repressive Arm of the Transcriptional Circadian Clock Extended Lifespan  

Comparison of four arrhythmic Drosophila mutants revealed distinct lifespan effects. While cycle and Clock mutants showed shortened lifespan (p<0.0001), per01 and tim01 mutants exhibited 15-20% lifespan extension (p<0.001). Lifespan extension was male-specific and independent of dietary protein restriction.  

Restoration of Per expression using UAS-GAL4 system reverted per01 lifespan to control levels, confirming the phenotype results from Per loss. Dietary restriction further extended lifespan in per01 mutants, indicating mechanisms independent of canonical DR pathways.  

Genetic tests showed per01 longevity is independent of TORC1 or insulin-like signaling inhibition. Overexpression of dominant-negative S6K or ablation of insulin-producing cells extended lifespan equally in controls and per01 mutants.  

### Period Mutants Exhibited a High Metabolic Rate Due to Mitochondrial Uncoupling  

per01 mutants displayed hyperphagia yet reduced lipid stores and increased lipid utilization during starvation (p<0.0001). CO2 production was elevated throughout circadian cycle (p<0.01), reversible by Per expression.  

Mitochondrial complex inhibitors (rotenone, oligomycin) reduced but did not eliminate respiratory differences between per01 and controls. Uncouplers (DNP, stearate) elevated control respiration to per01 levels without further increasing per01 respiration.  

Purified per01 mitochondria showed:  
- Increased O2 consumption through complexes I, II, and IV (p<0.05)  
- Higher leak respiration after oligomycin (p<0.001)  
- Reduced membrane potential by JC-1 assay (p<0.001)  
- Faster cold shock recovery (p<0.001)  

These results demonstrate per01 mutants exhibit mitochondrial uncoupling.  

### UCP4C was Necessary and Sufficient for Lifespan Extension  

Ucp4B/C expression was circadian-regulated in controls but constitutively high in per01 mutants (p<0.01). Disruption of UCP4B/C expression:  
- Reverted mitochondrial uncoupling phenotypes  
- Eliminated lifespan extension in per01 mutants (p<0.001)  

RNAi knockdown of UCP4B or UCP4C (but not UCP4A) reverted per01 lifespan. Ubiquitous UCP4C overexpression:  
- Induced uncoupling phenotypes similar to per01 mutants  
- Extended wild-type lifespan equivalently to per01 mutants  
- Did not further extend per01 lifespan  

These results demonstrate UCP4C mediates per01 longevity through mitochondrial uncoupling.  

### Lifespan Extension was Mediated by Intestinal Per Loss  

Tissue-specific rescue experiments showed:  
- Ubiquitous Per expression reverted lifespan (p<0.0001)  
- Neuronal Per expression did not affect lifespan  
- Intestinal Per expression (whole intestine or ISCs/EBs) fully reverted lifespan  

CRISPR-mediated period disruption:  
- Ubiquitous deletion extended wild-type lifespan  
- Intestinal or ISC/EB-specific deletion extended lifespan  

Intestinal mitochondrial membrane potential showed circadian regulation in controls (higher daytime) but constitutively low in per01 mutants.  

### UCP4C in Intestine was Necessary and Sufficient  

Intestinal UCP4C knockdown reverted per01 lifespan. Intestinal UCP4C overexpression:  
- Extended wild-type lifespan (p<0.0001)  
- Reduced mitochondrial membrane potential  
- Delayed age-related intestinal barrier dysfunction  
- Reduced age-related ISC/EB proliferation  

These effects correlated with reduced mitochondrial ROS production in intestinal cells.  

### Uncoupling Drugs Recapitulated Genetic Effects  

Low-dose 2,4-DNP (0.01-0.1 mM):  
- Extended wild-type lifespan (p<0.0001)  
- Did not further extend per01 lifespan  
- Reduced intestinal ROS and ISC proliferation  
- Slowed Notch-RNAi intestinal tumor growth  

These results demonstrate pharmacological uncoupling mimics genetic effects.  

The complete specification demonstrates that mitochondrial uncoupling, particularly in the intestine, represents a novel therapeutic strategy for extending lifespan and treating age-related disorders. The invention provides multiple approaches for achieving these benefits through genetic, pharmacological, and dietary interventions.
