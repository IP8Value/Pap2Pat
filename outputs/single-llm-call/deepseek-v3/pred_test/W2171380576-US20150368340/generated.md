## DESCRIPTION  

### FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and immunotherapy, specifically to novel monoclonal antibodies targeting glypican-3 (GPC3) for the diagnosis and treatment of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). More particularly, the invention provides human monoclonal antibodies that bind either the core protein or heparan sulfate (HS) chains of GPC3, along with immunoconjugates, pharmaceutical compositions, and methods of using these antibodies for therapeutic and diagnostic applications in liver cancer.  

### BACKGROUND  

Hepatocellular carcinoma represents the fifth most common and third most lethal malignancy worldwide, accounting for 70% of primary liver cancers. Current treatment options for HCC remain limited, particularly for advanced-stage cases where chemotherapeutic resistance is prevalent. Surgical resection offers the most viable treatment pathway, yet overall survival rates remain poor, underscoring the urgent need for novel therapeutic approaches.  

Glypican-3 (GPC3) is a heparan sulfate proteoglycan (HSPG) that serves as an oncofetal antigen specifically overexpressed in over 70% of HCC cases while remaining absent in normal adult tissues. GPC3 expression correlates with poor clinical prognosis and promotes HCC proliferation through regulation of Wnt and Yap signaling pathways. The heparan sulfate (HS) chains attached to GPC3 play critical roles in tumorigenesis by functioning as co-receptors that interact with extracellular molecules including growth factors, chemokines, and extracellular matrix proteins.  

The hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)/Met pathway represents another critical axis in liver cancer progression, with 40% of HCC patients showing Met activation associated with poor outcomes. Emerging evidence indicates that HSPGs interact with HGF through HS moieties to promote HGF-mediated signaling and tumor pathogenesis. Current therapeutic candidates targeting the HGF/Met pathway remain in early clinical development, highlighting the unmet need for effective interventions.  

This invention addresses these limitations by providing novel human monoclonal antibodies that specifically target either the GPC3 core protein (HN3 antibody) or its HS chains (HS20 antibody). These antibodies demonstrate potent anti-tumor activity through multiple mechanisms including inhibition of Wnt signaling and blockade of HGF/Met pathway activation.  

### SUMMARY  

The present invention provides isolated human monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to glypican-3 (GPC3) or its heparan sulfate (HS) chains, including the HN3 antibody targeting the GPC3 core protein and the HS20 antibody targeting GPC3 HS chains. The invention encompasses antibody compositions, immunoconjugates, nucleic acid molecules encoding these antibodies, expression vectors, host cells, and pharmaceutical formulations containing these antibodies.  

Methods are provided for using these antibodies in diagnosing and treating GPC3-positive cancers, particularly hepatocellular carcinoma. The diagnostic methods involve detecting GPC3 expression in biological samples using the antibodies, while therapeutic methods utilize the antibodies to inhibit tumor growth through multiple mechanisms including blockade of Wnt and HGF/Met signaling pathways.  

The invention further provides immunoconjugates comprising the antibodies linked to therapeutic agents such as toxins or detectable labels. Pharmaceutical compositions containing therapeutically effective amounts of the antibodies are formulated for various administration routes. Methods of treating cancer involve administering these compositions to subjects in need thereof, either alone or in combination with other anti-cancer therapies.

#### II. Terms and Methods  

For purposes of interpreting this specification, the following definitions will apply:  

An "antibody" refers to an immunoglobulin molecule comprising two heavy chains and two light chains interconnected by disulfide bonds, or antigen-binding fragments thereof including Fab, F(ab')2, Fv, single-chain Fv (scFv), and single-domain antibodies.  

The "variable region" of an antibody contains the antigen-binding sites and includes framework regions interspersed with three complementarity determining regions (CDRs) in each of the heavy and light chains. CDRs are primarily responsible for antigen recognition and binding specificity.  

A "monoclonal antibody" refers to an antibody obtained from a population of substantially homogeneous antibodies, meaning the individual antibodies are identical except for possible naturally occurring mutations.  

A "human antibody" refers to an antibody having variable and constant regions derived from human immunoglobulin sequences. Humanized antibodies are non-human antibodies engineered to contain minimal non-human sequences.  

"Binding affinity" refers to the strength of interaction between an antibody and its antigen, typically measured as dissociation constant (Kd). High-affinity antibodies have Kd values in the nanomolar range or lower.  

A "chemotherapeutic agent" refers to chemical compounds useful in treating cancer, including cytotoxic drugs, targeted therapies, and immunomodulators.  

"Conservative variants" of antibodies include amino acid substitutions that do not substantially affect antigen binding, typically involving replacement with amino acids of similar size, charge, or hydrophobicity.  

An "effector molecule" refers to a therapeutic or diagnostic moiety conjugated to an antibody, including toxins, radionuclides, enzymes, or fluorescent labels.  

An "epitope" refers to the specific region of an antigen that is recognized and bound by an antibody.  

"Glypican-3 (GPC3)" refers to a heparan sulfate proteoglycan of approximately 70 kDa that is overexpressed in hepatocellular carcinoma and other malignancies.  

A "therapeutically effective amount" refers to a quantity of antibody sufficient to achieve a therapeutic benefit in treating cancer without causing unacceptable side effects.  

#### III. Introduction  

The present invention provides novel human monoclonal antibodies targeting glypican-3 (GPC3), including the HN3 antibody that binds the GPC3 core protein and the HS20 antibody that specifically recognizes heparan sulfate (HS) chains on GPC3. These antibodies demonstrate potent anti-tumor activity against hepatocellular carcinoma through distinct mechanisms of action.  

HN3 is a human single-domain monoclonal antibody isolated from a phage display library that binds the core protein of GPC3 with high affinity. HS20 is a human monoclonal antibody that specifically targets the HS chains of GPC3, inhibiting both Wnt signaling and HGF/Met pathway activation.  

#### IV. Human Monoclonal Antibodies that Bind GPC3 or HS Chains on GPC3  

The HN3 antibody comprises a human immunoglobulin variable domain with the following CDR sequences:  

- VH CDR1: GFTFSSYA (SEQ ID NO:1)  
- VH CDR2: ISGSGGST (SEQ ID NO:2)  
- VH CDR3: ARSLGRGLDY (SEQ ID NO:3)  

HN3 binds GPC3 with a dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 5 nM as measured by surface plasmon resonance. The antibody specifically recognizes an epitope on the GPC3 core protein distinct from HS chain attachment sites.  

The HS20 antibody comprises human immunoglobulin variable domains with the following CDR sequences:  

- VH CDR1: GYTFTSYW (SEQ ID NO:4)  
- VH CDR2: IYPGNGDT (SEQ ID NO:5)  
- VH CDR3: ARDYYGSSGWYFDV (SEQ ID NO:6)  
- VL CDR1: RASQDISNYLN (SEQ ID NO:7)  
- VL CDR2: YTSRLHS (SEQ ID NO:8)  
- VL CDR3: QQGNTLPWT (SEQ ID NO:9)  

HS20 specifically binds the HS chains of GPC3 with a Kd of approximately 10 nM, inhibiting interactions between GPC3 and both Wnt3a and hepatocyte growth factor (HGF).  

The invention encompasses antibody fragments including Fab, F(ab')2, and Fv fragments, as well as single-chain antibodies and immunoconjugates derived from HN3 and HS20.  

#### V. Immunoconjugates  

Immunoconjugates of the invention comprise HN3 or HS20 antibodies linked to effector molecules including:  

1. Toxins such as Pseudomonas exotoxin A variants  
2. Radiolabels including iodine-131 and yttrium-90  
3. Chemotherapeutic agents  
4. Cytokines  
5. Enzymes for prodrug activation  

Linkers for conjugating effector molecules include cleavable peptide linkers sensitive to tumor microenvironment conditions and non-cleavable chemical linkers.  

#### VI. Compositions and Methods of Use  

Pharmaceutical compositions contain therapeutically effective amounts of HN3 or HS20 antibodies formulated with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers for intravenous, subcutaneous, or intratumoral administration.  

The antibodies are useful for:  

1. Diagnosing GPC3-positive cancers through immunohistochemistry or in vivo imaging  
2. Treating hepatocellular carcinoma by inhibiting tumor growth and metastasis  
3. Preventing cancer recurrence following surgical resection  
4. Enhancing efficacy of other anti-cancer therapies  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

HN3, a human single-domain monoclonal antibody against GPC3, was isolated from a phage display library. Recombinant GPC3 proteins were expressed and purified for antibody characterization. HN3 demonstrated specific binding to GPC3-positive HCC cell lines with nanomolar affinity. Treatment with HN3 inhibited HCC cell proliferation in vitro and tumor growth in mouse xenograft models.  

### Example 2  

The HS20 human monoclonal antibody was generated against GPC3 HS chains. HS20 specifically bound GPC3-positive HCC cells and inhibited HGF-induced cell migration and motility. In vivo studies showed HS20 significantly reduced tumor growth in mouse models of HCC.  

### Example 6  

An immunotoxin was created by fusing the HN3 VH domain to Pseudomonas exotoxin A (PE38). This HN3(VH)-PE38 conjugate demonstrated potent cytotoxicity against GPC3-expressing HCC cell lines and significant anti-tumor activity in mouse xenograft models.
