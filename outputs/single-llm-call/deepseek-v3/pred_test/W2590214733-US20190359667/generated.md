## FIELD

The present disclosure relates generally to the field of protein engineering and specifically to engineered transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) monomers capable of inhibiting TGF-β signaling. More particularly, the disclosure provides recombinant TGF-β monomers with structural modifications that enable potent inhibition of TGF-β signaling pathways while maintaining high affinity binding to TGF-β type II receptors.

## BACKGROUND

TGF-β superfamily members are multifunctional cytokines that regulate diverse cellular processes including proliferation, differentiation, migration, and apoptosis. TGF-β signaling is initiated when dimeric TGF-β ligands bind to cell surface receptors, forming a heterotetrameric complex comprising two type I and two type II receptors. This receptor assembly triggers intracellular signaling cascades that ultimately regulate gene expression.

Aberrant TGF-β signaling has been implicated in numerous human diseases. Excessive TGF-β signaling contributes to fibrosis, cancer progression, and metastasis, while deficient TGF-β signaling is associated with autoimmune disorders and impaired wound healing. Despite nearly two decades of preclinical studies and clinical trials, no TGF-β inhibitors have been approved for clinical use, highlighting the need for novel therapeutic approaches targeting this pathway.

Current TGF-β inhibitors face several limitations. Small molecule kinase inhibitors often lack specificity, leading to off-target effects. Antibody-based inhibitors have limited tissue penetration due to their large size. Natural TGF-β antagonists like decorin and latency-associated peptide have poor pharmacokinetic properties. There remains an unmet need for potent, specific TGF-β inhibitors with favorable pharmacological properties.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides engineered TGF-β monomers that function as dominant negative inhibitors of TGF-β signaling. These recombinant proteins comprise modified TGF-β monomers with structural alterations that prevent functional receptor complex assembly while maintaining high affinity binding to TGF-β type II receptors (TβRII).

In one embodiment, the invention provides a recombinant TGF-β monomer comprising: (a) a deletion of residues corresponding to α-helix 3 (residues 52-71 in mature TGF-β2); and (b) a substitution of the interchain disulfide-forming cysteine (Cys-77 in TGF-β2) with serine. The deletion of α-helix 3 disrupts both non-covalent dimerization and type I receptor (TβRI) binding while preserving the structural integrity of the TβRII binding interface.

Particular embodiments feature TGF-β2-based monomers with additional substitutions that enhance TβRII binding affinity. These include substitutions at residues involved in TβRII binding (e.g., K25R, I92V, N94R) and peripheral residues that optimize the binding interface (e.g., R26K, L89V, T95K, I98V). A preferred embodiment designated mmTGF-β2-7M contains seven such substitutions and exhibits nanomolar inhibitory activity against all TGF-β isoforms.

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the engineered TGF-β monomers and methods for inhibiting TGF-β signaling in subjects with TGF-β-mediated disorders. The small size (∼10 kDa) and high solubility of these inhibitors provide advantages over existing therapeutic approaches, including improved tissue penetration and reduced immunogenicity.

### III. Overview of Several Embodiments

The invention provides recombinant TGF-β monomers engineered to inhibit TGF-β signaling through dominant negative mechanisms. These monomers retain high affinity binding to TβRII while being impaired in their ability to recruit TβRI or form signaling-competent receptor complexes.

Key embodiments feature TGF-β monomers with deletion of the α-helix 3 region (residues 52-71 in mature TGF-β2) and substitution of the interchain disulfide-forming cysteine (Cys-77) with serine. This combination of modifications disrupts both covalent dimerization through the cysteine knot and non-covalent dimerization mediated by α-helix 3 interactions.

Particular embodiments utilize TGF-β2 as the scaffold due to its favorable solubility properties when modified as described. The engineered monomers may be derived from human, mouse, rat or other mammalian TGF-β isoforms, with human TGF-β2 being preferred in certain applications.

To enhance TβRII binding affinity, specific amino acid substitutions are introduced at residues critical for receptor interaction. These include substitutions that confer TGF-β2 with binding affinity comparable to TGF-β1 and TGF-β3, such as K25R, I92V, and N94R. Additional peripheral substitutions (R26K, L89V, T95K, I98V) may be included to optimize the binding interface.

A preferred embodiment designated mmTGF-β2-7M incorporates seven such substitutions (K25R, R26K, L89V, I92V, N94R, T95K, I98V) on a TGF-β2 scaffold with α-helix 3 deletion and C77S substitution. This variant exhibits nanomolar inhibitory activity against all TGF-β isoforms while maintaining high solubility at physiological pH.

The engineered monomers may further comprise affinity tags (e.g., avitags, histidine tags) for purification or detection, or modifications such as PEGylation to enhance pharmacokinetic properties. The core inhibitory function resides in the structural modifications to the TGF-β monomer itself, particularly the deletion of α-helix 3 which abrogates TβRI recruitment while preserving TβRII binding.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: An Engineered TGF-β Monomer that Functions as a Dominant Negative to Block TGF-β Signaling

This example describes the design, characterization, and inhibitory activity of mmTGF-β2-7M, a preferred embodiment of the invention.

### BACKGROUND

Native TGF-β1 and TGF-β3 monomers (with C77S substitutions) retain significant signaling activity due to their ability to non-covalently dimerize and assemble functional receptor complexes. Structural analysis suggested that removal of α-helix 3 would disrupt both dimerization and TβRI binding while preserving TβRII interaction.

Bacterial expression constructs were generated for TGF-β1, TGF-β2, and TGF-β3 with deletion of residues 52-71 (α-helix 3 and flanking regions) and C77S substitution. Additional substitutions were introduced to increase net positive charge and improve solubility. TGF-β2-based monomers (mmTGF-β2) showed superior folding and solubility compared to TGF-β1 or TGF-β3 counterparts.

To enhance TβRII binding affinity, mmTGF-β2 was further modified with seven substitutions (K25R, R26K, L89V, I92V, N94R, T95K, I98V) yielding mmTGF-β2-7M. These modifications confer TGF-β1-like binding characteristics while maintaining the structural integrity of the monomer.

### Results

**Physical Characterization:** mmTGF-β2-7M was expressed in E. coli and purified to homogeneity. NMR and crystallographic analysis confirmed proper folding with deletion of α-helix 3 replaced by a flexible loop. The structure maintained the characteristic TGF-β fold except for the engineered modifications.

**Receptor Binding:** SPR measurements demonstrated high affinity binding to TβRII (KD ∼50 nM), comparable to native TGF-β3. In contrast, mmTGF-β2-7M showed no detectable binding to TβRI even in the presence of TβRII.

**Solubility:** mmTGF-β2-7M exhibited significantly improved solubility at neutral pH compared to native TGF-β dimers or full-length monomers, enabling formulation at therapeutic concentrations.

**Inhibitory Activity:** In cell-based luciferase assays, mmTGF-β2-7M showed no detectable signaling activity up to micromolar concentrations. When co-administered with active TGF-β, mmTGF-β2-7M potently inhibited signaling with IC50 values of 19-68 nM against all three TGF-β isoforms.

**Mechanism of Action:** AUC and TR-FRET experiments demonstrated that mmTGF-β2-7M has reduced propensity for non-covalent dimerization and cannot assemble functional TβRI•TβRII complexes. The inhibitor functions by competitively binding TβRII and preventing recruitment of native TGF-β ligands.

### Discussion

The engineered TGF-β monomers of the invention provide potent, specific inhibition of TGF-β signaling through a novel dominant negative mechanism. The small size and high solubility of these inhibitors offer distinct advantages over existing therapeutic approaches targeting the TGF-β pathway.

Particular advantages of mmTGF-β2-7M include:
1) High specificity for TβRII over other receptors
2) Nanomolar inhibitory potency against all TGF-β isoforms
3) Improved tissue penetration due to small size (∼10 kDa)
4) Favorable solubility at physiological pH
5) High stability conferred by the conserved cystine knot structure

These properties make the engineered monomers particularly suitable for treating conditions driven by excessive TGF-β signaling, including fibrosis, cancer metastasis, and other proliferative disorders. The invention thus provides novel therapeutic agents addressing a significant unmet medical need.
