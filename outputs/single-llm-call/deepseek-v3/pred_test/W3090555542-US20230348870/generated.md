# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of gene therapy and genome editing. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for correcting mutations in the dystrophin gene using CRISPR/Cas9-based gene editing systems delivered via adeno-associated viral (AAV) vectors. The invention further relates to the targeted transduction of muscle satellite cells to achieve sustained dystrophin expression in subjects with Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD).  

## INTRODUCTION  

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a severe genetic disorder affecting approximately 1 in 5,000 male births. The disease is caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene, leading to progressive muscle degeneration, loss of mobility, and premature death. Current treatments, such as corticosteroids and exon-skipping therapies, provide limited symptomatic relief but fail to address the underlying genetic defect.  

A significant limitation of existing therapies is their inability to achieve long-term correction of dystrophin expression. Gene editing technologies, particularly CRISPR/Cas9 systems, offer a promising alternative by enabling permanent correction of the dystrophin gene. However, efficient delivery of gene-editing components to muscle stem cells (satellite cells) remains a challenge. The present invention addresses this limitation by providing optimized AAV vector compositions capable of transducing satellite cells and achieving durable gene correction.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a vector composition comprising a CRISPR/Cas9-based gene editing system for correcting mutations in the dystrophin gene. The composition includes:  

1. A guide RNA (gRNA) molecule targeting sequences flanking a mutated exon in the dystrophin gene, wherein the gRNA is operably linked to a promoter.  
2. A Cas9 protein or a nucleic acid encoding the Cas9 protein, wherein the Cas9 protein is selected from Staphylococcus aureus Cas9 (SaCas9) or Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 (SpCas9).  
3. A muscle-specific promoter operably linked to the Cas9-encoding nucleic acid, wherein the promoter is selected from MHCK7, CK8e, or SPc5-12.  

The vector composition may be delivered as a single AAV vector or as multiple AAV vectors encoding the gRNA and Cas9 components separately. The AAV vector is preferably selected from AAV8 or AAV9 due to their high tropism for satellite cells.  

The invention further provides methods for treating DMD by administering the vector composition to a subject, wherein the composition corrects the dystrophin gene in satellite cells, enabling sustained dystrophin expression. Additionally, the invention includes kits comprising the vector composition and instructions for use in gene editing.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### 1. Definitions  

The following terms are defined for clarity in interpreting the invention:  

- **Adeno-associated virus (AAV):** A non-pathogenic viral vector used for gene delivery, characterized by its ability to infect dividing and non-dividing cells with low immunogenicity.  
- **Allogeneic:** Derived from a genetically dissimilar donor of the same species.  
- **Autologous:** Derived from the same individual.  
- **CRISPR/Cas9 system:** A gene-editing system comprising a Cas9 nuclease and a guide RNA (gRNA) that directs the Cas9 protein to a specific DNA sequence for cleavage.  
- **Dystrophin:** A protein encoded by the DMD gene, essential for maintaining muscle fiber integrity. Mutations in dystrophin lead to Duchenne muscular dystrophy.  
- **Exon skipping:** A therapeutic strategy wherein specific exons are excluded during mRNA splicing to restore the reading frame of the dystrophin gene.  
- **Homology-directed repair (HDR):** A DNA repair mechanism that uses a donor template to achieve precise gene editing.  
- **Non-homologous end joining (NHEJ):** An error-prone DNA repair mechanism that often results in insertions or deletions (indels).  
- **Satellite cells:** Muscle stem cells responsible for muscle regeneration, capable of self-renewal and differentiation into myofibers.  

Additional definitions are provided for terms such as "coding sequence," "complementary," "enhancer," "fusion protein," "nuclease," "promoter," and "vector," as commonly understood in molecular biology.  

### 2. Dystrophin Gene  

The dystrophin gene is located on the X chromosome and encodes a large cytoskeletal protein critical for muscle stability. Mutations in the dystrophin gene disrupt its function, leading to muscle degeneration in DMD. Common mutations include frameshift deletions in exons 45–55, which can be corrected by CRISPR/Cas9-mediated exon excision.  

The invention targets these mutations by designing gRNAs that flank the mutated exon, enabling precise deletion and restoration of the dystrophin reading frame.  

### 3. CRISPR/Cas9-Based Gene Editing System  

The CRISPR/Cas9 system comprises:  
- A Cas9 protein (e.g., SaCas9 or SpCas9) engineered for improved efficiency and reduced off-target effects.  
- One or more gRNAs designed to target intronic sequences adjacent to the mutated exon.  
- A donor template (optional) for HDR-mediated correction.  

The system is delivered via AAV vectors, with AAV8 and AAV9 demonstrating superior transduction of satellite cells.  

### 4. Genetic Constructs  

The invention includes plasmid and viral vectors encoding the CRISPR/Cas9 components. Key features include:  
- Muscle-specific promoters (e.g., MHCK7) to restrict Cas9 expression to muscle tissue.  
- Regulatory elements (e.g., polyadenylation signals, enhancers) to optimize expression.  
- Codon-optimized Cas9 sequences for improved translation efficiency.  

### 5. Pharmaceutical Compositions  

The vector compositions are formulated for intramuscular or systemic delivery, optionally including pharmaceutically acceptable excipients such as isotonic buffers or vasoconstrictors.  

### 6. Administration  

Delivery methods include:  
- Intramuscular injection for localized treatment.  
- Systemic delivery (e.g., intravenous) for widespread muscle targeting.  
- Electroporation or nanoparticle-mediated delivery for enhanced transduction.  

### 7. Kits  

The invention provides kits comprising:  
- AAV vectors encoding CRISPR/Cas9 components.  
- Instructions for administration and dosage.  

### 8. Methods  

Methods for correcting dystrophin mutations include:  
1. Administering the vector composition to a subject.  
2. Allowing CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing in satellite cells.  
3. Monitoring dystrophin restoration via immunofluorescence or sequencing.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: AAV Serotype Profiling  

Pax7nGFP;Ai9;mdx mice were injected with AAV1, AAV2, AAV5, AAV6.2, AAV8, or AAV9 encoding CMV-Cre. Flow cytometry revealed AAV8 and AAV9 as the most efficient serotypes for satellite cell transduction (~60% recombination efficiency).  

### Example 2: Muscle-Specific Promoter Activity  

AAV9 vectors encoding Cre under MHCK7, CK8e, or SPc5-12 promoters were injected into mice. MHCK7 showed the highest activity in satellite cells (15.6% recombination), comparable to CMV (33%).  

### Example 3: Dystrophin Restoration  

AAV9-CRISPR-treated mdx mice exhibited sustained dystrophin expression after serial muscle injuries, confirming durable gene correction in satellite cells.  

### Example 4: Transplantation of Edited Satellite Cells  

CRISPR-corrected satellite cells were transplanted into irradiated mdx mice, resulting in dystrophin+ myofiber regeneration (5.95 ± 0.40 fibers/mm² vs. 1.46 ± 0.41 in controls).
