# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to a solar concentrator battery system incorporating an advanced image-based sun position sensor for high-precision solar tracking. More particularly, the invention provides an apparatus and method for accurately determining the sun's position through an optical imaging system coupled with specialized image processing algorithms, enabling real-time adjustment of solar panel orientation to maximize energy capture. The system integrates a reflecting Cassegrain telescope with adjustable magnification, a high-resolution imaging sensor, and a multi-stage digital processing pipeline that converts raw solar images into precise tracking coordinates. This technological advancement addresses critical limitations in existing solar tracking systems by achieving sub-0.1° tracking accuracy while maintaining operational stability across varying atmospheric conditions.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE RELATED ART  

Conventional solar tracking systems face significant limitations in accuracy, reliability, and cost-effectiveness. Existing sun position sensors predominantly utilize bar-shadow type photosensors or four-quadrant light detectors, which suffer from inherent drawbacks including sensitivity to weather conditions, photodiode mismatch, and limited angular resolution. These technologies demonstrate degraded performance under cloudy conditions due to reduced light intensity, often resulting in tracking errors exceeding 1° that substantially decrease energy capture in high-concentration photovoltaic systems.  

The viewing angle constraints of traditional sensors necessitate complex mechanical adjustments, while their limited dynamic range creates operational challenges during dawn, dusk, or partially obscured solar conditions. Current solutions attempting to address these issues through analog signal processing or mechanical compensation mechanisms introduce additional complexity without achieving the required precision. Furthermore, the manufacturing tolerances required for precision shadow bars and matched photodiode arrays lead to prohibitively high production costs for widespread deployment.  

Prior attempts at image-based sun tracking have been hampered by insufficient optical resolution, inadequate image processing techniques, and inability to maintain consistent performance across varying environmental conditions. Conventional CCD-based systems designed for aerospace applications prove impractical for terrestrial solar tracking due to their high power requirements and sensitivity to thermal variations. Similarly, CMOS-based solutions using simple pinhole optics fail to provide the necessary angular resolution for high-concentration photovoltaic applications. These limitations in the current state of art create an unmet need for a robust, high-accuracy sun tracking solution that maintains performance across diverse operating conditions while remaining economically viable for commercial deployment.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides an apparatus for sensing sun position through advanced image acquisition and processing techniques. The system comprises an image position sensing mechanism incorporating a reflecting Cassegrain telescope with adjustable magnification between 5× to 15×, optically coupled to a high-resolution digital imaging sensor. This optical assembly achieves superior modulation transfer function characteristics, enabling resolution below 0.01° per pixel through optimized mirror configurations and light path management.  

The tracking mechanism utilizes a dual-axis servo system responsive to real-time position corrections derived from image analysis. A control mechanism coordinates the interaction between image acquisition, processing, and mechanical adjustment through a closed-loop feedback system with configurable error thresholds. The full-color image acquiring unit captures solar images at resolutions up to 2304×1536 pixels, while maintaining compatibility with lower resolution sensors for cost-sensitive applications.  

A color pattern conversion unit transforms RGB image data into HSL color space, enabling robust solar disk identification across varying illumination conditions. The object recognition unit implements adaptive thresholding algorithms to isolate the solar image from atmospheric noise and cloud interference. An object boundary detecting unit applies Sobel edge detection to precisely identify the solar perimeter, while the article circle center benchmark unit calculates centroid coordinates through a three-point circle estimation algorithm.  

The azimuth/elevation angle difference calculating unit converts pixel displacement into angular tracking corrections with sub-pixel resolution. An output driving unit generates control signals for the tracking mechanism, incorporating configurable dead bands to prevent mechanical oscillation while maintaining optimal alignment. This integrated system achieves tracking accuracy below 0.04° under full illumination and maintains sub-0.1° precision during partial solar obscuration, representing a significant advancement over existing solar tracking technologies.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The schematic architecture of the apparatus comprises three primary subsystems: the optical imaging assembly, digital processing unit, and electromechanical tracking mechanism. The optical path begins with a light entrance hole of precisely calculated diameter to balance light intake against diffraction effects, followed by a series of reflective and refractive optical elements. The optical unit incorporates two concave mirrors arranged in Cassegrain configuration, a convex secondary mirror, and a right-angle prism that redirects the light path while minimizing aberrations.  

The casing provides structural integrity and thermal stability, constructed from lightweight aluminum alloy with black anodized interior surfaces to minimize stray light. Lateral plates house precision adjustment mechanisms for optical alignment, while access holes permit maintenance without full disassembly. A light reducing plate with neutral density filtration protects the imaging sensor from overexposure, working in conjunction with an infrared filtering plate that enhances contrast by blocking wavelengths above 760nm.  

The telescope assembly features interchangeable eyepieces providing adjustable magnification from 5× to 15×, with modulation transfer function values exceeding 0.8 at 20 cycles/mm across the entire magnification range. The image sensing element comprises either a high-resolution (2304×1536) or standard-resolution (640×480) CMOS sensor, selected based on application requirements. Signal lines transmit digitized image data to the processing unit via shielded cabling that maintains signal integrity in high-electromagnetic-interference environments.  

The tracking mechanism incorporates brushless DC motors with harmonic drive reducers, providing angular resolution of 0.005° and repeatability within 0.01°. The control mechanism implements a multi-rate control algorithm, with high-speed inner loops for motor current regulation and lower-speed outer loops for position tracking. The full-color image acquiring unit samples images at configurable intervals between 1-30Hz, automatically adjusting exposure parameters based on ambient light conditions.  

The color pattern conversion unit applies matrix transformations to convert RGB values into HSL color space, then isolates the luminance component for threshold determination. The object recognition unit implements adaptive binarization using dynamically calculated lightness thresholds that maintain solar disk identification across illumination variations from 10,000 to 100,000 lux. The object boundary detecting unit applies Sobel operators with 3×3 convolution kernels, followed by morphological operations to eliminate discontinuities in the detected perimeter.  

The article circle center benchmark unit selects optimal edge points for circle fitting, applying constraints to prevent collinear selections that would degrade center estimation accuracy. The azimuth/elevation angle difference calculating unit converts pixel coordinates to angular displacements using calibration matrices that account for optical distortion and sensor alignment. The output driving unit generates pulse-width-modulated signals to the motor controllers, incorporating adjustable dead bands from 1-10 pixels to balance tracking precision against system stability.  

This comprehensive embodiment achieves unprecedented tracking accuracy while maintaining reliability across diverse operating conditions, representing a significant advancement in solar energy collection technology. The system's modular design permits customization for various concentration ratios and tracker sizes, from small-scale rooftop installations to utility-scale solar farms.
