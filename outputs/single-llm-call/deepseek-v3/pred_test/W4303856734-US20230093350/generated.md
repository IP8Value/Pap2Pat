# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of non-invasive optical spectroscopy for measuring tissue oxygenation and metabolic function. More specifically, the invention pertains to an apparatus and method for simultaneously determining hemodynamic and metabolic states of biological tissues through measurement of cytochrome c oxidase (CCO) redox state along with oxygenated and deoxygenated hemoglobin concentrations using a super-continuum laser-based spectroscopy system.

## BACKGROUND

Tissue oxygenation measurement has become an essential tool in clinical and research settings for assessing organ function and viability. Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) has been widely used to study brain function by detecting blood oxygenation level dependent (BOLD) signals. Similarly, functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) measures changes in hemoglobin oxygenation states through optical techniques. However, these conventional methods focus solely on hemodynamic responses without providing direct information about cellular metabolic activity.

Cytochrome c oxidase (CCO) serves as the terminal enzyme in the mitochondrial electron transport chain, playing a crucial role in cellular energy production. The redox state of CCO provides a direct indicator of cellular metabolic activity and oxygen utilization. Measurement of CCO redox state offers valuable insights into tissue viability that cannot be obtained through hemodynamic measurements alone.

Current techniques for measuring CCO face significant limitations. The spectral signal of CCO is relatively weak compared to hemoglobin, typically less than 10% of the hemoglobin signal. Additionally, CCO exhibits a broad, featureless absorption spectrum that overlaps with hemoglobin spectra, making it difficult to distinguish from hemodynamic responses. Traditional light sources used in spectroscopy, such as tungsten-halogen lamps, lack sufficient brightness to reliably measure CCO redox state in vivo.

There exists a pressing need in the medical field for accurate, non-invasive measurement of tissue metabolic state. Conditions such as traumatic brain injury, stroke, and hemorrhagic shock could be better diagnosed and managed with real-time monitoring of both oxygenation and metabolic demand. Current methods cannot provide this combined information non-invasively at the bedside or in field settings.

Prior attempts to measure CCO have been limited by inadequate signal-to-noise ratios, susceptibility to motion artifacts, and inability to distinguish CCO signals from hemodynamic responses and other interferences. The development of a practical, high-sensitivity system capable of simultaneous measurement of CCO redox state along with hemoglobin oxygenation would represent a significant advancement in physiological monitoring.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides an apparatus and method for non-invasively measuring tissue hemodynamic and metabolic function through super-continuum infrared spectroscopy of cytochrome c oxidase (SCISCCO). The system employs an all-fiber integrated super-continuum laser (SCL) light source that generates high-brightness broadband output spanning from 670 nm to 2500 nm, covering the absorption spectra of CCO, oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO), and deoxygenated hemoglobin (HbR).

The apparatus comprises several key components: a super-continuum laser source, a fiber amplifier stage for power amplification, a probe configuration for tissue interrogation, detectors for signal acquisition, and processing units with machine-readable instructions for determining metabolic and oxygenation states. The system improves signal-to-noise ratio through several innovative techniques including a dual-arm differential measurement configuration, lock-in detection synchronized to a chopper, and polarization filtering.

The invention enables determination of tissue metabolic state by measuring changes in the redox state of CCO, while simultaneously assessing oxygenation through measurement of HbO and HbR concentrations. The system employs differential measurement techniques to distinguish CCO signals from hemodynamic responses, skin interference, and motion artifacts. The high brightness of the SCL source allows deeper tissue penetration and improved signal quality compared to conventional light sources.

The apparatus can identify organ or tissue functionality by analyzing correlations and dis-correlations between CCO redox state and hemoglobin oxygenation. This capability has important applications in early detection of brain injury, monitoring of neuronal metabolism and cerebral oxygenation, and assessment of tissue viability in scenarios with risk of ischemia or injury.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides novel techniques for measuring metabolic tissue state and tissue oxygenation through non-invasive optical spectroscopy. The system specifically targets cytochrome c oxidase (CCO), an enzyme critical to cellular energy production, as a marker of metabolic activity. CCO serves as the terminal enzyme in the mitochondrial electron transport chain, where it facilitates the transfer of electrons to oxygen molecules during ATP synthesis. The redox state of CCO provides direct insight into a neuron's internal metabolic activity.

The invention measures tissue oxygenation by detecting changes in oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO) and deoxygenated hemoglobin (HbR) concentrations. These measurements reveal important correlations and dis-correlations between CCO redox state and hemoglobin oxygenation that provide unique information about tissue viability and function. The system offers significant advantages over conventional techniques by simultaneously capturing both metabolic and hemodynamic information with high specificity.

A key innovation of the present invention is the use of a super-continuum laser infrared spectroscopy system for non-invasive interrogation of the redox state of CCO enzyme. The all-fiber integrated, super-continuum light source provides several advantages over traditional broadband sources. The high brightness of the SCL enables measurement of CCO signals that would otherwise be obscured by noise. The fiber-delivery system reduces motion artifacts and improves signal stability during measurements.

The system simultaneously measures HbO and HbR concentrations while providing enhanced signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) through several technical innovations. The pulsed nature of the SCL source allows synchronization with detection systems to further improve SNR. The high brightness and efficient fiber-coupling of SCL systems enable deeper tissue penetration and improved signal quality compared to conventional light sources.

The invention employs a dual-arm differential measurement configuration that enhances the system's ability to distinguish CCO signal from clutter or interference. This configuration provides quantitative measurement of CCO redox state with high sensitivity and selectivity. The system has been demonstrated to measure CCO in various organs and tissues, with particular application to neurological monitoring and early detection of brain injury or neuronal dysfunction.

The apparatus is particularly valuable for monitoring neuronal metabolism and cerebral oxygenation in clinical scenarios involving risk of brain ischemia or injury. The non-invasive nature of the measurements allows continuous monitoring without the risks associated with invasive probes. The system's ability to distinguish metabolic demand from oxygen supply provides critical information for managing conditions such as traumatic brain injury, stroke, and hemorrhagic shock.

FIG. 1 presents a block diagram of the optical set-up for in vitro laboratory measurements. The system comprises an all-fiber integrated super-continuum laser (SCL) that generates broadband light spanning from 670 nm to 2500 nm. The SCL output passes through a chopper for modulation and then into a tunable spectrometer. After spectral selection, the light passes through a polarizer to maintain consistent polarization state before being split by a beam splitter into sample and reference arms.

The sample arm interrogates the tissue or CCO sample while the reference arm provides a control beam for differential measurement. Spatial apertures ensure matched beam characteristics in both arms. Detectors in each arm connect to lock-in amplifiers synchronized to the chopper frequency, enabling sensitive detection of the modulated signal while rejecting ambient light interference. Data collection and processing units analyze the differential signals to determine chromophore concentrations.

FIG. 2A shows a block-diagram of the SCL system in greater detail. The light source comprises a seed laser followed by amplifier stages to boost power. The system utilizes modulational instability initiated super-continuum generation, where picosecond or nanosecond pump pulses generate broadband output through nonlinear processes in optical fibers. This approach provides spectral flatness and high power using commercially available components.

Super-continuum generation occurs in specially designed fibers where nonlinear effects such as four-wave mixing, self-phase modulation, and Raman scattering broaden the spectrum. FIG. 2B illustrates the optical output spectrum of the SCL, demonstrating broadband coverage from visible to short-wave infrared wavelengths. FIG. 3 compares the spectral density versus wavelength for the SCL against a black body radiator, showing the significant brightness enhancement provided by the SCL system.

### Empirical Examples

The invention has been empirically validated through measurement of CCO redox state in various experimental configurations. Bovine heart CCO samples were prepared in oxidized and reduced states for in vitro testing. The SCL system measured the CCO optical absorption spectrum, which exhibited a broad peak near 825 nm between 750 nm and 900 nm. Comparison with literature data confirmed accurate measurement of CCO characteristics.

The SCL system design enables precise measurement of absolute concentration changes in chromophores. The system collects reflected light from tissue and determines light attenuation at multiple wavelengths. Using known extinction coefficients and calculated optical pathlengths, the system computes absolute concentration changes of HbO, HbR, and CCO. This capability provides quantitative assessment of both metabolic and oxygenation states.

Human testing demonstrated the system's ability to measure physiological responses. A blood pressure test on the forearm showed expected changes in HbO and HbR without significant alteration in CCO redox state, consistent with forearm physiology. A breath holding test on the forehead revealed correlations between the SCL system and commercial fNIRS measurements for HbO, while also demonstrating changes in CCO redox state during breath holding.

A cognitive attention test involving 25 healthy human participants provided particularly valuable data. The test protocol involved alternating periods of rest and cognitive activity while measuring forehead hemodynamics and metabolism. Results showed that during attention tasks, HbO concentration increased while the redox state of CCO decreased, consistent with increased frontal lobe activity and metabolic demand.

The system's performance was further validated in swine animal models of hemorrhagic shock. The cart-based SCISCCO prototype successfully monitored cerebral hemodynamics and metabolism during controlled hemorrhage and subsequent treatment with partial REBOA (pREBOA) and valproic acid (VPA). Measurements correlated with expected physiological responses and provided unique insights into the metabolic effects of these interventions.

The cognitive attention test results merit particular discussion. During attention tasks, increased frontal lobe activity requires additional ATP production, leading to increased reduction of CCO as electrons flow through the electron transport chain. Concurrently, increased oxygen delivery raises HbO concentration. The system's ability to capture both phenomena simultaneously provides unprecedented insight into brain metabolism during cognitive activity.

Comparison with prior studies highlights the advantages of the SCL-based system. Conventional systems using lamp sources struggle to measure CCO redox state reliably due to insufficient brightness and SNR. The present invention achieves nearly an order of magnitude higher spectral density, enabling definitive measurement of CCO changes during cognitive tasks. The system's differential measurement configuration, polarization control, and lock-in detection further enhance SNR and measurement reliability.

The cart-based prototype demonstrates the invention's potential for clinical application. The integrated system combines SCL source, optical components, electronics, and processing in a portable package suitable for hospital use. Testing confirmed stability during transport and operation in clinical environments. The system's graphical user interface simplifies operation by trained personnel while providing comprehensive data display and analysis capabilities.

In summary, the empirical examples demonstrate the invention's ability to:
1. Accurately measure CCO redox state in vitro and in vivo
2. Simultaneously determine HbO and HbR concentrations
3. Distinguish metabolic demand from oxygen supply
4. Provide stable, reproducible measurements in clinical settings
5. Offer insights into tissue viability not available through conventional monitoring

The invention represents a significant advance in physiological monitoring by providing simultaneous, non-invasive measurement of both hemodynamic and metabolic parameters. This capability has broad applications in neurology, critical care, trauma management, and other medical fields where understanding tissue oxygenation and metabolism is critical to patient care.
