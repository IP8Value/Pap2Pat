# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to pharmaceutical compositions and methods for treating neurological disorders, particularly epilepsy, through the synergistic combination of decanoic acid and perampanel or other AMPA receptor inhibitors. The invention provides novel therapeutic approaches by exploiting the distinct but complementary mechanisms of action of these compounds on AMPA receptors, resulting in enhanced efficacy at lower doses compared to monotherapy.  

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION  

Epilepsy is a chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent seizures, affecting approximately 50 million people worldwide. Seizures arise from abnormal, excessive neuronal activity in the brain, often due to imbalances in excitatory and inhibitory neurotransmission. Despite the availability of numerous antiepileptic drugs, approximately one-third of patients remain refractory to current treatments, highlighting the need for improved therapeutic strategies.  

Current treatment options for epilepsy include pharmacological interventions such as sodium channel blockers, GABA enhancers, and glutamate receptor antagonists. However, many of these drugs exhibit dose-limiting side effects or lose efficacy over time. The ketogenic diet, particularly the medium-chain triglyceride (MCT) variant, has emerged as a nonpharmacological treatment for drug-resistant epilepsy. The MCT ketogenic diet is rich in medium-chain fatty acids, including decanoic acid, which are metabolized into ketones that provide an alternative energy source for the brain.  

While the MCT ketogenic diet has demonstrated efficacy in reducing seizure frequency, its implementation is challenging due to strict dietary requirements and potential gastrointestinal side effects. Recent studies have revealed that decanoic acid, a component of MCTs, exerts direct antiseizure effects by noncompetitively inhibiting AMPA receptors, independent of ketone production. AMPA receptors, which mediate fast excitatory neurotransmission, are implicated in the pathogenesis of epilepsy and other neurological disorders.  

Perampanel (Fycompa) is a clinically approved noncompetitive AMPA receptor antagonist used as an adjunctive treatment for partial-onset and generalized tonic-clonic seizures. Although effective, perampanel exhibits dose-dependent side effects, including dizziness, somnolence, and behavioral changes, which limit its utility in some patients. The binding site of perampanel on AMPA receptors has been well characterized, but its potential for synergistic interactions with other AMPA receptor inhibitors, such as decanoic acid, remains unexplored.  

Given the distinct binding sites of decanoic acid and perampanel on AMPA receptors, the present invention addresses the unmet need for combinatory therapies that enhance seizure control while minimizing adverse effects. By leveraging the synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors, the invention provides a novel approach to treating epilepsy and related neurological disorders.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention is based on the discovery of a synergistic interaction between decanoic acid and perampanel or other AMPA receptor inhibitors in the treatment of epilepsy and related neurological conditions. This synergy arises from the distinct but complementary binding sites of these compounds on AMPA receptors, resulting in enhanced receptor inhibition and superior seizure control compared to monotherapy.  

The combination of decanoic acid and perampanel allows for reduced dosing of perampanel, thereby mitigating its dose-dependent side effects while maintaining or improving therapeutic efficacy. The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions, kits, and methods for administering these compounds separately, sequentially, or as a co-formulation. Additionally, the invention extends to the use of decanoic acid and perampanel or AMPA receptor inhibitors in treating other disorders mediated by AMPA receptor dysfunction, including ischemia, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), cancer, and Alzheimer's disease.

### Decanoic Acid and Compositions Comprising the Same  

Decanoic acid, also known as capric acid, is a medium-chain fatty acid with the chemical formula C10H20O2. It may be provided in various forms, including free fatty acid, triglyceride, or pharmaceutically acceptable salt or ester forms. Medium-chain triglycerides (MCTs) comprising decanoic acid are particularly suitable for use in the invention, as they are readily metabolized and absorbed.  

MCTs may be homotriglycerides, wherein all three fatty acid moieties are decanoic acid, or heterotriglycerides, wherein decanoic acid is combined with other medium-chain fatty acids such as octanoic acid. The composition may be substantially free from long-chain fatty acids to maximize the therapeutic effects of decanoic acid.  

Natural sources of MCTs include coconut oil and palm kernel oil, which may be refined or fractionated to enrich the decanoic acid content. Synthetic MCTs may also be produced by esterification of glycerol with decanoic acid or other medium-chain fatty acids. The composition may further include long-chain triglycerides (LCTs) in minor amounts to provide additional nutritional benefits.  

The composition may include other substances such as minerals, vitamins, or excipients to enhance stability or palatability. For example, the composition may be formulated as an oil-in-water emulsion to improve absorption or as a solid dosage form for oral administration. The total fat content and energy value of the emulsion may be adjusted to meet the dietary needs of the patient.  

The composition may be delivered as part of a ketogenic diet, wherein the ratio of total fat content to protein and carbohydrate content is optimized to induce ketosis. The composition may be administered via various routes, including oral, enteral, or parenteral administration. The composition may also be incorporated into food substances or medical foods designed for patients with epilepsy or other neurological disorders.  

### Perampanel  

Perampanel is a noncompetitive AMPA receptor antagonist with the chemical name 2-(2-oxo-1-phenyl-5-pyridin-2-yl-1,2-dihydropyridin-3-yl) benzonitrile. It may be provided as a free base or as a pharmaceutically acceptable salt, such as the hydrochloride salt. Perampanel is typically administered orally in doses ranging from 2 mg to 12 mg per day, depending on the patient's response and tolerance.  

Perampanel binds to a specific site on the AMPA receptor, distinct from the binding site of decanoic acid, resulting in noncompetitive inhibition of receptor activity. The invention also encompasses other AMPA receptor inhibitors that bind to the same site as perampanel, including small molecules and polypeptides. Techniques for identifying and characterizing AMPA receptor inhibitors include electrophysiological assays, binding studies, and molecular modeling.  

The IC50 value for AMPA receptor inhibition by perampanel is approximately 5-8 μmol/L, as demonstrated in recombinant receptor studies. The invention includes AMPA receptor inhibitors with similar or improved potency, selectivity, or pharmacokinetic properties compared to perampanel.

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate the invention without limiting its scope. Conventional techniques in molecular biology, electrophysiology, and pharmacology were employed, as described in the relevant literature.  

### Example 1—Methods  

Direct AMPA receptor current recording was performed using Xenopus laevis oocytes expressing recombinant GluA1/2 or GluA2/3 subunits. Seizure-like activity was induced in rat hippocampal slices using pentylenetetrazol (PTZ), and the effects of perampanel and decanoic acid were analyzed.  

### Example 2—Direct Inhibition of AMPA Receptors by Perampanel and Decanoic Acid  

Perampanel inhibited AMPA receptor currents with IC50 values of 6.27 μmol/L (GluA1/2) and 8.11 μmol/L (GluA2/3), demonstrating noncompetitive inhibition. Decanoic acid also inhibited AMPA receptors, with distinct binding sites confirmed using a GluA3 mutant resistant to perampanel. Synergistic inhibition was observed when decanoic acid and perampanel were combined, with significantly reduced IC50 values for both compounds.  

### Example 3—Effects of Perampanel and Decanoic Acid on Seizure Models  

In rat hippocampal slices, combinatory treatment with decanoic acid and perampanel significantly reduced PTZ-induced seizure-like activity compared to monotherapy. Similar synergistic effects were observed in human neocortical slices, with near-abolition of epileptiform activity at combinatory doses.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention demonstrates that the combination of decanoic acid and perampanel provides synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors and enhanced seizure control. This synergy allows for reduced perampanel dosing, minimizing side effects while maintaining efficacy. The invention has significant clinical potential for treating epilepsy and other neurological disorders mediated by AMPA receptor dysfunction.
