### TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to compositions and methods for treating cystinosis, particularly ophthalmic, ocular, or corneal manifestations of cystinosis. More specifically, the invention provides novel therapeutic agents, N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA) and (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (diNACA), which demonstrate superior cystine-depleting activity and reduced cytotoxicity compared to existing treatments such as cysteamine. The invention further encompasses pharmaceutical formulations, including ophthalmic inserts, for effective delivery of these compounds.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cystinosis is a rare autosomal-recessive lysosomal storage disorder caused by mutations in the CTNS gene located on chromosome 17p13. The most common mutation is a 57 Kb deletion present in approximately 50% of cystinotic patients of Western European ancestry. The CTNS gene encodes cystinosin, a 367-amino acid integral membrane protein responsible for transporting cystine out of lysosomes. Loss of this transporter function results in intralysosomal cystine accumulation, leading to cellular dysfunction and progressive damage to multiple organs, including the kidneys, eyes, liver, muscles, pancreas, and brain.  

Cystinosis presents in three major clinical forms: infantile nephropathic cystinosis, intermediate cystinosis, and non-nephropathic (ocular) cystinosis. Infantile nephropathic cystinosis is the most severe form, characterized by renal Fanconi syndrome, growth retardation, hypophosphatemic/calcipenic rickets, and eventual end-stage renal disease. Ocular cystinosis manifests primarily as photophobia due to corneal cystine crystal accumulation, which occurs in all forms of the disease.  

Diagnosis of cystinosis typically involves measurement of leukocyte cystine levels, genetic testing for CTNS mutations, and clinical evaluation of symptoms. Current treatment relies on cysteamine, which enters lysosomes and reacts with cystine to form a mixed disulfide that can exit via alternative transport pathways. However, cysteamine therapy has significant limitations, including poor patient compliance due to unpleasant taste and odor, gastrointestinal side effects, and the need for frequent dosing. Moreover, oral cysteamine does not effectively reach avascular corneal tissues, necessitating separate ophthalmic formulations such as CYSTARAN® and CYSTADROPS® eye drops, which also require frequent administration and can cause discomfort.  

The ocular manifestations of cystinosis include corneal crystal accumulation, retinopathy, retinal degeneration, and pigmentary disturbances of the peripheral fundus. Standard treatments for these effects, such as cysteamine eye drops, are limited by poor tolerability and adherence. There remains an unmet need for novel therapeutic agents that can effectively reduce lysosomal cystine accumulation with improved safety and tolerability profiles.  

Existing marketed drugs for cystinosis include CYSTAGON® and PROCYSBI® (oral cysteamine bitartrate) and CYSTARAN® and CYSTADROPS® (cysteamine hydrochloride eye drops). While these agents provide some benefit, their limitations underscore the necessity for alternative treatments. The present invention addresses this need by providing NACA and diNACA as novel therapeutic options with demonstrated efficacy in reducing cystine levels and improving cell viability in cystinotic fibroblasts.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for treating cystinosis using N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA) and its dimer, (2R,2R′)-3,3′-disulfanediyl bis(2-acetamidopropanamide) (diNACA). These compounds exhibit superior cystine-depleting activity compared to cysteamine, the current standard of care, while also demonstrating enhanced cell viability in human cystinotic fibroblast cultures.  

NACA is a lipophilic prodrug of N-acetylcysteine (NAC), a known antioxidant. Unlike NAC, which has limited bioavailability, NACA provides more efficient delivery of NAC to intracellular compartments, resulting in higher glutathione (GSH) levels and enhanced antioxidant activity. DiNACA further serves as a prodrug for both NACA and NAC, offering sustained release and prolonged therapeutic effects.  

The invention encompasses various formulations for administering NACA and diNACA, including oral dosage forms, ophthalmic drops, ointments, and inserts. Ophthalmic inserts, in particular, provide a novel delivery method that overcomes the limitations of frequent eye drop administration. These inserts can be designed as bioerodible or non-bioerodible systems to achieve controlled release of the active agents.  

In addition to monotherapy, the invention includes methods for co-administering NACA or diNACA with other active agents, such as cysteamine or antioxidants, to enhance therapeutic outcomes. The compositions are formulated in pharmaceutically acceptable carriers and may include additives to improve stability, solubility, or bioavailability.  

The invention further provides methods for determining the presence of cystine or anti-cystine antibodies in biological samples, enabling monitoring of treatment efficacy. These methods utilize techniques such as liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) to quantify cystine levels and assess therapeutic response.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention is based on the discovery that NACA and diNACA effectively reduce lysosomal cystine accumulation in cystinotic fibroblasts while improving cell viability. These compounds offer significant advantages over existing treatments, including enhanced bioavailability, reduced toxicity, and the potential for less frequent dosing.  

N-acetylcysteine (NAC) is a well-known antioxidant that replenishes intracellular glutathione (GSH) levels. However, its therapeutic utility is limited by poor membrane permeability and rapid metabolism. NACA, the amide derivative of NAC, addresses these limitations by exhibiting greater lipophilicity and cellular uptake. Once inside cells, NACA is metabolized to NAC, which participates in GSH synthesis and redox homeostasis.  

DiNACA, the dimeric form of NACA, further extends the therapeutic window by serving as a prodrug for both NACA and NAC. This compound undergoes gradual hydrolysis in physiological conditions, providing sustained release of active metabolites. The invention includes detailed synthetic routes for preparing NACA and diNACA, as well as analytical methods for characterizing their purity and stability.  

The invention also encompasses formulations for ocular delivery, including solutions, suspensions, emulsions, and inserts. Ophthalmic inserts are particularly advantageous for treating corneal cystinosis, as they provide controlled release of NACA or diNACA over extended periods, reducing the need for frequent administration. These inserts can be fabricated from biodegradable polymers such as poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) or non-biodegradable materials like silicone.  

Methods for treating cystinosis include administering an effective amount of NACA or diNACA to a subject in need thereof. The dosage and frequency of administration depend on factors such as disease severity, patient age, and route of delivery. For ophthalmic applications, typical doses range from 0.1% to 5% (w/v) of NACA or diNACA in solution or insert form.  

The invention further provides protocols for assessing treatment efficacy, including measuring cystine levels in leukocytes or ocular tissues and monitoring clinical symptoms such as photophobia or corneal crystal density. These methods enable personalized therapy adjustments to optimize outcomes.  

### Cytotoxicity of NACA and diNACA in Cystinotic Fibroblast Cell Culture  

The cytotoxicity of NACA and diNACA was evaluated in human cystinotic fibroblasts (HCFs) using the MTT assay. Cells were incubated with concentrations of 25, 50, or 75 µM of NACA, diNACA, or cysteamine for 48 and 72 hours. Results demonstrated that neither NACA nor diNACA exhibited cytotoxicity at any concentration. In contrast, both compounds significantly increased cell viability compared to untreated controls. Cysteamine also showed no cytotoxicity at higher concentrations (50 and 75 µM) but did not enhance viability at the lowest concentration (25 µM). These findings indicate that NACA and diNACA are not only safe but also promote cellular health in cystinotic conditions.  

### Screening of Cystine-Depleting Activity of NACA and diNACA  

The cystine-depleting activity of NACA and diNACA was assessed in HCFs using a colorimetric assay based on papain activation. Cells were treated with 50 µM of NACA, diNACA, or cysteamine for 6, 24, 48, and 72 hours. NACA demonstrated the most rapid and potent cystine reduction, achieving statistically significant depletion at all time points. DiNACA also showed significant activity, particularly at early and mid-term time points. Cysteamine required 72 hours to achieve comparable effects. These results establish NACA and diNACA as superior agents for reducing lysosomal cystine accumulation.  

### Ophthalmic Tolerability of 1% NPI-001, 1% diNACA, 1% NAC or Vehicle  

Prototype ophthalmic formulations containing 1% NACA (NPI-001), 1% diNACA (NPI-002), 1% NAC, or vehicle were evaluated for ocular tolerability in rabbits. All formulations were well-tolerated, with no signs of irritation or adverse effects observed during the study period. These findings support the safety of NACA and diNACA for ophthalmic applications.  

### NACA (NPI-001), diNACA (NPI-002), NAC or Cysteamine Ophthalmic Insert  

The invention includes ophthalmic inserts designed for sustained release of NACA, diNACA, NAC, or cysteamine. Non-bioerodible inserts are composed of silicone or similar polymers, while bioerodible inserts utilize biodegradable materials such as PLGA. These inserts are engineered to release active agents over periods ranging from days to weeks, providing continuous therapy without frequent dosing.  

### Example 5  

In vitro release studies were conducted to evaluate the kinetics of NACA, diNACA, NAC, or cysteamine from ophthalmic inserts. Inserts were immersed in buffer solutions, and samples were collected at predetermined intervals for analysis by HPLC/MS/MS. Results demonstrated controlled release profiles, with adjustable rates achievable by modifying polymer composition or insert design. Stability testing under various storage conditions confirmed the integrity of the inserts over extended periods.  

In vivo studies in rabbits further validated the tolerability and efficacy of the inserts. Custom-fitted inserts were surgically placed in the conjunctival sac, and drug levels in ocular tissues were quantified. Significant delivery to the cornea and aqueous humor was observed, confirming the potential of inserts as an effective alternative to eye drops.  

Nonclinical studies were conducted to assess systemic safety, including toxicokinetics and histopathology. No adverse effects were noted, supporting the advancement of NACA and diNACA inserts into clinical evaluation. A Phase 2/3 clinical study is planned to further assess efficacy and safety in patients with ocular cystinosis.
