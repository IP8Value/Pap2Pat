# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for treating triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) through selective inhibition of protein kinase C (PKC) isozymes, particularly PKN2. More specifically, the invention provides methods utilizing chelerythrine or other PKC inhibitors as targeted therapeutic agents against TNBC tumors. The invention further encompasses diagnostic methods for identifying TNBC patients likely to respond to PKC inhibitor therapy based on PKN2 expression levels, as well as combination therapies incorporating PKC inhibitors with conventional chemotherapeutic agents.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Breast cancer represents the most prevalent malignancy among women globally, with triple-negative breast cancer constituting approximately 10-15% of all breast cancer cases. TNBC is clinically defined by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression, which renders it unresponsive to hormonal therapies or HER2-targeted treatments. Current standard of care for TNBC relies primarily on conventional chemotherapy, which often leads to rapid disease relapse and poor patient outcomes due to the aggressive nature of this cancer subtype.

Existing therapeutic approaches for TNBC face significant limitations. While numerous targeted therapies against poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP), epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), angiogenesis pathways, mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), and other molecular targets have been investigated in clinical trials, none have achieved regulatory approval for TNBC treatment. The lack of effective targeted therapies leaves TNBC patients with limited treatment options and worse prognosis compared to other breast cancer subtypes, highlighting the urgent need for novel therapeutic strategies.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for treating triple-negative breast cancer through targeted inhibition of protein kinase C isozymes, particularly PKN2. The method involves administering an effective amount of a PKC inhibitor, preferably chelerythrine or its derivatives, to selectively inhibit proliferation and induce apoptosis in TNBC cells while sparing non-TNBC breast cancer cells.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for treating TNBC comprising administering to a patient in need thereof a therapeutically effective amount of chelerythrine, wherein said treatment selectively targets TNBC cells overexpressing PKN2. Another embodiment includes a diagnostic method for identifying TNBC patients likely to respond to PKC inhibitor therapy by detecting elevated PKN2 expression levels in tumor samples. The invention further encompasses combination therapies incorporating PKC inhibitors with conventional chemotherapeutic agents such as taxanes, demonstrating synergistic effects against TNBC cell proliferation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

The term "comprising" as used herein shall be interpreted as open-ended language meaning the claimed methods may include additional steps or components beyond those explicitly listed.

Protein kinase C (PKC) serves as a critical target agent for TNBC treatment according to the present invention. The PKC family comprises serine/threonine protein kinases that play pivotal roles in various disease processes including cancer. Among PKC isozymes, PRKCA (PKCα) has been previously associated with TNBC, but the invention identifies PKN2 as a novel and more consistently overexpressed PKC isozyme across TNBC cell lines and tumors.

The role of PKC in disease processes is well-established, with particular relevance to cancer progression. The invention demonstrates an inverse relationship between ER status and PKC activity/abundance in breast cancer, where TNBC cells lacking ER expression show elevated PKC levels compared to hormone receptor-positive breast cancers. Malignant breast tissue exhibits significantly higher PKC expression than normal breast tissue, supporting its role in oncogenesis.

PRKCA has been associated with antiestrogen resistance and tumor aggressiveness in previous studies. The current invention confirms PRKCA as a central signaling node and therapeutic target for breast cancer stem cells, which share molecular characteristics with TNBC. However, the invention importantly identifies PRKCA overexpression in only a subset of TNBC cases, while demonstrating PKN2 overexpression across all tested TNBC cell lines.

The invention establishes PKC, particularly PKN2, as a specific therapeutic target agent for TNBC. Chelerythrine, a natural benzophenanthridine alkaloid and highly specific PKC inhibitor, serves as the preferred therapeutic agent in the invention. The invention demonstrates chelerythrine's selective anti-cancer effects against TNBC cell lines (MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and MDA-MB-468) compared to non-TNBC cell lines (MCF7, ZR-75-1, SK-BR-3, and MDA-MB-453).

Materials and methods for implementing the invention include specific reagents and antibodies. Chelerythrine and taxol are obtained from commercial sources, along with trichloroacetic acid (TCA), propidium iodide (PI), Hoechst 33258, and other essential reagents. Antibodies against cleaved PARP, PRKCA, PKN2, and β-actin are utilized for detection and analysis purposes.

Cell culture conditions for practicing the invention involve maintaining breast cancer cell lines in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and antibiotics. The invention provides detailed protocols for in vitro cell proliferation assays using sulforhodamine B (SRB) colorimetric methods, with specific cell seeding densities and incubation conditions optimized for TNBC cell lines.

The colony formation assay represents a critical component of the invention, demonstrating chelerythrine's selective inhibition of TNBC cell growth. The method involves treating cells with 5 μM chelerythrine for varying durations, followed by plating in drug-free medium and staining colonies after 7-10 days incubation.

Flow-cytometric analysis of cell cycle and apoptosis form essential aspects of the invention. For cell cycle analysis, cells are fixed in ethanol, stained with propidium iodide/RNase solution, and analyzed by flow cytometry to determine distribution across cell cycle phases. Apoptosis analysis utilizes Annexin V-FITC/PI staining followed by flow cytometry to quantify apoptotic cell fractions.

Assessment of cell morphological changes provides visual confirmation of chelerythrine's effects. The method involves staining chelerythrine-treated cells with Hoechst 33258 and visualizing nuclear morphology by fluorescence microscopy to identify characteristic apoptotic changes.

Gene expression data retrieval from the Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) database enables comparative analysis of PKC isozyme expression patterns between TNBC and non-TNBC cell lines. Quantitative real-time PCR and Western blotting methods are provided for validating mRNA and protein expression levels of specific PKC isozymes.

The invention includes detailed protocols for dual-drug combination assays, particularly combining chelerythrine with taxol. The method involves treating cells with various concentration combinations of both drugs and calculating combination index (CI) values to determine synergistic, additive, or antagonistic effects.

Results from practicing the invention demonstrate chelerythrine's selective inhibition of TNBC cell proliferation. Dose-response experiments show IC50 values of 2.6-4.2 μM for TNBC cell lines compared to >10 μM for non-TNBC lines. Time-course experiments confirm sustained growth inhibition specific to TNBC cells.

Colony formation assays provide independent confirmation of chelerythrine's selective anti-proliferative activity against TNBC cells. TNBC cell colony formation is dramatically reduced after 3 hours chelerythrine treatment and nearly abolished by 6 hours, while non-TNBC cells maintain colony-forming ability even after 48 hours treatment.

Cell cycle analysis reveals differential effects of chelerythrine on TNBC versus non-TNBC cells. While non-TNBC cell cycle distribution remains unchanged, TNBC cells show significant increases in sub-G0/G1 phase (indicative of apoptosis) and cell line-specific alterations in G0/G1 or G2/M phases.

Chelerythrine selectively induces apoptosis in TNBC cell lines, as demonstrated by multiple complementary methods. Fluorescence microscopy reveals characteristic apoptotic nuclear morphology (chromatin condensation and fragmentation) specifically in TNBC cells. Western blotting detects cleaved PARP, a biochemical marker of apoptosis, exclusively in TNBC lines after chelerythrine treatment.

Flow cytometric quantification confirms substantial apoptosis induction in TNBC cells (22.2-67.8% apoptotic fractions) compared to minimal effects in non-TNBC lines (<5% apoptotic fractions). Time-course experiments demonstrate progressive apoptosis induction in TNBC cells over 24 hours.

The invention identifies multiple PKC isozymes mediating chelerythrine's selective activity. While PRKCA shows elevated expression in some TNBC lines, PKN2 demonstrates consistent overexpression across all tested TNBC cell lines at both mRNA and protein levels. CCLE database analysis confirms PKN2 as one of the highest expressed PKC isoforms in breast cancer generally, with particularly elevated expression in TNBC.

Knockdown experiments establish PKN2's essential role in TNBC cell growth. shRNA-mediated PKN2 suppression significantly reduces colony formation in TNBC but not non-TNBC cells. Xenograft studies demonstrate PKN2 knockdown specifically impairs TNBC tumor growth in vivo.

The invention demonstrates chelerythrine's ability to enhance chemotherapy efficacy. Combination treatment with chelerythrine and taxol produces greater growth inhibition than either agent alone in all tested TNBC cell lines. Combination index analysis reveals additive to synergistic effects (CI values 0.75-1.14 at ED50).

In vivo xenograft studies confirm chelerythrine's selective anti-TNBC activity. Chelerythrine treatment (5 mg/kg every 3-4 days) significantly suppresses TNBC tumor growth compared to minimal effects on non-TNBC xenografts, mirroring the selective effects observed in vitro.

The invention thus provides a comprehensive method for targeted TNBC treatment through PKC inhibition, with particular emphasis on PKN2 as a novel therapeutic target and chelerythrine as a selective therapeutic agent. The methods encompass diagnostic applications, monotherapy approaches, and combination regimens with conventional chemotherapy.
