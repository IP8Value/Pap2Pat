# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of genetic engineering, particularly to the design and construction of genetically encoded systems for detecting and evolving biologically active agents. More specifically, the invention provides operon-based systems for producing, detecting, and evolving inhibitors of protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs), including photoswitchable enzyme constructs and microbial biosensors for inhibitor screening.  

## BACKGROUND  

Protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs) and protein tyrosine kinases (PTKs) regulate numerous cellular processes and are implicated in various diseases, including cancer, diabetes, and autoimmune disorders. While PTK inhibitors have been successfully developed as therapeutics, PTP inhibitors face challenges due to their conserved active sites and poor selectivity. The control of phosphatase activity through optogenetic actuators represents an innovative approach to modulating intracellular signaling with spatial and temporal precision. Current methods for discovering PTP inhibitors are resource-intensive and often yield molecules with limited efficacy or selectivity. There exists a need for scalable systems that can efficiently identify and evolve potent, selective inhibitors of PTPs, particularly those that target allosteric sites.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention relates to genetic engineering systems for constructing operons that produce biologically active agents, including fusion proteins comprising substrate recognition domains, DNA-binding domains, and anchoring units for RNA polymerase. The system includes reporter genes such as LuxAB bioreporters or fluorescent proteins like mClover, as well as antibiotic resistance markers for selection.  

Key components of the invention include a protein phosphatase, particularly protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B (PTP1B), and a protein light switch such as a LOV2 domain, forming a PTP1B-LOV2 chimera. The system further comprises a genetically encoded operon for detecting inhibitors of PTP1B, wherein inhibition of PTP1B activity is linked to expression of a reporter gene or survival under selective conditions.  

The invention provides methods for detecting inhibitors of an enzyme, particularly PTP1B, by utilizing a microbial biosensor system wherein enzyme inhibition modulates transcription of a survival gene. The system enables the evolution of inhibitors through directed mutagenesis and selection, including the evolution of photoswitchable enzymes and substrate domains selective for PTP1B.  

The invention further includes methods for controlling cell movement and signaling through light-induced conformational changes in photoswitchable enzyme constructs, altering catalytic activity in response to illumination. Methods are provided for detecting small molecule modulators by expressing fusion proteins in bacterial cells, contacting them with test compounds, illuminating to induce conformational changes, and measuring readouts such as fluorescence or luminescence.  

The system is applicable to treating diseases associated with phosphatase dysregulation, including diabetes, obesity, and cancer. The invention provides a photoswitchable protein tyrosine phosphatase enzyme construct comprising an N-terminal alpha helix and a C-terminal allosteric domain region, wherein illumination modulates catalytic activity.  

The genetically encoded system includes a biosensor for enzyme activity comprising a substrate recognition domain and fluorescent proteins for FRET-based detection. The system further includes operons for terpenoid biosynthesis, enabling the production and screening of natural product inhibitors. Methods are provided for transfecting bacteria with inhibitor detection operons and mevalonate pathway operons, growing cells under selective conditions, and isolating phosphatase inhibitor molecules.  

The invention further encompasses engineered bacterial cell lines producing phosphatase inhibitor molecules, particularly terpenoids, and methods for treating mammalian cell cultures to reduce phosphatase activity. The system is adaptable to polyketide and alkaloid biosynthesis pathways for expanding the diversity of inhibitor scaffolds.  

## DEFINITIONS  

**Operon**: A functional unit of genomic DNA containing a cluster of genes under the control of a single promoter, typically found in prokaryotes.  

**Phosphorylation-regulating enzymes**: Enzymes that catalyze the addition (kinases) or removal (phosphatases) of phosphate groups from substrate proteins.  

**Phosphorylation**: The covalent addition of a phosphate group to a molecule, particularly proteins, regulating their activity.  

**Optogenetic actuator**: A light-sensitive protein domain used to control the activity of a fused effector domain in response to illumination.  

**Dynamic range**: The ratio between the maximum and minimum measurable values of a system's output, here referring to the difference in enzyme activity between light and dark states.  

**Operably linked**: Refers to nucleic acid sequences placed in a functional relationship, wherein expression of one sequence is controlled by or affects expression of another.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF INVENTION  

The invention relates to genetic engineering systems for constructing operons that produce biologically active agents. The systems enable detection and construction of inhibitors for protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs), particularly PTP1B, through microbial operons that link inhibitor potency to cellular survival or reporter gene expression.  

### I. Protein Tyrosine Phosphatases (PTPs) and Protein Tyrosine Kinases (PTKs) in Relation to Disease  

PTPs and PTKs regulate critical signaling pathways and their dysregulation contributes to numerous diseases. The invention provides photoswitchable constructs of PTPs and PTKs for controlling their activity with light, enabling precise spatial and temporal modulation of signaling events. These constructs facilitate identification of specific alleles and screening of inhibitory molecules.  

References including Fan et al. describe LOV2 conjugates for optical control of protein function. The invention builds upon these technologies by developing fusion proteins comprising PTP1B and a LOV domain, creating a photoswitchable variant wherein light alters catalytic activity. Prior art includes WO2011133493 describing kinase-LOV fusions and U.S. Pat. No. 8,859,232 covering protein light switches.  

PTP1B serves as a model system due to its well-characterized structure and role in disease. Detailed biophysical studies, including X-ray crystallography and molecular dynamics simulations, reveal allosteric networks that inform inhibitor design. The invention targets these allosteric sites with terpenoid inhibitors identified through microbial screening systems.  

### II. Optogenetic Actuators  

The invention provides improved optogenetic actuators overcoming limitations of existing technologies. Current photoswitchable proteins rely primarily on blue light, limiting tissue penetration. The invention describes operons to evolve constructs responsive to red or infrared light, expanding applications in vivo.  

The photoswitchable PTP1B-LOV2 chimera exhibits light-dependent catalytic activity with an optimized dynamic range. Key features include:  
- Strategic placement of the LOV2 domain relative to the PTP1B catalytic site  
- Optimized linkers between domains to maximize light-induced conformational changes  
- Mutations enhancing photoswitching efficiency while maintaining phosphatase activity  

Biophysical characterization includes kinetic assays, crystallographic studies, and spectroscopic analyses confirming light-dependent activity modulation. The system is extended to other PTPs (e.g., STEP) and PTKs through analogous chimeric constructs.  

### III. Genetically Encoded System for Constructing and Detecting Biologically Active Agents  

The microbial inhibitor screening system comprises:  
1. A bacterial two-hybrid (B2H) module linking PTP1B inhibition to antibiotic resistance  
2. Terpenoid biosynthesis operons producing candidate inhibitor molecules  

The B2H system includes:  
- Src kinase phosphorylating a substrate domain  
- PTP1B dephosphorylating the substrate  
- Reporter gene expression controlled by phosphorylation state  

Terpenoid pathways incorporate:  
- Mevalonate pathway genes for isoprenoid precursor synthesis  
- Terpene synthases generating diverse hydrocarbon scaffolds  

The system identified novel PTP1B inhibitors (amorphadiene, β-bisabolene) binding allosteric sites with unexpected mechanisms. X-ray crystallography revealed amorphadiene induces reorganization of the α7 helix, creating a hydrophobic binding cleft. Kinetic studies demonstrate selectivity over related phosphatases (TC-PTP).  

The invention improves upon prior screening methods (e.g., U.S. Pat. No. 6,428,951) by directly linking inhibitor potency to cellular survival. The system enables:  
- High-throughput screening of terpenoid libraries  
- Directed evolution of improved inhibitors  
- Identification of novel scaffolds through uncharacterized terpene synthases  

### IV. Evolving High-Affinity Terpenoid Inhibitors of PTP1B  

The invention provides methods for evolving terpenoid inhibitors through:  
1. Site-saturation mutagenesis of terpene synthases  
2. Random mutagenesis of biosynthetic pathways  
3. Growth-coupled selection in the B2H system  

Key screening methodologies include:  
- FRET-based sensors monitoring PTP1B conformational changes  
- Tryptophan fluorescence assays detecting inhibitor binding  
- FACS sorting of high-producing clones  

A biophysical framework analyzes binding pocket properties to guide enzyme engineering. The system identifies plastic residues amenable to mutation while maintaining catalytic function.  

### VI. Evolving Optogenetic Actuators  

The invention describes operons for evolving improved photoswitchable constructs:  
- Phytochrome-based systems responsive to red/infrared light  
- Optimized dynamic range and recovery kinetics  
- Enhanced tissue penetration for in vivo applications  

Directed evolution strategies include:  
- Library generation through DNA shuffling  
- Growth-coupled selection in reporter strains  
- High-throughput characterization of switching parameters  

The system extends to other therapeutic targets through modular design principles.

## EXAMPLES  

**Example 1**: Estimation of IC50 for amorphadiene inhibition of PTP1B  
Kinetic assays measuring pNPP hydrolysis yielded an IC50 of 53 ± 8 μM (mean ± SD, n=5 independent experiments). Competitive inhibition experiments confirmed allosteric binding.  

**Example 2**: Terpenoid production in engineered E. coli  
Strains expressing amorphadiene synthase produced 18 mg/L terpenoid. Titers correlated with antibiotic resistance in the B2H system (R2=0.89).  

**Example 3**: Light-dependent activity of PTP1B-LOV2 chimera  
Illumination at 470 nm reduced catalytic activity by 78% (kcat dark=12.3 s-1 vs. light=2.7 s-1). Activity recovered fully within 30 minutes in darkness.
