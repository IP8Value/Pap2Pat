# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of malaria prophylaxis and treatment. Specifically, it pertains to novel pharmaceutical formulations of atovaquone for intramuscular administration, providing long-acting protection against Plasmodium infections. The invention further encompasses methods of preventing malaria using sustained-release atovaquone formulations, microparticle compositions, and related therapeutic applications.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Malaria remains a devastating global health concern, with Plasmodium falciparum infections causing hundreds of thousands of deaths annually. Current prophylactic treatments rely on oral administration of antimalarial drugs such as mefloquine or doxycycline, which require strict adherence to daily or weekly dosing regimens. Noncompliance with these regimens significantly diminishes their efficacy, leaving individuals vulnerable to infection.  

Existing antimalarial therapies primarily target the erythrocytic stage of the parasite lifecycle, offering suppressive rather than causal prophylaxis. While some drugs exhibit activity against liver-stage parasites, their pharmacokinetic profiles necessitate frequent dosing, limiting their practicality for long-term prevention. Additionally, emerging drug resistance further complicates treatment strategies.  

There exists a critical unmet need for a long-acting prophylactic formulation that ensures sustained therapeutic drug levels, minimizes dosing frequency, and provides reliable protection against both liver and blood-stage parasites. The present invention addresses these limitations through an intramuscularly administered atovaquone nanoformulation capable of conferring durable protection with a single injection.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention discloses the discovery of a long-acting injectable atovaquone formulation for malaria prophylaxis. Atovaquone, a hydroxynaphthoquinone compound, exhibits potent sub-nanomolar activity against Plasmodium species, including both liver-stage (causal) and erythrocytic (suppressive) parasites. The invention provides a sustained-release microparticle formulation that maintains therapeutic plasma concentrations for extended durations following a single intramuscular administration.  

Key aspects of the invention include a high-drug-load solid nanoparticle composition comprising atovaquone, polymeric stabilizers, and surfactants. The formulation demonstrates prolonged release kinetics, enabling prophylactic protection for at least four weeks in preclinical models. The invention further specifies dosage ranges, administration frequencies, and pharmacokinetic parameters necessary for efficacy.  

Additionally, the invention encompasses methods for preventing malaria in at-risk individuals by administering the disclosed formulation prior to potential exposure. The prophylactic effect is achieved through causal activity against liver-stage parasites, preventing the establishment of erythrocytic infection. The invention also describes combination therapies incorporating other antiparasitic agents to enhance efficacy or prevent resistance.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a comprehensive solution for malaria prophylaxis through an intramuscularly administered atovaquone formulation. The scope of the invention includes pharmaceutical compositions, methods of use, and manufacturing processes for the disclosed formulation.  

**Terminology**  
As used herein, "patient," "individual," or "subject" refers to a human or animal recipient of the disclosed formulation. "Preventing" or "prevention" denotes the inhibition of Plasmodium infection prior to clinical manifestation, particularly through action against liver-stage parasites. "Treatment" or "treating" refers to therapeutic intervention after infection establishment.  

**Atovaquone Composition**  
Atovaquone (2-[4-(4-chlorophenyl)cyclohexyl]-3-hydroxy-1,4-naphthoquinone) is a hydrophobic compound (log P = 4.7) with a plasma half-life conducive to sustained release. The invention utilizes high-purity atovaquone, optionally including isotopically labeled variants for pharmacokinetic studies.  

**Formulations**  
The invention encompasses both nanoparticle and non-nanoparticle formulations. A preferred embodiment comprises solid drug nanoparticles (SDNs) produced via emulsion-templated freeze-drying (ETFD), yielding particles with 70-80% drug loading. Stabilizing excipients include polymers (e.g., polyvinyl alcohol, hydroxymethyl propyl cellulose) and surfactants (e.g., α-tocopherol polyethylene glycol succinate, polysorbates).  

**Prophylactic Applications**  
The formulation provides causal prophylaxis against Plasmodium falciparum and other species by maintaining plasma concentrations >200 ng/mL. This threshold ensures complete protection against sporozoite challenge in preclinical models. The invention further contemplates use against other protozoal infections (e.g., Toxoplasma gondii).  

**Pharmaceutical Compositions**  
The invention describes injectable formulations comprising:  
- Atovaquone SDNs (20-80% w/w)  
- Pharmaceutically acceptable carriers (e.g., sterile water for injection)  
- Parenteral vehicles (e.g., poloxamers)  
- Surfactants (0.1-10% w/v)  

Dosage forms include unit-dose vials containing 50-500 mg atovaquone equivalents. Administration involves intramuscular injection (20-40 μL per site) using standard syringes.  

**Dosing Regimens**  
Effective prophylaxis requires:  
- Initial dose: 36-200 mg/kg (human equivalent dose)  
- Maintenance: Optional booster doses at 4-8 week intervals  
- Plasma monitoring: Ensure concentrations remain above 200 ng/mL during risk periods  

**Combination Therapies**  
The invention encompasses combination with:  
- Proguanil (for erythrocytic stage coverage)  
- Other antimalarials (e.g., artemisinins, quinolines)  
- Antibiotics with antiparasitic activity  

### Examples  

**Sporozoite Challenge Model**  
C57BL/6 mice received intramuscular ATQSDN7 (36-200 mg/kg) followed by intravenous P. berghei sporozoite challenge at varying intervals. Protection was assessed through 42-day blood smear monitoring. Results demonstrated complete prophylaxis when plasma atovaquone exceeded 200 ng/mL at challenge.  

**Liver Stage Breakthrough Testing**  
Paired challenges with sporozoites and erythrocytic parasites confirmed causal activity. While liver-stage challenges were uniformly prevented, blood-stage infections developed normally, indicating exclusive hepatic targeting.  

### Materials and Methods  

**SDN Preparation**  
Atovaquone SDNs were produced via ETFD using chloroform/water emulsions stabilized with polymer-surfactant combinations. Lyophilized monoliths were dispersed in aqueous media for characterization and dosing.  

**Pharmacokinetic Analysis**  
Plasma atovaquone levels were quantified via UPLC-MS/MS following sparse sampling protocols. Non-compartmental analysis yielded elimination half-lives of 105 hours (intramuscular) versus 9 hours (oral).  

**Efficacy Testing**  
The P. berghei ANKA-C57BL/6 mouse model provided stringent assessment of prophylactic duration. Protection correlated linearly with dose and time since administration.  

**Resistance Monitoring**  
Parasites from prophylaxis failures remained sensitive to oral atovaquone, confirming absence of resistance development under subtherapeutic conditions.
