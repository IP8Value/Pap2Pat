# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to methods for differentiating human pluripotent stem cells into functional hypothalamic neurons, particularly vasopressin (AVP)-producing neurons. More specifically, the invention provides a stepwise differentiation protocol involving precise regulation of bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) and sonic hedgehog (Shh) signaling pathways to generate dorsal and ventral hypothalamic precursor cells, followed by maturation into hormone-secreting neurons. The invention further encompasses methods for constructing integrated cellular structures containing both hypothalamic and pituitary tissues for research and therapeutic applications.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

The hypothalamus is a critical neuroendocrine structure that regulates fundamental physiological processes including body temperature, circadian rhythms, hunger, thirst, and stress responses through its control of pituitary hormone secretion. Hypothalamic dysfunction leads to severe disorders such as central diabetes insipidus (CDI), characterized by deficient antidiuretic hormone (AVP) production. Current hormone replacement therapies for CDI using desmopressin carry risks of hyponatremia and fail to address underlying hypothalamic pathology.  

Previous attempts to differentiate pluripotent stem cells into hypothalamic neurons have faced significant limitations. While some reports demonstrated hypothalamic neuron differentiation from human pluripotent stem cells, the efficiency remained low and functional hormone secretion was not achieved. Merkle et al. reported differentiation of AVP neurons with very low efficiency and without demonstration of AVP secretion. Existing protocols also lacked the ability to selectively generate dorsal versus ventral hypothalamic lineages or create integrated hypothalamic-pituitary tissue structures.  

The development of robust methods for generating functional hypothalamic tissue from human pluripotent stem cells would provide valuable tools for disease modeling, drug screening, and regenerative medicine. In particular, the ability to produce AVP-secreting neurons could enable cell replacement therapies for CDI while the generation of integrated hypothalamic-pituitary tissues could advance understanding of neuroendocrine regulation.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

Current methods for differentiating human pluripotent stem cells into hypothalamic neurons suffer from several limitations: (1) inefficient differentiation resulting in low yields of target cell types; (2) inability to selectively generate dorsal versus ventral hypothalamic lineages; (3) failure to demonstrate functional hormone secretion from differentiated neurons; and (4) lack of protocols for generating integrated hypothalamic-pituitary tissue structures that recapitulate physiological interactions.  

There exists a need for improved differentiation methods that can efficiently produce functional hypothalamic neurons, particularly AVP-producing neurons, from human pluripotent stem cells. Additionally, there is a need for methods to construct cellular structures containing both hypothalamic and pituitary tissues that maintain their functional interactions in vitro.  

### Solution to Problem  

The present invention provides a stepwise differentiation protocol that addresses these limitations through precise temporal control of developmental signaling pathways. The method involves:  

1. Formation of uniform aggregates of human pluripotent stem cells in serum-free medium containing knockout serum replacement (KSR) to maintain cell viability while allowing differentiation initiation.  

2. Addition of BMP4 signaling at specific concentrations (0.5-2.0 nM) between days 6-12 to induce rostral hypothalamic precursor cells expressing RAX.  

3. Concurrent or subsequent addition of Shh pathway activator (SAG) at 0.5-1.0 μM to ventralize precursors, with the relative concentrations of BMP4 and SAG determining dorsal versus ventral hypothalamic fate.  

4. Optional inclusion of Akt inhibitor (0.5 μM) from day 6 to enhance rostral hypothalamic identity and increase yield of AVP neuron precursors.  

5. Subsequent dissociation and plating of aggregates in two-dimensional culture with extracellular matrix components to promote neuronal maturation and hormone secretion capability.  

6. Demonstration of functional AVP secretion through radioimmunoassay measurement of hormone release in response to potassium chloride stimulation.  

7. Extension of the method to generate other hypothalamic neuron types (oxytocin, CRH, TRH, MCH, orexin, NPY, AgRP, POMC) by adjusting BMP4/SAG ratios and culture conditions.  

8. Construction of integrated hypothalamic-pituitary tissue structures through sequential application of BMP4, SAG, and FGF2 signals to induce both neural and surface ectoderm lineages within the same aggregate.  

The invention achieves efficient differentiation (over 80% RAX+ hypothalamic precursors) and demonstrates for the first time functional AVP secretion from stem cell-derived hypothalamic neurons. The protocol enables selective generation of dorsal or ventral hypothalamic lineages through precise modulation of BMP4 and SAG concentrations, providing unprecedented control over hypothalamic subtype specification.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

### 1. Method for Producing Cellular Structure Including Hypothalamic Tissue  

The hypothalamus develops from the rostral neural plate during embryogenesis, giving rise to distinct dorsal and ventral regions that generate different neuronal subtypes. The present invention provides methods for recapitulating this developmental process in vitro to produce hypothalamic tissue from human pluripotent stem cells.  

Human pluripotent stem cells suitable for the methods include embryonic stem cells (ESCs), embryonic germ cells (EG cells), and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). These cells are characterized by expression of pluripotency markers (OCT4, NANOG, SSEA-4) and the ability to differentiate into all three germ layers. iPSCs may be prepared by introducing reprogramming factors (OCT4, SOX2, KLF4, c-MYC) into somatic cells using viral or non-viral methods.  

For differentiation initiation, human pluripotent stem cells are dissociated to single cells using TrypLE Express containing DNase I and Y-27632 (10 mM) to prevent apoptosis. Cells are reaggregated in low-cell-adhesion 96-well V-bottom plates at 10,000 cells/well in growth factor-free chemically defined medium (gfCDM) supplemented with 5% KSR, non-essential amino acids, pyruvate, and 2-mercaptoethanol. The Y-27632 concentration is gradually reduced through medium changes every other day.  

At day 6, differentiation is directed toward hypothalamic fate by adding BMP4 (0.5-2.0 nM final concentration) and SAG (0.2-2.0 μM final concentration). BMP4 activates the bone morphogenetic protein signaling pathway through phosphorylation of SMAD1/5/8, while SAG activates the Shh pathway by binding to Smoothened. The combination of these signals induces RAX+ hypothalamic precursors with over 80% efficiency by day 20.  

The relative concentrations of BMP4 and SAG determine dorsal versus ventral hypothalamic fate. For dorsal hypothalamus (PAX6+/NKX2.1-), optimal conditions are 0.5-1.0 nM BMP4 with 0.5 μM SAG. For ventral hypothalamus (NKX2.1+/PAX6-), optimal conditions are 1.0-1.5 nM BMP4 with 1.0 μM SAG. Addition of Akt inhibitor (0.5 μM) from day 6 enhances rostral hypothalamic identity without affecting RAX or PAX6 expression levels.  

Aggregates are maintained in suspension culture until day 60-80, with medium changes every other day. From day 60, aggregates may be transferred to Transwell filters for gas-liquid interface culture in DFNB medium supplemented with ciliary neurotrophic factor (CNTF) to promote neuronal maturation.  

For optimal AVP neuron differentiation, aggregates are dissociated at day 60-80 using Accumax and plated on Matrigel/poly-D-lysine/laminin-coated coverslips in DFNB medium with 10% FBS, BDNF, NT-3, and LM22A-4. Under these conditions, AVP neurons appear by day 80-150, demonstrating characteristic magnocellular morphology (large soma size 40×20 μm) and immunoreactivity for AVP, neurophysin II, and copeptin.  

Functional maturation is confirmed by measuring AVP secretion via radioimmunoassay. Differentiated neurons show basal AVP secretion (0.4-2.0 pg/ml) that increases 2-3 fold upon stimulation with high potassium (100 mM KCl) for 30 minutes, demonstrating physiological responsiveness.  

The method can be adapted to produce other hypothalamic neuron types by adjusting BMP4/SAG ratios and culture conditions. For example, MCH neurons are preferentially generated from ventral hypothalamic conditions (1.5 nM BMP4/1.0 μM SAG), while CRH and TRH neurons arise under intermediate conditions.  

### EXAMPLES  

Example 1: Differentiation of dorsal hypothalamic neurons  
Human ESCs (KhES-1 line) were aggregated in gfCDM+5% KSR. At day 6, 0.5 nM BMP4 and 0.5 μM SAG were added. By day 30, aggregates contained >70% RAX+/PAX6+ dorsal hypothalamic precursors. Dissociation and plating at day 60 yielded AVP+ neurons by day 120, with 30-50 AVP+ cells per 1000 nuclei. Radioimmunoassay confirmed KCl-stimulated AVP secretion.  

Example 2: Ventral hypothalamic differentiation  
Using 1.5 nM BMP4 and 1.0 μM SAG from day 6, day 30 aggregates contained 65% RAX+/NKX2.1+ ventral precursors. Dissociation culture yielded MCH+ neurons that showed increased pro-MCH mRNA expression upon stimulation with NGF, dexamethasone and LiCl.  

Example 3: Integrated hypothalamic-pituitary structure  
Sequential treatment with BMP4 (1.0 nM days 6-12), SAG (0.5 μM days 6-30), and FGF2 (20 ng/ml days 18-30) generated aggregates containing both RAX+ hypothalamic tissue and E-cadherin+/pan-cytokeratin+ surface ectoderm. Further culture in serum-free medium promoted organization into structures resembling the hypothalamic-pituitary axis.  

### 2. Simultaneous Maturation of Hypothalamus and Pituitary  

The method was extended to generate integrated hypothalamic-pituitary tissues by modifying signaling conditions. Treatment with BMP4 (1.0 nM) and SAG (0.5 μM) from day 6 induced both RAX+ hypothalamic neuroepithelium and E-cadherin+ surface ectoderm within the same aggregate. Subsequent addition of FGF2 (20 ng/ml) from day 18 promoted pituitary lineage specification from the surface ectoderm component.  

Immunofluorescence analysis at day 60 revealed adjacent regions of CRH+ hypothalamic neurons and ACTH+ pituitary cells, demonstrating successful generation of functionally related tissues. Stimulation tests showed coordinated hormone release responses between the two tissue types, confirming physiological relevance of the integrated structure.  

### 3. Conclusion  

The present invention provides the first demonstration of functional AVP secretion from stem cell-derived hypothalamic neurons, representing a significant advance over previous differentiation methods. Key innovations include:  

1. Precise control of BMP4 and SAG concentrations to selectively generate dorsal or ventral hypothalamic lineages  
2. Use of Akt inhibitor to enhance rostral hypothalamic identity  
3. Optimization of dissociation culture conditions for neuronal maturation and hormone secretion  
4. Extension to generate integrated hypothalamic-pituitary tissue structures  

These methods enable production of hypothalamic neurons for disease modeling, drug screening, and potential cell replacement therapies for neuroendocrine disorders.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The cellular structures and differentiation methods described herein have broad applications in:  

1. Regenerative medicine: Transplantation of AVP neurons for treatment of central diabetes insipidus or other hypothalamic disorders. Autologous iPSC-derived neurons could provide personalized therapy without immune rejection.  

2. Drug development: Screening platforms for identifying compounds that modulate hypothalamic hormone secretion or treat neuroendocrine disorders. The system enables testing of drug efficacy and toxicity on human hypothalamic tissue.  

3. Disease modeling: In vitro models of hypothalamic disorders using patient-derived iPSCs to study disease mechanisms and test therapeutic interventions.  

4. Basic research: Tools for studying human hypothalamic development and physiology that overcome limitations of animal models.  

The invention represents a significant advancement in stem cell differentiation technology with wide-ranging applications in biomedicine and pharmaceutical development.
