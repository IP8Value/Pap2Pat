## DESCRIPTION  

### FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of catalytic conversion processes, specifically the conversion of alcohols, particularly bio-alcohols such as ethanol, into hydrocarbon products suitable for use as fuels or blendstocks. More particularly, the invention pertains to a novel catalytic system and process for converting fermentation-derived ethanol streams directly into hydrocarbon fractions without the need for extensive purification or dewatering.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Current methods for converting bio-alcohols to hydrocarbons face significant limitations, particularly in terms of catalyst efficiency and process economics. Conventional processes often require highly purified alcohol feeds, which necessitate costly and energy-intensive dewatering steps. Additionally, existing catalytic systems suffer from rapid deactivation due to coking, limiting their operational lifespan and requiring frequent regeneration.  

A critical obstacle in the field is the high water concentration present in fermentation-derived alcohol streams. Traditional catalysts are often sensitive to water, leading to reduced activity or selectivity when processing aqueous alcohol feeds. Furthermore, many hydrocarbon products derived from alcohol conversion contain undesirable levels of benzene, which poses regulatory and environmental challenges for fuel applications.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a catalytic conversion method that overcomes the limitations of prior art systems. The method utilizes a novel metal-loaded zeolite catalyst system that enables efficient conversion of alcohol streams, including those with significant water content, directly into hydrocarbon products.  

Key aspects of the invention include the ability to process fermentation streams without prior purification, significantly reducing processing costs. The catalytic system comprises a metal-loaded zeolite catalyst, particularly a bimetallic zeolite such as InV-ZSM-5, which demonstrates superior activity and durability compared to monometallic counterparts.  

The invention further incorporates a benzene alkylation catalyst that effectively reduces benzene content in the final hydrocarbon product by converting it to higher alkylated aromatics. The process can be implemented in various reactor configurations, including both two-stage and single-stage embodiments, operating in either continuous or batch modes.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The term "about" as used herein refers to numerical values that may vary by ±10% while still maintaining the functional characteristics described.  

For purposes of this invention, "alcohol" refers to any hydroxyl-containing organic compound, with particular emphasis on bio-alcohols such as ethanol, propanol, and butanol derived from fermentation processes. The invention is particularly effective with ethanol streams obtained from biomass fermentation, though other alcohols including methanol, isopropanol, and higher alcohols may also be processed.  

Fermentation streams suitable for processing typically contain alcohol concentrations ranging from about 5% to 95% by weight, with water comprising the majority of the balance. These streams may also contain minor amounts of fermentation byproducts such as acetaldehyde, methanol, diethyl ether, and organic acids, typically totaling less than 1% by weight.  

The process can accommodate fermentation streams at various stages of concentration, from dilute fermentation broths to nearly anhydrous alcohol. While conventional processes require extensive dewatering, the present invention can effectively process streams containing up to about 60% water by weight without significant loss of catalytic activity or product quality.  

The term "hydrocarbon" as used herein refers to organic compounds consisting exclusively of hydrogen and carbon atoms. The conversion process produces a range of hydrocarbon products including saturated hydrocarbons (alkanes), branched alkanes, olefins, and aromatic compounds. The hydrocarbon product typically contains molecules ranging from C2 to C20+, with the majority in the C3-C12 range suitable for fuel applications.  

The hydrocarbon product finds particular utility as a fuel or fuel blendstock, exhibiting properties comparable to conventional gasoline. The liquid fraction typically has an average molecular weight between about 90-110, specific gravity between 0.78-0.82, and research octane numbers exceeding 100. The product composition generally comprises 3-8% paraffins, 20-28% iso-paraffins, 5-8% olefins, 5-7% naphthenes, and 55-65% aromatics by volume.  

The versatility of the process allows for tuning of the hydrocarbon product distribution to meet specific fuel requirements. By adjusting process parameters such as temperature, pressure, and catalyst composition, the relative proportions of light gases, gasoline-range hydrocarbons, and heavier fractions can be controlled.  

The metal-loaded zeolite catalyst represents a critical component of the invention. In preferred embodiments, the catalyst comprises a zeolite framework loaded with at least two different metal species, particularly combinations of indium and vanadium. The zeolite portion of the catalyst typically has a silica-to-alumina ratio between about 20:1 to 50:1, with ZSM-5 being a particularly effective framework.  

The metal components are incorporated into the zeolite structure through ion exchange or impregnation methods, typically comprising between 0.1-5% by weight of the total catalyst. The metals may exist as cationic species within the zeolite channels or as small oxide clusters. In preferred bimetallic embodiments, the metals demonstrate synergistic interactions that enhance both activity and stability.  

The benzene alkylation catalyst serves to convert benzene present in the hydrocarbon product into higher alkylated aromatics such as toluene, xylene, and ethylbenzene. Suitable alkylation catalysts include acidic zeolites (e.g., H-ZSM-5, H-Beta) and Friedel-Crafts type catalysts. The alkylation catalyst may be physically mixed with the conversion catalyst or arranged in a separate reaction zone.  

The process may be conducted in a two-stage configuration where alcohol conversion occurs primarily in a first stage and benzene alkylation occurs in a subsequent stage. Alternatively, a single-stage process may employ a physical mixture of conversion and alkylation catalysts. Reaction temperatures typically range between about 250-450°C, with pressures ranging from atmospheric to about 500 psi.  

The reactor design may incorporate fixed beds, fluidized beds, or other conventional configurations, operating in either continuous or batch modes. Additional reaction zones or processes may be integrated for product fractionation or further upgrading.  

The process is particularly advantageous when integrated with biomass fermentation facilities, allowing direct processing of fermentation streams without intermediate purification. The biomass source may include corn, sugarcane, cellulosic materials, or other sugar-containing feedstocks.  

The zeolite catalyst may be prepared through various methods including ion exchange, impregnation, or solid-state reactions. A typical preparation involves contacting the zeolite with metal salt solutions, followed by drying and calcination at temperatures between 400-600°C. The resulting catalyst may be used in various physical forms including powders, pellets, extrudates, or structured configurations such as honeycombs or foams.  

The invention encompasses various catalyst composition variations, including different metal combinations (e.g., Cu-Fe-ZSM-5, Ga-V-ZSM-5), zeolite frameworks (e.g., ZSM-11, MCM-22), and preparation methods. The catalyst may also be supported on various refractory materials or structured substrates to enhance mechanical stability or heat transfer properties.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

A mixed catalyst comprising InV-ZSM-5 and H-ZSM-5 in a 3:1 weight ratio was prepared. The InV-ZSM-5 component contained 0.6 wt% vanadium and 1.6 wt% indium, prepared by sequential ion exchange of NH4-ZSM-5 with vanadium chloride and indium nitrate solutions followed by calcination at 500°C.  

The catalyst system was loaded into a fixed-bed reactor and tested with a feed comprising 40% ethanol in water at 350°C and atmospheric pressure. The product stream was analyzed by gas chromatography and mass spectrometry, showing complete ethanol conversion with a hydrocarbon yield of 60.9%. The liquid hydrocarbon fraction exhibited a research octane number of 105.7 and contained less than 1% benzene by volume.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

The catalyst loading was configured with separate beds of InV-ZSM-5 (upper bed) and H-ZSM-5 (lower bed) in a 2:1 weight ratio. The system was tested with pure ethanol feed at varying temperatures from 250-450°C.  

Analysis of the product stream showed temperature-dependent selectivity, with C3+ hydrocarbon yields exceeding 80% at 300-400°C. Carbon atom distribution analysis revealed a maximum in C5-C8 hydrocarbons at 350°C, with the liquid fraction comprising 62% aromatics by volume. The benzene content in the product was reduced to 0.5% through effective alkylation.
