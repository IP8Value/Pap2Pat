# DESCRIPTION

The present application incorporates by reference in their entirety all prior applications assigned to the assignee of the present invention relating to peptidomimetic compounds and their use in cancer treatment. 

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to novel peptidomimetic compounds that bind to the human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) protein and inhibit protein-protein interactions between HER2 and other epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFRs) in HER2-overexpressed cancers. More particularly, the invention provides cyclic peptidomimetics that specifically target domain IV of HER2, inhibit heterodimerization of HER2 with other EGFR family members, and demonstrate potent antiproliferative activity against HER2-positive cancer cell lines while showing minimal effects on normal cells. The compounds of the invention offer advantages over existing monoclonal antibody therapies and tyrosine kinase inhibitors in terms of stability, specificity, and mechanism of action.

## BACKGROUND ART

Epidermal growth factor receptors (EGFRs) play a critical role in regulating cell growth and differentiation under normal physiological conditions. The human EGFR family comprises four homologous receptor tyrosine kinases: HER1 (EGFR), HER2, HER3, and HER4. These receptors function through ligand-induced dimerization, where binding of a ligand to the extracellular domain induces conformational changes that activate intracellular kinase domains, initiating signaling cascades that regulate cellular processes. Dysregulation of EGFR signaling, particularly through overexpression of receptors or aberrant dimerization, has been strongly implicated in the development and progression of various cancers, including breast, lung, and ovarian cancers.

Among the EGFR family members, HER2 (also known as ErbB2 or neu) is particularly significant in cancer biology. HER2 lacks a known ligand but readily forms heterodimers with other ligand-bound EGFR family members, making it a key player in oncogenic signaling. HER2 overexpression occurs in approximately 20-30% of breast cancers and is associated with aggressive tumor behavior and poor prognosis. Similar patterns of HER2 overexpression and poor outcomes have been observed in subsets of gastric, ovarian, and lung cancers.

Current therapeutic approaches targeting HER2-positive cancers primarily involve monoclonal antibodies (such as trastuzumab and pertuzumab) or small molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs). Trastuzumab binds to domain IV of HER2's extracellular region, while pertuzumab targets domain II. These antibodies have demonstrated clinical efficacy but suffer from several limitations, including immunogenicity, poor tissue penetration, high production costs, and the development of resistance. TKIs, while effective in some cases, often lead to acquired resistance through mutations in the kinase domain, particularly the T790M mutation in EGFR.

The development of peptidomimetics as alternatives to antibodies and TKIs has gained attention due to their potential for improved stability, better tissue penetration, and lower immunogenicity. Peptidomimetics are designed to mimic the structure and function of natural peptides while incorporating modifications to enhance stability and bioavailability. Previous attempts to develop peptidomimetic inhibitors of EGFR interactions have focused on linear peptides targeting specific protein-protein interaction interfaces. However, these linear peptides are susceptible to rapid enzymatic degradation in vivo, limiting their therapeutic potential.

Several studies have explored peptides targeting HER2, including cyclic peptides incorporating D-Pro and L-Pro sequences known to induce β-turn structures. While these have shown some promise, none have demonstrated the combination of high specificity for HER2 among EGFR family members, effective inhibition of HER2 heterodimerization, and potent antiproliferative activity observed with the compounds of the present invention. There remains an unmet need for stable, specific, and potent peptidomimetic inhibitors of HER2-mediated signaling that can overcome the limitations of current therapeutic approaches.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel cyclic peptidomimetic compounds that specifically bind to domain IV of HER2 and inhibit protein-protein interactions between HER2 and other EGFR family members, particularly HER2-HER3 and EGFR-HER2 heterodimers. These compounds demonstrate potent antiproliferative activity against HER2-overexpressing cancer cell lines while showing minimal effects on normal cells, representing a significant advance in targeted cancer therapy.

A key aspect of the invention is Compound 18, a symmetric cyclic peptidomimetic incorporating a β-naphthyl moiety and a critical L-Pro-D-Pro sequence that induces a β-turn/β-hairpin structure in solution. This structural configuration positions functional groups optimally for binding to HER2's extracellular domain while providing exceptional stability against enzymatic degradation. Compound 18 exhibits nanomolar IC50 values against HER2-positive cancer cell lines (194 nM in SKBR-3 breast cancer cells and 18 nM in Calu-3 lung cancer cells), with significantly reduced activity (>50 μM) in HER2-negative cell lines and normal breast epithelial cells (40 μM in MCF-10A).

The invention demonstrates that the biological activity of these peptidomimetics depends critically on the chirality of both the Pro-Pro sequence and the β-amino acids in the structure. Changing this chirality results in dramatic reductions in antiproliferative activity, indicating a chiral switch mechanism. Compound 18 binds specifically to domain IV of HER2, as confirmed by surface plasmon resonance studies showing a Kd of 0.125 μM, consistent with its cellular IC50 values. This binding inhibits phosphorylation of HER2's kinase domain and subsequent downstream signaling.

In xenograft models of breast cancer, Compound 18 significantly suppresses tumor growth compared to controls, demonstrating in vivo efficacy. The compound shows particular promise in combination therapy, exhibiting synergistic effects with erlotinib in EGFR-mutated lung cancer cell lines (NCI-H1975). Unlike linear peptidomimetics, Compound 18 shows remarkable stability in serum, maintaining integrity for at least 48 hours.

The invention encompasses not only Compound 18 itself but also its stereoisomers, derivatives, and pharmaceutically acceptable salts. Additional embodiments include pharmaceutical compositions containing these compounds, methods of treating HER2-positive cancers by administering effective amounts of the compounds, and methods of inhibiting HER2 heterodimerization in cancer cells. The compounds may be formulated for various administration routes, including oral, parenteral, and topical delivery.

### Definitions/General Discussion

For purposes of interpreting this specification, the following definitions apply unless otherwise specified:

The term "peptidomimetic" refers to a small protein-like chain designed to mimic natural peptides while incorporating modifications to enhance stability or biological activity. In the context of this invention, peptidomimetics are cyclic structures incorporating both natural and non-natural amino acid components.

"Stereoisomers" includes all possible isomeric forms of the compounds, including enantiomers, diastereomers, and geometric isomers. The invention specifically recognizes that the biological activity of these compounds depends critically on stereochemistry, particularly the configuration of the Pro-Pro sequence and β-amino acids.

"Pharmaceutically acceptable salts" includes acid addition salts formed with inorganic or organic acids, as well as salts formed with bases when acidic groups are present in the compound.

"Treatment" refers to both therapeutic intervention in established disease and prophylactic measures to prevent disease onset or recurrence. In the context of cancer, treatment includes reducing tumor growth, inhibiting metastasis, alleviating symptoms, and improving quality of life.

"Effective amount" means a quantity sufficient to achieve a desired therapeutic or prophylactic effect, such as inhibiting cancer cell proliferation or HER2 heterodimerization, without causing unacceptable toxicity.

The compounds of the invention may be formulated with various pharmaceutically acceptable excipients for different routes of administration. Oral formulations may include tablets, capsules, or liquids containing appropriate fillers, binders, and disintegrants. Parenteral formulations may include sterile solutions or suspensions for intravenous, intramuscular, or subcutaneous injection. Topical formulations may include creams, ointments, or patches for transdermal delivery.

Dosage ranges will vary depending on factors including the specific compound used, route of administration, patient characteristics, and disease severity. Typical daily doses may range from 0.1 mg/kg to 100 mg/kg body weight, administered in single or divided doses. For Compound 18, effective doses in xenograft models were approximately 4 mg/kg administered twice weekly.

The invention further provides methods for determining appropriate dosing regimens through established pharmacological techniques, including measurement of plasma concentrations, monitoring of therapeutic effects, and assessment of toxicity. Dose escalation studies may be employed to establish maximum tolerated doses and optimal therapeutic windows.

Combination therapies are specifically contemplated, particularly combinations with existing HER2-targeted agents (such as trastuzumab or pertuzumab) or tyrosine kinase inhibitors (such as lapatinib or erlotinib). The compounds may be administered simultaneously, separately, or sequentially with other therapeutic agents.

### Example 1. Compound 18: Synthesis and Characterization

Compound 18 was synthesized using standard solid-phase Fmoc chemistry on 2-chlorotrityl chloride resin (3 g, 0.89 mmol/g). The resin was swollen in dichloromethane for 30 minutes before loading with Fmoc-Pro-OH (900 mg, 2.67 mmol) and DIEA (2.325 mL, 13.35 mmol) in dichloromethane. After agitation for 2 hours, the resin was washed sequentially with NMP, DCM/MeOH/DIEA (80:15:5), and dichloromethane before drying under vacuum. The substitution level was determined by UV absorption of the Fmoc-piperidine adduct.

Following deprotection with 20% piperidine/NMP, the remaining peptide sequence was assembled using an automatic peptide synthesizer with H-Pro-CTC resin (91 mg, 0.55 mmol/g, 50 μmol). Five equivalents each of Fmoc-protected amino acids and HCTU were used for each coupling, dissolved in 0.4 M 4-methylmorpholine in NMP. After completion of the sequence and final Fmoc removal, the peptide was cleaved from the resin using TFE/DCM (1:1) and cyclized in THF/DMF (4:1) with PyAOP (4 eq) and DIEA (7 eq). Side chain deprotection was achieved with TFA/water/TIPS (95:2.5:2.5), followed by precipitation with cold diethyl ether.

The crude product was purified by HPLC and characterized by mass spectrometry (calculated mass 1425.5893, observed m/z 1425.699) with >95% purity. LC-MS analysis confirmed the molecular weight and purity of the product. 1H 2D-NMR studies (Bruker AVIII 500 MHz and Varian VNMRS 700 MHz spectrometers) in DMSO-d6 revealed characteristic peaks consistent with the proposed structure. TOCSY and NOESY spectra confirmed the spatial arrangement of protons and allowed determination of the compound's solution conformation.

The structure features a β-naphthyl moiety critical for hydrophobic interactions with HER2 and an L-Pro-D-Pro sequence inducing a β-turn conformation that positions functional groups optimally for receptor binding. Molecular modeling studies suggest this conformation allows simultaneous interaction with key residues in HER2's domain IV, particularly Pro543, Phe573, Pro584, and Pro590.

### Example 2. Inhibition Testing

The antiproliferative activity of Compound 18 was evaluated against various cancer cell lines using CellTiter-Glo® luminescence assays. Cells (1 × 10^4/well) were seeded in 96-well plates and incubated overnight before treatment with serial dilutions of Compound 18 (0.005-50 μM) for 72 hours. Luminescence was measured after adding CellTiter-Glo® reagent, and IC50 values were calculated using Prism® software.

Compound 18 showed potent activity against HER2-overexpressing cell lines with IC50 values of 194 nM in SKBR-3 (breast cancer), 18 nM in Calu-3 (lung cancer), and 210 nM in BT-474 (breast cancer). In contrast, activity was significantly reduced in HER2-negative MCF-7 breast cancer cells (IC50 >50 μM) and normal MCF-10A breast epithelial cells (IC50 40 μM), demonstrating exceptional specificity for HER2-positive cancers.

The importance of stereochemistry was evident when testing analogs with altered chirality. Compounds with the L-Pro-D-Pro sequence and (R) configuration at the β-amino acid position showed nanomolar activity, while other stereoisomers exhibited IC50 values >5 μM, confirming the chiral switch phenomenon.

### Example 3. Competitive Binding Assays

Competitive binding studies using FITC-labeled compound 5 (known HER2 binder) demonstrated that Compound 18 specifically binds HER2 on BT-474 cells. As Compound 18 concentration increased (0.1-50 μM), fluorescence from FITC-compound 5 decreased proportionally, indicating competitive binding at the same HER2 site.

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis with immobilized HER2 extracellular domain (ECD) and domain IV alone confirmed specific binding to domain IV with a Kd of 0.125 μM (kon = 4.76 × 10^7 M^-1 s^-1, koff = 5.97 s^-1). No binding was observed to EGFR, HER3, or HER4 ECDs, demonstrating exceptional specificity for HER2. Control compounds showed no SPR response, while pertuzumab (positive control) bound as expected.

### Example 4. Testing Compound 18 Analogs for PPI Inhibition

Analog compounds were tested for their ability to inhibit HER2 heterodimerization using proximity ligation assays (PLA) in SKBR-3 cells. While Compound 18 significantly reduced HER2:HER3 and HER2:EGFR dimerization (evidenced by decreased red fluorescence spots), analogs with altered chirality showed markedly reduced activity. This confirms the structural specificity required for effective protein-protein interaction inhibition.

Enzyme fragment complementation (PathHunter®) assays in HER2-HER3-transfected U2OS cells further demonstrated that Compound 18 inhibits NRG-1-induced heterodimerization in a concentration-dependent manner, while inactive analogs showed no inhibition. These results correlate with the antiproliferative activity data, confirming the structure-activity relationship.

### Example 5. Dose-Response Curves for Heterodimerization

Detailed dose-response studies using the PathHunter® assay quantified Compound 18's inhibition of HER2-HER3 heterodimerization. In HER2-HER3-transfected U2OS cells stimulated with neuregulin-1 (NRG1), Compound 18 showed concentration-dependent inhibition of dimerization with an IC50 of approximately 0.5 μM. Analog 21 (a linear derivative) showed reduced potency (IC50 ~2 μM), while control compounds exhibited no significant inhibition up to 50 μM.

The assay measures β-galactosidase activity resulting from HER2-HER3 dimerization, where the kinase domain phosphorylation allows complementation of enzyme fragments. Compound 18's inhibition of this process confirms its mechanism of action at the extracellular domain level, distinct from tyrosine kinase inhibitors that target intracellular domains.

These examples demonstrate the invention's novel cyclic peptidomimetics that specifically target HER2 domain IV, inhibit critical protein-protein interactions in oncogenic signaling, and show potent antiproliferative activity against HER2-positive cancers with minimal effects on normal cells. The chiral specificity, stability, and mechanism of action represent significant advantages over existing therapies.
