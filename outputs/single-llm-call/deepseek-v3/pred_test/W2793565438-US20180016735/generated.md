## DESCRIPTION  

### FIELD  

The present invention relates to systems for and methods of treating cotton yarn to enhance its mechanical properties. More particularly, the invention concerns a novel cold alkaline aqueous solvent treatment process that partially dissolves cotton cellulose to form an adhesive gel matrix, which when combined with lignocellulosic reinforcement materials produces improved composite materials. The invention provides a plug-in technology step that can be integrated into existing commercial cotton yarn production lines to create enhanced textile products with superior strength characteristics while maintaining environmental sustainability.  

### BACKGROUND  

The textile industry represents one of the largest manufacturing sectors globally, with cotton production forming a substantial portion of this economic activity. Worldwide cotton production exceeds 25 million tons annually, occupying approximately 2.5% of the world's arable land. As demand for premium quality cotton fibers continues to grow alongside population expansion and rising living standards, there exists an ongoing need for improved processing methods that can enhance the mechanical properties of cotton yarn without compromising its natural advantages.  

Cotton fibers consist primarily of cellulose, a linear polymer of β(1-4)-linked D-glucose units that provides the structural framework of plant cell walls. The crystalline structure of cellulose contributes to cotton's strength, while its amorphous regions provide flexibility. Traditional methods for improving cotton fiber properties include the mercerization process, which involves treating cotton with concentrated alkaline solutions under tension to increase luster, strength, and affinity for dyes. However, conventional mercerization has limitations in the degree of property enhancement it can achieve.  

Recent studies have explored cold alkaline aqueous solvent treatments as alternative approaches for modifying cellulose structure. These methods employ solutions such as sodium hydroxide/urea (≈7% NaOH/12% urea) or lithium hydroxide hydrate/urea (4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea) at temperatures ranging from -5°C to -15°C. While these treatments have shown promise in dissolving cellulose for creating all-cellulose composites (ACCs), they typically aim for complete dissolution of the cellulose component. Previous research has focused primarily on creating ACCs composed of nearly 100% cellulose, which while demonstrating good mechanical properties, fail to leverage the potential benefits of incorporating other lignocellulosic materials.  

Existing methods face several limitations, including high material costs when using pure cellulose matrices, incomplete utilization of abundant agricultural byproducts, and insufficient mechanical property enhancement for certain applications. There remains a significant need for improved methods that can combine the advantages of cellulose dissolution with the reinforcement potential of other lignocellulosic materials to create superior composite materials at reduced costs. The present invention addresses these needs by providing a novel approach that partially dissolves cotton cellulose to create an adhesive gel matrix capable of binding with various reinforcement materials, resulting in composites with enhanced mechanical properties while maintaining environmental sustainability.

This approach represents a significant advancement over previous methods by combining the benefits of cellulose dissolution with the reinforcement potential of other abundant biomass materials.

The method employs specific cold alkaline solvent formulations, including lithium hydroxide hydrate/urea solutions at precisely controlled temperatures, to achieve optimal partial dissolution of cotton cellulose. This creates a viscous gel matrix that effectively binds with reinforcement materials such as osage orange wood particles and agave fibers, resulting in composite materials with enhanced mechanical properties.  

The invention encompasses multiple embodiments, including specific solvent compositions, treatment temperatures, matrix-to-reinforcement ratios, and processing parameters that have been optimized to produce materials with desired characteristics. Compared to conventional materials, the invention provides superior mechanical properties while utilizing low-cost, abundant raw materials and environmentally benign processing methods.  

Primary objects and advantages of the present invention include: providing an improved method for enhancing cotton yarn mechanical properties; creating composite materials from abundant, low-cost biomass sources; eliminating the need for synthetic adhesives in composite formation; producing fully biodegradable textile materials; and offering a scalable process that can be integrated into existing production systems. The invention achieves these advantages through its novel combination of partial cellulose dissolution with lignocellulosic reinforcement, resulting in materials that outperform conventional alternatives in multiple mechanical property metrics while maintaining environmental sustainability.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a novel method for treating cotton yarn to enhance its mechanical properties through a unique cold alkaline aqueous solvent treatment process. At its core, the invention involves partially dissolving cotton cellulose to form an adhesive gel matrix that binds with various lignocellulosic reinforcement materials, creating composite structures with superior strength characteristics. This approach differs fundamentally from previous methods by intentionally avoiding complete cellulose dissolution, instead creating a partially dissolved gel state that optimally binds reinforcement materials while maintaining the structural benefits of undissolved cellulose components.  

Several embodiments of the invention have been developed and optimized, each offering specific advantages depending on application requirements. In a preferred embodiment, the method employs a cold aqueous alkaline solvent consisting of 4.6% lithium hydroxide hydrate (LiOH·H₂O) and 15% urea in water, cooled to between -12.5°C and -15°C. This specific formulation and temperature range have been found to produce an ideal viscous gel state from cotton cellulose that maximizes binding with reinforcement materials while preserving sufficient undissolved cellulose structure for mechanical integrity.  

The cotton yarn treatment process involves multiple precisely controlled steps. First, the cotton material is immersed in the precooled alkaline solvent and stirred for approximately 15 minutes using a mixer fitted with a three-blade propeller at 350 rpm. This results in partial dissolution of the cotton cellulose, generating a white translucent cellulose gel with optimal adhesive properties. Reinforcement materials such as osage orange wood particles and agave fibers are then added to the vessel and stirred for an additional 10 minutes at the same speed, ensuring thorough mixing and binding with the cellulose gel matrix.  

The resultant composite is transferred to molding systems where it undergoes solvent-water exchange treatments. These treatments consist of submersion in tap water under vacuum for 15 minutes followed by continuous submersion in water for 24 hours with periodic water replacements. After initial soaking, the composite panels become sufficiently firm for mold removal, facilitating greater diffusion of solutes into the water. Following water treatment, panels are damp dried and then placed between steel plates under 0.5 psi (0.0034 MPa) pressure for drying in a vacuum oven at 60°C and 25 inches Hg for 24 hours. A final densification step subjects the panels to 8 tons pressure for 10 minutes at 180°C, completing the composite formation process.  

The system for treating cotton yarn according to the invention includes several key components: a temperature-controlled mixing vessel for solvent preparation and cellulose treatment; precision mixing equipment with controlled rpm settings; molding systems with specific dimensional configurations; water exchange tanks with vacuum capability; drying systems with controlled temperature and pressure parameters; and densification equipment capable of applying precise pressure and heat levels. This integrated system allows for reproducible production of composite materials with consistently enhanced mechanical properties.  

Examples of treated cotton yarn composites demonstrate the effectiveness of the invention. Panels produced with 25% cotton matrix and 75% reinforcement materials (osage orange wood and agave fiber combinations) exhibit tensile strength (σu), elastic modulus (E), flexural strength (σfm), and flexural modulus (Eb) values that are 108%, 151%, 183% and 203% higher respectively than control materials processed at higher temperatures (-5°C). Optimal results are achieved with solvent volumes of 60 ml per panel, reaction temperatures of -12.5°C, and cotton matrix concentrations between 30-35%. The lithium hydroxide hydrate/urea solvent formulation proves superior to sodium hydroxide-based alternatives, producing composites with significantly enhanced mechanical properties.  

The invention represents a significant advance in cotton yarn treatment technology by providing a method that enhances mechanical properties while utilizing low-cost, abundant materials and environmentally benign processes. Unlike conventional composites that require synthetic adhesives, the invention creates self-binding materials through partial cellulose dissolution, resulting in fully biodegradable products with superior performance characteristics. The detailed description of embodiments, processing parameters, and system components provided herein enables consistent reproduction of the invention's benefits across various applications in the textile and composite materials industries.  

### Examples  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention and demonstrate its effectiveness in enhancing cotton yarn properties through cold alkaline aqueous solvent treatment. These examples provide detailed procedures, material specifications, and test results that collectively establish the operational parameters and performance characteristics of the invention.  

Materials used in the examples include cotton linters as the cellulose source, osage orange wood (OOW) particles, and blue agave fibers (AF) as reinforcement materials. The alkaline solvent formulations tested include: 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea, 7% NaOH/12% urea, 9.8% KOH/12% urea, and several variations of these basic formulations. All solvents were prepared with distilled water and cooled to specified temperatures before use.  

The treatment process for each example followed a standardized protocol. Cotton material was immersed in the precooled solvent and stirred for 15 minutes at 350 rpm using a mixer with three-blade propeller. Reinforcement materials were then added and stirred for an additional 10 minutes. The resulting composite was transferred to polyethylene foam molds (105 mm W × 130 mm L × 5 mm D) sandwiched between polyethylene meshes. Solvent-water exchange treatments involved submersion in tap water under vacuum for 15 minutes followed by continuous submersion with six water replacements over 24 hours. After initial soaking, composites were removed from molds, damp dried, and pressed between steel plates at 0.5 psi while drying in a vacuum oven at 60°C for 24 hours. Final densification applied 8 tons pressure for 10 minutes at 180°C.  

Mechanical property testing evaluated tensile strength (σu), elastic modulus (E), flexural strength (σfm), and flexural modulus (Eb) according to ASTM standards. Testing compared composites prepared under different conditions to establish optimal parameters. Results showed that reaction temperature significantly affected mechanical properties, with -12.5°C producing optimal results. For example, composites prepared at -12.5°C exhibited mechanical property values 108-203% higher than those prepared at -5°C.  

Optical examination of treated composites revealed effective binding between matrix and reinforcement materials. Surface analysis showed relatively smooth surfaces with cotton matrix coating reinforcement fibers, while cross-sections displayed heterogeneous mixtures with good interfacial bonding. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) confirmed effective integration of components, with spectra showing characteristic cellulose peaks (1000-1200 cm⁻¹) and lignin bands (1500 cm⁻¹) from reinforcement materials.  

Pull-thru system testing evaluated processing parameters, identifying 60 ml solvent per panel as optimal volume. Comparison testing of various solvent formulations demonstrated the superiority of 4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea, which produced composites with significantly higher mechanical properties than NaOH or KOH-based solvents. Normalized results accounting for material costs showed particular advantages for formulations combining 25% cotton matrix with 75% OOW/AF reinforcement, offering excellent mechanical properties at reduced material costs.  

The examples collectively demonstrate that the invention provides a reproducible method for significantly enhancing cotton yarn properties through cold alkaline treatment. Specific formulation and processing parameters have been identified that optimize mechanical property enhancement while maintaining practical feasibility and cost-effectiveness. These examples establish the invention's technical feasibility and commercial potential for improving cotton-based materials.  

### Exemplary Embodiments  

The invention encompasses multiple exemplary embodiments that provide specific implementations of the cotton yarn treatment method and system. These embodiments illustrate variations in system configuration, solvent composition, processing parameters, and material formulations that can be employed to achieve desired results in different applications.  

One exemplary embodiment features a system for treating cotton yarn that includes a primary solvent bath followed by water bath rinsing. In this configuration, cotton yarn is pulled through the solvent bath at a rate of 0.5 meters per minute, ensuring adequate exposure to the alkaline solution. The system maintains the solvent bath at -12.5°C with a composition of 4.6% LiOH·H₂O and 15% urea. Following solvent treatment, the yarn passes through a water bath at 20°C for rinsing, with a pulling rate of 1 meter per minute to ensure thorough solvent removal. Total soaking time in the solvent bath is 15 minutes, while rinsing in the water bath continues for 30 minutes to complete the treatment process.  

An alternative embodiment incorporates an additional solvent gel bath in the system configuration. This three-bath system includes an initial solvent bath (4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea at -12.5°C), followed by a solvent gel bath (3.5% LiOH·H₂O/10% urea at -5°C), and concluding with a water rinse bath (20°C). In this embodiment, pulling rates are optimized at 0.4 m/min through the solvent bath, 0.6 m/min through the gel bath, and 1.2 m/min through the water bath. Soaking times are adjusted to 20 minutes in the solvent bath, 15 minutes in the gel bath, and 25 minutes in the water bath, providing graduated treatment stages that enhance matrix formation.  

Another embodiment utilizes dissolved cotton cellulose fiber in the treatment process. In this variation, a portion of cotton material is completely dissolved in the alkaline solvent to create a concentrated cellulose solution that is then mixed with the partial dissolution gel and reinforcement materials. The dissolved cellulose acts as an additional binding agent, with typical quantities ranging from 5-15% of total cellulose content. This embodiment employs a LiOH·H₂O concentration of 5.2% in the solvent and 4.0% in any additional gel bath, with urea concentrations maintained at 15% and 12% respectively.  

Temperature parameters across embodiments are carefully controlled, with solvent baths maintained between -12.5°C to -15°C for optimal partial dissolution. Gel baths when used are typically held at -5°C to -7.5°C, while water rinse baths remain at ambient temperature (20-25°C). These temperature gradients are critical to achieving the desired degree of cellulose dissolution and subsequent composite formation.  

The invention includes method embodiments that mirror these system configurations. One method embodiment involves treating cotton yarn by pulling it through a single solvent bath (4.6% LiOH·H₂O/15% urea at -12.5°C) followed by water rinsing, with specific rate and time parameters as described above. An alternative method embodiment incorporates the additional solvent gel bath step, providing intermediate treatment between initial solvent exposure and final rinsing.  

Further embodiments vary the alkaline solvent composition while maintaining the core inventive concept. These include formulations with LiOH·H₂O concentrations from 4.0-5.5%, urea concentrations from 12-18%, and optional additions of minor components such as zinc oxide or surfactants to modify solution properties. NaOH-based embodiments are also included, typically employing 7% NaOH with 12% urea, though these generally produce composites with slightly lower mechanical properties than the LiOH·H₂O formulations.  

The exemplary embodiments collectively demonstrate the flexibility and adaptability of the invention while maintaining its core advantages. Each embodiment provides specific implementation options that can be selected based on desired end-product characteristics, available equipment, and production requirements. The detailed parameters provided for each embodiment enable precise reproduction of the invention's benefits across various operational contexts.
