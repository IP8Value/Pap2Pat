# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel RNA molecules and methods for their generation and use. More specifically, the invention provides expression vectors encoding RNA molecules with double-stranded variable regions, libraries of such RNA molecules, and methods for identifying RNA molecules that affect biological parameters such as cell survival, differentiation, and viral replication. The RNA molecules of the invention may be used as therapeutics or research tools to modulate gene expression through RNA interference (RNAi) and other mechanisms.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Conventional methods for generating RNA libraries suffer from several limitations. Existing approaches typically focus on creating libraries targeting single genes through rational design, which restricts the potential mechanisms of action that can be explored. Current RNA interference (RNAi) libraries based on canonical pathways often produce constructs with suboptimal stem lengths that limit their potency. Many existing methods generate siRNA libraries rather than the more potent shRNA constructs, and their designs preclude mismatches in the RNA duplex that may contribute to biological activity. Furthermore, conventional approaches do not adequately explore the potential for non-canonical mechanisms of action beyond standard RNAi pathways. There remains a need for improved methods to generate diverse RNA molecule libraries that can identify sequences with therapeutic potential through unbiased functional screening.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides expression vectors for RNA molecules comprising a double-stranded variable region. These RNA molecules are characterized by a structure containing variable, non self-complementary, and complementary regions that enable diverse biological effects. The invention includes a set or library of recombinant expression vectors encoding such RNA molecules, allowing for comprehensive screening of sequences with desired activities.

A key aspect of the invention is the method of generating RNA molecules with double-stranded variable regions. This method involves synthesizing a nucleic acid intermediate II containing a random sequence region and processing it through several enzymatic steps to create the final RNA molecule construct. The method further includes creating a double-stranded intermediate III that serves as the template for RNA molecule production. An important feature of this process is the incorporation of restriction enzyme site asymmetry, which facilitates directional cloning and library construction.

Alternative methods for generating RNA molecules are also provided, including synthesizing a nucleotide intermediate I with a random sequence region and annealing it with a second nucleotide molecule to form the double-stranded structure. The invention enables production of sets or libraries of recombinant viruses carrying these RNA molecules for efficient delivery to target cells.

The RNA molecules of the invention have diverse applications, including methods for conferring protection against viral infection, inhibiting viral replication, and inducing cell differentiation. These effects are achieved through the molecules' ability to modulate gene expression patterns in target cells, either through canonical RNA interference pathways or through alternative mechanisms of action.

One embodiment involves creating circular intermediates that are then processed into final expression constructs. Another approach generates linear intermediates that are subsequently circularized. Screening methods for identifying functional RNA molecules include contacting target cells with expression vector libraries and selecting for desired phenotypic changes. Effective sequences can be isolated, amplified, and sequenced for further characterization. The RNA molecules of the invention may take various forms including shRNAs, siRNAs, miRNAs, and other RNAi-related species. ### EXPERIMENTAL DETAILS SECTION

The retroviral vector pQe2 was designed for dual RNAi expression, incorporating specific components to enable efficient shRNA production and delivery. Validation experiments confirmed effective shRNA knockdown using this vector system. The vector pSuper-Retro was modified by replacing the Bgl II-Mlu I fragment with a Bgl II-Bbs I-Mlu I fragment to create the linearized vector depicted in Figure 5.

Alternative vectors were developed by replacing the Bgl II cloning site and spacer sequence. A spacer sequence was added between Xcm I and Sfi I sites to optimize vector performance. Various other vectors and restriction enzyme combinations were identified as suitable for the method.

The library of partially self-complementary RNA molecules was created through a multi-step process. Single-stranded DNA molecules containing constant and random regions were synthesized and annealed with mismatched primers. Complementary strands were generated through enzymatic extension, followed by ligation of hairpin-loop linkers to staggered ends. The resulting nucleic acid intermediate II was amplified by PCR to create double-stranded intermediate III, which was then restricted and ligated into vectors downstream of polymerase-III promoters.

Additional approaches for creating RNA molecule libraries included:
1. Generating double-stranded intermediate IB with recessed primers
2. Using mismatched primers to create specific restriction sites
3. Ligating hairpin-loop linkers to extended oligonucleotides
4. Creating non-self-complementary loop sequences

A third approach involved creating reverse complements of random stem sequences, covalently linking two strands, and initiating second strand extension. This method utilized nicking enzymes and strand-displacing polymerases to generate final constructs.

Efficacy testing included sequencing inserts and verifying biological activity through apoptosis-inhibiting assays. 293T cells were treated with apoptosis inducers (CDDO or serum-free medium) to establish screening systems. Glucose/glucose oxidase techniques provided additional methods for apoptosis induction in test systems.

### Materials and Experimental Methods

Cell preparation followed standardized protocols for maintaining and expanding cell lines. Retroviral packaging cells were cultured under conditions optimized for high-titer virus production. Target cells were maintained in appropriate media with necessary growth factors prior to experimental procedures.

Infection protocols were calibrated to achieve approximately 30% GFP-positive cells, ensuring optimal representation of library variants. Cell survival assays were conducted under controlled conditions with defined endpoints for assessing RNA molecule effects.

### Results

Apoptosis protocols were established and calibrated using known inducers and inhibitors. Retroviral infection conditions were optimized to achieve consistent transduction rates, with 30% GFP-positive cells selected as the target for library screening.

FL5.12 cells were infected with the shRNA library and subjected to IL-3 withdrawal to select for protective sequences. Multiple cycles of withdrawal and recovery enriched for effective shRNAs, with GFP-positive cells increasing to 60% after four rounds. Sequencing of retrieved sequences identified enriched clones with statistically significant protective effects.

Hit optimization through random mutagenesis generated improved variants with enhanced activity. Specific nucleotide changes were identified as critical for improved protection, with both sequence context and mismatch patterns contributing to efficacy. The optimized sequences showed approximately double the protective effect of initial hits in apoptosis assays.

Additional screening applications included:
- Identifying RNA molecules that sustain stem cell pluripotency
- Inducing differentiation in progenitor cells
- Protecting against viral infection
- Inhibiting viral replication

Flow cytometry analysis confirmed phenotypic changes associated with effective RNA molecules. Sequence retrieval methods enabled identification of active variants, and confirmatory testing validated their effects in secondary assays. The results demonstrate the utility of the random shRNA library approach for identifying sequences with diverse biological activities.
