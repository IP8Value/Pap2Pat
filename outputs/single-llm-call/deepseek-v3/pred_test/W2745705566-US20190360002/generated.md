# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to genetic engineering tools, specifically to thermostable CRISPR-Cas9 systems derived from thermophilic microorganisms. More particularly, the invention provides a novel ThermoCas9 protein isolated from Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, along with methods and compositions for its use in genome editing and gene regulation across a broad temperature range. The invention further encompasses optimized guide RNA structures, protospacer adjacent motif (PAM) sequences, and expression systems that enable efficient genome manipulation in both thermophilic and mesophilic organisms.  

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION  

The CRISPR-Cas system represents an adaptive immune mechanism in prokaryotes that provides resistance against invading genetic elements. These systems are classified into two classes (1 and 2) and six types (I-VI) based on their complexity and signature proteins. Class 2 systems, including type-II CRISPR-Cas9 and type V CRISPR-Cas12a, have been widely adopted as genome engineering tools for both eukaryotic and prokaryotic organisms. These systems introduce targeted double-stranded DNA breaks through a ribonucleoprotein complex consisting of a single Cas endonuclease and an RNA guide.  

The guide RNA for Cas9 typically comprises a CRISPR RNA (crRNA) and a trans-activating CRISPR RNA (tracrRNA) duplex. For practical applications, this duplex has been simplified into a chimeric single guide RNA (sgRNA) to direct Cas9 activity. Target DNA cleavage by Cas9 requires recognition of a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM), which is a 3-8 nucleotide sequence adjacent to the targeted protospacer. The PAM sequence varies among different Cas9 proteins.  

Cas9 endonucleases contain two catalytic domains, RuvC and HNH. Mutations in these domains can convert Cas9 into a nickase or a catalytically inactive variant. The catalytically dead Cas9 (dCas9) has proven valuable for gene silencing and modulation of essential gene expression.  

Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 (SpCas9) is the most extensively characterized and widely used Cas9 variant for genome engineering. However, existing Cas9-based tools are limited by their mesophilic nature, restricting their application to temperatures below 42°C. This limitation has prevented the use of CRISPR-Cas technologies in obligate thermophiles and in applications requiring elevated temperatures.  

There remains a need for thermostable CRISPR-Cas systems that can function across a broad temperature range, particularly for genome editing in thermophilic microorganisms. The present invention addresses this need by providing ThermoCas9, a thermostable Cas9 orthologue from Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12, along with optimized methods for its use in genome editing and gene regulation.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides ThermoCas9, a novel RNA-guided DNA endonuclease derived from the type-IIC CRISPR-Cas system of Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12. ThermoCas9 demonstrates in vitro activity across an exceptionally broad temperature range of 20-70°C, with optimal activity between 37-60°C. The invention further encompasses optimized sgRNA structures that confer thermostability to the ThermoCas9-sgRNA ribonucleoprotein complex, enabling efficient genome editing at elevated temperatures.  

The invention provides methods for modifying the genetic material of eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells using ThermoCas9. These methods involve integrating a polynucleotide encoding ThermoCas9 into the host genome or introducing it via an expression vector, along with a guide RNA expression vector and optionally a repair oligonucleotide. The methods can be performed simultaneously or sequentially, depending on the specific application.  

The invention further provides isolated ThermoCas9 proteins and polypeptides, including catalytically inactive variants (ThermodCas9) for gene silencing applications. These proteins contain characteristic motifs essential for their function and thermostability, including specific amino acid sequences that distinguish them from mesophilic Cas9 orthologues.  

The invention encompasses specific PAM sequences recognized by ThermoCas9, including preferred sequences containing cytosine at the fifth position and adenine at the eighth position. These PAM sequences influence the temperature range at which ThermoCas9 can effectively bind, cleave, mark, or modify target nucleic acids.  

The invention provides expression vectors for delivering ThermoCas9 and guide RNAs to host cells, along with methods for transforming, transfecting, or transducing cells with these vectors. The vectors may include constitutive or inducible promoters, selectable markers, and other regulatory elements to control ThermoCas9 expression and activity.  

The invention further provides host cells transformed with ThermoCas9 expression systems, including prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, with particular utility in thermophilic microorganisms. These transformed cells can be used for various applications, including genome editing, gene regulation, and metabolic engineering.  

Kits for carrying out the invention are also provided, containing ThermoCas9 proteins or expression vectors, guide RNA components, and other reagents necessary for genome editing or gene regulation applications across a broad temperature range.  

## RNA GUIDES AND TARGET SEQUENCES  

The present invention provides optimized RNA guides and target sequences for use with ThermoCas9. The targeting system comprises a Cas protein of the invention and a target nucleic acid, which may be DNA or RNA. The invention encompasses guide RNAs (gRNAs) and targeting RNA molecules designed to form ribonucleoprotein complexes with ThermoCas9.  

The invention provides specific PAM sequences recognized by ThermoCas9, including 5'-NNNNCNRA-3' sequences where N represents any nucleotide, C is cytosine, R is purine (A or G), and A is adenine. Particularly preferred PAM sequences include those with cytosine at the fifth position and adenine at the eighth position, which enable efficient target binding and cleavage across the full temperature range of ThermoCas9 activity.  

The ribonucleoprotein complex of the invention comprises ThermoCas9 bound to a targeting RNA molecule. This complex is capable of binding, cleaving, marking, or modifying target nucleic acids at temperatures from 20-70°C. The invention provides methods for forming this complex and using it to manipulate target sequences in transformed non-human cells.  

The targeting RNA molecules of the invention typically range from 50-200 nucleotides in length, with preferred lengths between 80-150 nucleotides. The complementary portion of the RNA molecule that base-pairs with the target sequence typically ranges from 18-25 nucleotides, with 19-21 nucleotides being particularly effective. These RNA molecules demonstrate high specificity and affinity for their target sequences, with dissociation constants (Kd) in the nanomolar range.  

The invention encompasses CRISPR RNA (crRNA) molecules with specific structures optimized for ThermoCas9 activity. These include type I-E and type II CRISPR system components, with RNA molecules designed based on gene sequence information. The SEED portion of the spacer sequence, corresponding to positions 2-8 of the protospacer, is particularly important for target recognition and cleavage efficiency.  

The invention provides methods for detecting single base changes in target nucleic acids using ThermoCas9's stringent PAM requirements at lower temperatures. The PAM design rules of the invention enable precise targeting across different temperature ranges, with specific PAM sequences preferred for activity at different temperatures.  

## BINDING, CLEAVAGE, MARKING AND MODIFYING TEMPERATURES  

The Cas proteins of the invention exhibit optimal activity across a temperature range of 20-70°C, with particular advantages for genetic engineering applications requiring thermostability. This broad temperature range enables applications in molecular biology techniques that were previously limited by the thermal instability of mesophilic Cas9 proteins.  

ThermoCas9 demonstrates nucleic acid binding activity from 20-70°C, with optimal binding between 37-60°C. Cleavage activity occurs across a similar range, with efficient double-strand break formation observed between 25-65°C. Marking and modifying activities, including catalytically inactive variants for gene silencing, function across the same broad temperature spectrum.  

The tracrRNA component of the guide RNA significantly influences ThermoCas9's thermostability and activity. The full-length tracrRNA contains three predicted hairpin structures that contribute to complex stability at elevated temperatures. Truncation studies reveal that removal of the 3' hairpin reduces cleavage efficiency, while removal of both the middle and 3' hairpins drastically diminishes activity.  

The invention provides chimeric single-guide RNAs (sgRNAs) with optimized structures for ThermoCas9 activity. These include sgRNAs with one, two, or three stem-loop structures, with the three-loop configuration being preferred for maximum thermostability. The tracrRNA portion can be modified to enhance binding, cleavage, marking, or modifying activity at specific temperatures.  

Spacer length also influences ThermoCas9 activity, with optimal lengths ranging from 19-23 nucleotides. Spacers shorter than 18 nucleotides show significantly reduced cleavage efficiency. The invention provides methods for designing spacer sequences based on these findings to optimize genome editing efficiency.  

## FUNCTIONAL MOIETIES  

The invention encompasses fusion proteins comprising ThermoCas9 linked to functional moieties. These moieties may include marker proteins for visualization, nuclease domains for enhanced cleavage activity, or other functional domains for specialized applications. The fusion can be achieved through genetic linkage or chemical conjugation.  

Exemplary functional moieties include fluorescent proteins for target sequence visualization, transcriptional activators or repressors for gene regulation, and epitope tags for protein detection. These fusions expand the utility of ThermoCas9 beyond simple genome editing to include applications in gene regulation, imaging, and diagnostics.  

## NUCLEASE ACTIVITY  

The ribonucleoprotein complexes of the invention exhibit sequence-specific nuclease activity across a broad temperature range. ThermoCas9 generates double-strand breaks in target DNA, which can be repaired by endogenous cellular repair machinery through homologous recombination or non-homologous end joining.  

The invention provides methods for inactivating native nuclease activity through mutations in the RuvC or HNH domains, creating nickase or catalytically dead variants. These variants can be fused to functional moieties for applications requiring DNA binding without cleavage, such as gene silencing or imaging.  

The invention encompasses methods for reducing off-target effects by carefully controlling PAM recognition and spacer-protospacer matching. ThermoCas9 demonstrates high sensitivity to mismatches, particularly at PAM-proximal positions, which enhances targeting specificity compared to mesophilic Cas9 orthologues.  

## EXPRESSION VECTORS  

The invention provides expression vectors for delivering ThermoCas9 and associated components to host cells. These vectors typically include a polynucleotide encoding ThermoCas9, regulatory elements for controlled expression, and optionally a guide RNA expression cassette.  

The vectors may employ constitutive or inducible promoters, with the latter being particularly useful for controlling Cas9 expression timing. Exemplary inducible systems include chemically inducible promoters and temperature-sensitive expression systems. The vectors may also include selectable markers for identifying transformed cells.  

The invention provides methods for modifying target nucleic acids using these expression vectors, including transfection, transformation, or transduction protocols optimized for different host organisms. Delivery methods include electroporation, chemical transformation, and viral transduction, depending on the host cell type.  

## HOST CELLS  

The invention encompasses host cells transformed with ThermoCas9 expression systems. These include prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, with particular utility in thermophilic microorganisms. Preferred host cells include thermophilic bacteria such as Geobacillus and Bacillus species, as well as mesophilic organisms like Pseudomonas putida.  

The transformed cells of the invention find application in metabolic engineering, genome editing, and gene regulation. They are particularly valuable for industrial applications requiring elevated temperatures, such as biofuel production, enzyme manufacturing, and bioremediation.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Example 1: Isolation of Geobacillus thermodenitrificans  

Geobacillus thermodenitrificans T12 was isolated from compost samples and identified through genomic sequencing. The strain was found to possess a type-IIC CRISPR-Cas system encoding ThermoCas9, which was subsequently characterized and developed into a genome engineering tool.  

### Example 2: Defining the Essential Consensus Sequences for Cas9 in Geobacillus thermodenitrificans  

Protein sequence alignments and phylogenetic analysis revealed conserved motifs essential for ThermoCas9 function. These include characteristic domains shared with other Cas9 orthologues, as well as unique features contributing to thermostability. A homology model of ThermoCas9 was generated to predict its structure and functional domains.  

### Example 3: Identifying Core Amino Acid Motifs  

Sequence alignment identified conserved residues critical for ThermoCas9 activity and thermostability. Key motifs in the RuvC and HNH nuclease domains were characterized, along with unique structural features of the REC lobe that distinguish ThermoCas9 from mesophilic variants.  

### Example 4: Determination of the PAM Sequence  

The PAM sequence requirements for ThermoCas9 were determined through in vitro cleavage assays with randomized PAM libraries. Deep sequencing of cleaved products revealed a preferred 5'-NNNNCNRA-3' PAM, with particular importance of cytosine at the fifth position and adenine at the eighth position.  

### Example 5: In Vitro Determination of PAM Sequences  

Cleavage assays with defined PAM variants confirmed the sequence requirements across different temperatures. At lower temperatures (20-30°C), ThermoCas9 demonstrates stringent PAM requirements, while at optimal temperatures (37-60°C), it tolerates more variation in the PAM sequence.  

### Example 6: In Vivo Genome Editing  

ThermoCas9 was successfully employed for genome editing in Bacillus smithii at 55°C and Pseudomonas putida at 37°C. The system enabled efficient gene deletion and demonstrated the broad applicability of ThermoCas9 across temperature ranges and host organisms.  

### Example 7: Gene Silencing Applications  

A catalytically dead variant of ThermoCas9 (ThermodCas9) was developed for gene silencing in thermophilic hosts. This system effectively repressed target gene expression, demonstrating utility for metabolic engineering and functional genomics studies in thermophiles.  

The invention thus provides a comprehensive toolkit for genome engineering across a broad temperature range, with particular advantages for thermophilic microorganisms and applications requiring thermostable CRISPR-Cas systems.
