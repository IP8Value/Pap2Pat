# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to plant hormone sensing, specifically to non-invasive methods for detecting plant stress responses through hydrogel-based extraction and fluorescence analysis of phenolic compounds. More particularly, the invention provides a system for early detection of pathogen infections in crops by monitoring methyl salicylate and other blue fluorescent substances emitted by plants under stress conditions.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Plant defense mechanisms involve complex biochemical pathways that produce various secondary metabolites in response to pathogenic infections. Among these, phenylpropanoids such as caffeic acid, chlorogenic acid, and ferulic acid serve as phytoanticipins—antimicrobial compounds that accumulate during infections. Additionally, the phytohormone salicylic acid plays a central role in systemic acquired resistance, triggering immune responses against pathogens.  

Prior art in plant hormone sensing has relied on destructive techniques such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) and polymerase chain reaction (PCR), which require grinding plant tissues and are unsuitable for early field detection. Non-invasive fluorescence imaging methods have been explored but suffer from interference due to chlorophyll quenching in mesophyll cells. Existing hydrogel-based extraction techniques, developed for human sweat analysis, have not been adapted for plant hormone sensing despite their potential for non-invasive monitoring.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

Current methods for detecting plant pathogen infections are either destructive, expensive, or ineffective for early-stage diagnosis. There exists a need for a non-invasive, field-deployable system capable of detecting presymptomatic infections by monitoring plant stress markers such as methyl salicylate and phenolic compounds.  

### Solution to Problem  

The invention provides a hydrogel-based sensor system that extracts water-soluble phenolic compounds from plant leaves through passive diffusion. The hydrogel film, composed of agarose, selectively absorbs methyl salicylate and other blue fluorescent substances while excluding chlorophyll. Fluorescence emission or electrochemical changes in the hydrogel are then measured to quantify pathogen-induced stress responses.  

### Advantageous Effect of Invention  

This system enables early detection of infections before visible symptoms appear, allowing timely intervention. The non-destructive nature of hydrogel extraction preserves plant integrity, making it suitable for repeated monitoring. By avoiding chlorophyll interference, the invention provides higher sensitivity to blue fluorescent stress markers compared to conventional fluorescence imaging.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention introduces a receptor system for methyl salicylate based on diboroxane derivatives, phenylboronic acid derivatives, and boroxine derivatives. These compounds form complexes with methyl salicylate, inducing measurable fluorescence emission or electrochemical changes.  

The hydrogel film (preferably agarose) is applied to the leaf surface, where it extracts water-soluble phenolic compounds through micropores and trichome bases. Ethanol pretreatment enhances adhesion by reducing cuticular wax hydrophobicity. Extracted compounds are detected via:  

### <Fluorescence Emission Phenomenon>  

Excitation at 310 nm induces blue fluorescence (peaking at 428 nm) from methyl salicylate and chlorogenic acid complexes with boron-oxygen compounds. Intensity correlates with pathogen stress levels and is compared against reference values for healthy plants.  

### <Electrochemical Behavior>  

The invention measures current changes when methyl salicylate complexes with diboroxane derivatives in an electrochemical cell. Current deviations from baseline indicate infection severity.  

### <Methyl Salicylate Sensor>  

The sensor comprises:  
1) A recognition section with boron-oxygen compounds  
2) A detection section with optical/electrochemical transducers  
3) A computer program analyzing signal changes  
4) Output interfaces displaying infection probability  

### <Method for Early Detection of Pathogen Infection in Crop>  

The method involves:  
1) Installing hydrogel sensors near crops  
2) Detecting methyl salicylate concentration changes  
3) Correlating measurements with pathogen databases  
4) Alerting farmers when thresholds are exceeded  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Filter paper containing TBO (a diboroxane derivative) showed fluorescence emission at 428 nm when exposed to methyl salicylate vapor from infected tomato leaves.  

### Comparative Example 1  

Control filter paper with TBO showed no fluorescence when exposed to healthy plants.  

### Example 2  

TBO-impregnated hydrogel films detected volatile methyl salicylate signals from presymptomatic tobacco plants 48 hours before wilting symptoms appeared.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

This invention has broad applications in precision agriculture, including:  
- Early warning systems for bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum)  
- Monitoring of fungal infections in greenhouse crops  
- Integrated pest management systems  
- Quality control in medicinal plant cultivation  

The sensor's non-destructive nature enables continuous crop health monitoring without compromising yield. Commercial embodiments include disposable hydrogel patches and automated sensor networks for large-scale farming operations.
