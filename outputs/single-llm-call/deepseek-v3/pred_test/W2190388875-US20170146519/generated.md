## BACKGROUND  

Pattern recognition receptors (PRRs) serve as sentinel proteins within the innate immune system, detecting and responding to microbe- and danger-associated molecular patterns. These receptors initiate signaling pathways that lead to the expression of cytokines, chemokines, and effector molecules crucial for antimicrobial defense and immune coordination.  

PRR-mediated signaling frequently results in interferon (IFN) synthesis and secretion. Type I and III IFNs are produced through pathways involving phosphorylation of the transcription factor IRF3 by TBK1 and IKKε kinases. This process relies on adaptor proteins such as TRIF, IPS-1, and STING, which integrate signals from upstream PRRs.  

STING functions both as a PRR for cyclic dinucleotides and as an adaptor for cytoplasmic DNA sensors. Given its role in innate immune activation, STING has been explored as a therapeutic target. Pharmacologic IFN stimulation has been proposed as a broad-spectrum antiviral strategy, with agonists of TLRs, RIG-I/MDA5, and STING demonstrating antiviral and immunotherapeutic potential.  

However, existing STING activators like CMA and DMXAA exhibit species-specific activity, functioning in mice but not humans, limiting their clinical utility. Meanwhile, Alphaviruses such as Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) and Venezuelan encephalitis virus (VEEV) cause significant morbidity, yet no FDA-approved antiviral treatments exist. These viruses are highly sensitive to type I IFN, suggesting that STING pathway activation could provide therapeutic benefit.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses the first synthetic small molecule STING agonist active in human cells, designated G10. This compound stimulates STING-dependent IRF3 phosphorylation, IFN synthesis, and antiviral gene expression, effectively inhibiting Alphavirus replication.  

G10 induces IRF3/IFN-dependent responses without requiring IPS-1-mediated signaling. Through CRISPR/Cas9 studies, STING was identified as essential for G10's biological activity. The compound triggers secretion of bioactive IFN and establishes an antiviral state refractory to CHIKV, VEEV, and Sindbis virus replication.  

In vivo studies using the murine-specific STING agonist DMXAA demonstrate that pharmacologic STING activation blocks CHIKV replication, supporting the therapeutic potential of this approach. The invention further provides methods for identifying additional STING agonists and pharmaceutical compositions for treating Alphavirus infections.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1 Identification of a Novel IFN/IRF3-Inducing Molecule by High Throughput In Vitro Screening  

A high-throughput screen of 51,632 compounds identified G10 as a potent inducer of IRF3/IFN-dependent luciferase expression in human fibroblasts. The compound exhibited low cytotoxicity and strong signaling activation.  

### Example 2—G10 Induces IFN/IRF3- but not NF-κB-Dependent Transcription in Human Fibroblasts  

G10 dose-dependently activated IRF3-dependent reporter genes and endogenous ISGs. Unlike inflammatory stimuli, it did not induce NF-κB-dependent transcription, demonstrating pathway specificity.  

### Example 3—G10 Elicits Antiviral Activity Against New and Old World Alphaviruses  

G10 pretreatment potently inhibited CHIKV and VEEV replication (>3 log reduction) while showing minimal effects against West Nile or Vaccinia viruses, indicating Alphavirus-specific activity.  

### Example 4—IRF3 is Essential to G10-Induced Transcription and Anti-Alphaviral Activity  

CRISPR-generated IRF3-knockout cells lost G10 responsiveness. IRF3 phosphorylation was confirmed by immunoblotting. Anti-Alphavirus effects were abolished in IRF3-deficient cells, establishing its necessity.  

### Example 5—G10-Mediated IRF3 Activation and Anti-Alphaviral Activity Occur Independently of IPS-1/MAVS-Dependent Signaling  

IPS-1 knockout did not impair G10-induced IRF3 phosphorylation or antiviral effects, distinguishing its mechanism from RIG-I/MDA5 pathways.  

### Example 6—STING is Required for G10-Mediated IRF3 Activation, Gene Expression, and Anti-Alphaviral Activity  

STING-deficient cells showed complete loss of G10-induced IRF3 phosphorylation, gene expression, and antiviral activity. Thermal shift assays suggested indirect STING activation.  

### Example 7—G10 Induces STING-Dependent Synthesis and Secretion of Bioactive Interferon  

G10 triggered IFNβ and IFNλ1 mRNA production and subsequent IFN secretion. This response required STING but was diminished in IPS-1 knockout cells, suggesting pathway crosstalk.  

### Example 8—STAT1 is Required for G10-Mediated Anti-Alphaviral Activity and ISG Expression  

STAT1 knockout abolished G10's antiviral effects despite preserved activity of other IRF3 activators, revealing distinct STAT1-dependence for G10-mediated protection.  

### Example 9—G10 Triggers IRF3-Dependent Activity with Less Potency than 2′3′-cGAMP  

Comparative studies showed G10 induced faster IRF3 phosphorylation but required higher concentrations than 2′3′-cGAMP for equivalent gene induction, suggesting different activation mechanisms.
