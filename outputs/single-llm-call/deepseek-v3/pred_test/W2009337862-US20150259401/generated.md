# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to antibodies with increased half-life, particularly modified ferret immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies containing specific amino acid substitutions in the Fc region that enhance binding to the neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). More specifically, the invention provides immunoglobulin constructs comprising modified ferret Fc regions containing substitutions at position 252, including S252Y and S252M mutations, which demonstrate substantially improved pharmacokinetic properties in ferrets compared to unmodified antibodies. The invention further encompasses chimeric antibodies combining human variable regions with modified ferret constant regions, ferretized antibodies, and immunoglobulin fusion proteins incorporating the modified Fc regions. These modified antibodies are particularly useful for research applications in ferret models of human diseases, where extended antibody half-life and reduced immunogenicity are desired.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ferrets (Mustela putorius furo) have become an important laboratory model for studying human infectious diseases, particularly respiratory infections such as influenza. Ferrets are susceptible to infection with human influenza viruses and demonstrate clinical symptoms similar to those observed in humans, making them valuable for vaccine and therapeutic antibody development. However, the use of ferrets as models for antibody pharmacokinetics and efficacy studies presents several technical challenges. 

A significant limitation of the ferret model is the unusually short half-life of human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) in ferrets compared to other animal models. Preliminary studies have indicated that human IgG antibodies exhibit half-lives of approximately 31 hours in ferrets, substantially shorter than the 14-21 day half-lives observed in humans or the 6-12 day half-lives in cynomolgus monkeys. This rapid clearance necessitates frequent dosing in long-term studies and complicates pharmacokinetic and efficacy assessments. Furthermore, human antibodies administered to ferrets frequently elicit anti-drug antibody (ADA) responses that can accelerate antibody clearance and potentially interfere with therapeutic effects.

The neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) plays a central role in determining antibody half-life by protecting IgG from lysosomal degradation through a pH-dependent recycling mechanism. At acidic pH (6.0) in endosomes, FcRn binds to the Fc region of IgG, preventing degradation and facilitating recycling to the cell surface where the antibody is released at neutral pH (7.4). Engineering antibodies for enhanced FcRn binding at pH 6.0 while maintaining efficient release at pH 7.4 has been shown to extend antibody half-life in humans and other species. However, the specific interactions between ferret FcRn and antibody Fc regions have not been characterized, and no optimized ferret IgG variants with extended half-life have been reported.

There exists a significant unmet need for modified ferret antibodies with enhanced pharmacokinetic properties for use in ferret disease models. Such antibodies would enable longer dosing intervals, reduce animal usage, and improve the reliability of pharmacokinetic and efficacy studies by minimizing immunogenicity and variability between animals. The present invention addresses these needs by providing specifically engineered ferret immunoglobulin constructs with modified Fc regions that exhibit substantially extended half-lives in ferrets.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides modified ferret immunoglobulin G (IgG) antibodies and immunoglobulin constructs with increased half-life in ferrets, achieved through specific amino acid substitutions in the Fc region that enhance binding to ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). The invention is based on the discovery that substitution of serine at position 252 in the ferret IgG Fc region with tyrosine (S252Y) or methionine (S252M) dramatically improves FcRn binding affinity and extends serum half-life in ferrets.

In one embodiment, the invention provides an isolated antibody comprising a modified ferret IgG Fc region, wherein the Fc region comprises a substitution at amino acid residue 252. Particularly preferred substitutions include S252Y and S252M, with the S252Y substitution demonstrating particularly strong enhancement of FcRn binding and half-life extension. The modified antibodies exhibit at least a 2-fold, preferably a 3-fold or greater increase in serum half-life compared to unmodified ferret IgG in ferrets.

The invention further provides an isolated immunoglobulin construct comprising an antibody fragment operably linked to a modified ferret Fc region containing a substitution at position 252. The antibody fragment may be selected from Fab, F(ab')2, scFv, or other antigen-binding fragments, while the modified Fc region provides enhanced serum persistence through improved FcRn binding. In another embodiment, the invention provides an immunoglobulin fusion protein comprising a bioactive molecule operably linked to a modified ferret Fc region containing a substitution at position 252. The bioactive molecule may be a therapeutic protein, peptide, or other biologically active moiety that benefits from extended serum half-life.

Particularly preferred embodiments incorporate the YTE modification (S252Y combined with S254T and T256E substitutions) which provides synergistic enhancement of FcRn binding and half-life extension. The modified Fc regions of the invention may be incorporated into various antibody formats including chimeric antibodies, ferret antibodies, ferretized antibodies, or veneered antibodies. In chimeric embodiments, the antibody may comprise human or humanized Fab regions specific for a target antigen (such as respiratory syncytial virus F glycoprotein) combined with the modified ferret Fc region.

The invention further provides antibodies or immunoglobulin constructs comprising a moiety that specifically binds to respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) F glycoprotein, wherein the construct includes a modified ferret Fc region with substitutions at position 252 as described above. These constructs are particularly useful for studying RSV infection and antibody therapeutics in ferret models while benefiting from extended serum persistence and reduced immunogenicity.

### DEFINITIONS

As used herein, a "corresponding" unmodified antibody refers to an antibody having the same variable region and constant region framework as a modified antibody of the invention, but lacking the specific Fc region modifications that confer increased half-life. An "epitope" refers to the specific region of an antigen that is recognized and bound by an antibody. The term "antibody" encompasses intact immunoglobulin molecules as well as antibody fragments that retain antigen-binding capability.

An immunoglobulin or antibody molecule is a protein comprising at least two heavy (H) chains and two light (L) chains interconnected by disulfide bonds. Each heavy chain includes a variable region (VH) and constant regions (CH1, hinge, CH2, CH3), while each light chain includes a variable region (VL) and a constant region (CL). The Fc region comprises the CH2 and CH3 domains and is responsible for effector functions and serum half-life through interaction with Fc receptors. The hinge region provides flexibility between the Fab arms and Fc region of the antibody.

A Fab fragment is the antigen-binding portion of an antibody consisting of one light chain and the variable region and first constant domain of one heavy chain. A single-chain Fv (scFv) is a fusion protein of the variable regions of the heavy and light chains connected by a peptide linker. An isolated biomolecule is one that has been separated from components of its natural environment, while a substantially purified biomolecule is at least 60% free, preferably 75% free, and most preferably 90% free from other macromolecules.

An immunoglobulin fusion protein comprises an immunoglobulin-derived sequence (such as an Fc region) fused to a non-immunoglobulin protein or peptide. In vivo half-life refers to the time required for the serum concentration of an administered substance to decrease by half, while increased half-life indicates a statistically significant prolongation compared to a control. Recombinant molecules are those constructed by genetic engineering techniques rather than through natural means.

"Specifically binds" indicates that an antibody binds to a particular antigen with greater affinity than to unrelated antigens, typically with a dissociation constant (KD) of 10-7 M or lower, preferably 10-8 M or lower, and more preferably 10-9 M or lower. Cross-reactivity refers to binding to related antigens sharing similar epitopes. Affinity refers to the strength of binding between an antibody and its antigen, while KD is the equilibrium dissociation constant.

An immunoglobulin construct includes any molecule comprising an immunoglobulin-derived sequence, such as full-length antibodies, antibody fragments, or fusion proteins incorporating immunoglobulin domains. The constant region refers to the portion of the antibody that does not vary between antibodies of the same isotype, while the constant fragment (Fc) is the crystallizable portion of the antibody that mediates effector functions.

FcRn refers to the neonatal Fc receptor, a heterodimer consisting of an alpha chain and beta-2 microglobulin (β2m) that protects IgG from catabolism. The numbering of residues in antibody sequences follows the EU numbering system as described in Kabat et al., unless otherwise specified.

### Amino Acid Substitutions

Amino acid substitutions in the Fc region may be introduced using various mutagenesis techniques known in the art. Site-directed mutagenesis allows specific changes to be made at defined positions in a DNA sequence. PCR-mediated mutagenesis uses polymerase chain reaction to introduce mutations during DNA amplification. The use of thermostable ligase enables efficient joining of mutagenic oligonucleotides during PCR-based mutagenesis.

Mutagenic agents such as nitrous acid or alkylating agents may be used to introduce random mutations, followed by screening for desired properties. Screening of mutants may be performed using binding assays (e.g., FcRn binding at pH 6.0), pharmacokinetic studies, or other functional assays. Exemplary amino acid substitutions include conservative substitutions that maintain similar physicochemical properties (e.g., serine to threonine) or non-conservative substitutions that alter properties (e.g., serine to tyrosine).

The numbering system for amino acid positions follows the EU index as in Kabat et al., with position 252 corresponding to serine in wild-type ferret IgG. This position aligns with position 252 in human IgG when sequences are aligned according to standard immunoglobulin homology.

### Antibodies of the Invention

Antibodies according to the invention comprise modified ferret Fc regions containing specific amino acid substitutions that enhance FcRn binding and prolong serum half-life. These antibodies exhibit at least a 2-fold increase in half-life compared to unmodified counterparts when administered to ferrets. The modified antibodies maintain normal antigen-binding properties when incorporated into intact immunoglobulin molecules or fragments.

Characteristics of the antibodies include enhanced binding to ferret FcRn at pH 6.0 (typically at least 5-fold improved affinity), while maintaining efficient release at pH 7.4. The antibodies demonstrate reduced immunogenicity in ferrets compared to human or other heterologous antibodies, as evidenced by lack of rapid clearance suggestive of anti-drug antibody responses.

### Ferret Antibody Derivatives

Ferret antibody derivatives include any antibody format incorporating a modified ferret Fc region as described herein. Conservative amino acid substitutions replace one amino acid with another having similar properties (e.g., hydrophobic for hydrophobic, polar for polar). Conservative substitutions may be made in framework regions or complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) to maintain structure and function.

Mutations in framework regions may improve stability or expression while mutations in CDRs may modulate antigen binding affinity. Silent or neutral missense mutations do not significantly affect antibody function, while non-neutral missense mutations alter properties such as binding or stability. Mutant molecules may be designed based on structural modeling and tested experimentally for desired properties.

Covalent attachment of molecules such as polyethylene glycol (PEG) may further enhance pharmacokinetic properties. Chemical modifications include glycosylation, deglycosylation, or other post-translational modifications. Peptides may be chemically synthesized incorporating non-classical amino acids or other modifications. Immunoconjugates may be formed by linking antibodies to drugs, toxins, or other therapeutic moieties.

Immunoglobulin constructs include any molecule comprising a modified ferret Fc region as described herein. Examples include full-length antibodies, Fc fusion proteins, and antibody fragments such as Fab, F(ab')2, or scFv linked to Fc regions. The Fc regions may be modified as described to enhance half-life while maintaining other Fc-mediated functions.

### Antibody Preparation

Antibody preparation methods include hybridoma technology for producing monoclonal antibodies. Hybridomas are formed by fusing antibody-producing B cells with immortal myeloma cells. Production and screening of hybridomas involves culturing fused cells, selecting positive clones, and expanding those producing desired antibodies.

Antibody fragments may be generated by enzymatic digestion (e.g., pepsin for F(ab')2, papain for Fab) or recombinant methods. Phage display methods allow selection of antibody fragments from libraries displayed on phage surfaces. Antibody coding regions may be expressed in various host systems and isolated using affinity purification methods.

### Recombinant Production of Antibodies and Immunoglobulin Constructs

Recombinant production involves isolating DNA encoding antibody variable and constant regions, sequencing to confirm identity, and cloning into expression vectors. Vectors are transfected into host cells such as mammalian (e.g., CHO, HEK293), bacterial (E. coli), or yeast (Pichia) cells for protein production.

Molecular cloning techniques include restriction enzyme digestion, ligation, transformation, and selection of recombinant clones. Expression vectors typically contain promoters (e.g., CMV for mammalian cells), enhancers, selection markers (e.g., antibiotic resistance), and signal sequences for secretion. Cultured host cells are maintained under conditions promoting protein expression and secretion.

### Chimeric Antibodies

Chimeric antibodies combine variable regions from one species with constant regions from another. For the invention, human variable regions may be joined to ferret constant regions containing Fc modifications. Ferretized antibodies are non-ferret antibodies engineered to contain ferret constant regions, while veneering involves replacing surface residues to reduce immunogenicity.

CDR-grafting transfers complementarity-determining regions from one antibody to another framework. Chain shuffling mixes heavy and light chains from different antibodies to create new specificities. Immunoassays such as ELISA or surface plasmon resonance confirm specific antigen binding of modified antibodies.

### EXAMPLES

### Example 1

A pharmacokinetics study was conducted to evaluate human IgG antibody clearance in ferrets. A human IgG1 monoclonal antibody specific for respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) F glycoprotein was administered intravenously to ferrets at 2 mg/kg. Blood samples were collected over 21 days and antibody concentrations measured by electrochemiluminescent assay. The average terminal half-life was 31 ± 10 hours, with some animals showing rapid clearance suggestive of anti-drug antibody responses. These results demonstrated the short half-life and immunogenicity of human antibodies in ferrets.

### Example 2

Ferret IgG constant region sequences were cloned from ferret tissues. RNA from kidney, lung, liver and spleen was reverse transcribed and amplified using primers based on ferret and mink sequences. The cloned ferret heavy chain constant region showed 89% identity to mink, with differences concentrated in the hinge region. The light chain constant region was identical to partial database sequences and showed 94% identity to mink. These sequences enabled construction of chimeric human/ferret antibodies.

### Example 3

Chimeric ferret/human antibodies were prepared by combining human anti-RSV variable regions with ferret constant regions containing half-life extending substitutions (S252M, S252Y, YTE, N434S). Antibodies were expressed in HEK293 cells and purified by protein A/G chromatography. SDS-PAGE and size exclusion chromatography confirmed proper assembly. Antigen binding assays demonstrated retained specificity for RSV F glycoprotein, with IC50 values comparable to the original human antibody.

### Example 4

Soluble ferret FcRn was cloned and expressed by co-transfecting HEK293 cells with plasmids encoding the extracellular domain of ferret FcRn alpha chain (92% identical to dog) and beta-2 microglobulin. The His-tagged complex was purified by nickel affinity and size exclusion chromatography. SDS-PAGE under reducing conditions showed bands at expected molecular weights (30.1 kDa for alpha chain, 11.5 kDa for beta chain), with some heterogeneity likely due to glycosylation.

### Example 5

Chimeric antibody binding to ferret FcRn was tested using an AlphaScreen competitive binding assay at pH 6.0. The S252Y variant showed 24-fold stronger binding (IC50 = 3.2 μg/ml) compared to wild-type (IC50 = 78 μg/ml). The S252M variant showed intermediate improvement (IC50 = 13 μg/ml), while the YTE variant performed similarly to S252Y alone. These results demonstrated that position 252 substitutions significantly enhance ferret FcRn binding.

### Example 6

Pharmacokinetics of chimeric antibody variants were evaluated in ferrets following intravenous administration at 2 mg/kg. The S252Y variant showed the longest half-life (206 ± 44 hours), followed by YTE (213 ± 28 hours) and S252M (127 ± 38 hours), compared to wild-type (65 ± 27 hours). No rapid clearance suggestive of anti-drug antibodies was observed. These results confirmed that S252Y and related substitutions substantially extend antibody half-life in ferrets.
