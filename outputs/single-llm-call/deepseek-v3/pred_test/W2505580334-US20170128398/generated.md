## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Lactic acidemia (LA) is a common and serious complication of septic shock (SS) that is associated with poor prognosis. Lactate concentrations in blood are determined by the balance between its production and utilization by various organs. The liver plays a central role in lactate metabolism through the monocarboxylate transporter (MCT) system, which facilitates pH-dependent bidirectional transport of lactate across hepatocyte membranes. Once inside hepatocytes, lactate can be converted to glucose via gluconeogenesis or metabolized to pyruvate and subsequently oxidized in mitochondria through oxidative phosphorylation.  

Mitochondrial dysfunction has been proposed as a mechanism for LA in SS, though the evidence remains controversial. The oxidative phosphorylation process involves electron transfer through mitochondrial complexes I-IV to generate a proton gradient that drives ATP synthesis via complex V (ATP synthase). Previous studies of hepatic mitochondrial function in SS have yielded conflicting results, likely due to methodological differences. The Seahorse XF24 extracellular flux analyzer represents a significant technological advancement for measuring mitochondrial oxygen consumption rates (OCR) under various metabolic conditions, though it has not previously been applied to study hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction in SS.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods for treating septic shock and associated lactic acidemia using gentisic acid or its pharmaceutically acceptable salts, particularly gentisic acid sodium salt (GSS). The invention demonstrates that GSS administration effectively reverses both cardiovascular collapse (as measured by increased mean arterial pressure) and lactic acidemia in a canine model of septic shock.  

The invention further provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising gentisic acid or its salts for use in treating septic shock and lactic acidemia. The methods include identifying patients at risk of developing sepsis or septic shock, administering an effective amount of gentisic acid or its salts, and monitoring therapeutic response through measurements of lactate levels, serum pH, and hemodynamic parameters.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The following terms are defined for purposes of this invention: "Septic shock" refers to sepsis with persisting hypotension requiring vasopressors to maintain mean arterial pressure (MAP) ≥65 mmHg despite adequate fluid resuscitation. "Lactic acidemia" refers to arterial lactate concentration >2 mmol/L with associated acidemia (pH <7.35). "Effective amount" refers to a dose sufficient to increase MAP by ≥15 mmHg or decrease lactate concentration by ≥20% within 4 hours of administration.  

A preferred embodiment utilizes a canine model of Pseudomonas aeruginosa bacteremia to study septic shock. In this model, animals develop characteristic hemodynamic changes including decreased MAP and systemic vascular resistance (SVR), along with increased lactate levels. The model demonstrates hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction characterized by reduced maximal oxygen consumption rates (OCR), particularly in response to the protonophore FCCP.  

The invention demonstrates that gentisic acid sodium salt (GSS) administration at doses of 0.4-0.6 mg/kg/min effectively reverses hypotension and reduces lactate levels in septic shock. GSS treatment increases both coupled respiration (ADP-stimulated OCR) and maximal respiration (FCCP-stimulated OCR) in hepatic mitochondria, suggesting improved mitochondrial function. Notably, GSS treatment reduces lactate levels without significantly altering monocarboxylate transporter 1 (MCT1) protein concentrations in liver tissue.  

The invention provides methods for treating individuals with septic shock comprising administering an effective amount of gentisic acid or its salts. The effective amount may be determined by monitoring MAP, lactate levels, and serum pH, with dose adjustments made to achieve target hemodynamic and metabolic parameters. The methods include identifying patients at risk of developing sepsis based on clinical criteria such as systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS) criteria.  

Pharmaceutical compositions of the invention comprise gentisic acid or its pharmaceutically acceptable salts in formulations suitable for intravenous administration. Preferred formulations include gentisic acid sodium salt in 5% dextrose solution at concentrations of 1-5 mg/mL. The compositions may be prepared by dissolving gentisic acid or its salts in sterile aqueous solution under aseptic conditions.  

The invention further provides methods for treating lactic acidemia comprising administering gentisic acid or its salts in amounts sufficient to reduce lactate levels by ≥20% and elevate serum pH to ≥7.35. The treatment effects include reduction in lactate levels without requiring complete normalization of mitochondrial function, suggesting additional mechanisms beyond mitochondrial respiration.  

Comparative studies demonstrate that GSS treatment produces superior effects on lactate metabolism compared to norepinephrine (NE), despite similar improvements in MAP. While NE treatment restores maximal mitochondrial respiration, it fails to reduce lactate levels. In contrast, GSS treatment increases both coupled respiration and reduces lactate levels, with additional benefits including lower heart rates and absence of myocardial injury (as measured by troponin levels).  

The invention proposes several mechanisms for GSS effects, including antioxidant properties related to its phenolic structure (2,5-dihydroxybenzoic acid), potential enhancement of hepatic gluconeogenesis, and possible reduction in glycolysis rates. These mechanisms may operate independently of mitochondrial respiration improvements to reduce lactate levels.  

### RESULTS  

Experimental results demonstrate consistent time to septic shock condition across treatment groups (4.1-4.9 hours). Animal body weights (18-26 kg) and total fluid volumes administered (6±1 L in septic groups) were comparable among groups. All animals survived through the study duration.  

Hemodynamic measurements revealed baseline MAP of ~100 mmHg in all groups, decreasing to ~60 mmHg in septic groups at shock condition. GSS and NE treatments both restored MAP to baseline levels within minutes of infusion. The average GSS dose was 0.55±0.29 mg/kg/min, comparable to NE dose of 0.51±0.22 μg/kg/min.  

Cardiac output remained relatively stable across groups, while systemic vascular resistance decreased in septic groups at shock condition. GSS treatment produced lower heart rates compared to NE treatment at 3-5 hours post-treatment. Notably, NE treatment was associated with increased troponin levels suggesting myocardial injury, while GSS treatment showed no such effect.  

Lactate measurements demonstrated divergent responses: lactate increased in septic control and NE-treated groups, while decreasing in non-septic groups and remaining stable in GSS-treated septic group. Oxygen delivery (DO2) was lowest in septic control group, with NE treatment increasing DO2 primarily through increased hemoglobin levels.  

Mitochondrial function assessments revealed decreased maximal OCR in septic control group, which was restored by both NE and GSS treatments. GSS treatment uniquely increased coupled respiration beyond other groups. Spare respiratory capacity (difference between maximal and basal respiration) was significantly reduced in septic control group.  

### METHODS  

#### Animal Preparation  

The bacteremic model utilized Pseudomonas aeruginosa (ATCC 27853) infused at ~10^10 CFU/hour in normal saline. Animals (18-26 kg) were anesthetized with sufentanil citrate (0.05-0.3 μg/kg/min) and midazolam (5 μg/kg/min), mechanically ventilated with 100% oxygen to maintain PaO2 ~400 mmHg.  

Hemodynamic monitoring included femoral arterial catheter for MAP measurement and Swan-Ganz catheter for pulmonary artery pressures, cardiac output (thermodilution method), and mixed venous blood sampling. Systemic vascular resistance was calculated as (MAP-RAP)/CO × 80. Blood samples were analyzed for PO2, PCO2, pH, lactate, troponin, and other parameters.  

#### Experimental Protocols  

Five experimental groups were studied:  
1. Septic control (bacteremia + D5W)  
2. Non-septic control (saline + D5W)  
3. NE septic (bacteremia + norepinephrine)  
4. GSS septic (bacteremia + gentisic acid sodium salt)  
5. GSS non-septic (saline + gentisic acid sodium salt)  

Measurements were taken at baseline, shock condition (MAP 60-65 mmHg), and 3/5 hours post-treatment. NE and GSS were titrated to restore baseline MAP.  

#### Mitochondrial Preparation  

Liver tissue was harvested immediately post-euthanasia for mitochondrial isolation using MSHE buffer (70 mM sucrose, 210 mM mannitol, 5 mM HEPES, 1 mM EGTA, BSA). Mitochondria were resuspended in MAS buffer (70 mM sucrose, 220 mM mannitol, 10 mM KH2PO4, 5 mM MgCl2, 2 mM HEPES, 1 mM EGTA, BSA) for Seahorse XF24 analysis at ~20 μg protein/well.  

#### Mitochondrial Measurement of Oxygen Consumption Rates  

The Seahorse XF24 analyzer sequentially measured:  
1. Basal OCR (pyruvate 10 mM + malate 2 mM)  
2. Coupled OCR (ADP 4 mM)  
3. State oligomycin (oligomycin 2 μM)  
4. Maximal OCR (FCCP 4 μM)  
5. Non-mitochondrial OCR (rotenone 2 μM + antimycin A 2 μM)  

Spare respiratory capacity was calculated as (maximal - basal OCR). Complex IV activity was measured spectrophotometrically at 550 nm using reduced cytochrome c.  

#### Statistics  

Two-way ANOVA with Student-Newman-Keuls post-hoc testing was used for hemodynamic data (5 groups × 4 time points). One-way ANOVA was used for single time point comparisons. Significance was set at p<0.05. Results are reported as mean ± SD.
