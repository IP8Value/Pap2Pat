# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL DOMAIN  

The present invention relates to novel genetically modified strains of the filamentous fungus *Trichoderma reesei* and methods for their use in the industrial production of cellulases and hemicellulases. More particularly, the invention discloses engineered *Trichoderma reesei* Rut-C30 strains with enhanced enzyme production capabilities achieved through targeted genetic modifications. The modified strains exhibit significantly increased protein secretion rates while maintaining high enzymatic activity levels, making them particularly suitable for large-scale production of cellulolytic enzyme cocktails.  

The invention further relates to improved bioprocesses utilizing these modified *Trichoderma* strains for cellulase production, particularly processes employing low-cost carbon sources such as sugarcane molasses. The disclosed strains and processes enable the achievement of extracellular protein titers exceeding 80 g L<sup>-1</sup> with production rates of 0.24 g L<sup>-1</sup> h<sup>-1</sup>, representing the highest experimentally supported titers reported for *T. reesei*. The enzyme cocktails produced demonstrate saccharification efficiencies comparable to commercially available preparations when tested on pretreated lignocellulosic biomass.  

## PRIOR ART  

Cellulases and hemicellulases constitute a critical class of industrial enzymes with applications ranging from textile processing to biofuel production. These enzymes catalyze the breakdown of cellulose and hemicellulose, the major structural polysaccharides in plant cell walls, into fermentable sugars. The efficient production of these enzymes at industrial scale remains a significant challenge due to the complex regulation of their biosynthesis and the high costs associated with conventional production processes.  

Current techniques for increasing enzyme production in filamentous fungi typically involve random mutagenesis followed by screening or targeted genetic modifications of regulatory pathways. *Trichoderma reesei* has emerged as the predominant industrial platform for cellulase production, accounting for approximately 80% of commercial enzyme formulations used in lignocellulosic biofuel production. This predominance stems from the fungus's innate ability to secrete a potent yet compact set of carbohydrate-active enzymes capable of efficiently degrading plant biomass.  

Various *Trichoderma reesei* strains have been developed from the original QM6a isolate through mutagenesis and selection. The Rut-C30 strain, containing an 85-kb chromosomal deletion and a truncated *cre1* gene, represents one of the most studied hypercellulolytic mutants, capable of producing up to 20 g L<sup>-1</sup> of extracellular proteins. Industrial strains have been reported to achieve titers exceeding 100 g L<sup>-1</sup>, though details of their development and production processes remain proprietary.  

Several genetic modifications have been identified that improve cellulase production in *T. reesei*. These include constitutive expression of the major cellulase regulator XYR1, deletion of the repressor ACE1, removal of extracellular proteases such as SLP1 and PEP1, and supplementation of lacking enzymatic activities through heterologous gene expression. The transcription factor XYR1 has been identified as the master regulator of cellulase gene expression, with specific mutations (V821F and A824V) conferring resistance to carbon catabolite repression by glucose.  

Despite these advances, significant limitations persist in the prior art. Most reported modifications have been implemented individually rather than in combination, limiting their cumulative benefits. Technical challenges in fungal genetic engineering, including limited selectable markers and low transformation efficiencies, have historically restricted the introduction of multiple modifications in a single strain. Additionally, most academic research strains fail to achieve the enzyme titers reported for proprietary industrial strains. The high cost of conventional media components, particularly inducing carbon sources like lactose and microcrystalline cellulose, further limits the economic viability of existing production processes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel *Trichoderma reesei* Rut-C30 strains engineered through precise genetic modifications to achieve industrially relevant levels of cellulase production. The modified strains incorporate a combination of six genetic changes implemented through three rounds of CRISPR/Cas9-mediated engineering:  

1) Insertion of the *Talaromyces emersonii cel3a* β-glucosidase gene under control of the *xyn1* promoter at the *slp1* locus;  
2) Replacement of the *ace1* gene with a constitutively expressed *xyr1-V821F* allele under control of the *pdc1* promoter;  
3) Insertion of the *Aspergillus niger suc1* invertase gene with native regulatory sequences at the *pep1* locus.  

These modifications collectively address multiple limitations of prior art strains by:  
- Enhancing β-glucosidase activity through heterologous gene expression  
- Increasing transcription of cellulase genes while relieving carbon catabolite repression  
- Reducing proteolytic degradation of secreted enzymes  
- Enabling utilization of low-cost sucrose-rich carbon sources  

The invention further provides optimized bioprocesses utilizing these engineered strains with sugarcane molasses as the primary carbon source and molasses-grown yeast as an organic nitrogen source. These processes achieve extracellular protein titers of 80.6 g L<sup>-1</sup> with production rates of 0.24 g L<sup>-1</sup> h<sup>-1</sup>, representing the highest reported titers for publicly available *T. reesei* strains. The produced enzyme cocktails exhibit specific activities up to 72-fold higher than parental strains and demonstrate saccharification efficiencies comparable to commercial preparations.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE EMBODIMENTS  

The engineered *Trichoderma reesei* strains of the present invention were developed through targeted genetic modifications implemented using a single-plasmid CRISPR/Cas9 system and markerless donor cassettes. The CRISPR/Cas9 system employed a *Streptococcus pyogenes* Cas9 gene codon-optimized for expression in *T. reesei*, along with ribozyme-mediated guide RNAs targeting specific genomic loci. This system enabled precise editing of the fungal genome without leaving selectable markers or other extraneous sequences.  

Gene deletion techniques were employed to remove endogenous sequences encoding proteases and transcriptional repressors. The *slp1* gene, encoding a subtilisin protease accounting for approximately 18% of total extracellular proteolytic activity, was completely removed. Similarly, the *pep1* gene, responsible for about 42% of extracellular proteolytic activity, was deleted. The *ace1* gene, encoding a major repressor of cellulase gene expression, was also eliminated through replacement with the *xyr1-V821F* expression cassette.  

Gene disruption techniques were utilized to modify regulatory elements controlling enzyme production. The native *ace1* locus was disrupted through replacement with the *xyr1-V821F* expression cassette, placing this key activator under control of the constitutive *pdc1* promoter. This modification both increases overall cellulase gene expression and confers resistance to carbon catabolite repression by glucose.  

Gene overexpression techniques were implemented to supplement lacking enzymatic activities and expand carbon source utilization. The *Talaromyces emersonii cel3a* gene, encoding a highly active β-glucosidase, was inserted under control of the *xyn1* promoter to address a well-known deficiency in *T. reesei* secretomes. The *Aspergillus niger suc1* gene, encoding an extracellular invertase, was introduced with its native regulatory sequences to enable sucrose utilization.  

The genetic modifications were motivated by the need to develop *T. reesei* strains with enhanced enzyme production capabilities while maintaining compatibility with low-cost production processes. The specific modifications were selected based on their demonstrated individual effects and potential for synergistic combination.  

The engineered strains incorporate modifications targeting four key aspects of enzyme production:  
1) Carbon source utilization - enabling growth on sucrose-rich molasses  
2) Transcriptional regulation - increasing expression of secreted enzyme genes  
3) Secretion efficiency - reducing proteolytic degradation of secreted proteins  
4) Enzyme cocktail composition - supplementing deficient enzymatic activities  

Specific genetic modifications include:  
- The *Trichoderma reesei* Rut-C30 strain serves as the parental strain for all modifications  
- The *xyr1-V821F* allele under *pdc1* promoter control replaces the *ace1* gene  
- The *T. emersonii cel3a* gene under *xyn1* promoter control replaces the *slp1* gene  
- The *A. niger suc1* gene with native regulatory sequences replaces the *pep1* gene  

Key genetic elements modified include:  
- The *xyr1* gene encodes the major activator of cellulase gene expression in *T. reesei*  
- The *ace1* gene encodes a repressor of cellulase gene expression  
- The *slp1* and *pep1* genes encode major extracellular proteases  
- The *cel3a* gene encodes a highly active heterologous β-glucosidase  
- The *suc1* gene encodes an invertase enabling sucrose utilization  

Additional gene modifications may include:  
- Deletion of other protease-encoding genes to further reduce secretome proteolysis  
- Expression of heterologous lytic polysaccharide monooxygenases or ligninases  
- Modification of additional transcriptional regulators such as *ace3* or *lae1*  

Cultivation of the modified *Trichoderma* strains may be performed using various media compositions and fermentation conditions. Preferred embodiments utilize sugarcane molasses as the primary carbon source and molasses-grown yeast cells as an organic nitrogen source. The strains are particularly suitable for fed-batch fermentation processes with controlled pH, dissolved oxygen, and feeding rates.  

### Examples  

The CTBE_R2 strain represents an intermediate embodiment containing four genetic modifications:  
1) Deletion of *slp1*  
2) Insertion of *T. emersonii cel3a* under *xyn1* promoter control  
3) Deletion of *ace1*  
4) Insertion of *xyr1-V821F* under *pdc1* promoter control  

This strain demonstrates a threefold increase in extracellular protein production compared to the parental Rut-C30 strain when cultivated in lactose or glucose media. The specific β-glucosidase activity is increased approximately 26-fold relative to the parental strain.  

The CTBE_R4 strain represents the complete embodiment containing all six genetic modifications:  
1) Deletion of *slp1*  
2) Insertion of *T. emersonii cel3a* under *xyn1* promoter control  
3) Deletion of *ace1*  
4) Insertion of *xyr1-V821F* under *pdc1* promoter control  
5) Deletion of *pep1*  
6) Insertion of *A. niger suc1* with native regulatory sequences  

This strain maintains the enhanced production characteristics of CTBE_R2 while gaining the ability to utilize sucrose as a carbon source. In bioreactor cultivations using molasses-based media, CTBE_R4 achieves extracellular protein titers of 80.6 g L<sup>-1</sup> with a production rate of 0.24 g L<sup>-1</sup> h<sup>-1</sup>.  

Comparison of enzymatic production between CTBE_R2 and CTBE_R4 reveals:  
- Both strains show significantly increased protein titers compared to Rut-C30  
- CTBE_R4 demonstrates approximately 24% higher productivity than CTBE_R2 in bioreactor cultivations  
- Only CTBE_R4 can efficiently utilize sucrose-containing molasses  
- Both strains exhibit dramatically increased specific enzymatic activities  
- The enzyme cocktails from both strains show improved saccharification efficiency  

The complete CTBE_R4 strain represents a significant advance over prior art strains, combining high productivity with compatibility with low-cost production processes. The strain and associated processes provide a compelling alternative to proprietary industrial strains while being fully reproducible through publicly available genetic elements and methodologies.
