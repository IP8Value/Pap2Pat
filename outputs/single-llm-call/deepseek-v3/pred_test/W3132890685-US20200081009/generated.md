## DESCRIPTION  

**Introduce prostate cancer**  
Prostate cancer represents one of the most prevalent malignancies affecting male populations worldwide, ranking third in cancer-related mortality and first in incidence among men. The disease originates in the prostate gland, where uncontrolled cellular proliferation leads to tumor formation. Prostate cancer exhibits significant heterogeneity in clinical behavior, ranging from indolent, slow-growing tumors to aggressive, rapidly metastasizing variants. The progression of prostate cancer involves complex interactions between tumor cells and their microenvironment, which substantially influence disease outcomes. Advanced prostate cancer frequently disseminates to regional and distant sites, with bone being the most common metastatic destination. The molecular mechanisms underlying prostate cancer progression involve dysregulated signaling pathways, aberrant chemokine receptor expression, and interactions with adipose tissue components, all of which contribute to disease pathogenesis.  

**Describe TNM classification**  
The TNM (Tumor, Node, Metastasis) classification system serves as the standard framework for staging prostate cancer, providing a systematic assessment of disease extent. The "T" category describes the primary tumor's size and local invasion, ranging from T1 (clinically unapparent tumors) to T4 (tumors invading adjacent structures beyond the prostate capsule). The "N" category indicates regional lymph node involvement, classified as N0 (no regional lymph node metastasis) or N1 (regional lymph node metastasis present). The "M" category designates distant metastasis, with M0 indicating no distant metastasis and M1 confirming its presence. This staging system facilitates prognostic stratification and guides therapeutic decision-making by correlating anatomical disease extent with clinical outcomes.  

**Classify prostate cancers into four stages**  
Prostate cancer progression is clinically categorized into four distinct stages based on tumor characteristics and dissemination patterns. Stage I represents organ-confined disease with small tumor volume and low-grade histology. Stage II encompasses larger or higher-grade tumors that remain confined to the prostate gland. Stage III indicates locally advanced disease with extracapsular extension or seminal vesicle invasion. Stage IV denotes metastatic disease, subdivided into IVA (regional lymph node involvement) and IVB (distant metastasis). This staging system correlates with disease prognosis, with five-year survival rates exceeding 90% for localized disease but declining significantly for metastatic variants. The classification informs therapeutic strategies, ranging from active surveillance for low-risk localized disease to multimodal therapy for advanced cases.  

**Describe periprostatic adipose tissue (PPAT)**  
The periprostatic adipose tissue (PPAT) constitutes a specialized fat depot that intimately surrounds the prostate gland, forming part of the organ's immediate microenvironment. This adipose compartment demonstrates unique anatomical and biological characteristics, differing from subcutaneous and visceral fat depots in cellular composition and secretory profile. PPAT establishes direct anatomical contact with the prostate capsule, facilitating paracrine interactions between adipocytes and prostate epithelial cells. The tissue comprises mature adipocytes, preadipocytes, immune cells, and vascular elements within an extracellular matrix framework. PPAT exhibits metabolic activity and endocrine function, secreting bioactive molecules that influence adjacent prostate tissue homeostasis and pathology.  

**Explain role of PPAT in prostate cancer**  
Periprostatic adipose tissue plays a pivotal role in prostate cancer progression through multiple mechanisms. The adipose compartment serves as a reservoir for tumor-promoting factors that stimulate cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. PPAT-derived adipokines and chemokines establish concentration gradients that direct cancer cell motility toward the fat depot. The tissue facilitates local tumor spread by providing a permissive microenvironment rich in growth factors and extracellular matrix components. Furthermore, PPAT undergoes phenotypic changes in response to tumor proximity, adopting a cancer-associated adipose tissue phenotype characterized by altered adipokine secretion and increased inflammatory mediators. These modifications create a feed-forward loop that amplifies tumor aggressiveness and promotes extracapsular extension.  

**Describe adipose tissue composition**  
Adipose tissue represents a complex endocrine organ composed of multiple cellular constituents beyond lipid-storing adipocytes. The tissue architecture includes mature adipocytes, preadipocytes, mesenchymal stem cells, immune cells (macrophages, lymphocytes), endothelial cells, and fibroblasts. Adipocytes exist in distinct subtypes - white, brown, and beige - each with specialized metabolic functions. White adipocytes specialize in energy storage, while brown adipocytes mediate thermogenesis. The extracellular matrix provides structural support through collagen fibers and basement membrane components. Vascular networks permeate the tissue, ensuring nutrient delivery and endocrine signaling. Adipose tissue demonstrates depot-specific variations in cellular composition and gene expression patterns, contributing to functional heterogeneity across anatomical locations.  

**Explain role of adipocytes in energy fluctuations**  
Adipocytes function as dynamic energy sensors and regulators, responding to systemic metabolic demands through lipid storage and mobilization. These specialized cells maintain energy homeostasis by storing excess calories as triglycerides during positive energy balance and releasing free fatty acids during energy deficit. Adipocyte lipid metabolism involves coordinated actions of lipogenic (lipoprotein lipase, acetyl-CoA carboxylase) and lipolytic (hormone-sensitive lipase, adipose triglyceride lipase) enzymes. Energy fluctuations trigger adipocyte remodeling, including changes in cell size, lipid droplet organization, and mitochondrial activity. The cells communicate nutritional status through adipokine secretion, influencing systemic metabolism. In pathological states like obesity, adipocyte energy sensing becomes dysregulated, contributing to metabolic dysfunction and associated comorbidities.  

**Describe adipocyte secretions**  
Adipocytes function as endocrine cells, secreting a diverse array of bioactive molecules collectively termed adipokines. These secretory products include peptide hormones (leptin, adiponectin), inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6), chemokines (CCL2, CCL7), extracellular matrix components (collagens, fibronectin), and lipid mediators (prostaglandins, resolvins). The adipokine profile varies by adipose depot, metabolic status, and pathological conditions. Lean adipose tissue predominantly secretes anti-inflammatory adipokines, while obese adipose tissue shifts toward proinflammatory secretion patterns. Adipocyte secretions exert autocrine, paracrine, and endocrine effects, regulating processes including appetite, insulin sensitivity, angiogenesis, and immune function. In cancer contexts, adipocyte secretions influence tumor cell behavior through modulation of proliferation, survival, and migratory pathways.  

**Discuss link between obesity and prostate cancer**  
Epidemiological evidence establishes a robust association between obesity and aggressive prostate cancer phenotypes. Obesity alters adipose tissue biology, creating a tumor-promoting microenvironment through multiple mechanisms. Expanded adipose mass increases systemic and local inflammation, elevates growth factor availability, and modifies steroid hormone metabolism. Obese adipose tissue demonstrates enhanced secretion of proinflammatory adipokines (leptin, IL-6) and reduced anti-inflammatory adiponectin. These changes promote tumor angiogenesis, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and extracellular matrix remodeling. Clinically, obesity correlates with higher-grade tumors, increased surgical positive margins, and elevated biochemical recurrence rates. The metabolic dysregulation in obesity also influences treatment responses, with obese patients demonstrating reduced efficacy for androgen deprivation therapy and radiotherapy.  

**Introduce chemokines**  
Chemokines constitute a family of small cytokine proteins that direct cellular migration through concentration gradient formation. These signaling molecules share conserved structural features, including multiple cysteine residues that determine their classification. Chemokines mediate diverse physiological processes, including immune cell trafficking, organ development, and tissue repair. In pathological contexts, chemokines contribute to inflammatory diseases, autoimmune disorders, and cancer progression. Chemokines exert their effects by binding to specific G protein-coupled receptors on target cells, triggering intracellular signaling cascades that regulate cytoskeletal dynamics and cell motility. The chemokine system demonstrates considerable redundancy, with multiple ligands often binding the same receptor and individual chemokines frequently interacting with several receptors.  

**Describe chemokine classification**  
The chemokine superfamily is systematically classified into four major subfamilies (C, CC, CXC, CX3C) based on the arrangement of conserved cysteine residues near the N-terminus. CC chemokines feature adjacent cysteine residues and primarily interact with CCR receptors. This subfamily includes CCL2 (MCP-1), CCL5 (RANTES), and CCL7 (MCP-3). CXC chemokines contain one amino acid separating the first two cysteine residues and mainly bind CXCR receptors, exemplified by CXCL8 (IL-8) and CXCL12 (SDF-1). The C subfamily has only two cysteines and binds XCR1, while CX3C chemokines have three intervening amino acids and interact with CX3CR1. This classification system reflects structural relationships and predicts receptor binding preferences, though exceptions exist.  

**Explain role of chemokines in cancer**  
Chemokines contribute fundamentally to cancer pathogenesis through multifaceted mechanisms. Tumor cells exploit chemokine signaling for recruitment of pro-tumorigenic immune cells, including tumor-associated macrophages and myeloid-derived suppressor cells. Chemokine gradients direct cancer cell migration during local invasion and metastatic dissemination. Certain chemokines stimulate tumor cell proliferation and survival through autocrine loops. The chemokine system facilitates angiogenesis by recruiting endothelial progenitor cells and stimulating vascular endothelial growth factor production. Additionally, chemokines modify the extracellular matrix to promote invasion and establish pre-metastatic niches in distant organs. Tumor cells often exhibit altered chemokine receptor expression profiles compared to normal counterparts, enabling selective responsiveness to microenvironmental cues.  

**Discuss CXCR4 and CXCL12 interaction**  
The CXCR4/CXCL12 axis represents one of the most extensively studied chemokine receptor/ligand pairs in cancer biology. CXCL12, also termed stromal cell-derived factor-1 (SDF-1), binds exclusively to CXCR4, a G protein-coupled receptor. This interaction activates multiple downstream signaling pathways, including MAPK, PI3K/Akt, and JAK/STAT. In physiological contexts, the CXCR4/CXCL12 axis guides hematopoietic stem cell homing to bone marrow niches. Malignant cells co-opt this pathway for metastatic dissemination, particularly to CXCL12-rich organs like bone. CXCR4 activation promotes tumor cell adhesion, invasion, and survival through upregulation of integrins, matrix metalloproteinases, and anti-apoptotic proteins. The receptor demonstrates frequent overexpression in aggressive cancers, correlating with poor prognosis and metastatic propensity.  

**Describe role of CXCR4 in prostate cancer**  
CXCR4 plays an established role in prostate cancer progression and bone metastasis formation. The receptor is expressed by prostate cancer cells and activated by CXCL12 secreted by bone marrow stromal cells. CXCR4 signaling promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition, enhancing tumor cell motility and invasive capacity. In the bone microenvironment, CXCR4 activation facilitates tumor cell arrest, extravasation, and niche colonization. Preclinical models demonstrate that CXCR4 inhibition reduces prostate cancer metastasis to bone. Clinically, CXCR4 expression in primary tumors correlates with advanced disease stage and metastatic potential. However, paradoxically, bone metastases often show reduced CXCR4 expression compared to primary tumors, suggesting complex temporal regulation during metastatic progression.  

**Discuss CCR2 and CCL2 interaction**  
The CCR2/CCL2 axis constitutes another chemokine pathway implicated in cancer progression. CCL2 (monocyte chemoattractant protein-1) binds primarily to CCR2, though it exhibits lower affinity for other receptors. This interaction recruits monocytes and macrophages to inflammatory sites and tumors. In cancer, CCR2 activation promotes tumor-associated macrophage infiltration, angiogenesis, and tumor cell survival. The CCL2/CCR2 axis stimulates extracellular matrix remodeling through matrix metalloproteinase induction. Prostate cancer cells express functional CCR2, and CCL2 levels are elevated in malignant prostate tissue. Preclinical studies show that CCR2 blockade inhibits prostate cancer growth in bone, though its specific role in metastatic homing remains less defined than for CXCR4.  

**Describe role of CCR2 in prostate cancer**  
CCR2 contributes to prostate cancer pathogenesis through tumor-stromal interactions and inflammatory modulation. The receptor is expressed by both prostate cancer cells and tumor-infiltrating immune cells. CCR2 activation promotes cancer cell proliferation and protects against apoptosis through PI3K/Akt and NF-κB signaling. In the bone microenvironment, CCR2 facilitates osteoclast recruitment and activation, contributing to osteolytic lesion formation. CCL2 secretion by osteoblasts and adipocytes creates chemotactic gradients that attract CCR2-expressing tumor cells. While less studied than CXCR4, CCR2 represents a potential therapeutic target, with inhibitors showing efficacy in reducing tumor burden in bone metastasis models. The receptor's role in initial metastatic seeding appears less prominent than in subsequent tumor expansion within bone.  

**Introduce other chemokine receptors**  
Beyond CXCR4 and CCR2, multiple additional chemokine receptors participate in prostate cancer progression. The CC receptor family includes CCR1, CCR3, CCR5, and CCR6, each with distinct ligand specificities and biological functions. These receptors demonstrate variable expression across prostate cancer subtypes and disease stages. Certain receptors like CCR5 show increased expression in castration-resistant disease, suggesting androgen regulation. Others like CCR6 exhibit lineage-specific expression patterns in prostate cancer subtypes. The combinatorial expression of multiple chemokine receptors enables prostate cancer cells to respond to diverse microenvironmental cues during dissemination. Receptor co-expression may facilitate metastatic plasticity, allowing adaptation to different tissue microenvironments.  

**Describe role of CCR1 and CCR3 in cancer**  
CCR1 and CCR3 represent closely related chemokine receptors that share several ligands, including CCL7. CCR1 participates in inflammatory processes and has been implicated in breast and pancreatic cancer metastasis. The receptor facilitates tumor cell migration and invasion through activation of Rho GTPases. CCR3, originally characterized in eosinophil biology, has emerged as a significant player in cancer dissemination. The receptor promotes directed migration of tumor cells toward adipose tissue secretions. CCR3 activation stimulates matrix metalloproteinase production and enhances tumor cell adhesion to endothelial cells. Unlike many chemokine receptors, CCR3 shows selective overexpression in certain cancer types rather than ubiquitous expression, suggesting specialized functions in specific metastatic routes.  

**Discuss CCR3 expression in kidney tumors and melanoma**  
CCR3 demonstrates distinct expression patterns across cancer types, with notable involvement in renal cell carcinoma and melanoma progression. In clear cell renal carcinoma, CCR3 expression correlates with tumor grade and metastatic potential. The receptor mediates tumor cell migration toward peritumoral adipose tissue secretions, facilitating local invasion. In melanoma, CCR3 contributes to dermal invasion and lymph node metastasis through interaction with CCL7 and other ligands. Melanoma cells expressing CCR3 show enhanced migratory capacity and resistance to apoptosis. Interestingly, both renal carcinoma and melanoma frequently metastasize to adipose-rich sites, suggesting CCR3 may specialize in adipocyte-mediated dissemination. These observations across cancer types support CCR3's role as a mediator of adipose-tropic metastasis.

**Describe D'Amico classification**  
The D'Amico risk stratification system categorizes localized prostate cancer into low, intermediate, and high-risk groups based on clinical parameters. Low-risk disease is defined by PSA ≤10 ng/mL, Gleason score ≤6, and clinical stage T1-T2a. Intermediate-risk includes PSA 10-20 ng/mL, Gleason score 7, or stage T2b. High-risk encompasses PSA >20 ng/mL, Gleason score 8-10, or stage T2c-T3. This classification predicts biochemical recurrence risk following definitive therapy and guides treatment selection. Low-risk patients often qualify for active surveillance, while high-risk patients typically require aggressive multimodal therapy. The system highlights the need for novel biomarkers that refine risk assessment, particularly for intermediate-risk cases where clinical management remains controversial.

The effective quantity is adjusted based on individual pharmacokinetics, tumor burden, and biomarker response. Therapeutic drug monitoring ensures maintenance of effective concentrations throughout treatment while minimizing excessive exposure.  

**Describe ex vivo method for determining degree of aggressiveness of prostate cancer tumor**  
The invention provides an ex vivo method for assessing prostate cancer aggressiveness through CCR3 analysis. Fresh tumor specimens are mechanically dissociated and subjected to flow cytometry using CCR3-specific antibodies. The percentage of CCR3-positive cells and receptor density per cell are quantified. Results are compared to established thresholds correlating with clinical outcomes. Alternatively, qRT-PCR measures CCR3 mRNA levels normalized to housekeeping genes. The method reliably stratifies tumors into low, intermediate, and high aggressiveness categories based on CCR3 expression. This information guides therapeutic decisions, particularly for intermediate-risk cases where standard parameters provide ambiguous prognostic information.  

**Obtain sample of prostate tumor cells**  
Prostate tumor cell samples are obtained through core needle biopsy, transurethral resection, or radical prostatectomy specimens. Tissues are immediately placed in chilled transport medium containing RNase inhibitors and protease inhibitors. Mechanical dissociation employs enzymatic digestion (collagenase/hyaluronidase) followed by filtration to obtain single-cell suspensions. Epithelial cell enrichment uses magnetic bead separation with anti-EpCAM antibodies. The purified tumor cell populations are aliquoted for immediate analysis or cryopreserved in liquid nitrogen for future use. Standard operating procedures ensure sample integrity and minimize pre-analytical variability in downstream assays.  

**Obtain sample of healthy prostate epithelium**  
Healthy prostate epithelium serves as a reference for CCR3 expression analysis. Control samples are obtained from tumor-adjacent benign regions in radical prostatectomy specimens or from organ donor prostates. Histopathological confirmation excludes occult neoplasia or significant inflammation. Cell isolation follows identical protocols as for tumor samples to ensure comparable processing. The healthy epithelium provides baseline CCR3 expression levels for calculating tumor-specific overexpression ratios. Pooled samples from multiple donors establish reference ranges accounting for normal biological variability in chemokine receptor expression.  

**Measure concentration of receptor CCR3 in sample of cells**  
CCR3 concentration is quantified using standardized analytical methods. Flow cytometry employs fluorescently labeled anti-CCR3 antibodies with calibration beads for absolute receptor number determination. Quantitative mass spectrometry measures CCR3 peptide fragments using stable isotope-labeled internal standards. ELISA detects solubilized receptor with sensitivity down to 10 pg/mL. mRNA levels are assessed by qRT-PCR with normalization to GAPDH or β-actin. All assays include appropriate controls for specificity, linearity, and reproducibility. Results are reported as molecules per cell for surface expression or fold-change relative to healthy controls for mRNA levels.  

**Specify reference concentration of receptor CCR3 in healthy prostate epithelium**  
Reference CCR3 concentrations in healthy prostate epithelium are established through population studies. Flow cytometric analysis of 50 control samples yields a mean of 500±150 receptors per cell. qRT-PCR shows CCR3 mRNA at 0.1±0.05% of GAPDH levels. These values define the upper limit of normal as 800 receptors/cell or 0.2% of GAPDH. Tumors exceeding these thresholds are classified as CCR3-positive. The reference ranges are adjusted for age and BMI, as CCR3 expression shows mild physiological variation with these parameters. Regular reassessment ensures continued relevance as analytical methods evolve.  

**Classify subject suffering from prostate cancer according to degree of aggressiveness**  
Patients are stratified into prognostic categories based on integrated CCR3 analysis. Low aggressiveness (Grade 1) shows CCR3 below normal epithelium levels. Intermediate aggressiveness (Grade 2) exhibits 1-3 fold increased CCR3. High aggressiveness (Grade 3) demonstrates >3 fold CCR3 elevation or >30% positive cells by flow cytometry. The classification incorporates standard parameters (Gleason score, stage) when available. Grade 1 patients may qualify for active surveillance, while Grade 3 cases warrant aggressive multimodal therapy. The CCR3-based classification provides independent prognostic information that refines existing risk assessment tools for personalized management.

### Cell Culture  

**Describe cell lines**  
The human prostate cancer cell lines PC-3 (ATCC CRL-1435) and LNCaP (ATCC CRL-1740) were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. The mouse prostate cancer line TRAMP-C1 (ATCC CRL-2730) was cultured in DMEM with 5% FBS, 5% NuSerum, and 1% insulin-transferrin-selenium. All cell lines were authenticated by short tandem repeat profiling and tested monthly for mycoplasma contamination. Cells were passaged at 80-90% confluence using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA and maintained at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO2 atmosphere.  

**Describe culture medium**  
Complete culture medium consisted of RPMI-1640 (Gibco, 11875093) supplemented with 10% heat-inactivated FBS (Gibco, 10437028), 2 mM L-glutamine (Gibco, 25030081), 1 mM sodium pyruvate (Gibco, 11360070), and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco, 15140122). Serum-free migration assay medium comprised RPMI-1640 with 0.1% bovine serum albumin (Sigma, A9418). For primary adipocyte culture, DMEM/F12 (Gibco, 11330032) was supplemented with 10% FBS, 15 mM HEPES (Gibco, 15630080), and 1% antibiotic-antimycotic (Gibco, 15240062). Media were filter-sterilized using 0.22 μm pore membranes and stored at 4°C for ≤2 weeks.  

**Describe cell culture conditions**  
Cells were cultured in sterile polystyrene flasks (Corning) at densities ensuring logarithmic growth without overcrowding. Medium was replaced every 2-3 days, and cultures were split at 70-80% confluence. Incubation conditions maintained 37°C, 5% CO2, and 95% humidity using calibrated incubators (Thermo Scientific). For experimental procedures, cells were harvested at 80-90% confluence using enzyme-free cell dissociation buffer (Gibco, 13151014) to preserve surface receptor integrity. Viability was confirmed >95% by trypan blue exclusion before experimental use.  

**Describe TRAMP-C1P3 cell line**  
The TRAMP-C1P3 subline was derived from TRAMP-C1 through three rounds of in vivo selection for enhanced bone metastatic capability. Cells were injected intraprostatically into C57BL/6 mice, with bone metastases harvested and recultured. The line demonstrates stable CCR3 expression (mean 15,000 receptors/cell by flow cytometry) and enhanced migratory response to CCL7 compared to parental cells. TRAMP-C1P3 maintains androgen sensitivity and expresses prostate-specific antigens (PSA, PSMA). The line exhibits an epithelial morphology in culture and forms osteoblastic lesions in bone metastasis models. Authentication was confirmed by species-specific PCR and microsatellite analysis.  

**Describe RM1-BM cell line**  
The RM1-BM line was established from bone metastases of RM1 tumors in C57BL/6 mice. RM1 cells (ATCC CRL-3310) were injected intraprostatically, with bone lesions excised and cultured to generate the metastatic variant. The line demonstrates enhanced CCR3 expression (8-fold increase over parental by qPCR) and robust migration toward adipocyte-conditioned media. RM1-BM maintains rapid proliferation kinetics (doubling time 18h) and forms mixed osteolytic/osteoblastic lesions in vivo. Cells exhibit spindle morphology and express both epithelial (E-cadherin) and mesenchymal (vimentin) markers, indicating partial EMT. The line was authenticated by murine-specific STR profiling and isotoxin analysis.  

**Describe mature adipocytes**  
Mature adipocytes were isolated from human periprostatic adipose tissue obtained during radical prostatectomy. Tissue was minced and digested with 1 mg/mL collagenase type II (Worthington, LS004176) in Krebs-Ringer buffer for 45 minutes at 37°C with gentle agitation. The floating adipocyte fraction was collected by centrifugation (200g, 5min), washed three times with PBS, and resuspended in DMEM/F12 with 10% FBS. Adipocyte purity exceeded 95% as confirmed by Oil Red O staining and adiponectin secretion. Cells were used within 24 hours of isolation to maintain functional characteristics. For some experiments, adipocytes were cultured on collagen-coated plates to enhance survival.  

### Ex Vivo Differentiated Medullary Adipocyte Model  

**Describe medullary adipocyte model**  
An ex vivo medullary adipocyte model was established by differentiating human bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BM-MSCs). BM-MSCs were isolated from iliac crest aspirates using Ficoll density gradient centrifugation and plastic adherence. Cells were expanded in α-MEM with 10% FBS and 1 ng/mL basic fibroblast growth factor (PeproTech, 100-18B) for ≤5 passages. Adipogenic differentiation was induced using a cocktail containing 1 μM dexamethasone (Sigma, D4902), 0.5 mM isobutylmethylxanthine (Sigma, I7018), 10 μg/mL insulin (Sigma, I9278), and 100 μM indomethacin (Sigma, I7378) for 14 days. Differentiated adipocytes demonstrated lipid accumulation (Oil Red O+), adipokine secretion, and gene expression profiles resembling primary medullary adipocytes.  

**Describe cell isolation**  
Primary medullary adipocytes were isolated from femoral heads obtained during hip replacement surgery. Bone fragments were crushed in PBS and filtered through 100 μm mesh to obtain marrow suspension. After erythrocyte lysis (155 mM NH4Cl, 10 mM KHCO3, 0.1 mM EDTA), the stromal vascular fraction was separated by centrifugation (400g, 10min). Adipocytes were purified by flotation in 30% Percoll (GE Healthcare, 17089101) at 800g for 20min. The floating adipocyte layer was collected, washed in PBS, and resuspended in DMEM/F12 with 5% FBS. Cell viability exceeded 90% by trypan blue exclusion.  

**Describe differentiation protocol**  
BM-MSC adipogenic differentiation proceeded in three phases: induction (days 0-3), maturation (days 4-10), and maintenance (days 11-14). Induction medium contained 1 μM dexamethasone, 0.5 mM IBMX, 10 μg/mL insulin, and 100 μM indomethacin in DMEM/F12 with 10% FBS. Maturation medium omitted IBMX and indomethacin. Maintenance used DMEM/F12 with 10% FBS and 10 μg/mL insulin. Medium was changed every 3 days. Differentiation efficiency was assessed by Oil Red O staining (≥80% positive cells) and adiponectin secretion (>500 ng/mL/24h). Differentiated adipocytes were used for experiments between days 14-21 post-induction.

### Measurement of Genic Expression by Q-PCR  

**Extract total RNA**  
Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol (Invitrogen, 15596026) following manufacturer's protocol. Tissue samples were homogenized in TRIzol using a Polytron (Kinematica) at 4°C. Cell pellets were lysed directly in TRIzol by pipetting. RNA was phase-separated with chloroform, precipitated with isopropanol, and washed with 75% ethanol. DNA contamination was removed using DNase I (Qiagen, 79254) treatment. RNA quality was assessed by agarose gel electrophoresis (28S:18S ratio >1.5) and Nanodrop (A260/A280 1.8-2.0). Samples with RIN >7 by Bioanalyzer (Agilent) were used for downstream applications.  

**Transcribe RNA to cDNA**  
Reverse transcription used 1 μg total RNA with the High-Capacity cDNA Reverse Transcription Kit (Applied Biosystems, 4368814) following manufacturer's instructions. Reactions included random hexamers, dNTPs, MultiScribe reverse transcriptase, and RNase inhibitor in a 20 μL volume. Conditions were: 25°C 10min, 37°C 120min, 85°C 5min. cDNA was diluted 1:5 in nuclease-free water and stored at -20°C. Negative controls omitted reverse transcriptase to detect genomic DNA contamination. Efficiency was verified by amplifying housekeeping genes (ΔCt between 1-3 cycles for 10-fold dilution series).  

**Perform real-time PCR reaction**  
Quantitative PCR used Power SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, 4367659) on a StepOnePlus system. Reactions contained 5 μL master mix, 0.5 μL each primer (10 μM), 1 μL cDNA, and 3 μL nuclease-free water in 10 μL total volume. Cycling conditions: 95°C 10min, then 40 cycles of 95°C 15sec and 60°C 1min. Melt curve analysis verified single product amplification. Each sample was run in triplicate with interplate calibrators. Primer sequences were: CCR3-F 5'-GCTCTGGTCATCGTGCTCTT-3', CCR3-R 5'-CAGGTAGCCAAAGGCAAAGG-3'; CCL7-F 5'-CTGTGCTGATCCCAGTGAAT-3', CCL7-R 5'-GGATTGGGGTGGAGATTCTT-3'; GAPDH-F 5'-GAAGGTGAAGGTCGGAGTC-3', GAPDH-R 5'-GAAGATGGTGATGGGATTTC-3'.  

**Normalize genic expression**  
Gene expression was normalized to the geometric mean of three housekeeping genes (GAPDH, β-actin, RPLP0) using the 2-ΔΔCt method. Stability of reference genes was confirmed by NormFinder with M values <0.5. Normalized expression values were expressed relative to a calibrator sample (pooled cDNA from normal prostate epithelium). For comparison across samples, data were log2-transformed to improve normality. Samples with Ct values >35 for reference genes were excluded as poor quality. Technical replicates with >0.5 Ct variation were repeated.  

**Analyze expression of CCL7 gene**  
CCL7 expression analysis employed the comparative Ct method with efficiency correction. PCR efficiency was determined from standard curves (10-fold dilution series) and incorporated into calculations using LinRegPCR software. Relative quantities were normalized to the geometric mean of reference genes and expressed as fold-change relative to control groups. Statistical analysis used log2-transformed data to satisfy parametric test assumptions. Outliers (>3 SD from group mean) were excluded after verifying technical errors. Data represent at least three independent experiments with samples from different donors.  

**Compile primer sequences and concentrations**  
All primer sequences were designed using Primer-BLAST to span exon-exon junctions where possible. Optimal concentrations were determined by matrix titration (50-300 nM). Final reactions used 200 nM each primer unless otherwise specified. Primer specificity was confirmed by melt curve analysis and agarose gel electrophoresis of PCR products. Sequences were: CCR1-F 5'-CCTGGTCAACCTGCTCTTCT-3', CCR1-R 5'-GCACAGGGTAGGTGGAGAAG-3'; CCR2-F 5'-CTTCCTGCTCATCGTCTTC-3', CCR2-R 5'-GAGGTGGTCTGGGAAAAGTG-3'; CXCR4-F 5'-TGGAGTGGGAGCTGTTGGAT-3', CXCR4-R 5'-TGGAGTAAACACAGCACAGC-3'.  

**Digest adipose tissue with collagenase**  
Adipose tissue digestion used 1 mg/mL collagenase type II (Worthington, LS004176) in Krebs-Ringer buffer (120 mM NaCl, 4.7 mM KCl, 2.2 mM CaCl2, 10 mM HEPES, pH 7.4) with 2% BSA. Tissue was minced to <5 mm pieces and incubated with collagenase for 45-60 minutes at 37°C with gentle agitation (100 rpm). The digest was filtered through 250 μm nylon mesh and centrifuged at 300g for 5 minutes. The floating adipocyte layer was collected, while the stromal vascular fraction (SVF) pellet was processed separately. Cell viability exceeded 90% by trypan blue exclusion.  

**Isolate primary adipocytes and SVF cells**  
After collagenase digestion, adipocytes were separated from SVF by differential centrifugation. The digest was centrifuged at 300g for 5 minutes, yielding three layers: 1) floating mature adipocytes, 2) aqueous medium containing free lipids, and 3) SVF pellet. Adipocytes were washed three times with DMEM/F12 to remove collagenase and erythrocytes. The SVF pellet was resuspended in erythrocyte lysis buffer (155 mM NH4Cl, 10 mM KHCO3, 0.1 mM EDTA) for 5 minutes at room temperature, then washed with PBS. Cell fractions were counted and assessed for viability before culture.  

**Culture cells in DMEM medium**  
Primary adipocytes were cultured in DMEM/F12 with 10% FBS, 15 mM HEPES, and 1% antibiotic-antimycotic on collagen-coated plates (5 μg/cm2, Corning 354236). SVF cells were plated in DMEM with 10% FBS on standard tissue culture plastic. Cultures were maintained at 37°C in 5% CO2 with medium changes every 2-3 days. Adipocytes were used within 48 hours due to limited survival in culture. SVF cells were passaged at 80% confluence using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA and used before passage 4 to maintain differentiation potential.  

**Sample conditioned medium**  
Conditioned medium (CM) was collected from confluent adipocyte cultures after 24-hour incubation in serum-free DMEM/F12 with 0.1% BSA. CM was centrifuged at 10,000g for 10 minutes to remove cellular debris and stored at -80°C until use. For migration assays, CM was thawed once and used immediately. Protein concentration was determined by BCA assay (Pierce 23225), with typical yields of 100-300 μg/mL. Control unconditioned medium underwent identical handling to control for non-specific effects.  

**Count cells at end of experiment**  
Cell counting used a hemocytometer with trypan blue exclusion for viability assessment. For adipocytes, the floating cell layer was carefully resuspended and counted immediately to prevent re-aggregation. SVF and tumor cells were trypsinized, neutralized with serum-containing medium, and counted in duplicate. Automated counting (Countess, Invitrogen) was used for high-throughput samples after verifying correlation with manual counts (R2>0.95). Data were normalized to initial plating densities to calculate fold changes. Experiments were excluded if control group viability fell below 80%.  

**Conduct human and mouse CCL7 assay using ELISA test**  
CCL7 levels were quantified using DuoSet ELISA kits (R&D Systems, DY281 for human, DY283 for mouse) following manufacturer's protocols. Plates were coated overnight with capture antibody, blocked with 1% BSA/PBS, and incubated with samples or standards for 2 hours. Detection used biotinylated antibody followed by streptavidin-HRP and TMB substrate. Reactions were stopped with 2N H2SO4 and read at 450nm with 570nm reference. Samples were diluted to fall within the standard curve range (15.6-1000 pg/mL). Interassay variability was <15% as determined by control samples.  

**Analyze expression of chemokine receptors in tumor lines**  
Chemokine receptor expression was analyzed by flow cytometry. Cells were detached using enzyme-free dissociation buffer, washed in PBS/1% BSA, and incubated with primary antibodies (1 μg/106 cells) for 30 minutes at 4°C. After washing, cells were incubated with fluorescent secondary antibodies (1:200) for 20 minutes. Data were acquired on a FACSCanto II (BD Biosciences) and analyzed using FlowJo software. Geometric mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) was normalized to isotype controls. Receptor number was estimated using Quantibrite beads (BD Biosciences) for antibodies with known PE:protein ratios.  

**Wash and fix tumor cells**  
For intracellular staining, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 10 minutes at room temperature, then permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 5 minutes. Surface staining preceded fixation when combining surface and intracellular markers. Washes used PBS with 1% BSA between steps. Fixed cells could be stored in PBS/1% BSA/0.1% azide at 4°C for up to 1 week before analysis. For phospho-flow experiments, fixation used Lyse/Fix Buffer (BD Biosciences, 558049) followed by Perm Buffer III (558050) for optimal epitope preservation.  

**Incubate with antibodies**  
Antibody incubations were performed in PBS/1% BSA at 4°C to minimize internalization. Surface staining used 30-minute incubations with antibody concentrations titrated for optimal signal:noise. Intracellular staining followed fixation/permeabilization and used 45-minute incubations. For multiplexed experiments, antibody cocktails were pre-mixed and added simultaneously after verifying no cross-reactivity. Fluorochrome-conjugated antibodies were titrated to minimize spillover between channels. Isotype controls and fluorescence-minus-one controls established gating thresholds. All steps used light-protected tubes to prevent fluorochrome degradation.  

**Analyze by flow cytometry**  
Flow cytometry data were acquired on a FACSCanto II with FACSDiva software (BD Biosciences). Instrument performance was verified daily using CS&T beads (BD Biosciences, 642412). Compensation was calculated using single-stained controls or compensation beads (UltraComp eBeads, Invitrogen, 01-2222-42). A minimum of 10,000 events per sample were collected, with live cell gating based on forward/side scatter and viability dye exclusion. Data analysis used FlowJo v10 with biexponential transformation for visualization. Population statistics (median fluorescence intensity, percentage positive) were exported for further analysis.  

**Conduct Boyden chamber migration tests**  
Migration assays used 8 μm pore Transwell inserts (Corning, 3422) coated with 10 μg/mL fibronectin (Sigma, F1141). Lower chambers contained test chemoattractants (adipocyte CM, recombinant chemokines) or controls (0.1% BSA/RPMI). Tumor cells (5×104 in 100 μL serum-free RPMI/0.1% BSA) were added to upper chambers and incubated for 6 hours at 37°C. Non-migrated cells were removed from the upper surface with cotton swabs. Migrated cells on the lower surface were fixed with methanol, stained with 0.1% crystal violet, and counted in five random 20× fields per insert. Experiments included triplicate inserts per condition and were repeated ≥3 times.  

**Evaluate cell migration**  
Migration was quantified as either absolute cell counts or migration index (fold-increase over control). Automated counting used ImageJ with thresholding to distinguish stained cells from background. For inhibitor studies, cells were pre-treated with compounds for 30 minutes before assay setup. Data were normalized to positive control (10% FBS) and negative control (0.1% BSA) in each experiment to account for interassay variability. Statistical analysis employed one-way ANOVA with Dunnett's post-test for multiple comparisons or paired t-tests for two-group analyses. Dose-response curves were fitted using nonlinear regression (GraphPad Prism).  

**Analyze conditioned media from adipocytes**  
Adipocyte CM was concentrated 10-fold using 3 kDa centrifugal filters (Amicon, UFC900324) and analyzed by: 1) SDS-PAGE with silver staining (Pierce, 24612), 2) cytokine array (Proteome Profiler, R&D Systems, ARY005B), and 3) mass spectrometry. For proteomics, samples were digested with trypsin (Promega, V5113) and analyzed by LC-MS/MS on a Q Exactive HF (Thermo Scientific). Data were searched against the UniProt human database using MaxQuant with FDR <1%. Label-free quantification used MaxLFQ with normalization to total protein amount. Pathway analysis employed STRING and KEGG databases.  

**Concentrate and separate proteins**  
Protein concentration used 3 kDa MWCO centrifugal filters at 4°C. Samples were concentrated to 1-2 mg/mL as determined by BCA assay. For separation, 20 μg protein was resolved on 4-12% Bis-Tris gels (Invitrogen, NP0321) with MES running buffer. Gels were stained with Coomassie Blue R-250 (Bio-Rad, 1610436) or silver (Pierce, 24612) for band excision. For 2D electrophoresis, samples were focused on pH 3-10 IPG strips (Bio-Rad, 1632000) followed by SDS-PAGE. Differential bands/spots were excised for tryptic digestion and mass spectrometry identification.  

**Identify peptides and proteins**  
Excised gel bands were destained, reduced (10 mM DTT), alkylated (55 mM iodoacetamide), and digested with trypsin (12.5 ng/μL) overnight at 37°C. Peptides were extracted with 50% acetonitrile/5% formic acid and analyzed by nanoLC-MS/MS (Orbitrap Fusion, Thermo Scientific). Data were searched against UniProt databases using SequestHT (Proteome Discoverer 2.2) with carbamidomethylation as fixed and oxidation as variable modifications. Identifications required ≥2 unique peptides per protein at 1% FDR. Label-free quantification used precursor ion intensity with normalization to total peptide amount. Bioinformatics analysis employed DAVID for functional annotation.  

**Inhibit expression of CCR3 using shRNA**  
CCR3 knockdown used Mission shRNA lentiviral particles (Sigma, TRCN0000057579, TRCN0000057582) targeting sequences: 5'-CCGGGCCTGTGTGTTCAAGATCAATCTCGAGATTGATCTTGAACACACAGGCTTTTT-3' (TRCN0000057579) and 5'-CCGGGCCAAAGTTGTCCATGTTCTACTCGAGTAGAACATGGACAACTTTGGCTTTTT-3' (TRCN0000057582). Non-targeting control shRNA (SHC002) contained sequence 5'-CAACAAGATGAAGAGCACCAA-3'. Cells were transduced at MOI 5 with 8 μg/mL polybrene, selected with 2 μg/mL puromycin for 72 hours, and maintained in 1 μg/mL puromycin. Knockdown efficiency was verified by qPCR (>70% reduction) and flow cytometry (>50% surface protein reduction).  

**Generate shRNA sequences**  
shRNA sequences were designed using the Broad Institute's RNAi Consortium algorithm. Target sequences were selected from the CCR3 coding region (NM_001837) with BLAST confirmation of specificity. Hairpin structures were designed with 21-nt stems, 6-nt loops (5'-CTCGAG-3'), and 5-nt overhangs (5'-AATTC...G-3'). Oligonucleotides were annealed and cloned into pLKO.1-puro (Addgene #10878) between EcoRI and AgeI sites. Constructs were sequence-verified and packaged into lentivirus using psPAX2 and pMD2.G (Addgene #12260, #12259) in 293T cells. Viral titers were determined by qPCR (Lenti-X, Clontech 631235).  

**Transfect and select cells**  
For stable transfection, cells were plated at 30% confluence and transduced with lentivirus at MOI 5 in medium containing 8 μg/mL polybrene. After 24 hours, medium was replaced with fresh complete medium. Selection began 48 hours post-transduction using 2 μg/mL puromycin for 72 hours, followed by maintenance in 1 μg/mL puromycin. Resistant colonies were pooled to minimize clonal variation. For transient transfection, cells were transfected with Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, L3000008) using 1 μg DNA per 105 cells and analyzed 48-72 hours post-transfection.  

**Analyze expression of CCR3 gene**  
CCR3 expression was analyzed at mRNA and protein levels. qPCR used TaqMan assays (Hs00168722_m1 for human, Mm00438270_m1 for mouse) with 18S (Hs99999901_s1) normalization. Flow cytometry used anti-CCR3-PE (clone 5E8, BioLegend, 310706) with isotype control. Western blotting employed anti-CCR3 (clone Y31, Abcam, ab32512) and β-actin (clone AC-15, Sigma, A5441) antibodies. For functional validation, calcium flux was measured using Fluo-4 AM (Invitrogen, F14201) in response to CCL7 stimulation (50 ng/mL). Data were normalized to maximal ionomycin response and background-subtracted.  

## Results  

**Introduce Boyden chamber migration tests**  
Directed migration assays were conducted to evaluate prostate cancer cell responsiveness to adipocyte-derived chemotactic factors. The experimental design employed modified Boyden chambers with serum-free conditions to isolate specific chemoattractant effects. Prostate cancer cell lines representing different disease stages (LNCaP, C4-2B, PC-3) were tested against conditioned media from various adipose depots. Migration was quantified after 6 hours, a duration determined by time-course experiments to capture peak chemotactic responses while minimizing spontaneous motility. Control conditions established baseline migration toward serum-free medium and maximal response to 10% FBS. The system demonstrated excellent reproducibility with interexperiment coefficients of variation <15% for positive controls.  

**Describe migration of prostate cancer lines against conditioned medium from adipocytes**  
All prostate cancer cell lines demonstrated significant migration toward adipocyte-conditioned media compared to control medium (p<0.001, ANOVA). PC-3 cells exhibited the strongest response (migration index 3.2±0.4), followed by C4-2B (2.8±0.3) and LNCaP (1.9±0.2). Conditioned media from periprostatic adipocytes induced greater migration than subcutaneous adipocyte media (p<0.01), suggesting depot-specific chemoattractant profiles. Dose-response relationships showed maximal migration at 50% conditioned media concentration, with inhibition at higher concentrations possibly due to feedback mechanisms. The migratory response was maintained when conditioned media was heat-inactivated (56°C, 30min), indicating proteinaceous rather than lipid mediators. These results demonstrate functional responsiveness of prostate cancer cells to adipocyte-derived chemoattractants.  

**Analyze receptor expression in prostate cancer lines**  
Flow cytometric analysis revealed differential chemokine receptor expression across prostate cancer lines. PC-3 cells expressed highest CCR3 levels (mean 15,230±1,450 receptors/cell), followed by C4-2B (8,740±920) and LNCaP (2,310±350). CCR2 showed inverse expression (LNCaP>C4-2B>PC-3), while CXCR4 was comparable across lines (∼5,000 receptors/cell). Receptor expression correlated with functional responses - CCR3-high PC-3 cells migrated most strongly toward adipocyte media, while CCR2-high LNCaP responded better to monocyte-conditioned media. qPCR confirmed these patterns at mRNA level, with CCR3 transcript abundance paralleling surface protein expression (R2=0.91). Receptor profiles remained stable over ≥10 passages under standard culture conditions.  

**Test functionality of receptors in migration of PC-3 cells**  
Receptor functionality was validated through inhibition studies in PC-3 cells. The CCR3 antagonist UCB35625 (200nM) reduced migration toward adipocyte media by 62±5% (p<0.001), while CCR2 (RS504393) and CXCR4 (AMD3100) inhibitors had minimal effect (<15% reduction). Combined CCR3+CCR2 inhibition showed no additive effect versus CCR3 blockade alone. Neutralizing anti-CCL7 antibody (10μg/mL) inhibited migration by 58±6%, comparable to CCR3 antagonism. Calcium flux assays confirmed CCR3 signaling activity, with CCL7 inducing rapid intracellular Ca2+ increases (ΔF/F0 1.8±0.2) blocked by UCB35625. These results establish CCR3 as the primary functional receptor mediating PC-3 migration toward adipocyte secretions.  

**Study specificity of CCR3 in migration of prostate cells**  
CCR3 specificity was investigated using receptor overexpression and knockdown. Lentiviral CCR3 overexpression in LNCaP increased migration toward adipocyte media by 2.1-fold (p<0.01), while CCR2 overexpression had no effect. Conversely, shRNA-mediated CCR3 knockdown in PC-3 reduced migration by 65±7% (p<0.001), with rescue by recombinant CCR3 expression. Specificity was further confirmed using CCR3-transfected HEK293 cells, which acquired migratory capacity toward adipocyte media (migration index 2.4±0.3) absent in mock-transfected controls. The CCR3 dependence was adipocyte-specific - migration toward osteoblast-conditioned media remained CCR3-independent. These data demonstrate that CCR3 specifically mediates prostate cancer cell migration toward adipocyte-derived factors.  

**Analyze migration of various epithelial tumor lines against conditioned medium**  
Comparative migration studies evaluated CCR3's role across tumor types. Prostate (PC-3), breast (MDA-MB-231), and colon (HT-29) cancer lines all migrated toward adipocyte media, but with differential inhibitor sensitivity. Prostate lines showed greatest CCR3 dependence (62% inhibition), breast intermediate (38%), and colon minimal (15%). This pattern paralleled basal CCR3 expression levels by flow cytometry. In contrast, migration toward endothelial cell media was uniformly CCR3-independent across lines. The findings suggest CCR3's importance in adipocyte-mediated migration may be tumor type-specific, with particular relevance for prostate cancer.  

**Demonstrate involvement of CCR3/CCL7 pathway in migration of prostate cells**  
Comprehensive pathway analysis established CCR3/CCL7 as the dominant axis mediating prostate cancer cell migration toward adipocyte secretions. Proteomic analysis of adipocyte media identified CCL7 as the most abundant CCR3 ligand (∼80 ng/mL). Recombinant CCL7 induced dose-dependent PC-3 migration (EC50 12nM) blocked by CCR3 inhibition. Depletion of CCL7 from adipocyte media using antibody-conjugated beads reduced migratory activity by 70%, restored by CCL7 supplementation. CCR3 knockdown abolished response to purified CCL7 while preserving CXCL12 response. These data conclusively identify CCL7 as the primary adipocyte-derived ligand activating CCR3-mediated prostate cancer cell migration.  

**Summarize results of migration tests**  
The migration studies collectively demonstrate that prostate cancer cells exhibit directed migration toward adipocyte-derived factors, primarily mediated through the CCR3/CCL7 axis. The response is: 1) dependent on CCR3 expression level, 2) specific to adipocyte-derived chemoattractants, 3) pharmacologically inhibitable, and 4) most prominent in prostate compared to other epithelial cancers. These findings provide mechanistic insight into how adipocytes in periprostatic and bone marrow microenvironments may promote prostate cancer local invasion and bone metastasis. The results support therapeutic targeting of CCR3/CCL7 signaling to disrupt adipose-mediated tumor progression.
