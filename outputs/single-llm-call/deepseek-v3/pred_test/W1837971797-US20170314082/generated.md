# DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to recombinant protein production in mammalian cell cultures, particularly in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells. CHO cells have emerged as the predominant host system for industrial-scale production of recombinant proteins due to their capacity for proper protein folding, assembly, and post-translational modifications. The CHO-Dhfr expression system represents the most widely utilized platform for high-level expression of recombinant products in CHO cell lines.  

Gene amplification procedures are commonly employed to enhance recombinant protein production in CHO cells. However, CHO cells exhibit an inherently unstable karyotype characterized by frequent chromosome rearrangements arising from translocations and homologous recombination events, particularly when amplified with methotrexate hydrate (MTX). This genomic instability leads to unstable recombinant protein production observed in 8-63% of all recombinant CHO (rCHO) cell lines utilizing CHO-Dhfr/glutamine synthetase expression systems, both in the presence and absence of selection pressure.  

The need exists for early identification of stable clones during cell line development to improve the efficiency of recombinant protein production. Previous publications have documented the instability of recombinant protein production in CHO cell lines, particularly when propagated in the absence of selective pressure. The present invention addresses this problem by providing a method for selecting suitable candidate cell clones with stable recombinant protein production characteristics.  

The method comprises step a) determining the expression level of RNA from at least two endogenous genes in at least two cell clones. The genes for which expression level is determined include, but are not limited to, E130203B14, BX842664.2/Hist1h3c, Ptpre, Cspg4, and Fgfr2. Optionally, the Vsnl1 gene may also be included in the analysis. The expression level determination involves measuring RNA transcripts of these endogenous genes, providing critical information about the cellular state predictive of recombinant protein production stability.  

The method requires analysis of a minimum of two cell clones but can be advantageously applied to analyze many more clones simultaneously. Notably, the method can be carried out either prior to transfection with the recombinant gene or after transfection, providing flexibility in implementation. The type of recombinant protein produced by the clones is not relevant to the method's effectiveness, although antibodies represent a particularly preferred embodiment as the recombinant protein. In an ideal scenario, the method is applied to clones producing the same type of recombinant protein.  

While the method can be successfully implemented with analysis of just one gene, preferred embodiments involve determining expression levels of multiple genes. The analysis of more than one gene significantly enhances the predictive power of the method. The expression levels may be determined either in parallel or non-parallel fashion, with identical conditions maintained for determining expression levels across all clones. The early log phase of cell growth represents the preferred time point for expression level determination.  

The cell background of the clones is an important consideration, with CHO-K1 cell clones representing particularly preferred host cells. Quantitative RT-PCR serves as the preferred technique for specific detection of gene expression levels. The method involves log2 transformation of expression values followed by normalization to enable meaningful comparisons. Statistical analysis of the expression data facilitates identification of clones with desirable characteristics.  

Selection of cell clones for further expansion is based on the observed upregulation or downregulation patterns of the analyzed genes. When selecting from multiple clones, the best candidate clone is chosen based on optimal expression levels of the marker genes. Particularly preferred embodiments of the method involve defining what constitutes the best expression level profile, taking into account both upregulation and downregulation patterns of specific genes.  

The motivation for determining multiple gene expression levels lies in the enhanced predictive power achieved through combination of expression values. Preferred embodiments involve indirect comparison of expression profiles across clones. The selection process benefits from analysis of multiple clones, with three-dimensional representation of gene expression data and principal component analysis providing particularly powerful analytical tools.  

Further expansion of selected clones represents a critical step in the method. This may involve MTX selection to maintain selective pressure on the recombinant gene. The method is applicable to both ex vivo and in-vitro applications, with artificially modified gene expression through overexpression or downregulation of specific genes being particularly advantageous.  

The host cells for recombinant protein expression are preferably CHO cells, with recombinant protein production achieved through introduction of recombinant genes encoding the desired proteins. The term "comprising" is used in its broadest sense to include all embodiments where the specified elements are present along with optional additional components. The articles "a" and "an" are used to refer to one or more than one of the elements they modify. A suitable candidate cell clone is defined as one exhibiting gene expression patterns predictive of stable recombinant protein production.  

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples present various embodiments and aspects of the invention, demonstrating its practical application and advantages in recombinant protein production.  

### Example 1: General Outline of the Experiment  

The experiment was designed to establish a transcriptome profile of CHO cell clones and identify marker genes related to stable expression of recombinant genes. Six transfected clones were grown in parallel with and without MTX in the media over a 10-week period. Samples were collected at weeks 1, 2 (beginning) and weeks 9, 10 (end) of the study. Productivity measurements and recombinant gene copy number determinations were performed at each time point.  

### Example 2: Detailed Outline of the Experiment  

An expression vector containing the recombinant gene plus antibiotic resistance and recombinant Dhfr (rDhfr) gene was constructed and transfected into the host CHO cell line using nucleofection. Following transfection, antibiotic selection was performed, followed by amplification of the recombinant gene by MTX. Cloning was performed using ClonePix FL technology, resulting in six high-producing clones selected for further study.  

### Example 3: Cell Culture  

The CHO-der1 cell line, adapted for serum-free media and improved growth performance, served as the host cell line. Cells were cultured in suspension in serum-free media supplemented with L-glutamine, maintained at 37°C with 10% CO2 in shaking flasks. Cell concentrations were maintained between 2.0-3.0 × 10^5 cells/ml through twice-weekly passages. Samples for RNA and DNA isolation were collected at mid-log phase (day 3) of weeks 1, 2, 9, and 10.  

### Example 4: DNA Microarray  

Total RNA was isolated, quantified, and assessed for integrity before hybridization to a proprietary CHO-specific DNA microarray containing 61,223 probe sets targeting approximately 26,227 Chinese hamster unique gene IDs. Microarray data processing involved normalization using the robust multichip average algorithm, followed by statistical analysis to identify differentially expressed genes between stable and unstable clones.  

### Example 5: Quantitative Real-Time PCR (RT-qPCR)  

Fourteen differentially expressed genes identified by microarray were verified using RT-qPCR. Primer pairs and TaqMan-MGB® probes were designed to match microarray oligo regions. Relative expression calculations used Actb and Gapdh as reference genes. Data analysis involved log2 transformation of expression values, normalization, and statistical comparison using Welch two-tailed t-tests.  

### Example 6: Results  

Productivity data revealed stable clones showed only 9% variation in productivity over 10 weeks, while unstable clones declined by 67%. Recombinant gene copy numbers declined 45% in stable clones versus 61% in unstable clones. Transcriptome analysis identified 295 differentially expressed genes between stable and unstable clones, with 212 genes specifically associated with stability independent of MTX presence.  

### Example 7: Presence of MTX  

Analysis identified 199 genes differentially expressed between clones grown with and without MTX, with 83 genes common to both stability and MTX analyses. Five genes (E130203B14, BX842664.2/Hist1h3c, Ptpre, Cspg4, Fgfr2) were identified as unaffected by MTX presence while showing differential expression between stable and unstable clones.  

### Example 8: CRISPR/CAS9 Experiment  

A CRISPR/CAS9 genome editing experiment was designed to validate the functional role of identified marker genes in recombinant protein production stability. The experiment targeted specific genomic loci to modify expression of candidate genes, with predicted outcomes including altered stability profiles in edited cell lines.
