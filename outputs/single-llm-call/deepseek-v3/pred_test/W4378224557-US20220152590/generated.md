## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to a methane oxidation catalyst for reducing methane emissions in exhaust streams, particularly from lean-burn natural gas engines. More specifically, the invention pertains to a catalyst composition comprising palladium (Pd), platinum (Pt), and zirconium (Zr) supported on a mesoporous silica (SBA-15) material, exhibiting enhanced hydrothermal stability, sulfur resistance, and methane conversion efficiency.  

## BACKGROUND  

Natural gas (NG) is increasingly utilized as a transitional fuel in heavy-duty lean-burn engines due to its abundance, cost-effectiveness, and reduced greenhouse gas emissions compared to conventional fuels. However, lean-burn NG engines produce significant unburned methane ("methane slip") in exhaust streams, which is problematic due to methane's high global warming potential—approximately 86 times that of CO₂ over a 20-year period.  

Current catalytic solutions for methane oxidation, primarily Pd-based catalysts, suffer from limitations such as water inhibition and sulfur poisoning. Water inhibits catalytic activity by forming stable Pd(OH)₂ species or blocking oxygen interchange between the support and active Pd sites. Sulfur compounds in exhaust streams react with Pd to form inactive palladium sulfur oxides (Pd(SOₓ)), drastically reducing catalytic efficiency.  

While modifications such as Pt addition or support doping with TiO₂ or ZrO₂ have been explored, existing catalysts still exhibit insufficient stability and activity under real-world exhaust conditions. There remains a need for a methane oxidation catalyst that combines high methane conversion efficiency with long-term hydrothermal stability and sulfur resistance.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a methane oxidation catalyst comprising Pd, Pt, and Zr supported on mesoporous SBA-15 silica, wherein the catalyst exhibits superior methane conversion, hydrothermal stability, and sulfur resistance.  

In one embodiment, the catalyst comprises:  
- A support material of SBA-15 doped with 5–15 wt% Zr.  
- Active phases of 2 wt% Pd and 4 wt% Pt.  

The catalyst is prepared via incipient wetness impregnation, wherein Zr is first loaded onto SBA-15, followed by sequential impregnation of Pt and Pd, with intermediate calcination steps. The resulting catalyst demonstrates a T₅₀ (temperature at 50% methane conversion) below 500°C even after aging in sulfur- and water-containing exhaust streams.  

Key advantages of the invention include:  
1. **Enhanced Sulfur Resistance**: Zr acts as a sulfur scavenger, protecting Pd and Pt active sites from poisoning.  
2. **Hydrothermal Stability**: The hydrophobic nature of SBA-15 mitigates water inhibition.  
3. **High Methane Conversion**: The synergistic effect of Pt-Pd-Zr on SBA-15 ensures efficient methane oxidation under lean-burn conditions.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Methane Oxidation Catalyst  

The catalyst comprises a mesoporous SBA-15 support doped with Zr (5–15 wt%), loaded with 2 wt% Pd and 4 wt% Pt. The SBA-15 support provides high surface area (≥ 400 m²/g) and pore volume (≥ 0.5 cm³/g), ensuring optimal metal dispersion.  

### Support Material  

The SBA-15 support is synthesized via the Pluronic P123-templated method, yielding a hexagonal mesoporous structure with pore sizes of 3.8–5.6 nm. Zr doping is achieved via incipient wetness impregnation using zirconium(IV) oxynitrate hydrate, followed by calcination at 550°C.  

### Active Phases  

The active metals (Pd and Pt) are loaded sequentially via incipient wetness impregnation using palladium(II) nitrate and tetraammineplatinum(II) nitrate precursors. The final catalyst composition is denoted as 2Pd/4Pt/xZr/SBA-15, where x = 5, 10, or 15 wt% Zr.  

### Weight Ratio of Pt:Pd  

The optimal Pt:Pd weight ratio is 2:1 (4 wt% Pt : 2 wt% Pd). This ratio balances cost and performance, as Pt enhances sulfur resistance while Pd provides primary methane oxidation activity.  

### Concentration of Pt and Pd  

The total metal loading is 6 wt% (2 wt% Pd + 4 wt% Pt). This loading ensures sufficient active sites for methane conversion without excessive cost.  

### T₅₀ Value  

The catalyst exhibits a T₅₀ below 400°C when fresh and below 500°C after 40 hours of aging in sulfur- and water-containing exhaust streams.  

### Method of Preparation  

1. **Incipient Wetness Impregnation (IWI)**:  
   - Zr is first impregnated onto SBA-15, followed by drying (120°C) and calcination (550°C).  
   - Pt is then impregnated, dried, and calcined.  
   - Pd is impregnated last, followed by final calcination.  

2. **Alternative Methods**:  
   - Wet impregnation or simultaneous addition of metals may also be employed, though sequential IWI yields superior dispersion.  

### Calcination  

Calcination is performed at 550°C for 6 hours after each metal loading step to ensure proper oxide formation and metal-support interaction.  

### Gas Stream Composition  

The catalyst operates in exhaust streams containing:  
- 1 vol% CH₄  
- 10 vol% O₂  
- 6 vol% CO₂  
- 10 ppm SO₂  
- 10 vol% H₂O  
- Balance N₂  

### Methane Conversion  

The catalyst achieves >85% methane conversion at 500°C under fresh conditions and maintains >55% conversion after 40 hours of aging.  

### Sulfur Resistance  

Zr doping traps SO₂, preventing Pd and Pt poisoning. XPS data confirm reduced Pd(SOₓ) formation in Zr-doped catalysts.  

### Hydrothermal Stability  

The hydrophobic SBA-15 support minimizes water adsorption, while Zr stabilizes Pt in higher oxidation states (Pt²⁺, Pt⁴⁺), enhancing activity.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Catalysts with Pd and Pt on a Lanthanum-Doped Alumina Exhibit Enhanced Methane Conversion After Aging in the Presence of Sulfur and Water  

A 2Pd/4Pt/15Zr/SBA-15 catalyst was prepared via sequential IWI. After aging for 40 hours at 500°C in a sulfur- and water-containing stream, the catalyst maintained a T₅₀ of 481°C, compared to 581°C for an undoped counterpart.  

### Example 2: A Catalyst with a Lanthanum-Doped Alumina Support Exhibits Higher Activity in the Presence of Excess Water Vapor Than a Catalyst with an Alumina Support Not Doped with Lanthanum  

The 2Pd/4Pt/15Zr/SBA-15 catalyst showed 60% methane conversion after aging, while an undoped SBA-15 catalyst degraded to 30% conversion under identical conditions.  

### Example 3: Sulfur Resistance and Hydrothermal Stability of Catalysts Having an Alumina Support Doped with Lanthanum at Different Weight Ratios of Platinum and Palladium  

Catalysts with 10–15 wt% Zr retained >55% methane conversion after aging, whereas 5 wt% Zr catalysts showed only marginal improvement over undoped catalysts.  

### Example 4: Effect of Calcination Temperature on Catalyst Activity  

Calcination at 550°C optimized metal-support interaction, whereas lower temperatures (e.g., 300°C) resulted in insufficient oxide formation.  

### Example 5: Effect of Method Preparation on Catalyst Activity  

IWI-prepared catalysts exhibited superior activity compared to wet impregnation, due to better metal dispersion.
