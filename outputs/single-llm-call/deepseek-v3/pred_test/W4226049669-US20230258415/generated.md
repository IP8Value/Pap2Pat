# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of heat transfer enhancement in fluid systems. More specifically, the invention pertains to liquid-infused surfaces (LIS) with precisely engineered micro- and nano-scale surface textures that simultaneously enhance heat transfer while reducing drag in both laminar and turbulent fluid flows. The disclosed technology finds particular application in heat exchangers, microprocessor cooling systems, and other thermal management applications where efficient heat transfer with minimal energy input is desired.  

## BACKGROUND  

Enhancing heat transfer while minimizing drag in fluid systems has remained a persistent challenge in thermal engineering. Conventional approaches to increase heat transfer typically involve using rough or modified walls with features such as grooves, blades, ridges, or corrugations. While these surface modifications increase heat flux, they simultaneously increase momentum transport, resulting in higher drag forces and greater energy requirements for fluid pumping. Miniaturization of heat transfer systems provides another approach by increasing surface-to-volume ratios, but this also leads to increased wall friction and greater pressure drops.  

Prior art solutions such as superhydrophobic surfaces (SHS) have demonstrated some success in reducing friction while maintaining the benefits of reduced system size. However, these approaches have significant limitations, particularly when the thermal conductivity of the solid texture is similar to the contiguous liquid. Existing technologies struggle to achieve simultaneous heat transfer enhancement and drag reduction, especially in practical applications where the thermal conductivity ratios between solid and liquid phases cannot be precisely controlled. There remains an unmet need for heat transfer surfaces that can provide enhanced thermal performance across a wide range of operating conditions while maintaining or reducing fluid drag.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a system and method for enhanced heat transfer using liquid-infused surfaces (LIS) with precisely engineered micro- and nano-scale textures. The system comprises a substrate having a plurality of grooves or cavities formed in its surface, with an infusing liquid disposed within these surface features. The external working fluid flows over this liquid-infused surface, creating dynamic interfaces that induce beneficial flow characteristics while enhancing heat transfer.  

Key aspects of the system include the substrate with precisely dimensioned grooves, the selection of appropriate infusing liquids, and the working fluid flowing over the surface. The grooves may have various cross-sectional shapes including rectangular, U-shaped, V-shaped, or pyramidal configurations, with dimensions ranging from micro- to nano-scale. The infusing liquid is selected based on its thermal conductivity, viscosity, and surface tension properties relative to both the substrate material and the working fluid.  

The method of providing increased heat transfer involves forming the textured surface on a substrate, infusing the surface features with an appropriate liquid, and directing the working fluid flow over the infused surface. Variations of the method allow for optimization based on specific application requirements, including adjustments to groove geometry, infusing liquid properties, and flow conditions. The invention provides particular advantages when the thermal conductivity of the substrate material is similar to or less than that of the infusing liquid (κ_s ≤ κ_i), where conventional approaches typically fail to provide heat transfer enhancement.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The principles of the present invention relate to the discovery that carefully designed liquid-infused surfaces can simultaneously enhance heat transfer while reducing drag in both laminar and turbulent flows. The scope of the invention encompasses various embodiments of liquid-infused heat transfer surfaces, methods for their fabrication and use, and specific applications where enhanced thermal performance is desired.  

For purposes of this disclosure, certain terms are defined as follows: "liquid-infused surface" (LIS) refers to a textured surface where grooves or cavities are filled with an infusing liquid; "working fluid" refers to the primary fluid flowing over the LIS; "dispersive convection" refers to heat transfer caused by recirculation within the surface textures; and "random convection" refers to turbulent heat transfer mechanisms in the bulk flow.  

Exemplary embodiments of the invention include systems for increasing heat transfer through the use of liquid-infused surfaces. The substrate contains precisely engineered grooves or cavities with dimensions ranging from nanometers to micrometers. The grooves may have various cross-sectional shapes including rectangular configurations with sharp corners, rounded corner grooves, U-shaped profiles, V-shaped profiles, or pyramidal geometries. Groove orientation may be transverse, longitudinal, or at any angle relative to the primary flow direction. The distance between grooves (pitch) is carefully controlled to optimize thermal and fluid dynamic performance.  

The substrate material is selected based on its thermal conductivity relative to the infusing liquid. Preferred materials include metals, polymers, ceramics, or composites with thermal conductivities similar to or less than the infusing liquid (κ_s ≤ κ_i). The working fluid may be any heat transfer fluid including water, oils, liquid metals, or refrigerants. The infusing liquid is selected based on its thermal conductivity, viscosity, and surface tension properties, with particular attention to its conductivity ratio with the substrate material.  

Design criteria for the grooves and infusing liquid include considerations of thermal conductivity ratios, viscosity ratios, surface tension, and geometric parameters. The system maintains thermal communication with heat-generating or heat-absorbing components such as semiconductors or sensors through the substrate material.  

The method for providing increased heat transfer involves several steps: forming a textured surface with precise groove dimensions and spacing on a substrate; selecting and infusing an appropriate liquid into the surface textures based on the working fluid and thermal requirements; and directing the working fluid flow over the liquid-infused surface. The method may be modified based on specific application needs, including adjustments to groove geometry, infusing liquid properties, or flow conditions.
