## BACKGROUND  

Nucleosomes constitute the fundamental repeating unit of chromatin, comprising an octamer of histone proteins (H2A, H2B, H3, and H4) around which 147 base pairs of DNA are wrapped. Recent advances in cancer genomics have identified recurrent single-allele mutations in histones, termed "oncohistones", across diverse malignancies. These oncohistone mutations typically occur within the highly conserved histone fold domain (HFD) and generally represent a small fraction of the total histone pool within affected cells.  

Of particular relevance to the present invention, mutations in the histone fold domain of canonical H2A histones have been identified in various cancers. These mutations frequently disrupt critical nucleosome structural features, including DNA contact sites (e.g., R29Q substitutions) and regions involved in inter-nucleosomal interactions (e.g., the acidic patch). Such alterations reduce nucleosome stability in vitro and perturb chromatin organization in vivo, creating a permissive environment for oncogenic transformation when combined with other oncogenic mutations.  

The human genome encodes a specialized class of histone variants known as short histone H2A variants (sH2As), which are predominantly expressed during spermatogenesis. These sH2As exhibit several distinctive features compared to canonical H2As, including rapidly evolving sequences, divergent histone fold domains, modified acidic patches, and truncated C-terminal tails. The most well-characterized member of this family, H2A.B, forms unique nucleosomes that wrap approximately 120 base pairs of DNA and demonstrates pronounced nucleosome destabilizing properties.  

Notably, sequence analysis reveals that wild-type sH2As naturally possess many of the same amino acid substitutions that occur as cancer-associated mutations in canonical H2A histones. This observation, combined with emerging evidence linking nucleosome instability to oncogenesis, suggests that aberrant expression of sH2As outside their normal testicular context may confer oncohistone activity. Supporting this hypothesis, H2A.B transcripts have been detected in Hodgkin's lymphoma, and lymphoma cells expressing H2A.B exhibit accelerated growth rates compared to H2A.B-negative counterparts.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides methods for treating lymphoma in subjects expressing short histone H2A variants (sH2As). In one aspect, the method comprises detecting expression of at least one sH2A variant in a biological sample obtained from the subject, wherein elevated sH2A expression relative to control samples indicates susceptibility to treatment with anthracycline agents; and administering a therapeutically effective amount of an anthracycline agent to the subject exhibiting elevated sH2A expression.  

In particular embodiments, the sH2A variant is selected from the group consisting of H2A.B.1.1 (H2AFB2), H2A.B.1.2 (H2AFB3), and H2A.B.2 (H2AFB1). The detection of sH2A expression may be performed using techniques including but not limited to RNA sequencing, quantitative PCR, immunohistochemistry, or immunoassay. The lymphoma may be of various types, including Hodgkin's lymphoma, diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, or B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia.  

The invention further provides methods for inducing cytotoxicity in lymphoma cells expressing sH2A variants by administering an anthracycline agent to a subject identified as having elevated sH2A expression. Additionally, the invention encompasses methods for inhibiting proliferation of cancer cells through administration of anthracycline agents to subjects exhibiting aberrant sH2A expression.  

The anthracycline agents suitable for use in these methods include doxorubicin, daunorubicin, epirubicin, idarubicin, valrubicin, and aclarubicin, as well as pharmacologically active analogs and derivatives thereof. Administration may be performed via various routes including intravenous, intraarterial, or oral delivery, with dosing schedules adjusted according to clinical parameters and therapeutic response.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention is based on the discovery that short histone H2A variants (sH2As), particularly members of the H2A.B family, possess inherent oncohistone properties when expressed outside their normal testicular context. These sH2A variants exhibit nucleosome destabilizing characteristics that mirror cancer-associated mutations in canonical H2A histones, including modifications at critical DNA contact sites and disruption of inter-nucleosomal interactions.  

Aberrant expression of sH2As, particularly H2A.B variants, has been identified in a diverse array of malignancies, with particularly high frequency in certain lymphomas. Transcriptomic analyses reveal that approximately 50% of diffuse large B-cell lymphomas (DLBCLs) exhibit H2A.B expression, along with significant proportions of Hodgkin's lymphomas and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemias. This expression pattern is associated with distinct molecular signatures, including altered splicing patterns and upregulation of cancer-testis antigens.  

The invention provides methods for treating lymphoma in subjects expressing sH2A variants. Lymphomas treatable according to the methods include Hodgkin's lymphoma (both classical and nodular lymphocyte-predominant types), non-Hodgkin's lymphomas (including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, follicular lymphoma, mantle cell lymphoma, and Burkitt lymphoma), and lymphoblastic leukemias. Both adult and pediatric forms of these malignancies are encompassed within the scope of the invention.  

The treatment method involves several key steps. First, a biological sample is obtained from the subject suspected of having or diagnosed with lymphoma. Suitable sample types include whole blood, serum, plasma, lymph node tissue, bone marrow aspirate, or tumor biopsy specimens. The sample is then analyzed to detect expression levels of at least one sH2A variant, with particular emphasis on H2A.B family members (H2AFB1, H2AFB2, and H2AFB3).  

Detection of sH2A expression may be accomplished through various methodologies. Nucleic acid-based techniques include quantitative reverse transcription PCR (qRT-PCR), RNA sequencing, microarray analysis, or nucleic acid hybridization assays. Protein-based detection methods encompass immunohistochemistry, western blotting, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), or mass spectrometry-based proteomic approaches. For clinical applications, detection thresholds are established relative to appropriate control samples, with expression levels exceeding 1.5 transcripts per million (TPM) by RNA sequencing or equivalent thresholds by other methods considered indicative of significant sH2A expression.  

Upon identification of elevated sH2A expression, the subject is administered a therapeutically effective amount of an anthracycline agent. Anthracyclines represent a class of chemotherapeutic compounds characterized by a tetracyclic quinone structure and amino sugar moiety. These agents exert cytotoxic effects through multiple mechanisms, including intercalation into DNA, inhibition of topoisomerase II, generation of reactive oxygen species, and induction of DNA damage response pathways.  

Preferred anthracycline agents for use in the invention include doxorubicin, daunorubicin, epirubicin, idarubicin, valrubicin, and aclarubicin. The invention also encompasses structurally modified analogs and derivatives of these compounds that retain therapeutic activity against sH2A-expressing lymphomas. Pharmaceutical compositions may be formulated with appropriate excipients, stabilizers, or delivery vehicles to enhance stability, bioavailability, or tissue targeting.  

Administration of anthracycline agents follows established protocols adjusted for patient-specific factors. Typical dosing ranges include 40-75 mg/m² for doxorubicin, 30-60 mg/m² for daunorubicin, and 60-100 mg/m² for epirubicin, administered in repeated cycles with appropriate intervals for hematologic recovery. Dose adjustments may be made based on factors including patient age, organ function, prior therapies, and observed toxicities. Administration routes primarily include intravenous infusion, though alternative delivery methods such as intraarterial injection or liposomal formulations may be employed in specific clinical scenarios.  

The invention further provides methods for inducing cytotoxicity specifically in lymphoma cells expressing sH2A variants. The nucleosome destabilizing properties of sH2As create a unique chromatin environment that enhances susceptibility to anthracycline-mediated DNA damage. This selective vulnerability allows targeted eradication of malignant cells while potentially sparing normal tissues with minimal sH2A expression.  

Additionally, the invention encompasses methods for inhibiting proliferation of cancer cells through combined detection of sH2A expression and subsequent anthracycline administration. The anti-proliferative effects result from both direct cytotoxicity and interference with cell cycle progression, particularly during S-phase where sH2A-expressing cells demonstrate accelerated transit.

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention but are not intended to limit its scope.  

Example 1 demonstrates aberrant upregulation of sH2A variants, particularly H2A.B family members, across diverse cancer types. Transcriptomic analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) dataset reveals H2A.B expression in approximately 50% of diffuse large B-cell lymphomas (DLBCLs), with significant representation in uterine corpus endometrial carcinomas, bladder cancers, and cervical squamous cell carcinomas. Comparative sequence analysis shows that wild-type sH2As naturally possess amino acid substitutions equivalent to common cancer-associated mutations in canonical H2A histones, including modifications at critical nucleosome stability determinants.  

Example 2 characterizes H2A.B expression patterns in lymphoid malignancies. Analysis of multiple B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) datasets identifies H2A.B expression in 6-13% of specimens. Cancer Cell Line Encyclopedia (CCLE) data demonstrates particularly high frequency of H2A.B expression in lymphoma-derived cell lines, with 70% of Hodgkin's lymphoma and 25% of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma cell lines exhibiting detectable H2A.B transcripts.  

Example 3 investigates molecular consequences of H2A.B expression in cancer. Transcriptome analysis reveals association between H2A.B expression and distinct splicing patterns across multiple cancer types, including reduced utilization of alternative cassette exons and proximal polyadenylation sites. In B-ALL specimens, H2A.B expression correlates with decreased intron retention events. These findings suggest widespread effects on RNA processing in H2A.B-expressing malignancies.  

Example 4 evaluates therapeutic vulnerability of H2A.B-expressing lymphomas to anthracycline agents. Cell viability assays demonstrate selective cytotoxicity of aclarubicin in lymphoma cell lines expressing H2A.B, with significantly greater sensitivity compared to conventional chemotherapeutics such as etoposide or doxorubicin. Mechanistic studies reveal enhanced DNA damage response and apoptosis induction in H2A.B-positive cells following anthracycline exposure.  

Example 5 examines clinical correlates of H2A.B expression in lymphoma patients. Retrospective analysis of treatment outcomes shows improved response rates to anthracycline-containing regimens in H2A.B-positive lymphomas compared to H2A.B-negative cases. These findings support the utility of sH2A expression as a predictive biomarker for anthracycline sensitivity in lymphoid malignancies.
