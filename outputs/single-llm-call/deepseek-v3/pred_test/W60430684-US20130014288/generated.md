## BACKGROUND  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates to membrane proteins and methods for monitoring their trafficking and localization. Membrane proteins serve critical functions in cellular communication, transport, and signaling by spanning lipid bilayers to connect extracellular and intracellular environments. Despite their biological importance, studying membrane protein dynamics has been hindered by limitations in existing reporter systems. Conventional approaches utilizing reporter-tagged membrane proteins often suffer from poor signal-to-noise ratios, labor-intensive labeling protocols, and inability to selectively monitor surface-exposed populations.  

Existing reporter polypeptides such as fluorescent proteins and epitope tags have been fused to membrane proteins to enable visualization. However, these reporters frequently disrupt native protein folding, trafficking, and function. Additionally, they lack the capacity to distinguish between intracellular and surface-localized pools of the target protein. The deficiencies of current reporter-tagged membrane proteins create an urgent need for improved systems that preserve protein function while enabling specific detection of surface populations.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel recombinant protein constructs comprising a membrane protein, a reporter polypeptide, and a membrane-spanning linker domain that connects them. The membrane protein may be any integral membrane protein, including but not limited to transporters, channels, and receptors. The reporter polypeptide is preferably a fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) that binds and activates otherwise non-fluorescent small molecule fluorogens.  

The membrane-spanning linker domain is a critical component that positions the reporter polypeptide in the appropriate cellular compartment for detection. In preferred embodiments, the linker is derived from platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR) transmembrane domains or synthetic sequences designed to span lipid bilayers. The linker connects to either the N- or C-terminus of the membrane protein, with N-terminal fusions being particularly advantageous for certain applications.  

Exemplary FAP reporters include HL1.1-TO1 and HL4-MG, which bind malachite green derivatives and other fluorogens with high affinity and specificity. Epitope tags may also be incorporated to facilitate additional detection methods. The invention enables two powerful assays: a surface fluorescence depletion assay that selectively monitors plasma membrane-localized proteins, and an internal fluorescence accumulation assay that tracks intracellular pools. Together these form a genetically encoded biosensor system for comprehensive analysis of membrane protein trafficking.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The following describes various non-limiting embodiments of the invention. It should be understood that modifications and variations may be made without departing from the scope of the invention, and that features from different embodiments may be combined. Any conflicting material is superseded by this disclosure, and the right to amend claims is expressly reserved.  

As used herein, "non-cytosolic" refers to protein domains oriented toward extracellular spaces or organelle lumens rather than the cytoplasm. An "amino acid" includes all naturally occurring and synthetic variants. A "ligand" is any molecule that binds specifically to another, including antibodies and their fragments. "Recombinantly linked" means joined through genetic engineering techniques. An "epitope" is a molecular region recognized by an antibody.  

The reporter component may be any polypeptide that generates a detectable signal, either directly or through interaction with another molecule. The membrane-spanning linker is a hydrophobic sequence sufficient to traverse a lipid bilayer, typically 15-30 amino acids in length. Isolated nucleic acids encoding these components are provided, including sequences with at least 80% homology to the exemplified constructs.  

A key embodiment utilizes the fluorogen-activating protein (FAP) system. FAPs are engineered antibody fragments that bind and dramatically activate fluorogenic dyes. Exemplary fluorogens include malachite green derivatives with polyethylene glycol linkers (MG-11p) for cell-impermeant applications or esterified versions (MG-ester) for total cellular labeling. These small molecules are non-fluorescent in solution but exhibit >15,000-fold fluorescence enhancement upon FAP binding.  

The invention demonstrates particular utility for monitoring dynamic processes such as membrane protein trafficking. In one application, internalization of the β2 adrenergic receptor (B2AR) was visualized in real-time. Another embodiment provides a corrector-response assay for cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR), enabling quantitative measurement of pharmacological rescue. The system allows visualization of membrane proteins and monitoring of their trafficking in living cells with unprecedented specificity and temporal resolution.  

## EXPERIMENTAL  

### Materials and Methods  

Standard molecular biology techniques were used for all cloning procedures. HEK293, NIH 3T3, and C2C12 cell lines were maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. Transfections were performed using Minis TransIT1-LT1 Transfection Reagent according to manufacturer protocols.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

The expression vector pDisplaySacLac2 was constructed by amplifying the SacB gene using PCR with primers introducing appropriate restriction sites. The amplicon was cloned into pDisplayBlue using standard ligation techniques. Sequence verification confirmed the integrity of all constructs.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

A retroviral expression vector pBabeSacLac2 was generated by inserting SfiI-flanked open reading frames into the backbone. Seven different membrane protein ORFs were amplified by PCR and cloned into either pCR2.1-TOPO or pCR-Blunt II-TOPO vectors for sequencing. The verified fragments were then subcloned into the final expression vectors.  

Cell lines were transfected using optimized protocols with Minis TransIT1-LT1 reagent. For stable expression, the Phoenix Ecotropic Packaging System was used to produce transducing particles according to established methods. Fluorescence microscopy was performed on a Nikon Ti eclipse confocal system with appropriate filter sets for the fluorogens used.  

The B2AR internalization assay involved treating cells with agonist followed by time-lapse imaging of FAP fluorescence redistribution. Images were acquired every 15 seconds to capture the dynamic internalization process. Quantitative analysis was performed using ImageJ software to measure fluorescence intensity changes in defined cellular regions.  

Histograms of fluorescent images demonstrated specific labeling of surface proteins with minimal background. The FAP system enabled expression of human membrane proteins with N-terminal tags while preserving function. For the beta2 adrenergic receptor, vectors expressing B2AR with FAP or fusion protein domains showed expected membrane localization in fluorescence micrographs.  

The glucose transporter 4 (GLUT4) was similarly tagged and expressed in C2C12 cells. Fluorescence micrographs demonstrated proper trafficking to the plasma membrane and intracellular compartments. For cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR), constructs with extracellular FAP domains maintained channel function while enabling optical detection.  

The system enabled visualization of F508del-CFTR rescue by corrector compounds. Signal internalization assays selectively visualized surface-exposed protein, while the modular design facilitated generation of various FAP-tagged fusion proteins. Dynamic trafficking of proteins in living cells was monitored, and assays detecting protein translocation were developed.  

The FAP-CFTR platform provided a unique fluorescence assay for CFTR surface expression. Functional activity of FAP-CFTR was confirmed by iodide efflux assays. Corrector compounds CFFT-002 and C18 were tested using this system, with combination treatments showing synergistic effects.  

Flow cytometry quantified corrector efficacy by measuring MG-11p fluorescence activation. Immunoblotting confirmed biochemical rescue of F508del-CFTR, showing maturation of glycosylation patterns after corrector treatment. Microscope image acquisition protocols were optimized for both surface and total protein detection.  

Statistical analysis demonstrated significant improvements in CFTR trafficking with corrector combinations compared to single agents. Reagents including MG fluorogens and corrector compounds were obtained from verified sources. The system enabled detection of F508del-CFTR rescue by both pharmacological correctors and low temperature incubation.  

### Summary of Corrector Efficacy from FAP Labeling and Functional Assays  

Data from multiple assays consistently showed superior efficacy of corrector combinations. FIG. 13A illustrates the flow cytometry results demonstrating enhanced surface expression with CFFT-002 + C18 treatment. FIG. 13B shows corresponding functional rescue measured by iodide efflux.  

The C4 corrector showed limited efficacy as a single agent, while CFFT-002 provided intermediate improvement. The C18 corrector demonstrated the strongest single-agent activity. Combinations of correctors, particularly CFFT-002 + C18, showed synergistic effects exceeding individual compound actions.  

Immunoblot analysis confirmed these findings at the biochemical level, showing increased mature glycosylation of CFTR with corrector combinations. The FAP reporter polypeptide provided unique advantages including single-step labeling, live-cell compatibility, and quantitative output.  

The system successfully confirmed proper trafficking of FAP-tagged CFTR and demonstrated utility for corrector screening. An alternative embodiment with the FAP reporter appended to the cytosolic C-terminus was also constructed and validated, expanding the flexibility of the platform.
