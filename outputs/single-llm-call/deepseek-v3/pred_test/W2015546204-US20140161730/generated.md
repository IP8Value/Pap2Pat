# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to magnetic graphene-like nanoparticles and their use as contrast agents in magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). More particularly, the invention provides novel compositions comprising graphene-like nanostructures intercalated with paramagnetic metal ions that exhibit superior relaxivity properties compared to conventional MRI contrast agents. The invention further relates to methods of producing such magnetic compositions and their use in diagnostic imaging applications.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) has become one of the most important non-invasive imaging modalities in modern radiology, providing detailed anatomical images of various organs and tissues for improved diagnosis of pathologies and diseases. The detection and diagnostic confidence of MRI is significantly enhanced through the use of contrast agents (CAs) that alter the relaxation times of water protons in tissues.  

MRI contrast agents are generally classified as T₁ or T₂ agents based on whether they primarily affect longitudinal (T₁) or transverse (T₂) relaxation times. The effectiveness of these agents is quantified by their relaxivity, defined as the change in relaxation rate per unit concentration of the contrast agent. Current clinical T₁ MRI contrast agents are predominantly chelate complexes of gadolinium (Gd³⁺) ions, though manganese (Mn²⁺) complexes have also been investigated as alternatives.  

While gadolinium-based contrast agents have been widely used, concerns about nephrogenic systemic fibrosis in patients with renal impairment have prompted investigation of alternative agents. Manganese represents a biologically compatible alternative, being a natural cellular constituent that functions as a regulatory cofactor for various enzymes. However, conventional manganese chelate complexes exhibit suboptimal relaxivity compared to theoretical predictions.  

Recent developments in carbon nanostructures have opened new possibilities for MRI contrast agents. Gadolinium-ion carbon nanostructure complexes, including gadofullerenes and gadonanotubes, have demonstrated relaxivities significantly higher than conventional chelate complexes. These systems exploit the confinement of paramagnetic ions within carbon nanostructures to enhance relaxivity. However, the potential of manganese-ion carbon nanostructure complexes remains largely unexplored.  

Graphene, a two-dimensional carbon nanostructure, has attracted considerable interest for various material and biomedical applications. Theoretical studies predict diverse magnetic phenomena in graphene, though experimental verification remains limited. Recent advances in chemical synthesis have enabled large-scale production of graphene oxide, graphene nanoplatelets, and graphene nanoribbons. These materials offer unique opportunities for developing novel MRI contrast agents through the intercalation of paramagnetic metal ions.  

There remains a need in the art for improved MRI contrast agents that combine high relaxivity with favorable safety profiles. The present invention addresses this need by providing novel magnetic compositions comprising graphene-like nanostructures intercalated with paramagnetic metal ions, particularly manganese, that exhibit superior relaxivity properties compared to conventional contrast agents.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel magnetic compositions useful as contrast agents for magnetic resonance imaging. The compositions comprise graphene-like nanostructures intercalated with paramagnetic metal ions that exhibit unexpectedly high relaxivity values.  

In one aspect, the invention provides a magnetic composition comprising a graphene-like nanostructure having a thickness between 0.34 nm and 100 nm, wherein the nanostructure contains intercalated paramagnetic metal ions. The composition demonstrates a longitudinal relaxivity (r₁) of at least 40 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ at 0.47 Tesla and 40°C, significantly higher than conventional paramagnetic chelate complexes.  

The graphene-like nanostructure may be selected from graphene nanoplatelets, graphene nanoribbons, or other graphitic nano- or microstructures. In preferred embodiments, the nanostructure has a thickness between 0.34 nm and 10 nm. The paramagnetic metal is preferably manganese, though other paramagnetic or ferromagnetic metals may be used alone or in combination.  

The composition may further comprise a water-solubilizing moiety attached to the graphene-like nanostructure, facilitating dispersion in aqueous media. Suitable water-solubilizing moieties include surfactants such as Pluronic F127.  

Another aspect of the invention provides a method of performing magnetic resonance imaging comprising administering to a subject an effective amount of the magnetic composition and acquiring MR images. The composition may be formulated for oral or parenteral administration.  

The invention further provides kits containing the magnetic composition for use in MRI imaging. The kits may include instructions for use and other components necessary for administration.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel magnetic compositions comprising graphene-like nanostructures intercalated with paramagnetic metal ions that exhibit superior properties as MRI contrast agents. The term "magnetic" as used herein refers to materials exhibiting paramagnetic, diamagnetic, ferromagnetic, or superparamagnetic behavior.  

The compositions demonstrate exceptional relaxivity properties, with longitudinal relaxivity (r₁) values of at least 40 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ and transverse relaxivity (r₂) values of at least 100 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ at 0.47 Tesla and 40°C. These values represent a significant improvement over conventional paramagnetic chelate complexes, which typically exhibit r₁ values of 3-5 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ and r₂ values of 4-6 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ under similar conditions.  

The term "graphene-like nanostructure" refers to two-dimensional carbon nanostructures that may include single-layer graphene, few-layer graphene, graphene oxide, reduced graphene oxide, graphene nanoplatelets, graphene nanoribbons, and other graphitic structures with similar properties. These nanostructures typically have a thickness between 0.34 nm (the thickness of single-layer graphene) and 100 nm, preferably between 0.34 nm and 10 nm.  

Graphene nanoplatelets according to the invention preferably have a diameter between 10 nm and 10 μm and a thickness between 0.34 nm and 100 nm. Graphene nanoribbons preferably have a width between 5 nm and 500 nm and a length between 50 nm and 10 μm.  

The water-solubilizing moiety may be covalently or non-covalently attached to the graphene-like nanostructure. Suitable moieties include polyethylene glycol (PEG), polyvinylpyrrolidone (PVP), and block copolymers such as Pluronic surfactants. The moiety should be present in sufficient quantity to ensure stable dispersion of the nanostructures in aqueous media.  

Paramagnetic metals suitable for intercalation include manganese (Mn²⁺), gadolinium (Gd³⁺), and other lanthanides. Ferromagnetic metals such as iron, cobalt, and nickel may also be used. The metal is preferably present in an amount between 0.01% and 10% by weight of the composition.  

The magnetic compositions may be prepared by methods including oxidative exfoliation of graphite or carbon nanotubes followed by reduction in the presence of metal ions. Alternative methods include direct intercalation of metals into pre-formed graphene structures.  

For MRI applications, the compositions may be formulated for oral or parenteral administration. Effective doses typically range from 0.1 μmol/kg to 100 μmol/kg of body weight, depending on the specific composition and imaging requirements.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope.  

**Example 1: Synthesis of Graphene Nanoplatelets**  

Oxidized micro-graphite was prepared from analytical grade graphite using a modified Hummer's method. The oxidized graphite (200 mg) was suspended in water (200 ml) and sonicated for 1 hour. A portion (50 ml) was centrifuged to obtain oxidized graphene nanoplatelets. The remaining solution was treated with hydrazine hydrate (1.5 ml) and heated at 100°C for 12 hours to yield reduced graphene nanoplatelets.  

**Example 2: Synthesis of Graphene Nanoribbons**  

Multi-walled carbon nanotubes (150 mg) were suspended in concentrated sulfuric acid (30 ml) for 2 hours. Potassium permanganate (750 mg) was added and the mixture was heated at 55-70°C for 1 hour. The product was washed and isolated by centrifugation to yield graphene nanoribbons.  

**Example 3: Characterization of Magnetic Properties**  

SQUID magnetometry revealed that reduced graphene nanoplatelets exhibited superparamagnetic behavior at room temperature with a blocking temperature of 40 K. Graphene nanoribbons showed weak ferromagnetism at room temperature with a saturation magnetization of 0.1 emu/g at 2500 Oe.  

**Example 4: Relaxivity Measurements**  

Relaxivity measurements at 0.47 Tesla showed r₁ values of 40-60 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ and r₂ values of 100-300 mM⁻¹s⁻¹ for the graphene compositions, significantly higher than conventional contrast agents. NMRD profiles demonstrated unique field-dependent relaxivity behavior, particularly at low fields (<10 MHz).  

**Example 5: In Vitro MRI Phantom Studies**  

Phantom MRI experiments using 2D spin-echo imaging demonstrated significant contrast enhancement with the graphene-based compositions compared to controls. The signal intensity ratios confirmed the potential of these materials as effective MRI contrast agents.  

The examples demonstrate that confinement of paramagnetic metal ions within graphene-like nanostructures represents a novel strategy for developing high-relaxivity MRI contrast agents. The compositions exhibit superior properties compared to conventional agents while maintaining potential for biological compatibility.
