# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER  

The present disclosure relates generally to the field of organ transplantation. More specifically, the disclosed subject matter pertains to systems and methods for extracorporeal organ support, preservation, recovery, and regeneration. The disclosed technology enables prolonged normothermic extracorporeal maintenance of organs through cross-circulation with a living host, allowing for therapeutic interventions to repair and regenerate damaged organs that would otherwise be unsuitable for transplantation.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER  

Organ transplantation has emerged as a lifesaving treatment for end-stage organ failure, yet its full potential remains constrained by a critical shortage of donor organs. Current estimates indicate that up to 80% of donated lungs are not utilized due to injury at the time of death, including trauma, pulmonary contusion, aspiration, and ventilator-associated lung injury. Similar shortages affect other transplantable organs, creating what has been described as the greatest crisis facing biomedicine today.  

Current transplant procedures rely on static cold storage for organ preservation, which provides limited viability windows of typically 4-6 hours for lungs and up to 12 hours for other solid organs. While ex vivo lung perfusion (EVLP) systems have extended preservation times to approximately 6 hours and enabled evaluation of marginal quality donor lungs, these systems have not significantly increased the number of transplantable organs. Conventional EVLP systems utilize acellular perfusates and lack the physiologic regulation necessary for prolonged organ maintenance, limiting their ability to recover severely damaged organs.  

The limitations of current preservation techniques become particularly evident when attempting to recover organs injured by gastric aspiration, which causes severe damage to the pulmonary epithelium leading to pneumonitis and respiratory insufficiency. Previous attempts to recover lungs injured by gastric aspiration using EVLP have been unsuccessful, resulting in increased edema, failure to reduce inflammatory cytokines, and inability to improve tissue morphology.  

Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) systems, while capable of providing temporary cardiopulmonary support, are not designed for organ preservation and lack the specialized components needed for maintaining extracorporeal organs. Current ECMO systems also carry significant risks including hemolysis, coagulopathy, and systemic inflammation when used for prolonged periods.  

There exists a compelling need for improved organ preservation techniques that can extend viability windows beyond current limitations, enable recovery of severely damaged organs, and provide a platform for therapeutic interventions. Such systems should maintain physiologic conditions including proper oxygenation, nutrient delivery, waste removal, and hormonal regulation while minimizing ischemic injury and inflammation.  

The disclosed subject matter addresses these limitations by providing a normothermic extracorporeal support platform that utilizes cross-circulation with a living host to maintain physiologic conditions for prolonged periods. This system enables not only preservation but also active recovery and regeneration of damaged organs through therapeutic interventions performed during extracorporeal support.  

## SUMMARY  

The disclosed subject matter provides a system for normothermic extracorporeal support of organs through cross-circulation perfusion with a living host. The system enables prolonged support periods exceeding 36 hours while maintaining physiologic conditions and organ function. The extracorporeal host connection is established through vascular cannulation, creating a closed circuit that allows for continuous monitoring and intervention.  

The system comprises an extracorporeal chamber designed to maintain the organ at physiologic temperature and humidity while allowing for ventilation in the case of lungs. The chamber incorporates features for auto-regulation of blood flow based on organ demand and includes an integrated heat exchanger to maintain optimal temperature. The extracorporeal cross-circulation circuit utilizes pressure-controlled blood flow to ensure adequate perfusion without causing barotrauma or vascular injury.  

Remote monitoring capability is provided through integrated sensors measuring parameters including perfusion pressure, flow rates, oxygen saturation, and biochemical markers. The system includes an extracorporeal organ chamber stand with automatic height adjustability to optimize gravitational effects on perfusion. Organ-specific accessories accommodate various organ types and sizes, while a rechargeable power source provides backup during transport or power interruptions.  

A key component of the system is the biologic cuff device which serves as a biological bridge between the native vessel and extracorporeal circuit. This device enables secure cannulation while maintaining vascular integrity. The system further includes a draining cannula with patient safety clamp and automatic clamping mechanism to prevent air embolism or excessive blood loss during circuit interruptions.  

The disclosed method for normothermic extracorporeal support involves treating the extracorporeal organ with acute injury through therapeutic interventions while maintaining physiologic conditions. These interventions may include bronchoalveolar lavage, surfactant replacement, and alveolar recruitment for lungs, or analogous therapies for other organs. Following recovery, the extracorporeal organ may be transplanted with improved function and viability.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE DISCLOSED SUBJECT MATTER  

The disclosed invention provides a platform for cross-circulation support of extracorporeal organs, addressing limitations of current organ transplant methods by enabling long-term preservation, recovery, and regeneration. The system's capabilities include maintaining physiologic conditions for over 36 hours, supporting organ recovery from severe injury, and providing a platform for diagnostic and therapeutic interventions.  

Various applications of the invention include recovery of marginal donor organs, regeneration of severely injured organs, evaluation of organ function prior to transplantation, and research into organ physiology and therapeutics. The mobile platform for prolonged extracorporeal support comprises a perfusion circuit, monitoring systems, and therapeutic delivery mechanisms integrated into a transportable unit.  

The connection between the host and extracorporeal organ is established through vascular access points, typically the internal jugular veins, with flow rates adjusted to maintain optimal perfusion pressure. The system's uses extend beyond transplantation to include recovery and maintenance of organs for research, testing of pharmaceutical agents, and development of organ-specific therapies.  

Additional research applications include studying organ pathophysiology, testing novel preservation solutions, and evaluating the effects of ischemia-reperfusion injury. The system enables cross-circulation between the organ recipient and donor organ as a bridge to transplantation, or alternatively, the use of a healthy animal as a cross-circulation host for human organ recovery.  

For recovery of injured human donor organs, the system provides physiologic regulation unavailable in conventional EVLP systems. A particularly innovative application is the human-xenogeneic bioreactor, where a xenogeneic host provides cross-circulation support for human organs. The benefits of this approach include increased availability of support hosts and potential immunomodulatory effects.  

The human-xenogeneic bioreactor has additional uses in studying immune responses, testing immunosuppressive regimens, and developing tolerance induction protocols. The cross-circulation platform creates unique opportunities for organ recovery through continuous systemic regulation, access to host immune and reparative factors, and ability to perform repeated interventions.  

The systems, methods, and devices for cross-circulation support include a closed extracorporeal circuit with fluid conduits that enable auto-regulation of blood flow based on organ demand. Integrated circuit elements include pressure sensors, flow meters, oxygenation monitors, and temperature regulators that maintain homeostasis.

In pre-clinical testing using a swine model of cross-circulation, the system demonstrated excellent haemodynamic stability with minimal fluctuations in blood pressure or heart rate. Biochemical analysis revealed stable electrolyte levels, normal renal function markers, and absence of significant hemolysis throughout 36 hours of support.  

Haemolytic markers including plasma free hemoglobin remained within acceptable ranges, indicating minimal mechanical blood trauma. Pro-inflammatory markers showed transient elevation followed by return to baseline, while anti-inflammatory markers including IL-10 increased over time, suggesting modulation of the inflammatory response.  

Extracorporeal lung performance was assessed through multiple parameters. The trans-pulmonary pressure gradient remained stable between 5-15 mmHg, indicating preserved vascular integrity. Pressure volume loops demonstrated progressive improvement in lung compliance, approaching normal hysteresis patterns by 36 hours of support.  

Dynamic compliance measurements showed significant recovery from initial impairment, with values increasing over six-fold during the support period. Clearance of lactate and improvement in PaO2/FiO2 ratios indicated restoration of metabolic and gas exchange functions. The lungs maintained appropriate responses to performance challenges, with increasing capacity for oxygenation and ventilation under stress conditions.  

Analysis of lung weight revealed progressive reduction consistent with resolution of edema. Macroscopic appearance showed clearance of consolidation and hemorrhage, while airway bronchoscopy demonstrated resolution of inflammation and restoration of normal mucosal appearance. Histologic analysis confirmed repair of alveolar architecture, regeneration of epithelial lining, and restoration of vascular integrity.  

Scanning electron microscopy revealed reconstruction of alveolar ultrastructure, while transmission electron microscopy demonstrated regeneration of type I and II pneumocytes with reappearance of lamellar bodies and tight junctions. Metabolic activity assays showed preserved cellular function, and vasoresponsiveness testing confirmed maintained vascular reactivity.  

Quantification of neutrophils and macrophages in lung tissue showed resolution of inflammatory infiltrates over time. Analysis of the microvascular endothelium demonstrated intact barrier function and normal permeability characteristics. In the ischaemic recovery group, macroscopic analysis revealed similar patterns of recovery to those seen in aspiration-injured lungs.  

Bronchoscopic assessment showed clearance of airway inflammation, while analysis of BAL fluid demonstrated reduction in inflammatory cells and mediators. Measurement of inflammatory markers in BAL and serum revealed decreasing levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines and increasing anti-inflammatory mediators over the support period.  

The system enabled multiscale interventions including development of a compact transpleural imaging system for real-time monitoring. Delivery of microbeads and mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) demonstrated feasibility of targeted cellular therapy. The platform supported replacement of lung epithelium through decellularization of targeted bronchopulmonary segments followed by delivery of pulmonary epithelial cells.  

Viable extracorporeal lungs were maintained throughout the support period, with successful recovery of lungs subjected to ischaemia reperfusion injury. The system provided multiple benefits to extracorporeal lungs including extended duration of normothermic support, physiologic regulation, and ability to perform repeated interventions.  

Strategies for prolonged extracorporeal organ support included careful management of pulmonary venous drainage to maintain physiologic trans-pulmonary pressure gradients. Establishment of recipient safety protocols ensured stable host conditions throughout cross-circulation. Assessment of the system's ability to recover damaged lungs included comprehensive analysis of inflammatory cytokines in serum and BAL fluid.  

Preservation of lung integrity and function was demonstrated through histologic, ultrastructural, and functional parameters. A significant increase in anti-inflammatory IL-10 accompanied reduction in lung weight and airway edema. Recovery of cellular and structural integrity enabled interventions not currently feasible with existing ex vivo systems.  

The platform facilitated development of targeted micro-volume delivery techniques for region-specific interventions. Global, single-lobe and subsegmental delivery of MSCs was demonstrated, highlighting the precision enabled by the system. Investigation of prolonged cross-circulation between unrelated donor and recipient swine confirmed the system's ability to rescue severely damaged lungs without requiring genetic matching.  

Envisioned clinical applications include recovery of marginal donor organs, rehabilitation of injured organs, and evaluation of organ function prior to transplantation. The system also has potential applications in pharmaceutical testing, organ-specific therapy development, and research into organ physiology and pathology.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Methods  

A pilot study was designed to test the hypothesis that cross-circulation could provide sufficient extracorporeal support to enable therapeutic interventions and recovery of severely damaged lungs. The study was conducted with eight donor-recipient pairs of partially inbred swine with defined major histocompatibility complex loci.  

Animal selection criteria included age (5-8 months) and weight (30.6-53.5 kg) ranges to ensure consistency. Animal care followed NIH guidelines with approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee. Donor lung harvest procedures included standardized anesthesia with Telazol, buprenorphine, midazolam, fentanyl, and isoflurane.  

Heparin administration (30,000 U) preceded pulmonary artery cannulation for blood collection. Cannula placement included pulmonary artery (18-20 Fr), tracheal (7.5-8.0 mm endotracheal tube), and single lung venous cannulas (20G) inserted through a biological bridge created from the aortic arch.  

Lung storage for prolonged cold ischaemia used Perfadex solution with alprostadil for flush and static cold storage. Lung cannulation and conventional EVLP setup included circuit priming with autologous blood and initiation of ventilation at 6-8 bpm, tidal volume 6-8 mL/kg, PEEP 5 cmH2O, and FiO2 40%.  

Atelectatic lung region recruitment employed ventilator strategies (increased PEEP up to 12.5 cmH2O, inspiratory holds to 25 cmH2O) and manual techniques as needed. Cross-circulation was initiated following recipient preparation including bilateral internal jugular vein cannulation (16-18 Fr) with heparinization (15,000 U bolus, 25 U/kg/h infusion to maintain ACT 250-350 s).  

Circuit connection established flow between recipient and extracorporeal lungs with continuous monitoring of physiological parameters including pressures, flows, temperatures, and blood gases. Blood analysis included hourly point-of-care testing and periodic comprehensive panels (CBC, chemistry, coagulation).  

Extracorporeal lung monitoring incorporated pulmonary artery and venous pressure measurements, flow rates, hemogas analysis, and dynamic compliance calculations. HAL analysis assessed hepatic function, while BODIPY-surfactant uptake method evaluated type II pneumocyte function.  

Vasoconstriction/responsiveness testing used pharmacologic challenges to assess vascular reactivity. Metabolic activity assays employed Alamar Blue reagent with DNA quantification by PicoGreen. Myeloperoxidase activity assay measured neutrophil activity in lung tissue samples.  

Live cilia imaging utilized fluorescent microscopy to assess mucociliary function. Immunohistochemical staining protocols included antigen retrieval, blocking, and application of primary and secondary antibodies for markers including aquaporin 5, caspase 3, CD163, CD31, connexin 43, neutrophil elastase, surfactant protein C, ZO-1, and ZO-3.  

Histopathologic analysis employed H&E, Alcian blue, trichrome, silver reticulin, elastic van Gieson, and pentachrome stains. Blinded pathologic review used randomized, coded slides evaluated by an independent pulmonary pathologist.  

Thermal imaging utilized a FLIR T430sc infrared camera for surface temperature mapping. Electron microscopy included both scanning (Zeiss GeminiSEM 300) and transmission (JEOL JEM-1200 EXII) modalities with standardized preparation protocols.  

Therapeutic delivery into extracorporeal lung employed bronchoscopic techniques for decellularization of target lung regions and delivery of mesenchymal stem cells. Cell delivery used a bronchoscope with Renegade microcatheter system for precise targeting.  

Live transpleural imaging of near-infrared-labelled cells enabled real-time tracking of delivered therapeutics. Delivery of CSFE and fluorescent microbeads provided markers for cellular uptake and distribution studies. Human airway epithelial cells and embryonic stem cell-derived alveolar progenitor cells were delivered to assess engraftment potential.  

Statistical analysis employed one-way ANOVA with Tukey's multiple comparison post hoc tests and Student's t-tests using Prism software, with significance defined as p<0.05. Modification of described embodiments may include adaptation for various organ types, scaling for human applications, and integration with other extracorporeal systems.  

The scope of the invention encompasses application of its principles to organs including heart, kidney, liver, and pancreas. Embodiments may incorporate variations in circuit design, monitoring systems, and therapeutic delivery methods while maintaining the core concept of cross-circulation support.  

The invention is claimed as a system and method for normothermic extracorporeal organ support through cross-circulation enabling prolonged preservation, recovery, and regeneration of damaged organs. Specific claims include the integrated circuit design, biologic cuff device, therapeutic intervention protocols, and monitoring systems described herein.
