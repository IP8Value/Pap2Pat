## BACKGROUND  

Fungal terpenoids represent a structurally diverse class of specialized metabolites with significant commercial value in pharmaceutical, cosmetic, and food industries. These compounds serve critical ecological functions as defense molecules and signaling agents in nature. While terpenoid biosynthesis has been well-characterized in Ascomycota fungi, the metabolic pathways in Basidiomycota remain largely unexplored despite their potential as a rich source of novel terpenoid structures. Recent advances in genome sequencing and comparative annotation have begun to elucidate terpenoid biosynthetic mechanisms in basidiomycete fungi, revealing unique enzymatic pathways capable of producing rare and biologically active sesquiterpenoid compounds.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention identifies and characterizes a novel terpenoid synthase gene (SiTPS) from the endophytic basidiomycete fungus Serendipita indica. The invention provides isolated nucleic acid molecules encoding the SiTPS enzyme, which has been demonstrated to catalyze the formation of viridiflorol, a sesquiterpene alcohol with a 7/5/3 tricyclic aromadendrane scaffold. The invention encompasses heterologous expression systems for producing SiTPS and methods for biosynthesizing viridiflorol through both in vitro enzymatic reactions and in vivo microbial production systems.  

Key embodiments of the invention include expression cassettes containing the SiTPS coding sequence operably linked to regulatory elements for expression in prokaryotic and eukaryotic host cells. The invention further provides vectors, host cells, and kits for viridiflorol production, along with methods for isolating and purifying viridiflorol from culture media or plant tissues. The invention also encompasses sequence variants of SiTPS that maintain terpene synthase activity, including nucleic acid sequences optimized for expression in different host systems and amino acid sequences containing conservative substitutions that preserve enzymatic function.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides the SiTPS enzyme, a sesquiterpene synthase from Serendipita indica that catalyzes the formation of viridiflorol from farnesyl diphosphate (FPP). The SiTPS enzyme possesses characteristic terpene synthase motifs including the aspartate-rich DDXXD metal-binding domain and exhibits optimal activity in the presence of Mg2+ ions. The invention includes the wild-type SiTPS amino acid sequence as well as functional variants containing deletions, insertions, or substitutions that retain sesquiterpene synthase activity.  

The invention provides isolated nucleic acid molecules encoding SiTPS, including DNA sequences optimized for expression in various host systems. These nucleic acid sequences may incorporate degenerate codons while maintaining the encoded amino acid sequence, or may be codon-optimized for enhanced expression in specific host organisms. The invention encompasses both the native SiTPS coding sequence and modified sequences containing 5' and 3' untranslated regions, targeting sequences, or other regulatory elements.  

### Synthetic Pathways  

The invention discloses the viridiflorol biosynthetic pathway catalyzed by SiTPS, wherein farnesyl diphosphate undergoes cyclization through initial C1-C10 bond formation followed by secondary cyclizations and rearrangements to form the tricyclic viridiflorol structure. This pathway represents a novel route for microbial production of viridiflorol, which has previously been identified primarily in plant systems.  

### Expression of Enzymes  

The invention provides expression systems for producing SiTPS and viridiflorol in various host cells. These systems include expression cassettes containing the SiTPS coding sequence operably linked to promoter sequences functional in bacterial, fungal, or plant cells. Suitable promoters include constitutive, inducible, and tissue-specific promoters selected based on the desired expression pattern. The expression cassettes may further include targeting sequences for directing SiTPS to specific intracellular compartments, transcription termination sequences, and selectable marker genes.  

Vectors for SiTPS expression include plasmid vectors such as pET series vectors for bacterial expression and binary Ti plasmids for plant transformation. The invention encompasses methods for delivering these vectors to host cells through techniques including electroporation, microprojectile bombardment, and Agrobacterium-mediated transformation. For plant systems, the invention provides methods for regenerating transformed plant tissues and producing stably transformed plants capable of viridiflorol production.  

The invention further includes methods for analyzing terpene production in transformed cells, including gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS), nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy, and various molecular biology techniques such as PCR, Northern blotting, and Western blotting to confirm transgene expression and enzyme activity.  

### Hosts  

The invention defines suitable host cells for SiTPS expression and viridiflorol production, including bacterial cells (e.g., Escherichia coli), fungal cells (e.g., yeast), plant cells, and insect cells. Particular embodiments utilize organelles within host cells such as lipid droplets, smooth endoplasmic reticulum, or plastids as sites for terpenoid accumulation. The invention encompasses both prokaryotic and eukaryotic host systems optimized for sesquiterpene production through metabolic engineering approaches.  

### Manufacturing  

The invention provides methods for manufacturing viridiflorol through cultivation of SiTPS-expressing host cells followed by extraction and purification of the sesquiterpene products. These methods include growing transformed bacterial or fungal cultures under conditions that induce SiTPS expression and promote viridiflorol accumulation, followed by organic solvent extraction and chromatographic purification. For plant systems, the methods encompass growing transgenic plants expressing SiTPS and isolating viridiflorol from plant tissues through distillation or solvent extraction techniques.  

### Example 1: Materials and Methods  

This example describes the materials and methods for identifying and characterizing SiTPS. Fungal and plant materials included Serendipita indica cultures grown in complete medium and tomato seedlings (Solanum lycopersicum cv. Moneymaker) grown under controlled conditions. The SiTPS gene was identified through genome mining and phylogenetic analysis comparing its sequence to known basidiomycete terpene synthases.  

Heterologous expression of SiTPS was achieved in Escherichia coli using the pET28b+ vector system. Protein purification employed nickel-affinity chromatography followed by desalting. In vitro enzyme assays tested SiTPS activity with geranyl diphosphate (GPP), farnesyl diphosphate (FPP), and geranylgeranyl diphosphate (GGPP) substrates. An in vivo production system was developed by co-expressing SiTPS with FPP synthase in E. coli, with terpene products extracted using hexane and analyzed by GC-MS.  

### Example 2: Phylogenetic Analysis on SiTPS  

Phylogenetic analysis placed SiTPS in Clade I of basidiomycete sesquiterpene synthases, a group characterized by enzymes performing 1,10-cyclization of FPP. The analysis revealed closest sequence similarity to putative terpene synthases from other Serendipita species and unexpected homology to a lichen terpene synthase, suggesting functional convergence between these evolutionarily distant organisms.  

### Example 3: SiTPS Encodes a Viridiflorol Synthase  

Both in vitro and in vivo assays demonstrated that SiTPS specifically utilizes FPP to produce viridiflorol as its major product. GC-MS analysis confirmed the identity of viridiflorol through comparison with authentic standards and mass spectral databases. The proposed cyclization mechanism involves initial C1-C10 bond formation followed by secondary cyclizations to form the characteristic aromadendrane skeleton.  

### Example 4: SiTPS is Induced Upon Root-Colonization  

Gene expression analysis showed significant upregulation of SiTPS transcripts during colonization of tomato roots compared to growth on synthetic media. This induction suggests a potential role for SiTPS products in plant-fungal interactions, though the specific function remains to be fully elucidated.  

### Example 5: SiTPS Implication in Colonization of Tomato Roots  

While overexpression of SiTPS did not enhance root colonization ability, the antimicrobial activity of viridiflorol against phytopathogenic fungi suggests a potential defensive role. Growth inhibition assays demonstrated that viridiflorol at concentrations ≥100 μg/mL effectively prevents spore germination of Colletotrichum truncatum, indicating possible ecological functions in microbial competition within the rhizosphere.
