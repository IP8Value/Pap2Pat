# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates to the fields of molecular biology, oncology, and therapeutics, specifically targeting cancers associated with deficiencies in the MLL3 and MLL4 COMPASS family of histone H3 lysine 4 (H3K4) methyltransferases. MLL3 and MLL4, encoded by the genes KMT2C and KMT2D, respectively, are critical regulators of enhancer activity through their catalytic-dependent H3K4 mono-methylation and catalytic-independent interactions with cofactors such as UTX and p300. These enzymes play essential roles in various developmental processes, including myogenesis, adipogenesis, cardiogenesis, macrophage activation, and germinal center B cell development.  

Emerging evidence indicates that MLL3 and MLL4 function as tumor suppressors, with frequent loss-of-function mutations observed across a broad spectrum of cancers. Mutations in MLL3 and MLL4 are prevalent in both hematological malignancies and solid tumors, underscoring their importance in maintaining genomic stability and preventing oncogenesis. Additionally, MLL4 mutations are implicated in Kabuki syndrome, a developmental disorder characterized by multisystem abnormalities. Despite the well-documented occurrence of MLL3/4 mutations, the molecular mechanisms underlying their tumor-suppressive functions and the therapeutic vulnerabilities associated with their loss remain poorly understood.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a method for treating cancers characterized by MLL3 and/or MLL4 COMPASS deficiency, including loss-of-function mutations or reduced expression of these enzymes. The method involves administering a nucleotide synthesis inhibitor to a subject in need thereof, wherein the inhibitor selectively targets de novo purine or pyrimidine synthesis pathways.  

In one embodiment, the nucleotide synthesis inhibitor is a de novo purine synthesis inhibitor, such as lometrexol, pelitrexol, or methotrexate, which target glycinamide ribonucleotide formyltransferase (GARFT), phosphoribosylaminoimidazole succinocarboxamide synthetase (PAICS), or dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR), respectively. In another embodiment, the inhibitor is a de novo pyrimidine synthesis inhibitor, such as brequinar or leflunomide, which target dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH).  

The invention further provides a method for identifying subjects likely to respond to nucleotide synthesis inhibition by detecting biomarkers indicative of MLL3/4 deficiency, such as mutations in KMT2C or KMT2D, reduced H3K4me1 levels, or altered expression of purine metabolism genes. The disclosed therapeutic approach is applicable to a wide range of cancers, including colorectal cancer, breast cancer, bladder cancer, and hematological malignancies.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention is directed to compositions and methods for treating MLL3/4 COMPASS-deficient cancers by inhibiting nucleotide synthesis. The term "MLL3/4 COMPASS-deficient cancer" refers to a malignancy characterized by reduced or absent MLL3 or MLL4 activity due to genetic mutations, epigenetic silencing, or post-translational modifications. Such cancers exhibit increased dependency on de novo nucleotide synthesis pathways, rendering them susceptible to targeted metabolic inhibition.  

### MLL3 and MLL4 Functions  

MLL3 and MLL4 are members of the COMPASS family of histone methyltransferases that catalyze the mono-methylation of H3K4 at enhancer regions. These enzymes function as part of a multi-protein complex that includes subunits such as RBBP5, NCOA6, UTX, and PTIP. In addition to their catalytic activity, MLL3 and MLL4 facilitate enhancer activation through interactions with transcriptional coactivators. Loss of MLL3 or MLL4 disrupts enhancer-mediated gene regulation, leading to widespread transcriptional dysregulation and metabolic reprogramming.  

### MLL3/4 COMPASS-Deficient Cancers  

Cancers harboring MLL3 or MLL4 mutations exhibit distinct molecular features, including altered chromatin structure, aberrant enhancer activity, and upregulated nucleotide metabolism. These tumors are prevalent in colorectal cancer, breast cancer, and bladder cancer, among others. The invention provides methods for diagnosing such cancers by detecting MLL3/4 mutations, reduced H3K4me1 levels, or metabolic signatures indicative of increased nucleotide synthesis flux.  

### Nucleotide Synthesis Inhibitors  

The invention encompasses inhibitors targeting de novo purine and pyrimidine synthesis pathways. Purine synthesis inhibitors include:  
- **GARFT inhibitors** (e.g., lometrexol, pelitrexol)  
- **PPAT, PFAS, PAICS, ADSL, AdSS, and GMPS inhibitors**  
- **DHFR inhibitors** (e.g., methotrexate)  

Pyrimidine synthesis inhibitors include:  
- **CAD inhibitors**  
- **DHODH inhibitors** (e.g., brequinar, leflunomide)  
- **LIMPS inhibitors**  

Combinations of these inhibitors may be used to enhance therapeutic efficacy and overcome resistance mechanisms.  

### Pharmaceutical Compositions  

The nucleotide synthesis inhibitors may be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions comprising an effective amount of the active agent and a pharmaceutically acceptable excipient, carrier, or diluent. Suitable carriers include water-containing liquids, solid carriers, buffering agents, antioxidants, and stabilizing delivery vehicles. The compositions may be administered orally, intravenously, or via other routes, with pH-adjusted formulations to ensure stability.  

### Biomarker Detection and Treatment  

The invention includes a method for stratifying patients by:  
1. Obtaining a biological sample (e.g., tumor tissue, blood) from the subject.  
2. Detecting MLL3/4 deficiency via sequencing, immunohistochemistry, or metabolic profiling.  
3. Administering a nucleotide synthesis inhibitor if deficiency is confirmed.  

The "effective amount" refers to a dosage sufficient to inhibit tumor growth without undue toxicity, as determined by clinical parameters.

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Synthetic Lethality in MLL3/4-Deficient Cells  

A CRISPR dropout screen in MLL3/4 knockout mouse embryonic stem cells (mESCs) identified purine and pyrimidine synthesis genes as essential for viability. Metabolic profiling revealed increased flux through de novo purine synthesis in knockout cells, which exhibited heightened sensitivity to lometrexol (LTX), a GARFT inhibitor. RNA-seq and proteomics confirmed upregulation of purine metabolism genes, while Hi-C analysis demonstrated chromatin decompaction in MLL3/4-deficient cells.  

### Example 2: Efficacy in Colorectal Cancer  

MLL4-mutant colorectal cancer cell lines (HCT116, RKO) showed selective sensitivity to LTX compared to wild-type controls (Caco2, HT55). Xenograft studies demonstrated significant tumor growth inhibition and prolonged survival in LTX-treated mice bearing HCT116 tumors. Biomarker analysis confirmed downregulation of mitotic genes (PLK1, AURKA) in LTX-responsive tumors.  

### Example 3: Clinical Applications  

The invention provides a framework for patient stratification using MLL3/4 mutation status and metabolic profiling. Therapeutic regimens may combine nucleotide synthesis inhibitors with immunotherapy or targeted agents to enhance efficacy.  

## CONCLUSION  

The invention establishes nucleotide synthesis inhibition as a synthetic lethal strategy for MLL3/4-deficient cancers, with broad implications for precision oncology.
