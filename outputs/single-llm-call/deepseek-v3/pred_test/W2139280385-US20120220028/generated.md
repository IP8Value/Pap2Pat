# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to an enhancer for a promoter, particularly an enhancer derived from the major immediate-early (MIE) gene region of human herpesvirus 6 (HHV-6). More specifically, the invention provides a novel enhancer element located within the first intron of the HHV-6 MIE gene that significantly increases promoter activity, particularly in T-cell lines. The invention further relates to gene constructs incorporating this enhancer, methods for enhancing gene expression using said enhancer, and applications thereof in gene therapy and vaccine development.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Gene therapy has emerged as a promising approach for treating various genetic disorders, cancers, and infectious diseases. A critical component of successful gene therapy is the efficient delivery and expression of therapeutic genes in target cells. Viral promoters, particularly the major immediate-early (MIE) promoter from human cytomegalovirus (CMV), have been widely used to drive high-level expression of heterologous genes in mammalian cells. However, the CMV MIE promoter exhibits variable activity depending on cell type and can be silenced in certain contexts, limiting its utility.  

Prior art has explored various viral promoters and enhancers to improve gene expression. For example, enhancer elements from other herpesviruses have been investigated, but these often lack the cell-type specificity and strength required for optimal therapeutic applications. Additionally, while intronic sequences have been shown to enhance promoter activity in some cases, their effects are unpredictable and context-dependent. There remains a need for enhancer elements that provide consistent, strong, and cell-type-specific enhancement of promoter activity, particularly in T cells where existing promoters often underperform.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

### Problems to be Solved by the Invention  

The present invention addresses several limitations of prior art viral promoters and enhancers. First, existing enhancers often lack sufficient activity in T-cell lines, which are critical targets for many gene therapy applications. Second, the CMV MIE promoter, while widely used, exhibits variable performance across different cell types and can be subject to silencing. Third, while intronic sequences have been explored as potential enhancers, their effects are inconsistent, and optimal configurations for enhancing promoter activity remain unclear. The invention solves these problems by providing a novel enhancer derived from the first intron of the HHV-6 MIE gene that significantly boosts promoter activity, particularly in T cells.  

### Solutions to the Problems  

The invention introduces an intronic enhancer derived from the first intron of the HHV-6 MIE gene (HHV-6 MIE-in1) that dramatically increases promoter activity. This enhancer is particularly effective when combined with the HHV-6 MIE promoter (HHV-6 MIEp), resulting in a composite promoter-enhancer system with superior activity compared to the CMV MIE promoter in T-cell lines.  

The enhancer activity of HHV-6 MIE-in1 was demonstrated through luciferase reporter assays in various cell lines, including MRC-5, MeWo, U373, Molt-3, SupT1, and Jurkat cells. The results showed that the addition of HHV-6 MIE-in1 to HHV-6 MIEp increased promoter activity by several fold, with particularly strong enhancement observed in T-cell lines. In contrast, the CMV MIE promoter exhibited weaker activity in these cells.  

The invention further describes the construction of a recombinant varicella-zoster virus (VZV) vaccine strain incorporating the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 system to drive expression of the heterologous DsRed2 gene. This demonstrates the utility of the enhancer-promoter system in viral vectors for vaccine development and gene therapy.  

Key features of the invention include:  
1. The identification of the HHV-6 MIE promoter region and its enhancer activity.  
2. The discovery that the first intron of the HHV-6 MIE gene (HHV-6 MIE-in1) functions as a potent enhancer.  
3. The demonstration that HHV-6 MIEp-in1 outperforms the CMV MIE promoter in T-cell lines.  
4. The application of HHV-6 MIEp-in1 in recombinant viral vectors for heterologous gene expression.  
5. The provision of gene constructs, expression cassettes, and vectors incorporating HHV-6 MIEp-in1 for therapeutic and industrial applications.  

### Advantageous Effects of Invention  

The invention provides several advantages over prior art. First, the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 system exhibits stronger and more consistent activity in T-cell lines compared to the CMV MIE promoter. Second, the enhancer effect of HHV-6 MIE-in1 is specific and reproducible, overcoming the variability seen with other intronic enhancers. Third, the system is compatible with viral vectors, enabling its use in vaccine development and gene therapy. Fourth, the invention provides a toolkit for optimizing gene expression in diverse applications, from basic research to industrial protein production.

## DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

### (Enhancer for Promoter)  

The enhancer of the present invention is derived from the first intron of the HHV-6 MIE gene (HHV-6 MIE-in1). This enhancer significantly increases the activity of the HHV-6 MIE promoter, particularly in T-cell lines. The enhancer region spans approximately 300 base pairs and contains predicted transcription factor binding sites, including C/EBP and OCT-1 sites, which likely contribute to its activity.  

The enhancer functions by increasing the transcriptional activity of linked promoters, as demonstrated by luciferase reporter assays. When combined with the HHV-6 MIE promoter, the enhancer produces a composite system (HHV-6 MIEp-in1) that outperforms the CMV MIE promoter in T-cell lines while maintaining strong activity in other cell types.  

The enhancer is particularly useful for applications requiring high-level gene expression in T cells, such as T-cell therapies or vaccines targeting immune cells. It can be incorporated into various expression vectors, including viral vectors like the recombinant VZV described in the examples.  

### (Promoter)  

The promoter of the invention is the HHV-6 MIE promoter (HHV-6 MIEp), which regulates expression of the HHV-6 immediate-early genes. The promoter spans approximately 971 base pairs upstream of the IE1 gene and includes the R3 repeat region containing critical regulatory elements such as an NF-κB binding site.  

The HHV-6 MIE promoter exhibits cell-type-specific activity, with particularly strong performance in T-cell lines. This property makes it valuable for applications targeting immune cells. When combined with the HHV-6 MIE-in1 enhancer, the promoter's activity is significantly increased, creating a powerful expression system.  

### (Gene Construct)  

The gene construct of the invention comprises the HHV-6 MIE promoter operatively linked to the HHV-6 MIE-in1 enhancer and a heterologous gene of interest. The construct may also include additional regulatory elements such as polyadenylation signals and selectable markers.  

The construct is designed for high-level expression of the heterologous gene, particularly in T cells. It can be incorporated into various vectors, including plasmids and viral vectors, for delivery into target cells. The construct has been demonstrated to drive efficient expression of reporter genes (e.g., luciferase, DsRed2) and is suitable for therapeutic genes in gene therapy applications.  

### (Method for Enhancing Expression of Gene in Cell)  

The method involves introducing into a cell a nucleic acid construct comprising the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 system operatively linked to a gene of interest. The method is particularly effective for enhancing gene expression in T cells but can be applied to other cell types as well.  

The invention also provides a kit for enhancing gene expression, comprising the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 construct and optionally reagents for nucleic acid delivery and expression analysis.  

### (Use)  

The enhancer-promoter system of the invention has numerous applications, including:  
- Driving expression of antigen-encoding sequences in DNA vaccines  
- Producing therapeutic proteins in gene therapy  
- Generating recombinant viruses for vaccine development  
- Expressing heterologous genes in research and industrial settings  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

#### Introduction of MIE Promoter of HHV-6  

The HHV-6 MIE promoter was identified and characterized through reporter assays. A 971-bp region upstream of the IE1 gene was cloned into a luciferase reporter vector and tested in various cell lines.  

#### Materials and Methods  

The promoter region was amplified by PCR and inserted into the pGL3-basic vector. Constructs were transfected into MRC-5, MeWo, U373, Molt-3, SupT1, and Jurkat cells using Lipofectamine 2000. Luciferase activity was measured 48 hours post-transfection.  

#### Results  

The HHV-6 MIE promoter showed stronger activity than the CMV promoter in T-cell lines (Molt-3, SupT1, Jurkat) but weaker activity in adherent lines (MRC-5, MeWo, U373). Addition of the first intron (HHV-6 MIE-in1) enhanced promoter activity in all cell types, with particularly strong effects in T cells.  

### Example 2  

#### Examination of Poly-A Addition Reaction Derived from Herpes Virus  

The functionality of the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 system was tested in the context of a recombinant varicella-zoster virus expressing DsRed2.  

#### Materials and Methods  

The HHV-6 MIEp-in1-DsRed2 cassette was inserted into a VZV bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC) using Tn7-mediated transposition. Recombinant virus was reconstituted by transfecting MRC-5 cells with BAC DNA.  

#### Results  

Infected cells showed both GFP (from the BAC) and DsRed2 fluorescence, confirming expression from the HHV-6 MIEp-in1 system. Western blotting detected DsRed2 protein in infected cells, demonstrating the system's utility in viral vectors.  

### Example 3  

#### Construction of Specific Deletion System  

To map functional regions of the HHV-6 MIE promoter, deletion mutants were created and tested in reporter assays.  

#### Materials and Methods  

Serial 5' deletions of the promoter were generated by PCR and cloned into pGL3-basic. Constructs were transfected into various cell lines as in Example 1.  

#### Results  

Deletion of the region containing the NF-κB site (nt -381 to -552) significantly reduced promoter activity, identifying this as a critical regulatory region.  

### Example 4  

#### Introduction of Nucleic Acid Molecule Encoding Gene for Expression  

The HHV-6 MIEp-in1 system was used to drive expression of a therapeutic gene (mumps virus HN) in a recombinant VZV vaccine candidate.  

#### Materials and Methods  

The HN gene was inserted downstream of HHV-6 MIEp-in1 in a VZV BAC vector. Recombinant virus was generated and tested for HN expression.  

#### Results  

The recombinant virus expressed HN protein, demonstrating the system's utility for multivalent vaccine development.
