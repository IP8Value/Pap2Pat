## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cancer therapeutics and diagnostics. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for enhanced delivery of therapeutic and imaging agents to tumors through modulation of vascular permeability.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Accurate diagnosis and effective treatment of solid tumors remain significant challenges in oncology. While advances in imaging technologies and therapeutic agents have improved outcomes for some cancer types, many tumors remain difficult to detect at early stages and resistant to available treatments.  

The Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect describes a phenomenon wherein macromolecules and nanoparticles preferentially accumulate in tumor tissue compared to normal tissues. This effect results from the leaky vasculature and poor lymphatic drainage characteristic of solid tumors. While the EPR effect has been exploited for drug delivery, its effectiveness is limited by the modest degree of vascular permeability in many tumor types.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods for enhanced delivery of agents to tumors by combining vascular-active permeability entities with therapeutic or imaging agents. In one embodiment, the invention provides a kit comprising a vascular-active agent and a nanoparticle formulation containing a therapeutic or diagnostic payload.  

The composition of the invention may include nanoparticles ranging from 10 nm to 1000 nm in diameter, with preferred embodiments between 50 nm and 200 nm. These nanoparticles may be formulated from various lipid, polymer, or metallic compositions and may carry chemotherapeutic drugs, radionuclides, or contrast agents for imaging.  

Key benefits of this approach include significantly improved tumor-to-normal tissue ratios of therapeutic and imaging agents, enabling more effective cancer treatment with reduced systemic toxicity. The method shows particular promise for treating difficult-to-access tumors such as glioblastoma multiforme.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention utilizes vascular-active permeability entities to enhance delivery of therapeutic or imaging agents to tumors. These entities include cytokines such as TNF-α, bacterial preparations like C. novyi-NT, and microtubule-targeting agents such as combretastatin A4P.  

The delivery system functions by administering both the vascular-active agent and the therapeutic or imaging agent, either simultaneously or in close temporal proximity. The vascular-active agent induces an enhanced permeability state in tumor vasculature (termed E2PR), allowing greater accumulation of co-administered agents.  

Administration may occur through various routes including intravenous, intra-arterial, or direct tumor injection. Optimal timing between administration of the vascular-active agent and the therapeutic/imaging agent falls within a window of 12 hours before to 12 hours after administration.  

The nanoparticle component of the invention may range in size from 10 nm to 1000 nm, with preferred embodiments between 50 nm and 200 nm. These nanoparticles may be composed of lipids (including phosphatidylcholine, cholesterol, and PEGylated lipids), polymers (such as PLGA or chitosan), or metallic cores (including gold or iron oxide).  

Therapeutic or imaging agents may be attached to or encapsulated within the nanoparticles through various means. Covalent conjugation, electrostatic interactions, or hydrophobic embedding may be employed depending on the agent's properties. For encapsulated agents, techniques such as pH gradient loading or chemical trapping may be utilized.  

Therapeutic agents suitable for use with the invention include various chemotherapeutic drugs. Particularly effective agents include doxorubicin, paclitaxel, cisplatin, and other DNA-interacting or microtubule-targeting compounds. The invention allows these agents to achieve therapeutic effects at lower systemic doses due to enhanced tumor accumulation.  

Imaging agents compatible with the invention include various radionuclides for diagnostic and therapeutic applications. Suitable radionuclides include iodine-125, iodine-131, indium-111, and technetium-99m for imaging, as well as yttrium-90 and lutetium-177 for therapy. Contrast agents for MRI (gadolinium chelates) and CT (iodinated compounds) may also be employed.  

Non-invasive detection techniques such as SPECT/CT, PET/CT, or MRI may be used to monitor agent distribution and verify enhanced tumor accumulation. These imaging methods allow real-time assessment of treatment efficacy and potential adjustment of therapeutic protocols.  

Vascular-active permeability agents suitable for inducing E2PR include cytokines (particularly TNF-α), bacterial preparations (such as C. novyi-NT), and microtubule-targeting agents (including combretastatin A4P and vinorelbine). These agents work through distinct but complementary mechanisms to enhance vascular permeability specifically in tumor tissue.  

The mechanisms of action involve both direct effects on vascular endothelial cells and indirect effects mediated through inflammatory pathways. TNF-α, for example, induces cytoskeletal changes in endothelial cells while also stimulating leukocyte recruitment, both contributing to enhanced permeability.  

The invention includes kits for assessment and therapeutic uses containing both the vascular-active agent and the therapeutic/imaging formulation. These kits may include separate vials for reconstitution or pre-mixed formulations, along with instructions for administration protocols.  

The method is applicable to various solid tumors including colorectal carcinoma, glioblastoma, breast cancer, and pancreatic cancer. The approach shows particular efficacy in tumors with traditionally poor drug penetration such as glioblastoma multiforme.  

Current limitations of chemotherapeutic agents include poor tumor penetration and dose-limiting systemic toxicity. The present invention addresses these limitations by both increasing tumor accumulation and reducing normal tissue exposure through the E2PR effect.  

The Enhanced EPR (E2PR) phenomenon represents a significant improvement over conventional EPR, achieving tumor-to-blood ratios exceeding 22:1 in preclinical models. This enhancement results from pharmacologic manipulation of tumor vasculature rather than reliance on inherent vascular abnormalities.  

In a glioblastoma multiforme (GBM) model, E2PR mediated by TNF-α facilitated significant accumulation of nanoparticles in brain tumors that normally show poor drug penetration. This accumulation translated to prolonged survival in treated animals, demonstrating the method's potential for treating challenging tumor types.  

The versatility of this approach allows combination with various existing therapeutic regimens. The vascular-active component may be tailored to the specific tumor type and combined with different nanoparticle formulations depending on the desired therapeutic or diagnostic outcome.  

In summary, the invention provides a general method for enhancing tumor delivery of therapeutic and imaging agents through controlled modulation of vascular permeability. This approach significantly improves upon existing EPR-based delivery methods and shows promise for treating a wide range of solid tumors.  

### Example 1  

The following materials and methods were used to demonstrate the invention:  

CT26 murine colorectal adenocarcinoma cells and GL261 glioma cells were maintained in appropriate media under standard culture conditions. Reagents included Bolton-Hunter reagent, TNF-α, various radioiodines, control antibodies, and liposomal doxorubicin (Doxil). Lipid components for nanoparticle formulation included hydrogenated egg phosphatidylcholine, cholesterol, and DSPE-PEG2000.  

Animal models included both subcutaneous CT26 tumors in BALB/c mice and orthotopic GL261 brain tumors in C57BL6 mice. Tumor implantation followed standard protocols with appropriate anesthesia and stereotactic guidance for brain tumors.  

A monoclonal antibody against C. novyi-NT liposomase was generated using peptide immunization and hybridoma technology. Antibodies were radioiodinated using IODO-GEN method and purified by size exclusion chromatography.  

Liposomes were prepared by thin film hydration followed by extrusion through polycarbonate membranes. The resulting nanoparticles had mean diameters of approximately 100 nm with low polydispersity. Radioiodinated Bolton-Hunter reagent was encapsulated using a chemical trapping method based on arginine entrapment.  

Biodistribution studies quantified tumor and normal tissue accumulation of radiolabeled agents. SPECT/CT imaging provided non-invasive assessment of agent distribution over time. Statistical analysis of survival data utilized log-rank tests to determine significance between treatment groups.  

Cell culture and animal experiments were conducted under sterile conditions with appropriate controls. All procedures followed institutional guidelines for animal welfare and experimental design.  

### Example 2  

Bacterial infection with C. novyi-NT was investigated as a means to enhance antibody accumulation in tumors. Mice bearing CT26 tumors received intravenous injections of C. novyi-NT spores along with radiolabeled antibodies.  

Results demonstrated significant enrichment of both specific (anti-liposomase) and non-specific (IgG control) antibodies in infected tumors compared to uninfected controls. Biodistribution analysis revealed tumor-to-normal tissue ratios exceeding 4:1, indicating selective accumulation mediated by the bacterial infection.  

### Example 3  

The effects of bacterial infection and the cytokine TNF-α on vascular permeability were compared. TNF-α administration produced similar enhancement of IgG accumulation as C. novyi-NT infection, with peak accumulation occurring 72-96 hours post-injection.  

Liposome experiments demonstrated that TNF-α treatment increased tumor retention of 125I-labeled SSLs, achieving tumor-to-blood ratios up to 22:1. SPECT/CT imaging revealed different kinetics for nanoparticles versus antibodies, with liposome accumulation peaking at 24 hours.  

Comparative studies showed that E2PR effects extend to larger particles (>1 μm) compared to conventional EPR (~100 nm). The timing and volume dependence studies indicated optimal administration windows and applicability to tumors of varying sizes.  

### Example 4  

Therapeutic applications were evaluated using Doxil and radiolabeled liposomes in combination with TNF-α. While single agents showed modest effects, combination therapy produced complete tumor regression and long-term cures in over 75% of animals.  

In the glioblastoma multiforme model, TNF-α enabled significant accumulation of nanoparticles in brain tumors that was not observed with nanoparticles alone. Combination treatment extended survival up to 103 days in this aggressive model, demonstrating the method's potential for treating challenging malignancies.
