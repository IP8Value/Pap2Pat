## DESCRIPTION

Renal disorders represent a significant burden on global healthcare systems, affecting millions of patients worldwide with substantial morbidity and mortality. These conditions encompass a spectrum of diseases affecting kidney structure and function, ranging from acute insults to chronic progressive conditions. The clinical manifestations of nephropathies typically include impaired filtration function, electrolyte imbalances, fluid retention, and proteinuria, often progressing to end-stage organ failure without appropriate intervention. 

Acute kidney injury (AKI) constitutes a sudden decline in renal function characterized by rapid deterioration of filtration capacity, typically occurring over hours to days. This condition manifests through elevated serum creatinine levels, decreased urine output, and potential electrolyte disturbances. The pathophysiology of AKI involves multiple mechanisms including hypoperfusion, direct tubular toxicity, inflammatory responses, and obstructive processes. 

The etiological factors contributing to AKI development can be systematically categorized into three principal groups based on their anatomical relationship to the kidney. Pre-renal causes represent the most common category, involving conditions that reduce renal perfusion without primary parenchymal damage, such as hypovolemia, heart failure, or medications affecting renal blood flow. Intrinsic renal causes encompass direct damage to kidney structures, including acute tubular necrosis, glomerulonephritis, interstitial nephritis, and vascular pathologies. Post-renal causes involve obstruction of urinary outflow at any level from the renal pelvis to the urethra, including conditions such as ureteral obstruction, bladder outlet obstruction, or urethral strictures.

Clinical diagnosis of AKI relies on established consensus criteria incorporating both functional and structural parameters. The Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes (KDIGO) guidelines define AKI based on either an absolute increase in serum creatinine of ≥0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours or a 50% increase from baseline within 7 days, or reduced urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours. While serum creatinine remains the most widely used biomarker for AKI diagnosis, significant limitations exist regarding its sensitivity and specificity. Creatinine levels may not rise until approximately 50% of renal function is lost, and its concentration is influenced by numerous non-renal factors including muscle mass, age, gender, and nutritional status.

The search for alternative biomarkers with improved diagnostic characteristics has identified several molecular candidates with potential clinical utility. Current practice for evaluating renal disease biomarkers typically involves measurement of proteins or metabolites in blood or urine samples, often combined with functional assessments. Particular attention is given to identifying high-risk patient populations who may benefit from early biomarker testing, including those undergoing major surgery, receiving nephrotoxic medications, or with pre-existing chronic kidney disease.

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) represents a progressive condition characterized by persistent structural or functional renal abnormalities lasting more than three months. The global prevalence of CKD continues to rise, driven by aging populations and increasing rates of diabetes and hypertension. Reliable histological and serological markers are urgently needed to improve risk stratification and guide therapeutic decisions in CKD patients. Several biomarkers have shown promise in predicting disease progression, including markers of tubular injury, fibrosis, and inflammation.

End-stage renal disease (ESRD) marks the final phase of CKD, requiring renal replacement therapy through dialysis or transplantation. Identifying patients at risk for rapid progression to ESRD remains a critical clinical challenge. Proteinuria has long served as a key risk marker for CKD progression, but alternative biomarkers may provide additional prognostic information. The relationship between CKD and cardiovascular disease (CVD) is particularly significant, as cardiovascular complications represent the leading cause of mortality in this population. Biomarker candidates for CVD risk assessment in CKD patients include markers of vascular calcification, endothelial dysfunction, and chronic inflammation.

Recent advances in molecular biology have identified numerous protein biomarker candidates associated with both AKI and CKD progression. Gene expression analysis of human kidney cell lines, particularly proximal tubular epithelial cells, has revealed distinct transcriptional profiles associated with disease states. Among these molecular markers, the extracellular matrix proteoglycan versican has emerged as a promising candidate for renal disease risk assessment.

Versican demonstrates significant potential as an AKI risk factor and has been implicated in various vascular tumor markers. Its pathogenic role in crescentic glomerulonephritis has been demonstrated through multiple experimental models. The inhibition of versican and development of antibodies targeting specific versican domains represent potential therapeutic strategies. Particular attention has focused on the determination of versican isoforms V0 and V1, which show distinct expression patterns in renal pathology. Molecular mapping studies have revealed differential distributions of these isoforms in various disease states.

Increased expression of specific versican isoforms has been observed during tumor cell growth and renal injury. Antibodies targeting the NH2-terminal domain or glycosaminoglycan attachment domain of versican have shown potential for diagnostic and therapeutic applications. ## EXAMPLES

Chronic kidney disease represents a growing global health concern, with increasing prevalence and significant associated morbidity and mortality. The causes of CKD are diverse, including diabetes, hypertension, glomerulonephritis, and inherited disorders, while the consequences encompass progressive loss of renal function, cardiovascular complications, and eventual need for renal replacement therapy. The identification of reliable biomarkers for CKD progression remains an urgent unmet need in nephrology.

Versican has emerged as a potential biomarker candidate due to its involvement in extracellular matrix remodeling and association with renal pathology. The following examples illustrate the application and validation of versican isoforms as biomarkers for renal disease progression.

### Example 1

A patient cohort comprising 74 individuals with various proteinuric kidney diseases underwent diagnostic kidney biopsies. RNA isolation from biopsy specimens was performed using the RNeasy® Micro Kit, followed by reverse transcription into cDNA. Real-time PCR analysis was conducted using isoform-specific TaqMan® Gene Expression Assays for VCAN0 and VCAN1, with cyclophilin A serving as the reference gene.

Cell culture methods employed human renal proximal tubule cells (HK2) and human kidney fibroblasts (TK-173) maintained under standard conditions. Versican isoform expression analysis revealed significant correlations with estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) at follow-up. Patients were classified as having stable or progressive disease based on serum creatinine doubling or progression to ESRD during follow-up.

Comparative analysis demonstrated significantly higher expression of both VCAN0 (1.56-fold, p=0.034) and VCAN1 (1.67-fold, p=0.011) in progressive versus stable patients. Linear regression analysis established the predictive value of versican isoforms independent of traditional risk factors. The combination of glomerulosclerosis, baseline creatinine, and VCAN1 expression provided the strongest predictive model (adjusted R2=0.2438, p=0.0001).

These results demonstrate that versican isoforms V0 and V1 serve as biomarkers of progressive renal disease, providing prognostic information beyond conventional clinical parameters.

### Example 2

An extended patient cohort provided additional kidney biopsy specimens for validation studies. RNA isolation and real-time PCR methods followed the same protocols as in Example 1. Cell culture methods included additional human cell lines for comparative versican expression analysis.

Versican isoform expression again correlated significantly with follow-up renal function parameters. The differential expression between stable and progressive patients confirmed the findings of Example 1, with cell-specific expression patterns observed across different renal cell types. Immunohistochemical studies localized versican expression primarily to the tubulointerstitial compartment, with increased staining in areas of fibrosis.

The validation cohort results reinforced the conclusion that versican isoforms V0 and V1 represent biomarkers of progressive renal disease, with consistent performance across different patient populations.

### Discussion

The potential of molecular biomarkers to improve CKD management is increasingly recognized. Versican demonstrates particular promise as a biomarker for histopathological damage and disease progression. The results from Examples 1 and 2 consistently show that versican isoform expression provides prognostic information that could guide therapeutic decisions.

The structure and function of versican as an extracellular matrix proteoglycan explains its involvement in renal pathology. Differential expression patterns of versican isoforms in various renal compartments suggest distinct roles in disease pathogenesis. The predictive performance of versican isoforms, particularly when combined with traditional risk factors, supports their potential clinical utility.

In conclusion, versican represents a promising biomarker candidate for chronic kidney disease, with particular value in predicting disease progression and identifying patients who may benefit from intensified therapeutic interventions.

### Example 3

A glomerulonephritis mouse model was employed to evaluate versican expression in experimental renal injury. Renal versican mRNA expression analysis revealed a 3.5-fold increase (p=0.02) in diseased animals compared to controls after 14 days. These findings confirm versican as a marker of renal injury in experimental models, supporting the human data observations.

### Example 4

Immunohistochemical analysis of versican protein expression in human renal biopsies demonstrated specific localization patterns. Versican protein was qualified as a marker through systematic comparison with clinical and histological parameters. The protein expression patterns correlated with mRNA findings, validating versican as a consistent biomarker across different detection platforms.
