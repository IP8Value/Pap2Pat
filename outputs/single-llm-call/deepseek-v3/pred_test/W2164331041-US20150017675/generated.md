## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cell penetrating peptides (CPPs) represent a class of peptides capable of translocating across cellular membranes through non-endocytic mechanisms. These peptides have gained significant attention for their potential applications in diagnostics and therapeutics due to their ability to deliver cargo molecules into cells. Among CPPs, the Model Amphipathic Peptide (MAP) with the amino acid sequence KLALKLALKALKAALKLA-NH2 has been extensively studied. MAP is believed to adopt an alpha-helical conformation where hydrophobic side chains align along one hemicircumference of the helix while positively charged side chains align along the opposite side.  

The mechanisms underlying CPP internalization remain an active area of research. Initial studies suggested that MAP uptake occurs through non-endocytic pathways, as evidenced by its uptake at low temperatures and under energy-depleted conditions. However, subsequent investigations have revealed mixed results, with some evidence supporting both energy-dependent and independent mechanisms. Studies using giant lipid membrane vesicles demonstrated that MAP uptake persists even in the absence of endocytosis, further complicating the understanding of its internalization pathways.  

MAP and other CPPs have been explored for their ability to shuttle various cargo molecules, including proteins, nucleic acids, and small polar compounds, across plasma membranes. For instance, MAP conjugated to polylysine has been used to form multiplexes with siRNA, demonstrating enhanced gene silencing efficiency compared to conventional transfection methods. Similarly, MAP conjugated to peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) via disulfide or thioether linkages has shown successful intracellular delivery, with endosomal release playing a critical role in nuclear uptake.

### Invention  

The present invention provides conjugates for detecting cellular redox state, comprising a first segment and a second segment linked via a redox-sensitive linkage. The first segment includes a cell-penetrating peptide (CPP), such as the Model Amphipathic Peptide (MAP), which facilitates cellular uptake. The second segment includes a cargo peptide, such as CLKANL, which is detectable upon internalization and reduction.  

The redox-sensitive linkage is preferably a disulfide bond, which is cleavable under reducing conditions within the cell. The conjugate further includes a fluorophore/quencher pair, such as fluorescein amidite (FAM) and 5(6)-carboxytetramethylrhodamine (TAMRA), wherein the fluorophore is quenched by the quencher until the disulfide bond is reduced, enabling fluorescence detection.  

The cell-penetrating peptide may be selected from, but not limited to, MAP, penetratin, TAT, or transportan. The cargo peptide may vary depending on the intended application, with examples including CLKANL or other sequences suitable for conjugation and detection.  

The invention further provides a method for detecting cellular redox state, comprising: (a) contacting a cell with the conjugate; (b) detecting uptake of the conjugate by monitoring fluorescence of the first segment (e.g., TAMRA); and (c) measuring redox state by detecting fluorescence of the second segment (e.g., FAM) upon reduction of the disulfide bond.  

The method enables real-time monitoring of cellular redox state, with applications in drug discovery, diagnostic imaging, and therapeutic delivery. For example, the conjugate may be used to screen for redox-modifying agents by assessing changes in fluorescence signal in response to test compounds. Additionally, the invention may be applied in optical or scintigraphic imaging to visualize redox dynamics in vivo.  

### Materials and Methods  

Reagents used in the invention include, but are not limited to, N-acetylcysteine (NAC), 1-chloro-2,4-dinitrobenzene (CDNB), l-buthionine sulfoximine (BSO), diamide, reduced glutathione (GSH), and oxidized glutathione (GSSG). Peptide synthesis and labeling are performed using standard FMOC solid-phase chemistry, with purification by HPLC and analysis by mass spectrometry.  

Cell culture and transfection protocols involve growing cells (e.g., BJ fibroblasts, IMR90 fibroblasts, H9c2 cardiomyocytes) in Dulbecco's Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with fetal bovine serum (FBS). Stable cell lines expressing redox-sensitive green fluorescent protein (roGFP) are generated using retroviral transduction and selection with puromycin.  

Reductide assays are conducted in GSH-containing buffers, with fluorescence measured using a plate reader (e.g., Synergy HT) at excitation/emission wavelengths of 485 nm/528 nm (FAM) or 530 nm/590 nm (TAMRA). Live-cell imaging is performed using fluorescence microscopy (e.g., Olympus FV1000) with cells maintained at 37°C in 5% CO2.  

Flow cytometry is employed to analyze TAMRA and FAM fluorescence in cells treated with redox-modifying agents. Statistical analysis includes Student's t-test for comparing two groups and one-way ANOVA for multiple groups, with p-values <0.05 considered significant.  

### Statistical Analysis  

Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical comparisons are performed using Student's t-test for two groups or one-way ANOVA for multiple groups. P-values <0.05 are considered statistically significant, and p-values <0.01 are considered highly significant.  

## Results  

### Effects of GSH/GSSG on Reductide Redox-Dependent Fluorescence  

Reductide fluorescence in GSH-containing buffer demonstrates time-dependent increases in FAM emission intensity, indicative of disulfide bond reduction. The presence of GSSG slows the development of FAM fluorescence and reduces maximal intensity, reflecting the dependence of reduction kinetics on glutathione redox potential. TAMRA emission intensity, by contrast, remains constant over time, confirming its role as a stable reporter of conjugate uptake.  

### Flow Cytometry  

Flow cytometry reveals time-dependent increases in both TAMRA and FAM signals, with TAMRA fluorescence predominating in cells pretreated with reducing agents (e.g., NAC). FAM signal exhibits slower kinetics due to export of the reduced cargo peptide, as observed in live-cell imaging.  

### Reductide Response to a Small Library of Redox Modifying Compounds  

Screening with a redox-modifying compound library (e.g., Enzo Life Sciences Screen-Well™ REDOX library) demonstrates dose-dependent changes in FAM fluorescence. Antioxidants generally increase FAM signal, while pro-oxidants decrease it, with exceptions at high concentrations where some antioxidants exhibit pro-oxidant effects.  

## Discussion  

### Reductide Uptake as Well as Reduction Depends on Cellular Redox State  

The rate of FAM signal development reflects both cellular uptake and intracellular reduction of the disulfide bond. Redox state influences uptake kinetics, as evidenced by faster TAMRA signal appearance in reduced versus oxidized cells. This property enables targeted delivery of imaging or therapeutic agents to cells with specific redox profiles.  

### Pro-Oxidants Activate an Antioxidative Response  

Low-dose pro-oxidants (e.g., H2O2) induce antioxidative gene expression, leading to increased reduction capacity and FAM signal. High doses overwhelm this response, causing oxidative stress and signal suppression. These findings align with published studies on redox-sensitive transcription factors (e.g., Nrf2) and antioxidant enzyme regulation.
