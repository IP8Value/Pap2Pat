## DESCRIPTION  

### TECHNICAL FIELD AND INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel compositions and methods for regulating Src kinase activity through interaction with the Na+/K+-ATPase. Specifically, the invention provides peptide inhibitors and activators derived from the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit that specifically target the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src signaling complex. These compositions have therapeutic applications in diseases where Src kinase activity is dysregulated, including cancer, cardiovascular diseases, and other pathological conditions characterized by aberrant cell signaling. The invention further provides assays for identifying small molecule modulators of this pathway.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Cardiotonic steroids such as ouabain and marinobufagenin have long been used for their positive inotropic effects in heart failure. These compounds bind to and inhibit Na+/K+-ATPase, the enzyme responsible for maintaining cellular sodium and potassium gradients. Recent studies have revealed that Na+/K+-ATPase also functions as a signal transducer, forming a receptor complex with Src family kinases that mediates many of the cellular effects of cardiotonic steroids.  

Src family kinases are non-receptor tyrosine kinases that regulate diverse cellular processes including proliferation, differentiation, and survival. Aberrant Src activity is implicated in numerous diseases, making it an attractive therapeutic target. Current Src inhibitors suffer from lack of specificity and off-target effects due to the high conservation of the kinase domain across protein kinases.  

The interaction between Na+/K+-ATPase and Src represents a novel regulatory mechanism. The Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit contains specific domains that bind to and inhibit Src kinase activity. When cardiotonic steroids bind to Na+/K+-ATPase, they induce conformational changes that release this inhibition, activating Src and downstream signaling pathways. This discovery reveals the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex as a unique therapeutic target that could allow pathway-specific modulation of Src activity.  

While cardiotonic steroids can modulate this pathway, their use as drugs is limited by their potent inhibition of Na+/K+-ATPase ion transport function, which leads to toxicity. There remains an unmet need for compounds that specifically target the signaling function of Na+/K+-ATPase without affecting its ion pumping activity. The present invention addresses this need by providing peptide modulators derived from the Src-interacting domains of Na+/K+-ATPase, as well as methods for identifying additional small molecule modulators of this pathway.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method for regulating Src kinase activity through its interaction with Na+/K+-ATPase. The invention identifies the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex as a receptor for ouabain-provoked signal transduction and provides specific targets for developing modulators of this pathway.  

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising peptide inhibitors or activators derived from the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit. Specifically, the invention provides peptides derived from the ND1 domain (amino acids 379-435) of Na+/K+-ATPase that bind to and inhibit Src kinase activity. The invention further provides cell-permeable versions of these peptides conjugated to protein transduction domains such as HIV-Tat.  

The invention also provides peptides derived from the Src kinase domain that interact with Na+/K+-ATPase. These peptides can be used to activate or inhibit the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src signaling pathway depending on their specific sequence and binding properties.  

The compositions of the invention include isoform-specific Src inhibitors and activators that target particular Src family members or specific pools of Src associated with Na+/K+-ATPase. The invention further provides methods for developing small molecule modulators of this pathway and rapid screening assays to identify such compounds.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT  

Src family kinases are key regulators of cell signaling with diverse physiological roles. The development of specific Src inhibitors has been challenging due to the high conservation of kinase domains across protein kinases. The present invention overcomes this limitation by targeting the unique interaction between Src and Na+/K+-ATPase.  

The invention provides peptide inhibitors derived from the ND1 domain of Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit. These peptides, exemplified by the 20-amino acid NaKtide (peptide 3 in Fig. 4A), specifically bind to and inhibit Src kinase activity with an IC50 of approximately 70 nM. The invention further provides cell-permeable versions of these inhibitors, such as HIV-Tat conjugated NaKtide (pNaKtide), which shows enhanced potency (IC50 ~4 nM) and specifically targets the Na+/K+-ATPase-associated pool of Src in cells.  

Conversely, the invention provides peptide activators derived from the Src kinase domain that can disrupt the inhibitory interaction between Na+/K+-ATPase and Src. These peptides mimic the regions of Src that interact with Na+/K+-ATPase and can be used to activate Src signaling in a controlled manner.  

The interaction between Na+/K+-ATPase and Src represents a novel regulatory mechanism. Na+/K+-ATPase binds to and inhibits Src through specific domains including the ND1 region. Binding of cardiotonic steroids to Na+/K+-ATPase induces conformational changes that release this inhibition, activating Src and downstream signaling pathways. The peptides of the invention mimic or disrupt these interactions to provide precise control over Src activity.  

The Src-inhibitory peptides of the invention offer several advantages over existing Src inhibitors. They show greater specificity for the Na+/K+-ATPase-associated pool of Src and have minimal effects on other kinases. For example, NaKtide inhibits Lyn kinase with much lower potency (IC50 ~2.5 μM) compared to Src, and shows no activity against PKC even at 10 μM concentration.  

The invention further provides methods for developing small molecule modulators of the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src interaction. By identifying the key interacting domains and residues, the invention enables rational design of compounds that specifically target this pathway. Screening assays using the disclosed peptides can identify additional modulators with therapeutic potential.  

#### Binding of Src to Na+/K+-ATPase Forms a Functional Signaling Complex  

The interaction between Na+/K+-ATPase and Src was characterized using multiple complementary approaches. Co-immunoprecipitation experiments demonstrated that endogenous Na+/K+-ATPase and Src form a complex in LLC-PK1 cells. Fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) analysis confirmed this interaction in live cells, showing energy transfer between Src-CFP and YFP-tagged Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit.  

In vitro binding assays mapped the interaction domains between these proteins. The kinase domain of Src binds specifically to the ND1 region (amino acids 379-435) of Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit. This interaction was confirmed using GST pull-down assays with purified proteins. Binding was concentration-dependent and occurred in the absence of detergent, indicating a direct interaction.  

Functional studies showed that Na+/K+-ATPase regulates Src activity. Knockdown of Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit in PY-17 cells increased basal Src activity, which could be rescued by re-expression of wild-type α1 subunit. Interestingly, a pumping-null mutant (D371E) also rescued Src activity, demonstrating that the regulatory interaction is independent of ion transport function.  

Ouabain treatment induced Src activation by disrupting the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex, as shown by decreased FRET efficiency between the proteins. This activation led to downstream phosphorylation of ERK1/2, which was blocked by the Src inhibitor PP2. The invention's peptide inhibitor pNaKtide similarly blocked ouabain-induced ERK activation by specifically targeting the Na+/K+-ATPase-associated Src pool.  

These findings establish the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex as a functional signaling receptor that mediates the effects of cardiotonic steroids. The complex represents a novel therapeutic target for diseases involving dysregulated Src activity. The domain mapping and functional characterization provided herein enable the development of specific modulators of this pathway.  

#### Functional Characterization of Src-Interacting Na+/K+-ATPase Using RNA Interference Assay  

The functional significance of the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src interaction was further investigated using RNA interference. Stable knockdown of Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit in PY-17 cells increased basal Src activity and ERK1/2 phosphorylation. This phenotype was rescued by expression of rat α1 subunit, confirming the specificity of the knockdown.  

Notably, expression of a pumping-null mutant (D371E) also restored normal Src regulation, demonstrating that the ion transport and signaling functions of Na+/K+-ATPase are separable. This finding is crucial for therapeutic development, as it shows that compounds can target the signaling function without affecting electrolyte homeostasis.  

The RNAi approach also revealed that Na+/K+-ATPase knockdown abolished ouabain-induced activation of Src and ERK1/2, confirming that these effects require intact Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complexes. In contrast, EGF-induced ERK activation was unaffected, demonstrating pathway specificity.  

FRET analysis in knockdown and rescue cell lines provided quantitative measures of Na+/K+-ATPase/Src interaction. These assays can be used to screen for compounds that modulate the complex formation. The invention thus provides both research tools and therapeutic leads for targeting this pathway.  

### Example III  

Binding of the ND1 domain to Src was demonstrated using GST pull-down assays. GST-ND1 (amino acids 379-435) specifically pulled down His-tagged Src from solution, while control GST showed no binding. The interaction was direct and did not require additional cellular factors.  

### Example IV  

The kinase domain of Src (KD1) was shown to bind Na+/K+-ATPase in reciprocal pull-down assays. GST-tagged Src kinase domain pulled down Na+/K+-ATPase from cell lysates, confirming the reciprocal nature of the interaction.  

### Example V  

Functional studies confirmed the role of ND1 and KD1 in Na+/K+-ATPase-mediated regulation of Src. Expression of YFP-ND1 in cells reduced Src activity, while peptides derived from KD1 disrupted the inhibitory complex and activated Src signaling.  

### Example VI  

The therapeutic potential of ND1-derived peptides was demonstrated in prostate cancer cells. pNaKtide inhibited cell growth by specifically targeting Src activity, showing the utility of these compounds in cancer treatment.  

### Example VII  

Peptide P3 (NaKtide) was identified as a potent Src inhibitor. P3 inhibited Src activity in vitro with an IC50 of 70 nM, and the cell-permeable version (pNaKtide) blocked ouabain-induced signaling in cells. P3 showed excellent membrane permeability and specificity for the Na+/K+-ATPase-associated Src pool.  

The invention thus provides comprehensive compositions and methods for targeting the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src signaling complex. These advances enable precise modulation of Src activity for therapeutic applications while minimizing off-target effects.
