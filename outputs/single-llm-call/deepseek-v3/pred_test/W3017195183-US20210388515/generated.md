# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present disclosure relates generally to electrochemical cells for hydrogen gas production and electricity generation. More specifically, the disclosure pertains to protonic ceramic electrochemical cells (PCECs) incorporating triple conducting oxide electrodes capable of operating reversibly between electrolysis and fuel cell modes at reduced temperatures. The disclosed technology encompasses novel electrode compositions, cell configurations, and systems for efficient hydrogen production and power generation.  

## BACKGROUND  

The increasing share of renewable energy sources in power generation has created significant challenges for grid stability due to the intermittent nature of solar and wind resources. Hydrogen gas production through water electrolysis has emerged as a promising solution for energy storage, as hydrogen can be subsequently converted back to electricity when needed. High-temperature electrolysis using solid oxide electrolysis cells (SOECs) offers advantages over low-temperature alternatives due to improved thermodynamic efficiency and reduced electrical energy requirements.  

Conventional solid oxide cells typically employ oxygen-ion conducting electrolytes that operate at temperatures above 700°C. Proton-conducting ceramic electrolysis cells (PCECs) have been developed as an alternative technology capable of operating at intermediate temperatures (400-700°C) while maintaining high efficiency. These cells utilize proton-conducting ceramic membranes that enable direct production of dry hydrogen gas without subsequent separation from water vapor.  

Despite these advantages, widespread deployment of PCEC technology has been hindered by limitations in electrode materials. Conventional mixed ionic-electronic conducting (MIEC) electrodes confine electrochemical reactions to triple phase boundaries (TPBs) where electrolyte, electrode, and gas phases meet. The development of triple conducting oxides (TCOs) that simultaneously transport protons, oxygen ions, and electrons could extend reaction zones throughout the electrode bulk, significantly enhancing performance. However, few viable TCO materials have been identified due to the difficulty of incorporating sufficient proton conductivity while maintaining adequate electronic and oxygen-ion conduction.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present disclosure provides electrochemical cells, systems, and methods for efficient hydrogen production and electricity generation. The disclosed technology features a protonic ceramic electrochemical cell comprising a first electrode, a proton-conducting membrane, and a second electrode. The first electrode incorporates a triple conducting layered perovskite material capable of simultaneous proton, oxygen-ion, and electron conduction.  

In various embodiments, the first electrode comprises a praseodymium nickel cobaltite (PrNi0.5Co0.5O3-δ, PNC) perovskite material that demonstrates exceptional triple conductivity and catalytic activity for both water oxidation and oxygen reduction reactions. The proton-conducting membrane may include barium cerate-zirconate based materials such as BaCe0.4Zr0.4Y0.1Yb0.1O3 (BCZYYb4411). The second electrode typically comprises a nickel-cermet material suitable for hydrogen evolution and oxidation reactions.  

The disclosure further provides systems for hydrogen gas production incorporating the electrochemical cells along with steam sources, power supplies, and gas handling components. The systems enable reversible operation between electrolysis mode for hydrogen production and fuel cell mode for electricity generation. Methods of operating the electrochemical cells include applying potential differences to split water into hydrogen and oxygen gases in electrolysis mode, and generating electricity through electrochemical combination of hydrogen and oxygen in fuel cell mode.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following detailed description provides specific embodiments of the disclosed invention with reference to accompanying illustrations. It will be apparent that various modifications and variations can be made without departing from the scope of the invention.  

The negative electrode (cathode in electrolysis mode) comprises a nickel-based cermet material such as Ni-BaCe0.4Zr0.4Y0.1Yb0.1O3. The positive electrode (anode in electrolysis mode) consists of the triple conducting PNC perovskite material. The electrolyte separating the electrodes is a dense proton-conducting ceramic membrane, typically 10-50 μm thick, composed of BCZYYb4411 or similar barium cerate-zirconate materials.  

The term "configured" as used herein refers to structural arrangements specifically adapted to perform designated functions. The term "substantially" indicates a degree of deviation that does not negate the stated characteristic. Numerical values modified by "about" or "approximately" encompass variations of ±10% from the specified value.  

Referring to FIG. 1, an electrochemical cell 100 includes a first electrode 102, a proton-conducting membrane 104, and a second electrode 106. The first electrode 102 comprises the triple conducting layered perovskite material PNC having the general formula PrNi0.5Co0.5O3-δ. This material exhibits an orthorhombic perovskite structure (space group Pbnm) with lattice parameters a = 5.405 Å, b = 5.380 Å, and c = 7.617 Å at 600°C.  

The proton-conducting membrane 104 consists of BCZYYb4411 electrolyte material with thickness ranging from 10 to 50 μm. Alternative electrolyte materials include BaZr0.8Y0.2O3-δ, BaCe0.9Y0.1O3-δ, and their doped derivatives. The second electrode 106 is typically a nickel-cermet composite such as Ni-BCZYYb4411 with porosity between 30-50% to facilitate gas transport.  

The electrochemical cell 100 may be configured in planar, tubular, or other geometric forms with active surface areas ranging from 1 cm² to 100 cm². Cell fabrication involves tape-casting of electrode and electrolyte layers followed by co-sintering at temperatures between 1400-1500°C.  

FIG. 3 illustrates a system 300 for producing hydrogen gas and generating electricity. The system includes a steam source 302 providing humidified gas to an electrochemical apparatus 304 containing one or more electrochemical cells 100. A power source 308 applies potential differences across the cells during electrolysis operation. Optional components include gas analyzers, heat exchangers, and hydrogen storage vessels.  

During electrolysis mode operation, steam from source 302 contacts the first electrode 102 while the second electrode 106 is exposed to dilute hydrogen or inert gas. Application of a potential difference (1.2-1.6 V) splits water molecules at the first electrode, producing oxygen gas and protons. The protons migrate through membrane 104 to the second electrode 106 where they combine with electrons to form hydrogen gas.  

In fuel cell mode, hydrogen gas interacts with the second electrode 106, dissociating into protons and electrons. The protons permeate through membrane 104 while electrons travel through an external circuit. At the first electrode 102, oxygen gas combines with protons and electrons to form water, generating electricity in the process.  

The system enables rapid switching between modes (within seconds) or delayed transitions depending on operational requirements. Produced hydrogen and oxygen gases may be stored for later use or immediately consumed in fuel cell operation. Thermal energy from exhaust streams can be recovered to improve system efficiency.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1 - Electrolysis Mode Performance  

An electrochemical cell with PNC first electrode and BCZYYb4411 electrolyte (30 μm thick) demonstrated current densities of 1.31 A/cm² at 1.4 V and 600°C, 0.82 A/cm² at 550°C, and 0.62 A/cm² at 500°C using humidified air (~10% H2O) and 10% H2/90% Ar as reactant gases. The cell maintained stable operation for over 200 hours at 500°C with minimal degradation.  

### Example 2 - Comparison of Electrolysis Mode Performance  

The PNC-based cell outperformed conventional protonic ceramic electrolysis cells, showing 2-3 times higher current densities at equivalent operating conditions. At 500°C and 1.4 V, the cell achieved 0.84 A/cm² compared to 0.34-0.58 A/cm² for prior art cells with alternative electrode materials.
