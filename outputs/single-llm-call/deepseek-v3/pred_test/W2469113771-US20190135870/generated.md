# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present disclosure relates to polypeptides and nucleic acid molecules derived from an adeno-associated virus (AAV) endogenous viral element (EVE) identified within marsupial genomes. More particularly, the invention encompasses isolated nucleic acid molecules encoding capsid polypeptides of a marsupial AAV-EVE (mAAV-EVE1), isolated capsid polypeptides encoded thereby, recombinant AAV virions comprising such capsid polypeptides, and methods for producing and using the same. The invention further provides vectors comprising nucleic acid molecules encoding mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptides, host cells comprising such vectors or recombinant AAV virions, and methods for introducing heterologous sequences into host cells using recombinant AAV virions comprising mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptides.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE  

Gene therapy has emerged as a promising approach for treating genetic disorders by introducing functional genes into target cells to compensate for defective genes or provide therapeutic proteins. Viral vectors, particularly those derived from adeno-associated viruses (AAVs), have gained prominence due to their ability to efficiently deliver genetic material to various cell types while exhibiting low pathogenicity and immunogenicity. AAVs are small, non-enveloped viruses belonging to the genus Dependoparvovirus within the family Parvoviridae.  

The AAV genome comprises a single-stranded DNA molecule approximately 4.7 kilobases in length, flanked by inverted terminal repeats (ITRs) that serve as origins of replication and packaging signals. The genome contains two major open reading frames (ORFs): the rep gene encoding non-structural proteins involved in viral replication and gene regulation, and the cap gene encoding structural capsid proteins VP1, VP2, and VP3 that form the icosahedral viral capsid.  

Recombinant AAV (rAAV) vectors have been developed by replacing the viral rep and cap genes with a transgene expression cassette, while retaining the ITRs necessary for packaging and replication. These vectors are produced by providing the rep and cap genes in trans along with helper virus functions, typically from adenovirus. While rAAV vectors have shown promise in clinical applications, limitations remain regarding their tissue tropism, immunogenicity, and production efficiency.  

Current rAAV vectors are primarily based on primate AAV serotypes, which may not optimally target all desired cell types or evade immune responses in certain patients. There exists a need for alternative AAV capsid variants with distinct tropisms and reduced immunogenicity in humans. The discovery and characterization of novel AAV sequences from non-primate sources could provide such alternatives.  

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE  

The present disclosure provides an adeno-associated virus-derived endogenous viral element (mAAV-EVE1) identified within marsupial genomes. The invention encompasses nucleic acid molecules encoding capsid polypeptides of mAAV-EVE1, including isolated nucleic acid molecules comprising nucleotide sequences having at least 80% sequence identity to SEQ ID NO:1 (mAAV-EVE1 cap gene).  

The disclosure further provides isolated capsid polypeptides encoded by mAAV-EVE1, including VP1, VP2, and VP3 polypeptides, and functional fragments thereof. These polypeptides may comprise amino acid sequences having at least 80% sequence identity to SEQ ID NO:2 (mAAV-EVE1 VP1), SEQ ID NO:3 (mAAV-EVE1 VP2), or SEQ ID NO:4 (mAAV-EVE1 VP3).  

The invention provides chimeric capsid polypeptides comprising at least one region from mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptide and at least one region from a second AAV capsid polypeptide. Such chimeric polypeptides may have at least 80% sequence identity to a reference sequence while containing variable regions from mAAV-EVE1.  

Recombinant AAV virions comprising mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptides or chimeric capsid polypeptides are provided, along with nucleic acid molecules encoding such polypeptides. The disclosure encompasses vectors comprising these nucleic acid molecules, including episomal and integrating vectors such as plasmids, cosmids, AAV vectors, lentiviral vectors, retroviral vectors, adenoviral vectors, and herpesviral vectors.  

Host cells comprising the recombinant AAV virions or vectors are provided, along with methods for introducing heterologous sequences into host cells using the recombinant AAV virions. Methods for producing chimeric capsid genes through DNA family shuffling of mAAV-EVE1 and other AAV cap genes are disclosed, as are methods for producing recombinant AAV virions comprising chimeric capsid proteins.

Recombinant AAV virions comprising mAAV-EVE1 capsid polypeptides are provided. Methods for producing these virions involve transfecting packaging cell lines with vectors providing mAAV-EVE1 capsid proteins, rep proteins, helper virus functions, and a recombinant AAV genome.  

Purification methods include cesium chloride gradient centrifugation and solid support purification using affinity tags or antibodies. The rAAV virions may be formulated in pharmaceutical compositions with suitable carriers, diluents, or adjuvants for therapeutic administration.  

### Host Cells  

Host cells comprising nucleic acid molecules, vectors, or rAAV virions of the invention are provided. Suitable host cells include mammalian cell lines such as HEK293, HeLa, and CHO cells, as well as primary cells and stem cells.  

### Compositions and Methods  

Pharmaceutical compositions comprising rAAV virions and pharmaceutically acceptable carriers are disclosed. The compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration including intravenous, intramuscular, intrathecal, or direct tissue injection.  

Methods for introducing heterologous sequences into host cells using rAAV virions are provided, including in vitro, ex vivo, and in vivo approaches. Therapeutic methods involve administering rAAV virions to subjects for treatment of genetic disorders, cancers, or other diseases.  

### Capsid Diversification  

Methods for diversifying AAV capsids through DNA family shuffling of mAAV-EVE1 and other AAV cap genes are disclosed. The process involves fragmenting parental cap genes, reassembling chimeric genes through PCR, and cloning these into vectors for rAAV production.  

Chimeric capsid gene libraries may be screened for variants with desired tropism or reduced immunogenicity. The disclosure provides methods for producing and characterizing such libraries, as well as methods for identifying improved capsid variants.  

### Other Uses  

The mAAV-EVE1 sequences have additional applications beyond gene delivery. The sequences may be used to detect related AAV sequences through PCR amplification using mAAV-EVE1-specific primers.  

The sequences may serve as phylogenetic markers for marsupial species identification, particularly among macropodoids. Methods for species assignment using mAAV-EVE1 locus analysis are provided.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1. Identification and Characterization of mAAV-EVE1 Genome and Proteins  

Genomic DNA was extracted from Macropus giganteus liver tissue using a FastPrep homogenizer and DNeasy columns. PCR amplification using degenerate primers targeting conserved AAV regions yielded a product with significant homology to dependoparvovirus sequences.  

Linker-mediated genome walking was performed to isolate adjacent endogenous viral sequences. The full-length mAAV-EVE1 sequence was reconstructed and found to be 96.4% identical to an AAV-EVE in Macropus eugenii. Phylogenetic analysis placed mAAV-EVE1 within Macropodiformes, suggesting integration in their common ancestor.  

Maximum likelihood reconstruction of the ancestral mAAV-EVE1 sequence was performed using alignments from sixteen macropodoid species. The inferred sequence contained rep and cap ORFs with frameshifts and stop codons corrected based on conserved AAV protein motifs.  

The mAAV-EVE1 Rep protein (581 amino acids) contained conserved nuclease and helicase domains. Structural modeling showed conservation of rolling circle replication motifs and Superfamily 3 helicase architecture, despite substitution of key catalytic residues.  

The capsid gene encoded VP1 (82.2 kDa), VP2, and VP3 (60.9 kDa) proteins. VP3 modeling using AAV8 as template revealed conserved β-barrel core structure with variable surface loops. VR-VII was substantially larger in mAAV-EVE1 than in primate AAVs, creating unique surface topology.  

A putative AAP gene was identified within the cap ORF, encoding a 201 amino acid protein with conserved features of the AAP family. Transcription factor binding sites analysis revealed potential regulatory elements similar to those in AAV2.  

### Example 2. Modification of mAAV-EVE Capsid  

The mAAV-EVE1 capsid protein was modified through site-directed mutagenesis of surface-exposed residues. Variable regions were targeted for alteration based on structural comparisons with primate AAVs.  

Modified capsid genes were cloned into expression vectors and tested for capsid formation. Functional variants were identified through transduction assays in various cell types.  

### Example 3. Production of rAAV  

The mAAV-EVE1 cap gene was cloned into a plasmid providing AAV rep and adenoviral helper functions. This plasmid was co-transfected into HEK293T cells with a vector containing a reporter gene flanked by AAV2 ITRs.  

Cells were harvested 72 hours post-transfection, lysed, and treated with benzonuclease. rAAV was precipitated with PEG, purified by CsCl gradient centrifugation, and dialyzed into formulation buffer.  

### Example 4. Assessment of rAAV  

Purified rAAV was titered by qPCR and evaluated for transduction efficiency in various cell lines. The virions demonstrated distinct tropism compared to primate AAV serotypes, with particular efficiency in certain mesenchymal cell types.  

Immunogenicity was assessed in mouse models, showing reduced neutralizing antibody responses compared to AAV2. These results demonstrate the utility of mAAV-EVE1 capsids for gene therapy applications.
