# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase S (EndoS), specifically engineered mutant forms of EndoS exhibiting enhanced transglycosylation activity and reduced hydrolysis activity toward immunoglobulin G (IgG) glycoproteins. More particularly, the invention provides novel EndoS mutant enzymes capable of efficient glycan remodeling of therapeutic antibodies through optimized enzymatic transglycosylation reactions.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Glycoproteins constitute a major class of biomolecules wherein carbohydrate moieties are covalently attached to polypeptide backbones. The sugar chains of glycoproteins play crucial roles in biological functions including protein folding, stability, intracellular trafficking, and molecular recognition. Posttranslational glycosylation represents a fundamental modification process that generates structural and functional diversity in proteins.  

Among glycoproteins, antibodies constitute a particularly important class, with immunoglobulin G (IgG) representing the most abundant antibody isotype in human serum. The biological activity of IgG antibodies, including effector functions such as antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), is significantly influenced by the structure of N-linked glycans attached to the conserved asparagine residue at position 297 (N297) in the Fc region.  

Endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidases (ENGases) constitute a family of enzymes that hydrolyze the β-1,4-glycosidic bond between the two N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) residues in the chitobiose core of N-linked glycans. Various ENGases have been identified from different microbial sources, including EndoA from Arthrobacter protophormiae, EndoD from Streptococcus pneumoniae, EndoM from Mucor hiemalis, EndoH from Streptomyces plicatus, EndoF2 from Elizabethkingia meningoseptica, EndoE from Enterococcus faecalis, and EndoS from Streptococcus pyogenes.  

The EndoS enzyme from Streptococcus pyogenes exhibits unique specificity toward IgG Fc glycans. The EndoS D233Q mutant, wherein aspartic acid at position 233 is replaced with glutamine, demonstrates improved transglycosylation activity compared to wild-type EndoS. EndoS2, a related enzyme from Streptococcus pyogenes serotype M49, has also been engineered for glycan remodeling applications.  

Current limitations of known EndoS enzymes include substantial residual hydrolysis activity that necessitates high donor-to-acceptor ratios during transglycosylation reactions. This technical limitation impedes large-scale applications of enzymatic glycan remodeling for therapeutic antibody production. There exists a pressing need for improved EndoS variants exhibiting enhanced transglycosylation efficiency while minimizing product hydrolysis.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Technical Problem  

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the insufficient transglycosylation efficiency and excessive hydrolysis activity exhibited by known EndoS enzymes during glycan remodeling reactions. Existing EndoS mutants require high concentrations of expensive sugar donor substrates and demonstrate suboptimal product yields due to competing hydrolysis reactions. These limitations hinder cost-effective large-scale production of homogeneous antibody glycoforms for therapeutic applications.  

### Solution to Problem  

The present invention provides novel mutant forms of EndoS engineered through systematic site-directed mutagenesis. These mutants incorporate specific amino acid substitutions at critical positions within the enzyme's structure, substantially improving transglycosylation efficiency while minimizing hydrolysis activity.  

Key mutations include substitutions at positions D233, Q303, E350, D405, and other residues identified as influencing substrate binding and catalytic activity. Particularly effective mutant enzymes combine the D233Q mutation with additional mutations such as Q303L, E350Q, or D405A. The invention further encompasses multiple mutants incorporating combinations of these beneficial substitutions.  

### Advantageous Effects of Invention  

The mutant EndoS enzymes of the present invention provide significant advantages over prior art enzymes:  
1) Substantially improved transglycosylation efficiency exceeding 90% product yield  
2) Markedly reduced hydrolysis activity toward reaction products  
3) Effective operation at lower donor-to-acceptor ratios (5:1 vs 20:1 for prior enzymes)  
4) Faster reaction kinetics enabling shorter processing times  
5) Compatibility with one-pot transglycosylation systems using natural glycan donors  

These improvements enable more efficient and cost-effective production of homogeneous antibody glycoforms for therapeutic applications and research purposes.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The following describes embodiments of the present invention in detail. Amino acid residues are designated using standard one-letter codes. N297-linked sugar chains refer to N-glycans attached to asparagine at position 297 in the Fc region of IgG antibodies. These glycans typically contain a core structure of two N-acetylglucosamine (GlcNAc) residues with additional sugar moieties including mannose, galactose, fucose, and sialic acid.  

Sugar donor molecules for transglycosylation reactions include synthetic sugar oxazolines such as sialylglycan-oxazoline (SG-Oxa) and natural glycopeptides like sialoglycopeptide (SGP) derived from egg yolk. Acceptor molecules comprise deglycosylated antibodies or Fc-containing proteins prepared by enzymatic removal of native glycans.  

EndoS is a 108 kDa enzyme comprising 995 amino acid residues with a modular structure containing catalytic and carbohydrate-binding domains. The wild-type enzyme exhibits hydrolysis activity toward IgG Fc glycans, cleaving the β-1,4-glycosidic bond between the two core GlcNAc residues. The EndoS D233Q mutant demonstrates enhanced transglycosylation activity by stabilizing the oxazolinium ion intermediate through a substrate-assisted mechanism.  

The present invention provides further improved EndoS mutants incorporating additional amino acid substitutions that optimize transglycosylation while minimizing hydrolysis. These mutants maintain the overall structural framework of EndoS while introducing specific modifications at key positions influencing substrate binding and catalysis.  

### <Mutant Enzyme of the Present Invention>  

The mutant enzymes of the present invention comprise EndoS variants incorporating specific amino acid substitutions that confer improved transglycosylation characteristics. Essential mutations include the D233Q substitution combined with at least one additional mutation selected from: H122A, P184L, D279S, Y282F, Q303L, Y348F, E350Q, Y402F, D405A, and R406A.  

These mutations are strategically located in regions critical for transglycosylation activity, particularly residues interacting with the α1-3 arm and branched mannose regions of the substrate glycan. The mutations alter size, charge, or hydrophobicity at key positions to optimize donor binding and intermediate stabilization while destabilizing hydrolysis transition states.  

The mutant enzymes exhibit significantly reduced hydrolysis activity while maintaining or improving transglycosylation efficiency. Preferred embodiments include:  
- D233Q/Q303L showing <20% hydrolysis at 48 hours with 96% transglycosylation yield  
- D233Q/E350Q demonstrating 97% transglycosylation efficiency  
- D233Q/D405A exhibiting rapid transglycosylation kinetics (78% yield at 2 hours)  

Particularly effective combinations incorporate mutations at multiple positions:  
- D233Q/Q303L/E350Q  
- D233Q/Y402F/D405A  
- D233Q/D279S/Y282F  

These multiple mutants demonstrate negligible hydrolysis activity while maintaining high transglycosylation yields, enabling efficient glycan remodeling at low donor-to-acceptor ratios.  

### <Gene, Host Cell, Method of Producing Enzyme>  

The present invention provides recombinant genes encoding the mutant EndoS enzymes, suitable expression vectors, host cells, and production methods. The genes are constructed by site-directed mutagenesis of the wild-type EndoS gene, introducing specified nucleotide substitutions corresponding to desired amino acid changes.  

Preferred expression systems utilize E. coli host cells transformed with plasmid vectors containing GST-fusion constructs of mutant EndoS genes. Protein expression is induced by IPTG, followed by affinity purification using glutathione sepharose chromatography. Large-scale production employs additional purification steps including gel filtration chromatography to obtain highly pure, active enzyme preparations.  

### <Sugar Remodeling>  

The invention provides methods for sugar remodeling of antibodies using the mutant EndoS enzymes. The process involves:  
1) Preparing acceptor molecules by enzymatic deglycosylation of IgG or Fc-containing proteins using wild-type EndoS  
2) Providing sugar donor molecules including synthetic oxazolines or natural glycopeptides  
3) Conducting transglycosylation reactions under optimized conditions (pH 7.4, 28-37°C)  
4) Monitoring reaction progress by electrophoretic or mass spectrometric analysis  
5) Purifying remodeled glycoproteins using affinity and hydroxyapatite chromatography  

Preferred reaction conditions utilize 5-10 molar equivalents of donor to acceptor, with enzyme concentrations of 0.1-1 mg/mL. Reaction times range from 2-24 hours depending on the specific mutant enzyme employed.  

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the present invention without limiting its scope.  

### Example 1  

#### Design, Expression and Purification of EndoS Mutant Enzymes  

EndoS mutants were designed based on structural analysis and sequence alignment. Site-directed mutagenesis introduced specified amino acid changes into the EndoS gene cloned in pGEX-4T-3 vector. E. coli BL21(DE3) transformants were cultured in LB medium with ampicillin, induced with 0.1 mM IPTG at 16°C for 20 hours. Cells were lysed by sonication in PBS buffer containing lysozyme and DNaseI.  

Supernatants were affinity purified using glutathione sepharose, buffer-exchanged to PBS, and concentrated to 2-7 mg/mL. Large-scale preparations employed additional gel filtration chromatography using Superdex 200. Protein yields ranged from 5-15 mg per liter of culture, with purity >95% as assessed by SDS-PAGE.  

### Example 2  

#### Preparation of SG-Oxa Sugar Donor  

Sialylglycan-oxazoline (SG-Oxa) was synthesized from sialoglycopeptide (SGP) through chemical conversion. The product structure was confirmed by mass spectrometry and NMR spectroscopy. SG-Oxa solutions were prepared in 50 mM Tris-HCl buffer (pH 7.4) at concentrations of 10-100 mM for transglycosylation reactions.  

### Example 3  

#### Preparation of (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-Trastuzumab Acceptor  

Trastuzumab (100 mg) was incubated with wild-type EndoS (500 μg) in 50 mM phosphate buffer (pH 6.0) at 37°C for 4 hours. The reaction mixture was purified sequentially using protein A affinity chromatography and hydroxyapatite chromatography, yielding 85.7 mg of (Fucα1,6)GlcNAc-trastuzumab. Product identity was confirmed by LC-MS (observed m/z 49505.5 vs calculated 49506.0 for heavy chain).  

### Example 4  

#### Measurement of Hydrolysis and Transglycosylation Activities  

Hydrolysis activity was assayed using trastuzumab substrate (500 μg) with mutant enzymes (10 μg) in 50 mM Tris-HCl (pH 7.4) at 30°C. Aliquots were analyzed by capillary electrophoresis at 1, 2, 4, 8, 24, and 48 hours to quantify deglycosylation.  

Transglycosylation activity was measured using deglycosylated trastuzumab (800 μg), SG-Oxa (5 eq), and mutant enzymes (16 μg) under similar conditions. Reaction progress was monitored by electrophoresis, with product yields calculated from peak areas.  

Key results:  
- D233Q/Q303L: 96% transglycosylation at 24 h, <20% hydrolysis at 48 h  
- D233Q/D405A: 78% transglycosylation at 2 h  
- D233Q/Y402F/D405A: No detectable hydrolysis at 48 h  

These data demonstrate the superior performance of the invented mutant enzymes compared to prior art variants.
