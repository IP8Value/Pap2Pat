# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) biology and regenerative medicine. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and compositions for expanding hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) through modulation of Musashi-2 (MSI2) expression and activity, particularly via inhibition of Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase (CYP1B1) and activation of MSI2. The disclosed methods and compositions have applications in stem cell transplantation, treatment of hematopoietic disorders, and ex vivo expansion of therapeutic cell populations.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Umbilical cord blood (CB) has emerged as an important source of HSPCs for transplantation therapies. However, the limited number of HSPCs in a single CB unit presents significant challenges for clinical applications, particularly in adult patients where cell doses are often insufficient for successful engraftment. Current ex vivo expansion techniques have shown limited success in generating clinically relevant numbers of functional HSPCs while maintaining their stem cell properties.  

A critical need exists for understanding the molecular pathways that regulate HSPC self-renewal and expansion. While several signaling pathways and transcription factors have been implicated in HSPC maintenance, the precise mechanisms controlling these processes remain incompletely understood. The RNA-binding protein MSI2 has been identified as a key regulator of HSPC function, with its expression highest in primitive HSC compartments and progressively decreasing upon differentiation. Previous studies have shown that MSI2 overexpression enhances HSC self-renewal activity, while its loss of function significantly impairs reconstitution capacity. However, the upstream regulators of MSI2 expression and its downstream effectors in human HSPCs have not been fully characterized, limiting the development of targeted approaches to manipulate this pathway for therapeutic benefit.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing novel methods and compositions for expanding HSPCs through modulation of the MSI2 pathway. The invention is based on the discovery that overexpression of MSI2 induces pro-self-renewal phenotypes in HSPCs by attenuating aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) signaling. Specifically, MSI2 post-transcriptionally represses CYP1B1, an effector of AHR signaling, leading to enhanced HSPC self-renewal and expansion.  

The invention provides methods for increasing self-renewal and expansion of HSPCs, wherein the HSPCs are CD34+ cells derived from various sources including umbilical cord blood, peripheral blood, or bone marrow. The methods may be performed in vivo, ex vivo, or in vitro, and involve contacting HSPCs with a CYP1B1 inhibitor. Suitable CYP1B1 inhibitors include, but are not limited to, 2,4,3',5'-tetramethoxystilbene (TMS), other stilbenoids, flavonoids, coumarins, alkaloids, synthetic molecules, natural products, antibodies, antisense nucleic acid molecules, siRNA molecules, aptamers, and peptibodies. The CYP1B1 inhibitor is preferably used at concentrations below its IC50 value to achieve selective inhibition without toxicity.  

The methods further demonstrate that CYP1B1 inhibition increases the frequency and/or number of CD34+ cells while maintaining their stem cell properties. In additional embodiments, the invention provides for contacting HSPCs with both a CYP1B1 inhibitor and an MSI2 activator. Suitable MSI2 activators include MSI2 mRNA or DNA molecules, fragments thereof, MSI proteins, or functional fragments thereof, which may be conjugated to protein transfection reagents for enhanced delivery.  

The expanded HSPCs produced by these methods are suitable for therapeutic administration to subjects with diseases or disorders treatable by HSPCs, including hematopoietic disorders, malignancies, autoimmune diseases, immunodeficiencies, and conditions resulting from chemotherapy or radiation treatment. The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising CYP1B1 inhibitors and MSI2 activators, formulated for use in cell culture or direct administration.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

For purposes of the present invention, the following terms are defined as follows:  

A hematopoietic stem cell (HSC) refers to a multipotent stem cell capable of self-renewal and differentiation into all blood cell lineages. Hematopoietic progenitor cells (HPCs) are more committed progeny of HSCs with limited self-renewal capacity but retaining multilineage differentiation potential. Differentiation denotes the process by which stem cells mature into specialized cell types. Culturing refers to the maintenance and propagation of cells under controlled conditions in vitro. Hematopoietic lineage encompasses all blood cell types derived from HSCs, including myeloid (granulocytes, monocytes, erythrocytes, megakaryocytes) and lymphoid (T cells, B cells, NK cells) lineages.  

### Methods of Expanding HSPCs  

The invention provides methods for expanding HSPCs by inhibiting CYP1B1 to increase self-renewal and expansion. This approach maintains the primitive state of HSPCs while increasing their frequency and number. The methods involve testing cell populations for biomarkers characteristic of HSPCs, such as CD34, CD38, CD90, and CD45RA, and separating HSPCs from heterogeneous cell populations using positive or negative selection techniques based on these markers. Following expansion, hematopoietic stem cells may be further separated from progenitor cells based on differential marker expression.  

The expansion methods may be performed in vivo, ex vivo, or in vitro, using HSPCs derived from a single subject or multiple subjects. The expanded HSPCs are particularly suitable for transplantation applications. CYP1B1 inhibition may be achieved by contacting HSPCs with small molecule inhibitors including stilbenoids (e.g., TMS), flavonoids, coumarins, or alkaloids. Alternatively, CYP1B1 activity may be modulated using synthetic molecules, natural products, antibodies or fragments thereof, antisense nucleic acids, siRNA molecules, aptamers, or peptibodies.  

In parallel or sequential approaches, HSPCs may be contacted with MSI2 activators to enhance expansion. Suitable MSI2 activators include MSI2 mRNA or DNA molecules, fragments thereof, MSI proteins, or functional fragments thereof, optionally conjugated to protein transfection reagents for improved cellular uptake. The methods further encompass contacting HSPCs with combinations of CYP1B1 inhibitors and MSI2 activators, potentially with additional agents that induce hematopoiesis, to achieve synergistic effects on HSPC expansion.  

### Expanded HSPCs for Therapeutic Use  

The invention provides expanded HSPCs for therapeutic applications, including transplantation and treatment of various disorders. The HSPCs may be autologous or allogenic, with expansion achieved in vivo or ex vivo using the disclosed methods. The expanded HSPCs are particularly useful for treating subjects with hematopoietic disorders, malignancies, autoimmune diseases, immunodeficiencies, or those who have received chemotherapy or radiation. Administration involves delivering a therapeutically effective amount of expanded HSPCs to reconstitute the hematopoietic system.  

### Compositions  

The invention encompasses compositions containing CYP1B1 inhibitors, MSI2 activators, or combinations thereof. These compositions may be formulated for cell culture applications or as pharmaceutical preparations. Cell culture embodiments include media supplements containing optimized concentrations of CYP1B1 inhibitors and/or MSI2 activators for ex vivo HSPC expansion. Pharmaceutical compositions may combine these active agents with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or delivery vehicles for in vivo use.  

### Methods of Culturing Leukemic Cells  

The invention further provides methods for producing leukemic cell cultures using CYP1B1 inhibitors or MSI2 activators. These cultures serve as valuable tools for screening assays to identify additional compounds affecting hematopoiesis or leukemogenesis. The methods involve standard techniques for cell growth and maintenance, with monitoring of proliferation, differentiation, and death parameters.  

### Example 1: Inhibition of Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase Causes Expansion of Hematopoietic Stem and Progenitor Cells  

This example describes a comprehensive experimental approach to validate the effects of CYP1B1 inhibition on HSPC expansion. Using a murine model with appropriate animal care and ethics approval, human hematopoietic cells were isolated and characterized by flow cytometry. HSPC fractions were sorted based on surface marker expression, and MSI2 expression was analyzed across different populations.  

Lentiviral vectors were designed and produced to modulate MSI2 expression, with validation of transduction efficiency and biological effects. Cord blood cells transduced with these vectors were subjected to clonogenic progenitor assays, with analysis of colony formation and secondary colony-forming unit potential. Cultures were supplemented with AHR agonists to assess pathway interactions.  

Detailed cellular analyses included assessment of cell cycle progression, proliferation rates, quiescence, and apoptosis using intracellular flow cytometry. RNA-sequencing data were processed to identify differentially expressed genes and predict AHR targets. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) compared gene expression patterns between experimental conditions.  

Functional validation involved transplantation experiments using immunodeficient NSG mice, with analysis of human chimerism and multilineage engraftment. Limiting dilution assays quantified stem cell frequency, while UV cross-linking immunoprecipitation sequencing (CLIP-seq) characterized RNA-protein interactions. Statistical analyses confirmed the reproducibility and significance of observed effects.  

### Results & Discussion  

The experimental results demonstrate that MSI2 plays a central role in post-transcriptionally controlling human HSPC self-renewal. Lentiviral overexpression of MSI2 significantly enhanced colony-forming unit capacity, particularly secondary CFU-GEMM formation, indicating preservation of primitive cell function. In vitro cultures showed altered cell cycling patterns with reduced apoptosis, consistent with enhanced survival of stem cell populations.  

Transplantation experiments revealed that MSI2 overexpression increased short-term repopulating cell activity, while knockdown impaired clonogenic potential. RNA-seq analysis identified distinct gene expression programs associated with MSI2 modulation, particularly affecting AHR signaling pathways. Functional studies confirmed CYP1B1 as a key effector of these effects, with its inhibition phenocopying MSI2 overexpression in promoting ex vivo expansion.  

Mechanistic insights revealed that MSI2 attenuates AHR signaling through post-transcriptional regulation of CYP1B1. Pathway analysis and GSEA confirmed the central role of this axis in maintaining stem cell properties. These findings provide a molecular basis for developing targeted approaches to expand functional HSPCs for therapeutic applications.  

### Example 2: CYP1B1 Knockdown Increases the Number of CD34 Marked HSPCs  

This example demonstrates that specific knockdown of CYP1B1 using shRNA lentiviruses significantly increases the number of CD34+ cells in lineage-depleted cord blood samples. The results confirm CYP1B1 as a critical regulator of HSPC maintenance and validate its targeting as a strategy for stem cell expansion.  

### Example 3: High Throughput Screen to Test CYP Family Inhibitors  

A screening approach was developed to identify additional compounds affecting CYP family members, particularly CYP1B1. The screen evaluated multiple compounds for their ability to promote HSPC expansion while maintaining stem cell properties, identifying both responsive and non-responsive agents. This platform enables discovery of novel modulators with potential therapeutic applications.  

### Example 4: Combinatorial HSPC Promoting Effects of SR1 with TMS  

This example demonstrates synergistic effects when combining the AHR antagonist SR1 with the CYP1B1 inhibitor TMS. The combinatorial treatment resulted in significantly greater HSPC expansion than either agent alone, while better preserving stem cell function. These findings support development of combination therapies for optimal ex vivo expansion protocols.
