# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for treating ischemic conditions, particularly ischemic heart disease and ischemia-reperfusion injury. More specifically, the invention provides a carbohydrate composition extracted from Panax ginseng that demonstrates cardioprotective effects by preserving mitochondrial function and activating cell survival pathways during ischemic events.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Ischemic heart disease (IHD) remains the leading cause of mortality worldwide, accounting for approximately 7.4 million deaths annually. The primary pathological mechanism involves myocardial damage due to coronary occlusion, leading to deprivation of oxygen and nutrients. While timely reperfusion can reduce short-term mortality, it often triggers additional cellular damage through oxidative stress and inflammation—a phenomenon known as ischemia-reperfusion injury. Current treatment options, including pharmacological interventions and surgical procedures, are limited by adverse effects and incomplete protection against reperfusion injury.  

Traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) has explored natural compounds for cardiovascular protection, with Panax ginseng being a prominent candidate. Ginseng extracts have demonstrated therapeutic effects, including reduction of myocardial infarct size and improved lipid profiles. While ginsenosides—glycoside compounds in ginseng—have been extensively studied, polysaccharides from ginseng have been largely overlooked despite evidence of immunomodulatory, antioxidative, and anticancer properties. Prior art lacks effective methods to isolate and utilize ginseng polysaccharides for cardioprotection, particularly in mitigating ischemia-reperfusion injury. Thus, there remains an unmet need for novel treatments derived from ginseng polysaccharides that can protect cardiomyocytes during ischemic events.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a method of treating ischemic conditions by administering a carbohydrate composition extracted from Panax ginseng. The carbohydrate composition comprises acidic polysaccharides with a molecular weight of approximately 3.8 × 10^4 Da, containing glucose (76.31%), galactose (12.70%), and galacturonic acid (5.64%) as primary monosaccharides. The composition is isolated through a series of steps including: (i) obtaining a crude Panax ginseng extract by solvent extraction; (ii) fractionating the crude extract using high-speed counter-current chromatography (HSCCC); (iii) precipitating the active fraction with ethanol; and (iv) purifying the precipitate via anion-exchange chromatography.  

The carbohydrate composition protects cells against hypoxia-reoxygenation-induced death by preserving mitochondrial function, reducing oxidative stress, and activating the RISK pathway (PI3K/Akt and ERK1/2 signaling). It is formulated into pharmaceutical compositions for oral or intravenous administration, optionally combined with excipients such as stabilizers or carriers. The invention further includes methods of preventing ischemia-reperfusion injury by contacting cardiomyocytes with the carbohydrate composition prior to reperfusion.  

Key benefits of the invention include enhanced cardioprotection, reduced oxidative damage, and improved mitochondrial respiration compared to existing therapies. The carbohydrate composition is non-toxic at therapeutic doses and demonstrates superior efficacy compared to whole ginseng extracts.

### Carbohydrate Composition  

The carbohydrate composition is extracted from Panax ginseng and comprises:  
- **Carbohydrate portion (90.28% w/w)**: Predominantly acidic polysaccharides with uronic acid content of 17.93%. The polysaccharides have an apparent molecular weight of 3.8 × 10^4 Da and include glucose, galactose, and galacturonic acid.  
- **Protein portion (9.85% w/w)**: Determined by Bradford assay.  

The composition is isolated via:  
1. **Crude extraction**: Panax ginseng root is dried, cut, and extracted with ethanol or water.  
2. **Fractionation**: The crude extract is subjected to HSCCC using a butanol-water solvent system.  
3. **Ethanol precipitation**: Active fractions are precipitated with 70% ethanol.  
4. **Anion-exchange chromatography**: The precipitate is purified on a DEAE column eluted with NaCl gradients.  

### Therapeutic Methods  

The carbohydrate composition is administered in an effective amount (e.g., 12.5–200 μg/mL) to:  
- Reduce apoptosis by inhibiting caspase-3/7 and caspase-9 activation.  
- Attenuate oxidative stress by lowering ROS and malondialdehyde (MDA) levels while increasing superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity.  
- Preserve mitochondrial membrane potential (MMP) and ATP production.  
- Activate the RISK pathway via GR/ER-dependent phosphorylation of PI3K/Akt, ERK1/2, and eNOS.  

### Pharmaceutical Formulations  

The composition is formulated as tablets, capsules, or injectables with pharmaceutically acceptable excipients. Preferred routes include oral or intravenous administration.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Extraction and Purification  

1. **Crude Extract Preparation**: Panax ginseng roots were extracted with ethanol (1:10 w/v) at 80°C for 2 hours. The extract was concentrated under vacuum.  
2. **HSCCC Fractionation**: The crude extract (4.12 g) was separated using a butanol-water (1:1) solvent system. Active fractions (F2, F3) were pooled.  
3. **Ethanol Precipitation**: F2/F3 was precipitated with 70% ethanol, yielding a bioactive precipitate.  
4. **Anion-Exchange Chromatography**: The precipitate was purified on a DEAE-650M column, eluting with 0.5 M NaCl to isolate the acidic polysaccharide fraction (AP1).  

### Example 2: Pharmacological Characterization  

**Cell Culture**: H9c2 cardiomyocytes were subjected to hypoxia (3 h) followed by reoxygenation (4 h). AP1 (200 μg/mL) was added 1 h before hypoxia.  

**Results**:  
- AP1 reduced LDH and CK release by 40% (p < 0.001).  
- Caspase-3/7 activity decreased by 50% (p < 0.01).  
- MMP and ATP levels were restored to 85% of normal (p < 0.05).  
- RISK pathway activation was confirmed by increased p-Akt and p-ERK1/2.  

**Conclusion**: AP1 demonstrates significant cardioprotection via mitochondrial preservation and RISK pathway activation.
