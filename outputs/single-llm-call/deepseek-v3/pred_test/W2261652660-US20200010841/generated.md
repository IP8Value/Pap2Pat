# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF INVENTION  

The present invention relates to a novel expression system for regulating transgene expression in plants. More specifically, the invention provides a tightly regulated, reversible expression system that enables precise control over male sterility and fertility restoration in plants for hybrid seed production. The system utilizes tapetum-specific promoters, modified transcription factors, and light-responsive protein degradation mechanisms to achieve stringent spatiotemporal expression of genes of interest.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION AND PRIOR ART  

Hybrid vigor, or heterosis, is a phenomenon wherein hybrid offspring exhibit superior traits compared to their parents. Hybrid crops have revolutionized agriculture by delivering higher yields, improved stress tolerance, and enhanced uniformity. The history of hybrid breeding dates back to the early 20th century, with the discovery of male sterility systems enabling large-scale production of hybrid seeds. Traditional hybrid seed production relies on emasculation, the manual removal of male reproductive organs to prevent self-pollination. However, emasculation is labor-intensive and impractical for many crops.  

Male sterility systems offer an efficient alternative by eliminating the need for manual emasculation. Genetic male sterility (GMS) arises from mutations that disrupt pollen development, but its utility is limited due to difficulties in maintaining sterile lines. Cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS) relies on mitochondrial mutations and is widely used but requires specific restorer genes. Cytoplasmic-genetic male sterility (CGMS) combines CMS with nuclear restorer genes but remains restricted to certain species.  

Transgenic male sterility systems have emerged as versatile alternatives. Tapetum ablation, the targeted destruction of tapetal cells, disrupts pollen development and induces sterility. Various genes have been employed for this purpose, including BARNASE, an RNase that degrades RNA in tapetal cells, and RNase T1, which similarly induces cell death. Other approaches include the mammalian uncoupling protein, which disrupts energy metabolism, and the diphtheria toxin A-chain, which inhibits protein synthesis. The pro-apoptotic gene BAX, autophagy regulator AtBECLIN1, melon ethylene receptor genes, apoptotic protein inhibitors, and cysteine proteases have also been explored for inducing male sterility.  

Fertility restoration is critical for hybrid seed production. In the BARNASE/Barstar system, Barstar inhibits BARNASE activity to restore fertility. Other systems employ antisense RNA, site-specific recombination, or conditional expression to reverse sterility. However, existing systems face limitations such as incomplete restoration, leaky expression, or regulatory concerns over toxic transgenes.  

### LIMITATIONS OF PRIOR ART  

Prior art systems suffer from several drawbacks, including inconsistent sterility, partial fertility restoration, and reliance on toxic genes. Many systems exhibit leaky expression in non-target tissues, leading to unintended phenotypic effects. The requirement for specific restorer genes limits their applicability across species. Additionally, regulatory concerns over foreign genes hinder commercialization.  

### Objectives of the Present Invention  

The present invention aims to overcome these limitations by providing a tightly regulated, reversible expression system for modulating male sterility. Key objectives include:  
- Developing a tapetum-specific expression system using the TA29 promoter.  
- Engineering a regulatory component for stringent expression control via light-responsive protein degradation.  
- Creating a reversible system that enables complete fertility restoration in hybrid offspring.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides a modular expression system comprising three DNA constructs:  
1. **Male Sterility (MS) DNA Construct**: Contains a tapetum-specific promoter (e.g., TA29 with TGTA mutation) driving a sterility-inducing gene (e.g., BECLIN1).  
2. **Expression Subsystem (ES) DNA Construct**: Encodes a fusion protein (HFR1NT131-TBPm3) that complements the TGTA-mutated promoter to enable high-level expression.  
3. **Restoration Subsystem (RS) DNA Construct**: Expresses COP1L105A, a mutant form of COP1 that degrades the fusion protein to abolish transgene expression.  

The system further includes:  
- DNA vectors harboring the MS, ES, or RS constructs.  
- Recombinant host cells (e.g., Agrobacterium) transformed with these vectors.  
- Transgenic plants or seeds derived from such host cells.  
- Methods for producing transgenic or hybrid plants with controlled sterility/fertility.  

The reversible expression system allows:  
- Induction of male sterility by expressing BECLIN1 in tapetal cells.  
- Fertility restoration in hybrids via COP1L105A-mediated degradation of HFR1NT131-TBPm3.  
- Switching off transgene expression in F1 progeny for trait modulation.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Scope of the Invention  

The invention encompasses DNA constructs, vectors, host cells, transgenic plants, and methods for regulating gene expression in plants. The system is particularly suited for hybrid seed production but may also be applied to other agronomic traits.  

### Expression Subsystem (ES) Construction  

The ES construct comprises:  
- A tapetum-specific promoter (e.g., TA29 with TGTA mutation) driving a gene of interest (e.g., gusA or BECLIN1).  
- A second cassette expressing HFR1NT131-TBPm3 under a constitutive promoter (e.g., Pcec).  
The fusion protein HFR1NT131-TBPm3 binds the TGTA-mutated promoter to enable high-level expression.  

### Restoration Subsystem (RS) Construction  

The RS construct expresses COP1L105A under a tapetum-specific promoter (e.g., PA9). COP1L105A interacts with HFR1NT131 to target the fusion protein for degradation, abolishing transgene expression.  

### Generation of F1 Hybrids  

Crossing MS transgenic plants (female) with RS transgenic plants (male) yields F1 hybrids with restored fertility. The COP1L105A in the F1 tapetum degrades HFR1NT131-TBPm3, shutting off BECLIN1 expression.  

### Key Components  

- **Promoters**: TA29 (tapetum-specific), PA9 (tapetum-specific), Pcec (constitutive).  
- **Genes**: BECLIN1 (sterility), gusA (reporter), HFR1NT131-TBPm3 (expression driver), COP1L105A (degradation inducer).  
- **Terminators**: nosT (transcriptional terminator).  

### Applications  

The system enables:  
- Production of male-sterile lines for hybrid breeding.  
- Fertility restoration in hybrid offspring.  
- Conditional expression of agronomic traits.  

### EXAMPLES  

#### Example 1: Construction of DNA Vectors  

- The TA29 promoter was mutated (TATATATG → TGTATATG) via site-directed mutagenesis.  
- Arabidopsis BECLIN1 was cloned into the pBI101 vector under the mutated TA29 promoter.  
- HFR1NT131-TBPm3 was expressed under the Pcec promoter in the same vector.  

#### Example 2: Plant Transformation  

- Tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum) was transformed via Agrobacterium-mediated gene transfer.  
- Transgenic lines were selected using antibiotic resistance markers (kanamycin/hygromycin).  

#### Example 3: Fertility Restoration  

- MS transgenic plants (expressing BECLIN1) were crossed with RS transgenic plants (expressing COP1L105A).  
- F1 hybrids exhibited normal pollen viability and seed set, confirming complete fertility restoration.
