# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of natural language processing (NLP), specifically word sense disambiguation (WSD). WSD refers to the computational process of determining the correct meaning (sense) of a word within a given context sentence. The invention introduces a novel method for aligning glosses (definition sentences) from multiple word sense inventories to improve WSD accuracy, particularly for rare and zero-shot word senses.  

## BACKGROUND  

Word sense disambiguation is a fundamental challenge in natural language processing due to the inherent ambiguity of human language. Supervised WSD models, which rely on human-annotated datasets, have achieved state-of-the-art performance by fine-tuning pre-trained language models. However, these models suffer from two major limitations. First, their performance degrades significantly when predicting rare or zero-shot word senses due to insufficient training examples. Second, they are often restricted to a single predefined word sense inventory (typically WordNet), limiting their generalizability across different lexical resources.  

Existing approaches, such as GlossBERT and Bi-Encoder Models (BEM), encode word senses by comparing context sentences with glosses from a single inventory. While effective for common senses, these methods struggle with low-resource scenarios where annotated data is scarce. Knowledge-based approaches that leverage lexical knowledge bases (e.g., Wikipedia, WordNet) partially mitigate this issue but still rely on a single source of sense definitions.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention addresses these limitations by proposing a gloss alignment algorithm that integrates lexical knowledge from multiple word sense inventories. The algorithm aligns parallel glosses (definitions of the same meaning) across different dictionaries using a maximum-weighted bipartite matching optimization. This alignment enables the generation of a unified semantic equivalence knowledge base, which is used to train a general WSD model.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. **Gloss Alignment Algorithm**: A method for aligning glosses from multiple inventories by maximizing textual similarity scores, computed using pre-trained sentence encoders (e.g., SBERT).  
2. **Semantic Equivalence Recognizer**: A neural model trained on aligned gloss-context pairs to determine whether a word in a context sentence and a gloss are semantically equivalent.  
3. **Transfer Learning Framework**: A two-stage training process where the general model is first pre-trained on aligned inventories and then fine-tuned on task-specific datasets to achieve expert performance.  

The invention demonstrates superior performance on standard WSD benchmarks, achieving state-of-the-art results on both all-words and low-shot (few-shot and zero-shot) WSD tasks. Additionally, it improves accuracy on the Word-in-Context (WiC) task by 6% over baseline models.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Word Sense Predicting Model  

The invention introduces a word sense predicting model that leverages aligned glosses from multiple inventories. The model architecture consists of a semantic encoder (e.g., BERT or RoBERTa) that generates contextualized representations for both target words (in context sentences) and glosses. These representations are compared using a binary classifier to predict semantic equivalence.  

### Gloss Alignment of Word Sense Inventories  

Gloss alignment is formulated as a maximum-weighted bipartite matching problem. Given two sets of glosses (S₁ and S₂) from different inventories, the goal is to find a matching that maximizes the total textual similarity between aligned pairs. The similarity is computed using a pre-trained sentence encoder (e.g., SBERT), and the optimization is solved via linear programming.  

### Training Data Generation  

Positive and negative gloss-context pairs are generated by contrasting aligned and non-aligned glosses across inventories. For example:  
- **Positive Pairs**: A context sentence from one gloss paired with its aligned gloss from another inventory.  
- **Negative Pairs**: A context sentence paired with a non-aligned gloss.  

These pairs are used to train the semantic equivalence recognizer.  

### Model Training and Evaluation  

The model is trained in two stages:  
1. **General Model**: Pre-trained on gloss-context pairs derived from aligned inventories.  
2. **Expert Model**: Fine-tuned on task-specific datasets (e.g., SemCor for WSD).  

Evaluation on benchmark datasets shows that the general model outperforms supervised baselines by 13.1% on zero-shot senses, while the expert model achieves state-of-the-art performance on all-words WSD (1.2% improvement over prior work).  

### Applications  

The invention is applicable to:  
- **Machine Translation**: Improving accuracy by resolving ambiguous word senses.  
- **Information Retrieval**: Enhancing search results by disambiguating query terms.  
- **Lexical Resource Enrichment**: Augmenting dictionaries with aligned glosses from multiple sources.  

### Example Implementations  

1. **Process 400**: Aligns glosses from multiple inventories, computes semantic equivalence scores, and predicts the correct sense of a word in context.  
2. **Process 500**: Matches glosses between two inventories using maximum-weighted bipartite matching and generates positive/negative training pairs.  
3. **Process 600**: Uses the semantic equivalence recognizer to score glosses and predict word senses.  
4. **Process 700**: Fine-tunes the general model on task-specific data to produce an expert model.  

The invention’s novelty lies in its ability to unify disparate lexical resources into a cohesive knowledge base, enabling robust WSD even for rare and zero-shot word senses.
