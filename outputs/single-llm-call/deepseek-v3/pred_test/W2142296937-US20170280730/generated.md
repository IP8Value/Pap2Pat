## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the fields of microbiology, vector control, and infectious disease prevention. More specifically, the invention pertains to novel compositions and methods utilizing Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P) for controlling disease transmission by mosquitoes and other insect vectors. The invention further relates to the use of Csp_P and its bioactive metabolites for inhibiting Plasmodium and dengue virus infection in mosquitoes.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Mosquito-borne diseases such as malaria and dengue fever continue to pose significant public health challenges worldwide. Current control strategies relying primarily on chemical insecticides face growing limitations due to the development of insecticide resistance in mosquito populations and environmental concerns. There remains an urgent need for novel, environmentally sustainable approaches to control mosquito populations and block pathogen transmission.  

The mosquito gut microbiota has emerged as an important factor influencing vector competence for human pathogens. Certain bacterial species naturally found in mosquito midguts have been shown to reduce infection by Plasmodium parasites and dengue virus through various mechanisms including immune system activation and production of anti-pathogen molecules. However, naturally occurring mosquito gut bacteria typically do not persist at sufficiently high levels or exhibit broad enough activity to serve as effective biocontrol agents.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P), a novel bacterial isolate with potent entomopathogenic and anti-pathogen properties. Csp_P demonstrates exceptional ability to colonize mosquito midguts and exhibits broad-spectrum activity against multiple disease-causing pathogens.  

Csp_P displays remarkable anti-Plasmodium activity, significantly inhibiting infection by P. falciparum in Anopheles gambiae mosquitoes. The bacterium also shows strong anti-dengue activity, reducing dengue virus infection in Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. These anti-pathogen effects are observed both in vivo following mosquito colonization and in vitro through direct pathogen inhibition.  

The invention further demonstrates that Csp_P exhibits entomopathogenic activity against both larval and adult stages of multiple mosquito species. Exposure to Csp_P results in rapid mosquito mortality through mechanisms that may include production of toxic metabolites or interference with vital physiological functions.  

The anti-pathogen activities of Csp_P appear to be mediated by stable secondary metabolites produced during biofilm growth. These bioactive compounds retain activity even after biofilm desiccation, suggesting potential for formulation into practical control products. The invention encompasses compositions containing live Csp_P bacteria, bacterial extracts, or purified metabolites for use in disease control strategies.  

Methods of the invention include direct exposure of mosquitoes to Csp_P through baited sugar sources, breeding water treatment, or other delivery mechanisms. The invention provides novel approaches for preventing disease transmission by simultaneously reducing mosquito populations and blocking pathogen development within vector insects.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The following detailed description illustrates the invention by way of example, not by way of limitation. The description will clearly enable one skilled in the art to make and use the invention, and describes several embodiments, adaptations, variations, alternatives and uses of the invention.  

The invention provides novel compositions and methods utilizing Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P) for controlling disease transmission by insect vectors. Csp_P is a Gram-negative bacterium isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes that exhibits unique combinations of entomopathogenic and anti-pathogen properties.  

Csp_P demonstrates exceptional ability to colonize mosquito midguts, achieving colonization rates of 80-97% in laboratory studies. The bacterium persists at high densities (10^4-10^5 CFU/midgut) following introduction through sugar meals or blood meals. This robust colonization capacity enables sustained exposure of pathogens to Csp_P's anti-infective activities within the vector insect.  

The bacterium displays broad-spectrum entomopathogenic activity against multiple mosquito species. Exposure to Csp_P results in rapid mortality of both larval and adult stages of Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti. Adult mosquitoes ingesting Csp_P through sugar meals show significantly reduced longevity compared to controls. Larval exposure to Csp_P in breeding water causes nearly complete mortality within 2-3 days.  

Csp_P exhibits potent anti-Plasmodium activity, significantly inhibiting infection by P. falciparum in Anopheles gambiae. Mosquitoes colonized with Csp_P show reduced oocyst counts following infectious blood meals. The bacterium also demonstrates strong anti-dengue activity, lowering dengue virus infection rates in Aedes aegypti. These anti-pathogen effects occur through both direct pathogen inhibition and potential modulation of mosquito immune responses.  

The anti-pathogen activities are particularly associated with Csp_P biofilm growth. Biofilm preparations show strong inhibition of both Plasmodium developmental stages and dengue virus infectivity in vitro. Remarkably, these inhibitory effects persist after biofilm desiccation, suggesting the presence of stable bioactive metabolites.

### II. Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P)  

Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P) is a Gram-negative bacterium isolated from the midgut of field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. The bacterium exhibits potent insecticidal activity against multiple mosquito species and demonstrates broad-spectrum anti-pathogen properties.  

Molecular characterization of Csp_P's 16S rDNA gene sequence shows 98% similarity to Chromobacterium haemolyticum and Chromobacterium aquaticum. The bacterium grows in standard culture conditions, forming tan-colored colonies on LB agar that darken with age. Unlike some related Chromobacterium species, Csp_P does not produce violacein pigment.  

The invention provides methods of controlling insect vectors by exposing them to compositions containing Csp_P. Effective control can be achieved using live bacteria, bacterial extracts, or purified metabolites. Compositions may incorporate Csp_P in various formulations including liquids, powders, or baits.  

Csp_P produces bioactive compounds with insecticidal and anti-pathogen activity. Extraction procedures may involve culture filtration, solvent extraction, or other methods to isolate these active components. The resulting extracts retain significant activity against both insect vectors and human pathogens they transmit.

### Example 1: Chromobacterium Csp_P Reduces Malaria and Dengue Infection in Vector Mosquitoes  

Mosquito-borne diseases including malaria and dengue fever remain major global health burdens. The gut microbiota of vector mosquitoes influences their competence to transmit pathogens. Certain midgut bacteria can reduce Plasmodium and dengue virus infection through immune activation or production of anti-pathogen factors.  

Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P) was isolated from field-collected Aedes aegypti mosquitoes. The bacterium forms biofilms that significantly affect its behavior and metabolite production. Csp_P demonstrates potent anti-Plasmodium and anti-dengue activity both in vitro and in vivo following mosquito colonization.  

All animal procedures were approved by the Johns Hopkins University Animal Care and Use Committee. Mosquitoes were reared under standard conditions with antibiotic treatment to reduce native microbiota when indicated. Csp_P was introduced via sugar meals containing 10^6-10^8 CFU/ml.  

Csp_P colonized mosquito midguts at high rates (80-97%) and persisted at 10^4-10^5 CFU/midgut. The bacterium significantly reduced adult mosquito longevity and caused nearly complete larval mortality within 2-3 days. Mosquitoes colonized with Csp_P showed significantly lower P. falciparum oocyst counts and dengue virus titers compared to controls.  

In vitro assays demonstrated Csp_P's direct anti-pathogen effects. Biofilm preparations strongly inhibited P. falciparum asexual stages, ookinetes, and gametocytes. Dengue virus infectivity was abolished after exposure to Csp_P biofilm or supernatant. These activities were heat-sensitive but persisted after biofilm desiccation.  

Mechanistic studies ruled out viral sequestration or pH effects as primary inhibition mechanisms. Csp_P also induced expression of mosquito immune genes, suggesting potential immune modulation contributes to anti-pathogen effects. The bacterium showed broad antibacterial activity against other mosquito gut bacteria.  

These findings demonstrate Csp_P's potential as a multi-functional biocontrol agent capable of reducing mosquito populations while blocking pathogen transmission. The stability of bioactive metabolites suggests promise for practical formulation and deployment.  

### Conclusion  

Insect-bacteria associations represent promising avenues for disease control by simultaneously targeting vectors and pathogens. Chromobacterium sp_Panamam (Csp_P) exhibits unique combinations of entomopathogenic and anti-pathogen activities that make it particularly valuable for controlling mosquito-borne diseases.  

Csp_P's ability to colonize mosquito midguts, kill vector insects, and inhibit Plasmodium and dengue virus development provides multiple points of intervention in disease transmission cycles. The production of stable bioactive metabolites enables diverse formulation approaches for field applications.  

The invention encompasses use of Csp_P as a source of novel transmission-blocking compounds and as a live microbial insecticide. These complementary strategies offer sustainable solutions to the growing challenges of insecticide resistance and environmental concerns associated with current control methods.  

### Example 2: Live Chromobacterium Csp_P is a General Mosquitocidal, and Malaria and Dengue Transmission-Blocking Agent  

This example demonstrates Csp_P's efficacy against multiple mosquito species and pathogen transmission stages. Adult Anopheles gambiae and Aedes aegypti mosquitoes were exposed to Csp_P through sugar meals or breeding water treatment.  

Csp_P rapidly colonized mosquito guts, reaching 10^4-10^5 CFU/midgut within 24 hours. The bacterium caused significant mortality in both species, with Anopheles showing particular susceptibility. Larval exposure resulted in complete mortality within 48-72 hours across tested concentrations.  

Pathogen blocking was demonstrated through significant reductions in P. falciparum oocyst counts and dengue virus titers in surviving mosquitoes. Comparative studies showed Csp_P's superior activity relative to other Chromobacterium strains. Biofilm-derived metabolites retained full mosquitocidal and anti-pathogen activity after partial purification.  

These results confirm Csp_P's potential as a broad-spectrum vector control agent. The bacterium's multi-functional activity against diverse mosquito species and human pathogens supports its development for integrated disease management programs.
