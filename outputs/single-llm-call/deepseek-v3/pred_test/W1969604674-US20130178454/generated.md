# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of androgen replacement therapy and specifically to novel compositions and methods for administering testosterone while minimizing undesirable prostatic effects. More particularly, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising testosterone or its analogues in combination with ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) inhibitors, which selectively promote anabolic effects on muscle tissue while substantially reducing stimulation of prostate growth. The invention further relates to methods of treating conditions associated with low testosterone levels, including age-related hypogonadism, sarcopenia, and functional limitations, using these novel therapeutic combinations. Additionally, the invention encompasses diagnostic kits and formulations for optimized delivery of these therapeutic agents.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Testosterone serves as the primary male sex hormone and plays crucial roles in various physiological processes including the development of male reproductive tissues, promotion of secondary sexual characteristics, maintenance of muscle mass and strength, regulation of bone density, and influence on red blood cell production. In adult males, testosterone is primarily secreted by the testes under the control of the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis. The hormone exerts its effects through binding to androgen receptors in target tissues, where it can either act directly or after conversion to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) by the enzyme 5α-reductase.

Testosterone deficiency, clinically referred to as hypogonadism, manifests through a constellation of symptoms including decreased libido, erectile dysfunction, reduced muscle mass and strength, increased body fat, decreased bone mineral density, fatigue, and depressed mood. Hypogonadism may result from primary testicular failure (primary hypogonadism), hypothalamic-pituitary dysfunction (secondary hypogonadism), or a combination of both factors. The prevalence of hypogonadism increases with age, with approximately 20% of men over 60 years and 50% of men over 80 years meeting biochemical criteria for testosterone deficiency.

Current testosterone replacement therapies include intramuscular injections, transdermal gels and patches, buccal tablets, and subcutaneous pellets. While these modalities effectively restore serum testosterone levels and alleviate symptoms of hypogonadism, they are associated with several potential adverse effects. Of particular concern is the stimulation of prostate growth, which may exacerbate symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) or potentially promote the progression of preclinical prostate cancer. Other side effects include erythrocytosis, acne, gynecomastia, and suppression of spermatogenesis.

The relationship between testosterone therapy and prostate health remains complex and incompletely understood. While testosterone does not appear to initiate prostate cancer, it may stimulate the growth of existing prostate cancer cells. This concern has significantly limited the use of testosterone therapy in older men, particularly those with a history of prostate cancer or significant BPH symptoms. The development of therapeutic strategies that can harness the beneficial effects of testosterone on muscle, bone, and sexual function while minimizing stimulation of prostate growth represents a major unmet need in the field of androgen replacement therapy.

Testosterone levels in women, while substantially lower than in men, play important physiological roles in libido, bone health, and muscle maintenance. However, exogenous testosterone administration in women carries risks of virilization, including hirsutism, voice deepening, and clitoromegaly. The need for alternative approaches that can provide the benefits of androgen therapy while avoiding these undesirable effects is particularly acute in postmenopausal women and those with androgen insufficiency syndromes.

The limitations of current testosterone replacement therapies have spurred interest in developing selective androgen receptor modulators (SARMs) that preferentially target muscle and bone while sparing the prostate. While several SARMs have shown promise in preclinical studies, none have yet achieved clinical approval for testosterone replacement indications. The present invention addresses these limitations through a novel approach combining testosterone with specific inhibitors of ornithine decarboxylase (ODC), a key enzyme in polyamine biosynthesis that mediates testosterone's effects on prostate growth while being dispensable for its anabolic actions on muscle.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for achieving selective anabolic effects of testosterone while minimizing stimulation of prostate growth. The invention is based on the discovery that testosterone's effects on prostate tissue are mediated through activation of the ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) pathway, while its anabolic effects on muscle occur through distinct mechanisms involving follistatin-mediated pathways. By combining testosterone administration with ODC inhibitors, the invention achieves dissociation of testosterone's beneficial anabolic effects from its undesirable prostatic stimulation.

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising testosterone or its biologically active analogues in combination with one or more ODC inhibitors. In preferred embodiments, the ODC inhibitor is α-difluoromethylornithine (DFMO), a specific, irreversible inhibitor of ODC that has been approved for human use in other indications. The compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration including oral, transdermal, intramuscular, and subcutaneous delivery. The invention provides specific dosage ranges and administration regimens that optimize the therapeutic ratio between anabolic effects and prostate sparing.

The invention further provides methods for treating conditions associated with low testosterone levels, including age-related hypogonadism, sarcopenia, muscle wasting disorders, and functional limitations. The methods comprise administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of testosterone or its analogue in combination with an ODC inhibitor, wherein the combination therapy increases muscle mass and strength while substantially preventing stimulation of prostate growth. The methods include specific protocols for dose titration, monitoring of therapeutic effects, and assessment of prostate safety parameters.

The invention also encompasses diagnostic kits for identifying subjects who would benefit from the combination therapy and for monitoring therapeutic responses. The kits may include reagents for measuring serum testosterone levels, markers of muscle anabolism, and indicators of prostate growth or function. The kits may further include instructional materials for proper use of the therapeutic combinations and interpretation of test results.

In specific embodiments, the invention provides:
1. A pharmaceutical composition comprising testosterone or its analogue and an ODC inhibitor, wherein the composition promotes muscle growth while substantially preventing prostate growth stimulation.
2. A method for treating low testosterone-associated disorders comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of testosterone or its analogue in combination with an ODC inhibitor.
3. A method for increasing muscle mass and strength in a subject while minimizing prostate stimulation, comprising co-administering testosterone and an ODC inhibitor.
4. A kit for diagnosing and treating low testosterone conditions, comprising testosterone, an ODC inhibitor, and means for assessing therapeutic response and prostate safety.

The invention is supported by extensive preclinical data demonstrating that:
- Follistatin, a mediator of testosterone's effects on muscle, increases lean mass without affecting prostate growth
- Testosterone but not follistatin activates the ODC pathway in prostate tissue
- ODC inhibition blocks testosterone's effects on prostate while preserving its anabolic actions on muscle
- The combination of testosterone and DFMO provides superior therapeutic selectivity compared to testosterone alone

The invention addresses critical limitations of current testosterone replacement therapies by providing a mechanism-based approach to dissociate testosterone's beneficial anabolic effects from its undesirable prostatic stimulation. This represents a significant advance in the field of androgen therapy with broad applications in the treatment of age-related functional decline, hypogonadism, and muscle wasting disorders.

### Testosterone

Testosterone (17β-hydroxyandrost-4-en-3-one) is a steroid hormone belonging to the androgen class. Its chemical formula is C19H28O2, with a molecular weight of 288.43 g/mol. Testosterone serves as the primary male sex hormone and anabolic steroid, playing crucial roles in the development of male reproductive tissues, promotion of secondary sexual characteristics, and maintenance of muscle mass and bone density.

## EXAMPLES

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope. These examples demonstrate the therapeutic principles, experimental approaches, and potential applications of the invention.

### Example 1

This example demonstrates the differential effects of testosterone and follistatin on muscle and prostate tissues, and the discovery that ODC inhibition blocks testosterone's effects on prostate while preserving its anabolic actions on muscle.

Testosterone's anabolic effects on skeletal muscle were compared with those of recombinant follistatin (rFst) in animal models. While both testosterone and rFst increased muscle mass in castrated mice, only testosterone stimulated prostate growth. Microarray analysis revealed that testosterone but not rFst activated the ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) pathway in prostate tissue.

Administration of the ODC inhibitor DFMO in combination with testosterone resulted in maintenance of muscle anabolic effects while substantially preventing prostate growth stimulation. These findings demonstrate that ODC inhibition provides a mechanism to dissociate testosterone's beneficial effects on muscle from its undesirable stimulation of prostate growth.

The example further shows that:
- Follistatin mediates testosterone's effects on muscle through distinct pathways
- ODC is essential for testosterone's prostate effects but dispensable for muscle effects
- The testosterone/DFMO combination provides selective anabolic therapy
- This approach has significant advantages over existing androgen therapies

Potential applications of this discovery include:
- Treatment of age-related functional decline
- Management of hypogonadism with reduced prostate risk
- Muscle wasting conditions requiring anabolic support
- Other clinical scenarios benefiting from selective androgen action
