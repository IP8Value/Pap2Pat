# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to GEDI (Genetically Encoded Death Indicator) polypeptides and their use in detecting cell death. Specifically, the invention provides isolated nucleic acid sequences encoding GEDI polypeptides, vectors and cells comprising such sequences, and methods for monitoring calcium flux and cell death using the GEDI polypeptides. The invention further provides methods for screening agents that modulate cell death and kits comprising the GEDI polypeptides.  

## BACKGROUND  

Calcium ions play a critical role in cellular homeostasis, and disruptions in calcium ion gradients are associated with various pathological conditions, including neurodegenerative diseases. The ability to monitor calcium flux and detect cell death accurately is essential for understanding disease mechanisms and developing therapeutic interventions. Conventional methods for detecting cell death often rely on dyes or stains that may be toxic or provide delayed signals. There is a need for improved tools that can detect cell death acutely and specifically without interfering with cellular physiology.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides methods for detecting cell death by monitoring calcium flux using GEDI polypeptides. The GEDI polypeptides are engineered to fluoresce upon detecting elevated calcium levels associated with cell death, providing an early and specific indicator of irreversible cell death.  

The invention further provides isolated nucleic acid sequences encoding the GEDI polypeptides. These sequences may be operably linked to expression control sequences, such as promoters, enhancers, and tissue-specific regulatory elements, to direct their expression in desired cell types.  

Vectors and cells comprising the nucleic acid sequences are also provided. The vectors may be viral or non-viral and include expression vectors suitable for transfection or transduction into mammalian cells. The cells may be neural cells, cardiac cells, or other cell types in which calcium flux and cell death are of interest.  

Methods for monitoring calcium flux and cell death are described, wherein the GEDI polypeptides are expressed in cells, and fluorescence signals are detected using optical means such as microscopes or automated imaging systems. The methods may be adapted for high-throughput screening of agents that modulate cell death.  

Tissue-specific promoters may be used to restrict GEDI expression to particular cell types, such as neurons or cardiomyocytes. Fluorescent labels, including green, red, and blue fluorescent proteins, may be incorporated into the GEDI constructs to enable multiplexed detection of cell death alongside other biosensors.  

Distinguishable markers, such as nuclear localization signals, may be included to enhance signal resolution in specific subcellular compartments. Modified calcium binding motifs with altered dissociation constants may be employed to tune the sensitivity of the GEDI polypeptides to calcium concentrations.  

The GEDI polypeptides may be expressed in various cell types, including primary neurons, induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived neurons, and other cell lines. Transgenic animals expressing the GEDI polypeptides are also provided for in vivo studies of cell death.

### Vectors  

The invention encompasses vectors comprising nucleic acid sequences encoding GEDI polypeptides. These vectors may be expression vectors designed for transient or stable expression in mammalian cells. Examples of mammalian expression vectors include plasmid-based systems such as pGW1, pCAGGs, and phSyn1.  

Viral vectors may also be employed, including retrovirus-based vectors, lentivirus-based vectors, and other viral delivery systems. Examples of viral vectors include those derived from adeno-associated virus (AAV), adenovirus, and herpes simplex virus (HSV).  

Retrovirus-based vectors may be produced using packaging cell lines such as HEK293T or Phoenix cells. Lentiviral vectors may be self-inactivating to enhance safety. Virus vector plasmids, such as those incorporating the Tol2 transposon system, may be used for stable integration in zebrafish or other model organisms.  

Non-viral transfection methods, including lipofection, electroporation, and nanoparticle delivery, may also be used to introduce GEDI constructs into cells.  

Expression control sequences, such as promoters, enhancers, and tissue-specific regulatory elements, may be included to direct GEDI expression. Examples of eukaryotic promoters include the human synapsin (hSyn1) promoter for neuronal expression and the CAGGs promoter for ubiquitous expression. Tissue-specific promoters may restrict expression to neural or cardiac cells.  

Detectable markers, such as fluorescent proteins (e.g., EGFP, mRuby, TagBFP), may be incorporated into GEDI constructs to enable multiplexed imaging. Luminescence or fluorescence assays may be used to quantify GEDI signals, and pseudo-ratiometric standardization may enhance signal accuracy.  

The GEDI polypeptides may include modified calcium binding motifs, such as EF-hand motifs or calmodulin derivatives, with optimized dissociation constants for detecting cell death-associated calcium flux. Localization sequences, such as nuclear localization signals (NLS), may be included to target GEDI polypeptides to specific subcellular compartments.  

### Cells  

The invention provides cells expressing GEDI polypeptides, including neural cells (e.g., primary neurons, iPSC-derived neurons) and cardiac cells. These cells may be used in vitro or in vivo to study calcium flux and cell death.  

### Animals  

Transgenic animals expressing GEDI polypeptides are also provided. Methods for making transgenic animals include embryonic stem cell manipulation, iPSC differentiation, and organotypic slice cultures. Brain slice cultures may be used to study cell death in specific brain regions.  

### Methods of Use  

The invention provides methods for monitoring calcium flux and cell death using GEDI polypeptides. Optical means, such as microscopes or automated time-lapse confocal microscopy, may be employed to detect fluorescence signals. High-throughput and automated methods may be used for large-scale screening of agents that modulate cell death.  

Signal intensity ratios may be empirically determined to establish thresholds for cell death classification.  

### Kits  

Kits comprising GEDI polypeptides, nucleic acid sequences, vectors, and detection reagents are provided for research and diagnostic applications.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Genetically encoded calcium indicators (GECIs) were repurposed as GEDIs to detect cell death. Rat primary cortical neurons expressing GCaMP6f or RGEDI were imaged before and after NaN3-induced death. RGEDI showed a specific response to cell death, with minimal interference from physiological calcium transients. A pseudo-ratiometric RGEDI-P2A-EGFP construct enabled automated classification of cell death.  

### Example 2  

Novel GEDI variants were designed, including RGEDI-P2A-3xBFP and GC150-P2A-mApple. These variants exhibited robust responses to cell death with optimized calcium binding affinities.  

### Example 3  

GEDIs were used as in vitro cell death reporters in rat cortical neurons. A GEDI threshold was established to classify cells as live or dead, and the method was validated in neurodegenerative disease models.  

### Example 4  

GEDIs were employed in vivo in zebrafish larvae. Co-injection of neuroD:GC150-P2A-mApple and neuroD:NTR-BFP enabled longitudinal imaging of cell death in motor neurons.  

### Example 6  

Automated microscopy and GEDI signals were used for high-throughput survival analysis. Neurons were classified as dead or alive based on GEDI ratios, enabling large-scale drug screening.  

This concludes the detailed description of the invention. The examples provided demonstrate the utility of GEDI polypeptides for detecting cell death in various experimental and therapeutic contexts.
