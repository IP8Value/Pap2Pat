# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and molecular biology, specifically targeting the KRAS oncogene activation pathway. KRAS is a small GTPase that functions as a molecular switch regulating cellular signal transduction pathways. When mutated, KRAS becomes constitutively active, leading to uncontrolled cell proliferation and cancer development. The invention provides novel compounds and methods for inhibiting KRAS activation, particularly in cancer cells harboring KRAS mutations. More specifically, the invention discloses flavagline derivatives that effectively inhibit KRAS activation by disrupting its interaction with prohibitins and phospholipids in the plasma membrane, thereby preventing KRAS nanoclustering and downstream signaling.

## BACKGROUND ART

RAS proteins are small GTPases that function as molecular switches regulating signal transduction pathways controlling cell growth, differentiation, and survival. The RAS family includes three isoforms: HRAS, NRAS, and KRAS, which share 82-90% sequence identity. These proteins cycle between inactive GDP-bound and active GTP-bound states, with the cycle regulated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) and guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs). Oncogenic mutations, particularly at codons 12, 13, and 61, prevent GAP-mediated GTP hydrolysis, locking RAS in a constitutively active state.

KRAS exists as two splice variants, KRAS4A and KRAS4B, with KRAS4B being the predominant isoform in most cancers. The different RAS isoforms exhibit distinct posttranslational modifications and intracellular localization patterns. KRAS4A and KRAS4B contain polybasic stretches that mediate their association with phospholipids in plasma membrane nanoclusters. KRAS4B can be phosphorylated at S181, influencing its subcellular localization. While HRAS is palmitoylated at two residues, KRAS4A and NRAS undergo single palmitoylation in their hypervariable regions. These modifications determine their distribution within plasma membrane microdomains and subsequent signaling outputs.

Current therapeutic strategies targeting KRAS mutations face significant challenges. While compounds inhibiting the G12C mutant KRAS protein have entered clinical trials, these are ineffective against other common KRAS mutations. The development of broad-spectrum KRAS inhibitors has been hampered by the protein's picomolar affinity for GTP and the lack of suitable binding pockets in its active conformation. Alternative approaches targeting downstream effectors or upstream regulators have shown limited clinical success due to pathway reactivation and compensatory mechanisms.

Flavaglines represent a class of natural anti-tumor compounds isolated from Aglaia plants, characterized by a cyclopenta[b]benzofuran scaffold. These compounds target prohibitins 1 and 2 (PHB1/PHB2) and eukaryotic initiation factor 4A (eIF4A). Prohibitins are evolutionarily conserved proteins that form functional oligomers at the plasma membrane, where they scaffold signaling complexes. Previous studies have shown that PHB1 binds directly to CRAF kinase and is required for its activation. Some PHB ligands, such as fluorizoline and rocaglamide, inhibit the PHB1-CRAF interaction and KRAS-mediated tumorigenesis. However, these compounds show limited efficacy against tumors with activated BRAF mutations.

The limitations of current kinase inhibitors highlight the need for novel approaches targeting KRAS activation directly. There remains an unmet medical need for compounds that can effectively inhibit KRAS activation across multiple mutation types while maintaining favorable pharmacokinetic and safety profiles.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The present invention provides flavagline derivatives that effectively inhibit KRAS activation by disrupting its interaction with prohibitins and phospholipids in the plasma membrane. These compounds prevent KRAS nanoclustering and subsequent effector binding, thereby inhibiting downstream signaling. The flavagline derivatives of the invention exhibit potent activity against multiple KRAS mutants with IC50 values in the low nanomolar range.

### R8 is —H, —OCH3, —Br, —F, —Cl;

In specific embodiments, the invention provides flavagline derivatives where R8 is selected from hydrogen, methoxy, bromo, fluoro, or chloro substituents. These modifications significantly influence the compound's ability to inhibit KRAS activation. The methoxy substitution at position 8 (R8 = —OCH3) is particularly important for maintaining anti-KRAS activity, as its suppression markedly reduces inhibitory potency.

The R9 position is preferably substituted with —O—CH3 or —O—(CH2)2—NH—CH3 groups. The introduction of a dimethylaminoethoxy group at this position enhances the compound's ability to disrupt KRAS nanoclustering and inhibit interaction with downstream effectors. The specific combination of R8 as methoxy and R9 as dimethylaminoethoxy represents a particularly effective inhibitor of KRAS activation.

The flavagline derivatives of the invention demonstrate selective inhibition of KRAS nanoclustering without significantly affecting HRAS or NRAS clustering. This selectivity arises from the compounds' ability to specifically disrupt KRAS interactions with phosphatidylserine and phosphatidic acid in the plasma membrane, interactions that are mediated by prohibitins. The inhibition occurs at nanomolar concentrations, with minimal effects on other cellular processes.

The invention provides methods for specifically inhibiting proliferation of cell populations dependent on KRAS signaling by administering effective amounts of these flavagline derivatives. The compounds are effective against multiple KRAS mutants, including but not limited to G12D, G12V, and G12C variants, making them broadly applicable across different cancer types.

Pharmaceutical compositions according to the invention comprise one or more flavagline derivatives in combination with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents. ## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The invention may be practiced through various embodiments demonstrating the in vitro and in vivo effects of flavagline derivatives on KRAS signaling. In vitro studies utilize cell-based assays to show inhibition of plasma membrane-associated prohibitins and subsequent effects on KRAS activation. Treatment with flavagline derivatives inhibits GTP-loading of KRAS and disrupts formation of KRAS nanoclusters at the plasma membrane, as demonstrated by fluorescence lifetime imaging microscopy (FLIM-FRET) and electron microscopy techniques.

The inhibitory effects on cancer cell growth are quantified using MTT and soft agar assays. Flavagline derivatives show potent growth inhibition against various KRAS-mutant cell lines, including HCT-116 (colorectal), ASPC-1 (pancreatic), and Calu-1 (lung) cancer cells, with IC50 values in the 6-20 nM range. The compounds effectively inhibit colony formation in soft agar, demonstrating their ability to block anchorage-independent growth characteristic of transformed cells.

In vivo studies employ autochthonous mouse models to evaluate therapeutic efficacy. In a KRAS G12D-driven non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) model, induction of KRAS expression followed by treatment with flavagline derivatives at 2.5 mg/kg body weight three times weekly for six weeks significantly reduces lung nodule formation. These studies confirm the compounds' ability to inhibit KRAS-driven tumor growth in physiological settings.

The materials and methods for practicing the invention include standard cell culture techniques using appropriate cell lines and culture conditions. Cells are transfected with DNA or siRNA using established protocols to modulate gene expression. Active RAS pull-down assays are performed using CRAF-RBD or Ral-GDS-RA domains to precipitate GTP-bound RAS, followed by SDS-PAGE and western blotting for detection and quantification.

Additional experimental methods include:
- NanoBiT assays to quantify KRAS activation in live cells
- Cellular thermal shift assays (CETSA) to demonstrate target engagement
- Bimolecular fluorescence complementation (BiFC) to visualize protein interactions
- Nanobar-based assays to study RAS distribution patterns
- Immunogold labeling and electron microscopy to analyze RAS nanoclustering
- Lipid binding assays to characterize interactions with phospholipids

These methods collectively provide comprehensive characterization of the flavagline derivatives' mechanisms of action and therapeutic potential against KRAS-driven cancers. The invention thus encompasses both the compounds themselves and methods for their use in research and therapeutic applications.
