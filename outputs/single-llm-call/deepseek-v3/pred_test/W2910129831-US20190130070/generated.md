# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to observational databases and, more particularly, to systems and methods for modeling disease progression using observational data. The invention provides a computational framework for analyzing longitudinal patient data to construct probabilistic models that characterize disease progression across multiple clinical domains. Specifically, the disclosed systems and methods address challenges in tracking disease progression from real-world evidence sources such as disease registries and electronic health records, where data may be irregularly sampled, incomplete, or high-dimensional. The technical field encompasses computer-implemented statistical modeling, machine learning applications in healthcare, and computational methods for analyzing medical data to derive clinically meaningful insights about disease trajectories.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention introduces a novel disease progression modeling system that overcomes limitations in existing approaches for tracking chronic disease development. Chronic conditions such as neurodegenerative disorders often exhibit complex progression patterns that are difficult to characterize using conventional staging systems. The invention provides a computational framework based on continuous-time hidden Markov models (CTHMM) that can identify distinct disease states and model transitions between these states from observational clinical data.  

A key motivation for the invention is the recognition that current methods for tracking disease progression suffer from several limitations. First, while diseases progress continuously over time, clinical observations are typically made at discrete, irregular intervals. Second, individual patient records often cover only fragments of the complete disease trajectory, requiring aggregation of data from multiple patients with potentially misaligned progression timelines. Third, existing staging systems frequently focus on single symptom domains, failing to capture the multifaceted nature of disease progression across motor, cognitive, and functional domains simultaneously.  

The invention applies discriminative feature analysis to observational data to identify clinical features most relevant for tracking disease progression. The disclosed disease progression prediction system processes high-dimensional clinical assessments to extract lower-dimensional representations that capture essential progression patterns. The system implements a method for generating disease progression models through a two-step process: first determining the optimal number of disease states through a data-driven approach, then constructing a probabilistic model that characterizes transitions between these states.  

The system comprises a computer program product that implements the modeling framework, including components for receiving observational data, generating progression models, identifying discriminative clinical features, and ranking features by their predictive power. The resulting disease progression model provides a comprehensive view of disease states across the entire progression pathway covered by the input data, characterizes progression as transitions between disease states, estimates expected durations of disease states, and enables inference of individual patient state sequences.

Ranking component 114 further analyzes these features to determine their relative importance, enabling selection of the most informative markers. Utility-based analysis evaluates the clinical relevance and predictive power of the generated models. System 100 offers design flexibility to accommodate different disease characteristics through configurable model parameters. Classification schemes within the system can incorporate probabilistic and statistical-based analyses to quantify uncertainty in state assignments and progression predictions.  

Real-world examples of observational data suitable for processing by system 100 include disease registry data and electronic health records. Disease registry data provides longitudinal information from standardized clinical assessments but may have limited availability for certain patient populations. Electronic health records offer broader population coverage but often contain more heterogeneous and less structured data. The system generates a refined database by applying initial feature filtering to select clinically relevant assessments, composite feature engineering to create informative derived variables, and disease progression modeling to characterize state transitions.  

The input data processed by system 100 includes both observational data (clinical assessments collected over time) and knowledge data (existing clinical understanding of disease progression patterns). Analyzing observational data involves extracting features related to disease progression as well as features relevant for other clinical tasks. The output database produced by the system includes a reduced database containing the most discriminative features and a sub-database specifically optimized for disease progression tracking.

Finally, the system generates a final disease progression model incorporating the selected features.
