## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present disclosure relates generally to ophthalmic surgical devices and methods. More particularly, embodiments of the invention pertain to laser-based systems and techniques for treating conjunctivochalasis through thermal conjunctivoplasty procedures.  

## BACKGROUND  

Conjunctivochalasis represents a common degenerative condition characterized by loose, redundant folds of conjunctival tissue that prolapse onto the lower eyelid margin. The conjunctiva comprises the thin, transparent mucous membrane covering the anterior surface of the sclera and lining the inner surfaces of the eyelids. With advancing age, the conjunctiva loses elasticity and develops excessive laxity, forming redundant folds that disrupt normal tear film dynamics.  

Patients with conjunctivochalasis typically present with symptoms including chronic eye irritation, foreign body sensation, blurred vision, and epiphora. The condition frequently coexists with dry eye syndrome, as the redundant conjunctival folds interfere with proper tear distribution across the ocular surface. In severe cases, the prolapsed conjunctival tissue becomes mechanically irritated by eyelid movement during blinking, further exacerbating ocular surface inflammation.  

Diagnosis of conjunctivochalasis involves clinical examination to identify redundant conjunctival folds that obliterate the inferior tear meniscus. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) provides cross-sectional imaging that quantifies the severity of tissue redundancy and monitors post-treatment changes. Current treatment modalities aim to surgically reduce or excise the excess conjunctival tissue to restore normal ocular surface anatomy.  

Existing surgical techniques for conjunctivochalasis include thermocautery, electrocautery, and paste-pinch-cut procedures. Thermocautery and electrocautery utilize high temperatures exceeding water's boiling point to shrink or remove tissue, but frequently cause full-thickness burns with associated pain, prolonged healing, and potential complications including pyogenic granuloma formation. The paste-pinch-cut technique employs mechanical excision with fibrin glue application but requires operating room facilities and still involves weeks of postoperative inflammation.  

These limitations motivate the development of improved surgical techniques that provide precise tissue modification with minimal collateral damage. Laser thermal conjunctivoplasty (LTC) represents an advantageous approach by enabling controlled energy delivery confined to targeted tissue depths. Previous attempts using visible wavelength argon lasers suffered from excessive blood absorption leading to hemorrhage. The present invention addresses these shortcomings through novel near-infrared laser systems optimized for water absorption in conjunctival tissue.

The present disclosure describes a laser thermal conjunctivoplasty (LTC) system, device, and method for treating conjunctivochalasis. The system includes a handheld laser probe configured to deliver line-focused near-infrared laser energy to targeted conjunctival tissue while integrated forceps stabilize the treatment area.  

The handheld laser probe incorporates a line-focused laser light source that generates a collimated beam transformed into an elongated focal spot measuring approximately 10 mm in length and 1 mm in width. This linear beam geometry enables efficient treatment of elongated conjunctival folds while maintaining precise spatial control. The probe design includes light focusing optics comprising a cylindrical lens that shapes the laser output from an optical fiber into the desired line focus pattern.  

The laser beam exhibits optimized focus characteristics with a width between 0.5-2 mm and length between 5-15 mm to match typical conjunctival fold dimensions. The system employs a diode laser operating at 1.45 μm wavelength selected for strong water absorption in conjunctival tissue while minimizing blood absorption. Alternative suitable laser wavelengths include the range of 1.4-1.5 μm where water absorption remains high but blood absorption is minimized.  

The handheld laser probe operates in pulsed mode with adjustable parameters including pulse duration between 50-500 ms, duty cycle between 5-50%, and repetition rate of 0.5-2 Hz. The integrated forceps maintain proper tissue positioning and working distance while the line-focused laser beam delivers controlled thermal energy to the grasped conjunctival fold.  

The complete LTC system comprises the handheld laser probe coupled to a laser source via optical fiber, along with a control system featuring a foot pedal interface. The control circuitry includes a modulator for precise pulse parameter adjustment and an optical switch/shutter for safety. System schematics illustrate the integration of laser source, control electronics, and delivery components.  

The handheld probe configuration incorporates ergonomic design elements facilitating single-handed operation. The forceps component includes angled platforms that position conjunctival tissue at the optimal focal plane while maintaining normal incidence illumination. Ray trace simulations demonstrate efficient light delivery through the focusing optics to the treatment plane.  

Methods of laser thermal conjunctivoplasty according to the invention involve optimizing laser pulse parameters to achieve controlled tissue heating. Pulse durations between 100-300 ms at 10-30% duty cycle produce optimal temperature profiles between 62-100°C for collagen shrinkage without boiling. Theoretical modeling of temperature distributions in conjunctival tissue confirms confinement of thermal effects to the targeted depth.  

## EXAMPLES  

An ex vivo experiment was conducted to optimize laser parameters for conjunctival shrinkage using porcine eyes as model systems. The experimental setup incorporated thermal imaging and OCT monitoring to evaluate LTC effects. A thermal camera recorded temperature changes during laser application while OCT provided real-time tissue structural imaging.  

The experimental workflow involved marking conjunctival regions with parallel reference lines, applying laser treatment, and quantifying shrinkage through image analysis. Temperature monitoring revealed characteristic increase-decrease cycles corresponding to laser pulses, with peak temperatures remaining below 100°C at optimal parameters.  

OCT imaging captured dynamic tissue changes during LTC, with particle image velocimetry (PIV) analysis demonstrating centripetal tissue movement confirming shrinkage. Microscope images documented progressive shrinkage with increasing laser power from 21% at 2.33W to 45% at 3.66W peak power. Duty cycle studies showed 20% provided optimal shrinkage (36%) while avoiding excessive temperatures (>100°C at 30%).  

Quantitative analysis established relationships between laser parameters and tissue effects. Shrinkage percentage increased with both peak power and duty cycle but reached plateaus beyond 3W and 20% respectively. Temperature measurements confirmed maintenance of therapeutic ranges (62-88°C) at optimal settings. Control experiments verified that forceps application alone contributed minimally (10%) to observed shrinkage.  

In vivo animal studies evaluated wound healing responses following LTC. Clinical assessments monitored conjunctival hyperemia, hemorrhage, and blanching over postoperative periods. OCT imaging tracked changes in conjunctival thickness and surface morphology. Histochemical staining characterized tissue remodeling at cellular levels.  

An exemplary 1460-nm diode laser system was developed for clinical LTC applications. The system incorporated a fiber-coupled high-power laser module (12W maximum) with integrated aiming beam. Custom control circuits enabled programmable adjustment of pulse parameters through a software interface. The foot pedal trigger provided intuitive surgical control.  

System characterization confirmed linear power-current relationships with lasing threshold at 6.8A. Spectral analysis showed stable peak wavelength (~1456nm) across power levels with minimal broadening (3.1-3.9nm). Temporal measurements verified precise control of pulse widths (100-300ms) and sequences.  

The handheld LTC probe design incorporated a cylindrical lens (f=5.8mm) creating a 10×1mm line focus at the treatment plane. Angled forceps maintained proper tissue positioning and working distance. Ray tracing simulations confirmed optimal light delivery to conjunctival folds grasped by the forceps.  

Laser parameter selection was guided by thermal modeling of conjunctival tissue. Calculations based on thermal diffusivity (1.3×10^-7 m^2/s) and tissue thickness (0.5mm) determined optimal pulse durations (<0.5s) and repetition rates (1Hz). Experimental validation confirmed these parameters achieved controlled heating with adequate thermal relaxation between pulses.  

The experimental procedure utilized porcine eyes with marked conjunctival regions. Thermal imaging recorded temperature profiles during LTC while OCT monitored structural changes. Post-treatment measurements quantified shrinkage by comparing pre- and post-LTC images of reference marks. Statistical analysis of multiple samples established reproducible treatment effects.  

This comprehensive experimental characterization demonstrates the efficacy and safety of the disclosed LTC system for conjunctivochalasis treatment. The optimized combination of laser parameters, probe design, and surgical technique provides superior outcomes compared to existing treatment modalities.
