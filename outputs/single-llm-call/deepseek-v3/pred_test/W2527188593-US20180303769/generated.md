# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Protein interaction networks play a fundamental role in mediating cellular responses to pathogenic agents. These networks exhibit a small-world property wherein a limited number of highly connected hub proteins serve as critical nodes that facilitate interactions between diverse pathways. Disease networks frequently exploit such hub proteins to propagate pathogenic effects within host cells. The small-world architecture of these networks enables pathogenic agents to efficiently hijack host cellular machinery through interactions with a select group of hub proteins.  

Multiplex approaches for therapy have emerged as a promising strategy to counteract pathogenic agents that exploit interconnected host pathways. Traditional therapeutic strategies often target individual pathogenic components, which may lead to limited efficacy due to the redundancy and adaptability of pathogenic networks. By contrast, multiplex therapeutic approaches simultaneously target multiple nodes within pathogenic networks, thereby disrupting the interconnected pathways that mediate pathogenicity. Such strategies offer the potential for broad-spectrum inhibition of pathogenic agents while minimizing the likelihood of resistance development.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a method of inhibiting pathogenic agents by targeting host hub proteins that are exploited by such agents. The method involves administering an effective amount of Bithionol, which acts as an inhibitor of caspase-dependent pathogenic agents. Bithionol disrupts the activity of host caspases, including caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9, thereby reducing the cytotoxicity induced by a range of bacterial toxins, viral pathogens, and other pathogenic agents.  

The invention further provides a composition for inhibiting pathogenic agents, comprising Bithionol as an active ingredient. The composition may be formulated for oral, parenteral, or systemic administration and is effective in preventing or treating infections caused by caspase-dependent pathogenic agents. The composition may further include additional therapeutic agents, such as antibiotics, to enhance efficacy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention employs a cell-based multiplex approach to identify and inhibit pathogenic agents that exploit interconnected host pathways. This approach involves screening for compounds that simultaneously reduce cytotoxicity induced by multiple toxins while inhibiting host caspases. Bithionol was identified as a multiplex hit capable of inhibiting both toxin-mediated cytotoxicity and caspase activity, making it a potent broad-spectrum therapeutic agent.  

Bithionol, a previously known anti-helminthic agent, has been repurposed as an inhibitor of pathogenic agents by virtue of its ability to inhibit host caspases. The compound demonstrates dose-dependent inhibition of caspases-3, -6, -7, -1, and -9, with IC50 values ranging from 11 to 21 μM. This inhibitory activity translates into protective effects against a variety of pathogenic agents, including bacterial toxins, ricin, Botulinum neurotoxin A, and Zika virus.  

Host pathways and proteins exploited by pathogenic agents were studied to identify key intervention points. Caspases were identified as critical hub proteins that mediate the cytotoxic effects of multiple toxins. These enzymes form part of a caspase cascade that executes programmed cell death, making them ideal targets for therapeutic intervention. Pathogenic agents, including bacterial toxins and viruses, exploit these caspases to induce host cell death.  

For the purposes of this invention, the term "pathogen" refers to any biological agent capable of causing disease, including bacteria, viruses, and toxins. A "pathogenic agent" specifically denotes a component of a pathogen, such as a bacterial toxin or viral protein, that contributes to pathogenicity. The terms "prevent" and "treat" encompass prophylactic and therapeutic interventions, respectively, aimed at reducing the severity or incidence of pathogenic effects.  

Bithionol inhibits caspase-dependent pathogenic agents by directly targeting host caspases. Biochemical assays confirmed that Bithionol reduces the proteolytic activity of caspases-3, -6, -7, -1, and -9, thereby attenuating the cytotoxic effects of toxins that rely on these enzymes. In vivo analysis demonstrated that Bithionol protects against lethal doses of Botulinum neurotoxin A and reduces the pathogenicity of anthrax toxins, ricin, and Zika virus.  

The dosage of Bithionol for inhibiting pathogenic agents ranges from 5 to 50 mg/kg, depending on the route of administration and the specific pathogenic agent targeted. Oral administration achieves serum concentrations sufficient to inhibit caspase activity, with peak concentrations exceeding the EC50 values observed in cellular assays.  

The method of inhibiting caspase-dependent pathogenic agents comprises administering Bithionol to a subject in need thereof. The composition for inhibiting caspase-dependent pathogenic agents includes Bithionol as the active ingredient, optionally combined with pharmaceutically acceptable carriers or additional therapeutic agents.  

Administration of the Bithionol composition may be performed via oral, parenteral, or systemic routes. The composition is prepared in a pharmaceutically acceptable form, such as tablets, capsules, injectable solutions, or topical formulations, to ensure optimal bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy.  

### Combination Compositions and Treatments  

Combining Bithionol with antibiotics enhances its efficacy against bacterial pathogens. The invention provides a composition comprising Bithionol and an antibiotic, wherein the antibiotic targets the pathogen directly while Bithionol inhibits host caspases exploited by pathogenic toxins. This dual-action approach improves therapeutic outcomes by simultaneously targeting the pathogen and its host-mediated toxicity.  

The mixture of Bithionol and antibiotic may be formulated for co-administration or sequential administration. Parenteral administration of the composition ensures rapid delivery of both agents, particularly in acute infections. The dosage of the combination composition is adjusted based on the specific antibiotic used and the severity of the infection.  

The method of treating specific pathogenic agents involves administering the Bithionol-antibiotic composition to subjects infected with caspase-dependent pathogens. This approach is particularly effective against bacterial toxins that exploit host caspases, such as those produced by Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Bacillus anthracis, and Clostridium botulinum.

### Administration of the Bithionol Composition  

Parenteral administration refers to routes of drug delivery that bypass the gastrointestinal tract, including intravenous, intramuscular, and subcutaneous injection. Systemic administration denotes drug delivery methods that achieve widespread distribution within the body, such as oral or intravenous routes.  

Administering the Bithionol composition involves preparing a pharmaceutical dose suitable for the intended route of administration. For oral delivery, Bithionol is formulated into tablets or capsules with appropriate excipients to ensure stability and absorption. Injectable formulations are prepared as sterile solutions or suspensions for immediate use.  

Preparation of pharmaceutical doses includes optimizing the concentration of Bithionol to achieve therapeutic serum levels while minimizing adverse effects. Dosage forms are tested for stability, bioavailability, and compatibility with other therapeutic agents.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1. Identification of Host Hub Proteins Exploited by Multiple Pathogenic Toxins  

Host hub proteins exploited by multiple pathogenic toxins were identified by analyzing the sensitivity of human B-cells to Pseudomonas aeruginosa exotoxin A (PE). Genetic mutations in host proteins associated with altered PE sensitivity were mapped, revealing caspases as critical hub proteins. These proteins were subsequently used as targets in drug screens to identify broad-spectrum inhibitors.  

### Example 2. The Effect of Caspase Mutations on the Sensitivity of Human B-Cells to P. aeruginosa Exotoxin A  

The effect of caspase mutations on PE sensitivity was investigated using B-lymphoblastoid cells from diverse human populations. Caspase-3 and -7 mutations significantly associated with altered toxin sensitivity, confirming their role in mediating pathogenicity. These findings validated caspases as therapeutic targets for inhibiting toxin-induced cytotoxicity.  

### Example 3. A Multiplexed Cellular Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Cytotoxic Activities of Bacterial Toxins Exploiting Unique but Interconnected Host Pathways  

A multiplexed cellular screen identified Bithionol as a compound capable of reducing cytotoxicity induced by diphtheria toxin, cholera toxin, and PE. Bithionol demonstrated broad-spectrum inhibitory activity, qualifying it as a multiplex hit for further development as an anti-pathogenic agent.  

### Example 4. A Multiplex Protein Function-Based Screen for CCL Drugs that Inhibit Proteolytic Activities of Host Caspase-3, -6, and -7  

A parallel biochemical screen confirmed Bithionol’s ability to inhibit caspases-3, -6, and -7. The compound reduced proteolytic activity in a dose-dependent manner, further supporting its mechanism of action as a caspase inhibitor.  

### Example 5. Bithionol Reduces the Pathogenicity of a Range of Toxins by Inhibiting Host Caspases  

Bithionol reduced cytotoxicity induced by diphtheria, cholera, and PE toxins with an EC50 of 10 μM. The compound also inhibited caspases-1, -3, -6, -7, and -9, demonstrating its potential as a broad-spectrum anti-pathogenic agent.  

### Example 6. Bithionol Inhibits Cytotoxic Activity of Anthrax Toxins  

Bithionol neutralized anthrax toxin-mediated cytotoxicity by inhibiting caspase-1 activity. The compound provided substantial protection against lethal factor (LF) and protective antigen (PA) toxicity in cellular assays.  

### Example 7. Bithionol Inhibits Ricin and Botulinum Neurotoxin A-Induced Death In Vitro and In Vivo  

Bithionol reduced ricin-mediated cytotoxicity and protected mice from lethal doses of Botulinum neurotoxin A. The compound’s efficacy in vivo confirmed its therapeutic potential against caspase-dependent toxins.  

### Example 8. Bithionol Acts as a Zika Virus Inhibitor  

Bithionol inhibited Zika virus replication in infected cells, with EC50 values ranging from 5.5 to 6.7 μM. The compound’s ability to cross the blood-brain barrier suggests its utility in treating neurotropic viral infections.  

### Example 9. Material and Methods  

Detailed experimental procedures, including cell culture conditions, drug screening protocols, and in vivo studies, are provided to enable replication of the disclosed findings.
