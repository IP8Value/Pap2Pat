# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of oncology and radiation therapy, particularly to methods and compositions for enhancing the efficacy of radiation therapy in the treatment of esophageal cancer. Esophageal cancer remains one of the most aggressive malignancies, with a poor prognosis and limited treatment options. Current therapeutic approaches, including radiation therapy and chemotherapy, often yield unsatisfactory outcomes due to the inherent resistance of cancer cells and the severe side effects associated with high-dose radiation. Despite advancements in treatment modalities, the five-year survival rate for esophageal cancer patients remains below 25%, underscoring the urgent need for novel therapeutic agents that can improve treatment efficacy while minimizing toxicity.  

A significant limitation of radiation therapy is the development of radioresistance in cancer cells, which necessitates higher radiation doses to achieve tumor control. However, increasing radiation doses also elevates the risk of damage to surrounding healthy tissues, leading to complications such as pneumonitis, esophagitis, and cardiac toxicity. Conventional radiosensitizers, such as cisplatin and 5-fluorouracil, have demonstrated limited success due to their systemic toxicity and narrow therapeutic windows. Thus, there remains a critical unmet need for compounds that can selectively enhance the radiosensitivity of cancer cells without exacerbating adverse effects on normal tissues.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention is motivated by the discovery of armillaridin (AM), a protoilludane norsesquiterpenoid ester compound isolated from the medicinal fungus *Armillaria mellea*, as a potent anti-proliferative and radiosensitizing agent for esophageal cancer. Armillaridin exhibits unexpected cytotoxic activity against human esophageal cancer cell lines, including squamous cell carcinoma and adenocarcinoma, by inducing apoptosis and cell cycle arrest at the G2/M phase. Furthermore, armillaridin significantly enhances the susceptibility of esophageal cancer cells to radiation therapy, as demonstrated by in vitro and in vivo studies.  

A first aspect of the invention provides a composition of a medicament comprising armillaridin or a pharmaceutically acceptable derivative thereof for use in treating cancer, particularly esophageal cancer. The medicament may be formulated for administration in combination with radiation therapy, wherein armillaridin acts as a radiosensitizer to improve therapeutic outcomes.  

A second aspect of the invention relates to a method of enhancing the susceptibility of esophageal cancerous cells to radiation therapy. The method comprises administering an effective amount of armillaridin to a subject in need thereof, followed by radiation therapy. The radiation therapy may be delivered at clinically relevant doses, such as 2 Gy per fraction, and the administration of armillaridin may be performed prior to, concurrently with, or after radiation exposure.  

Further embodiments of the method include combining armillaridin with additional therapeutic agents, such as alkylating agents, anthracyclines, plant alkaloids, topoisomerase inhibitors, cytotoxic antibiotics, or tyrosine kinase inhibitors, to augment anti-tumor effects.  

A third aspect of the invention provides a method of treating esophageal cancer in a subject, comprising administering an effective amount of armillaridin to the subject. The method includes a dosing regimen wherein armillaridin is administered intraperitoneally, orally, or intravenously, at a dose ranging from 1 mg/kg to 100 mg/kg, preferably 80 mg/kg, three times per week. Long-term exposure to armillaridin has been shown to inhibit tumor growth in xenograft models without significant toxicity, as evidenced by stable body weight and leukocyte counts.  

A fourth aspect of the invention discloses a pharmaceutical composition comprising armillaridin and a pharmaceutically acceptable excipient. The composition may be formulated for various routes of administration, including topical, transmucosal, oral, or parenteral delivery, and may be provided in dosage forms such as tablets, capsules, injectables, or creams.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

### Definitions  

As used in this disclosure, the term "treating" or "treatment" refers to the administration of a compound to a subject with the intent to alleviate, ameliorate, or eliminate a disease or disorder, including but not limited to esophageal cancer.  

An "effective amount" refers to a quantity of armillaridin sufficient to exert a therapeutic effect, such as inhibiting cancer cell proliferation or enhancing radiosensitivity, without causing undue adverse effects.  

The terms "compound," "agent," or "medicament" encompass armillaridin, its pharmaceutically acceptable salts, derivatives, and prodrugs, unless otherwise specified.  

"Administered," "administering," or "administration" refers to the delivery of armillaridin to a subject via any suitable route, including oral, intravenous, intraperitoneal, or topical application.  

A "subject" or "patient" is a human or animal, preferably a human, diagnosed with or at risk of developing esophageal cancer.  

### Discovery and Source of Armillaridin  

The present invention is based on the unexpected discovery of the protoilludane norsesquiterpenoid ester compound armillaridin (CH-205-O), isolated from the mycelium of *Armillaria mellea*. *Armillaria mellea* is a medicinal fungus traditionally used in Chinese medicine for treating neurological disorders, but its anti-cancer properties have not been previously elucidated.  

Previous chemical studies of *Armillaria mellea* identified various bioactive compounds, but armillaridin's unique structure (3-chloro-6-hydroxy-4-methoxy-2-methyl-,3-formyl-2,2a,4a,5,6,7,7a,7b-octahydro-2a-hydroxy-6,6,7b-trimethyl-1H-cyclobut(e)inden-2-yl ester) and its anti-cancer effects were not recognized until the present invention.  

### Preparation and Chemical Structure  

Armillaridin is prepared by extraction and purification from *Armillaria mellea* mycelium, followed by chromatographic separation to achieve high purity (>95%). The compound is characterized by its acicular crystalline form and solubility in dimethylsulfoxide (DMSO) for experimental and pharmaceutical use.  

### Method of Enhancing Radiosensitivity  

The invention provides a method of enhancing the susceptibility of esophageal cancerous cells to radiation therapy, comprising:  
1. Administering armillaridin to a subject at a dose of 0.125–5.0 μM for 24 hours prior to radiation.  
2. Delivering radiation therapy at a dose of 0.5–4 Gy, preferably 2 Gy per fraction, using a linear accelerator.  

Radiation parameters include a 6 MeV electron beam at a dose rate of 2.4 Gy/min, ensuring full electron equilibrium for accurate dosing.  

### Combination with Additional Agents  

Armillaridin may be combined with other anti-cancer agents to enhance therapeutic efficacy. Non-limiting examples include:  
- **Alkylating agents**: cisplatin, cyclophosphamide.  
- **Anthracyclines**: doxorubicin, epirubicin.  
- **Plant alkaloids**: vincristine, vinblastine.  
- **Topoisomerase inhibitors**: etoposide, topotecan.  
- **Cytotoxic antibiotics**: bleomycin, mitomycin.  
- **Tyrosine kinase inhibitors**: imatinib, gefitinib.  

### Method of Treating Esophageal Cancer  

The invention further provides a method of treating esophageal cancer, comprising administering armillaridin at a dose of 80 mg/kg intraperitoneally three times per week for 12 doses. The treatment regimen is well-tolerated, with no significant impact on body weight or leukocyte counts.  

### Pharmaceutical Composition  

A pharmaceutical composition comprises armillaridin and a pharmaceutically acceptable excipient, such as a buffer, stabilizer, or solubilizing agent. The composition may contain 0.1–10% w/w of armillaridin and may be formulated for oral, intravenous, or topical administration.  

### Routes of Administration  

The medicament may be administered via:  
- **Topical routes**: creams, gels, or patches.  
- **Transmucosal routes**: sublingual or buccal tablets.  
- **Parenteral routes**: intravenous or intraperitoneal injection.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Cell Viability and Radiosensitization  

Human esophageal cancer cell lines (CE81T/VGH, TE-2, BE-3, SKGT-4) were treated with armillaridin (0–20 μM) for 24–72 hours. Cell viability was assessed via MTT assay, revealing IC50 values of 3.4–6.9 μM. Clonogenic assays demonstrated a sensitizer enhancement ratio of 1.6 when cells were pre-treated with 5.0 μM armillaridin before 2 Gy irradiation.  

### Example 2: In Vivo Xenograft Study  

Nude mice bearing CE81T/VGH xenografts were treated with armillaridin (80 mg/kg, 3x/week) for 4 weeks. Tumor volume was reduced by 60% compared to controls, with no significant leukopenia or weight loss.  

### Example 3: Long-Term Exposure Effects  

Chronic administration of armillaridin (12 doses over 4 weeks) inhibited tumor growth without cumulative toxicity, supporting its use as a long-term therapeutic agent.
