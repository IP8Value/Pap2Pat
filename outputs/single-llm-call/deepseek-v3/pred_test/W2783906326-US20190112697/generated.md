# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to an electrical steel sheet with improved magnetic properties, particularly suited for high-frequency applications. More specifically, it concerns a silicon-gradient electrical steel sheet having optimized crystal grain structure, silicon concentration distribution, and internal stress configuration to minimize iron losses while maintaining manufacturability.  

## BACKGROUND  

Iron loss in electrical steel sheets comprises two principal components: hysteresis loss and eddy-current loss. Hysteresis loss results from irreversible domain wall displacement during alternating magnetization, while eddy-current loss arises from induced circulating currents within the material. Conventional electrical steel sheets face fundamental limitations in reducing these losses simultaneously, particularly at high operating frequencies exceeding 10 kHz.  

Common electrical steel sheets typically contain 3-3.5% silicon by weight, offering a balance between magnetic properties and mechanical workability. While increasing silicon content improves resistivity and reduces eddy currents, excessive silicon (>4%) drastically reduces ductility, making cold rolling impractical. The 6.5% silicon steel, produced through chemical vapor deposition (CVD) siliconizing, achieves near-zero magnetostriction but suffers from brittleness and elevated hysteresis losses.  

Prior approaches include ultrathin steel sheets, high-silicon compositions, dust cores, and Mn-Zn ferrites, each with inherent limitations. Japanese Patent No. 5533801 discloses a siliconizing method but fails to address stress distribution effects. Japanese Examined Patent Application Publication No. 6-45881 describes 6.5% Si steel with limited high-frequency performance. Japanese Patent No. 5648335 proposes grain-oriented silicon steel unsuitable for high-frequency applications due to anisotropic losses.  

A critical limitation of existing techniques involves the trade-off between specific resistance enhancement and mechanical properties. Alloying elements like Al, P, and Mn can increase resistivity but degrade magnetic properties or processability when exceeding optimal ranges. Japanese Unexamined Patent Application Publication No. 2005-240185 attempts gradient silicon distribution but lacks control over grain structure and stress states.  

The present invention overcomes these limitations through a precisely engineered silicon-gradient steel sheet with:  
1) Controlled average aspect ratio (0.7-4.0) of crystal grains in the surface region  
2) Optimized thickness proportion (10-40%) of the high-silicon surface layer  
3) Defined silicon concentration gradients (surface: 2.5-6.5%, inner: ≤2.0%)  
4) Engineered stress distribution (surface tension: 50-200 MPa, inner compression: 50-200 MPa)  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides an electrical steel sheet comprising three distinct regions: a surface part, boundary part, and inner part. The surface part contains crystal grains with an average aspect ratio (parallel-to-surface dimension vs. depth direction) between 0.7 and 4.0, preferably textured with {100}<0vw> orientation. The inner part maintains coarse equiaxed grains for reduced hysteresis loss.  

The steel sheet exhibits a silicon concentration gradient where the surface part averages 2.5-6.5% Si while the inner part maintains ≤2.0% Si, with a transitional boundary part having a silicon concentration gap ≥0.4%. This configuration creates complementary stress states - tensile stress (50-200 MPa) in the surface part and compressive stress (50-200 MPa) in the inner part, effectively reducing eddy-current losses through controlled magnetic flux distribution.  

Production involves preparing a low-silicon steel sheet (0.15-2.0% Si, ≤0.020% C), followed by CVD siliconizing and diffusion treatment to establish the gradient structure. The resulting sheets (0.03-0.5 mm thickness) demonstrate superior high-frequency performance with iron losses reduced by 30-50% compared to conventional 6.5% Si steel at frequencies above 6 kHz.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention encompasses silicon-gradient electrical steel sheets and their manufacturing methods. The sheets feature a laminated structure where silicon concentration decreases continuously from the surface toward the center, creating distinct magnetic property distributions.  

### Structural Characteristics  

The silicon-gradient steel sheet comprises:  
1) Surface part: 10-40% of total thickness, containing 2.5-6.5% Si  
2) Boundary part: Transition zone with ≥0.4% Si concentration gradient  
3) Inner part: Core region with ≤2.0% Si  

This configuration generates beneficial stress states through lattice mismatch - the high-silicon surface contracts more than the low-silicon core during cooling, inducing tensile surface stress and compressive core stress.  

### Crystal Grain Structure  

The surface part contains crystal grains with average aspect ratio (length/thickness) between 0.7 and 4.0, preferably 1.2-3.5. Grains are elongated parallel to the sheet surface with predominant {100}<0vw> texture. The inner part contains equiaxed grains averaging 20-200 μm diameter.  

### Stress Distribution  

X-ray diffraction measurements confirm:  
- Surface part: 50-200 MPa tensile stress parallel to sheet surface  
- Inner part: 50-200 MPa compressive stress parallel to sheet surface  

This stress configuration aligns magnetic domains to:  
1) Enhance surface layer permeability  
2) Redirect inner layer magnetization vectors toward thickness direction  
3) Reduce eddy currents through magnetic flux confinement  

### Production Method  

1) Prepare base steel: 0.15-2.0% Si, ≤0.020% C, 0.1-1.5% Mn, ≤0.030% P, ≤0.005% S  
2) Cold roll to 0.03-0.5 mm thickness  
3) CVD siliconizing at 1100-1300°C using SiCl4/H2 gas  
4) Diffusion annealing at 900-1200°C for 1-10 hours  
5) Final annealing at 800-1100°C for stress relief  

### Example 1  

A 0.20 mm thick steel sheet (0.8% Si, 0.012% C) underwent siliconizing at 1200°C for 2 hours, forming:  
- Surface: 5.2% Si, 60 μm thickness  
- Boundary: 5.2→1.5% Si over 20 μm  
- Inner: 1.5% Si  

Iron loss at 20 kHz, 0.1 T:  
- Invention: 45 W/kg  
- 6.5% Si steel: 68 W/kg (34% reduction)  

### Example 2  

A 0.10 mm thick sheet (1.2% Si) processed similarly showed:  
- Surface: 4.8% Si, 25 μm thickness  
- Boundary: 4.8→1.8% Si over 15 μm  
- Inner: 1.8% Si  

Iron loss at 50 kHz, 0.05 T:  
- Invention: 28 W/kg  
- Conventional: 42 W/kg (33% reduction)  

### Example 3  

A 0.35 mm thick sheet (0.5% Si) yielded:  
- Surface: 3.5% Si, 100 μm thickness  
- Boundary: 3.5→0.8% Si over 40 μm  
- Inner: 0.8% Si  

Iron loss at 10 kHz, 0.2 T:  
- Invention: 32 W/kg  
- 6.5% Si steel: 48 W/kg (33% reduction)  

The invention achieves superior high-frequency performance through synergistic control of silicon gradient, grain structure, and stress distribution - enabling efficient magnetic cores for high-frequency reactors, transformers, and electric vehicle components.
