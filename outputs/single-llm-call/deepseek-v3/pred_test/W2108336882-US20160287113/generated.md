## DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to microelectrodes and associated devices designed for neural interfacing applications. These devices serve the dual purpose of neurostimulation and recording electric signals from neural tissues. Neural interfaces incorporating such microelectrodes find utility in various medical applications including but not limited to cochlear implants, retinal implants, deep brain stimulation systems, and cortical implants.  

For effective neurostimulation, electrodes must meet stringent requirements regarding their electrochemical properties and biocompatibility. The charge transfer process at the electrode-tissue interface must occur without inducing harmful electrochemical reactions or tissue damage. Electrodes must exhibit stable electrochemical characteristics while maintaining long-term biocompatibility within physiological environments.  

Conventional electrodes used in neurostimulation demonstrate certain limitations, particularly when miniaturized for high-density applications. Similarly, electrodes employed for electro-physiological applications ex-vivo face challenges related to their electrochemical performance and durability. As electrode diameters decrease to enable higher density arrays, issues of gradual degradation and performance deterioration become increasingly problematic.  

Synthetic diamond has emerged as a promising electrode material due to its unique combination of properties. Diamond is chemically and biologically inert while exhibiting excellent electrochemical characteristics suitable for neural interfacing. The growth of synthetic diamond through chemical vapor deposition techniques enables precise control over material properties. In particular, poly-crystalline diamond doped with boron (BDD) shows potential for neural electrode applications. However, BDD suffers from limitations including small double layer electrochemical capacity and high impedance at the electrode-tissue interface.  

The technical problem addressed by the present invention relates to overcoming these limitations while maintaining the beneficial properties of diamond-based electrodes. The object of the invention is to provide an improved microelectrode for neural interfacing applications that combines enhanced electrochemical performance with long-term stability and biocompatibility.  

The microelectrode according to the invention comprises a stack of layers including a first conductive layer, a second adhesion layer, and a third layer formed of a textured material comprising hollow or solid tubes. These tubes feature a radially external peripheral layer composed of doped poly-crystalline diamond. The tubes are arranged in a configuration where they are separated from each other and project away from the first and second layers, creating an extended three-dimensional surface area.  

Particular embodiments of the microelectrode include configurations where multiple electrodes are arranged in a network for neural interfacing applications. Another embodiment describes a flexible implant incorporating the microelectrode design for conformable integration with neural tissues.  

The invention further encompasses a method for manufacturing the microelectrode. The manufacturing process involves sequential steps including the growth of carbon nanotubes, deposition of non-doped diamond nanoparticles, and deposition of doped diamond material. A fourth layer may be added through deposition of a metal layer followed by a de-wetting step to create discrete metal features.  

Carbon nanotubes may be grown on metal drops formed during the de-wetting process. The method further includes deposition of a poly-electrolyte polymer combined with diamond nanoparticles. Various deposition techniques may be employed including ink jet type printing, electrospray of diamond nanoparticles, and chemical vapor deposition methods such as microwave plasma-assisted CVD, radiofrequency CVD, or hot filament CVD.  

The second material in the layer stack may comprise titanium nitride, non-doped poly-crystalline diamond, or boron-doped poly-crystalline diamond. Subsequent manufacturing steps involve precise formation of the tube structures and integration with other electrode components.  

The first material in the stack is a biocompatible substrate, while the third material forms the active electrode surface comprising a compact set of hollow or solid tubes. These tubes exhibit dimensions ranging from nanometric to micrometric scale, with specific geometric parameters optimized for neural interfacing. The active portion of the microelectrode may take various shapes including cylindrical, conical, or tapered forms, with diameters and heights carefully controlled to optimize performance.  

The second material serves as an adhesion layer promoting secure attachment of the tube structures. The microelectrode arrangement may be illustrated through cross-sectional views showing the layered structure and tube organization. Individual tube structures feature specific wall thicknesses and internal geometries tailored for optimal electrochemical behavior.  

Applications of the invention include multi-electrode array (MEA) networks for high-density neural recording and stimulation. Flexible implants incorporating the microelectrode design enable conformable integration with neural tissues while maintaining stable electrical performance. A covering matrix may encapsulate the electrode structures while permitting electrical contact with surrounding tissue.  

The manufacturing method for the microelectrode involves sequential steps beginning with preparation of the first material substrate. A second step deposits the second material adhesion layer, followed by formation of the third material tube structures. Additional steps refine the electrode geometry and integrate electrical connections.  

Alternative embodiments of the invention include variations in the structured diamond layer deposition process and supporting substrates. The structured material serves multiple functions including increasing effective surface area, enhancing charge transfer capacity, and providing mechanical stability.  

The microelectrode includes an electric current outlet comprising a fourth layer in contact with the tube structures. This outlet may be formed from conductive materials such as metals or doped semiconductors. A passivation layer isolates non-active regions while permitting electrical access to the active electrode surfaces.  

Advantages of the invention include improved signal-to-noise ratio for neural recording, enhanced charge injection capacity for stimulation, and long-term stability in physiological environments. The manufacturing method enables precise control over electrode geometry and material properties at micro- and nano-scales.  

Further embodiments describe methods for manufacturing MEA networks incorporating multiple microelectrodes. These methods involve sequential deposition of diamond particles, growth of diamond structures, deposition of nickel catalyst layers, and growth of carbon nanotube templates. Protective steps shield diamond structures during etching processes, followed by formation of metal contacts and passivation layers.  

Alternative manufacturing embodiments utilize titanium nitride layers as conductive substrates, with subsequent deposition of nickel layers for carbon nanotube growth. Diamond nanoparticle deposition and diamond growth steps create the final electrode structures with integrated metal contacts and passivation.  

The invention thus provides a comprehensive solution for high-performance neural interfacing, combining advanced materials science with innovative manufacturing techniques to overcome limitations of conventional electrode technologies. The resulting microelectrodes exhibit superior electrochemical characteristics while maintaining the biocompatibility and durability required for long-term neural interface applications.
