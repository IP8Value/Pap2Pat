# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to methods and systems for remediating heavy metal-contaminated soil. More specifically, the invention pertains to an electrochemical filtration process utilizing asymmetrical alternating current electrochemistry (AACE) in conjunction with a recirculating soil washing system to efficiently remove heavy metal cations from contaminated soil while preserving soil nutrients and minimizing chemical consumption.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Soil contamination has emerged as a global environmental crisis due to increasing industrialization and agricultural demands. Heavy metal contamination, in particular, poses severe risks to ecosystems and human health, given the persistence and toxicity of metals such as cadmium, lead, and copper. Conventional soil remediation techniques face significant limitations. Soil washing with strong chelating agents, such as ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), can liberate heavy metals from soil particles but suffers from high chemical consumption, secondary pollution from untreated effluent, and excessive nutrient loss.  

High-surface-area sorbents have been explored to immobilize heavy metals, but their adsorption kinetics are slow, capacity is limited, and long-term stability remains uncertain. Phytoremediation, while energy-efficient, requires extended treatment periods and risks secondary contamination through biomass accumulation.  

Electrochemical methods offer an alternative by reducing heavy metal cations to their zero-valent metallic states, enhancing immobilization and enabling recovery. However, existing direct current (DC) electrochemical approaches suffer from inefficiencies, including high voltage requirements, slow ion migration, and energy losses from water electrolysis. These drawbacks hinder practical field applications.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention introduces an AACE-based remediation system that overcomes the limitations of existing methods. The system comprises a recirculating soil washing loop and an electrochemical filtration device equipped with amidoxime-functionalized porous carbon (Ami-PC) electrodes. Heavy metal cations are first mobilized from soil by a chelating agent, such as EDTA, forming anionic complexes (MEDTA²⁻). The washing effluent is then passed through the AACE filter, where an asymmetrical alternating voltage is applied to facilitate heavy metal deposition.  

Key advantages of the AACE method include rapid remediation speed, minimal chemical consumption due to EDTA recycling, and high sustainability. The method selectively removes heavy metals while preserving essential soil nutrients, making it suitable for agricultural land remediation. The invention further encompasses the fabrication of Ami-PC electrodes, which enhance electrodeposition efficiency through amidoxime functionalization.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The AACE remediation method integrates a recirculating chelating agent washing system with an electrochemical filtration device. The soil is excavated and placed in a treatment cylinder, where an EDTA solution is circulated to extract heavy metal cations. The washing effluent is directed to the AACE filter, which consists of two parallel Ami-PC electrodes separated by a porous membrane.  

The Ami-PC electrodes are fabricated by coating carbon felt with a slurry of polyacrylonitrile (PAN) and activated carbon, followed by hydrothermal functionalization with amidoxime groups. These electrodes exhibit a porous morphology with fiber diameters of approximately 20 μm, providing high surface area for heavy metal adsorption.  

The AACE process operates in three steps. In Step I, ions are dispersed in the washing effluent. In Step II, a 5 V bias establishes an electrical double layer on the electrode surface, where amidoxime groups competitively bind heavy metal cations. In Step III, a −10 V bias reduces the adsorbed cations to zero-valent metallic particles, while EDTA anions are repelled. Nutrient cations, such as Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺, remain in solution due to their lower reduction potentials.  

Remediation performance was evaluated using synthetically contaminated soil samples. The AACE method reduced Cu, Pb, and Cd concentrations below regulatory thresholds, with filtration efficiencies exceeding 90%. Comparative studies demonstrated superior performance over DC methods, which suffered from low efficiency and co-precipitation of nutrient ions.  

X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) confirmed that the AACE method predominantly yields zero-valent metal particles, whereas DC methods produce metal hydroxides. Long-term testing showed stable filtration efficiency over multiple cycles, with EDTA recyclability and electrode regenerability via acid elution.  

Plant assays using pea sprouts (Pisum sativum) validated the agricultural feasibility of the AACE method. Treated soil exhibited negligible Cd bioavailability, and plant growth matched that of uncontaminated controls. In contrast, conventional EDTA washing caused nutrient depletion and stunted growth.  

The invention further includes methods for characterizing heavy metal species, optimizing flow rates, and treating mixed contaminations. Detailed fabrication protocols and experimental parameters are provided in the appended materials.
