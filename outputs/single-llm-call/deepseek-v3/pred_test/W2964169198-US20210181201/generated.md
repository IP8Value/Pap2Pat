# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of extracellular vesicle (EV) research, particularly to the development of recombinant extracellular vesicles (rEV) as biological reference materials. The invention provides compositions and methods for producing rEV that mimic the physical and biochemical characteristics of endogenous EVs, enabling their use in diagnostic, therapeutic, and analytical applications, including calibration of EV isolation and detection instruments, normalization of experimental data, and quality control in EV-related research.  

## BACKGROUND  

Liquid biopsies have emerged as a promising non-invasive approach for disease diagnosis, monitoring, and therapeutic assessment. Extracellular vesicles (EVs), which are membrane-bound nanoparticles released by cells into biofluids, play a critical role in intercellular communication and disease pathogenesis, including cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, and autoimmune diseases. EVs carry molecular cargo reflective of their cell of origin, making them valuable biomarkers for disease detection and progression.  

EVs are heterogeneous in size, biogenesis, and molecular composition, complicating their isolation and characterization. Current EV isolation techniques, such as differential ultracentrifugation (dUC), size exclusion chromatography (SEC), and density gradient centrifugation, suffer from low recovery rates, contamination with non-EV particles, and poor reproducibility. Additionally, the lack of standardized reference materials hinders inter-laboratory comparisons and method validation.  

Existing reference materials, such as polystyrene beads, silica beads, and liposomes, lack the biological complexity of EVs, including surface markers, lipid composition, and refractive index (RI), leading to inaccurate measurements. While biological reference materials derived from biofluids or cell cultures are commercially available, they lack specificity and traceability, limiting their utility for quality control and method validation.  

There is a pressing need for a biological reference material that (1) closely resembles endogenous EVs in physical and biochemical properties, (2) is distinguishable from sample EVs for tracking purposes, and (3) behaves similarly to sample EVs under various experimental conditions. The present invention addresses this need by providing recombinant extracellular vesicles (rEV) as a versatile and reproducible reference material for EV research and clinical applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention provides recombinant extracellular vesicles (rEV) as a biological reference material that closely mimics the physical and biochemical properties of endogenous sample EVs. The rEV are produced by expressing a self-assembling protein, such as the HIV-1 gag polyprotein, fused to a heterologous marker (e.g., EGFP) in a host cell system. The resulting rEV exhibit EV-like characteristics, including size distribution, buoyant density, refractive index, and molecular composition, while being trackable via the heterologous marker.  

### Composition of rEV  
The rEV comprise:  
- A self-assembling protein (e.g., HIV-1 gag polyprotein) that drives vesicle formation.  
- A heterologous marker (e.g., EGFP protein or mRNA) for detection and quantification.  
- EV-associated proteins (e.g., tetraspanins CD9, CD63, CD81) and lipids (e.g., phosphatidylserine, cholesterol) characteristic of endogenous EVs.  

### Physical and Biochemical Characteristics  
The rEV share key properties with endogenous EVs:  
- Size distribution: 50–180 nm, as measured by nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA).  
- Buoyant density: 1.086–1.119 g/mL, comparable to sample EVs isolated from biofluids.  
- Refractive index: ~1.37, similar to endogenous EVs.  
- Morphology: Cup-shaped, as observed by transmission electron microscopy (TEM).  

### Method for Determining Recovery Rate of Sample EVs  
The invention includes a method for assessing the efficiency of EV isolation techniques by spiking rEV into biofluid samples (e.g., plasma, urine) and quantifying their recovery after isolation. The steps include:  
1. **Mixing**: Adding a known quantity of rEV to the biofluid sample.  
2. **Isolating**: Applying an EV isolation technique (e.g., SEC, dUC, density gradient centrifugation).  
3. **Detecting**: Quantifying the recovered rEV using fluorescence-, protein-, or RNA-based assays.  
4. **Determining recovery rate**: Calculating the ratio of recovered rEV to spiked rEV.  

### Definitions  
- **Sample extracellular vesicle (sample EV)**: Naturally occurring EVs isolated from biofluids or cell culture media.  
- **Endogenous EV**: EVs derived from host cells without recombinant modification.  
- **Heterologous marker**: A foreign molecule (e.g., EGFP, luciferase) incorporated into rEV for detection.  

### Detection Methods  
The heterologous marker enables rEV quantification via:  
- **Fluorescence**: fNTA, high-resolution flow cytometry (HR-FC), or microplate reader.  
- **Protein-based assays**: ELISA (e.g., anti-p24) or Western blot (e.g., anti-EGFP).  
- **RNA-based assays**: RT-qPCR for EGFP mRNA.  

### Preferred Embodiments  
- The heterologous marker is a light-emitting protein (e.g., EGFP) or its mRNA.  
- The self-assembling protein is HIV-1 gag, which recruits host EV biogenesis machinery (e.g., ESCRT proteins).  

### Biofluid Compatibility  
The rEV are compatible with various biofluids, including:  
- Plasma or serum.  
- Urine.  
- Cell culture media.  

### Isolation and Detection Techniques  
- **Isolation**: Any EV isolation method (e.g., SEC, dUC, immune-affinity capture).  
- **Detection**: Quantification of rEV via fluorescence intensity, protein concentration, or RNA levels.  

### Advanced Applications  
- **Calibration of devices**: rEV are used to standardize EV measurement instruments (e.g., NTA, flow cytometry).  
- **Method evaluation**: rEV assess the performance of EV isolation protocols.  

### Example Workflows  
1. **Production of rEV**: Transfect HEK293T cells with gag-EGFP plasmid, isolate rEV via OptiPrep density gradient centrifugation.  
2. **Spiking into biofluids**: Add rEV to plasma or urine, isolate EVs, and quantify recovery.  
3. **Normalization**: Use rEV to correct for inter-user variability in EV enumeration.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Recombinant Extracellular Vesicles (rEV) Share Physical and Biochemical Characteristics with Endogenous Sample EVs  
- rEV were produced by transfecting HEK293T cells with gag-EGFP and isolated via OptiPrep density gradient (ODG) centrifugation.  
- Comparative analysis confirmed that rEV and mock EVs (from untransfected cells) exhibit similar size, density, protein composition (ALIX, TSG101, CD9), and lipid profiles.  

### Example 2: Identification of rEV Detection Methods  
- rEV were quantified using fluorescent NTA (fNTA), HR-FC, and Western blot for gag-EGFP.  
- fNTA and HR-FC provided high sensitivity, while ELISA enabled precise quantification at lower concentrations.  

### Example 3: Optimized Storage Conditions for rEV  
- rEV retained stability when stored at −80°C for 12 months or lyophilized with trehalose.  
- Fluorescence intensity and particle integrity were preserved under these conditions.  

### Example 4: Use of rEV to Estimate EV Isolation Efficiency  
- rEV spiked into plasma were recovered with varying efficiency: SEC (~100%), ODG (~30%), dUC (~10%).  
- Recovery rates were consistent across fluorescence- and protein-based detection methods.  

### Example 5: rEV Enable Accurate Comparison of EV Numbers in Cancer vs. Healthy Samples  
- Normalization with rEV revealed a 2.5-fold increase in EV concentration in breast cancer patient plasma compared to healthy controls.  

### Example 6: Loading EGFP mRNA into rEV  
- A modified gag-EGFP plasmid with the PSI sequence enhanced EGFP mRNA packaging into rEV, enabling RNA-based detection.
