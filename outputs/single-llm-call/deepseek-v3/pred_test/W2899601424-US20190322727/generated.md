# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to novel monoclonal antibodies and immunoassay methods for detecting bacterial proteins associated with antibiotic resistance. More specifically, the invention provides monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins, which confer resistance to colistin in Gram-negative bacteria. The invention further encompasses immunoassay methods utilizing these antibodies for rapid and sensitive detection of colistin-resistant bacteria in clinical, environmental, and food samples.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Antibiotics have played a critical role in public health by enabling effective treatment of bacterial infections. However, the emergence and spread of antibiotic-resistant bacteria threaten modern medicine's ability to combat infectious diseases. Colistin, also known as polymyxin E, represents a last-resort antibiotic for treating infections caused by multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacteria. The recent discovery of plasmid-mediated colistin resistance genes, particularly mcr-1 and mcr-2, poses a significant public health threat as these resistance determinants can spread horizontally between bacterial species.  

The mcr-1 gene encodes a phosphoethanolamine transferase that modifies lipid A in bacterial lipopolysaccharide, reducing colistin binding and conferring resistance. Since its initial identification, mcr-1 has disseminated globally across multiple bacterial species and environments. Subsequent discovery of mcr-2 through mcr-8 variants demonstrates the expanding genetic diversity of mobile colistin resistance. Current methods for detecting colistin resistance suffer from limitations including long turnaround times, technical complexity, and inability to directly detect functional resistance proteins.  

Phenotypic methods such as broth microdilution require bacterial culture and demonstrate variable results due to colistin's physicochemical properties. Genotypic methods like PCR detect resistance genes but cannot confirm functional protein expression. There exists an urgent need for reliable detection methods that directly identify MCR proteins as markers of clinically relevant colistin resistance. The present invention addresses this need by providing monoclonal antibodies and immunoassays for sensitive and specific detection of MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind to MCR-1 and MCR-2 proteins associated with colistin resistance. These antibodies enable development of sensitive immunoassays for detecting colistin-resistant bacteria. The invention encompasses hybridoma cell lines producing monoclonal antibodies with high affinity for MCR proteins. The antibodies may be labeled with detectable markers including enzymes, fluorophores, or radioisotopes for use in various immunoassay formats.  

A key aspect of the invention involves sandwich ELISA methods utilizing monoclonal antibodies for detecting MCR proteins. The assays demonstrate high sensitivity with detection limits as low as 0.01 ng/mL for MCR-1 in buffer solutions. The invention further provides kits containing monoclonal antibodies and reagents for detecting colistin-resistant bacteria in clinical, food, and environmental samples.  

The monoclonal antibodies offer several advantages over existing detection methods. They provide direct detection of functional resistance proteins rather than indirect genetic markers. The immunoassays are rapid, sensitive, and adaptable to various testing platforms. The antibodies specifically recognize MCR proteins across different bacterial strains and demonstrate minimal cross-reactivity with unrelated proteins. These features make the invention valuable for surveillance and control of colistin-resistant bacteria in healthcare and agricultural settings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The following definitions apply throughout this specification unless specifically indicated otherwise. The singular forms "a," "an," and "the" include plural referents unless the context clearly dictates otherwise. The term "about" when referring to numerical values means ±10% of the recited value. An "antibody" refers to an immunoglobulin molecule capable of specific binding to a target antigen. A "monoclonal antibody" refers to an antibody produced by a single clone of B cells or hybridoma cells.  

The "catalytic domain" of MCR proteins refers to the C-terminal portion responsible for phosphoethanolamine transferase activity. A "chimeric" antibody contains variable regions from one species and constant regions from another. The phrase "consisting essentially of" limits the scope to the specified materials or steps and those that do not materially affect the basic characteristics of the invention.  

"Detecting the formation of an immunological complex" refers to identifying antibody-antigen binding through direct or indirect detection methods. An "effective amount" means a quantity sufficient to achieve a desired biological effect. "Humanized" antibodies contain human framework regions with non-human antigen-binding complementarity-determining regions.  

An "isolated" antibody or protein has been separated from components that naturally accompany it. "MCR protein" refers to any member of the mobile colistin resistance protein family. "MCR-1 protein" and "MCR-2 protein" specifically denote these variants. "Resistance to colistin" means reduced susceptibility to colistin's antibacterial effects.  

The invention provides monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind MCR-1 and/or MCR-2 proteins. These antibodies were developed by immunizing animals with recombinant MCR-1 catalytic domain and screening hybridomas for specific binding. The antibodies demonstrate high affinity with dissociation constants in the nanomolar range.  

The monoclonal antibodies enable various immunoassay formats for detecting MCR proteins. In sandwich ELISA, one antibody captures MCR proteins while a second labeled antibody detects bound antigen. The assays detect MCR-1 at concentrations as low as 0.01 ng/mL with a dynamic range spanning three orders of magnitude. The antibodies show minimal cross-reactivity with unrelated bacterial proteins.  

Genetic conservation among mcr variants allows the antibodies to recognize multiple MCR family members. The catalytic domains of MCR-1 and MCR-2 share 87.4% amino acid identity, enabling cross-reactive antibody binding. Some monoclonal antibodies specifically recognize MCR-1 while others bind both MCR-1 and MCR-2.  

The invention includes methods for producing recombinant MCR proteins as assay standards and immunogens. Full-length and catalytic domain constructs were expressed in E. coli and purified by affinity chromatography. Mass spectrometry confirmed protein integrity, with peptide coverage exceeding 60% of the MCR-1 sequence.  

Antibody production involves immunizing animals with purified MCR proteins, generating hybridomas, and screening for specific monoclonal antibodies. The resulting antibodies were characterized by ELISA, Western blot, and affinity measurements. Isotyping revealed predominantly IgG1 antibodies with kappa light chains.  

A preferred embodiment utilizes monoclonal antibody MCR-1-7 in sandwich ELISA. This IgG2b antibody demonstrates high affinity for both MCR-1 and MCR-2. When paired with polyclonal capture antibodies, it enables sensitive detection of MCR proteins in complex matrices including meat samples.  

The antibodies may be labeled with various detection moieties including enzymes, fluorophores, or radioisotopes. Horseradish peroxidase and alkaline phosphatase serve as preferred enzyme labels. Biotinylation allows secondary detection with streptavidin conjugates. Fluorescent labels include FITC, PE, and quantum dots.  

The invention provides kits containing monoclonal antibodies for MCR protein detection. Kits may include microtiter plates, detection reagents, wash buffers, and protein standards. Written instructions describe assay procedures and result interpretation. Components are packaged in suitable containers to maintain stability.  

Numerical ranges in this disclosure include all intermediate values and subranges. For example, a concentration range of 0.01-50 ng/mL encompasses 0.1 ng/mL, 1 ng/mL, 10 ng/mL, etc. The following examples illustrate but do not limit the invention's scope.  

### Materials and Methods  

All animal procedures complied with institutional guidelines and were approved by the appropriate animal care committee. Bacterial strains included mcr-1-positive E. coli and control strains. Recombinant MCR proteins were expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3) using pET vectors with N-terminal His-tags.  

Protein purification employed HisTrap FF chromatography followed by size-exclusion chromatography. The catalytic domain was cleaved from its His-tag using TEV protease. Protein concentrations were determined by UV absorbance at 280 nm using calculated extinction coefficients.  

Polyclonal antibodies were produced in rabbits immunized with cMCR-1. Sera were collected and IgG purified by Protein A affinity chromatography. Monoclonal antibodies were generated by immunizing mice with cMCR-1, fusing splenocytes with myeloma cells, and screening hybridomas by ELISA.  

ELISAs used black microtiter plates coated with capture antibodies. After blocking, samples were added followed by detection antibodies conjugated to HRP. Chemiluminescent substrate generated signals measured as counts per second. Limit of detection was defined as mean background plus three standard deviations.  

Western blots separated proteins by SDS-PAGE before transfer to PVDF membranes. Membranes were probed with primary antibodies followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Detection used chemiluminescent substrates with image capture on X-ray film.  

Meat samples were spiked with bacteria, enriched in broth containing colistin, and processed for ELISA. Bacterial pellets were lysed and supernatants tested for MCR proteins. Statistical analysis employed Student's t-test with p<0.05 considered significant.  

### Results and Discussion  

Recombinant MCR proteins were successfully expressed and purified. Full-length MCR-1 and MCR-2 appeared smaller than predicted on SDS-PAGE, likely due to hydrophobic transmembrane domains. Mass spectrometry confirmed intact MCR-1 with 60.6% sequence coverage.  

Polyclonal antibodies demonstrated high titers (>1:8000) against cMCR-1 by ELISA. Western blot confirmed specificity for MCR-1 in bacterial lysates. Nine monoclonal antibodies were characterized, including six that cross-reacted with MCR-1 and MCR-2 and three specific for MCR-1.  

Sandwich ELISA using monoclonal antibody MCR-1-7 detected cMCR-1 at 0.01 ng/mL and cMCR-2 at 0.1 ng/mL. Intra-assay precision was <5% CV while inter-assay precision was <15% CV. The assay detected MCR-1 in meat samples spiked with as few as 0.4 cfu/g of mcr-1-positive bacteria.  

The monoclonal antibodies provide sensitive tools for detecting colistin-resistant bacteria. Their ability to directly identify functional resistance proteins offers advantages over genetic methods. The invention's immunoassays are rapid, specific, and adaptable to various testing environments.
