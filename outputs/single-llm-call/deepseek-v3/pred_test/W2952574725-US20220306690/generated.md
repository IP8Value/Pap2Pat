# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) infection remains a significant global health challenge despite advances in combination antiretroviral therapy (cART). The introduction of cART has transformed HIV/AIDS from a fatal disease into a chronic condition by substantially reducing morbidity and mortality among infected individuals across diverse populations. However, the emergence of drug resistance mutations and the adverse effects associated with currently approved anti-HIV drugs underscore the urgent need for novel antiviral agents that target unexplored mechanisms or viral components.  

The HIV-1 capsid protein (CA) represents a promising antiviral target due to its essential role in multiple stages of the viral replication cycle. The CA protein consists of two structurally distinct domains: an N-terminal domain (CA-NTD) comprising seven α-helices and a C-terminal domain (CA-CTD) containing four α-helices connected by a flexible linker. These domains facilitate the formation of hexameric and pentameric building blocks, which assemble into a conical capsid core during viral maturation. The capsid core participates in critical processes such as uncoating, nuclear entry, integration of viral DNA into the host genome, and viral assembly.  

The uncoating process, wherein the capsid core disassembles in the host cytoplasm, is closely linked to reverse transcription initiation. Furthermore, CA facilitates nuclear import of the preintegration complex, enabling viral DNA integration. During viral assembly, the Gag polyprotein precursor assembles at the plasma membrane and undergoes proteolytic cleavage to form the mature capsid core. Given its multifunctional role, CA presents a unique opportunity for therapeutic intervention by disrupting viral replication at multiple stages.  

Several compounds targeting CA have been reported, including benzodiazepines (BD), benzimidazoles (BM), pyrrolopyrazolones (BI-1 and BI-2), and PF-3450074 (PF74). These inhibitors bind the CA protein at distinct sites, often interfering with interactions between CA and host factors such as cleavage and polyadenylation specific factor 6 (CPSF6) and nucleoporin 153 (NUP153). Despite their potential, none of these compounds has progressed to clinical trials, highlighting the need for more potent and selective CA inhibitors.  

Recent developments have identified GS-CA1 and its analog GS-6207 as highly potent CA inhibitors with superior antiviral activity compared to existing drugs. These compounds exhibit picomolar efficacy in inhibiting HIV-1 replication and demonstrate long-acting pharmacokinetic properties in preclinical studies. Structural analyses suggest that GS-CA1 and GS-6207 bind the same pocket as PF74, CPSF6, and NUP153 but engage in more extensive interactions with CA, resulting in enhanced binding affinity and antiviral potency.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides cyclic or linear peptides capable of binding the HIV-1 capsid protein with high affinity, thereby inhibiting viral replication. The peptides comprise a defined sequence incorporating structural motifs derived from known CA-binding molecules, including GS-CA1, PF74, CPSF6, and NUP153. These peptides exhibit enhanced binding affinity to CA, as demonstrated by computational docking and experimental validation using microscale thermophoresis (MST) assays.  

In one embodiment, the peptide comprises a cyclic structure with a core sequence incorporating hydrophobic and polar uncharged amino acids that facilitate interactions with key residues in the CA binding pocket. Variants of the peptide include modifications such as additional amino acids, fluorescent labels, chelators, or radionuclides for diagnostic or therapeutic applications. The peptide binds the HIV-1 capsid protein at a site shared by PF74 and GS-CA1, engaging residues critical for viral replication, such as L56, M66, Q67, and A105.  

The binding affinity of the peptide to CA is significantly higher than that of PF74, as evidenced by MST assays showing a dissociation constant (Kd) of approximately 32 nM for the peptide compared to 212 nM for PF74. This enhanced affinity translates to superior inhibitory activity, making the peptide a promising candidate for antiviral therapy. The peptide may be synthesized using conventional solid-phase peptide synthesis or expressed recombinantly in host cells. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the peptide and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier are also provided for therapeutic use in treating HIV infection.

### Overview  

The invention is based on structural insights derived from the GS-CA1-bound CA hexamer crystal structure, which revealed a binding site shared by PF74, CPSF6, and NUP153. GS-CA1 engages residues L56, M66, Q67, N74, and A105, mutations in which confer resistance to the inhibitor. Comparative analyses demonstrated that GS-CA1 and PF74 share overlapping binding modes, with key structural motifs superposing precisely.  

GS-6207, an analog of GS-CA1, exhibits enhanced binding affinity due to additional interactions with CA residues such as K70. Docking studies revealed that GS-6207 adopts a distinct conformation compared to GS-CA1, with its trifluoromethyl moiety solvent-exposed. Resistance mutations, including Q67H, further validate the functional importance of the binding pocket.  

The designed cyclic peptide (Pep-2-cyclic) incorporates structural features from GS-CA1, PF74, and host factor-derived peptides. Docking simulations positioned Pep-2-cyclic in the same binding pocket, engaging critical CA residues. MST assays confirmed its superior binding affinity (Kd = 32 nM) compared to PF74 (Kd = 212 nM), supporting its potential as an antiviral agent.  

### Cyclic Peptides  

The cyclic peptide of the invention comprises the sequence X1-X2-X3, wherein X1 is a hydrophobic amino acid, X2 is a polar uncharged amino acid, and X3 is an aromatic amino acid. Alternative sequences may include substitutions or extensions while retaining binding affinity. The peptide may further comprise modifications such as fluorescent dyes, chelators, or radionuclides for diagnostic or therapeutic applications.  

The peptide binds the HIV-1 capsid protein at a pocket formed by CA-NTD and CA-CTD, interacting with residues L56, M66, and Q67. Its inhibitory activity is attributed to disrupting CA interactions with host factors or stabilizing nonfunctional capsid structures. The peptide is synthesized via solid-phase methods or expressed in recombinant host cells and formulated into pharmaceutical compositions for administering to subjects in need of HIV treatment.  

### Linear Peptides  

Linear peptides of the invention share the core sequence X1-X2-X3 but lack the cyclic constraint. These peptides may include C-terminal modifications such as amidation or acetylation to enhance stability. Like their cyclic counterparts, linear peptides bind CA with high affinity and inhibit viral replication.  

Polynucleotides encoding the linear peptide may be cloned into expression vectors and introduced into host cells for large-scale production. Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the linear peptide are administered to subjects to treat HIV infection, with dosing regimens tailored to achieve sustained antiviral effects.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

The HIV-1 CA structure (PDB: 4XFZ) was prepared for docking simulations using Schrödinger Suite. Hydrogen atoms were added, bond orders assigned, and water molecules optimized to generate a refined CA-hexamer model.  

### Example 2  

Docking simulations of GS-CA1, GS-6207, and CAP-1 were performed using Induced-Fit Docking (IFD). The compounds were docked into a grid centered on the PF74 binding site, and the top-scoring poses were selected for analysis.  

### Example 3  

The cyclic peptide Pep-2-cyclic was designed and docked into the CA-hexamer structure. IFD predicted its binding mode and affinity, which were subsequently validated experimentally.  

### Example 4  

Pep-2-cyclic was synthesized and purified. MST assays measured its binding affinity to fluorescently labeled CA-hexamers, yielding a Kd of 32 nM. Comparative analyses confirmed its superiority over PF74 (Kd = 212 nM), establishing its potential as a novel CA inhibitor.
