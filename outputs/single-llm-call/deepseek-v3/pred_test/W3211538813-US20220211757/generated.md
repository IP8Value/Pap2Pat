# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of immunotherapy, specifically to engineered gamma delta (γδ) T cells and methods for their production and use in treating diseases such as cancer and infectious diseases. The invention provides compositions and methods for generating off-the-shelf γδ T cell therapies through genetic engineering of pluripotent human cells, including hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs).  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Gamma delta (γδ) T cells are a subset of T lymphocytes that possess innate-like immune functions, including direct cytotoxicity against tumor cells and pathogen-infected cells. Unlike conventional alpha-beta (αβ) T cells, γδ T cells do not require major histocompatibility complex (MHC) recognition for activation, making them attractive candidates for allogeneic cell therapies. However, current methods for generating γδ T cells face limitations, including low cell yields, variability in functionality, and the risk of graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) when used in allogeneic settings.  

Conventional methods for generating γδ T cells rely on ex vivo expansion from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), which is inefficient and often results in heterogeneous cell populations. Additionally, autologous γδ T cell therapies are patient-specific, limiting scalability and increasing costs. There is a need for novel methods to produce large quantities of homogeneous, functional γδ T cells suitable for off-the-shelf applications.  

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a method for engineering γδ T cells from pluripotent human cells, such as HSCs, through genetic modification and in vitro differentiation. This approach enables the production of standardized, allogeneic γδ T cell therapies with enhanced tumor-targeting capabilities and reduced immunogenicity.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides off-the-shelf gamma delta (γδ) T cell therapies generated through the genetic engineering of pluripotent human cells, such as hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). The engineered γδ T cells express exogenous gamma and delta chain polypeptides, enabling their use in adoptive immunotherapy for cancer and infectious diseases.  

The method involves transducing pluripotent human cells with vectors encoding γδ T cell receptor (TCR) genes, followed by in vitro differentiation into functional γδ T cells. The resulting cells exhibit potent antitumor activity, resistance to allorejection, and the ability to be further engineered with chimeric antigen receptors (CARs) or other therapeutic transgenes.  

Key aspects of the invention include:  
1. Engineered γδ T cells produced from transduced pluripotent human cells.  
2. Compositions comprising gamma and delta chain polypeptides or polynucleotides encoding them.  
3. Methods for producing functional γδ T cells through in vitro differentiation of transduced pluripotent cells.  
4. Therapeutic applications of the engineered γδ T cells, including cancer treatment and infectious disease management.  

The invention also encompasses γδ T cells with additional genetic modifications, such as knockout of HLA genes to reduce immunogenicity, incorporation of suicide genes for safety control, and expression of CARs for enhanced tumor targeting. These cells can be cryopreserved and administered to patients as an off-the-shelf therapy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Gamma Delta T Cells and Their Recognition Ability  

Gamma delta (γδ) T cells are a unique subset of T lymphocytes that bridge innate and adaptive immunity. Unlike conventional αβ T cells, γδ T cells recognize antigens in an MHC-independent manner, allowing them to target a broad range of tumor and infected cells. Their ability to rapidly produce cytokines and exert cytotoxic activity makes them ideal candidates for immunotherapy.  

### Limitations of Current Methods for Generating γδ T Cells  

Current methods for producing γδ T cells rely on isolating and expanding cells from peripheral blood, which is inefficient and yields heterogeneous populations. Autologous approaches are patient-specific, limiting scalability, while allogeneic γδ T cells risk inducing GvHD due to residual αβ T cell contamination. There is a need for standardized, scalable methods to generate functional γδ T cells for off-the-shelf use.  

### Invention's Solution: Gene Engineering of Pluripotent Human Cells  

The invention provides a solution by genetically engineering pluripotent human cells, such as HSCs, to express γδ TCRs, followed by in vitro differentiation into functional γδ T cells. This approach enables large-scale production of homogeneous γδ T cells with defined specificity and functionality.  

### Pluripotent Human Cells and Their Multipotentiality  

Pluripotent human cells, including HSCs and induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), can differentiate into various hematopoietic lineages, including T cells. The invention leverages this multipotentiality by directing differentiation toward the γδ T cell lineage through genetic engineering and culture conditions.  

### Unexpected Discovery of γδ T Cell Development  

The inventors discovered that transduced pluripotent human cells could be efficiently differentiated into γδ T cells in vitro using an artificial thymic organoid (ATO) system. This system mimics thymic conditions, promoting T cell development while maintaining γδ TCR expression.  

### Co-Expression of Disease-Targeting Molecules  

The engineered γδ T cells can co-express additional targeting molecules, such as CARs, to enhance their antitumor activity. For example, γδ T cells engineered with a BCMA-specific CAR exhibited superior tumor killing compared to conventional CAR-T cells.  

### Method of Making Engineered Functional T Cells  

The method comprises:  
1. Transducing pluripotent human cells with vectors encoding γδ TCR genes.  
2. Differentiating the transduced cells in vitro using an ATO system.  
3. Expanding the differentiated cells in cytokine-supplemented media.  
4. Optionally enriching for γδ T cells using cell sorting or magnetic bead isolation.  

### Therapeutic Applications  

The engineered γδ T cells can be used to treat various cancers, including hematological malignancies and solid tumors. They can also target infectious diseases by recognizing pathogen-associated molecules.  

### Materials and Methods  

The invention includes compositions comprising gamma and delta chain polypeptides, polynucleotides encoding them, and vectors for their expression. These components enable the production of engineered γδ T cells with defined specificity.  

### Engineered γδ T Cell Characteristics  

The engineered γδ T cells exhibit:  
- High purity and homogeneity.  
- Potent cytotoxicity against tumor cells.  
- Resistance to allorejection due to low HLA expression.  
- Stability upon cryopreservation.  

### Applications of Engineered γδ T Cells  

The cells can be used in adoptive immunotherapy, either alone or in combination with other treatments, such as checkpoint inhibitors or bispecific antibodies.  

### Proof of Principle  

Data from preclinical models demonstrate the antitumor efficacy of engineered γδ T cells, including their ability to persist in vivo and mediate tumor regression without inducing GvHD.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Cloning Human Vγ9Vδ2 TCRs  

Human Vγ9Vδ2 TCR genes were cloned into lentiviral vectors and transduced into HSCs. The transduced cells were differentiated into γδ T cells in vitro, demonstrating successful TCR expression and functionality.  

### Example 2: Generating γδ T Cells Through Gene Engineering  

HSCs were transduced with γδ TCR vectors and differentiated into γδ T cells using an ATO system. The resulting cells exhibited cytotoxic activity against tumor cell lines.  

### Example 3: Differentiating into Transgenic γδ T Cells  

Transduced HSCs were differentiated into γδ T cells and expanded ex vivo. The cells maintained γδ TCR expression and functionality after cryopreservation.  

### Example 4: Engineering HSC-γδT to Express Additional Targeting Molecules  

γδ T cells were further engineered to express a BCMA-specific CAR, enhancing their tumor-killing capacity in vitro and in vivo.  

### Example 5: Utilizing CAR-γδT Cells for Off-the-Shelf Therapy  

CAR-engineered γδ T cells were tested in a murine xenograft model, demonstrating potent antitumor activity without GvHD.  

### Example 6: Disrupting HLA Genes to Reduce Immunogenicity  

CRISPR-Cas9 was used to knockout B2M and CIITA genes in γδ T cells, reducing their immunogenicity and enhancing persistence in allogeneic hosts.
