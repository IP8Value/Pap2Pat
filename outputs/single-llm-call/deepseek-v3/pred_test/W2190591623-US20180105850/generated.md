# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to genetically engineered microorganisms for the production of L-ornithine and related compounds. More specifically, the invention provides recombinant eukaryotic cells, particularly yeast strains of Saccharomyces cerevisiae, that have been genetically modified to enhance biosynthesis and accumulation of L-ornithine through targeted pathway engineering. The invention further encompasses methods for producing L-ornithine and downstream polyamines using these engineered microbial cell factories.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

L-ornithine serves as a key metabolic intermediate in the biosynthesis of polyamines such as putrescine, spermidine, and spermine, which have important industrial and pharmaceutical applications. Current commercial production of L-ornithine primarily relies on whole-cell biotransformation using bacterial strains such as Corynebacterium glutamicum. However, bacterial production systems face limitations including the need for complex media components and challenges in genetic manipulation.  

Existing microbial production processes for L-ornithine suffer from several drawbacks. Whole-cell biotransformation approaches using bacterial strains often require supplementation of expensive precursors and exhibit limited pathway regulation capabilities. While thermophilic enzymes have been explored for improved stability, their integration into microbial hosts remains challenging. Current bacterial production strains also demonstrate suboptimal carbon flux partitioning between biomass formation and product accumulation.  

There exists a significant need for improved microbial strategies for L-ornithine production. Eukaryotic cell factories, particularly yeast, offer several advantages including well-established genetic tools, robust growth characteristics, and the ability to perform complex post-translational modifications. However, engineering yeast for efficient L-ornithine production requires overcoming multiple metabolic challenges including pathway compartmentalization, feedback regulation, and competing carbon fluxes.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a eukaryotic cell factory for enhanced production of L-ornithine. The invention involves genetically modifying ornithine carbamoyltransferase activity while simultaneously enhancing both L-ornithine biosynthesis from α-ketoglutarate and carbon channeling toward precursor formation. The engineered cells demonstrate significantly improved L-ornithine titers compared to wild-type strains.  

Key aspects of the invention include: attenuation of L-ornithine consumption pathways through genetic modifications to ornithine carbamoyltransferase (ARG3); enhancement of mitochondrial L-ornithine biosynthesis through overexpression of pathway enzymes (ARG5,6, ARG7, ARG8); optimization of precursor supply by engineering α-ketoglutarate biosynthesis; and alleviation of carbon flux limitations imposed by the Crabtree effect.  

The invention further provides methods for producing polyamines from the engineered L-ornithine overproducing strains. By introducing additional genetic modifications to direct flux toward polyamine biosynthesis, the invention enables production of putrescine, spermidine, spermine, and related compounds.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention utilizes Saccharomyces cerevisiae as a chassis host due to its well-characterized genetics, established industrial applications, and favorable growth characteristics. S. cerevisiae offers several advantages including robust growth on simple media, tolerance to low pH conditions, and well-developed genetic tools for metabolic engineering.  

A critical challenge in constructing L-ornithine over-producing yeast strains stems from the compartmentalization of L-arginine biosynthesis. The pathway spans both mitochondrial and cytosolic compartments, requiring coordinated engineering of biosynthesis and transport processes. The invention employs conventional genetic engineering techniques including promoter replacements, gene deletions, and heterologous gene expression to overcome these challenges.  

For purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined:  

A "modified" host organism refers to a eukaryotic or prokaryotic cell that has been genetically altered through human intervention to exhibit changed properties compared to its wild-type counterpart.  

An "open reading frame (ORF)" denotes a DNA sequence that contains a series of codons capable of being translated into a polypeptide, beginning with an initiation codon and ending with a termination codon.  

"Recombinant" describes nucleic acid molecules or cells that have been modified through genetic engineering techniques to contain or express heterologous genetic material.  

"Recombinant eukaryotic cells" and "recombinant prokaryotic cells" refer to eukaryotic or prokaryotic cells respectively that contain introduced genetic material through human intervention.  

An "endogenous" nucleic acid or protein is one that originates from and is naturally present in the host organism.  

A "heterologous" nucleic acid or protein originates from a different organism than the host or from an artificial sequence not naturally occurring in the host.  

A "gene" refers to a DNA sequence that encodes a functional product, which may be either RNA or protein.  

The "genome" constitutes the complete set of genetic material present in an organism.  

"Nucleic acid" describes a macromolecule composed of nucleotide units, including DNA and RNA.  

"Peptide," "polypeptide," and "protein" describe chains of amino acid residues, with the terms often used interchangeably though typically differing by length.  

An "enzyme" is a protein that catalyzes a specific biochemical reaction.  

A "promoter" is a DNA sequence that directs transcription of an associated gene.  

A "terminator" is a DNA sequence that signals the end of transcription.  

"Overexpression" refers to the production of a gene product at levels higher than normally observed in the wild-type organism.  

"Overproducing" describes a cell that produces a metabolite at levels exceeding those of wild-type cells.  

The "Crabtree effect" denotes the phenomenon whereby yeast produces ethanol under aerobic conditions in the presence of high glucose concentrations.  

A "vector" is a DNA molecule used to transfer genetic material into a host cell.  

"Sequence identity" refers to the percentage of identical residues between two aligned nucleic acid or protein sequences.  

The invention provides modified eukaryotic cells for L-ornithine overproduction through specific genetic modifications. In preferred embodiments, the eukaryotic cells belong to the genus Saccharomyces, particularly the species S. cerevisiae. Alternative yeast species suitable for modification include but are not limited to Kluyveromyces lactis, Pichia pastoris, and Yarrowia lipolytica.  

The invention also encompasses prokaryotic cell types that may be modified for L-ornithine production. Suitable gram-negative bacteria genera include Escherichia, Pseudomonas, and Salmonella. Appropriate gram-positive bacteria genera include Bacillus, Corynebacterium, and Streptomyces.  

Key modifications to yeast cells for enhanced L-ornithine production include:  

Reduction of L-ornithine consumption pathways through attenuation of ornithine carbamoyltransferase activity by replacing the native ARG3 promoter with weaker promoters such as HXT1 or KEX2.  

Further reduction of L-ornithine degradation activity by deleting the CAR2 gene encoding ornithine transaminase.  

Increase of arginase activity through overexpression of the CAR1 gene to enhance conversion of L-arginine to L-ornithine.  

Enhancement of carbon channeling from α-ketoglutarate through overexpression of key TCA cycle enzymes including pyruvate carboxylase (PYC2), citrate synthase (CIT1), aconitase (ACO2), and isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDP1).  

The L-ornithine biosynthetic pathway in yeast involves conversion of α-ketoglutarate to L-ornithine via L-glutamate in mitochondria. The invention proposes multiple strategies to improve pathway efficiency:  

Enhancing the endogenous mitochondrial pathway through overexpression of enzymes ARG5,6, ARG7, and ARG8.  

Relocalizing the L-ornithine 'acetylated derivatives cycle' by overexpressing N-acetylornithine synthase (ARG2).  

Overexpressing mitochondrial transporters ORT1 (L-ornithine exporter) and AGC1 (glutamate/aspartate exchanger) to improve metabolite trafficking.  

Introducing a heterologous cytosolic L-ornithine pathway using bacterial genes argAEC and argBEc from E. coli combined with argJCg, argCCg and argDCg from C. glutamicum.  

Carbon channeling from glucose to α-ketoglutarate is improved through:  

Overexpression of pyruvate carboxylase (PYC2) to enhance anaplerotic flux.  

Overexpression of citrate synthase (CIT1) to drive TCA cycle flux.  

Increasing pyruvate dehydrogenase activity through overexpression of its E1 alpha subunit (PDA1).  

Overexpression of aconitase (ACO2) and isocitrate dehydrogenase (IDP1) to enhance α-ketoglutarate production.  

Increasing mitochondrial pyruvate carrier activity to improve precursor supply.  

Decreasing carbon substrate uptake through expression of MTH1-ΔT to attenuate glucose transport and reduce Crabtree effect.  

Increasing NADH alternative oxidase (AOX) activity to relieve respiratory limitations.  

The engineered eukaryotic cells convert carbon sources such as glucose to L-ornithine with high efficiency. The produced L-ornithine may be incorporated into various products including pharmaceuticals, nutraceuticals, and industrial chemicals.  

The invention further provides methods for producing polyamines from the engineered L-ornithine overproducing strains:  

To increase putrescine production, the invention involves increasing ornithine decarboxylase (ODC) activity through overexpression of SPE1, decreasing ODC antizyme (OAZ1) activity, and deleting methylthioadenosine phosphorylase.  

For enhanced spermidine production, the invention includes down-regulating spermidine synthase while overexpressing yeast polyamine oxidase.  

Spermine production is increased by expressing heterologous spermine synthase (SPS) from eukaryotic or prokaryotic sources combined with increased activities of ODC, S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase (SAMDC) and/or spermidine synthase (SPDS).  

Polyamine export is facilitated by overexpressing export proteins (TPO1, QDR3) while down-regulating genes associated with polyamine uptake (AGP2, SAM3, DUR3).  

Strain resistance to polyamine toxicity is improved by down-regulating SR protein kinase, putative serine/threonine protein kinase, BRP1 and FES1 while overexpressing QDR3 and YAP1.  

The invention also enables production of agmatine by directing flux from ornithine to arginine and subsequently to agmatine through overexpression of ARG3, ARG1, ARG10 and/or ARG4 combined with deletion of native arginase (CAR1) and introduction of heterologous arginine decarboxylase.  

Putrescine may be produced from agmatine by introducing genes encoding agmatinase activity. Spermidine can be produced from agmatine by expressing appropriate enzymes or via condensation of L-aspartate semialdehyde and putrescine.  

Competing reactions that drain carbon flux away from polyamine synthesis are deleted or down-regulated to maximize product yields.  

The invention further provides methods for producing polyamines from bacterial sources:  

Escherichia coli WL3110 may be modified to increase ornithine and putrescine production by overexpressing ornithine decarboxylase and ornithine biosynthetic genes, then transformed with plasmids for overexpression of endogenous or heterologous speE and speD genes.  

Corynebacterium glutamicum strain ATCC 13032 may be engineered for increased ornithine production by overexpressing ornithine decarboxylase and deleting or disrupting the carbamoyl-transferase argF gene.  

The genetically modified eukaryotic cells of the invention exhibit attenuated activity of ornithine carbamoyltransferase while demonstrating enhanced L-ornithine biosynthesis from α-ketoglutarate. These cells overexpress at least one gene selected from the group consisting of genes encoding L-ornithine biosynthetic enzymes, transporters, and regulatory proteins.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Strain Construction of Ornithine-Overproducing Strains  

Engineered yeast strains were constructed using the MOPE (Modular Pathway Engineering) strategy and DNA assembler method. Plasmids were constructed by amplifying promoters, terminators, and genes from S. cerevisiae genomic DNA. Expression modules were assembled using a one-step PCR strategy and co-transformed into yeast strains. Assembled pathways were verified by PCR and restriction endonuclease digestion. The ARG3 promoter was replaced with weaker promoters (HXT1 or KEX2) to attenuate ornithine carbamoyltransferase activity. Selected genes were integrated into the chromosome of yeast background strains.  

### L-Arginine Leaky Auxotroph Enables L-Ornithine Overproduction  

The L-ornithine biosynthesis pathway and its regulation were engineered by replacing the native ARG3 promoter with weaker promoters (HXT1 or KEX2). Strains with the HXT1 promoter (M1a) produced 24 mg/L L-ornithine, while the KEX2 promoter strain (M1b) showed 76% higher titer (42 mg/L). Intracellular L-arginine levels decreased only ∼30% compared to control, indicating maintenance of essential L-arginine synthesis. Further deletion of CAR2 (encoding ornithine aminotransferase) in strain M1c resulted in a small increase to 45 mg/L L-ornithine.  

### Pathway Re-Localization and Subcellular Trafficking Engineering Elevates L-Ornithine Synthesis  

The compartmentalized L-ornithine biosynthesis pathway was engineered through three strategies:  

1) Enhancing mitochondrial biosynthesis by overexpressing ARG5,6, ARG7 and ARG8 (strain M1cM2f, 59 mg/L) and further overexpressing ARG2 (strain M1cM2g, 80 mg/L).  

2) Improving mitochondrial trafficking by overexpressing transporters ORT1 (strain M1cM2h, 115 mg/L) and AGC1 (strain M1cM2k, 149 mg/L), combined with cytosolic glutamate pathway overexpression (GDH1 in strain M1cM2l, 173 mg/L).  

3) Relocating the complete pathway to cytosol using bacterial genes (strain M1cM2q, 192 mg/L), demonstrating that heterologous cytosolic expression outperformed mitochondrial pathway engineering.  

### 'Crabtree Negative' S. cerevisiae Construction Enables Efficient Carbon Channeling to L-Ornithine  

To alleviate Crabtree effect limitations, three strategies were implemented:  

1) Overexpressing TCA cycle enzymes PDA1, PYC2, CIT1, ACO2 and IDP1 (strain M1cM2qM3a, 245 mg/L).  

2) Overexpressing alternative NADH oxidase HaAOX1 (strain M1cM2qM3c, 258 mg/L) and combined with NDI1 (strain M1cM2qM3d, 278 mg/L).  

3) Attenuating glucose uptake through MTH1-ΔT overexpression (strain M1cM2qM3e, 778 mg/L), which eliminated ethanol production while reducing glucose uptake rate.  

Fed-batch cultivation of strain M1cM2qM3a achieved 5.1 g/L L-ornithine with 56 g/L biomass, demonstrating scalability.  

### Urea Cycle Engineering Enables L-Ornithine Titre Improvement  

Overexpression of arginase (CAR1) in strain M1dM2qM3e increased L-ornithine titer to 1,041 mg/L (34% increase), representing a 23-fold improvement over the initial engineered strain. This modification enhanced the 'epi-arginase' regulatory complex formation between arginase and ornithine carbamoyltransferase, optimizing flux control.  

### Polyamine Production Engineering  

Putrescine production was achieved by:  
- Deleting OAZ1 (encoding ODC antizyme)  
- Overexpressing SPE1 (ornithine decarboxylase)  
- Overexpressing TPO1 (polyamine exporter)  

Spermidine production was enhanced by:  
- Overexpressing SPE2 (S-adenosylmethionine decarboxylase)  
- Overexpressing SPE3 (spermidine synthase)  

Spermine biosynthesis was implemented by:  
- Codon-optimizing and expressing CANSDH and CANSDC genes  
- Cloning into plasmid pYX212 and transforming into strain PUT-B(KanMX)  

Polyamine export was improved by:  
- Deleting AGP2, SAM3, and DUR3 (polyamine uptake genes)  
- Transforming with export-enhancing plasmids  

Agmatine production was achieved by:  
- Deleting CAR1 (arginase)  
- Overexpressing ARG3, ARG1, and ARG4 (arginine biosynthesis)  
- Introducing E. coli speA (arginine decarboxylase)  

### Bacterial Polyamine Production  

E. coli WL3110 was modified for ornithine and putrescine production by:  
- Overexpressing ornithine biosynthetic genes  
- Overexpressing speE and speD (spermidine synthase)  

C. glutamicum ATCC 13032 was engineered for spermidine production by:  
- Increasing ornithine production  
- Overexpressing ornithine decarboxylase  
- Deleting argF (carbamoyl-transferase)  

The examples demonstrate successful implementation of the invention's strategies for high-level L-ornithine and polyamine production in both eukaryotic and prokaryotic systems.
