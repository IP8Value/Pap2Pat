## DESCRIPTION  

### FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to methods for modulating insulin-independent glucose transport in mammalian cells, particularly skeletal muscle cells. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods utilizing Teneurin C-terminal Associated Peptide (TCAP) to increase glucose uptake and energy availability in skeletal muscle cells through novel mechanisms independent of insulin signaling pathways. The invention has applications in treating metabolic disorders such as diabetes, enhancing exercise performance, and improving recovery from muscle trauma.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Teneurins are a family of transmembrane proteins that play essential roles in central nervous system development and maintenance. Structurally, teneurins are type-II transmembrane proteins containing several functional domains that confer specialized actions on their extracellular and intracellular regions. The most distal extracellular region contains a β-barrel structure unique to metazoans, associated with an extended amino-acid chain termed the Teneurin C-terminal Associated Peptide (TCAP).  

TCAP exhibits primary structure similarity to peptides in the Secretin superfamily and has been shown to regulate various physiological processes. The teneurin/TCAP system interacts with latrophilins (LPHNs), which belong to the adhesion G protein-coupled receptor family. This system represents an ancient regulatory mechanism that modulates cell adhesion and metabolism, originating from prokaryotes through lateral gene transfer.  

Skeletal muscle is responsible for approximately 40% of glucose-associated energy requirements and 80% of glucose uptake under insulin-stimulated conditions. Impaired glucose metabolism in skeletal muscle is a hallmark of insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes, often manifesting as reduced muscle function and performance. Current treatments for diabetes focus on insulin-dependent mechanisms, leaving a significant unmet need for therapies that can bypass insulin resistance through alternative glucose transport pathways.  

Glycogen storage diseases represent another class of disorders where impaired glucose metabolism leads to muscle dysfunction. Furthermore, conditions involving muscle trauma or excessive exercise can benefit from enhanced glucose uptake and energy availability to support recovery and repair processes. The limitations of existing therapies highlight the need for novel approaches to modulate skeletal muscle glucose metabolism through insulin-independent mechanisms.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods for increasing energy available to skeletal muscle cells through administration of TCAP peptides. The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing isolated TCAP peptides, particularly TCAP-1, having specific amino acid sequences that modulate glucose uptake independently of insulin signaling.  

The carboxy terminal end of TCAP peptides mediates their biological activity in increasing glucose uptake through novel mechanisms involving calcium flux and mitochondrial activation. The invention demonstrates that TCAP administration enhances glucose transport into skeletal muscle cells, providing increased energy availability that is particularly advantageous in conditions of metabolic stress.  

Applications of the invention include enhancing exercise performance by increasing energy availability during physical activity, as well as preventing or treating diabetes through insulin-independent glucose uptake mechanisms. The invention specifically discloses the use of TCAP-1 for long-term modulation of energy metabolism in skeletal muscle, with demonstrated effects on glucose homeostasis and muscle function.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention establishes that TCAP peptides, particularly TCAP-1, play a fundamental role in regulating energy metabolism in mammalian skeletal muscle. TCAP exerts its effects through modulation of glucose uptake and utilization pathways that operate independently of insulin resistance mechanisms. Detailed studies demonstrate TCAP's ability to enhance glucose transport into muscle cells while simultaneously activating mitochondrial energy production systems.  

The teneurin/TCAP-latrophilin system represents an evolutionarily conserved mechanism for coordinating cellular metabolism with physiological demands. TCAP's effects on energy production and usage in animals include activation of calcium-dependent signaling pathways that stimulate glucose transporter translocation to cell membranes. These actions are particularly relevant for treating conditions involving muscle trauma, diabetes, and hypoglycemia, where conventional insulin-mediated pathways may be compromised.  

#### Definitions  

For purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:  

"Administering to the cell(s)" refers to delivering TCAP peptides to target cells through any suitable means, including but not limited to injection, oral delivery, transdermal application, or direct tissue application.  

"Effective amount" and "therapeutically effective amount" denote quantities of TCAP sufficient to produce a measurable increase in glucose uptake or energy availability in skeletal muscle cells without causing significant adverse effects.  

"Homeostasis" refers to the maintenance of stable internal conditions in skeletal muscle cells, particularly with respect to energy balance and glucose metabolism.  

"Insulin resistance" describes the impaired biological response to insulin that characterizes type 2 diabetes and metabolic syndrome.  

"Muscle fatigue" indicates the decline in muscle performance resulting from prolonged activity or metabolic insufficiency.  

"Muscle trauma" encompasses any injury or damage to skeletal muscle tissue requiring repair and regeneration processes.  

"Neuronal cells" are nerve cells that may interact with skeletal muscle through neuromuscular junctions and shared metabolic pathways.  

"Obvious chemical equivalents" include modified forms of TCAP peptides that retain biological activity while potentially offering improved stability or delivery characteristics.  

"Pharmaceutically acceptable carrier" refers to any non-toxic vehicle suitable for delivering TCAP peptides to target tissues while maintaining peptide stability and bioavailability.  

"Skeletal muscle cells" are striated muscle fibers responsible for voluntary movement and major contributors to whole-body glucose metabolism.  

"TCAP" and its variants encompass naturally occurring and synthetic forms of Teneurin C-terminal Associated Peptides, including sequence modifications that preserve or enhance biological activity.  

#### Applications: The Use of TCAP to Increase Energy Available to Skeletal Muscle Cells  

The invention provides novel applications of TCAP peptides to modulate skeletal muscle energy metabolism. The importance of glucose uptake in skeletal muscle cells is well-established, with TCAP offering a unique mechanism to enhance transport independent of insulin signaling pathways.  

TCAP administration counteracts the detrimental effects of insulin resistance on skeletal muscle cells by activating alternative glucose uptake mechanisms. This property makes TCAP particularly valuable for enhancing exercise performance, where increased glucose availability can delay fatigue and improve endurance.  

Therapeutic applications include treatment of both type 1 and type 2 diabetes through mechanisms that bypass insulin resistance. TCAP also promotes recovery from muscle trauma or exercise-induced injury by providing increased energy substrates for repair processes. Additional applications extend to treating glycogen storage disorders and hypoxia conditions where conventional energy pathways are compromised.  

Detailed studies demonstrate TCAP's effects on glucose uptake in skeletal muscle cells through calcium-mediated signaling and mitochondrial activation. These actions collectively increase energy availability to muscle cells, with broad implications for metabolic regulation and physical performance.  

#### Pharmaceutical Compositions and Modes of Administration  

The invention encompasses pharmaceutical compositions containing TCAP peptides formulated for optimal delivery to skeletal muscle tissues. Administration methods are designed to ensure TCAP reaches its sites of action while maintaining peptide stability and bioavailability.  

Formulations include injectable preparations, oral delivery systems with protective coatings, inhalation formulations, transdermal patches, and rectal administration vehicles. Protective coatings may be employed to shield TCAP from enzymatic degradation or acidic environments in the gastrointestinal tract.  

Direct administration to target tissues represents an effective delivery strategy, particularly for localized effects. Alternative approaches include using nucleic acids encoding TCAP to enable endogenous peptide production in target cells. Viral vectors and other carrier systems may be employed to enhance TCAP delivery and tissue specificity.  

Pharmaceutical compositions are prepared using established methods adapted for peptide stability, with inactive viral carriers representing one potential delivery platform. The invention provides multiple administration routes to accommodate diverse therapeutic needs and patient populations.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1—Peptide Synthesis  

Mouse TCAP-1 peptide was synthesized based on the determined sequence of mouse TCAP-1. The synthesis process involved creating a mouse paralogue of the putative peptide sequence using an automated peptide synthesizer operating with continuous flow Fmoc chemistry.  

Diisopropyl ethyl amine and Fmoc-amino acid were coupled in sequential reactions, with deprotection achieved using piperidine. The final peptide underwent cleavage and deprotection procedures before desalting and lyophilization.  

Additional synthetic products included rat K37-OT K8-TCAP-1 and biotinylated K37-TCAP-1, which underwent purification processes to ensure product integrity. A scrambled TCAP control peptide was produced for comparative studies.  

Antisera against TCAP-1 were generated by conjugating peptides to keyhole limpet hemocyanin (KLH) and immunizing rabbits with the conjugated products. Collected antisera were assessed for binding specificity to confirm reagent quality.  

FITC conjugation to TCAP-1 produced labeled peptides that were purified and characterized for subsequent uptake studies. Investigations of TCAP-1 uptake in plasma involved intravenous or subcutaneous administration followed by blood sample collection and analysis.  

The peptide synthesis process included autoradiography procedures to track labeled compounds. Functional positron emission tomography studies were conducted to visualize TCAP-1 distribution and activity. PET image acquisition and analysis protocols were established to quantify results.  

Molecular studies included RT-PCR RNA extraction from target tissues, followed by reverse transcription and PCR amplification. Signal transduction studies focused on MEK-ERK-AMPK phosphorylation pathways, with additional investigations of DAG and IP3 production.  

Analytical methods included BCA assays for protein quantification, ELISAs for specific protein detection, DAG assays, IP3 assays, and Fluo-4 fluorescence measurements. Functional PET studies incorporated standardized animal preparation, injection procedures, image acquisition, and analysis protocols.  

Toxicity assessments were performed to establish safety profiles, with RNA extraction and reverse transcription PCR used to evaluate cellular responses. Gel electrophoresis and western blot analysis provided protein detection and quantification capabilities.
