# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Chlamydia trachomatis is an obligate intracellular bacterial pathogen responsible for significant sexually transmitted infections (STIs) worldwide. The World Health Organization estimates over 90 million new cases occur annually, with severe consequences including pelvic inflammatory disease, ectopic pregnancy, and infertility. Current treatment relies on antibiotic administration, but rising resistance and high reinfection rates highlight the urgent need for effective vaccines.  

Resolution of Chlamydia infection depends critically on CD4+ T-cell mediated immunity, particularly Th1-type responses characterized by interferon-gamma (IFNγ) production. While antibody responses occur, they provide limited protection compared to cell-mediated immunity. The major outer membrane protein (MOMP) has emerged as a leading vaccine candidate due to its surface exposure and conserved immunogenic epitopes. However, traditional parenteral vaccination fails to induce robust mucosal immunity at genital tract surfaces where protection is needed.  

A critical barrier has been the lack of adjuvants capable of stimulating cell-mediated immunity when delivered to mucosal surfaces. Nasal administration offers distinct advantages by targeting nasal-associated lymphoid tissue (NALT), which provides interconnected immunity to distant mucosal sites including the reproductive tract. The hostile mucosal environment necessitates protective delivery systems to preserve antigen integrity and facilitate uptake by dendritic cells.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for treating or preventing Chlamydial infections through novel vaccine formulations. The invention utilizes recombinant vault-like particles as carriers for immunogenic peptides, particularly fragments of MOMP. These pharmaceutical compositions enable effective nasal mucosal immunization while protecting antigen integrity.  

The methods involve administering vault nanoparticles containing Chlamydia antigens to induce protective cell-mediated and antibody responses. The vault structure facilitates dendritic cell uptake and antigen presentation while minimizing inflammatory pathology. Compositions may include homologous proteins from related Chlamydia species and can be combined with additional immunostimulatory agents.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The invention relates to Chlamydia vaccines based on vault-like particles serving as antigen carriers. Vaults are naturally occurring ribonucleoprotein complexes with a hollow barrel structure approximately 400Å wide and 700Å long. The vault structure comprises multiple copies of three proteins: major vault protein (MVP), vault poly-ADP ribose polymerase (VPARP), and telomerase-associated protein 1 (TEP1), along with small untranslated vault RNA (vRNA).  

Poly ADP-ribosylation is a post-translational modification catalyzed by VPARP that may contribute to vault stability and function. The invention utilizes recombinant vault-like particles that maintain the structural features of native vaults while enabling precise modifications. MVP alone can self-assemble into vault-like particles when expressed in insect cells using baculovirus vectors.  

An embodiment of the invention comprises vault-like particles containing immunogenic peptides from Chlamydia MOMP. The MOMP fragments may include conserved epitopes that stimulate protective T-cell responses. Homologous proteins from related Chlamydia species (e.g., C. muridarum, C. pneumoniae) may also be incorporated. The vaccine compositions are designed for nasal mucosal administration to target NALT and generate genital tract immunity.  

The invention describes methods for inducing cell-mediated immune responses through vault particle internalization by dendritic cells. The vault size (similar to small microbes) facilitates phagocytosis, while the hollow interior protects encapsulated antigens. The particles induce Th1-polarized responses characterized by IFNγ production without excessive inflammation. Antibody responses against the carried antigens develop concurrently, providing humoral immunity.  

Subjects for treatment include humans and other mammals susceptible to Chlamydia infection. The vaccines may prevent initial infection or reduce disease severity in infected individuals. Immunization methods involve nasal administration of vault particles in pharmaceutically acceptable carriers. Dosages typically range from 50-500 μg of vault protein administered in multiple doses.  

The invention provides methods for modifying vault components to enhance immunogenicity. MVP may be engineered with N-terminal or C-terminal additions including: immunogenic peptides, receptor-binding domains (e.g., Fc regions), polynucleotide-binding domains, or sensor proteins. VPARP modifications may include addition of peptide tags (e.g., GAL4, MS2) or reporter proteins (e.g., luciferase).  

Vault-like particles can be produced by expressing modified MVP, VPARP, or TEP1 in insect cells using baculovirus vectors. The particles self-assemble and can be purified by density gradient centrifugation. Antigens are incorporated either by fusion to vault-targeting domains or passive loading into the internal cavity.  

The invention further provides kits containing vault vaccine compositions with instructions for immunization. Additional agents may be co-administered including cytokines, TLR agonists, or other adjuvants to modulate immune responses. The vaccines reduce infertility risk by preventing Chlamydia-induced fallopian tube damage through early pathogen clearance.  

Key definitions: MVP refers to major vault protein from any species including Rattus norvegicus or Homo sapiens. VPARP indicates vault poly-ADP ribose polymerase. TEP1 is telomerase-associated protein 1. vRNA denotes vault RNA. Vault-like particles are recombinant structures resembling native vaults, which may lack some natural components.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Chlamydia-vault-like particles (CVLPs) were prepared by expressing a MOMP-INT fusion construct in Sf9 insect cells. The 366 amino acid MOMP sequence (minus signal peptide) was fused to the minimal interaction domain (mINT) of VPARP (aa 1563-1724) via PCR ligation. Rat MVP with a C-terminal cysteine-rich tag (cp-MVP-z) was co-expressed to form vault particles.  

Purified vault-like particles were analyzed by Western blot using anti-MOMP antibodies, confirming proper MOMP incorporation. Negative stain electron microscopy showed intact vault structures comparable to native vaults. The Fc-binding "z" tag on cp-MVP-z facilitated IgG binding, enhancing dendritic cell uptake through Fc receptor-mediated endocytosis.  

### Example 2  

Mice were immunized intranasally with CVLPs (200 μg/dose) at 2-week intervals. Following genital challenge with C. muridarum, vaccinated mice showed significantly reduced bacterial burden compared to controls. Flow cytometry revealed increased IFNγ+IL-4-CD4+ T cells in iliac lymph nodes, indicating Th1 polarization.  

Antibody analysis demonstrated MOMP-specific IgG with a Th1-associated IgG2c bias. The IgG2c:IgG1 ratio correlated with protection. Notably, CVLP immunization induced earlier pathogen clearance than natural infection, suggesting superior mucosal immunity. Reduced IL-17 responses indicated minimized immunopathology.  

### Example 3  

Vault particle uptake was evaluated using bone marrow-derived dendritic cells (BMDCs). Fluorescently labeled vaults showed rapid internalization, with localization to cytoplasmic compartments. Vault-containing BMDCs secreted IL-12p70 and CXCL10 but not pro-inflammatory TNFα or IL-6, confirming Th1-polarized activation without excessive inflammation.  

### Example 4  

Immunization with MOMP-containing vaults induced serum antibodies recognizing both MOMP and MVP, while control vaults did not break tolerance to MVP. This unique property may serve as a vaccine efficacy marker. The vault platform demonstrated advantages over traditional adjuvants by combining mucosal delivery with controlled immunostimulation.  

The complete specification demonstrates that vault nanoparticles containing Chlamydia antigens represent a novel vaccine platform for inducing protective mucosal immunity while minimizing inflammatory pathology. The invention provides methods for preventing and treating Chlamydia infections through targeted immune stimulation.
