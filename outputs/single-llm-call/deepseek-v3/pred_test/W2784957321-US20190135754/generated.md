# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The construction of enantioenriched quaternary stereocenters represents a significant challenge in synthetic organic chemistry due to the steric congestion surrounding such carbon centers. Transition metal-catalyzed allylic alkylation has emerged as one of the most powerful methods for constructing these stereocenters, particularly when employing prochiral nucleophiles that proceed through tetrasubstituted enolates. While this transformation has been studied for over fifty years, the use of α-substituted lactones or lactams as prochiral nucleophiles remains significantly underdeveloped.  

Recent advances in palladium-catalyzed decarboxylative enantioselective allylic alkylation of enol carbonates derived from γ-butyrolactones have demonstrated moderate to high levels of enantioselectivity. However, these methods suffer from limited electrophile scope and challenging nucleophile synthesis, which restricts their practical application. There exists a pressing need for new reaction protocols that utilize inexpensive transition metal catalysts with easily accessible prochiral nucleophiles to construct these valuable chiral building blocks.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a nickel-catalyzed allylic alkylation method for preparing compounds of Formula (IX). The method comprises treating a compound of Formula (I) with a compound of Formula (IIa) or (IIb) in the presence of a nickel catalyst and a chiral ligand L in an organic solvent. The nickel catalyst is preferably a Ni(0) source, and the chiral ligand L is selected from bidentate phosphine ligands, with (R)-P-phos being particularly preferred.  

The invention further describes compounds of Formula (XI) prepared by treating a compound of Formula (XII) with a Ni(0) source and ligand L. The methods enable the preparation of pharmaceutical agents through subsequent synthetic transformations of the compounds of Formula (IX) or (XI).  

Additional embodiments include compounds of Formula (VI), Formula (VII), and Formula (VIII), which are valuable synthetic intermediates derived from the nickel-catalyzed allylic alkylation products. These compounds feature enantioenriched quaternary stereocenters and multiple functional handles for further synthetic manipulations.

### Overview

### Exemplary Methods  

The invention provides methods for preparing a compound of Formula (IX) by treating a compound of Formula (I) with a compound of Formula (IIa) or (IIb) in the presence of a nickel catalyst and chiral ligand L in an organic solvent. The nickel catalyst is preferably a Ni(0) source such as Ni(COD)2, and the chiral ligand L is preferably a bidentate phosphine ligand.  

In Formula (I), X represents O or NR3, where R3 is selected from hydrogen, alkyl, aryl, or acyl groups. R1 represents an ester group, preferably —CO2R where R is alkyl. R2 represents hydrogen or alkyl. The organic solvent is preferably diethyl ether, and the reaction is conducted at temperatures between -10°C to 25°C.  

For preparing compounds of Formula (XI), the method comprises treating a compound of Formula (XII) with a Ni(0) source and ligand L in an organic solvent. The nickel source is preferably Ni(COD)2 at 5-10 mol% loading, and the solvent is preferably diethyl ether.  

The invention further provides methods for preparing pharmaceutical agents by synthesizing a compound of Formula (IX) or (XI) followed by one or more synthetic transformations. Additional methods include preparing a compound of Formula (VI) by treating a compound of Formula (III) with a reducing agent such as lithium aluminium hydride.  

### Transition Metal Catalysts  

The methods employ transition metal catalysts, particularly nickel catalysts prepared by combining a nickel source with a chiral ligand. Preferred nickel sources include Ni(COD)2, with catalyst loadings ranging from 0.01 mol% to 10 mol%. More preferably, the catalyst loading is 5-10 mol%.  

Specific embodiments utilize Ni(COD)2 at 10 mol% loading with (R)-P-phos as the chiral ligand. The nickel source and ligand are typically combined in situ immediately before the reaction.  

### Chiral Ligands  

The chiral ligands provide the enantioselectivity in the methods by creating an asymmetric environment around the metal center. The ligands complex to the nickel metal and influence the orientation of reacting molecules to control product stereochemistry.  

Preferred ligands are bidentate phosphines, particularly substantially enantiopure phosphorous-based ligands. Bisphosphine ligands are most preferred, with (R)-P-phos demonstrating superior performance. The ligand loading typically ranges from 1-20 mol%, preferably 12 mol%.  

The methods produce compounds with high enantioenrichment, typically 70-90% ee as measured by chiral HPLC or NMR analysis. The enantioenrichment applies to compounds of Formula (IX), (VI), (VII), (VIII), and (XI).

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1: Nickel-Catalyzed Enantioselective Allylic Alkylation of Lactones and Lactams with Unactivated Allylic Alcohols  

The nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation was investigated using α-ethoxycarbonyl lactone 1a and allyl alcohol (2a) with Ni(COD)2 and (R)-BINAP in diethyl ether at 0°C. The product 3aa was obtained in good yield with moderate enantioselectivity.  

Screening of commercially available ligand scaffolds identified chiral bisphosphine ligands as superior. Optimization revealed that (R)-P-phos (L4) delivered product 3aa in 82% yield and 82% ee at 10 mol% catalyst loading in Et2O. Lower temperatures improved ee to 85% at -10°C.  

The reaction scope was examined with various lactones and lactams. α-Methoxycarbonyl lactone 1b provided product 3ba in comparable yield and ee. Bicyclic lactone 1c gave product 3ca in slightly lower yield. Various substituted allyl alcohols reacted with good ee (78-90% ee) at 10°C.  

Electron-rich aryl substituents gave higher yields than electron-deficient ones. Heteroaryl and aliphatic electrophiles were also tolerated. Lactam substrates 4a-4b provided products 5aa-5ba in good yields with higher ee (up to 90% ee) than lactone counterparts.  

Product derivatizations demonstrated synthetic utility. Reduction of lactone 3aa gave diol 6 (88% yield). Vinyl Grignard addition to 3aa afforded enone 7 (67% yield). Reduction of lactam 5aa provided piperidine 9. Wacker oxidation of 5aa gave aldehyde 10 (75% yield).
