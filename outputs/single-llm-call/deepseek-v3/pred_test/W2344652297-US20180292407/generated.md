# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of monoclonal antibody production. More particularly, it concerns an improved method for isolating antibody-producing cells and generating monoclonal antibodies with high specificity, especially against post-translationally modified proteins. The invention provides novel procedures for fixing, staining, and isolating cells capable of producing monoclonal antibodies, followed by preparation of cDNA and monoclonal antibodies from the isolated cells.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Antibodies have become indispensable tools in biomedical research, diagnostics, and therapeutics due to their high specificity and affinity for target molecules. Monoclonal antibodies, in particular, offer uniform binding characteristics and unlimited production potential. Traditional methods for monoclonal antibody production involve immunization of animals, fusion of antibody-producing cells with myeloma cells to create hybridomas, and subsequent screening of antibody-producing clones.  

However, existing methods face several limitations. The need for novel antibody production platforms has become increasingly apparent, particularly for generating antibodies against post-translationally modified proteins such as phosphorylated proteins. Prior art methods struggle to isolate rare antigen-specific plasma cells (ASPCs) from immunized animals, especially when targeting specific protein modifications. Conventional techniques often fail to maintain RNA integrity during cell processing, leading to difficulties in amplifying immunoglobulin genes from isolated cells.  

Several references in the prior art describe methods for antibody production. US Patent No. 5,627,052 describes a method for producing monoclonal antibodies using hybridoma technology. US Patent No. 7,429,487 discloses a method for isolating antigen-specific B cells. However, these methods do not adequately address the challenges of maintaining RNA integrity during cell processing or efficiently isolating rare ASPCs.  

The limitations of prior art methods include: inability to preserve mRNA during cell processing, low efficiency in isolating rare antigen-specific cells, difficulty in distinguishing between antibodies recognizing modified versus unmodified forms of proteins, and challenges in amplifying full-length immunoglobulin genes from processed cells. These limitations underscore the need for an improved method of monoclonal antibody production.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

The present invention overcomes limitations of prior art methods by providing a novel procedure for isolating cells capable of producing monoclonal antibodies and subsequently preparing monoclonal antibodies from the isolated cells. The invention addresses key problems in the field by introducing a fixing step that preserves both antibodies and mRNA, followed by specialized processing steps that enable efficient isolation of rare antigen-specific cells while maintaining RNA integrity.  

The novel method comprises several key steps: (1) a fixing step using a reversible crosslinking reagent to preserve both antibodies and mRNA; (2) a cell membrane lysis step to expose intracellular immunoglobulins; (3) a staining step using labeled antigens of interest; (4) a cell isolation step to separate single antibody-producing cells; and (5) preparation of monoclonal antibodies from the isolated cells. Optional steps include decrosslinking treatment, heating treatment, and reduction treatment to facilitate RNA extraction and cDNA preparation.  

Key advantages of the present invention include: ability to isolate rare antigen-specific plasma cells with high purity, maintenance of RNA integrity throughout the process, efficient amplification of full-length immunoglobulin genes from fixed cells, and generation of highly specific monoclonal antibodies, particularly against post-translationally modified proteins. The method is particularly suited for producing phosphorylation site-specific monoclonal antibodies that do not cross-react with unmodified proteins.  

### Solution to Problem  

The present invention solves the aforementioned problems by focusing on antibody-producing cells and developing a novel procedure that maintains both antibody binding capacity and mRNA integrity throughout the isolation process. The method assumes signal strength of labeled antigen binding to intracellular immunoglobulins and addresses the difficulties of prior art methods through several innovative steps.  

The procedure begins with immunization of a non-human animal with an antigen of interest, followed by collection of lymph nodes or similar tissues. The cells undergo a fixing step using a reversible crosslinking reagent, which preserves both antibodies and mRNA while allowing subsequent decrosslinking. This is followed by a cell membrane lysis step using detergents to expose intracellular immunoglobulins while maintaining cellular structure.  

A staining step then reacts the cell population with labeled antigen of interest, allowing identification of antigen-specific cells. The method includes rigorous measures to suppress mRNA degradation, including use of RNase inhibitors throughout the process. Cell isolation is performed using a cell sorter to separate single antibody-producing cells based on their binding to labeled antigen.  

Following isolation, the method includes preparation of cDNA from mRNA extracted from the fixed cells. Special treatments such as decrosslinking, heating, and reduction facilitate RNA extraction and cDNA synthesis. Finally, monoclonal antibodies are prepared from the isolated cells using well-known methods, with the option to prepare antibody fragments as needed.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENT  

The present invention provides a method for isolating a cell capable of producing a monoclonal antibody and a method for producing a monoclonal antibody. The method comprises several sequential steps designed to preserve cell integrity, maintain RNA quality, and enable efficient isolation of antigen-specific antibody-producing cells.  

### (1) Fixing Step  

The fixing step represents a critical component of the invention, serving to preserve both antibodies and mRNA within the cells while maintaining antigen-binding capacity. The step begins with immunization of a non-human animal with an antigen of interest. Non-human animals suitable for this method include, but are not limited to, guinea pigs, rats, rabbits, and mice. The antigen of interest may be any molecule for which monoclonal antibodies are desired, particularly peptides, proteins, or modified proteins such as phosphorylated peptides.  

Immunization is performed by administering the antigen to the animal, typically with an adjuvant, through routes such as intramuscular or subcutaneous injection. Establishment of immunity is verified through methods such as serum antibody titer measurement. Following successful immunization, lymph nodes or similar tissues are collected from the animal, and lymph fluid, lymph tissue, blood cell samples, or bone marrow are prepared for processing.  

The collected cells undergo a cell fixing treatment with a crosslinking reagent. The use of a reversible crosslinking reagent is essential, as it allows subsequent decrosslinking of mRNA for cDNA preparation. Suitable reversible crosslinking reagents include paraformaldehyde (PFA), with concentrations typically ranging from 1% to 4%. The fixing treatment is performed under controlled conditions, typically at 4°C for 8-30 minutes, to achieve optimal fixation while preserving RNA integrity.  

After fixation, the fixed cell population is separated from the solution by centrifugation or similar methods. The fixing step serves multiple purposes: it preserves the three-dimensional structure of antibodies within the cells, maintains mRNA in a stable state, and inactivates cellular RNases that could degrade RNA. Importantly, the reversible nature of the crosslinking allows subsequent decrosslinking of mRNA for cDNA synthesis.  

Optimal fixation conditions may be adjusted based on the specific cell type and antigen being targeted. Factors such as fixation time, temperature, and reagent concentration can be modified to achieve the desired balance between antibody preservation and RNA accessibility. Good fixation conditions are characterized by maintained antigen-binding capacity of intracellular immunoglobulins and preservation of mRNA that can be subsequently decrosslinked and amplified.  

### (2) Cell Membrane Lysis Step  

Following fixation, the cells undergo a cell membrane lysis step to expose intracellular immunoglobulins while maintaining overall cellular structure. This step is crucial for allowing labeled antigens access to intracellular antibodies while preserving mRNA within the cell.  

The lysis step employs detergents to selectively permeabilize cell membranes without complete cell disruption. Suitable detergents include Triton X-100, NP-40, and saponin, typically used at concentrations ranging from 0.1% to 1.0%. The detergent concentration is carefully controlled to achieve sufficient permeabilization for antigen access while maintaining cellular integrity for subsequent sorting steps.  

Treatment conditions for lysis are optimized to balance membrane permeabilization with preservation of intracellular components. Typical conditions include incubation on ice for 10-30 minutes with gentle agitation. The lysed cell population is then ready for the staining step, having exposed intracellular immunoglobulins while maintaining mRNA in a protected state.  

This step represents a significant improvement over prior art methods, as it allows specific labeling of intracellular antibodies while maintaining RNA integrity. The controlled permeabilization enables subsequent isolation of cells based on intracellular antibody binding while preserving mRNA for immunoglobulin gene amplification.  

### (3) Staining Step  

The staining step involves reacting the fixed and permeabilized cell population with labeled antigen of interest to identify cells producing antibodies specific to the target antigen. This step is performed under conditions that maintain RNA integrity while allowing specific antigen-antibody interactions.  

The labeled antigen is typically conjugated to a fluorescent dye such as DyLight 488 or similar detectable markers. The staining solution may also include secondary antibodies labeled with different fluorophores to enhance detection specificity. To maintain RNA integrity, the staining solution includes RNase inhibitors at appropriate concentrations.  

Treatment conditions for the staining reaction are carefully controlled to achieve specific binding while minimizing non-specific interactions. Typical conditions include incubation on ice for 15-30 minutes with protection from light. For phosphorylation site-specific antibody production, the staining solution may include both modified and unmodified forms of the antigen, allowing subtraction of cells binding to the unmodified form.  

The staining step enables identification and subsequent isolation of rare antigen-specific plasma cells with high specificity. By targeting intracellular immunoglobulins, the method overcomes limitations of prior art methods that rely on surface immunoglobulin expression, which is often low in antibody-secreting cells.  

### (4) Cell Isolation Step  

The cell isolation step identifies and separates single cells that have reacted with the labeled antigen of interest. This step is performed using a cell sorter capable of single-cell deposition, such as a fluorescence-activated cell sorter (FACS).  

Cells are identified based on their binding to labeled antigen, typically displaying high fluorescence intensity in the relevant channel. Single antibody-producing cells are selected and separated into individual wells of a plate for subsequent processing. The method allows isolation of cells producing antibodies with expected binding ability to the antigen of interest, potentially yielding multiple different monoclonal antibodies from a single experiment.  

To enhance specificity, the isolation step may include measures to eliminate nonspecifically stained cells. This can involve use of labeled antigen of no interest as a negative selector, allowing exclusion of cells that bind nonspecifically. For phosphorylation site-specific antibodies, cells binding to unmodified peptide are actively excluded during sorting.  

Throughout the isolation step, RNase inhibitors are maintained in all solutions to suppress degradation of mRNA. Suitable RNase inhibitors include RNaseOUT, typically used at concentrations of 400 units per reaction. Additional precautions include cleaning of the FACS sample line with RNase-decontaminating solutions and use of RNase-free reagents.  

The cell isolation step yields single antibody-producing cells ready for mRNA extraction and cDNA preparation. The high purity of isolated cells facilitates subsequent amplification of immunoglobulin genes and production of monoclonal antibodies with desired specificity.  

### (6) Antigen Specific Monoclonal Antibody Preparing Step  

The final step involves preparation of antigen-specific monoclonal antibodies from the isolated cells. This begins with preparation of cDNA from mRNA extracted from the fixed cells. The fixed cells undergo decrosslinking treatment, typically using Proteinase K at 50°C for 1 hour, to reverse formaldehyde-induced crosslinks and make mRNA accessible for reverse transcription.  

From the prepared cDNA, immunoglobulin heavy and light chain variable region genes are amplified using techniques such as 5' RACE PCR. The amplified V genes are then cloned into expression vectors and transfected into host cells for antibody production. Well-known methods for recombinant antibody production may be employed, including mammalian expression systems such as HEK293 cells.  

The method allows preparation of both full-length antibodies and antibody fragments as needed. Antibody genes may be further manipulated to produce fragments such as Fabs or scFvs through standard molecular biology techniques. The specificity of produced antibodies is confirmed through techniques such as ELISA, western blotting, or immunofluorescence.  

Throughout this step, measures are maintained to suppress degradation of mRNA, including continued use of RNase inhibitors in all solutions. The resulting monoclonal antibodies exhibit high specificity for the target antigen, particularly when targeting post-translational modifications such as protein phosphorylation.  

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the present invention but should not be construed as limiting the scope of the invention.  

### Example 1  

OKT10 hybridoma cells producing antibody against CD38 were fixed with 2% PFA for 8 minutes at 4°C. After fixation, cells were permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 and stained intracellularly with DyLight 488-labeled CD38 and anti-mouse IgG. Fixed and stained cells were analyzed by FACS, and single hybridoma cells were isolated based on CD38 binding and anti-mouse IgG staining.  

Isolated single cells were treated with Proteinase K at 50°C for 1 hour to reverse crosslinks, followed by mRNA extraction and cDNA synthesis. Immunoglobulin heavy chain variable (VH) genes were amplified by 5' RACE PCR with an 82% success rate. Amplified V genes were cloned into expression vectors and transfected into 293 cells for antibody production. The resulting monoclonal antibodies showed specific binding to CD38 by ELISA.  

### Example 2  

OKT10 hybridoma cells were mixed with Jurkat cells at ratios from 1:100 to 1:10,000 to simulate rare antigen-specific plasma cells. The mixed cells were fixed with PFA and stained intracellularly with anti-mouse IgG, DAPI, and DsRed (to label damaged cells). FACS analysis identified a discrete population of anti-mouse IgGhigh, DsRednegative cells (R3 gate) clearly distinguishable from Jurkat cells.  

Single-cell isolation of the R3-gated population followed by 5'RACE PCR resulted in VH gene amplification with 87% success for 1:100 mixtures and 47% success for 1:10,000 mixtures. This demonstrated the method's ability to isolate rare antibody-producing cells with high efficiency.  

### Example 3  

Guinea pigs were immunized with a triphospho-p53 peptide (phosphorylated at S15, T18, and S20). Lymph node cells were fixed with PFA and stained intracellularly with anti-guinea pig IgG, triphospho-p53-DyLight 488 (TP-p53), and unmodified-p53-DyLight 550 (UM-p53). FACS identified phospho-p53-specific plasma cells as anti-guinea pig IgGhigh, TP-p53high, UM-p53negative (R4 gate), representing <0.01% of lymphocytes.  

Single R4-gated cells were isolated, and VH/VL gene pairs were amplified with 82% success (79/96). Gene pairs were transfected into 293FT cells, producing mAbs that reacted with triphospho-p53 peptide in 96% (47/48) of cases. One clone (#23) specifically recognized p53 phosphorylated at T18, with KD=0.2 nM, showing no cross-reactivity with unmodified p53 or other phospho-sites by peptide competition assays.  

### Comparative Example 1 (Method in NPL 4)  

The same guinea pig lymph node cells as in Example 3 were stained using a prior art method (ERIAA) with ER-tracker, TP-p53, UM-p53, and anti-guinea pig IgG. Phospho-p53-specific plasma cells appeared as a less discrete population (ER-trackerhigh, TP-p53medium, UM-p53negative, anti-guinea pig IgGmedium).  

Single-cell isolation and 5'RACE PCR showed only 34% success for paired V gene amplification, with just 16% of expressed mAbs reacting with triphospho-p53 peptide. This demonstrated the superior sensitivity and specificity of the present invention compared to prior methods.  

### Example 3 (Continued)  

Clone #23 was further characterized for specificity to pT18-p53. Immunofluorescence showed nuclear staining in etoposide-treated 293FT cells that was abolished by λ-phosphatase treatment. No signal was seen in Saos-2 cells expressing T18A p53 mutant. Western blot detected a single band comigrating with pan-p53 antibody, eliminated by λ-phosphatase treatment. Preincubation with pT18 peptide abolished immunoreactivity, confirming specificity.  

### Example 4  

Guinea pigs were immunized with pT68-CHK2 peptide. Lymph node cells were fixed, stained with anti-guinea pig IgG, pT68-CHK2-DyLight 488, and unmodified-CHK2-DyLight 550, and pT68-CHK2-specific plasma cells were isolated by FACS. VH/VL genes were amplified, and recombinant antibodies were produced.  

One clone (#34) specifically detected CHK2 phosphorylated at T68 with KD=0.08 nM, demonstrating the method's reproducibility for producing phosphorylation site-specific mAbs.
