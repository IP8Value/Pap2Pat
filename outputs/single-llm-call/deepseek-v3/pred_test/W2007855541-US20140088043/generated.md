# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to omega-3 fatty acid derivatives, particularly those derived from marine phospholipids (PLs). More specifically, the invention encompasses compositions comprising omega-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) bound to phospholipids, including phosphatidylcholine (PC), phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), and other glycerophospholipids, as well as their use in nutritional, pharmaceutical, and therapeutic applications. The invention further includes methods for administering these compositions to treat or prevent disorders associated with lipid metabolism, inflammation, cognitive decline, and cardiovascular disease.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Omega-3 fatty acids have been extensively studied for their health benefits, particularly in cardiovascular and neurological health. Epidemiological studies have demonstrated that populations with high dietary intake of marine-derived omega-3 fatty acids, such as eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA), exhibit lower incidences of coronary heart disease (CHD), inflammatory disorders, and cognitive impairments. The American Heart Association recommends regular consumption of omega-3 fatty acids to maintain cardiovascular health, particularly for individuals with elevated triglyceride levels.  

Despite these benefits, conventional omega-3 supplements, such as fish oil and Lovaza (a prescription omega-3 ethyl ester formulation), exhibit limitations in bioavailability and tolerability. Many individuals experience gastrointestinal side effects, including nausea and fishy aftertaste, which reduce compliance. Additionally, the esterified forms of omega-3 fatty acids in these supplements require enzymatic hydrolysis for absorption, leading to variable efficacy.  

There remains a need for improved omega-3 formulations that enhance bioavailability, reduce adverse effects, and provide targeted delivery to tissues such as the brain, liver, and cardiovascular system. The present invention addresses these limitations by utilizing phospholipid-bound omega-3 fatty acids, which exhibit superior absorption and tissue distribution compared to triglyceride (TG) or ethyl ester forms.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel omega-3 phospholipid compositions comprising marine-derived phospholipids bound to EPA, DHA, and other long-chain PUFAs. These compositions exhibit improved bioavailability, reduced gastrointestinal side effects, and enhanced tissue-specific delivery compared to conventional omega-3 supplements.  

The phospholipid structure of the invention includes a glycerol backbone esterified with two fatty acid moieties, at least one of which is an omega-3 fatty acid, and a phosphorous-containing headgroup, such as choline, ethanolamine, or serine. The omega-3 fatty acid derivatives may be derived from natural sources, including krill oil, fish roe, and marine by-products, or synthesized through chemical or enzymatic processes.  

Specific embodiments of the invention include:  
- Phospholipid compounds wherein the omega-3 fatty acid moiety is EPA, DHA, or docosapentaenoic acid (DPA).  
- Ether phospholipids and non-ether phospholipids with varying degrees of saturation.  
- Compositions further comprising antioxidants, such as astaxanthin, to enhance stability.  
- Formulations for oral, intravenous, or topical administration, including enteric-coated capsules, emulsions, and liposomal delivery systems.  

The invention also encompasses methods for treating or preventing disorders associated with dysregulated lipid metabolism, inflammation, or neurological dysfunction, including cardiovascular disease, Alzheimer’s disease, and metabolic syndrome.  

## DEFINITIONS  

- **Phospholipid**: A lipid consisting of a glycerol backbone esterified with two fatty acids and a phosphate-containing headgroup.  
- **Ether phospholipid**: A phospholipid wherein one or both fatty acids are linked to the glycerol backbone via an ether bond.  
- **Non-ether phospholipid**: A phospholipid wherein fatty acids are linked to the glycerol backbone via ester bonds.  
- **Long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acid (LC-PUFA)**: A fatty acid with 20 or more carbon atoms and two or more double bonds.  
- **Omega-3 fatty acid**: A polyunsaturated fatty acid with the first double bond at the third carbon from the methyl end.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

### Omega-3 Fatty Acid Derivatives  

The invention provides omega-3 fatty acid derivatives bound to phospholipids, which exhibit superior bioavailability and tissue distribution compared to triglyceride or ethyl ester forms. These derivatives are derived from marine sources, including krill oil, fish roe, and fish by-products, or synthesized through enzymatic or chemical processes.  

### Improved Bioavailability and Reduced Adverse Reactions  

Phospholipid-bound omega-3 fatty acids are absorbed more efficiently in the intestine due to their amphiphilic nature, which facilitates micelle formation without requiring bile salts. This results in higher plasma concentrations and reduced gastrointestinal side effects, such as nausea and fishy aftertaste, commonly associated with conventional omega-3 supplements.  

### Phospholipid Compounds  

The phospholipid compounds of the invention comprise:  
- A glycerol backbone esterified with two fatty acid moieties, at least one of which is an omega-3 fatty acid (e.g., EPA, DHA, or DPA).  
- A phosphate-containing headgroup, such as choline (PC), ethanolamine (PE), or serine (PS).  

### Structural Variations  

The fatty acid moieties may vary in chain length, saturation, and positional distribution (sn-1 or sn-2). Preferred embodiments include phospholipids with EPA or DHA in the sn-2 position, which exhibit enhanced tissue uptake, particularly in the brain.  

### Sources of Phospholipid Compounds  

Natural sources include:  
- **Krill oil**: Comprising ~40% phospholipids, predominantly PC with EPA and DHA.  
- **Fish roe**: Rich in phospholipids, particularly from herring, salmon, and mackerel.  
- **Marine by-products**: Including fish liver and processing waste.  

Synthetic production methods include enzymatic transesterification to incorporate omega-3 fatty acids into phospholipid structures.  

### Therapeutic Applications  

The compositions are effective in treating or preventing:  
- **Cardiometabolic disorders**: Including hypertriglyceridemia, atherosclerosis, and insulin resistance.  
- **Neurological conditions**: Such as Alzheimer’s disease, cognitive decline, and ADHD.  
- **Inflammatory diseases**: Including arthritis, ulcerative colitis, and metabolic inflammation.  

### Formulation and Administration  

The compositions may be formulated as:  
- **Oral supplements**: Including softgel capsules, powders, or liquid emulsions.  
- **Enteric-coated tablets**: To prevent gastric degradation.  
- **Intravenous emulsions**: For rapid delivery in clinical settings.  

Excipients may include antioxidants, emulsifiers, and flavoring agents to enhance stability and palatability.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

A composition comprising krill oil (40% phospholipids), fish oil (30% triglycerides), and omega-3 ethyl esters (30%) was prepared. The mixture exhibited enhanced bioavailability in human clinical trials compared to fish oil alone.  

### Example 2  

A phospholipid-rich formulation was prepared by mixing krill oil (50%) with omega-3 ethyl esters (50%). The formulation demonstrated improved absorption kinetics in a rodent model.  

### Example 3  

A krill oil and omega-3 ester blend (70:30 ratio) was tested in a human study, showing significant reductions in plasma triglycerides compared to placebo.  

### Example 4  

A krill oil concentrate (60% phospholipids) was combined with DHA-enriched ethyl esters (40%) to produce a brain-targeted supplement.  

### Example 5  

A fish oil and krill oil blend (50:50) was formulated for cardiovascular support, demonstrating synergistic effects on HDL cholesterol.  

### Example 6  

A krill oil-based emulsion was developed for intravenous administration, showing rapid incorporation into plasma lipoproteins.  

### Example 7  

A high-PC krill oil extract (80% phospholipids) was combined with astaxanthin to produce an antioxidant-rich supplement.  

### Example 8  

A krill oil and omega-3 ester mixture (60:40) was encapsulated in enteric-coated softgels to minimize gastric irritation.
