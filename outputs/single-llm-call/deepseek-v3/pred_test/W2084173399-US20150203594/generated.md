# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the field of polymeric materials used in medical and diagnostic applications. Conventional polymeric materials derived from nonrenewable resources have been widely utilized as supports in various diagnostic assays and biosensors. However, the commercial use of such materials presents several disadvantages, including environmental concerns related to their production and disposal.  

Cellulose and its derivatives have emerged as viable alternatives to synthetic polymers due to their inherent properties such as non-toxicity, hydrophilicity, and chemical resistivity. Among cellulose derivatives, nitrocellulose has been particularly employed as a supporting material for conjugating antibodies in diagnostic assays. Nevertheless, nitrocellulose suffers from several limitations including relatively low mechanical strength, flammability, and strong affinity with proteins that leads to non-specific adsorption.  

Nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC), produced from wood fibers through mechanical or chemical disintegration, exhibits advantageous properties such as high surface area, strong hydrogen bonding capacity, and excellent mechanical strength. Recent developments have enabled the production of nearly transparent and smooth films from NFC-gel. It has been discovered that precursor pulps with low negative charge used to manufacture NFC significantly reduce water impregnation while facilitating analyte penetration into the supporting material. This effect is attributed to the small porosity and compact structure of the resulting NFC films.  

TEMPO-mediated oxidation provides a method for selectively converting primary hydroxyl groups of cellulose to carboxyl groups, which can be further utilized in immunoassays. While TEMPO-oxidized NFC-gel has been used to produce highly transparent films with low air permeability, the high negative charge of these films results in poor water resistance. Surface-specific post-oxidation of novel, water-resistant NFC films offers an alternative pathway to create immunodiagnostic platforms with low porosity, high stability, and suitable conjugation sites for protein immobilization. The dense and closed structure of NFC films reduces water diffusion, thereby limiting oxidation reactions within the film's structure and consequently enhancing water resistance.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the limitations of current polymeric and cellulosic materials by providing surface-modified nanocellulose films with enhanced properties for diagnostic applications. The invention involves the topological modification of water-resistant NFC films through TEMPO-mediated oxidation to introduce carboxyl groups on their surface. These carboxyl groups are subsequently converted to amine-reactive groups via EDC/NHS coupling chemistry, enabling the conjugation of antibodies and other biomolecules.  

The method of the invention offers several advantages over existing technologies. First, the surface modification preserves the water resistance of the NFC films while providing controlled functionalization sites. Second, the invention enables precise conjugation of biomolecules through either inkjet printing or physical adsorption techniques. Third, the resulting biointerfaces exhibit reduced non-specific protein adsorption compared to conventional nitrocellulose supports. Fourth, the invention provides a sustainable alternative to petroleum-based polymeric materials.  

The activated NFC films produced according to the invention find particular utility in diagnostic applications where controlled immobilization of antibodies or other biomolecules is required. The invention further provides methods for characterizing the modified surfaces using techniques such as quartz crystal microbalance with dissipation (QCM-D), contact angle measurements, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM), and atomic force microscopy (AFM).  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method for topological modification of water-resistant NFC films through TEMPO-mediated oxidation followed by EDC/NHS activation. As illustrated in FIG. 1, the process begins with the preparation of NFC films from precursor pulps with low negative charge. These films are then subjected to surface-specific TEMPO-oxidation to introduce carboxyl groups while maintaining the film's structural integrity and water resistance.  

The raw materials for NFC film preparation typically include bleached hardwood pulp, which is mechanically processed using a grinder and subsequently disintegrated through microfluidization. The resulting NFC-gel is filtered to remove excess water and rolled to consolidate the film structure before drying between blotting boards under pressure.  

TEMPO-oxidation is performed using a system comprising 2,2,6,6-tetramethylpiperidine-1-oxyl radical (TEMPO), sodium bromide, and sodium hypochlorite in aqueous solution at pH 10. The oxidation reaction is carefully controlled with durations ranging from 10 to 300 seconds to achieve surface-specific carboxylation without compromising the film's structural integrity. The reaction is quenched by adding ethanol and washing with copious amounts of water to remove residual oxidation chemicals.  

Following carboxylation, the NFC films are activated through EDC/NHS coupling chemistry. The carboxylated films are treated with a solution containing 0.1 M EDC and 0.4 M NHS in sodium acetate buffer (pH 5) for 20 minutes. This reaction converts surface carboxyl groups to amine-reactive NHS-esters, which can subsequently react with primary amines present in antibodies or other biomolecules.  

The activated NFC films can be functionalized through either physical adsorption or inkjet printing of biomolecules. For physical adsorption, solutions containing the target biomolecules (typically at concentrations of 100 μg/mL) are applied to the activated surface and allowed to react for sufficient time (typically 20-30 minutes). For inkjet printing, solutions of fluorescently labeled biomolecules (typically at 1 mg/mL concentration) are precisely deposited onto the activated surface using commercial piezoelectric printers.  

The resulting biointerfaces find application in various diagnostic platforms, including immunoassays and biosensors. The invention further provides methods for blocking non-specific binding sites using protein solutions such as Superblock, and for verifying the activity of conjugated antibodies through specific antigen binding assays.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention but should not be construed as limiting its scope.  

**Materials Used:** All chemicals were obtained from commercial suppliers and used without further purification unless otherwise specified. NFC films were prepared from bleached hardwood pulp as described in the detailed embodiments.  

**Example 1 - Preparation of NFC Films and Model Surfaces:**  
NFC films were prepared by mechanical processing of bleached hardwood pulp followed by microfluidization and filtration. The resulting NFC-gel was rolled to consolidate the film structure and dried between blotting boards under pressure. NFC model surfaces for QCM-D measurements were prepared by spin-coating colloidal nanofibrils onto PEI-coated SiO₂ crystals.  

**Example 2 - Functionalization of NFC Films with TEMPO-Mediated Oxidation:**  
NFC films were oxidized using TEMPO/NaBr/NaClO system at pH 10 for varying durations (10-300 s). The reaction was quenched with ethanol and the films were thoroughly washed with water. Contact angle measurements showed increased hydrophilicity (contact angle reduced from 44° to <15°), while conductometric titration confirmed increased carboxyl content (from 72 μeq/g to 237 μeq/g).  

**Example 3 - Analysis of Unmodified and Activated Films:**  
XPS analysis confirmed the presence of NHS-esters on activated films through detection of nitrogen signal and increased carbonyl component in C1s spectra. AFM imaging revealed topological changes from fibrillar structure in unmodified films (RMS roughness 35.4 nm) to gel-like surface after TEMPO-oxidation (RMS roughness 44.5 nm) and more uniform surface after EDC/NHS activation (RMS roughness 49.6 nm).  

**Example 4 - QCM-D Monitoring of Reactions:**  
QCM-D was used to monitor TEMPO-oxidation and EDC/NHS activation in real-time. TEMPO-oxidation resulted in significant frequency shift (-75 Hz) indicating carboxyl group formation and water coupling. EDC/NHS treatment showed stable NHS-ester formation with no desorption during extended rinsing. Reactivity tests with BSA demonstrated covalent conjugation on activated surfaces, with approximately 50% of adsorbed protein remaining after alkaline washing.  

**Example 5 - Inkjet Printing of Antibodies on Activated NFC Films:**  
Dansyl- and FITC-stained anti-human IgG (1 mg/mL) was printed onto activated NFC films using a commercial piezoelectric printer. Fluorescence imaging confirmed successful conjugation, with printed patterns remaining visible after ethanolamine treatment and alkaline washing. AFM analysis of printed areas showed uniform antibody layers covering the cellulose fibrils, with reduced RMS roughness (25 nm) compared to unmodified films.  

The invention thus provides novel surface-modified NFC films with controlled topography and reactivity for diagnostic applications, along with methods for their production and characterization. The examples demonstrate the utility of these materials in creating specific biointerfaces for antibody conjugation and antigen detection.
