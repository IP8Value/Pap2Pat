# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates to the field of intracellular pathogen detection, particularly addressing limitations in current diagnostic methodologies. Intracellular pathogens such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis and Mycobacterium avium subspecies paratuberculosis (MAP) present significant challenges for detection due to their residence within host immune cells. Conventional serological immunoassays fail to effectively identify these microorganisms in biological fluids since the pathogens remain concealed within cellular compartments.  

Current gold-standard detection relies on polymerase chain reaction (PCR) following DNA extraction from infected leukocytes. While PCR demonstrates high specificity and sensitivity, the methodology remains labor-intensive, time-consuming, and dependent on homogeneous DNA samples. Complex biological matrices such as blood or tissue biopsies require extensive processing to obtain PCR-grade DNA, increasing turnaround time while reducing overall DNA yield. Alternative culture-based detection methods, though effective for slow-growing pathogens like MAP, require weeks to months for sufficient bacterial proliferation before analysis. Furthermore, many intracellular pathogens exhibit poor culturability due to biological interferences or inherent growth characteristics.  

Nanotechnology has emerged as a transformative approach for medical diagnostics, leveraging unique size-dependent properties of nanomaterials for pathogen sensing. Among promising developments, magnetic relaxation nanosensors (MRS) utilize polymer-coated iron oxide nanoparticles functionalized with affinity ligands to detect targets through changes in water proton relaxation times (T2). Unlike conventional magnetic nanoparticle assays measuring intrinsic particle properties, MRS technology amplifies detection signals through nanoparticle-induced magnetic field effects on surrounding water molecules. This mechanism enables sensitive target identification in turbid or minimally processed samples without requiring additional signal amplification steps.  

The present invention discloses a novel hybridizing magnetic relaxation nanosensor (hMRS) system demonstrating rapid, specific detection of MAP genomic markers. Unlike clustering-based MRS methodologies requiring nanoparticle aggregation, the disclosed hMRS platform operates through low-valency binding interactions that induce concentration-dependent T2 increases. This unique binding mechanism provides enhanced sensitivity at low target concentrations—critical for detecting scarce intracellular pathogens in clinical specimens.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a hybridizing magnetic relaxation nanosensor (hMRS) system for detecting Mycobacterium avium subspecies paratuberculosis (MAP). The hMRS comprises polyacrylic acid-coated iron oxide nanoparticles conjugated to oligonucleotide probes complementary to the MAP-specific IS900 genomic element. Upon binding to target DNA, the nanosensors induce measurable increases in water proton relaxation times (T2) correlating with MAP concentration.  

Key advantages of the hMRS system include:  
1) Single-copy genome detection sensitivity in minimally processed samples  
2) Specific identification of MAP without cross-reactivity to other mycobacteria  
3) Rapid detection within 30 minutes compared to multi-hour PCR protocols  
4) Elimination of enzymatic amplification requirements  
5) Compatibility with crude DNA extracts from blood, tissues, or cultured isolates  

Performance validation demonstrates superior clinical correlation compared to nested PCR (nPCR) methodologies, particularly in identifying MAP within peripheral blood samples from Crohn's disease patients. The hMRS platform achieves 100% concordance with direct nPCR results while outperforming culture-based nPCR in diagnostic accuracy.

### Intracellular Pathogen Detection Challenges  

Bacterial infections caused by intracellular pathogens present unique diagnostic hurdles due to microbial sequestration within host cells. Conventional immunoassays targeting extracellular antigens prove ineffective when pathogens reside within phagosomes or other intracellular compartments. Even PCR-based methods face limitations from required DNA purification steps that reduce sensitivity in complex matrices like blood or inflamed tissues.  

The disclosed hMRS technology addresses these challenges through magnetic nanoparticle probes detecting genomic markers without requiring pure DNA extracts. This innovation stems from understanding nanoparticle-water interactions where target binding alters local magnetic fields to modify proton relaxation times.  

### Hybridizing Magnetic Relaxation Nanosensors (hMRS)  

The hMRS platform comprises three functional components:  
1) Superparamagnetic iron oxide core generating magnetic fields  
2) Polyacrylic acid polymer coating providing colloidal stability  
3) Oligonucleotide capture probes complementary to MAP IS900 sequence  

Nanoparticles exhibit an average diameter of 78±3 nm with zeta potential -35 mV. Each particle carries approximately 55 oligonucleotides (5'-ATGTGGTTGCTGTGT-3') conjugated via copper-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition. The system demonstrates R1 and R2 relaxivities of 45 mM-1s-1 and 60 mM-1s-1 respectively at 0.47T.  

### Mycobacterium avium subspecies paratuberculosis (MAP)  

MAP represents an exemplary intracellular pathogen causing Johne's disease in ruminants and implicated in human Crohn's disease. Diagnostic challenges arise from its slow growth (doubling time >24 hours) and intracellular residence within macrophages. The IS900 genomic element provides an ideal detection target due to MAP-specific conservation across all clinical isolates.

The hMRS system demonstrates multiple improvements over conventional PCR:  
- Eliminates DNA purification requirements through crude extract compatibility  
- Reduces detection time from 6+ hours to 30 minutes  
- Achieves single-copy sensitivity without enzymatic amplification  
- Maintains performance in blood, tissues, and cultured isolates  
- Provides quantitative results correlating with pathogen load

## DESIGN AND SYNTHESIS OF HMRS  

### Probe Construction  

IS900-specific hMRS were prepared through:  
1) Propargyl group incorporation on polyacrylic acid-coated nanoparticles via carbodiimide chemistry  
2) Copper-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition with azide-modified oligonucleotides  
3) Magnetic purification using MACS columns  
4) Characterization of size, zeta potential, and relaxivity  

### Binding Mechanism  

The hMRS system operates through:  
1) Heating-induced DNA denaturation exposing target sequences  
2) Probe hybridization during cooling phases  
3) Nanoparticle-induced T2 increases without clustering  
4) Signal stabilization within 30 minutes post-hybridization  

Dynamic light scattering confirms absence of nanoparticle aggregation (81±5 nm diameter post-binding), indicating single-particle detection mechanism.  

### Specificity Validation  

The hMRS demonstrates:  
- Exclusive ΔT2+ signals with MAP DNA  
- No cross-reactivity to M. tuberculosis or environmental mycobacteria  
- Signal abrogation by complementary oligonucleotide competitors  
- Specificity maintained in mixed DNA samples  

### Sensitivity Profiling  

Key findings include:  
- Single genome copy detection (5.2 fg)  
- Linear response from 1-106 MAP copies  
- Equivalent sensitivity in pure vs crude DNA extracts  
- 100-fold improvement over nPCR in crude samples  

### Clinical Isolate Testing  

hMRS screening of cultured isolates shows:

- Correlation with patient clinical status  

### Tissue and Blood Applications

## METHODS  

### hMRS Preparation  

1) Synthesize propargyl-functionalized iron oxide nanoparticles  
2) Conjugate azide-modified oligonucleotides via click chemistry  
3) Purify using magnetic separation columns  
4) Characterize size, charge, and relaxivity  

### Bacterial Culture Processing  

1) Grow MAP in BACTEC 12B media  
2) Quantify growth using BACTEC 460 TB analyzer  
3) Heat-inactivate cultures at 121°C for 10 minutes  

### DNA Extraction  

**Crude DNA Protocol:**  
1) Resuspend pellets in TE buffer  
2) Heat 30 minutes at 100°C  
3) Centrifuge to remove debris  

**Pure DNA Protocol:**  
1) Perform phenol-chloroform extraction  
2) Ethanol precipitate DNA  
3) Wash and resuspend in TE buffer  

### Detection Assays  

**hMRS Protocol:**  
1) Mix sample with nanoparticles (6 μg Fe/mL)  
2) Heat 3 minutes at 95°C twice  
3) Measure T2 at 30 minutes post-heating  

**nPCR Protocol:**  
1) Perform two amplification rounds  
2) Analyze products via gel electrophoresis  

### Relaxation Measurements  

1) Acquire T2 values using 0.47T Minispec relaxometer  
2) Apply CPMG pulse sequence (1.5 ms spacing)  
3) Calculate ΔT2 as sample T2 minus control  

### Statistical Analysis  

1) Perform ROC analysis comparing hMRS vs PCR  
2) Calculate sensitivity/specificity at optimal ΔT2 cutoff  
3) Apply two-proportion z-test for method comparisons
