# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology, specifically to compositions and methods for protecting the ocular surface epithelium from damage caused by oxidative stress and other insults. More particularly, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions containing dynamin inhibitors such as Dynasore and its analogs for preventing and treating ocular surface diseases characterized by epithelial damage, including dry eye disease and solution-induced corneal staining.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The ocular surface comprises stratified squamous mucosal epithelia of the cornea and conjunctiva covered by a tear film. These epithelial cells undergo continuous renewal through a process where basal cells divide and their progeny differentiate as they migrate toward the surface. The apical cell layer develops specialized membrane folds called microplicae that express membrane-associated mucins such as MUC16. These mucins bind galectin-3 (LGALS3) oligomers to form a glycocalyx barrier that prevents penetration of foreign substances.  

The glycocalyx works in concert with tight junctions between cells to create both transcellular and paracellular barriers. As cells reach the surface, they flatten, lose mucin expression, reduce metabolic activity, and ultimately undergo desquamation. Complete turnover of the ocular surface epithelium occurs within 5-7 days in humans.  

Being directly exposed to the environment, the ocular surface is vulnerable to damage from ultraviolet light, microorganisms, allergens, and other insults. Such damage disrupts the epithelial barrier and increases desquamation. A common manifestation of ocular surface damage is dry eye disease (keratoconjunctivitis sicca), which affects over 20% of the population in developed countries.  

Clinicians routinely assess ocular surface damage using vital dyes such as fluorescein and rose bengal. Fluorescein staining was first used in 1882 to detect corneal epithelial defects, while rose bengal gained popularity in the 1930s for diagnosing dry eye based on its characteristic punctate staining pattern. More recently, fluorescein has also been used for dry eye diagnosis. Another phenomenon called solution-induced corneal staining (SICS) occurs when multipurpose contact lens solutions cause vital dye uptake.  

Despite their widespread use, the mechanisms by which vital dyes stain damaged ocular surface cells remain poorly understood. Early studies showed that healthy monolayer cultures take up rose bengal unless blocked by tear components like mucins. Later work demonstrated that differentiated corneal epithelial cells with an intact glycocalyx exclude rose bengal autonomously, suggesting that punctate staining in dry eye reflects glycocalyx damage.  

Fluorescein, a hydroxyxanthine derivative structurally related to rose bengal, shows different uptake properties. While mucins don't block fluorescein penetration, the plasma membrane itself acts as the primary barrier. Recent studies using monolayer cultures show that a small percentage of cells actively concentrate fluorescein, appearing hyperfluorescent. Damaging stresses or contact lens solutions increase the number of hyperfluorescent cells, which often show early apoptotic features. This suggests fluorescein hyperstaining represents active dye concentration in compromised but still living cells.  

When the plasma membrane is damaged, cells activate repair mechanisms involving membrane remodeling. The present invention stems from the hypothesis that vital dyes might enter cells through endocytic vesicles formed during membrane repair. Investigations of this mechanism led to the unexpected discovery that dynamin inhibitors like Dynasore protect the ocular surface from damage, representing a novel therapeutic approach.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides methods and compositions for protecting the ocular surface epithelium from damage. The invention is based on the discovery that dynamin inhibitors such as Dynasore and its analogs prevent oxidative stress-induced damage to ocular surface epithelial cells and their glycocalyx barrier.  

One aspect of the invention provides a method of protecting the ocular surface in a subject by administering an effective amount of a dynamin inhibitor. The inhibitor may be Dynasore, Dyngo-4a, or another compound that inhibits dynamin GTPase activity. The method prevents damage to epithelial cells and their glycocalyx, thereby maintaining barrier function and preventing vital dye uptake.  

Another aspect provides pharmaceutical compositions containing dynamin inhibitors formulated for ocular administration. The compositions may include carriers, excipients, and other agents suitable for topical application to the eye. The formulations can be solutions, gels, ointments, or other dosage forms appropriate for ocular delivery.  

The invention offers several advantages over existing approaches. First, by protecting the ocular surface from damage rather than treating established injury, it addresses the root cause of conditions like dry eye. Second, the compositions can prevent damage from various insults including oxidative stress, surfactants, and other damaging agents. Third, the treatment may resolve chronic ocular surface disease over time as protected cells replace damaged ones during normal epithelial turnover.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The following detailed description illustrates the invention by way of example, not by way of limitation.  

### Definitions  

**Vital dyes** refer to stains like fluorescein and rose bengal used clinically to assess ocular surface integrity. These water-soluble dyes are taken up by damaged epithelial cells.  

**Staining mechanisms** involve active dye uptake by living cells through compromised barriers. Oxidative stress stimulates uptake by damaging the plasma membrane and glycocalyx, creating passages for dye penetration.  

The **dye uptake mechanism** in monolayer cultures involves stress-stimulated endocytosis, as shown by inhibition with endocytosis blockers. However, in stratified cultures modeling the ocular surface, Dynasore inhibits uptake without affecting endocytosis, indicating an alternative protective mechanism.  

An **oxidative stress model** using tert-butyl hydroperoxide (tBHP) demonstrates that stress damages cells and stimulates dye uptake. This damage includes plasma membrane permeabilization and glycocalyx disruption, as shown by trypan blue staining and LGALS3 shedding.  

**Damage stimulates dye uptake** by compromising cellular barriers. Stress increases fluorescein and rose bengal uptake in both monolayer and stratified cultures, though the patterns differ between these models.  

**Damage stimulates endocytosis** in monolayer but not stratified cultures. This difference highlights the importance of using differentiated models that better represent the ocular surface.  

**Endocytosis inhibitors** like chlorpromazine (clathrin-mediated), genistein (caveolin-mediated), and Dynasore (dynamin-dependent) block stress-stimulated dye uptake in monolayers. Only Dynasore and Dyngo-4a work in stratified cultures, suggesting a different mechanism.  

**Differentiated corneal epithelial cell cultures** are created by growing cells in high-calcium medium for 7 days. These stratified mounds develop mucosal differentiation and glycocalyx barrier function, better modeling the ocular surface than monolayers.  

**Dye uptake in differentiated cultures** shows uniform staining without cytoplasmic concentration seen in monolayers. Oxidative stress increases uptake but doesn't stimulate endocytosis in these cultures.  

**Dynasore effects** include blocking stress-stimulated dye uptake in both culture models and ex vivo mouse eyes. Protection occurs when applied with stress but not after damage is done, indicating it prevents rather than reverses injury.  

An **alternative mechanism of Dynasore** involves protecting cells and glycocalyx from stress damage. This preserves barrier function and prevents dye uptake, distinct from its endocytosis inhibition.  

**Treatment and treating** refer to administering dynamin inhibitors to protect the ocular surface from damage or to manage existing ocular surface disease.  

**Prevent and inhibit** mean stopping or reducing ocular surface damage before it occurs, maintaining epithelial integrity.  

An **effective amount** is a quantity of dynamin inhibitor sufficient to protect ocular surface cells from damage without causing significant adverse effects.  

**Carriers** include pharmaceutically acceptable vehicles for delivering dynamin inhibitors to the eye, such as saline solutions, gels, or ointments.  

A **protein** is a biological macromolecule composed of amino acids. In this context, it includes galectins like LGALS3 involved in glycocalyx structure.  

An **isolated polypeptide or protein** refers to these molecules substantially free from other biological components, such as recombinantly produced proteins.  

**Substantially the same** means having at least 80% sequence identity to a reference protein while maintaining its functional properties.  

**Homeostatic protein clusterin (CLU)** is a glycoprotein involved in cell protection and membrane repair. Its levels change in inflammatory disorders.  

**CLU's sealing mechanism** involves binding damaged membranes to prevent further injury. At the ocular surface, it may help maintain epithelial integrity.  

**CLU's binding to ocular surface** could contribute to barrier function and protect against stress-induced damage.  

**Combination therapy with Dynasore** might enhance ocular surface protection by addressing multiple protective mechanisms simultaneously.  

**Human clusterin structure** includes different isoforms with various functions. Isoform 2 is a secreted form involved in cytoprotection.  

**Clusterin isoform 2 preproprotein sequence** provides the basis for producing recombinant protein for therapeutic use.  

**Clusterin as used in the invention** refers to its application in protecting the ocular surface, alone or with dynamin inhibitors.  

**Recombinant human clusterin production** involves expressing the protein in suitable host cells and purifying it for pharmaceutical use.  

**Pharmaceutical compositions** containing dynamin inhibitors or clusterin are formulated for ocular delivery with appropriate carriers and excipients.  

**Polypeptides substantially the same as human clusterin** retain its protective functions and can be used similarly in the invention.  

**Administration of Dynasore or analogs** involves topical application to the eye in formulations optimized for ocular delivery and retention.  

**Compositions and formulations for topical administration** include solutions, suspensions, gels, and ointments suitable for ocular use.  

**Pharmaceutical formulations** contain dynamin inhibitors or clusterin in concentrations effective for protecting the ocular surface.  

**Dosages and desired drug concentrations** are adjusted to achieve therapeutic effects while minimizing irritation or other side effects.  

**Maintenance therapy** involves regular administration to sustain ocular surface protection, particularly in chronic conditions like dry eye.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

**Hugo nomenclature** is used throughout for genes and proteins to maintain consistency with scientific literature.  

The **experimental model** employed human corneal limbal epithelial (HCLE) cells cultured as monolayers or stratified/differentiated mounds, along with ex vivo mouse eyes.  

The **study design** investigated mechanisms of vital dye uptake under oxidative stress, with or without endocytosis inhibitors.  

**Statistical analysis** used Student's t-tests to compare groups, with significance set at p<0.05. Experiments were performed in triplicate and repeated at least twice.  

The **hypothesized dye uptake mechanism** proposed that stress stimulates endocytosis, allowing dye entry via repair vesicles.  

**Oxidative stress** was chosen as the damaging stimulus because of its relevance to ocular surface diseases like dry eye.  

**tBHP preparation** involved diluting stock solutions into culture media at concentrations (3-10 mM) determined by dose-response experiments.  

**Cell culture experiments** compared monolayer and stratified HCLE cells under various conditions to assess dye uptake and cell damage.  

**Endocytic inhibitors** included chlorpromazine (8 μg/mL), genistein (200 μM), Dynasore (40-80 μM), and Dyngo-4a (15 μM), each dissolved in DMSO.  

**Inhibitor treatment** occurred simultaneously with stress application in most experiments, with some testing post-stress administration.  

The **vital dye assay** used fluorescein and rose bengal to assess epithelial damage after 10-minute staining followed by PBS washes.  

**Data analysis** quantified dye uptake by plate reader measurements and evaluated results statistically.  

**Human corneal epithelial cell culture** employed authenticated HCLE cells grown under standard or differentiation conditions.  

**Cell line authentication** confirmed expression of appropriate markers and chromosomal characteristics.  

**Cell differentiation** was induced by high-calcium medium for 7 days, creating stratified mounds with glycocalyx barrier function.  

**Mouse eye organ culture** used enucleated eyes from C57Bl/6J mice maintained in keratinocyte media.  

**Euthanasia and eye enucleation** followed approved protocols, with immediate processing for organ culture experiments.  

The **vital dye staining assay** for organ cultures mirrored cell culture methods, using clinical fluorescein solutions.  

**Apoptosis assays** employed Annexin V binding (early apoptosis) and TUNEL staining (late apoptosis) to assess cell damage.  

**Endocytosis assays** used Alexa Fluor 568-transferrin uptake to quantify endocytic activity under various conditions.  

**Metabolic assays for cytotoxicity** included MTT and WST-1 tests to measure cell viability and damage.  

The **trypan blue exclusion assay** assessed plasma membrane integrity by dye penetration into damaged cells.  

**Lectin binding assays** used fluorescein-jacalin to evaluate glycocalyx integrity through O-glycan detection.  

**Western blotting** analyzed LGALS3 shedding into culture media as an indicator of glycocalyx damage.  

**Polymerase chain reaction** detected ACTB fragments in media to assess cell lysis versus specific glycoprotein shedding.  

**Results of oxidative stress experiments** showed increased dye uptake and sublethal damage in both culture models.  

**Oxidative stress experiments** demonstrated that stress stimulates dye uptake in all models, with different patterns between monolayers and stratified cultures.  

**Differentiation assessment** using rose bengal staining confirmed glycocalyx barrier function in stratified cultures.  

**Glycocalyx barrier function** under oxidative stress showed reduced protection, allowing increased dye penetration.  

**Endocytosis role** in dye uptake was confirmed in monolayers but not stratified cultures, where Dynasore worked through alternative mechanisms.  

**Endocytosis inhibitors' effects** differed between models, with only Dynasore and Dyngo-4a effective in stratified cultures.  

The **link between stress-stimulated endocytosis and dye uptake** was clear in monolayers but absent in more physiologically relevant models.  

**Endocytosis inhibitor effects on stratified cells** showed that Dynasore's protection doesn't depend on endocytosis inhibition.  

**Differences between monolayer and stratified cells** highlighted the importance of using differentiated models for ocular surface studies.  

**Dynasore effects on stratified cultures** demonstrated protection against stress-induced damage independent of endocytosis.  

**Dyngo-4a effects** mirrored Dynasore's but at lower concentrations, confirming the dynamin inhibitor class effect.  

**Mouse eye organ culture validation** confirmed Dynasore's protective effects at the ocular surface level.  

**Dynasore effects in organ culture** showed prevention but not reversal of stress-induced damage when administered after insult.  

**Alternative hypotheses about Dynasore's mechanism** led to discovery of its protective effects on cells and glycocalyx.  

**Cell protection against oxidative stress** was demonstrated through metabolic assays showing Dynasore's preservation of viability.  

**Glycocalyx protection** was evidenced by reduced LGALS3 shedding in Dynasore-treated cultures.  

**Clinical applications** include using dynamin inhibitors to prevent and treat ocular surface diseases involving epithelial damage.  

**Therapeutic value** derives from Dynasore's ability to protect ocular surface cells during turnover, potentially resolving chronic conditions.  

**Future research directions** include investigating dynamin-dependent and independent mechanisms of protection.  

**Potential treatments** could address dry eye, SICS, and other disorders characterized by ocular surface epithelial damage.  

**Conclusions** affirm that dynamin inhibitors represent a novel approach to ocular surface protection with significant therapeutic potential.
