## DESCRIPTION  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

The global health challenge posed by HIV-1 infection remains a critical concern, necessitating the development of effective vaccines. A central focus of vaccine design involves inducing broadly neutralizing antibodies (bNAbs) capable of preventing viral entry into host cells. The primary target for such antibodies is the envelope glycoprotein (Env) complex, a trimeric structure composed of gp120 and gp41 subunits, which mediates viral attachment and membrane fusion.  

Current strategies for vaccine development include creating structural mimics of the native Env spike to elicit bNAbs. Soluble gp140 proteins, which lack the transmembrane domain, have been explored as potential immunogens. However, these proteins often exhibit instability, leading to disintegration or non-native conformations that fail to induce effective neutralizing responses.  

Modifications such as the introduction of a disulfide bond (SOS) between gp120 and gp41, along with a stabilizing substitution (I559P) in gp41, have improved trimer stability. Proteolytic cleavage between gp120 and gp41 is also essential for native-like trimer formation, facilitated by optimizing the cleavage site and co-expressing the furin protease. Among the most advanced soluble trimers is the BG505 SOSIP.664 construct, derived from a subtype A founder virus. These trimers closely resemble native viral spikes structurally and antigenically, presenting bNAb epitopes while occluding non-neutralizing antibody epitopes.  

Despite these advances, limitations remain in the production of cleaved Env glycoproteins. Current methods rely on transient transfection, which is inefficient for large-scale manufacturing. There is a need for improved production methods that enable consistent, high-yield expression of native-like trimers suitable for clinical applications.  

### BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method for producing cleaved HIV-1 Env glycoproteins using a stable mammalian cell line system. The invention involves an expression vector encoding an HIV Env glycoprotein and furin protease, transfected into mammalian cells such as 293T or CHO cells. The vector integrates into the host genome via the Flp-In™ system, ensuring stable and high-level expression of the Env glycoprotein.  

The method includes culturing the transfected cells under conditions that promote the secretion of fully cleaved, native-like Env trimers. These trimers are subsequently purified and characterized for structural and antigenic authenticity. The invention enables scalable production of Env glycoproteins suitable for vaccine development and structural studies.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

#### Expression Vector  

The expression vector of the invention comprises a plasmid designed for high-efficiency expression of HIV Env glycoproteins in mammalian cells. The plasmid includes a promoter, such as the cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter, to drive constitutive expression of the Env gene. A weaker promoter, such as the EF1 Alpha promoter, controls furin expression to minimize cytotoxicity while ensuring efficient proteolytic cleavage of the Env glycoprotein.  

The Env glycoprotein encoded by the vector is derived from HIV-1 strains, preferably the BG505 SOSIP.664 or B41 SOSIP.664 variants. These proteins include modifications such as the SOS disulfide bond and the I559P substitution to enhance stability. The vector also contains polyadenylation sequences to ensure proper mRNA processing and termination.  

#### Mammalian Cell Line  

The invention utilizes mammalian cell lines, including 293T and CHO cells, for stable expression of the Env glycoprotein. These cells are modified to integrate the expression vector into their genome via the Flp-In™ system, which employs Flp recombinase to target a specific recombination site (FRT). This system ensures single-copy integration and stable expression over multiple passages.  

Transfection of the expression vector into mammalian cells is achieved using methods such as lipofection or electroporation. Following transfection, cells are selected using hygromycin resistance, and high-expressing clones are isolated via limiting dilution and fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS).  

#### Production of Cleaved Env Glycoproteins  

The method involves culturing the stable cell lines in growth medium optimized for protein production. Supernatants containing secreted Env glycoproteins are harvested and purified using affinity chromatography, such as 2G12 antibody-based capture, followed by size-exclusion chromatography (SEC) to isolate native-like trimers.  

The purified trimers are characterized for structural integrity using techniques such as blue native PAGE (BN-PAGE), SDS-PAGE, and Western blotting. Antigenic properties are assessed via surface plasmon resonance (SPR) and ELISA, confirming the presence of bNAb epitopes and the absence of non-neutralizing epitopes. Negative-stain electron microscopy (EM) and differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) further validate the native-like morphology and stability of the trimers.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

A vector co-expressing the BG505 SOSIP.664 Env glycoprotein and furin protease was designed. The Env gene was cloned into the pcDNA5/FRT vector under the control of the CMV promoter, while the furin gene was inserted under the EF1 Alpha promoter. The resulting plasmid, designated pAM/C BG505, was used for stable transfection of mammalian cells.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

The pAM/C BG505 vector was co-transfected with the pOG44 Flp recombinase vector into 293T and CHO Flp-In™ cells. Stable cell lines were selected using hygromycin resistance, and high-expressing clones were isolated via limiting dilution. Clone 293T #13-3-5 and CHO B-D7 demonstrated consistent Env expression and were propagated for large-scale trimer production.  

### EXAMPLE 3  

Intracellular Env expression was monitored over ten passages, confirming stable production without genetic instability. ELISA measurements indicated sustained trimer yields of 2–3 µg/mL in culture supernatants, with cell viability remaining above 90%.  

### EXAMPLE 4  

BG505 SOSIP.664 trimers purified from stable cell lines were analyzed by BN-PAGE and SEC, showing homogeneity comparable to trimers produced by transient transfection. Reducing SDS-PAGE confirmed complete cleavage into gp120 and gp41ECTO subunits, with no detectable degradation products. Western blotting with HIV-Ig further verified the absence of proteolytic clipping or aggregation.  

### EXAMPLE 5  

SPR and ELISA analyses demonstrated strong binding of bNAb PGT145 to the trimers, while non-neutralizing antibody F105 showed minimal reactivity. These results confirm the structural authenticity of the trimers produced by stable cell lines.  

### EXAMPLE 6  

Negative-stain EM imaging revealed native-like morphology for the trimers, with >80% of particles adopting the correct quaternary structure.  

### EXAMPLE 7  

DSC assays indicated a sharp thermal transition at ~66°C, consistent with the stability of trimers produced by transient transfection.  

### EXAMPLE 8  

The invention was extended to other HIV-1 strains, including subtype B (B41 SOSIP.664) and subtype C Env glycoproteins. These proteins were similarly expressed and purified, demonstrating the versatility of the production method.  

### EXAMPLE 9  

B41 SOSIP.664 trimers produced in serum-free medium showed no V3 clipping, in contrast to trimers cultured in fetal calf serum (FCS), which exhibited proteolytic degradation. This finding highlights the importance of serum-free conditions for producing high-quality trimers.
