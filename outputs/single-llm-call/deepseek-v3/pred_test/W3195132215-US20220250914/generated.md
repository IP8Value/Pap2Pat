# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of electrochemical exfoliation, particularly to systems and methods for producing graphene-like nanosheets from petroleum coke through an electrochemical exfoliation process. The invention further discloses a reactor configuration optimized for high-yield exfoliation of layered carbonaceous materials, including needle coke, into graphene-family nanomaterials.  

## BACKGROUND  

Electrochemical exfoliation has emerged as a promising method for producing graphene from graphite due to its scalability and relatively mild reaction conditions compared to chemical oxidation-reduction methods. However, conventional electrochemical exfoliation techniques face limitations when applied to non-graphitic carbon sources such as petroleum coke. These limitations include poor electrical contact maintenance during exfoliation, low yields of high-quality graphene, and difficulties in separating exfoliated products from unreacted material.  

Petroleum coke, a byproduct of oil refining, represents an abundant but underutilized carbon source. While needle coke possesses a layered structure similar to graphite, its conversion into high-value graphene has remained challenging due to structural differences and impurities. Existing methods for coke-to-graphene conversion typically produce oxidized materials or require energy-intensive processing steps. There exists a need for improved electrochemical exfoliation systems capable of efficiently converting petroleum coke into high-quality graphene materials while addressing the limitations of current techniques.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses a reactor system for electrochemical exfoliation of layered carbon materials, particularly petroleum coke, into graphene-like nanosheets. The reactor comprises a porous chamber configured to contain a bed of parent material, working and counter electrodes arranged to establish electrical contact through the bed, and a pressure source maintaining compression during exfoliation. The system enables continuous electrical contact throughout the exfoliation process despite material expansion.  

The invention further discloses a method for electrochemical exfoliation comprising: pretreating petroleum coke particles with acid to enhance wettability, loading the pretreated material into the reactor under compression, applying voltage in an electrolyte solution to induce exfoliation, and separating the exfoliated nanosheets through multi-stage centrifugation. The method produces graphene-like materials with lateral dimensions of 1-3 μm and thicknesses below 100 nm, as confirmed by electron microscopy and Raman spectroscopy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

### Introduction to Electrochemical Exfoliation  

Electrochemical exfoliation involves the application of electrical potential across a carbonaceous parent material immersed in an electrolyte solution. Under applied bias, electrolyte ions intercalate between the layered structure of the parent material, causing expansion and eventual separation into individual nanosheets. This process differs from mechanical exfoliation by providing more controlled layer separation and from chemical oxidation methods by preserving the intrinsic electronic properties of graphene.  

### Limitations of Current Techniques  

Conventional electrochemical exfoliation methods using graphite monoliths suffer from interrupted electrical contact as exfoliation progresses, limiting reaction yield and uniformity. When applied to petroleum cokes, these methods typically produce small graphene quantum dots or require additional post-processing steps. Existing systems also lack efficient separation protocols to isolate high-quality graphene from unexfoliated material and impurities.  

### Advantages of the Disclosed Method  

The disclosed compression reactor maintains electrical continuity throughout exfoliation by applying constant pressure to the expanding material bed. The method achieves graphene production from needle coke with minimal oxidation, as evidenced by Raman spectra showing distinct 2D peaks and electron diffraction patterns revealing hexagonal crystalline order. The two-stage centrifugation separation protocol enables isolation of graphene sheets from unreacted coke and non-graphitic byproducts.  

### Compression Reactor Design  

The reactor comprises a container housing a porous chamber constructed from chemically resistant materials such as cellulose or polypropylene. The porous chamber features pore sizes between 1-10 μm to permit electrolyte flow while retaining parent material particles. A working electrode, typically platinum wire, extends into the parent material bed, while counter electrodes formed from copper mesh surround the porous chamber.  

The system includes a pressure application mechanism consisting of weighted plates or spring-loaded fixtures maintaining 0.5-2 kg/cm² pressure on the material bed during operation. This compression prevents loss of electrical contact as intercalation causes material expansion. The reactor operates with 0.1-1 M ammonium sulfate electrolyte at applied potentials of 5-15 V for 1-4 hours.  

### Parent Material Characteristics  

The invention utilizes petroleum coke, particularly needle coke grades, as parent material due to their layered planar structure with interlayer spacing similar to graphite. Needle coke exhibits long-range crystalline order with needle-like morphology and high aromaticity, making it particularly suitable for graphene production. The parent material is processed into particulate form with particle sizes between 1-100 μm, preferably 10-50 μm, to optimize electrolyte penetration and exfoliation efficiency.  

### Exfoliation Process  

The electrochemical exfoliation process begins with pretreatment of the parent material using nitric acid at elevated temperatures (100-150°C) to enhance surface wettability. The pretreated material undergoes size classification via centrifugation to remove undersized particles before loading into the reactor. During exfoliation, applied voltage drives intercalation of ammonium ions between coke layers at rates proportional to the potential difference. The process produces exfoliated nanosheets with largest lateral dimensions of 0.5-5 μm and thicknesses of 1-10 graphene layers.  

### Product Separation and Processing  

Following exfoliation, the reaction mixture undergoes sequential centrifugation steps: first at 2000-4000 rpm to sediment unexfoliated coke particles, then at 5000-8000 rpm to separate graphene sheets from smaller non-graphitic material. The isolated graphene product may undergo post-treatment including thermal annealing at 500-1100°C to enhance electrical conductivity, which increases from ~50 S/m to over 400 S/m after high-temperature treatment.  

### Reactor Embodiments  

Several reactor configurations are disclosed:  

**Embodiment 1**: Horizontal porous chamber with top-mounted working electrode and surrounding counter electrode mesh.  

**Embodiment 2**: Vertical chamber configuration with lateral pressure application via spring-loaded plates.  

**Embodiment 3**: Multi-chamber reactor allowing continuous feed of parent material and product removal.  

**Embodiment 4**: System incorporating in-line centrifugation for immediate product separation.  

**Embodiment 5**: Scalable industrial reactor with multiple parallel exfoliation chambers.  

**Embodiment 6**: Automated pressure regulation system maintaining optimal compression throughout exfoliation.  

### Method Embodiments  

The invention discloses multiple method variations:  

**Embodiment 7**: Process using mixed sulfuric/nitric acid pretreatment.  

**Embodiment 8**: Method incorporating intermediate sonication steps to enhance exfoliation.  

**Embodiment 9**: Continuous flow process with recycled electrolyte and unexfoliated material.  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

Needle coke fines (CK-1) were pretreated with 6M nitric acid at 120°C for 4 hours, washed, and centrifuged at 4000 rpm to remove small particles. The retained material was loaded into a cellulose membrane reactor with platinum working electrode and copper mesh counter electrode. Electrochemical exfoliation proceeded at 12V in 0.1M ammonium sulfate for 2 hours under 0.75 kg compression. The product was washed and subjected to two-step centrifugation (2000 rpm then 5000 rpm) to isolate graphene sheets.  

Analysis by SEM revealed graphene-like sheets with 1-3 μm lateral dimensions. TEM showed hexagonal crystalline structure with <5 layer thickness. Raman spectroscopy displayed distinct G (1570 cm⁻¹) and 2D (2690 cm⁻¹) peaks with ID/IG ratio of 0.3, confirming high-quality graphene production.  

### Example 2  

Electrical conductivity measurements compared pretreated and exfoliated materials. Parent coke showed conductivity of 0.07 S/m, while exfoliated and separated graphene sheets exhibited 56.9 S/m. Annealing at 500°C increased conductivity to 250 S/m, and treatment at 1100°C further improved it to 474 S/m.  

### Example 3  

Dry graphene powder was annealed at temperatures from 500-1100°C for 12-24 hours. Conductivity increased with temperature, reaching 345 S/m at 900°C and 474 S/m at 1100°C. Time variation showed minimal effect, with only 3% increase from 12 to 24 hours at 500°C.
