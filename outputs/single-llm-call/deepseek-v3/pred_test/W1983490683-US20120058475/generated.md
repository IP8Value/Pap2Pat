## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of cytogenetics and molecular genetics, and more specifically to improved methods for detecting chromosomal inversions. The invention provides novel probe kits and hybridization techniques for sensitive identification of inversion events in chromosomes, with particular application in cancer research, prenatal screening, and genetic disorder diagnosis.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Chromosomal aberrations have been extensively studied as markers of genetic disease and cancer progression. Cancer cells frequently exhibit characteristic chromosomal abnormalities that serve as diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets. Among these structural variations, tumor-specific chromosome aberrations represent important biomarkers that have led to the discovery of numerous cancer-related genes. While translocations and large deletions have been well characterized, inversions remain among the most challenging structural rearrangements to detect using conventional methods.

Inversions are intra-chromosomal rearrangements resulting from two breaks within the same chromosome followed by reinsertion of the intervening segment in reversed orientation. The genetic effects of inversions can be significant, potentially disrupting gene function or regulatory elements when breakpoints occur within coding or regulatory sequences. These rearrangements typically arise from misrepair of DNA double-strand breaks, with radiation exposure being a known causative agent.

Standard karyotype analyses using G- or R-banding techniques have limited ability to detect inversions, particularly those smaller than 10-20 megabases. Whole chromosome painting by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) fails to reveal inversions because the hybridization signal appears identical on both chromatids regardless of orientation. These limitations have hindered comprehensive analysis of inversion frequency and their potential role in disease.

The fundamental structure of chromosomes and their constituent DNA molecules underlies these detection challenges. Each chromatid consists of a single DNA molecule with specific 5' to 3' polarity, maintained through complementary base pairing during genome replication. Inversion formation requires that the excised segment reverse its orientation to preserve this polarity, causing the affected sequences to "switch places" between sister chromatids.

Chromosome "paints" composed of fluorescently labeled DNA probes have been used in conventional FISH analyses, but lack strand specificity. The CO-FISH (Chromosome Orientation FISH) technique represents an advance by enabling strand-specific hybridization, but has been limited to repetitive centromeric or telomeric sequences due to technical constraints in probe design and preparation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a sensitive method for detection of chromosomal inversions that overcomes the resolution limitations of conventional cytogenetic techniques. The method utilizes specially designed probe kits for sensitive detection of chromosomal inversions through strand-specific hybridization to single-stranded sister chromatids.

The invention describes a comprehensive method for detecting inversions that involves several key steps: First, single-stranded sister chromatids are generated through selective degradation of nascent DNA strands following bromodeoxyuridine (BrdU) incorporation during DNA replication. Non-repetitive probes are then generated through bioinformatic selection of unique sequence targets and chemical synthesis of directionally oriented oligonucleotides. These probes are hybridized to the prepared sister chromatids under conditions that maintain strand specificity. Finally, hybridized probes are detected through fluorescence microscopy, with inversions appearing as abrupt switches of signal from one chromatid to its sister.

This approach provides significant advantages over prior methods, including: (1) the ability to detect inversions at substantially higher resolution (potentially down to kilobase scale); (2) strand-specific detection that reveals orientation changes directly; (3) applicability to any chromosomal region, not just repetitive elements; and (4) compatibility with standard cytogenetic preparations and microscopy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The invention provides a novel method for detecting inversions in chromosomes that combines the cell-by-cell resolution of cytogenetics with the molecular specificity of DNA sequence recognition. This directional genomic hybridization methodology, termed "chromatid painting," represents a significant advance over previous CO-FISH technology by enabling whole-chromosome or targeted regional analysis using unique sequence probes.

The method utilizes carefully designed single-stranded DNA probes that maintain consistent 5' to 3' directionality relative to the reference genome. These probes are identified through comprehensive analysis of genome databases to select suitable unique sequences spaced at desired intervals along target chromosomes. The probe design process involves multiple verification steps to ensure specificity, appropriate melting temperatures, and lack of cross-hybridization potential.

Probe synthesis involves chemical production of oligonucleotides matching the selected sequences, followed by fluorescent labeling using standard nucleotide incorporation techniques. The labeled probes are pooled to create comprehensive "paints" for whole chromatids or focused sets for targeted regions of interest.

Critical to the method is the preparation of single-stranded sister chromatid targets. This is achieved through selective degradation of BrdU-substituted nascent strands following DNA replication, leaving complementary single strands on each sister chromatid. These prepared chromosomes are then hybridized with the directional probes under optimized conditions that maintain strand specificity.

Detection is performed using standard fluorescence microscopy, with signal patterns revealing inversion events as abrupt transitions of hybridization signal from one chromatid to its sister. The method can be applied to metaphase chromosome spreads from various cell types, including clinical specimens and cultured cells.

### Selection of Sequences:

The invention includes methods for identifying large contiguous DNA sequences suitable for use as hybridization targets. These contigs are selected from reference genome databases based on length, unique sequence content, and appropriate chromosomal coverage. Within these contigs, shorter sequences are selected as probes based on multiple criteria including uniqueness, melting temperature, and directional consistency.

The invention provides specific protocols for generating sequence-specific DNA probes by PCR amplification. These include detailed PCR conditions, primer design parameters, and labeling methods. Alternative approaches using oligo tiling and commercial synthesis are also described, including use of specialized software for probe design optimization.

Probe labeling methods include PCR incorporation of modified nucleotides, terminal transferase-mediated addition, and chemical conjugation. Specific reaction conditions are provided for each labeling approach, including concentrations, incubation times, and purification steps. The example details preparation of probes labeled with AlexaFluor 594-5-dUTP as well as other fluorescent and non-fluorescent labels.

### EXAMPLE 3

This example describes application of CO-FISH using probes generated by PCR amplification. The process involves: (1) preparation of single-stranded chromatid targets as described; (2) hybridization with directional probes under optimized conditions; (3) washing to remove non-specifically bound probes; and (4) detection of specifically hybridized probes by fluorescence microscopy.

### Hybridization:

The invention specifies optimal hybridization mixture compositions and reaction conditions. Key parameters include probe concentration, hybridization buffer composition, temperature profiles, and incubation times. Post-hybridization washing steps are detailed, including solution compositions, temperatures, and durations for effective background reduction while maintaining specific signal.

### Detection:

Probe detection is performed using standard fluorescence microscopy equipment. The invention describes microscope configurations, filter sets, camera settings, and image capture parameters optimized for signal visualization and analysis. Specific protocols are provided for counterstaining, mounting, and preserving hybridized slides.

### Results:

Application of the method demonstrates successful detection of chromosomal inversions at significantly higher resolution than conventional techniques. PCR-generated and tiled oligo probes both produce strong, specific signals on single chromatids, with appropriate controls showing minimal background. The results confirm incorporation of fluorescent labels into probes and their specific hybridization to target sequences.

The method reliably detects both large and small inversions, with signal patterns matching expected chromatid switching for inverted segments. Background signals can be further reduced through increased stringency conditions without compromising specific hybridization. The technique is compatible with various labeling strategies, allowing multiplex detection using different fluorophores.
