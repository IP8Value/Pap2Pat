### DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to a thermostable variant of the Amadoriase enzyme, which exhibits improved stability under elevated temperatures while retaining catalytic activity. This enzyme is particularly useful in diagnostic applications, food processing, and therapeutic contexts where enhanced thermal resistance is required.  

### STATE OF THE ART  

Glycation is a non-enzymatic reaction between reducing sugars and amino groups in proteins, leading to the formation of advanced glycation end-products (AGEs). This reaction is implicated in various pathological conditions, including diabetes, atherosclerosis, and neurodegenerative diseases. Measurement of glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c) is a critical diagnostic tool for diabetes management, as it reflects long-term glycemic control. Current methods for HbA1c detection rely on enzymatic assays using Amadoriase enzymes, which cleave glycated amino acids to produce detectable byproducts such as hydrogen peroxide. However, these enzymes are often unstable at elevated temperatures, limiting their shelf life and utility in high-temperature applications.  

Existing HbA1c measurement techniques include immunoassays, chromatography, and enzymatic methods. While immunoassays are widely used, they suffer from cross-reactivity and high costs. Chromatographic methods, though accurate, are labor-intensive and require specialized equipment. Enzymatic assays using Amadoriase enzymes offer a cost-effective alternative but are hindered by the enzyme's thermal instability.  

Amadoriase enzymes are also employed in the food industry to mitigate acrylamide formation during thermal processing. Acrylamide, a carcinogenic compound, forms via the Maillard reaction between asparagine and reducing sugars. Current strategies to reduce acrylamide, such as modifying processing conditions or using additives, are often ineffective or alter food quality. A thermostable Amadoriase could provide a viable solution by degrading glycated precursors before acrylamide formation.  

Therapeutic applications of Amadoriase enzymes are also under exploration, particularly for degrading AGEs in vivo. However, the enzyme's instability and limited activity on intact proteins pose significant challenges. Thus, there is a pressing need for Amadoriase variants with improved thermostability to enhance their utility in diagnostics, food processing, and therapeutics.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides an isolated thermostable Amadoriase protein comprising specific amino acid substitutions that confer enhanced thermal stability. Preferred embodiments include variants with mutations selected from the group consisting of S67C/P121C (SS03) and D295C/K303C (SS17), which exhibit significantly higher thermostability compared to the wild-type enzyme.  

The invention further encompasses derivatives of the thermostable Amadoriase protein, including crystallized forms or isomorphs thereof, as well as recombinant vectors and host cells encoding the protein. The thermostable Amadoriase variants are suitable for use in the food industry to reduce acrylamide formation, as diagnostic tools for HbA1c detection, and as biosensors for glycemic monitoring.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The Amadoriase enzyme belongs to the family of fructosyl amino acid oxidases (FAOX), which catalyze the oxidative cleavage of glycated amino acids to yield free amines, glucosone, and hydrogen peroxide. The wild-type Amadoriase I enzyme (SEQ ID NO: 1) is derived from *Aspergillus fumigatus* and exhibits limited thermal stability. The invention discloses thermostable variants of this enzyme, engineered through rational design to introduce stabilizing disulfide bonds.  

Key mutations include the substitution of serine 67 and proline 121 with cysteine (SS03 variant; SEQ ID NO: 3) and the substitution of aspartate 295 and lysine 303 with cysteine (SS17 variant; SEQ ID NO: 4). These mutations form additional disulfide bonds that rigidify the enzyme's structure, as confirmed by X-ray crystallography (PDB IDs: 5OC2, 5OC3). The SS17 variant, in particular, retains 50% activity at 60°C and exhibits detectable activity even at 95°C.  

The polynucleotide sequences encoding these variants (SEQ ID NO: 5 and 7) can be cloned into recombinant vectors and expressed in host cells, such as *E. coli*. The production method involves culturing the host cells under conditions that induce protein expression, followed by purification using affinity chromatography. The purified enzymes can be employed in various applications, including:  

1. **Food Industry**: The thermostable Amadoriase degrades glycated amino acids during thermal processing, reducing acrylamide formation in baked, fried, or roasted foods.  
2. **Diagnostics**: The enzyme is incorporated into biosensors for HbA1c detection, where its stability ensures prolonged shelf life and reliability.  
3. **Therapeutics**: The enzyme's enhanced stability makes it a candidate for degrading AGEs in vivo, potentially mitigating complications of diabetes and aging.  

The invention also provides a kit for measuring glycated hemoglobin, comprising the thermostable Amadoriase, a proteolytic enzyme for sample digestion, and reagents for detecting hydrogen peroxide. The method involves digesting a blood sample to release glycated valine, deglycating the valine using the thermostable Amadoriase, and quantifying the resulting hydrogen peroxide via colorimetric assay.  

Protein expression and purification protocols are detailed, including steps for cloning the Amadoriase I gene, introducing double-cysteine mutations, and verifying constructs via sequencing. The enzymes are expressed in *E. coli*, induced with IPTG, and purified using nickel-affinity chromatography. Enzyme activity is assayed spectrophotometrically by monitoring glucosone formation, and thermal stability is assessed by measuring residual activity after heat treatment.  

In summary, the invention provides thermostable Amadoriase variants with broad applications in diagnostics, food processing, and therapeutics. These variants address the limitations of existing enzymes by combining rational design with experimental validation to achieve superior performance under high-temperature conditions.
