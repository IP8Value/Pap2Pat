# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

Programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1), also known as CD279, is an immune checkpoint receptor that plays a critical role in regulating immune responses. PD-1 is expressed on activated T cells, B cells, and myeloid cells, where it interacts with its ligands PD-L1 and PD-L2 to deliver inhibitory signals that suppress immune activation. This mechanism serves to prevent autoimmunity and limit collateral tissue damage during immune responses. However, in the context of cancer, PD-1-mediated suppression can inhibit anti-tumor immune responses, allowing tumors to evade immune surveillance. The discovery of PD-1 and its role in immune regulation has led to the development of therapeutic strategies aimed at blocking PD-1/PD-L1 interactions to enhance anti-tumor immunity.  

## SUMMARY  

Soluble PD-1 (sPD-1) has emerged as a potential biomarker for assessing immune status and predicting clinical outcomes in cancer patients. The present invention discloses methods for utilizing sPD-1 levels to select patients who are likely to benefit from cancer vaccine therapy. Specifically, baseline sPD-1 levels and changes in sPD-1 following vaccination serve as prognostic and predictive markers for survival in patients with metastatic melanoma treated with autologous dendritic cell vaccines (DCV).  

The invention provides a method for selecting cancer patients for vaccine therapy by measuring sPD-1 levels in blood samples obtained before and after vaccination. Patients with baseline sPD-1 levels below 100 pg/mL or those with baseline levels between 100 pg/mL and 200,000 pg/mL who exhibit a decrease in sPD-1 after vaccination are identified as likely to achieve long-term survival. Conversely, patients with baseline sPD-1 levels exceeding 200,000 pg/mL or those who exhibit an increase in sPD-1 levels post-vaccination are less likely to benefit from DCV therapy.  

Additionally, the invention discloses methods for predicting survival outcomes in cancer patients receiving vaccine therapy by monitoring sPD-1 dynamics. A decrease in sPD-1 levels following vaccination correlates with improved survival, particularly in DCV-treated patients. The invention further encompasses methods for treating cancer patients by administering vaccine therapy in combination with immune checkpoint inhibition, tailored to the patient's sPD-1 profile. Variations of these methods include adjusting treatment regimens based on sPD-1 thresholds and incorporating other immunomodulatory agents to enhance therapeutic efficacy.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

Soluble PD-1 (sPD-1) functions as an immune checkpoint molecule that reflects the balance between immune activation and suppression. Elevated sPD-1 levels may indicate an active but suppressed immune response, while low levels may suggest an absence of immune engagement. The present invention leverages sPD-1 as a prognostic biomarker to stratify cancer patients for vaccine therapy.  

Previous studies have reported inconsistent associations between sPD-1 levels and clinical outcomes across various cancers. In non-small cell lung cancer, higher sPD-1 levels were linked to prolonged progression-free survival in patients treated with EGFR inhibitors. Conversely, in hepatocellular carcinoma, elevated sPD-1 levels were associated with poorer prognosis. These discrepancies highlight the context-dependent nature of sPD-1 as a biomarker and underscore the need for cancer-specific and therapy-specific evaluations.  

The present disclosure focuses on sPD-1 dynamics in metastatic melanoma patients treated with autologous DCV or tumor cell vaccines (TCV). Blood samples were collected at baseline (week-0) and after three weekly vaccine injections (week-4) to measure sPD-1 levels. Baseline sPD-1 levels ranged from 0 to 511,063 pg/mL, with a median of 119,845 pg/mL. Notably, baseline sPD-1 levels alone were not prognostic for survival in the overall patient population. However, in DCV-treated patients, extremely high baseline sPD-1 levels (>200,000 pg/mL) were associated with poor survival, while levels below 100 pg/mL correlated with favorable outcomes.  

The experimental design involved evaluating sPD-1 levels at week-0 and week-4 to assess their predictive value. In DCV-treated patients, a decrease in sPD-1 levels post-vaccination was associated with significantly longer survival. For example, patients with baseline sPD-1 levels below 100 pg/mL or those exhibiting a decrease in sPD-1 after vaccination had a median survival exceeding four years, compared to less than two years for patients with high/increased sPD-1 levels.  

The invention discloses a method for selecting cancer patients for DCV therapy based on sPD-1 criteria. Patients are classified into two cohorts: (1) "sPD-1 low/decreased," defined by baseline sPD-1 <100 pg/mL or baseline levels between 100 pg/mL and 200,000 pg/mL with a post-vaccination decrease; and (2) "sPD-1 high/increased," defined by baseline sPD-1 >200,000 pg/mL or an increase in sPD-1 post-vaccination. This classification correctly predicted survival outcomes in 80% of DCV-treated patients.  

The invention also provides a method for treating cancer patients by administering DCV therapy to those meeting the "sPD-1 low/decreased" criteria. For patients with high/increased sPD-1 levels, the method includes combining DCV with immune checkpoint inhibition therapy, such as anti-PD-1 or anti-PD-L1 monoclonal antibodies (e.g., nivolumab, pembrolizumab). The disclosed methods are applicable to various cancer types, including melanoma, lung cancer, and hepatocellular carcinoma, and can be adapted to other cancer vaccines, such as peptide-based or viral vector vaccines.  

Immune checkpoint inhibition therapy is defined broadly to include any agent that blocks PD-1/PD-L1 interactions, including monoclonal antibodies, small molecules, and fusion proteins. Treatment encompasses diagnosis, monitoring, and administration of therapeutic agents, including vaccines and checkpoint inhibitors. The invention also describes numerical thresholds for sPD-1 levels (e.g., 100 pg/mL, 200,000 pg/mL) and methods for measuring sPD-1 using ELISA or other immunoassays.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate the application of the invention in a clinical trial setting. Patients with metastatic melanoma were randomized to receive DCV or TCV. Blood samples were collected at week-0 and week-4, and sPD-1 levels were quantified.  

**Patient Characteristics**: The trial included 39 patients (17 DCV-treated, 22 TCV-treated) with paired blood samples. Baseline characteristics were balanced between treatment arms.  

**Vaccine Products**: DCV consisted of autologous dendritic cells loaded with tumor antigens, while TCV comprised irradiated autologous tumor cells. Both vaccines were administered subcutaneously with GM-CSF.  

**Blood Sample Analysis**: sPD-1 levels were measured using a quantitative ELISA. Baseline sPD-1 levels varied widely, with no significant difference between treatment arms.  

**Statistical Methods**: Survival outcomes were analyzed using Fisher’s exact test and log-rank tests. sPD-1 dynamics were compared using non-parametric tests.  

**Results**:  
- In DCV-treated patients, baseline sPD-1 <100 pg/mL or a post-vaccination decrease predicted long-term survival (median >4 years).  
- Patients with baseline sPD-1 >200,000 pg/mL had poor outcomes (median survival <1 year).  
- Changes in sPD-1 levels were not predictive in TCV-treated patients.  

**Graphical Data**: Dot plots and survival curves demonstrated the predictive value of sPD-1 criteria in DCV-treated patients. For example, a dot plot of baseline sPD-1 levels vs. survival showed clear stratification between high- and low-risk patients.  

**False Positives/Negatives**: Two DCV-treated patients classified as "high/increased" survived >5 years, suggesting additional biomarkers may refine predictions.  

**Discussion**: The data support sPD-1 as a prognostic and predictive marker for DCV therapy. Limitations include small sample size and retrospective analysis. Future studies should validate these findings in larger cohorts and explore combinations with checkpoint inhibitors.  

**Definitions**:  
- "Treatment" includes diagnosis, monitoring, and therapy administration.  
- Numerical thresholds (e.g., 100 pg/mL) may vary ±20% due to assay variability.  

**Closing Remarks**: The invention provides a novel method for personalizing cancer vaccine therapy based on sPD-1 dynamics. Variations include adjusting thresholds for different cancers or incorporating additional biomarkers.
