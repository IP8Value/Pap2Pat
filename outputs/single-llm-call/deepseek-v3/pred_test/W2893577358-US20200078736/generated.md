### FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to ion-selective membranes fabricated through layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolytes on nanoporous substrates. More particularly, the invention pertains to methods for controlling ion transport properties through nanostructured polyelectrolyte membranes by tuning deposition parameters and crosslinking chemistries.  

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Ion-exchange membranes play a critical role in electrochemical separation processes such as electrodialysis, which is widely used for water desalination and purification. Conventional electrodialysis relies on ion-exchange membranes that selectively transport either cations or anions while blocking species of opposite charge. The ion transport mechanism in these membranes depends on fixed charged groups attached to the polymer backbone, which create electrostatic interactions with mobile ions in solution.  

While effective, conventional electrodialysis membranes face several limitations including high manufacturing costs, limited tunability of transport properties, and chemical instability under harsh operating conditions. The development of nanoporous membranes has provided new opportunities for controlling ion transport through manipulation of pore surface chemistry and geometry. However, existing nanoporous membranes often lack the scalability and precise control over ion selectivity required for industrial applications.  

Recent advances in polyelectrolyte materials have motivated the exploration of nanostructured polyelectrolytes for membrane applications. Prior art has demonstrated various nanoporous membrane configurations, including anodic alumina, mesoporous silica, and ion-tracked polymeric membranes. However, these approaches have not fully addressed the need for inexpensive, large-area membranes with tunable ion transport properties. The ability to control both ionic selectivity and conductivity through systematic modification of membrane chemistry remains an unmet need in the field.  

### SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method for fabricating ion-selective membranes through layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolytes on nanoporous supports. The invention utilizes alternating deposition of cationic and anionic polymers to build up nanostructured polyelectrolyte films with precisely controlled thickness and composition.  

Key advantages of the invention include the ability to tune ion transport properties by: (1) varying the number of polyelectrolyte bilayers deposited, (2) selecting specific polyelectrolyte combinations, and (3) applying different crosslinking chemistries to modify charge density. The LbL deposition process enables large-scale membrane fabrication using inexpensive aqueous polymer solutions and simple dip coating techniques.  

The resulting membranes demonstrate superior ionic selectivity while maintaining adequate conductivity for practical applications. The crosslinked polyelectrolyte films exhibit enhanced chemical stability compared to conventional ion-exchange membranes. These properties make the invention particularly suitable for water purification, electrodialysis, and energy storage applications.  

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The invention addresses the need for large-scale production of ion-selective membranes by employing layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolytes. This bottom-up assembly method allows precise control over membrane properties while using inexpensive materials and simple fabrication techniques.  

The LbL deposition process offers several advantages for membrane fabrication, including: (1) compatibility with various substrate materials and geometries, (2) control over film thickness at the nanometer scale, and (3) ability to incorporate diverse functional groups through polymer selection. The method involves sequential adsorption of oppositely charged polyelectrolytes from aqueous solutions, forming stable thin films through electrostatic interactions.  

Polyelectrolytes suitable for the invention include any water-soluble polymers containing ionizable groups. Preferred embodiments utilize poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) as the anionic polymer and poly(ethyleneimine) (PEI) as the cationic polymer. These materials were selected for their complementary functional groups (carboxylic acids and amines) that enable both electrostatic assembly and subsequent chemical crosslinking.  

The LbL deposition process begins with preparation of the nanoporous substrate. Suitable substrates include polymeric membranes (e.g., polycarbonate), ceramic materials (e.g., anodic alumina), and other porous supports with pore sizes ranging from 10 nm to 1 μm. The substrate is first cleaned and surface-treated to ensure proper polyelectrolyte adhesion.  

Film growth occurs through alternating immersion in polyelectrolyte solutions. On planar surfaces, the film thickness typically increases linearly or exponentially with the number of deposited bilayers. For nanoporous substrates, the growth mechanism involves formation of a dense polyelectrolyte gel within the pores, influenced by factors such as pore size, ionic strength, and polymer molecular weight.  

The invention incorporates cooperative elements in the polyelectrolyte structure to enhance film properties. These include electrostatic attractions between oppositely charged groups and hydrogen bonding between complementary functional groups. The balance of these interactions determines the mechanical stability and swelling behavior of the resulting membranes.  

Crosslinking represents a critical aspect of the invention, serving to: (1) stabilize the polyelectrolyte structure, (2) modify charge density, and (3) tune transport properties. Two crosslinking approaches are particularly effective: (1) glutaraldehyde (GA) mediated amine-amine coupling and (2) carbodiimide (EDC) mediated amide bond formation between amines and carboxylic acids. These reactions selectively modify the relative abundance of charged groups in the membrane, thereby controlling ionic selectivity.  

The ionic selectivity and conductivity of the membranes can be systematically adjusted by varying: (1) the number of deposited bilayers, (2) the polyelectrolyte composition, and (3) the crosslinking chemistry. Membranes with 3-5 bilayers of PAA/PEI crosslinked with glutaraldehyde demonstrate optimal balance of cation selectivity and conductivity for electrodialysis applications.  

#### Example: LbL Deposition of Bilayer PAA and PEI Polyelectrolytes  

A specific embodiment of the invention involves LbL deposition of PAA and PEI polyelectrolytes on track-etched polycarbonate membranes. This example illustrates the fabrication process and characterization methods used to verify membrane performance.  

The polyelectrolyte film assembly begins with preparation of 0.1 wt% PEI solution (pH 10.4) and 0.2 wt% PAA solution (pH 3.2). Nanoporous polycarbonate support membranes (0.05 μm pore size) are first treated with NaOH to remove surface contaminants, followed by UV-ozone treatment to activate the surface.  

The LbL assembly process consists of sequential immersion in PEI and PAA solutions. The first bilayer is deposited using 5-minute immersion times, while subsequent bilayers use 1-minute immersion periods. This alternating deposition builds up the polyelectrolyte film with precise control over thickness and composition.  

Crosslinking is performed after deposition using either glutaraldehyde (GA) or N-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)-N'-ethylcarbodiimide hydrochloride (EDC). GA crosslinking specifically targets primary amines in the PEI layers, while EDC promotes amide bond formation between PEI amines and PAA carboxylic acids. These distinct chemistries produce membranes with different charge density profiles and transport properties.  

Membrane synthesis is completed by punching smaller test samples from the master membrane. The fabrication process yields reproducible membranes with active areas up to 64 cm², demonstrating scalability for industrial applications.  

Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) confirms successful polyelectrolyte deposition and crosslinking. Characteristic peaks at 1714 cm⁻¹ (carboxylic acid C=O stretch) and 1550 cm⁻¹ (amine N-H bend) verify the presence of both polymer components. The relative intensity changes of these peaks after crosslinking provide quantitative information about the modification of functional groups.  

Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) reveals the morphology and uniformity of the polyelectrolyte layers. Plan-view images show complete pore coverage after deposition of 3 bilayers with crosslinking. Cross-sectional analysis demonstrates coherent interfaces between the polyelectrolyte film and substrate, with film thickness proportional to the number of deposited bilayers (approximately 250 nm per bilayer).  

Membrane conductivity is evaluated using electrochemical impedance spectroscopy. Measurements in 10 mM and 100 mM NaCl solutions show that conductivity decreases initially with polyelectrolyte deposition but stabilizes after complete pore coverage. Crosslinked membranes maintain adequate conductivity for practical applications while showing improved chemical stability.  

Ionic selectivity is determined through potentiometric measurements of membrane potential. The invention demonstrates tunable cation selectivity, with transference numbers (t₊) ranging from 0.6 to 0.9 depending on deposition parameters and crosslinking chemistry. GA-crosslinked membranes with 5 bilayers show the highest cation selectivity due to preferential reduction of positive fixed charges.  

The invention provides a versatile platform for fabricating ion-selective membranes with tailored transport properties. By combining LbL deposition with selective crosslinking, the method enables independent control over ionic selectivity and conductivity - a significant advancement over conventional membrane technologies. These nanostructured polyelectrolyte membranes show particular promise for electrodialysis, water purification, and energy storage applications.
