# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and vaccine production, specifically to methods for purifying human papillomavirus (HPV) virus-like particles (VLPs) composed of L1 proteins. More particularly, the invention provides an improved purification process that enhances the purity, structural integrity, and immunogenicity of HPV VLPs produced in yeast expression systems. The disclosed methods address critical limitations in current HPV VLP purification techniques by incorporating reducing agent treatment combined with controlled heating and chilling steps prior to chromatographic separation.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Human papillomavirus (HPV) infection represents a significant global health burden, with certain high-risk HPV types being the primary causative agents of cervical cancer and other anogenital malignancies. HPV is a non-enveloped virus with an icosahedral capsid composed of two structural proteins: the major capsid protein L1 and the minor capsid protein L2. The L1 protein has the intrinsic ability to self-assemble into VLPs that mimic the native viral structure but lack viral DNA, making them safe and effective vaccine antigens.  

Currently, three HPV vaccines utilizing L1 VLPs are commercially available, protecting against HPV types responsible for approximately 70-90% of cervical cancer cases worldwide. These vaccines are produced using recombinant DNA technology, with the L1 protein expressed in either yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) or insect cell systems. While effective, current HPV vaccines face several limitations including high production costs, complex purification processes, and challenges in maintaining VLP structural integrity during manufacturing.  

The production of HPV VLPs in yeast expression systems offers advantages such as low cost and scalability, but presents technical challenges in downstream processing. Conventional purification methods often result in suboptimal VLP yields, contamination with host cell proteins, and compromised VLP structure that negatively impacts immunogenicity. Current purification approaches typically involve multiple steps including cell disruption, precipitation, ultracentrifugation, and various chromatography techniques. These methods are not only time-consuming and expensive but can also damage the delicate quaternary structure of VLPs.  

Previous attempts to improve HPV VLP purification have focused primarily on chromatographic techniques, including ion exchange, size exclusion, and affinity chromatography. However, these methods alone often fail to adequately remove contaminating proteins while preserving VLP structure. Other approaches such as salt treatment, redox refolding, and VLP maturation have shown limited success in improving VLP quality. The art lacks an efficient, scalable purification method that consistently yields HPV VLPs with high purity and preserved conformational epitopes critical for vaccine efficacy.  

The structural integrity of HPV VLPs is paramount for their immunogenicity, as neutralizing antibodies recognize specific conformational epitopes on properly assembled particles. Current purification methods frequently result in partial disassembly or aggregation of VLPs, reducing their vaccine potential. There remains an unmet need for a robust purification process that maintains VLP structure while efficiently removing contaminants, thereby improving both the quality and cost-effectiveness of HPV VLP production.  

## DISCLOSURE  

### Technical Problem  

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the development of an efficient, scalable method for purifying HPV L1 proteins that maintains their structural integrity and immunogenicity. Current purification techniques suffer from several limitations including low yield, high cost, and compromised VLP quality. The invention specifically solves the problem of removing contaminating proteins while preserving the native conformation of HPV L1 VLPs, which is essential for their antigenic properties.  

### Technical Solution  

The present invention provides a novel method for purifying HPV L1 proteins comprising several key steps that collectively solve the aforementioned technical problems. The method involves culturing transformed host cells expressing HPV L1 protein, disrupting the cells to release intracellular contents, treating the resulting homogenate with a reducing agent, and performing chromatographic purification.  

An essential aspect of the invention involves the addition of a reducing agent to the cell homogenate prior to purification. Suitable reducing agents include β-mercaptoethanol (β-ME), dithiothreitol (DTT), tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine (TCEP), or reduced glutathione, with concentrations ranging from 0.1% to 5% (v/v for liquid agents or w/v for solid agents). The reducing agent treatment is performed for 10 minutes to 24 hours at temperatures between 4°C and 37°C. This step facilitates proper protein folding and disulfide bond rearrangement critical for VLP formation.  

In preferred embodiments, the method further includes a controlled heating and chilling process following reducing agent treatment. The homogenate is heated to temperatures between 45°C and 65°C for 5 to 60 minutes, then rapidly chilled to 0°C to 10°C for 5 to 60 minutes. This thermal cycling step promotes the selective precipitation of contaminating proteins while maintaining HPV L1 in solution, significantly enhancing purification efficiency.  

Chromatographic purification is then performed using one or more chromatography techniques selected from ion exchange chromatography, heparin affinity chromatography, size exclusion chromatography, and hydrophobic interaction chromatography. Particularly advantageous results are obtained using sequential heparin affinity chromatography steps, where the first chromatography removes the majority of contaminants and the second chromatography further purifies the HPV L1 VLPs.  

The invention also encompasses an alternative purification method where the reducing agent-treated homogenate undergoes ammonium sulfate precipitation prior to chromatography. In this embodiment, the homogenate is treated with 30% to 50% saturated ammonium sulfate to precipitate the HPV L1 protein, which is then resolubilized and subjected to chromatographic purification as described above.  

### Advantageous Effects  

The purification method of the present invention provides several advantageous effects compared to existing techniques. First, it significantly enhances the purity of HPV L1 VLPs, with final preparations containing greater than 95% L1 protein as determined by SDS-PAGE analysis. Second, the method preserves the structural integrity of VLPs, as evidenced by electron microscopy showing properly assembled particles and dynamic light scattering demonstrating uniform particle size distribution.  

Third, the purified VLPs exhibit superior immunogenicity, eliciting higher titers of neutralizing antibodies in animal models compared to VLPs purified by conventional methods. This enhanced immunogenicity results from better preservation of conformational epitopes recognized by neutralizing monoclonal antibodies. Fourth, the method is scalable and cost-effective, requiring fewer purification steps and yielding higher quantities of immunogenic VLPs per batch compared to existing techniques.  

## MODES OF THE INVENTION  

The invention provides several embodiments for purifying HPV L1 proteins. In one embodiment, the method comprises: (a) culturing host cells transformed with an expression vector containing an HPV L1 gene; (b) disrupting the cells to obtain a homogenate; (c) adding β-mercaptoethanol to a final concentration of 1-3% (v/v); (d) incubating at 25-37°C for 1-4 hours; (e) heating to 50-60°C for 15-30 minutes; (f) chilling to 4°C for 15-30 minutes; (g) performing heparin affinity chromatography; and (h) performing a second heparin affinity chromatography step.  

In another embodiment, the method comprises: (a) culturing transformed host cells; (b) disrupting cells; (c) adding DTT to 1-5 mM final concentration; (d) incubating at 4-25°C for 2-12 hours; (e) adding ammonium sulfate to 40% saturation; (f) collecting the precipitate; (g) dissolving the precipitate in buffer; and (h) performing ion exchange chromatography followed by size exclusion chromatography.  

## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific implementations of the invention without limiting its scope:  

**Example 1: Cell Culture Method**  
Saccharomyces cerevisiae Y2805 transformed with YEGα-MCS-HP16 L1 gene-opt plasmid was cultured in YPDG medium containing 1% yeast extract, 2% peptone, and varying concentrations of glucose/galactose (1:1 ratio totaling 2-8%). Cultures were grown for 6 days at 30°C with shaking at 230 rpm.  

**Example 2: Purification Using T-1 Method**  
Cells were disrupted with glass beads using a vortex mixer. The lysate was centrifuged at 14,000×g for 10 min. The supernatant was treated with 2% β-ME for 2 hours at 30°C, heated to 55°C for 20 minutes, then chilled to 4°C for 20 minutes. The sample was loaded onto a heparin column equilibrated with 10 mM sodium phosphate buffer (pH 7.2) containing 0.15 M NaCl. Bound proteins were eluted with a linear NaCl gradient (0.15-1 M). HPV L1-containing fractions were pooled and subjected to a second heparin chromatography under identical conditions.  

**Example 3: Analysis of Purified VLPs**  
Purified HPV16 L1 VLPs were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blot, showing >95% purity. Transmission electron microscopy revealed properly assembled 50-60 nm VLPs. Dynamic light scattering showed a monodisperse population with hydrodynamic diameter of 55±5 nm. ELISA using neutralizing monoclonal antibodies H16.V5 and H16.E70 demonstrated preserved conformational epitopes.  

**Example 4: Immunogenicity Assessment**  
BALB/c mice were immunized three times with 10 or 1000 ng of purified HPV16 L1 VLPs. Sera collected 10 days after the final immunization showed high titers of anti-HPV16 L1 IgG (1:10,000-1:100,000) and neutralizing antibodies against HPV16 pseudovirus (50% neutralization titers of 1:1,000-1:10,000).  

**Example 5: Comparison of Purification Methods**  
The T-1 method (with β-ME treatment and heating/chilling) yielded 7.6-fold more HPV16 L1 protein than conventional methods while maintaining superior VLP quality as assessed by all analytical criteria.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The HPV L1 protein purification method of the present invention has significant industrial applicability in vaccine production. The method enables large-scale manufacturing of high-quality HPV VLPs for prophylactic vaccines against HPV infection and associated cancers. The improved purity and immunogenicity of the VLPs produced by this method may enhance vaccine efficacy while reducing production costs. Additionally, the principles of the invention may be adapted for purifying VLPs of other viruses, expanding its utility in vaccine development.
