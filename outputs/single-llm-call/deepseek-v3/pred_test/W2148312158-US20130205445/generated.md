# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to methods for enhancing seed yield and plant growth by modulating sugar transport mechanisms in plant cells. More specifically, the invention focuses on the tonoplast monosaccharide transporter (TMT) protein and its role in facilitating vacuolar sugar accumulation, which directly impacts plant development and seed production.  

The TMT protein family serves as a critical component in the intracellular distribution of monosaccharides, particularly glucose and fructose, between the cytosol and vacuole. Through targeted overexpression of TMT proteins, particularly TMT1, the invention provides a means to significantly increase vacuolar sugar storage capacity, thereby improving photosynthetic efficiency, reducing respiratory carbon loss, and ultimately enhancing seed biomass and oil/protein content. This technology has broad applications in agriculture, particularly for oilseed crops such as rapeseed, where increased yield per cultivation area carries substantial economic importance.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Plants serve as the foundation of the global food industry, with oilseed crops like rapeseed (Brassica napus) being particularly valuable for their high lipid storage capacity. Arabidopsis thaliana has long served as a model organism for studying plant physiology and genetic manipulation due to its well-characterized genome and rapid life cycle. Numerous attempts have been made to enhance plant yield through genetic modifications targeting various metabolic pathways.  

The Yellow Stripe-like (YEP) protein family has been identified as playing a role in enhancing harvest indices through improved nutrient transport. A critical aspect of plant productivity involves the efficient transport of sugars from the cytosol to the vacuole for storage. The tonoplast monosaccharide transporter (TMT) protein family facilitates this vacuolar sugar transport, with Arabidopsis thaliana possessing three isoforms: TMT1, TMT2, and TMT3.  

Gene knockout studies have demonstrated that disruption of TMT function leads to significant reductions in vacuolar glucose and fructose accumulation. Arabidopsis thaliana contains several monosaccharide transport proteins, but the TMT family shows unique structural and functional characteristics. The TMT proteins share significant sequence similarity across species and contain conserved domains essential for proton-coupled sugar transport.  

The cDNA sequence and corresponding amino acid sequence of TMT1 have been fully characterized, revealing a 12-transmembrane domain structure typical of major facilitator superfamily transporters. The TMT1 protein (At1g20840) has been shown to localize specifically to the vacuolar membrane (tonoplast) where it mediates energy-dependent monosaccharide uptake. Structural analysis indicates that TMT proteins operate as proton antiporters, coupling sugar import to proton export from the vacuole.  

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION  

The object of the present invention is to provide a method for increasing seed yield and plant growth through genetic manipulation of vacuolar sugar transport systems. The invention achieves this through the creation and utilization of overexpression mutants of TMT proteins, particularly TMT1, which demonstrate enhanced vacuolar monosaccharide accumulation capacity.  

Overexpression of TMT1 leads to significant alterations in intracellular sugar distribution, resulting in increased vacuolar storage of glucose and fructose. This modified compartmentation correlates with enhanced expression of photosynthesis-related genes, reduced respiratory carbon loss, and improved carbon use efficiency. Plants expressing elevated TMT1 levels exhibit substantially increased seed weights, with gains of up to 28% compared to wild-type plants. The increased seed biomass comprises both elevated lipid content (up to 12% increase) and protein content (up to 14% increase).  

The principle of enhancing yield through vacuolar sugar transport modification extends beyond TMT1 to include homologous and analogous transport proteins. For the purposes of this invention, "homologues" are defined as proteins sharing at least 70% amino acid sequence identity with TMT1 while maintaining equivalent transport function. "Analogues" refer to proteins from different species that perform similar vacuolar monosaccharide transport functions regardless of sequence similarity.  

The preferred embodiment utilizes TMT1 due to its demonstrated efficacy in enhancing seed yield, though the invention encompasses all functional TMT isoforms. Gene expression may be achieved through constitutive promoters such as the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter or through inducible promoter systems allowing temporal and spatial control of transgene expression.  

Methods for introducing and expressing TMT genes in plant cells include Agrobacterium-mediated transformation, biolistic delivery, and other established plant transformation techniques. The invention incorporates marker genes and selection genes to facilitate identification of successfully transformed cells. Stable integration of TMT expression cassettes into the plant genome ensures heritable trait transmission.  

The economic benefits of this technology are substantial, particularly for crops where oil or protein production represents the primary harvestable product. By increasing yield per unit area, the invention enables either greater production from existing cultivation areas or equivalent production from reduced land use. The method has been demonstrated in Arabidopsis thaliana and is readily transferable to agriculturally significant species including rapeseed, soybean, sunflower, and other oilseed crops.  

## BEST MODE FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION  

### Examples  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope:  

1. A plasmid construct for TMT1 protein overexpression was created by introducing a c-myc motif into the Attmt1-cDNA sequence following amino acid 375. This modification allowed for potential immunodetection of the recombinant protein while maintaining transport function.  

2. An expression cassette was produced utilizing the cauliflower mosaic virus 35S promoter to drive constitutive TMT1 expression. The cassette was isolated via NotI restriction digestion and subsequently introduced into the plant transformation vector pART27.  

3. The cDNA sequence of Attmt1 containing the integrated c-myc motif was verified by complete sequencing. A cloning diagram detailing the production of the overexpression construct pSR6 demonstrates the precise genetic architecture of the transformation system.  

4. Attmt1 overexpression lines were generated using the floral dip transformation process with Agrobacterium tumefaciens strain GV3101. Transgenic cell lines were selected based on kanamycin resistance, with subsequent Northern blot analysis confirming TMT1 overexpression in selected lines.  

5. T-DNA insertions into exon regions of Attmt1 and Attmt2 cDNA were mapped to verify genomic integration sites. Transgenic plants were cultivated under controlled conditions for detailed seed analysis.  

6. Quantitative analysis revealed significant increases in fatty acid content in seeds from TMT1 overexpressor lines, with lipid content increases up to 12% compared to wild-type seeds. Protein content showed parallel increases up to 14%.  

7. The thousand-kernel weight of Arabidopsis seeds increased substantially in TMT1 overexpressor lines, with gains ranging from 13% to 28% across different transgenic lines.  

8. Comprehensive analysis of oil and lipid contents demonstrated consistent improvements in both quantity and quality of storage products in TMT1 overexpressor seeds.  

9. While the number of seeds per silique remained consistent across genotypes (approximately 42 seeds), the total weight of seeds per plant increased dramatically in TMT1 overexpressors, reaching up to 218 mg per plant compared to 125 mg in wild-type plants.  

10. Developmental analysis showed accelerated growth in TMT1 overexpressor lines, with visible differences apparent after both 15 and 34 days of growth. These phenotypic improvements correlated with the measured increases in seed yield and quality.  

The examples demonstrate that targeted overexpression of TMT1 creates a synergistic improvement in multiple aspects of plant performance, including growth rate, photosynthetic efficiency, and seed production. The technology provides a robust platform for yield enhancement in commercial crop species.
