## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates generally to the field of cancer diagnostics and therapeutics, specifically to compositions and methods for assessing and treating metastatic cancer. Cancer cell invasion is a critical step in metastasis, the leading cause of cancer-related mortality. During invasion, cancer cells breach the basement membrane and migrate through the dense extracellular matrix of the tumor stroma to intravasate.  

Lamellipodium formation is a key process in cancer cell migration and invasion. These thin membrane protrusions at the leading edge of migrating cells are driven by the assembly of branched F-actin networks. The cytoskeletal regulator Lamellipodin (Lpd) localizes to lamellipodia and plays a crucial role in their formation. Lpd binds directly to both Ena/VASP proteins, which enhance actin filament elongation, and the Scar/WAVE complex, which activates the Arp2/3 complex to nucleate branched actin networks.  

Lpd-mediated cell migration has been shown to play important roles in cancer metastasis. The interaction between Lpd and its binding partners is regulated by phosphorylation events mediated by proto-oncogenic kinases c-Src and c-Abl. Despite this understanding, current biomarkers for metastatic cancer remain limited in their predictive value and reliability. There exists a significant unmet need for improved diagnostic methods capable of early detection of metastatic potential and for targeted therapeutic interventions that can effectively inhibit metastasis.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides compositions and methods for assessing and treating metastatic cancer based on Lamellipodin (Lpd) expression and activity. The invention is based on the discovery that Lpd serves as a reliable biomarker for metastatic cancer, with Lpd levels correlating strongly with metastatic potential and clinical outcomes.  

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for assessing metastatic cancer in a subject. The method comprises obtaining a biological sample from the subject, determining the level of Lpd protein or Lpd gene expression in the sample, and correlating the Lpd levels with the metastatic state of the cancer. The method may further comprise diagnosing or prognosing metastatic cancer based on the correlation.  

In another aspect, the invention provides an alternative method for assessing metastatic cancer comprising detecting Lpd protein using an Lpd-specific antibody, determining the amount of Lpd protein present, and correlating the Lpd protein levels with the metastatic state of the cancer. The antibody may be labeled with a detectable moiety for quantitative detection.  

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method comprising detecting Lpd gene expression through nucleic acid hybridization techniques. The method involves determining the level of Lpd gene expression and correlating this level with the metastatic state of the cancer. Stringent hybridization conditions may be employed to ensure specific detection of Lpd mRNA.  

The invention further provides methods involving extracting total RNA or protein from a biological sample, determining the level of Lpd protein or gene expression, correlating these levels with metastatic state, and administering an appropriate therapeutic regimen based on the correlation.  

Therapeutic agents according to the invention include nucleic acids such as siRNA targeting Lpd mRNA, antibodies that specifically bind Lpd protein, and small molecule inhibitors of Lpd activity or its interactions with binding partners. The invention also provides diagnostic kits containing reagents for detecting Lpd protein or nucleic acids, along with instructions for use in assessing metastatic cancer.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present invention addresses the critical need for reliable biomarkers of metastatic cancer and provides targeted therapeutic strategies based on the newly discovered role of Lamellipodin (Lpd) in cancer progression. Current biomarkers for metastatic potential suffer from limited specificity and predictive value, often failing to identify high-risk patients before overt metastasis occurs. The invention overcomes these limitations by leveraging the strong correlation between Lpd expression levels and metastatic behavior.  

The invention provides comprehensive definitions of terms used throughout. As used herein, "biomarker" refers to a biological molecule whose presence, absence, or quantity correlates with a particular disease state or clinical outcome. "Detect" encompasses any method for qualitatively or quantitatively identifying the presence of a molecule, including but not limited to immunological assays, hybridization techniques, and mass spectrometry.  

The term "effective amount" refers to a quantity of therapeutic agent sufficient to achieve a desired biological effect without causing unacceptable toxicity. "Invasive cancer" denotes malignancies capable of penetrating surrounding tissues, while "metastatic cancer" refers to cancers that have spread from the primary site to distant organs.  

The invention encompasses methods for assessing metastatic cancer by obtaining biological samples including but not limited to tissue biopsies, blood samples, or other bodily fluids. Samples may be fixed, labeled, and prepared for analysis through standard histological techniques including dehydration, clearing, sectioning, and mounting.  

Antibodies according to the invention include isolated monoclonal antibodies that specifically bind Lpd. These may be human antibodies, humanized antibodies, or chimeric antibodies containing rodent hypervariable regions grafted onto human framework regions. Antibody fragments including Fab, F(ab')2, and single-chain variable fragments are also encompassed.  

The invention provides nucleic acid probes that selectively hybridize to Lpd mRNA under stringent conditions. Such probes may be labeled with detectable moieties including fluorophores, enzymes, or radioactive isotopes. Probe lengths may range from 15 to 100 nucleotides, with modifications including phosphorothioate linkages or 2'-O-methyl substitutions to enhance stability.  

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting Lpd mRNA represent another aspect of the invention. These may comprise 19-25 nucleotide duplexes with 1-4 nucleotide 3' overhangs. Chemical modifications including 2'-fluoro or locked nucleic acid substitutions may be incorporated to enhance stability and specificity.  

Therapeutic compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration including oral, parenteral, or topical delivery. Pharmaceutical carriers include sterile aqueous solutions, oily suspensions, tablets, capsules, suppositories, and other dosage forms known in the art.  

The invention provides detailed methods for diagnosing metastatic cancer based on Lpd expression. One such method comprises obtaining a biological sample, extracting RNA or protein, determining Lpd expression levels, correlating these levels with metastatic potential, and administering appropriate therapeutics. The correlation may involve comparison to predetermined reference values or to control samples from healthy tissue.  

Alternative diagnostic methods employ Lpd-specific antibodies in immunological assays such as ELISA, Western blot, or immunohistochemistry. Nucleic acid-based detection methods include quantitative PCR, Northern blot, and in situ hybridization. These techniques may be adapted for high-throughput screening in clinical settings.  

The invention further provides methods for treating subjects at risk of metastatic cancer by correlating Lpd expression levels with metastatic potential and administering an effective course of therapy. Therapeutic agents may include Lpd inhibitors, antibodies blocking Lpd interactions, or nucleic acids reducing Lpd expression. Combination therapies incorporating standard chemotherapeutic agents are also envisioned.  

Dosage regimens are tailored based on factors including patient age, disease severity, and therapeutic index. Toxicity and efficacy may be determined through standard preclinical models, with human doses extrapolated from animal data using established conversion factors.  

Diagnostic kits according to the invention contain reagents for detecting Lpd protein or nucleic acids along with appropriate controls. ## EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention without limiting its scope:  

Example 1 demonstrates the correlation between Lpd expression and breast cancer metastasis. Analysis of publicly available datasets revealed that Lpd mRNA levels were significantly elevated in metastatic breast tumors compared to matched healthy tissue. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that high Lpd expression correlated with reduced metastasis-free survival across multiple independent patient cohorts.  

Immunohistochemical staining of tumor tissue microarrays with anti-Lpd antibodies provided complementary protein-level data. Quantitative analysis demonstrated that increased Lpd staining intensity, particularly at the plasma membrane, correlated significantly with reduced disease-free and metastasis-free intervals. These findings establish Lpd as a robust biomarker for metastatic potential.  

Example 2 establishes Lpd's functional role in metastasis using orthotopic mouse models. Stable knockdown of Lpd in highly metastatic LM2 breast cancer cells did not affect primary tumor growth but dramatically reduced lung metastasis incidence and burden. Intravital imaging revealed that Lpd-depleted tumors exhibited fewer motile cells with impaired directional migration and protrusion formation.  

Example 3 investigates Lpd's mechanism in cancer cell invasion. EGF-stimulated protrusion assays showed that Lpd depletion reduced lamellipodial persistence and distance without affecting protrusion speed. Lpd was rapidly recruited to the cell edge following growth factor stimulation, and its depletion delayed protrusion initiation. Barbed-end assays demonstrated that Lpd promotes actin polymerization downstream of EGFR activation.  

Example 4 examines Lpd's role in chemotactic responses. Micropipette assays generating localized EGF gradients revealed that control cells formed protrusions toward the gradient, while Lpd-depleted cells showed random protrusion orientation. Rescue experiments demonstrated that Lpd's interaction with Ena/VASP proteins was essential for chemosensing, while its binding to Scar/WAVE was required for random migration.  

Example 5 characterizes the regulation of Lpd interactions by tyrosine kinases. Immunoprecipitation experiments showed that c-Src phosphorylates Lpd and enhances its binding to the Scar/WAVE complex without affecting Ena/VASP interactions. Mutational analysis identified specific tyrosine residues critical for these regulatory events. Functional assays demonstrated that phosphorylation-site mutants failed to rescue invasion defects in Lpd-depleted cells.  

Example 6 evaluates therapeutic targeting of Lpd in metastatic cancer. Pharmacological inhibition of c-Src or c-Abl kinases reduced breast cancer cell invasiveness in 3D assays. Similarly, siRNA-mediated Lpd knockdown impaired matrix degradation at invadopodia, suggesting that Lpd inhibition may block multiple steps in the metastatic cascade.  

These examples collectively demonstrate that Lpd serves as both a reliable biomarker for metastatic potential and a promising therapeutic target. The invention provides comprehensive methods for leveraging these discoveries in clinical practice, from early detection to targeted intervention.
