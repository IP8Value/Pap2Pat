# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates to the field of cell culture, specifically to methods for culturing human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) in a defined, xeno-free, and serum-free system. Keratinocyte culturing is an essential technique for regenerative medicine, particularly for treating severe skin disorders such as epidermolysis bullosa and large burns. Traditional methods for culturing HEKs rely on co-culture with murine 3T3-J2 fibroblast feeder layers, bovine serum, and cholera toxin, as described in the Rheinwald and Green (R&G) protocol.  

The current gold standard protocol using the R&G method presents significant limitations. The use of mouse feeder cells introduces xenogeneic components that pose risks of zoonotic pathogen transmission and immunogenic reactions in patients. Additionally, bovine serum and cholera toxin are classified as high-risk ancillary materials under U.S. Pharmacopeia guidelines, complicating regulatory approval for clinical applications. Existing attempts to develop feeder-free and xeno-free alternatives have been largely unsuccessful, often still requiring initial expansion on 3T3 feeders or relying on undefined supplements such as bovine pituitary extract.  

There remains an unmet clinical need for a fully defined, xeno-free, and serum-free system that supports the robust expansion of HEKs while maintaining their proliferative capacity and differentiation potential. Such a system would eliminate regulatory hurdles associated with xenogeneic components and provide a safer platform for cell-based therapies.  

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION  

The present invention provides a novel keratinocyte culturing method that overcomes the limitations of existing systems by utilizing defined laminin matrices in a xeno-free and serum-free environment. The disclosed method eliminates the need for murine feeder cells, bovine serum, or cholera toxin, thereby providing a clinically compliant system for HEK expansion.  

The system employs laminin matrices, specifically Laminin-511 (LN-511) and Laminin-421 (LN-421), which have been shown to support HEK adhesion, proliferation, and long-term culture. These laminins are coated onto tissue culture plates to provide a biomimetic substrate that mimics the natural basement membrane environment of keratinocytes. The cell culture medium used in this system is a chemically defined Keratinocyte Growth Medium (KGM-CD), free from animal-derived components.  

Key features of the invention include:  
- Confirmation of keratinocyte progenitor markers (e.g., KRT5, KRT14, p63) via immunofluorescence and flow cytometry.  
- A detailed plating and culturing process that ensures high viability and proliferative capacity.  
- A trypsinization protocol optimized for laminin-coated substrates.  
- A maintenance system that prevents premature differentiation and senescence.  

Additionally, the invention encompasses a kit for culturing keratinocytes, comprising pre-coated laminin matrices, defined culture medium, and instructions for use.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The present patent application discloses a fully defined, xeno-free, and serum-free system for culturing human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs). The following sections provide a detailed description of the invention, including definitions, methodologies, and experimental validation.  

### Definitions and Terminology  
- **Keratinocytes**: Epithelial cells that constitute the majority of the epidermis, responsible for barrier function and wound healing.  
- **Basement Membrane (BM)**: A specialized extracellular matrix that underlies epithelial tissues, containing laminins, collagens, and proteoglycans.  
- **Laminin Isoforms**: Heterotrimeric proteins composed of α, β, and γ chains (e.g., LN-511: α5β1γ1; LN-421: α4β2γ1).  

### Laminin Matrices  
The invention utilizes laminin matrices to replace murine feeder cells. Laminin-511 and Laminin-421 were identified as optimal substrates due to their presence in the epidermal BM and their ability to sustain HEK proliferation. These laminins are coated onto tissue culture plates at a concentration of 2.5 µg/cm².  

### Cell Culture Medium  
The culture medium (KGM-CD) is chemically defined and free from animal-derived components. Key ingredients include:  
- Basal medium (DMEM/F12)  
- Insulin  
- Epidermal growth factor (EGF)  
- Hydrocortisone  
- Ascorbic acid  

Optional supplements may include trace elements or antioxidants to enhance cell viability.  

### System Summary  
The disclosed system combines laminin-coated substrates with a defined medium to achieve:  
- High colony-forming efficiency.  
- Maintenance of basal progenitor markers over multiple passages.  
- Genetic stability confirmed by karyotyping.  

### EXAMPLES  

#### Example 1: Isolation and Primary Culture of HEKs  
1. HEKs were isolated from surgical waste using dispase II and trypsin-EDTA.  
2. Cells were seeded on LN-511 or LN-421-coated plates in KGM-CD.  
3. Immunofluorescent staining confirmed expression of laminin isoforms and keratinocyte markers.  

#### Example 2: Long-Term Culture and Proliferation  
1. HEKs were serially passaged on laminin substrates.  
2. Population doublings were calculated, showing comparable growth to the R&G method.  
3. qPCR analysis demonstrated stable expression of basal (KRT5, KRT14) and differentiation markers (KRT1, KRT10).  

#### Example 3: Organotypic Culture and Graft Formation  
1. HEKs were seeded on de-epidermalized dermis (DED) and lifted to an air-liquid interface.  
2. Histology revealed stratified epidermis with intact basal and differentiated layers.  

#### Example 4: In Vivo Transplantation  
1. HEK grafts were transplanted onto nude mice using a flap method.  
2. H&E staining and immunostaining confirmed functional human epidermis formation.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a breakthrough in keratinocyte culturing by eliminating xenogeneic components while maintaining cell proliferation and differentiation capacity. The disclosed method offers significant advantages for clinical applications, including:  
- Compliance with regulatory standards for cell-based therapies.  
- Enhanced safety by removing animal-derived materials.  
- Scalability for large-scale production of epidermal grafts.  

The scope of this disclosure encompasses variations in laminin combinations, medium formulations, and culture conditions that fall within the inventive concept.
