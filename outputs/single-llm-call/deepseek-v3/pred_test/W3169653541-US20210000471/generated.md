# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD  

The present invention relates to arteriovenous grafts used for hemodialysis access. More specifically, the invention pertains to an optimized arteriovenous graft design that reduces pathological flow fields at the graft-to-vein anastomosis by tailoring the anastomosis angle to maintain physiological shear strain rates. The invention further discloses specific geometric configurations, including micro-digit grooves and lachrymiform indents at the venous anastomosis end, which collectively improve graft patency by minimizing thrombus formation.  

## BACKGROUND  

Hemodialysis is a life-sustaining procedure required by over 300,000 patients annually in the United States alone to manage end-stage renal disease. This treatment necessitates reliable vascular access to divert arterial blood into a hemodialyzer before returning it to venous circulation. Arteriovenous grafts, typically constructed from polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), are surgically implanted to provide durable access points for repeated hemodialysis cannulation. These grafts are anastomosed to an artery at one end and a vein at the other, with the body of the graft tunneled subcutaneously to facilitate percutaneous access.  

Despite advances in graft materials, arteriovenous grafts suffer from high failure rates, with up to 30% failing within the first year due to thrombosis or stenosis at the graft-to-vein anastomosis. A primary contributor to graft failure is pathological flow dynamics at the venous anastomosis, where non-physiological shear strain rates promote intimal hyperplasia and thrombus formation. Shear rates below 50 s⁻¹ increase fibrin deposition, while rates exceeding 1000 s⁻¹ cause endothelial micro-injuries and platelet aggregation. Current graft designs do not account for the critical role of anastomosis angle in modulating these shear forces, leading to suboptimal flow patterns that accelerate graft failure.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY  

The present invention provides an arteriovenous graft with an optimized venous anastomosis angle that minimizes pathological shear strain rates. Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations demonstrate that a 30° anastomosis angle optimally balances high and low shear rate extremes, reducing thrombogenic flow perturbations. The graft further incorporates micro-digit grooves and a lachrymiform indent at the venous end to further refine flow fields. These geometric features are pre-formed during manufacturing, eliminating reliance on surgical technique to achieve hemodynamic optimization. The invention also specifies biocompatible materials and dimensional tolerances to ensure clinical feasibility.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following disclosure provides a comprehensive description of the invention, including definitions, hemodynamic principles, graft configurations, and computational validation.  

### Definitions  
- **Arteriovenous graft**: A synthetic conduit connecting an artery to a vein for hemodialysis access.  
- **Anastomosis angle**: The angle between the longitudinal axis of the graft and the vein at the point of attachment.  
- **Shear strain rate**: The rate of deformation due to shear stress in a fluid (units: s⁻¹).  
- **Micro-digit grooves**: Microscopic longitudinal channels at the venous anastomosis end to modulate flow separation.  
- **Lachrymiform indent**: A teardrop-shaped depression at the graft's venous end to reduce flow stagnation.  

### Hemodynamic Principles  
Blood flow in arteriovenous grafts must maintain shear strain rates between 50 s⁻¹ and 1000 s⁻¹ to avoid thrombogenesis. The anastomosis angle critically influences these rates by altering flow velocity profiles. At angles >40°, high-velocity jets create localized regions of excessive shear (>1000 s⁻¹), while angles <20° promote flow stagnation and low shear zones (<50 s⁻¹). The invention solves this by pre-forming the graft with a 30° anastomosis angle, which CFD simulations identify as the optimal compromise.  

### Graft Configurations  
The graft features a tapered design, transitioning from 4 mm (arterial end) to 7 mm (venous end) to match typical vessel diameters. The venous end is pre-cut at a 30° angle and includes:  
1. **Micro-digit grooves**: 10–20 longitudinal channels, each 100–200 µm wide and 50–100 µm deep, spaced 200–300 µm apart. These disrupt boundary layer separation.  
2. **Lachrymiform indent**: A 1–2 mm depression aligned with the graft's inner curvature to redirect flow away from stagnation points.  

### Computational Validation  
CFD models simulating pulsatile blood flow (Reynolds number ≈1800) confirm that the 30° angle reduces pathological shear areas by 60% compared to conventional 90° designs. The grooves and indent further reduce shear rate extremes by 25% by promoting laminar flow transitions.  

### Materials and Dimensions  
The graft is fabricated from biocompatible PTFE or polyurethane, with a wall thickness of 500–700 µm. Total length ranges from 150–200 mm, with tolerances of ±2 mm to accommodate anatomical variations.  

### Method of Use  
The graft is selected based on the patient's venous diameter and surgically implanted with the pre-formed venous end aligned to the vein at the prescribed 30° angle. This alignment is facilitated by visual markers on the graft exterior.

Micro-digit grooves reduced flow separation vortices by 40%, quantified by particle tracking velocimetry in silico.

A prototype graft with a 30° angle and grooves maintained patency in a porcine model for 6 months without thrombosis, compared to 3 months for standard grafts.

Grafts with anastomosis angles of 30°±5° retained 90% of the hemodynamic benefit, confirming robustness to minor surgical deviations.
