# DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to methods for characterizing macrophage polarization states using high-resolution transcriptome analysis. More specifically, the invention provides novel methods for identifying M1-like and M2-like macrophages through RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis, which enables detection of differential gene expression, alternative splicing events, and novel cell surface markers with superior resolution compared to conventional microarray techniques.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Macrophages are key immune cells involved in tissue homeostasis and inflammatory responses. These cells exhibit remarkable plasticity, allowing them to adopt distinct functional phenotypes in response to environmental stimuli. Broadly, macrophages can be classified into classically activated M1-like macrophages, which drive inflammatory responses, and alternatively activated M2-like macrophages, which promote tissue repair and immunoregulation.  

M1-like macrophages are typically induced by interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) alone or in combination with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) or tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α). These cells exhibit an IL-12high, IL-23high, and IL-10low cytokine profile and express surface markers such as CD86, CD64, and CD16. Conversely, M2-like macrophages are induced by cytokines such as interleukin-4 (IL-4) and interleukin-13 (IL-13), leading to an IL-12low, IL-23low phenotype with expression of CD23.  

Transcriptional reprogramming plays a central role in macrophage polarization. Previous studies have relied on microarray-based gene expression profiling to assess transcriptional changes associated with M1 and M2 polarization. While these studies have provided valuable insights, microarrays suffer from limitations including restricted dynamic range, high background noise, and inability to detect alternative splicing events and novel transcripts.  

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) has emerged as a powerful alternative to microarray analysis, offering several advantages including increased sensitivity, broader dynamic range, and the ability to detect alternative splicing, novel transcripts, and low-abundance mRNA species. Despite these advantages, prior to the present invention, RNA-seq had not been systematically applied to characterize transcriptional changes in human macrophage polarization.  

## SHORT DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention addresses the need for high-resolution transcriptome data to better understand macrophage polarization. By applying RNA-seq to M1-like and M2-like macrophages, the invention provides novel insights into human macrophage biology, including the identification of previously unrecognized marker genes, differential promoter usage, alternative transcription start sites, and splicing variants.  

A key aspect of the invention is a method for identifying M1-like and M2-like macrophages based on RNA-seq analysis. In one preferred embodiment, the method comprises isolating RNA from macrophage populations, performing RNA-seq, and analyzing differential gene expression to classify polarization states. Another preferred embodiment involves detecting novel cell surface markers, such as CD120b, SLAMF7, CD1a, CD1b, CD93, and CD226, which exhibit distinct expression patterns in M1-like and M2-like macrophages.  

The invention further provides methods for distinguishing macrophage subsets using quantitative PCR (qPCR), hybridization arrays, or flow cytometry based on RNA-seq-derived marker genes. These methods enable improved characterization of macrophage polarization states in research and clinical settings.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

Macrophages are highly plastic immune cells that adopt distinct functional phenotypes in response to environmental cues. The present invention leverages RNA-seq technology to provide unprecedented resolution in analyzing transcriptional changes associated with macrophage polarization.  

RNA-seq offers significant advantages over microarray analysis, including a broader dynamic range, lower background noise, and the ability to detect novel transcripts and splicing variants. In the context of macrophage polarization, RNA-seq enables the identification of differentially expressed genes with higher sensitivity and accuracy.  

Analysis of RNA-seq data from M1-like and M2-like macrophages revealed novel marker genes associated with polarization states. Notably, genes such as PDLIM7 exhibit differential splicing patterns between M1-like and M2-like macrophages, which were undetectable by microarray analysis. Additionally, RNA-seq identified differential promoter usage and alternative transcription start sites in over 50 gene loci, providing new insights into transcriptional regulation during macrophage polarization.  

A key limitation of microarray technology is its inability to detect low-abundance transcripts and splicing variants. RNA-seq overcomes these limitations, enabling the identification of novel cell surface markers such as CD120b and SLAMF7 for M1-like macrophages, and CD1a, CD1b, CD93, and CD226 for M2-like macrophages. These markers were validated using flow cytometry, demonstrating their utility in distinguishing macrophage subsets.  

The invention provides several method embodiments for identifying macrophage polarization states. In one aspect, the method comprises designing primers for qPCR based on RNA-seq-identified marker genes. In another aspect, the method involves designing hybridization probes for microarray analysis of polarization-specific transcripts. A further aspect includes the use of binding molecules, such as antibodies, to detect cell surface markers identified through RNA-seq.  

Among the novel markers identified, CD120b (TNFR2) and SLAMF7 were found to be preferentially expressed in M1-like macrophages, while CD1a, CD1b, CD93, and CD226 were upregulated in M2-like macrophages. These markers provide new tools for studying macrophage biology in health and disease.  

The advantages of RNA-seq over microarray analysis are further highlighted in network-based analyses of macrophage polarization. RNA-seq data revealed enriched biological pathways and gene networks, such as the apolipoprotein L (APOL) family and leukocyte immunoglobulin-like receptors (LILRs), which were poorly represented in microarray data. These findings underscore the superior information content of RNA-seq for understanding macrophage biology.  

### EXAMPLES  

The following examples illustrate specific embodiments of the invention but are not intended to limit its scope.

#### Example 1: Macrophage Model System  
Human CD14+ monocytes were isolated from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and differentiated into macrophages using granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF). Polarization was induced with IFN-γ (M1-like) or IL-4 (M2-like). Surface marker expression was analyzed by flow cytometry, revealing distinct profiles for M1-like (CD64high, CD86high) and M2-like (CD23high) macrophages.  

#### Example 2: Microarray-Based Profiling  
Gene expression profiling was performed using Illumina Human HT-12V3 BeadChips. Principal component analysis (PCA) and hierarchical clustering segregated M1-like and M2-like macrophages based on known marker genes. However, microarray data exhibited limited dynamic range and failed to detect low-abundance transcripts.  

#### Example 3: RNA-Seq Transcriptome Analysis  
RNA-seq was performed on M1-like and M2-like macrophages, yielding high-resolution transcriptome data. Comparison with microarray data demonstrated superior detection of differential gene expression and splicing variants by RNA-seq.  

#### Example 4: Differential Expression Analysis  
RNA-seq identified 1,736 genes upregulated in M1-like macrophages and 822 genes upregulated in M2-like macrophages, surpassing the detection capacity of microarrays. Fold-change distributions revealed a broader dynamic range for RNA-seq data.  

#### Example 5: Exon-Level Resolution  
RNA-seq enabled exon-level analysis of macrophage markers such as CD68, CD64, and CD23. Differential exon usage was validated by qPCR, demonstrating the utility of RNA-seq for isoform-specific detection.  

#### Example 6: Network Analysis  
A priori knowledge-based network analysis using RNA-seq data revealed enriched pathways in M1-like and M2-like macrophages, including APOL and LILR gene families, which were underrepresented in microarray data.  

#### Example 7: Splice Variant Detection  
RNA-seq identified differential splicing variants in genes such as PDLIM7, with M1-like macrophages preferentially expressing PDLIM7 v1 and M2-like macrophages expressing PDLIM7 v2.  

#### Example 8: Novel Marker Validation  
Newly identified surface markers (CD120b, SLAMF7, CD1a, CD1b, CD93, CD226) were validated by flow cytometry, confirming their utility for distinguishing macrophage subsets.  

In conclusion, the invention provides novel methods for characterizing macrophage polarization states using RNA-seq, enabling the identification of previously unrecognized markers and transcriptional regulatory mechanisms. These advances have significant implications for understanding macrophage biology in health and disease.
