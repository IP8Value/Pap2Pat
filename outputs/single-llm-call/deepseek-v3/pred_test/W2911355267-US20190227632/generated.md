# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates generally to the field of haptic feedback devices and sensory integration systems. More specifically, the invention pertains to a wearable fingertip device capable of applying controlled compressive and shear forces to the finger pad for investigating and manipulating cutaneous sensations. The device facilitates the study of how tactile and non-tactile inputs contribute to fingertip position estimation and provides a platform for delivering precise haptic feedback in human-machine interfaces.  

## BACKGROUND  

Current devices for studying cutaneous sensations and delivering haptic feedback suffer from several limitations. Existing systems often lack the ability to independently control both normal (compressive) and tangential (shear) forces on the finger pad, limiting their utility in studying force-direction encoding properties of tactile afferents. Many conventional haptic devices either focus solely on compressive forces or provide shear forces without precise control over magnitude and direction. Additionally, current wearable fingertip devices frequently interfere with natural finger movements or fail to maintain consistent contact with the finger pad during motion. There remains a need for a compact, wearable device that can apply controlled combinations of compressive and shear forces while allowing natural finger positioning and movement.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides a novel wearable fingertip device for applying controlled cutaneous sensations through precise application of compressive and shear forces. The device comprises an upper assembly and a lower assembly configured to interface with a user's fingertip while allowing natural finger positioning and movement.  

The device includes several key components that enable its functionality. An upper platform serves as the primary interface with the finger pad, while a shear plate provides controlled tangential displacement of the skin surface. The device incorporates compressive racks and pinions to generate normal forces, along with dedicated motors for independently controlling both compressive and shear force application. An angular sensor monitors finger position, while a finger support structure maintains proper device alignment during use.  

The invention applies compressive force through a motor-driven pinion engaging with compressive rack apertures in the upper platform. Shear force is generated through controlled displacement of the shear plate relative to the upper platform. The device can apply these forces independently or concurrently, allowing investigation of combined force effects on tactile perception.  

A wearable fingertip embodiment of the device offers particular advantages for studying cutaneous sensations. The compact form factor allows natural hand movements while maintaining consistent contact with the finger pad. The device's ability to apply controlled combinations of normal and tangential forces enables precise investigation of how tactile inputs contribute to fingertip position estimation.  

In a first aspect, the invention provides a device comprising an upper assembly with an upper platform for finger pad contact, a shear plate for tangential displacement, and mechanisms for applying both compressive and shear forces. The upper assembly connects to a lower assembly that houses drive motors and electronics.  

The first aspect components operate through coordinated motor control. A compressive motor drives a pinion that engages with rack apertures in the upper platform to generate normal forces. A separate shear motor displaces the shear plate to create tangential skin stretch. Force sensors provide feedback for precise force control.  

In a second aspect, the invention provides a method for applying controlled cutaneous sensations. The method involves positioning a wearable device on the fingertip, applying predetermined combinations of compressive and shear forces, and monitoring the resulting perceptual effects. The method allows systematic investigation of how tactile inputs contribute to fingertip position estimation.  

The second aspect operation includes calibrating force application based on individual finger pad characteristics, delivering controlled force combinations, and recording perceptual responses. The method enables quantification of the relative contributions of tactile and non-tactile inputs to position estimation.  

In a third aspect, the invention provides a system for studying sensory integration. The system includes the wearable device, control electronics, and data acquisition components. The system facilitates investigation of how the central nervous system integrates tactile and non-tactile inputs for fingertip position estimation.  

The third aspect components work together to deliver controlled stimuli while recording perceptual and physiological responses. The system allows precise manipulation of tactile inputs while controlling for non-tactile factors, enabling isolation of tactile contributions to position estimation.
