# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND  

The present invention relates generally to quantum computing, and more specifically to systems and methods for optimizing Clifford quantum circuits. Quantum circuits are fundamental building blocks of quantum algorithms, representing sequences of quantum gates applied to qubits to perform computations. Among quantum circuits, Clifford circuits represent an important subclass that consists of gates from the Clifford group, including Hadamard (H), Phase (S), and controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates, as well as Pauli X, Y, and Z gates.  

Clifford circuits play a crucial role in various quantum computing applications, including quantum error correction, randomized benchmarking, quantum state distillation, and shadow tomography. A key property of Clifford circuits is that they form a unitary 2-design, meaning random Clifford operators can substitute for Haar random unitaries in applications depending only on second-order moments. However, implementing Clifford circuits efficiently remains challenging due to the computational complexity of synthesizing optimal gate sequences.  

Conventional approaches to Clifford circuit synthesis face several limitations. Template matching techniques, while effective for universal quantum circuits, do not fully exploit the specific algebraic structure of Clifford circuits. Similarly, peephole optimization methods are constrained by the need to store large databases of optimal circuits and perform numerous lookups, making them impractical for circuits with more than six qubits. Furthermore, existing synthesis methods often produce suboptimal gate counts, particularly for entangling gates like CNOT, which are typically more expensive to implement physically than single-qubit gates.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention discloses novel systems and methods for optimizing Clifford quantum circuits through partitioned template matching and symbolic peephole optimization. The invention addresses the limitations of conventional synthesis techniques by leveraging the unique properties of Clifford circuits to achieve more efficient gate counts, particularly for entangling gates.  

The system comprises several key components: a partition component that divides a Clifford circuit into distinct computation, swap, and Pauli stages; a template component that applies Clifford-specific template matching to the computation stage; a floating component that implements gate pushing techniques; a SWAP component that optimizes swap gate placement; and a symbolic component that facilitates peephole optimization through symbolic Pauli gates.  

The method involves first partitioning the input Clifford circuit by pushing Pauli and swap gates to the end of the circuit. The computation stage is then optimized using specialized templates that take advantage of Clifford circuit properties. Swap gates are aligned with entangling gates to minimize their implementation cost. Symbolic peephole optimization is performed by introducing symbolic Pauli gates to represent entangling interactions between qubit subsets, enabling local optimization while preserving global circuit functionality.  

The invention can be implemented as a computer-implemented method, a system comprising specialized hardware components, or a computer program product stored on non-transitory computer-readable media. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements over conventional techniques, achieving optimal CNOT gate counts for 97.9% of tested 6-qubit circuits and reducing average CNOT counts by 64.7% for larger circuits.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION  

The following detailed description provides a comprehensive explanation of the invention's various embodiments and implementations. While specific examples are provided, these should not be construed as limiting the scope of the invention, which is defined by the appended claims.  

Quantum circuits form the basis for implementing quantum algorithms on physical quantum computers. A Clifford circuit is a specific type of quantum circuit composed exclusively of gates from the Clifford group, which includes Hadamard (H), Phase (S), and controlled-NOT (CNOT) gates, along with Pauli X, Y, and Z gates. The significance of Clifford circuits stems from their role in quantum error correction, randomized benchmarking, and other fundamental quantum computing applications.  

Clifford circuits possess unique mathematical properties that distinguish them from general quantum circuits. Most notably, Clifford gates map tensor products of Pauli matrices to other tensor products of Pauli matrices. This property enables efficient simulation of Clifford circuits using classical computers and facilitates specialized optimization techniques that are not applicable to general quantum circuits.  

The present invention introduces two novel optimization techniques specifically designed for Clifford circuits: partitioned template matching and symbolic peephole optimization. These methods address key limitations in conventional circuit synthesis approaches by exploiting the algebraic structure of Clifford circuits.  

Partitioned template matching begins by dividing the input Clifford circuit into three distinct stages: computation, swap, and Pauli. This partitioning is achieved by systematically pushing Pauli gates to the end of the circuit using commutation rules specific to Clifford gates. The computation stage, containing only H, S, CNOT, and CZ gates, is then optimized using a library of Clifford-specific templates. These templates represent sequences of gates that collectively implement the identity operation, allowing replacement of matched subsequences with more efficient alternatives.  

The swap stage is optimized separately by aligning swap gates with existing entangling gates. This technique recognizes that a swap gate can be implemented at the effective cost of one entangling gate when properly merged with a CNOT or CZ gate. The optimization process involves resynthesizing the swap stage to maximize such alignments, significantly reducing the overall gate count.  

Symbolic peephole optimization represents a novel extension of conventional peephole techniques. The method introduces symbolic Pauli gates (SPGs) - single-qubit gates parameterized by binary variables - to represent entangling interactions between a selected subset of qubits and the remainder of the circuit. These symbolic gates enable local optimization of subcircuits while preserving the global functionality of the complete circuit.  

The optimization process for a subcircuit with SPGs involves several key steps. First, entangling CNOT gates are partitioned into groups based on their control qubits. Each entangling CNOT is then expanded using symbolic variables, transforming the subcircuit into a Clifford+SPG circuit. This transformed circuit is optimized using dynamic programming techniques that respect the temporal ordering of SPGs and preserve the overall phase of the circuit. The optimization minimizes a cost function that accounts for both CNOT gates and SPGs, ensuring efficient implementation.  

The system architecture for implementing these optimization techniques comprises several specialized components. The partition component receives an input Clifford circuit and divides it into computation, swap, and Pauli stages. The template component applies Clifford-specific templates to the computation stage, while the floating component implements gate pushing techniques to facilitate template matching. The SWAP component optimizes the swap stage through gate alignment, and the symbolic component manages the creation and optimization of circuits containing SPGs.  

These components operate iteratively to progressively optimize the input circuit. In one embodiment, the system first applies partitioned template matching to the computation stage, followed by swap optimization. Symbolic peephole optimization is then performed on selected subcircuits, with the process repeating until no further improvements are found. A final pass of template matching may be applied to reduce single-qubit gate counts.  

The invention can be implemented in various forms, including as a computer-implemented method, a specialized hardware system, or a software product. In one embodiment, the optimization system receives a Clifford unitary or circuit as input, processes it through the described components, and outputs an optimized circuit with reduced gate count. The system may include a user interface for parameter configuration and result visualization.  

Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the disclosed techniques. For 6-qubit Clifford circuits with known optimal CNOT counts, the methods recover optimal implementations for 97.9% of test cases. For larger circuits simulating Hamiltonian evolution, the techniques achieve an average 64.7% reduction in CNOT gate count compared to conventional synthesis methods. These improvements are particularly significant given that entangling gates typically represent the most expensive operations in physical implementations.

By leveraging the unique properties of Clifford circuits, the disclosed methods achieve superior results compared to general-purpose optimization techniques. The use of symbolic Pauli gates enables local optimization while maintaining global correctness, overcoming limitations of conventional peephole approaches. The partitioned structure allows targeted optimization of different circuit segments using appropriate techniques, maximizing efficiency gains.
