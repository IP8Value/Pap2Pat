# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

### Field of the Invention  

The present invention relates generally to microfluidic devices and more specifically to pumpless microfluidic systems for cell culture applications. Microfluidic devices are miniaturized systems that manipulate small volumes of fluids through channels with dimensions typically ranging from tens to hundreds of micrometers. These devices have found widespread applications in biological research, particularly in the field of organ-on-a-chip technology where they are used to create physiologically relevant models of human tissues and organs.  

### Description of Related Art  

Microfluidic devices have revolutionized biological research by enabling precise control over cellular microenvironments. Traditional microfluidic systems rely on external pumps to drive fluid flow through the microchannels. While effective, these pump-based systems present several limitations including high cost, complexity of operation, and potential for bubble formation or leakage. The requirement for external pumps and tubing creates significant barriers to adoption, particularly for laboratories without specialized microfluidic expertise.  

In the context of cell culture applications, maintaining dynamic culture conditions is essential for proper cell function and differentiation. Organ-on-a-chip devices attempt to recreate key aspects of human physiology by subjecting cells to fluid flow and mechanical forces that mimic natural tissue environments. Current approaches using external pumps often fail to provide the precise, stable flow conditions required for optimal cell culture while also being cumbersome to operate.  

Alternative approaches to pump-free fluid delivery have been explored, including rocker-based platforms that create bidirectional flow through gravity-induced motion. However, these systems generate complex, non-uniform flow patterns that result in inconsistent shear stress distributions across cell monolayers. Additionally, rocker platforms remain relatively expensive and require significant laboratory space.  

There exists a clear need for simpler, more accessible microfluidic perfusion systems that can provide stable, controlled flow conditions without requiring external pumps or complex instrumentation. Such systems would significantly lower the barrier to entry for organ-on-a-chip technology while maintaining the precise control over cellular microenvironments that is essential for physiologically relevant cell culture.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides an apparatus and method for generating controlled fluid flow in microfluidic devices without requiring external pumps or power sources. The apparatus comprises a fluid reservoir connected to a microfluidic channel and an evaporation reservoir containing a wetting, wicking or hydrophilic structure. The hydrophilic structure extends from the evaporation reservoir and serves to continuously draw fluid from the fluid reservoir through the microfluidic channel via capillary action and evaporation.  

The method involves providing a microfluidic device with an inlet fluid reservoir and an outlet evaporation reservoir containing a hydrophilic wicking element. As fluid is drawn through the wicking element and evaporates, it creates continuous flow through the microfluidic channel without requiring external pressure or pumping mechanisms. The flow rate can be precisely controlled by adjusting parameters such as the length, diameter, and material properties of the wicking element.  

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention introduces a novel microfluidic apparatus that enables pumpless fluid flow for cell culture and organ-on-a-chip applications. The apparatus comprises several key components including a microfluidic channel, a fluid reservoir, an evaporation reservoir, and a hydrophilic wicking structure that connects these elements.  

The microfluidic channel forms the central pathway through which fluid flows and interacts with cultured cells. The channel is typically fabricated from biocompatible materials such as polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) bonded to a glass substrate, though other materials may be used. The dimensions of the channel are carefully controlled to achieve desired flow characteristics and shear stresses appropriate for specific cell types.  

The fluid reservoir serves as the source of culture medium or other fluids to be perfused through the system. It is connected to the inlet of the microfluidic channel and may include features to minimize evaporation from this reservoir. The evaporation reservoir is positioned at the outlet of the microfluidic channel and contains the hydrophilic wicking structure which extends outward from the device.  

The hydrophilic wicking structure, which may comprise threads, fibers, or other porous materials, serves as both a capillary conduit and evaporation surface. As fluid is drawn through the wicking structure by capillary action, it continuously evaporates from the exposed surfaces, creating a sustained flow through the entire system. The rate of flow is determined by the balance between capillary uptake and evaporation rate, both of which can be tuned by adjusting the properties of the wicking structure.  

This pumpless apparatus offers significant advantages over traditional pump-based systems. It eliminates the need for expensive precision pumps and complex tubing connections that are prone to leaks and bubble formation. The system operates entirely passively, requiring no external power source or moving parts. This makes it particularly suitable for long-term cell culture applications within standard incubator environments.  

The invention is especially valuable for organ-on-a-chip applications where precise control over fluid flow and shear stress is critical for proper cell function. The market for organ-on-a-chip technology is growing rapidly as these systems offer more physiologically relevant alternatives to traditional cell culture and animal models. The present invention addresses a key limitation in current organ-on-a-chip systems by providing a simple, accessible method for creating dynamic culture environments.  

Maintaining appropriate fluidic conditions is essential for recreating physiological microenvironments in vitro. Different cell types require specific ranges of fluid shear stress for proper differentiation and function. The present invention allows precise control over these parameters through careful design of the microfluidic channel dimensions and wicking structure properties.  

The pumpless apparatus represents an elegant solution to the challenges of traditional perfusion systems. By relying on fundamental physical principles of capillary action and evaporation, it provides stable, long-term flow without complex equipment. This makes the technology particularly accessible to non-specialist users who may not have expertise in microfluidic systems.  

The term "microfluidic" as used herein refers to devices or systems that handle fluid volumes in the microliter to nanoliter range, typically through channels with cross-sectional dimensions between 1-1000 micrometers. The microfluidic channel in the present invention may incorporate various features such as valves, chambers, or surface modifications to enhance its functionality for specific applications.  

The solid substrate supporting the microfluidic components may be fabricated from materials including but not limited to PDMS, glass, thermoplastics, or hydrogels. Standard soft lithography techniques are typically employed for device fabrication, though other methods such as injection molding or 3D printing may be used. The fabrication process involves creating a master mold, casting the microfluidic structures, and bonding them to a substrate to form enclosed channels.  

The fluid reservoir and evaporation reservoir are integral components of the device design. The fluid reservoir must maintain sufficient volume to sustain continuous flow while minimizing evaporation losses. The evaporation reservoir is specifically designed to maximize evaporation from the wicking structure while preventing backflow or contamination.  

The wetting, wicking or hydrophilic structure may take various forms including threads, porous membranes, or patterned hydrophilic surfaces. The material properties of this structure, including its porosity, surface energy, and geometry, are carefully selected to achieve desired flow characteristics. Natural or synthetic fibers such as rayon, polyester, or glass may be used, with surface treatments applied to enhance wettability if needed.  

Control of flow rate is achieved through multiple parameters including the length and diameter of the wicking structure, environmental conditions such as temperature and humidity, and air circulation around the evaporation surface. Surface tension plays a critical role in maintaining continuous flow through the system, with the wicking structure acting as a conduit for both capillary transport and evaporation.  

The apparatus can achieve flow rates ranging from 0.15 to 6 µL/min, which is suitable for many cell culture applications. Different cell types have specific requirements for fluid shear stress, typically ranging from 0.002 to 0.135 dyn/cm². The present invention allows tuning of these parameters by adjusting the wicking structure properties and channel dimensions.  

Controlled fluid shear stress is particularly important for maintaining proper cell morphology and function. The apparatus enables precise delivery of these mechanical cues without the complexity of traditional perfusion systems. The wicking structure material may comprise various natural or synthetic fibers arranged as single strands or arrays to optimize performance.  

In one embodiment, the wicking structure consists of an array of hydrophilic pillars extending from the evaporation reservoir. This configuration maintains constant fluid volume in the system while allowing controlled evaporation. The positioning of the wicking structure relative to the microfluidic channel is carefully designed to ensure consistent flow without drying out the channel.  

The fundamental operating principle relies on evaporation-driven fluid flow. As fluid evaporates from the exposed surfaces of the wicking structure, it creates a continuous pull that draws fresh fluid from the reservoir through the microfluidic channel. An optional dead volume reservoir may be incorporated to prevent channel drying during temporary interruptions in flow.  

The apparatus offers several advantages including simplicity of operation, low cost, and compatibility with standard cell culture practices. It eliminates the need for external equipment while maintaining precise control over culture conditions. The system is particularly suitable for long-term experiments where stable flow must be maintained for days or weeks.  

In more complex embodiments, the invention may incorporate multiple parallel channels for co-culture applications. This allows simultaneous culture of different cell types under controlled flow conditions, enabling studies of cell-cell interactions and tissue-tissue interfaces. The multichannel configuration maintains the pumpless operation principle while expanding experimental capabilities.  

The invention encompasses both the apparatus design and methods for its use in cell culture applications. Various arrangements of components and operational parameters fall within the scope of the invention, as do methods for fabricating and utilizing the devices.  

### Examples  

The pumpless microfluidic apparatus has been successfully implemented in various organ-on-chip configurations. In one example, master molds were fabricated using standard soft lithography techniques. SU-8 photoresist was spun onto silicon wafers to create templates for PDMS casting. The microfluidic channel design included features for optimal fluid distribution and cell culture.  

PDMS layers were cast from these molds, cured, and bonded to glass coverslips using oxygen plasma treatment. Inlet and outlet reservoirs were created by punching holes in the PDMS at appropriate locations. The completed devices were sterilized and prepared for cell culture by treatment with ethanol and phosphate-buffered saline (PBS).  

Human intestinal epithelial Caco-2 cells were cultured in the devices to model intestinal barrier function. Channels were coated with Matrigel Basement Membrane Matrix to promote cell attachment and differentiation. Cells were seeded into the devices and maintained under flow conditions created by the pumpless system.  

Similarly, human lung epithelial A549 cells were cultured to model alveolar epithelium. The hydrophilic wicking fibers were characterized using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to verify their structure and capillary properties. Flow behavior was quantified using fluorescent nanoparticle tracking to confirm stable, controlled perfusion.  

Cells were observed and characterized under flow conditions using phase contrast and fluorescence microscopy. Immunostaining demonstrated proper formation of tight junctions and other epithelial characteristics in Caco-2 cells, while A549 cells showed appropriate responses to controlled shear stress. These experiments validated the apparatus's ability to maintain physiologically relevant culture conditions without external pumps.
