## DESCRIPTION

### FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates generally to the field of ophthalmic therapeutics and specifically to compositions and methods for treating corneal neovascularization using lipid mediators. More particularly, the invention encompasses the use of aspirin-triggered lipoxin analogs (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1) for the dual purpose of resolving inflammation and inhibiting pathological angiogenesis in ocular tissues. The scope of the invention includes pharmaceutical formulations containing these lipid mediators, methods of administration, and their application in treating conditions characterized by excessive corneal vascularization and inflammation. The invention further covers the use of these compounds in regulating vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) pathways and modulating immune cell infiltration in ocular tissues. The therapeutic applications extend to both inflammatory and non-inflammatory ocular conditions where angiogenesis contributes to disease pathology.

### BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Corneal neovascularization represents a significant clinical challenge in ophthalmology, often leading to visual impairment and blindness. The healthy cornea maintains its transparency through an avascular state, but pathological conditions can trigger the growth of new blood and lymphatic vessels into this normally privileged tissue. Current treatments for corneal neovascularization include corticosteroids, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, and anti-VEGF therapies, all of which present substantial limitations. Corticosteroids, while effective at reducing inflammation, carry risks of elevated intraocular pressure, cataract formation, and delayed wound healing. Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs often prove insufficient for severe cases and may cause corneal melting. Anti-VEGF therapies, though promising, require frequent administration and primarily target only one aspect of the angiogenic cascade without addressing the underlying inflammatory drivers. There exists a pressing need for therapeutic agents that can simultaneously address both the inflammatory and angiogenic components of corneal neovascularization through mechanisms distinct from current approaches. Such agents would ideally possess favorable safety profiles suitable for chronic use in ocular tissues while demonstrating efficacy across multiple pathways involved in disease pathogenesis.

### BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel therapeutic agents and methods for treating corneal neovascularization through administration of specialized pro-resolving lipid mediators, particularly aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4 analog (ATLa), resolvin D1 (RvD1), and resolvin E1 (RvE1). These compounds demonstrate dual anti-inflammatory and pro-resolving properties while simultaneously inhibiting pathological angiogenesis through multiple mechanisms. The invention encompasses the discovery that these lipid mediators significantly reduce inflammatory corneal hemangiogenesis by early regulation of resolution mechanisms in innate immune responses. ATLa exhibits particular potency by directly inhibiting VEGF-A-mediated angiogenesis in addition to its anti-inflammatory effects. The invention further includes pharmaceutical compositions containing these lipid mediators formulated for ocular administration, including subconjunctival, topical, and intraocular delivery. Experimental results demonstrate that treatment with these compounds reduces infiltration of neutrophils and macrophages into corneal tissue while decreasing expression of inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-1α, and IL-1β. The invention also covers the unexpected finding that these mediators selectively regulate hemangiogenesis without significantly affecting lymphangiogenesis, providing targeted control of pathological blood vessel growth while preserving beneficial lymphatic functions. Related patents in this field have focused on either anti-inflammatory or anti-angiogenic approaches separately, whereas the current invention provides the first unified treatment strategy addressing both aspects through a single class of compounds.

ATLa, a stable synthetic analog of aspirin-triggered lipoxin A4, exhibits enhanced metabolic stability while retaining the biological activities of native lipoxins. These compounds act through specific G-protein coupled receptors including ALX/FPR2 in humans and Fpr-rs2 in mice, modulating inflammatory responses and promoting resolution through mechanisms distinct from immunosuppression. The invention demonstrates that ATLa exerts potent effects on angiogenesis by reducing neutrophil and macrophage infiltration into corneal tissues while simultaneously downregulating expression of VEGF-A, VEGF-C, and VEGFR2. This dual mechanism provides superior therapeutic efficacy compared to approaches targeting either inflammation or angiogenesis alone.
