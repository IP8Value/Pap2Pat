# DESCRIPTION  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Amyloid diseases represent a class of debilitating disorders characterized by the abnormal accumulation of protein aggregates in tissues. Among these conditions, Alzheimer's disease stands as a particularly devastating neurodegenerative disorder affecting millions worldwide. The pathological hallmarks of Alzheimer's disease include extracellular amyloid plaques composed primarily of Aβ42 peptide aggregates and intraneuronal neurofibrillary tangles.  

The Aβ42 peptide, a 42-amino acid fragment derived from proteolytic processing of the amyloid precursor protein (APP), plays a central role in Alzheimer's disease pathogenesis. While historically considered inert, recent evidence suggests that soluble oligomeric forms of Aβ42, rather than mature fibrils, represent the primary neurotoxic species. These oligomers, including dimers, trimers, and tetramers, appear during early stages of Aβ42 assembly and may serve as precursors to larger aggregates.  

Current research models for studying Aβ42 oligomerization face significant limitations. Existing systems often focus on later-stage fibril formation rather than the critical early oligomerization events. Furthermore, many models lack the ability to monitor oligomerization through simple phenotypic readouts, complicating high-throughput screening approaches.  

The yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae offers distinct advantages as a model organism for studying protein aggregation. Yeast prions, particularly the [PSI+] prion formed by the Sup35 protein, provide valuable insights into amyloid formation mechanisms. Sup35p consists of three functional domains: an N-terminal prion domain (N), a middle domain (M), and a C-terminal release factor domain (RF). The prion properties of Sup35p depend entirely on its N-terminal domain, while the RF domain performs essential translational termination functions.  

Prion aggregates of Sup35p propagate through a mechanism involving the molecular chaperone Hsp104. This AAA+ ATPase shears amyloid fibers into smaller, transmissible units in an ATP-dependent manner. Guanidine hydrochloride inhibits Hsp104's ATPase activity, thereby curing yeast of the [PSI+] prion state. These features of the yeast prion system provide a foundation for developing novel models of protein aggregation.  

Previous attempts to model Aβ42 aggregation in simple organisms have met with limited success. While Escherichia coli systems have demonstrated utility in studying protein solubility, they lack the sophisticated protein quality control machinery present in eukaryotic cells. Existing yeast models of neurodegenerative diseases primarily focus on later-stage aggregation events rather than the initial oligomerization steps critical for toxicity.  

The present invention addresses these limitations through development of a novel yeast model system that specifically monitors early Aβ42 oligomerization events. This system employs an Aβ42-MRF fusion protein, where the Aβ42 peptide is fused to the MRF domains of Sup35p. The invention takes advantage of the well-characterized nonsense suppression assay in yeast, which provides a simple phenotypic readout for protein aggregation status. Through this system, the invention enables high-throughput screening for compounds that modulate Aβ42 oligomerization, offering significant advantages over existing models.  

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for studying and modulating Aβ42 oligomerization using a yeast model system. The invention centers on a fusion protein comprising the Aβ42 peptide linked to the MRF domains of the yeast translation termination factor Sup35p (AβMRF). This fusion protein forms SDS-stable oligomers that impair the translational termination activity of the RF domain, providing a readily scorable phenotypic readout.  

The yeast model system of the invention utilizes the ade1-14 nonsense allele as a reporter for AβMRF oligomerization status. In this system, oligomerization of AβMRF leads to impaired translational termination at the ade1-14 premature stop codon, resulting in adenine prototrophy (Ade+) and white colony color on complex media. Conversely, monomeric AβMRF maintains proper translational termination, yielding adenine auxotrophy (Ade-) and red colony color. This simple colorimetric assay enables rapid screening of conditions or compounds that affect Aβ42 oligomerization.  

The invention further encompasses specific mutations in the Aβ42 portion of AβMRF that disrupt oligomerization. These include double (F19,20T) and triple (F19,20T/I31P) substitutions in aggregation-critical regions of Aβ42. These mutant forms (Aβm1MRF and Aβm2MRF) serve as important controls, demonstrating reduced oligomerization and restored translational termination activity compared to wild-type AβMRF.  

The invention provides methods for modulating AβMRF oligomerization through chemical and genetic means. Guanidine hydrochloride treatment enhances AβMRF oligomerization, while deletion of HSP104 reduces oligomer formation. These effects demonstrate the system's responsiveness to known modulators of protein aggregation and provide tools for validating screening approaches.  

A key aspect of the invention involves methods for identifying therapeutic agents that interfere with Aβ42 oligomerization. The system enables screening of diverse compound libraries, including small molecules, peptides, nucleic acids, and natural products. Potential therapeutic agents identified through this system may find application in treating Alzheimer's disease and other amyloid disorders.  

The invention further provides yeast strains and kits for practicing the screening methods. These include strains containing the ade1-14 reporter allele along with various AβMRF constructs, as well as hsp104Δ derivatives for studying chaperone effects. The kits may incorporate these strains along with appropriate media and controls for high-throughput screening applications.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for screening agents that interfere with Aβ42 oligomerization, along with therapeutic applications of agents identified through these methods. The invention establishes a yeast cell-based assay system that specifically monitors early oligomerization events of Aβ42, distinct from later-stage fibril formation.  

The yeast cell-based assay of the invention comprises several key components. Central to the system is the AβMRF fusion protein, which links the Aβ42 peptide to the MRF domains of Sup35p. This fusion protein forms SDS-stable oligomers through interactions mediated by the Aβ42 portion, while the MRF portion provides a functional readout of oligomerization status through its translational termination activity.  

The assay utilizes specific yeast strains containing the ade1-14 nonsense allele as a reporter for AβMRF function. The ade1-14 allele contains a premature stop codon in the ADE1 gene, requiring efficient translational termination for proper adenine biosynthesis. Strains expressing oligomeric AβMRF show increased readthrough of the ade1-14 stop codon, resulting in adenine prototrophy and white colony color. This phenotypic readout enables simple visual assessment of AβMRF oligomerization status.  

The invention encompasses various yeast strains optimized for screening applications. These include strains with deletions of SUP35 and/or HSP104 genes, which facilitate studies of chaperone effects on AβMRF oligomerization. The strains may carry different plasmid-based AβMRF constructs under inducible promoters, allowing controlled expression of the fusion protein.  

To address potential limitations in using yeast cells for drug screening, the invention provides methods to overcome issues of drug permeability and metabolism. These include use of strains with altered membrane transport properties and metabolic pathways, as well as protocols for permeabilizing cells to enhance compound access.  

The invention provides kits containing the essential components for practicing the screening methods. These kits may include yeast strains with various AβMRF constructs, appropriate media formulations, and control compounds known to affect oligomerization. The kits facilitate standardized implementation of the screening platform across different laboratory settings.  

The screening methods of the invention accommodate diverse types of candidate compounds. These include but are not limited to small organic molecules (MW <2500 Da), peptides, peptidomimetics, nucleic acids (antisense oligonucleotides, ribozymes), antibodies, and natural products. The system supports screening of individual compounds as well as complex mixtures and combinatorial libraries.  

Small molecule libraries suitable for screening may encompass various chemical classes, including heterocycles, steroids, triterpenoids, and saccharides. The invention provides methods for structural modification of hit compounds to enhance potency, stability, or other pharmacological properties. These include modifications such as unnatural amino acid incorporation, terminal functionalization, and esterification/amidification of carboxyl groups.  

For protein-based therapeutics, the invention enables screening of antibodies, antibody fragments, and engineered binding proteins. The system can assess both direct interference with Aβ42 oligomerization and modulation of cellular factors that influence aggregation pathways.  

The invention further provides methods for evolving potential drugs through combinatorial approaches. These include directed evolution of peptides or proteins, as well as structure-activity relationship studies based on initial screening hits. The phenotypic readout of the yeast system facilitates rapid iteration and optimization of candidate therapeutics.  

### Model of Aβ42 Oligomerization  

The activity of Sup35p and its assay in vivo forms the basis for monitoring AβMRF oligomerization. The invention utilizes the nonsense suppression assay, wherein compromised translational termination at the ade1-14 premature stop codon leads to adenine prototrophy. This assay provides a sensitive and quantitative measure of AβMRF functionality.  

The construction of AβMRF fusion protein involved precise molecular cloning strategies. The Aβ42 peptide was fused to the N-terminus of MRF, with an HA epitope tag inserted between the M and RF domains. This configuration allows immunological detection of the fusion protein while maintaining the functional integrity of both Aβ42 and MRF portions.  

The invention encompasses specific mutations in the Aβ42 portion of AβMRF designed to disrupt oligomerization. These include F19,20T and F19,20T/I31P substitutions in aggregation-critical regions of Aβ42. These mutations significantly reduce oligomer formation while preserving the overall structure of the fusion protein.  

Expression of AβMRF, Aβm1MRF, and Aβm2MRF in yeast cells demonstrates the system's ability to distinguish different oligomerization states. Wild-type AβMRF forms extensive oligomers and causes strong nonsense suppression, while the mutant forms show progressively reduced oligomerization and suppression activity. These differential effects validate the system's sensitivity to specific structural features of Aβ42.  

### Example 2  

Analysis of yeast lysates by immunoblotting confirms the formation of SDS-stable oligomers by AβMRF. These oligomers resist dissociation in 1% SDS at room temperature, requiring boiling for complete disaggregation. The oligomeric species migrate at positions consistent with dimers, trimers, and tetramers in both polyacrylamide and agarose gel systems.  

The effect of boiling on SDS-stable oligomers demonstrates their amyloid-like properties. Only harsh denaturing conditions completely disrupt the oligomers, similar to the behavior of natural Aβ42 aggregates. This stability profile suggests that the AβMRF oligomers recapitulate key structural features of pathogenic Aβ42 species.  

Anti-Aβ antibodies recognize the oligomers, though with decreasing efficiency as oligomer size increases. This pattern reflects limited antibody access to epitopes within larger oligomeric complexes, consistent with observations of natural Aβ42 aggregates. The differential recognition provides additional validation of the oligomers' structural authenticity.  

### Example 3  

Analysis of point mutants of AβMRF by SDS-electrophoresis and immunoblotting reveals specific effects on oligomerization. The F19,20T mutation (Aβm1MRF) significantly reduces oligomer formation, while the additional I31P mutation (Aβm2MRF) nearly abolishes oligomerization. These results confirm the importance of these residues in Aβ42 self-association.  

The detection of residual Aβm2MRF dimers suggests that some oligomerization capacity remains even with multiple disruptive mutations. This observation aligns with the partial nonsense suppression phenotype of Aβm2MRF-expressing cells and demonstrates the system's ability to detect subtle differences in aggregation propensity.  

The implications of these results extend to understanding Aβ42 oligomerization mechanisms. The differential effects of specific mutations support a model where discrete interaction interfaces mediate progressive assembly of oligomers. This insight informs strategies for developing targeted inhibitors of Aβ42 aggregation.  

### Example 4  

The effect of guanidine on AβMRF oligomers reveals unexpected modulation of aggregation. Contrary to its prion-curing effects on [PSI+], guanidine enhances AβMRF oligomerization. This result suggests distinct mechanisms governing Sup35p prion formation versus Aβ42 oligomerization in yeast.  

Deletion of HSP104 reduces AβMRF oligomerization, contrasting with guanidine's effects. This differential response indicates that Hsp104 influences AβMRF aggregation through mechanisms separate from guanidine's target. The findings highlight the complexity of cellular factors modulating Aβ42 oligomerization.  

The implications of these results for therapeutic development are significant. They suggest that different approaches may be required to inhibit Aβ42 oligomerization versus disaggregating mature fibrils. The yeast system provides a unique platform for distinguishing these mechanisms and identifying specific modulators.  

### Example 5  

Co-immunoprecipitation experiments demonstrate physical interaction between Hsp104 and AβMRF. This interaction occurs specifically through the Aβ42 portion, as MRF alone does not co-precipitate with Hsp104. The finding suggests direct chaperone engagement with Aβ42 sequences in the cellular context.  

Hsp104 deletion reduces both total AβMRF protein levels and oligomer proportions. These effects suggest dual roles for Hsp104 in stabilizing AβMRF and promoting its oligomerization. The results imply complex chaperone functions in managing Aβ42 aggregation states.  

The role of chaperones in protecting against amyloid aggregates gains support from these findings. The invention's system enables detailed study of how chaperone machinery interacts with different Aβ42 aggregation states, informing strategies to enhance cellular clearance of toxic oligomers.  

### A Suppressible Allele  

The translational readthrough of ade1-14 nonsense mutation provides the foundation for screening drugs that reduce oligomerization. As AβMRF oligomerization increases readthrough, compounds that inhibit oligomer formation will decrease readthrough and adenine prototrophy. This inverse relationship enables straightforward identification of potential therapeutics.  

The proposed screen for drugs reducing translational readthrough takes advantage of simple colorimetric and growth assays. Potential inhibitors will cause a shift from white/Ade+ to red/Ade- phenotype, proportional to their efficacy in blocking Aβ42 oligomerization. The system's sensitivity allows detection of partial effects, enabling structure-activity studies.  

### Example 7  

The yeast strains and media used in the invention are specifically optimized for AβMRF studies. The 74D-694 background strain (ade1-14) provides the nonsense suppression reporter system, while sup35Δ and hsp104Δ derivatives enable controlled expression of AβMRF variants and study of chaperone effects.  

Plasmid construction for MRF and AβMRF involved sophisticated molecular biology techniques. The Aβ42 fragment was PCR-amplified with flanking restriction sites and inserted into Sup35p expression vectors. Precise replacement of the native SUP35 promoter with the copper-inducible CUP1 promoter allows controlled expression of AβMRF fusions.  

Site-directed mutagenesis of the Aβ42 region generated the oligomerization-deficient controls Aβm1MRF and Aβm2MRF. These constructs were shuffled into centromeric plasmids (pRS313, pRS316) for stable maintenance in yeast. Extensive restriction analysis and sequencing confirmed the integrity of all constructs.  

Immunoblot analysis of protein products utilized antibodies against Aβ (6E10), RF (BE4), and HA tags. These reagents enable specific detection of AβMRF and its oligomeric states. The yeast growth assay provides phenotypic confirmation of AβMRF functionality under various conditions.  

The immunoblotting procedure for visualizing SDS-stable oligomers was optimized for both polyacrylamide and agarose gels. Sample treatment with 1-2% SDS at room temperature preserves oligomeric species, while boiling in SDS with β-mercaptoethanol yields complete monomers. This flexibility allows characterization of different aggregation states.  

Agarose gels provide better resolution for larger oligomeric species, complementing the detailed separation of smaller species in polyacrylamide gels. The combined approach enables comprehensive analysis of AβMRF's oligomerization spectrum under various experimental conditions.  

Aβ42 polymerization and immunoblotting protocols were established to validate the system. Recombinant Aβ42 forms detectable oligomers and fibrils under defined conditions, providing reference standards for comparison with AβMRF species. These controls ensure the biological relevance of findings from the yeast system.  

The immunoprecipitation procedure demonstrates protein-protein interactions critical to the system's function. Co-precipitation of Hsp104 with AβMRF but not MRF confirms specific engagement of chaperones with the Aβ42 portion. This interaction study capability enhances the system's utility for mechanistic investigations.
