# DESCRIPTION  

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates to magnesium zinc oxide (MZO) based high voltage thin film transistors (HVTFTs) fabricated on glass substrates. More particularly, the invention provides a novel MZO-HVTFT structure with enhanced thermal stability and high voltage blocking capability through interface engineering and a unique ring-shaped gate design. The disclosed transistors are particularly suitable for use in building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV), transparent solar photovoltaic systems on glass (PV-SOG), and other high voltage applications requiring stable performance at elevated temperatures.  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Solar energy has emerged as a crucial sustainable energy source to address growing global energy demands while reducing environmental impact. Conventional photovoltaic (PV) systems typically employ crystalline silicon solar panels connected to string inverters, which convert the generated DC power to AC power for grid connection. However, these conventional systems face limitations in efficiency, particularly under partial shading conditions, and require significant space for installation.  

Micro-inverter technology was developed to overcome these limitations by connecting individual inverters to each solar panel, thereby improving system efficiency and enabling module-level power management. While micro-inverters offer advantages over traditional string inverters, they still present challenges in terms of cost, reliability, and integration with building materials.  

Building integrated photovoltaics (BIPV) represents an important advancement in solar technology by incorporating PV elements directly into building materials such as windows, facades, and roofs. A critical component of BIPV systems is the transparent solar PV system on glass (PV-SOG), which requires high-performance electronic components that maintain transparency while providing reliable power conversion.  

Existing transistor technologies face significant limitations in meeting the requirements of PV-SOG applications. Silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) transistors, while capable of handling high voltages, are expensive to produce and not suitable for transparent applications. Amorphous silicon (a-Si) and polycrystalline silicon (poly-Si) HVTFTs suffer from poor thermal stability and limited performance at high voltages. Organic TFTs and indium gallium zinc oxide (IGZO) HVTFTs similarly fail to provide the necessary combination of high voltage capability, thermal stability, and transparency required for advanced PV-SOG applications.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a magnesium zinc oxide high voltage thin film transistor (MZO-HVTFT) that overcomes the limitations of prior art devices. The MZO-HVTFT features a novel structure including a passivation layer and a Mg composition specifically tailored for enhanced TFT stability. The transistor employs a circular-symmetric ring design that enables uniform electric field distribution and high blocking voltage capability.  

A key aspect of the invention involves interface modification through the incorporation of a transition layer between the channel and dielectric layers. The transition layer has an optimized thickness and composition gradient that prevents interdiffusion of materials while reducing interface trap density. The channel length and offset distance are carefully designed to balance blocking capability with driving current.  

The MZO-HVTFT can be fabricated on various substrate materials, including transparent glass, making it particularly suitable for building-integrated photovoltaic applications. The invention further encompasses application articles incorporating the MZO-HVTFT, such as PV-SOG systems and self-powered smart glass technologies.  

The method of manufacturing the MZO-HVTFT involves precise deposition techniques to create the layered structure, including the critical transition layer. The fabrication process enables production of high-performance transistors with excellent thermal stability and voltage blocking characteristics.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The MZO-HVTFT of the present invention represents a significant advancement in high voltage transistor technology for glass-based applications. The transistor structure provides multiple advantages over conventional designs, including improved thermal stability, higher blocking voltage capability, and better interface properties.  

As used in this specification, the terms "a" and "an" mean one or more, and the term "about" means within 10% of the stated value unless otherwise specified.  

The MZO-HVTFT structure comprises several key components arranged in a bottom-gate inverted-staggered configuration. A dielectric insulating layer, preferably silicon dioxide (SiO₂), serves as the gate dielectric. The channel layer consists of magnesium zinc oxide (MgₓZn₁₋ₓO) with a carefully controlled magnesium composition (x = 0.03 in preferred embodiments) to optimize performance.  

The transition layer represents a critical innovation of the present invention. This layer consists of a modulation-doped MgₓZn₁₋ₓO composition with a graded magnesium concentration that decreases from the dielectric interface (y ≈ 1) to the channel interface (y ≈ 0.03). The transition layer typically has a thickness of about 10 nm and serves multiple functions, including acting as a diffusion barrier and reducing interface trap density.  

Electrode materials include chromium for the gate electrode and titanium/gold bilayers for source and drain contacts. The ring-shaped gate design represents another important feature of the invention, providing symmetrical electric field distribution compared to conventional rectangular designs. Offset distances between gate and source/drain electrodes are carefully controlled to optimize performance.  

Substrate materials for the MZO-HVTFT preferably include transparent glass, enabling applications in see-through electronics. The invention encompasses various applications of the MZO-HVTFT, including but not limited to PV-SOG systems, self-powered smart glass technologies, high voltage switching applications, digital radiology systems, and portable electronic devices.  

The method of manufacturing the MZO-HVTFT involves several key steps. Deposition methods include sputtering for metal layers, plasma-enhanced chemical vapor deposition (PECVD) for dielectric layers, and metal-organic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) for semiconductor layers. The passivation layer formation uses photoresist coating to protect the channel from environmental effects.  

### Material Preparation and Device Fabrication Process  

The HVTFT fabrication process begins with preparation of a glass substrate, typically 0.4 mm thick. A chromium gate electrode layer is deposited by sputtering and patterned using dry etching. A 200 nm SiO₂ gate dielectric layer is then deposited by PECVD. The channel layer is deposited by MOCVD at 400°C using diethyl zinc and bis(methylcyclopentadienyl)magnesium as precursors.  

Three channel layer configurations are fabricated: pure ZnO, Mg₀.₀₃Zn₀.₉₇O (MZO), and modulation-doped MZO (m-MZO). The m-MZO structure includes the critical transition layer between the channel and dielectric. Source and drain electrodes are formed by electron beam evaporation of titanium/gold bilayers followed by lift-off processing.  

### Device Testing Conditions  

Electrical characterization employs specialized equipment to handle high voltage measurements. Low bias testing uses an HP-4156C semiconductor parameter analyzer with current resolution to 1×10⁻¹⁵ A. High voltage testing employs a Tektronix 370 system with appropriate safety measures, including immersion in Fluorinert FC-40 dielectric fluid during measurement. Temperature-dependent measurements use an Agilent 1500B system in a light-tight probe station.  

### Material Characterizations  

Material analysis employs transmission electron microscopy (TEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDS), and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) to examine interface properties and elemental distributions. TEM samples are prepared using focused ion beam (FIB) milling to achieve precise cross-sections of device structures.  

### Device Design  

The ring structure design represents a key innovation for high voltage operation. Compared to rectangular designs, the circular configuration provides more uniform electric field distribution, reducing peak field strength by approximately 50%. This enables higher blocking voltages without device failure.  

### Transfer Characteristics and Thermal Stability  

The MZO-HVTFT demonstrates superior transfer characteristics compared to ZnO-based devices. At room temperature, MZO devices show better subthreshold slope (S.S.) and higher on-current. Thermal stability testing reveals a threshold voltage shift (ΔVth) of only -6 V for MZO devices compared to -10.5 V for ZnO devices when temperature increases from 294 K to 367 K.  

Activation energy analysis shows a steeper falling rate (0.177 eV/V) for MZO compared to ZnO (0.107 eV/V), indicating lower trap density in MZO devices. The m-MZO configuration shows further improvement with a falling rate of 0.246 eV/V, corresponding to approximately 40% lower total trap density compared to standard MZO devices.  

### High Voltage Blocking Capability  

The m-MZO HVTFT demonstrates exceptional high voltage performance. Devices with 10 μm channel length block 300 V with on-current of 3.5×10⁻⁵ A. Longer channel devices (25 μm) achieve 609 V blocking capability while maintaining on/off ratio of 3.3×10⁸. Output characteristics show stable operation up to 200 V drain bias.  

Interface engineering through the transition layer enables these improvements by reducing maximum electric field strength from 1,520 kV/cm in MZO devices to 1,410 kV/cm in m-MZO devices. This field reduction allows higher operating voltages without device breakdown.
