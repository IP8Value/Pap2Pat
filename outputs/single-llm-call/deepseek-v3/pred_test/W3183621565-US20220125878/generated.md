## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention relates generally to the fields of virology, immunology, and medicine. More specifically, the invention provides compositions and methods for treating viral infections, particularly severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) infection, by inhibiting viral binding to liver/lymph node-specific ICAM-3 grabbing nonintegrin (L-SIGN).  

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Coronaviruses are a large family of viruses that cause illness ranging from the common cold to more severe diseases. Severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2 (SARS-CoV-2) is the causative agent of coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19), which has resulted in a global pandemic with significant morbidity and mortality. SARS-CoV-2 infection is known to bind to angiotensin-converting enzyme 2 (ACE2) receptors on host cells, facilitating viral entry and infection.  

While ACE2 is expressed in various tissues including the lungs, kidneys, and intestines, it is notably absent or minimally expressed in vascular endothelial cells. This presents a paradox as COVID-19 patients frequently develop coagulopathy and thrombosis, conditions typically associated with endothelial dysfunction. The mechanisms by which SARS-CoV-2 induces endothelial activation and coagulopathy despite the absence of ACE2 expression in endothelial cells have remained unclear.  

There exists a significant unmet medical need for novel compositions and methods that can effectively treat SARS-CoV-2 infection, particularly in addressing the coagulopathy and thrombosis associated with severe COVID-19. Current therapeutic approaches have limited efficacy in preventing these complications, highlighting the importance of identifying alternative viral receptors and developing targeted interventions.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides a method for treating viral infection in a subject, particularly SARS-CoV-2 infection, by administering an inhibitor that prevents binding of SARS-CoV-2 to L-SIGN. The invention is based on the discovery that L-SIGN serves as a novel receptor for SARS-CoV-2 on liver sinusoidal endothelial cells (LSECs) and lymphatic endothelial cells (LECs), and that this interaction contributes to COVID-19-associated coagulopathy.  

The pharmaceutical composition of the invention comprises an effective amount of an L-SIGN inhibitor, which may include anti-L-SIGN antibodies, mannan, or recombinant L-SIGN/Fc proteins. Administration of these inhibitors reduces tissue damage associated with SARS-CoV-2 infection by preventing viral binding to L-SIGN-expressing endothelial cells.  

The method specifies optimal timing for administration, which may be prior to, during, or after exposure to SARS-CoV-2. Repeated administration may be necessary to maintain therapeutic efficacy throughout the course of infection. The invention provides specific dosage ranges and infusion rates tailored to achieve maximum inhibition of L-SIGN-mediated viral entry while minimizing potential side effects.  

By blocking L-SIGN-mediated viral entry, the invention reduces vascular deficits and endothelial dysfunction associated with SARS-CoV-2 infection. The method is particularly effective in treating liver dysfunction resulting from viral infection of LSECs, as well as lymph node dysfunction caused by infection of LECs. These therapeutic effects address the coagulopathy and thrombosis that characterize severe COVID-19 cases.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention encompasses compositions and methods for treating SARS-CoV-2 infection by inhibiting viral binding to L-SIGN. As used herein, "treating" or "therapy" refers to both therapeutic intervention in active infection and prophylactic measures to prevent infection or reduce its severity. A "therapeutically effective amount" refers to the quantity of inhibitor sufficient to measurably reduce viral binding to L-SIGN or ameliorate symptoms of infection.  

Dosage and administration parameters are critical to the invention's efficacy. The L-SIGN inhibitor may be administered intravenously, subcutaneously, or via other appropriate routes, typically in carrier solutions compatible with biological systems. The "subject" refers to any human or animal susceptible to SARS-CoV-2 infection. "Pharmaceutically acceptable" formulations meet standards for purity, sterility, and stability required for clinical use.  

The invention builds upon conventional methods for studying viral entry mechanisms. SARS-CoV-2 spike protein binding to L-SIGN was characterized using plasmids encoding viral and host proteins, followed by protein purification and cell culture techniques. COS-7 cell transfection, protein A agarose purification, and buffer exchange methods were employed to produce recombinant proteins. Protein purity was verified through established examination techniques.  

Key findings demonstrate that L-SIGN binds SARS-CoV-2 spike protein in a Ca2+-dependent manner. Experiments with Spike/Fc proteins and L-SIGN-expressing cell lines confirmed this interaction. The binding requires high-mannose-type N-glycans on the viral spike protein, particularly at Asn343. Pseudovirus infection assays showed L-SIGN-mediated viral entry into endothelial cells, which could be blocked by anti-L-SIGN antibodies or mannan.  

L-SIGN expression was specifically detected in LSECs and lymph node LECs through immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence staining. Importantly, authentic SARS-CoV-2 virus infected L-SIGN-expressing LSECs but not control cells. COVID-19 patient liver samples showed increased L-SIGN expression and viral presence in LSECs, along with elevated von Willebrand factor (vWF) and factor VIII (FVIII) expression - markers of endothelial activation and coagulopathy.  

The invention encompasses various embodiments, including but not limited to monoclonal antibodies against L-SIGN, carbohydrate-based inhibitors like mannan, and recombinant L-SIGN/Fc fusion proteins that competitively inhibit viral binding. The scope of the invention includes all pharmaceutically acceptable salts, solvates, and hydrates of the active compounds.
