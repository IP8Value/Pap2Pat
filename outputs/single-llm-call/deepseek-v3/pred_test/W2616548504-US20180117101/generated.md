# DESCRIPTION  

The present invention relates to prior applications concerning the use of genetically modified probiotics for therapeutic purposes. This application builds upon and improves previous approaches by providing a novel probiotic system capable of effectively treating phenylketonuria (PKU) through targeted phenylalanine metabolism in the intestinal tract.  

## BACKGROUND  

The invention introduces a genetically modified probiotic capable of delivering functional phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL) enzyme to the intestinal tract for the treatment of phenylketonuria. The probiotic system comprises Lactobacillus or Lactococcus species engineered to express PAL from Anabaena variabilis (AvPAL), which has been codon-optimized for expression in the probiotic host. The modified probiotic survives gastric transit and remains metabolically active in the small intestine where it catabolizes dietary phenylalanine, thereby reducing systemic phenylalanine levels in PKU patients.  

## Advantages Over Conventional Technologies  

Phenylketonuria represents a significant unmet medical need, with current treatments being either ineffective or burdened by substantial limitations. Liver transplantation, while potentially curative, carries significant surgical risks, requires lifelong immunosuppression, and faces severe donor organ shortages. Gene therapy approaches remain experimental and present risks of insertional mutagenesis and unpredictable long-term expression.  

Traditional dietary phenylalanine restriction imposes severe lifestyle limitations, with medical foods being costly, unpalatable, and nutritionally incomplete. Cofactor therapy using tetrahydrobiopterin shows limited efficacy, helping only a small subset of PKU patients with specific PAH mutations. PEGylated PAL (PEG-PAL) requires frequent injections and induces immune responses that diminish therapeutic efficacy over time.  

Yeast-derived PAL preparations suffer from poor stability and rapid clearance when administered orally. Naked PAL enzyme delivery fails due to gastric degradation and intestinal protease cleavage. The present invention overcomes these limitations by providing a safe, orally administered probiotic that delivers functional PAL enzyme directly to the intestinal lumen, where it can metabolize dietary phenylalanine before systemic absorption occurs.  

## SUMMARY  

The invention provides a genetically modified probiotic comprising Lactobacillus or Lactococcus species engineered to express phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL) enzyme. The probiotic is modified to include: a codon-optimized PAL gene under control of a constitutive promoter, ribosome binding sequences optimized for the host, and transcriptional terminators to ensure proper expression. The engineered probiotic survives gastric transit and colonizes the small intestine where it actively metabolizes phenylalanine into trans-cinnamic acid and ammonia.  

This approach provides several advantages over existing PKU treatments: oral administration eliminates injection requirements, gut confinement prevents systemic immune responses, and continuous enzyme production maintains therapeutic efficacy. The probiotic can be produced cost-effectively at scale and formulated for convenient daily dosing.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS  

The invention introduces a genetically modified probiotic (GMP) system designed to deliver functional PAL enzyme to the intestinal tract. The probiotic is engineered to survive stomach acid and maintain enzymatic functionality in the small intestine environment. Safety for oral administration is ensured through selection of probiotic strains with established human use histories.  

Production begins with in vitro modification of the probiotic host. Electroporation or chemical transformation introduces the PAL gene construct into selected Lactobacillus or Lactococcus strains. The Lactobacilli and Lactococci genera are particularly suitable due to their gastric acid resistance, intestinal colonization capability, and established safety profiles as human probiotics.  

Strain specificity is crucial, as different probiotic strains exhibit varying levels of acid/bile resistance, colonization efficiency, and protein expression capacity. The PAL gene from Anabaena variabilis (AvPAL) is codon-optimized for the selected probiotic host to maximize translation efficiency. Additional modifications ensure proper transcription, translation, and intracellular localization of the PAL enzyme.  

The genetic construct includes a strong constitutive promoter, optimized ribosome binding site with appropriate spacer sequence, and transcriptional terminator sequences. For intracellular localization, secretion tags may be added to direct PAL to specific cellular compartments. The complete gene construct is introduced into the probiotic host via high-retention plasmids or chromosomal integration.  

Plasmid designs incorporate antibiotic resistance markers for selection and may include shuttle vectors with E. coli origins of replication for initial propagation. Following transformation, clones are verified by sequencing and tested for in vitro phenylalanine metabolism capability.  

The final product is delivered as purified probiotic organisms rather than culture medium, providing superior stability and dosing accuracy. Dosage is calculated based on colony forming units (CFUs) required to achieve target phenylalanine reduction, with animal model testing used to validate efficacy. Blood collection and phenylalanine monitoring enable precise dosage optimization.  

The purified probiotic formulation offers significant shipping and storage advantages over liquid cultures or frozen preparations. Lyophilized powders maintain viability at room temperature for extended periods, enhancing treatment accessibility. This delivery method improves patient compliance through convenient administration and reduces costs compared to current PKU therapies.  

### EXAMPLE 1  

Lactobacillus reuteri strain 100-23C serves as the probiotic host in this embodiment. The strain is selected for its rodent intestinal colonization properties and established safety profile. The AvPAL enzyme is chosen for its high catalytic efficiency, stability in foreign hosts, and ability to fold without chaperones.  

The AvPAL gene undergoes codon optimization for L. reuteri expression, with addition of a ribosome binding site (RBS) and optimized spacer sequence. A transcriptional terminator from the ermGT resistance gene is appended to ensure proper transcription termination. The construct utilizes a high-expression constitutive promoter from the erythromycin resistance B gene (ermB).  

For secretion capability, a secretion tag sequence is incorporated into the construct. The complete gene cassette is cloned into a shuttle vector containing an E. coli origin of replication for initial propagation and an erythromycin resistance gene for selection. PCR product purification and ligation create the final plasmid construct.  

Transformation proceeds first into E. coli for plasmid verification, followed by electroporation into L. reuteri 100-23C cells. Successful transformants are confirmed by plasmid isolation and sequencing. The resulting probiotic strain, designated pHENOMMenal, demonstrates robust phenylalanine metabolism in vitro while maintaining all desirable probiotic characteristics.  

### EXAMPLE 2  

This embodiment describes oral administration protocols for the PHEnominal probiotic. Freeze-dried probiotic powder is mixed with food for convenient daily dosing. Phenylalanine levels are monitored through regular blood testing, with dosage adjusted based on metabolic response. The probiotic demonstrates significant phenylalanine reduction within 3-4 days of treatment initiation, with effects sustained during continuous administration.  

### EXAMPLE 3  

Functional demonstration of AvPAL enzyme activity shows increased trans-cinnamic acid production proportional to phenylalanine concentration. Spectrophotometric assays at 280nm confirm enzyme functionality, with pHENOMMenal lysates producing significantly more trans-cinnamate than control cells at all time points measured (p<0.05 to p<0.0001).  

### EXAMPLE 4  

An alternative embodiment utilizes Lactobacillus acidophilus strains as probiotic hosts. Gene construct design and verification follow similar principles to Example 1. Trans-cinnamic acid detection employs spectrophotometric methods with standard curves for quantification. Experimental procedures maintain anaerobic conditions appropriate for probiotic growth. Data analysis demonstrates functional AvPAL enzyme production in L. acidophilus, with results presented in tabular and graphical formats.
