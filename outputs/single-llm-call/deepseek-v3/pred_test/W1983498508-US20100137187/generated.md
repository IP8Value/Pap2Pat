## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION  

Prostate cancer represents a significant health burden worldwide, with androgen-resistant forms being particularly challenging to treat. Current therapeutic approaches for advanced prostate cancer, including chemotherapy and radiation, often demonstrate limited efficacy and substantial toxicity. The development of resistance to androgen deprivation therapy remains a major obstacle in clinical management.  

Existing drug delivery systems for prostate cancer treatment suffer from several limitations. Small molecule drugs frequently exhibit poor bioavailability and nonspecific distribution throughout the body. While oligonucleotide-based therapies show promise for targeted intervention, their clinical application has been hampered by inefficient cellular uptake and rapid degradation in biological systems. Traditional delivery vehicles such as liposomes often fail to achieve sufficient intracellular concentrations of therapeutic agents, particularly within the nuclear compartment where many molecular targets reside.  

There exists a pressing need for improved delivery methods that can transport therapeutic payloads specifically to prostate cancer cells while achieving efficient nuclear localization. Such delivery systems should maintain stability in physiological conditions, demonstrate minimal toxicity to normal tissues, and provide enhanced pharmacokinetic properties compared to existing modalities. The development of novel cell-penetrating peptides capable of addressing these challenges would represent a significant advancement in the field of targeted cancer therapeutics.  

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel cell-penetrating peptides (CPPs) designed for enhanced delivery of therapeutic agents to cancer cells, particularly androgen-resistant prostate cancer cells. These CPPs comprise a glutamate-rich peptide sequence covalently linked to a nuclear localization signal (NLS) peptide derived from the Oct6 transcription factor. The glutamate peptide is bound to the N-terminus of the NLS peptide through a peptide linker consisting of alanine or glycine residues.  

In preferred embodiments, the CPP includes a fluorescent label such as fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) attached to facilitate tracking and quantification. The NLS peptide component is specifically derived from the Oct6 transcription factor sequence, providing enhanced nuclear targeting capabilities. The CPP structure further includes a therapeutic payload conjugated to the C-terminus of the NLS peptide.  

The therapeutic payload may consist of various bioactive molecules including but not limited to nucleic acids (such as peptide nucleic acids or PNAs), liposomes containing therapeutic compounds, or diagnostic imaging agents. These payloads are selected for their ability to inhibit cancer cell growth, with particular efficacy demonstrated against prostate cancer cell lines. The complete CPP construct demonstrates superior cellular uptake and nuclear localization compared to either component peptide alone.  

A representative embodiment of the invention includes the peptide sequence designated as SEQ ID NO: 3, which combines the glutamate-rich domain with the Oct6 NLS sequence. This construct exhibits rapid internalization by target cells and efficient translocation to the nuclear compartment, making it particularly suitable for delivering therapeutic agents that act on nuclear targets such as transcription factors or DNA.  

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION  

The present invention provides novel cell-penetrating peptide constructs specifically designed to overcome limitations in current therapeutic delivery systems for prostate cancer. These CPPs incorporate several innovative features that synergistically enhance their performance as delivery vehicles. The addition of a glutamate-rich peptide sequence to the Oct6 nuclear localization signal creates a hybrid molecule with properties superior to either component alone.  

The glutamate peptide component (EEEAA) serves multiple functions in the CPP structure. First, it enhances cellular uptake through interactions with cell surface receptors. Second, it provides spacing between functional domains of the peptide to optimize their independent activities. The linker region between the glutamate peptide and NLS, typically composed of alanine or glycine residues, maintains flexibility while preventing steric interference between domains.  

The Oct6 NLS portion of the construct confers specific nuclear targeting capabilities. Unlike many NLS peptides that remain trapped in endosomal compartments, the inventive CPP demonstrates efficient nuclear localization when combined with the glutamate peptide. This nuclear targeting is particularly valuable for delivering therapeutic agents that act on nuclear components such as transcription factors or DNA.  

Cancer cells frequently overexpress glutamate receptors, providing a potential mechanism for the enhanced uptake observed with these CPPs. The inventive peptides appear to exploit this characteristic of malignant cells, demonstrating preferential uptake in cancer cell lines compared to normal cells. The temperature dependence and azide sensitivity of uptake suggest an active transport mechanism for the complete CPP construct.  

The modular design of these CPPs allows for versatile therapeutic applications. By conjugating different payloads to the C-terminus, the same delivery platform can be adapted for various therapeutic purposes. Experimental results demonstrate successful delivery of peptide nucleic acids (PNAs) to prostate cancer cells, with subsequent nuclear localization and biological activity.  

### Examples  

Experimental studies with the inventive CPPs have yielded several significant findings. Peptide synthesis and purification protocols have been optimized to produce high-quality constructs with consistent performance. The uptake kinetics of these peptides by live cells has been characterized, revealing rapid internalization and concentration-dependent accumulation.  

Comparative studies demonstrate that the combination of glutamate peptide and Oct6 NLS produces dramatically better uptake than either component alone. The addition of a lysyl peptide (KKK) to the glutamate sequence enhances uptake approximately 10-fold, while inclusion of the complete Oct6 NLS produces a 100-fold improvement. This synergistic effect suggests cooperative interactions between the peptide domains.  

Structure-activity relationships have been explored through amino acid substitution studies. Replacement of glutamate residues with phenylalanine, asparagine, or tyrosine completely abrogates uptake, indicating the critical role of the glutamate-rich domain. Competition experiments confirm the sequence specificity of uptake, as modified peptides fail to inhibit internalization of the active CPP.  

Temperature dependence studies reveal that uptake is optimal at physiological temperatures, with marked reduction at lower temperatures. Inhibition by sodium azide suggests an energy-dependent transport mechanism for the complete CPP construct. Imaging studies demonstrate nuclear colocalization of the fluorescently labeled CPP, with approximately 50% of internalized peptide accumulating in the nuclear compartment.  

The utility of these CPPs for therapeutic delivery has been demonstrated through conjugation to peptide nucleic acids (PNAs). The hybrid molecule 353-PNA shows concentration-dependent uptake by DU-145 prostate cancer cells, with a distinct population of highly fluorescent cells observable at low micromolar concentrations. Importantly, the PNA payload maintains its nuclear localization capability when conjugated to the CPP.  

## Experimental Procedures  

Peptides and PNAs were synthesized using standard FMoc chemistry and purified by high-performance liquid chromatography. All constructs were characterized by mass spectrometry and sequencing to verify identity and purity. Cell culture experiments utilized established prostate cancer lines (DU-145, LNCaP) and pancreatic cancer cells (PANC-1) maintained under standard conditions.  

Uptake studies employed flow cytometry with appropriate controls for viability and fluorescence calibration. Quantitative analysis used geometric mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) as the primary metric for comparison between samples. Nuclear localization was assessed using imaging flow cytometry and live cell microscopy with DRAQ5 nuclear staining.  

Statistical analysis was performed using appropriate software packages with significance thresholds set at p<0.05. Experimental data were collected from multiple independent replicates to ensure reproducibility. All procedures included appropriate controls for specificity, cytotoxicity, and methodological artifacts.
