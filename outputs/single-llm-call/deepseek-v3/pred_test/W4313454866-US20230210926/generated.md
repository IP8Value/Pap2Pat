## BACKGROUND  

### Field  

The present invention relates generally to the field of in vivo ultrasound imaging. More particularly, the invention provides novel acoustic reporter genes (ARGs) that enable non-invasive visualization of cellular gene expression patterns in living organisms using ultrasound imaging techniques.  

### Description of the Related Art  

The ability to visualize cellular processes in living organisms is crucial for both basic biological research and clinical applications. While optical imaging techniques have been widely used, they suffer from limited penetration depth due to light scattering in tissues. Ultrasound imaging provides an attractive alternative as it can penetrate deep into tissues while providing excellent spatial and temporal resolution.  

First-generation acoustic reporter genes (ARGs) based on gas vesicle (GV) gene clusters enabled ultrasound imaging of gene expression in vivo. However, these initial constructs had several limitations including poor expression at physiological temperatures, high metabolic burden on host cells, and the production of only linear ultrasound contrast that required destructive imaging methods.  

## SUMMARY  

The present invention provides novel compositions and methods for non-invasive in vivo imaging using second-generation acoustic reporter genes. The invention includes nucleic acid compositions encoding optimized gas vesicle gene clusters from Serratia sp. 39006 (bARGSer) and Anabaena flos-aquae (mARGAna). These constructs provide dramatically improved acoustic properties including 9-fold (bARGSer) and 38-fold (mARGAna) stronger non-linear ultrasound contrast compared to previous ARGs.  

The delivery compositions of the invention enable expression of these ARGs in various cell types including mammalian cells (e.g., cancer cells, stem cells) and probiotic bacterial cells (e.g., E. coli Nissle 1917, Salmonella Typhimurium). The engineered cells produce gas vesicles that scatter ultrasound non-linearly, enabling long-term dynamic imaging without destruction of the contrast agents.  

The invention further provides methods for treating or preventing diseases by administering these engineered cells and monitoring their distribution and gene expression patterns using ultrasound imaging. Specific applications include imaging tumor-homing bacteria, tracking therapeutic cells, and performing ultrasound-guided biopsies of genetically defined cell populations.  

Key advantages of the invention include:
- Robust expression at physiological temperatures (37°C)
- Production of strong non-linear ultrasound contrast
- Long-term stability in vivo
- High sensitivity (detection down to 10^5-10^7 cells/mL)
- Compatibility with deep tissue imaging (>1 cm penetration)
- Spatiotemporal resolution of gene expression patterns

### Acoustic Reporter Genes for Nondestructive In Vivo Imaging  

The invention provides second-generation acoustic reporter genes (ARGs) that overcome limitations of previous constructs. Through genomic mining of 288 gas vesicle-producing species, we identified two gene clusters with superior properties:  

1. The bacterial ARG from Serratia sp. 39006 (bARGSer) produces 9-fold stronger non-linear contrast than previous bacterial ARGs when expressed in E. coli Nissle 1917 (EcN). This enables non-invasive imaging of probiotic bacteria colonizing tumors at depths >1 cm.  

2. The mammalian ARG from Anabaena flos-aquae (mARGAna) produces 38-fold stronger contrast than previous mammalian ARGs. When expressed in human breast cancer cells, it enables long-term monitoring of gene expression in orthotopic tumors and ultrasound-guided biopsies.  

Key improvements include:  
- Enhanced non-linear scattering by omitting GvpC reinforcement protein  
- Optimized stoichiometry of structural (GvpA) and assembly proteins  
- Robust expression at 37°C in both bacterial and mammalian cells  
- Reduced metabolic burden through genetic optimization  

The gas vesicle gene clusters can be delivered using various vectors including plasmids, transposons, and viral vectors. Expression is controlled by inducible (e.g., pBAD, TRE), constitutive (e.g., CMV), or tissue-specific promoters.  

### Methods of Imaging and Treatment  

The invention provides methods for:  

1. Treating or preventing disease by administering engineered cells and monitoring their distribution using ultrasound imaging.  

2. Monitoring cell-based therapies by tracking reporting therapeutic cells expressing ARGs.  

3. Imaging target sites by administering ARG-expressing cells and obtaining ultrasound images.  

4. Detecting unique cell types/states based on ARG expression patterns.  

A typical imaging protocol involves:  
1. Administering ARG-expressing cells to subject  
2. Applying ultrasound pulses at 0.3-3 MPa pressure  
3. Detecting scattered ultrasound using xAM or BURST sequences  
4. Reconstructing 3D images of gene expression patterns  

The methods enable applications including:  
- Tracking tumor-homing bacteria  
- Monitoring stem cell therapies  
- Guiding biopsies to genetically defined regions  
- Studying microbiome dynamics  

## EXAMPLES  

### Example 1  

We cloned and screened 15 distinct polycistronic operons from GV-expressing species. The Serratia sp. 39006 cluster (bARGSer) showed 19.2 dB stronger xAM signal than the next best cluster when expressed in E. coli at 30°C (9-fold intensity gain).  

For in vivo testing, we:  
1. Formed subcutaneous MC26 tumors in mice  
2. Intravenously injected EcN expressing bARGSer  
3. Induced GV expression with L-arabinose  
4. Imaged tumors using pAM, BURST and xAM ultrasound  

Results showed:  
- Clear ultrasound contrast in colonized tumors  
- Signal localized to tumor core/necrotic interface  
- Successful re-expression after GV collapse  
- High specificity (no signal without induction)  

### Example 2  

The A. flos-aquae cluster (mARGAna) was optimized for mammalian cells by:  
1. Titrating GvpA stoichiometry (peak at 8-fold excess)  
2. Omitting GvpC to enhance non-linear contrast  
3. Adding WPRE-hGH polyA elements for transcript stability  

In orthotopic breast cancer models:  
- xAM signal appeared by day 4 post-induction  
- 3D visualization of expression patterns achieved  
- Specific to viable tumor cells (absent in necrotic regions)  
- Enabled ultrasound-guided biopsy of defined populations  

The invention provides significant advances over prior art by enabling non-destructive, dynamic imaging of gene expression with unprecedented sensitivity and specificity. The compositions and methods have broad applications in research, diagnostics and therapeutics.
