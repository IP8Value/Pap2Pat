# DESCRIPTION  

## TECHNICAL FIELD  

The present invention relates to a non-aggregated immunostimulatory oligonucleotide capable of inducing interferon-α (IFN-α) production while preventing high-order aggregate formation. More specifically, the invention provides modified CpG oligodeoxynucleotides (ODNs) with phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tails that enhance solubility, stability, and immunostimulatory activity without requiring guanine hexamer-mediated aggregation.  

## BACKGROUND ART  

Immunostimulatory oligonucleotides have been extensively studied as Toll-like receptor (TLR) 9-dependent innate immune activators and vaccine adjuvants. These oligonucleotides are broadly categorized into four types based on their structural and functional properties: A/D type, B/K type, C type, and P type ODNs. A/D type ODNs, characterized by a phosphodiester backbone with a poly-G tail at the 3′ end, predominantly stimulate IFN-α production from plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDCs). B/K type ODNs, composed entirely of a phosphorothioate backbone, activate B cells and induce interleukin-6 (IL-6) secretion. C type ODNs exhibit intermediate properties, inducing both IFN-α and IL-6, though with lower IFN-α induction compared to A/D type ODNs. Recently, P type ODNs, which contain palindromic sequences and exhibit a cytokine profile similar to C type ODNs but with enhanced IFN-α induction, have been described.  

A critical distinction among these ODNs is their IFN-α induction profile. A/D type ODNs are known for their strong IFN-α induction, whereas B/K type ODNs exhibit minimal IFN-α but robust IL-6 production. These differences correlate with distinct biological effects in vivo, such as varying adjuvant efficacy in vaccine formulations. For instance, B/K type ODNs have demonstrated superior adjuvanticity for antibody production in certain vaccine models, while A/D type ODNs have shown better protective immune responses in others.  

A major limitation of A/D type ODNs is their propensity to form high-order aggregates due to guanine hexamer sequences, which facilitate G-quadruplex DNA structures. While aggregation enhances IFN-α induction, it also leads to uncontrolled precipitation, complicating clinical formulation and administration. Previous attempts to mitigate aggregation, such as thermolytic protective groups, have shown promise but remain unproven in human trials.  

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION  

### Solution to Problem  

The present invention addresses the challenge of uncontrolled aggregate formation in A/D type immunostimulatory ODNs by introducing a modified oligonucleotide design. The invention provides a nucleic acid comprising a CpG motif-containing core sequence linked to a phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail, which prevents high-order aggregate formation while maintaining IFN-α induction.  

Key modifications include the addition of a phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail (e.g., poly-dAs40) at the 3′ end of the oligonucleotide. This modification eliminates the need for guanine hexamer sequences, thereby preventing aggregation while preserving immunostimulatory activity. The invention further encompasses an agent for delivering the immunostimulatory oligonucleotide, wherein the agent is bound to a moiety with biological activity, such as a targeting ligand or stabilizing polymer.  

### Advantageous Effects of Invention  

The invention offers several advantages over conventional A/D type ODNs. First, the modified oligonucleotides exhibit superior solubility in saline, facilitating clinical formulation and administration. Second, the elimination of aggregation-dependent immunostimulation ensures consistent biological activity and reduces batch-to-batch variability. Third, the phosphorothioate tail enhances cellular uptake, improving immunostimulatory efficacy without requiring additional delivery agents such as cationic lipids. Finally, the modified ODNs demonstrate enhanced adjuvanticity in vivo, making them suitable for vaccine applications.  

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS  

The invention encompasses various embodiments, including but not limited to:  

1. **Immunostimulatory Oligonucleotide Composition**: The oligonucleotide comprises a CpG motif-containing core sequence (e.g., a 12-mer palindromic sequence) linked to a phosphorothioate polydeoxynucleotide tail (e.g., dAs40). The phosphorothioate content is critical for preventing aggregation and maintaining activity.  
2. **Prevention of High-Order Aggregates**: The modified oligonucleotide avoids consecutive guanine sequences, eliminating G-quadruplex formation.  
3. **Delivery Systems**: The oligonucleotide may be complexed with delivery agents such as schizophyllan (SPG) or formulated in pharmaceutical compositions for injection.  
4. **Therapeutic Applications**: The invention includes methods for stimulating immunity, vaccine adjuvants, and prophylactic agents against infectious diseases.  

### EXAMPLES  

Experimental validation of the invention is demonstrated through the following examples:  

1. **Immunostimulatory Activity**: Human PBMCs were stimulated with D35-dAs40 and D35core-dAs40, showing IFN-α and IL-6 induction comparable to original D35 without aggregation.  
2. **Physical Properties**: Dynamic light scattering (DLS) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) confirmed the absence of large aggregates in modified ODNs.  
3. **In Vivo Adjuvanticity**: Cynomolgus monkeys immunized with influenza vaccine and modified ODNs exhibited enhanced antibody responses compared to conventional adjuvants.  

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY  

The invention has broad applications in immunotherapy, vaccine development, and pharmaceutical formulations. The non-aggregating design ensures scalability, stability, and clinical feasibility, making it suitable for commercial production.
