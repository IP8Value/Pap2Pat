# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of pharmacology and more specifically to the use of flavaglines for the inhibition of RAS activation, particularly KRAS. The invention provides methods and compositions for treating cancers associated with RAS mutations by targeting the interaction between RAS and prohibitins, which are essential for RAS activation.

## BACKGROUND ART

RAS proteins, including KRAS, NRAS, and HRAS, are small GTPases that play a crucial role in cellular signaling pathways. These proteins act as molecular switches, cycling between an active GTP-bound state and an inactive GDP-bound state. The activation cycle of RAS proteins is tightly regulated by GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs) and guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs). Mutations in RAS genes, particularly in codons 12, 13, and 61, can lead to constitutive activation of RAS, resulting in uncontrolled cell proliferation and tumor formation. KRAS mutations are particularly prevalent in pancreatic, lung, and colon cancers, making them a significant target for therapeutic intervention.

Recent advances in cancer therapy have led to the development of drugs targeting specific RAS mutations, such as KRAS G12C inhibitors. However, the development of broad-spectrum RAS inhibitors remains challenging due to the high affinity of GTP for RAS and the complexity of RAS signaling pathways. Prohibitins, a family of evolutionarily conserved proteins, have been shown to play a role in the regulation of RAS activation. Prohibitins, particularly prohibitin 1 (PHB1) and prohibitin 2 (PHB2), are known to interact with RAS and are involved in the formation of signaling complexes at the plasma membrane.

Flavaglines, a class of natural compounds isolated from plants of the genus Aglaia, have been shown to target prohibitins and inhibit the activation of RAS. Specifically, rocaglamide, a member of the flavagline family, has been demonstrated to inhibit the GTP loading of RAS, thereby leading to its inactivation. This inhibition is achieved by disrupting the interaction between RAS and prohibitins, which are essential for the formation of RAS nanoclusters and the activation of downstream signaling pathways.

## DISCLOSURE OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for inhibiting RAS activation in cells by administering a therapeutically effective amount of a flavagline compound. The flavagline compound is selected from a group of compounds that specifically target prohibitins and inhibit the interaction between RAS and prohibitins. The method is particularly useful for treating cancers associated with RAS mutations, such as KRAS, NRAS, and HRAS mutations.

One key substituent is R8, which can be —H, —OCH3, —Br, —F, or —Cl. The presence of these substituents at the R8 position significantly affects the potency and selectivity of the flavagline compounds in inhibiting RAS activation.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for inhibiting RAS activation in cells by targeting the interaction between RAS and prohibitins. The methods involve administering a therapeutically effective amount of a flavagline compound to a subject in need thereof. ### Synthesis of Flavagline Compounds

The flavagline compounds of the present invention can be synthesized using known methods in organic chemistry. For example, rocaglamide can be synthesized as described in the literature (Ribeiro et al., 2012; Thuaud et al., 2009, 2011). The synthesis involves the preparation of the cyclopenta[b]benzofuran core and the introduction of specific substituents, such as R8, to enhance the potency and selectivity of the compound.

### Inhibition of RAS Activation

The flavagline compounds of the present invention inhibit RAS activation by disrupting the interaction between RAS and prohibitins. This inhibition is achieved by targeting the prohibitin complex (PHB1 and PHB2) at the plasma membrane, which is essential for the formation of RAS nanoclusters and the activation of downstream signaling pathways. The inhibition of RAS activation is demonstrated using various in vitro and in vivo assays, including the NanoBiT assay, active RAS pulldown assay, and soft agar colony formation assay.

### In Vitro Assays

#### NanoBiT Assay

The NanoBiT assay is used to quantify the activation of RAS in cells. The assay involves the expression of a split luciferase system, where the large fragment (LgBit) is fused to RAS and the small fragment (SmBit) is fused to the RAS-binding domain (RBD) of CRAF. The interaction between RAS and CRAF-RBD is detected by the complementation of the split luciferase fragments, which results in the production of a luminescent signal. Treatment with flavagline compounds, such as rocaglamide, reduces the luminescent signal, indicating the inhibition of RAS activation.

#### Active RAS Pulldown Assay

The active RAS pulldown assay is used to detect the GTP-bound form of RAS in cells. The assay involves the use of a GST-fused RAS-binding domain (RBD) of CRAF to precipitate GTP-bound RAS from cell lysates. Treatment with flavagline compounds, such as rocaglamide, reduces the amount of GTP-bound RAS, indicating the inhibition of RAS activation.

### In Vivo Assays

#### Soft Agar Colony Formation Assay

The soft agar colony formation assay is used to assess the ability of flavagline compounds to inhibit the growth of cancer cells in vitro. Cancer cells, such as HCT-116, ASPC-1, and Calu-1, which carry KRAS mutations, are treated with flavagline compounds and seeded in soft agar. The formation of colonies is quantified, and treatment with flavagline compounds, such as rocaglamide, reduces the number of colonies, indicating the inhibition of cell growth.

#### Animal Experiments

The efficacy of flavagline compounds in inhibiting RAS-driven tumorigenesis is assessed using autochthonous mouse models. For example, a KRAS G12D-driven non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) mouse model is used to evaluate the effects of rocaglamide treatment. Mice are treated with rocaglamide at a concentration of 2.5 mg/kg body weight intraperitoneally (i.p.) three times a week for 6 weeks. Treatment with rocaglamide reduces the number of lung nodules, indicating the inhibition of KRAS-driven tumorigenesis.

### Mechanism of Action

The mechanism of action of flavagline compounds in inhibiting RAS activation involves the disruption of the interaction between RAS and prohibitins. Prohibitins, particularly PHB1, are essential for the formation of RAS nanoclusters and the activation of downstream signaling pathways. Treatment with flavagline compounds, such as rocaglamide, prevents the interaction between RAS and PHB1, leading to the inhibition of RAS nanoclustering and the activation of downstream effectors, such as the RAF/MEK/ERK pathway.

### Safety and Toxicity

The safety and toxicity of flavagline compounds, such as rocaglamide, are evaluated using in vivo studies. Mice are treated with rocaglamide at the concentrations employed in the efficacy studies, and liver and kidney toxicity are assessed by measuring liver enzyme activities and blood creatine levels. No significant toxic effects are observed, indicating that flavagline compounds are safe for therapeutic use.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel approach for inhibiting RAS activation in cells by targeting the interaction between RAS and prohibitins. The use of flavagline compounds, such as rocaglamide, offers a promising therapeutic strategy for treating cancers associated with RAS mutations. The methods and compositions of the present invention are effective in inhibiting RAS activation, reducing cell growth, and preventing tumorigenesis in both in vitro and in vivo models.
