# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to a bivalent vaccine comprising outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) derived from *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A. The vaccine is designed to induce a broad-spectrum protective immune response against both *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*, which are causative agents of enteric fever. The vaccine is particularly useful for preventing systemic infection and reducing the bacterial load in the intestines, thereby providing comprehensive protection against typhoidal and paratyphoidal infections.

## BACKGROUND AND PRIOR ART OF THE INVENTION

Enteric fever, caused by *Salmonella enterica* serovars Typhi and Paratyphi A, is a significant global health burden, affecting millions of people annually and leading to substantial morbidity and mortality. Despite the availability of licensed vaccines against *S. Typhi*, such as the live-attenuated oral vaccine Ty21a and the parenteral polysaccharide Vi-vaccine, there remains a critical need for a vaccine that can protect against both *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*. The current Vi-vaccine is ineffective against *S. Paratyphi A* due to the absence of the Vi polysaccharide in this serovar. Therefore, the development of a bivalent vaccine that can confer protection against both pathogens is essential.

Outer membrane vesicles (OMVs) are naturally secreted by Gram-negative bacteria and have emerged as promising candidates for next-generation acellular vaccines. OMVs contain various pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), including lipopolysaccharides (LPS), outer membrane proteins, and other immunogenic components, which can stimulate both innate and adaptive immune responses. The unique composition of OMVs makes them potent immunogens that can induce long-term protective immunity without the need for additional adjuvants.

Previous studies have demonstrated the potential of OMVs as vaccines against various pathogens, including *Neisseria meningitidis* and *Vibrio cholerae*. However, the development of a bivalent OMV-based vaccine specifically targeting *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* has not been fully explored. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a bivalent OMV-based vaccine that can effectively protect against both serovars of *Salmonella*.

## OBJECTIVES OF THE INVENTION

The primary objectives of the present invention are as follows:

1. **To Develop a Bivalent Vaccine**: To create a bivalent vaccine comprising OMVs derived from *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* that can induce a broad-spectrum protective immune response against both serovars.
2. **To Enhance Immune Response**: To ensure that the vaccine can activate both humoral and cell-mediated immune responses, thereby providing comprehensive protection against systemic and intestinal infections.
3. **To Reduce Bacterial Load**: To reduce the bacterial load in the intestines and systemic organs, thereby preventing the spread of infection and reducing the severity of enteric fever.
4. **To Provide Long-Term Protection**: To develop a vaccine that can induce long-lasting immunity, offering sustained protection against *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*.
5. **To Ensure Safety and Efficacy**: To ensure that the vaccine is safe for human use and demonstrates high efficacy in preclinical and clinical trials.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Bacterial Strains and Culture Conditions

The OMV antigens were prepared from *S. Typhi* strain C-6953 and *S. Paratyphi A* strain C-6915, which were obtained from the National Institute of Cholera and Enteric Diseases (NICED) culture bank. These strains were stored in 20% glycerol in brain heart infusion broth (Difco, USA) at −80°C. Prior to experimentation, each strain was grown in Tryptic Soy Broth (TSB; Difco, USA) at 37°C under shaking conditions (100 rpm) or on plates in Tryptic Soy Agar (TSA; Difco, NJ, USA).

### Preparation of OMVs

OMVs were prepared from the *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* strains using a modified method adapted from Sinha et al. Cells were grown to the exponential phase at 37°C under shaking conditions. The culture was then centrifuged at 8000 rpm for 40 minutes at 4°C, and the supernatant was filtered through a 0.22 μm bacterial filter (Millipore, USA). OMVs were subsequently purified by ultra-centrifugation (4 hours, 140,000 x g, 4°C) using a Sorvall T-865 rotor and re-suspended in Phosphate-Buffered Saline (PBS, pH 7.4). The protein concentration was determined using the modified Lowry protein assay kit (Pierce, USA), and the reducing sugar in LPS O-Ag was measured using the method described by Dubois et al. The amount of protein in each batch ranged from 400 to 440 μg/ml, and the exact amount was calculated before use in immunization or in vitro studies.

### Negative Staining of OMVs and OMV-Secreting Bacteria

A 5 μl aliquot of secreted OMVs was placed on a carbon-coated grid and allowed to absorb for 1 minute. The grid was then washed with two drops of Tris-HCl buffer and stained with a 2% aqueous solution of uranyl acetate. For negative staining of bacteria, the same procedure was followed using log-phase live bacterial cells. Both the negatively stained OMVs and bacteria-secreting OMVs were observed under a Tecnai 12 (Bio Twin Transmission Electron Microscope, FEI, the Netherlands) operating at 80 kV.

### Animals

Seven-week-old female BALB/c mice were obtained from the animal resource division of NICED, Kolkata. The mice were housed in a controlled environment with a temperature of 25°C and humidity of 75%. They were provided with sterile food and water. All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the standard operating procedures outlined by the Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals (CPCSEA), Ministry of Environment and Forests, Government of India. The animal experimental protocol was approved by the Institutional Animal Ethical Committee of NICED with project approval number PRO/117/June, 2015–June, 2018.

### Collection of Serum and Stool

Blood was collected from the lateral tail vein of the mice at various time points (0th, 14th, 21st, 28th, 35th, 78th, and 90th days) after the first oral immunization. The collected blood was centrifuged at 1000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C, and the serum was stored at −20°C. Stools were collected aseptically from the mice at the same time intervals, homogenized in protease inhibitor-containing PBS, and centrifuged at 10,000 x g for 10 minutes to remove debris. The supernatant was collected and stored at −20°C for further use.

### SDS-PAGE and Immunoblot

The total protein content of the OMVs, along with OMV-associated LPS, was determined by SDS-PAGE. OMVs were incubated with Proteinase-K and SDS at 60°C for 2 hours to isolate the OMV-associated LPS. The samples were then boiled in SDS-PAGE buffer and loaded onto a 12% SDS-PAGE gel. The gel was stained with Coomassie or silver stain. Immunoblot analysis was performed to determine the immunogenic components present in the OMVs, mature LPS, and outer membrane proteins (OMPs). The samples were electrophoresed, transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes, and probed with anti-OMV serum from immunized mice and convalescent serum from mice. The blots were developed using alkaline phosphatase substrate BCIP/NBT or Amersham™ ECL™ Prime Western Blotting Detection Reagent.

### Dot Blot Assay

LPS from the *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A* strains was blotted directly onto a nitrocellulose membrane in different concentrations. The membrane was washed with Tris-Buffered Saline (TBS) containing 0.1% Tween-20, incubated with anti-OMV antiserum from immunized mice, and then incubated with anti-mouse IgG-HRP secondary antibody. The blot was developed by chemiluminescence.

### ELISA

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) were performed to measure different immunoglobulins, including IgG and its subtypes (IgG1, IgG2a, IgG3), IgA, sIgA, and IgM. Disposable polystyrene micro-titer wells were coated with the OMVs and incubated for 18 hours at 4°C. The wells were washed and blocked with Bovine Serum Albumin (BSA). Serially diluted serum samples were added, followed by HRP-conjugated goat anti-mouse immunoglobulin. The substrate o-phenyl-Di-amine (OPD) was added, and the reaction was stopped with 2 N sulfuric acid. The optical density (OD) at 492 nm was measured. The same procedure was followed for ELISAs against Vi-polysaccharide of *S. Typhi*.

### Ex Vivo Studies on Isolated Dendritic Cells

#### Splenocyte Re-Stimulation Assay

One week after the last immunization, splenic cells from immunized BALB/c mice were cultured in complete RPMI 1640 medium containing 10% FBS. The cells were treated with 10 μg/ml of the bivalent OMV and incubated at 37°C for 24 hours in the presence of 5% CO2. Cytokines, including IFN-γ, IL-6, and IL-17, were measured using cytokine ELISA kits.

#### Isolation of Bone Marrow-Derived Dendritic Cells (BMDCs)

Immature bone marrow cells were isolated from naïve BALB/c mice and cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS and Granulocyte-Macrophage Colony Stimulating Factor (GM-CSF) until maturation. The matured BMDCs were stimulated with 10 μg/ml of the bivalent OMV for 24 hours in the presence of 5% CO2. Th17-polarizing cytokines were measured using cytokine ELISA kits.

#### BMDC-Splenic CD4 T Cell Co-Culture

BMDCs were isolated from naïve BALB/c mice and cultured as described above. Splenic cells from both naïve and immunized BALB/c mice were collected, and CD4 T cells were isolated using a Dynal® Mouse CD4 Negative Isolation Kit. The CD4 T cells were co-cultured with BMDCs at a 4:1 ratio for 8 hours in U-bottom microtiter plates under the same conditions, with a stimulation of 10 μg/ml bivalent OMV. Th1 and Th17-polarizing cytokines were measured using cytokine ELISA kits.

## The Non-Limiting Advantages are Given Below:

1. **Broad-Spectrum Protection**: The bivalent OMV-based vaccine provides comprehensive protection against both *S. Typhi* and *S. Paratyphi A*, addressing the limitations of existing vaccines that are specific to *S. Typhi*.
2. **Enhanced Immune Response**: The vaccine induces a robust immune response, including the production of high levels of IgG, IgA, and IgM antibodies, as well as the activation of Th1 and Th17 cell-mediated immune responses.
3. **Reduced Bacterial Load**: The vaccine significantly reduces the bacterial load in the intestines and systemic organs, thereby preventing the spread of infection and reducing the severity of enteric fever.
4. **Long-Term Immunity**: The vaccine confers long-lasting immunity, with detectable antibody titers up to 90 days post-immunization.
5. **Safety and Efficacy**: The vaccine is safe for use and demonstrates high efficacy in preclinical studies, making it a promising candidate for phase-I clinical trials.
6. **Natural Adjuvant Properties**: The OMVs contain natural adjuvants such as LPS and outer membrane proteins, eliminating the need for additional adjuvants and enhancing the overall immunogenicity of the vaccine.
7. **Oral Administration**: The vaccine can be administered orally, making it convenient and suitable for mass vaccination programs in endemic regions.
