# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of pharmacology and biotechnology, specifically to methods and compositions for upregulating the expression of utrophin A, a protein that can compensate for the absence of dystrophin in Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD). The invention provides a novel approach to treating DMD by utilizing a small molecule, nabumetone, to enhance the expression of utrophin A, thereby potentially ameliorating the symptoms of the disease.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a severe, X-linked genetic disorder characterized by progressive muscle degeneration and weakness. It is caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene, which encodes a protein crucial for maintaining the structural integrity of muscle fibers. In the absence of dystrophin, the dystrophin-associated protein complex (DAPC) is disrupted, leading to muscle fiber damage during contraction, chronic inflammation, and calcium misregulation. Over time, this results in the replacement of muscle fibers with fatty and fibrous tissue, ultimately leading to loss of mobility, respiratory and cardiac complications, and a shortened lifespan.

Utrophin, an autosomal homologue of dystrophin, shares a high degree of structural and functional similarity with dystrophin. Unlike dystrophin, utrophin is expressed in fetal muscle and persists in adult muscle at the neuromuscular and myotendinous junctions. Studies in animal models of DMD have shown that overexpression of utrophin can restore normal muscle function, making it an attractive therapeutic target. However, no utrophin upregulation therapy is currently available for clinical use in DMD patients.

Traditional approaches to developing new drugs for DMD, such as high-throughput screening and lead optimization, are time-consuming and costly. An alternative strategy is to repurpose existing drugs, which have already been approved for other conditions, to accelerate the development of new therapies. This approach leverages the existing knowledge of pharmacokinetics, bioavailability, and safety profiles of these drugs, potentially reducing the time and resources required for clinical trials.

This upregulation of utrophin A can potentially compensate for the absence of dystrophin, thereby ameliorating the symptoms of Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD).

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Generation and Validation of C2C12Utrn Stable Cell Line

**Objective**: To generate and validate a stable cell line expressing a luciferase reporter under the control of the human utrophin A promoter.

**Methods**:
1. **Cell Culture**: C2C12 mouse muscle cells were cultured in high glucose DMEM with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 2 mM L-glutamine, 100 U/ml penicillin, and 0.1 mg/ml streptomycin.
2. **Transfection**: C2C12 cells were transfected with a construct containing a 2.3 kb human utrophin A promoter region linked to a luciferase reporter gene using Lipofectamine 2000.
3. **Selection**: Transfected cells were selected using hygromycin B (250 mg/ml) to obtain stably transfected cells.
4. **Validation**: Clones were validated using a luciferase assay and PCR with utrophin A promoter-specific primers.

**Results**:
- A stable cell line, named C2C12utrn, was generated and validated. The C2C12utrn cells exhibited robust luciferase activity driven by the utrophin A promoter.

### Example 2: Development of Utrophin Promoter Activation Assay

**Objective**: To develop a high-throughput utrophin promoter activation assay using the C2C12utrn stable cell line.

**Methods**:
1. **Optimization of Cell Number**: The optimal number of C2C12utrn cells per well was determined to be 3000 cells, resulting in 70% confluence at the time of luciferase quantification.
2. **DMSO Tolerance**: The tolerance of the C2C12utrn cell line to DMSO was evaluated, and concentrations up to 0.2% were found to have little effect on luciferase activity.
3. **Positive Control Selection**: Four compounds known to upregulate utrophin (L-arginine, okadaic acid, heregulin, and trichostatin A) were tested for their ability to activate the utrophin A promoter. Trichostatin A (TSA) was selected as the positive control due to its robust and dose-dependent activation of the promoter.

**Results**:
- The utrophin promoter activation assay was optimized and validated, with a Z-factor of 0.6 and a percentage coefficient of variation (% CV) under 10%.

### Example 3: Primary Screen of Prestwick Chemical Library

**Objective**: To screen the Prestwick Chemical Library for compounds that activate the utrophin A promoter.

**Methods**:
1. **Screening**: The C2C12utrn cells were treated with compounds from the Prestwick Chemical Library at a final concentration of 2 µg/ml (approximately 5 µM).
2. **Hit Identification**: Hits were identified based on a threshold of 20% upregulation of luciferase activity.
3. **Hit Confirmation**: High-throughput dose-response curves were generated for the initial hits to confirm their dose-dependent activation of the utrophin A promoter.

**Results**:
- Out of 1120 compounds, 20 hits were identified, and 14 of these hits were confirmed upon dose-response testing. Nabumetone, an FDA-approved drug, was selected for further validation due to its high fold-change and lack of cellular toxicity.

### Example 4: Independent Validation of Nabumetone

**Objective**: To independently validate the effect of nabumetone on endogenous utrophin A expression in C2C12 cells.

**Methods**:
1. **Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qRT-PCR)**: C2C12 cells were treated with nabumetone (25 µM) for 24 hours, and utrophin A mRNA levels were measured using a TaqMan qRT-PCR assay.
2. **Western Blotting**: C2C12 cells were treated with nabumetone (25 µM) for 4 days, and utrophin protein levels were measured by Western blotting.

**Results**:
- Treatment with nabumetone resulted in a statistically significant 1.8-fold increase in endogenous utrophin A mRNA and a 1.2-fold increase in utrophin protein levels, confirming its ability to upregulate utrophin A expression.
