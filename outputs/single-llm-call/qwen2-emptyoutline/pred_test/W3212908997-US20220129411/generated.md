# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Quantum computation presents numerous challenges, one of which is the efficient generation of quantum circuits that realize a given unitary operation. This problem, known as quantum circuit synthesis or optimization, is particularly critical for the Clifford group, a subgroup of all unitary transformations. The Clifford group is pivotal in various quantum computing applications, including quantum error correction, quantum state distillation, randomized benchmarking, entanglement studies, and shadow tomography. Elements of the Clifford group are frequently encountered in physical-level and fault-tolerant quantum circuits and can sometimes constitute an entire quantum algorithm.

A key property of the Clifford group is its status as a unitary 2-design, ensuring that a random, uniformly distributed element of the Clifford group shares the same second-order moments as a Haar random unitary operator. This property allows random Clifford operators to serve as substitutes for Haar random unitaries in applications dependent on second-order moments. Unlike Haar random unitaries, Clifford operators can be efficiently implemented by quantum circuits. For instance, randomized benchmarking measures the decay rate of a signal generated by sequences of random Clifford operators, providing a scalable fidelity metric for multi-qubit operations. Classical shadows, another application, offer a succinct classical description of multi-qubit quantum states that can be efficiently measured without full state tomography.

Clifford circuits also facilitate basis change transformations in quantum simulation algorithms. Simultaneous diagonalization of mutually commuting Pauli operators by a Clifford basis change can reduce circuit depth in quantum chemistry Hamiltonian simulations. Similarly, tapering off qubits for quantum simulations by mapping Pauli-type symmetries to single-qubit Pauli Z can remove qubits from the simulation.

Previous studies have focused on asymptotically optimal implementations of n-qubit Clifford circuits, achieving this with \( \Theta(n^2 / \log(n)) \) gates. However, practical aspects of Clifford circuit optimization aim to minimize the number of entangling gates, particularly CNOT gates, which are more costly in physical implementations. This focus on minimizing CNOT gates, while ignoring connectivity constraints, is motivated by the higher cost and error rates associated with entangling gates compared to single-qubit gates.

Despite the existence of asymptotically optimal synthesis methods, current approaches to exactly optimal Clifford circuit synthesis are computationally prohibitive for more than six qubits. Therefore, efficient heuristics are essential for practical applications, especially for circuits with more than six qubits.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides heuristic methods for the synthesis and optimization of Clifford circuits, focusing on minimizing the number of entangling gates, particularly CNOT gates. The invention bridges the gap between non-scalable methods for exactly optimal Clifford circuit synthesis and suboptimal but asymptotically optimal synthesis methods. The core of the invention involves a bidirectional synthesis approach and two circuit optimization techniques: a Clifford-specific extension of template matching and symbolic peephole optimization.

The bidirectional synthesis approach reduces the number of qubits by recursively applying transformations that act trivially on a selected qubit. The template matching technique partitions the circuit into "compute," "swap," and "Pauli" stages, optimizing the "compute" stage using templates and the "swap" stage by aligning swaps with two-qubit gates. Symbolic peephole optimization introduces symbolic Pauli gates (SPGs) to optimize subcircuits spanning small sets of qubits, reducing the number of SPGs and CNOTs.

The invention has been numerically evaluated using two sets of benchmarks: a database of optimal Clifford circuits for six qubits and a toy model of Hamiltonian evolution for up to 64 qubits. The results demonstrate that the proposed methods recover optimal CNOT counts for 97.9% of the six-qubit circuits and reduce the average CNOT count by 64.7% for Hamiltonian evolution circuits compared to existing methods.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Bidirectional Synthesis Approach

The bidirectional synthesis approach constructs Clifford circuits by recursively reducing the number of qubits. Given an n-qubit Clifford unitary \( U \), the approach sequentially constructs Clifford circuits \( L_1, L_2, \ldots, L_n \) and \( R_1, R_2, \ldots, R_n \) such that:
\[ C_j = L_j^{-1} C R_j^{-1} \]
where \( C_j \) acts trivially on the first \( j \) qubits. This results in a circuit implementing \( C \) with a CNOT cost of at most \( 3n^2 / 4 + O(n) \).

### Template Matching for Clifford Circuits

Template matching is extended to Clifford circuits by partitioning the circuit into "compute," "swap," and "Pauli" stages. The "compute" stage consists of Hadamard (H), Phase (S), CNOT, and CZ gates. The "swap" and "Pauli" stages are optimized separately. The "compute" stage is optimized using templates, and the "swap" stage is optimized by aligning swaps with two-qubit gates. Templates are applied to reduce the gate count, and Hadamard and Phase gates are pushed to one side of the two-qubit gates to increase optimization opportunities.

### Symbolic Peephole Optimization

Symbolic peephole optimization introduces symbolic Pauli gates (SPGs) to optimize subcircuits spanning small sets of qubits. The subcircuit acting on a subset of qubits \( A \) is optimized to reduce the number of SPGs. The optimization respects the temporal order of SPGs and preserves the overall phase of the circuit. The method uses dynamic programming to minimize the cost function, which includes the number of CNOTs and SPGs.

### Full Algorithm

The full algorithm combines the bidirectional synthesis approach and the two optimization techniques. The process begins by synthesizing the circuit using the bidirectional "greedy" compiler. The synthesized circuit is then partitioned into three stages: "compute," "swap," and "Pauli." Template matching and swap gate merging are performed until no further optimization is achieved. Symbolic peephole optimization is then applied, followed by a final pass of template matching to reduce the single-qubit gate count.

### Numerical Evaluation

The proposed methods were evaluated using two sets of benchmarks. The first benchmark involved 1,003 randomly sampled six-qubit Clifford unitaries, with the methods recovering optimal CNOT counts for 97.9% of the circuits. The second benchmark involved a toy model of Hamiltonian evolution for up to 64 qubits, with the methods reducing the average CNOT count by 64.7% compared to existing methods.

### Conclusion

The invention provides efficient heuristic methods for the synthesis and optimization of Clifford circuits, focusing on minimizing the number of entangling gates. The bidirectional synthesis approach and the two optimization techniques—template matching and symbolic peephole optimization—demonstrate significant improvements in gate count reduction, making the methods highly valuable for practical quantum computing applications.
