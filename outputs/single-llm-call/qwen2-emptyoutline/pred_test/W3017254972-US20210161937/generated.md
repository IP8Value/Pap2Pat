# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and pharmacology, specifically to methods and compositions for extending lifespan and improving healthspan in organisms. The invention involves the activation of mitochondrial uncoupling, particularly through the upregulation of uncoupling proteins (UCPs), to achieve beneficial effects on metabolism, cellular homeostasis, and longevity.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Circadian rhythms are endogenous, approximately 24-hour cycles that are present in most living organisms, including humans. These rhythms are controlled by a network of molecular clocks, which are composed of transcriptional feedback loops regulating the expression of genes that control various cellular functions. In Drosophila melanogaster, the core circadian clock includes the transcriptional activators Clock (CLK) and Cycle (CYC), which induce the expression of the repressors Period (PER) and Timeless (TIM). These repressors, in turn, inhibit the activity of CLK and CYC, forming a negative feedback loop.

Disruption of circadian rhythms has been implicated in various age-related diseases and shortened lifespan. However, the specific mechanisms by which circadian regulation affects metabolism and longevity remain poorly understood. Recent studies have shown that loss of the repressive arm of the circadian clock, particularly the loss of the Period (PER) protein, can extend lifespan in male Drosophila. This lifespan extension is accompanied by increased mitochondrial uncoupling, a process that dissociates the electron transport chain from ATP synthesis, leading to increased respiration and heat production.

Mitochondrial uncoupling is primarily mediated by uncoupling proteins (UCPs), which are located in the inner mitochondrial membrane. UCPs allow protons to leak back into the mitochondrial matrix, thereby dissipating the proton gradient and reducing the efficiency of ATP production. This process has been shown to have several beneficial effects, including reduced reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, improved cellular homeostasis, and extended lifespan.

Despite these findings, there is a need for methods and compositions that can effectively activate mitochondrial uncoupling to achieve similar beneficial effects in a broader range of organisms, including humans. The present invention addresses this need by providing methods and compositions for activating mitochondrial uncoupling, particularly through the upregulation of UCPs, to extend lifespan and improve healthspan.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Mitochondrial Uncoupling**: A process in which the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane is dissipated, leading to increased respiration and heat production, and reduced ATP synthesis.
- **Uncoupling Proteins (UCPs)**: Proteins located in the inner mitochondrial membrane that facilitate the leakage of protons back into the mitochondrial matrix, thereby dissipating the proton gradient.
- **Period (PER)**: A protein that is part of the core circadian clock in Drosophila and other organisms, acting as a repressor of the transcriptional activators Clock and Cycle.
- **Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS)**: Chemically reactive molecules containing oxygen, which can cause cellular damage and are implicated in aging and various diseases.
- **Intestinal Homeostasis**: The maintenance of a stable and healthy state in the intestine, including proper cellular proliferation and barrier function.

### Activation of Mitochondrial Uncoupling has Beneficial Effects

The activation of mitochondrial uncoupling has been shown to have several beneficial effects on metabolism, cellular homeostasis, and longevity. In particular, increased mitochondrial uncoupling can:

1. **Reduce Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) Production**: Mitochondrial uncoupling reduces the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which in turn reduces the production of ROS. ROS are known to cause cellular damage and are implicated in aging and various diseases, including cancer and neurodegenerative disorders.

2. **Improve Intestinal Homeostasis**: Increased mitochondrial uncoupling in the intestine can prevent age-related cellular proliferation and barrier dysfunction. This is particularly important because the maintenance of intestinal homeostasis is a major predictor of mortality in many organisms.

3. **Extend Lifespan**: Studies in Drosophila have shown that loss of the repressive arm of the circadian clock, particularly the loss of the Period (PER) protein, extends lifespan by increasing mitochondrial uncoupling. This lifespan extension is mediated by the upregulation of uncoupling proteins (UCPs), particularly UCP4C, in the intestine.

- **UCP Agonists**: Small molecules or peptides that directly activate UCPs, such as UCP4C, to increase mitochondrial uncoupling.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Loss of the Repressive Arm of the Transcriptional Circadian Clock Extended Lifespan Independent of Dietary Protein Restriction and Reduced Insulin-like Signaling

To investigate the effects of loss of the repressive arm of the circadian clock on lifespan, we examined the lifespans of four established arrhythmic Drosophila mutants: cycle (cyc01), period (per01), and timeless (tim01), and flies ubiquitously expressing a dominant-negative form of Clock (DN-Clock). Consistent with other reports, functional disruption of the circadian transcriptional activators Cycle or Clock shortened the lifespan of male flies relative to controls. In contrast, functional disruption of the circadian transcriptional repressors Per and Tim significantly increased lifespan (15–20%) relative to controls. This lifespan extension was male-specific and independent of dietary protein restriction and reduced insulin-like signaling.

To confirm that the lifespan extension was due to the loss of Per protein, we restored Per expression using the UAS–GAL4 system. Expressing either of the two independent period transgenes using the timeless–GAL4 driver or the ubiquitin–GAL4 driver in the per01 null background reverted the lifespan of per01 mutants to that of control animals. These results demonstrate that loss of Per expression extends lifespan.

#### Period Mutants Exhibited a High Metabolic Rate Due to Mitochondrial Uncoupling

To investigate the metabolic changes associated with the lifespan extension of per01 mutants, we measured various metabolic parameters. per01 mutants exhibited hyperphagia (increased feeding), decreased starvation resistance, low levels of lipid storage, and increased starvation-induced lipid utilization relative to controls. These metabolic changes suggested a high metabolic rate.

Consistent with this, per01 mutants produced more CO2 throughout the circadian cycle relative to controls, indicating increased respiration. This increased respiration was not due to increased mitochondrial ATP synthesis but rather to mitochondrial uncoupling. To directly test this, we measured mitochondrial function in vitro. per01 mutant mitochondria exhibited increased oxygen consumption, increased leak respiration, and decreased membrane potential, all hallmarks of mitochondrial uncoupling. Additionally, per01 mutants recovered faster from cold shock, suggesting increased heat production.

#### Endogenous Uncoupling Protein UCP4C was Necessary for Period Mutant Lifespan Extension and Sufficient to Extend Wild-Type Lifespan

To determine if the increased mitochondrial uncoupling observed in per01 mutants was necessary for their lifespan extension, we genetically manipulated the expression of endogenous uncoupling proteins (UCPs). We found that the expression of UCP4B and UCP4C was circadian-regulated in control flies and constitutively high in per01 mutants. Disruption of UCP4B/C expression in per01 mutants reverted the mitochondrial uncoupling phenotypes and the lifespan extension to that of controls.

To further test the role of UCP4C in lifespan extension, we overexpressed UCP4C in wild-type flies using the UAS–GAL4 system. Flies overexpressing UCP4C exhibited mitochondrial uncoupling phenotypes similar to per01 mutants, including increased leak respiration, decreased membrane potential, and increased CO2 production. Most importantly, the constitutive expression of UCP4C extended the lifespan of otherwise wild-type flies to the same extent as per01 mutants. These results demonstrate that UCP4C is both necessary and sufficient for the lifespan extension observed in per01 mutants.

#### Lifespan Extension was Mediated by Loss of Per Specifically in the Intestine

To determine the tissue-specific effects of Per loss on lifespan extension, we rescued period expression in different organ systems using the UAS–GAL4 system. While ubiquitous expression of Per protein during adulthood reverted the lifespan of per01 mutants to that of controls, neuronal expression of Per did not. In contrast, intestinal expression of Per in the whole intestine or specifically in intestinal stem cells (ISCs) and enteroblasts (EBs) reverted the lifespan of per01 mutants to that of controls. These results suggest that loss of Per in the intestine is required for the lifespan extension of per01 mutants.

To directly test if loss of Per in the intestine is sufficient to extend lifespan, we used a UAS–GAL4-based CRISPR system to disrupt the period gene. Ubiquitous deletion of per via the daughterless GeneSwitch driver (daGS–Gal4) was sufficient to extend the lifespan of control flies but not per01 mutants. Similar to per01 mutants, flies with ubiquitous disruption of period were completely arrhythmic in constant darkness. Tissue-specific CRISPR-mediated disruption of period in either the whole intestine or specifically in ISCs/EBs during either development or adulthood also extended lifespan. These results demonstrate that the intestinal circadian clock plays a crucial role in limiting lifespan in Drosophila.
