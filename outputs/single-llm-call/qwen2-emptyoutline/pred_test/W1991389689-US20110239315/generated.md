# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and biotechnology, particularly to the design and use of transcription activator-like (TAL) effector proteins for the specific modulation of gene expression in human cells. More specifically, the invention provides methods and compositions for constructing TAL effector proteins with designed repeat architectures and for using these proteins to activate the expression of target genes in human cells. The invention further encompasses the use of a modular cloning strategy, termed "Golden TAL Technology," to facilitate the rapid and accurate assembly of TAL effector proteins with desired DNA-binding specificities.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Transcription activator-like (TAL) effectors are key virulence factors of the plant pathogen *Xanthomonas* that bind to promoter regions of plant genes and act as DNA sequence-specific transcriptional activators. TAL effectors contain a central domain of tandem repeats, typically 34 amino acids long, with each repeat corresponding to one DNA base pair. The specificity of each repeat is encoded by two hypervariable amino acids (position 12 and 13) per repeat, known as repeat-variable diresidues (RVDs). The modular architecture of TAL effectors allows for the design of proteins with novel and predictable DNA-binding specificities.

While TAL effectors are naturally found in plant pathogens, their modular nature and predictable DNA-binding properties make them attractive tools for targeted genome editing and gene regulation in non-plant organisms, including human cells. Zinc finger (ZF) proteins, which contain arrays of ZF domains targeting specific DNA sequences, are currently used for these purposes. However, the DNA-binding specificities of TAL effectors are easier to predict compared to ZF proteins, making TAL effectors a promising alternative for gene regulation and genome editing.

Recent studies have shown that TAL effector derivatives can induce the expression of human genes, suggesting their potential for therapeutic applications. However, the construction of TAL proteins with specific repeat architectures remains challenging due to the highly repetitive nature of the repeats. Traditional cloning techniques are often inefficient and prone to errors, limiting the practical utility of TAL effectors in human cells.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for constructing and using TAL effector proteins to specifically modulate gene expression in human cells. The invention includes a modular cloning strategy, termed "Golden TAL Technology," which facilitates the rapid and accurate assembly of TAL effector proteins with designed repeat architectures. The TAL effector proteins constructed using this technology can be used to activate the expression of target genes in human cells, including endogenous genes.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for constructing a TAL effector protein with a designed repeat architecture. The method involves the following steps:
1. Cloning single TAL effector repeats as individual modules with flanking BpiI restriction sites.
2. Combining one to six repeats into an assembly vector using a BpiI cut-ligation reaction.
3. Assembling the repeat modules, along with N-terminal and C-terminal modules, into a final expression vector using a BsaI cut-ligation reaction.

In another aspect, the invention provides a TAL effector protein with a designed repeat architecture, wherein the protein is capable of binding to a specific DNA sequence and activating the expression of a target gene in human cells. The TAL effector protein may include a C-terminal activation domain (AD) from a heterologous source, such as the VP16 AD from the herpes simplex virus, to enhance its transcriptional activation activity.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for modulating the expression of a target gene in human cells using a TAL effector protein. The method involves introducing a TAL effector protein with a designed repeat architecture into human cells, wherein the TAL effector protein binds to a specific DNA sequence in the promoter region of the target gene and activates its expression.

The invention further provides reporter constructs and methods for evaluating the activity of TAL effector proteins in human cells. These constructs include a minimal promoter and a reporter gene, such as luciferase, with a target DNA sequence recognized by the TAL effector protein inserted upstream of the minimal promoter.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Construction of TAL Effector Proteins with Designed Repeat Architectures

The present invention provides a modular cloning strategy, termed "Golden TAL Technology," for constructing TAL effector proteins with designed repeat architectures. This technology allows for the rapid and accurate assembly of TAL effector proteins with specific DNA-binding specificities, facilitating their use in gene regulation and genome editing applications.

#### Cloning of Single TAL Effector Repeats

Single TAL effector repeats are cloned as individual modules with flanking BpiI restriction sites. The type IIs sites are designed to generate specific overhangs that determine the position of the repeat in a repeat array while preserving the amino acid sequence. For each repeat position, four key repeats (NI, HD, NN, NG) are cloned that specify the four DNA base pairs. These modules are subcloned into a pUC57 derivative with a mutated BsaI site using SmaI and T4-DNA ligase.

#### Assembly of Repeat Modules

One to six repeats can be combined into an assembly vector using a BpiI cut-ligation reaction. The assembly vectors contain a lacZ gene flanked by BpiI sites, which is replaced by the inserted repeats and used for blue-white selection of correct assembly products. The assembly vectors are designed to accommodate different numbers of repeats, allowing for the construction of TAL effector proteins with 1 to 24 specific repeats.

#### Final Assembly of TAL Effector Proteins

The repeat modules, along with N-terminal and C-terminal modules, are assembled into a final expression vector using a BsaI cut-ligation reaction. The N-terminal module includes a green fluorescent protein (GFP) tag for visualization, and the C-terminal module includes a heterologous activation domain, such as the VP16 AD from the herpes simplex virus, to enhance transcriptional activation activity. The final expression vector is designed to be compatible with various expression systems, including mammalian cells.

### Functional Testing of TAL Effector Proteins in Human Cells

The TAL effector proteins constructed using Golden TAL Technology are tested for their ability to modulate gene expression in human cells. Reporter constructs are used to evaluate the activity of TAL effector proteins in human cells. These constructs include a minimal promoter and a reporter gene, such as luciferase, with a target DNA sequence recognized by the TAL effector protein inserted upstream of the minimal promoter.

#### Reporter Assays in Human Cells

Reporter assays are performed in human cell lines, such as Hek293T-Rex, Huh7, and HeLa cells, to evaluate the transcriptional activation activity of TAL effector proteins. The reporter constructs are co-transfected into human cells along with plasmids expressing the TAL effector proteins. Luciferase activity is measured to quantify the level of transcriptional activation. The data show that TAL effector proteins with a designed repeat architecture and a heterologous activation domain, such as the VP16 AD, can specifically activate the expression of reporter genes in human cells.

#### Modulation of Endogenous Gene Expression

The TAL effector proteins are also tested for their ability to modulate the expression of endogenous human genes. Synthetic TAL effector proteins with repeats that match DNA sequences in the promoter regions of target genes, such as PUMA, IFNα1, and IFNβ1, are constructed and introduced into human cells. Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) is used to measure the levels of target gene transcripts. The data show that TAL effector proteins can specifically induce the expression of endogenous human genes, although the level of induction may vary depending on the target promoter and the cell type.

### Applications of TAL Effector Proteins

The TAL effector proteins constructed using Golden TAL Technology have a wide range of applications in molecular biology and biotechnology. These applications include:

1. **Gene Regulation**: TAL effector proteins can be used to activate or repress the expression of specific genes in human cells, providing a powerful tool for studying gene function and developing gene therapies.
2. **Genome Editing**: TAL effector proteins can be fused to nuclease domains, such as the FokI nuclease, to create TAL-effector nucleases (TALENs) for targeted genome editing. TALENs can be used to introduce precise modifications into the genome, such as insertions, deletions, or substitutions.
3. **Therapeutic Applications**: TAL effector proteins can be used to activate the expression of therapeutic genes in human cells, potentially treating genetic disorders or enhancing the immune response to viral infections.

### Advantages of Golden TAL Technology

The Golden TAL Technology offers several advantages over traditional cloning methods for constructing TAL effector proteins:

1. **Rapid Assembly**: The modular cloning strategy allows for the rapid and accurate assembly of TAL effector proteins with designed repeat architectures in a matter of days.
2. **Error-Free Ligation**: The use of type IIs restriction enzymes and specific overhangs ensures error-free ligation, reducing the risk of PCR-based errors and repeat-specific effects on translation.
3. **Flexibility**: The technology is flexible and can be adapted to construct TAL effector proteins with different N- and C-termini, tags, and vectors, making it suitable for a wide range of applications.
4. **Scalability**: The modular nature of the technology allows for the construction of TAL effector proteins with a flexible number of repeats, from 1 to 24, based on the available assembly modules.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Objective**: To demonstrate the functional replacement of the TAL effector activation domain (AD) by heterologous ADs in plant cells.

**Method**: TAL effector proteins AvrBs3 and Hax3 were modified by deleting their endogenous ADs and replacing them with the ADs of the herpes simplex virus (HSV) VP16 and the yeast GAL4 transcription activators, respectively. The modified TAL effector proteins were expressed in *Nicotiana benthamiana* leaf cells, and their transcriptional activation activity was evaluated using a reporter assay with a β-glucuronidase (GUS) reporter gene.

**Results**: The modified TAL effector proteins with deleted ADs showed reduced transcriptional activation activity compared to the native TAL effector proteins. The addition of the VP16 AD and GAL4 AD led to a slight increase in activity, but it was still substantially lower than that of the original TAL effector proteins. These results demonstrate that the endogenous AD is a major determinant of TAL effector function in plant cells, and that the VP16 AD only partially complements the natural TAL effector AD in planta.

### Example 2

**Objective**: To demonstrate the ability of TAL effector proteins to direct transcriptional activation in human cells.

**Method**: Native TAL effector proteins AvrBs3 and Hax3, as well as their derivatives with deleted ADs and substituted with the VP16 AD, were expressed in human Hek293T-Rex cells. The transcriptional activation activity of the TAL effector proteins was evaluated using a reporter assay with a luciferase reporter gene driven by a minimal human cytomegalovirus (CMV) promoter.

**Results**: Native TAL effector proteins AvrBs3 and Hax3 were able to specifically activate the expression of the luciferase reporter gene in human cells. The derivatives with deleted ADs showed low transcriptional activation activity, while the derivatives with the VP16 AD showed strong transcriptional activation activity, 2–3 times higher than the original TAL effector proteins. These results demonstrate that TAL effector proteins can function as transcriptional activators in human cells and that the substitution of the endogenous AD by the VP16 AD strongly enhances their activity.

### Example 3

**Objective**: To demonstrate the functional testing of TAL effector proteins in different human cell lines.

**Method**: TAL effector proteins with the VP16 AD were expressed in different human cell lines, including Hek293T-Rex, Huh7, and HeLa cells. The transcriptional activation activity of the TAL effector proteins was evaluated using a reporter assay with a luciferase reporter gene driven by a minimal CMV promoter.

**Results**: The TAL effector proteins with the VP16 AD were able to specifically activate the expression of the luciferase reporter gene in all tested human cell lines. The level of transcriptional activation varied between the cell lines, with Hek293T-Rex cells showing the highest activity and HeLa cells showing the lowest activity. These results demonstrate that TAL effector proteins with the VP16 AD are functional as transcriptional activators in different human cell lines.

### Example 4

**Objective**: To demonstrate the construction of synthetic TAL effector proteins with designed repeat architectures using Golden TAL Technology.

**Method**: Single TAL effector repeats were cloned as individual modules with flanking BpiI restriction sites. One to six repeats were combined into assembly vectors using a BpiI cut-ligation reaction. The repeat modules, along with N-terminal and C-terminal modules, were assembled into a final expression vector using a BsaI cut-ligation reaction. The resulting synthetic TAL effector proteins were designed to target specific DNA sequences in the promoter regions of the human PUMA, IFNα1, and IFNβ1 genes.

**Results**: The synthetic TAL effector proteins were successfully constructed using Golden TAL Technology. The proteins were expressed in human cells and localized to the nucleus, indicating correct nuclear import. These results demonstrate the feasibility and efficiency of Golden TAL Technology for constructing TAL effector proteins with designed repeat architectures.

### Example 5

**Objective**: To demonstrate the ability of synthetic TAL effector proteins to modulate the expression of endogenous human genes.

**Method**: Synthetic TAL effector proteins with repeats that match DNA sequences in the promoter regions of the human PUMA, IFNα1, and IFNβ1 genes were expressed in human Hek293T-Rex cells. The expression levels of the target genes were measured using quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR).

**Results**: The synthetic TAL effector proteins were able to specifically induce the expression of the target genes. The levels of PUMA, IFNα1, and IFNβ1 transcripts were moderately increased, with IFNα1 and IFNβ1 showing 3.1-fold and 3.5-fold upregulation, respectively. These results demonstrate that synthetic TAL effector proteins can be used to modulate the expression of endogenous human genes, providing a powerful tool for gene regulation and therapeutic applications.
