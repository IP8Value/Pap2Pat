# DESCRIPTION

## Materials and Methods

### Antibodies, Inhibitors and Product Used

The human prostate cancer cell lines C4-2B and PC-3 were utilized in this study. These cell lines were obtained from DSMZ (Braunschweig, Germany) and ATCC (CRL-1435™), respectively, and were provided by Olivier Cuvillier from IPBS, Toulouse, France. The cells were cultured in RPMI medium supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum (FCS) and 1% streptomycin and penicillin (all reagents were purchased from GIBCO-Thermo Fisher Scientific, Eugene, OR, USA). The cell lines were maintained in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2 at 37°C and were used within 2 months after thawing of frozen aliquots.

For the migration assays, the following antibodies and inhibitors were used:
- Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) directed against CCR3 (clone 444-11, reference D085-3, MBL International, Woburn, MA, USA)
- Monoclonal antibodies directed against CXCR1 (Clone 42705, reference MAB330), CXCR2 (clone 48311, reference MAB331), and CXCR4 (clone 44716, reference MAB172) (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA)
- Control IgG (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA)
- Pharmacological inhibitors: CCR1/CCR3 inhibitor, UCB35625 (Tocris, Bristol, UK); CCR2 inhibitor, sc-202525 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA); CXCR1/2 inhibitor, SB225002 (Tocris, Bristol, UK); and CXCR4 inhibitor, AMD3100 (Sigma-Aldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA).

### Cell Culture

Human subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) and bone marrow adipocytes (BM-Ads) were collected from patients undergoing hip replacement surgery at the orthopedic surgery unit of Purpan Hospital, Toulouse, France. The collection procedures were approved by the French Ministry of Education and Research (authorization DC-2017-2914; 31 May 2017). All patients provided informed consent. BM-Ads were isolated by flotation and washed at least three times with DMEM medium supplemented with 1% bovine serum albumin (BSA) (Sigma-Aldrich, Saint Louis, MO, USA) and 1% penicillin and streptomycin (DMEM 1% BSA). SAT was cleaned of blood vessels and connective tissue and washed in DMEM 1% BSA. Conditioned media (CM) were obtained by incubating 1 g of SAT or BM-Ads overnight in 8 mL of DMEM 1% BSA.

### Ex Vivo Differentiated Medullary Adipocyte Model

To establish an ex vivo differentiated medullary adipocyte model, human bone marrow stromal cells (BMSCs) were isolated from bone marrow aspirates and differentiated into adipocytes using standard protocols. The differentiated adipocytes were then used to prepare conditioned media (BM-Ad-CM) for subsequent experiments.

### Human Medullary Adipocyte Samples

Human medullary adipocyte samples were collected from patients undergoing hip replacement surgery. The samples were processed as described for the isolation of BM-Ads. The conditioned media (BM-Ad-CM) were prepared and used in migration assays to assess the chemotactic potential of these adipocytes.

### Human Visceral Adipose Tissue Samples for Analysis of the Expression of CCL7 by Q-PCR

Visceral adipose tissue samples were collected from patients undergoing abdominal surgery. The samples were immediately snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C until further processing. Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany), and cDNA was synthesized using the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). Quantitative real-time PCR (Q-PCR) was performed using the SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) on a StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). The relative expression of CCL7 was normalized to the housekeeping gene GAPDH.

### Human Periprostatic Adipose Tissue Samples

Periprostatic adipose tissue (PPAT) samples were collected from patients undergoing prostatectomy. The samples were processed as described for the isolation of BM-Ads. Conditioned media (PPAT-CM) were prepared and used in migration assays to assess the chemotactic potential of these adipocytes.

### Human Yellow Marrow and Subcutaneous Adipose Tissue Samples

Yellow marrow and subcutaneous adipose tissue samples were collected from patients undergoing various surgical procedures. The samples were processed as described for the isolation of BM-Ads. Conditioned media (Yellow Marrow-CM and Subcutaneous Adipose Tissue-CM) were prepared and used in migration assays to assess the chemotactic potential of these adipocytes.

### Mouse Perigonadal Adipose Tissue Samples

Mouse perigonadal adipose tissue samples were collected from wild-type and genetically modified mice. The samples were processed as described for the isolation of BM-Ads. Conditioned media (Perigonadal Adipose Tissue-CM) were prepared and used in migration assays to assess the chemotactic potential of these adipocytes.

### Measurement of Genic Expression by Q-PCR

Total RNA was extracted from the adipose tissue samples using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). cDNA was synthesized using the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). Quantitative real-time PCR (Q-PCR) was performed using the SYBR Green PCR Master Mix (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) on a StepOnePlus Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). The relative expression of target genes was normalized to the housekeeping gene GAPDH.

## Results

### The Ligand of CCR3, CCL7 is Involved in the Migration of Prostate Tumor Cells Against Conditioned Medium of Adipocytes

To investigate the chemotactic potential of adipocyte-conditioned media (CM) on prostate cancer (PCa) cell migration, conditioned media were prepared from human primary bone marrow adipocytes (BM-Ads) and paired subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT). Directed migration assays were performed using two PCa cell lines, C4-2B (low aggressiveness) and PC-3 (high aggressiveness). The results showed that BM-Ad-CM supported the directed migration of both PCa cell lines, with the ability of BM-Ad-CM to chemoattract tumor cells being at least equivalent to or greater than the 10% FCS positive control. In contrast, SAT-CM was significantly less chemoattractant than BM-Ad-CM.

To further explore the role of chemokine receptors in this process, various antagonists and blocking monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) were used in the migration assays. The dual inhibitor UCB35625 (targeting CCR1/CCR3) and a neutralizing mAb against CCR3 significantly inhibited the migration of both PC3 and C4-2B cell lines by 40 to 60%. No significant effect was observed with antagonists or blocking mAbs directed against CCR2, CXCR1, CXCR2, and CXCR4. These results indicate that the CCR3/CCL7 axis is involved in the directed migration of PCa cells towards BM-Ad-CM.

### Validation of the Involvement of CCR3 in Migration in Response to Adipocyte Secretions in a Mouse Tumor Line Model (TRAMP-C1P3)

To validate the involvement of CCR3 in the migration of PCa cells in response to adipocyte secretions, a mouse tumor line model (TRAMP-C1P3) was used. Conditioned media from mouse perigonadal adipose tissue (Perigonadal Adipose Tissue-CM) were prepared and used in migration assays. The results showed that Perigonadal Adipose Tissue-CM supported the directed migration of TRAMP-C1P3 cells, and this effect was significantly inhibited by the CCR3 neutralizing mAb. These findings confirm the role of CCR3 in the migration of PCa cells in response to adipocyte secretions in both human and mouse models.

### The Receptor CCR3 is Involved in Tumor Progression in Mouse Models In Vivo

To further investigate the role of CCR3 in tumor progression, in vivo studies were conducted using mouse models. TRAMP-C1P3 cells were injected into wild-type and CCR3 knockout mice, and tumor growth and metastasis were monitored. The results showed that CCR3 knockout mice exhibited significantly reduced tumor growth and metastasis compared to wild-type mice. These findings provide strong evidence that CCR3 plays a critical role in tumor progression in vivo.

### Immunohistochemical Quantification of the Vitamin B12 Transport Protein (TCH), Cell Surface Receptor (TCH-R) and Ki-67 in Human Tumor Xenografts

To assess the expression of CCR3 and its ligand CCL7 in human PCa xenografts, immunohistochemical analysis was performed. Human PCa xenografts were established in nude mice, and tissue sections were stained for CCR3, CCL7, and the proliferation marker Ki-67. The results showed that CCR3 and CCL7 were highly expressed in the xenografts, and the expression correlated with increased Ki-67 staining, indicating active tumor cell proliferation. These findings support the role of the CCR3/CCL7 axis in promoting tumor growth and progression.

### Sphingosine Kinase-1 Activity and Expression in Human Prostate Cancer Resection Specimens

To investigate the role of sphingosine kinase-1 (SK1) in PCa, the activity and expression of SK1 were measured in human prostate cancer resection specimens. Tissue samples were collected from patients undergoing radical prostatectomy, and SK1 activity was assessed using a commercially available assay kit. The results showed that SK1 activity and expression were significantly increased in PCa specimens compared to benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) specimens. Additionally, the expression of SK1 correlated with the expression of CCR3, suggesting a potential interaction between these pathways in PCa progression.

### The Expression of the Receptor CCR3 Plays a Role in the Migration of Prostate Cancer Cells Induced by Medullary Adipocytes

To further elucidate the role of CCR3 in the migration of PCa cells induced by medullary adipocytes, additional experiments were conducted using human primary BM-Ads. Conditioned media from BM-Ads were prepared from both lean and obese patients, as well as from young and elderly patients. The results showed that the chemoattractive potential of BM-Ad-CM was significantly increased in obese and elderly patients compared to lean and younger controls. This effect was completely abrogated by the CCR3 neutralizing mAb, confirming the involvement of the CCR3/CCL7 axis in the migration of PCa cells induced by medullary adipocytes.

In summary, the present invention provides a novel method for inhibiting the migration of prostate cancer cells by targeting the CCR3/CCL7 axis. The invention is based on the discovery that conditioned media from bone marrow adipocytes (BM-Ads) support the directed migration of prostate cancer cells, and this effect is mediated by the chemokine receptor CCR3 and its ligand CCL7. The invention has significant therapeutic implications for the prevention and treatment of prostate cancer metastasis, particularly to bone.
