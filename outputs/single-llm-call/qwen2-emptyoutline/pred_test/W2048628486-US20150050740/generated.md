# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for enhancing genetic transformation efficiency in bacteria, particularly in bacteria that are difficult to transform due to the presence of multiple restriction-modification (R-M) systems. The invention provides a pipeline for mimicking the DNA methylation patterns of target bacteria in a modified *Escherichia coli* host, thereby overcoming the restriction barriers posed by R-M systems.

## BACKGROUND ART

Genetic manipulation of bacteria has been a cornerstone in microbiology, enabling researchers to study bacterial metabolism, physiology, and pathogenesis, as well as to develop microbial biotechnology. However, the genetic manipulation of many bacterial species remains challenging due to the presence of restriction-modification (R-M) systems. These systems, which are widespread in bacteria and archaea, protect prokaryotic cells against invading DNA by recognizing and degrading foreign DNA with unrecognized methylation patterns. While some laboratory model species are amenable to genetic manipulation, many non-model species and strains are refractory to transformation, limiting their utility in research and biotechnology.

The difficulty in transforming bacteria with multiple R-M systems has led to the development of various strategies, such as in vitro or in vivo plasmid modification, heat inactivation of REases, and gene knockouts. However, these methods often fall short in achieving high transformation efficiencies, particularly in bacteria with complex R-M systems. There is a pressing need for a robust and efficient method to overcome the restriction barriers posed by multiple R-M systems in bacteria.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses the limitations of existing methods by providing a novel pipeline, termed Mimicking of DNA Methylation Patterns (MoDMP), for enhancing genetic transformation efficiency in bacteria. The MoDMP pipeline involves the following steps:

1. **Generation of an *E. coli* Host Lacking R-M Systems**: An *E. coli* strain is generated that lacks all known R-M systems and orphan methyltransferases (MTases). This strain, designated as *E. coli* EC135, is derived from the *E. coli* TOP10 background and is devoid of the *dam*, *dcm*, *hsdRMS*, *mrr*, *mcrA*, and *mcrBC* genes. This ensures that the host does not methylate DNA or restrict DNA with foreign methylation patterns.

2. **Cloning and Expression of Target Bacterial MTases**: Active MTases from the target bacteria are cloned into the *E. coli* EC135 strain. These MTases are expressed to alter the DNA methylation patterns of the *E. coli* host, making it resemble the methylation patterns of the target bacteria.

3. **Co-Expression of Multiple MTases**: Multiple active MTases from the target bacteria are co-expressed in the *E. coli* EC135 strain. This is achieved by constructing plasmids that carry multiple MTase genes, ensuring that the DNA methylation patterns of the *E. coli* host closely match those of the target bacteria.

4. **Preparation and Transformation of Plasmids**: Plasmids intended for transformation into the target bacteria are prepared in the *E. coli* EC135 strain expressing the target bacterial MTases. These plasmids, now carrying the correct methylation patterns, are used to transform the target bacteria, significantly enhancing transformation efficiency.

The MoDMP pipeline has been successfully applied to three representative bacterial strains: *Nitrobacter hamburgensis* X14, *Bacillus cereus* ATCC 10987, and *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* TA208. These strains, which are known to be difficult to transform due to the presence of multiple R-M systems, were successfully transformed using the MoDMP pipeline, achieving transformation efficiencies that were several orders of magnitude higher than those obtained with conventional methods.

## SPECIFIC EMBODIMENTS

### Example 1: Generation and Validation of the *E. coli* EC135 Strain

To generate the *E. coli* EC135 strain, the *dam* and *dcm* genes were deleted from the *E. coli* TOP10 background. A wild-type *recA* allele was introduced prior to *dam* inactivation to counteract the inviability of the *dam recA* double mutant strain. The resulting strain, *E. coli* EC135, lacks all known R-M systems and orphan MTases, making it an ideal host for the MoDMP pipeline.

Validation of the *E. coli* EC135 strain was performed using various assays, including dot blot analysis and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS). The results confirmed that the *E. coli* EC135 strain does not methylate DNA and does not restrict DNA with foreign methylation patterns.

### Example 2: Cloning and Characterization of Putative MTases

Putative MTases from the target bacteria were cloned into the pBAD43 vector and expressed in the *E. coli* EC135 strain. The DNA methylation activity of these MTases was analyzed using dot blot assays and LC-MS. Thirteen putative MTase genes exhibited methyl transfer activity, and their specificities were determined. The results showed that the *E. coli* EC135 strain expressing these MTases could effectively mimic the DNA methylation patterns of the target bacteria.

### Example 3: Co-Expression of Multiple Active MTases

Multiple active MTases from the target bacteria were co-expressed in the *E. coli* EC135 strain using plasmids constructed in *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*. The co-expression of these MTases resulted in the alteration of the DNA methylation patterns of the *E. coli* host, closely resembling the methylation patterns of the target bacteria. The effectiveness of the co-expression was verified using dot blot assays and restriction enzyme digestion analysis.

### Example 4: Application of the MoDMP Pipeline to Bacterial Transformation

The MoDMP pipeline was applied to enhance the genetic transformation efficiency of three difficult-to-transform bacterial strains: *Nitrobacter hamburgensis* X14, *Bacillus cereus* ATCC 10987, and *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* TA208. Plasmids prepared in the *E. coli* EC135 strain expressing the target bacterial MTases were used to transform these strains. The results demonstrated significant improvements in transformation efficiency, with the *Nitrobacter hamburgensis* X14 strain being successfully transformed for the first time, and the transformation efficiencies of *Bacillus cereus* ATCC 10987 and *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* TA208 being increased by several orders of magnitude.

In addition, the MoDMP pipeline was used to perform direct gene inactivation in *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* TA208 using non-replicative integration plasmids. The successful inactivation of the *upp* gene in *Bacillus amyloliquefaciens* TA208 validated the efficacy of the MoDMP pipeline in enabling precise genetic manipulation in difficult-to-transform bacteria.

## Conclusion

The MoDMP pipeline provides a robust and efficient method for enhancing genetic transformation efficiency in bacteria, particularly those with multiple R-M systems. By mimicking the DNA methylation patterns of target bacteria in a modified *E. coli* host, the MoDMP pipeline overcomes the restriction barriers posed by R-M systems, enabling high-efficiency transformation and precise genetic manipulation. The adaptability of the MoDMP pipeline to different bacterial groups suggests its universal potential in advancing bacterial genetics and biotechnology.
