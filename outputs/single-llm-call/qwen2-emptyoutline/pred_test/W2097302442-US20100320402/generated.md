# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

This invention relates to the field of radiation therapy, specifically to a method and apparatus for delivering spatially fractionated radiation therapy in a three-dimensional (3D) lattice configuration. The invention aims to improve the therapeutic outcome of radiation therapy by concentrating high doses of radiation within the tumor volume while minimizing exposure to surrounding healthy tissues. This approach leverages the principles of spatially fractionated radiation therapy (SFGRT) and enhances it through the use of advanced radiation delivery techniques, such as intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) and robotic-controlled linear accelerators.

## BACKGROUND

High-dose GRID radiotherapy, also known as spatially fractionated GRID radiotherapy (SFGRT), has been a less frequently utilized treatment modality since its inception in the early 1930s. Initially, two-dimensional (2D) GRID fields were employed, typically with orthovoltage beams, to create spatially alternated dose distributions. The grids were composed of a shield with an array of openings, usually circular or square, ranging in size from 0.5 to 1.5 cm. This technique was primarily used for treating advanced bulky tumors, as it allowed for higher dose delivery with acceptable skin toxicity due to the presence of undamaged skin surrounding highly exposed regions, facilitating improved repair.

Despite its limited use, significant and dramatic tumor regressions have been observed and reported in patients treated with GRID therapy. However, traditional 2D GRID treatments still expose a considerable volume of normal tissue to high radiation doses, particularly superficial tissues that are not part of the clinical target volume. This limitation has hindered the broader clinical adoption of GRID therapy.

Recent advancements in radiobiology have provided new insights into the mechanisms underlying the effectiveness of spatially fractionated radiation. These include the bystander effect, where tumor cells in low-dose regions are affected by factors such as TNF-α, TRAIL, and Ceramide, which are induced in cells under the high-dose regions. Additionally, the abscopal effect, where distant tumors or metastatic lesions not directly irradiated show a robust response, has been documented. These findings suggest that high-dose GRID radiotherapy could induce rapid and higher rates of tumor cell apoptosis in bulky and hypoxic tumors compared to conventional radiotherapy.

To address the limitations of 2D GRID therapy and capitalize on these biological insights, a new approach called 3D LATTICE Radiotherapy (LRT) has been developed. This technique involves creating a 3D dose lattice within the tumor volume, where high doses are concentrated at specific vertices, and the dose rapidly falls off between these vertices. This results in a peak-to-valley dose distribution that maximizes the therapeutic effect while minimizing damage to surrounding healthy tissues.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for delivering 3D LATTICE Radiotherapy (LRT) to treat tumors. The method involves generating a 3D dose lattice within the tumor volume, where high doses are concentrated at specific vertices, and the dose rapidly decreases between these vertices. The 3D LATTICE can be created using various advanced radiation delivery techniques, including intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT), aperture-modulated arc therapy, and robotic-controlled linear accelerators with multiple focused non-coplanar beams. The invention also encompasses the use of real-time target tracking technologies to ensure the integrity of the peak-to-valley dose distribution.

The key features of the invention include:
1. **3D Dose Lattice Formation**: Concentrating high doses within the tumor volume at specific vertices, with a rapid dose fall-off between these vertices.
2. **Advanced Delivery Techniques**: Utilizing IMRT, aperture-modulated arc therapy, and robotic-controlled linear accelerators to achieve the desired 3D dose distribution.
3. **Real-Time Target Tracking**: Employing technologies such as Synchrony and Calypso 4D to maintain the accuracy and integrity of the dose distribution.
4. **Biological Enhancements**: Leveraging the bystander and abscopal effects to enhance the therapeutic outcome of the treatment.

The invention offers several advantages over traditional 2D GRID therapy, including reduced exposure of normal tissues to high radiation doses, improved targeting of the tumor volume, and the potential for enhanced therapeutic outcomes due to the biological effects of spatially fractionated radiation.

## DESCRIPTION OF INVENTION

### Introduction

The invention pertains to a method and apparatus for delivering 3D LATTICE Radiotherapy (LRT) to treat tumors. The primary objective is to create a 3D dose lattice within the tumor volume, where high doses are concentrated at specific vertices, and the dose rapidly decreases between these vertices. This approach aims to maximize the therapeutic effect while minimizing damage to surrounding healthy tissues.

### Technical Approach

#### 3D Dose Lattice Formation

The 3D LATTICE is formed by creating a highly heterogeneous dose distribution within the tumor volume. High doses are concentrated at specific vertices, and the dose rapidly falls off between these vertices, resulting in a peak-to-valley dose distribution. The vertices are strategically placed within the tumor volume to ensure that the high-dose regions are confined to the target area, while the low-dose regions minimize exposure to surrounding healthy tissues.

#### Advanced Delivery Techniques

Several advanced radiation delivery techniques can be employed to achieve the 3D LATTICE:

1. **Intensity-Modulated Radiation Therapy (IMRT)**: IMRT involves modulating the intensity of the radiation beam to create a highly conformal dose distribution. By using multiple conformal arcs or a single arc with interdigitation, a 3D LATTICE can be generated. The maximum dose is delivered to the vertices, with a rapid dose fall-off between them.

2. **Aperture-Modulated Arc Therapy**: This technique involves using dynamic multileaf collimators (MLCs) to modulate the radiation beam during rotational delivery. By adjusting the MLCs in real-time, a 3D LATTICE can be created with precise control over the dose distribution.

3. **Robotic-Controlled Linear Accelerators**: Systems like the CyberKnife, which use a robotic arm to deliver multiple focused non-coplanar beams, can be employed to create a 3D LATTICE. Each beam is focused on a specific vertex within the tumor volume, resulting in a highly localized dose distribution.

#### Real-Time Target Tracking

To ensure the accuracy and integrity of the 3D LATTICE, real-time target tracking technologies are employed. These technologies, such as Synchrony and Calypso 4D, continuously monitor the position of the tumor and adjust the radiation delivery in real-time to account for any movement. This ensures that the high-dose vertices remain within the tumor volume, even if the tumor moves during treatment.

### Biological Enhancements

The 3D LATTICE approach leverages the bystander and abscopal effects to enhance the therapeutic outcome of the treatment. The bystander effect occurs when tumor cells in low-dose regions are affected by factors such as TNF-α, TRAIL, and Ceramide, which are induced in cells under the high-dose regions. The abscopal effect, where distant tumors or metastatic lesions not directly irradiated show a robust response, further contributes to the overall effectiveness of the treatment.

### Clinical Applications

The 3D LATTICE Radiotherapy (LRT) technique is particularly suitable for treating advanced and bulky tumors, such as those found in lung and pancreatic cancers. The ability to concentrate high doses within the tumor volume while minimizing exposure to surrounding healthy tissues makes it a promising approach for improving therapeutic outcomes and reducing treatment-related toxicity.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Lung Tumor Treatment

In a study involving a large lung tumor of 390 cc, a 3D LATTICE was generated using RapidArc IMRT technology. The 3D LATTICE resulted in higher maximum dose to the tumor volume (GTV) and a greater volume of the GTV receiving high-dose radiation compared to a conventional 2D GRID. The LATTICE plan also exposed a lower volume of lung tissue to doses above 1 Gy, demonstrating the advantage of the 3D LATTICE in sparing normal tissues.

#### Example 2: Pancreatic Mass Treatment

For a 185 cc pancreatic mass, a 3D LATTICE was achieved using the CyberKnife system. Eleven vertices were distributed throughout the tumor volume, and beams from 40-50 non-coplanar directions were used for each isocenter. The peak-to-valley dose distribution was pronounced, with high doses confined within the tumor volume and a rapid dose fall-off between vertices. The treatment plan was verified using a 3D QA phantom and Gafchromic films, showing good agreement between the measurements and the plans.

### Future Directions

The 3D LATTICE Radiotherapy (LRT) technique has the potential to revolutionize the field of radiation therapy. Further research is needed to determine the optimal configuration of the 3D dose lattice, including the number and locations of the vertices, and the size of the high-dose regions. Additionally, the use of heavy charged particle beams, such as proton and carbon ion beams, with spot-scanning nozzles, may offer even greater precision and control in delivering 3D LATTICE radiotherapy.
