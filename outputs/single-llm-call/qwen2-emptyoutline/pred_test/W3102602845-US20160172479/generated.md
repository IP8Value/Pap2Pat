# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of semiconductor devices, specifically to the fabrication of high-mobility two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) devices. More particularly, the invention pertains to a method and apparatus for fabricating split-gate devices on GaAs/AlGaAs 2DEG heterostructures using a flip-chip technique. This technique avoids the potential damage and contamination associated with traditional fabrication methods, thereby preserving the high electron mobility of the 2DEG material.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

In the realm of semiconductor technology, the fabrication of high-mobility two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG) devices is crucial for advanced applications in quantum computing, nanoelectronics, and other cutting-edge technologies. Traditional methods for fabricating split-gate devices on GaAs/AlGaAs 2DEG heterostructures involve direct processing steps on the 2DEG material. These steps often include electron-beam and optical lithography, which can lead to several issues:

1. **Contamination**: The use of polymer resists and chemicals during the gate fabrication process can leave residues that are difficult to remove. These residues can trap charges and generate undesirable fluctuations in the electron density of the 2DEG.
2. **Mobility Degradation**: Exposure to electron beams or optical lithography can cause radiation damage, leading to a reduction in electron mobility. This damage can arise from heating, electrostatic charging, ionization, displacement, and hydrocarbon contamination.
3. **Thermal Strain**: Differential thermal contractions between the 2DEG material and the gate metals can induce strain, particularly at low temperatures, affecting the electron mobility.
4. **Material Wastage**: Low-yield processes can result in the wastage of valuable high-mobility 2DEG material, especially when fabricating complex devices.

To address these challenges, there is a need for a novel fabrication technique that minimizes the risk of contamination, preserves electron mobility, and enhances the overall yield and reusability of the 2DEG material. The present invention fulfills this need by introducing a flip-chip technique for fabricating split-gate devices on GaAs/AlGaAs 2DEG heterostructures.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for fabricating split-gate devices on GaAs/AlGaAs 2DEG heterostructures using a flip-chip technique. The key aspects of the invention are as follows:

1. **Flip-Chip Substrate**: All processing steps required to fabricate the electrostatic gates are performed on a separate substrate, typically a sapphire plate. This approach avoids direct exposure of the 2DEG material to potentially damaging fabrication processes.
2. **Gate Fabrication**: The gates are fabricated on the flip-chip substrate using conventional electron-beam lithography. A thin layer of chromium is deposited to allow charge evacuation during the e-beam exposure, followed by the deposition of titanium and gold layers. The lift-off process is carefully controlled to ensure a near-perfect removal of excess metal, preventing gate leakage.
3. **Insulation Layer**: An aluminum oxide layer is deposited on the flip-chip to further prevent potential gate leakages.
4. **Assembly**: The 2DEG wafer is placed on a sample holder, and the ohmic contacts are fabricated directly on the GaAs/AlGaAs material. The flip-chip is then aligned and placed on top of the 2DEG, with a sapphire top plate held in place by BeCu springs to apply gentle mechanical pressure. Optical interference fringes are used to fine-tune the alignment of the flip-chip device.
5. **Performance**: The flip-chip devices exhibit high yield and robust performance, demonstrating pinch-off and quantized conductance both at zero field and in the quantum Hall regime. The technique is non-destructive and allows for the re-use of the 2DEG material, making it suitable for high-mobility applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Embodiment 1

#### Flip-Chip Substrate Preparation

The first step in the fabrication process involves preparing the flip-chip substrate. A sapphire plate is selected as the substrate due to its excellent insulating properties and thermal stability. The sapphire plate is cleaned thoroughly to ensure a contaminant-free surface. A thin layer of chromium (50 nm) is deposited on the sapphire plate to facilitate charge evacuation during the electron-beam exposure. This is followed by the spinning of a mixed MAA/PMMA resist, which is then exposed and developed using conventional electron-beam lithography techniques.

#### Gate Fabrication

After the resist is developed, a bilayer of titanium (5 nm) and gold (150 nm) is deposited on the sapphire plate using electron-beam evaporation. The lift-off process is performed with extreme care to achieve a near-perfect removal of excess metal, ensuring that no residual metal stands up along the edges, which could cause gate leakage or introduce an undesirable buffer space between the flip-chip gates and the 2DEG. An aluminum oxide layer (30 to 100 nm thick) is then deposited on the flip-chip to further prevent potential gate leakages.

#### Assembly with 2DEG Wafer

The 2DEG wafer, typically a GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure, is placed on a sample holder. Ohmic contacts are fabricated directly on the GaAs/AlGaAs material using either indium diffusion or the evaporation of Ge/Au/Ni/Au with a shadow mask to avoid lithography. The 2DEG wafer is connected to the contacts of the sample holder using indium-soldered gold wires. The flip-chip is then aligned and placed on top of the 2DEG, with a sapphire top plate held in place by four BeCu springs that apply gentle mechanical pressure. Optical interference fringes between the top plate and the flip-chip are used to fine-tune the alignment of the device.

#### Performance Evaluation

The flip-chip devices are tested for their performance in both zero-field and quantum Hall regimes. Conductivity versus pinch-off gate voltage measurements are taken at 25 mK using a GaAs/AlGaAs heterostructure with a moderate mobility of 2 × 10^6 cm²/V·s. The results show clear quantization of the conductance, with no pronounced hysteresis in the pinch-off curve. The devices also demonstrate robust performance in high magnetic fields up to 9 T at 20 mK, with good quantization of the integer quantum Hall (IQH) and fractional quantum Hall (FQH) plateaus.

### Embodiment 2

#### Enhanced Alignment Mechanism

In this embodiment, the alignment mechanism is enhanced to improve the precision and reliability of the flip-chip placement. Instead of relying solely on optical interference fringes, a microalignment system is integrated into the assembly process. This system uses precision micrometers and a high-resolution camera to achieve sub-micron accuracy in the alignment of the flip-chip with the 2DEG wafer. The microalignment system ensures that the contact area between the flip-chip gates and the 2DEG is minimized, reducing the air gap and improving the gate efficiency.

#### Advanced Insulation Layer

To further enhance the performance of the flip-chip devices, an advanced insulation layer is used. In addition to the aluminum oxide layer, a thin layer of silicon dioxide (SiO₂) is deposited on the flip-chip. The SiO₂ layer provides additional dielectric strength and reduces the risk of gate-to-gate crosstalk, which can be a significant issue in high-density devices. The combined use of aluminum oxide and silicon dioxide ensures a highly reliable and stable insulation layer.

#### High-Mobility Material Integration

This embodiment focuses on integrating the flip-chip technique with ultra-high mobility GaAs/AlGaAs 2DEG heterostructures. The flip-chip devices are tested on samples with mobilities exceeding 10^7 cm²/V·s. The results demonstrate that the flip-chip process can be successfully integrated with the highest mobility 2DEG materials, maintaining the high electron mobility and achieving excellent quantization of the conductance. The devices are also characterized in the quantum Hall regime at very low temperatures and high magnetic fields, confirming their robust performance and potential for future studies of sensitive quantum Hall states.

#### Reusability and Flexibility

One of the key advantages of the flip-chip technique is its non-destructive nature, allowing for the re-use of the 2DEG material. In this embodiment, the flip-chip devices are designed to be easily removable and re-mountable on different parts of the 2DEG wafer. This flexibility is particularly useful for high-mobility materials, where different parts of the wafer may have varying mobilities and electron densities. The ability to swap gates and measure different devices on the exact same piece of material enhances the versatility and efficiency of the fabrication process.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a novel and effective method for fabricating split-gate devices on GaAs/AlGaAs 2DEG heterostructures using a flip-chip technique. This approach addresses the limitations of traditional fabrication methods, offering a high-yield, non-destructive, and flexible solution for the production of high-mobility devices. The invention has significant potential for advancing the field of quantum computing and nanoelectronics, enabling the study of sensitive quantum Hall states and other high-mobility phenomena.
