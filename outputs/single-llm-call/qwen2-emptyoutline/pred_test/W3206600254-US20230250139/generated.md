# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology, specifically to the field of cellular and molecular biology. More particularly, the invention pertains to a method and system for recording and reading out biological information over time using genetically encoded, self-assembling protein chains. The invention provides a novel approach to capturing and storing temporal information about cellular activities, such as gene expression, in a format that can be easily read out using standard immunofluorescence and imaging techniques.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Reading out biological signals and processes that occur over time in living cells, organs, and organisms is crucial for advancing both basic and translational biological research. Traditional methods for monitoring biological activities, such as the imaging of genetically encoded fluorescent signal reporters, enable real-time observation but are labor-intensive and equipment-intensive. Long-term live imaging often requires monopolizing a single microscope for the duration of the experiment, limiting the number of cells that can be observed. Fixed-tissue imaging methods, while scalable, can only capture snapshots of physiological signals at specific time points and cannot support continuous recording.

Several studies have demonstrated the recording of cellular histories in nucleic acids for readout through sequencing after cells or tissues are dissociated and/or lysed. However, these methods often require the destruction of the cells or tissues, making it difficult to study intact biological systems. There is a need for a method that can record continuous time histories of physiological signals and allow for scalable signal history readout without altering normal cell physiology or cell health.

The present invention addresses this need by providing a system for recording biological information along growing protein chains made of fully genetically encoded self-assembling proteins. These protein chains, referred to as Expression Recording Islands (XRIs), bear different labels that encode different cellular states or functions. The continuous growth of the protein chain allows for the recording of the presence of different label-bearing proteins over time, enabling later readout via immunostaining and imaging after cell or tissue fixation.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and system for recording and reading out biological information over time using genetically encoded, self-assembling protein chains. The invention includes the following key components:

1. **Expression Recording Islands (XRIs)**: Genetically encoded, self-assembling protein chains that grow continuously over time, bearing different labels that encode different cellular states or functions.
2. **Label-Bearing Proteins**: Proteins with specific epitope tags (e.g., HA, FLAG, V5) that are expressed in response to specific biological events.
3. **Insulator Component**: A component fused to the lateral edge of the filament-forming monomer to reduce unwanted lateral growth and encourage linear assembly.
4. **Delivery Vectors**: Adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) or other suitable vectors for delivering the XRI genes into cells.
5. **Readout Methods**: Standard immunofluorescence and imaging techniques for visualizing and quantifying the recorded biological information.

The invention enables the recording of continuous time histories of physiological signals, such as gene expression, in a format that can be easily read out using routine laboratory techniques. The XRIs can be used in various biological systems, including cultured cells and the living mammalian brain, without altering normal cell physiology or cell health.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Compositions and Components

The present invention utilizes genetically encoded, self-assembling protein chains (XRIs) to record and store biological information over time. The XRIs are composed of filament-forming proteins that grow continuously, bearing different labels that encode different cellular states or functions. The key components of the invention are:

1. **Filament-Forming Protein (1POK(E239Y))**: A human-engineered protein that forms stable, linear filaments in mammalian cells. The protein is modified to include an insulator component to reduce unwanted lateral growth and encourage linear assembly.
2. **Insulator Component (MBP Tag)**: A highly monomeric protein fused to the lateral edge of the filament-forming monomer to block unwanted lateral binding and growth of the protein assembly.
3. **Label-Bearing Proteins**: Proteins with specific epitope tags (e.g., HA, FLAG, V5) that are expressed in response to specific biological events. The presence and density of these labels along the growing protein chain encode the temporal information of the cellular events.
4. **Delivery Vectors**: Adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) or other suitable vectors for delivering the XRI genes into cells. The vectors are designed to express the filament-forming protein and the label-bearing proteins in a controlled manner.

### Expression and Use of XRI Components

The XRIs are expressed in cells using AAVs or other suitable vectors. The expression of the filament-forming protein and the label-bearing proteins is driven by specific promoters, such as the constitutive human ubiquitin (UBC) promoter or the c-fos promoter. The continuous growth of the protein chain allows for the recording of the presence of different label-bearing proteins over time. The temporal information is stored in the order of the epitope tags along the growing protein chain.

### Molecules and Compounds

### Controls

To ensure the accuracy and reliability of the XRI system, the following controls are used:

1. **Negative Controls**: Cells or tissues without the expression of the XRI components.
2. **Positive Controls**: Cells or tissues with the expression of the XRI components and known biological events.
3. **Calibration Experiments**: Experiments to establish the time axis and calibrate the XRI system.

### Administration Means

The XRI components are administered to cells or tissues using the following means:

1. **AAV Injection**: AAVs are injected into the cells or tissues to deliver the XRI genes.
2. **Chemical Induction**: Chemicals, such as 4-OHT, are used to induce the expression of specific genes in a controlled manner.

### Assessment Methods

The recorded biological information is read out using the following methods:

1. **Immunofluorescence**: Standard immunofluorescence techniques are used to visualize and quantify the recorded biological information.
2. **Imaging**: Confocal microscopy or other imaging techniques are used to visualize the XRIs and the distribution of the epitope tags.
3. **Image Analysis**: Image analysis software is used to quantify the intensity profiles of the epitope tags and extract the temporal information.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Objective

To demonstrate the ability of XRIs to record and read out biological information over time in cultured mouse hippocampal neurons.

#### Method

1. **Cell Culture**: Primary cultures of mouse hippocampal neurons were prepared and maintained in a neuron incubator.
2. **AAV Transduction**: AAVs were used to deliver the XRI genes into the neurons. The AAVs were designed to express the filament-forming protein (1POK(E239Y)) and the label-bearing proteins (HA, FLAG, V5) in a controlled manner.
3. **Chemical Induction**: 4-OHT was used to induce the expression of the FLAG-bearing protein at specific time points.
4. **Fixation and Immunostaining**: The neurons were fixed and immunostained against the HA and FLAG tags.
5. **Imaging and Analysis**: Confocal microscopy was used to visualize the XRIs and the distribution of the epitope tags. Image analysis software was used to quantify the intensity profiles of the epitope tags and extract the temporal information.

#### Results

The XRIs were successfully formed in the neurons and showed a clear pattern of the FLAG tag along the growing protein chain. The temporal information of the 4-OHT induction was accurately recorded and read out using standard immunofluorescence and imaging techniques.
