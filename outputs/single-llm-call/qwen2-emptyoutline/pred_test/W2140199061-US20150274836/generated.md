# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and medicine, specifically to the development of novel mesothelin-specific monoclonal antibodies and their use in the diagnosis and treatment of cancer. More particularly, the invention provides fully human single-domain antibodies (VH) that recognize a unique epitope in the C-terminal region of mesothelin, which is closely associated with the cell surface. These antibodies exhibit strong complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) activities, making them valuable therapeutic agents for treating mesothelin-expressing cancers.

## BACKGROUND

Mesothelin is a cell surface glycoprotein that is highly expressed in various malignancies, including malignant mesotheliomas, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and breast cancer. The mesothelin (MSLN) gene encodes a precursor protein that is processed into an N-terminal protein and a C-terminal membrane-bound mature mesothelin. Several antimesothelin monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) have been developed, including SS1P and MORAb-009, which are currently in clinical trials. However, most of these antibodies recognize the N-terminal region of mesothelin, far from the cell surface, and primarily rely on antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) for their antitumor activity. Complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) has been suggested as an important additional mechanism for cancer therapeutic antibodies, but existing mesothelin mAbs have not shown significant CDC activity.

The present invention addresses this limitation by providing novel mesothelin-specific monoclonal antibodies that recognize a unique epitope in the C-terminal region of mesothelin, close to the cell surface. These antibodies exhibit strong CDC and ADCC activities, making them promising therapeutic candidates for mesothelin-expressing cancers.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides novel mesothelin-specific monoclonal antibodies and their use in the diagnosis and treatment of cancer. The invention also provides immunoconjugates and fusion proteins comprising the mesothelin-specific monoclonal antibodies, as well as compositions and methods for their use in diagnosing and treating cancer. The antibodies and their derivatives can be used in various therapeutic and diagnostic applications, including but not limited to, in vitro and in vivo assays, imaging, and targeted drug delivery.

### II. Terms and Methods

#### Mesothelin
Mesothelin is a cell surface glycoprotein encoded by the MSLN gene. It is highly expressed in various malignancies, including malignant mesotheliomas, cholangiocarcinoma, ovarian cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, and breast cancer. Mesothelin is processed into an N-terminal protein and a C-terminal membrane-bound mature mesothelin.

#### Monoclonal Antibodies
Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) are antibodies that are produced by identical immune cells, all derived from a single parent cell. They are highly specific for a particular epitope on an antigen and can be used in various therapeutic and diagnostic applications.

#### Single-Domain Antibodies (VH)
Single-domain antibodies (VH) are the smallest functional antigen-binding units derived from heavy chain-only antibodies found in camelids and sharks. They consist of a single variable domain and can bind antigens with high affinity and specificity.

#### Complement-Dependent Cytotoxicity (CDC)
Complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) is a mechanism by which antibodies activate the complement system to lyse target cells. This mechanism is important for the antitumor activity of therapeutic antibodies.

#### Antibody-Dependent Cell-Mediated Cytotoxicity (ADCC)
Antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) is a mechanism by which antibodies recruit immune cells, such as natural killer (NK) cells, to lyse target cells. This mechanism is important for the antitumor activity of therapeutic antibodies.

### IV. Mesothelin-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies

The present invention provides novel mesothelin-specific monoclonal antibodies that recognize a unique epitope in the C-terminal region of mesothelin, close to the cell surface. These antibodies are fully human single-domain antibodies (VH) and exhibit strong CDC and ADCC activities.

#### Discovery and Production of the SD1 Human Antibody
To find a new antimesothelin mAb targeting a site close to the cell surface, a C-terminal mesothelin peptide was designed and used for screening an engineered human antibody domain phage display library. After four rounds of panning, a phage clone named SD1 was selected for further analysis. SD1 binds both the C-terminal peptide and full-length mesothelin, indicating that it recognizes a unique epitope in the C-terminal region of mesothelin.

To investigate SD1 as a potential therapeutic, it was converted into a clinically relevant hFc fusion protein (SD1-hFc). A stable cell line was established with a high expression level (>70 mg/L) in the culture supernatant of HEK-293F cells, and the purity of SD1-hFc protein was more than 95%.

#### Full-Length Antibodies
Full-length antibodies are composed of two heavy chains and two light chains, forming a Y-shaped structure. The variable regions of the heavy and light chains form the antigen-binding sites, while the constant regions mediate effector functions such as CDC and ADCC.

#### Antibody Fragments
Antibody fragments, such as Fab, F(ab')2, and scFv, are smaller than full-length antibodies and can penetrate tissues more effectively. They retain the antigen-binding specificity of the parent antibody and can be used in various therapeutic and diagnostic applications.

### Example 2

#### Binding Properties of SD1-hFc to Mesothelin Protein in Cancer Cells

To analyze the binding properties of SD1 to mesothelin protein in cancer cells, Western blot analysis and pull-down assays were conducted using cancer cell lysates. Initial Western blot analysis of various cancer cell lysates using SD1-hFc could not detect a mesothelin band under reducing conditions even in high mesothelin-expressing cell lines such as A431/H9 and NCI-H226, indicating that SD1-hFc did not recognize denatured mesothelin protein. By conducting pull-down assays to detect endogenous mesothelin proteins in solution, SD1-hFc successfully pulled down mature mesothelin protein from three different cancer cell lines (A431/H9, NCI-H226, and KMBC). The molecular weight of mature mesothelin (~40 kDa) was consistent with previous studies.

In ELISA assays, SD1-hFc bound both full-length human mesothelin protein and peptide and did not bind full-length mouse mesothelin protein, bovine serum albumin, or other irrelevant proteins. Competition ELISAs showed that the C-terminal peptide blocked the binding of SD1-hFc to full-length human mesothelin, indicating that SD1-hFc recognizes the C-terminal end of mesothelin. SD1-hFc binds native mesothelin molecules on human tumor cells with high affinity and excellent specificity.

### Example 4

#### Antitumor Activity of SD1-hFc in Mice

To evaluate the antitumor activity of SD1-hFc in vivo, immunodeficient mice-bearing tumor xenografts were used. Athymic nude mice bearing A431/H9 tumors were treated with 50 mg/kg of SD1-hFc. The number of mesothelin sites in A431/H9 is comparable with that of malignant mesothelioma cells endogenously expressing mesothelin, and their implantation in mice consistently results in aggressive tumor growth. Twenty days after inoculation of tumor cells, the average tumor size in mice treated with SD1-hFc alone was significantly reduced (average 300 mm³) compared with the control group (average 1,000 mm³), showing that SD1-hFc is very active as a single agent.

To evaluate SD1-induced CDC in mice, CDC activity was examined using mouse sera, and SD1-hFc killed 11% of A431/H9 cells in the presence of 30% mouse serum freshly drawn from nude mice, indicating that mouse complement was 10-fold less active than human complement for SD1-hFc. SD1 was able to induce ADCC with purified mouse NK cells and killed mesothelin-positive cancer cells (H9) with different E:T ratios. The purity of mouse NK cells was around 50%. Taken together, SD1-hFc caused growth inhibition of tumor xenografts in nude mice. Both CDC and ADCC may contribute to the antitumor activity of SD1-hFc observed in vivo.
