# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel long-acting antimalarial prophylaxis method and composition. Specifically, the invention involves the use of a nanoformulated atovaquone preparation that can be administered intramuscularly to provide sustained protection against malaria for an extended period, thereby addressing the issue of non-compliance associated with traditional oral dosing regimens.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Malaria, caused primarily by the parasite *Plasmodium falciparum*, continues to be a significant global health burden, affecting millions of people annually and leading to numerous fatalities, particularly among children. Despite advancements in malaria control and the development of new antimalarial drugs, the incidence of malaria in travelers to endemic regions has been rising steadily. Current prophylactic strategies often rely on daily or weekly oral dosing regimens, which are frequently associated with non-compliance, thereby reducing their effectiveness.

Atovaquone, a broad-spectrum antiprotozoal agent, has been used for the prophylaxis and treatment of *Pneumocystis pneumonia* in immunosuppressed patients for over two decades. Its safety, low metabolic activity, and minimal drug interactions make it an attractive candidate for malaria prophylaxis. However, the requirement for frequent dosing limits its practicality and adherence.

Recent developments in nanotechnology have opened new avenues for creating long-acting formulations of drugs. Long-acting injectable (LAI) formulations, in particular, have shown promise in providing sustained drug release over extended periods, thereby improving patient compliance and therapeutic outcomes. The present invention leverages these advancements to create a long-acting nanoformulation of atovaquone that can be administered intramuscularly to provide reliable and sustained protection against malaria.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a long-acting antimalarial prophylaxis method and composition comprising a nanoformulated atovaquone preparation. The nanoformulated atovaquone is designed to be administered intramuscularly and provides sustained release of the active ingredient over an extended period, typically up to one month or more.

The nanoformulated atovaquone is prepared using a high-drug content solid drug nanoparticle (SDN) formulation. The SDN formulation is created using a combination of atovaquone and a binary mixture of polymer and surfactant excipients. The resulting nanoparticles are characterized by their small size, high drug loading, and stability, which enable sustained release of atovaquone from the injection site.

The invention further includes a method for preparing the nanoformulated atovaquone, which involves the following steps:
1. Emulsion-templated freeze drying (ETFD) to create a library of SDN formulations.
2. Screening the SDN formulations based on particle size, stability, and release kinetics.
3. Selecting the most promising SDN formulations for further evaluation and optimization.
4. Preparing the selected SDN formulations for intramuscular administration.

The nanoformulated atovaquone provides reliable and sustained protection against malaria, as demonstrated in preclinical studies using the *Plasmodium berghei* ANKA-C57BL/6 mouse model. The formulation is effective in preventing the development of parasitemia following sporozoite challenge, with a plasma atovaquone concentration threshold of approximately 200 ng/mL being associated with efficacy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Examples

#### Example 1: Preparation and Characterization of Nanoformulated Atovaquone

A library of solid drug nanoparticles (SDNs) containing atovaquone was prepared using the emulsion-templated freeze drying (ETFD) method. The SDN formulations were created using a combination of atovaquone and a binary mixture of polymer and surfactant excipients. Seven candidate polymers and six surfactants were chosen from the FDA CDER list of inactive ingredients. The resulting 42 formulations, all 80 wt% atovaquone, 13 wt% polymer, and 7 wt% surfactant, were assessed by dynamic light scattering to determine their particle size and stability.

Eleven candidate formulations that met the following criteria were selected for further evaluation:
1. Complete aqueous dispersion at 0.5 mg/mL.
2. SDN Z-average diameter <1000 nm.
3. Dynamic light-scattering analysis <5% standard deviation within the measured Z-average diameter.
4. Measured polydispersity index <0.4.

The 11 selected formulations were further characterized by in vitro rapid equilibrium dialysis to determine their drug release kinetics. Five formulations (ATQSDN2, 4, 6, 7, 8) providing a 50-fold range of drug-release rates were progressed to in vivo testing.

#### Example 2: In Vivo Efficacy of Nanoformulated Atovaquone

The efficacy of the selected nanoformulated atovaquone formulations was evaluated in the *Plasmodium berghei* ANKA-C57BL/6 mouse model. Mice were injected intramuscularly with the test formulations and challenged with sporozoites at various intervals after dosing. The mice were monitored for 42 days for the development of parasitemia.

Results showed that ATQSDN7 and ATQSDN8 provided complete protection against sporozoite challenge for up to 4 weeks after a single intramuscular dose. The protection was dose-dependent, with higher doses providing longer-lasting protection. Plasma atovaquone concentrations >200 ng/mL at the time of challenge were associated with efficacy.

#### Example 3: Causal vs. Suppressive Prophylaxis

To determine whether the observed prophylaxis was causal (targeting the liver stage), suppressive (acting on erythrocytic stages), or both, paired challenges with sporozoite or erythrocytic parasites were conducted at intervals after dosing. Mice injected with 36 mg/kg ATQSDN7 were challenged with 5000 *P. berghei* sporozoites or 150,000 infected erythrocytes. The results indicated that ATQSDN7 provided substantial causal activity, protecting against all sporozoite challenges but failing against all challenges by erythrocytic forms.

#### Example 4: Atovaquone Concentrations in Mouse Plasma

Plasma atovaquone concentrations were measured at various intervals after intramuscular injection of ATQSDN7. The results showed that atovaquone was readily detected in plasma by 6 hours after injection, and levels were dose-proportional. The plasma atovaquone concentrations >200 ng/mL at the time of challenge were associated with efficacy. The plasma half-life of atovaquone after intramuscular injection was significantly longer (105 h) compared to oral administration (9 h), indicating sustained release from the depot in muscle.

#### Example 5: Atovaquone Resistance Testing

To evaluate whether atovaquone resistance contributed to failure after sporozoite challenge, the atovaquone sensitivity of parasites that appeared after inadequate prophylaxis was tested. Mice treated with 36 mg/kg ATQSDN7 and challenged 2 weeks after injection developed parasitemia. Erythrocytic parasites from these mice were subinoculated into naive mice, which were then treated with oral atovaquone at the lowest curative dose. The results showed that the parasites remained atovaquone-sensitive, indicating therapeutic failure rather than drug resistance.

### Materials and Methods

#### Preparation of ETFD Monoliths

Materials were purchased and used as received without further purification. Atovaquone, polymers, and surfactants were dissolved in appropriate solvents to prepare stock solutions. The stock solutions were mixed in the proportion of 100 µL atovaquone, 63.7 µL polymer, 31.5 µL surfactant, and 304.8 µL water to provide a solid mass ratio of 80% atovaquone:13% polymer:7% surfactant in a 1:4 oil:water mix. The mixtures were emulsified using a Covaris S2x for 30 seconds, then immediately cryogenically frozen and lyophilized to produce dry porous monoliths.

#### Formation of SDNs

The lyophilized monoliths were dispersed in water and characterized by dynamic light scattering to determine their particle size and stability. The 11 initial hits that passed the selection criteria were further evaluated for drug release kinetics using a rapid equilibrium dialysis model of an intramuscular depot.

#### In Vivo Efficacy and Pharmacokinetics

All studies were conducted with *Plasmodium berghei* (ANKA 2.34 strain) in 6-week-old C57BL/6 male mice. Mice were injected intramuscularly with the test nanoformulations and challenged with sporozoites or erythrocytic parasites at various intervals after dosing. Blood samples were collected at regular intervals to monitor parasitemia and measure plasma atovaquone concentrations using UPLC-MS/MS.

#### Generation of Atovaquone-Resistant Erythrocytic *P. berghei*

C57BL/6 male mice were inoculated with infected erythrocytes and treated with oral doses of atovaquone to generate atovaquone-resistant parasites. The resistant parasites were subinoculated into naive mice and treated with oral atovaquone to confirm their resistance.

#### Atovaquone Sensitivity of Parasites from Animals That Failed Prophylaxis

Mice dosed with 36 mg/kg ATQSDN7 and challenged with sporozoites were monitored for parasitemia. Erythrocytic parasites from parasitemic animals were subinoculated into naive mice, which were then treated with oral atovaquone to test their sensitivity.

#### Statistical Analyses

Descriptive values in the text are mean ± standard deviation. Plasma half-life was estimated by linear regression of log concentration on time in a sparse sampling non-compartmental analysis using Phoenix WinNonlin 8.0. Fisher’s exact test was used to compare the pooled proportions of untreated mice and mice treated with successful regimens.

## Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel long-acting antimalarial prophylaxis method and composition comprising a nanoformulated atovaquone preparation. The nanoformulated atovaquone is designed to be administered intramuscularly and provides sustained release of the active ingredient over an extended period, typically up to one month or more. The invention addresses the issue of non-compliance associated with traditional oral dosing regimens and offers a promising new tool for malaria prevention.
