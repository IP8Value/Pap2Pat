# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method and system for improving the accuracy and reliability of radial artery blood pressure measurements and derived indexes in critically ill patients, particularly during the early postoperative period following cardiac surgery. The invention addresses the issue of signal distortion caused by the dynamic characteristics of fluid-filled tubing, which can lead to inaccurate readings of systolic pressure (sAP), pulse pressure variation (PPV), and left ventricular contractility (dP/dt).

## SUMMARY

The invention provides a method and system for correcting the distortions in radial artery blood pressure signals caused by the dynamic characteristics of fluid-filled tubing. The method involves continuous monitoring of arterial pressure using a double-head pressure transducer and the identification of episodes of overdamping (Ov) and attenuation (At). By analyzing the pressure signal during stable periods, the method corrects the distorted raw signals to provide accurate measurements of sAP, PPV, and dP/dt. The system includes a data processing unit that applies the correction algorithms to the recorded pressure signals, thereby ensuring reliable and accurate monitoring of hemodynamic parameters in critically ill patients.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention is directed to a method and system for enhancing the accuracy and reliability of radial artery blood pressure measurements and derived indexes in critically ill patients, particularly those recovering from cardiac surgery. The invention addresses the common issue of signal distortion caused by the dynamic characteristics of fluid-filled tubing, which can significantly affect the accuracy of hemodynamic monitoring.

### Background and Problem Statement

Invasive monitoring in critically ill patients is essential for the continuous measurement of arterial pressure, cardiac output, and the derivation of dynamic predictors of fluid responsiveness. However, the pressure signal can be altered by the dynamic characteristics of the fluid-filled tubing used in the monitoring system. These alterations can lead to inaccuracies in the measurement of systolic pressure (sAP), pulse pressure variation (PPV), and left ventricular contractility (dP/dt), which are critical parameters for guiding clinical decisions and patient management.

### Method Overview

The method of the present invention involves the following steps:

1. **Continuous Monitoring**: An independent continuous recording of arterial pressure is initiated using a double-head pressure transducer. The transducer is connected to a radial artery catheter placed in the patient's radial artery. The recording is maintained for at least 18 hours to capture a comprehensive dataset.

2. **Identification of Artifacts**: The entire recorded dataset is examined for episodes of overdamping (Ov) and attenuation (At). Overdamping is characterized by a decrease in systolic pressure (sAP), an increase in diastolic pressure (dAP), and an unchanged mean pressure (mAP). Attenuation is defined by a decrease in sAP, dAP, and mAP.

3. **Stable Period Analysis**: Three periods of 10 minutes during the first hour of monitoring are selected for detailed analysis. These periods are chosen under the assumption that the dynamic characteristics of the fluid-filled tubing remain constant. This allows for the correction of the distorted raw signals.

4. **Signal Correction**: The distorted raw signals are corrected using a data processing algorithm. The algorithm accounts for the identified episodes of Ov and At and adjusts the pressure waveforms to provide accurate measurements of sAP, PPV, and dP/dt.

5. **Statistical Analysis**: The corrected and raw signals are compared using a paired t-test to determine the significance of the corrections. The results are used to validate the effectiveness of the correction method.

### System Components

The system of the present invention comprises the following components:

1. **Radial Artery Catheter**: A catheter is inserted into the patient's radial artery to measure arterial pressure continuously.

2. **Double-Head Pressure Transducer**: A double-head pressure transducer is connected to the radial artery catheter to record the pressure signals. The transducer is capable of providing independent and simultaneous recordings of the pressure waveform.

3. **Data Acquisition Unit**: A data acquisition unit collects the pressure signals from the transducer and stores them for further analysis. The unit is designed to maintain a high sampling rate to ensure the accuracy of the recorded data.

4. **Data Processing Unit**: The data processing unit applies the correction algorithms to the recorded pressure signals. The unit is equipped with advanced signal processing capabilities to identify and correct episodes of Ov and At. The corrected signals are then used to derive accurate measurements of sAP, PPV, and dP/dt.

5. **User Interface**: A user interface is provided to display the corrected pressure waveforms and derived indexes. The interface also allows healthcare providers to review the raw and corrected signals, facilitating real-time monitoring and decision-making.

### Detailed Method Steps

#### Continuous Monitoring

The method begins with the insertion of a radial artery catheter into the patient's radial artery. The catheter is connected to a double-head pressure transducer, which is calibrated to ensure accurate measurements. The transducer records the arterial pressure continuously for at least 18 hours. The recorded data is stored in a digital format for subsequent analysis.

#### Identification of Artifacts

The entire recorded dataset is analyzed to identify episodes of overdamping (Ov) and attenuation (At). The analysis is performed using a set of predefined criteria:
- **Overdamping (Ov)**: A decrease in systolic pressure (sAP), an increase in diastolic pressure (dAP), and an unchanged mean pressure (mAP).
- **Attenuation (At)**: A decrease in systolic pressure (sAP), diastolic pressure (dAP), and mean pressure (mAP).

The episodes of Ov and At are marked in the dataset for further processing.

#### Stable Period Analysis

Three periods of 10 minutes each are selected from the first hour of monitoring. These periods are chosen based on the assumption that the dynamic characteristics of the fluid-filled tubing remain constant. The selection of these periods ensures that the correction algorithms can be applied accurately.

#### Signal Correction

The distorted raw signals are corrected using a data processing algorithm. The algorithm takes into account the identified episodes of Ov and At and adjusts the pressure waveforms accordingly. The correction process involves the following steps:
- **Baseline Adjustment**: The baseline of the pressure waveform is adjusted to account for any shifts in the mean pressure.
- **Amplitude Correction**: The amplitude of the pressure waveform is corrected to eliminate the effects of overdamping and attenuation.
- **Waveform Reconstruction**: The corrected waveform is reconstructed to provide accurate measurements of sAP, PPV, and dP/dt.

#### Statistical Analysis

The corrected and raw signals are compared using a paired t-test to determine the significance of the corrections. The statistical analysis provides quantitative evidence of the improvement in the accuracy of the pressure measurements. The results are used to validate the effectiveness of the correction method.

Accurate and reliable monitoring of hemodynamic parameters is crucial for the management of critically ill patients, especially those recovering from cardiac surgery. The invention helps to reduce the risk of misdiagnosis and inappropriate treatment due to signal distortions, thereby improving patient outcomes.
