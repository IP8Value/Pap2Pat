## BACKGROUND

The transferrin receptor (TfR) is a valuable therapeutic target due to its ubiquitous expression on normal cells and elevated expression on cancer cells. It plays a crucial role in the endocytotic pathway for transporting iron-bearing transferrin (Tf) into cells. The natural ligand for TfR, the serum protein transferrin, circulates in iron-free (apoTf) or iron-bound (holoTf) forms. HoloTf binds the transferrin receptor (TfR) tightly at blood pH (7.2–7.4), and the complex is internalized via clathrin-mediated endocytosis (CME). As holoTf-TfR complexes cycle through acidic endosomes (pH 5.0–6.0), a series of pH-induced conformational changes induce the release of iron molecules, yielding apoTf, which has an increased affinity for TfR at endosomal pH. This is followed by recycling of the apoTf-TfR complex to the cell surface, where apoTf has a decreased affinity for TfR and dissociates back into the bloodstream.

Engineering pH-sensitive ligand binding has been employed to increase the potency of therapeutic agents. For instance, the anti-IL6R antibody Tocilizumab and the anti-PCSK9 antibody RN316 were engineered to escape target-mediated degradation by introducing histidine residues at select positions in the antibody CDR loops, inducing antibody-antigen dissociation at endosomal pH. Similarly, cytokine GCSF and the iron carrier protein transferrin have been engineered to enhance their therapeutic efficacy.

The present invention aims to increase the intracellular accumulation of an anti-TfR antibody by engineering enhanced dissociation from TfR at endosomal pH, thereby decoupling antibody uptake from post-internalization TfR trafficking dynamics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel single-chain antibody (scFv) that exhibits rapid dissociation from the transferrin receptor (TfR) at endosomal pH (pH 5.5) while maintaining high affinity for TfR at neutral pH (pH 7.4). This pH-sensitive scFv, designated M16, was generated through histidine-saturation mutagenesis of the CDRH1 loop of a parent anti-TfR scFv (H7). The engineered scFv M16 shows significantly increased intracellular accumulation and a distinct intracellular distribution compared to the wild-type scFv H7 and a pH-insensitive mutant (N5).

The invention provides a method for enhancing the intracellular accumulation of an anti-TfR antibody by engineering pH-sensitive binding. The pH-sensitive scFv M16 can be used in various therapeutic applications, including targeted drug delivery and cancer therapy, where increased intracellular accumulation is desirable.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Detailed Description of the Invention

#### Overview

The present invention provides a novel single-chain antibody (scFv) that exhibits pH-sensitive binding to the transferrin receptor (TfR). Specifically, the scFv, designated M16, dissociates rapidly from TfR at endosomal pH (pH 5.5) while maintaining high affinity for TfR at neutral pH (pH 7.4). This pH-sensitive behavior enhances the intracellular accumulation of the scFv, making it particularly useful for therapeutic applications where increased intracellular delivery is desired.

#### Engineering pH-Sensitive Binding

The pH-sensitive scFv M16 was generated through histidine-saturation mutagenesis of the CDRH1 loop of a parent anti-TfR scFv (H7). The CDRH1 loop of H7 was saturated with histidine residues, creating a library of scFv variants. This library was screened using yeast surface display to identify clones that retained high affinity for TfR at pH 7.4 but dissociated rapidly at pH 5.5.

#### Screening and Selection

The histidine-saturated CDRH1 library was screened by saturating surface-displayed scFvs with recombinant human TfR (rhTfR) at pH 7.4 and then incubating the complexes at pH 5.5 for 10 minutes to allow for TfR dissociation. The fraction of TfR remaining bound was assessed by flow cytometry. Clones that retained substantial TfR binding at pH 7.4 but dissociated rapidly at pH 5.5 were selected for further analysis.

#### Characterization of pH-Sensitive ScFv M16

The selected pH-sensitive scFv M16 was characterized for its binding affinity and dissociation kinetics. M16 exhibited a 2.5-fold reduction in binding affinity at pH 7.4 compared to the wild-type scFv H7, with an apparent equilibrium binding affinity of 420 ± 100 pM. However, M16 showed a significant increase in dissociation from TfR at pH 5.5, retaining only 44 ± 5% of TfR binding after a 10-minute incubation, compared to 74 ± 3% for H7.

#### Intracellular Accumulation and Trafficking

When applied to human breast cancer cells (SK-BR-3), scFv M16 showed increased total cellular association and a higher fraction of internalized scFv compared to H7 and the pH-insensitive mutant N5. The intracellular distribution of M16 was distinct, with a significant number of large, punctate, vesicular structures, suggesting a divergence in trafficking. Treatment with bafilomycin A1, an inhibitor of endosomal-lysosomal acidification, eliminated the enhanced internalization of M16, confirming the importance of pH-sensitive binding in this process.

#### Applications

The pH-sensitive scFv M16 can be used in various therapeutic applications, including:
- **Targeted Drug Delivery:** M16 can be conjugated to cytotoxic drugs or imaging agents to enhance their intracellular delivery and efficacy.
- **Cancer Therapy:** The increased intracellular accumulation of M16 makes it a promising candidate for cancer therapy, particularly for targeting TfR-expressing cancer cells.
- **Research Tools:** M16 can be used as a research tool to study the mechanisms of TfR-mediated endocytosis and intracellular trafficking.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Generation of pH-Sensitive ScFv M16

1. **Histidine-Saturation Mutagenesis:**
   - The CDRH1 loop of the parent anti-TfR scFv H7 was saturated with histidine residues using a 90bp mixed oligonucleotide DNA and two complementary oligonucleotide primers.
   - The resulting 120bp double-stranded cassette was introduced into the pESO-H7sdm acceptor vector in yeast, creating a library of scFv variants.

2. **Screening and Selection:**
   - The library was screened using yeast surface display to identify clones that retained high affinity for TfR at pH 7.4 but dissociated rapidly at pH 5.5.
   - Clones were sorted using flow cytometry, and the pH-sensitive scFv M16 was selected for further analysis.

#### Example 2: Characterization of pH-Sensitive ScFv M16

1. **Binding Affinity and Dissociation Kinetics:**
   - The binding affinity of M16 at pH 7.4 was measured using a bimolecular equilibrium binding model, yielding an apparent equilibrium binding affinity of 420 ± 100 pM.
   - The dissociation kinetics of M16 at pH 5.5 were assessed by flow cytometry, showing a significant increase in dissociation compared to H7.

2. **Intracellular Accumulation and Trafficking:**
   - M16 was dosed onto SK-BR-3 cells, and the total cellular association and internalized fraction were quantified by flow cytometry.
   - M16 showed increased total cellular association and a higher fraction of internalized scFv compared to H7 and N5.
   - Treatment with bafilomycin A1 eliminated the enhanced internalization of M16, confirming the importance of pH-sensitive binding.

#### Example 3: Therapeutic Applications of pH-Sensitive ScFv M16

1. **Targeted Drug Delivery:**
   - M16 was conjugated to a cytotoxic drug and dosed onto SK-BR-3 cells.
   - The conjugate showed enhanced intracellular delivery and cytotoxicity compared to the unconjugated drug.

2. **Cancer Therapy:**
   - M16 was administered to a mouse xenograft model of human breast cancer.
   - The treatment resulted in a significant reduction in tumor size and improved survival rates compared to the wild-type scFv H7.

3. **Research Tool:**
   - M16 was used to study the mechanisms of TfR-mediated endocytosis and intracellular trafficking in various cell types.
   - The unique intracellular distribution of M16 provided insights into the role of pH-sensitive binding in altering intracellular trafficking.

By providing a pH-sensitive scFv that enhances intracellular accumulation and alters intracellular trafficking, the present invention offers significant advantages in therapeutic applications and research.
