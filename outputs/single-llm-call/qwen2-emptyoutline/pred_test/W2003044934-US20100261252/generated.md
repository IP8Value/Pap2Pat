# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology and medicine, specifically to the production and use of immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1) proteases. More particularly, the invention concerns methods for expressing and purifying the protease domain of IgA1 proteases from bacteria, such as *Haemophilus influenzae*, in *Escherichia coli* (E. coli) and its use in treating IgA1 deposition disorders, including IgA nephropathy (IgAN).

## BACKGROUND

Immunoglobulin A1 (IgA1) proteases are a group of bacterial enzymes that specifically cleave the hinge region of human IgA1 and some great apes. These proteases are not known to cleave human IgA2, IgG, IgM, or IgA from other species such as mice, rats, or monkeys. The C. ramosum IgA protease, however, can cleave human IgA2m(1) allotype. The structure and function of IgA1 proteases vary among different bacterial species, with serine, metallo, and cysteine types identified. Serine-type IgA1 proteases are typically expressed as a precursor protein comprising a signal peptide, an IgA1 protease domain, and a C-terminal α protein and β-core domain. The β-core domain facilitates secretion of the α protein-protease domain polypeptide, which is cleaved to form the mature protease. Metallo-type IgA1 proteases contain a signal sequence and propeptide that aid in anchoring the peptide to the cell membrane and include sites for metal ion binding in the protease domain. The structure of cysteine-type IgA1 proteases remains undetermined.

IgA1 proteases have significant therapeutic potential, particularly in the treatment of IgA nephropathy (IgAN), a disease characterized by the deposition of IgA1 antibodies in the glomerulus, leading to kidney dysfunction and, in severe cases, kidney failure. Exogenous proteolytic enzymes, such as chymopapain and subtilisin, have been tested to remove IgA1 deposits in animal models, but these enzymes lack specificity and can degrade a variety of other proteins. In contrast, the IgA1-specific protease from *H. influenzae* has shown promise in reducing the deposition of glomerular IgA1 immune complexes in animal models. However, the production of IgA1 proteases in sufficient quantities for therapeutic use has been challenging due to the low natural production in bacteria such as *H. influenzae*, *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*, and *Neisseria meningitidis*. Additionally, these bacteria are pathogenic, making large-scale production impractical.

Previous attempts to express recombinant IgA1 proteases in *E. coli* have resulted in low yields and poor solubility. The present invention addresses these issues by providing a method for expressing and purifying the protease domain of IgA1 proteases from *H. influenzae* in *E. coli* with significantly increased yield and solubility.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for expressing and purifying the protease domain of IgA1 proteases from *Haemophilus influenzae* in *Escherichia coli* (E. coli). The method involves cloning the gene encoding the protease domain of the IgA1 protease into an expression vector, expressing the protease domain in *E. coli* under optimized conditions, and purifying the expressed protease domain. The invention also includes pharmaceutical compositions comprising the purified IgA1 protease and methods of using the protease to treat IgA1 deposition disorders, such as IgA nephropathy.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for expressing a soluble and active IgA1 protease domain in *E. coli*. The method includes the steps of:
1. Cloning the gene encoding the protease domain of the IgA1 protease into an expression vector.
2. Transforming *E. coli* cells with the expression vector.
3. Culturing the transformed *E. coli* cells under conditions that promote the expression of the protease domain.
4. Harvesting the expressed protease domain from the *E. coli* cells.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for purifying the expressed IgA1 protease domain. The method includes the steps of:
1. Lysing the *E. coli* cells to release the expressed protease domain.
2. Purifying the protease domain using affinity chromatography, anion-exchange chromatography, and size-exclusion chromatography.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising the purified IgA1 protease and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition is suitable for administration to a subject to treat IgA1 deposition disorders, such as IgA nephropathy.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method of treating IgA1 deposition disorders, such as IgA nephropathy, in a subject. The method includes administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of the purified IgA1 protease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **IgA1 Protease**: An enzyme that specifically cleaves the hinge region of human IgA1. It can be of serine, metallo, or cysteine type, depending on the bacterial source.
- **Protease Domain**: The functional part of the IgA1 protease that is responsible for the cleavage of IgA1. In the context of this invention, it refers to the protease domain of the IgA1 protease from *H. influenzae*.

- **Recombinant Protein**: A protein produced by recombinant DNA technology, where the gene encoding the protein is introduced into a host cell and expressed.
- **Soluble Protein**: A protein that remains in solution and does not form aggregates or precipitates.
- **Active Protein**: A protein that retains its biological activity and functions as intended.
- **Pharmaceutical Composition**: A preparation containing one or more active ingredients and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier, suitable for administration to a subject.
- **Therapeutically Effective Amount**: The amount of an active ingredient required to achieve a desired therapeutic effect in a subject.

### Derivatives of IgA Protease Polypeptides

Derivatives of IgA1 protease polypeptides can be created through various modifications, including truncations, substitutions, and fusions. These derivatives retain the ability to cleave the hinge region of human IgA1 and can be used in therapeutic applications. For example, the protease domain of the IgA1 protease from *H. influenzae* can be expressed without the α protein and β-core domains, resulting in a smaller, more manageable protein that is easier to produce and purify.

### IgA Deposition Disorders

IgA1 deposition disorders, such as IgA nephropathy (IgAN), are characterized by the accumulation of IgA1 antibodies in tissues, leading to inflammation and tissue damage. IgAN is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide and can lead to kidney dysfunction and, in severe cases, kidney failure. The exact mechanisms underlying IgA1 deposition are not fully understood, but it is believed that abnormal glycosylation of IgA1 and the formation of immune complexes play a role. ### Representative Embodiments of the Disclosure

#### Cloning of IgA1 Protease into Expression Vectors

The gene encoding the protease domain of the IgA1 protease from *H. influenzae* was amplified from the pFG26 plasmid using PCR. The amplified PCR fragment was digested with Nde I and BamHI and cloned into the pET21a and pColdIV expression vectors. The resulting constructs, pET-S-IGAN and pCold-S-IGAN, encode the protease domain with a signal peptide, while pET-IGAN and pCold-IGAN encode the protease domain without the signal peptide.

#### Expression of IgA1 Protease in E. coli

The expression vectors were transformed into various *E. coli* strains, including BL21(DE3), BL21(DE3)pLysS, ArcticExpress(DE3), BL21(DE3)pGro7, C41(DE3), C43(DE3), C41(DE3)pLysS, C43(DE3)pLysS, Origami B(DE3), Origami B(DE3)pLysS, Tuner(DE3), and KRX. The transformed cells were cultured in LB medium containing carbenicillin, and expression was induced with IPTG at various concentrations and temperatures. The total cell lysates were assayed for protein concentration and analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blotting to determine the expression levels of the IgA1 protease.

#### Purification of IgA1 Protease from E. coli C41 (DE3)

The cell pellets from the best-performing *E. coli* strain, C41(DE3), were lysed, and the soluble fraction was purified using a nickel column, an anion-exchange column, and a size-exclusion column. The final yield of purified IgA1 protease was 20-40 mg/L, with a purity of more than 95%.

#### Characterization of IgA1 Protease

The purified IgA1 protease was characterized using an Experion automated electrophoresis system to measure its proteolytic activity. The average activity of the purified IgA1 protease from *E. coli* C41 (DE3) was approximately 46 ng/μL/min per ng of IgA1 protease, which is significantly higher than the activity of the IgA1 protease from *H. influenzae* (approximately 27 ng/μL/min per ng of IgA1 protease).

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Cloning of IgA1 Protease into Expression Vectors**

The gene encoding the protease domain of the IgA1 protease from *H. influenzae* was amplified from the pFG26 plasmid using PCR. The forward primer included an Nde I restriction site, and the reverse primer included a BamHI restriction site. The PCR product was digested with Nde I and BamHI and cloned into the pET21a and pColdIV expression vectors. The resulting constructs, pET-S-IGAN and pCold-S-IGAN, encode the protease domain with a signal peptide, while pET-IGAN and pCold-IGAN encode the protease domain without the signal peptide.

### Example 2

**Expression of IgA1 Protease in E. coli**

The expression vectors were transformed into various *E. coli* strains, including BL21(DE3), BL21(DE3)pLysS, ArcticExpress(DE3), BL21(DE3)pGro7, C41(DE3), C43(DE3), C41(DE3)pLysS, C43(DE3)pLysS, Origami B(DE3), Origami B(DE3)pLysS, Tuner(DE3), and KRX. The transformed cells were cultured in LB medium containing carbenicillin, and expression was induced with IPTG at various concentrations and temperatures. The total cell lysates were assayed for protein concentration and analyzed by SDS-PAGE and Western blotting to determine the expression levels of the IgA1 protease. The pET-IGAN construct resulted in the highest levels of soluble IgA1 protease in several cell strains, with C41 (DE3) producing the highest titer of protease under the optimal conditions of 0.4 mM IPTG induction at 20°C for 24 hours.

### Example 3

**Purification of IgA1 Protease from E. coli C41 (DE3)**

The cell pellets from the best-performing *E. coli* strain, C41(DE3), were lysed using B-PER II Bacterial Extraction Reagent and Benzonase Nuclease. The soluble fraction was purified using a nickel column, an anion-exchange column, and a size-exclusion column. The final yield of purified IgA1 protease was 20-40 mg/L, with a purity of more than 95%. The purified IgA1 protease was confirmed to have the correct molecular weight (∼100 kDa) and proteolytic activity.

### Example 4

**Characterization of IgA1 Protease**

The purified IgA1 protease was characterized using an Experion automated electrophoresis system to measure its proteolytic activity. The average activity of the purified IgA1 protease from *E. coli* C41 (DE3) was approximately 46 ng/μL/min per ng of IgA1 protease, which is significantly higher than the activity of the IgA1 protease from *H. influenzae* (approximately 27 ng/μL/min per ng of IgA1 protease). The higher activity of the *E. coli*-produced IgA1 protease is likely due to the absence of impurities and degradation products found in the *H. influenzae* sample.
