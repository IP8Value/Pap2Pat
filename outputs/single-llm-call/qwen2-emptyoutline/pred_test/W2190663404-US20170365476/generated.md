# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Hyperdoped black silicon represents a significant advancement in the field of semiconductor materials, particularly for applications in optoelectronics. This material is characterized by its unique combination of high dopant concentrations and a light-trapping surface morphology, which together enable it to absorb a broad range of photon energies, including those below the bandgap of silicon. The ability to absorb sub-bandgap photons is particularly valuable for applications such as infrared photodetectors and intermediate band photovoltaics, where traditional silicon-based devices are limited by their bandgap energy.

The fabrication of hyperdoped black silicon involves several methods, each with its own advantages and limitations. One common method is the use of femtosecond (fs) laser pulses in the presence of dopants, which can be in the form of a gas or a thin film. This process involves the melting of silicon, diffusion of dopants into the melt, and rapid resolidification, leading to the formation of a hyperdoped layer with a light-trapping surface. However, the resulting material often contains amorphous and pressure-induced crystalline phases, which can degrade its performance. Additionally, thermal annealing, while improving crystallinity, can deactivate the sub-bandgap optical absorptance, presenting a significant challenge in device design.

This invention addresses these challenges by introducing a novel method of nanosecond (ns) laser annealing to hyperdoped black silicon. This method not only improves the crystallinity and removes unwanted phases but also maintains the high sub-bandgap optical absorptance and light-trapping surface morphology. Furthermore, ns laser annealing can reactivate the sub-bandgap optical absorptance after it has been deactivated by thermal annealing, providing a flexible and robust approach to fabricating high-performance optoelectronic devices.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method for enhancing the crystallinity and optical absorptance of hyperdoped black silicon while maintaining its light-trapping surface morphology. The method involves the use of nanosecond (ns) laser annealing to treat hyperdoped black silicon, which has been fabricated using femtosecond (fs) laser pulses in the presence of dopants. The ns laser annealing process smooths the surface on the nanometer scale, converts amorphous and pressure-induced silicon phases to conventional crystalline silicon, and places or maintains dopant atoms in optically active, non-equilibrium positions. This results in a material that is highly crystalline, highly absorptive to both above-bandgap and sub-bandgap wavelengths, and electrically rectifying.

Key aspects of the invention include:
1. **Nanosecond Laser Annealing**: The use of ns laser pulses to anneal hyperdoped black silicon, which improves crystallinity and maintains high sub-bandgap optical absorptance.
2. **Surface Morphology Preservation**: The ns laser annealing process preserves the light-trapping surface morphology, ensuring high optical absorptance.
3. **Reactivation of Sub-Bandgap Absorptance**: The method can reactivate sub-bandgap optical absorptance after it has been deactivated by thermal annealing.
4. **Combination with Thermal Annealing**: The invention demonstrates the design flexibility provided by combining thermal and ns laser annealing, allowing for the production of highly crystalline and optically absorptive hyperdoped black silicon.

The invention is particularly useful for the fabrication of optoelectronic devices, such as infrared photodetectors and intermediate band photovoltaics, where high crystallinity and sub-bandgap optical absorptance are critical.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Introduction

Hyperdoped black silicon is a silicon material that is both supersaturated with low-solubility dopants and possesses a light-trapping surface. This combination makes it a strong absorber of photon energies both above and below the bandgap of silicon, including in the visible and infrared regions of the electromagnetic spectrum. The strong broadband absorptance in hyperdoped black silicon has led to its exploration for use in silicon-based infrared photodetectors and intermediate band photovoltaics.

### Fabrication Methods

Hyperdoped black silicon can be fabricated using various methods, including:
- **Femtosecond Laser Irradiation**: This method involves irradiating silicon with fs laser pulses in the presence of dopants, typically in the form of a gas or a thin film. The process involves silicon melting, dopant diffusion into the melt, and rapid resolidification, leading to the formation of a hyperdoped layer with a light-trapping surface.
- **Pulsed Laser Melting of Ion Implanted Silicon**: This method involves ion implantation followed by pulsed laser melting to achieve hyperdoping.
- **Chemical Etching**: This method can produce black silicon but does not introduce hyperdoping.
- **Direct Laser Texturing**: This method can produce black silicon but does not introduce hyperdoping.

### Challenges in Fabrication

One of the main challenges in fabricating hyperdoped black silicon is achieving high crystallinity while maintaining high sub-bandgap optical absorptance. Thermal annealing, which is commonly used to improve crystallinity, often deactivates the sub-bandgap optical absorptance. Additionally, the presence of amorphous and pressure-induced crystalline phases can degrade the material's performance.

### Nanosecond Laser Annealing

The present invention addresses these challenges by introducing a method of nanosecond (ns) laser annealing to hyperdoped black silicon. The ns laser annealing process involves the following steps:
1. **Preparation of Hyperdoped Black Silicon**: Hyperdoped black silicon is fabricated using fs laser pulses in the presence of dopants. The resulting material has a rough surface morphology consisting of nm-scale granular structures overlying µm-scale conical structures.
2. **Nanosecond Laser Annealing**: The hyperdoped black silicon is irradiated with ns laser pulses. The ns laser pulses smooth the surface on the nm scale, convert amorphous and pressure-induced silicon phases to conventional crystalline silicon, and place or maintain dopant atoms in optically active, non-equilibrium positions.
3. **Preservation of Surface Morphology**: The ns laser annealing process preserves the light-trapping surface morphology, ensuring high optical absorptance.
4. **Reactivation of Sub-Bandgap Absorptance**: If the sub-bandgap optical absorptance has been deactivated by thermal annealing, ns laser annealing can reactivate it while maintaining high crystallinity.

### Experimental Details

#### Fabrication of Hyperdoped Black Silicon
- **Materials**: Monocrystalline silicon wafers (p-type, boron-doped, 7-14 ohm-cm) were used.
- **Process**: The silicon wafers were placed in a vacuum chamber, evacuated to < 0.1 Torr, and then filled with sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) gas to a pressure of 500 Torr. Femtosecond laser pulses (800 nm, 100-fs pulse duration, 1 kHz repetition rate) were focused onto the silicon surface with a pulse diameter of 0.5-1 mm. Each pulse had a fluence of 8 kJ/m², and the pulses were scanned across the silicon surface so that each site was exposed to 200 pulses on average.

#### Nanosecond Laser Annealing
- **Laser Source**: Nanosecond laser pulses were generated by ArF (193 nm), XeCl (308 nm, 25 ns FWHM pulse duration), and Nd:YAG (355 nm, 4 ns FWHM pulse duration) excimer lasers.
- **Process**: The ns laser pulses were focused to achieve fluences of 0.6-2.2 J/cm² and scanned across the hyperdoped black silicon surface in ambient air. Each site on the surface was typically exposed to a single ns laser pulse, though exposures of up to 40 pulses were also carried out to determine the effects of multiple exposures.

### Characterization Techniques

#### Crystallinity
- **Raman Spectroscopy**: Raman spectra were measured using a 10 mW HeNe laser beam (632.8 nm) and a CCD detector. The relative crystallinity was determined by analyzing the areas under the spectral curves corresponding to amorphous and crystalline silicon phases.

#### Surface Morphology
- **Field Emission Scanning Electron Microscopy (FESEM)**: FESEM was used to image the surface morphology of the hyperdoped black silicon at an angle of 45 degrees.

#### Microstructure
- **Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)**: Cross-sectional bright field TEM and selected area diffraction (SAD) were used to investigate the microstructure of the hyperdoped black silicon.

#### Optical Absorptance
- **Spectrophotometry**: The optical absorptance of the hyperdoped black silicon was measured using an ultraviolet-visible-near infrared (UV-VIS-NIR) spectrophotometer and a Fourier transformed infrared (FTIR) spectrometer.

#### Electrical Properties
- **Current-Bias Behavior**: The current-bias behavior of the hyperdoped black silicon was measured using a probe station and parametric analyzer. Metal electrodes were deposited on the samples using electron beam evaporation and thermal evaporation.

### Results

#### Surface Morphology
- **Before Annealing**: The hyperdoped black silicon fabricated with fs laser pulses had a rough surface morphology consisting of nm-scale granular structures overlying µm-scale conical structures.
- **After Annealing**: Nanosecond laser annealing smoothed the surface on the nm scale while preserving the µm-scale conical structures. The surface became single-crystalline, and the amorphous and pressure-induced crystalline phases were removed.

#### Crystallinity
- **Raman Spectra**: Raman spectra showed a significant reduction in the amorphous silicon phase and the removal of pressure-induced crystalline phases after ns laser annealing. The width of the crystalline silicon peak at 520 cm⁻¹ was reduced, indicating improved crystallinity.

#### Optical Absorptance
- **Above-Bandgap and Sub-Bandgap**: The above-bandgap and sub-bandgap optical absorptance of the hyperdoped black silicon remained high after ns laser annealing. The sub-bandgap absorptance, which was initially high, was reactivated after thermal annealing and subsequent ns laser annealing.

#### Electrical Properties
- **Rectification**: The hyperdoped black silicon diodes exhibited rectification behavior, with rectification ratios of 50±20 at ±10 V. Rapid thermal annealing after electrode deposition shifted the diode turn-on voltage from 0.2±0.1 V to 0.5-1.5 V, indicating the formation of a rectifying homojunction.

### Conclusion

Nanosecond laser annealing is a powerful technique for enhancing the crystallinity and optical absorptance of hyperdoped black silicon while maintaining its light-trapping surface morphology. This method can reactivate sub-bandgap optical absorptance after it has been deactivated by thermal annealing, providing a flexible and robust approach to fabricating high-performance optoelectronic devices. The combination of thermal and ns laser annealing offers design flexibility, allowing for the production of hyperdoped black silicon that is highly crystalline, highly absorptive to both above-bandgap and sub-bandgap wavelengths, and electrically rectifying. This invention has significant implications for the development of advanced optoelectronic devices, such as infrared photodetectors and intermediate band photovoltaics.
