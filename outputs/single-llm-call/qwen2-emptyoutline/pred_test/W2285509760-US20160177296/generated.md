# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Avian influenza virus (AIV) outbreaks in poultry have led to the global culling of millions of birds and the net loss of billions of dollars. These outbreaks are of significant concern, not only because of the high degree of virulence observed in poultry, resulting in severe economic consequences, but also due to the potential transmission to mammalian species, including humans. Current vaccination strategies for birds are limited. The most efficacious vaccines must be administered subcutaneously or intramuscularly, which is an impediment to successfully immunizing large numbers of poultry in a short period. Protective antibody production in naïve birds takes two to three weeks to achieve following vaccination. Improper storage and handling often lead to vaccine failure. To elicit efficient protection, the vaccine must be hemagglutinin (HA)-subtype specific to the outbreak virus. Over time, stockpiles of vaccines become obsolete, necessitating the generation of new vaccines. These limitations highlight the need for a prophylactic that offers universal protection against any subtype or strain of AIV and provides rapid protection in the face of an outbreak.

RNA interference (RNAi) technology has emerged as a promising approach for developing antivirals. Short interfering RNAs (siRNAs) can effectively knock down the expression of specific genes, including viral genes, thereby inhibiting viral replication. However, the challenge of intracellular delivery of these RNAi-mediating agents has historically been an obstacle to harnessing their full potential. Traditional delivery methods, such as viral vectors and synthetic carriers, have limitations, including hepatotoxicity, tumorigenesis, and low delivery efficiencies.

The present invention addresses these challenges by utilizing a novel RNAi delivery platform called transkingdom RNAi (tkRNAi). This platform employs nonpathogenic Escherichia coli (E. coli) engineered to transcribe small hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) from a plasmid. The E. coli are equipped with the invasin gene (Inv) and the listeriolysin O gene (hylA), enabling them to deliver shRNAs to mucosal epithelial cells. The invasin gene facilitates receptor-mediated endocytosis, while the listeriolysin O gene aids in the rupture of the endosomal membrane, releasing the shRNAs into the cytoplasm. The tkRNAi vector is a diaminopimelic acid (Dap) auxotrophic mutant and is kanamycin resistant, ensuring its safety and controllability.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The invention relates to a method and composition for inhibiting avian influenza virus (AIV) replication in avian cells using transkingdom RNAi (tkRNAi). Specifically, the invention provides a tkRNAi vector that constitutively generates shRNAs targeting conserved sequences within the viral nucleoprotein (NP) and polymerase acidic (PA) genes. The tkRNAi vector is designed to deliver these shRNAs to mucosal epithelial cells, where they inhibit AIV replication and reduce viral shedding. The tkRNAi vector offers several advantages over conventional AIV vaccines and existing RNAi antiviral approaches, including targeted delivery to mucosal epithelium, non-integration into the host genome, and a confirmed safety record for clinical purposes. The vectors can be quickly adapted to respond to viral mutations and can be administered intranasally, orally, or as an aerosol suitable for inhalation. This antiviral approach represents a scalable and clinically applicable technology for controlling AIV in poultry and potentially in humans.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Design of siRNAs and Use of Viral Specific siRNAs to Inhibit Avian Influenza in an Avian Tissue Model

The invention involves the design and use of siRNAs targeting conserved sequences within the viral NP and PA genes to inhibit AIV replication in avian cells. The siRNA sequences were selected based on their high degree of conservation across different AIV subtypes and strains. The NP and PA genes were chosen because they play crucial roles in viral replication and are highly conserved, making them ideal targets for siRNA-mediated knockdown.

To ensure the specificity and efficacy of the siRNAs, the sequences were analyzed using the BLAST heuristic algorithm to verify the lack of significant sequence complementarity to any known chicken gene. The siRNA sequences were then cloned into a plasmid (pmbv43) that also encodes the invasin gene (Inv) and the listeriolysin O gene (hylA). The resulting plasmids, designated as NP/pmbv43 and PA/pmbv43, were transformed into CEQ221 competent E. coli cells. The transformed E. coli cells, now referred to as anti-AIV/NP and anti-AIV/PA vectors, were cultured and characterized to ensure proper expression and delivery of the shRNAs.

### Inhibiting Avian Influenza Replication in a Chicken Cell Model Using a Unique RNAi Delivery Technology

The tkRNAi vectors were tested in a chicken primary hepatocellular carcinoma epithelial cell line (LMH) to evaluate their ability to inhibit AIV replication. The LMH cells were chosen as a representative avian tissue model due to their commercial availability, good reproducibility, and suitability for transfection studies. The presence of β(1) integrin receptors on the surface of LMH cells was confirmed using conventional PCR, ensuring that the invasin protein expressed by the tkRNAi vectors could interact with these receptors and facilitate intracellular uptake.

The optimal concentration of the tkRNAi vectors was determined by assessing cytotoxicity and invasion efficiency. The maximum allowable concentration of the anti-AIV/scramble vector, which did not induce cytotoxicity, was found to be 7.8 × 10^5 CFU/mL. This concentration was used in subsequent experiments to evaluate the antiviral activity of the anti-AIV/NP and anti-AIV/PA vectors.

Intracellular uptake of the tkRNAi vectors was verified using a red fluorescent protein (RFP)-tagged vector. Fluorescent microscopy and flow cytometry demonstrated that the RFP-tagged vectors were efficiently taken up by LMH cells, with invasion efficiencies of 74.1% and 75.7% at 20 and 36 hours post-invasion, respectively.

### Prophylactic Treatment of Chicken Populations with Anti-AIV Vectors

The antiviral activity of the anti-AIV vectors was evaluated by infecting LMH cells with AIV subtypes H8N4 and H6N2. The cells were pre-treated with the anti-AIV/NP, anti-AIV/PA, and a cocktail of both vectors (anti-AIV/cocktail) before viral infection. The viral titers in the cell culture supernatants were quantified using the 50% tissue culture infectious dose (TCID50) assay.

The results showed that the anti-AIV vectors significantly reduced viral titers compared to untreated control samples. The anti-AIV/cocktail vector, in particular, demonstrated the most pronounced antiviral activity, with log10 reductions ranging from 1.9 to 4.0 in viral titers. The adjusted mean viral titers for the anti-AIV/cocktail vector were significantly lower than those of the positive control (PC) samples, indicating the effectiveness of the tkRNAi vectors in inhibiting AIV replication.

The anti-AIV vectors offer several advantages over conventional AIV vaccines and existing RNAi antiviral approaches. They are engineered to target mucosal epithelium, making them ideal for preventing respiratory diseases. The vectors do not integrate into the host genome, eliminating the risk of tumorigenesis. The tkRNAi platform has a confirmed safety record for clinical purposes and can be quickly adapted to respond to viral mutations. The vectors can be administered intranasally, orally, or as an aerosol, making them suitable for mass application in poultry populations.

## GLOSSARY OF CLAIM TERMS

- **Avian Influenza Virus (AIV):** A group of influenza viruses that primarily infect birds, causing a range of symptoms from mild to severe, including high mortality rates in domestic poultry.
- **Transkingdom RNAi (tkRNAi):** A technology that uses nonpathogenic E. coli engineered to transcribe and deliver small hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) to target cells, facilitating RNA interference (RNAi) in the target cells.
- **Small Hairpin RNAs (shRNAs):** RNA molecules that form a hairpin structure and can be processed into short interfering RNAs (siRNAs) to mediate RNA interference (RNAi).
- **Invasin Gene (Inv):** A gene that encodes a protein facilitating the interaction of bacteria with β(1) integrin receptors on the surface of target cells, leading to receptor-mediated endocytosis.
- **Listeriolysin O Gene (hylA):** A gene that encodes a pore-forming toxin aiding in the rupture of the endosomal membrane, allowing the release of shRNAs into the cytoplasm of target cells.
- **Diaminopimelic Acid (Dap) Auxotrophic Mutant:** A bacterial strain that requires exogenous diaminopimelic acid for growth, ensuring controlled and safe use of the bacteria.
- **Kanamycin Resistant:** A characteristic of bacteria that can survive in the presence of kanamycin, an antibiotic, used to select for bacteria containing the desired plasmid.
- **Nucleoprotein (NP) Gene:** A viral gene encoding the nucleoprotein, a structural protein involved in the replication and assembly of the viral genome.
- **Polymerase Acidic (PA) Gene:** A viral gene encoding the polymerase acidic protein, a component of the viral RNA polymerase complex involved in viral transcription and replication.
- **Chicken Primary Hepatocellular Carcinoma Epithelial (LMH) Cells:** A cell line derived from chicken liver, used as a model for studying avian diseases and evaluating antiviral treatments.
- **50% Tissue Culture Infectious Dose (TCID50):** A measure of the infectious titer of a virus, representing the dilution of the virus that causes a cytopathic effect in 50% of the wells in a tissue culture assay.
- **Cytopathic Effect (CPE):** Visible changes in the morphology of cells in culture, indicating the presence of a virus or other pathogen.
- **Multiplicity of Infection (MOI):** The ratio of infectious viral particles to the number of target cells, used to standardize viral infection experiments.
- **Red Fluorescent Protein (RFP):** A protein that emits red light when excited by specific wavelengths, used to visualize and track the presence of the tkRNAi vectors in cells.
- **Flow Cytometry:** A technique used to analyze and sort cells based on their physical and chemical properties, including the presence of fluorescent markers.
- **Toll-Like Receptors (TLRs):** A class of proteins that play a key role in the innate immune response by recognizing and binding to pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs), including bacterial endotoxins.
- **Lipopolysaccharide (LPS):** A component of the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria that acts as an endotoxin, stimulating the innate immune response.
