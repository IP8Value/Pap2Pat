## BACKGROUND

The field of biotechnology and nanotechnology has seen significant advancements in the development of materials and methods for the purification and characterization of biomolecules. One of the critical challenges in this field is the efficient and selective capture of proteins from complex biological samples, such as cell lysates, for high-resolution structural analysis. Traditional methods often involve multiple steps and can be time-consuming, leading to potential loss of sample integrity and increased risk of contamination.

Graphene oxide (GO) has emerged as a promising material due to its unique properties, including large surface area, tunable chemistry, and excellent mechanical strength. However, the functionalization of GO to create specific binding sites for biomolecules has been a subject of extensive research. The present invention addresses the need for a robust and efficient method to functionalize GO with nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) to create a monolayer that can selectively capture histidine-tagged proteins from cell lysates. This innovation not only simplifies the purification process but also enhances the resolution and quality of structural analysis using techniques such as transmission electron microscopy (TEM) and cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM).

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for the synthesis and use of graphene oxide (GO) functionalized with nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA) to form a monolayer that can selectively capture histidine-tagged proteins from cell lysates. The invention includes the following key aspects:

1. **Synthesis of GO-NTA**: The invention involves the synthesis of GO-NTA by activating the carboxylic acid groups of GO with thionyl chloride (SOCl2) and subsequently reacting with the tris-t-butyl ester of lysine NTA. Deprotection of the t-butyl ester groups using trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) yields the final GO-NTA product.

2. **Monolayer Formation**: The GO-NTA sheets are dispersed in a mixture of isopropanol (IPA) and water to form a stable monolayer at the air-water interface. This monolayer is then transferred onto various substrates, such as silicon wafers, copper grids, and holey carbon grids, using the Langmuir-Schaefer (L-S) transfer technique.

3. **Characterization of GO-NTA Monolayers**: The GO-NTA monolayers are characterized using techniques such as epifluorescence microscopy, atomic force microscopy (AFM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and selected area electron diffraction (SAED). These characterizations confirm the formation of single-layer GO-NTA sheets with uniform thickness and distribution.

4. **Affinity Capture of His6-Proteins**: The GO-NTA monolayers are modified with 4-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and bovine serum albumin (BSA) to reduce non-specific binding. The modified monolayers are then used to capture histidine-tagged proteins, such as His6-T7 bacteriophage and His6-GroEL, from cell lysates. The capture efficiency and specificity are demonstrated using negative-stain TEM and cryo-EM.

5. **Single Particle Analysis**: The captured His6-GroEL is subjected to single particle analysis using EMAN 2.1 software, yielding a gold standard resolution of 8.1 Å. The results are compared with a published high-resolution cryo-EM map, confirming the accuracy and reliability of the method.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Synthesis of GO-NTA

The synthesis of GO-NTA involves several steps to functionalize graphene oxide (GO) with nitrilotriacetic acid (NTA). The process begins with the production of GO using Hummer's method, which involves the oxidation of graphite to produce a material with a high density of oxygen-containing functional groups, such as carboxylic acids, ketones, aldehydes, and hydroxyl groups.

1. **Activation of GO Carboxylic Acid Groups**:
   - GO (335 mg) is stirred in a mixture of thionyl chloride (SOCl2) (60 mL) and dimethylformamide (DMF) (1.5 mL) at 70°C for 3 days. The SOCl2 and DMF are then evaporated, and the residue is washed with dry dichloromethane (DCM) (3 × 50 mL).
   - Acetonitrile (ACN) (50 mL) and triethylamine (Et3N) (3 mL) are added, and the mixture is stirred for 30 minutes.
   - Tris(O-t-butyl)-Nα,Nα-dicarboxymethyllysine ester (533 mg) is added, and the mixture is stirred at 100°C for 3 days. The product is washed with tetrahydrofuran (THF) and water (9,000 rpm for 15 minutes, 3 times for each solvent) and vacuum-dried at 60°C for 24 hours.

2. **Deprotection of t-Butyl Ester Groups**:
   - TFA (10 mL) in THF (30 mL) is added to the dried t-butyl-NTA ester intermediate (180 mg) and stirred at 60°C for 5 hours. The product is washed with THF and water (11,000 rpm × 15 minutes, 3 times for each solvent).

### Monolayer Formation

The GO-NTA sheets are dispersed in a mixture of isopropanol (IPA) and water to form a stable monolayer at the air-water interface. The Langmuir-Schaefer (L-S) transfer technique is used to transfer the monolayer onto various substrates, such as silicon wafers, copper grids, and holey carbon grids.

1. **Exfoliation of GO-NTA**:
   - The GO-NTA sheets are ultrasonically exfoliated at 1 mg/mL by suspending the powder in a 5:1 mixture of methanol (MeOH) and water (H2O) using probe sonication at 150 watts for five cycles (45 seconds sonication followed by 45 seconds of rest in each cycle).
   - The product is centrifuged at 1200 g for 10 minutes, and the supernatant of exfoliated GO-NTA sheets is removed from the sediment of aggregated sheets and subjected to another 5 rounds of sonication.
   - A final centrifugation at 1200 g for 10 minutes is performed before removing the supernatant to yield a GO-NTA solution that is stored for subsequent grid coating experiments.

2. **Langmuir-Trough Setup**:
   - Exfoliated GO-NTA is deposited at the air-water interface of a Kibron μTrough via a syringe pump fitted with a 20 mL syringe. The GO-NTA dispersion is loaded into the syringe and slowly introduced at the air-water interface at a rate of 100 μL/min until the surface pressure reaches 15 mN/m.
   - The film is allowed to relax for 5 minutes, followed by slow compression of the film to 15 mN/m. IPA is then added to the subphase, and the film is transferred to the desired substrate by L-S transfer.

### Characterization of GO-NTA Monolayers

The GO-NTA monolayers are characterized using various techniques to confirm the formation of single-layer GO-NTA sheets with uniform thickness and distribution.

1. **Epifluorescence Microscopy**:
   - Fluorescein-modified GO-NTA (F-PABA-GO-NTA) is deposited onto 1500 mesh grids in the dark by L-S transfer. The grids are allowed to dry in the dark for 1 day before being sandwiched between a glass and cover slip with 5 μL of double deionized water and sealed with nail polish. The glass slide is then mounted on a light microscope for epifluorescence imaging.

2. **Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM)**:
   - AFM is used to determine the thickness and lateral distribution of GO-NTA sheets deposited onto solid substrates by L-S transfer. The analysis reveals the presence of GO-NTA monolayer sheets with a thickness of approximately 1.3 nm, which is consistent with previously reported values for single-layer GO.

3. **Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)**:
   - SEM is used to visualize the morphology of GO-NTA monolayers deposited onto silicon wafers. The images show the presence of GO-NTA monolayer sheets with uniform thickness and distribution.

4. **Selected Area Electron Diffraction (SAED)**:
   - SAED analysis of GO-NTA L-S films deposited onto bare 2000 mesh grids from the air-IPA/H2O interface reveals a hexagonal diffraction pattern, indicative of a single layer of graphenic material. The measured intensity of the inner and outer peaks confirms the presence of a single GO-NTA layer.

### Affinity Capture of His6-Proteins

The GO-NTA monolayers are modified with 4-aminobenzoic acid (PABA) and bovine serum albumin (BSA) to reduce non-specific binding. The modified monolayers are then used to capture histidine-tagged proteins, such as His6-T7 bacteriophage and His6-GroEL, from cell lysates.

1. **Modification with PABA**:
   - GO-NTA (1 mg/mL) is partially deactivated by adding PABA (30 mg) to a 10 mL GO-NTA dispersion. This mixture is probe sonicated at 150 W for 30 seconds of continuous sonication, followed by shaking for 24 hours on a rotary mixer. The PABA-GO-NTA is then exfoliated and washed as described above for GO-NTA exfoliation.

2. **Modification with BSA**:
   - Following L-S transfer of GO-NTA or PABA-GO-NTA onto EM grids and overnight drying in a desiccator, the grids are placed on a strip of Teflon before adding BSA (10 μL of 0.1 mg/mL) and incubating for 5 minutes, followed by 3 × 20 μL double deionized water washes. The modified grids are then stored in a desiccator until use.

3. **Capture of His6-T7 Bacteriophage**:
   - Purified C-terminal gp10 His6-T7 bacteriophage is initially prepared at a concentration of 10^12 particles/mL, with dilution to 10^10 particles/mL in HEPES buffer (pH = 7.4). The GO-NTA modified grids are placed on a Teflon strip, 1 mM NiSO4 (10 μL) is added, and the grids are incubated for 5 minutes before washing with double deionized water (2 × 20 μL) and HEPES buffer (1 × 20 μL). Purified phage (3.5 μL) is then applied to the surface and incubated for 2 minutes before washing with HEPES (2 × 20 μL), double deionized water (1 × 20 μL), and staining with 2% uranyl acetate (5 μL).

4. **Capture of His6-GroEL**:
   - The ASKA Library is used to express N-terminal His6-GroEL. Cells containing the N-His6-GroEL gene overexpression vector are grown to OD = 0.6 (in 100 mL of LB broth using a 37°C shaker/incubator) and induced with a final concentration of 1.0 mM IPTG, before allowing the cells to grow for an additional 4 hours. After centrifugation and removal of the supernatant, the cell pellet is re-suspended in lysis buffer (20 mM HEPES, 100 mM NaCl, pH = 7.4, 100 μg aprotinin, 174 μg phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride (PMSF), and 500 μg of lysozyme) and allowed to sit for 20 minutes. Further disruption of the cell membranes is effected by 110 W probe sonication (35 pulses at 1 second/pulse), followed by centrifugation at 11,000 g for 10 minutes. The supernatant containing His6-GroEL is diluted 10-fold and assayed for affinity binding using the Ni2+-activation procedure described above, except that N-His6-GroEL lysate (5 μL) is applied to the surface and incubated for 2 minutes. The grids are then washed and stained with 2% uranyl acetate as described above.

### Single Particle Analysis

The captured His6-GroEL is subjected to single particle analysis using EMAN 2.1 software, yielding a gold standard resolution of 8.1 Å. The results are compared with a published high-resolution cryo-EM map, confirming the accuracy and reliability of the method.

1. **Cryo-EM Imaging**:
   - Samples are prepared as described above for negative stain TEM imaging, except that BSA-PABA-GO-NTA modified grids are exposed to His6-GroEL lysate, after which the excess solution is removed by blotting twice for 6 seconds per blot using an offset setting of −1 at 80% humidity using a Vitrobot device (FEI Company). The grids are then plunged into liquid ethane for cryofixation and imaged at 300 kV on an FEI Titan Krios equipped with a Gatan K2 Summit direct electron detection camera using low-dose techniques.

2. **Data Processing**:
   - Direct electron detector movie frames are processed in Appion to produce a set of averaged, motion-compensated micrographs to be used in subsequent steps. The micrographs have a 1.32 Å²/pixel resolution across a 4096 Å × 4096 Å array. EMAN 2.1 software is used for reconstruction of 5363 particles that are manually picked from 217 micrographs using a box size of 256. Automatic contrast transfer function (CTF) estimation and structure factor are determined from the incoherent sum of particles using e2ctf and phase-flipped to generate high-pass CTF-corrected particle stacks. Defocus is estimated to range between 0.4 μm–4 μm, but 55% of the particles are defocused between 2 μm–3 μm, which results in a somewhat jagged CTF slope. Particles are binned 2X for class averaging, and 12 classes are chosen to create an initial model with imposed D7 symmetry. The classes contain a mix of top and side views. In the refinement steps, the input set of particles is divided into even/odd halves, each containing 2682 particles. Two independent refinements are generated, producing a gold standard of 8.1 Å (using 0.143 criteria) after 12 iterations over two refinements with an angular sampling of 1.76 degrees. Additionally, we perform Fourier shell correlation against an existing high-resolution cryo-EM map, EMD-5001. The maps are rotated and translated using Chimera to fit the volumes together. The correlation of our model against EMD-5001 (4.2 Å) gives an approximate resolution of 9 Å.
