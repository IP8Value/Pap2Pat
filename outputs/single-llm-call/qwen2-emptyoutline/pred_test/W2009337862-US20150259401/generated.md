# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and biotechnology, specifically to the development of chimeric antibodies with ferret constant regions. These chimeric antibodies are designed to minimize immune responses and extend the half-life of therapeutic antibodies in ferrets, a model organism widely used in the study of respiratory diseases and vaccine efficacy.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Ferrets are valuable animal models for studying human respiratory diseases, particularly influenza and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). They exhibit clinical symptoms similar to those observed in humans and are therefore frequently used to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vaccines and therapeutic antibodies. However, the use of human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) in ferrets has been limited by their short half-life and the induction of anti-drug antibodies (ADAs), which can compromise the effectiveness of the treatment and confound experimental results.

One strategy to mitigate these issues is to create chimeric antibodies by replacing the constant regions of human mAbs with those from the target animal species. This approach has been successfully applied in other animal models, such as mice and monkeys, to reduce immunogenicity and extend the half-life of the antibodies. The present invention builds on this concept by developing chimeric antibodies with ferret constant regions, incorporating specific amino acid substitutions known to enhance FcRn binding and thus prolong the half-life of the antibodies in ferrets.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides chimeric antibodies with ferret constant regions and specific amino acid substitutions that enhance binding to the ferret neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). These chimeric antibodies are designed to minimize immune responses and extend the half-life in ferrets, making them suitable for long-term multidose studies and therapeutic applications.

The invention includes:
1. Chimeric antibodies comprising human variable regions and ferret constant regions.
2. Specific amino acid substitutions in the ferret constant regions to enhance FcRn binding and extend the half-life.
3. Methods for preparing and purifying these chimeric antibodies.
4. Uses of these chimeric antibodies in ferret models for the study of respiratory diseases and vaccine efficacy.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### General

The present invention is directed to chimeric antibodies with ferret constant regions that are designed to minimize immune responses and extend the half-life in ferrets. These chimeric antibodies are particularly useful in the study of respiratory diseases and vaccine efficacy, where the use of ferrets as a model organism is common.

### DEFINITIONS

- **Chimeric Antibody**: An antibody composed of variable regions from one species and constant regions from another species.
- **FcRn (Neonatal Fc Receptor)**: A receptor that binds to the Fc region of antibodies and plays a crucial role in the recycling and prolonged half-life of antibodies.
- **Half-Life**: The time required for the concentration of a substance in the body to decrease by half.
- **Anti-Drug Antibodies (ADAs)**: Antibodies produced by the immune system in response to a therapeutic agent, which can neutralize the agent and reduce its efficacy.

### Amino Acid Substitutions

The chimeric antibodies of the present invention incorporate specific amino acid substitutions in the ferret constant regions to enhance binding to the ferret FcRn and extend the half-life. These substitutions include:

- **S252M**: Substitution of serine at position 252 with methionine.
- **S252Y**: Substitution of serine at position 252 with tyrosine.
- **YTE**: Combination of S252Y, S254T, and T256E substitutions.
- **N434S**: Substitution of asparagine at position 434 with serine.

These substitutions have been shown to improve binding to FcRn at low pH, which is critical for the recycling of antibodies and their prolonged half-life in vivo.

### Antibodies of the Invention

The chimeric antibodies of the present invention comprise human variable regions and ferret constant regions. The human variable regions are specific for the F glycoprotein of respiratory syncytial virus (RSV), ensuring that the antibodies do not bind to endogenous ferret proteins, which could affect pharmacokinetics. The ferret constant regions are derived from the cloned ferret immunoglobulin G (IgG) heavy chain (HC) and kappa light chain (LC) constant regions.

### Ferret Antibody Derivatives

The ferret antibody derivatives of the present invention are chimeric antibodies with the following characteristics:

- **Human Variable Regions**: Specific for the F glycoprotein of RSV.
- **Ferret Constant Regions**: Derived from the cloned ferret IgG HC and LC constant regions.
- **Amino Acid Substitutions**: Incorporating S252M, S252Y, YTE, or N434S substitutions to enhance FcRn binding and extend the half-life.

### Immunoglobulin Constructs of the Invention

The immunoglobulin constructs of the present invention are designed to express the chimeric antibodies with the specified amino acid substitutions. These constructs include:

- **Expression Plasmids**: Containing the human variable regions and ferret constant regions with the specified amino acid substitutions.
- **Recombinant Production**: Using HEK293E cells for transient expression and purification of the chimeric antibodies.

### Antibody Preparation

The chimeric antibodies of the present invention are prepared by cloning the human variable regions and ferret constant regions into expression plasmids. The plasmids are then transfected into HEK293E cells for transient expression. The expressed antibodies are purified using a combination of protein A and protein G columns to ensure high purity and yield.

### Recombinant Production of Antibodies and Immunoglobulin Constructs

The recombinant production of the chimeric antibodies involves the following steps:

1. **Cloning**: Cloning the human variable regions and ferret constant regions into expression plasmids.
2. **Transfection**: Transfecting the expression plasmids into HEK293E cells.
3. **Expression**: Culturing the transfected cells to allow for the expression of the chimeric antibodies.
4. **Purification**: Purifying the expressed antibodies using a combination of protein A and protein G columns.
5. **Analysis**: Analyzing the purified antibodies by SDS-PAGE and size exclusion high-performance liquid chromatography (SE-HPLC) to ensure proper assembly and purity.

### Chimeric Antibodies

The chimeric antibodies of the present invention are characterized by their ability to bind to the RSV F glycoprotein and their enhanced binding to ferret FcRn. The binding affinity of the chimeric antibodies to ferret FcRn is significantly improved by the specified amino acid substitutions, resulting in extended half-lives in ferrets.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Cloning of DNA Encoding Ferret Immunoglobulin Constant Sequences and FcRn**

RNA was isolated from ferret kidney, lung, liver, and spleen using Trizol reagent and the RNeasy kit. The RNA was reverse-transcribed using an oligo(dT) primer and the Superscript III First Strand Synthesis System. The cDNA product was amplified by PCR with primers designed for the ferret IgG heavy chain (HC) constant region, kappa light chain (LC) constant region, FcRn alpha chain, or β2-microglobulin (β2m). The PCR products were cloned into mammalian expression vectors under the control of the CMV promoter. The plasmid for expressing ferret FcRn also included coding sequence for a 6× His tag downstream of the cloning site.

### Example 2

**Preparation of Chimeric Antibodies and Variants**

The HC and LC variable regions from a humanized mAb specific for the F glycoprotein of RSV were cloned into their respective expression plasmids containing the ferret IgG HC constant regions or kappa LC constant regions. The chimeric HC expression plasmid was then mutated using the QuikChange Kit to generate variants with the specified amino acid substitutions. The chimeric antibodies were expressed in HEK293F cells and purified using an AKTA purification system. The purified products were analyzed by SDS-PAGE and SE-HPLC.

### Example 3

**Preparation of Recombinant Soluble Ferret FcRn**

The plasmids encoding the extracellular portion of the α chain and β2 microglobulin were co-transfected into HEK293E cells. The His-tagged ferret FcRn α chain/β2 microglobulin complex secreted by the cells was purified from the culture media using a HisTrap HP column followed by a HiLoad 26/60 Superdex 200 size exclusion column. The purity was assessed by SDS-PAGE and SE-HPLC.

### Example 4

**Ferret FcRn Binding Assays**

Using an AlphaScreen-based protein-protein interaction assay, chimeric antibodies were assayed for ferret FcRn binding in a competitive binding format. Titrated amounts of chimeric mAb were added to biotinylated wild-type (WT) chimeric mAb, His-tagged ferret FcRn, nickel-coated AlphaScreen acceptor beads, and streptavidin-coated AlphaScreen donor beads. The plate was incubated for 1 hour at room temperature, and the amount of luminescence was measured using an Envision plate reader.

### Example 5

**Pharmacokinetic Studies in Ferrets**

Male ferrets were intravenously injected with a single 2 mg/kg dose of the chimeric mAb variants. Blood samples were collected at various time points, and plasma was isolated. The concentrations of the chimeric mAbs in the plasma samples were determined using an electrochemiluminescent assay. The average terminal half-lives of the S252M, S252Y, and YTE variants were 127 ± 38, 206 ± 44, and 213 ± 28 hours, respectively, compared to 65 ± 27 hours for the WT chimeric mAb.

### Example 6

**Measuring Ferret Immune Responses to Test Antibodies**

Ferret anti-human mAb antibodies were detected in a bridging assay that involved coating human mAb on plates, adding titrating amounts of ferret plasma sample, and detecting bound ferret antibodies with the same human mAb, this time labeled with ruthenium. No ADAs were detected in the final plasma samples, taken 504 hours after the second dose, suggesting a lack of ADA response.

This detailed description and the examples provided herein demonstrate the utility and advantages of the chimeric antibodies of the present invention in minimizing immune responses and extending the half-life in ferrets, making them valuable tools for the study of respiratory diseases and vaccine efficacy.
