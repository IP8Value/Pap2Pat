# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cell (HSPC) expansion and maintenance. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and compositions for enhancing the expansion and therapeutic use of HSPCs by modulating the expression of Musashi-2 (MSI2) through the cooperative action of Upstream Stimulatory Factor 2 (USF2) and Pleomorphic Adenoma Gene 1 (PLAG1).

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) are essential for the continuous production of all blood cell types throughout life. The ability of HSCs to self-renew while maintaining the capacity to differentiate into various mature blood cells is crucial for their regenerative properties. Understanding the molecular mechanisms that govern HSC self-renewal and differentiation is of significant interest both scientifically and clinically. One such key regulator of HSC function is the Musashi-2 (MSI2) RNA-binding protein. MSI2 is highly expressed in primitive HSCs and plays a critical role in maintaining their self-renewal capacity. Conversely, its loss of function leads to a significant depletion in HSC reconstitution ability, while moderate overexpression enhances HSC self-renewal.

Despite the importance of MSI2 in HSC biology, the precise mechanisms that regulate its expression remain poorly understood. Recent studies have identified the transcription factors Upstream Stimulatory Factor 2 (USF2) and Pleomorphic Adenoma Gene 1 (PLAG1) as key regulators of MSI2 expression in HSCs. USF2 and PLAG1 function cooperatively to enhance MSI2 transcription, thereby promoting HSC expansion and maintenance. This cooperative regulation is particularly significant because it offers a novel approach to expand HSCs ex vivo, which has important implications for hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and regenerative medicine.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for expanding and maintaining hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) by modulating the expression of Musashi-2 (MSI2) through the cooperative action of Upstream Stimulatory Factor 2 (USF2) and Pleomorphic Adenoma Gene 1 (PLAG1). ## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Methods of Expanding HSPCs

The present invention provides methods for expanding hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) by modulating the expression of Musashi-2 (MSI2) through the cooperative action of Upstream Stimulatory Factor 2 (USF2) and Pleomorphic Adenoma Gene 1 (PLAG1). The methods involve the following steps:

1. **Isolation of HSPCs**: HSPCs can be isolated from various sources, including umbilical cord blood, bone marrow, and peripheral blood. Techniques for isolating HSPCs are well-known in the art and include, but are not limited to, density gradient centrifugation, magnetic bead separation, and fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS).

2. **Introduction of USF2 and PLAG1**: Nucleic acid molecules encoding USF2 and PLAG1 are introduced into the isolated HSPCs. This can be achieved using various gene delivery methods, such as viral vectors (e.g., lentiviral, retroviral, adenoviral vectors), plasmid transfection, electroporation, and nucleofection. The nucleic acid molecules can be delivered individually or as a single construct containing both USF2 and PLAG1 coding sequences separated by a self-cleaving peptide (e.g., P2A, T2A).

3. **Culture Conditions**: The HSPCs expressing USF2 and PLAG1 are cultured under conditions that support HSPC expansion. Suitable culture conditions include the use of serum-free media supplemented with cytokines and growth factors known to support HSPC proliferation and survival, such as stem cell factor (SCF), thrombopoietin (TPO), and FLT3 ligand (FL).

4. **Assessment of HSPC Expansion**: The expansion of HSPCs is assessed by measuring the increase in the number of HSPCs over time. This can be done using flow cytometry to quantify the frequency of HSPC markers, such as CD34, and by performing colony-forming unit (CFU) assays to assess the functional capacity of the expanded HSPCs.

### Expanded HSPCs for Therapeutic Use

The expanded HSPCs obtained by the methods described herein are suitable for therapeutic use, particularly in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and regenerative medicine. The expanded HSPCs can be used to treat various hematological disorders, including but not limited to, aplastic anemia, leukemia, lymphoma, and genetic blood disorders. The expanded HSPCs can be administered to patients using standard transplantation protocols, such as intravenous infusion.

### Compositions

The invention also provides compositions for expanding HSPCs by modulating the expression of MSI2 through the cooperative action of USF2 and PLAG1. These compositions include:

1. **Nucleic Acid Molecules**: Nucleic acid molecules encoding USF2 and PLAG1. These can be DNA or RNA molecules, including cDNA, genomic DNA, and synthetic nucleic acids. The nucleic acid molecules can be designed to express USF2 and PLAG1 individually or as a single construct containing both coding sequences.

2. **Vectors**: Vectors for delivering the nucleic acid molecules into HSPCs. Suitable vectors include viral vectors (e.g., lentiviral, retroviral, adenoviral vectors) and non-viral vectors (e.g., plasmids, liposomes, nanoparticles).

3. **Host Cells**: Host cells expressing USF2 and PLAG1. These can be HSPCs or other cell types that can be used for ex vivo expansion of HSPCs.

### Methods of Culturing Leukemic Cells

The invention also provides methods for culturing leukemic cells by modulating the expression of USF2 and PLAG1. These methods involve the following steps:

1. **Isolation of Leukemic Cells**: Leukemic cells can be isolated from patient samples or established cell lines. Techniques for isolating leukemic cells are well-known in the art and include, but are not limited to, density gradient centrifugation, magnetic bead separation, and FACS.

2. **Introduction of USF2 and PLAG1**: Nucleic acid molecules encoding USF2 and PLAG1 are introduced into the isolated leukemic cells. This can be achieved using various gene delivery methods, such as viral vectors (e.g., lentiviral, retroviral, adenoviral vectors), plasmid transfection, electroporation, and nucleofection.

4. **Assessment of Leukemic Cell Growth**: The growth of leukemic cells is assessed by measuring the increase in cell number over time. This can be done using techniques such as cell counting, flow cytometry, and viability assays.

### Example 1: Inhibition of Cytochrome P450 1B1 Oxidase Causes Expansion of Hematopoietic Stem and Progenitor Cells

#### Materials and Methods

**Cell Lines and Culture Conditions**: K562 cells were maintained in RPMI-1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% penicillin-streptomycin, and 1% L-glutamine. Cord blood (CB) samples were obtained with informed consent and processed to isolate Lin-CD34+ HSPCs using magnetic bead separation.

**Gene Delivery**: Nucleic acid molecules encoding USF2 and PLAG1 were introduced into K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs using lentiviral vectors. Control cells were transduced with empty vector.

**Flow Cytometry**: Cells were stained with antibodies against CD34 and analyzed using a flow cytometer. The frequency of CD34+ cells was quantified using FlowJo software.

**Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qPCR)**: Total RNA was isolated from cells using Trizol-LS reagent. cDNA was synthesized using a reverse transcription kit, and qPCR was performed using specific primers for MSI2 and CYP1B1. The relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method.

**Western Blotting**: Total protein was extracted from cells and resolved on SDS-PAGE gels. Proteins were transferred to PVDF membranes and probed with antibodies against MSI2, CYP1B1, and β-actin. Blots were visualized using chemiluminescent detection.

#### Results

**Expression of USF2 and PLAG1 Enhances MSI2 Transcription**: K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs transduced with USF2 and PLAG1 showed a significant increase in MSI2 transcript levels compared to control cells. The increase in MSI2 expression was more pronounced in cells co-overexpressing USF2 and PLAG1, suggesting a synergistic effect.

**Repression of CYP1B1 Protein**: Co-overexpression of USF2 and PLAG1 in K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs resulted in the selective repression of CYP1B1 protein, while CYP1B1 mRNA levels remained unchanged. This is consistent with the known post-transcriptional regulation of CYP1B1 by MSI2.

**Enhanced HSPC Expansion**: Lin-CD34+ HSPCs co-overexpressing USF2 and PLAG1 showed a significant increase in the proportion and total number of CD34+ cells after 7 days of culture. This indicates that the cooperative action of USF2 and PLAG1 promotes HSPC expansion.

### Results & Discussion

The results demonstrate that the cooperative action of USF2 and PLAG1 enhances MSI2 transcription and promotes HSPC expansion. The selective repression of CYP1B1 protein by MSI2 further supports the role of MSI2 in maintaining HSPC self-renewal. These findings have important implications for the ex vivo expansion of HSPCs for therapeutic use, particularly in hematopoietic stem cell transplantation and regenerative medicine.

### Example 2: CYPB1 Knockdown Increases the Number of CD34 Marked HSPCs

#### Materials and Methods

**Cell Lines and Culture Conditions**: K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs were cultured as described in Example 1.

**Gene Delivery**: Short hairpin RNAs (shRNAs) targeting CYP1B1 were introduced into K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs using lentiviral vectors. Control cells were transduced with non-targeting shRNA.

**Flow Cytometry**: Cells were stained with antibodies against CD34 and analyzed using a flow cytometer. The frequency of CD34+ cells was quantified using FlowJo software.

**Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qPCR)**: Total RNA was isolated from cells, and qPCR was performed to measure the expression levels of CYP1B1 and MSI2.

**Western Blotting**: Total protein was extracted from cells, and Western blotting was performed to measure the protein levels of CYP1B1 and MSI2.

#### Results

**Knockdown of CYP1B1 Increases the Number of CD34+ HSPCs**: K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs transduced with shRNAs targeting CYP1B1 showed a significant increase in the frequency of CD34+ cells compared to control cells. This indicates that the inhibition of CYP1B1 promotes HSPC expansion.

**Increased MSI2 Expression**: Knockdown of CYP1B1 in K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs resulted in a significant increase in MSI2 transcript and protein levels. This suggests that CYP1B1 negatively regulates MSI2 expression, and its inhibition enhances MSI2 levels.

### Example 3: High Throughput Screen to Test CYP Family Inhibitors

#### Materials and Methods

**Cell Lines and Culture Conditions**: K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs were cultured as described in Example 1.

**High Throughput Screening**: A library of small molecule inhibitors targeting the CYP family was screened for their ability to enhance HSPC expansion. The compounds were tested at various concentrations, and the effects on HSPC expansion were assessed using flow cytometry to measure the frequency of CD34+ cells.

**Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qPCR)**: Total RNA was isolated from cells treated with the most effective inhibitors, and qPCR was performed to measure the expression levels of CYP1B1 and MSI2.

**Western Blotting**: Total protein was extracted from cells treated with the most effective inhibitors, and Western blotting was performed to measure the protein levels of CYP1B1 and MSI2.

#### Results

**Identification of Effective Inhibitors**: Several small molecule inhibitors were identified that significantly enhanced HSPC expansion by increasing the frequency of CD34+ cells. The most effective inhibitors also increased MSI2 transcript and protein levels while reducing CYP1B1 protein levels.

### Example 3: Combinatorial HSPC Promoting Effects of SR1 with TMS

#### Materials and Methods

**Cell Lines and Culture Conditions**: K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs were cultured as described in Example 1.

**Gene Delivery and Compound Treatment**: Nucleic acid molecules encoding USF2 and PLAG1 were introduced into K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs using lentiviral vectors. The cells were then treated with the small molecule inhibitor SR1 and the cytokine TMS. Control cells were transduced with empty vector and treated with vehicle.

**Flow Cytometry**: Cells were stained with antibodies against CD34 and analyzed using a flow cytometer. The frequency of CD34+ cells was quantified using FlowJo software.

**Quantitative Real-Time PCR (qPCR)**: Total RNA was isolated from cells, and qPCR was performed to measure the expression levels of CYP1B1 and MSI2.

**Western Blotting**: Total protein was extracted from cells, and Western blotting was performed to measure the protein levels of CYP1B1 and MSI2.

#### Results

**Combinatorial Effects on HSPC Expansion**: K562 cells and Lin-CD34+ HSPCs co-overexpressing USF2 and PLAG1 and treated with SR1 and TMS showed a synergistic increase in the frequency of CD34+ cells compared to cells treated with either USF2/PLAG1 overexpression or SR1/TMS treatment alone. This indicates that the combination of USF2/PLAG1 overexpression and SR1/TMS treatment has a potent combinatorial effect on HSPC expansion.

**Enhanced MSI2 Expression**: The combination of USF2/PLAG1 overexpression and SR1/TMS treatment resulted in a significant increase in MSI2 transcript and protein levels, further supporting the role of MSI2 in promoting HSPC expansion.
