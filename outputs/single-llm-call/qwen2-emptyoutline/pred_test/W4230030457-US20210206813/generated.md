# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a genetically encoded system for constructing and detecting biologically active agents, specifically microbial inhibitor screening systems. The invention further pertains to the identification and development of high-affinity terpenoid inhibitors of protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs), particularly protein tyrosine phosphatase 1B (PTP1B). Additionally, the invention encompasses optogenetic actuators and photoswitchable constructs designed to enhance the functionality and specificity of these screening systems.

## BACKGROUND

Despite significant advancements in structural biology and computational chemistry, designing small molecules that bind tightly and selectively to disease-relevant proteins remains a formidable challenge. The free energetic contributions of water molecule rearrangements and structural changes in binding partners are particularly difficult to predict and incorporate into molecular design. Consequently, drug development often begins with screens of large compound libraries, which can be resource-intensive and subject to serendipity.

Nature has endowed living systems with the catalytic machinery to produce a vast array of biologically active molecules, forming a diverse natural library. These molecules have evolved to perform essential metabolic and ecological functions, such as the phytochemical recruitment of predators of herbivorous insects. However, they often exhibit useful medicinal properties as well. Over the years, screens of environmental extracts and natural product libraries, augmented by combinatorial (bio)chemistry, have uncovered a diverse set of therapeutics, including aspirin and paclitaxel. Unfortunately, these screens are often resource-intensive, limited by low natural titers, and largely dependent on serendipity.

Protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs) are an important class of drug targets that could benefit from new approaches to inhibitor discovery. These enzymes catalyze the hydrolytic dephosphorylation of tyrosine residues and, together with protein tyrosine kinases (PTKs), play crucial roles in numerous diseases, including cancer, autoimmune disorders, and heart disease. While significant progress has been made in developing inhibitors of PTKs, therapeutic inhibitors of PTPs have lagged behind due to the well-conserved, positively charged active sites of PTPs, which make them difficult to inhibit with selective, membrane-permeable molecules.

## DEFINITIONS

- **Protein Tyrosine Phosphatases (PTPs)**: Enzymes that catalyze the hydrolytic dephosphorylation of tyrosine residues, playing crucial roles in various signaling pathways.
- **Protein Tyrosine Kinases (PTKs)**: Enzymes that catalyze the phosphorylation of tyrosine residues, often acting in concert with PTPs to regulate cellular processes.
- **Bacterial Two-Hybrid (B2H) System**: A genetic system used to detect protein-protein interactions by linking the interaction to a reporter gene.
- **Terpenoids**: A large and diverse class of natural products derived from isoprene units, often exhibiting biological activity.
- **Optogenetic Actuators**: Genetic constructs that respond to light, allowing for precise control of cellular processes.
- **Photoswitchable Constructs**: Genetic elements that can change their state in response to light, enabling reversible control of protein function.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF INVENTION

### I. Protein Tyrosine Phosphatases (PTPs) and Protein Tyrosine Kinases (PTKs) in Relation to Disease

Protein tyrosine phosphatases (PTPs) and protein tyrosine kinases (PTKs) are critical regulators of cellular signaling pathways. PTPs catalyze the removal of phosphate groups from tyrosine residues, while PTKs add phosphate groups to tyrosine residues. These enzymes play pivotal roles in various physiological processes, including cell growth, differentiation, and apoptosis. Dysregulation of PTP and PTK activities is implicated in a wide range of diseases, including cancer, autoimmune disorders, and cardiovascular diseases.

PTP1B, a member of the PTP family, is a particularly important therapeutic target due to its involvement in insulin signaling and glucose homeostasis. Inhibition of PTP1B has been shown to enhance insulin sensitivity and improve glucose tolerance, making it a promising target for the treatment of type 2 diabetes, obesity, and HER2-positive breast cancer. However, the development of selective and potent inhibitors of PTP1B has been challenging due to the well-conserved, positively charged active site of PTPs, which makes them difficult to target with small molecules.

### II. Optogenetic Actuators

Optogenetic actuators are genetic constructs that respond to light, enabling precise control of cellular processes. These actuators can be engineered to modulate protein function, gene expression, and signaling pathways in a spatiotemporal manner. In the context of the present invention, optogenetic actuators can be used to enhance the functionality and specificity of the microbial inhibitor screening system.

For example, optogenetic actuators can be designed to control the expression of the B2H system components, such as the substrate domain and the SH2 domain, in response to light. This allows for the temporal regulation of the B2H system, enabling the fine-tuning of the screening conditions. Additionally, optogenetic actuators can be used to control the production of terpenoids, allowing for the generation of specific terpenoid profiles in response to light stimuli.

### III. Genetically Encoded System for Constructing and Detecting Biologically Active Agents: Microbial Inhibitor Screening Systems

The present invention provides a genetically encoded system for constructing and detecting biologically active agents, specifically microbial inhibitor screening systems. The system utilizes a bacterial two-hybrid (B2H) system in which the inhibition of a target protein, such as PTP1B, is linked to cell survival. This system is coupled with metabolic pathways for the biosynthesis of structurally varied terpenoids, enabling the identification of novel inhibitors.

#### Development of the B2H System

The B2H system is designed to link the inhibition of a target protein to cell survival. The system consists of several key components:
1. **Substrate Domain**: A domain that can be phosphorylated by a kinase, such as Src.
2. **SH2 Domain**: A domain that recognizes and binds to the phosphorylated substrate domain.
3. **Kinase**: An enzyme, such as Src, that phosphorylates the substrate domain.
4. **Target Protein**: The protein of interest, such as PTP1B, which dephosphorylates the substrate domain.
5. **Gene of Interest (GOI)**: A gene whose expression is controlled by the interaction between the substrate domain and the SH2 domain.

In the absence of the target protein, the substrate domain is phosphorylated by the kinase, leading to the formation of a protein-protein interaction that activates the expression of the GOI. The presence of the target protein dephosphorylates the substrate domain, preventing the interaction and the expression of the GOI. By linking the expression of the GOI to cell survival (e.g., through the expression of an antibiotic resistance gene), the B2H system can be used to screen for inhibitors of the target protein.

#### Integration of Metabolic Pathways

The B2H system is coupled with metabolic pathways for the biosynthesis of structurally varied terpenoids. Terpenoids are a large and diverse class of natural products derived from isoprene units, often exhibiting biological activity. By integrating metabolic pathways for the production of terpenoids, the system can generate a diverse set of natural products that can be screened for their ability to inhibit the target protein.

For example, the system can be engineered to produce amorphadiene, β-bisabolene, and other terpenoids. These terpenoids can be screened for their ability to inhibit PTP1B by transforming the B2H system into a strain of Escherichia coli and monitoring cell survival in the presence of different terpenoids. Terpenoids that enable cell survival at high concentrations of antibiotic are identified as potential inhibitors of the target protein.

### IV. Evolving High-Affinity Terpenoid Inhibitors of PTP1B

The present invention includes the identification and development of high-affinity terpenoid inhibitors of PTP1B. Through the use of the B2H system and metabolic pathways for terpenoid biosynthesis, the invention has identified two previously unknown terpenoid inhibitors of PTP1B: amorphadiene and β-bisabolene. Both inhibitors exhibit distinct binding mechanisms and biological activity, making them promising scaffolds for lead development.

#### Biophysical Analysis of PTP1B Inhibitors

Amorphadiene and β-bisabolene are highly nonpolar and bind to the allosteric site of PTP1B, stabilizing the WPD loop in an open conformation. This binding mechanism is distinct from traditional PTP inhibitors, which typically bind to the active site and compete with substrates. The high affinity and selectivity of these terpenoids for PTP1B make them valuable starting points for drug development.

#### Biological Activity of PTP1B Inhibitors

The biological activity of amorphadiene and β-bisabolene was confirmed by examining their influence on insulin receptor (IR) phosphorylation in mammalian cells. Both terpenoids increased IR phosphorylation, indicating their ability to inhibit PTP1B and enhance insulin signaling. This finding suggests that these terpenoids have the potential to be developed into therapeutic agents for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and other related conditions.

### VI. Evolving Optogenetic Actuators: Photoswitchable Constructs

The present invention also includes the development of optogenetic actuators and photoswitchable constructs to enhance the functionality and specificity of the microbial inhibitor screening system. These constructs can be engineered to respond to light, allowing for precise control of cellular processes.

#### Design of Optogenetic Actuators

Optogenetic actuators can be designed to control the expression of the B2H system components, such as the substrate domain and the SH2 domain, in response to light. This allows for the temporal regulation of the B2H system, enabling the fine-tuning of the screening conditions. For example, the expression of the substrate domain can be induced by blue light, while the expression of the SH2 domain can be induced by red light. This dual-light control system allows for the precise manipulation of the B2H system, enhancing its sensitivity and specificity.

#### Photoswitchable Constructs

Photoswitchable constructs can be designed to control the production of terpenoids in response to light stimuli. For example, the expression of terpene synthases can be regulated by light, allowing for the generation of specific terpenoid profiles in response to light stimuli. This enables the screening of a diverse set of terpenoids under controlled conditions, enhancing the efficiency and effectiveness of the inhibitor discovery process.

## Examples

### Example 1: Development of the B2H System for PTP1B Inhibition

The B2H system was developed to link the inhibition of PTP1B to cell survival. The system consists of a substrate domain fused to the omega subunit of RNA polymerase, an SH2 domain fused to the 434 phage cI repressor, Src kinase, PTP1B, an operator for 434cI, a binding site for RNA polymerase, and a gene of interest (GOI) for spectinomycin resistance. The system was optimized to enhance the transcriptional response by adjusting promoters and ribosome binding sites. The final plasmid-borne detection system required the inactivation of PTP1B to permit growth at high concentrations of antibiotic.

### Example 2: Identification of Terpenoid Inhibitors of PTP1B

The B2H system was coupled with metabolic pathways for the biosynthesis of structurally varied terpenoids. Five well-characterized terpene synthases were used to produce amorphadiene, β-bisabolene, β-humulene, α-bisabolene, and abietadiene. The pathways were screened for their ability to produce inhibitors of PTP1B by transforming E. coli with plasmids harboring both the pathway of interest and the B2H system. Amorphadiene and β-bisabolene were identified as inhibitors of PTP1B, with IC50 values of 53 ± 8 μM and 13 ± 2 μM, respectively.

### Example 3: Biophysical Analysis of PTP1B Inhibitors

Amorphadiene and β-bisabolene were found to bind to the allosteric site of PTP1B, stabilizing the WPD loop in an open conformation. X-ray crystallography revealed that amorphadiene binds to the same allosteric site targeted by benzbromarone derivatives, causing the 7 helix to reorganize and creating a hydrophobic cleft. Kinetic analyses confirmed that both terpenoids bind to the C-terminal allosteric site of PTP1B and exhibit high selectivity for PTP1B over TC-PTP.

### Example 4: Biological Activity of PTP1B Inhibitors

The biological activity of amorphadiene and β-bisabolene was confirmed by examining their influence on insulin receptor (IR) phosphorylation in HEK293T cells. Both terpenoids increased IR phosphorylation, indicating their ability to inhibit PTP1B and enhance insulin signaling. This finding suggests that these terpenoids have the potential to be developed into therapeutic agents for the treatment of type 2 diabetes and other related conditions.

### Example 5: Development of Optogenetic Actuators and Photoswitchable Constructs

Optogenetic actuators were designed to control the expression of the B2H system components in response to light. The expression of the substrate domain was induced by blue light, while the expression of the SH2 domain was induced by red light. This dual-light control system allowed for the precise manipulation of the B2H system, enhancing its sensitivity and specificity. Photoswitchable constructs were also developed to control the production of terpenoids in response to light stimuli, enabling the screening of a diverse set of terpenoids under controlled conditions.

These examples demonstrate the versatility and potential of the genetically encoded system for constructing and detecting biologically active agents, specifically microbial inhibitor screening systems, in the identification and development of high-affinity terpenoid inhibitors of PTP1B.
