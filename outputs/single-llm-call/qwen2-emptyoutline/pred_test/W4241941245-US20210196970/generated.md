# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapy, specifically to the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma (CCA). More particularly, the invention pertains to a method and composition for enhancing the efficacy of chemotherapy using a combination of cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) and the FOLFIRINOX regimen.

### Brief Description of the Related Art

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) is a rare and aggressive malignancy that arises in the intrahepatic or extrahepatic biliary tract. It is often diagnosed in advanced stages, leading to poor clinical outcomes with a five-year survival rate of less than 20%. Surgical resection or liver transplantation at an early stage are the primary curative treatments for CCA. However, the disease has a high recurrence rate after surgical resection, and chemoresistance poses a significant challenge in administering adjuvant chemotherapy. Standard treatments for recurrent CCA include gemcitabine and fluorouracil (5-FU), either individually or in combination. The FOLFIRINOX regimen, consisting of fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin, has shown promise in increasing overall survival in patients with metastatic pancreatic cancer but is associated with significant toxicity.

Cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) has emerged as a novel approach to targeted cancer treatment. CAP selectively induces apoptosis and DNA damage in tumor cells without damaging normal tissue. Recent studies have demonstrated the potential of CAP in combination with other therapies to enhance anti-cancer effects. The Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS) paired with the Canady Helios Cold Plasma Scalpel has shown efficacy in reducing the viability of solid tumor cells and is currently under clinical trial for various cancers.

Despite advances in chemotherapy and plasma therapy, there is a critical need for more effective and less toxic treatments for CCA. The present invention addresses this need by providing a method and composition that combines CAP with the FOLFIRINOX regimen to achieve synergistic anti-cancer effects.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and composition for treating cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) using a combination of cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) and the FOLFIRINOX regimen. The FOLFIRINOX regimen consists of fluorouracil (5-FU), leucovorin, irinotecan, and oxaliplatin. The method involves administering the FOLFIRINOX regimen to a patient or cell culture, followed by treatment with CAP. The combination of these treatments has been shown to significantly reduce the viability and proliferation of CCA cells, demonstrating a synergistic effect.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for treating cholangiocarcinoma comprising:
1. Administering a therapeutically effective amount of the FOLFIRINOX regimen to a patient or cell culture.
2. Treating the patient or cell culture with cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) at a power setting of 120p (28.7 W) for a duration of 1 to 7 minutes.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a composition comprising:
1. A therapeutically effective amount of the FOLFIRINOX regimen.
2. A device capable of generating cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) at a power setting of 120p (28.7 W).

The invention further provides methods for reducing the dose of the FOLFIRINOX regimen while maintaining or enhancing its therapeutic efficacy when combined with CAP. This combination therapy offers a promising approach to improving the treatment of cholangiocarcinoma by reducing the overall chemotherapeutic burden and minimizing toxicity.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Experiments

To evaluate the synergistic effects of the FOLFIRINOX regimen and cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) on cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) cells, a series of in vitro experiments were conducted using the human intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (ICCA) cell line, KKU-055. The experiments aimed to determine the optimal doses of FOLFIRINOX and CAP that would achieve the maximum reduction in cell viability and proliferation.

#### Cell Culture

The KKU-055 cell line was obtained from Sekisui XenoTech, LLC and cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% Pen Strep. Cells were seeded in 12-well plates at a density of 100,000 cells/well or 50,000 cells/well in 1 mL of complete media and incubated for 24 hours at 37°C and 5% CO2 prior to treatment.

#### FOLFIRINOX Treatment

The FOLFIRINOX regimen was prepared by diluting the four drugs (oxaliplatin, leucovorin, irinotecan, and 5-fluorouracil) in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and combining them in a stock solution at the clinical dose ratio. The clinical dose was converted to a concentration suitable for in vitro use. The FOLFIRINOX stock solution was further diluted with complete DMEM media to achieve the desired concentrations. After 24 hours of incubation, the FOLFIRINOX solution was added to the wells, and the cells were incubated for an additional 24 hours.

#### Cold Plasma Device

The CAP treatments were generated using a US Medical Innovations LLC 22–601 MCa high-frequency electrosurgical generator paired with the Canady Helios Cold Plasma System (CHCPS) and the Canady Helios Cold Plasma Scalpel. The device was operated at a constant helium flow rate of 3 L/min and a power setting of 120p (28.7 W). The distance between the tip of the plasma scalpel and the media surface was maintained at 1.5 cm. After CAP treatment, the cells were returned to the incubator for up to 72 hours.

#### Cell Viability Assay

Cell viability was assessed using the Thiazolyl Blue Tetrazolium Bromide (MTT) assay. Cells were incubated with MTT solution for 3 hours at 37°C and 5% CO2. The absorbance of the dissolved formazan product was measured at 570 nm using a BioTek Synergy HTX microplate reader. Viability was calculated by normalizing to non-treated control cells.

#### Confocal Microscopy and Ki67 Staining

Confocal microscopy was used to analyze cell proliferation by staining with Ki67, a marker of cell proliferation. Cells were fixed with ice-cold methanol, stained with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated Ki-67 Rabbit mAb, and counterstained with DAPI. Images were captured using a Zeiss Confocal 510 LSM microscope and analyzed with Zeiss ZenLite software. Ki-67 positivity was calculated using Microsoft Excel.

#### Cell Cycle Analysis

The cell cycle was analyzed using the IncuCyte Live-Cell Analysis System. A stable KKU-055 cell line was established by transfecting the cells with the IncuCyte Red/Green Lentivirus Reagent, which labels cells in different phases of the cell cycle. Phase contrast images were collected at 1-hour intervals for up to 72 hours after treatment. The percent of cell confluence and detailed cell counts per well were quantified using the IncuCyte Cell By Cell Analysis and plotted in Microsoft Excel.

### Results

#### FOLFIRINOX Regimen Reduced Cholangiocarcinoma Cell Viability

A serial dilution of six doses of the FOLFIRINOX regimen was performed to establish a baseline toxicity measurement. The results showed that cell viability decreased significantly at doses equal to or higher than 3.4 µM 5-FU. The highest dose of FOLFIRINOX (53.8 µM 5-FU, 13.1 µM leucovorin, 5.1 µM irinotecan, and 3.7 µM oxaliplatin) reduced cell viability to 19%.

#### Assessment of the Combined Treatment of CAP and FOLFIRINOX

CAP treatment alone significantly reduced cell viability, with the highest treatment of 7 minutes reducing viability to 3%. When FOLFIRINOX and CAP were combined, the reduction in cell viability was even more pronounced. The combination of FOLFIRINOX (6.7 µM 5-FU, 1.7 µM leucovorin, 0.6 µM irinotecan, and 0.5 µM oxaliplatin) and 5 minutes of CAP achieved a 91% reduction in cell viability. This combination was significantly more effective than either treatment alone.

#### Decrease in Cell Proliferation

Ki67 staining and confocal microscopy revealed a significant decrease in cell proliferation following the combined treatment of FOLFIRINOX and CAP. The combination treatment resulted in a marked reduction in the number of Ki67-positive cells, indicating a decrease in cell proliferation.

#### Induction of Cell Cycle Arrest with Combination Treatment

IncuCyte analysis showed that the combination of FOLFIRINOX and CAP induced cell cycle arrest. Cells treated with the combination therapy exhibited a significant reduction in the number of cells in the mitotic phase and an increase in the number of cells in the apoptotic phase. This suggests that the combination treatment effectively halted cell proliferation and induced apoptosis.

### Discussion

The present study demonstrates the synergistic effects of combining cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) with the FOLFIRINOX regimen in treating cholangiocarcinoma (CCA). Both CAP and FOLFIRINOX individually reduced cell viability and proliferation, but the combination treatment was significantly more effective. The synergistic effect was observed at lower doses of FOLFIRINOX, suggesting that the combination therapy could reduce the overall chemotherapeutic burden and minimize toxicity.

The mechanisms underlying the synergistic effects of CAP and FOLFIRINOX remain to be fully elucidated. However, the results suggest that CAP enhances the cytotoxic effects of FOLFIRINOX by inducing apoptosis and cell cycle arrest. The selective nature of CAP in targeting tumor cells without damaging normal tissue makes it a promising adjuvant therapy for CCA.

Future studies should focus on elucidating the molecular mechanisms responsible for the synergistic effects and identifying the optimal dose of each treatment for clinical application. Additionally, in vivo studies are necessary to validate the findings and assess the safety and efficacy of the combination therapy in a clinical setting.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method and composition for treating cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) using a combination of cold atmospheric plasma (CAP) and the FOLFIRINOX regimen. The combination therapy has been shown to significantly reduce cell viability and proliferation, demonstrating a synergistic effect. This approach offers a promising strategy for improving the treatment of CCA by reducing the overall chemotherapeutic burden and minimizing toxicity. Further research is warranted to optimize the treatment protocol and translate these findings into clinical practice.
