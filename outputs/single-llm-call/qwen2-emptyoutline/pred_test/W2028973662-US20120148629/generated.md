# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of probiotics and their use in managing food allergies. More specifically, the invention pertains to a method and composition comprising the probiotic strain *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287, which is effective in reducing the severity of allergic symptoms in sensitized individuals when administered during the management phase of food allergy.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

The prevalence of allergic diseases has been increasing dramatically in recent decades. Allergic sensitization often begins in early childhood, primarily to common food allergens such as cow's milk, eggs, and wheat. Subsequent exposure to these allergens triggers a complex immune response involving CD4+ T cells, which secrete cytokines such as IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13. The development of allergic manifestations can be addressed through two main approaches: prevention, which aims to inhibit the development of Th-2 conditioning, and management, which focuses on alleviating symptoms in sensitized individuals.

Probiotics, defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) as "living micro-organisms that when administered in adequate amounts confer a health benefit to the host," have gained significant attention for their potential to modulate the host immune system. Studies have explored the beneficial effects of probiotics, particularly strains of *Lactobacillus* and *Bifidobacterium*, in managing atopic diseases. However, the effectiveness of probiotics can vary depending on the specific strain and the timing of intervention.

The importance of intervening at the appropriate time window—either during the prevention phase or the management phase—has been under-investigated. The present invention addresses this gap by evaluating the benefits of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 in a murine model of food allergy. The results demonstrate that while the strain does not prevent sensitization, it significantly reduces allergic symptoms in sensitized mice when administered during the management phase.

## Prevention of Allergies can be Achieved on Different Levels:

Preventing allergies involves multiple strategies, including dietary modifications, environmental controls, and the use of probiotics. Probiotics have shown promise in modulating the immune system to prevent the development of allergic sensitization. However, the effectiveness of probiotics can vary based on the specific strain and the timing of intervention. The present invention focuses on the use of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287, which has been shown to be effective in managing allergic symptoms in sensitized individuals.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and composition for managing food allergies using the probiotic strain *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287. The method involves administering the probiotic to sensitized individuals during the management phase of food allergy, thereby reducing the severity of allergic symptoms. The composition comprises *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.

The invention is based on the discovery that *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287, when administered to sensitized mice, significantly reduces the severity of allergic symptoms induced upon oral challenge. This effect is accompanied by a decrease in the production of IL-13 from restimulated mesenteric lymph node (MLN) cells and a significant reduction in the relative expression levels of IL-13 and Th-2 associated chemokines CCL11 (eotaxin-1) and CCL17 (TARC) in the ileum.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Effect of the Composition:

The composition of the present invention, comprising *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287, is effective in managing food allergies by reducing the severity of allergic symptoms in sensitized individuals. The probiotic strain modulates the immune response, specifically by decreasing the production of IL-13 and reducing the expression of Th-2 associated chemokines CCL11 and CCL17 in the ileum.

### Composition of the Invention or for Use in the Invention:

The composition of the invention comprises *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 in a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The probiotic strain can be administered in various forms, including capsules, tablets, powders, or liquids. The composition may also include additional ingredients such as prebiotics, vitamins, and minerals to enhance the overall health benefits.

### Target Group:

The target group for the composition of the invention includes individuals who are sensitized to food allergens and are experiencing allergic symptoms. The composition is particularly useful for managing symptoms in individuals who have already developed sensitization and are looking for a natural and effective way to reduce the severity of their allergic reactions.

## Experimental Results, Section 1:

The in vivo effect of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 was tested in a murine model of food allergy. BALB/c mice were sensitized to ovalbumin (OVA) via the oral route and challenged as described in the methods section. *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 was administered to mice via drinking water (5 × 10^8 CFU/mL; ad libitum) during the prevention phase (5 days before the first sensitization until the end of the experiment) or in the last week of the experiment following the last sensitization (day 43–49; management phase).

After challenge, animals in the positive control group developed statistically significant clinical scores of food allergy compared to the negative control group. Sensitized mice treated with *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 in the management phase developed less severe clinical symptoms than the positive control group. However, mice consuming *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 strain during the prevention phase of the experiment did not exhibit significantly reduced symptoms upon OVA challenge. These findings suggest that *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 strain acts more effectively during the management phase following challenge and not during the prevention phase.

## Experimental Results, Section 2:

To determine the immunological parameters contributing to the observed beneficial effect of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 on clinical scores, the impact on serum levels of mouse mast-cell protease 1 (MMCP-1) and OVA-specific antibodies (IgE, IgG1, and IgG2a) was investigated. MMCP-1 levels were significantly increased in the positive control group of animals when compared to negative control, non-sensitized mice. Administration of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 did not statistically decrease MMCP-1 in either the preventive or the management phases of the model.

OVA-specific IgE, IgG1, and IgG2a levels increased significantly in the positive control group when compared to the negative control group. However, the administration of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 did not significantly impact the levels of the immunoglobulin subtypes, neither in prevention nor in symptom management phases of the model. These data suggest that *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 is unlikely to lead to a reduction in allergic scores via its effect on mast cell activation and humoral immunity.

## Analytical Methods:

### Reagents and Bacterial Biomass:

*Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) strain NCC 2287 is a dairy starter strain from the Nestlé culture collection (NCC) that was deposited at Collection Nationale de Cultures de Microorganismes at Institut Pasteur, Paris, France (CNCM I-4154). Bacterial biomass was produced by culturing NCC 2287 under standard conditions. Growth curves were determined for the strain, and bacterial cells were harvested by centrifugation 3 hours after entering the stationary phase. The biomass was washed twice in cold PBS and frozen in PBS containing 20% glycerol at −80°C.

### Isolation and Culture of Th2-Skewed Human PBMC:

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC) were isolated from healthy donors using Ficoll-Paque density gradient centrifugation. PBMC were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% L-glutamine, 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin, and 0.1% Gentamycin. Cells were stimulated with OVA (1 mg/mL) and incubated for 72 hours at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. Supernatants were collected and stored at −20°C for cytokine analysis.

### Cytokine ELISA:

Cytokine levels in the supernatants of cultured PBMC and MLN cells were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits. Mouse IL-4, IL-5, and IL-10 were measured using the mouse Th-1/Th-2 multiplex kit (Meso Scale Discovery, Gaithersburg, MD, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. IL-13 was measured using a Mouse IL-13 (DY413E) ELISA kit from R&D Systems (Abington, England).

### Evaluation of ELISA Results:

Optical densities were measured at 450 nm using a microplate reader. Concentrations of cytokines were calculated by converting OD values to pg/mL using a polynomial standard curve. Data were analyzed using the Kruskal-Wallis test followed by the Wilcoxon test, with corrections applied following the Bonferroni-Sidak procedure. Results were considered significant with a P value ≤ 0.05.

### Quantitative Gene Expression Levels by Real-Time PCR:

Total RNA was extracted from ileum and jejunum tissues using the SV Total RNA Isolation System kit (Promega, Dübendorf, Switzerland). RNA was quantified using the Quant-IT Ribogreen Reagent kit (Promega). Reverse transcription was performed using the Multiscribe Reverse Transcriptase kit (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Real-time PCR was conducted on a quantitative ABI-Prism 7900HT using low-density arrays designed online on the Applied Biosystems website. The quantification was normalized with the mean of three house-keeping genes: β-actin, GAPDH, and HPRT. The relative mRNA expression was determined using the ΔCt method, and fold changes were calculated using the formula 2−ΔCt × K, where K is a 10^6 factor.

## OVA Food Allergy Mouse Model:

### Serum MMCP-1:

Serum levels of mouse mast-cell protease 1 (MMCP-1) were analyzed using an ELISA kit purchased from Moredun Scientific (Penicuik, Scotland) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The MMCP-1 concentration was obtained by converting OD values to pg/mL using a polynomial standard curve.

### Isolation and Culture of Mesenteric Lymph Node (MLN) Lymphocytes:

Mesenteric lymph nodes (MLN) were homogenized with a syringe plunger in a cell strainer. Cells were centrifuged and washed twice in RPMI medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 1% L-glutamine, 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin, and 0.1% Gentamycin. Cells (3 × 10^5 cells/well) were cultured in 96-well flat-bottom plates in the absence or presence of OVA (1 mg/mL). After 72 hours of culture, plates (including supernatant and cells) were frozen at −20°C.

### Cytokines in Supernatant of Mesenteric Lymph Node Cell and Splenocyte Cultures:

Cytokine levels in the supernatants of MLN cell cultures were measured using the mouse Th-1/Th-2 multiplex kit (Meso Scale Discovery, Gaithersburg, MD, USA) according to the manufacturer's instructions. IL-13 was measured using a Mouse IL-13 (DY413E) ELISA kit from R&D Systems (Abington, England).

### Low Density Array Analyses of Gene Expression in Intestine:

Total RNA was extracted from ileum and jejunum tissues using the SV Total RNA Isolation System kit (Promega, Dübendorf, Switzerland). RNA was quantified using the Quant-IT Ribogreen Reagent kit (Promega). Reverse transcription was performed using the Multiscribe Reverse Transcriptase kit (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA). Real-time PCR was conducted on a quantitative ABI-Prism 7900HT using low-density arrays designed online on the Applied Biosystems website. The quantification was normalized with the mean of three house-keeping genes: β-actin, GAPDH, and HPRT. The relative mRNA expression was determined using the ΔCt method, and fold changes were calculated using the formula 2−ΔCt × K, where K is a 10^6 factor.

## EXAMPLE 1

In a murine model of food allergy, *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 was administered to sensitized mice during the management phase. The probiotic was given via drinking water (5 × 10^8 CFU/mL; ad libitum) for 8 days following the last sensitization. Upon oral challenge with OVA, the mice treated with *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 exhibited significantly reduced clinical scores of food allergy compared to the positive control group. This reduction in symptoms was accompanied by a decrease in the production of IL-13 from restimulated MLN cells and a significant reduction in the relative expression levels of IL-13 and Th-2 associated chemokines CCL11 and CCL17 in the ileum.

## EXAMPLE 2

In a separate experiment, the effect of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 on the prevention of allergic sensitization was evaluated. The probiotic was administered to mice via drinking water (5 × 10^8 CFU/mL; ad libitum) starting 5 days before the first sensitization and continuing throughout the experimental period. Upon oral challenge with OVA, the mice treated with *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 did not exhibit significantly reduced symptoms compared to the positive control group. These results indicate that *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 is more effective in managing allergic symptoms in sensitized individuals rather than preventing sensitization.

## EXAMPLE 3

To investigate the immunological parameters contributing to the observed beneficial effect of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287, the impact on serum levels of mouse mast-cell protease 1 (MMCP-1) and OVA-specific antibodies (IgE, IgG1, and IgG2a) was assessed. MMCP-1 levels were significantly increased in the positive control group of animals when compared to negative control, non-sensitized mice. Administration of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 did not statistically decrease MMCP-1 in either the preventive or the management phases of the model. OVA-specific IgE, IgG1, and IgG2a levels increased significantly in the positive control group when compared to the negative control group. ## EXAMPLE 4

To further identify potential mechanisms via which *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 exerts its beneficial effect, the ex vivo cytokine profile of antigen-restimulated lymphocytes isolated from the MLN was evaluated. MLN cells from the negative control group secreted low levels of Th-2 cytokines (IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-13). In comparison, MLN cells from the positive control group secreted increased levels of IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, and IL-13. MLN cells from mice administered *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 during the prevention phase exhibited similar levels of Th-2 cytokines as the positive control group. Of interest were the MLN cells of mice given *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 during the management phase, which demonstrated a trend to decreased IL-13 cytokine production but not IL-4, IL-5, and IL-10 levels compared to the positive control.

These examples collectively demonstrate the effectiveness of *Lactococcus lactis* (L. lactis) NCC 2287 in managing food allergies by reducing the severity of allergic symptoms in sensitized individuals, particularly through the modulation of IL-13 and Th-2 associated chemokines in the ileum.
