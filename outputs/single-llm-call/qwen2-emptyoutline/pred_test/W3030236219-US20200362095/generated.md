# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Bottlebrush polymers have gained significant attention in recent years due to their unique properties and versatile applications. These polymers, characterized by a dense array of side chains grafted along a linear backbone, offer advantages in drug delivery, molecular imaging, self-assembly, and polymer network fabrication. Among the various synthetic strategies for bottlebrush polymers, graft-through polymerization of macromonomers (MMs) stands out for its ability to achieve complete backbone functionalization and facile access to multi-block copolymers. One of the most efficient methods for graft-through bottlebrush polymer synthesis is ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-based MMs, which is known for its high efficiency and compatibility with a wide range of functional groups.

Despite the advancements in bottlebrush polymer synthesis, the exploration of triblock bottlebrush copolymers (TBCs) has been limited. Triblock copolymers, consisting of three distinct blocks, have been extensively studied for their unique self-assembly properties and applications in biomedicine. However, the synthesis and characterization of TBCs with a bottlebrush architecture have remained relatively unexplored. This invention addresses this gap by introducing a novel class of thermoresponsive ABC TBC amphiphiles. These TBCs are designed to self-assemble into micelles at room temperature and form injectable hydrogels at physiological temperatures, making them ideal for local drug delivery and controlled release applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel class of triblock bottlebrush copolymers (TBCs) and methods for their synthesis and use. Specifically, the invention provides ABC TBC amphiphiles that self-assemble into micelles at room temperature and form injectable hydrogels at physiological temperatures. The TBCs are synthesized via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-based macromonomers (MMs) corresponding to polylactic acid (PLA), polyethylene glycol (PEG), and poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM). The resulting TBCs exhibit unique self-assembly properties and are capable of encapsulating and releasing a variety of bioactive payloads, including small-molecule drugs and proteins. The invention further includes methods for preparing the TBCs, formulations containing the TBCs, and methods of using the TBCs for local drug delivery and controlled release applications.

## Definitions

### Chemical Definitions

- **Bottlebrush Polymer**: A type of polymer characterized by a dense array of side chains grafted along a linear backbone.
- **Macromonomer (MM)**: A large monomer unit that can be polymerized to form a polymer chain.
- **Ring-Opening Metathesis Polymerization (ROMP)**: A chemical reaction used to polymerize cyclic olefins, such as norbornene, to form linear or branched polymers.
- **Polylactic Acid (PLA)**: A biodegradable and biocompatible polymer derived from renewable resources, commonly used in biomedical applications.
- **Polyethylene Glycol (PEG)**: A biocompatible and non-toxic polymer widely used in drug delivery and other biomedical applications.
- **Poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM)**: A thermoresponsive polymer that exhibits a lower critical solution temperature (LCST) around 32°C, making it suitable for temperature-sensitive applications.
- **Micelle**: A colloidal aggregate of amphiphilic molecules in an aqueous solution, typically with a hydrophobic core and a hydrophilic shell.
- **Hydrogel**: A three-dimensional network of hydrophilic polymers that can absorb and retain large amounts of water or biological fluids.

### Other Definitions

- **Thermoresponsive**: Describing a material that changes its properties in response to temperature variations.
- **Injectable Hydrogel**: A hydrogel that can be delivered to a target site via injection and forms a stable gel in situ.
- **Local Drug Delivery**: The targeted delivery of a drug to a specific site in the body, minimizing systemic exposure and side effects.
- **Controlled Release**: The gradual and predictable release of a drug from a delivery system over an extended period.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF CERTAIN EMBODIMENTS

### Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers

The invention provides a novel class of triblock bottlebrush copolymers (TBCs) with a unique ABC block sequence. The TBCs are synthesized via sequential ring-opening metathesis polymerization (ROMP) of norbornene-based macromonomers (MMs) corresponding to polylactic acid (PLA), polyethylene glycol (PEG), and poly(N-isopropylacrylamide) (PNIPAM). The resulting TBCs, denoted as "ABC," consist of a hydrophobic PLA block, a hydrophilic PEG block, and a thermoresponsive PNIPAM block. The ABC TBCs exhibit unique self-assembly properties, forming micelles at room temperature and injectable hydrogels at physiological temperatures.

### Methods for Preparing Triblock Bottlebrush Copolymers

The synthesis of the ABC TBCs involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of Norbornene-Based Macromonomers**:
   - **Synthesis of Norbornene-N-Hydroxysuccinimidyl (NHS)-Ester**: This step involves the preparation of a norbornene derivative functionalized with an NHS ester group, which can be used to couple with amine-terminated polymers.
   - **Synthesis of Norbornene-PEG Macromonomer (Nor-PEG)**: Nor-PEG is synthesized by coupling a commercially available PEG with the norbornene-NHS ester.
   - **Synthesis of Norbornene-PNIPAM Macromonomer (Nor-PNIPAM)**: Nor-PNIPAM is synthesized by coupling a commercially available amine-terminated PNIPAM with the norbornene-NHS ester.
   - **Synthesis of Norbornene-Propanol**: This step involves the preparation of a norbornene derivative functionalized with a propanol group, which can be used to terminate the polymerization reaction.

2. **Ring-Opening Metathesis Polymerization (ROMP)**:
   - The ROMP reaction is initiated using a Grubbs 3rd generation bispyridyl complex (G3) catalyst. The reaction proceeds sequentially, first with the PLA macromonomer, followed by the PEG macromonomer, and finally the PNIPAM macromonomer. The reaction mixture is quenched with excess ethyl vinyl ether (EVE) to terminate the polymerization. The resulting TBC is purified by dialysis and lyophilization.

### Particles and Hydrogels

The ABC TBCs self-assemble into micelles in aqueous solutions at room temperature. The micelles have a hydrodynamic diameter of approximately 100 nm and can encapsulate a variety of bioactive payloads, including small-molecule drugs and proteins. When heated above the lower critical solution temperature (LCST) of PNIPAM (approximately 32°C), the micelles undergo rapid gelation, forming a physically crosslinked network of hydrophobic PLA and PNIPAM domains connected by PEG domains. The resulting hydrogels are injectable and can be used for local drug delivery and controlled release applications.

### Formulations, Kits, and Administration

The invention further provides formulations containing the ABC TBCs and methods for their administration. The formulations can be prepared by dissolving the TBCs in dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and slowly diluting with saline buffer to obtain an aqueous solution with less than 1% organic solvent. The drug-loaded micellar solutions can be spin-concentrated to a TBC concentration of approximately 10 wt%, where they behave as free-flowing fluids at room temperature. The formulations can be administered intratumorally or to other target sites for local drug delivery. Kits containing the necessary components for preparing the formulations are also provided.

### Methods of Treatment and Use

The ABC TBCs are particularly useful for local drug delivery and controlled release applications, especially in the context of cancer immunotherapy. The TBCs can encapsulate and release a combination of a chemotherapeutic agent and a TLR7/8 agonist, such as paclitaxel (PTX) and resiquimod (R848), respectively. The controlled release of these agents from the TBC hydrogels promotes tumor-controlling immunologic effects while reducing dose-limiting systemic and local toxicities. The invention further includes methods for treating cancer and other diseases by administering the TBC formulations to a subject in need thereof.

## EXAMPLES

### Thermoresponsive ABC Bottlebrush Copolymer Amphiphiles

The synthesis and characterization of the ABC TBCs are described in detail below. The TBCs were synthesized via sequential ROMP of norbornene-based macromonomers corresponding to PLA, PEG, and PNIPAM. The resulting TBCs were characterized using size exclusion chromatography (SEC), nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), differential scanning calorimetry (DSC), and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA). The TBCs were found to self-assemble into micelles at room temperature and form injectable hydrogels at physiological temperatures.

### General Methods and Instruments

All solvents and reagents were used as received unless otherwise specified. Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectra were recorded on a Bruker AVANCE III 400 MHz spectrometer. Size exclusion chromatography (SEC) was performed using a Waters Alliance 2695 system equipped with a Waters 2414 refractive index detector and a PLgel 5 μm Mixed-D column. Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) and thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) were performed using a TA Instruments Q2000 DSC and a TA Instruments Q500 TGA, respectively. Dynamic light scattering (DLS) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) were used to characterize the self-assembly properties of the TBCs.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-N-Hydroxysuccinimidyl (NHS)-Ester

Norbornene-NHS ester was synthesized by coupling a commercially available norbornene derivative with N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) using a carbodiimide coupling agent. The reaction was carried out in dichloromethane (DCM) at room temperature for 24 hours. The product was purified by column chromatography on silica gel using a hexanes/ethyl acetate gradient.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PEG Macromonomer (Nor-PEG)

Nor-PEG was synthesized by coupling a commercially available methoxy-terminated PEG (Mw = 5 kDa) with the norbornene-NHS ester in DCM at room temperature for 24 hours. The product was purified by dialysis against distilled water and lyophilized.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-PNIPAM Macromonomer (Nor-PNIPAM)

Nor-PNIPAM was synthesized by coupling a commercially available amine-terminated PNIPAM (Mw = 5.5 kDa) with the norbornene-NHS ester in DCM at room temperature for 24 hours. The product was purified by dialysis against distilled water and lyophilized.

### Synthesis of Norbornene-Propanol

Norbornene-propanol was synthesized by coupling a commercially available norbornene derivative with propanol using a carbodiimide coupling agent. The reaction was carried out in DCM at room temperature for 24 hours. The product was purified by column chromatography on silica gel using a hexanes/ethyl acetate gradient.

### Ring-Opening Metathesis (ROMP) Polymerization

The ROMP reaction was initiated using a Grubbs 3rd generation bispyridyl complex (G3) catalyst. The reaction proceeded sequentially, first with the PLA macromonomer, followed by the PEG macromonomer, and finally the PNIPAM macromonomer. The reaction mixture was quenched with excess ethyl vinyl ether (EVE) to terminate the polymerization. The resulting TBC was purified by dialysis against distilled water and lyophilized.
