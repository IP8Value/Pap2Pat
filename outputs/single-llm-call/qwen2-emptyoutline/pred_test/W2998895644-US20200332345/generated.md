## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular diagnostics, specifically to the rapid and accurate identification and differentiation of Francisella tularensis subspecies and subtypes using quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) assays. The invention provides a comprehensive and sensitive method for distinguishing between the virulent select agent F. tularensis subspecies and the non-select agent F. tularensis subsp. novicida, which is crucial for public health and biodefense applications.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Francisella tularensis is a highly virulent, facultative intracellular bacterium that causes tularemia, a zoonotic disease with significant public health and biodefense implications. F. tularensis is classified as a Tier 1 select agent due to its potential for deliberate misuse, causing mass casualties and severe economic and infrastructural damage. The bacterium comprises four subspecies: F. tularensis subsp. tularensis (type A), F. tularensis subsp. holarctica (type B), F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica, and F. tularensis subsp. novicida. Among these, F. tularensis subsp. tularensis, F. tularensis subsp. holarctica, and F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica are considered select agents, while F. tularensis subsp. novicida is not.

Type A strains, particularly subtype A.I, are the most virulent and can cause severe and often fatal infections. Accurate and rapid identification of F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes is essential for appropriate medical management and public health response. Current diagnostic methods, such as conventional PCR and qPCR, have limitations in terms of specificity, sensitivity, and the ability to differentiate between the various subspecies and subtypes.

There is a critical need for a fast, accurate, and sensitive assay that can definitively distinguish the virulent select agent F. tularensis subspecies from the non-select agent F. tularensis subsp. novicida and other closely related bacteria. This invention addresses this need by providing a series of singleplex and multiplex qPCR assays that can accurately identify and differentiate F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a set of rapid, accurate, and sensitive qPCR assays for the identification and differentiation of Francisella tularensis subspecies and subtypes. The invention includes singleplex and multiplex qPCR assays that can distinguish between the virulent select agent F. tularensis subspecies (F. tularensis subsp. tularensis, F. tularensis subsp. holarctica, and F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica) and the non-select agent F. tularensis subsp. novicida. Additionally, the invention provides a two-tier multiplex qPCR platform that can identify the presence of bacteria, confirm the presence of F. tularensis, and differentiate between the various subspecies and subtypes.

The key features of the invention include:
1. **Singleplex qPCR Assays**: These assays target specific genomic regions to detect and differentiate F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes. Each assay is highly sensitive and specific, with a limit of detection (LOD) ranging from 2 fg to 7 fg of genomic DNA.
2. **Multiplex qPCR Platforms**: The invention includes a two-tier multiplex qPCR platform. The tier 1 platform can identify the presence of bacteria, confirm the presence of F. tularensis, and differentiate between hypervirulent subtype A.I and other subspecies. The tier 2 platform can further differentiate between subtype A.II, type B, and F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica.
3. **Amplicon Sequencing**: The invention also provides a method for amplicon sequencing to confirm the identity of F. tularensis strains and provide strain-specific information.

The invention is particularly useful for public health laboratories, including the CDC and other agencies, for rapid and accurate identification of F. tularensis in clinical and environmental samples.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Example 1

#### Singleplex qPCR Assays

The singleplex qPCR assays are designed to detect and differentiate F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes. Each assay targets a specific genomic region and uses a hydrolysis probe for high specificity and sensitivity. The following assays are included in the invention:

1. **4Pan1 qPCR Assay**: This assay detects all F. tularensis subspecies. It targets a 116-bp region within the gene encoding an organic solvent tolerance protein (ostA). The LOD for this assay is 3 fg of genomic DNA.
2. **3Pan qPCR Assay**: This assay detects the three virulent F. tularensis subspecies (F. tularensis subsp. tularensis, F. tularensis subsp. holarctica, and F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica) but excludes F. tularensis subsp. novicida. It targets a region encoding a hypothetical protein (FTL_1858). The LOD for this assay is 5 fg of genomic DNA.
3. **A1d qPCR Assay**: This assay specifically detects F. tularensis subsp. tularensis subtype A.I. It targets a 114-bp sequence within an oxidoreductase gene (FTT_0516). The LOD for this assay is 7 fg of genomic DNA.
4. **A2c qPCR Assay**: This assay detects F. tularensis subsp. tularensis subtype A.II. It targets a 101-bp region within the mviN gene (FTW_1702). The LOD for this assay is 5 fg of genomic DNA.
5. **B2 qPCR Assay**: This assay detects F. tularensis subsp. holarctica (type B). It targets a region encoding a hypothetical protein (FTS_0806). The LOD for this assay is 5 fg of genomic DNA.
6. **M3 qPCR Assay**: This assay detects F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica. It targets a region encoding a major facilitator superfamily transporter (FTM_1104). The LOD for this assay is 2 fg of genomic DNA.
7. **N1 qPCR Assay**: This assay specifically detects F. tularensis subsp. novicida. It targets a 140-bp region within the gene encoding a metabolite:H+ symporter family protein (FTN_0003). The LOD for this assay is 3 fg of genomic DNA.

#### Multiplex qPCR Platforms

The invention includes a two-tier multiplex qPCR platform for rapid and accurate identification of F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes.

1. **Tier 1 Quadruplex qPCR Platform**: This platform includes the U16S, 4Pan1, 3Pan, and A1d qPCR assays. It can identify the presence of bacteria, confirm the presence of F. tularensis, and differentiate between hypervirulent subtype A.I and other subspecies. The LOD for this platform ranges from 10 fg to 100 fg of genomic DNA.
   - **U16S Assay**: Detects the presence of bacteria by targeting a conserved region in the 16S rRNA gene.
   - **4Pan1 Assay**: Detects all F. tularensis subspecies.
   - **3Pan Assay**: Detects the three virulent F. tularensis subspecies.
   - **A1d Assay**: Detects F. tularensis subsp. tularensis subtype A.I.

2. **Tier 2 Quadruplex qPCR Platform**: This platform includes the U16S, A2c, B2, and M3 qPCR assays. It can further differentiate between subtype A.II, type B, and F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica. The LOD for this platform ranges from 10 fg to 100 fg of genomic DNA.
   - **U16S Assay**: Detects the presence of bacteria by targeting a conserved region in the 16S rRNA gene.
   - **A2c Assay**: Detects F. tularensis subsp. tularensis subtype A.II.
   - **B2 Assay**: Detects F. tularensis subsp. holarctica (type B).
   - **M3 Assay**: Detects F. tularensis subsp. mediasiatica.

### Example 2

#### Amplicon Sequencing for Strain Confirmation and Characterization

To further confirm the identity of F. tularensis strains and provide strain-specific information, the invention includes a method for amplicon sequencing. The primers used in the tier 1 multiplex qPCR platform are synthesized with PGM-compatible barcodes and used in conventional PCR. The resulting amplicons are then sequenced on the Ion Torrent PGM system and analyzed for content. This method can confirm F. tularensis clade identity and provide valuable strain information about the presence of any single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs), implicating potential relatedness.

#### Verification of Assay Specificity and Sensitivity

To verify the specific and sensitive detection of F. tularensis strains and to confirm the presence of Francisella-like organisms in ticks, additional analyses were performed. The average CT values obtained in the singleplex and multiplex U16S qPCR assays when testing DNA extracted from tick lysates were 21.87 and 22.47, respectively, confirming the presence of bacterial DNA. PCR amplifications using primers known to detect F. tularensis-like endosymbionts confirmed the presence of these organisms in the majority of Dermacentor sp. ticks. The qPCR assays accurately detected the relevant F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes, even when the DNA was spiked with tick lysates, confirming the absence of inhibition.

#### Application in Public Health and Biodefense

The qPCR assays and multiplex platforms described in this invention are particularly useful for public health laboratories, including the CDC and other agencies, for rapid and accurate identification of F. tularensis in clinical and environmental samples. These assays can facilitate treatment decisions and the identification of the point source during a tularemia outbreak, enhancing public health and biodefense preparedness.

In summary, the present invention provides a comprehensive and sensitive method for the rapid and accurate identification and differentiation of F. tularensis subspecies and subtypes, addressing a critical need in public health and biodefense.
