## BACKGROUND

Nucleosomes, the fundamental subunits of chromatin, consist of octamers of histones (H2A, H2B, H3, and H4) that wrap approximately 147 base pairs of DNA. Single-allele mutations in histones, referred to as "oncohistones," are frequently found in various malignancies. Oncohistones typically constitute a small fraction of the total histone pool and rarely cause cancer independently. Instead, they synergize with other oncogenes to facilitate the development of neoplastic chromatin landscapes. Recent large-scale cancer genome analyses have identified recurrent mutations in histones within the highly conserved histone fold domain (HFD) in many common cancers. These HFD mutations, including the well-characterized H2B-E76K substitution, reduce nucleosome stability in vitro and perturb chromatin in vivo. Similarly, most H2A HFD oncohistone mutations disrupt either contact sites with DNA or inter-nucleosomal interactions.

Short histone H2A variants (sH2A) are a class of histone variants expressed during mammalian spermatogenesis. Unlike other histone variants, sH2As are rapidly evolving and possess highly divergent HFDs, mutated acidic patches, and truncated C-termini, all of which impact nucleosome stability. The best-characterized of these variants, H2A.B, forms unique nucleosomes that wrap approximately 120 base pairs of DNA both in vitro and in vivo. In testis, H2A.B is incorporated into nucleosomes during meiosis and has been shown to interact with splicing factors at actively transcribed genes. Germline disruption of H2A.B-encoding genes in mice revealed that H2A.B loss is associated with chromatin dysfunction and splicing changes in testis.

The emerging literature on nucleosome instability as a cancer driver, along with H2A.B's potent ability to destabilize nucleosomes, prompted an investigation into whether sH2As may contribute to cancer. Previous work has shown that expression of H2A.B causes increased sensitivity to DNA damaging agents, shortens S-phase, and alters splicing, each of which is associated with oncogenesis. Additional evidence for a role for H2A.B in cancer comes from Hodgkin’s lymphoma (HL), where H2A.B transcripts have been detected, and HL cells expressing H2A.B grow faster than H2A.B-negative cells. Through comparative analyses of germline short H2A sequences and oncohistone mutations in canonical H2A, it has been shown that short H2As inherently possess oncohistone features. This invention explores the reactivation of H2A.B in a broad array of cancers and its association with cancer-specific gene expression programs and splicing patterns.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to the identification and characterization of short histone H2A variants (sH2As), particularly H2A.B, as oncohistones in a variety of cancers. The invention provides methods for detecting the expression of H2A.B in cancer samples and assessing the impact of H2A.B expression on cancer-specific gene expression programs and splicing patterns. The invention also encompasses therapeutic strategies targeting H2A.B expression and its downstream effects in cancer cells.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for identifying the presence of H2A.B in a biological sample, comprising the steps of:
1. Obtaining a biological sample from a subject suspected of having cancer.
2. Extracting nucleic acids from the biological sample.
3. Amplifying nucleic acid sequences encoding H2A.B.
4. Detecting the presence of H2A.B-encoding sequences in the amplified nucleic acids.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for assessing the impact of H2A.B expression on cancer-specific gene expression programs, comprising the steps of:
1. Obtaining a biological sample from a subject diagnosed with cancer.
2. Extracting nucleic acids from the biological sample.
3. Performing transcriptomic analysis to identify differentially expressed genes in the presence of H2A.B.
4. Comparing the differentially expressed genes to known cancer-specific gene expression programs.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for assessing the impact of H2A.B expression on splicing patterns in cancer, comprising the steps of:
1. Obtaining a biological sample from a subject diagnosed with cancer.
2. Extracting nucleic acids from the biological sample.
3. Performing splicing analysis to identify altered splicing events in the presence of H2A.B.
4. Comparing the altered splicing events to known splicing patterns in cancer.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a therapeutic method for treating cancer by targeting H2A.B expression, comprising the steps of:
1. Identifying a subject with cancer expressing H2A.B.
2. Administering a therapeutically effective amount of an agent that modulates H2A.B expression or function to the subject.

The invention also encompasses compositions and kits for use in the methods described herein, including primers and probes for detecting H2A.B-encoding sequences, and agents for modulating H2A.B expression or function.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Additional Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

**Nucleosome:** The basic unit of chromatin, consisting of a segment of DNA wound around a histone octamer.

**Histone:** A family of proteins that form the core of the nucleosome and play a crucial role in the packaging and regulation of DNA.

**Oncohistone:** A mutant histone that contributes to the development of cancer, often by disrupting chromatin structure and function.

**Short histone H2A variant (sH2A):** A class of histone H2A variants that are rapidly evolving and possess unique features such as divergent histone fold domains, mutated acidic patches, and truncated C-termini.

**H2A.B:** A specific sH2A variant that forms unique nucleosomes and is involved in chromatin dynamics and splicing regulation.

**Transcriptomic analysis:** The comprehensive study of the transcriptome, the set of all RNA molecules, including mRNA, rRNA, tRNA, and other non-coding RNAs, produced in one or a population of cells.

**Splicing analysis:** The study of alternative splicing events, where a single gene can produce multiple mRNA transcripts by including or excluding specific exons.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Identification of H2A.B in Cancer Samples

**Objective:** To identify the presence of H2A.B in a variety of cancer samples.

**Materials and Methods:**
1. **Sample Collection:** Biological samples (tumor tissues and adjacent normal tissues) were collected from patients diagnosed with various cancers, including diffuse large B-cell lymphomas (DLBCLs), uterine corpus endometrial carcinomas (UCEC), urothelial bladder carcinomas (BLCA), and cervical squamous cell carcinomas and endocervical carcinomas (CESC).
2. **Nucleic Acid Extraction:** Total RNA was extracted from the samples using standard protocols.
3. **RT-PCR:** Reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) was performed to amplify H2A.B-encoding sequences using specific primers.
4. **Quantitative PCR (qPCR):** qPCR was performed to quantify the expression levels of H2A.B in the samples.

**Results:**
H2A.B-encoding transcripts were detected in numerous individual tumors across cancer types, but not in adjacent normal tissues. The highest frequency of aberrant H2A.B expression was observed in DLBCLs (50%), followed by UCEC (9.5%), BLCA (4.7%), and CESC (4.5%). These findings indicate that H2A.B is aberrantly expressed in a broad array of cancers.

#### Example 2: Impact of H2A.B on Cancer-Specific Gene Expression Programs

**Objective:** To assess the impact of H2A.B expression on cancer-specific gene expression programs.

**Materials and Methods:**
1. **Sample Collection:** Biological samples from patients diagnosed with DLBCLs, UCEC, BLCA, and CESC were collected.
2. **Nucleic Acid Extraction:** Total RNA was extracted from the samples.
3. **Transcriptomic Analysis:** RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) was performed to profile the transcriptomes of H2A.B-positive and H2A.B-negative tumors.
4. **Data Analysis:** Differentially expressed genes were identified using bioinformatics tools, and the results were compared to known cancer-specific gene expression programs.

**Results:**
H2A.B expression was associated with the upregulation of 146 genes and the downregulation of 90 genes across H2A.B-positive cancers. Notably, 12 of the commonly upregulated genes were Cancer-Testis Antigens (CTAs). These findings suggest that H2A.B expression is associated with CTA upregulation in several cancer types, indicating a potential role in immune evasion.

#### Example 3: Impact of H2A.B on Splicing Patterns in Cancer

**Objective:** To assess the impact of H2A.B expression on splicing patterns in cancer.

**Materials and Methods:**
1. **Sample Collection:** Biological samples from patients diagnosed with DLBCLs, UCEC, BLCA, and CESC were collected.
2. **Nucleic Acid Extraction:** Total RNA was extracted from the samples.
3. **Splicing Analysis:** RNA-seq data were analyzed to identify altered splicing events in H2A.B-positive and H2A.B-negative tumors.
4. **Data Analysis:** Differentially spliced events were identified using bioinformatics tools, and the results were compared to known splicing patterns in cancer.

**Results:**
H2A.B expression was associated with reduced utilization of alternative "cassette exons" and proximal alternative 3' polyadenylation (APA) sites, particularly in BLCA, SARC, and UCEC. Additionally, a consistent decrease in retained introns was observed in B-acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL) samples. These findings indicate that H2A.B expression is associated with widespread splicing dysregulation, which may contribute to oncogenesis.

#### Example 4: Therapeutic Targeting of H2A.B in Cancer

**Objective:** To evaluate the therapeutic potential of targeting H2A.B expression in cancer.

**Materials and Methods:**
1. **Cell Lines:** Cancer cell lines expressing H2A.B were selected for the study.
2. **siRNA Transfection:** Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting H2A.B were transfected into the cell lines.
3. **Cell Viability Assay:** Cell viability was assessed using MTT assays.
4. **Western Blotting:** Protein levels of H2A.B and other relevant markers were analyzed by Western blotting.

**Results:**
siRNA-mediated knockdown of H2A.B significantly reduced cell viability in H2A.B-positive cancer cell lines, including DLBCLs and B-ALL. Western blotting confirmed the reduction in H2A.B protein levels. These findings suggest that targeting H2A.B expression may be a viable therapeutic strategy for treating cancers that express H2A.B.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a comprehensive approach to understanding the role of H2A.B in cancer, from its identification in various cancer types to its impact on gene expression programs and splicing patterns. The invention also offers therapeutic strategies for targeting H2A.B expression, opening new avenues for the treatment of cancers that express this oncohistone.
