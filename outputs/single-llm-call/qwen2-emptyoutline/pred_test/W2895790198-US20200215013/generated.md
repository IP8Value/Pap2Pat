# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical compositions and methods for treating epilepsy and other neurological disorders. More specifically, the invention pertains to the synergistic use of perampanel and decanoic acid for the inhibition of AMPA receptors and the reduction of seizure activity.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Epilepsy is a chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent, unprovoked seizures. Despite the availability of several antiepileptic drugs (AEDs), a significant proportion of patients with epilepsy remain refractory to conventional treatments. Perampanel (Fycompa) is a noncompetitive AMPA (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid) receptor antagonist that is used as an adjunctive treatment for partial-onset and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures. However, perampanel has dose-dependent behavioral side effects, which limit its use in some patients.

The medium chain triglyceride (MCT) ketogenic diet is a nonpharmacologic treatment for patients with drug-resistant epilepsy. Recent studies have shown that medium chain fatty acids, particularly decanoic acid (DA), exert a direct antiseizure effect through noncompetitive inhibition of AMPA receptors. In silico analysis suggests that DA binds to the AMPA receptor at a site distinct from that of perampanel, raising the possibility of a synergistic effect when the two compounds are used in combination.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel pharmaceutical composition comprising perampanel and decanoic acid for the treatment of epilepsy and other neurological disorders. The combination of perampanel and decanoic acid results in a synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors, leading to enhanced seizure control and potentially reduced side effects compared to the use of either compound alone.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Combination

The present invention is directed to a pharmaceutical composition comprising perampanel and decanoic acid, which, when used in combination, exhibit a synergistic effect in inhibiting AMPA receptors and reducing seizure activity. This combination therapy offers a promising approach for the treatment of epilepsy and other neurological disorders, particularly in patients who are refractory to conventional treatments.

### Decanoic Acid and Compositions Comprising the Same

Decanoic acid (DA) is a medium chain fatty acid that has been shown to exert a direct antiseizure effect through noncompetitive inhibition of AMPA receptors. In the context of the present invention, DA is used in combination with perampanel to enhance the inhibition of AMPA receptors and improve seizure control. The effective concentration of DA in the pharmaceutical composition ranges from about 10 μmol/L to about 1000 μmol/L. This range ensures that DA is present in sufficient quantities to achieve the desired synergistic effect while minimizing potential side effects.

### Perampanel

Perampanel is a noncompetitive AMPA receptor antagonist that is currently used as an adjunctive treatment for partial-onset and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures. In the pharmaceutical composition of the present invention, perampanel is present in an amount ranging from about 0.1 μmol/L to about 5 μmol/L. This concentration range is effective in inhibiting AMPA receptors and, when combined with DA, results in a synergistic enhancement of the antiseizure effect.

### Treatment

The pharmaceutical composition of the present invention is particularly useful for the treatment of epilepsy and other neurological disorders characterized by excessive neuronal excitability. The combination of perampanel and DA is effective in reducing seizure activity and improving seizure control in patients. The synergistic effect of the combination allows for the use of lower doses of perampanel, potentially reducing the incidence of dose-dependent side effects such as dizziness, somnolence, and irritability.

### Administration

The pharmaceutical composition of the present invention can be administered to a subject in need thereof using various routes, including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, and subcutaneous administration. The choice of administration route depends on the specific needs of the patient and the severity of the condition being treated. For example, oral administration may be preferred for long-term management of epilepsy, while intravenous administration may be more suitable for acute situations requiring rapid onset of action.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Methods

#### AMPA Receptor Oocyte Electrophysiology

Recordings of recombinant AMPA receptor inward currents from Xenopus oocytes were conducted as previously described. Perampanel was purchased from Apexmol Technology Co. Ltd. (Beijing, China).

#### Seizure-Like Activity Analysis

Seizure-like activity was induced in rat entorhinal cortex-hippocampal slices as described previously. Perampanel (0, 100, or 500 nmol/L) and DA (increasing concentrations at 10-minute intervals) were then applied. The frequency of the discharges was measured at minute intervals, averaged every 5 minutes, and normalized to baseline. IC50 values (half maximal inhibitory concentration) were calculated using a Hill plot with statistical analysis by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with Dunnett's post hoc test.

#### In Vitro Human Brain Slice Studies

Neocortical human brain slices were derived from patients undergoing elective neurosurgical resection of a neoplasm, where 7 of 8 had a history of epileptic seizures. Preoperative informed consent was obtained for the use of resected brain tissue. This study was approved by the Newcastle and North Tyneside 2 Research Ethics Committee (06/Q1003/51), with clinical governance approved by the Newcastle Upon Tyne Hospitals National Health Service (NHS) Trust (CM/PB/3707). A human neocortical slice (450 μm) preparation was used as has been described previously. Statistical analysis was performed using a Mann-Whitney test.

### Example 2—Direct Inhibition of AMPA Receptors by Perampanel and Decanoic Acid

Prior to investigating the synergistic effect between perampanel and decanoic acid, the individual effects of perampanel on AMPA receptor currents were studied. Perampanel inhibited AMPA receptor currents with an IC50 of 8.11 μmol/L against GluA2/3 (SEM 2.05) and 6.27 μmol/L against GluA1/2 (SEM 1.02) in a noncompetitive manner. Perampanel reduced maximal responses to 75.2% (at 2.5 μmol/L) and 16.9% (at 5 μmol/L), demonstrating the direct inhibition of AMPA receptors by perampanel through noncompetitive inhibition without subunit specificity.

Recent studies propose distinct binding sites for perampanel and DA on AMPA receptors, with perampanel binding at the S1-M1 and S2-M4 linker and DA binding to the M3 region. To confirm this, a GluA3 mutant resistant to the AMPA receptor antagonist GYKI was expressed and assessed for sensitivity to perampanel and DA. Perampanel (20 μmol/L) reduced the wild-type AMPA receptor (GluA3) glutamate-induced currents by 94.7%, but the mutant receptor currents by only 54.3% (P < 0.0001). In contrast, DA (1 mmol/L) reduced both currents by 74.5% (SEM 6.4) and 72.8% (SEM 0.3), respectively, strongly supporting the proposed distinct sites of action.

The existence of distinct AMPA receptor binding sites for DA and perampanel raises the possibility of synergism. Therefore, the sensitivity of AMPA receptors to DA at two concentrations of perampanel (1 and 4 μmol/L) was tested. For GluA2/3, the IC50 for DA decreased from 0.52 mmol/L (no perampanel) to 0.15 mmol/L (1 μmol/L perampanel) or 0.12 mmol/L (4 μmol/L perampanel) (P < 0.0001). For GluA1/2, the IC50 for DA decreased from 1.16 mmol/L (no perampanel) to 0.21 mmol/L (1 μmol/L perampanel) or 0.10 mmol/L (4 μmol/L perampanel) (P < 0.0001). Repeating this approach using perampanel with two concentrations of DA (50 and 100 μmol/L) also showed a significant increase in the potency of perampanel, decreasing IC50 values from 8.1 μmol/L (no DA) to 1.7 and 1.6 μmol/L in the presence of DA at 50 and 100 μmol/L, respectively, in GluA2/3 and from 6.2 μmol/L in GluA1/2 to 2.1 and 2.2 μmol/L, respectively (both P < 0.0001). These results imply synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors by DA and perampanel.

To validate a synergistic effect of both perampanel and DA on AMPA receptor currents, an isobolographic analysis was used. This approach can distinguish between additive and synergistic effects of two compounds with similar effects. In this analysis, the isobolic line represents additive effects of the compounds against both GluA1/2 and GluA2/3 receptors. From the results provided, combinatory treatment with perampanel and DA provides points at fixed ratios of each compound that lie below the isobolic line, indicating a synergistic effect. This analysis, therefore, supports a direct synergistic effect of perampanel and DA against AMPA receptors.

### Example 3—Effects of Perampanel and Decanoic Acid on Seizure Models

#### Effects of Perampanel and Decanoic Acid on an Ex Vivo (Animal) Seizure Model

To investigate a synergistic effect in controlling seizure-like activity and to avoid the confounders of systemic metabolism or pharmacokinetic interactions, the effects of combinatory perampanel and DA on PTZ-induced seizure-like activity in rat hippocampal slices were studied. Increasing DA concentrations reduced epileptiform activity at 300 μmol/L with a block at 1000 μmol/L, consistent with earlier data. Repeating the assay with added perampanel (100 and 500 nmol/L) and data normalized to perampanel alone, showed that DA (10 μmol/L) combined with perampanel (100 nmol/L) caused a significant reduction in seizure activity from 95.6% (confidence interval [CI] 81 to 110) in the absence of DA to 76.8% (CI 63-90, P = 0.048) in its presence, and a block at 600 μmol/L. A similar activity is also shown at higher perampanel (500 nmol/L) concentrations, reducing baseline (10 μmol/L) inhibition to 69.6% (CI 53-86, P = 0.015). These data also demonstrate a reduced IC50 for DA from 352 μmol/L (CI 200-621) to 196 μmol/L (145-264) and 122 μmol/L (49-302, P = 0.0252) at 100 and 500 nmol/L, respectively.

#### Effects of Perampanel and Decanoic Acid on Ex Vivo Human Brain Slice Seizure Activity

The translational potential of the observed synergism between perampanel and DA in reducing epileptiform activity in human brain explants was investigated. Slices were obtained from 8 patients undergoing elective neurosurgery for removal of intraaxial neoplasms. Using a proconvulsant artificial cerebrospinal fluid, epileptiform activity was induced, and the impact of DA, perampanel, and combinatory treatment was examined. Baseline epileptiform activity had a mean (95% CI) area power of 1690 μV² (CI 1192-2191 μV²; n = 21). In the presence of DA (100 μmol/L) alone, the activity was reduced (1088 μV², CI: 393-1782 μV²; n = 8) but this difference was not statistically significant (P = 0.1390). The application of perampanel (0.5 μmol/L) alone also did not have a significant effect (P = 0.7554) on activity when compared to baseline activity (1621 μV², CI: 681-2562 μV²; n = 10). In contrast, the concurrent application of DA and perampanel significantly (p < 0.0001) reduced the area power of epileptiform activity (144.7 μV², CI: 15.6-274 μV²; n = 11) with a virtual abolition of the pathologic network LFP (local field potential) activity. In addition, the combination of compounds had a significantly greater effect when compared with both DA (P = 0.0005) and perampanel (P = 0.0002) alone.

## SUMMARY

The data presented herein confirm that the combinatory use of decanoic acid and perampanel is effective in directly reducing AMPA receptor-generated currents and in reducing PTZ-induced seizure-like activity in hippocampal slice preparations. Furthermore, this synergistic effect is also evident with epileptiform activity generated in resected human neocortical tissue, emphasizing the translational potential for this therapeutic approach in patients with epilepsy. The mechanistic studies of perampanel inhibition of AMPA receptors and the levels of decanoic acid in both human and animal models support the synergistic inhibition of AMPA receptors through combinatory treatment with perampanel and dietary medium chain triglycerides containing decanoic acid. This combination therapy may provide both improved seizure control and reduced side effects in patient populations.
