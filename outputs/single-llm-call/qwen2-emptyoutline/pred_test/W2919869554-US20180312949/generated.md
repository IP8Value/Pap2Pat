# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of metallic glasses and metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs). More specifically, the invention pertains to the design and production of gold-based metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs) with controlled color and enhanced hardness. These composites are particularly useful in the manufacturing of luxury products such as jewelry, watches, and ornamental articles, where both aesthetic and mechanical properties are crucial.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Metallic glasses, also known as amorphous metals, are materials that possess a disordered atomic structure, unlike conventional crystalline metals. This unique structure endows metallic glasses with a range of superior properties, including high strength, excellent corrosion resistance, and unique processing capabilities. However, the formation of bulk metallic glasses is limited to specific compositional ranges, making it challenging to design alloys with an optimized combination of desired properties, especially when these properties are mutually exclusive, such as strength and toughness.

One strategy to overcome these limitations is the creation of metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs), which incorporate crystalline dendrites into the metallic glass matrix. The properties of these composites can be tailored by adjusting the morphology and volume fraction of the dendrites. Damage-tolerant MGMCs have been developed, demonstrating unusual combinations of strength and toughness. However, the focus has primarily been on optimizing mechanical properties for engineering applications, with less attention paid to non-structural properties like optical properties, which are increasingly important in the field of luxury goods.

Gold, due to its inherent appeal and historical significance, is widely used in luxury products such as jewelry, watches, and ornamental articles. Pure gold is soft and easily scratched, necessitating the use of gold alloys to enhance its mechanical properties. These alloys are designed to meet specific hallmarking criteria and to exhibit desired optical properties, such as color. Traditional gold alloys are classified by their color, which is controlled by the addition of various alloying elements.

Recent developments in precious-metal-based metallic glasses have aimed to combine the inherent appeal of gold with the unique properties of metallic glasses. However, these efforts have been limited by the narrow compositional ranges that allow for the formation of bulk metallic glasses. Gold-based metallic glasses typically exhibit a "pale" white-gold color and are prone to tarnishing upon exposure to ambient air.

The present invention addresses these challenges by introducing a method to design gold-based MGMCs with controlled color and high hardness. By precipitating finely dispersed gold-rich microdendrites in a white-gold metallic glass matrix, the invention enables the production of MGMCs with visually uniform colors and significantly improved mechanical properties.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for producing gold-based metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs) with controlled color and enhanced hardness. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Alloy Preparation**: Producing alloy ingots by inductive melting of elemental constituents in a quartz tube under an inert atmosphere.
2. **Sample Processing**: Melting the alloy ingots in a furnace and rapidly quenching them in a water bath to form rod samples and plate coupons.
3. **Microstructure Characterization**: Investigating the microstructure of the MGMCs using scanning electron microscopy (SEM) and secondary ion mass spectroscopy (SIMS).
4. **Phase Analysis**: Analyzing the amorphicity/crystallinity of the metallic glass and crystalline phases using x-ray diffraction (XRD).
5. **Thermal Analysis**: Studying the phase transition thermodynamics using differential scanning calorimetry (DSC).
6. **Color Measurement**: Measuring the CIELAB color coordinates of the MGMCs using a spectrophotometer.
7. **Hardness Testing**: Determining the Vickers hardness of the MGMCs using a microhardness tester.

The invention also discloses the composition of the MGMCs, which are represented by the formula:
\[ {Au}_{65.2 - 15.2x}{Cu}_{22.4 + 3.1x}{Ag}_{12.4 - 9.4x}{Pd}_{3x}{Si}_{18.5x} \]
where \( x \) ranges from 0 to 1, representing the molar fraction of the metallic glass phase within the MGMC.

The resulting MGMCs exhibit a visually uniform color and high hardness, making them suitable for use in luxury products where both aesthetic and mechanical properties are essential.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Methods of Processing the Alloy Ingots of the Sample Metallic Glass-Forming Alloys

The first step in the production of the gold-based metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs) involves the preparation of alloy ingots. The alloy ingots are produced by inductive melting of the appropriate amounts of elemental constituents in a quartz tube under an inert atmosphere. The purity levels of the constituent elements are as follows: Au 99.99%, Cu 99.995%, Ag 99.95%, Pd 99.95%, and Si 99.9999%.

The elemental constituents are weighed and placed in a quartz tube, which is then sealed and evacuated to remove any residual air. The tube is backfilled with an inert gas, such as argon, to prevent oxidation during the melting process. The quartz tube is placed in an induction furnace, and the elements are melted to form a homogeneous alloy ingot. The melting temperature is typically set to 950°C to ensure complete dissolution of the elements.

### Methods of Processing the Sample Metallic Glass Plates

Once the alloy ingots are prepared, they are processed to form rod samples and plate coupons. The alloy ingots are melted in a furnace at 950°C under a high-purity argon atmosphere inside quartz cylindrical or rectangular tubes with 0.5 mm thick walls. The molten alloy is then rapidly quenched in a room-temperature water bath to form the desired shapes.

For rod samples, the quartz tubes are cylindrical, and the resulting rods have a diameter of approximately 2 mm. For plate coupons, the quartz tubes are rectangular, and the resulting plates have dimensions of 20 mm × 20 mm × 1 mm. The cross-sections of the rod samples and the surfaces of the plate coupons are finely polished to a 1 μm diamond mirror finish to facilitate subsequent characterization and testing.

### Method for Measuring the CIELAB Color Coordinates

The CIELAB color coordinates of the MGMCs are measured using a Konica Minolta CM-700d spectrophotometer with an aperture size of 8 mm. The plate coupons are placed in the spectrophotometer, and the CIELAB coordinates (L*, a*, b*) are recorded. The L* coordinate represents the luminosity or reflectivity of the alloy, while the a* and b* coordinates represent the red-green and blue-yellow chromaticities, respectively.

Measurements are taken at the four corners of each plate coupon to ensure consistency and accuracy. The reported values are the averages of the measurements taken at these four locations. The CIELAB coordinates provide a quantitative measure of the color of the MGMCs, allowing for precise control and optimization of the color properties.

### Method for Performing Differential Scanning Calorimetry

Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) is used to study the phase transition thermodynamics of the MGMCs. Discs are sectioned from 2-mm diameter rods of the MGMCs, and the DSC scans are performed at a scan rate of 20 K/min. The glass transition temperature (Tg), crystallization temperature (Tx), solidus temperature (Ts), and liquidus temperature (Tl) are identified from the DSC scans.

The DSC data provide valuable information about the thermal stability and phase behavior of the MGMCs. The glass transition temperature (Tg) and crystallization temperature (Tx) are indicators of the amorphous nature of the metallic glass matrix, while the solidus and liquidus temperatures (Ts and Tl) provide insights into the crystalline phase transitions. The DSC analysis helps in understanding the thermal processing window and the stability of the MGMCs under different conditions.

By following the methods described above, the present invention enables the production of gold-based metallic glass matrix composites (MGMCs) with controlled color and enhanced hardness. These composites offer a unique combination of aesthetic and mechanical properties, making them ideal for use in luxury products such as jewelry, watches, and ornamental articles.
