# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for modifying the surface of nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC) films to enhance their suitability for diagnostic applications. More specifically, the invention involves the topological modification of water-resistant NFC films using TEMPO-mediated oxidation followed by EDC/NHS activation to introduce amine-reactive sites. These modified NFC films can be used as a platform for the conjugation of antibodies and other biomolecules, thereby providing a robust and stable support for immunoassays and biosensors.

Polymeric materials derived from nonrenewable resources have been widely used in various medical and diagnostic applications. However, these materials often pose environmental concerns and may exhibit limitations such as low mechanical strength and high non-specific adsorption. Cellulose and its derivatives, particularly nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC), offer a sustainable and biocompatible alternative. NFC, produced from wood fibers through mechanical or chemical disintegration, possesses unique properties such as high surface area, good mechanical strength, and chemical resistivity. Despite these advantages, the high porosity of NFC films can be a drawback in diagnostic applications, as it can lead to non-specific adsorption and reduced stability.

To address these challenges, the present invention introduces a method for modifying the surface of NFC films to reduce porosity, enhance water resistance, and provide functional groups for the conjugation of biomolecules. The method involves TEMPO-mediated oxidation to introduce carboxyl groups on the surface of NFC films, followed by EDC/NHS activation to convert these carboxyl groups into amine-reactive sites. This approach not only improves the water resistance and stability of the NFC films but also provides a platform for the efficient and specific conjugation of antibodies and other proteins.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for modifying the surface of nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC) films to enhance their suitability for diagnostic applications. The method comprises the following steps:

1. **Preparation of NFC Films**: Nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC) films are prepared from wood fibers through mechanical or chemical disintegration. The films are then dried and stored under ambient conditions.

2. **TEMPO-Mediated Oxidation**: The NFC films are subjected to TEMPO-mediated oxidation to introduce carboxyl groups on their surface. This step involves treating the NFC films with a solution containing TEMPO, sodium bromide (NaBr), and sodium hypochlorite (NaClO) at a controlled pH. The oxidation reaction is quenched by adding ethanol and washing the films with water to remove excess chemicals.

3. **EDC/NHS Activation**: The carboxylated NFC films are further treated with a solution of 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) and N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) to convert the carboxyl groups into amine-reactive sites. This step involves incubating the carboxylated NFC films in a buffer solution containing EDC and NHS for a specified period, followed by rinsing with water to remove excess chemicals.

4. **Conjugation of Biomolecules**: The activated NFC films can be used to conjugate antibodies and other biomolecules. This can be achieved through inkjet printing or physical adsorption of the biomolecules onto the activated NFC films. The conjugation is confirmed by various analytical techniques such as quartz crystal microbalance with dissipation (QCM-D), contact angle measurements, X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM), and atomic force microscopy (AFM).

The invention also provides NFC films modified by the above method, which exhibit improved water resistance, reduced porosity, and suitable conjugation sites for biomolecules. These modified NFC films can be used as a platform for various diagnostic applications, including immunoassays and biosensors.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS OF THE INVENTION

### Preparation of Nanofibrillated Cellulose (NFC) Films

Nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC) films are prepared from wood fibers through a series of mechanical and chemical processes. In one embodiment, bleached hardwood pulp (e.g., birch) is mechanically processed using a Masuko grinder with multiple passes and then further disintegrated by a Microfluidics M110P fluidizer. The resulting NFC-gel is filtered to remove excess water and then rolled with a smooth metal rolling pin to consolidate the structure of the film. The prepared films are dried between clean blotting boards under external pressing and stored under ambient conditions until use.

### TEMPO-Mediated Oxidation

The NFC films are subjected to TEMPO-mediated oxidation to introduce carboxyl groups on their surface. In one embodiment, the oxidation is performed by dissolving 0.13 mmol TEMPO and 4.7 mmol NaBr in 100 mL of water. Sodium hypochlorite (NaClO) is then added to the solution, and the pH is adjusted to 10 using 1 M HCl. The NFC films (2.5 cm x 2.5 cm) are placed in a glass petri dish containing the prepared TEMPO solution and kept for a specified period (e.g., 10 to 300 seconds). The oxidation reaction is quenched by adding ethanol and washing the films with a large amount of water to remove excess carboxylation chemicals.

### EDC/NHS Activation

The carboxylated NFC films are further treated with a solution of 1-ethyl-3-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)carbodiimide (EDC) and N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS) to convert the carboxyl groups into amine-reactive sites. In one embodiment, the carboxylated NFC films are incubated in a solution containing 0.1 M EDC and 0.4 M NHS in 10 mM sodium acetate buffer (pH 5) for 20 minutes. The films are then rinsed with water to remove excess chemicals and dried between clean blotting boards to prevent film buckling.

### Conjugation of Biomolecules

The activated NFC films can be used to conjugate antibodies and other biomolecules. In one embodiment, the conjugation is achieved through inkjet printing of fluorescently labeled antibodies (e.g., dansyl or FITC-stained antihuman IgG) using an Epson R800 inkjet printer. The printed NFC films are treated with 0.1 M ethanolamine at pH 8.5 for 15 minutes to remove excess NHS-esters and dried at ambient conditions. The conjugation is confirmed by fluorescence imaging, CLSM, and AFM.

Alternatively, the conjugation can be achieved through physical adsorption of antibodies and other proteins onto the activated NFC films. In one embodiment, FITC-stained or unstained antihuman IgG (100 μg/mL) in 10 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) is adsorbed on the activated NFC films for 20 minutes. The films are then treated with 0.1 M ethanolamine at pH 8.5 for 15 minutes, rinsed with 10 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) and 10 mM NaCl (pH 10) to remove electrostatically bound antibodies, and dried at ambient conditions. The adsorbed antibodies are imaged using CLSM.

### Characterization of Modified NFC Films

The modified NFC films are characterized using various analytical techniques to confirm the successful surface modification and conjugation of biomolecules. In one embodiment, the changes in surface hydrophilicity are monitored using contact angle measurements. The increase in negatively charged groups (carboxyls) is measured using conductometric titration. The surface chemistry is examined using X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS), and the topological changes are investigated using confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) and atomic force microscopy (AFM).

### Examples

#### Example 1: Preparation and TEMPO-Mediated Oxidation of NFC Films

NFC films were prepared from bleached hardwood pulp (birch) using a Masuko grinder and Microfluidics M110P fluidizer. The resulting NFC-gel was filtered, rolled, and dried between clean blotting boards. The NFC films were then subjected to TEMPO-mediated oxidation by dissolving 0.13 mmol TEMPO and 4.7 mmol NaBr in 100 mL of water, adding sodium hypochlorite (NaClO), and adjusting the pH to 10 using 1 M HCl. The NFC films were placed in the prepared TEMPO solution for 10 seconds, and the oxidation reaction was quenched by adding ethanol and washing the films with water.

#### Example 2: EDC/NHS Activation of Carboxylated NFC Films

The carboxylated NFC films from Example 1 were treated with a solution of 0.1 M EDC and 0.4 M NHS in 10 mM sodium acetate buffer (pH 5) for 20 minutes. The films were then rinsed with water and dried between clean blotting boards.

#### Example 3: Conjugation of Antihuman IgG on Activated NFC Films

The activated NFC films from Example 2 were used to conjugate antihuman IgG through inkjet printing. Fluorescently labeled antihuman IgG (1 mg/mL) in 10 mM phosphate buffer (pH 7.4) was printed on the activated NFC films using an Epson R800 inkjet printer. The printed films were treated with 0.1 M ethanolamine at pH 8.5 for 15 minutes and dried at ambient conditions. The conjugation was confirmed by fluorescence imaging, CLSM, and AFM.

#### Example 4: Characterization of Modified NFC Films

The modified NFC films from Example 3 were characterized using contact angle measurements, conductometric titration, XPS, CLSM, and AFM. The contact angle measurements showed a significant reduction in the contact angle, indicating increased hydrophilicity. Conductometric titration confirmed the increase in negatively charged groups (carboxyls). XPS revealed the presence of nitrogen, confirming the formation of NHS-esters. CLSM and AFM images showed the uniform distribution of the conjugated antibodies on the NFC films.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for modifying the surface of nanofibrillated cellulose (NFC) films to enhance their suitability for diagnostic applications. The method involves TEMPO-mediated oxidation to introduce carboxyl groups on the surface of NFC films, followed by EDC/NHS activation to convert these carboxyl groups into amine-reactive sites. The modified NFC films exhibit improved water resistance, reduced porosity, and suitable conjugation sites for biomolecules, making them ideal for use in immunoassays and biosensors. The invention offers a sustainable and biocompatible alternative to traditional polymeric materials, addressing the limitations associated with nonrenewable resources and environmental concerns.
