# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to methods and compositions for predicting the prognosis and recurrence risk in hormone receptor-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2-negative (HR+/HER2−) breast cancer (BC) patients. More particularly, the invention pertains to the use of high expression levels of pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) as biomarkers for predicting poor clinical outcomes in luminal BC.

### 2. The Prior Art

Breast cancer (BC) is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in women worldwide. The disease is highly heterogeneous and is classified into several subtypes based on gene expression profiles and receptor status. Among these subtypes, luminal BC, characterized by the presence of hormone receptors (HRs) and the absence of HER2 amplification, is the most common. Despite advancements in treatment modalities, a significant proportion of luminal BC patients experience disease recurrence, particularly within the first 10 years post-diagnosis. Accurate prediction of recurrence risk is crucial for tailoring adjuvant therapies and improving patient outcomes.

Traditional clinicopathological parameters, such as tumor size, nodal status, and histological grade, have been used to predict recurrence risk. However, these parameters often lack the precision needed for individualized treatment planning. Multigene expression signatures, such as OncotypeDx and MammaPrint, have been developed to provide more accurate risk assessments. These assays, while valuable, are expensive and require specialized equipment, limiting their widespread use.

Recent studies have highlighted the role of metabolic reprogramming in cancer progression. Pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) and O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) are key metabolic regulators that have been implicated in various cancers, including BC. PKM2, a glycolytic enzyme, regulates the Warburg effect, a hallmark of cancer metabolism. O-GlcNAc, a post-translational modification, integrates nutrient status and influences cellular signaling pathways. Elevated levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc have been associated with poor clinical outcomes in BC, but their specific roles in predicting recurrence in luminal BC remain unclear.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for predicting the prognosis and recurrence risk in HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients. The invention is based on the discovery that high expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc are strongly associated with poor clinical outcomes, including increased recurrence risk and decreased disease-free survival (DFS) in luminal BC.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for predicting the prognosis of an HR+/HER2− breast cancer patient, comprising the steps of:
1. Obtaining a biological sample from the patient;
2. Determining the expression level of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in the sample;
3. Comparing the expression levels to a reference level; and
4. Predicting a poor prognosis if the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc are higher than the reference level.

In another aspect, the invention provides a kit for predicting the prognosis of an HR+/HER2− breast cancer patient, comprising:
1. Reagents for detecting the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in a biological sample; and
2. Instructions for using the reagents to predict the prognosis based on the expression levels.

The invention further provides a method for identifying HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients at high risk of recurrence, comprising the steps of:
1. Obtaining a biological sample from the patient;
2. Determining the expression level of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in the sample;
3. Comparing the expression levels to a reference level; and
4. Identifying the patient as being at high risk of recurrence if the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc are higher than the reference level.

The invention also provides a method for stratifying HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients for adjuvant therapy, comprising the steps of:
1. Obtaining a biological sample from the patient;
2. Determining the expression level of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in the sample;
3. Comparing the expression levels to a reference level; and
4. Recommending adjuvant therapy if the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc are higher than the reference level.

The invention further provides a method for monitoring the response to therapy in an HR+/HER2− breast cancer patient, comprising the steps of:
1. Obtaining a biological sample from the patient before and after therapy;
2. Determining the expression level of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc in the samples;
3. Comparing the expression levels before and after therapy; and
4. Evaluating the response to therapy based on changes in the expression levels.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Definition

The terms "PKM2" and "pyruvate kinase isoenzyme M2" refer to the M2 isoform of the pyruvate kinase enzyme, which plays a critical role in glycolysis and the Warburg effect in cancer cells. The term "O-GlcNAc" refers to O-linked β-N-acetylglucosamine, a post-translational modification that integrates nutrient status and influences cellular signaling pathways.

The term "biological sample" includes, but is not limited to, tissue biopsies, blood samples, and other bodily fluids. The term "reference level" refers to a predetermined threshold or standard level of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc expression, which is established based on a population of healthy individuals or patients with known clinical outcomes.

### High Expression of Pyruvate Kinase Isoenzyme M2 (PKM2) is Associated With Poor Clinical Outcome in Luminal Breast Cancer

The present invention is based on the discovery that high expression levels of PKM2 are associated with poor clinical outcomes in luminal breast cancer. This finding is supported by the analysis of the METABRIC dataset, which revealed that patients with high PKM2 expression exhibited significantly worse 10-year overall survival (OS) and relapse-free survival (RFS) compared to those with low PKM2 expression.

In a retrospective cohort study of 162 HR+/HER2− luminal BC patients, high expression levels of PKM2 were significantly associated with a higher risk of cancer recurrence and worse DFS. These results were further validated using Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, which showed that high PKM2 expression was associated with worse overall survival and DFS.

### High Expression of O-Linked β-N-Acetylglucosamine (O-GlcNAc) and PKM2 Can Predict Poor Prognosis of HR+/HER2- Breast Cancer

The invention further demonstrates that high expression levels of O-GlcNAc, in combination with PKM2, can predict poor prognosis in HR+/HER2− breast cancer. In the retrospective cohort study, high expression levels of O-GlcNAc were also significantly associated with a higher risk of cancer recurrence and worse DFS. When the expression levels of O-GlcNAc and PKM2 were combined, the predictive power for poor prognosis was even greater, as evidenced by the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis and multivariate Cox regression analysis.

### Logistic Regression and Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) Curve Analysis

To further validate the predictive value of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc, logistic regression and ROC curve analyses were performed. The logistic regression analysis revealed that the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc, in combination with clinical parameters such as invasive tumor size, T stage, and CTS5, significantly contributed to the prediction of 10-year DFS. The ROC curve analysis showed that the best performance was achieved with the O-GlcNAc-PKM2-T stage model, which had an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.722. This indicates that the combination of O-GlcNAc, PKM2, and T stage serves as an excellent prognostic discriminator in HR+/HER2− luminal BC.

In summary, the present invention provides a novel and effective method for predicting the prognosis and recurrence risk in HR+/HER2− breast cancer patients using the expression levels of PKM2 and O-GlcNAc. This method offers a simple, cost-effective, and clinically relevant approach for improving patient outcomes and guiding adjuvant therapy decisions.
