# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of gene regulation in mammalian cells has seen significant advancements, particularly with the advent of technologies that allow for precise control over gene expression. Traditional methods often rely on the regulation of transcription, where the production of mRNA is modulated to control gene expression. However, recent developments have shifted focus towards post-transcriptional regulation, specifically through the manipulation of RNA self-cleavage. This approach involves the incorporation of self-cleaving RNA motifs, known as ribozymes, into the mRNA of target genes. Upon expression, these ribozymes can spontaneously cleave the mRNA, leading to its degradation and preventing the translation of the encoded protein. The introduction of small molecules that inhibit ribozyme self-cleavage can preserve the integrity of the mRNA, thereby reinstating gene expression.

This invention builds on the foundational work of identifying and characterizing ribozymes that can function efficiently in mammalian cells. Specifically, a modified version of the hammerhead ribozyme, derived from *Schistosoma mansoni* (Sm1), designated as N79, has been shown to exhibit robust self-cleavage activity in various mammalian cell lines. The invention further extends this work by developing a high-throughput screening (HTS) assay to identify small molecules capable of inhibiting ribozyme self-cleavage in mammalian cells. This approach has led to the identification of several potent inhibitors, including adenosine and uridine analogs, which can be used to control gene expression in both in vitro and in vivo settings.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for controlling gene expression in mammalian cells through the modulation of RNA self-cleavage. The invention involves the use of self-cleaving RNA motifs, specifically ribozymes, embedded within the mRNA of a target gene. Upon expression, these ribozymes can cleave the mRNA, leading to its degradation and the absence of gene expression. The invention further provides small molecules that can inhibit ribozyme self-cleavage, thereby preserving the integrity of the mRNA and reinstating gene expression.

Key aspects of the invention include:

3. **Methods of Delivering Nucleic Acids**: Techniques for delivering the ribozyme-containing expression vectors into mammalian cells.
4. **Methods of Identifying Ribozyme Regulators**: A high-throughput screening (HTS) assay for identifying small molecules capable of inhibiting ribozyme self-cleavage.
5. **Methods of Treatment and Administration**: Therapeutic applications of the identified ribozyme inhibitors for controlling gene expression in vivo.
6. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: Formulations of the ribozyme inhibitors for use in pharmaceutical compositions.
7. **Exemplification**: Detailed examples of the screening process, characterization of identified inhibitors, and in vivo applications.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Self-Cleaving RNA Mutant Motifs

The invention utilizes a modified hammerhead ribozyme, designated as N79, derived from *Schistosoma mansoni* (Sm1). This ribozyme has been optimized for efficient self-cleavage in mammalian cells. The N79 ribozyme is designed to be embedded within the mRNA of a target gene, such that upon expression, the ribozyme can cleave the mRNA, leading to its degradation and the absence of gene expression. The N79 ribozyme has been shown to function effectively in a variety of mammalian cell lines, including primary cells in vivo.

### Expression Vectors and Cell Lines

To implement the gene regulation system, stable cell lines are generated that carry an integrated mammalian expression vector. The vector contains a reporter gene, such as luciferase, under the control of a strong promoter (e.g., CMV promoter). Two copies of the N79 ribozyme are inserted into the mRNA of the reporter gene. The stable cell lines are generated by cotransfection of HEK 293 cells with the reporter construct and an expression vector encoding puromycin resistance, followed by selection in puromycin-containing medium. The resulting cell lines express very low basal levels of the reporter gene but can be induced to express the reporter gene upon administration of specific inhibitors of ribozyme self-cleavage.

### Methods of Delivering Nucleic Acids

The ribozyme-containing expression vectors can be delivered into mammalian cells using various methods, including transfection, viral transduction, and electroporation. Transfection can be performed using lipofection reagents or calcium phosphate precipitation. Viral transduction can be achieved using adenoviral, lentiviral, or adeno-associated viral (AAV) vectors. Electroporation can be used for primary cells and difficult-to-transfect cell lines.

### Methods of Identifying Ribozyme Regulators

A high-throughput screening (HTS) assay has been developed to identify small molecules capable of inhibiting ribozyme self-cleavage in mammalian cells. The HTS assay uses the stable cell lines described above, which carry the N79 ribozyme and a reporter gene. The cells are seeded in 384-well plates and treated with a library of compounds at a final concentration of 4 μg/mL. After a 48-hour incubation period, the level of reporter gene expression is measured using a plate reader. Compounds that induce reporter gene expression in cells carrying functional N79 ribozymes but not in cells carrying inactive N79 ribozymes are considered positive hits.

### Methods of Treatment and Administration

The identified ribozyme inhibitors can be used to control gene expression in both in vitro and in vivo settings. In vitro, the inhibitors can be added to the culture medium of cells expressing the ribozyme-containing constructs. In vivo, the inhibitors can be administered via various routes, including intravenous, intraperitoneal, or subcutaneous injection. The combination of toyocamycin and 5-fluorouridine (FUR) has been shown to provide synergistic inhibition of ribozyme function, leading to more efficient induction of gene expression and reduced toxicity.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

Pharmaceutical compositions of the ribozyme inhibitors can be formulated for various routes of administration. The compositions may include pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, and stabilizers. For intravenous administration, the inhibitors can be dissolved in sterile water or saline. For oral administration, the inhibitors can be formulated into tablets, capsules, or solutions. For topical administration, the inhibitors can be formulated into creams or gels.

### Exemplification

#### Methods:

**Generation of Reporter Cell Lines for Cell-Based Screening**

Stable human (HEK-derived) cell lines were generated by cotransfection of HEK 293 cells with a reporter construct containing the luciferase gene under the control of the CMV promoter and two copies of the N79 ribozyme, along with an expression vector encoding puromycin resistance. The cells were selected in puromycin-containing medium to generate stable cell lines. The stable cell lines were tested for their basal level of luciferase expression and for the extent of induction of luciferase expression achieved after transfection with an anti-sense morpholino oligonucleotide known to block the cleavage of N79. One of these cell lines, termed HEK79, showed a 20-fold induction of luciferase expression after oligonucleotide administration and was chosen for high-throughput screening.

**High-Throughput Screening of Compound Libraries**

The HEK79 cells were seeded in 384-well plates and exposed to a library of 58,076 compounds at a final concentration of 4 μg/mL for 48 hours. The level of luciferase expression was then measured using a plate reader. Compounds that induced luciferase expression in cells carrying functional N79 ribozymes but not in cells carrying inactive N79 ribozymes were considered positive hits. Through this primary screening effort and subsequent analysis of similar analogs of positive molecules, 15 ribozyme inhibitors were identified.

**Characterization of Positive Compounds**

The identified molecules were divided into three groups based on structural similarities. The first group consists of adenosine analogs, including tubercidin, tubercidin-monophosphate, tubercidin-triphosphate, tubercidin-cyclic monophosphate, sangivamycin, 8-azaadenosine, tricyclic nucleoside, and toyocamycin. All members of this group contain a modified 7-position on the purine and a 2'-OH group on the sugar base. The second group consists of uridine analogs, including 5-bromouridine, 5-fluorouridine (FUR), and 5-fluorouracil (5FU). All members of this group contain a 5-position modification on the pyrimidine and a 2'-OH group on the sugar base. The third group consists of RNA-binding dyes, including Syto-83, ethidium bromide, and acridine orange.

**Mechanisms Responsible for Ribozyme Inhibition: RNA Incorporation vs. RNA Binding**

To examine the mechanisms responsible for ribozyme inhibition, the N79 ribozyme was incubated with either tubercidin or toyocamycin in an in vitro catalytic reaction. Neither tubercidin nor toyocamycin was able to inhibit ribozyme self-cleavage in vitro, indicating that their mechanism of inhibition is not likely due to direct ribozyme-compound interactions. Instead, covalent incorporation of these analogs into the ribozyme-containing RNA was required for the inhibition of RNA self-cleavage. This was demonstrated by the complete loss of ribozyme self-cleavage when tubercidin-triphosphate was used in place of ATP in an in vitro transcription reaction to generate ribozyme RNA carrying substitutions in all adenosine positions.

**Synergistic Inhibition of Ribozyme Function by Combined Administration of Toyocamycin and FUR**

The combined administration of toyocamycin (an adenosine analog) and FUR (a uridine analog) was tested for its ability to inhibit ribozyme function. Varying amounts of each compound were combined and added to HEK79 cells, and the fold induction of luciferase expression was determined. The combination of the two compounds resulted in more efficient inhibition of the ribozyme than was possible with either compound alone. In vivo, a single pulse of a cocktail containing toyocamycin and FUR was injected into the ears of nude mice, and the induction of gene expression was monitored using bioluminescence imaging. The results demonstrated a pronounced induction of gene expression in the left ear carrying the functional N79 ribozyme, while the right ear carrying the inactive N79 showed little change in gene expression.

### Conclusion

The invention provides a novel method for controlling gene expression in mammalian cells through the modulation of RNA self-cleavage. The use of the N79 ribozyme and the identification of small molecule inhibitors, particularly adenosine and uridine analogs, offer a powerful tool for both research and therapeutic applications. The high-throughput screening assay and the detailed characterization of the identified inhibitors provide a robust foundation for further development and optimization of this gene regulation system.
