# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cancer immunotherapy, particularly to the generation of allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell (HSC)-engineered invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells and their derivatives for use in off-the-shelf cell therapy. The invention encompasses methods for producing these cells, their characterization, and their application in treating various types of cancer, including both hematological and solid tumors.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer immunotherapy has emerged as a promising approach for treating various malignancies. Among the different modalities, T-cell-based therapies, such as chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-engineered T (CAR-T) cells, have shown remarkable success, particularly in hematological cancers. However, the current T-cell therapies are predominantly autologous, meaning that T cells are collected from a patient, modified, and then infused back into the same patient. This approach is time-consuming, logistically challenging, and costly. Moreover, for patients with heavily lymphopenic pretreatment or rapidly proliferative diseases, it may not always be feasible to produce autologous cell products.

Allogeneic cell products, which can be manufactured at a large scale and distributed readily to treat a broad range of cancer patients, are highly desirable. However, conventional αβ T cells used in allogeneic settings risk inducing graft-versus-host disease (GvHD) due to histocompatibility leukocyte antigen (HLA) incompatibility. To mitigate this risk, additional gene editing is often required to ablate endogenous T-cell receptor (TCR) expression, which increases manufacturing complexity.

Invariant natural killer T (iNKT) cells represent a unique subset of T cells that do not induce GvHD and possess potent antitumor properties. However, the low frequency of iNKT cells in human blood makes it challenging to generate sufficient numbers for therapeutic use. The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a method to generate large numbers of allogeneic HSC-engineered iNKT cells (AlloHSC-iNKT cells) and their derivatives, which can be used as off-the-shelf cellular therapies for cancer.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for generating allogeneic HSC-engineered iNKT cells (AlloHSC-iNKT cells) and their derivatives, such as CAR-engineered AlloHSC-iNKT cells (AlloBCAR-iNKT cells) and HLA-ablated universal HSC-iNKT cells (UHSC-iNKT cells). The method involves the following steps:

1. **Isolation and Transduction of HSCs**: Human CD34+ hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) are isolated from cord blood (CB) or granulocyte-colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF)-mobilized peripheral blood stem cells (PBSCs). The HSCs are then transduced with a lentiviral vector encoding an iNKT TCR gene and a suicide/positron emission tomography (PET) imaging gene (sr39TK).

2. **Differentiation in Artificial Thymic Organoid (ATO) Culture**: The transduced HSCs are cultured in a two-stage ATO/α-galactosylceramide (αGC) culture system. In the first stage, the HSCs differentiate into iNKT cells over a period of 8 weeks. In the second stage, the differentiated iNKT cells are expanded using αGC-loaded peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) for an additional 2-3 weeks.

3. **CAR Engineering and HLA Ablation**: The mature AlloHSC-iNKT cells can be further engineered to express CARs or undergo CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing to ablate HLA-I and HLA-II molecules, resulting in AlloBCAR-iNKT cells and UHSC-iNKT cells, respectively.

The invention also includes the characterization of the generated AlloHSC-iNKT cells and their derivatives, demonstrating their high purity, yield, antitumor efficacy, safety, and low immunogenicity. The AlloHSC-iNKT cells and their derivatives are suitable for off-the-shelf allogeneic cell therapy applications, offering a significant advantage over conventional T-cell therapies in terms of manufacturing scalability and therapeutic potential.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Generation of Allogeneic HSC-Engineered iNKT (AlloHSC-iNKT) Cells

#### Isolation and Transduction of HSCs

Human CD34+ hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) are isolated from either cord blood (CB) or granulocyte-colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF)-mobilized peripheral blood stem cells (PBSCs). The isolated HSCs are transduced with a lentiviral vector (Lenti/iNKT-sr39TK) encoding an iNKT TCR gene and a suicide/PET imaging gene (sr39TK). The transduction is performed using a standard protocol, and the transduction efficiency is typically over 50%.

#### Differentiation in Artificial Thymic Organoid (ATO) Culture

The transduced HSCs are cultured in a two-stage ATO/α-galactosylceramide (αGC) culture system. In the first stage, the HSCs are differentiated into iNKT cells over a period of 8 weeks. The ATO culture is generated by combining MS5-DLL4 cells with the transduced HSCs in a serum-free ATO culture medium. The ATOs are harvested and dissociated into single cells, which are then placed in the second stage αGC expansion culture for an additional 2-3 weeks. This results in a significant expansion of the iNKT cells, yielding a high-purity AlloHSC-iNKT cell product.

### Characterization of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

#### Phenotype and Functionality

The generated AlloHSC-iNKT cells are characterized by their phenotype and functionality. They express high levels of memory T cell markers (e.g., CD45RO), NK cell markers (e.g., CD161), and inflammatory tissue/tumor homing markers (e.g., CCR4, CCR5, and CXCR3). When stimulated with αGC, the AlloHSC-iNKT cells proliferate vigorously and secrete high levels of Th0/Th1 cytokines (e.g., IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-2) but limited amounts of Th2 cytokines (e.g., IL-4) and Th17 cytokines (e.g., IL-17). Intracellular staining shows that the AlloHSC-iNKT cells produce high levels of effector cytokines (e.g., IFN-γ, TNF-α, and IL-2) and cytotoxic molecules (e.g., perforin and granzyme B).

#### Transcriptome Profiling

Deep RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) analysis reveals a distinctive transcription factor signature of AlloHSC-iNKT cells, including high expression of ZBTB16 (PLZF), TBX21 (T-bet), NFKB1 (NF-κB1), and JUN (c-Jun), and low expression of GATA3 and RORC. The AlloHSC-iNKT cells also express high levels of NK activating receptors and low levels of NK inhibitory receptors, suggesting a superior antitumor NK function. Additionally, they express high levels of tissue inflammatory homing markers, indicating a strong capacity to home to and penetrate tumor sites. Importantly, the AlloHSC-iNKT cells express much lower levels of HLA-I and HLA-II molecules compared to endogenous T and NK cells, suggesting a natural resistance to allorejection.

### Tumor Targeting of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

#### Intrinsic NK Function

The AlloHSC-iNKT cells exhibit a potent antitumor NK function, characterized by high expression of NK activating receptors (e.g., NKG2D and DNAM-1) and low expression of NK inhibitory receptors (e.g., KIRs). They produce high levels of cytotoxic molecules (e.g., perforin and granzyme B) and effectively kill various human tumor cell lines, including melanoma, leukemia, lung cancer, prostate cancer, and multiple myeloma. The antitumor efficacy of AlloHSC-iNKT cells is maintained even after cryopreservation, which is a significant advantage over conventional NK cells.

#### CAR-Engineered AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

The AlloHSC-iNKT cells can be further engineered to express CARs, such as a B cell maturation antigen (BCMA)-targeting CAR (BCAR). The resulting AlloBCAR-iNKT cells exhibit enhanced antitumor efficacy through a triple mechanism involving NK activating receptors, iNKT TCR, and CAR. In in vitro and in vivo studies, AlloBCAR-iNKT cells effectively kill multiple myeloma cells and demonstrate a higher antitumor efficacy compared to conventional BCAR-T cells. Importantly, AlloBCAR-iNKT cells do not induce GvHD, allowing for long-term survival of treated animals.

### Safety and Immunogenicity

#### Graft-Versus-Host (GvH) Response

The AlloHSC-iNKT cells and their derivatives do not induce GvH responses, as evidenced by the lack of IFN-γ production in mixed lymphocyte reaction (MLR) assays and the absence of GvHD in xenograft mouse models. The cells are also equipped with a suicide switch (sr39TK/GCV) for additional safety control.

#### Host-Versus-Graft (HvG) Response

The AlloHSC-iNKT cells and their derivatives are resistant to host T-cell-mediated allorejection, as they express low levels of HLA-I and HLA-II molecules. They also express low levels of NK activating receptor ligands, making them resistant to host NK-cell-mediated allorejection.

### Development of HLA-Ablated Universal HSC-iNKT (UHSC-iNKT) Cells and Derivatives

To further enhance the resistance to host T-cell-mediated allorejection, the AlloHSC-iNKT cells can be engineered to ablate HLA-I and HLA-II molecules using CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing. The resulting UHSC-iNKT cells and their derivatives (e.g., UBCAR-iNKT cells) maintain the antitumor efficacy and safety features of the original AlloHSC-iNKT cells, with the added advantage of complete HLA ablation.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Generation and Characterization of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

Human CD34+ HSCs were isolated from cord blood and transduced with the Lenti/iNKT-sr39TK vector. The transduced HSCs were cultured in a two-stage ATO/αGC culture system, resulting in the generation of AlloHSC-iNKT cells. Flow cytometry analysis confirmed the high purity and yield of the AlloHSC-iNKT cells. The cells expressed high levels of memory T cell markers, NK cell markers, and inflammatory tissue/tumor homing markers. When stimulated with αGC, the AlloHSC-iNKT cells proliferated vigorously and secreted high levels of Th0/Th1 cytokines and cytotoxic molecules.

#### Example 2: Antitumor Efficacy of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

The antitumor efficacy of AlloHSC-iNKT cells was evaluated in vitro and in vivo. In vitro, the AlloHSC-iNKT cells effectively killed various human tumor cell lines, including melanoma, leukemia, lung cancer, prostate cancer, and multiple myeloma. In vivo, the AlloHSC-iNKT cells suppressed tumor growth in a human melanoma xenograft NSG mouse model, demonstrating their potent antitumor efficacy.

#### Example 3: Generation and Characterization of AlloBCAR-iNKT Cells

The AlloHSC-iNKT cells were further engineered to express a BCMA-targeting CAR (BCAR). The resulting AlloBCAR-iNKT cells maintained the high purity and yield of the original AlloHSC-iNKT cells and exhibited enhanced antitumor efficacy through a triple mechanism involving NK activating receptors, iNKT TCR, and CAR. In vitro and in vivo studies demonstrated the superior antitumor efficacy of AlloBCAR-iNKT cells compared to conventional BCAR-T cells.

#### Example 4: Safety and Immunogenicity of AlloHSC-iNKT Cells

The safety and immunogenicity of AlloHSC-iNKT cells were evaluated using in vitro MLR assays and in vivo xenograft mouse models. The AlloHSC-iNKT cells did not induce GvH responses and were resistant to host T-cell and NK-cell-mediated allorejection. The cells were also equipped with a suicide switch (sr39TK/GCV) for additional safety control.

#### Example 5: Generation and Characterization of UHSC-iNKT Cells

The AlloHSC-iNKT cells were further engineered to ablate HLA-I and HLA-II molecules using CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing. The resulting UHSC-iNKT cells and their derivatives (e.g., UBCAR-iNKT cells) maintained the antitumor efficacy and safety features of the original AlloHSC-iNKT cells, with the added advantage of complete HLA ablation. In vitro and in vivo studies demonstrated the enhanced resistance to host T-cell-mediated allorejection and the maintenance of antitumor efficacy.

## ILLUSTRATIVE VECTOR SEQUENCES

### Vector Sequence for Lenti/iNKT-sr39TK

The Lenti/iNKT-sr39TK vector is a lentiviral vector encoding an iNKT TCR gene and a suicide/PET imaging gene (sr39TK). The vector sequence is as follows:

```
5'-[CMV promoter]-[iNKT TCRα-F2A-TCRβ-P2A-sr39TK]-[WPRE]-[LTR] 3'
```

### Vector Sequence for Retro/BCAR-tEGFR

The Retro/BCAR-tEGFR vector is a retroviral vector encoding a BCMA-targeting CAR gene and a truncated epidermal growth factor receptor (tEGFR) reporter gene. The vector sequence is as follows:

```
5'-[SFFV promoter]-[BCMA scFV-41BB-CD3ζ-P2A-tEGFR]-[WPRE]-[LTR] 3'
```

### CRISPR-Cas9/B2M-CIITA-gRNAs Complex

The CRISPR-Cas9/B2M-CIITA-gRNAs complex is used for gene editing to ablate HLA-I and HLA-II molecules. The gRNA sequences are as follows:

- **gRNA (B2M)**: `CGCGAGCACAGCUAAGGCC`
- **gRNA (CIITA)**: `GAUAUUGGCAUAAGCCUCCC`

These sequences are designed to target the B2M and CIITA genes, respectively, and are used in conjunction with the Cas9 enzyme to achieve precise gene editing.
