# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of ophthalmology and specifically to the use of non-human models for studying and treating retinal disorders. More particularly, the invention provides a non-human model lacking the Dp71 protein, which is crucial for the proper functioning of Müller glial cells in the retina. The invention also encompasses methods for diagnosing and treating retinal disorders, such as retinal edema and diabetic retinopathy, using this non-human model.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Müller cells, the principal glia of the vertebrate retina, play a critical role in maintaining retinal homeostasis. These cells are involved in various functions, including potassium buffering, water drainage, and the maintenance of the blood-retina barrier (BRB). The inwardly rectifying potassium channel Kir4.1 and the water transport protein Aquaporin-4 (AQP4) are essential for these functions. The dystrophin gene product, Dp71, is known to regulate the expression and subcellular distribution of Kir4.1 and AQP4 in Müller cells. Genetic inactivation of Dp71 leads to mislocation of these channels, which increases the vulnerability of retinal nerve cells to transient ischemia and other retinal injuries.

Experimental retinal detachment is a well-established model of retinal injury that has been used to study the functional implications of Dp71 and the associated protein complex in Müller cell-mediated maintenance of retinal integrity. Previous studies have shown that retinal detachment causes alterations in water transport and impairments in fluid absorption of Müller cells, associated with a downregulation of Dp71 expression. Similar functional deficits are observed in Müller cells from Dp71-null mice even without experimental retinal detachment. Additionally, the permeability of the blood-retina barrier is dramatically increased in untreated Dp71-null mice compared to controls.

These findings highlight the importance of Dp71 in maintaining retinal homeostasis and suggest that Dp71-null mice can serve as a valuable model for developing novel therapeutic strategies for retinal disorders, particularly those involving edema and vascular permeability.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Non Human Model According to the Invention

The present invention provides a non-human model, specifically a Dp71-null mouse, for studying and treating retinal disorders. The Dp71-null mouse is generated by replacing, via homologous recombination, most of the first and unique exon of Dp71 and a small part of the first intron with a sequence encoding a β-gal-neomycin resistance chimeric protein (β-geo). This genetic modification abolishes the expression of Dp71 without interfering with the expression of other products of the DMD (Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy) gene.

In the Dp71-null mouse model, the absence of Dp71 leads to several functional and morphological alterations in Müller cells. Specifically, the mislocation of Kir4.1 and AQP4 channels, impaired potassium and water homeostasis, and increased permeability of the blood-retina barrier are observed. These alterations mimic the changes seen in retinal disorders such as retinal edema and diabetic retinopathy, making the Dp71-null mouse a valuable tool for understanding the pathophysiology of these conditions and for developing novel therapeutic strategies.

### Uses of the Non Human Model According to the Invention

The Dp71-null mouse model can be used for various purposes, including but not limited to:

1. **Studying the Pathophysiology of Retinal Disorders**: The model allows researchers to investigate the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying retinal disorders, particularly those involving edema and vascular permeability. By comparing the retinal function and structure of Dp71-null mice with wild-type controls, researchers can gain insights into the role of Dp71 in maintaining retinal homeostasis.

2. **Developing Diagnostic Tools**: The model can be used to develop and validate diagnostic tools for retinal disorders. For example, changes in the expression and localization of Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4 in Müller cells can serve as biomarkers for early detection of retinal disorders.

3. **Testing Therapeutic Interventions**: The Dp71-null mouse model can be used to test the efficacy of potential therapeutic interventions for retinal disorders. This includes evaluating the effects of drugs, gene therapies, and other treatments on the expression and function of Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4, as well as on the permeability of the blood-retina barrier.

### Diagnostics Methods According to the Invention

The invention provides methods for diagnosing retinal disorders in a subject. These methods involve comparing the expression and localization of Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4 in Müller cells of the subject with a reference standard. Altered expression or localization of these proteins indicates the presence of a retinal disorder.

1. **Immunohistochemistry**: Tissue samples from the retina of the subject are processed and stained with antibodies specific for Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4. The localization and intensity of the staining are compared with a reference standard. Mislocation or reduced expression of these proteins suggests the presence of a retinal disorder.

2. **Western Blot Analysis**: Protein extracts from the retina of the subject are analyzed by Western blot using antibodies specific for Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4. The band densities are quantified and compared with a reference standard. Reduced protein levels indicate the presence of a retinal disorder.

3. **Quantitative RT-PCR**: Total RNA is extracted from the retina of the subject, and the expression levels of Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4 are measured by quantitative RT-PCR. The expression levels are normalized to a housekeeping gene and compared with a reference standard. Reduced mRNA levels suggest the presence of a retinal disorder.

### Therapeutic Methods and Pharmaceutical Compositions According to the Invention

The invention provides methods for treating retinal disorders in a subject. These methods involve administering a therapeutically effective amount of a composition that modulates the expression or function of Dp71, Kir4.1, or AQP4 in Müller cells.

1. **Gene Therapy**: Viral vectors, such as adeno-associated viruses (AAVs), can be used to deliver genes encoding Dp71, Kir4.1, or AQP4 to Müller cells. This approach aims to restore the expression and function of these proteins, thereby improving retinal homeostasis and reducing the permeability of the blood-retina barrier.

2. **Small Molecule Drugs**: Small molecule drugs that modulate the expression or function of Dp71, Kir4.1, or AQP4 can be developed and administered to subjects with retinal disorders. These drugs can target specific pathways involved in the regulation of these proteins, such as inflammatory pathways or signaling cascades.

3. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a compound that modulates the expression or function of Dp71, Kir4.1, or AQP4, and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. These compositions can be formulated for various routes of administration, including intravitreal injection, oral administration, or topical application.

## EXAMPLE

### Example 1: Generation and Characterization of Dp71-Null Mice

**Materials and Methods**

**Ethics Statement**: All animal procedures were conducted in accordance with UPMC University and INSERM Institutional Animal Care Committee guidelines.

**Animals**: The Dp71-null mice were generated by replacing, via homologous recombination, most of the first and unique exon of Dp71 and a small part of the first intron with a sequence encoding a β-gal-neomycin resistance chimeric protein (β-geo). C57BL/6J mice were used as controls.

**Induction of Retinal Detachment**: Local retinal detachment was induced in the right eye of adult mice by subretinal injection of sodium hyaluronate. The left eye, sham-injected, served as a control.

**Immunohistochemistry**: Enucleated eyes were fixed, cryoprotected, and sectioned. Sections were permeabilized, blocked, and incubated with primary antibodies targeting Dp71, Kir4.1, AQP4, and β-dystroglycan. Secondary antibodies conjugated to Alexa fluor were used for visualization.

**Western Blot Analysis**: Retinal protein extracts were resolved using NuPAGE Tris-Acetate gels and transferred to PVDF membranes. Blots were incubated with primary antibodies and detected using horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies.

**Quantitative RT-PCR**: Total RNA was extracted from the retina, and reverse transcription was performed. PCR amplifications were performed using SYBR Green, and the Ct values were analyzed using the ΔCt method.

**Electrophysiological Recordings**: Membrane currents of acutely isolated glial cells were recorded using the patch-clamp technique. Voltage-clamp records were performed, and the amplitudes of the steady-state inward currents were measured.

**Müller Cell Swelling**: Acutely isolated retinal slices were loaded with Mitotracker Orange and examined using a confocal laser scanning microscope. The cross-sectional area of glial cell somata was measured to determine the extent of swelling.

**Quantification of Blood-Retinal Barrier Permeability**: Vascular permeability was quantified using the Evans blue method. Evans blue was injected into the jugular vein, and the dye was extracted from the retina and plasma for absorbance measurement.

**Results**

**Expression and Localization of Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4**: In control retinas, Dp71, Kir4.1, and AQP4 were strongly expressed and localized in the endfeet of Müller cells and around blood vessels. In Dp71-null mice, the expression of Dp71 was abolished, and the localization of Kir4.1 and AQP4 was altered. Retinal detachment further exacerbated these changes, leading to a significant decrease in the expression and mislocation of Kir4.1 and AQP4.

**Electrophysiological Properties**: Whole-cell patch-clamp recordings showed a significant decrease in inward current amplitudes in Müller cells from detached retinas of C57BL/6 mice. In Dp71-null mice, the decrease in inward current amplitudes was not significant, but the membrane capacitance was increased, indicating cellular hypertrophy.

**Müller Cell Swelling**: Hypotonic stress caused a significant increase in the size of Müller cell somata in retinal slices from detached retinas of C57BL/6 mice. In Dp71-null mice, Müller cell somata already displayed osmotic swelling in the absence of retinal detachment. The application of adenosine or inhibitors of phospholipase A2 and cyclooxygenase prevented the osmotic swelling of Müller cells from Dp71-null mice.

**Blood-Retina Barrier Permeability**: The permeability of the blood-retina barrier was significantly increased in Dp71-null mice compared to C57BL/6 mice. This increase in permeability was further exacerbated by retinal detachment.

**Conclusion**

The Dp71-null mouse model exhibits functional and morphological alterations in Müller cells that mimic the changes seen in retinal disorders such as retinal edema and diabetic retinopathy. This model is a valuable tool for studying the pathophysiology of these conditions and for developing novel therapeutic strategies.
