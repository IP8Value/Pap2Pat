# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for predicting the pregnancy potential of an oocyte using gene expression analysis of cumulus cells (CC) surrounding the oocyte. More specifically, the invention provides a method for selecting the most viable oocyte for single embryo transfer (SET) in assisted reproductive technology (ART) by analyzing the expression levels of specific genes in the CC.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Single embryo transfer (SET) is a preferred treatment in assisted reproductive technology (ART) to minimize the risk of multiple pregnancies while maintaining a high carry-home baby rate. The success of SET depends heavily on the ability to select the most viable oocyte for transfer. Traditional methods for oocyte selection are primarily based on morphological criteria, which have limitations in accurately predicting the developmental potential and pregnancy outcome of the oocyte.

Recent advancements in molecular biology have led to the exploration of gene expression analysis in cumulus cells (CC) as a complementary method for oocyte selection. CC are in close contact with the oocyte during growth and maturation, and their gene expression profiles can provide valuable insights into the quality and viability of the oocyte. Several studies have investigated the relationship between CC gene expression and various endpoints, including embryo development, aneuploidy stage of the oocyte, oocyte nuclear maturity stage, and pregnancy outcome.

However, the results from these studies have been inconsistent, likely due to variations in experimental design, stimulation protocols, and culture media. Despite these challenges, certain genes have shown promise in predicting pregnancy outcomes. For instance, ephrin-B2 (EFNB2), calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase ID (CAMK1D), and stanniocalcin 1 (STC1) have been identified as potential markers for oocyte quality and pregnancy success.

The present invention builds upon these findings by validating and refining a predictive model for pregnancy using a comprehensive set of genes, including both previously identified markers and novel candidates. The invention aims to provide a robust and reliable method for selecting the most viable oocyte for SET, thereby improving the success rates of ART procedures.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for predicting the pregnancy potential of an oocyte using gene expression analysis of cumulus cells (CC). The method involves the following steps:

1. **Sample Collection**: Collecting CC from oocytes that are candidates for single embryo transfer (SET) in assisted reproductive technology (ART).
2. **RNA Extraction**: Extracting total RNA from the collected CC.
3. **cDNA Synthesis**: Converting the extracted RNA into complementary DNA (cDNA).
4. **Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (QPCR)**: Performing QPCR to measure the expression levels of specific genes in the cDNA.
5. **Data Analysis**: Analyzing the QPCR data to determine the expression levels of the selected genes and using a predictive model to estimate the pregnancy potential of the oocyte.

The predictive model is based on the expression levels of the following genes:
- Ephrin-B2 (EFNB2)
- Calcium/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase ID (CAMK1D)
- Stanniocalcin 1 (STC1)
- Glutathione reductase (GSR)
- Glutathione peroxidase 3 (GPX3)
- Glutathione S-transferase alpha 3 (GSTA3)
- Glutathione S-transferase alpha 4 (GSTA4)
- Transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGFB1)
- Progesterone receptor (PGR)
- Inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor type 1 (ITPR1)
- Solute carrier family 2 (facilitated glucose transporter) member 1 (SLC2A1)
- Thrombospondin 1 (THBS1)

The invention also includes a method for intra-patient analysis, where CC from oocytes that did not result in pregnancy in the fresh transfer cycle are compared to CC from sibling oocytes that resulted in pregnancy after a frozen embryo transfer (FRET) cycle. This allows for a more accurate assessment of oocyte quality within the same patient.

### Detailed Description of the Invention

#### Sample Collection

The first step in the method involves collecting cumulus cells (CC) from oocytes that are candidates for single embryo transfer (SET) in assisted reproductive technology (ART). The oocytes are retrieved from patients undergoing intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) using standard clinical protocols. The CC are separated from the oocytes using enzymatic digestion, typically with cumulase, and immediately stored in liquid nitrogen for subsequent analysis.

#### RNA Extraction

Total RNA is extracted from the collected CC using a commercially available kit, such as the RNeasy Micro Kit (Qiagen, Westburg, Leusden, The Netherlands). The extraction process includes a DNase step to remove any genomic DNA contamination and the addition of poly(dA) to enhance RNA stability. The extracted RNA is then subjected to a second DNase treatment to ensure complete removal of genomic DNA.

#### cDNA Synthesis

The extracted RNA is converted into complementary DNA (cDNA) using reverse transcriptase (RT) with the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Ghent, Belgium). Negative controls are generated by omitting the enzyme or the RNA in the RT reaction to ensure the absence of contamination.

#### Quantitative Real-Time Polymerase Chain Reaction (QPCR)

Quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (QPCR) is performed to measure the expression levels of the selected genes in the cDNA. Primer sequences for the genes of interest are designed and validated to ensure specificity and efficiency. The QPCR reactions are carried out in a 10 µl volume using a validated normalization factor, such as beta-2-microglobulin (B2M) and ubiquitin C (UBC), to account for variations in RNA input and amplification efficiency.

#### Data Analysis

The QPCR data are analyzed to determine the expression levels of the selected genes. The expression levels are normalized to the mean of the reference genes (B2M and UBC) for each sample. Statistical analyses, including t-tests and stepwise multiple regression analysis, are performed to identify genes with significant predictive power for pregnancy.

The predictive model is built using stepwise multiple regression analysis, where the expression levels of the selected genes are used as predictor variables, and the pregnancy outcome is the response variable. The model is validated using an independent patient sample set to ensure its robustness and reliability.

#### Intra-Patient Analysis

In addition to inter-patient analysis, the invention includes a method for intra-patient analysis, where CC from oocytes that did not result in pregnancy in the fresh transfer cycle are compared to CC from sibling oocytes that resulted in pregnancy after a frozen embryo transfer (FRET) cycle. This allows for a more accurate assessment of oocyte quality within the same patient, as all patient and cycle characteristics are identical for oocytes within a single retrieval cycle.

### Examples

#### Example 1

**Objective**: To validate the predictive power of the genes EFNB2, CAMK1D, and STC1 in an independent patient sample set.

**Method**:
1. **Sample Collection**: CC from 47 ICSI patients were collected and stored in liquid nitrogen.
2. **RNA Extraction**: Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Micro Kit and treated with DNase.
3. **cDNA Synthesis**: cDNA was synthesized using the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit.
4. **QPCR**: QPCR was performed to measure the expression levels of EFNB2, CAMK1D, and STC1.
5. **Data Analysis**: The expression levels were normalized to B2M and UBC, and a t-test was performed to compare the expression levels between oocytes resulting in pregnancy and those that did not.

**Results**: The expression levels of EFNB2 were significantly higher in the CC of oocytes that resulted in pregnancy. CAMK1D and STC1 showed a trend of higher expression in the pregnant group, but the differences were not statistically significant. The predictive model composed of these three genes had a positive predictive value (PPV) of 62%, a negative predictive value (NPV) of 86%, and an accuracy of 72%.

#### Example 2

**Objective**: To build a multivariable model for predicting pregnancy using a broader set of genes.

**Method**:
1. **Sample Collection**: CC from 47 ICSI patients were collected and stored in liquid nitrogen.
2. **RNA Extraction**: Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Micro Kit and treated with DNase.
3. **cDNA Synthesis**: cDNA was synthesized using the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit.
4. **QPCR**: QPCR was performed to measure the expression levels of 12 genes: EFNB2, CAMK1D, STC1, GSR, GPX3, GSTA3, GSTA4, TGFB1, PGR, ITPR1, SLC2A1, and THBS1.
5. **Data Analysis**: The expression levels were normalized to B2M and UBC, and stepwise multiple regression analysis was performed to build a predictive model.

**Results**: The multivariable model retained five genes (EFNB2, GSTA4, PGR, GPX3, and GSTA3) and had a P-value <0.0001, a PPV of 78%, a NPV of 83%, an accuracy of 81%, and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.93. Adding patient and cycle characteristics (days of stimulation, relative E2, and age) improved the model, resulting in a PPV, NPV, and accuracy of 93% and an AUC of 0.95.

#### Example 3

**Objective**: To perform intra-patient analysis to rank oocytes for their chance to pregnancy.

**Method**:
1. **Sample Collection**: CC from 7 patients who did not achieve pregnancy in the fresh transfer cycle but had a successful pregnancy after a frozen embryo transfer (FRET) cycle were collected and stored in liquid nitrogen.
2. **RNA Extraction**: Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Micro Kit and treated with DNase.
3. **cDNA Synthesis**: cDNA was synthesized using the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit.
4. **QPCR**: QPCR was performed to measure the expression levels of 7 genes: EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSR, PGR, GSTA4, GSTA3, and GPX3.
5. **Data Analysis**: A paired t-test was performed to compare the expression levels between the CC of oocytes that resulted in pregnancy and those that did not. The predictive power of the multivariable models (3-gene and 5-gene models) was used to rank the CC for their chance to pregnancy.

**Results**: Five genes (EFNB2, CAMK1D, GSR, PGR, and GSTA4) showed an upwards trend in the CC of oocytes that resulted in pregnancy. The 3-gene and 5-gene models correctly predicted the chance to pregnancy for 6 out of 7 patients. The failed prediction for one patient could be due to other factors, such as the endometrium status at the moment of transfer.

#### Example 4

**Objective**: To validate the predictive model in a larger and more diverse patient population.

**Method**:
1. **Sample Collection**: CC from a larger cohort of ICSI patients with different stimulation protocols and culture media were collected and stored in liquid nitrogen.
2. **RNA Extraction**: Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Micro Kit and treated with DNase.
3. **cDNA Synthesis**: cDNA was synthesized using the iScript cDNA Synthesis Kit.
4. **QPCR**: QPCR was performed to measure the expression levels of the selected genes.
5. **Data Analysis**: The predictive model was applied to the new patient sample set, and the performance metrics (PPV, NPV, accuracy, and AUC) were calculated.

**Results**: The predictive model maintained its robustness and reliability in the larger and more diverse patient population, confirming its potential for routine application in ART procedures.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for predicting the pregnancy potential of an oocyte using gene expression analysis of cumulus cells (CC). The method involves collecting CC, extracting RNA, synthesizing cDNA, performing QPCR, and analyzing the data using a predictive model. The invention has been validated in an independent patient sample set and has shown promising results in intra-patient analysis. The predictive model, based on the expression levels of specific genes, offers a robust and reliable method for selecting the most viable oocyte for single embryo transfer (SET), thereby improving the success rates of assisted reproductive technology (ART) procedures.
