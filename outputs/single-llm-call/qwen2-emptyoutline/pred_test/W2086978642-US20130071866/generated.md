# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method and system for evaluating the severity of illness in critically ill patients admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU). More specifically, the invention pertains to a novel biomarker, the ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES), which combines blood adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels and serum lactate levels to provide a real-time and reliable prognostic indicator of critical illness.

## BACKGROUND ART

The evaluation of the severity of illness in critically ill patients is crucial for effective management and prognosis in the intensive care unit (ICU). Traditional scoring systems such as the Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation (APACHE) II score, the Simplified Acute Physiology Score (SAPS) II, and others have been widely used but suffer from several limitations. These include time-consuming evaluation processes, the unavailability of data from automatic equipment, and inter-examiner discrepancies. Consequently, these scores are not always utilized in the daily practice of many ICUs.

Adenosine 5′-triphosphate (ATP) is a key molecule in cellular bioenergetics, serving as the "energy currency" of organisms. Its levels are indicative of cell viability, proliferation, and energy transmission. ATP release from damaged cells and tissues has been recognized as an alarm signal, or alarmin, which can trigger immune responses. However, the rapid degradation of ATP in serum makes it challenging to measure accurately. Therefore, monitoring ATP levels in blood cells, which represent the net value of intracellular ATP production and degradation, is more feasible.

Hyperlactatemia, characterized by elevated serum lactate levels, is a common condition in critically ill patients and is associated with increased organ failure, metabolic dysfunction, and mortality. The combination of blood ATP levels and serum lactate levels has the potential to serve as a more reliable and real-time biomarker for the progression of critical illness.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Solved by the Invention

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a method and system for evaluating the severity of illness in critically ill patients admitted to the ICU. The invention aims to overcome the limitations of existing scoring systems by introducing a novel biomarker, the ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES), which combines blood ATP levels and serum lactate levels. This biomarker is designed to offer a real-time and reliable prognostic indicator of critical illness, thereby improving patient management and outcomes.

### Means to Solve the Object

The present invention achieves the aforementioned object through the following means:

1. **Measurement of Blood ATP Levels**: The invention utilizes a novel phenol-based extraction method to measure blood ATP levels accurately. This method overcomes the limitations of traditional chaotropic extraction reagents, which often result in co-precipitation of ATP with insoluble proteins.

2. **Calculation of A-LES**: The ATP-lactate energy risk score (A-LES) is calculated as the ratio of serum lactate (mM) to blood ATP (mM). This score provides a comprehensive and real-time assessment of the patient's energy status and metabolic function.

3. **Comparison with APACHE II Score**: The A-LES is compared with the APACHE II score to evaluate its effectiveness as a prognostic marker. The invention demonstrates that A-LES is as useful as the APACHE II score for predicting mortality and provides additional insights into the change in illness severity during ICU stay.

### Effect of the Invention

The present invention offers several significant advantages over existing methods for evaluating the severity of illness in critically ill patients:

1. **Real-Time Monitoring**: The A-LES can be measured quickly and easily, providing real-time information about the patient's condition. This allows for timely interventions and improved patient management.

2. **Reliability**: The A-LES is a reliable biomarker that correlates well with the severity of illness and patient outcomes. It is less prone to inter-examiner discrepancies and data availability issues compared to traditional scoring systems.

3. **Comprehensive Assessment**: The A-LES provides a comprehensive assessment of the patient's energy status and metabolic function, which is crucial for understanding the progression of critical illness.

4. **Predictive Value**: The A-LES has a high predictive value for mortality and can be used to stratify patients into different risk categories, aiding in resource allocation and treatment planning.

## MODE OF CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### Measurement of Blood ATP Levels

1. **Sample Collection**: Blood samples are collected from the antecubital vein of healthy individuals and ICU patients into Vacutainer tubes or syringes containing ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) or sodium heparin. For ICU patients, blood samples are usually collected from the arterial line into EDTA vacutainers.

2. **Extraction of ATP**: The blood sample is mixed with a phenol-based extraction reagent (Tris-EDTA-saturated phenol, phenol-TE) and thoroughly shaken for 20 seconds. The mixture is then stored at -20°C until use.

3. **Measurement of ATP**: The extracted blood sample is centrifuged to achieve phase separation. The upper aqueous phase is diluted and injected into a luciferin/luciferase mixture. The bioluminescence product is measured using a luminometer, and the blood ATP level is calculated from a calibration curve.

### Calculation of A-LES

1. **Measurement of Serum Lactate**: Serum lactate levels are measured using a blood gas analyzer.

2. **Calculation of A-LES**: The A-LES is calculated as the ratio of serum lactate (mM) to blood ATP (mM).

### Comparison with APACHE II Score

1. **Evaluation of Severity**: The severity of illness is evaluated using the APACHE II score within the first 24 hours of ICU admission and every 24 hours thereafter.

2. **Statistical Analysis**: The A-LES and APACHE II scores are compared using nonparametric Mann-Whitney’s U test and Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves are constructed to evaluate the accuracy of risk prediction.

### Clinical Application

1. **Patient Stratification**: The A-LES is used to stratify patients into different risk categories based on their A-LES values. Patients with A-LES values <20 are considered to have a favorable prognosis, while those with A-LES values ≥20 are at higher risk of mortality.

2. **Monitoring and Intervention**: The A-LES is monitored regularly during ICU stay to track changes in the patient's condition. Timely interventions are made based on the A-LES values to improve patient outcomes.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Objective**: To establish the normal range of blood ATP levels in healthy individuals.

**Method**: Blood samples were collected from 155 healthy individuals ranging in age from 0 to 92 years. Blood ATP levels were measured using the phenol-based extraction method.

**Results**: The mean blood ATP level (SD) in healthy subjects was 0.62 (0.19) mM. There was no significant sex difference in ATP levels. The ATP levels tended to be slightly lower in subjects aged ≥60 years, but the difference was not significant.

### Example 2

**Objective**: To compare ATP and lactate levels in arterial and central venous blood.

**Method**: Blood samples were collected from 7 representative ICU patients. ATP and lactate levels were measured in both arterial and central venous blood.

**Results**: Regression analysis showed almost perfect correlation between ATP, lactate, and A-LES levels in arterial and central venous blood, with high correlation coefficients (rs = 1.00 for ATP, rs = 0.98 for lactate, and rs = 1.00 for A-LES).

### Example 3

**Objective**: To evaluate the A-LES as a prognostic marker in ICU patients.

**Method**: Blood ATP and serum lactate levels were measured in 42 ICU patients. The A-LES was calculated and compared with the APACHE II score.

**Results**: The A-LES was significantly higher in non-survivors than in survivors. ROC analysis showed that the A-LES was as useful as the APACHE II score for predicting mortality. The A-LES also provided a sensitive evaluation score of changes in illness severity during ICU stay.
