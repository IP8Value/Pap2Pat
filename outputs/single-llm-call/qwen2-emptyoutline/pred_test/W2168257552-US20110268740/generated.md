# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of immunology and virology, specifically to the isolation and characterization of natural human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) directed against the extracellular domain (M2e) of the influenza A virus matrix protein 2 (M2). These antibodies exhibit high affinity binding to M2e and demonstrate potent prophylactic and therapeutic activities against influenza A virus infection in a mouse model. The invention further encompasses methods for producing these antibodies and their use as immunotherapeutics for the prevention and treatment of influenza A virus infections.

## RELATED ART

Influenza A virus remains a significant global health threat, causing millions of severe illnesses and hundreds of thousands of deaths annually. Current influenza vaccines primarily induce antibodies against the hemagglutinin (HA) and neuraminidase (NA) proteins, which are subject to frequent antigenic drift and shift, necessitating annual updates. Passive immunization with monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) targeting HA is effective but suffers from the same limitations as vaccines due to antigenic variability. The matrix protein 2 (M2), particularly its extracellular domain (M2e), has emerged as a promising target for both active and passive immunization strategies due to its high conservation across different influenza A virus strains. However, natural human M2e-specific antibodies have not been reported until now. The development of such antibodies offers a significant advantage in terms of minimal immunogenicity, reduced off-target reactivity, and high affinity, making them ideal candidates for therapeutic applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides natural human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) specific for the extracellular domain (M2e) of the influenza A virus matrix protein 2 (M2). These antibodies exhibit high affinity binding to M2e and demonstrate potent prophylactic and therapeutic activities against influenza A virus infection in a mouse model. The invention further encompasses methods for isolating these antibodies using mammalian cell display technology and their use as immunotherapeutics for the prevention and treatment of influenza A virus infections.

### Detailed Description of the Invention

#### Isolation of M2e-Specific Human Monoclonal Antibodies

The invention involves the isolation of natural human monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) specific for the extracellular domain (M2e) of the influenza A virus matrix protein 2 (M2) using a novel method based on mammalian cell display. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from an individual with high M2e titers were isolated and screened for M2e-specific B cells using the M2e consensus peptide (M2e-cons) conjugated to the virus-like particle Qβ as a bait. The immunoglobulin variable regions (VRs) of the heavy chains (HCs) and light chains (LCs) of the isolated B cells were amplified by RT-PCR and assembled into single-chain antibody (scFv) coding regions. Two separate libraries, one encoding scFv antibodies with κ light chains and the other with λ light chains, were cloned into a Sindbis cell surface display vector. The resulting scFv-κ and scFv-λ sublibraries were used to infect BHK cells, and M2e-specific scFv antibodies were isolated by fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS). The scFv antibodies were then fused to the Fc region of mouse IgG2c (msFc-γ2c) to allow in vivo efficacy testing in a mouse model of influenza A infection.

#### In Vitro Analysis of M2e-Specific Monoclonal Antibodies

The affinity of the isolated M2e-specific antibodies (D005, E040, and F052) for M2e was determined using an ELISA-based method. The antibodies were found to bind M2e-cons peptide with high affinity, with dissociation constants (Kd) of 4-5 nM. The ability of the antibodies to cross-react with a peptide covering M2e from a recent Influenza A H5N1 isolate (A/VN/1203/2004) was also assessed by ELISA, and all antibodies bound both peptides to a similar extent, with EC50 values of 5-10 ng/ml (45-90 pM). Binding of the antibodies to native, cell surface M2 was confirmed using a clone of L929 cells expressing full-length M2 derived from Influenza A/PR/8/34 (H1N1).

#### Prophylactic Activity of M2e-Specific Antibodies Against Influenza Virus Infection

The protective activity of the M2e-specific antibodies was evaluated in an in vivo mouse model of influenza A infection. Groups of mice were passively immunized intraperitoneally (i.p.) with 500 μg of scFv-msFc-γ2c antibodies D005, E040, or F052, while a control group received the same amount of mouse IgG. Two days later, the animals were infected intranasally with a lethal dose (4 × LD50) of mouse-adapted (m.a.) influenza A/PR/8/34 and monitored for morbidity and mortality. Control mice treated with mouse IgG exhibited a dramatic drop in body temperature and substantial weight loss within a few days after infection and had to be euthanized on days 7 or 8 due to the severity of the symptoms. In contrast, all animals treated with M2-specific antibodies survived the lethal challenge, hardly developed any fever, and only displayed a transient weight loss.

#### Titration of M2e-Specific Antibody D005

To determine the amount of antibody required to achieve protection in a prophylactic setting, groups of mice were treated with decreasing amounts of scFv-msFc-γ2c D005 or, as a control, with 200 μg of mouse IgG. Two days later, the animals were infected with a lethal dose of m.a. influenza A/PR/8/34 and monitored for 21 days. Control mice quickly succumbed to the infection and all had to be euthanized due to the severity of the disease within less than two weeks after challenge. In contrast, all animals that had received at least 20 μg of D005 antibody survived the challenge. Even at the lowest dose of 6 μg D005, half of the mice recovered and survived infection, indicating that the M2-specific antibody is a potent prophylactic agent.

#### Therapeutic Activity of M2e-Specific Antibody D005

The therapeutic activity of M2e-specific antibody D005 was tested in a mouse model of influenza A infection. Groups of mice were infected with a lethal dose of m.a. influenza A/PR/8/34 and treated with a single injection of 200 μg scFv-msFc-γ2c D005 1, 2, or 3 days after challenge. Animals treated with 200 μg D005 or mouse IgG two days prior to the infection served as positive and negative controls, respectively. All mice treated with the control antibody succumbed to the disease, whereas all mice that had received the D005 antibody prophylactically survived the lethal challenge. Importantly, almost all animals which had been treated therapeutically within two days after infection survived the challenge. Even animals that had been treated 3 days post-infection appeared to withstand the infection longer than the control group.

#### Prophylactic Activity of Fully Human IgG1κ-D005

To evaluate the potential of the M2e-specific antibody for human use, D005 was produced as a fully human IgG1κ in 293T cells using an EBV-based episomal expression system. The protective activity of IgG1κ-D005 was compared to its scFv-msFc-γ2c counterpart in the influenza A mouse model. Groups of mice were treated with equimolar amounts of IgG1κ-D005, scFv-D005-msFc-γ2c, or human IgG. Two days later, the animals were infected with a lethal dose of influenza A/PR/8/34 and closely monitored for 16 days. Mice treated with control human IgG displayed a dramatic drop in body temperature and weight loss and had to be euthanized between 8 and 11 days after infection due to the severity of the symptoms. In contrast, all animals treated with M2-specific antibodies survived the lethal challenge, hardly experienced any fever, and showed only a transient drop in body weight. Thus, D005 was protective both as a scFv-msFc-γ2c fusion protein and as a fully human IgG1κ, indicating that the human antibody was able to recruit effector functions in the mouse system and that IgG1κ-D005 may be used as a valuable prophylactic or therapeutic agent in humans.

### EXAMPLE 1

**Isolation of M2e-Specific B Cells**

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were isolated from 10 ml of heparinized blood by Ficoll gradient. PBMCs were pre-incubated with Alexa 647 nm-labeled Qβ and human gamma globulin and then stained with M2e coupled to Qβ in combination with a Qβ-specific mouse mAb, as well as the M2-specific mouse mAb 14C2. Additional markers for B cell identification were used, including PE-labeled mouse anti-human IgM, mouse anti-human IgD, mouse anti-human CD14, and mouse anti-human CD3 antibodies, and PE-TexasRed-labeled mouse anti-human CD19 antibody. After staining, cells were washed and filtered, and 334 M2-specific B cells were sorted on a FACSVantage® SE flow cytometer.

### EXAMPLE 2

**Production of Sindbis-Based scFv Library**

A Sindbis virus-based scFv cell surface display library was produced from the antigen-specific B cells. BHK cells were infected with the Sindbis library, and cells displaying M2-specific scFv antibodies were isolated using M2e coupled to RNase A in combination with an RNase-specific rabbit polyclonal antibody and a FITC-labeled donkey anti-rabbit IgG antibody. Alternatively, cells displaying M2-specific scFv antibodies were isolated using Qβ-M2e in combination with M2-specific mouse mAb 14C2 and a FITC-labeled donkey anti-mouse IgG antibody. Each cell was sorted into a well of a 24-well plate containing 50% confluent BHK feeder cells. Upon virus spread, the infected cells were tested by FACS analysis for M2-binding to identify virus clones encoding M2-specific scFv antibodies.

### EXAMPLE 3

**Expression and Purification of scFv-msFc-γ2c Fusion Proteins**

Fusion proteins were generated carrying an N-terminal human scFv fused to a C-terminal mouse Fc-γ2c domain. scFv coding regions were PCR amplified from Sindbis virus-containing supernatants by RT-PCR, digested with the restriction endonuclease Sfi1, and cloned into the expression vector pCEP-SP-Sfi-msFc-γ2c. This vector drives expression of scFv-msFc-γ2c fusion proteins under the control of a CMV promoter. Expression of the scFv-msFc-γ2c fusion proteins was done by transfecting the expression vectors into HEK-293T cells using Lipofectamine Plus. For large-scale production and purification, stable protein-expressing cells were enriched by selection in the presence of 1 μg/ml puromycin. Pools of resistant cells were maintained in serum-free medium on Poly-L-Lysine coated dishes or in roller bottles for up to 3 weeks. Supernatants containing the respective antibodies were collected twice a week and filtered through a 0.22 μM Millex GV sterile filter. Both types of antibodies were purified by affinity chromatography over a protein A-Sepharose column.

### EXAMPLE 4

**ELISA Analysis of Antibody Binding to M2e**

ELISA plates were coated with RNAse-M2e-cons or RNAse-M2e-VN at a concentration of 4 μg/ml in coating buffer for one hour at 37°C. The plates were then washed with wash buffer and blocked for 2 h at 37°C with 3% BSA in wash buffer. The plates were then washed again and incubated with 3-fold serial dilutions of scFv-msFc-γ2c in wash buffer containing 1% BSA. Plates were incubated for 2 h at room temperature and then extensively washed with wash buffer. Specifically bound antibodies were detected with HRPO-labeled, Fcγ-specific, goat anti-mouse IgG antibody. After extensive washing with wash buffer, plates were developed with a 0.4 mg/ml solution of 1, 2-ortho-phenylenediamine dihydrochloride (OPD) in citric acid buffer containing 0.01% H2O2. After 10 min, the reaction was stopped with a 5% solution of H2SO4 in H2O, and plates were read at 450 nm on an ELISA reader.

### EXAMPLE 5

**Affinity Measurement by Friguet ELISA**

A 10 ng/ml solution of scFv-D005-msFc-γ2c, scFv-E040-msFc-γ2c, or scFv-F052-msFc-γ2c was incubated in the presence of different concentrations of M2e-cons peptide (3-fold serial dilutions corresponding to 10 nM to 0.17 pM) in PBS/1% BSA. After 2 h at room temperature, free antibody was detected by a classical ELISA. ELISA plates that had been coated with RNAse-M2e-cons conjugate at a concentration of 20 ng/ml at 4°C overnight were washed with wash buffer and blocked for 2 h at 37°C with 3% BSA in wash buffer. The plates were then washed again and incubated with the solution binding reactions for 30 min at room temperature. After extensive washing with wash buffer, bound scFv-Fcγ2c fusion proteins were detected by a 1 h incubation at room temperature with a HRPO-labeled, Fcγ-specific, goat anti-mouse IgG antibody. After extensive washing with wash buffer, plates were developed as described above. The Kd values were determined as the EC50 of the ELISA signal as a function of the M2e-cons peptide concentration present in the solution binding reaction.

### EXAMPLE 6

**Mouse Model of Influenza A Infection**

Prophylactic and therapeutic activity of antibodies was tested in a mouse model of Influenza A infection. Six-week-old female C57BL/6 mice (6 per group) were infected intranasally with a lethal dose of mouse-adapted (m.a.) influenza A virus PR8 (4 × LD50), followed by close monitoring of weight loss and fever (twice daily at peak of infection). Antibodies were injected intraperitoneally either 2 days before (prophylactic setting) or 1 to 3 days after infection (therapeutic setting). One day later, mice were bled to verify the presence of the antibodies in the blood by ELISA. Antibodies were readily detectable in the sera of all mice, except for one mouse receiving 500 μg scFv-D005-msFc-γ2c and one mouse receiving 60 μg scFv-D005-msFc-γ2c; these mice were subsequently removed from the analysis. All animal experiments were carried out in accordance with protocols approved by the Swiss Federal Veterinary Office. Animals whose body temperature reached 30°C were considered moribund and had to be euthanized immediately due to abortion criteria defined by the Veterinary Office.
