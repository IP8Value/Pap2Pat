# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

The field of organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) has seen significant advancements, particularly in the development of microcavity structures to enhance the performance of these devices. Microcavity OLEDs are designed to manipulate the spectral characteristics of the emitted light, leading to saturated colors and an expanded color gamut. These features make microcavity OLEDs highly desirable for use in active matrix OLED (AMOLED) displays. Additionally, microcavity structures have been shown to improve light extraction efficiency by modifying the light distribution within the device. However, the effectiveness of these enhancements varies across different colors, with green microcavity OLEDs demonstrating significant improvements in current efficiency, while blue microcavity devices have shown limited gains.

This invention addresses the challenges associated with achieving consistent and significant enhancements in the performance of microcavity OLEDs across different colors. Specifically, it focuses on optimizing the reflectivity of the semitransparent electrode and the cavity length to maximize the photon outcoupling efficiency and photopic luminosity. By carefully tuning these parameters, the invention aims to achieve high current efficiencies and quantum efficiencies in red, green, and blue phosphorescent OLEDs.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method for enhancing the performance of microcavity organic light-emitting diodes (OLEDs) by optimizing the reflectivity of the semitransparent electrode and the cavity length. The invention provides a systematic approach to improving the light outcoupling efficiency and luminance output of red, green, and blue phosphorescent OLEDs. Key aspects of the invention include the use of dielectric distributed Bragg reflectors (DBRs) with high reflectivity and low absorption loss, and the precise tuning of the cavity length to align with the peak reflectivity of the DBR substrates. This optimization leads to significant enhancements in the current efficiency, quantum efficiency, and photopic luminosity of the OLEDs, resulting in improved overall performance and color quality.

## DETAILED DISCLOSURE

### METHODS AND MATERIALS

The invention involves the fabrication of microcavity OLEDs using dielectric distributed Bragg reflectors (DBRs) and the optimization of the cavity length to achieve enhanced performance. The DBRs are composed of alternating layers of titanium dioxide (TiO₂) and silicon dioxide (SiO₂), with the reflectance maxima tuned to specific wavelengths for red, green, and blue emitting devices. The fabrication process includes the following steps:

1. **Substrate Preparation**: Indium tin oxide (ITO)-coated DBR substrates are prepared, with the reflectance maxima of the DBRs tuned to 610 nm for red, 550 nm for green, and 474 nm for blue emitting devices. The substrates undergo ultraviolet ozone treatment for 15 minutes to enhance surface properties.

2. **Hole Injection Layer Deposition**: A 25-nm-thick poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene)-polystyrenesulfonic acid (PEDOT:PSS) layer is spin-coated onto the ITO substrates and baked at 180°C for 15 minutes in air to form the hole injection layer.

3. **Device Fabrication**: The OLED devices are fabricated by sequentially depositing the following layers via thermal evaporation:
   - A 20-to 45-nm-thick 1,1-bis[(di-4-tolylamino)phenyl]cyclohexane (TAPC) layer as a hole transport layer.
   - A 20-to 30-nm-thick 4,4'-N,N'-dicarbazole-biphenyl (CBP) doped with 7 wt% tris(2-phenylpyridine)iridium (Ir(ppy)₃) as a green emitter.
   - A 15-nm-thick 3,5'-N,N'-dicarbazolebenzene (mCP) doped with 15 wt% iridium(III)bis[2-methyldibenzo-(f,h)quinoxaline](acetylacetonate) (Ir(MDQ)₂(acac)) and a 15-nm-thick 2,2',2''-(1,3,5-benzenetriyl)tris(1-phenyl-1H-benzimidazole) (TPBi) doped with 15 wt% Ir(MDQ)₂(acac) as a red emitter.
   - A 20-nm-thick mCP doped with 5 wt% iridium(III) bis[(4,6-difluorophenyl)pyridinato-N,C²⁹] picolinate (FIrpic) as a blue emitter.
   - A 45-to 60-nm-thick tris[3-(3-pyridyl)-mesityl]borane (3TPYMB) as an electron transporting layer for green and red emitting devices.
   - A 45-to 55-nm-thick 3,5,3',5'-tetra-3-pyridyl-[1,1':3',1''-terphenyl] (B3PyPB) as an electron transporting layer for blue emitting devices.
   - A 1-nm-thick lithium fluoride (LiF) layer as an electron injection layer.
   - A 100-nm-thick aluminum (Al) layer as the cathode.

4. **Light Extraction Enhancement**: To extract the substrate mode, a macrolens is attached to the substrate using an index-matching gel.

5. **Characterization**: The current-luminance-voltage characteristics are measured using a Keithley Series 2400 source meter and a Keithley Series 6485 picoammeter with a calibrated Newport silicon photodiode. The luminance is calibrated using a Konica Minolta luminance meter (LS-100). The electroluminescence spectra are obtained using an Ocean Optics HR4000 spectrometer.

### Optimization of Reflectivity and Cavity Length

#### Reflectivity Optimization

The reflectivity of the semitransparent electrode plays a crucial role in the performance of microcavity OLEDs. High reflectivity and low absorption loss are essential for enhancing the light output. The use of dielectric DBRs, such as SiO₂/TiO₂ multilayers, provides a significant advantage over metallic electrodes due to their high reflectivity and low absorption. The peak reflectivities of the DBR substrates for blue, green, and red devices are tuned to 474 nm, 550 nm, and 610 nm, respectively, with reflectivities of 62%, 75%, and 65%. The calculated enhancement ratios for blue, green, and red devices are 0.581, 1.00, and 0.685, normalized by the green enhancement.

#### Cavity Length Tuning

The cavity length is another critical parameter that affects the performance of microcavity OLEDs. By tuning the thickness of the organic layers, the cavity length can be adjusted to optimize the resonant wavelength and enhance the light outcoupling efficiency. For green emitting devices, the thicknesses of the TAPC and emitting layers are varied to achieve different cavity lengths. The devices are labeled as G1, G2, and G3, with the following layer thicknesses:
- **G1**: 35-nm TAPC, 30-nm CBP:Ir(ppy)₃
- **G2**: 30-nm TAPC, 25-nm CBP:Ir(ppy)₃
- **G3**: 30-nm TAPC, 20-nm CBP:Ir(ppy)₃

The EL spectra of these devices show significant enhancements in intensity and narrow full widths at half maximum (FWHM) due to the strong microcavity effects. The peak wavelength of the DBR devices decreases from 572 nm to 528 nm with decreasing cavity length. The G2 DBR device, with a resonant wavelength of 550 nm, exhibits the highest EL intensity and current efficiency.

For red emitting devices, the thickness of the 3TPYMB layer is varied to adjust the cavity length. The devices are labeled as R1, R2, and R3, with the following layer thicknesses:
- **R1**: 52-nm 3TPYMB
- **R2**: 55-nm 3TPYMB
- **R3**: 61-nm 3TPYMB

The R2 DBR device, with a resonant wavelength of 612 nm, shows the highest enhancement in EL intensity and current efficiency. The enhancement ratios for the integrated EL intensity and current efficiency are 66.8% and 89%, respectively.

For blue emitting devices, the thickness of the B3PyPB layer is varied to adjust the cavity length. The devices are labeled as B1, B2, and B3, with the following layer thicknesses:
- **B1**: 45-nm B3PyPB
- **B2**: 49-nm B3PyPB
- **B3**: 53-nm B3PyPB

The B1 DBR device, with a resonant wavelength of 470 nm, shows the highest enhancement in EL intensity. However, the B3 DBR device is the only device that shows an enhancement in current efficiency, despite the decrease in luminosity.

### Double Emitting Layer (DEL) Structure

To further enhance the device efficiency, the invention demonstrates the use of a double emitting layer (DEL) structure. The DEL structure confines the emitting zone within the center of the emitting layer, preventing exciton quenching and facilitating carrier injection. The resonant peak wavelength of the DBR device is tuned to 555 nm to maximize the luminance enhancement. The device structure includes a TCTA host material with a hole mobility of 3 × 10⁻⁴ cm²/V·s and a low electron mobility of 10⁻⁸ cm²/V·s, and a CBP host material with high electron and hole mobilities of 3 × 10⁻⁴ and 2 × 10⁻³ cm²/V·s, respectively.

The current density-voltage characteristics of the single and double emitting layer devices with and without DBR show that the DEL devices have a significantly higher current density. The current efficiency of the DEL OLEDs with DBR reaches a maximum of 224 cd/A, corresponding to an external quantum efficiency of 27%. The angle dependence of the emitting light for the non-cavity device and the DBR device with and without a macrolens shows a strong forward emission distribution, with an additional 89% enhancement in intensity when using a macrolens. The total enhancement factor of 2.4 (51.2831.89) promises an external quantum efficiency of 50%.

### Conclusion

The invention provides a comprehensive method for enhancing the performance of microcavity OLEDs by optimizing the reflectivity of the semitransparent electrode and the cavity length. The use of dielectric DBRs and the precise tuning of the cavity length lead to significant improvements in the current efficiency, quantum efficiency, and photopic luminosity of red, green, and blue phosphorescent OLEDs. The double emitting layer (DEL) structure further enhances the device efficiency, achieving the highest reported current efficiency for single emitting unit devices without light extraction. This invention offers a robust solution for improving the performance and color quality of OLEDs, making them more suitable for advanced display applications.
