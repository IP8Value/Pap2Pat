# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel vaccine vector system based on the Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV) derived from the Copenhagen strain of Vaccinia virus (VACV). Specifically, the invention provides a recombinant SCV (rSCV) vaccine capable of inducing immune responses against multiple pathogens, including Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), Zika virus (ZIKV), and VACV itself. The vaccine is designed to be administered to non-human primates (NHPs) and humans, offering protection against these pathogens with a single or double vaccination regimen.

## BACKGROUND

Poxvirus-based vaccine vector systems have gained significant attention due to their numerous advantages, including the ability to accommodate large recombinant immunogen payloads, cold chain-independent distribution, lack of DNA integration, and potential for needle-free vaccine delivery. Various poxvirus vectors, such as NYVAC, ALVAC, fowlpox, and Modified Vaccinia Ankara (MVA), have been developed and evaluated in both preclinical and clinical settings. While these vectors have shown promise, they often face limitations in inducing robust immune responses in naive individuals, necessitating heterologous prime-boost strategies.

The Sementis Copenhagen Vector (SCV) represents a significant advancement in vaccine vector technology. SCV is a replication-defective VACV strain, engineered by deleting the D13L gene, which encodes an essential viral assembly protein. This deletion ensures that SCV cannot produce infectious viral progeny in vaccine recipients, enhancing its safety profile. Recombinant SCV vaccines are produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells, which offer several advantages over traditional cell lines like primary chicken embryo fibroblasts (CEFs) used for VACV-based vaccines.

Previous studies have demonstrated the immunogenicity and protective efficacy of SCV-based vaccines in various animal models. For instance, a single-construct, multi-pathogen SCV vaccine encoding the structural gene cassettes of CHIKV and ZIKV (SCV-ZIKA/CHIK) has shown promising results in mouse challenge models. Additionally, SCV vaccines have been shown to protect against ectromelia virus and are non-pathogenic in SCID mice, further validating their safety and efficacy.

Given the ongoing global health challenges posed by CHIKV and ZIKV, the development of a single-construct, multi-pathogen vaccine is highly desirable. Such a vaccine would simplify manufacturing and formulation processes, reduce the "shot burden," and lower costs, thereby improving compliance and accessibility.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a recombinant Sementis Copenhagen Vector (rSCV) vaccine capable of inducing immune responses against multiple pathogens, specifically Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), Zika virus (ZIKV), and Vaccinia virus (VACV). The vaccine is designed to be administered to non-human primates (NHPs) and humans, offering protection against these pathogens with a single or double vaccination regimen.

The rSCV vaccine is constructed by inserting the structural gene cassettes of CHIKV and ZIKV into the SCV backbone. The vaccine is produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells, ensuring high purity and consistency. The vaccine is formulated for intramuscular administration and has been shown to induce robust neutralizing antibody responses against CHIKV, ZIKV, and VACV in NHPs.

Key features of the invention include:
1. **Safety**: The rSCV vaccine is replication-defective, ensuring that it cannot produce infectious viral progeny in vaccine recipients.
2. **Immunogenicity**: The vaccine induces strong neutralizing antibody responses against CHIKV, ZIKV, and VACV, providing protection against these pathogens.
3. **Protection**: The vaccine has been shown to protect NHPs against ZIKV challenge, reducing viremia and preventing disease progression.
4. **Manufacturing**: The vaccine is produced in CHO cells, offering significant advantages over traditional cell lines used for VACV-based vaccines.
5. **Versatility**: The rSCV platform can be easily modified to incorporate additional pathogens, making it a versatile tool for developing multi-pathogen vaccines.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Construction of Vaccines

The recombinant Sementis Copenhagen Vector (rSCV) vaccine is constructed by inserting the structural gene cassettes of Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) and Zika virus (ZIKV) into the SCV backbone. The SCV backbone is derived from the Copenhagen strain of Vaccinia virus (VACV) and is rendered replication-defective by the deletion of the D13L gene, which encodes an essential viral assembly protein. This deletion ensures that the rSCV vaccine cannot produce infectious viral progeny in vaccine recipients, enhancing its safety profile.

The structural gene cassettes of CHIKV and ZIKV are inserted into the SCV backbone at specific loci. The CHIKV structural polyprotein cassette (C-E3-E2-6K-E1) is derived from a 2006 isolate from Reunion Island (strain 06-021), while the ZIKV prME gene is derived from a 2015 Brazilian isolate (ZikaSPH2015). These inserts are designed to express the structural proteins necessary to elicit a robust immune response against CHIKV and ZIKV.

The rSCV vaccine is produced in Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells, which have been modified to express the D13L gene and the host range protein CP77. The use of CHO cells offers several advantages over traditional cell lines used for VACV-based vaccines, including high purity, consistency, and ease of scale-up for large-scale production.

### Example 2

In a study involving non-human primates (NHPs), the rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine was evaluated for its immunogenicity and protective efficacy. NHPs were vaccinated once with 10^7 pfu (n = 5) or twice with 10^8 pfu of rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK (n = 5), or twice with 10^8 pfu of the rSCV-control vaccine (n = 4). The vaccine was administered intramuscularly into the right quadriceps in a volume of 0.5 ml.

Post-vaccination, all NHPs vaccinated with rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK generated anti-ZIKV-neutralizing antibody responses, while no significant responses were observed in the rSCV-control group. A second immunization with 10^8 pfu of rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK resulted in a significant increase in anti-ZIKV neutralizing antibody responses, with a mean fold change (FC) in 50% Focus Reduction Neutralization Test (FRNT50) titres between days 35 and 70 of 5.5 ± SEM 1.4.

### Example 3

NHPs vaccinated with rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK were challenged with ZIKV PRVABC59 to evaluate the protective efficacy of the vaccine. Post-challenge, serum ZIKV RNA copy numbers were determined by quantitative reverse-transcriptase PCR (qRT-PCR). NHPs vaccinated with rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK showed significantly lower serum ZIKV RNA copy numbers compared to the rSCV-control group, with none of the NHPs vaccinated with 2 × 10^8 pfu of rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK showing serum ZIKV RNA copy numbers above the lower limit of reliable quantification (LLoRQ).

### Conclusion

The rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine has been shown to induce robust neutralizing antibody responses against CHIKV, ZIKV, and VACV in NHPs. The vaccine provides significant protection against ZIKV challenge, reducing viremia and preventing disease progression. The use of CHO cells for vaccine production offers significant advantages over traditional cell lines, ensuring high purity and consistency. The rSCV platform is versatile and can be easily modified to incorporate additional pathogens, making it a valuable tool for developing multi-pathogen vaccines.

### Example 4

In another study, the rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine was evaluated for its ability to induce neutralizing antibody responses against multiple CHIKV isolates. NHPs vaccinated with rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK generated neutralizing antibodies to all four CHIKV isolates tested, including the West African (WA), East, Central, and South African (ECSA), and Asian genotypes. The neutralizing antibody responses to the Asian isolates were initially lower but increased significantly over time, demonstrating the vaccine's ability to induce broad cross-neutralizing responses.

### Example 5

The rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine was also evaluated for its ability to induce anti-VACV antibody responses. NHPs vaccinated with rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK showed detectable anti-VACV neutralizing antibody responses and IgG ELISA antibody responses. After two vaccinations with 10^8 pfu of rSCV vaccines, the mean reciprocal 50% Plaque Reduction Neutralization test (PRNT50) titre against VACV was 79 ± SEM 24, and the mean reciprocal endpoint ELISA titre was 800 ± SEM 163.

### Example 6

The rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine was further evaluated for its safety and tolerability in NHPs. No fever or significant loss of body weight was observed post-vaccination. One NHP developed a rash in the inguinal area, which was attributed to environmental factors rather than the vaccine. Overall, the vaccine was well-tolerated, with no serious adverse events reported.

### Example 7

In a final study, the rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine was evaluated for its potential to induce T-cell responses. While poxvirus vaccine systems are known to induce T-cell responses, this aspect was not specifically assessed in the current study. However, the robust neutralizing antibody responses observed suggest that the vaccine is capable of inducing a comprehensive immune response, including both humoral and cellular components.

In conclusion, the rSCV-ZIKA/CHIK vaccine represents a significant advancement in multi-pathogen vaccine technology. It is safe, immunogenic, and protective, offering a promising solution to the ongoing global health challenges posed by CHIKV and ZIKV. The rSCV platform is versatile and can be easily modified to incorporate additional pathogens, making it a valuable tool for developing multi-pathogen vaccines.
