## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of medical microbiology and immunology, specifically to the development of a broad-spectrum conjugate vaccine for the prevention of infections caused by Gram-negative bacterial pathogens, particularly Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA). The vaccine comprises conjugates of KP O polysaccharides (OPS) with PA flagellin proteins, providing a comprehensive and effective prophylactic strategy against these clinically significant pathogens.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nosocomial infections pose a significant risk to hospitalized patients and are associated with various types of infections, including surgical site infections, wound infections, pneumonias, intravenous catheter-based infections, urinary tract infections, and septicemia. The emergence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) among nosocomially-associated bacteria, including resistance to last-resort antibiotics like colistin, has heightened clinical risks and threatens a return to the pre-antibiotic era. Two Gram-negative bacterial pathogens, Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA), are of particular concern due to their increasing acquisition and spread of antibiotic resistance. These pathogens are responsible for a substantial proportion of nosocomial infections and have been identified by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) and the World Health Organization as being of highest concern.

Vaccine approaches are not subject to the evasion mechanisms that mediate resistance to antibiotics, making them a promising adjunct to combat AMR bacterial infections. However, there are currently no licensed human vaccines for KP and PA. The development of a broad-spectrum vaccine that targets both pathogens would offer a straightforward way to reduce their combined incidence. PA flagella proteins, particularly flagellin (Fla), are well-studied virulence factors and protective antigens. KP expresses both a capsule polysaccharide (K antigen) and a lipopolysaccharide-associated O polysaccharide (OPS) on its surface. While KP OPS has been shown to be accessible to antibodies and mediate opsonophagocytic uptake, isolated bacterial polysaccharides are generally poorly immunogenic. Linking these polysaccharides to a protein carrier enhances their immunogenicity and induces immunological memory.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a broad-spectrum conjugate vaccine for preventing infections caused by Klebsiella pneumoniae (KP) and Pseudomonas aeruginosa (PA). The vaccine comprises conjugates of KP O polysaccharides (OPS) with PA flagellin proteins. Specifically, the vaccine includes conjugates of the four prevalent KP OPS types (O1, O2, O3, O5) with the two PA flagellin types (FlaA and FlaB).

The invention encompasses the following aspects:
1. **Conjugate**: A conjugate comprising a KP O polysaccharide (OPS) chemically linked to a PA flagellin protein. The conjugate is designed to enhance the immunogenicity of the polysaccharide and induce a robust immune response.
2. **Compositions**: Pharmaceutical compositions comprising the conjugates, optionally in combination with adjuvants and other excipients, for use in immunizing subjects against KP and PA infections.
3. **Methods of Treatment**: Methods for preventing or treating infections caused by KP and PA by administering the conjugate vaccine to a subject.
4. **Methods of Making the Conjugate**: Methods for producing the conjugates, including genetic engineering of reagent strains for KP OPS production, purification of KP OPS and PA flagellin proteins, and chemical conjugation of the polysaccharides to the flagellin proteins.
5. **Genetically Engineered Strains**: Genetically engineered strains of KP and PA used in the production of the conjugate vaccine, including strains with deletions of specific genes to facilitate OPS production and purification.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Conjugate

The conjugate of the present invention comprises a KP O polysaccharide (OPS) chemically linked to a PA flagellin protein. The conjugation process involves linking the reducing end of the KP OPS to lysine residues on the PA flagellin protein using thioether-aminooxy chemistry. This method ensures that the polysaccharide chain remains intact and extends from the protein surface, maintaining its immunogenic properties.

The conjugate is designed to enhance the immunogenicity of the KP OPS, which is typically poorly immunogenic when presented as an isolated polysaccharide. By linking the polysaccharide to a protein carrier, the conjugate can elicit a stronger and more durable immune response, including the induction of immunological memory.

### Compositions

The pharmaceutical compositions of the present invention comprise the conjugates described above, optionally in combination with adjuvants and other excipients. Suitable adjuvants include, but are not limited to, aluminum salts (alum), saponins, and oil-in-water emulsions. The compositions may be formulated for various routes of administration, including intramuscular, subcutaneous, and intradermal injection.

The compositions may also include preservatives, stabilizers, and buffers to ensure stability and safety. The final product is typically sterile and pyrogen-free, suitable for use in human subjects.

### Methods of Treatment

The methods of treatment of the present invention involve administering the conjugate vaccine to a subject to prevent or treat infections caused by KP and PA. The vaccine can be administered to individuals at risk of nosocomial infections, such as hospitalized patients, healthcare workers, and individuals with compromised immune systems.

The vaccine is typically administered in a series of doses, with the initial dose followed by one or more booster doses. The dosing schedule and route of administration are determined based on the specific needs of the subject and the desired immune response. The vaccine has been shown to induce high levels of anti-OPS and anti-flagellin antibodies, which provide protection against both KP and PA infections.

### Methods of Making the Conjugate

The conjugates of the present invention are produced through a multi-step process involving genetic engineering, fermentation, purification, and chemical conjugation.

#### Genetic Engineering

Genetically engineered strains of KP are created to produce the desired OPS types. The strains are modified to delete specific genes, such as guaBA (guanosine biosynthesis) and wzabc (capsule synthesis), to facilitate OPS production and purification. The resulting strains are guanine auxotrophic and non-encapsulated, making them highly attenuated and suitable for large-scale fermentation.

Recombinant PA flagellin proteins (FlaA and FlaB) are produced in E. coli using codon-optimized synthetic genes. The flagellin proteins are expressed with a 6xHis tag for easy purification.

#### Fermentation and Purification

KP reagent strains are grown in large-scale fermentation cultures to produce the desired OPS types. The OPS is released from the bacterial cells using nitrous acid deamination and purified through a series of steps, including microfiltration, tangential flow filtration, ion exchange chromatography, and size exclusion chromatography. The purified OPS is characterized for purity, molecular size, and monosaccharide composition.

Recombinant PA flagellin proteins are produced in E. coli and purified using nickel affinity chromatography, followed by cation and anion exchange membrane chromatography. The purified proteins are characterized for purity, integrity, and endotoxin content.

#### Chemical Conjugation

The purified KP OPS is chemically linked to the purified PA flagellin proteins using thioether-aminooxy chemistry. The reducing end of the KP OPS is modified with an aminooxy-thiol linker, and the PA flagellin proteins are derivatized with a maleimide molecule. The conjugation reaction is monitored by SDS-PAGE and HPLC-SEC to ensure the formation of the desired conjugate.

The conjugate is purified by size exclusion chromatography and characterized for polysaccharide and protein content. The final product is sterile and pyrogen-free, suitable for use in human subjects.

### Genetically Engineered Strains

The genetically engineered strains of KP used in the production of the conjugate vaccine are created to facilitate the production and purification of the desired OPS types. The strains are modified to delete specific genes, such as guaBA (guanosine biosynthesis) and wzabc (capsule synthesis), to create reagent strains that are guanine auxotrophic and non-encapsulated. These modifications make the strains highly attenuated and suitable for large-scale fermentation.

The reagent strains are used to produce the four prevalent KP OPS types (O1, O2, O3, O5). The strains are grown in large-scale fermentation cultures, and the OPS is released and purified using a scalable manufacturing approach. The purified OPS is then used in the conjugation process to create the final vaccine product.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Production and Characterization of KP OPS and PA Flagellin Proteins

**Genetic Engineering of KP Reagent Strains:**
KP B5055 (O1:K2) was used to engineer reagent strain CVD 3001 by deleting the genes for guaBA (guanosine biosynthesis, attenuating) and wzabc (removes capsule, attenuating and simplifying OPS purification). The mutant was compared to wild-type phenotypically by measuring guanine auxotrophy, lack of capsule expression, and attenuation. CVD 3001 was highly attenuated in the mouse model of infection with an IP LD50 > 109 CFU compared to ~104 CFU for B5055.

A similar strategy was utilized to generate reagent strain CVD 3002 by deleting the wzabc genes from KP 4425/51 (O5:K57). KP 7380 is a non-encapsulated O2 isolate and was used as a reagent strain without further modification. KP 390 (O3:K11) was refractory to genetic engineering and was used as a reagent strain in its native form.

**Fermentation and Purification of KP OPS:**
KP OPS was obtained from fermentation cultures of KP reagent strains and purified by a scalable manufacturing approach to yield large amounts of highly purified polysaccharide with low residual protein (<1%) and endotoxin (<0.01 endotoxin units/μg). HPLC-SEC analyses of the purified polysaccharides revealed that they were each comprised of a relatively homogenous molecular size population that was similar among the 4 OPS types.

**Production and Purification of PA Flagellin Proteins:**
PA FlaA1 and FlaB, expressed as recombinant proteins in E. coli, generated protein bands at the predicted molecular weight based on amino acid sequence and defined peaks by HPLC-SEC congruent with the monomeric form, with less than 0.1 endotoxin units/μg protein.

**Chemical Conjugation:**
KP OPS molecules were linked at the reducing end with an aminoooxy-thiol linker to PA Fla protein lysines that had been derivatized with a maleimide molecule. Conjugation by this approach generated sun-type neoglycoconjugates for which the OPS chain remains intact and extends from the protein surface. HPLC-SEC analysis of the purified glycoconjugates indicated that each maintained a relatively narrow size distribution that was similar among the different conjugates, with roughly equivalent polysaccharide to protein ratios.

**Immunogenicity and Protective Efficacy:**
Rabbits immunized with the conjugate formulation generated maximal anti-OPS IgG levels after three doses that were comparably high for all four OPS types. In contrast, immunization with unconjugated admixed vaccine components failed to induce significant anti-OPS IgG titers. High antibody levels against both PA flagellin proteins were also seen in rabbits immunized with the four-component conjugate formulation.

Passive transfer of conjugate-induced antisera to mice provided significant protection against IV infection with encapsulated KP B5055 (O1 OPS type) and 700603 (O3 OPS type) isolates. Transfer of immune sera also reduced the level of KP organisms in blood, liver, and spleen tissues. Additionally, the conjugate-induced antisera protected mice against PA burn wound infection, demonstrating the broad-spectrum efficacy of the vaccine.

These results demonstrate the effectiveness of the conjugate vaccine in inducing a robust immune response and providing protection against infections caused by both KP and PA.
