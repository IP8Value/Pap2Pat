# DESCRIPTION

## 1. FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to the identification and use of metalloproteinase 7 (MMP-7) as a biomarker for renal cell carcinoma (RCC). The invention provides methods and compositions for detecting and quantifying MMP-7 in biological samples, particularly serum, to diagnose, monitor, and predict the prognosis of RCC.

## 2. BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is one of the most common and lethal forms of kidney cancer. Early detection and accurate prognosis are critical for improving patient outcomes. However, there is a lack of validated biomarkers for the early detection of RCC, particularly in asymptomatic patients, and for monitoring the disease's progression and response to treatment. Conventional diagnostic methods, such as imaging techniques and histopathological analysis, often detect RCC at advanced stages when the disease is more difficult to treat.

Recent advances in proteomics and serological screening have led to the identification of potential biomarkers for various cancers. One such candidate is metalloproteinase 7 (MMP-7), a member of the matrix metalloproteinase family. MMP-7 is known to play a role in the degradation of extracellular matrix components and has been implicated in tumor invasion and metastasis. Previous studies have shown that MMP-7 is overexpressed in several types of cancer, including RCC, and is associated with poor prognosis.

The present invention builds upon these findings by providing a method for detecting and quantifying MMP-7 in serum using a homogeneous, fluorescent, dual-monoclonal immunoassay. This method offers high sensitivity and specificity, making it a valuable tool for the early detection, prognosis, and monitoring of RCC.

## 3. SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for detecting and quantifying metalloproteinase 7 (MMP-7) in a biological sample, particularly serum, to diagnose, monitor, and predict the prognosis of renal cell carcinoma (RCC). The method involves the use of a homogeneous, fluorescent, dual-monoclonal immunoassay that utilizes specific antibodies against MMP-7.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for detecting MMP-7 in a biological sample, comprising:
1. Providing a biological sample suspected of containing MMP-7.
2. Contacting the biological sample with a first monoclonal antibody specific for MMP-7, wherein the first antibody is labeled with a fluorescent donor moiety.
3. Contacting the biological sample with a second monoclonal antibody specific for MMP-7, wherein the second antibody is labeled with a fluorescent acceptor moiety.
4. Measuring the fluorescence resonance energy transfer (FRET) signal generated by the interaction of the donor and acceptor moieties.
5. Determining the presence or absence of MMP-7 in the biological sample based on the FRET signal.

In another aspect, the invention provides a kit for detecting MMP-7 in a biological sample, comprising:
1. A first monoclonal antibody specific for MMP-7, labeled with a fluorescent donor moiety.
2. A second monoclonal antibody specific for MMP-7, labeled with a fluorescent acceptor moiety.
3. Instructions for performing the immunoassay.

The invention also provides a method for diagnosing renal cell carcinoma (RCC) in a subject, comprising:
1. Obtaining a biological sample from the subject.
2. Detecting the presence or absence of MMP-7 in the biological sample using the method described above.
3. Diagnosing RCC in the subject based on the presence of MMP-7.

Additionally, the invention provides a method for monitoring the progression or response to treatment of RCC in a subject, comprising:
1. Obtaining a first biological sample from the subject at a first time point.
2. Detecting the concentration of MMP-7 in the first biological sample using the method described above.
3. Obtaining a second biological sample from the subject at a second time point.
4. Detecting the concentration of MMP-7 in the second biological sample using the method described above.
5. Comparing the concentration of MMP-7 in the first and second biological samples to monitor the progression or response to treatment of RCC.

## MMP-7

Metalloproteinase 7 (MMP-7), also known as matrilysin, is a member of the matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) family of zinc-dependent endopeptidases. MMP-7 is synthesized as a zymogen (pro-MMP-7) and is activated by proteolytic cleavage to produce the active form (MMP-7). Both forms of MMP-7 have been implicated in various physiological and pathological processes, including tissue remodeling, inflammation, and cancer.

In the context of renal cell carcinoma (RCC), MMP-7 has been shown to be overexpressed in tumor tissues and is associated with advanced clinicopathological stages and poor prognosis. The presence of pro-MMP-7 in the serum of RCC patients has been identified as a potential biomarker for the early detection and monitoring of the disease. The specific immunoreactivity of pro-MMP-7 in RCC patient sera suggests that it may be a useful target for developing diagnostic assays.

## 4. EXPERIMENTAL SECTION AND EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Identification of pro-MMP-7 as an Immunogenic Protein in RCC Cell Line Supernatant

#### Materials and Methods

**Cell Culture and Sample Preparation:**
The human CAL54 cell line, established from the malignant pleural effusion of an RCC patient, was obtained from the German Collection of Microorganisms and Cell Cultures (DSMZ GmbH). The cell line was maintained in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, 2 mM glutamine, 100 kU/L streptomycin, 400 kU/L penicillin, 400 μg/L hydrocortisone, and 100 mM sodium pyruvate. When the cell density reached 4 × 10^9 cells/L, the cells were switched to serum-free DMEM and incubated at 37°C, 5% CO2, for 48 hours. Cell supernatants containing secreted proteins were collected, filtered through a 0.2 μm filter, and dialyzed against 20 mM Tris-HCl (pH 6.8). The samples were then precipitated with ice-cold 80% ethanol for 1 hour and centrifuged at 38,000g for 25 minutes at 4°C. The precipitates were washed with 80% ethanol.

**2DE and Immunoblotting:**
Ethanol precipitates were resuspended in isoelectrofocusing medium containing 8 M urea, 2% CHAPS, 2% Triton X-100, 8% Pharmalyte 3-10, 100 mM DTT, 2% Tergitol NP7, and traces of bromophenol blue. Total secreted protein (100 μg for analytical gels and 300 μg for preparative gels) was loaded onto nonlinear immobilized pH gradient (IPG) strips (pH 3-10, 18 cm long) and isoelectric focusing was performed. After the first dimension, the IPG strips were equilibrated and then subjected to SDS-PAGE. The gels were silver-stained, and gel images were scanned.

For immunoblotting, proteins were transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes and incubated with serum pools (1:100 dilution) from 6 RCC patients (stages II to IV) or 10 healthy donors. The immunoproduct was visualized using a monoclonal antihuman IgG-alkaline phosphatase conjugate and a nitroblue tetrazolium/5-bromo-4-chloro-3-indolyl phosphate (NBT-BCIP) colorimetric substrate. Gel matching and spot evaluation were performed using Melanie II software.

**Protein Identification:**
Proteins of interest were excised and digested in-gel using sequencing-grade trypsin. Peptide mixtures were analyzed using a BIFLEX III MALDI-TOF mass spectrometer, and tandem mass spectrometry was performed on a quadrupole time-of-flight mass spectrometer (QSTAR).

#### Results

Three hundred seventeen protein spots were identified as secreted by the CAL54 cell line. Computer-assisted subtraction of the control sera spot pattern from the patient sera spot pattern revealed 12 different spots. One spot, with an apparent mass of 29 kDa and pI 7.5, was identified as pro-MMP-7. The peptide mass spectrum of this spot showed the presence of two peptides corresponding to pro-MMP-7, which were sequenced by tandem mass spectrometry and matched residues 42-50 and 78-86 of human pro-MMP-7.

### Example 2: Development of a Homogeneous Fluoroimmunoassay for MMP-7

#### Materials and Methods

**Antibodies and Recombinant Protein:**
Recombinant human MMP-7 was purchased from R&D Systems. Four 6-week-old female BALB/c mice were immunized with 26 μg human recombinant MMP-7. Monoclonal antibodies were generated and purified using protein A Fast Flow affinity chromatography. Four clones (01S, 04S, 21S, and 29S) were screened for their ability to recognize MMP-7 in a homogeneous fluoroimmunoassay.

**Western Blot Analysis:**
Total protein from CAL54 cell supernatant and sera from RCC patients and healthy controls were loaded onto NuPAGE 4%-12% Bis-Tris gels and transferred onto nitrocellulose membranes. The membranes were probed with the monoclonal antibodies (01S, 04S, 21S, and 29S) at a 1:1000 dilution and visualized using a goat antimouse IgG-alkaline phosphatase conjugate.

**Immunoassay:**
A homogeneous sandwich fluoroimmunoassay was developed using time-resolved amplified cryptate emission (TRACE) technology. Purified antihuman MMP-7 monoclonal antibodies of clones 29S and 01S were coupled to AF647 fluorophore and europium cryptate, respectively. The immunoassay was performed by incubating 50 μL of each patient's sample/calibrator, 50 μL AF647-conjugated antibody solution, and 50 μL cryptate-conjugated antibody solution at 37°C on a Brahms Kryptor automate.

#### Results

The immunoassay had a coefficient of linear correlation (r^2) > 0.99, with an intraassay CV of 4.2% at 3.1 μg/L. The average recovery was 96% (9%), with recoveries ranging from 89% to 109%. Low MMP-7 concentrations were detected in serum from healthy controls (median 2.13 μg/L, range 0.17-3.5 μg/L). In contrast, MMP-7 was significantly higher (P < 0.0001) in patients with localized (median 7.26 μg/L, range 3.12-27.9 μg/L) and metastasized (median 7.87 μg/L, range 4.25-30.5 μg/L) RCC. Of the 30 RCC patient serum samples measured, 28 (93%, 95% CI 78%-99%) had an increased MMP-7 concentration.

### Example 3: Clinical Validation of MMP-7 as a Biomarker for RCC

#### Materials and Methods

**Patient and Control Samples:**
Serum samples were collected from 30 RCC patients, 40 patients with benign or malignant conditions, and 30 healthy individuals. All samples were obtained at the time of RCC diagnosis and before immunotherapy or surgery. Venous blood samples were collected in vacutainer tubes without clot accelerator and centrifuged within 30 minutes at 2000g for 15 minutes at 4°C. The supernatants were stored at -20°C until analysis.

**Statistical Analysis:**
The distribution of MMP-7 concentrations was tested using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Nonparametric data were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. ROC curve analysis was performed to quantify serum MMP-7 positivity and statistical uncertainty.

#### Results

The immunoassay detected significantly higher concentrations of MMP-7 in the serum of RCC patients compared to healthy controls and patients with benign or malignant conditions. The sensitivity of the assay for detecting RCC was 93% (95% CI 78%-99%) at a specificity of 75% (59%-87%). These results demonstrate the potential of MMP-7 as a biomarker for the early detection and monitoring of RCC.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method and composition for detecting and quantifying MMP-7 in biological samples, particularly serum, to diagnose, monitor, and predict the prognosis of renal cell carcinoma (RCC). The homogeneous, fluorescent, dual-monoclonal immunoassay offers high sensitivity and specificity, making it a valuable tool for the early detection and management of RCC. Further clinical validation and large-scale studies are warranted to fully assess the utility of MMP-7 as a biomarker for RCC.
