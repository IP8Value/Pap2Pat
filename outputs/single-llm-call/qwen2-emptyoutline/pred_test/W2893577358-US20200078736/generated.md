## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of nanoporous membranes and, more specifically, to the fabrication of ion-selective and chemically robust nanoporous membranes using layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolytes. The invention provides a method for creating large-area, inexpensive, and tunable nanoporous membranes suitable for various applications, including water purification, chemical separation, and energy storage.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Nanoporous membranes have gained significant attention due to their ability to control ion transport, which is crucial for applications such as water purification, chemical separation, and energy storage. The relative ratio of pore surface area to electrolyte volume in nanoporous membranes allows for the tuning of ion transport by controlling the surface charge on the pore walls. Various responsive chemistries have been employed to alter surface charge and, consequently, ion transport through nanoporous membranes. Additionally, the shape of the nanopores, such as conical shapes, can influence ion rectification behavior, providing further control over ion transport.

For large-scale applications, the cost-effective production of nanoporous membranes is essential. Layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolytes offers a promising solution for fabricating functional nanoporous membranes. LbL deposition is a bottom-up approach that involves the sequential deposition of oppositely charged polyelectrolytes on a substrate to form bilayers. This method is versatile and can be applied to a variety of substrates, including anodic alumina, mesoporous silica, and track-etched polycarbonate membranes. The simplicity and low cost of LbL deposition make it an attractive technique for large-scale membrane manufacturing.

Polyelectrolytes typically consist of an anionic polymer and a cationic polymer, which are sequentially deposited on a substrate to form a bilayer. Commonly used anionic polymers contain sulfonate groups, while cationic polymers contain amine groups. These functional groups enable LbL assembly via electrostatic and hydrogen bonding attractions. However, the use of carboxylic acid-containing anionic polymers and the subsequent cross-linking of the polyelectrolyte films offer additional opportunities for tuning the properties of the membranes.

Cross-linking the polyelectrolyte films can modify their ionic selectivity and conductivity by altering the charge density within the films. Glutaraldehyde (GA) and N-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)-N′-ethylcarbodimide hydrochloride (EDC) are two common cross-linking agents used in this context. GA cross-links primary amines, reducing the cationic fixed charge in the membrane, while EDC cross-links primary amines to carboxylic acids, reducing both cationic and anionic fixed charges. The choice of cross-linking agent and the number of bilayers deposited can significantly influence the ionic selectivity and conductivity of the resulting membranes.

Despite the potential benefits of LbL-deposited polyelectrolyte membranes, there is a need for a comprehensive method to fabricate and characterize these membranes, particularly for large-scale applications. The present invention addresses this need by providing a detailed method for creating ion-selective and chemically robust nanoporous membranes using LbL deposition and cross-linking techniques.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for fabricating ion-selective and chemically robust nanoporous membranes using layer-by-layer (LbL) deposition of polyelectrolytes. The method includes the following steps:

1. **Preparation of Substrate**: Track-etched nanoporous polycarbonate (PC) support membranes are treated to remove a thin polyvinyl pyrrolidone (PVP) layer. The membranes are etched in a sodium hydroxide (NaOH) solution and treated with UV-ozone to prepare the surface for polyelectrolyte deposition.

2. **LbL Deposition**: The prepared PC membranes are sequentially dipped in aqueous solutions of poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) and poly(ethyleneimine) (PEI) to form bilayers. The number of bilayers deposited can be adjusted to achieve the desired film thickness and properties.

3. **Cross-Linking**: The polyelectrolyte films are cross-linked using either glutaraldehyde (GA) or N-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)-N′-ethylcarbodimide hydrochloride (EDC) to modify the charge density and improve the chemical stability of the films. GA cross-links primary amines, reducing the cationic fixed charge, while EDC cross-links primary amines to carboxylic acids, reducing both cationic and anionic fixed charges.

4. **Characterization**: The resulting membranes are characterized using Fourier transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR) to verify the deposition of the polyelectrolyte layers and the efficacy of cross-linking. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) is used to assess the uniformity and thickness of the polyelectrolyte films. Electrochemical measurements, including ionic selectivity and conductivity, are performed to evaluate the performance of the membranes.

The invention further provides nanoporous membranes fabricated using the above method, which exhibit improved ionic selectivity and conductivity compared to uncoated membranes. The membranes are suitable for various applications, including water purification, chemical separation, and energy storage.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Example: LbL Deposition of Bilayer PAA and PEI Polyelectrolytes

#### Preparation of Substrate

The first step in the fabrication of ion-selective and chemically robust nanoporous membranes is the preparation of the substrate. Track-etched nanoporous polycarbonate (PC) support membranes with a pore size of 0.05 μm and a diameter of 90 mm are used as the substrate. The membranes are treated to remove a thin polyvinyl pyrrolidone (PVP) layer by etching them in a 4.5 M NaOH solution for 5 minutes, followed by rinsing with deionized (DI) water. The membranes are then treated with UV-ozone for 10 minutes on each side to further prepare the surface for polyelectrolyte deposition.

#### LbL Deposition

The prepared PC membranes are sequentially dipped in aqueous solutions of poly(acrylic acid) (PAA) and poly(ethyleneimine) (PEI) to form bilayers. The PAA solution is prepared at a concentration of 0.2 wt% and adjusted to pH 3.2, while the PEI solution is prepared at a concentration of 0.1 wt% and adjusted to pH 10.4. The first bilayer (BL) is formed by immersing the membrane in the PEI solution for 5 minutes, rinsing with DI water, and then immersing in the PAA solution for 5 minutes. Subsequent bilayers are deposited by dipping the membrane in the polymer solutions for 1 minute each. The number of bilayers deposited can be adjusted to achieve the desired film thickness and properties.

#### Cross-Linking

The polyelectrolyte films are cross-linked using either glutaraldehyde (GA) or N-(3-dimethylaminopropyl)-N′-ethylcarbodimide hydrochloride (EDC) to modify the charge density and improve the chemical stability of the films. The GA cross-linked membranes are immersed in a 25% GA solution for 12 hours and then washed with copious amounts of DI water. The EDC cross-linked membranes are immersed in a 100 mM EDC solution in water for 12 hours and then washed with copious amounts of water.

#### Characterization

The resulting membranes are characterized using various techniques to verify the deposition of the polyelectrolyte layers and the efficacy of cross-linking, as well as to assess the uniformity and thickness of the polyelectrolyte films and the performance of the membranes.

**Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR)**:
FTIR spectroscopy is used to verify the deposition of the polyelectrolyte layers and the efficacy of cross-linking. FTIR spectra are recorded for all samples, both as made and after cross-linking. The spectra reveal the presence of characteristic peaks associated with the carboxylic acid CO stretch in PAA at 1714 cm−1 and the N–H bend from PEI at 1550 cm−1. The degree of cross-linking can be observed from the FTIR spectra, with the "N–H" and "CO" absorbance peaks decreasing in intensity upon cross-linking, indicating a reduction in the number of amine and carboxylic acid functional groups in the polyelectrolyte.

**Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM)**:
SEM is used to assess the uniformity and thickness of the polyelectrolyte films. Plan view and cross-sectional SEM images are taken to determine the relative uniformity and thickness of the self-assembled polymers layers. The images show that the polyelectrolyte films become more uniform and cover the nanopores completely as the number of bilayers increases. Cross-sectional analysis reveals the thickness of the deposited polyelectrolyte, with the thickness increasing as the number of bilayers increases. The thickness of the 5BL GA membrane, for example, is measured to be 1230 ± 193 nm on average.

**Electrochemical Measurements**:
Electrochemical measurements, including ionic selectivity and conductivity, are performed to evaluate the performance of the membranes. Ionic selectivity is determined from the resulting transmembrane voltage, which arises when two different concentrations of NaCl solutions are placed on opposite sides of the membrane. The transmembrane voltage is measured using a potentiometric method, and the cation and anion transference numbers are calculated from the slopes of the transmembrane voltage curves. The ionic conductivity of the membranes is evaluated using impedance spectroscopy, where the resistance of stacks of membranes is measured to obtain an average resistance per membrane.

The results show that the ionic selectivity and conductivity of the membranes are influenced by the number of deposited bilayers and the choice of cross-linking agent. The GA cross-linked membranes exhibit higher cationic selectivity compared to the EDC cross-linked membranes, with the 5BL GA cross-linked membranes showing the largest slope of 36.54 mV, indicating good cationic selectivity. The ionic conductivity of the membranes decreases with the coatings but regains a portion of it upon cross-linking the polyelectrolyte. The conductivity of the 5BL GA membranes is similar to that of the uncoated PC membrane, demonstrating the ability to control ionic selectivity independent of ionic conductivity.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a method for fabricating ion-selective and chemically robust nanoporous membranes using LbL deposition of polyelectrolytes and cross-linking techniques. The method allows for the control of ionic selectivity and conductivity, making the membranes suitable for various applications, including water purification, chemical separation, and energy storage.
