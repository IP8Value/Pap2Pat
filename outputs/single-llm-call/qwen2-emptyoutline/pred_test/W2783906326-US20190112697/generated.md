# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a silicon (Si)-gradient steel sheet and, more particularly, to a steel sheet having a specific silicon concentration gradient and microstructure that exhibits improved magnetic properties, particularly at high frequencies. The invention is applicable to the production of core materials for transformers, reactors, and other electrical devices operating at high frequencies.

## BACKGROUND

Silicon (Si) is a well-known element used to improve the magnetic properties of steel. The resistivity of steel increases with the Si content, which helps reduce eddy current losses caused by alternating current (AC) magnetization. When the Si content is increased to 6.5%, the saturation magnetostriction falls to zero, and the permeability becomes maximal. However, increasing the Si content beyond 4% significantly reduces the ductility of the steel, making it challenging to produce thin steel sheets using conventional rolling processes. Recent advancements in chemical vapor deposition (CVD) siliconizing processes have enabled the industrial production of 6.5% Si steel sheets, which are primarily used as core materials for low-noise transformers and reactors operating at audible frequencies.

Si-gradient steel sheets, developed through CVD siliconizing processes, exhibit lower iron losses at high frequencies compared to 6.5% Si steel sheets, despite having a lower average Si content. These sheets are particularly suitable for high-frequency reactors driven at 10 kHz or more. Additionally, Si-gradient steel sheets show minimal loss deterioration under compressive stress, making them desirable core materials. Despite these advantages, the fundamental research on Si-gradient steel sheets is limited, and their characteristics are not yet fully understood.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a Si-gradient steel sheet with improved magnetic properties, particularly at high frequencies. The steel sheet includes a surface part and an inner part, each characterized by specific silicon concentrations, microstructural features, and mechanical properties. The invention aims to reduce iron losses and enhance the overall performance of the steel sheet in high-frequency applications.

The invention is defined by the following features:
1. The average aspect ratio of crystal grains included in the surface part is between 0.7 and 4.0, indicating a preferred orientation of the grains.
2. The thickness of the surface part is between 10% and 40% of the total thickness of the steel sheet.
3. The average Si concentration in the surface part is between 2.5% and 6.5%.
4. The average Si concentration in the inner part is 2.0% or less.
5. The Si concentration gap in the boundary part is 0.4% or more.
6. The surface part exhibits tensile stress of 50 to 200 MPa in the direction parallel to the surface, while the inner part exhibits compressive stress of 50 to 200 MPa in the same direction.
7. The thickness of the steel sheet is between 0.03 and 0.5 mm.
8. The carbon (C) content is 0.020% or less.
9. The Si content is between 0.15% and 2.0%.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Average Aspect Ratio of Crystal Grains Included in Surface Part: Ratio of Dimension of Crystal Grains in Parallel-to-Surface Direction to Dimension of Crystal Grains in Perpendicular-to-Surface Direction (Depth Direction) is 0.7 or More and 4.0 or Less

The average aspect ratio of the crystal grains in the surface part of the Si-gradient steel sheet is a critical parameter that influences its magnetic properties. The aspect ratio is defined as the ratio of the dimension of the crystal grains in the direction parallel to the surface to the dimension of the grains in the perpendicular-to-surface direction (depth direction). An aspect ratio between 0.7 and 4.0 ensures that the crystal grains are oriented in a manner that minimizes magnetic anisotropy and enhances the magnetic permeability of the steel sheet. This orientation is crucial for reducing eddy current losses and improving the overall magnetic performance of the material.

### Thickness of Surface Part: 10% to 40% of Thickness of Steel Sheet (Preferable Condition)

The thickness of the surface part of the Si-gradient steel sheet is another important factor that affects its magnetic properties. The surface part, which contains a higher concentration of silicon, should constitute between 10% and 40% of the total thickness of the steel sheet. This range ensures that the surface part is thick enough to provide the necessary magnetic benefits while maintaining the structural integrity and manufacturability of the steel sheet. A surface part that is too thin may not provide sufficient magnetic enhancement, while a surface part that is too thick may compromise the mechanical properties of the sheet.

### Average Si Concentration in Surface Part: 2.5% to 6.5% (Preferable Condition)

The average silicon concentration in the surface part of the Si-gradient steel sheet is a key parameter that determines its magnetic properties. The surface part should have an average Si concentration between 2.5% and 6.5%. This range is optimal for achieving high magnetic permeability and low iron losses at high frequencies. A higher Si concentration in the surface part reduces eddy current losses by increasing the resistivity of the material, while a lower Si concentration ensures that the material remains ductile and easy to process.

### Average Si Concentration in Inner Part: 2.0% or Less (Preferable Condition)

The average silicon concentration in the inner part of the Si-gradient steel sheet is also a critical parameter. The inner part should have an average Si concentration of 2.0% or less. This lower Si concentration in the inner part helps maintain the ductility and formability of the steel sheet, which is essential for manufacturing processes. Additionally, the lower Si concentration in the inner part reduces the overall cost of the material without significantly compromising its magnetic properties.

### Si Concentration Gap in Boundary Part: 0.4% or More (Preferable Condition)

The Si concentration gap in the boundary part of the Si-gradient steel sheet is a significant factor that influences its magnetic properties. The boundary part, which is the region between the surface part and the inner part, should have a Si concentration gap of 0.4% or more. This concentration gradient creates a stress distribution within the steel sheet, with tensile stress in the surface part and compressive stress in the inner part. The stress distribution is crucial for reducing the coercive force and improving the magnetic permeability of the material.

### Surface Part: Tensile Stress of 50 to 200 MPa in Direction Parallel to Surface, Inner Part: Compressive Stress of 50 to 200 MPa in Direction Parallel to Surface (Preferable Condition)

The stress distribution within the Si-gradient steel sheet is a critical factor that affects its magnetic properties. The surface part of the steel sheet should exhibit tensile stress of 50 to 200 MPa in the direction parallel to the surface, while the inner part should exhibit compressive stress of 50 to 200 MPa in the same direction. This stress distribution is created by the Si concentration gradient and helps reduce the coercive force and improve the magnetic permeability of the material. The tensile stress in the surface part aligns the magnetization vectors in the direction of the applied magnetic field, while the compressive stress in the inner part suppresses the formation of magnetic domains, thereby reducing hysteresis losses.

### Thickness of Steel Sheet: 0.03 to 0.5 mm (Preferable Condition)

The thickness of the Si-gradient steel sheet is an important parameter that affects its magnetic and mechanical properties. The steel sheet should have a thickness between 0.03 and 0.5 mm. This range ensures that the sheet is thin enough to minimize eddy current losses while being thick enough to maintain the necessary mechanical strength and durability. A thinner sheet is more suitable for high-frequency applications, while a thicker sheet is more appropriate for applications requiring higher mechanical strength.

### C: 0.020% or Less

The carbon (C) content in the Si-gradient steel sheet is a critical parameter that affects its magnetic properties. The C content should be 0.020% or less. A low C content is essential for maintaining the magnetic permeability and reducing the coercive force of the material. Excessive C content can lead to the formation of carbides, which can degrade the magnetic properties of the steel sheet.

### Si: 0.15% to 2.0%

The silicon (Si) content in the Si-gradient steel sheet is a key parameter that determines its magnetic properties. The Si content should be between 0.15% and 2.0%. This range ensures that the material has sufficient resistivity to reduce eddy current losses while maintaining the necessary ductility and formability. A higher Si content increases the resistivity and reduces eddy current losses, but it can also reduce the ductility of the material, making it more difficult to process.

## Example 1

A Si-gradient steel sheet was produced using a CVD siliconizing process. The steel sheet had a thickness of 0.1 mm and a surface part with a thickness of 0.04 mm (40% of the total thickness). The average Si concentration in the surface part was 6.0%, and the average Si concentration in the inner part was 1.5%. The Si concentration gap in the boundary part was 0.5%. The average aspect ratio of the crystal grains in the surface part was 2.5. The surface part exhibited tensile stress of 150 MPa in the direction parallel to the surface, while the inner part exhibited compressive stress of 150 MPa in the same direction. The C content was 0.015%, and the Si content was 1.8%.

The magnetic properties of the steel sheet were evaluated, and it was found that the steel sheet had a lower permeability, lower residual magnetic flux density (B_r), and a larger coercive force (H_c) compared to a 6.5% Si steel sheet. The hysteresis loss coefficient (k_h) was higher, and the eddy current loss coefficient (k_e) was lower. The steel sheet showed lower iron losses at high frequencies (10 kHz or more) compared to the 6.5% Si steel sheet.
