# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method for predicting in vivo hepatotoxicity of single-stranded oligonucleotides (SSOs) using an in vitro assay. Specifically, the invention provides a method for assessing the hepatotoxic potential of SSOs by evaluating their effects on primary mammalian hepatocytes and non-parenchymal cells. The method involves the gymnotic delivery of SSOs to the cells and the measurement of various cellular parameters, including lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release, intracellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP) levels, glutathione (GSH) levels, caspase-3/7 activity, cytokine secretion, and microRNA (miR-122) release. The invention also includes the use of this method to screen and prioritize SSOs for further development, thereby reducing the risk of hepatotoxicity in vivo.

## BACKGROUND

Single-stranded oligonucleotides (SSOs) are a class of therapeutic agents that can bind to specific RNA targets through Watson-Crick hybridization, leading to the recruitment of RNAseH and subsequent degradation of the target RNA. This mechanism has been exploited to modulate the expression of disease-relevant genes, making SSOs an attractive drug modality. Despite significant advancements in the field, the clinical success of SSOs has been limited, with only a few FDA-approved drugs. One of the major challenges in SSO development is the occurrence of hepatotoxicity and immunotoxicity, which can complicate drug development and limit the therapeutic window.

Hepatotoxicity and immunotoxicity associated with SSOs can manifest as accumulation and toxicity in the liver and kidney, as well as inflammatory reactions at the site of injection and flu-like symptoms. Preclinical assessments of SSO safety typically involve rodent in vivo tests, which are resource-intensive and ethically challenging. Moreover, these tests often fail to accurately predict the in vivo effects of SSOs, leading to high attrition rates in drug development.

Recent studies have identified certain sequence motifs that are associated with hepatotoxicity risk, but no in vitro test has been able to reliably recapitulate the in vivo effects of SSOs. Therefore, there is a critical need for an in vitro assay that can predict the hepatotoxic potential of SSOs before initiating animal studies. Such an assay would enable the early identification and prioritization of safer SSO candidates, thereby reducing the risk of hepatotoxicity and improving the efficiency of drug development.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for predicting in vivo hepatotoxicity of single-stranded oligonucleotides (SSOs) using an in vitro assay. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Isolation of Primary Mammalian Hepatocytes and Non-Parenchymal Cells**: Primary hepatocytes and non-parenchymal cells are isolated from a mammalian source, such as mice or humans.

2. **Gymnotic Delivery of SSOs**: The isolated cells are cultured in appropriate conditions, and SSOs are administered to the cells without the assistance of any delivery technology.

3. **Measurement of Cellular Parameters**: Various cellular parameters are measured to assess the hepatotoxic potential of the SSOs. These parameters include:
   - **Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) Release**: LDH release into the culture medium is measured to evaluate cell membrane integrity and cytotoxicity.
   - **Intracellular Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) Levels**: Intracellular ATP levels are measured to assess cell viability and metabolic activity.
   - **Glutathione (GSH) Levels**: Intracellular GSH levels are measured to evaluate oxidative stress.
   - **Caspase-3/7 Activity**: Caspase-3/7 activity is measured to assess apoptosis induction.
   - **Cytokine Secretion**: The secretion of cytokines, such as IL-1α and MIP-1α, is measured to evaluate inflammatory responses.
   - **MicroRNA (miR-122) Release**: The release of miR-122 into the culture medium is measured to assess liver-specific damage.

4. **Data Analysis and Interpretation**: The measured parameters are analyzed to determine the hepatotoxic potential of the SSOs. SSOs that cause significant changes in these parameters are identified as potentially hepatotoxic.

The invention also encompasses the use of this method to screen and prioritize SSOs for further development, thereby reducing the risk of hepatotoxicity in vivo. The method is applicable to both rodent and human primary hepatocytes, providing a comprehensive and translational approach to SSO safety assessment.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Prediction of In Vivo Toxicity

The present invention provides a method for predicting the in vivo hepatotoxicity of single-stranded oligonucleotides (SSOs) using an in vitro assay. The method involves the isolation of primary mammalian hepatocytes and non-parenchymal cells, the gymnotic delivery of SSOs to these cells, and the measurement of various cellular parameters to assess hepatotoxicity. The method is designed to recapitulate the in vivo hepatotoxic profile of SSOs, thereby enabling the early identification and prioritization of safer SSO candidates.

### Primary Mammalian Hepatocyte Cells

Primary hepatocytes are isolated from a mammalian source, such as mice or humans, using established protocols. For example, primary mouse hepatocytes can be isolated from 10- to 13-week-old male C57Bl6 mice by a retrograde two-step collagenase liver perfusion. The isolated hepatocytes are then cultured in appropriate conditions, such as William’s Medium E (WME) supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, penicillin, and streptomycin, to maintain their viability and functionality.

### Administering the Oligonucleotide—Gymnotic Delivery (Gymnosis)

The isolated hepatocytes are treated with SSOs using gymnotic delivery, which involves the administration of SSOs to the cells without the assistance of any delivery technology. Gymnotic delivery ensures that the SSOs are taken up by the cells via the natural pathways, thereby mimicking the in vivo situation. The SSOs are typically administered at various concentrations, ranging from 1 to 30 μM, and the cells are incubated with the SSOs for a period of 2 to 3 days.

### Control Data/Reference Values

Control data and reference values are essential for interpreting the results of the in vitro assay. Control data are obtained by treating the cells with a vehicle control, such as phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and measuring the baseline levels of the cellular parameters. Reference values are obtained by testing a panel of SSOs with known in vivo hepatotoxicity profiles. These reference values serve as a benchmark for comparing the hepatotoxic potential of new SSO candidates.

### (Determination of) Hepatotoxicity

Hepatotoxicity is determined by measuring various cellular parameters, including LDH release, intracellular ATP levels, GSH levels, caspase-3/7 activity, cytokine secretion, and miR-122 release. Each parameter provides insight into different aspects of hepatotoxicity, such as cell membrane integrity, cell viability, oxidative stress, apoptosis, inflammation, and liver-specific damage.

- **Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH) Release**: LDH release into the culture medium is measured using a Cytotoxicity Detection Kit. Increased LDH release indicates cell membrane damage and cytotoxicity.
- **Intracellular Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP) Levels**: Intracellular ATP levels are measured using a CellTiter-Glo® Luminescent Cell Viability Assay. Decreased ATP levels indicate reduced cell viability and metabolic activity.
- **Glutathione (GSH) Levels**: Intracellular GSH levels are measured using a fluorescent assay with monochlorobimane. Reduced GSH levels indicate oxidative stress.
- **Caspase-3/7 Activity**: Caspase-3/7 activity is measured using a Caspase-Glo® 3/7 Assay. Increased caspase-3/7 activity indicates apoptosis induction.
- **Cytokine Secretion**: The secretion of cytokines, such as IL-1α and MIP-1α, is measured using a multiplex ELISA. Increased cytokine secretion indicates inflammatory responses.
- **MicroRNA (miR-122) Release**: The release of miR-122 into the culture medium is measured using a TaqMan miRNA assay. Increased miR-122 release indicates liver-specific damage.

### Parent Oligonucleotide

The parent oligonucleotide is the original SSO that is used as a reference for generating a library of variants. The parent oligonucleotide is typically a known SSO with a specific sequence and chemical modifications that have been previously characterized for their biological activity and safety profile.

### Library of Variants

A library of variants is generated by introducing various modifications to the parent oligonucleotide. These modifications can include changes in the sequence, chemical modifications, and stereochemistry. The library of variants is then screened using the in vitro assay to identify SSOs with improved safety profiles and reduced hepatotoxicity.

### Stereodefined Oligonucleotide Embodiments:

Stereodefined oligonucleotides are SSOs with defined stereochemistry at specific positions. The stereochemistry of the internucleoside linkages can significantly affect the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of SSOs, including their hepatotoxicity. By generating stereodefined oligonucleotides, it is possible to optimize the safety and efficacy of SSOs.

### Primary Hepatocytes Assay Example:

#### Example 1: Isolation of Primary Mouse Hepatocytes

Primary mouse hepatocytes were isolated from 10- to 13-week-old male C57Bl6 mice using a retrograde two-step collagenase liver perfusion. The isolated hepatocytes were cultured in William’s Medium E (WME) supplemented with 10% fetal calf serum, penicillin, and streptomycin. The cells were pre-cultured for 3 to 4 hours to allow for attachment to the cell culture plates before the start of treatment with SSOs.

#### Example 2: Gymnotic Delivery of SSOs

The isolated hepatocytes were treated with SSOs using gymnotic delivery. SSOs were administered at concentrations ranging from 1 to 30 μM, and the cells were incubated with the SSOs for 3 days. The SSOs were directed against mouse myd88 mRNA and included both safe and toxic variants.

#### Example 3: Measurement of LDH Release

After 3 days of treatment, the culture medium was collected, and LDH release was measured using a Cytotoxicity Detection Kit. The results showed that toxic SSOs (36, 37, 43, and 47) caused a significant increase in LDH release, while safe SSOs (32, 33, and 35) did not.

#### Example 4: Measurement of Intracellular ATP Levels

Intracellular ATP levels were measured using a CellTiter-Glo® Luminescent Cell Viability Assay. The results showed that toxic SSOs (37, 43, and 47) caused a marked reduction in ATP levels, while safe SSOs (32, 33, and 35) had a minimal effect.

#### Example 5: Measurement of GSH Levels

Intracellular GSH levels were measured using a fluorescent assay with monochlorobimane. The results showed that toxic SSOs (37, 43, and 47) induced a significant reduction in GSH levels, while safe SSOs (32, 33, and 35) did not.

#### Example 6: Measurement of Caspase-3/7 Activity

Caspase-3/7 activity was measured using a Caspase-Glo® 3/7 Assay. The results showed that toxic SSOs (36, 37, 43, and 47) induced a clear increase in caspase-3/7 activity after 48 hours of treatment, while safe SSOs (32 and 35) did not.

#### Example 7: Measurement of Cytokine Secretion

The secretion of cytokines, such as IL-1α and MIP-1α, was measured using a multiplex ELISA. The results showed that toxic SSOs (36, 37, and 43) induced a significant increase in the secretion of these cytokines, while safe SSOs (32 and 35) did not.

#### Example 8: Measurement of miR-122 Release

The release of miR-122 into the culture medium was measured using a TaqMan miRNA assay. The results showed that toxic SSOs (37 and 43) caused a significant increase in miR-122 release, while safe SSOs (32 and 35) did not.

#### Example 9: Validation with a Larger Set of SSOs

To further validate the in vitro assay, a larger set of 34 SSOs targeting different mRNAs and showing a broad spectrum of mouse in vivo hepatotoxicity was tested. The results showed a good correlation between in vivo hepatotoxicity and in vitro readouts for 32 out of 34 SSOs, with only 2 SSOs where in vivo toxicity was not predicted with the cellular assay.

## Definitions

### Oligonucleotide

An oligonucleotide is a short polymer of nucleotides, typically ranging from 10 to 30 nucleotides in length. Oligonucleotides can be single-stranded or double-stranded and are used in various applications, including gene silencing, gene editing, and diagnostic assays.

### Antisense Oligonucleotides (ASOs)

Antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) are single-stranded oligonucleotides that bind to complementary RNA sequences through Watson-Crick base pairing. ASOs can modulate gene expression by recruiting RNAseH to cleave the target RNA, leading to its degradation.

### Modified Internucleoside Linkages

Modified internucleoside linkages are chemical modifications introduced into the phosphodiester backbone of oligonucleotides to enhance their stability, pharmacokinetic properties, and biological activity. Common modifications include phosphorothioate, phosphorodithioate, and boranophosphate linkages.

### Modulation of Expression

Modulation of expression refers to the alteration of gene expression levels through the use of antisense oligonucleotides, small interfering RNAs (siRNAs), or other gene regulatory molecules. This can be achieved by promoting or inhibiting the transcription, splicing, or translation of target genes.

### Modified Oligonucleotides

Modified oligonucleotides are oligonucleotides that have been chemically modified to enhance their stability, pharmacokinetic properties, and biological activity. Common modifications include the introduction of locked nucleic acid (LNA) residues, 2'-O-methoxyethyl (MOE) modifications, and phosphorothioate linkages.

### RNase H Activity and Recruitment

RNase H is an enzyme that cleaves the RNA strand in an RNA-DNA hybrid. Antisense oligonucleotides can recruit RNase H to cleave the target RNA, leading to its degradation and modulation of gene expression.

### Efficacy

Efficacy refers to the ability of a drug or therapeutic agent to produce a desired effect. In the context of antisense oligonucleotides, efficacy is typically measured by the degree of target gene knockdown and the resulting biological or therapeutic effect.

### Conjugate Moieties

Conjugate moieties are chemical groups or molecules attached to oligonucleotides to enhance their delivery, stability, or biological activity. Common conjugate moieties include cholesterol, galactose, and polyethylene glycol (PEG).

### Stereodefined Oligonucleotides

Stereodefined oligonucleotides are oligonucleotides with defined stereochemistry at specific positions. The stereochemistry of the internucleoside linkages can significantly affect the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties of oligonucleotides, including their hepatotoxicity.

## Immunotoxicity Biomarker Assay:

In addition to the hepatotoxicity assay, the present invention also provides an immunotoxicity biomarker assay to evaluate the potential for SSOs to induce immune responses. The assay involves the measurement of cytokine secretion and other immune-related markers in primary hepatocytes and non-parenchymal cells treated with SSOs. The results of the immunotoxicity biomarker assay can be used in conjunction with the hepatotoxicity assay to provide a comprehensive assessment of the safety profile of SSOs.
