# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method and system for the remediation of heavy metal-contaminated soil. More specifically, the invention pertains to an asymmetrical alternating current electrochemistry (AACE) method that combines a recirculating soil washing system with an electrochemical filtration device to efficiently remove heavy metals from contaminated soil while minimizing the consumption of chelating agents and preventing secondary pollution.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Soil contamination, particularly by heavy metals, is a significant environmental issue affecting both urban and agricultural lands. Heavy metals, such as copper (Cu), lead (Pb), and cadmium (Cd), can accumulate in soil due to anthropogenic activities such as mining, industrial processes, and improper waste disposal. These contaminants pose serious health risks to humans and wildlife, leading to various toxicological effects and environmental degradation.

Traditional methods for remediating heavy metal-contaminated soil, such as soil washing with strong chelating agents, have several limitations. These methods often require large amounts of chelating agents, generate significant volumes of contaminated washing effluent, and can lead to the loss of essential soil nutrients. Additionally, the high costs and environmental impacts associated with these methods limit their widespread application.

Another approach involves the use of high-surface-area sorbents to immobilize heavy metals. However, this method suffers from slow capture rates and low capacities, necessitating long-term monitoring to ensure the stability of immobilized heavy metals.

Electrochemical methods, particularly those involving direct current (DC) electrochemistry, have shown promise in the remediation of heavy metal-contaminated soil. These methods typically apply a DC electric field to the soil to transport heavy metal species by electroosmosis and deposit them on the negative electrode. However, the high DC voltages required, the slow ion migration speeds in soil, and the significant energy losses associated with water splitting at the electrodes limit the practicality and efficiency of these methods.

There is a clear need for an innovative and efficient method to remediate heavy metal-contaminated soil that addresses the limitations of existing techniques. The present invention fulfills this need by providing a novel AACE method that combines a recirculating soil washing system with an electrochemical filtration device to achieve high degrees of heavy metal removal while minimizing the consumption of chelating agents and preventing secondary pollution.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and system for the remediation of heavy metal-contaminated soil using asymmetrical alternating current electrochemistry (AACE). The method comprises the following steps:

1. Excavating contaminated soil from a site and placing it in a treatment cylinder.
2. Attaching two reservoir tanks containing a chelating agent, such as ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) solution, to the treatment cylinder.
3. Circulating the chelating agent solution through the contaminated soil to mobilize heavy metal cations.
4. Transporting the mobilized heavy metal cations to an AACE filtration device.
5. Applying an asymmetrical alternating current (AACE) to the AACE filtration device to electrochemically reduce the heavy metal cations to their zero-valent metallic states and deposit them on the working electrode.
6. Recycling the chelating agent solution for repeated use.

The AACE filtration device comprises two parallel amidoxime-functionalized porous carbon (Ami-PC) electrodes and a separator. The Ami-PC electrodes are designed to enhance the electrodeposition efficiency by providing strong chelation sites that compete with the chelating agent to bind heavy metal cations and stabilize them on the electrode surface.

The AACE method offers several advantages over existing soil remediation techniques:
- **High Efficiency**: The AACE method achieves high degrees of heavy metal removal from contaminated soil under a range of different concentrations.
- **Recycling of Chelating Agents**: The method recycles the chelating agent solution, significantly reducing the overall consumption of chelating agents and preventing secondary pollution.
- **Preservation of Soil Nutrients**: The AACE method selectively removes heavy metals while preserving essential soil nutrients, ensuring that the treated soil remains fertile and suitable for agricultural use.
- **Energy Efficiency**: The AACE method operates at lower voltages compared to traditional DC electrochemical methods, reducing energy consumption and operational costs.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Construction of the AACE Remediation System

The AACE remediation system consists of a recirculating chelating agent washing system and an AACE filtration device. The soil is excavated from contaminated sites and placed in a treatment cylinder. Two reservoir tanks containing a chelating agent solution, such as ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA), are attached to the treatment cylinder. A water pump circulates the EDTA solution through the contaminated soil column, mobilizing heavy metal cations by forming heavy metal-EDTA complexes.

The AACE filtration device is connected to the recirculating system and is composed of two parallel amidoxime-functionalized porous carbon (Ami-PC) electrodes and a separator. The Ami-PC electrodes are fabricated by coating a carbon felt with a precursor slurry of polyacrylonitrile (PAN) and activated carbon, followed by a hydrothermal reaction to substitute the nitrile functional groups in PAN with amidoxime functional groups. The amidoxime functional groups serve two primary functions: they modify the carbon felt surface to hydrophilic, thereby fully utilizing the high-surface area of the electrodes, and they provide strong chelation sites that compete with EDTA to bind heavy metal cations, promoting the electrodeposition efficiency.

### Working Principle of the AACE Method

The AACE method operates by applying an asymmetrical alternating current to the Ami-PC electrodes. The waveform of the applied bias consists of a 5 V positive bias followed by a −10 V negative bias, with durations of 0.5 ms and 2 ms, respectively. The process can be explained in three steps:

1. **Step I**: All ions are randomly distributed in the washing effluent.
2. **Step II**: A 5 V positive bias is applied, causing the formation of an electrical double layer on the surface of the Ami-PC electrode, with anions in the inner layer. The chelation sites of amidoxime compete with EDTA to bind heavy metal cations, stabilizing the heavy metal-EDTA complexes on the electrode surface.
3. **Step III**: The bias is reversed to −10 V, electrochemically reducing the heavy metal cations to their zero-valent metallic states and depositing them on the working electrode. EDTA anions lose their affinity for these charge-neutral particles and are repelled by the negative bias.

### Remediation Performance

Experiments were conducted to evaluate the remediation performance of the AACE method using synthetically contaminated soil samples spiked with different heavy metals (Cu, Pb, and Cd). The AACE method successfully reduced the concentrations of Cu, Pb, and Cd in the contaminated soil samples to below the California Human Health Screening Levels (CHHSL) for residential scenarios. The method also recycled the EDTA solution for repeated use, consuming very limited EDTA.

The AACE filtration efficiency, defined as the percentage of heavy metal cations electrodeposited by the AACE filter from the washing effluent, can be promoted by allowing a slower soil washing flow rate. The optimal flow rate, providing a filtration efficiency above 90%, was determined for each soil sample.

### Remediation Mechanism

The heavy metal species extracted by the AACE method and a direct current (DC) method were characterized and compared to investigate the remediation mechanism. SEM images showed that the AACE method produced micrometer-sized heavy metal particles attached to the Ami-PC electrode, while the DC method resulted in a uniform thick shell on the electrode, showing no crystal structure. EDS analysis confirmed the presence of strong peaks of Cu, Pb, and Cd for the AACE method, while the DC method accumulated a large amount of Ca on the electrode.

XPS analysis revealed that the heavy metal species extracted by the AACE method were predominantly in their zero-valent metallic states, while those extracted by the DC method were primarily in their metal hydroxide forms. TEM images further confirmed the polycrystalline nature of the Cu, Pb, and Cd particles extracted by the AACE method.

### Long-Term Performance and Plant Assay

The long-term performance of the AACE filter and the recyclability of the EDTA solution were evaluated over 25 treatment cycles. The recycled EDTA solution showed minimal decay in its extraction ability, and the AACE filtration efficiency remained high throughout the cycles. The AACE filter was successfully regenerated using 0.1 M HCl as the elution solution with a DC reverse bias of 1 V applied to the electrode.

Plant assays using pea (Pisum sativum) sprouts demonstrated the feasibility of the AACE method for agricultural land remediation. The AACE method effectively reduced the Cd concentration in the soil to levels below the safety threshold, and the planted pea sprouts met international food standards. No significant differences in pea growth were observed between the blank control and the AACE remediated soil, indicating that the treated soil remained fertile and suitable for cultivation.

### Conclusion

The AACE method provides a fast, efficient, and environmentally friendly solution for the remediation of heavy metal-contaminated soil. By combining a recirculating soil washing system with an electrochemical filtration device, the AACE method achieves high degrees of heavy metal removal while minimizing the consumption of chelating agents and preventing secondary pollution. The method's ability to selectively remove heavy metals while preserving soil nutrients and its long-term stability and regenerability make it a promising technology for large-scale soil remediation and the recovery of valuable heavy metals from waste streams in various manufacturing and chemical industries.
