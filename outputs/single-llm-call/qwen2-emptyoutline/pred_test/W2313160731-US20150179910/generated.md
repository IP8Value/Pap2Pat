# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of heat transfer fluids (HTFs) and, more specifically, to novel benzyl ethers synthesized as potential HTFs for use in high-temperature industrial processes, particularly in concentrated solar power (CSP) systems. The invention encompasses the synthesis of these ethers, their physical and thermal properties, and their suitability as HTFs.

## BACKGROUND ART

The selection of heat transfer fluid and the system design in industrial processes are critical factors for achieving maximum heat transfer with minimal input energy and cost. Heat transfer fluids (HTFs) and thermal oils for high-temperature applications vary widely in chemical composition. These fluids can be synthetic, including ester and diester, polyglycol and water-glycol based fluids, as well as silicone-based greases and oils. Non-synthetic HTFs often consist of petroleum or mineral oils, some of which contain water. For high operating temperatures in heat storage and transport, liquid sodium or molten nitrate salts (sodium nitrate-potassium nitrate) can be used. Historically, most organic heat transfer fluids based on aromatic or aliphatic petrochemicals were used in the temperature range of 0 to 350°C. However, at higher temperatures, these fluids tend to decompose or oxidize, leading to a loss of performance and potential safety hazards.

Synthetic organic HTFs have gained increasing interest due to their superior thermal properties, despite being more expensive than non-synthetic alternatives. These fluids are evaluated based on several key characteristics, including high heat capacity, high thermal conductivity, high flash point, a broad operating temperature range (low melting point and high boiling point), low viscosity and density, and high vapor pressure. Additionally, environmental and safety considerations, such as toxicity and potential pollution, are also important.

Many traditional HTFs exhibit poor heat transfer characteristics, requiring higher flow rates and greater pumping power to achieve the same energy transfer efficiency as systems using water. This inefficiency increases operational costs and energy consumption. Therefore, there is a continuous need for the development of new HTFs that can operate efficiently at high temperatures while maintaining desirable physical and thermal properties.

## DISCLOSURE OF THE INVENTION

The present invention addresses the aforementioned needs by providing novel benzyl ethers synthesized as potential heat transfer fluids (HTFs). These ethers are designed to have high specific heat capacities, stability at high temperatures, and other favorable properties that make them suitable for use in high-temperature industrial processes, particularly in concentrated solar power (CSP) systems.

### Summary of the Invention

The invention involves the synthesis of benzyl ethers through a one-pot etherification-Suzuki cross-coupling protocol. This protocol can be carried out solventless with palladium on carbon (Pd/C) as a catalyst under ambient atmosphere, making it more resource-efficient than conventional reaction processes. The synthesized ethers have been calculated to have a relatively high specific heat capacity, which has been validated experimentally in some cases. Thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) has shown that the extended ethers, such as 8b, are stable up to 300°C, even in air, making them suitable for high-temperature applications.

### Detailed Description of the Invention

#### Synthesis of Benzyl Ethers

The benzyl ethers of the present invention are synthesized through a one-pot etherification-Suzuki cross-coupling protocol. This protocol combines the etherification of bromobenzyl bromides with alcohols and a subsequent Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling reaction. The reactions can be carried out solventless with Pd/C as a catalyst under ambient atmosphere, which reduces the need for solvents and simplifies the process.

1. **Etherification Step**: The etherification step involves the reaction of bromobenzyl bromides with alcohols under phase transfer catalysis (PTC) using ground potassium hydroxide as a base and tetralkylammonium halides as phase transfer catalysts. The reactions can be heated up to 75°C without noticeable decomposition of the ammonium salt phase transfer catalyst. For example, the reaction of 4-bromobenzyl bromide with 2-phenylethanol yields 4-bromobenzyl 2-phenylethyl ether (2i) in sufficient purity for further use.

2. **Suzuki Cross-Coupling Step**: The Suzuki cross-coupling step involves the reaction of the bromobenzyl ethers with arylboronic acids under Pd/C catalysis. The reactions are carried out at 75°C for 24 hours under normal atmosphere. For example, the reaction of 4-bromobenzyl 2-phenylethyl ether with 4-methoxyboronic acid yields the desired biphenylmethyl phenylpropyl ether (5a) in 69% isolated yield.

#### Physical and Thermal Properties

The physical and thermal properties of the synthesized benzyl ethers have been estimated using group contribution methods. Key properties include:

- **Isobaric Heat Capacity (Cp)**: The isobaric heat capacity of the liquid ethers has been estimated using a three-level group contribution method. The specific heat capacities of the extended ethers, such as 8b, are appreciably high, with experimental measurements showing good agreement with the estimated values.
- **Melting Point (Tm)**: The melting points of the ethers have been estimated using Marrero and Gani's model. For simple molecules, there is good agreement between the calculated and measured values. However, for extended ethers, the calculated values do not match the measured values due to the complex geometric structure and molecular packing.
- **Boiling Point (Tb)**: The boiling points of the ethers have also been estimated using Marrero and Gani's model.
- **Critical Temperature (Tc)**: The critical temperatures of the ethers have been estimated using the same model.
- **Density**: Density measurements have shown a linear relationship between density and temperature up to 90°C, with a decrease in density as temperature increases.
- **Thermal Stability**: Thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA) has shown that the extended ethers, such as 8b, are stable up to 300°C, even in air.

## MODE(S) FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### General Procedure for Synthesis

1. **Etherification Step**:
   - **Reagents**: Bromobenzyl bromide, alcohol, ground potassium hydroxide, tetralkylammonium halide.
   - **Conditions**: Solventless, 75°C, 24 hours.
   - **Procedure**: Mix the bromobenzyl bromide and alcohol with ground potassium hydroxide and tetralkylammonium halide. Heat the mixture to 75°C and stir for 24 hours. After the reaction, add water and filter the solidified reaction product to obtain the ether in sufficient purity.

2. **Suzuki Cross-Coupling Step**:
   - **Reagents**: Bromobenzyl ether, arylboronic acid, Pd/C catalyst, benzyltributylammonium bromide (BTBAC).
   - **Conditions**: Solventless, 75°C, 24 hours, normal atmosphere.
   - **Procedure**: Mix the bromobenzyl ether and arylboronic acid with Pd/C catalyst and BTBAC. Heat the mixture to 75°C and stir for 24 hours. After the reaction, isolate the product using standard techniques such as column chromatography on silica gel.

### Example Synthesis

#### Example 1: Synthesis of 4-Bromobenzyl 2-Phenylethyl Ether (2i)

1. **Etherification Step**:
   - **Reagents**: 4-bromobenzyl bromide (10.0 g, 38.5 mmol), 2-phenylethanol (5.0 g, 38.5 mmol), ground potassium hydroxide (2.0 g), tetrabutylammonium bromide (0.5 g).
   - **Conditions**: Solventless, 75°C, 24 hours.
   - **Procedure**: Mix 4-bromobenzyl bromide, 2-phenylethanol, ground potassium hydroxide, and tetrabutylammonium bromide in a round-bottom flask. Heat the mixture to 75°C and stir for 24 hours. After the reaction, add water and filter the solidified reaction product to obtain 4-bromobenzyl 2-phenylethyl ether (2i) in sufficient purity.

2. **Suzuki Cross-Coupling Step**:
   - **Reagents**: 4-bromobenzyl 2-phenylethyl ether (2i) (5.0 g, 15.4 mmol), 4-methoxyboronic acid (2.5 g, 18.5 mmol), Pd/C (0.5 g, 5 wt% Pd), BTBAC (0.5 g).
   - **Conditions**: Solventless, 75°C, 24 hours, normal atmosphere.
   - **Procedure**: Mix 4-bromobenzyl 2-phenylethyl ether (2i), 4-methoxyboronic acid, Pd/C catalyst, and BTBAC in a round-bottom flask. Heat the mixture to 75°C and stir for 24 hours. After the reaction, isolate the product using column chromatography on silica gel to obtain the desired biphenylmethyl phenylpropyl ether (5a) in 69% isolated yield.

### Physical and Thermal Property Estimation

   - **Method**: Three-level group contribution method.
   - **Formula**:
     \[
     C_p = \sum_{i} N_i C_{p,i} + \sum_{j} M_j C_{p,j} + \sum_{k} O_k C_{p,k} + C_{p,0}
     \]
     \[
     C_{p,i} = a_i + b_i T + d_i T^2
     \]
   - **Results**: The specific heat capacities of the extended ethers, such as 8b, are appreciably high, with experimental measurements showing good agreement with the estimated values.

2. **Melting Point (Tm)**:
   - **Method**: Marrero and Gani's model.
   - **Formula**:
     \[
     T_m = \sum_{i} N_i T_{m1i} + \sum_{j} M_j T_{m2j} + \sum_{k} O_k T_{m3k} + T_{m0}
     \]
   - **Results**: For simple molecules, there is good agreement between the calculated and measured values. However, for extended ethers, the calculated values do not match the measured values due to the complex geometric structure and molecular packing.

3. **Boiling Point (Tb)**:
   - **Method**: Marrero and Gani's model.
   - **Formula**:
     \[
     T_b = \sum_{i} N_i T_{b1i} + \sum_{j} M_j T_{b2j} + \sum_{k} O_k T_{b3k} + T_{b0}
     \]

4. **Critical Temperature (Tc)**:
   - **Method**: Marrero and Gani's model.
   - **Formula**:
     \[
     T_c = \sum_{i} N_i T_{c1i} + \sum_{j} M_j T_{c2j} + \sum_{k} O_k T_{c3k} + T_{c0}
     \]

5. **Density**:
   - **Method**: Density measurements.
   - **Results**: There is a linear relationship between density and temperature up to 90°C, with a decrease in density as temperature increases.

6. **Thermal Stability**:
   - **Method**: Thermal gravimetric analysis (TGA).
   - **Results**: The extended ethers, such as 8b, are stable up to 300°C, even in air.
