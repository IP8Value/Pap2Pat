## BACKGROUND

### 1. Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of neurology and more specifically to methods and compositions for treating spinal cord injury (SCI). The invention provides a novel monoclonal antibody, MHC1, that specifically targets and inhibits the opening of connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels, thereby reducing secondary damage and improving functional recovery after SCI.

### 2. Description of Related Art

Spinal cord injury (SCI) is a devastating condition that affects thousands of individuals annually, leading to significant physical and psychological disabilities. The primary injury, caused by the initial trauma, is followed by a cascade of secondary events that exacerbate the damage. These secondary events include edema, loss of blood flow, metabolic crisis, and spreading depolarizations, which collectively create a cytotoxic post-injury environment. This environment is characterized by elevated levels of extracellular molecules and ions such as glutamate, lactate, potassium (K+), nitric oxide, arachidonate, reactive oxygen species, and ammonia. These factors contribute to increased lesion size and hinder axonal regrowth, further complicating recovery.

Connexin 43 (Cx43) is a key protein that forms gap junctions and hemichannels in astrocytes within the spinal cord. Previous studies have shown that Cx43 expression is upregulated after SCI, and its hemichannels play a crucial role in the release of proinflammatory and neurotoxic factors, contributing to secondary damage. However, existing treatments that target Cx43 have limitations, often affecting both gap junctions and hemichannels, which can have unintended side effects.

There is a need for a therapeutic agent that specifically targets and inhibits Cx43 hemichannels without affecting gap junctions, thereby reducing secondary damage and improving functional recovery after SCI.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel monoclonal antibody, MHC1, that specifically inhibits the opening of connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels without affecting gap junctions. The invention encompasses methods and compositions for treating spinal cord injury (SCI) by administering MHC1 to reduce secondary damage, improve functional recovery, and preserve neural tissue.

The invention includes the following aspects:
1. A monoclonal antibody, MHC1, that specifically binds to the extracellular domain of Cx43 and inhibits the opening of Cx43 hemichannels.
2. Methods for treating SCI by administering MHC1 to a subject in need thereof.
3. Kits and diagnostics for detecting and monitoring the activity of Cx43 hemichannels in biological samples.

The invention demonstrates that MHC1 significantly reduces secondary injury, improves locomotion function, attenuates gliosis, preserves white and gray matter, and protects neurons. These findings suggest that MHC1 represents a new therapeutic strategy for treating SCI.

## DESCRIPTION

### I. ANTIBODIES

The present invention provides a novel monoclonal antibody, MHC1, that specifically targets and inhibits the opening of connexin 43 (Cx43) hemichannels. MHC1 is a mouse-human chimeric antibody containing the mouse variable domains and human constant domains. The antibody binds to the extracellular domain of Cx43 with high affinity and specificity, effectively inhibiting the opening of Cx43 hemichannels without affecting gap junctions.

#### Specificity and Binding Affinity

MHC1 was tested for its specificity to Cx43 using various assays. Immunocytochemistry and immunofluorescence studies on HeLa-Cx43 cells, which stably express Cx43, showed positive labeling with MHC1, confirming its binding to Cx43. In contrast, parental HeLa cells that do not express Cx43 were not labeled. The binding affinity of MHC1 to the Cx43 extracellular domain peptide was measured using ELISA and Bio-layer interferometry (Octet RED96). ELISA results showed a maximal binding at 15,018 ± 663.5, with an EC50 of 7.63 × 10–2 ± 2.19 × 10–2 nM. Bio-layer interferometry measured the dynamic binding affinity with an on and off rate, yielding a KD of 421 ± 7 nM.

#### Functional Inhibition of Cx43 Hemichannels

MHC1 was tested for its ability to inhibit the opening of Cx43 hemichannels in response to low extracellular Ca2+ and Mg2+. HeLa-Cx43 cells were incubated in Ca2+ and Mg2+-free medium, which induced hemichannel opening, as measured by increased ethidium bromide (EtBr) uptake. Preincubation with MHC1 (66.7 nM) significantly inhibited hemichannel opening, while IgG (66.7 nM) had no effect. MHC1 also inhibited hemichannel opening in medium containing normal levels of Ca2+ and Mg2+.

To further validate the specificity of MHC1, primary spinal cord astrocytes were treated with IL-1β, a proinflammatory factor known to activate Cx43 hemichannels. Preincubation with MHC1 (66.7 nM) significantly inhibited hemichannel opening, as measured by EtBr uptake. Mechanical loading, which mimics SCI, also induced hemichannel opening in primary spinal cord astrocytes, and this opening was inhibited by preincubation with MHC1.

### II. TREATMENT OF DISEASES

The present invention provides methods for treating spinal cord injury (SCI) by administering MHC1 to a subject in need thereof. The invention demonstrates that MHC1 significantly reduces secondary damage, improves functional recovery, and preserves neural tissue after SCI.

#### In Vivo Efficacy

MHC1 was tested in two partial SCI mouse models. In both models, MHC1 was administered intraperitoneally 30 minutes after SCI. Results showed that MHC1 significantly improved hind limb function, as measured by the Basso Mouse Scale (BMS), and reduced lesion size, gliosis, and neuronal loss. In the broad impact model, MHC1-treated mice showed a significant improvement in BMS scores starting from day 2 after SCI, with continual and gradual improvement over the assessment period. In the focused impact model, MHC1-treated mice displayed a greater improvement in BMS scores from day 26 to the end of the assessment period (day 56).

MHC1 also preserved white and gray matter, as measured by Eriochrome cyanine R (ECR) staining, and protected neurons, as indicated by the expression of the neuronal marker microtubule-associated protein 2 (MAP2). Additionally, MHC1 significantly reduced the expression of glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP), a marker for reactive astroglia and glial scars.

#### Late-Onset Treatment

The efficacy of MHC1 was also tested in a late-onset treatment paradigm. MHC1 was administered intracranial ventricular (ICV) 30 days after SCI. While MHC1 was detected in the injury region, it did not improve hind limb function or reduce gliosis, suggesting that MHC1 is most effective when administered during the acute phase of SCI.

### III. KITS AND DIAGNOSTICS

The present invention also provides kits and diagnostics for detecting and monitoring the activity of Cx43 hemichannels in biological samples. These kits can be used for research and clinical purposes to assess the effectiveness of MHC1 treatment and to monitor the progression of SCI.

#### Kit Components

The kits of the invention may include the following components:
1. MHC1 antibody
2. Control antibodies (e.g., IgG)
3. Ethidium bromide (EtBr) for hemichannel activity assays
4. Fluorescent dyes (e.g., FITC-dextran) for exclusion of nonspecific dye uptake
5. Buffers and reagents for cell culture and staining
6. Instructions for use

#### Diagnostic Applications

The diagnostic methods of the invention can be used to:
1. Detect the presence and activity of Cx43 hemichannels in biological samples.
2. Monitor the effectiveness of MHC1 treatment in reducing hemichannel activity.
3. Assess the extent of secondary damage and neural tissue preservation after SCI.

### IV. EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Specificity and Binding Affinity of MHC1

MHC1 was tested for its specificity to Cx43 using HeLa-Cx43 cells and primary spinal cord astrocytes. Immunocytochemistry and immunofluorescence studies confirmed the binding of MHC1 to Cx43. The binding affinity was measured using ELISA and Bio-layer interferometry, demonstrating high specificity and affinity.

#### Example 2: Functional Inhibition of Cx43 Hemichannels

MHC1 was tested for its ability to inhibit the opening of Cx43 hemichannels in response to low extracellular Ca2+ and Mg2+ and IL-1β treatment. Results showed that MHC1 significantly inhibited hemichannel opening, as measured by EtBr uptake, without affecting gap junctions.

#### Example 3: In Vivo Efficacy of MHC1

MHC1 was administered to mice subjected to partial SCI using two different models. Results showed that MHC1 significantly improved hind limb function, reduced lesion size, gliosis, and neuronal loss. MHC1 also preserved white and gray matter, as measured by ECR staining and MAP2 expression.

#### Example 4: Late-Onset Treatment with MHC1

MHC1 was administered ICV 30 days after SCI. While MHC1 was detected in the injury region, it did not improve hind limb function or reduce gliosis, suggesting that MHC1 is most effective when administered during the acute phase of SCI.

#### Example 5: Diagnostic Application of MHC1

A kit containing MHC1 and necessary reagents was used to detect and monitor the activity of Cx43 hemichannels in biological samples. The kit was effective in assessing the effectiveness of MHC1 treatment and monitoring the progression of SCI.

These examples demonstrate the utility and effectiveness of MHC1 in treating SCI and provide a foundation for further clinical and research applications.
