# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cell reprogramming, particularly to methods and compositions for enhancing the efficiency of direct cell conversion. More specifically, the invention provides a novel approach to modulate intracellular tension and cell adhesion to promote the reprogramming of somatic cells into induced neurons (iNs) and other cell types. The invention encompasses the use of small molecule inhibitors, genetic modifications, and engineered biomaterials to achieve these effects, thereby facilitating the generation of functional iNs for therapeutic and research purposes.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cell reprogramming is a powerful technique that enables the conversion of one cell type into another, bypassing the pluripotent state. This process holds significant promise for regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and drug discovery. Direct cell conversion, in particular, offers a faster and more direct method for generating desired cell types from somatic cells. For instance, fibroblasts can be directly converted into neurons, cardiomyocytes, and other cell types using transcription factors, microRNAs, and biophysical factors. However, the low conversion efficiency of these methods has hindered their clinical translation.

Recent studies have highlighted the importance of the actin cytoskeleton and focal adhesions (FAs) in sensing and transducing extracellular signals during cell reprogramming. The mechanical properties of cells, such as stiffness and deformability, are known to change during the early stages of reprogramming. These changes are thought to be mediated by the actin cytoskeleton and FAs, which play a crucial role in mechanotransduction. Despite this, the precise mechanisms by which these structural and mechanical changes influence the reprogramming process remain poorly understood.

There is a need for methods and compositions that can effectively modulate intracellular tension and cell adhesion to enhance the efficiency of cell reprogramming. Such advancements could significantly improve the yield and quality of induced neurons and other cell types, thereby accelerating their application in regenerative medicine and therapeutic discovery.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Introduction

The present invention is directed to methods and compositions for enhancing the efficiency of cell reprogramming by modulating intracellular tension and cell adhesion. The invention is based on the discovery that reducing intracellular tension and altering cell adhesion can promote a more open chromatin structure, which facilitates the activation of neuronal genes and the suppression of mesenchymal genes. This, in turn, enhances the reprogramming efficiency of somatic cells into induced neurons (iNs) and other cell types.

### Mechanisms of Action

#### Reduction of Intracellular Tension

Intracellular tension is primarily regulated by the actin cytoskeleton and focal adhesions (FAs). The actin cytoskeleton provides structural support and mediates cell movement, while FAs serve as integrin-based connections between the extracellular matrix (ECM) and the actin cytoskeleton. During the early stages of cell reprogramming, the actin cytoskeleton and FAs undergo significant changes, which are thought to be important for the reprogramming process.

The present invention demonstrates that reducing intracellular tension can enhance the reprogramming efficiency of somatic cells into induced neurons. This is achieved by treating the cells with small molecule inhibitors that disrupt the actin cytoskeleton or inhibit focal adhesion kinase (FAK). For example, blebbistatin, a non-muscle myosin II inhibitor, and PF573228, a FAK inhibitor, have been shown to increase the reprogramming efficiency of fibroblasts into iNs.

#### Modulation of Cell Adhesion

Cell adhesion is another critical factor that influences the reprogramming process. The present invention shows that modulating cell adhesion using biomaterials can also enhance the reprogramming efficiency. For example, culturing fibroblasts on polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) membranes with microgrooves or binary colloidal crystals (BCCs) composed of spherical particles with distinct sizes can reduce cell spreading and focal adhesion formation, thereby promoting a more open chromatin structure and enhancing the reprogramming efficiency.

### Methods and Compositions

#### Small Molecule Inhibitors

The invention provides methods for enhancing the reprogramming efficiency of somatic cells into induced neurons by treating the cells with small molecule inhibitors that reduce intracellular tension. Suitable small molecule inhibitors include, but are not limited to, blebbistatin, Y-27632 (a Rho-kinase inhibitor), and PF573228 (a FAK inhibitor).

Blebbistatin is a potent inhibitor of non-muscle myosin II, which plays a crucial role in actin cytoskeleton dynamics and cell contractility. Treatment with blebbistatin reduces intracellular tension, leading to a more open chromatin structure and enhanced reprogramming efficiency. The optimal concentration of blebbistatin for enhancing reprogramming efficiency is approximately 10 μM.

Y-27632 is a Rho-kinase inhibitor that prevents stress fiber formation and contraction. Treatment with Y-27632 also reduces intracellular tension and enhances reprogramming efficiency, although to a lesser degree than blebbistatin. The optimal concentration of Y-27632 for enhancing reprogramming efficiency is approximately 20 μM.

PF573228 is a FAK inhibitor that reduces focal adhesion formation and intracellular tension. Treatment with PF573228 enhances reprogramming efficiency by promoting a more open chromatin structure and the activation of neuronal genes. The optimal concentration of PF573228 for enhancing reprogramming efficiency is approximately 5 μM.

#### Genetic Modifications

The invention also provides methods for enhancing the reprogramming efficiency of somatic cells into induced neurons by genetically modifying the cells to reduce intracellular tension. Suitable genetic modifications include, but are not limited to, siRNA knockdown of non-muscle myosin heavy chain IIA (MYH9) and IIB (MYH10).

siRNA knockdown of MYH9 and MYH10 reduces the expression of non-muscle myosin II, leading to a reduction in intracellular tension and enhanced reprogramming efficiency. The siRNA sequences for MYH9 and MYH10 are commercially available and can be used to transfect the cells using standard transfection protocols.

#### Engineered Biomaterials

The invention provides methods for enhancing the reprogramming efficiency of somatic cells into induced neurons by culturing the cells on biomaterials that modulate cell adhesion. Suitable biomaterials include, but are not limited to, PDMS membranes with microgrooves and BCCs composed of spherical particles with distinct sizes.

PDMS membranes with microgrooves can be fabricated using soft lithography techniques. The microgrooves reduce cell spreading and focal adhesion formation, leading to a more open chromatin structure and enhanced reprogramming efficiency. The optimal microgroove size for enhancing reprogramming efficiency is approximately 10 μm.

BCCs can be fabricated by mixing two colloids and depositing them into culture wells. The BCCs reduce cell spreading and focal adhesion formation, leading to a more open chromatin structure and enhanced reprogramming efficiency. The optimal particle size for enhancing reprogramming efficiency is approximately 2 μm.

### Applications

The methods and compositions described herein have a wide range of applications in regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and therapeutic discovery. For example, the enhanced reprogramming efficiency of somatic cells into induced neurons can be used to generate large numbers of functional iNs for transplantation in neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson's disease and Alzheimer's disease. The iNs can also be used to create disease models for studying the underlying mechanisms of neurological disorders and for screening potential therapeutic compounds.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Transient Nuclear Deformation Primes Epigenetic State and Promotes Cell Reprogramming

In this example, we demonstrate that transient nuclear deformation can prime the epigenetic state and promote cell reprogramming. Fibroblasts were transduced with doxycycline (Dox)-inducible lentiviral vectors encoding the reprogramming factors Brn2, Ascl1, and Myt1l (BAM) and seeded onto tissue culture polystyrene dishes coated with laminin. Dox was added one day later to initiate the expression of the transgenes, and the cells were cultured in neuronal medium (N3 medium) from day 1 to the conclusion of the experiment.

Immunofluorescence analysis revealed that by day 1 of the reprogramming process, actin assembled into a network with a cage-like structure around the nucleus, but this structure along with the majority of the cytoskeleton gradually disappeared by day 3. High-throughput quantitative deformability cytometry (q-DC) and atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements showed that cell stiffness increased by day 1 and decreased by day 3, coinciding with the observed cytoskeletal changes. These results suggest that the actin cytoskeleton and mechanical properties of cells are modulated during the early phase of reprogramming, possibly playing a role in iN conversion.

#### Example 2: Reduction of Intracellular Tension and Cell Adhesion Promotes Open Chromatin Structure and Enhances Cell Reprogramming

In this example, we demonstrate that reducing intracellular tension and cell adhesion can promote a more open chromatin structure and enhance cell reprogramming. Fibroblasts were transduced with BAM and cultured in the presence of blebbistatin (10 μM) or PF573228 (5 μM) for 7 days. The reprogramming efficiency was determined by immunostaining for neuronal β-III tubulin (TUBB3) and counting the number of TUBB3+ iN cells.

Treatment with blebbistatin and PF573228 significantly increased the reprogramming efficiency compared to the control. Immunofluorescence analysis showed that blebbistatin and PF573228 treatment increased the expression of histone marks associated with open chromatin structure (e.g., histone H3 acetylation [AcH3], tri-methylated histone H3 on lysine 4 [H3K4me3], and monomethylated histone H3 on lysine 4 [H3K4me1]) and decreased the expression of heterochromatin marks (e.g., tri-methylated histone H3 on lysine 27 [H3K27me3] and tri-methylated histone H3 on lysine 9 [H3K9me3]). These results suggest that reducing intracellular tension and cell adhesion can promote a more open chromatin structure and enhance the reprogramming efficiency.

#### Example 3: 3D Spheroid Culture to Increase Cell Reprogramming Efficiency

In this example, we demonstrate that culturing fibroblasts in 3D spheroids can increase the reprogramming efficiency. Fibroblasts were transduced with BAM and cultured in 3D spheroids for 7 days. The reprogramming efficiency was determined by immunostaining for TUBB3 and counting the number of TUBB3+ iN cells.

Culturing fibroblasts in 3D spheroids significantly increased the reprogramming efficiency compared to 2D culture. Immunofluorescence analysis showed that 3D spheroid culture increased the expression of histone marks associated with open chromatin structure and decreased the expression of heterochromatin marks. These results suggest that 3D spheroid culture can promote a more open chromatin structure and enhance the reprogramming efficiency.

#### Example 4: Mechano-Epigenetic Mapping for Guided Gene Activation and Silencing

In this example, we demonstrate the use of mechano-epigenetic mapping to guide gene activation and silencing during cell reprogramming. Fibroblasts were transduced with BAM and cultured in the presence of blebbistatin (10 μM) or PF573228 (5 μM) for 7 days. The cells were then subjected to chromatin immunoprecipitation-quantitative polymerase chain reaction (ChIP-qPCR) to analyze the expression of histone marks at the promoter regions of neuronal genes (e.g., Ascl1, Brn2, Myt1l, and TUBB3).

ChIP-qPCR analysis revealed significant increases in AcH3, H3K4me3, and H3K4me1 at the promoter regions of neuronal genes in blebbistatin- and PF573228-treated cells compared to the control. These results suggest that reducing intracellular tension and cell adhesion can promote the activation of neuronal genes and the suppression of mesenchymal genes, thereby enhancing the reprogramming efficiency.

In this example, we demonstrate that matrix stiffness can regulate the epigenetic state and enhance cell reprogramming. Immunofluorescence analysis showed that softer substrates increased the expression of histone marks associated with open chromatin structure and decreased the expression of heterochromatin marks. These results suggest that matrix stiffness can regulate the epigenetic state and enhance the reprogramming efficiency.
