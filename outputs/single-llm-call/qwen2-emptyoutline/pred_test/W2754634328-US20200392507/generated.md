# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of medical therapeutics, specifically to the treatment of hypertrophic scars. More particularly, the invention pertains to a novel method and composition for reducing the size and severity of hypertrophic scars by simultaneously silencing the expression of transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) using small interfering RNA (siRNA) technology. The invention further involves the use of a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) to enhance the delivery of siRNA to the target tissue, thereby achieving a synergistic reduction in scar size and an improvement in the overall therapeutic outcome.

## BACKGROUND

Hypertrophic scars (HS) are a significant clinical challenge due to their persistent and often disfiguring nature. These scars result from an imbalance between the deposition and degradation of extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins during the wound healing process. Transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) are key regulators of fibrosis and play crucial roles in the formation and maintenance of hypertrophic scars. TGF-β1 promotes fibroblast proliferation and collagen synthesis, while COX-2 is involved in the production of prostaglandins, which can modulate the inflammatory response and fibroblast activity.

Despite numerous treatment modalities, including surgical excision, radiation, corticosteroid injections, and laser therapy, the management of hypertrophic scars remains suboptimal. Recent advances in RNA interference (RNAi) technology have opened new avenues for therapeutic intervention. Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) can specifically target and silence the expression of TGF-β1 and COX-2, thereby reducing the excessive deposition of ECM and promoting the resolution of hypertrophic scars.

However, efficient delivery of siRNAs to the target tissue remains a significant challenge. Biodegradable polymers, such as histidine-lysine co-polymers (HKP), have been developed to enhance the delivery of siRNAs. HKP can form stable nanoparticles with siRNAs, facilitating their uptake by target cells and prolonging their therapeutic effect.

The present invention addresses these challenges by providing a novel method and composition for the simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 using HKP-packaged siRNAs. This approach not only achieves a synergistic reduction in scar size but also demonstrates a novel anti-fibrotic mechanism of action, making it a promising therapeutic strategy for the treatment of hypertrophic scars.

## DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and composition for reducing the size and severity of hypertrophic scars by simultaneously silencing the expression of transforming growth factor beta 1 (TGF-β1) and cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) using small interfering RNA (siRNA) technology. The invention further involves the use of a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP) to enhance the delivery of siRNA to the target tissue.

### Composition

The composition of the invention comprises:

1. **Small Interfering RNAs (siRNAs)**: Specific siRNAs targeting the mRNA sequences of TGF-β1 and COX-2. These siRNAs are designed to efficiently silence the expression of the target genes, thereby reducing the production of pro-fibrotic factors and promoting the resolution of hypertrophic scars.

2. **Histidine-Lysine Co-Polymer (HKP)**: A biodegradable polymer that forms stable nanoparticles with the siRNAs. The HKP enhances the delivery of siRNAs to the target tissue, facilitates their uptake by cells, and prolongs their therapeutic effect.

### Method

The method of the invention involves the following steps:

1. **Design and Selection of siRNAs**: siRNAs targeting the mRNA sequences of TGF-β1 and COX-2 are designed and selected based on their silencing efficiency and toxicity profiles. The siRNAs are tested in vitro to ensure their ability to effectively silence the target genes.

2. **Preparation of HKP-siRNA Nanoparticles**: The selected siRNAs are mixed with HKP in an aqueous solution at an optimized N/P ratio (4/1; weight/weight). The mixture self-assembles into nanoparticles, which are then lyophilized into a dry powder and re-formulated with an aqueous solution before use.

3. **Intradermal Administration**: The HKP-siRNA nanoparticles are administered intradermally to the hypertrophic scar tissue. Multiple injections are made at different sites of the scar to ensure even distribution of the therapeutic agent.

4. **Therapeutic Evaluation**: The effectiveness of the treatment is evaluated by measuring the size of the hypertrophic scar, the expression levels of TGF-β1, COX-2, and other pro-fibrotic factors, and the induction of apoptosis in fibroblasts/myofibroblasts.

### Mechanism of Action

The simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 using HKP-packaged siRNAs results in a synergistic reduction in the size of hypertrophic scars. The mechanism of action includes:

1. **Downregulation of Pro-Fibrotic Factors**: Silencing TGF-β1 and COX-2 leads to a significant reduction in the expression of pro-fibrotic factors such as α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA), collagen 1 (Col1A1), and collagen 3 (Col3A1). This reduces the excessive deposition of ECM and promotes the resolution of hypertrophic scars.

2. **Induction of Apoptosis**: The treatment induces apoptosis in fibroblasts/myofibroblasts, which are the primary cells responsible for the excessive production of ECM. This helps to reduce the number of active fibroblasts and further contributes to the reduction of scar size.

3. **Modulation of TGF-β Signaling Pathway**: COX-2 modulates the activation of the TGF-β signaling pathway, and silencing COX-2 results in a decrease in the expression levels of downstream proteins such as SMAD2/3 and PAI-1. This further amplifies the anti-fibrotic effect of the treatment.

### Advantages

1. **Synergistic Effect**: The simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 achieves a synergistic reduction in scar size, which is more effective than silencing either gene individually.

2. **Enhanced Delivery**: The use of HKP enhances the delivery of siRNAs to the target tissue, ensuring a higher therapeutic effect and minimizing off-target effects.

3. **Novel Mechanism of Action**: The treatment not only reduces the expression of pro-fibrotic factors but also induces apoptosis in fibroblasts/myofibroblasts, providing a novel anti-fibrotic mechanism of action.

4. **Clinical Viability**: The method and composition of the invention are clinically viable, with a therapeutic dose that is significantly lower than other siRNA-based treatments, making it a promising therapeutic strategy for the treatment of hypertrophic scars.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions

In this example, we designed and tested siRNAs targeting the mRNA sequences of TGF-β1 and COX-2. The siRNAs were selected based on their silencing efficiency and toxicity profiles. Human fibroblasts isolated from hypertrophic scar tissue were transfected with the selected siRNAs targeting either TGF-β1 or COX-2 individually or in combination. The measurements of target gene silencing after the transfection indicated significant knockdown of target gene expression, with either TGF-β1siRNA or COX-2siRNA themselves, or upon combination TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs. Notably, the combination treatment also downregulated the expression of other pro-fibrotic factors such as α-SMA, Col1A1, and Col3A1. These results indicate that silencing TGF-β1 and COX-2 simultaneously in fibroblasts downregulates multiple pro-fibrotic factors.

### Example 2: Simultaneous Silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 Gene Expressions Resulted in Activation of Apoptosis of Fibroblasts/Myofibroblasts

We further investigated the fate of fibroblasts when pro-fibrotic factors were downregulated. Electron microscope images of the fibroblast cells transfected with the TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs combination illustrated that the treatment induced apoptosis in fibroblasts. FACS analyses of the hypertrophic scar fibroblasts (HSFs) treated with the TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs combination revealed a marked increase in the apoptotic cell population compared to those treated with either TGF-β1siRNA or COX-2siRNA individually. Fibroblasts treated with the TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs combination showed a significant reduction in the expression of α-SMA protein, as measured by immunofluorescence staining. These results indicate that the simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 gene expressions activates apoptosis in fibroblasts/myofibroblasts.

### Example 3: HKP Enhances siRNA Delivery Into Human Hypertrophic Scar

To ensure efficient siRNA delivery to the hypertrophic scar, we used a biodegradable histidine-lysine co-polymer (HKP). When HKP and siRNA are mixed in an aqueous solution with an optimized N/P ratio (4/1; weight/weight), self-assembly of nanoparticles occurs. The lyophilized HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA) nanoplex powder, once reconstituted, has an average size of about 150 nm in diameter and a Zeta potential of about 40 mV. The nanoparticles are stable at 4°C and exhibit potent silencing activity for TGF-β1 up to 12 months. In a human hypertrophic scar tissue implant mouse model, qRT-PCR analysis of the tissue samples from HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) treatments revealed TGF-β1 knockdown and COX-2 knockdown in a dose-dependent manner. The HKP-packaged siRNA illustrated a quick dispersion and lasting release, detectable even after 48 hours. These results indicate that HKP enhances siRNA delivery into human hypertrophic scar tissue.

### Example 4: HKP-Packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA Reduces Size of Human Hypertrophic Scar

In this example, we evaluated the therapeutic effect of HKP-packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA on human hypertrophic scar tissue. Human hypertrophic scar tissues were implanted onto nude mice subcutaneously. After four weeks, 20 μg/50 μl/cm³ of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) was administered to each scar implant using 5 aliquots into 5 different sites of the scar, with three repeated injections at 5-day intervals. The HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) combination-treated HS implants showed a significant reduction in size of implanted tissues at day 28 post-treatment, by about 45% compared to the untreated group. Tissue samples from the implants were further analyzed, and the results showed significant silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2, as well as downregulation of α-SMA and Col1A1. These results provide strong evidence that the simultaneous inhibition of TGF-β1 and COX-2 enhances scar reduction.

### Example 5: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) Reduces Size of Human Skin Grafts

Similar to the hypertrophic scar implants, human skin grafted onto nude mice was used to evaluate the therapeutic effect of HKP-packaged TGF-β1/COX-2siRNA. Four weeks after the surgery, 20 μg/50 μl/cm³ of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) was injected into each skin graft using 5 aliquots into 5 different sites of the graft, with three repeated injections at 5-day intervals. The HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) combination-treated human skin grafts resulted in a significant size reduction at day 28 post-treatment, by about 40% compared to the untreated group. Tissue samples from the skin grafts were further analyzed, and the results showed significant silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2, as well as downregulation of α-SMA and Col1A1. These results are similar to those observed with the hypertrophic scar implants and provide further evidence that simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 enhances scar reduction.

### Example 6: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Demonstrates a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Mechanism of Action

To investigate the underlying biology of the observed scar tissue reductions, we measured the hydroxyproline acid level from the tissue samples and compared the differences between the treated and control groups. In both human scar and skin implants, HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) treatment resulted in a significant downregulation of hydroxyproline acid expression. Histological analyses using H&E, Masson's trichrome staining, and immunohistochemistry (IHC) staining against human α-SMA, VEGF, and CD3 demonstrated marked differences in tissue structure and expression levels of pro-fibrotic factors. TUNEL assays further confirmed a significant increase in apoptosis of fibroblasts in the treated tissue samples. These results indicate that HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) demonstrates a novel anti-fibrotic mechanism of action by inducing apoptosis in fibroblasts and downregulating pro-fibrotic factors.

### Example 7: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Induced Apoptotic Activities of the Fibroblast/Myofibroblasts In Vivo

We further confirmed the induction of apoptosis in fibroblasts/myofibroblasts in vivo. Histological images illustrated a marked increase in apoptosis of fibroblasts in the treated tissue samples. Quantitative analysis confirmed a significant increase in the number of apoptotic cells. These results are consistent with the in vitro findings and provide strong evidence that HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) induces apoptotic activities of fibroblasts/myofibroblasts in vivo.

### Example 8: HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) Serves as a Novel Anti-Fibrotic Therapeutic Agent

The therapeutic benefits observed in the human hypertrophic scar models further validate the potential of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) as a novel anti-fibrotic therapeutic agent. The enhanced potency of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) observed in this study is attributed to the dual-targeted drug design and the HKP-enhanced delivery to the site of action. The significant silencing effects on TGF-β1 and COX-2 expressions, with only microgram levels of the siRNA inhibitors, support higher transfection efficiency of HKP-packaged siRNA nanoparticles. The activation and upregulation of fibroblast apoptosis within human hypertrophic scars, both in vitro and in vivo, reveal a novel underlying mechanism of action. These results indicate that HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) is a clinically viable novel siRNA therapeutic to reduce human skin hypertrophic scarring.

## Discussion

The dermal wound healing process involves multiple interactions within a complex network of pro-fibrotic and anti-fibrotic molecules. TGF-β1 and COX-2 are critical regulators of fibrosis and play crucial roles in the formation and maintenance of hypertrophic scars. The simultaneous silencing of TGF-β1 and COX-2 using HKP-packaged siRNAs achieves a synergistic reduction in scar size by downregulating pro-fibrotic factors and inducing apoptosis in fibroblasts/myofibroblasts. The enhanced delivery of siRNAs using HKP ensures a higher therapeutic effect and minimizes off-target effects. The results from this study provide strong evidence that HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2 siRNAs) is a promising therapeutic strategy for the treatment of hypertrophic scars.

## Materials and Methods

### siRNA Sequence Design and Selection

25-mer blunt-ended siRNA duplexes targeting homologous regions of human, mouse, and porcine TGF-β1 or COX-2 mRNA sequences were designed. Eight siRNAs for each gene were screened in human PC3 cells. Briefly, 2.5 × 10^5 PC3 cells were seeded on the wells of 6-well plates on the day before transfection in 2 ml of ATCC-formulated F-12K medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum. On the next day, cells were transfected with Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, CA) packaged siRNA complexes according to the manufacturer's recommendation. After 48-hour incubation, total RNAs were isolated from the cells with RNAqueous-4 PCR kit (Ambion, TX) followed by qRT-PCR analyses. Based on the potencies of target gene silencing of each siRNA sequence, the most potent siRNA was selected for future tests.

### Growth Inhibition In Vitro

Cells were seeded into the wells of 6-well plates at a density of 10^6 cells per well in 3 ml of media. Six hours later, the media was replaced with fresh media containing HKP alone, HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs), HKP (TGF-β1siRNA), or HKP (COX-2siRNA) at concentrations of 10 μg/ml HKP loaded with 5 μg/ml siRNA. For the Annexin V/PI assay, transfected fibroblasts were harvested and resuspended at a density of 10^6 cells/ml and then washed with cold PBS twice. Cells were resuspended in 60 μl of binding buffer, and Annexin-V-FITC (4 μl; 20 μg/ml) (Dead Cell Apoptosis Kit with Annexin V Alexa Fluor® 488 & Propidium Iodide, Invitrogen, CA) was added. Cells were incubated in the dark on ice for 15 minutes. Before processing, PI (4 μl; 50 μg/ml) was added to the suspension. For cell cycle analysis, the transfected fibroblasts were harvested and washed with PBS three times. Cells were fixed with 70% ethanol at 4°C overnight and then washed twice with PBS. Cell pellets were resuspended in propidium iodide (500 μl; 50 μg/ml) and kept at 4°C overnight. The suspension was run through a single cell sieve to make a single cell suspension, which was analyzed by FACS (EPICS Altra, Beckman Coulter, Holland).

### Nanoparticle Preparation

Optimized histidine-lysine polymers (HKP) were used for siRNA delivery in vivo. One of the HKP peptides, H3K4b, having a lysine core with four branches that contain multiple repeats of histidines and lysines, was used for packaging siRNAs targeting TGF-β1 and COX-2. The HKP carrier: siRNA payload ratio was 4:1 by mass. The HKP and siRNA self-assembled into nanoparticles with an average size of 150 nm in diameter. These HKP (siRNA) nanoparticles suspended in an aqueous solution were injected into the scar tissue.

### Mouse

The 8-week-old male nude mice (nu/nu Balb/c) were purchased from the Center for Experimental Animals in Shanghai, China. Animal housing and experiment protocols were approved by the Independent Ethics Committee of Shanghai Ninth People's Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine.

### Human Hypertrophic Scar and Skin Tissue Implant Models

#### Human Hypertrophic Scar Tissue Implant Model
Skin hypertrophic scar tissue was obtained from a surgical excision with informed consent and was trimmed off subcutaneous fat and cut into pieces of 2 cm². 6–8 weeks old male nude mice were anesthetized with 10% chloral hydrate, and a piece of trimmed hypertrophic scar tissue was implanted under the skin on the mouse back. Scar tissue was fixed to the mouse deep fascia with 4–5 sutures before the skin was closed.

#### Human Skin Tissue Grafting Model
The tissue samples used in experiments were from skin excisions of three women (ages 23–36) who had undergone breast reconstruction for the treatment of macromastia. All patients had signed the informed consent voluntarily. Skin excisions were acquired in sterile conditions, cut into sizes of 2 cm × 1.6 cm, and kept in 20% FBS DMEM media at 4°C before use. Male nude mice of 6–8 weeks old were anesthetized with 10% chloral hydrate, and a piece of skin (2 cm × 1.6 cm) was excised from the back. A same-size human skin was grafted to replace the excision by sutures to subcutaneous fascia and surrounding mouse skin. A stack of sterile cotton was placed on top of the graft and tightly wrapped. Stitches were taken off two weeks later.

### Therapeutic Evaluation with the Human Tissue Implant Models

Four weeks after the human hypertrophic scar implantation, 20 μg/50 μl/cm³ of HKP (TGF-β1/COX-2siRNAs) was injected into the scar established on the mouse body. To ensure even drug distribution, injections were performed into 5 areas—4 quadrants and 1 center. Every dose of the drug was injected in 5 equal aliquots. Scars were injected three times for the period of 15 days (once per 5 days). Scar size was evaluated before and after treatment. At day 1, 2, 5, and at the end of the experiment, animals were euthanized, and scar tissue was immediately harvested and homogenized in Trizol solution with Polytrone (Brinkmann Homogenizer Polytron PT 10/35). Total RNA was extracted and RNA levels of TGF-β1, COX-2, α-SMA, Col1a1, and Col3a1 were analyzed by qRT-PCR. The in situ Cell Death Detection Kit from Roche (South San Francisco, CA, USA) was used for detection of apoptotic cells from the STP705-treated scar tissues, following the vendor's instructions.

### Statistical Analysis

Mean ± SD was used for cell culture results, and mean ± SE was used for in vivo results. Student's t-test was used to determine significance between two groups. P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. IBM SPSS statistics ver. 20 was used for statistical analysis.
