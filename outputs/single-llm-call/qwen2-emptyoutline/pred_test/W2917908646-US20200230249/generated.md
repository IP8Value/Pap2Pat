# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of pharmaceutical chemistry, specifically to the development of peptide therapeutics. More particularly, the invention pertains to the design and synthesis of peptide-oligourea hybrids that exhibit improved pharmacological properties, such as enhanced stability and prolonged in vivo activity, while maintaining or improving the biological activity of the parent peptide. The invention is particularly applicable to the development of GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) agonists, which are valuable therapeutic agents for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and other related conditions.

## BACKGROUND

Peptide therapeutics have gained significant attention in pharmaceutical research and development due to their ability to target extracellular and intracellular protein-protein interactions (PPIs) effectively. However, peptides often suffer from poor membrane permeability, low bioavailability, and short in vivo half-lives, which limit their therapeutic potential. Various strategies have been explored to overcome these limitations, including sequence modifications, conjugation to molecules with albumin-binding properties, and fusion to large proteins. Despite these efforts, the development of stable and long-acting peptide therapeutics remains challenging.

Foldamers, a class of synthetic polymers that adopt well-defined secondary structures, have emerged as a promising alternative to peptides. Among foldamers, oligoureas have attracted particular interest due to their structural similarity to peptides, metabolic compatibility, water solubility, and resistance to proteases. Oligoureas can form helical structures that do not disrupt the propagation of peptide α-helices, making them suitable for designing peptide-oligourea hybrids.

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a critical endocrine peptide hormone that regulates insulin secretion in response to glucose levels. The GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) is a G protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) that plays a crucial role in glucose homeostasis. GLP-1R agonists are widely used for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and have shown promise in addressing other conditions such as obesity, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), and cognitive disorders. However, the rapid degradation of GLP-1 by proteases, particularly dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) and neutral endopeptidase 24.11 (NEP 24.11), limits its therapeutic utility.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a series of GLP-1-oligourea hybrids that exhibit moderate to excellent affinity to the GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) and demonstrate prolonged in vivo activity. The invention involves the strategic replacement of specific amino acid residues in the GLP-1 sequence with oligourea triads, resulting in peptide-oligourea hybrids that maintain or enhance the biological activity of the parent peptide while improving its stability and pharmacokinetic properties.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a GLP-1-oligourea hybrid comprising a sequence of GLP-1-NH2 with a glycine residue at position 2 and one or more oligourea triads replacing specific amino acid residues. The oligourea triads are designed to mimic the α-helical conformation of the native peptide, ensuring that the hybrid maintains its binding affinity to GLP-1R. The invention further includes methods for synthesizing these hybrids using solid-phase peptide synthesis techniques and microwave-assisted coupling reactions.

The invention also encompasses the use of these GLP-1-oligourea hybrids in the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and other conditions responsive to GLP-1R agonists. The improved stability and prolonged in vivo activity of the hybrids make them suitable for once-daily or once-weekly administration, enhancing patient compliance and therapeutic outcomes.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Peptide-Oligourea Hybrids Design and Functional Assay

The design of the GLP-1-oligourea hybrids began with the native GLP-1-NH2 sequence, with a glycine residue introduced at position 2 to prevent degradation by DPP-4. The key aspect of the design was to identify the optimal number of consecutive amino acid residues that could be replaced by oligourea triads without significantly affecting the binding affinity to GLP-1R. Based on structural models, it was determined that replacing four consecutive amino acids with three consecutive ureido residues (Xu3) would provide the best overlap of terminal atoms and maintain the α-helical conformation.

Initial hybrids were synthesized with alanine triads (Alau) to avoid any negative impact from the side chains of the oligourea residues. The agonistic activity of these hybrids was evaluated using functional assays in cells expressing the GLP-1R, measuring the receptor-mediated cAMP production. Many of the Alau triad hybrids exhibited potent activity, demonstrating the good overlap of the oligourea backbone with the peptide backbone.

Subsequent rounds of synthesis focused on introducing native side chains into the oligourea triads to recover important side chain interactions. The most active hybrids were selected for further evaluation. Notably, the hybrid GG2[YuEuAu]14–17 (9) showed a better affinity (0.18 nM) than the native peptide (0.24 nM). Other hybrids, such as GG2[DuαAuAu]9–12 (2) and GG2[FuαIuAu]22–25 (16), retained substantial affinity with GLP-1R, despite the significant modifications.

### Enzymatic and Mouse Plasma Degradation Studies

To assess the stability of the GLP-1-oligourea hybrids, enzymatic degradation studies were conducted using NEP 24.11 and mouse plasma. The native peptide GLP-1-G2 (1) had a half-life of 3.6 hours in the presence of NEP 24.11 and 16 minutes in mouse plasma. Several hybrids, including GG2[YuEuAuAuAuAu]14–21 (23), showed improved stability in both assays, with a significant increase in half-life in mouse plasma (over 3.8-fold improvement for hybrid 22).

### In Vivo Studies

The in vivo efficacy of the GLP-1-oligourea hybrids was evaluated in normal mice and db/db mice. Blood glucose concentration studies demonstrated that the hybrids 14, 22, and 23 had prolonged activity, maintaining a decrease in blood glucose concentration for up to 4 hours after dosing. Intraperitoneal glucose tolerance tests (IPGTT) confirmed the prolonged activity of these hybrids, with significant effects observed even 4 hours after dosing.

### Pharmacokinetic Evaluation

A pharmacokinetic study was conducted to evaluate the in vivo half-life of the native peptide GLP-1-G2 (1) and the hybrid 22. The hybrid 22 exhibited a longer half-life compared to the native peptide, consistent with the improved stability observed in the enzymatic and plasma degradation studies.

### Combination with Protraction Strategy

The invention further explores the combination of the peptide/oligourea hybrid approach with protraction strategies, such as functionalizing the peptide with a fatty acid chain to promote binding to albumin. Hybrid 24, a semaglutide analogue with an oligourea fragment replacing the last four amino acids, was synthesized and evaluated. The hybrid 24 showed similar in vitro and in vivo activity to semaglutide but with improved stability in mouse plasma and pancreatin, suggesting potential for oral administration.

### Methods

#### Synthesis of GLP-1 Analogues

The GLP-1-oligourea hybrids were synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis starting from Sieber amide resin. The synthesis was conducted using a Liberty BlueTM microwave peptide synthesizer. N-Fmoc-α amino acids and activated monomers were coupled using standard coupling reagents and microwave irradiation. The azido group was reduced, and the final product was cleaved from the resin and purified by HPLC.

#### In Vitro Pharmacology (EC50)

The agonist activity of the hybrids at the GLP-1 receptor was determined by measuring cAMP production in βTC6 cells using the HTRF detection method. The results were expressed as a percentage of the control response to 10 nM GLP-1, and EC50 values were calculated using GraphPad Prism.

#### Enzymatic Degradation (NEP 24.11 and Pancreatin)

Stability of the hybrids to NEP 24.11 and pancreatin was assessed by conducting protease reactions and analyzing the degradation products by HPLC and LC-MS, respectively.

#### Mouse Plasma Stability

The stability of the hybrids in mouse plasma was evaluated by incubating the compounds in plasma/PBS and analyzing the degradation products by LC-MS.

#### Animal Studies

All animal studies were conducted in accordance with ethical guidelines and approved protocols. Blood glucose experiments and IPGTTs were performed in normal and db/db mice to evaluate the in vivo efficacy of the hybrids. Pharmacokinetic studies were conducted to determine the in vivo half-life of the hybrids.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel approach to improving the pharmaceutical properties of bioactive peptides by incorporating oligourea triads. The GLP-1-oligourea hybrids exhibit enhanced stability, prolonged in vivo activity, and maintained or improved biological activity, making them valuable candidates for the development of therapeutic agents for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and other related conditions.

### Examples

#### Example 1: Synthesis and Characterization of GLP-1-oligourea Hybrid 9 (GG2[YuEuAu]14–17)

**Synthesis:**
1. Sieber amide resin (160 mg, 0.62 mmol/g) was loaded with the N-terminal sequence of GLP-1-NH2.
2. N-Fmoc-α amino acids and activated monomers were coupled using standard coupling reagents and microwave irradiation.
3. The azido group was reduced, and the final product was cleaved from the resin.
4. The crude product was purified by HPLC to obtain the pure GLP-1-oligourea hybrid 9.

**Characterization:**
- **Mass Spectrometry:** The molecular weight of the hybrid 9 was confirmed by mass spectrometry.
- **HPLC Analysis:** The purity of the hybrid 9 was determined by HPLC, showing a single peak with a retention time of 15.2 minutes.
- **In Vitro Activity:** The hybrid 9 exhibited an EC50 value of 0.18 nM in the cAMP production assay, indicating improved affinity compared to the native peptide.

#### Example 2: Enzymatic and Plasma Stability of GLP-1-oligourea Hybrid 22 (GG2[AuAuAu]28–31)

**Enzymatic Degradation (NEP 24.11):**
1. Stock solutions of the hybrid 22 and NEP 24.11 were prepared.
2. Protease reactions were conducted, and aliquots were taken at various time points.
3. The degradation products were analyzed by HPLC, showing a half-life of 4.5 hours for hybrid 22.

**Plasma Stability:**
1. Stock solutions of the hybrid 22 were prepared in water.
2. The hybrid 22 was incubated in mouse plasma/PBS and aliquots were taken at various time points.
3. The degradation products were analyzed by LC-MS, showing a half-life of over 60 minutes for hybrid 22.

#### Example 3: In Vivo Efficacy of GLP-1-oligourea Hybrid 23 (GG2[YuEuAuAuAuAu]14–21)

**Blood Glucose Study:**
1. Normal mice were treated with the hybrid 23 via intravenous injection.
2. Blood glucose concentration was measured at various time points.
3. The hybrid 23 showed a significant decrease in blood glucose concentration for up to 4 hours after dosing.

**IPGTT Study:**
1. Fasted normal mice were subjected to an intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test 2 hours after dosing with the hybrid 23.
2. The hybrid 23 demonstrated good control of blood glucose concentration during the IPGTT, confirming its prolonged in vivo activity.

#### Example 4: Pharmacokinetic Evaluation of GLP-1-oligourea Hybrid 22

1. Mice were treated with the hybrid 22 via intravenous injection.
2. Blood samples were collected at various time points, and the plasma was separated by centrifugation.
3. The samples were analyzed by LC-MS/MS to determine the concentration of the hybrid 22 in plasma.
4. The hybrid 22 exhibited a longer half-life compared to the native peptide GLP-1-G2, consistent with the improved stability observed in the enzymatic and plasma degradation studies.

#### Example 5: Combination with Protraction Strategy

**Synthesis of Hybrid 24 (Semaglutide Analogue with Oligourea Fragment):**
1. The semaglutide analogue with an oligourea fragment replacing the last four amino acids was synthesized using solid-phase peptide synthesis.
2. The azido group was reduced, and the final product was cleaved from the resin and purified by HPLC.

**In Vivo Efficacy:**
1. db/db mice were treated with the hybrid 24 via intraperitoneal injection.
2. Blood glucose concentration was measured at various time points.
3. The hybrid 24 showed similar in vivo activity to semaglutide but with improved stability in mouse plasma and pancreatin, suggesting potential for oral administration.

These examples illustrate the successful design, synthesis, and evaluation of GLP-1-oligourea hybrids, demonstrating their potential as improved therapeutic agents for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus and other related conditions.
