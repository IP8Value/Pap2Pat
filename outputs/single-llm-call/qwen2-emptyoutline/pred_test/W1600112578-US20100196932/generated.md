# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Alzheimer's disease (AD) is a severe neurodegenerative disorder characterized by the extracellular deposition of amyloid plaques and the intraneuronal accumulation of neurofibrillary tangles in the brain of affected individuals. A 42 amino acid long Aβ42 peptide, generated by proteolytic processing of the amyloid precursor protein (APP), is a major component of the amyloid plaques. Historically, Aβ42 fibers have been considered the primary pathogenic agents of AD. However, recent findings suggest that fibrillar aggregates may represent inert dead-end products of the Aβ42 aggregation pathway. Instead, considerable evidence now indicates that the primary neurotoxic effects are associated with soluble SDS-stable assemblies of Aβ42, such as 56 kDa Aβ42 dodecamers or smaller, low-n (dimers, trimers, and tetramers) oligomers of Aβ42, which appear during the early stages of Aβ42 assembly. These oligomers are believed to be more toxic and are the focus of therapeutic interventions aimed at inhibiting the earliest stages of Aβ42 oligomerization.

Yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae is a simple and readily manipulable organism that has been successfully used as a model for various medicinal studies, including neurodegenerative disorders associated with the deposition of amyloid aggregates. One of the most valuable contributions of yeast biology to the investigation of neurodegenerative disorders in animals was made by studying yeast prions. The yeast translational termination factor Sup35p can form self-propagating infectious amyloid aggregates that arise spontaneously in the cell and manifest a prion phenotype referred to as [PSI+]. The essential Sup35p protein is composed of three domains: the 124 amino acid long N-terminal domain (N), which is glutamine and asparagine-rich and required for prion properties; the highly charged middle (M) domain; and the C-terminal RF (release factor) domain, which performs termination of protein translation and is essential for viability.

The yeast chaperone Hsp104, a member of the AAA+ protein family, is required for the successful maintenance of the [PSI+] prion. Hsp104 shears the SDS-stable Sup35p prion amyloid aggregates into smaller structures in an ATP-dependent manner, maintaining them in numbers sufficient for successful transmission to daughter cells. The ATPase activity of Hsp104 is inhibited by millimolar concentrations of guanidine, which is therefore employed as a yeast prion-curing agent.

This invention describes a yeast model of the initial steps of Aβ42 oligomerization. The model utilizes the easily scored activity of Sup35p's MRF domain, which is impaired in AβMRF fusions due to the formation of SDS-stable low-n oligomers by the Aβ42 portion. The model system represents a convenient tool to perform chemical and genetic screens for agents that interfere with the earliest steps of Aβ42 oligomerization.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a yeast model system for studying the initial steps of Aβ42 oligomerization. Specifically, the invention provides a method for assessing the ability of compounds to inhibit the formation of Aβ42 oligomers by utilizing a fusion protein comprising the Aβ42 peptide and the MRF domain of the yeast translational termination factor Sup35p. The activity of the MRF domain is impaired when the Aβ42 portion forms SDS-stable low-n oligomers, which can be detected by the yeast phenotype. The invention also includes methods for screening compounds that interfere with Aβ42 oligomerization and the use of the model system for identifying potential therapeutic agents for Alzheimer's disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Model of Aβ42 Oligomerization

The present invention provides a yeast model system for studying the initial steps of Aβ42 oligomerization. The model utilizes a fusion protein, AβMRF, which consists of the Aβ42 peptide fused to the MRF domain of the yeast translational termination factor Sup35p. The MRF domain is essential for the termination of protein translation, and its activity can be conveniently assayed in vivo by examining the efficiency with which protein synthesis terminates at a premature stop codon (a nonsense-suppression assay). The assay uses the ade1-14 nonsense allele. Strains carrying this mutation and expressing fully active NMRF (Sup35p containing the N, M, and RF domains) are white and Ade+ when NMRF is in the aggregated [PSI+] prion form. In contrast, strains expressing an aggregation-deficient and fully functional form of Sup35p lacking the non-essential N-terminal domain (MRF) are red and Ade-. This well-established system reliably distinguishes between fully active monomers and malfunctioning aggregated forms of NMRF.

To establish the model of Aβ42 oligomerization, the Aβ42 peptide was fused to the MRF domain of Sup35p, and the resulting protein, AβMRF, was mutagenized in its Aβ42 portion according to a recent model of Aβ42 oligomerization. The model suggests that binding of one Aβ42 molecule to another occurs through four regions: amino acids 15–21, 24–32, 35–37, and 40–42 of one molecule bind to the corresponding regions in another molecule. Substitutions of Phe19, Phe20, and Ile31 were previously shown to inhibit aggregation of Aβ42 in vitro and prevent its neurotoxic effects. To obtain an oligomerization-deficient control for the AβMRF fusion protein, the first and second aggregation-important regions of Aβ42 were disrupted by making double AβF19,20TMRF (Aβm1MRF) or triple AβF19,20T/I31PMRF (Aβm2MRF) substitutions in the Aβ42 portion of AβMRF.

Yeast cells expressing the AβMRF fusion protein were white on complex medium and grew on -Ade, suggesting that the translation termination activity of the fusion protein was impaired. In contrast, yeast expressing Aβm1MRF or Aβm2MRF were dark pink on complex medium and Ade-, suggesting that the efficiency of translation termination was almost completely restored by the mutations in the Aβ42 portion of the fusion protein. No growth difference was detected in the control experiment on +Ade medium. These results are consistent with the hypothesis that the presence of Aβ42 in the fusion protein caused it to aggregate into SDS-stable oligomers, thereby affecting its translation termination activity.

### Example 2

To test whether AβMRF formed SDS-stable oligomers, yeast lysates treated with 1% SDS at room temperature were analyzed by immunoblotting. Prionized NMRF ([PSI+]) migrates in the form of SDS-stable aggregates, while MRF, which is unable to prionize, is monomeric. The pool of AβMRF contained both monomers and SDS-stable complexes migrating at the predicted positions for AβMRF low-n oligomers (dimers, trimers, and tetramers). The SDS-stable oligomers of AβMRF were able to withstand treatment with 2% SDS at room temperature and disaggregated into monomers only after boiling. These observations support the hypothesis that the presence of Aβ42 confers AβMRF with the ability to form low-n oligomers similar to the oligomerization of the Aβ42 peptide in vitro and in the human brain.

### Example 3

To obtain additional evidence that it is the intact Aβ42 peptide fused to the MRF that conferred the AβMRF fusion protein with the ability to form low-n oligomers, point mutants of AβMRF were analyzed by SDS-electrophoresis and immunoblotting. Mutations in the aggregation-important regions of Aβ42 were expected to inhibit oligomerization of the fusion protein. Consistent with this expectation, disruption of a single aggregation-important region of Aβ42 (Aβm1MRF) reduced its ability to form low-n oligomers. Disruption of a second aggregation-important region (Aβm2MRF) further inhibited oligomerization of the protein, although small amounts of Aβm2MRF were found in the form of dimers. This is likely due to the fact that this construct still retained two out of four aggregation-important regions intact. The presence of Aβm2MRF dimers explains the observation that these cells were dark pink on complex medium and not entirely as red as when a non-tagged MRF protein was expressed. The mere presence of Aβ42 on the N-terminus of MRF might slightly inhibit the translation termination activity of the fusion protein. Nevertheless, the fact that point mutations in the aggregation-important regions of Aβ42 impeded oligomerization of AβMRF and restored its activity illustrates the ability of the system to clearly distinguish between different levels of AβMRF oligomerization.

### Example 4

As growth of [PSI+] yeast in the presence of the prion curing agent guanidine increased the size of SDS-resistant Sup35p aggregates, the effect of guanidine on AβMRF oligomers was investigated. Sup35Δ cells expressing AβMRF, Aβm1MRF, or Aβm2MRF were grown in the presence of 6.3 mM guanidine. Strikingly, guanidine dramatically increased the amount of AβMRF and Aβm1MRF oligomers, while depleting the monomeric pool of the proteins. No such effect was detected with the control Aβm2MRF protein. As guanidine enhances the Sup35p [PSI+] aggregate size by inhibiting the ATPase activity of Hsp104, it was hypothesized that guanidine's effect on the accumulation of AβMRF oligomers was likewise due to Hsp104 inhibition. Contrary to this hypothesis, deletion of HSP104 decreased the proportion of AβMRF oligomers in three independent hsp104Δ clones. In addition, hsp104Δ led to a decrease in the total amount of AβMRF in the cells by ~40%, while having no effect on the level of Aβm2MRF protein. As might be expected, such a decrease in the total amount of AβMRF in hsp104Δ cells resulted in more frequent readthrough of the premature stop codon of the ade1-14 allele: hsp104Δ caused AβMRF-expressing sup35Δ cells to grow slightly better on -Ade, while having no effect on Aβm2MRF-expressing sup35Δ cells.

### Example 5

To test if Hsp104 interacts with AβMRF, HA-tagged AβMRF and HA-tagged MRF were used to co-immunoprecipitate Hsp104. Hsp104 co-immunoprecipitated with AβMRF but not with MRF. This is consistent with observations suggesting that Hsp104 interacts with Aβ42 in vitro. It is hypothesized that in the system, oligomers of AβMRF undergo continuous disaggregation, and at the same time, oligomers and monomers of AβMRF undergo degradation, as a result of interaction between an unknown cellular factor(s) and the Aβ42 portion of AβMRF. Hsp104 binds to the Aβ42 portion of AβMRF and may physically impede interaction between Aβ42 and the factors that trigger degradation and disaggregation of the fusion protein. Deletion of HSP104 led to a ~40% decrease in the total amount of the AβMRF protein, possibly as a result of increased susceptibility of AβMRF to degradation-triggering factors. At the same time, deletion of HSP104 shifted the equilibrium between oligomers and monomers such that the monomer's share in the overall pool of AβMRF increased from 34 to 47%, possibly as a result of AβMRF disaggregation.

### A Suppressible Allele

The invention also includes a suppressible allele of the yeast translational termination factor Sup35p, which can be used to study the effects of Aβ42 oligomerization on protein function. The suppressible allele, ade1-14, is a nonsense mutation that results in the production of a truncated, inactive version of Ade1p. When the efficiency of translational termination at the premature stop codon of the ade1-14 allele is compromised, the cells gain the ability to grow on -Ade and do not accumulate red pigment. This system provides a convenient and reliable method to assess the activity of the MRF domain in the presence of Aβ42 oligomers.

### Example 7

To further validate the yeast model system, the effect of guanidine on AβMRF oligomerization was examined in the absence of HSP104. Guanidine stimulated oligomerization of AβMRF even in the absence of HSP104, suggesting that guanidine affects other cellular factors involved in the disaggregation and degradation of AβMRF. This finding supports the hypothesis that Hsp104 protects AβMRF from the effects of such factors. The yeast model system described in this invention provides an opportunity to examine the ability of different compounds (proteinaceous or chemical) to interfere with the process of AβMRF oligomerization. Abrogation of oligomerization of the AβMRF will lead to the accumulation of AβMRF monomers, causing inhibition of growth in the absence of adenine and a redder color on complex medium, thus providing a simple functional readout. This model system represents a convenient tool to test or perform chemical and genetic screens for agents that interfere with the earliest steps of Aβ42 oligomerization.
