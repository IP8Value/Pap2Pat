# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Human activity monitoring plays a pivotal role in enhancing the quality of life, particularly for aging populations who increasingly rely on caregivers or loved ones for their well-being. Ubiquitous or ambient computing offers a promising solution by seamlessly integrating wireless sensors and computers, thereby improving daily efficiency and autonomy. The deployment of low-cost, widespread wireless sensors can catalyze the ambient computing revolution, providing essential support for individuals with mental or physical disabilities who may require immediate assistance.

Traditional methods for human activity detection, such as camera systems, on-body sensors (infrared sensors, accelerometers, gyroscopes), and FMCW radar, have been widely utilized. However, these methods come with significant drawbacks. Video monitoring demands substantial computational resources and raises privacy and security concerns, making it unsuitable for ubiquitous use. Wearable sensors, while effective, can be cumbersome and unreliable due to user forgetfulness or disinterest. Radar systems, though advanced, are often expensive and not commercially viable for widespread implementation.

Radio Frequency Identification (RFID) technology, known for its low cost and widespread use in inventory tracking, security, and asset management, has emerged as a promising alternative for human activity tracking. RFID systems operate through backscattering, where a reader transmits a continuous wave (CW) to a tag, which then retransmits a modulated signal back to the reader. By analyzing the Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) and phase information of the tag readings, various human activities can be classified. However, existing RFID-based systems suffer from limited spatial sensitivity and detection range, requiring the user to be in close proximity or within the line of sight between the tag and reader. This limitation restricts the practicality of these systems in real-world scenarios.

To address these challenges, a novel approach has been developed that leverages the cross-polarization of RFID reader and tag antennas. This approach aims to enhance the spatial sensitivity and detection range of RFID-based human activity detection systems, making them more practical and reliable for widespread use.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method and system for enhancing the spatial sensitivity and detection range of RFID-based human activity detection systems by utilizing cross-polarized RFID reader and tag antennas. The invention exploits the cross-polarization between the reader and tag antennas to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and enable the detection of subtle body movements over a larger area.

In a conventional RFID-based ranging system, the magnitude and phase of the backscattered wave from the RFID tag to the reader are recorded to determine the tag's location. Most existing systems use linearly polarized (LP) tags with circularly polarized (CP) readers, which can lead to significant polarization mismatch loss and reduced detection sensitivity. The present invention proposes a cross-polarized configuration, where the reader antenna is circularly polarized (CP) and the tag antenna is also circularly polarized but with the opposite handedness (e.g., right-hand circular polarized (RHCP) reader and left-hand circular polarized (LHCP) tag).

When electromagnetic waves are reflected off a conductive surface, such as human tissues, the handedness of the polarization is flipped. This phenomenon allows the reflected signal to be received by the reader without polarization mismatch loss, thereby enhancing the SNR and improving the detection of human activities. The cross-polarized configuration suppresses the direct line-of-sight (LOS) backscattering signal, which acts as noise, while amplifying the reflected signal that carries information about human movements.

The invention includes a custom-designed circularly polarized RFID tag that can be easily integrated into existing RFID systems. The tag is fabricated using a spiral antenna design, which ensures frequency independence and consistent performance across a wide range of frequencies. The system also incorporates data preprocessing techniques to calibrate the environment and reduce the impact of multipath effects, allowing for accurate and reliable detection of human activities.

Experiments conducted in both anechoic chamber and real-life environments demonstrate the effectiveness of the cross-polarized configuration in enhancing spatial sensitivity and detection range. The system can accurately detect a variety of activities, including nodding, shaking, hand-waving, and respiration, over a larger area compared to traditional LP tags. The improved SNR and extended range make the system suitable for ambient computing applications in homes, offices, and healthcare settings.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The present invention provides a method and system for enhancing the spatial sensitivity and detection range of RFID-based human activity detection systems by utilizing cross-polarized RFID reader and tag antennas. The following detailed description outlines the theoretical basis, design, and experimental validation of the proposed system.

### Theory

The invention exploits the cross-polarization between the RFID reader antenna and the tag antenna to improve the detection sensitivity and range of human activities. In a conventional RFID-based ranging system, the magnitude (in the form of Received Signal Strength Indicator, or RSSI) and phase of the backscattered wave from the RFID tag to the reader are recorded to extract the tag's location. Many RFID-based human activity detection systems use an on-body approach, where the tag is attached to the body. This approach has the benefit of a larger monitoring area and higher sensitivity due to the proximity to the human body, and can even detect complex body movements when multiple tags are deployed on different parts of the body. However, on-body approaches have several drawbacks, primarily because they need to be comfortable to wear and must be remembered to be worn, which makes them undesirable for long-term monitoring.

The alternative approach, referred to as tag-free or off-body, involves fixing the tag's location, for example, mounting tags on the wall. In this configuration, the change in RSSI and phase of the backscattered wave is due to dynamic body movements in a static environment. In most of these works, the line-of-sight (LOS) signal that is directly backscattered by RFID tags is the dominant contributor to the received signal. The reflection is usually much weaker due to reflection loss at the reflecting surface and larger path loss from a longer traveling distance. As a result, if the human activity occurs far from the line between the tag and the reader, the associated change in RSSI or phase is significantly smaller than the LOS signal, which limits the detection range and sensitivity to subtle body movements.

To address this challenge, the invention focuses on suppressing the noise to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) in the RFID-based non-contact system for human activity detection. Here, the "signal" is the wave reflected by the human body, which carries information about body movements, while the "noise" is the LOS backscattering wave from the tag. The invention proposes a cross-polarized configuration, where the reader antenna and tag antenna are circularly polarized but with opposite handedness (e.g., RHCP reader and LHCP tag).

Cross-polarization refers to the electric field polarization of two signals/antennas being orthogonal to each other. Due to the fields being perpendicular, the electric fields do not align, resulting in no power transmission, which is referred to as polarization mismatch loss. This loss can be more than 30 dB, which significantly suppresses the received LOS backscattering wave (noise). In contrast, the off-body reflected "signal" that carries information about human movements is not suppressed by the cross-polarization configuration. Whenever the interaction with the human body occurs, the handedness of the reflected CP wave is flipped to the co-polarization configuration to the receiving antenna, so there is no polarization mismatch loss for the "signal."

Experiments conducted in an anechoic chamber with a linearly polarized (LP) tag and a left-hand circular polarized (LHCP) tag using a right-hand circular polarized (RHCP) RFID reader demonstrate the effectiveness of the cross-polarized configuration. In the absence of reflective objects, the RSSI level for the LHCP tag is significantly lower than that of the LP tag due to the polarization mismatch. However, when a human or metallic sheet is present, the RSSI with the LHCP tag increases by 10-20 dB, indicating that the cross-polarized configuration can effectively suppress the LOS signal while enabling the reception of the reflected signal.

### Design and Setup

The proposed RFID-based non-contact human activity detection system consists of a regular RFID reader and a custom-designed circularly polarized RFID tag. The RFID reader used for measurement is the Impinj RFID R420 reader with a maximum sensitivity of -72 dBm. The reader is equipped with a left-hand circular polarized (LHCP) antenna (S9028PC) with a gain of 9 dBi. The RFID reader is connected to a PC running a Java program written using the Impinj Octane SDK. The reader operates in the MAXTHROUGHPUT mode, which provides around 250 tag reads per second, or one tag read every 4 ms, and operates at 31.5 dBm output power.

#### Circular RFID Tag Design

Since most commercially available RFID tags are linearly polarized, a custom-designed circularly polarized RFID tag was developed for the study. Spiral antennas are a common choice for circular polarization due to their frequency-independent characteristics, meaning that their antenna pattern, bandwidth, and polarization remain unchanged with respect to frequency. The RFID chip used on the circularly polarized tag is the same Impinj Monza R6 chip (Murata LXMS21ACMF-183) as on the SmartTrac LP tag. To achieve maximum power transfer from the tag antenna to the chip, a conjugate match must be designed to cancel the reactance of the chip with the reactance of the antenna. This is accomplished using a T-shaped matching network feed, where both arms of the Archimedean spiral symmetrically extend outward to match the 20-145j impedance of the chip to its complex conjugate impedance.

The tag was fabricated on 0.8 mm thick FR4 material to maintain a low cost and lightweight design, with a radius of 13 cm. The antenna simulation and fabricated model are shown in Figure 3(a) and (b), with simulated results in (c)-(e). The realized gain of the CP tag is 3.3 dBi, and the axial ratio bandwidth is 105 degrees. In comparison, the SmartTrac Belt RFID tag has a simulated realized gain of only 0.3 dB.

The characterization of the proposed spiral CP tags was conducted in an anechoic chamber to reduce interference and eliminate unwanted multipath effects. The measured RSSI readings show that the CP tag in co-polarized mode has around 13 dBm better RSSI than the linear tag, which is consistent with the expected 12 dB increase in RSSI due to the 3 dB difference in gain and 3 dB polarization matching. The cross-polarization loss of the tag to the reader antenna is greater than 30 dB, confirming that the CP tag operates as designed.

#### Data Preprocessing

The RFID system requires a reader to collect backscattered tag information, which can extract the RSSI and phase information of the tags from the backscattered signal. The Impinj R420 RFID reader uses frequency hopping spread spectrum transmission, meaning that the reader hops through the 50 channels within the 902-928 MHz frequency band every 10 seconds. This feature reduces interference between multiple readers but introduces variations in both RSSI and phase information, making it challenging to extract meaningful data.

To calibrate the environment, a 10-second measurement is conducted to ensure that the Impinj R420 hops through all 50 channels. The RSSI and phase data are saved for each of the 50 frequencies. The collected RSSI data is referenced to the original saved calibration data by simple subtraction to find the difference between the calibrated and measured values. For phase measurements, a method is implemented to remap the phase measurements at different channels to a chosen fixed frequency, which has an initial phase closest to π to give the remapped response a wide [0 2π] range without phase wrapping. This technique results in a flat response without frequency hopping variations, allowing for better visualization of the response of human activities.

### Experiments

#### Spatial Sensitivity Enhancement Test

To validate the theory, experiments were conducted in both anechoic chamber and real-life environments. In the anechoic chamber, an LHCP reader antenna (S9028PC) and a custom-designed RHCP tag were placed on opposing walls 1.5 meters away from a corner along the walls. The tag and antenna were 1.2 meters above the floor. An experiment was performed to compare the RSSI change between the CP tag and LP tag for the same gesture (nodding head) at various locations around the room. The RSSI variation must be identifiable and correlated to the body movement to avoid being considered noise. A simple sliding window algorithm was implemented to record the activity, and the largest variation was measured. The experiment was repeated for the linear tags in both horizontal (parallel to the floor) and vertical (perpendicular to the floor) orientations.

The results show that a single CP tag can cover and detect small body motions in a large room, with over 230% increase in coverage area compared to traditional LP tags. The vertically linear polarized tag has slightly better spatial sensitivity than a horizontally LP tag due to the "donut"-shaped radiation pattern, which provides better radiation coverage across all angles on the plane parallel to the floor when the tag is vertically placed.

#### Non-Repetitive Activities

A variety of non-repetitive activities, such as nodding, shaking, and hand-waving, were performed to verify the effectiveness of the CP tag. The vertically polarized linear tag was used for comparison. The activities were performed at location C4, and the results show that the CP tag demonstrated large variations in RSSI and phase, while the LP tag showed minimal or no detectable variations. The phase of the RFID signal varies less and is more difficult to observe when the RSSI variations are small, indicating that the RSSI response is more sensitive than the phase. The same tests were performed on three different users, showing similar trends despite differences in the speed or position of the arms.

#### Repetitive Activities

Repetitive activities, such as hand-waving and respiration, were also tested. The user performed hand-waving at a rate of 45 times per minute, and the measured results with the CP tag showed clear, discernible, and repetitive transient hand-waving responses. The frequency response correlated well with the expected hand-waving rate. In contrast, the LP tag showed small and uncorrelated RSSI variations and indicated incorrect hand-waving rates from the frequency response.

Respiration, another repetitive movement due to the contraction and expansion of the chest, was also detected using the CP tag. The user sat at location C6, which generated the largest variations in RSSI. Clear transient domain respiration patterns and a 14 breaths per minute count in the frequency domain were observed from the measurements with the CP tag, while the respiration pattern was barely retrievable from the LP tag.

#### Signal-to-Noise Ratio

An FFT was performed on head nodding activity to examine the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) with both LHCP-RHCP and LP-RHCP configurations. The SNR plot at various locations corresponding to different distances between the tag and the human body showed that the cross-polarized configuration had superior SNR performance with an extended range. The SNR was defined as the ratio between the main peak and any other peaks excluding harmonics. At close range, the frequency responses between LP and CP tags were similar, but at a farther range, the signal level of the LP tag was degraded to the noise level, while the CP tag maintained a discernible peak.

### Conclusion

The present invention demonstrates how the polarization configuration between the reader antenna and tag antenna affects the performance of RFID-based non-contact human activity detection systems. By utilizing a cross-circular polarization configuration, the invention significantly improves the signal-to-noise ratio, leading to a larger sensing range and higher spatial sensitivity compared to conventional circular reader and linear tag combinations. The custom-designed spiral CP tags and data preprocessing techniques ensure accurate and reliable detection of human activities in real-life environments. The system provides a low-cost, non-intrusive solution for ambient computing applications in homes, offices, and healthcare settings, enabling the development of advanced healthcare solutions and enhancing the quality of life for individuals with mental or physical disabilities.
