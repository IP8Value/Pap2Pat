## EXAMPLE 1. DECONSTRUCTION OF BIOMASS AND EXTRACTION OF METALS IN VARIOUS IONIC LIQUIDS

The present invention relates to a method for the deconstruction of lignocellulosic biomass and the extraction of metals using various ionic liquids. More specifically, the invention provides a process for the efficient fractionation of lignocellulosic biomass into cellulose-rich pulp, lignin, and hemicellulose, while simultaneously extracting valuable metals from the biomass. The method utilizes a low-cost ionic liquid, triethylammonium hydrogen sulfate ([TEA][HSO4]), which effectively deconstructs the biomass at mild temperatures and allows for the recovery and recycling of the ionic liquid.

### Background

Lignocellulosic biomass is a complex and abundant renewable resource that consists primarily of cellulose, hemicellulose, and lignin. The utilization of lignocellulosic biomass for the production of biofuels and chemicals is a promising alternative to fossil resources. However, the recalcitrance of lignocellulose poses significant challenges for its efficient processing. Traditional pretreatment methods, such as steam explosion, dilute acid, and organosolv processes, often require high temperatures and pressures, leading to high energy consumption and operational costs.

Ionic liquids (ILs) have emerged as a promising class of solvents for the pretreatment of lignocellulosic biomass due to their unique properties, including low vapor pressure, high thermal stability, and tunable solvent properties. Among the various ionic liquids, [TEA][HSO4] stands out for its low cost and effectiveness in biomass deconstruction. This ionic liquid can fractionate lignocellulose at atmospheric pressure and moderate temperatures, making it an attractive option for industrial applications.

### Objectives

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide a method for the deconstruction of lignocellulosic biomass using [TEA][HSO4] ionic liquid, which achieves high yields of cellulose-rich pulp, lignin, and hemicellulose while minimizing the formation of undesirable by-products. Additionally, the method aims to extract valuable metals from the biomass, thereby enhancing the overall value of the process.

### Methodology

#### Biomass Pretreatment

The method involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of the Ionic Liquid Solution**: Triethylammonium hydrogen sulfate ([TEA][HSO4]) is prepared by reacting triethylamine with sulfuric acid. The resulting ionic liquid is mixed with water to form an 80% [TEA][HSO4] and 20% water solution.

2. **Biomass Treatment**: The lignocellulosic biomass, such as Miscanthus x giganteus, is mixed with the ionic liquid solution at a biomass-to-solvent ratio of 1:10 (g/g). The mixture is heated to 120°C and maintained at this temperature for a specified duration, typically ranging from 2 to 24 hours.

3. **Fractionation**: After the pretreatment, the mixture is cooled, and the cellulose-rich pulp is separated by filtration. The filtrate, containing dissolved lignin and hemicellulose, is collected for further processing.

4. **Lignin Precipitation**: The filtrate is diluted with water to precipitate the lignin. The precipitated lignin is collected by filtration and washed with ethanol to remove residual ionic liquid.

5. **Hemicellulose Recovery**: The filtrate from the lignin precipitation step is analyzed for the presence of hemicellulose monomers and their derivatives, such as furfural. The hemicellulose can be further processed to recover valuable sugars or converted into other chemical products.

6. **Metal Extraction**: The filtrate is also analyzed for the presence of metals, which can be extracted using appropriate methods, such as solvent extraction or precipitation.

#### Recycling of the Ionic Liquid

The ionic liquid can be recovered and recycled for multiple pretreatment cycles. The process involves:

1. **Drying the Ionic Liquid**: The filtrate from the lignin precipitation step is dried using a rotary evaporator or a parallel evaporator to remove the water and ethanol.

2. **Reconstitution of the Ionic Liquid**: The dried ionic liquid is reconstituted with water to the desired concentration (80% [TEA][HSO4] and 20% water) and used for the next pretreatment cycle.

### Results

The method using [TEA][HSO4] ionic liquid for the deconstruction of lignocellulosic biomass has shown the following results:

- **Cellulose-Rich Pulp**: The cellulose-rich pulp obtained after 8 hours of pretreatment at 120°C exhibited a high yield of glucose (77%) after enzymatic saccharification. The glucan recovery in the pulp was consistently high, ranging from 85% to 95%.

- **Lignin**: The lignin yield and delignification were both high, with up to 82% lignin recovery and 88% delignification. The lignin isolated was characterized by HSQC NMR spectroscopy, which revealed the cleavage of ether bonds and the formation of condensed lignin structures.

- **Hemicellulose**: The hemicellulose was primarily solubilized as monomers and converted into furfural. The hemicellulose mass balance indicated that a significant portion of the hemicellulose was converted into unidentified degradation products, which may contribute to the formation of pseudolignin.

- **Metal Extraction**: The filtrate from the pretreatment process was found to contain trace amounts of metals, which can be extracted and recovered for further use.

- **Recycling of the Ionic Liquid**: The ionic liquid was successfully recovered and recycled for multiple pretreatment cycles with high efficiency (>99% recovery rate). The fractionation efficiency was maintained, and the ionic liquid exhibited self-cleaning properties, preventing the build-up of lignin fragments or inorganic salts.

### Advantages

- **Low Cost**: The use of [TEA][HSO4] ionic liquid is significantly cheaper compared to other ionic liquids, making the process economically viable.
- **High Efficiency**: The method achieves high yields of cellulose-rich pulp, lignin, and hemicellulose, with minimal formation of undesirable by-products.
- **Recyclability**: The ionic liquid can be recovered and recycled for multiple pretreatment cycles, reducing the overall operational costs.
- **Metal Extraction**: The process allows for the simultaneous extraction of valuable metals from the biomass, enhancing the overall value of the process.

### Applications

The method has several potential applications, including:

- **Biofuel Production**: The cellulose-rich pulp can be used for the production of bioethanol and other biofuels.
- **Chemical Production**: The lignin and hemicellulose can be further processed to produce a variety of chemicals and materials.
- **Metal Recovery**: The extraction of metals from the biomass can be used for various industrial applications, such as catalysts and electronic components.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for the efficient deconstruction of lignocellulosic biomass using [TEA][HSO4] ionic liquid. The method achieves high yields of cellulose-rich pulp, lignin, and hemicellulose, while also allowing for the extraction of valuable metals. The ionic liquid can be recovered and recycled for multiple pretreatment cycles, making the process economically viable and environmentally sustainable. The method has significant potential for industrial scale-up and can contribute to the development of a sustainable biorefinery industry.

## EXAMPLE 2. COPPER SOLUBILITY AND ELECTRODEPOSITION OF COPPER FROM VARIOUS IONIC LIQUIDS COPPER SOLUBILITY

The present invention also relates to a method for improving the solubility and electrodeposition of copper in various ionic liquids, particularly in the context of the deconstruction of lignocellulosic biomass. The method involves the use of specific ionic liquids that enhance the solubility of copper, thereby facilitating its recovery and electrodeposition.

### Background

Copper is a valuable metal with numerous industrial applications, including electronics, catalysis, and corrosion protection. The recovery and purification of copper from various sources, including lignocellulosic biomass, is of significant interest. Traditional methods for copper recovery, such as leaching and solvent extraction, often involve the use of harsh chemicals and high temperatures, leading to environmental and economic concerns.

Ionic liquids have been explored as alternative solvents for metal recovery due to their unique properties, such as low vapor pressure, high thermal stability, and tunable solvent properties. However, the solubility of metals, including copper, in ionic liquids can be limited, affecting the efficiency of metal recovery and electrodeposition processes.

### Objectives

The primary objective of the present invention is to provide a method for enhancing the solubility and electrodeposition of copper in ionic liquids, particularly in the context of the deconstruction of lignocellulosic biomass. The method aims to improve the efficiency of copper recovery and electrodeposition, thereby enhancing the overall value of the biomass processing.

### Methodology

#### Solubility Enhancement

The method involves the use of specific ionic liquids that enhance the solubility of copper. The ionic liquids are selected based on their ability to form stable complexes with copper ions, thereby increasing the solubility of copper in the ionic liquid medium. The ionic liquids used in the method include:

- **1-Butyl-3-methylimidazolium hexafluorophosphate ([BMIM][PF6])**: This ionic liquid has been shown to form stable complexes with copper ions, enhancing the solubility of copper.
- **1-Ethyl-3-methylimidazolium bis(trifluoromethylsulfonyl)imide ([EMIM][TFSI])**: This ionic liquid is known for its high thermal stability and ability to dissolve a wide range of metals, including copper.
- **Triethylammonium hydrogen sulfate ([TEA][HSO4])**: This ionic liquid, which is also used for the deconstruction of lignocellulosic biomass, has been found to enhance the solubility of copper due to its acidic nature.

#### Electrodeposition

The method for the electrodeposition of copper from the ionic liquid solutions involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of the Copper Solution**: The ionic liquid solution containing dissolved copper is prepared by adding a copper salt, such as copper sulfate, to the ionic liquid. The solution is stirred to ensure complete dissolution of the copper salt.

2. **Electrodeposition Setup**: A copper electrode is used as the cathode, and a platinum electrode is used as the anode. The electrodes are immersed in the ionic liquid solution containing dissolved copper.

3. **Application of Current**: A direct current (DC) is applied to the electrodes, causing the copper ions to migrate towards the cathode and deposit as metallic copper. The current density and deposition time are optimized to achieve high purity and uniformity of the deposited copper layer.

4. **Characterization of Deposited Copper**: The deposited copper layer is characterized using techniques such as scanning electron microscopy (SEM), energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX), and X-ray diffraction (XRD) to determine the morphology, composition, and crystal structure of the deposited copper.

### Results

The method using specific ionic liquids for the solubility enhancement and electrodeposition of copper has shown the following results:

- **Solubility Enhancement**: The ionic liquids [BMIM][PF6], [EMIM][TFSI], and [TEA][HSO4] significantly enhanced the solubility of copper in the ionic liquid medium. The solubility of copper was found to be higher in [BMIM][PF6] and [EMIM][TFSI] compared to [TEA][HSO4], but [TEA][HSO4] still showed a notable improvement in copper solubility.

- **Electrodeposition Efficiency**: The electrodeposition of copper from the ionic liquid solutions was highly efficient, with high purity and uniformity of the deposited copper layer. The current density and deposition time were optimized to achieve the best results.

- **Characterization of Deposited Copper**: The deposited copper layer was characterized using SEM, EDX, and XRD. The results showed a smooth and uniform copper layer with high purity and a well-defined crystal structure.

### Advantages

- **Enhanced Solubility**: The use of specific ionic liquids significantly enhances the solubility of copper, improving the efficiency of copper recovery.
- **High Purity and Uniformity**: The electrodeposition of copper from the ionic liquid solutions results in high purity and uniformity of the deposited copper layer.
- **Environmental Friendly**: The method is environmentally friendly, as it avoids the use of harsh chemicals and high temperatures.
- **Versatility**: The method can be applied to various ionic liquids, making it versatile for different applications.

### Applications

The method has several potential applications, including:

- **Copper Recovery**: The method can be used for the recovery of copper from various sources, including lignocellulosic biomass.
- **Electronics**: The high purity and uniformity of the deposited copper layer make it suitable for use in electronic devices.
- **Catalysis**: The deposited copper can be used as a catalyst in various chemical reactions.
- **Corrosion Protection**: The deposited copper can be used for corrosion protection in various industrial applications.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for enhancing the solubility and electrodeposition of copper in ionic liquids, particularly in the context of the deconstruction of lignocellulosic biomass. The method uses specific ionic liquids that enhance the solubility of copper, leading to efficient recovery and electrodeposition. The method is environmentally friendly and has significant potential for various industrial applications, including copper recovery, electronics, catalysis, and corrosion protection.
