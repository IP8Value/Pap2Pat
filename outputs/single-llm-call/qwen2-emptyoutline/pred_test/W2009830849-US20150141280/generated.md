# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of medical diagnostics, specifically to the identification and use of circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) as biomarkers for the detection of Luminal A-like breast cancer. The invention provides a method for identifying and quantifying specific miRNAs in blood samples that are indicative of Luminal A-like breast cancer, thereby facilitating early and non-invasive diagnosis of this common breast cancer subtype.

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

Breast cancer is a prevalent and heterogeneous disease, affecting millions of women globally. It is the most common female malignancy in Europe and North America and a leading cause of cancer-related deaths among women. The disease is characterized by distinct tumor phenotypes, which are influenced by various molecular alterations and initiating events. The classification of breast tumors into subtypes, such as Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-positive, and triple-negative, is primarily based on the expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2/neu). Targeted therapies, including hormonal therapy for ER-positive tumors and trastuzumab for HER2-positive tumors, have significantly improved patient outcomes. However, the hematogenous spread of malignant cells to distant organs, leading to metastasis, remains the primary cause of death in breast cancer patients.

Current diagnostic methods for breast cancer, such as mammography and invasive tissue biopsies, have limitations. Mammography is the most widely used screening tool, but it often requires confirmation through invasive tissue biopsies for accurate diagnosis and subtype determination. The lack of reliable circulating biomarkers for breast cancer has hindered the development of minimally invasive diagnostic tools. Blood, due to its accessibility and the ability to reflect physiological and pathological processes, presents an excellent medium for biomarker discovery.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small non-coding RNA molecules that regulate gene expression at the post-transcriptional level. They have shown significant potential as cancer-specific biomarkers due to their involvement in tumorigenesis and their detectability in the circulation. Altered miRNA expression patterns in breast cancer tissues have been correlated with molecular subtypes and hormonal receptor status. Circulating miRNAs have been proposed as potential biomarkers for the early detection of breast cancer, particularly for the Luminal A subtype, which is the most common and generally associated with a good prognosis.

## OBJECT OF THE INVENTION

The primary object of the present invention is to provide a method for the identification and quantification of specific circulating miRNAs that are differentially expressed in women with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. The invention aims to develop a minimally invasive diagnostic tool that can be used in combination with existing imaging techniques to facilitate early and accurate diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for diagnosing Luminal A-like breast cancer by analyzing the expression levels of specific circulating miRNAs in blood samples. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Sample Collection**: Collecting venous blood samples from individuals suspected of having Luminal A-like breast cancer and from healthy control subjects.
2. **RNA Extraction**: Extracting total RNA from the blood samples using standard methods.
3. **miRNA Profiling**: Performing miRNA expression profiling using microarray technology or quantitative real-time PCR (RQ-PCR) to identify differentially expressed miRNAs.
4. **Data Analysis**: Analyzing the miRNA expression data using statistical methods to identify miRNAs that are significantly upregulated or downregulated in the blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls.
5. **Validation**: Validating the identified miRNAs in an independent cohort of blood samples using RQ-PCR to confirm their diagnostic potential.
6. **Biomarker Panel**: Developing a panel of circulating miRNAs (e.g., miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652) that can be used as a biomarker for Luminal A-like breast cancer.

The invention further provides a kit for performing the method, comprising reagents and materials necessary for RNA extraction, miRNA profiling, and data analysis. The kit may also include software for analyzing the miRNA expression data and generating diagnostic reports.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Validation Study Statistical Analysis

The validation study involved the collection of blood samples from 110 women, including 54 patients with newly diagnosed Luminal A-like breast cancer and 56 healthy control subjects. Total RNA was extracted from the blood samples using TRI Reagent BD, and the quality and quantity of RNA were assessed using NanoDrop spectrophotometry and Agilent Bioanalyzer RNA 6000 NanoChip Kit Series II.

MiRNA expression profiling was performed using TaqMan human miRNA arrays and assays, following the manufacturer's instructions. The data were analyzed using a nonlinear artificial neural network (ANN) based data mining algorithm to identify miRNAs with altered expression in the blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. The ANN algorithm utilized a feed-forward back-propagation approach with a three-layer architecture, a sigmoid transfer function, and early stopping on unseen data to prevent overfitting.

The performance of individual miRNA probes was determined by developing ANN models using the algorithm described above. This process generated a rank order of miRNAs based on their classification performance. The top-ranked miRNAs were selected for further validation using RQ-PCR.

### Results

The ANN data mining algorithm identified 76 miRNAs with detectable and altered expression in the blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. A subset of three candidate miRNAs (miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652) was chosen for validation using RQ-PCR in an independent cohort of 44 patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer and 46 healthy controls.

The validation study confirmed that miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were significantly downregulated in the blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer compared to healthy controls. The expression levels of these miRNAs were also measured in tumor tissue and tumor-associated normal (TAN) tissue from patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer. MiR-181a and miR-652 were found to be underexpressed in both the circulation and tumor tissue of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer.

The association between miRNA expression and clinicopathological parameters, such as tumor grade, nodal status, and tumor size, was also investigated. MiR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 were significantly downregulated in the blood of patients with Luminal A-like breast cancer, irrespective of tumor grade, nodal status, or stage of disease. MiR-181a was significantly downregulated in the blood of patients with node-positive disease compared to healthy controls but not in node-negative disease.

The biomarker potential of the identified miRNAs was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. The combination of miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652 provided an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.80, with a sensitivity and specificity of 77% and 74%, respectively. The addition of miR-223 did not improve the sensitivity or specificity profile.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a method for the early and non-invasive diagnosis of Luminal A-like breast cancer using a panel of circulating miRNAs. The identified miRNAs (miR-29a, miR-181a, and miR-652) show significant promise as biomarkers for Luminal A-like breast cancer, with a sensitivity and specificity profile that exceeds that of several currently used clinical biomarkers. The method can be used in combination with mammography to improve the accuracy and speed of diagnosis, ultimately leading to better patient outcomes. Further large-scale validation studies are required to confirm the clinical utility of the miRNA panel for Luminal A-like breast cancer detection.
