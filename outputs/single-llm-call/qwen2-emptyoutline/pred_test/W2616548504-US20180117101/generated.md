# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Phenylketonuria (PKU) is a genetic disorder affecting approximately 1 in 15,000 individuals worldwide. PKU arises from the inheritance of any two of the 700+ loss-of-function mutations in the gene encoding the hepatic enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH). The deficiency of PAH leads to the accumulation of phenylalanine (phe) and its alternative metabolites, such as phenylketone, in the blood. These metabolites can reach neurotoxic levels, leading to severe neurological damage, including tremors, seizures, and mental retardation. Even with treatment, PKU patients may experience secondary conditions such as mood disorders, minor cognitive defects, early onset osteopenia, and markers of systemic inflammation. Furthermore, pregnant women with PKU must maintain very low blood phe levels to prevent cardiac and neurological birth defects in their infants.

The primary treatment for PKU involves the restriction of dietary phe. Since phe is an essential amino acid and cannot be removed from the diet entirely, synthetic medical foods and supplements are often used. However, these alternatives can be expensive, not universally covered by insurance, and are often reported to have unfavorable flavor and odor. Consequently, many PKU patients relax or abandon the restrictive diet upon leaving their parents, highlighting the need for more patient-friendly and cost-effective treatments.

Previous studies have explored the use of phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL) to metabolize phe. Oral administration of PAL has shown promise in lowering blood phe levels in PKU mice, but challenges such as the degradation of PAL by intestinal proteases and the need for protection against stomach acid have limited its effectiveness. To address these issues, a novel approach involves the use of a genetically engineered probiotic to express and deliver PAL to the intestinal lumen, where it can metabolize phe before it enters the bloodstream.

## Advantages Over Conventional Technologies

The proposed invention offers several advantages over existing treatments for PKU:

1. **Patient Compliance**: The use of a probiotic-based treatment is more palatable and easier to adhere to compared to a strict dietary regimen or synthetic medical foods. This is particularly important for maintaining long-term treatment adherence, especially among younger patients and those who find the current dietary restrictions challenging.

2. **Cost-Effectiveness**: Probiotics are generally less expensive to produce and distribute compared to specialized medical foods and supplements. This makes the treatment more accessible to a broader range of patients, including those in resource-limited settings.

3. **Reduced Immunogenicity**: Unlike naked PAL or PEG-PAL, which can trigger a systemic immune response, the PAL expressed within the probiotic remains localized in the gut, reducing the risk of immunogenic reactions. This is crucial for ensuring the long-term efficacy of the treatment.

4. **Simplified Administration**: The probiotic can be administered orally, eliminating the need for injections or complex dietary modifications. This simplifies the treatment process and enhances patient convenience.

5. **Potential for Long-Term Colonization**: The engineered probiotic has the potential to colonize the gut for extended periods, providing sustained phe metabolism and reducing the frequency of dosing required.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a genetically engineered probiotic, specifically *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C, designed to express phenylalanine ammonia lyase (PAL) for the treatment of phenylketonuria (PKU). The probiotic, named "pHENOMMenal," is engineered to produce PAL, which catalyzes the conversion of phenylalanine (phe) into trans-cinnamate and ammonia. By expressing PAL within the probiotic, the invention aims to reduce the levels of phe in the intestinal lumen, thereby preventing its absorption into the bloodstream and mitigating the symptoms of PKU.

The invention includes the following key components:
- **Genetic Engineering of *L. reuteri* 100-23C**: The probiotic is modified to express the PAL gene from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413 (AvPAL). The gene is optimized for expression in *L. reuteri* and includes a constitutive promoter and a transcriptional terminator to ensure efficient and stable expression.
- **In Vitro and In Vivo Efficacy**: The engineered probiotic demonstrates in vitro activity in converting phe to trans-cinnamate. In vivo studies using a mouse model of PKU show a significant reduction in blood phe levels following oral administration of the probiotic.
- **Safety and Immunogenicity**: The probiotic is designed to minimize immunogenic responses, making it suitable for long-term use without the risk of developing systemic immunity to the PAL enzyme.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Example 1: Genetic Engineering of *L. reuteri* 100-23C

The first step in the development of pHENOMMenal involved the genetic engineering of *Lactobacillus reuteri* 100-23C to express the PAL gene from *Anabaena variabilis* ATCC 29413 (AvPAL). The native promoter of AvPAL was replaced with a high-production constitutive promoter from the erythromycin resistance B gene (ermB) to enhance expression levels. Additionally, the codons in the AvPAL gene were optimized to match the codon usage of *L. reuteri* to ensure efficient translation. A transcriptional terminator from the ermGT resistance gene was added to the construct to stabilize the mRNA and prevent read-through transcription.

The full construct, designated as FuzErmAvPAL, was synthesized and inserted into the plasmid pSLERGT. The resulting plasmid was then introduced into *L. reuteri* 100-23C using electroporation. Successful transformation was confirmed by selecting for erythromycin resistance and verifying the presence of the AvPAL gene through PCR and sequencing.

### Example 2: In Vitro Activity of pHENOMMenal

To assess the in vitro activity of the engineered probiotic, *L. reuteri* 100-23C cells expressing AvPAL were grown to mid-log phase and lysed. The lysate was incubated with varying concentrations of phe, and the production of trans-cinnamate was monitored by measuring the absorbance at 280 nm. The results showed a significant increase in trans-cinnamate production in the lysate from pHENOMMenal cells compared to the control cells, indicating the successful expression and activity of AvPAL.

### Example 3: In Vivo Efficacy in a Mouse Model of PKU

The in vivo efficacy of pHENOMMenal was evaluated using a mouse model of PKU. PAHenu2 mutant mice on the C57BL6/J background were used for the study. The mice were fed a standard diet containing 0.79% phe, and the probiotic was administered orally by mixing freeze-dried pHENOMMenal cells with powdered mouse chow. The dose of the probiotic was 2.0 x 10^7 cfu/gram chow for male mice and 1.0 x 10^7 cfu/gram chow for female mice.

Plasma phe levels were measured at various time points before and after the administration of the probiotic. The results showed a significant reduction in plasma phe levels in mice treated with pHENOMMenal compared to the control group. Specifically, a mean reduction of 598.6 ± 289.5 μM phe was observed after seven days of treatment, and this reduction was maintained after fourteen days of treatment.

### Example 4: Safety and Immunogenicity

To evaluate the safety and immunogenicity of pHENOMMenal, plasma samples from treated mice were analyzed for the presence of anti-AvPAL antibodies using an ELISA. The results showed that the levels of anti-AvPAL antibodies remained below the threshold for a significant immune response, indicating that the probiotic did not trigger a systemic immune reaction.
