# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of cell therapy has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the context of regenerative medicine and the treatment of various diseases. Traditional exogenous cell therapies, however, face substantial challenges, including high costs and stringent regulatory requirements. These challenges arise from the need to isolate and expand cells in vitro, which often involves complex and resource-intensive processes. An alternative approach to obtaining a sufficient number of cells for therapeutic purposes is cytokine-based cell mobilization, such as the use of granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) for the mobilization of hematopoietic stem cells. While effective, this method requires multiple hospital visits and is associated with a range of side effects, from mild flu-like symptoms to more severe conditions.

Intraoperative cell therapies represent a promising new class of approaches that can overcome many of these limitations. These therapies involve harvesting cells from the patient immediately before or during the initial surgical procedure and re-administering them during the same session. This approach not only reduces the time and cost associated with cell processing but also minimizes the risk of contamination and the need for extensive regulatory approval. One area where intraoperative cell therapies show significant potential is in bone healing. Each year, approximately 7.9 million bone fractures are reported in the United States, with 5 to 10% resulting in impaired bone healing. Predicting patients at risk and providing them with additional treatment during the initial surgical intervention can significantly reduce the incidence of non-union cases and associated healthcare costs.

The present invention focuses on the development of a novel method for the enrichment of CD31+ cells, which are known for their angiogenic, osteogenic, and immunosuppressive properties. CD31, also known as Platelet/Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule 1 (PECAM-1), is expressed on the surface of various cell types, including lymphocytes and monocytes. These cells have been successfully used in vivo for treating ischemic vascular disease and have shown promise in bone regeneration. The invention aims to provide a minimally manipulative and efficient method for isolating CD31+ cells from whole blood, thereby facilitating their use in intraoperative cell therapies.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and system for the enrichment of CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population, such as whole blood. The method involves the use of aptamers, which are oligonucleotides capable of binding to specific targets with high specificity and affinity. The invention includes a device for cell enrichment, wherein the aptamers are immobilized on a solid support, such as neutravidin agarose beads. The device is designed to allow the reversible binding of CD31+ cells, enabling their efficient isolation and subsequent release without the need for chemical or temperature modifications.

The invention also encompasses kits for performing the cell enrichment method and methods of treatment using the enriched CD31+ cells. The enriched cells exhibit enhanced angiogenic and osteogenic potential, making them suitable for use in various therapeutic applications, particularly in the context of bone healing and vascular disease.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. CD31+ Cells

CD31, also known as Platelet/Endothelial Cell Adhesion Molecule 1 (PECAM-1), is a transmembrane glycoprotein expressed on the surface of various cell types, including endothelial cells, platelets, and leukocytes. CD31 plays a crucial role in cell-cell adhesion, migration, and signaling, and is involved in processes such as angiogenesis, inflammation, and immune responses. CD31+ cells, which express this molecule, have been extensively studied for their therapeutic potential in regenerative medicine. These cells possess angiogenic and osteogenic properties, making them valuable for the treatment of ischemic vascular disease and bone healing.

### II. Aptamers

Aptamers are synthetic oligonucleotides that can bind to specific target molecules with high affinity and specificity. Unlike antibodies, aptamers can be chemically synthesized and are stable under a wide range of conditions, including long-term storage at room temperature. They can be easily modified and manipulated, offering significant advantages in terms of reproducibility and scalability. The aptamers used in the present invention are specifically designed to bind to CD31+ cells. The high specificity and affinity of these aptamers enable the efficient and selective enrichment of CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population.

### III. Devices for Enrichment of Cells

The invention provides a device for the enrichment of CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population. The device comprises a microcentrifuge tube with a filter at the base, through which the cell suspension is passed. The filter is designed to retain the neutravidin agarose beads, which are coated with biotinylated aptamers specific for CD31. The device is assembled by removing the polyethylene filter from the microcentrifuge tube and replacing it with a 4 mm diameter filter extracted from a 20 μm cell strainer. The filter is secured to the base of the tube using a thin layer of cyanoacrylate glue. Neutravidin agarose beads are then packed within the tube and incubated with the biotinylated aptamers to allow the aptamers to bind to the beads. The device is designed to allow the reversible binding of CD31+ cells to the aptamer-coated beads, enabling their efficient isolation and subsequent release.

### IV. Methods of Enriching Cells from a Mixed Cell Population

The method of enriching CD31+ cells from a mixed cell population involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells (PBMCs):** PBMCs are obtained from whole blood using a density centrifugation method. The blood sample is diluted with PBS and layered onto a density centrifugation medium. After centrifugation, the PBMCs layer is aspirated and washed to remove platelets.

2. **Coating of Neutravidin Agarose Beads:** Neutravidin agarose beads are diluted in PBS and filtered through a 100 μm filter. The beads are then incubated with biotinylated aptamers specific for CD31. After incubation, the beads are washed to remove unbound aptamers and resuspended in a binding buffer.

3. **Enrichment of CD31+ Cells:** The PBMCs are resuspended in the binding buffer and passed through the device at a controlled flow rate. The CD31+ cells bind to the aptamer-coated beads, while the CD31- cells flow through and are discarded. The device is then rinsed with PBS to remove unbound cells.

4. **Release of CD31+ Cells:** The beads with bound CD31+ cells are resuspended by mixing with additional medium. The cells are released from the beads due to the shear forces generated by the mixing process. The released cells are collected by centrifugation and can be used for further analysis or therapeutic applications.

### V. Kits

The invention also provides kits for performing the method of enriching CD31+ cells. The kits include the following components:

1. **Microcentrifuge Tubes with Filters:** Pre-assembled microcentrifuge tubes with 4 mm diameter filters at the base.
2. **Neutravidin Agarose Beads:** Neutravidin agarose beads for coating with biotinylated aptamers.
3. **Biotinylated Aptamers:** Biotinylated aptamers specific for CD31.
4. **Binding Buffer:** A buffer solution for resuspending PBMCs and aptamer-coated beads.
5. **Flow Cytometry Staining Buffers and Antibodies:** Buffers and antibodies for flow cytometric analysis of the enriched cell population.
6. **Instructions for Use:** Detailed instructions for assembling the device and performing the cell enrichment method.

### VI. Methods of Treatment

The enriched CD31+ cells obtained using the method of the present invention can be used in various therapeutic applications, particularly in the context of bone healing and vascular disease. The cells exhibit enhanced angiogenic and osteogenic potential, making them suitable for use in intraoperative cell therapies. The following methods of treatment are contemplated:

1. **Bone Healing:** The enriched CD31+ cells can be administered to patients with bone fractures or impaired bone healing. The cells promote the formation of new blood vessels and bone tissue, accelerating the healing process and reducing the risk of non-union.
2. **Vascular Disease:** The enriched CD31+ cells can be used to treat ischemic vascular disease by promoting the formation of new blood vessels and improving blood flow to affected areas.
3. **Immunosuppression:** The CD31+ cells have immunosuppressive properties, which can be harnessed to modulate immune responses in various inflammatory and autoimmune conditions.

## EXAMPLES

### Materials and Methods

#### PBMC Preparation

Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) were obtained from apheresis leukoreduction filters using a density centrifugation method. The cell suspension was diluted with Dulbecco’s phosphate buffered saline (PBS) 1:1 before being layered onto density centrifugation medium (Ficoll-paque plus, VWR, #17-1440-02) and centrifuged for 30 min at 400 g without brake. The PBMCs layer was aspirated and rinsed twice at 200 g for 8 min to remove platelets. PBMCs were then used for subsequent aptamer validation or frozen for system optimization experiments.

#### Aptamer Specificity

A commercially available biotinylated aptamer for CD31 was purchased (APTSCI, monolabeled (only biotinylated) #CD31-2196BCI, bi-labeled (biotinylated and with additional Cy5) #CD31-2196FBCI). To validate its specificity, PBMCs were resuspended at a concentration of 10^7 cells/ml. 100 μl of cells were incubated with the biotinylated aptamer at different concentrations for 30 min at 4°C. Cells were then centrifuged for 5 min at 1200 RPM and resuspended in 100 μl flow cytometry staining buffer (eBioscience, #00-4222-26). Cells were stained for CD31 (Alexa fluor 488, CD31, Biolegend, #303110) and biotin (PE anti-biotin antibody, Biolegend, #409004) before being analyzed on a BD LSRFortessa with HTS. For flow cytometric analyses of the release kinetics of the biotinylated CD31 aptamer, a version bi-labeled with additional fluorochrome Cy5 was used.

#### Magnetic Bead Studies

To benchmark our new approach, established and commercially available bead sorting strategies were used as comparison. In certain studies, CD31+ cells were isolated by immunomagnetic cell separation (MACS System; Milteny Biotec) according to the manufacturer’s instruction using the CD31 Microbead kit (Milteny Biotec, #130-091-935). For the aptamer, magnetic cell sorting was performed using a cell isolation kit for CD31+ cells (APTSCI, #CD31-2196BCI) and following the manufacturer’s instruction.

### Aptamer Selection and Validation

The ability of an aptamer to bind CD31+ cells was initially confirmed by comparing suspended cells labeled with the aptamer and a CD31 antibody. PBMCs were incubated with different concentrations of the biotinylated aptamer before staining for both CD31 and biotin using antibodies. This analysis demonstrated that the aptamer was highly sensitive for the CD31 population, with the sensitivity going up to 99.0±1.3% for an aptamer concentration of 5 μg/ml. In addition, the specificity was also very high, with values between 96.9±4.1% and 87.9±4.8% for aptamer concentrations of 0.5 μg/ml and 5 μg/ml, respectively.

### Development of a CD31+ Cell Enrichment System

The general principle of the system was as follows: PBMCs that contain a mixture of CD31- and CD31+ cells were run through the system. The CD31+ cells adhered to the aptamer-coated beads while CD31- cells flowed through and were discarded. CD31+ cells were then released from beads by imposing fluid shear with a simple mixing of the beads with attached cells and additional medium. Cells released were then collected by centrifugation.

First, we optimized the aptamer concentration for the coating of the beads. Increasing the aptamer concentration resulted in an overall increase in the capture of cells (yield), as measured using a biophysically induced (flow release) and a subsequent DNase-based release. No significant difference was observed between the initial cell viability and that of flow-released cells. However, at the highest aptamer concentration, flow-based separation did result in a decrease in cell viability after release, likely due to the overall stronger avidity between the cells and these beads. To determine if adherent cells were retained after intended release, we applied DNase 1 to digest aptamers after flow release. The use of DNase 1 significantly affected cell viability at all conditions, and increasing aptamer concentration led to an increase in retained cells. Importantly, the flow-based release strategy resulted in an enrichment of CD31+ cells at a lower aptamer concentration but not at the highest aptamer concentrations. Therefore, subsequent steps for optimization were focusing on aptamer concentrations of 5 μg/ml and 10 μg/ml, as these provided an appropriate compromise between cell yield, CD31+ purity, and cell viability.

### CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations

The next studies quantified the ability of the optimized system to enrich CD31+ cells from fresh PBMCs derived from probands' whole blood samples. The column used in these studies utilized beads coated with 5 μg/ml of aptamer, a 50/50 ratio of coated to uncoated beads, and a total bead volume of 800 μl (400 μl uncoated and 400 μl aptamer coated). The use of these columns resulted in a significant increase in the percentage of isolated cells that were CD31+, from 64±9 to 87±3%, while retaining a high cell viability of 97±1%. The yield of the CD31+ cells was 25.1±0.9%. Further analysis revealed that the CD31+ fraction was significantly enriched in monocytes (CD31+CD14+), as compared to the initial PBMC population. A significant increase in naïve T cells (CD3+CD31+) was also observed.

### Angiogenic and Osteogenic Potential of CD31+ Enriched Cell Populations

To confirm the previously reported regenerative potential of enriched CD31+ cells, both an osteogenic and angiogenic in vitro assay were utilized. An in vitro tube formation assay validated the increased angiogenic potential of the enriched cell population; the total length of capillary-like tubes was increased 1.9±0.2 fold, as compared to the control condition. The osteogenic potential was also confirmed, as conditioned medium from enriched cells increased calcification by a factor of 1.8±0.3 in MSCs, as compared to the control condition.
