# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of virology and biotechnology, specifically to the identification, characterization, and use of a novel adeno-associated virus (AAV) element derived from the genome of marsupials. More particularly, the invention pertains to the reconstruction of an ancient AAV genome and its potential applications in gene therapy and other biotechnological fields.

## BACKGROUND OF THE DISCLOSURE

Adeno-associated viruses (AAVs) are small, non-enveloped viruses belonging to the family Parvoviridae. They are dependent on helper viruses for their replication and have been widely studied for their potential as gene delivery vectors. The discovery and characterization of endogenous viral elements (EVEs) in various species have provided valuable insights into the evolutionary history and functional diversity of AAVs.

Recent research has identified a lineage-specific endogenous AAV element (mAAV-EVE1) in the genome of marsupials, particularly within the suborder Macropodiformes. This element, which is a fossil insertion dating back to the common ancestor of all Macropodiformes, has been found to be highly conserved and phylogenetically informative. The mAAV-EVE1 element is characterized by the presence of viral sequences that are orthologous to contemporary AAVs, but with significant genetic modifications that render the encoded proteins non-functional.

The identification and reconstruction of the mAAV-EVE1 genome offer a unique opportunity to study the evolutionary constraints and functional adaptations of AAVs over millions of years. Furthermore, the reconstructed ancient AAV genome can serve as a valuable resource for the development of novel gene therapy vectors and other biotechnological applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE DISCLOSURE

The present invention provides a reconstructed ancient adeno-associated virus (AAV) genome, designated mAAV-EVE1, derived from the genome of marsupials, particularly the suborder Macropodiformes. The invention encompasses the nucleotide sequence of the mAAV-EVE1 genome, the encoded proteins, and the methods for producing and using these sequences and proteins.

In one aspect, the invention provides an isolated nucleic acid molecule comprising a nucleotide sequence encoding the mAAV-EVE1 genome. The nucleotide sequence includes the rep and cap genes, which encode the non-structural (Rep) and capsid (Cap) proteins, respectively. The mAAV-EVE1 genome is characterized by the presence of conserved domains and motifs that are essential for AAV function, but with specific modifications that render the encoded proteins non-functional.

In another aspect, the invention provides an isolated recombinant AAV (rAAV) comprising the mAAV-EVE1 genome. The rAAV can be produced using standard molecular biology techniques and can be used for various applications, including gene therapy, vaccine development, and basic research.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for producing the mAAV-EVE1 genome and the encoded proteins. The method involves the cloning and expression of the mAAV-EVE1 nucleotide sequence in a suitable host cell, such as a bacterial or mammalian cell. The expressed proteins can be purified and characterized for their structural and functional properties.

In a further aspect, the invention provides compositions and methods for using the mAAV-EVE1 genome and the encoded proteins. The compositions can include the isolated nucleic acid molecules, the rAAV particles, and the purified proteins. The methods can involve the use of these compositions for gene delivery, immunization, and other therapeutic and diagnostic applications.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1. Identification and Characterization of mAAV-EVE1 Genome and Proteins

#### Materials and Methods

**Sample Acquisition:** Tissue samples from the eastern grey kangaroo (Macropus giganteus) were obtained in accordance with the provisions of a General Licence issued by the NSW National Parks and Wildlife Service. Genomic DNA was extracted from liver, muscle, and brain tissues using a FastPrep FP120 tissue homogeniser and the DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen).

**PCR Amplification:** Genomic DNA was subjected to PCR amplification using Platinum Taq PCR SuperMix (Invitrogen) and primer pairs recognizing conserved regions of the AAV genome. The thermal cycling conditions were 94°C for 5 minutes followed by 35 cycles of 94°C for 30 seconds, 55°C for 30 seconds, and 72°C for 30 seconds, with a final 5-minute extension at 72°C.

**Linker-Mediated Genome Walking:** Linker-mediated "genome walking" was performed using the GenomeWalker Universal Kit (Clontech) to isolate adjacent endogenous viral sequences. Adapter-ligated genomic DNA fragments were amplified using unique sequence/adapter-specific primer pairs. Amplified PCR products were cloned into pCR4-TOPO (Invitrogen) and sequenced.

#### Results

Amplicon sequencing revealed significant sequence similarity to numerous dependoparvovirus genomes, with the primate AAV serotypes AAVhu.S17 and AAV13 displaying the most significant similarity. Full-length endogenous AAV sequences were obtained and analyzed, revealing several stop codons and frameshifts scattered throughout the viral rep and cap genes. The mAAV-EVE1 sequence was 96.4% identical to the longest of eleven AAV-EVEs previously identified in the tammar wallaby (Macropus eugenii) genome.

### Example 2. Modification of mAAV-EVE Capsid

#### Materials and Methods

**Mutagenesis:** Site-directed mutagenesis was performed to introduce specific mutations into the mAAV-EVE1 cap gene. Mutagenesis was carried out using the QuikChange Site-Directed Mutagenesis Kit (Agilent) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

**Expression and Purification:** The mutated cap genes were cloned into expression vectors and transfected into HEK293 cells. The expressed capsid proteins were purified using ultracentrifugation and chromatography.

#### Results

Capsid variants with altered properties, such as improved tissue tropism and reduced immunogenicity, were successfully generated. The functional properties of the capsid variants were characterized using in vitro and in vivo assays.

### Example 3. Production of rAAV

#### Materials and Methods

**Vector Construction:** Plasmids containing the mAAV-EVE1 genome and helper virus components were constructed using standard molecular biology techniques.

**Transfection and Production:** HEK293 cells were transfected with the plasmids using Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen). The rAAV particles were harvested and purified using ultracentrifugation and chromatography.

#### Results

Recombinant AAV particles containing the mAAV-EVE1 genome were successfully produced and purified. The purified rAAV particles were characterized for their purity, titer, and infectivity using standard assays.

### Example 4. Assessment of rAAV

#### Materials and Methods

**In Vitro Transduction:** The rAAV particles were used to transduce various cell types, including HEK293 cells, HeLa cells, and primary human fibroblasts. Transduction efficiency was assessed using fluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry.

**In Vivo Delivery:** The rAAV particles were administered to mice via intramuscular injection. Tissue samples were collected at various time points, and transgene expression was assessed using quantitative PCR and Western blotting.

#### Results

The rAAV particles demonstrated high transduction efficiency in vitro and in vivo. Transgene expression was detected in the target tissues, confirming the potential of the mAAV-EVE1 genome for gene therapy applications.
