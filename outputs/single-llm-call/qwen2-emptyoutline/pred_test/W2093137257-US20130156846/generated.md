# DESCRIPTION

## INTRODUCTION

The present invention relates to methods and compositions for preventing or reducing the misincorporation of β-N-methylamino-L-alanine (BMAA) into proteins during protein synthesis. BMAA is a non-protein amino acid that has been implicated in various neurodegenerative diseases due to its ability to be mistakenly incorporated into proteins, leading to protein misfolding and aggregation. The invention provides a method for inhibiting the incorporation of BMAA into proteins by administering L-serine, a canonical amino acid, which competes with BMAA for incorporation into the protein chain. This competitive inhibition can prevent the formation of misfolded proteins and the subsequent cellular damage associated with neurodegenerative diseases.

## SUMMARY

The present invention provides a method for preventing or reducing the misincorporation of β-N-methylamino-L-alanine (BMAA) into proteins during protein synthesis. The method involves administering an effective amount of L-serine to a subject. L-serine competes with BMAA for incorporation into the protein chain, thereby reducing the likelihood of BMAA misincorporation and the subsequent formation of misfolded proteins. The invention also encompasses pharmaceutical compositions comprising L-serine and methods for treating or preventing neurodegenerative diseases associated with BMAA misincorporation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Background

Protein synthesis is a highly regulated process that ensures the accurate assembly of the 20 canonical amino acids into proteins. However, errors in this process can lead to the misincorporation of non-protein amino acids, such as β-N-methylamino-L-alanine (BMAA), into proteins. BMAA is a non-protein amino acid that has been found to be associated with proteins in various organisms, including humans. The misincorporation of BMAA into proteins can result in protein misfolding, aggregation, and subsequent cellular damage, which has been linked to neurodegenerative diseases such as amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS), Parkinson's disease, and Alzheimer's disease.

### Mechanism of BMAA Misincorporation

BMAA can be mistakenly incorporated into proteins during protein synthesis due to its structural similarity to the canonical amino acid L-serine. The aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, which are responsible for attaching the correct amino acid to the corresponding tRNA, can sometimes mischarge BMAA onto the tRNA for L-serine. This mischarged tRNA can then be incorporated into the growing polypeptide chain, leading to the formation of misfolded proteins. The misfolded proteins can aggregate and cause cellular dysfunction, ultimately leading to neurodegeneration.

### Role of L-Serine in Competitive Inhibition

L-serine, a canonical amino acid, can compete with BMAA for incorporation into proteins. By increasing the concentration of L-serine in the cellular environment, the likelihood of BMAA misincorporation can be significantly reduced. This competitive inhibition is based on the principle that the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases have a higher affinity for the canonical amino acid L-serine compared to the non-protein amino acid BMAA. Therefore, the presence of a higher concentration of L-serine can effectively outcompete BMAA for incorporation into the protein chain.

### Treatment and Prevention of Neurodegenerative Diseases

The method of the present invention can be used to treat or prevent neurodegenerative diseases associated with BMAA misincorporation. ### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Inhibition of BMAA Incorporation by L-Serine in Cell Cultures

**Objective:** To demonstrate that L-serine can inhibit the incorporation of BMAA into proteins in human cell cultures.

**Methods:**
1. **Cell Culture:** Human MRC-5 fibroblast cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 4 mM L-glutamine, 100 U/mL penicillin, and 100 µg/mL streptomycin at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 and 95% air.
2. **Treatment:** Cells were incubated with 3H-BMAA (31.25 nM) in the presence or absence of L-serine (0, 50, 100, and 250 µM) for 16 hours.
3. **Protein Isolation:** Cell proteins were isolated by trichloroacetic acid (TCA) precipitation and washed three times in TCA.
4. **Radiolabel Quantification:** The amount of radiolabel present in the cell proteins was quantified by liquid scintillation counting (LSC).

**Results:**
- The incorporation of 3H-BMAA into cell proteins was significantly reduced in the presence of L-serine in a concentration-dependent manner.
- The highest concentration of L-serine (250 µM) almost completely inhibited the incorporation of 3H-BMAA into proteins.

**Conclusion:**
L-serine effectively competes with BMAA for incorporation into proteins, thereby reducing the misincorporation of BMAA and the potential for protein misfolding and aggregation.

#### Example 2: Effect of L-Serine on BMAA-Induced Protein Aggregation and Apoptosis

**Objective:** To evaluate the effect of L-serine on BMAA-induced protein aggregation and apoptosis in human cell cultures.

**Methods:**
1. **Cell Culture:** Human MRC-5 fibroblast cells were cultured as described in Example 1.
2. **Treatment:** Cells were incubated with 300 µM BMAA in the presence or absence of 300 µM L-serine for 96 hours with daily medium changes.
3. **Autofluorescence Imaging:** Cellular autofluorescence, indicative of protein aggregation, was visualized using an inverted fluorescent microscope.
4. **Apoptosis Assay:** Apoptosis was measured by simultaneous staining with propidium iodide (PI) and Annexin V using the BD Pharminigen™ Annexin V–FITC apoptosis detection kit and flow cytometry.

**Results:**
- BMAA treatment induced cellular autofluorescence, indicating protein aggregation, which was significantly reduced by co-incubation with L-serine.
- BMAA treatment also increased the percentage of cells stained with Annexin V, indicating apoptosis, which was reduced by co-incubation with L-serine.

**Conclusion:**
L-serine can effectively reduce BMAA-induced protein aggregation and apoptosis, suggesting its potential therapeutic use in preventing or treating neurodegenerative diseases associated with BMAA misincorporation.

#### Example 3: Identification of BMAA in Hydrolyzed Proteins by LC/MS/MS

**Objective:** To confirm the presence of BMAA in hydrolyzed proteins using liquid chromatography tandem mass spectrometry (LC/MS/MS).

**Methods:**
1. **Cell Culture and Treatment:** Human MRC-5 fibroblast cells were cultured and treated with BMAA as described in Example 1.
2. **Protein Hydrolysis:** Cell proteins were hydrolyzed in boiling 6 M HCl at 110°C for 16 hours.
3. **LC/MS/MS Analysis:** Hydrolyzed samples were analyzed using a triple quadrupole LC/MS/MS instrument. Retention times, unique daughter ion (m/z 258), and ratios of m/z transitions (m/z 459 to m/z 171 relative to m/z 459 to m/z 289 and m/z 459 to m/z 119) were used to identify BMAA.

**Results:**
- BMAA was positively identified in the hydrolyzed proteins, confirming its incorporation into the protein chain.
- The recovery of BMAA from the hydrolyzed proteins was linear and dose-dependent, further supporting the protein synthesis-dependent incorporation of BMAA.

**Conclusion:**
LC/MS/MS analysis confirms the presence of BMAA in hydrolyzed proteins, validating the protein synthesis-dependent mechanism of BMAA incorporation and the effectiveness of L-serine in preventing this misincorporation.
