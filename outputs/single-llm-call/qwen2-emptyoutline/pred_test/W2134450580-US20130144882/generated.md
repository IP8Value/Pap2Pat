# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The field of gene expression analysis has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly with the advent of network-based approaches. Traditional methods for identifying biomarkers and classifying diseases often rely on individual gene expression levels, which can lead to the overlooking of functionally related genes with subtle expression changes. These subtle changes, when considered in isolation, may not provide sufficient discriminatory power for accurate disease classification. However, when these genes are analyzed in the context of their interactions within a biological network, their collective expression patterns can reveal valuable insights.

The present invention addresses this limitation by introducing a novel method called Integrative eXpression Profiling (IXP). IXP leverages the ant colony optimization reordering (ACOR) algorithm to reorder genes in a disease-specific protein-protein interaction (PPI) network. This reordering process groups functionally related genes together, allowing for the integration of their expression values in a way that enhances the detection of subtle but coordinated expression changes. The resulting integrated expression profiles can significantly improve the accuracy of microarray-based disease classification.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for improving the accuracy of microarray-based disease classification by integrating gene expression data using a network-based approach. The method, referred to as Integrative eXpression Profiling (IXP), involves the following steps:

1. **Selection of Seed Genes**: Identify disease-associated genes from databases such as AlzGene and OMIM.
2. **Construction of Disease-Specific PPI Network**: Use a nearest neighbor expansion algorithm to construct a disease-specific PPI network from an integrated human PPI database.
3. **Reordering of Network Nodes**: Apply the ant colony optimization reordering (ACOR) algorithm to reorder the nodes in the PPI network, grouping functionally related genes together.
4. **Integration of Gene Expressions**: Map the gene expression profiles of each sample onto the reordered gene list and integrate the expression values using a Gaussian function as the influence function.

The integrated expression profiles generated by this method can be used as input features for machine learning classifiers, such as support vector machines (SVMs), to improve the accuracy of disease classification. The invention has been demonstrated to outperform traditional methods and other network-based approaches in the classification of Alzheimer's disease (AD) using microarray datasets.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

### Selection of Seed Genes

The first step in the IXP method is the selection of seed genes associated with the disease of interest. For the purpose of this embodiment, Alzheimer's disease (AD) is used as a case study. Seed genes are selected from curated databases such as AlzGene (http://www.alzgene.org/) and OMIM (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/omim). These databases contain a comprehensive list of genes that have been implicated in AD through various genetic studies and literature reviews. The selection of seed genes is crucial as they serve as the starting point for constructing the disease-specific PPI network.

### Construction of Disease-Specific PPI Network

Once the seed genes are identified, the next step is to construct a disease-specific PPI network. This is achieved using a nearest neighbor expansion algorithm applied to an integrated human PPI database, such as the Human Annotated and Predicted Protein Interaction (HAPPI) database. The HAPPI database consolidates protein interaction data from multiple sources, providing a comprehensive and reliable resource for network construction.

The nearest neighbor expansion algorithm starts with the seed genes and expands the network by including their interacting partners. This process is repeated iteratively until a predefined network size is reached or until no new interactions can be added. The resulting network is specific to the disease of interest and captures the interactions between the seed genes and their neighbors, thereby providing a more comprehensive view of the disease-related biological processes.

### Reordering of Network Nodes

The third step in the IXP method involves reordering the nodes in the disease-specific PPI network using the ant colony optimization reordering (ACOR) algorithm. The ACOR algorithm is a metaheuristic optimization technique inspired by the foraging behavior of ants. In the context of network reordering, the algorithm simulates the movement of ants across the network nodes, iteratively optimizing the order of nodes based on the density distribution of "ant colonies" on the network.

The ACOR algorithm reorders the nodes in such a way that functionally related genes are grouped together. This reordering process reveals the system-level features of the network and helps in identifying clusters of genes with similar or related functions. The reordered adjacency matrix of the network is visualized to assess the effectiveness of the reordering process. The resulting matrix often exhibits a fractal-like pattern, indicating that the ACOR algorithm has successfully grouped functionally related genes together.

### Integration of Gene Expressions

The final step in the IXP method is the integration of gene expression data. The gene expression profiles of each sample are mapped onto the reordered gene list generated in the previous step. The expression values are then integrated using a Gaussian function as the influence function. The Gaussian function assigns higher weights to genes that are closely positioned in the reordered list, effectively capturing the coordinated expression of functionally related genes.

The integrated expression profiles are generated for each sample and can be used as input features for machine learning classifiers. In this embodiment, a support vector machine (SVM) classifier is used to classify the samples into disease and control groups. The IXP method has been shown to significantly improve the accuracy of disease classification compared to traditional methods and other network-based approaches.

### Experimental Validation

To validate the effectiveness of the IXP method, the invention was applied to a microarray dataset for Alzheimer's disease (AD). The dataset GSE5281, consisting of 151 samples (67 controls and 84 AD patients), was used as the testing set. Another smaller dataset, GSE1297, with 31 samples (9 controls and 22 AD patients), was used as the training set. The results of the blinded classification on GSE5281 showed that the ACOR-based IXP approach improved the classification accuracy from 74.83% to 82.78% using an SVM classifier. This improvement is significant, especially when compared to a recently published 1372-probe signature for AD, which achieved only 61.59% accuracy under the same conditions.

### Comparative Analysis

The IXP method was also compared to other network-based approaches, including those based on network ranking (similar to the PageRank algorithm), graph clustering, and random ordering. The results consistently showed that the ACOR-based IXP approach outperformed these alternative methods in terms of classification accuracy. The superior performance of the IXP method can be attributed to its ability to capture both local and global topological characteristics of the disease-specific PPI network, thereby enhancing the detection of subtle but coordinated gene expression changes.

### Conclusion

The present invention, Integrative eXpression Profiling (IXP), represents a significant advancement in the field of gene expression analysis and disease classification. By leveraging the ant colony optimization reordering (ACOR) algorithm to reorder genes in a disease-specific PPI network, the IXP method integrates gene expression data in a way that enhances the detection of functionally related genes with subtle expression changes. The resulting integrated expression profiles can be used as input features for machine learning classifiers, leading to improved accuracy in disease classification. The invention has been validated using microarray datasets for Alzheimer's disease and has shown superior performance compared to traditional methods and other network-based approaches.
