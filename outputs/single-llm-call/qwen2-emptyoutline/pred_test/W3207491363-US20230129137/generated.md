# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Cardiovascular diseases, particularly myocardial infarction (MI), represent a significant global health burden. The adult mammalian heart has a limited regenerative capacity, making it challenging to replace cardiomyocytes lost due to MI or other cardiac disorders. Recent studies have shown that postnatal cardiomyocytes retain some latent capacity for proliferation, suggesting that targeting mechanisms of cardiomyocyte cell cycle regulation could lead to promising new therapies for heart disease. One such mechanism involves the Hippo signaling pathway, which plays a crucial role in fetal cardiac development. Two downstream components of the Hippo pathway, YES-associated protein (YAP) and transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ), have been linked to cardiac regeneration. The pharmaceutical product TT-10, a fluorine substituent of TAZ-12, has been shown to promote cardiomyocyte proliferation and reduce infarct size in mice. However, the effects of TT-10 on long-term cardiac function have been limited. To address this, the present invention focuses on encapsulating TT-10 into poly-lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) nanoparticles (NPs) to extend the duration of TT-10 delivery and improve its therapeutic efficacy for treating MI.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method and composition for enhancing the regenerative capacity of the heart following myocardial infarction (MI) by administering a pharmaceutical composition comprising TT-10 encapsulated in poly-lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA) nanoparticles (NPs). The encapsulation of TT-10 in PLGA NPs extends the duration of drug release, thereby improving the potency and longevity of its therapeutic effects. Specifically, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs and a method for treating MI by administering the composition to a subject in need thereof. The administration of TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs has been shown to promote cardiomyocyte proliferation, reduce apoptosis, and improve vascularity in the peri-infarct region, leading to significant improvements in cardiac function and reduced infarct size.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Myocardial Infarction (MI):** A condition characterized by the death of heart muscle tissue due to the interruption of blood supply, often caused by a blockage in one or more coronary arteries.
- **Cardiomyocytes:** Specialized cells that make up the heart muscle, responsible for the contraction and relaxation of the heart.
- **Hippo Signaling Pathway:** A cellular signaling pathway that regulates organ size and tissue homeostasis by controlling cell proliferation, apoptosis, and stem cell self-renewal.
- **YES-associated protein (YAP):** A transcriptional coactivator that, when activated, translocates to the nucleus and promotes the expression of genes involved in cell proliferation and survival.
- **Transcriptional coactivator with PDZ-binding motif (TAZ):** A protein that functions similarly to YAP and is also involved in regulating cell proliferation and survival.
- **TT-10:** A fluorine substituent of TAZ-12, a small molecule that promotes cardiomyocyte proliferation and survival.
- **Poly-lactic-co-glycolic acid (PLGA):** A biodegradable and biocompatible polymer commonly used in drug delivery systems to encapsulate and slowly release therapeutic agents.
- **Nanoparticles (NPs):** Particles with dimensions in the nanometer range, used in various applications including drug delivery, imaging, and diagnostics.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The pharmaceutical compositions of the present invention comprise TT-10 encapsulated in PLGA nanoparticles (NPs). The PLGA NPs are prepared using a single-emulsion (oil/water phase) technique, which ensures uniform loading and controlled release of TT-10. The encapsulation of TT-10 in PLGA NPs provides several advantages over free TT-10, including extended drug release, improved bioavailability, and reduced toxicity.

#### Preparation of PLGA NPs

PLGA NPs are prepared by dissolving PLGA in dichloromethane and ultrasonically emulsifying the solution with or without TT-10. The resulting emulsion is then mixed with a dimethylamine borane-water solution and further ultrasonicated. The mixture is subsequently transferred to a PVA-water solution, stirred, and centrifuged to remove bulk aggregates. The NPs are washed, lyophilized, and stored at -80°C. The size and morphology of the NPs are characterized using nanoparticle tracking analysis and scanning electron microscopy. The release profile of TT-10 from the NPs is monitored using a dialysis device and measured with a UV/Vis spectrophotometer.

#### Characteristics of TT-10-Loaded PLGA NPs

- **Size and Morphology:** The average diameter of the TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs is approximately 155.4 ± 0.7 nm, with a narrow size distribution. The NPs are spherical and have a smooth surface.
- **Loading Efficiency:** The loading efficiency of TT-10 in the PLGA NPs is approximately 2.50 ± 0.08 μg of TT-10 per mg of PLGA NPs.
- **Release Profile:** Approximately 47% of the encapsulated TT-10 is released within the first day, and 90% is released by day 9. This extended release profile ensures sustained therapeutic effects.

### ASPECTS

1. **Pharmaceutical Composition:** A pharmaceutical composition comprising TT-10 encapsulated in PLGA nanoparticles (NPs) for the treatment of myocardial infarction (MI).
2. **Method of Treatment:** A method for treating MI in a subject, comprising administering a therapeutically effective amount of a pharmaceutical composition comprising TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs to the subject.
3. **Dosage and Administration:** The pharmaceutical composition is administered via intramyocardial injection into the peri-infarct region of the heart. The total dosage of TT-10 used in both the TT-10-SOL and TT-10-NP groups is 67.2 ng.
4. **Therapeutic Effects:** Administration of TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs has been shown to:
   - Promote cardiomyocyte proliferation and reduce apoptosis.
   - Improve vascularity in the peri-infarct region.
   - Significantly reduce infarct size and improve cardiac function, as measured by left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), left ventricular fractional shortening (LVFS), left ventricular end-systolic diameter (LVESD), and left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD).
5. **Mechanism of Action:** The therapeutic effects of TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs are mediated by the activation of Hippo/Yap signaling, which promotes cardiomyocyte proliferation and survival. Additionally, the NPs enhance the bioavailability and sustained release of TT-10, leading to prolonged therapeutic effects.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Effect of TT-10 on Cardiomyocyte Proliferation and Survival

Human induced pluripotent stem cell–derived cardiomyocytes (hiPSC-CMs) were cultured in varying concentrations of TT-10 (0, 2 μM, 10 μM, 20 μM, or 100 μM) for 48 hours. The presence of markers for cell proliferation (Ki67 expression), S phase (BrdU incorporation), M phase (histone 3 phosphorylation [PH3]), and cytokinesis (Aurora B) was evaluated via immunofluorescence. The proportion of cells displaying each marker increased as the TT-10 concentration was raised from 0 to 2 μM, peaked at 10 to 20 μM TT-10, and then declined at 100 μM TT-10. TT-10 treatment (10 μM) also significantly reduced measures of hiPSC-CM apoptosis (TUNEL staining) and increased the proportion of hiPSC-CMs with Yap-positive nuclei. These results suggest that the effect of TT-10 on cell cycle progression and apoptosis is mediated, at least in part, by increases in Yap signaling.

#### Example 2: NP-Mediated Delivery of TT-10 for Myocardial Repair in a Mouse MI Model

MI was induced in mice via permanent ligation of the left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery. The animals were randomly assigned to treatment with Dulbecco’s PBS (DPBS), empty PLGA NPs (Empty-NPs), TT-10 solution (TT-10-SOL), or TT-10-loaded PLGA NPs (TT-10-NPs). A fifth group of animals (the sham group) underwent all surgical procedures for MI induction except arterial ligation. The mean diameters of the Empty- and TT-10-NPs were 123.3 ± 1.7 nm and 155.4 ± 0.7 nm, respectively. Loading efficiency was found to be 2.50 ± 0.08 μg of TT-10 per mg PLGA NPs. When the TT-10-NPs were incubated in DPBS, 47% of the encapsulated TT-10 was released during the first day and 90% was released by day 9.

Echocardiographic assessments of left ventricular (LV) ejection fraction (LVEF), LV fractional shortening (LVFS), LV end-systolic diameter (LVESD), and LV end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD) were performed at weeks 1 and 4 after treatment. Ejection fraction and fractional shortening were significantly greater in the TT-10-NP group at both time points, while end-systolic diameter and end-diastolic diameter were significantly lower at week 4. Infarct size and heart weight/bodyweight ratio were also significantly lower in the TT-10-NP group compared to the other treatment groups.

#### Example 3: TT-10-NP Administration Promotes Cardiomyocyte Proliferation and Angiogenesis

Subsets of mice were sacrificed 1 week after MI induction, and cardiomyocytes expressing Ki67, PH3, and nuclear Yap were quantified via immunofluorescence in border zone (BZ) heart sections. All three markers were significantly more common in BZ cardiomyocytes from TT-10-NP animals than in BZ cardiomyocytes from any other group. Apoptotic cardiomyocytes were significantly more common in BZ sections obtained from all groups at 72 hours after MI induction than in the corresponding region of hearts from sham animals, but measurements were significantly lower in TT-10-NP animals. TT-10-NP administration also promoted angiogenesis, as evidenced by the increased number of vascular structures that stained positively with the endothelial marker isolectin B4 or for the expression of α-smooth muscle actin in the peri-infarct region.

These examples demonstrate that the encapsulation of TT-10 in PLGA NPs significantly enhances its therapeutic efficacy for treating myocardial infarction by promoting cardiomyocyte proliferation, reducing apoptosis, and improving vascularity in the peri-infarct region. The extended release profile of TT-10 from the NPs ensures sustained therapeutic effects, leading to significant improvements in cardiac function and reduced infarct size.
