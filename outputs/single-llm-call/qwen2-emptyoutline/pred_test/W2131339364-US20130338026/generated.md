# DESCRIPTION

## PRIOR ART

Endometrial carcinoma is a significant health concern, affecting approximately 2–3% of women during their lifetime. While the majority of these tumors are localized and resectable at diagnosis, a notable percentage (15%–20%) recur, and metastatic cases often have poor responses to conventional treatments such as chemotherapy and radiation. The development of novel markers and therapeutic targets is crucial for improving patient outcomes. One promising approach involves identifying genes that are altered by somatic genetic events, which can drive tumor progression. The 8q24 region, frequently amplified in endometrial cancer, has been identified as a potential harbinger of aggressive disease. While MYC, located on 8q24, is a well-known oncogene, the specific role of this amplification in endometrial cancer has not been fully elucidated. Additionally, the neighboring gene ATAD2, which has been implicated in poor prognosis in other cancers, may also play a significant role in endometrial cancer. Previous research has shown that ATAD2 acts as a co-regulator of MYC and that its overexpression is associated with poor outcomes in various cancer types. However, the detailed mechanisms and clinical implications of ATAD2 overexpression in endometrial cancer remain to be fully explored.

## SUMMARY OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

The present invention relates to the identification and characterization of ATAD2 as a key target of the 8q24 amplification in endometrial cancer. The invention provides methods and compositions for detecting and quantifying ATAD2 expression and copy number, which can serve as prognostic markers for identifying patients at high risk of relapse or metastasis. Furthermore, the invention discloses the use of ATAD2 as a therapeutic target, particularly in MYC-dependent cancers. The invention includes methods for inhibiting ATAD2 expression or activity, such as through the use of RNA interference (RNAi) or small molecule inhibitors, and the use of these methods in the treatment of endometrial cancer and other MYC-driven cancers. The invention also encompasses the use of ATAD2 expression and copy number as biomarkers for stratifying patients for personalized therapy and for monitoring treatment response.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PRESENT INVENTION

### Patient Series

The invention is based on a comprehensive analysis of endometrial carcinoma primaries and metastases collected from patients treated at Haukeland University Hospital, Norway. The tumors were collected and processed according to ethical guidelines approved by the Norwegian Data Inspectorate, Norwegian Social Sciences Data Services, and the Regional Research Ethics Committee in Medicine, Western Norway. All participants provided written informed consent. The primary investigation series included tumors with genome-wide copy-number data, and a subset of these tumors had corresponding expression profiles. Additional validation series were used to corroborate the findings, including internal and external validation datasets, a qPCR series, and a FISH series.

### RNA Analysis

RNA was extracted from the collected tumors and hybridized to Agilent 44K arrays according to the manufacturer's instructions. Signal intensities were evaluated using BRB-ArrayTools, and the arrays were batch median normalized. This process allowed for the comprehensive analysis of gene expression patterns in the tumors, providing insights into the relationship between 8q24 amplification and gene expression.

### DNA Analysis

Genomic DNA was analyzed to identify copy-number alterations, particularly focusing on the 8q24 region. Fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) was performed using the MYC Spectrum Orange FISH probe kit and Chromosome enumeration probe 8 (CEP8) to detect 8q24 amplifications. The FISH analysis was conducted on tissue microarrays (TMAs) representing the highest-grade areas in each tumor. Probe and control signals were counted in 40–60 cells within areas of optimal tissue digestion and no overlapping nuclei. Amplifications were scored when the MYC/CEP8 ratio was greater than 1.0.

### Statistics

Statistical analyses were performed to relate molecular data to clinical phenotypes. Pearson’s χ2 and two-sided Student’s t-tests were used to evaluate associations between 8q24 amplification, ATAD2 expression, and clinical outcomes. Multivariate linear regression analysis was employed to predict ATAD2 expression levels. Univariate and multivariate survival analyses were conducted using the log-rank and Mantel-Cox methods. MYC signaling strength and gene expression levels were presented as Z-scores.

### Results and Discussion

#### Assessment of MYC as a Target of the 8Q24 Amplification

An integrated analysis of copy-number and expression data from a series of 82 endometrial cancers revealed that both MYC and genes upregulated by MYC were overexpressed in tumors with 8q24 amplification. However, variations in MYC expression alone explained only a small proportion of variations in MYC signaling strength. This suggested that other genes within the 8q24 region might also contribute to MYC pathway activation.

#### Expression of ATAD2 Correlates Strongly with 8Q24 Amplification and MYC Signaling

Expression of ATAD2 was found to be more strongly associated with 8q24 amplification and MYC signaling strength than any other gene in the peak region of the amplification. This correlation was observed even among samples without 8q24 amplification, indicating that ATAD2 plays a significant role in MYC pathway activation. The strong association between ATAD2 expression and MYC signaling strength was confirmed in multiple validation datasets, including internal and external validation series, qPCR series, and FISH series.

#### Amplification of 8Q24 and Expression of ATAD2, but Not MYC, Are Associated with Disease Progression

Amplification of 8q24 and high expression of ATAD2 were associated with reduced progression-free survival and increased risk of disease-specific death. These findings were consistent across multiple datasets and were particularly pronounced in non-endometrioid, high-grade, and ER-negative tumors. In contrast, MYC expression was not significantly associated with disease progression or risk of death. Metastases exhibited higher rates of 8q24 amplification, ATAD2 expression, and MYC signaling strength compared to primary tumors, further supporting the role of ATAD2 in disease progression.

#### Extension to Other Cancer Types and Normal Tissue

The relationship between 8q24 amplification and ATAD2 expression was also investigated in other cancer types, including ovarian, breast, and glioblastoma cancers. In all three cancer types, ATAD2 expression was strongly correlated with 8q24 amplification and MYC signaling strength. ATAD2 expression was higher in cancer tissues compared to adjacent normal tissue, and the correlation between ATAD2 expression and MYC signaling strength was more robust than that of MYC expression.

#### ATAD2 Expression is Correlated with E2F Gene Expression and ATAD2 Copy Number in an Additive Manner

The expression of ATAD2 was found to be influenced by multiple factors, including ATAD2 copy number, E2F1 expression, and ESR1 expression. These factors collectively explained a significant portion of the variation in ATAD2 expression, highlighting the complex regulatory mechanisms involved in ATAD2 overexpression.

#### Dependency on MYC Predicts Dependency on ATAD2 and Response to HDAC Inhibitors in Endometrial and Breast Cancer Cells

Functional studies in endometrial and breast cancer cell lines demonstrated that knockdown of ATAD2 or MYC resulted in highly correlated decreases in cell viability. This suggested that ATAD2 and MYC are functionally interdependent in cancer cells. Treatment with the HDAC inhibitor Trichostatin-A, which indirectly inhibits bromodomain-containing proteins like ATAD2, also reduced cell viability in MYC-dependent cell lines. These findings support the potential of ATAD2 as a therapeutic target in MYC-driven cancers.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a comprehensive understanding of the role of ATAD2 in endometrial cancer and its potential as a therapeutic target. The identification of ATAD2 as a key target of the 8q24 amplification and its strong association with MYC pathway activation and poor clinical outcomes offer new opportunities for developing targeted therapies and improving patient stratification. The methods and compositions disclosed herein can be used to detect and quantify ATAD2 expression and copy number, and to inhibit ATAD2 expression or activity, thereby providing valuable tools for the diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of endometrial cancer and other MYC-driven cancers.
