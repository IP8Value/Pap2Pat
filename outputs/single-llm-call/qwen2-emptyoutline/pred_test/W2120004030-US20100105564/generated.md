# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and medical diagnostics, particularly to the identification and use of gene expression profiles in morphologically normal breast epithelium and stroma for predicting clinical outcomes and assigning treatments in breast cancer patients. The invention provides compositions and methods for diagnosing and treating breast cancer based on the expression levels of specific genes in normal breast tissues.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Breast cancer is a leading cause of cancer-related deaths among women. Despite significant advances in treatment, approximately 26% of patients with early-stage breast cancer develop metastasis and succumb to the disease. Current prognostic indicators are insufficiently reliable for predicting outcomes in such patients. Microarray technology has been widely used for gene expression profiling in breast cancer and other malignancies, allowing for the identification of gene expression changes between normal and tumor tissues. These approaches have led to the identification of breast cancer subtypes and potential prognostic markers, although their clinical utility remains uncertain.

The normal human breast consists of ductal epithelium and surrounding stroma. The stroma, which accounts for more than 80% of the breast volume, provides nutritional and structural support for the epithelium. Carcinoma of the breast is thought to originate from epithelial cells or progenitor epithelial cells of the terminal duct-lobular unit. However, growing evidence suggests that the stroma plays a crucial role in cancer initiation and progression. Little is known about the gene expression profiles in morphologically normal breast stroma or epithelium adjacent to breast tumor tissue.

Laser capture microdissection (LCM) allows for the isolation of nearly pure cell populations from heterogeneous environments, making it suitable for microarray gene expression analysis. This approach has been used to compare gene expression profiles between normal human breast epithelium and tumor tissue, revealing distinct gene expression patterns in epithelium derived from regions of the breast adjacent to tumors. However, these studies have typically involved small sample sizes and have not considered stromal gene expression.

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a comprehensive analysis of gene expression in morphologically normal breast epithelium and stroma derived from both breast cancer patients and healthy individuals undergoing reduction mammoplasty. The invention identifies specific gene expression profiles that can be used to predict clinical outcomes and guide treatment decisions in breast cancer patients.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Identification of Differentially Expressed Genes in Normal Breast Epithelium and Stroma

To identify differentially expressed genes in normal breast epithelium and stroma, laser capture microdissection (LCM) was used to isolate nearly pure populations of epithelial and stromal cells from morphologically normal breast tissue. RNA was extracted from these cell populations and subjected to microarray analysis using Agilent whole genome arrays. The expression levels of a plurality of genes were determined, and the data were normalized and analyzed using various statistical methods, including hierarchical clustering, multidimensional scaling, and class distinction.

The analysis revealed that the gene expression profiles of morphologically normal stroma and epithelium from breast cancer patients were not statistically distinct from those of healthy individuals undergoing reduction mammoplasty. The analysis also identified specific genes and gene sets that were differentially expressed in normal breast epithelium and stroma. These genes and gene sets can be used as biomarkers for predicting clinical outcomes and guiding treatment decisions.

### Example 2

#### Validation of Differentially Expressed Genes by Immunohistochemistry

To validate the differentially expressed genes identified by microarray analysis, immunohistochemistry was performed on frozen tissue sections using commercially available antibodies. The expression of selected proteins, such as Kit, von Willebrand factor, and CD31, was confirmed in the tissue sections. The results of the immunohistochemistry were consistent with the microarray data, confirming the differential expression of these genes in normal breast epithelium and stroma.

### Example 3

#### Prediction of Clinical Outcomes Using Gene Expression Profiles

To predict clinical outcomes in breast cancer patients, the expression levels of the differentially expressed genes identified in normal breast epithelium and stroma were determined in a cohort of breast cancer patients. The expression levels were compared to a reference gene expression profile, and the patients were stratified into high-risk and low-risk groups based on the comparison. The clinical outcomes of the patients, including metastasis, recurrence, and response to treatment, were monitored over time. The results showed that the gene expression profiles were predictive of clinical outcomes, with high-risk patients having a higher likelihood of metastasis and recurrence.

### Example 4

#### Assignment of Treatments Based on Gene Expression Profiles

To assign treatments to breast cancer patients, the expression levels of the differentially expressed genes identified in normal breast epithelium and stroma were determined in a cohort of breast cancer patients. The expression levels were compared to a reference gene expression profile, and the patients were assigned to different treatment groups based on the comparison. The treatment groups included chemotherapy, hormone therapy, and targeted therapy. The clinical outcomes of the patients, including response to treatment and survival, were monitored over time. The results showed that the assignment of treatments based on gene expression profiles improved the clinical outcomes of the patients, with a higher response rate and longer survival in the appropriately treated groups.

By providing compositions and methods for identifying and using gene expression profiles in morphologically normal breast epithelium and stroma, the present invention offers a powerful tool for predicting clinical outcomes and assigning treatments in breast cancer patients. The invention has the potential to improve the accuracy of prognostic predictions and the effectiveness of treatment decisions, ultimately leading to better patient outcomes.
