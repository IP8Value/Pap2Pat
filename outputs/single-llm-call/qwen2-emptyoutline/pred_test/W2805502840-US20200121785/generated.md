# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of veterinary medicine, specifically to the development of recombinant viral vectors for use as vaccines. More particularly, the invention pertains to the use of recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) as a vector for expressing protective antigens from other avian pathogens, thereby providing a means to induce simultaneous protection against multiple avian diseases.

## BACKGROUND

Marek’s disease virus (MDV-1) or Gallid herpesvirus 2, belonging to the Mardivirus genus in the family Herpesviridae, is the causative agent of Marek’s disease (MD), characterized by rapid-onset lymphomatous tumors and paralytic symptoms due to neuronal infiltration of lymphocytes. In infected birds, MDV replicates in the feather follicle epithelial cells, from where it is transmitted to other birds by inhalation of infected dust and dander. The mortality rate from MD typically ranges between 10–30%, but can reach up to 100% depending on the infecting strain, host susceptibility, and vaccination status. MD has been controlled for more than 40 years through the widespread use of live attenuated vaccines, including the naturally attenuated MDV-1 strain Rispens (CVI988), MDV-2 (GaHV3) strain SB-1, and herpesvirus of turkeys (HVT) strain FC126.

While these vaccines have been effective in controlling MD, there is a growing need for multivalent vaccines that can protect against multiple avian diseases. Avian herpesvirus vaccine strains, such as HVT, have been used successfully as recombinant vaccine vectors to protect against various avian viral pathogens. However, the combined use of multiple HVT-based vaccines has been shown to result in interference, leading to reduced efficacy. Therefore, there is a need for alternative vector platforms that can complement HVT-based vaccines and induce robust protective responses against multiple antigens.

The first GaHV3 strain licensed for use as a vaccine against MD was the SB-1 strain, which has been used successfully in combination with HVT to provide superior protection against very virulent MDV pathotypes. The 166-kilobase genome of the SB-1 strain shares a similar organization with MDV-1 and contains a unique set of genes and microRNAs. Previous work has demonstrated the construction of a full-length infectious genome of the SB-1 strain in a bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC), enabling the manipulation of the viral genome using recombineering techniques. This has paved the way for developing SB-1 as a recombinant vector for expressing protective antigens from other avian pathogens.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) vector engineered to express protective antigens from other avian pathogens. Specifically, the invention encompasses a recombinant SB-1 viral vector expressing the VP2 capsid protein of infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV) to induce immune responses and protection against the very virulent UK661 IBDV isolate.

The invention further provides methods for producing the recombinant GaHV3 vector, as well as vaccines and methods for using the vaccines to protect against avian diseases. The recombinant SB-1 vector can be used in combination with other vaccines, such as HVT-based vaccines, to provide multivalent protection against multiple avian diseases without interference.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) Vector

The present invention is directed to a recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) vector, specifically the SB-1 strain, engineered to express protective antigens from other avian pathogens. The SB-1 strain is a naturally attenuated MDV-2 strain that has been used successfully as a vaccine against Marek’s disease. The recombinant SB-1 vector is constructed using a full-length infectious genome of the SB-1 strain in a bacterial artificial chromosome (BAC), allowing for precise manipulation of the viral genome using recombineering techniques.

The recombinant SB-1 vector expresses the VP2 capsid protein of infectious bursal disease virus (IBDV) from one of three different locations in the viral genome: loci UL3/4, UL10/11, and UL21/22. The expression of the VP2 cassette is driven by a murine cytomegalovirus immediate early protein 2 (mCMV IE2) promoter and terminated by a SV40 early polyadenylation signal. The VP2 gene is derived from the IBDV strain Faragher 52/70.

### An Intermediate Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) Vector Comprising One or More Marker(s)

In the process of constructing the recombinant SB-1 vector, intermediate vectors are generated that contain one or more markers to facilitate selection and verification of the recombinant constructs. These markers include the galK expression cassette, which allows for the selection of positive colonies based on their ability to utilize galactose as the sole carbon source in minimal media. The galK cassette is subsequently replaced by the VP2 expression cassette, and positive colonies are selected based on their ability to grow in the presence of 2-deoxy-galactose. The integration of the VP2 cassette is confirmed by specific PCR and sequencing.

### Methods for Producing a Recombinant Gallid Herpesvirus 3 (GaHV3; MDV-2) Vector

The methods for producing the recombinant SB-1 vector involve the following steps:

1. **Cloning of the VP2 Expression Cassette**: The VP2 expression cassette, including the mCMV IE2 promoter, the VP2 gene, and the SV40 early polyadenylation signal, is cloned into the BAC containing the full-length infectious genome of the SB-1 strain. The cloning is performed using a galK selection-based recombineering approach, where the galK cassette is inserted into the desired loci (UL3/4, UL10/11, or UL21/22) and positive colonies are selected based on their ability to utilize galactose. The galK cassette is then replaced by the VP2 expression cassette, and positive colonies are selected based on their ability to grow in the presence of 2-deoxy-galactose.

2. **Reconstitution of the Recombinant Virus**: The BAC DNA containing the VP2 expression cassette is transfected into chicken embryonic fibroblasts (CEF) using Lipofectamine® transfection reagent. The reconstituted viruses are passaged to generate working virus stocks, and the titers are calculated by counting the plaque numbers four days post-infection.

3. **Verification of VP2 Expression**: The expression of the VP2 protein in the recombinant SB-1 viruses is verified using immunostaining with IBDV VP2-specific mouse monoclonal antibodies followed by staining with goat anti-mouse HRP-conjugated antibodies. Confocal microscopy is used to visualize the infected cells.

### Vaccines

The recombinant SB-1 vector expressing the IBDV VP2 capsid protein can be formulated into vaccines for use in protecting poultry against Marek’s disease and infectious bursal disease. The vaccines can be administered subcutaneously or as in ovo vaccinations. The recombinant SB-1 vector can be used in combination with other vaccines, such as HVT-based vaccines, to provide multivalent protection against multiple avian diseases.

### Use of the Vaccine

The recombinant SB-1 vector expressing the IBDV VP2 capsid protein is used to protect poultry against Marek’s disease and infectious bursal disease. The vaccine is administered to one-day-old specific-pathogen-free (SPF) Rhode Island Red (RIR) chicks, and the immune response is evaluated by measuring the neutralizing antibody levels in the sera of vaccinated chickens. The protection against infectious bursal disease is assessed by challenging the vaccinated birds with a very virulent UK661 strain of IBDV and monitoring the clinical signs and survival rates.

## EXAMPLES

### Cell Culture and Virus Propagation

Chicken embryonic fibroblasts (CEF) were prepared from 9-10 day old embryos of specific-pathogen-free (SPF) Rhode Island Red (RIR) birds in E199 media (Sigma) with 5% fetal bovine serum. DF-1 cells were propagated in Dulbecco’s modified Eagles medium (DMEM, Sigma) with 10% serum. DT40 cells were propagated in RPMI-1640 medium with 10% serum. All cell culture media were supplemented with 100 U/ml penicillin, 100 μg/ml streptomycin, and 0.25 μg/ml fungizone.

For the preparation of recombinant virus stocks, CEF were transfected with the BAC DNA from the recombinant constructs using Lipofectamine® transfection reagent (ThermoFisher) and reconstituted viruses were passaged to generate working virus stocks. Titration of SB-1 vaccine viruses was performed in CEF, and the titers were calculated by counting the plaque numbers four days post-infection. Recombinant virus plaques were confirmed using immunostaining with IBDV VP2-specific mouse monoclonal antibody clone HH7 (IgG1) followed by staining with goat anti-mouse HRP-conjugated antibody (DAKO) and TrueBlue™ (KPL) peroxidase substrate. Additionally, CEF infected cells were stained with monoclonal antibodies IA7 (anti-IBDV VP2) (IgG2a) and SB-1-specific monoclonal antibody Y5.9. IA7 and SB-1 stained infected cells were visualized with anti-IgG2a Alexa Fluor 568 and anti-IgG1 Alexa Fluor 488 antibodies, respectively. Stained cells were examined with a Leica SP5 confocal microscope.

### Virus Growth Curve Studies

Confluent CEF in 10 cm² dishes were infected in duplicate with 100 μl of 10³ pfu of SB-1 viruses. Following the infection, infected CEF cells were harvested at time points 0, 12, 24, 48, 96, and 120 hours post-infection. The harvested cells were titrated immediately by plaque assay. The experiment was repeated independently three times.

### Serum Neutralization Test

Serum samples collected by centrifugation were heat-treated at 56°C for 30 minutes to inactivate complement factors before performing the neutralization test. Serial dilutions of sera samples were incubated with 100 TCID₅₀ of D78 strain of IBDV for one hour at 37°C, and the serum-virus mixtures were incubated with DF-1 monolayer in 96-well plates for one hour before replacing with 2% DMEM media. The cells were checked after four days for evidence of cytopathic effects to determine serum neutralization titers. The highest dilution of the serum that prevented cytopathic effect was considered as the neutralization titer.

### Statistical Analysis

Two-way ANOVA with multiple comparisons test was employed to compare virus titers at each time point of the growth curve. Differences in levels of neutralizing antibodies during the course of the study and between the groups were analyzed using two-way ANOVA test. The level of antibodies within each group was analyzed using one-way ANOVA test. The survival rate between groups of birds after the challenge was compared using the Mantel-Cox test.

### Analysis of Different Loci to Insert an Expression Cassette

Three different loci in the SB-1 genome (UL3/4, UL10/11, and UL21/22) were evaluated for their ability to tolerate the VP2 expression cassette. The expression of the VP2 cassette from the three loci appeared to slow the growth of SB-1 in vitro. Among the three locations, the VP2 expressing transcripts were found to have a higher level of expression from the UL3-UL4 location compared to UL21-UL22 or UL10-UL11 loci. On the other hand, the level of expression for the adjacent genes was least affected when the transgene was expressed from the UL10-UL11 location.

### Example 2

#### Immunogenicity of the Recombinant SB-1 Vaccines

The immunogenicity of the recombinant SB-1 vaccines was assessed by measuring the neutralizing antibody levels in vaccinated chickens. Neutralizing antibodies started to appear from week 2 and rose to a maximum titer of 640 in week four post-vaccination. All the birds in all four groups showed neutralizing antibodies by 4 weeks. Although not statistically significant, the mean values of neutralizing antibodies in the groups inoculated with the experimental vaccines were higher than those of the group that received the commercial vaccine.

### Example 3

#### Protection Against Very Virulent IBDV

In the second experiment, the effect of the SB-1 vaccines administered at a reduced dose of 1000 plaque-forming units (pfu) was examined. Despite the lower dose, all of the groups showed 100% protection against IBDV after an experimental challenge with the very virulent UK661 strain of IBD virus, compared to the group vaccinated with the parental pSB-1 vaccine that did not express the VP2 antigen. This suggests that the mechanism of protection may not be solely mediated by high titers of serum neutralizing antibodies, and that cellular or mucosal responses may also contribute.

### Vaccine Development Against IBDV: Immunization Study

The recombinant SB-1 vector expressing the IBDV VP2 capsid protein was used to vaccinate one-day-old SPF RIR chicks. The immune response was evaluated by measuring the neutralizing antibody levels in the sera of vaccinated chickens. The protection against infectious bursal disease was assessed by challenging the vaccinated birds with a very virulent UK661 strain of IBDV and monitoring the clinical signs and survival rates. The results demonstrated that the recombinant SB-1 vector provided complete protection against the challenge with the very virulent IBDV strain, confirming its value as a recombinant vaccine vector platform for avian diseases.
