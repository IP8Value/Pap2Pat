# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of cancer diagnostics and therapy, particularly to the use of phosphatidylserine (PS) as a biomarker and therapeutic target. More specifically, the invention pertains to methods and compositions for enhancing the efficacy of PS-targeting therapies and imaging modalities by modulating the surface PS exposure on cancer cells using electric fields.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cancer is a leading cause of death worldwide, affecting individuals regardless of age, sex, or social status. Despite advances in chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and surgery, the mortality rate for cancer remains high. Immunotherapy has emerged as a promising addition to the arsenal of cancer treatments, but there is still an urgent need for new therapeutic approaches that improve patient survival with fewer toxic side effects.

Imaging plays a crucial role in personalized cancer medicine, aiding in the detection, staging, and monitoring of cancer. Biomarkers, such as proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, glycoproteins, and carbohydrates, are essential for the early detection and targeted treatment of cancer. Among these biomarkers, phosphatidylserine (PS) stands out due to its unique dual role as a diagnostic tool and a therapeutic target.

PS is a lipid that is normally found on the cytosolic side of the cell membrane. In cancer cells, the asymmetry of PS distribution is disrupted, leading to the exposure of PS on the outer cell membrane. This exposure of PS on cancer cells can be exploited for both imaging and therapeutic purposes. Various PS-targeting agents, such as antibodies, peptides, and nanovesicles, have been developed for cancer diagnosis and treatment. However, the efficacy of these agents can be limited by the variability in surface PS exposure among different cancer cells.

Recent studies have shown that the application of electric fields can modulate the surface PS exposure on cancer cells, potentially enhancing the efficacy of PS-targeting therapies. This invention aims to provide methods and compositions for modulating surface PS exposure using electric fields to improve the effectiveness of PS-targeting diagnostics and therapies.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Phosphatidylserine

Phosphatidylserine (PS) is a phospholipid that is normally found on the cytosolic side of the cell membrane. In normal cells, PS is maintained in this position by a group of ATPases and amino phospholipid translocases (flippases). However, in cancer cells, the asymmetry of PS distribution is disrupted, leading to the exposure of PS on the outer cell membrane. This exposure of PS on cancer cells can be exploited for both imaging and therapeutic purposes.

PS plays a crucial role in various cellular activities, including presynaptic neurotransmitter release, post-synaptic receptor activity, mitochondrial membrane integrity, and stimulation of protein kinase C in memory generation. In cancer cells, the exposure of PS on the outer cell membrane serves as a biomarker for cancer and can be targeted by various PS-targeting agents.

### PS-Targeting Drugs

PS-targeting drugs are designed to bind to PS on the outer cell membrane of cancer cells. These drugs can be used for both imaging and therapeutic purposes. Examples of PS-targeting drugs include:

- **Antibodies**: Monoclonal antibodies such as bavituximab and PGN635 specifically target PS and can be used for imaging and therapy.
- **Peptides**: Peptide-based agents such as PPS1D1 can bind to PS and exhibit cytotoxic effects on cancer cells.
- **Nanovesicles**: Nanovesicles composed of SapC (saposin C) and DOPS (dioleoylphosphatidylserine) can selectively target and kill cancer cells.

### Altering the Surface PS to Increase the Efficacy of PS Targeting Modalities

The efficacy of PS-targeting therapies can be limited by the variability in surface PS exposure among different cancer cells. Some cancer cells have low surface PS exposure and are less sensitive to PS-targeting agents, while others have high surface PS exposure and are more sensitive. To overcome this limitation, the present invention provides methods for modulating the surface PS exposure on cancer cells using electric fields.

#### Mechanism of Action

The application of electric fields can modulate the surface PS exposure on cancer cells by altering the activity of calcium channels and flippases. Increased intracellular calcium levels inhibit the activity of flippases, leading to the externalization of PS on the cell membrane. The electric field can be applied using a non-contact, parallel-plate capacitor device, which generates a direct current (DC) electric field. The electric field can be applied at varying magnitudes (e.g., 75–150 V/cm) to achieve the desired modulation of surface PS exposure.

#### Advantages

The use of electric fields to modulate surface PS exposure offers several advantages over traditional methods:

- **Non-Invasive**: The non-contact, parallel-plate capacitor device does not directly contact the cells or cell medium, minimizing the risk of side effects.
- **Selective**: The electric field primarily affects cancer cells, leaving normal, untransformed cells unaffected.
- **Synergistic Effects**: The modulation of surface PS exposure can enhance the efficacy of PS-targeting therapies, leading to synergistic effects when combined with existing treatments such as chemotherapy, radiation, and immune checkpoint inhibitors.

### Phosphatidylserine as a Tumor Imaging Target

PS can be used as a target for tumor imaging using various imaging modalities, including optical imaging, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), positron emission tomography (PET), and single-photon emission computed tomography (SPECT).

#### Optical Imaging

Optical imaging using PS-targeting agents such as CellVue Maroon (CVM)-tagged SapC-DOPS nanovesicles has been successfully used to detect brain tumors and arthritic joints in mice. The optical imaging intensity depends on the optical imaging angle, which varies with cancer type in different animal models. While optical imaging is highly sensitive, it has limitations in human patients due to challenges associated with signal detection in deep tissues and autofluorescence.

#### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

MRI is a widely used method for tumor detection, offering excellent soft tissue contrast and spatial resolution. PS-targeting agents such as iron oxide particles encapsulated inside SapC-DOPS nanocarriers can be used for selective MRI of tumors. The application of these agents has been shown to significantly increase the R2 and R2* relaxation rates in cancer cells, leading to a clear reduction in T1 relaxation time.

#### PET/SPECT Imaging

PET and SPECT are imaging techniques commonly used in the clinic to detect gamma rays emitted from radioactive tracers. Radiolabeled PS-targeting agents such as 89Zr-labeled PGN635 and 74As-labeled bavituximab have been used to develop PET imaging techniques for tumor detection. These agents have shown strong accumulation in tumors undergoing apoptosis, providing valuable information for treatment monitoring.

### Electric Field

Electric fields can be used to modulate the surface PS exposure on cancer cells, enhancing the efficacy of PS-targeting therapies. The application of electric fields involves the use of a non-contact, parallel-plate capacitor device to generate a direct current (DC) electric field. The electric field can be applied at varying magnitudes to achieve the desired modulation of surface PS exposure.

#### General Considerations for Using Electric Field-Based Therapies

When applying electric fields for therapeutic purposes, several considerations must be taken into account to ensure safety and efficacy:

- **Dissipated Energy**: The dissipated energy should be kept as low as possible to avoid thermal effects on the tissues.
- **Electrode Placement**: Correct placement of electrodes is crucial to ensure that the electric field is applied to the target tissue without affecting normal cells.
- **Biological Properties**: Factors such as cell cycle phase, extracellular environment, and ionic concentrations in the intracellular compartments must be considered to optimize the application of electric fields.

#### Tumor-Treating Fields (TTFields)

TTFields are an intermediate frequency (100–300 kHz) alternating electric field approved by the FDA for the treatment of glioblastoma multiforme (GBM). TTFields are applied to the scalp using transducer arrays to deliver low-intensity electric fields. The mechanism of action involves interfering with the orientation of tubulin in the mitotic spindle, leading to mitotic catastrophe and cell death. TTFields have been shown to be well-tolerated and effective in combination with conventional therapies.

#### Electroporation

Electroporation is a phenomenon that increases cell membrane permeability by inducing an electric field across the cell membrane. Irreversible electroporation (IRE) can be used to permanently permeabilize the cell membrane, leading to cell death. IRE has been used in the treatment of various cancers, including melanoma, sarcoma, and metastatic breast cancer. The combination of IRE with chemotherapy or radiation therapy can enhance treatment efficacy.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Modulation of Surface PS Exposure Using Electric Fields**

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Cells:** U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin.
- **Electric Field Application:** A non-contact, parallel-plate capacitor device was used to apply a direct current (DC) electric field to the cells. The electric field was applied at varying magnitudes (75–150 V/cm) for 30 minutes.
- **Surface PS Detection:** Surface PS exposure was detected using annexin V-FITC staining and flow cytometry.

**Results:**
- Application of DC electric fields at 75–150 V/cm resulted in a significant increase in surface PS exposure on U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells.
- The increase in surface PS exposure was dose-dependent, with higher electric field magnitudes leading to greater increases in surface PS exposure.
- The total cellular PS levels remained unchanged, indicating that the electric field primarily affected the distribution of PS on the cell membrane.

### Example 2

**Enhancement of PS-Targeting Therapy Efficacy Using Electric Fields**

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Cells:** U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin.
- **Electric Field Application:** A non-contact, parallel-plate capacitor device was used to apply a DC electric field at 100 V/cm for 30 minutes.
- **PS-Targeting Agent:** SapC-DOPS nanovesicles were prepared and incubated with the cells for 24 hours.
- **Cell Viability Assay:** Cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay.

**Results:**
- Application of the DC electric field followed by treatment with SapC-DOPS nanovesicles resulted in a significant reduction in cell viability compared to treatment with SapC-DOPS alone.
- The combination of electric field application and SapC-DOPS treatment led to a synergistic effect, with a greater reduction in cell viability observed at lower concentrations of SapC-DOPS.

### Example 3

**Optical Imaging of Tumors Using PS-Targeting Agents**

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Animal Model:** U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells were implanted subcutaneously in nude mice.
- **PS-Targeting Agent:** CellVue Maroon (CVM)-tagged SapC-DOPS nanovesicles were prepared and administered intravenously.
- **Optical Imaging:** Multi-angle rotational optical imaging was performed using a rotational bed to obtain images at different angles.

**Results:**
- Administration of CVM-tagged SapC-DOPS nanovesicles resulted in strong optical imaging signals at the tumor site.
- The optical imaging intensity varied with the imaging angle, with optimal signals obtained at 10° and 20° for different tumor models.
- The optical imaging signals were enhanced following irradiation, indicating the potential of this method for monitoring treatment efficacy.

### Example 4

**MRI of Tumors Using PS-Targeting Agents**

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Animal Model:** U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells were implanted subcutaneously in nude mice.
- **PS-Targeting Agent:** Iron oxide particles encapsulated inside SapC-DOPS nanocarriers were prepared and administered intravenously.
- **MRI:** T2*-weighted imaging was performed using a 7T MRI scanner.

**Results:**
- Administration of iron oxide particle-loaded SapC-DOPS nanocarriers resulted in a significant decrease in T2* signal intensity in the tumor region.
- The decrease in signal intensity was observed throughout the tumor and was sustained for up to 24 hours post-injection.
- Inductively coupled plasma atomic emission spectroscopy (ICP-AES) analysis confirmed a 5-fold higher concentration of iron in the tumor of mice treated with SapC-DOPS-IO compared to free iron oxide-treated mice.

### Example 5

**PET Imaging of Tumors Using PS-Targeting Agents**

**Materials and Methods:**
- **Animal Model:** U87ΔEGFR-Luc glioblastoma cells were implanted subcutaneously in nude mice.
- **PS-Targeting Agent:** 89Zr-labeled PGN635 was prepared and administered intravenously.
- **PET Imaging:** PET imaging was performed using a small-animal PET scanner.

**Results:**
- Administration of 89Zr-labeled PGN635 resulted in strong accumulation in the tumor region, with tumor-to-blood ratios of up to 13.
- The PET imaging signals were enhanced in tumors undergoing apoptosis, indicating the potential of this method for monitoring treatment efficacy.
- The combination of 89Zr-labeled PGN635 with other therapies, such as chemotherapy or radiation, further enhanced the imaging signals and treatment outcomes.
