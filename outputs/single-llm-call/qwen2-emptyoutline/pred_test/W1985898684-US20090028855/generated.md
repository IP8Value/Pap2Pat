# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a novel small molecule inhibitor of Akt, designated as API-1, and methods for its use in treating cancers characterized by hyperactivated Akt. Akt, also known as protein kinase B (PKB), is a serine/threonine kinase that plays a crucial role in various cellular processes, including cell survival, proliferation, metabolism, and angiogenesis. Aberrant activation of the Akt pathway is frequently observed in human malignancies, making Akt an attractive target for the development of anticancer therapies.

Several Akt inhibitors have been reported, but many lack sufficient selectivity and efficacy, particularly in vivo. For instance, perifosine, a lipid-based non-selective Akt inhibitor, has shown limited success in clinical trials due to adverse side effects. There is a significant unmet need for potent and selective Akt inhibitors that can effectively inhibit Akt activity with minimal adverse effects.

The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel small molecule, API-1, which specifically inhibits Akt by binding to the pleckstrin homology (PH) domain and blocking Akt membrane translocation. This mechanism of action allows API-1 to effectively inhibit Akt activity in both in vitro and in vivo settings, making it a promising candidate for the treatment of cancers with hyperactivated Akt.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a novel small molecule inhibitor of Akt, designated as API-1, and methods for its use in treating cancers characterized by hyperactivated Akt. API-1 is a pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidine derivative that specifically inhibits Akt by binding to the PH domain and blocking Akt membrane translocation. This unique mechanism of action allows API-1 to effectively inhibit Akt activity, leading to the suppression of cell growth and induction of apoptosis in cancer cells with hyperactivated Akt.

The invention encompasses the following aspects:
1. **API-1 Compound**: A novel small molecule inhibitor of Akt, designated as API-1, having the chemical structure of pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidine.
2. **Pharmaceutical Compositions**: Pharmaceutical compositions comprising API-1 and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.
3. **Methods of Use**: Methods for treating cancers characterized by hyperactivated Akt, comprising administering an effective amount of API-1 to a subject in need thereof.
4. **In Vitro and In Vivo Efficacy**: Demonstrated efficacy of API-1 in inhibiting Akt activity, suppressing cell growth, and inducing apoptosis in cancer cells with hyperactivated Akt, both in vitro and in vivo.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### MATERIALS AND METHODS

**Cell Lines and Compounds**:
All cell lines used in this study were either purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC) or described previously. The 2300 compounds screened for Akt inhibition were sourced from the NCI/DTP Open Chemical Repository. HA-tagged Akt1, AKT2, and AKT3 expression plasmids were previously described. Wild-type human AKT1 constructs were created using reverse transcription-PCR with MCF10A RNA as the template. The PCR products were cloned into the BamH1-EcoRI sites of the pCMV-Myc-Tag2 vector. The AKT1-E17K plasmid was generated by site-directed mutagenesis of the wild-type AKT1 construct.

**Screening for Inhibition of Akt-Transformed Cell Growth**:
NIH3T3 cells transformed with AKT2 or transfected with the LXSN vector were plated into 96-well plates. Cells were treated with 5 μM concentrations of each compound, and cell growth was assessed using a CellTier 96 One Solution Cell Proliferation kit. Compounds that inhibited growth in AKT2-transformed but not LXSN-transfected NIH3T3 cells were selected for further analysis.

**In Vitro Protein Kinase and Apoptosis Assays**:
In vitro kinase assays were performed as previously described. Apoptosis was detected using annexin V staining according to the manufacturer's instructions. Recombinant Akt and PDK1 were purchased from Upstate Biotechnology.

**API-1 and Akt Protein Binding Assay**:
API-1 was immobilized on NHS-activated Sepharose beads through covalent linkage using its amino group. The coupled beads were incubated with recombinant Akt1 or GST fusion proteins (e.g., GST-PH, GST-KD, or GST-CT) overnight at 4°C. Binding proteins were separated by 10% SDS-PAGE and immunoblotted with anti-Akt1 and anti-GST antibodies.

**Anti-tumor Activity in Nude Mouse Tumor Xenograft Model**:
Tumor cells were injected subcutaneously into the flanks of 8-week-old female nude mice. When tumors reached approximately 100 mm³, animals were randomized and treated intraperitoneally with vehicle or API-1 (10 mg/kg/day). Tumor growth was monitored, and the effect of API-1 on Akt phosphorylation was assessed by immunoblotting.

### EXAMPLE 1

**Identification of API-1 as a Small Molecule Akt Inhibitor**:
To identify small molecule inhibitors of Akt, 2300 compounds from the NCI/DTP Open Chemical Repository were screened for their ability to inhibit the growth of AKT2-transformed NIH3T3 cells but not LXSN-transfected NIH3T3 cells. Triple experiments identified 32 compounds that inhibited growth only in AKT2-transformed cells. One of these compounds, designated as API-1, was characterized further. API-1 specifically inhibits the kinase activity and phosphorylation levels of Akt in living cells. The chemical structure of API-1 is a pyrido[2,3-d]pyrimidine derivative, which is structurally related to the antibiotic sangivamycin.

### EXAMPLE 2

**Inhibition of Akt Kinase Activity by API-1**:
HEK293 cells were transfected with HA-tagged wild-type Akt1, AKT2, and AKT3. After serum starvation, cells were treated with API-1 for 60 minutes before EGF stimulation. Immunoprecipitation with anti-HA antibody followed by in vitro kinase assays showed that API-1 inhibited EGF-induced kinase activity of Akt1, AKT2, and AKT3. Additionally, API-1 reduced the phosphorylation levels of Akt at Thr308 and Ser473 in OVCAR3 cells, which express elevated levels of phospho-Akt, with an IC50 of approximately 0.8 μM.

### EXAMPLE 3

**Direct Binding of API-1 to Akt Protein and Inhibition of Akt Membrane Translocation**:
To explore the mechanism of API-1's action, a protein kinase-compound binding assay was performed. API-1 was immobilized on NHS-activated Sepharose beads and incubated with recombinant Akt protein. Immunoblotting analysis revealed that API-1 bound to Akt. Further experiments using GST-PH, GST-KD, and GST-CT fusion proteins showed that the PH domain of Akt bound to API-1. Immunofluorescence staining of HeLa cells transfected with Myc-Akt and treated with API-1 demonstrated that API-1 abrogated IGF1-induced Akt membrane translocation. Similar results were observed with the GFP-Akt and GFP-PH domain of Akt, confirming that API-1 inhibits Akt through binding to the PH domain and blocking Akt membrane translocation.

### EXAMPLE 4

**API-1 Does Not Inhibit Upstream Activators of Akt**:
To determine if API-1 affects upstream activators of Akt, such as PI3K and PDK1, HEK293 cells were treated with API-1 or PI3K inhibitor wortmannin before EGF stimulation. In vitro PI3K kinase assays showed that API-1 did not inhibit PI3K activity. Similarly, API-1 did not affect in vitro PDK1 kinase activity or the phosphorylation levels of PDK1-Ser241. Co-immunoprecipitation and immunoblotting experiments demonstrated that API-1 had no effect on the interaction of Rictor-mTOR (mTORC2) and Raptor-mTOR (mTORC1) complexes, although it inhibited phospho-mTOR-S2841, indicating that API-1 directly inhibits Akt.

### EXAMPLE 5

**Selectivity of API-1 for Akt Over Other Kinases**:
API-1 was tested for its effects on the enzymatic activities of PKA, PKC, SGK, and other signaling molecules such as ERK, JNK, p38, and STAT3. In vitro kinase assays and immunoblotting analysis showed that API-1 did not inhibit the activities of PKA, PKC, or SGK. Additionally, API-1 did not alter the phosphorylation levels of Stat3, JNK, p38, or ERK1/2, demonstrating its selectivity for Akt.

### EXAMPLE 6

**Inhibition of Constitutively Active Akt and Downstream Targets by API-1**:
HEK293 cells were transfected with HA-myr-Akt1, HA-Akt1-E40K, and HA-myr-AKT2. After treatment with API-1, in vitro kinase assays and immunoblotting analysis revealed that API-1 inhibited the kinase activity and phosphorylation levels of all four forms of constitutively active Akt, including the naturally occurring mutant AKT1-E17K. API-1 also inhibited the phosphorylation of downstream targets of Akt, such as glycogen synthase kinase 3β (GSK3β), mTOR/p70 S6K, Bad, and FOXO3a, in OVCAR3 and H661 cells.

**Comparison with API-2**:
API-1 was compared with API-2 (triciribine) for their potency in inhibiting Akt. HEK293 cells were transfected with wild-type Myc-AKT1 and constitutively active Myc-AKT1-E17K. After treatment with API-1 or API-2, in vitro kinase assays and immunoblotting analysis showed that API-1 was more potent than API-2 in inhibiting both growth factor-induced Akt and constitutively active Akt.

**Inhibition of Tumor Growth in Vitro and in Vivo**:
API-1 was tested for its ability to inhibit cell growth and induce apoptosis in cancer cell lines with hyperactivated Akt. Treatment with API-1 significantly inhibited cell proliferation and induced apoptosis in OVCAR3, OVCAR8, MDA-MB-468, and MCF7 cells, which express elevated levels of Akt, but had minimal effects on OVCAR5 and MDA-MB-435s cells, which express low levels of activated Akt. In a nude mouse tumor xenograft model, API-1 significantly inhibited the growth of OVCAR3 and PANC-1 tumors with hyperactivated Akt, while having little effect on OVCAR5 and COLO357 tumors with low levels of activated Akt. These results demonstrate the potential of API-1 as a therapeutic agent for treating cancers with hyperactivated Akt.
