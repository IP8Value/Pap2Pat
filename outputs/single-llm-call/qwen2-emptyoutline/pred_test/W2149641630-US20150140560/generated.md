## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method and apparatus for detecting and quantifying DNA in solutions using an asynchronous magnetic bead rotation (AMBR) microviscometer. Specifically, the invention provides a label-free, cost-effective, and rapid means of measuring changes in solution viscosity due to DNA concentration and length variations, which can be applied in various diagnostic and analytical contexts.

## BACKGROUND

Sensitive and cost-effective DNA detection methods are essential in a wide range of applications, including clinical diagnostics, drug development, food safety, and forensic sciences. Traditional methods such as quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR), restriction fragment length polymorphism (RFLP), and ligation detection reaction (LDR) rely heavily on fluorescence for DNA detection due to its simplicity and high sensitivity. However, these methods often require specific chemical modifications or pre-labeling of DNA, which can be costly and time-consuming.

An alternative approach to DNA detection involves measuring the viscosity of the solution. The viscosity of a double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) solution at a known temperature is influenced by the mass concentration and the average length of the DNA strands. Changes in solution viscosity can indicate DNA concentration and length, making it a promising method for DNA detection without the need for labeling. However, existing viscosity measurement techniques have limitations in terms of sensitivity and practicality.

The present invention addresses these limitations by introducing an asynchronous magnetic bead rotation (AMBR) microviscometer. This device measures the rotational motion of a free-floating magnetic bead in a rotating magnetic field, using changes in this motion to infer the physical properties of the surrounding solution. The AMBR microviscometer is capable of detecting and quantifying DNA in solutions with high sensitivity and accuracy, making it a valuable tool for various diagnostic and analytical applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and apparatus for detecting and quantifying DNA in solutions using an asynchronous magnetic bead rotation (AMBR) microviscometer. The method involves placing a magnetic bead in a solution containing DNA, subjecting the bead to a rotating magnetic field, and measuring the bead's rotational motion to determine the solution viscosity. Changes in viscosity are indicative of changes in DNA concentration and length, allowing for the detection and quantification of DNA in the solution.

The apparatus includes a magnetic bead, a rotating magnetic field generator, and a detection system for measuring the bead's rotational motion. The magnetic bead is free-floating in the solution and rotates asynchronously with respect to the driving magnetic field when the rotation rate of the external field exceeds a critical value. The rotational motion of the bead is viscosity-dependent, and the rate of asynchronous rotation is used to infer the solution viscosity.

The invention offers several advantages over existing DNA detection methods. It is label-free, requiring no specific chemical modifications or pre-labeling of DNA. It is also cost-effective, rapid, and suitable for use with small sample volumes. The AMBR microviscometer can be applied to various DNA reactions, including restriction digestion and PCR, and can be integrated into microfluidic devices for high-throughput analysis.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Asynchronous Magnetic Bead Rotation (AMBR)

The asynchronous magnetic bead rotation (AMBR) microviscometer is a novel device for measuring the viscosity of solutions containing DNA. The device operates by placing a magnetic bead in the solution and subjecting it to a rotating magnetic field. The bead rotates asynchronously with respect to the driving magnetic field when the rotation rate of the external field exceeds a critical value. The rotational motion of the bead is viscosity-dependent, and the rate of asynchronous rotation is used to infer the solution viscosity.

The magnetic bead is typically a paramagnetic microbead with a diameter ranging from 7.6 to 45 μm. The bead is free-floating in the solution and is suspended between two glass slides with a minimum gap of 210 μm to prevent contact with the solid surfaces. The bead concentration in the solution is 0.0075% w/v, and a non-specific blocking agent, such as bovine serum albumin, is added to minimize non-specific interactions.

The rotating magnetic field is generated using orthogonal Helmholtz coils, and the field strength is typically 2.7 mT. The driving frequency of the magnetic field is controlled to ensure that the bead rotates asynchronously. Image stacks of the bead's rotational motion are recorded at a rate of 10 frames per second, and the periodicity of the bead rotation is determined using image analysis software. The rotation period of the bead is directly proportional to the solution viscosity, allowing for the accurate measurement of viscosity changes in the solution.

### Analytes

The present invention is particularly useful for detecting and quantifying DNA in solutions. The viscosity of a double-stranded DNA (dsDNA) solution at a known temperature is influenced by the mass concentration and the average length of the DNA strands. Changes in solution viscosity can indicate DNA concentration and length, making it a valuable method for DNA detection without the need for labeling.

In restriction digestion reactions, the solution viscosity decreases as longer DNA strands are cut into shorter pieces. In PCR reactions, the solution viscosity increases as the length of the DNA increases through polymerization of the target sequence. The AMBR microviscometer can detect these changes in viscosity, providing a sensitive and accurate means of monitoring DNA reactions.

The invention can be applied to various DNA reactions, including restriction digestion and PCR. In restriction digestion reactions, the restriction enzymes, corresponding buffers, and lambda DNA are mixed and incubated at 37 °C for 1 hour. The resulting solutions are then placed in a 25 °C water bath before being measured by the AMBR microviscometer. In PCR reactions, the reagents are mixed, and the reaction is carried out in a thermal cycler. Samples are taken at different cycles and stored in a −20 °C freezer before being measured by the AMBR microviscometer.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

#### Calibration of AMBR Viscometer

To calibrate the AMBR microviscometer, a series of glycerol/water solutions with varying glycerol mass fractions were analyzed. The solutions were prepared by mixing glycerol and water at different ratios, and the viscosity of each solution was verified using an Ubbelohde viscometer. The AMBR microviscometer was used to measure the bead rotation period in each solution, and the results were compared to the Ubbelohde viscosity values.

A linear relationship was found between the solution viscosity and the bead rotation period. The microviscometer results matched both the Ubbelohde viscosity values and the theoretically predicted values for the mixtures over a viscosity range from 0.89 to 2.8 cP. A correlation curve relating the bead rotation period with the solution viscosity was constructed, yielding excellent uniformity. The experimentally observed linear relationship between rotation period and viscosity agrees well with the theory developed for the paramagnetic AMBR system.

The linear correlation is robust to variation in the magnetic field driving frequency and for three different bead sizes tested, with the 45 μm beads giving optimal linear correlation results. However, the linearity does not hold as well for a frequency close to the instability threshold, and the measurement of rotation period is less reliable due to jerky motion affecting the image analysis. Therefore, the higher driving frequency regime was chosen for the DNA measurements.

### Example 2

#### Viscosity Measurement of DNA Aqueous Solutions

The viscosity of DNA aqueous solutions was measured using the AMBR microviscometer. Lambda DNA was digested with EcoRI, forming DNA fragments of various lengths. The viscosities of the DNA EcoRI digest solutions at different concentrations were estimated using the measured bead rotation periods. A linear relationship was found between the solution viscosity and the DNA concentration, confirming the assumption that these solutions were in the dilute solution regime.

The viscosities of the DNA solutions measured using the AMBR microviscometer were within the theoretically estimated upper and lower bounds. The results demonstrate that the AMBR microviscometer is sensitive to viscosity changes caused by DNA size changes and can detect DNA sequence variation using a site-specific restriction endonuclease.

### Example 3

#### Preparation of Digestion Reaction Samples

In the digestion reactions, the restriction enzymes, corresponding buffers, lambda DNA, and nuclease-free water were mixed and incubated at 37 °C for 1 hour. After the reaction, the solutions were placed in a 25 °C water bath before being measured by the AMBR microviscometer. The AMBR microviscometer detected a clear difference in bead rotation period between the digested and undigested lambda DNA solutions, confirming that the device is sensitive to viscosity changes caused by DNA size changes.

### Example 4

#### Preparation of PCR Samples

PCR reactions were performed using lambda DNA as the template. The forward primer (5'-GGTGCTTTATGACTCTGCCGC-3') and the reverse primer (5'-CGGCACTGGCAAGCAACTGA-3') were used, and the PCR master mix was purchased from Promega. The reactions were carried out in a thermal cycler with an initial denaturation at 95 °C for 30 seconds, followed by six amplification cycles. Each cycle consisted of denaturation at 95 °C for 30 seconds, annealing at 60 °C for 1 minute, and extension at 72 °C for 5 minutes. The reactions were stopped and held at 4 °C after each cycle, and samples were taken at different cycles and stored in a −20 °C freezer before being measured by the AMBR microviscometer.

The AMBR microviscometer detected the formation of PCR products in real time. The reactions with higher initial template concentrations reached the maximum product concentration sooner than those with lower template concentrations. The plot of reaction cycle number versus the log of initial DNA concentration yielded a linear correlation. Comparing the AMBR measurements with the gel electrophoresis results on the same samples confirmed that the viscosity-based method is approximately 5 cycles delayed, relative to gel electrophoresis detection.

### Example 5

#### Gel Electrophoresis

Gel electrophoresis was used to verify the DNA solution results measured by the AMBR microviscometer. A 0.8% agarose gel was prepared, and 1 μL of the reaction solution was diluted and loaded onto the gel. The gel electrophoresis was conducted in a 1× TBE buffer at 10 V/cm for 2 hours. The fluorescent signal intensities of the 4500 bp bands were estimated using ImageJ, confirming the accuracy of the AMBR microviscometer measurements.

In conclusion, the asynchronous magnetic bead rotation (AMBR) microviscometer provides a label-free, cost-effective, and rapid means of detecting and quantifying DNA in solutions. The device is sensitive to viscosity changes caused by DNA concentration and length variations, making it a valuable tool for various diagnostic and analytical applications.
