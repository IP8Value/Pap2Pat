# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND TO THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of renewable polymers and specifically to the use of terpenes as monomers for the synthesis of novel polymers. Terpenes, a class of hydrocarbon compounds derived from natural sources such as citrus and wood waste, have gained significant attention due to their potential as sustainable alternatives to fossil fuel-derived monomers. The global demand for polymers, primarily derived from petroleum, has led to increased environmental concerns and a push towards more eco-friendly and renewable materials. Despite the availability of terpenes on a multi-tonne scale, their direct use in polymerization has been limited due to challenges such as low reactivity and poor polymer properties.

Traditional approaches to utilizing terpenes in polymer science have involved modifying their structures to create more reactive monomers. For instance, methods such as Baeyer-Villiger oxidation and ring-opening metathesis have been explored to produce polymers with specific properties. However, these methods often require stringent conditions, such as low temperatures and anhydrous environments, which limit their commercial viability. Additionally, many of these approaches focus on a narrow range of terpenes, excluding the majority of commercially available terpenes and terpenoids.

The present invention addresses these limitations by providing a novel and efficient method to functionalize terpenes, specifically (+)-α-pinene, (-)-β-pinene, (R)-(+)-limonene, and (R)-(-)-carvone, into acrylate and methacrylate monomers. These monomers can be readily polymerized using standard free radical techniques, resulting in polymers with a wide range of properties suitable for various applications, including coatings and adhesives. The invention also includes a catalytic one-pot approach to synthesize these monomers, further enhancing the sustainability and scalability of the process.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for the synthesis of terpene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers and their subsequent polymerization into useful materials. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Functionalization of Terpenes**: The terpenes (+)-α-pinene, (-)-β-pinene, (R)-(+)-limonene, and (R)-(-)-carvone are converted into alcohol derivatives using hydroboration/oxidation or reduction protocols.
2. **Esterification**: The alcohol derivatives are then esterified with acrylic acid or methacrylic acid using propyl phosphonic anhydride (T3P®) as a coupling agent, producing the corresponding acrylate and methacrylate monomers.
3. **Polymerization**: The monomers are polymerized using standard free radical techniques, optionally in the presence of a chain transfer agent such as dodecanethiol (DDM), to control molecular weight and achieve desired properties.

Additionally, the invention includes a catalytic one-pot approach to synthesize β-pinene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers using palladium acetate and benzoquinone under mild conditions. This method offers a more sustainable and scalable route to produce the monomers.

The resulting polymers exhibit a wide range of properties, including high glass transition temperatures (Tg) and excellent mechanical properties, making them suitable for various applications, such as coatings, adhesives, and other industrial uses.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Terpinolene and Gamma-Terpinene as Chain Transfer Agents

While the primary focus of the invention is on the synthesis and polymerization of terpene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers, the use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as chain transfer agents is also described. These compounds, derived from the same family of terpenes, can be effectively utilized to control the molecular weight of the resulting polymers. The use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as chain transfer agents offers several advantages, including:

1. **Controlled Molecular Weight**: By adjusting the concentration of the chain transfer agent, the molecular weight of the polymer can be precisely controlled, allowing for the optimization of properties such as glass transition temperature (Tg), mechanical strength, and solubility.
2. **Environmental Friendliness**: Terpinolene and gamma-terpinene are natural products, making them a more sustainable alternative to traditional chain transfer agents like dodecanethiol (DDM).
3. **Versatility**: These chain transfer agents can be used in conjunction with a variety of terpene-based monomers, providing flexibility in the design of new polymers.

### Functionalization of Terpenes

The first step in the synthesis of terpene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers involves the functionalization of the terpenes into alcohol derivatives. This is achieved using hydroboration/oxidation for (+)-α-pinene, (-)-β-pinene, and (R)-(+)-limonene, and reduction with lithium aluminum hydride for (R)-(-)-carvone. The hydroboration/oxidation protocol involves the addition of borane to the double bond of the terpene, followed by oxidation to form the alcohol. The reduction of (R)-(-)-carvone involves the conversion of the ketone functionality to an alcohol using lithium aluminum hydride.

The alcohol derivatives are obtained as single diastereomers in most cases, except for the limonene derivative, which is obtained as a 1:1 mixture of diastereomers. This step is crucial as it provides the necessary functional groups for the subsequent esterification reactions.

### Esterification

The alcohol derivatives are then esterified with acrylic acid or methacrylic acid using propyl phosphonic anhydride (T3P®) as a coupling agent. T3P® promotes the formation of the ester bond while generating a water-soluble triphosphate by-product, which is environmentally benign compared to the chloride by-products produced using acryloyl or methacryloyl chloride. This step is performed under mild conditions, ensuring the sustainability and scalability of the process.

The resulting terpene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers are readily polymerizable using standard free radical techniques. The choice of monomer (acrylate vs. methacrylate) significantly affects the properties of the resulting polymers. For example, the presence of a methyl group in the methacrylate monomers increases steric hindrance, leading to higher glass transition temperatures (Tg) and improved mechanical properties.

### Polymerization

The terpene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers are polymerized using standard free radical techniques, such as thermal initiation with azobisisobutyronitrile (AIBN) or photoinitiation with benzoyl peroxide. The polymerization can be carried out in the presence of a chain transfer agent, such as dodecanethiol (DDM) or terpinolene, to control the molecular weight of the resulting polymers. The molecular weight of the polymer can be adjusted by varying the concentration of the chain transfer agent, allowing for the optimization of properties such as Tg, mechanical strength, and solubility.

For example, the polymerization of α-pinene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers in the presence of 0.5%, 1%, and 5% DDM produced polymers with molecular weights ranging from 4800 to 23,600 g/mol. The glass transition temperatures (Tg) of these polymers varied from 12°C to 142°C, with the methacrylate polymers exhibiting significantly higher Tg values due to the increased steric hindrance and chain stiffness.

### Catalytic One-Pot Approach

In addition to the two-step approach described above, the invention also includes a catalytic one-pot method to synthesize β-pinene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers. This method utilizes palladium acetate and benzoquinone under mild conditions to achieve high yields of the desired monomers. The benzoquinone serves multiple roles, acting as an oxidant, ligand for palladium, and polymerization inhibitor for the monomer produced. The use of methacrylic acid as both solvent and reactant further enhances the sustainability and scalability of the process.

Initial results using 10 mol% palladium acetate, 1 equivalent of benzoquinone in DMSO at 50°C gave a 16% yield of two new monomers in a 1:1 ratio. Optimization of the reaction conditions, including catalyst loading, benzoquinone, temperature, and atmosphere, allowed for a yield of 82% (10:1 ratio of the two monomers) using just 2 mol% palladium acetate and 2 equivalents of benzoquinone in air at 50°C.

### Applications

The terpene-based polymers produced using the methods described in this invention exhibit a wide range of properties, making them suitable for various applications. For example, the carvone-based methacrylate polymer, with a high Tg of 117°C, is ideal for powder coatings. When combined with a commercial crosslinker, such as trimethylolpropane tris(3-mercaptopropionate) (TMPMP), and processed into a powder, the material can be applied to metal surfaces using an electrostatic air gun and cured to form a transparent, highly glossy coating with excellent chemical resistance and mechanical properties.

Standard mechanical tests, including the acetone-rub test, pencil hardness scratch testing, and impact tests, confirmed that the coating exhibited properties characteristic of high-performance acrylic coatings. The coating showed a fully cross-linked structure, a pencil hardness of 2B, and a König hardness of 185 seconds. Adhesion tests using a Gitterschnitt (lattice cutter) also demonstrated results consistent with other acrylic powder coating materials.

### Conclusion

In conclusion, the present invention provides a novel and efficient method for the synthesis of terpene-based acrylate and methacrylate monomers and their subsequent polymerization into useful materials. The method involves the functionalization of terpenes into alcohol derivatives, esterification with acrylic or methacrylic acid, and polymerization using standard free radical techniques. Additionally, a catalytic one-pot approach is described, offering a more sustainable and scalable route to produce the monomers. The resulting polymers exhibit a wide range of properties, making them suitable for various applications, including coatings, adhesives, and other industrial uses. The use of terpinolene and gamma-terpinene as chain transfer agents further enhances the versatility and sustainability of the process.
