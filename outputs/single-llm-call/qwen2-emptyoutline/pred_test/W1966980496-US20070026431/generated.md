# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Cyclin D1 is a critical regulator of the G1 phase of the cell cycle and is frequently overexpressed in various types of cancer. Overexpression of cyclin D1 can lead to uncontrolled cell proliferation, making it a potential therapeutic target. While the transcriptional regulation of cyclin D1 has been well studied, the mechanisms governing its ubiquitination and subsequent degradation have not been fully elucidated. This invention addresses the gap by identifying the specific kinase responsible for phosphorylating cyclin D1 at threonine-286 (Thr286) and the E3 ubiquitin ligase complex that mediates its degradation. The invention provides methods and compositions for regulating cyclin D1 stability, which can be applied in the development of cancer therapeutics.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the identification of the F-box protein FBXW8 as the specific E3 ubiquitin ligase responsible for the degradation of cyclin D1 in cancer cells. The invention further identifies the MAPK signaling cascade as the kinase responsible for phosphorylating cyclin D1 at Thr286, which is a prerequisite for its recognition and degradation by the FBXW8-containing E3 ligase complex. The invention provides methods and compositions for modulating cyclin D1 stability, including nucleic acid molecules, polypeptide production methods, expression vectors, fusion proteins, covalently modified proteins, and components for polyubiquitination assays. These methods and compositions can be used in the development of therapeutic agents for treating cancers characterized by aberrant cyclin D1 expression.

## DEFINITIONS

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Cyclin D1**: A protein that regulates the G1 phase of the cell cycle and is frequently overexpressed in various types of cancer.
- **FBXW8**: An F-box protein that specifically recognizes and targets cyclin D1 for ubiquitination and degradation.
- **MAPK (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase)**: A family of serine/threonine kinases that play a central role in signal transduction pathways, including the regulation of cell proliferation and survival.
- **Cullin 1 (CUL1)**: A scaffold protein that forms part of the SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complex.
- **Cullin 7 (CUL7)**: A scaffold protein that forms part of the SCF-like E3 ubiquitin ligase complex.
- **SKP1 (S-Phase Kinase-associated Protein 1)**: An adaptor protein that links F-box proteins to the Cullin scaffold in SCF and SCF-like complexes.
- **Ubiquitination**: The process by which ubiquitin molecules are attached to a target protein, marking it for degradation by the proteasome.
- **Polyubiquitination**: The attachment of multiple ubiquitin molecules to a target protein, enhancing its degradation.
- **Proteasome**: A large protein complex that degrades ubiquitinated proteins.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### FBXW8 Protein (F-BOX WD-40 Domain Protein 8)

FBXW8 is an F-box protein that contains WD-40 repeat motifs. It functions as a substrate recognition component of the SCF (SKP1-CUL1-F-box protein) or SCF-like (SKP1-CUL7-F-box protein) E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. FBXW8 specifically recognizes and binds to cyclin D1 in a phosphorylation-dependent manner, targeting it for ubiquitination and subsequent degradation by the 26S proteasome. The interaction between FBXW8 and cyclin D1 is mediated by the phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at threonine-286 (Thr286), which is catalyzed by the MAPK signaling cascade.

### MAPK (Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase)

The MAPK signaling cascade is a key pathway in cellular signal transduction, regulating various cellular processes including cell proliferation, differentiation, and survival. In the context of this invention, the MAPK cascade is responsible for phosphorylating cyclin D1 at Thr286. This phosphorylation event is essential for the subsequent recognition and degradation of cyclin D1 by the FBXW8-containing E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. The MAPK cascade includes several kinases, such as MEK and ERK, which are activated in response to various mitogenic signals.

### CULLIN 1 (CUL1)

Cullin 1 is a scaffold protein that forms part of the SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. It interacts with SKP1 and F-box proteins to assemble the SCF complex, which is responsible for recognizing and ubiquitinating specific substrates. In the context of this invention, CUL1 forms part of the SCF complex that includes FBXW8 and is involved in the ubiquitination and degradation of cyclin D1.

### Cullin 7 (CUL7)

Cullin 7 is a scaffold protein that forms part of the SCF-like E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. It interacts with SKP1 and F-box proteins to assemble the SCF-like complex, which is responsible for recognizing and ubiquitinating specific substrates. In the context of this invention, CUL7 forms part of the SCF-like complex that includes FBXW8 and is involved in the ubiquitination and degradation of cyclin D1.

### SKP1 (S-Phase Kinase-associated Protein 1)

SKP1 is an adaptor protein that links F-box proteins to the Cullin scaffold in SCF and SCF-like complexes. It facilitates the assembly of the E3 ubiquitin ligase complex by bridging the F-box protein and the Cullin scaffold. In the context of this invention, SKP1 interacts with FBXW8 and CUL1 or CUL7 to form the SCF or SCF-like complex that targets cyclin D1 for ubiquitination and degradation.

### Cyclin D1

Cyclin D1 is a key regulator of the G1 phase of the cell cycle and is frequently overexpressed in various types of cancer. Overexpression of cyclin D1 can lead to uncontrolled cell proliferation, making it a potential therapeutic target. The stability of cyclin D1 is regulated by its phosphorylation at threonine-286 (Thr286) by the MAPK signaling cascade. Phosphorylation at Thr286 is a prerequisite for the recognition and degradation of cyclin D1 by the FBXW8-containing E3 ubiquitin ligase complex.

### Nucleic Acid Molecules, Polypeptide Production Methods, Expression Vectors, Fusion Proteins

The invention provides nucleic acid molecules encoding FBXW8, MAPK, CUL1, CUL7, SKP1, and cyclin D1. These nucleic acid molecules can be used to produce the corresponding polypeptides using standard molecular biology techniques. The invention also provides expression vectors for expressing these polypeptides in host cells, as well as fusion proteins comprising these polypeptides fused to tags or other functional domains. These fusion proteins can be used for various applications, including purification, detection, and functional studies.

### Covalently Modified Proteins

The invention provides methods for covalently modifying FBXW8, MAPK, CUL1, CUL7, SKP1, and cyclin D1 to alter their function or stability. For example, covalent modification of cyclin D1 at Thr286 can prevent its recognition and degradation by the FBXW8-containing E3 ubiquitin ligase complex. Similarly, covalent modification of FBXW8 can enhance or inhibit its ability to recognize and degrade cyclin D1.

### Components for Polyubitquitination Assays

The invention provides components for conducting polyubiquitination assays to study the ubiquitination and degradation of cyclin D1. These components include recombinant FBXW8, MAPK, CUL1, CUL7, SKP1, and cyclin D1, as well as E1, E2, and ubiquitin. The assays can be used to screen for compounds that modulate the ubiquitination and degradation of cyclin D1, which can be developed as therapeutic agents for treating cancers characterized by aberrant cyclin D1 expression.

## EXAMPLES

### Cyclin D1 Protein is Destabilized Specifically in S Phase in Cancer Cells

To investigate the mechanism and importance of cyclin D1 proteolysis, we assessed the expression profile of cyclin D1 during cell cycle progression from quiescence in normal and cancer cell lines. Normal cells and cancer cells were released from quiescence at G0/G1 phase and cell cycle profiles were determined by flow-cytometric cell cycle analyses. In normal cells, cyclin D1 levels remained constant during S phase, while in cancer cells, cyclin D1 levels showed a dramatic reduction during S phase. Pulse-chase analysis confirmed that cyclin D1 is destabilized specifically in S phase in cancer cells.

### Cyclin D1 Protein is Degraded During the S Phase Through the Ubiquitin-proteasome Pathway in Cancer Cells

To determine if cyclin D1 destabilization during S phase was due to increased proteolysis through the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, we treated cancer cells with MG132, a proteasome inhibitor. Treatment with MG132 resulted in the accumulation of cyclin D1 in S phase, confirming that cyclin D1 is degraded through the 26S proteasome pathway in cancer cells. Further experiments using immunoprecipitation and immunoblotting confirmed that cyclin D1 is polyubiquitinated during S phase, and this polyubiquitination occurs primarily in the cytoplasm.

### Expression of Cyclin D1 Protein in the Nucleus Decreases Through the G1-S Transition in Tumor Cells

We investigated the subcellular localization of cyclin D1 during the cell cycle in cancer cells. Cyclin D1 accumulated in the nucleus during G1 phase and exited into the cytoplasm in S phase. This relocalization coincided with increased degradation of cyclin D1, suggesting that the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic redistribution of cyclin D1 is linked to its degradation.

### Phosphorylation of Cyclin D1 Protein is Mediated by the Same Mechanism as Cyclin D1 Gene Expression, Via the MAPK Signaling Pathway

We identified a D-domain in cyclin D1 that is required for its phosphorylation by the MAPK signaling cascade. In vitro kinase assays confirmed that purified ERK2 efficiently phosphorylated cyclin D1 at Thr286, and this phosphorylation was dependent on the presence of the D-domain. Deletion of the D-domain or mutation of Thr286 to alanine (T286A) prevented phosphorylation of cyclin D1 by ERK2. These results demonstrate that the MAPK signaling cascade is responsible for the phosphorylation of cyclin D1 at Thr286, which is a prerequisite for its recognition and degradation by the FBXW8-containing E3 ubiquitin ligase complex.

### Example 5

We demonstrated that FBXW8 specifically associates with cyclin D1 in a Thr286 phosphorylation-dependent manner. In vitro ubiquitination assays confirmed that FBXW8 ubiquitinates cyclin D1 in a Thr286 phosphorylation-dependent manner. Overexpression of FBXW8 reduced endogenous levels of cyclin D1, while depletion of FBXW8 using siRNA resulted in the accumulation of cyclin D1. These results indicate that FBXW8 is the specific E3 ubiquitin ligase responsible for the degradation of cyclin D1 in cancer cells.

### FBXW8-Mediated Cyclin D1 Degradation in the Cytoplasm is Required for Cancer Cell Proliferation

We examined the biological significance of enhanced cyclin D1 degradation in the cytoplasm during S phase in cancer cells. Depletion of FBXW8, CUL1, or CUL7 using siRNA significantly reduced cell proliferation, indicating that rapid turnover of cyclin D1 is essential for cancer cell proliferation. Constitutive expression of the nuclear protein cyclin D1 T286A, which is resistant to polyubiquitination and nuclear export, rescued cells from growth arrest, further confirming the importance of FBXW8-mediated cyclin D1 degradation in cancer cell proliferation.

### Conclusion

This invention provides a comprehensive understanding of the mechanisms governing the ubiquitination and degradation of cyclin D1 in cancer cells. The identification of FBXW8 as the specific E3 ubiquitin ligase responsible for cyclin D1 degradation and the MAPK signaling cascade as the kinase responsible for its phosphorylation at Thr286 opens new avenues for the development of therapeutic agents targeting cyclin D1 stability. The methods and compositions provided by this invention can be used to modulate cyclin D1 stability, offering promising strategies for the treatment of cancers characterized by aberrant cyclin D1 expression.
