# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of cancer therapeutics, specifically to the treatment of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC). More particularly, the invention provides a method for selectively inhibiting the proliferation of TNBC cells using chelerythrine, a specific inhibitor of protein kinase C (PKC). The invention further encompasses the use of chelerythrine in combination with chemotherapeutic agents to enhance the efficacy of cancer treatment.

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

Breast cancer is the most common cancer in women worldwide, with an estimated 1.67 million new cases diagnosed annually. Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a particularly aggressive subtype, characterized by the absence of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) expression. Due to the lack of these receptors, TNBC does not respond to hormonal therapy or anti-HER2 agents, making chemotherapy the primary treatment option. However, TNBC often shows rapid disease relapse and a higher likelihood of metastasis, leading to poor patient outcomes.

Protein kinase C (PKC) is a family of serine/threonine protein kinases involved in various cellular processes, including cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. PKC has been implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple cancers, including breast cancer. Specifically, the PKC isoform PKN2 has been shown to be highly expressed in TNBC cells compared to non-TNBC cells. Chelerythrine, a natural benzophenanthridine alkaloid, is a potent and specific inhibitor of PKC. Previous studies have demonstrated the anti-cancer and cytotoxic activities of chelerythrine in various cancer types, but its effects on TNBC have not been extensively explored.

The present invention addresses the need for new targeted therapies for TNBC by providing a method for selectively inhibiting the proliferation of TNBC cells using chelerythrine. Additionally, the invention explores the potential of combining chelerythrine with chemotherapeutic agents to enhance treatment efficacy.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for selectively inhibiting the proliferation of triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) cells using chelerythrine, a specific inhibitor of protein kinase C (PKC). The method involves administering chelerythrine to TNBC cells, thereby reducing their proliferation and inducing apoptosis. The invention also encompasses the use of chelerythrine in combination with chemotherapeutic agents to enhance the anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects on TNBC cells.

In one embodiment, the invention provides a method for treating TNBC comprising the step of administering an effective amount of chelerythrine to a subject in need thereof. The chelerythrine may be administered alone or in combination with a chemotherapeutic agent, such as taxol.

In another embodiment, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising chelerythrine and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition may further include one or more chemotherapeutic agents.

In yet another embodiment, the invention provides a method for inhibiting the growth of TNBC cells in vitro or in vivo, comprising the step of contacting the cells with an effective amount of chelerythrine.

The invention further provides a method for identifying compounds that selectively inhibit the proliferation of TNBC cells, comprising the steps of: (a) contacting TNBC cells and non-TNBC cells with a test compound; (b) measuring the proliferation of the cells; and (c) comparing the proliferation of the TNBC cells to the non-TNBC cells, wherein a greater reduction in the proliferation of the TNBC cells compared to the non-TNBC cells indicates that the test compound selectively inhibits the proliferation of TNBC cells.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENTS

### Introduction

Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) is a highly aggressive form of breast cancer that lacks the expression of estrogen receptor (ER), progesterone receptor (PR), and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2). The absence of these receptors makes TNBC resistant to targeted therapies, leaving chemotherapy as the primary treatment option. However, TNBC often shows rapid disease relapse and a higher likelihood of metastasis, leading to poor patient outcomes. Therefore, there is an urgent need for new targeted therapies to improve the treatment of TNBC.

### Protein Kinase C (PKC) and Chelerythrine

Protein kinase C (PKC) is a family of serine/threonine protein kinases that play critical roles in various cellular processes, including cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis. PKC has been implicated in the pathogenesis of multiple cancers, including breast cancer. The PKC isoform PKN2 has been shown to be highly expressed in TNBC cells compared to non-TNBC cells, suggesting its potential as a therapeutic target.

Chelerythrine is a natural benzophenanthridine alkaloid isolated from *Chelidonium majus* and possesses diverse pharmacological activities, including potent anti-cancer and cytotoxic activities. Chelerythrine is a specific inhibitor of PKC, making it a promising candidate for the treatment of TNBC.

### Method for Selectively Inhibiting Proliferation of TNBC Cells

#### Administration of Chelerythrine

The method of the present invention involves administering chelerythrine to TNBC cells to selectively inhibit their proliferation. Chelerythrine can be administered in various forms, including but not limited to oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and topical administration. The effective dose of chelerythrine will depend on various factors, such as the severity of the cancer, the age and weight of the patient, and the route of administration. In general, an effective dose of chelerythrine for treating TNBC is in the range of about 0.1 μM to about 10 μM, preferably about 1 μM to about 5 μM.

#### Combination Therapy

The invention also encompasses the use of chelerythrine in combination with chemotherapeutic agents to enhance the anti-proliferative and pro-apoptotic effects on TNBC cells. Suitable chemotherapeutic agents include, but are not limited to, taxol, doxorubicin, cisplatin, and paclitaxel. The combination therapy can be administered simultaneously or sequentially, depending on the specific treatment regimen.

### In Vitro and In Vivo Studies

#### In Vitro Studies

In vitro studies were conducted to evaluate the anti-proliferative effects of chelerythrine on TNBC cells. Four TNBC cell lines (MDA-MB-231, BT-549, HCC1937, and MDA-MB-468) and four non-TNBC cell lines (MCF7, ZR-75-1, SK-BR-3, and MDA-MB-453) were treated with various concentrations of chelerythrine ranging from 0.625 μM to 10 μM for 72 hours. The results showed that chelerythrine inhibited the growth of TNBC cell lines in a dose-dependent manner, with IC50 values ranging from 2.6 μM to 4.2 μM. In contrast, the non-TNBC cell lines were relatively resistant to chelerythrine treatment, with IC50 values greater than 10 μM.

Further studies were conducted to evaluate the effects of chelerythrine on cell cycle progression and apoptosis. Chelerythrine treatment caused a significant increase in the percentage of cells in the G2/M phase in MDA-MB-468 cells and a substantial proportion of nuclei with sub-G0/G1 DNA content characteristic of apoptosis in all TNBC cell lines. These findings suggest that chelerythrine selectively induces apoptosis in TNBC cells.

#### In Vivo Studies

In vivo studies were conducted to evaluate the anti-tumor effects of chelerythrine in xenograft models. TNBC cell line MDA-MB-231 and non-TNBC cell line ZR-75-1 were injected subcutaneously into the hind limbs of nude mice. Chelerythrine was administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 5 mg/kg at 3-4 day intervals. The results showed that chelerythrine significantly inhibited the growth of MDA-MB-231 tumors compared to ZR-75-1 tumors.

### Mechanism of Action

The mechanism of action of chelerythrine in selectively inhibiting the proliferation of TNBC cells is believed to involve the inhibition of PKC, particularly the PKN2 isoform. PKN2 is highly expressed in TNBC cells and plays a crucial role in cell proliferation and survival. By inhibiting PKN2, chelerythrine disrupts the signaling pathways that promote TNBC cell growth and survival, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.
