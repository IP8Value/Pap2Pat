# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to a method and system for delivering an immunogenic biological agent to specific regions of the human intestine using a radio-controlled capsule (RCC). More specifically, the invention pertains to the oral administration of a recombinant adenovirus (rAd) vaccine targeting the ileum or jejunum to induce systemic and mucosal immune responses against influenza.

Influenza is a significant global health concern, causing annual epidemics and occasional pandemics. Traditional influenza vaccines are administered via intramuscular injection, which primarily induces systemic immune responses. However, mucosal immunity, particularly in the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts, plays a crucial role in preventing viral entry and replication. Oral vaccines have the potential to induce both systemic and mucosal immune responses, providing broader protection against influenza.

The development of effective oral vaccines has been challenging due to the harsh environment of the gastrointestinal tract, which can degrade the vaccine before it reaches the target site. Additionally, the precise delivery of the vaccine to specific regions of the intestine, such as the ileum or jejunum, is essential for optimal immune induction. The present invention addresses these challenges by utilizing a radio-controlled capsule (RCC) to deliver the rAd vaccine directly to the ileum or jejunum, ensuring the vaccine's integrity and targeted release.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method and system for delivering an immunogenic biological agent, specifically a recombinant adenovirus (rAd) vaccine, to the ileum or jejunum of the human intestine using a radio-controlled capsule (RCC). The method involves administering the RCC orally to a subject, monitoring its progression through the digestive tract using gamma scintigraphy, and triggering the release of the vaccine contents at the desired location in the ileum or jejunum by radio signal.

The invention further encompasses the detailed composition of the rAd vaccine, the methods for isolating and analyzing peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs), and the assays for measuring antibody-secreting cells (ASCs), serum antibodies, hemagglutination inhibition (HAI), and microneutralizing (MN) antibodies. Additionally, the invention includes methods for assessing mucosal immune responses using nasal and fecal samples and flow cytometric analysis of B cell immunophenotyping.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### I. Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Immunogenic Biological Agent**: Any substance capable of inducing an immune response in a host, including but not limited to vaccines, antigens, and adjuvants.
- **Recombinant Adenovirus (rAd) Vaccine**: A genetically engineered adenovirus designed to express a specific antigen, such as the hemagglutinin (HA) protein of the influenza virus.
- **Radio-Controlled Capsule (RCC)**: A capsule that can be remotely triggered to release its contents at a specific location in the digestive tract using a radio signal.
- **Gamma Scintigraphy**: A medical imaging technique that uses gamma rays to visualize the movement and release of the RCC in the digestive tract.
- **Peripheral Blood Mononuclear Cells (PBMCs)**: A fraction of blood cells that includes lymphocytes and monocytes, commonly used in immunological studies.
- **Antibody-Secreting Cells (ASCs)**: B cells that have differentiated into plasma cells and are actively producing antibodies.
- **Hemagglutination Inhibition (HAI)**: A serological test used to measure the presence of antibodies that inhibit the agglutination of red blood cells by a virus.
- **Microneutralizing (MN) Antibodies**: Antibodies that can neutralize a virus by preventing it from infecting cells.
- **Mucosal Immune Responses**: Immune responses that occur in mucosal tissues, such as the respiratory and gastrointestinal tracts.

### II. Immunogenic Biological Agents

The immunogenic biological agent used in the present invention is a recombinant adenovirus (rAd) vaccine expressing the hemagglutinin (HA) protein of the influenza virus. The rAd vaccine is produced under Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) conditions and is formulated as a liquid suspension containing 1e11 infectious units (IU) in 0.9 ml of sucrose buffer. The particle to IU ratio of the vaccine is 27.8.

The rAd vaccine is designed to elicit both systemic and mucosal immune responses against influenza. The HA protein is a key surface antigen of the influenza virus and is responsible for binding to host cell receptors and facilitating viral entry. By expressing the HA protein, the rAd vaccine primes the immune system to recognize and neutralize the influenza virus.

### III. Targeted Delivery Systems

The targeted delivery system used in the present invention is a radio-controlled capsule (RCC). The RCC is designed to withstand the acidic environment of the stomach and the enzymatic activity of the small intestine, ensuring the integrity of the vaccine until it reaches the desired location. The capsule is equipped with a radio receiver that can be triggered to release the vaccine contents upon receiving a specific radio signal.

The RCC is administered orally to the subject, who is fasted overnight to ensure the capsule's smooth passage through the digestive tract. The capsule's progression is monitored using gamma scintigraphy, which allows for real-time visualization of the capsule's location. Once the capsule reaches the ileum or jejunum, a radio signal is transmitted to trigger the release of the vaccine contents. The release is confirmed by the dispersion of a radioactive tracer, which is detected by gamma scintigraphy.

### IV. Immune Responses and Vaccines

The present invention demonstrates the ability of the rAd vaccine delivered by the RCC to induce robust immune responses, both systemic and mucosal. The following sections detail the methods and results of the immune response assessments.

#### A. Antibody-Secreting Cells (ASCs)

Influenza HA-specific IgG and IgA ASCs were measured in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) on days 0 and 7 after vaccination. The results showed a significant increase in the number of HA-specific ASCs in both the ileum-targeted and jejunum-targeted groups. Specifically, the ileum-targeted group had a median of 54 IgA ASCs and 200 IgG ASCs per 1 × 10^6 PBMCs, while the jejunum-targeted group had a median of 27 IgA ASCs and 111 IgG ASCs per 1 × 10^6 PBMCs. The number of responders in both IgA and IgG ASCs in the jejunum-targeted group was less than in the ileum-targeted group, but not significantly different. The HA-specific IgG ASCs numbers in the ileum-targeted group were significantly different from those in the jejunum-targeted group (P = 0.023), but not significantly different in HA-specific IgA ASCs numbers (P = 0.08).

#### B. Serum Antibodies

Serum antibody responses to HA were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). The results showed that the RCC delivery of the rAd vaccine to the intestine induced antibody responses to HA in 12 out of 12 subjects in the ileum-released group and 9 out of 12 subjects in the jejunum-released group. The EC50 values of the ELISA titers were calculated, and a significant increase in the change of EC50 greater than 1.3 was observed in the majority of subjects, indicating a positive immune response.

#### C. Influenza Hemagglutination Inhibition (HAI)

HAI responses were measured on days 0 and 28 against MDCK-derived A/CA/07/2009. The results showed that the vaccine elicited seroconversions in 67% of subjects given vaccine to the ileum and 44% of subjects given vaccine to the jejunum. While the difference in response rate was not significant, the ileum group trended higher in terms of seroconversion rates.

#### D. Microneutralizing (MN) Antibodies

Neutralizing antibody responses to influenza were measured by MN assay. The results showed a trend where the ileum group appeared better than the jejunum group in terms of the frequency of responders. The frequency of subjects with 2-fold increases in MN titers in the ileum group (11 out of 12) was higher than the frequency in the jejunum group (7 out of 12), but not significantly different (P = 0.15 by Fisher’s exact test). After removing subjects that had MN titers greater than 40 prior to vaccination, the geometric mean titers (GMT) and geometric fold titer response (GMFR) were calculated. The results showed that serum neutralizing antibody titers to influenza were generated by oral immunization, with a greater than 5-fold increase in the GMT after immunization in the ileum group.

#### E. Mucosal Immune Responses Measured by ELISA

Nasal wash and fecal samples were collected from human volunteers to assess mucosal immune responses. Total IgA was quantified by ELISA to normalize for yield. The fold rise in HA-specific IgA antibody titers was measured pre and post immunization. The results showed that the number of subjects with detectable increases in anti-HA IgA titers (either in fecal or nasal IgA) was nine out of twelve in the ileum-targeted group and ten out of twelve in the jejunum-targeted group. The magnitudes were similar between groups, with a slightly higher response in the ileum delivery group.

#### F. Intestinal Mucosal Homing Receptors

To explore the mucosal homing potential of B cells in the peripheral blood post immunization, flow cytometric analysis was performed. The results showed a marked increase in β7(high) CD19+ B cells post vaccination in 21 out of 24 subjects. The ileum-targeted group had a median of 4.98% of β7(high) B cells on day 7, while the jejunum-targeted group had a median of 2.91% of β7(high) B cells on day 7. The β7(high) B cells expressed high levels of α4 integrin and CCR9, suggesting that these B cells will likely home back to the small intestine.

#### G. Non-Intestinal Mucosal Homing Receptors

The migratory capacity of pre-plasma B cells to non-intestinal mucosal tissues was also assessed. The results showed that the rAd oral vaccine generated CD19+ CD27(high) CD38(high) pre-plasma B cells. A major subset of these cells expressed high levels of β7 and β1 integrins, suggesting that they have tropism to mucosal tissues outside the gut, such as the mammary gland, salivary gland, respiratory tract, and urogenital tract.

### V. Examples

#### Example 1: Oral Administration and Monitoring of RCC

**Objective**: To evaluate the feasibility and safety of oral administration of the rAd vaccine using the RCC.

**Method**: Twenty-four male volunteers aged 18-49 were enrolled in an open-label, Phase I, pharmacodynamic study. The subjects were fasted overnight and given one dose of 1e11 IU of the rAd vaccine in 0.9 ml of liquid, provided in a size 000 radio-controlled capsule. The capsule's progression through the digestive tract was monitored by gamma scintigraphy, and the capsule contents were released in the upper small intestine (jejunum) or lower small intestine (ileum) by radio signal. Release was confirmed by the dispersion of the radioactive tracer.

**Results**: Confirmed delivery to the ileum or jejunum occurred in 24 subjects out of 25 attempted. The subject with poor delivery had the contents of the RCC released in the colon or rectum and was replaced. The capsule's release was successfully triggered in all 24 subjects, and no adverse events were reported.

#### Example 2: Assessment of Immune Responses

**Objective**: To assess the immune responses induced by the rAd vaccine delivered by the RCC.

**Method**: PBMCs were isolated from blood samples collected on days 0 and 7 after vaccination. ELISPOT assays were performed to measure HA-specific IgG and IgA ASCs. Serum antibodies to HA were measured by ELISA, and HAI and MN assays were conducted to assess neutralizing antibody responses. Nasal wash and fecal samples were collected to measure mucosal immune responses by ELISA. Flow cytometric analysis was performed to assess the expression of mucosal homing receptors on B cells.

**Results**: The results showed a significant increase in the number of HA-specific ASCs in both the ileum-targeted and jejunum-targeted groups. The ileum-targeted group had higher median numbers of IgG and IgA ASCs compared to the jejunum-targeted group. Serum antibody responses to HA were detected in 12 out of 12 subjects in the ileum-released group and 9 out of 12 subjects in the jejunum-released group. HAI and MN titers showed seroconversions in 67% and 44% of subjects in the ileum and jejunum groups, respectively. Mucosal immune responses were detected in nasal and fecal samples, with a higher response in the ileum delivery group. Flow cytometric analysis revealed a marked increase in β7(high) CD19+ B cells post vaccination, indicating the induction of intestinal homing B cells.

#### Example 3: Comparison of Ileum and Jejunum Delivery

**Objective**: To compare the immune responses induced by the rAd vaccine delivered to the ileum and jejunum.

**Method**: The immune responses in the ileum-targeted and jejunum-targeted groups were compared using the same methods as described in Example 2.

**Results**: The ileum-targeted group showed higher responder rates and higher HA-specific ASCs numbers compared to the jejunum-targeted group. The ileum group also trended higher in terms of HAI and MN titers, although the differences were not statistically significant. The ileum group had a higher induction of β7(high) B cells, suggesting more efficient immune induction in the ileum.

#### Example 4: Development of Enteric-Coated Tablet

**Objective**: To develop an enteric-coated tablet for the rAd vaccine based on the findings from the RCC study.

**Method**: Based on the successful delivery and immune responses observed in the RCC study, an enteric-coated tablet was developed to target the release of the rAd vaccine to the ileum. The tablet was designed to withstand the acidic environment of the stomach and release the vaccine in the ileum.

**Results**: The enteric-coated tablet was successfully developed and tested in a subsequent clinical trial. The tablet showed similar immune responses to the RCC, confirming the feasibility of using an enteric-coated tablet for oral delivery of the rAd vaccine.

## Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel method and system for delivering an immunogenic biological agent, specifically a recombinant adenovirus (rAd) vaccine, to the ileum or jejunum of the human intestine using a radio-controlled capsule (RCC). The invention demonstrates the ability of the rAd vaccine to induce robust systemic and mucosal immune responses against influenza, with the ileum-targeted delivery showing superior immune induction. The development of an enteric-coated tablet based on the findings from the RCC study further validates the potential of this oral vaccine platform for broad protection against influenza and other mucosal pathogens.
