# DESCRIPTION

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to novel monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and chimeric antibodies that specifically recognize and neutralize platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC). The invention further encompasses methods for using these antibodies in the diagnosis and treatment of various diseases and conditions, including cancer, fibrosis, and neurological disorders involving the disruption of the blood-brain barrier (BBB).

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Platelet-derived growth factors (PDGFs) play critical roles in development and are implicated in various pathological conditions, such as cancer, fibrosis, and neurological disorders. PDGF-CC, a member of the PDGF family, has been extensively studied over the past decade. Upon binding, PDGF-CC activates the PDGF receptor alpha (PDGFRα) subunits, leading to receptor autophosphorylation and subsequent intracellular signaling events that promote cell proliferation, migration, contraction, and survival. Unlike other PDGF family members, PDGF-CC requires proteolytic cleavage for activation, which is primarily mediated by tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) in the central nervous system (CNS) and by other proteases in other tissues.

PDGF-CC has been implicated in the pathogenesis of various cancers, including gliomas, sarcomas, leukemias, and epithelial cancers. Additionally, it drives pathological mesenchymal responses in vascular disorders and fibrotic diseases. Dysregulation in the PDGF/PDGFR system, such as constitutive activation of PDGFRs or mutations affecting ligand and receptor activities, contributes to the development of osteosarcomas, lung carcinomas, gliomas, astrocytomas, and medulloblastomas. PDGF-CC also serves as a potent proto-oncogene and mitogen for mesenchymal cells, including stem cells, smooth muscle cells, and endothelial cells.

Recent studies have highlighted the role of PDGF-CC in tumor biology, particularly in facilitating tumor growth through the recruitment of cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and enhancing tumor angiogenesis. Overexpression of PDGF-CC has been linked to liver steatosis, fibrosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma in transgenic mouse models. Increased PDGF-CC expression has also been observed in experimental models of heart and lung fibrosis, and it has been shown to mediate renal interstitial fibrosis.

PDGF-CC has been recognized as a regulator of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity. Intraventricular injection of active PDGF-CC protein can disrupt the BBB, and inhibition of the PDGF-CC/PDGFRα axis reduces BBB dysfunction, offering therapeutic benefits in animal models of stroke, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, seizures, and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. A phase 2 clinical trial in human ischemic stroke subjects treated with thrombolysis and the PDGFRα inhibitor imatinib demonstrated significant improvements in clinical outcomes.

Despite the importance of PDGF-CC in various diseases, there is a lack of molecular tools specifically targeting PDGF-CC signaling. The present invention addresses this gap by providing high-affinity monoclonal antibodies and chimeric antibodies that can neutralize PDGF-CC, thereby offering potential therapeutic and diagnostic applications.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides novel monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and chimeric antibodies that specifically recognize and neutralize platelet-derived growth factor CC (PDGF-CC). These antibodies have high affinity and specificity for human PDGF-CC and can block PDGF-CC-induced activation of PDGFRα. The invention further encompasses methods for using these antibodies in the diagnosis and treatment of various diseases and conditions, including cancer, fibrosis, and neurological disorders involving the disruption of the blood-brain barrier (BBB).

In one aspect, the invention provides a monoclonal antibody (mAb) that specifically binds to human PDGF-CC and has a dissociation constant (Kd) in the low nanomolar range. The mAb is capable of neutralizing PDGF-CC-induced activation of PDGFRα and does not cross-react with other PDGF ligands.

In another aspect, the invention provides a chimeric antibody (ch6B3) comprising the variable regions of a murine mAb (6B3) and the constant regions of a human IgG1. The ch6B3 antibody specifically binds to human PDGF-CC, blocks PDGF-CC-induced activation of PDGFRα, and prevents PDGF-CC-mediated opening of the blood-retinal barrier (BRB).

In yet another aspect, the invention provides methods for using the mAbs and chimeric antibodies in the diagnosis and treatment of diseases and conditions associated with PDGF-CC. These methods include detecting PDGF-CC expression in tissue samples, neutralizing PDGF-CC activity in vivo, and preventing PDGF-CC-mediated disruption of the BBB.

In a further aspect, the invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising the mAbs or chimeric antibodies and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. These compositions are useful for treating various diseases and conditions, including cancer, fibrosis, and neurological disorders.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Treatment Methods and Pharmaceutical Formulations

The present invention provides methods for using the monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) and chimeric antibodies (ch6B3) in the treatment of diseases and conditions associated with PDGF-CC. These methods involve administering an effective amount of the mAb or ch6B3 to a subject in need thereof. The mAb or ch6B3 can be formulated into pharmaceutical compositions suitable for various routes of administration, including intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, and intraperitoneal injection.

The pharmaceutical compositions of the invention may include one or more pharmaceutically acceptable carriers, excipients, or diluents. Suitable carriers include, but are not limited to, water, saline, dextrose, glycerol, ethanol, or the like, and combinations thereof. The compositions may also include stabilizers, preservatives, buffers, and antioxidants to enhance the stability and shelf life of the formulations.

The mAb or ch6B3 can be administered alone or in combination with other therapeutic agents, such as chemotherapeutic drugs, anti-inflammatory agents, or other antibodies targeting different pathways involved in the disease process. The dosage and frequency of administration will depend on the specific condition being treated, the severity of the condition, and the response of the subject to the treatment.

### PDGF-C/PDGF-CC Detection/Diagnosis Assays

The mAbs and ch6B3 of the invention can be used in various assays for detecting and diagnosing diseases and conditions associated with PDGF-CC. These assays include enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs), Western blotting, immunohistochemistry, and flow cytometry.

In an ELISA, the mAb or ch6B3 can be used to coat the wells of a microtiter plate, and the presence of PDGF-CC in a sample can be detected using a labeled secondary antibody. In Western blotting, the mAb or ch6B3 can be used to detect PDGF-CC in protein extracts from tissue samples. In immunohistochemistry, the mAb or ch6B3 can be used to stain tissue sections to visualize PDGF-CC expression in different cell types and tissues. In flow cytometry, the mAb or ch6B3 can be used to detect PDGF-CC on the surface of cells in suspension.

These assays can be used to monitor the expression and activity of PDGF-CC in various tissues and to assess the effectiveness of treatments targeting PDGF-CC.

### Kits

The invention also provides kits for detecting and diagnosing diseases and conditions associated with PDGF-CC. The kits may include one or more of the mAbs or ch6B3, along with other reagents necessary for performing the assays, such as buffers, substrates, and detection reagents. The kits may also include instructions for using the reagents and interpreting the results.

### Drug Screening Assay

The mAbs and ch6B3 of the invention can be used in drug screening assays to identify compounds that modulate PDGF-CC activity. These assays can be performed in vitro using cell-based systems or in vivo using animal models.

In a cell-based assay, the mAb or ch6B3 can be used to block PDGF-CC-induced activation of PDGFRα, and the ability of test compounds to reverse this inhibition can be assessed. In an animal model, the mAb or ch6B3 can be used to prevent PDGF-CC-mediated opening of the BBB or BRB, and the ability of test compounds to enhance or inhibit this effect can be evaluated.

These drug screening assays can be used to identify new therapeutic agents for treating diseases and conditions associated with PDGF-CC.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1—Generation of Monoclonal Antibodies to Human PDGF-CC

To produce monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) against PDGF-CC, recombinant human PDGF-CC core domain protein was used for immunizations. Hybridomas were generated by the EMBL monoclonal antibody facility in Rome, Italy. Sixteen hybridomas that reacted strongly with PDGF-CC in both ELISA and immunoblots were selected. The hybridomas were grown in HM20 medium containing DMEM, 20% FBS, 50 μg/ml gentamicin, 200 mM L-glutamate, and 10% hybridoma cloning factor at 37°C with 5% CO2. The hybridomas were subcloned by serial dilution and expanded. To purify the mAb IgGs, the expanded cultures were grown in reduced serum media, and the purification was carried out using HiTrap-Protein G columns. The collected media was passed through the column, and nonspecific binding was removed by washing with PBS. The elution was carried out using a 0.1M glycine-HCl buffer, pH 3.0. The purified IgGs were then dialyzed against PBS.

### Example 2—Amino Acid Sequence of Antibodies

The amino acid sequences of the variable heavy and light chains of the selected mAbs (6B3, 11F5, 19C7) were determined using standard laboratory techniques. The CDR sequences of 19C7 and 6B3 were identical, which explained the cross-competition results observed in the binding assays.

### Example 3—Generation of Chimeric Anti-PDGF-CC Antibody 6B3 (ch6B3)

The cDNA sequences of the murine mAb 6B3 were synthesized and cloned upstream of human IgG1 heavy and light chain kappa constant regions in pEE6.4 and pEE14.4 glutamine synthetase (GS) expression vectors, respectively. The pEE6.4ch6B3HC and pEE14.4ch6B3LC vectors were digested with NotI/SalI restriction enzymes, and the HC cassette from pEE6.4ch6B3HC was cloned into the pEE14.4ch6B3LC plasmid to create the double gene vector pDGVch6B3. Expression using the GS system in both transient (Freestyle 293) and stable Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells was conducted, and the PDGF-CC binding efficacy of ch6B3 was assessed using ELISA, Biacore, and PDGF-CC neutralization activity in vitro.

### Example 4—Identification of the 6B3 Binding Epitope on PDGF-C

To identify the binding epitope for the 6B3 antibody, a combination of CDR sequence determination and molecular modeling of the target antigen was used. Four putative antigen-binding sites were predicted using the Multiple Fragment Molecular Dynamics (MFMD) method. Peptides corresponding to the four identified putative binding sites were synthesized and analyzed using a biosensor chip. Pre-incubation of 6B3 with peptide 3 reduced the binding capacity of 6B3 for immobilized PDGF-CC in a dose-dependent manner, confirming that peptide 3 contains the epitope for 6B3.

### Example 5—Biosensor Confirmation of Human PDGF-CC Specificity

Biosensor analysis was performed to confirm the specificity of the mAbs for human PDGF-CC. Both PDGF-CC and PDGF-DD antigens were immobilized to the biosensor chip via primary amine groups using standard conditions. Anti-PDGF-CC mAbs (6B3, 11F5, 19C7, ch6B3) and a control anti-PDGF-DD mAb were passed over the chip containing immobilized antigens. The results showed that the mAbs specifically recognized PDGF-CC without any cross-reactivity with PDGF-DD.

### Example 6—PDGF-Ligand Induced Receptor Activation and Cell Proliferation

To assess the neutralizing capacity of the mAbs, the ability of the mAbs to block PDGF-CC-induced activation of PDGFRα was evaluated using PDGFRα-expressing porcine aorta endothelia (PAE) cells. Pre-incubation of activated human PDGF-CC with 6B3 IgG abolished phosphorylation of PAE-expressed PDGFRα, while an equivalent amount of the ligand alone led to strong phosphorylation of the receptor. Similar results were obtained with the other selected clones (11F5, 14D5, 19B1, 19C7).

### Example 7—Immunohistochemistry—Tissue Screens

To explore the expression pattern of PDGF-CC in human tissues, immunohistochemistry was performed using the 6B3 IgG in 17 control and 16 tumour tissues. The results showed that PDGF-CC was highly expressed in various normal and cancer tissues, with increased expression in tumour tissues compared to control tissues. The expression pattern of PDGF-CC was observed in the cytoplasm of epithelial cells, stromal compartments, and blood vessels. Significantly increased PDGF-CC expression was detected in stroma and blood vessels of various tumours, including bladder, brain, breast, colon, kidney, pancreas, and prostate.

### Example 8—In Vivo Therapy Studies

To assess the therapeutic potential of the ch6B3 antibody, in vivo therapy studies were conducted. The ch6B3 antibody was injected intraperitoneally into mice prior to the intraocular injection of active human PDGF-CC. The integrity of the blood-retinal barrier (BRB) was evaluated by measuring tracer extravasation into the perivascular tissue. Intraocular injection of active human PDGF-CC induced extravasation of the tracer, while pretreatment with ch6B3 prevented the extravasation, demonstrating the ability of ch6B3 to block PDGF-CC-induced opening of the BRB.

### Example 9—Modulation of PDGF-CC Mediated Opening of the Blood-Brain Barrier by Anti-PDGF-C Antibody 6B3

To further investigate the therapeutic potential of the 6B3 antibody, its ability to modulate PDGF-CC-mediated opening of the blood-brain barrier (BBB) was evaluated. The 6B3 antibody was injected intraperitoneally into mice prior to the intraventricular injection of active human PDGF-CC. The integrity of the BBB was assessed by measuring tracer extravasation into the brain tissue. Intraventricular injection of active human PDGF-CC induced extravasation of the tracer, while pretreatment with 6B3 prevented the extravasation, demonstrating the ability of 6B3 to block PDGF-CC-induced opening of the BBB.
