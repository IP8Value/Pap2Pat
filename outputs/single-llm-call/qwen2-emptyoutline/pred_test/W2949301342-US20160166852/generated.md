# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF INVENTION

The field of optogenetics has revolutionized the study of neural circuits and the treatment of neurological disorders. Traditional optogenetics involves the use of light-sensitive proteins, such as channelrhodopsin-2 (ChR2), to modulate the activity of neurons. However, current techniques are limited by their invasive nature and the shallow depth of light penetration into tissue. These limitations restrict the applicability of optogenetics to superficial brain regions and necessitate surgical interventions, which pose significant risks and ethical concerns.

To address these challenges, two novel approaches—x-optogenetics and u-optogenetics—are proposed. X-optogenetics utilizes x-ray excitable nanophosphors to convert x-ray energy into visible light, enabling deep and non-invasive stimulation of neurons. U-optogenetics, on the other hand, employs ultrasonic waves to induce sonoluminescence, which can also activate light-sensitive proteins without the need for invasive procedures. Both methods offer transformative improvements to optogenetics, expanding its potential for clinical and research applications.

## BRIEF SUMMARY

The present invention relates to methods and systems for deep and non-invasive optogenetic stimulation of neurons. Specifically, the invention encompasses two primary embodiments: x-optogenetics and u-optogenetics. X-optogenetics involves the use of x-ray excitable nanophosphors that convert x-ray energy into visible light, which can then activate light-sensitive proteins in neurons. U-optogenetics utilizes ultrasonic waves to induce sonoluminescence, generating light that activates the same proteins. Both methods aim to overcome the limitations of traditional optogenetics by providing deeper tissue penetration and eliminating the need for invasive procedures.

## DETAILED DISCLOSURE

### Embodiment 1: X-Optogenetics

#### X-Ray Excitable Nanophosphors

X-optogenetics relies on the use of x-ray excitable nanophosphors to convert x-ray energy into visible light. These nanophosphors are designed to absorb x-ray energy, promoting electrons to higher energy states. Upon returning to their ground state, these electrons emit visible light with energy equal to the band gap between the two states. The nanophosphors are biocompatible and emit light at wavelengths that effectively activate light-sensitive ion channels or pumps in neurons.

Several types of nanophosphors have been identified as suitable for x-optogenetics. For instance, Gd2O2S and LiGa5O8 doped with Cr3+, Eu3+, or Tb3+ have been shown to absorb both UV and x-ray radiation and emit visible light. The choice of nanophosphor depends on factors such as solubility, conversion efficiency, emission wavelength, and size distribution. Nanophosphors with a diameter of 150 nm and a quantum efficiency of 50% are particularly effective, as they can emit more than 10 photons per nanophosphor under an acceptable x-ray dose.

#### Nanoparticle Targeting

To ensure efficient activation of light-sensitive proteins, the nanophosphors must be targeted to the vicinity of the rhodopsins. This can be achieved through functionalization of the nanoparticles with monoclonal antibodies or small peptide sequences that specifically bind to the rhodopsins. For example, antibodies specific to ChR2 can be conjugated to the nanophosphors via a reaction between their free amine groups and the carboxylic acid coating of the nanoparticles. This targeting strategy minimizes the distance between the light source and the rhodopsins, thereby reducing light loss due to tissue absorption.

#### X-Ray Focusing

Focusing x-rays is crucial for achieving precise control over neuronal activation and minimizing the overall x-ray dose. Polycapillary lenses and Fresnel zone plates are two effective methods for focusing x-rays. Polycapillary lenses can produce an intense microspot with a size as low as 5 µm, making them suitable for single neuron targeting. Zone plates, on the other hand, modulate the amplitude or phase-shift of incoming x-rays, resulting in constructive interference at a focal point. Both methods are effective for focusing x-rays, but polycapillary lenses are generally preferred due to their higher transmission efficiency.

#### X-Ray Carbon Nano-Tube (CNT) Sources

Conventional x-ray sources are not suitable for the high-frequency pulsing required in x-optogenetics. Carbon nanotube (CNT) field-emission cathodes, however, can produce soft x-rays and are capable of pulsing at high rates. These CNT sources can deliver x-ray pulses with durations ranging from 5 to 100 ms, matching the requirements for optogenetic stimulation.

#### X-Ray Dose

The safety of x-optogenetics is ensured by limiting the x-ray dose to an acceptable level. The maximum effective radiation dose for x-optogenetics is set at 10 mSv, which is comparable to the dose administered during a CT scan. Theoretical calculations show that with a 150 nm diameter nanophosphor and a 50% quantum efficiency, the emitted light is sufficient to activate the rhodopsins under this dose. The conversion efficiency of the nanophosphors and the size distribution of the particles are critical parameters that can be optimized to enhance the performance of x-optogenetics.

### Embodiment 2: U-Optogenetics via Sonoluminescence

#### Sonoluminescence Mechanism

U-optogenetics leverages the phenomenon of sonoluminescence, where ultrasonic waves cause air bubbles to collapse, emitting short bursts of light. This process is enhanced by the introduction of chemiluminescent agents, such as fluoresceinyl Cypridina luminescent analog (FCLA), which react with oxygen free radicals in the air bubbles to emit luminescence. Under ultrasonic waves with a pressure of 200 kPa, FCLA molecules dissolved in water emit strong chemiluminescence at a peak wavelength of 532 nm, which is suitable for activating light-sensitive proteins in neurons.

#### Ultrasonic Stimulation

Ultrasonic waves with a frequency of 100 kHz can penetrate tissue to a depth of approximately 3 cm, making u-optogenetics a viable alternative to traditional optogenetics. Low-frequency ultrasound waves maximize penetration depth, while acoustic complementary metamaterials can further enhance this depth by canceling out aberrating layers in bone. The use of ultrasonic waves eliminates the need for ionizing radiation, making u-optogenetics a safer and non-invasive method for optogenetic stimulation.

#### Targeting Chemiluminescent Agents

Similar to x-optogenetics, the effectiveness of u-optogenetics depends on the targeted delivery of chemiluminescent agents to the vicinity of the rhodopsins. Monoclonal antibodies or small peptide sequences can be used to functionalize the chemiluminescent agents, ensuring their specific binding to the light-sensitive proteins. This targeting strategy ensures that the emitted light is in close proximity to the rhodopsins, maximizing the activation efficiency.

### Embodiment 3: Combined X-Optogenetics and U-Optogenetics

In some applications, a combined approach using both x-optogenetics and u-optogenetics may be advantageous. For example, x-optogenetics can be used for deep tissue stimulation, while u-optogenetics can provide a safer and non-invasive option for shallower regions. The combination of these methods can offer a versatile and comprehensive solution for optogenetic stimulation, addressing the limitations of each individual technique.

### Embodiment 4: Temporal Control and Pulse Trains

Both x-optogenetics and u-optogenetics can be designed to deliver light stimuli in sub-second pulse trains over extended periods. The use of CNT x-ray sources and ultrasonic waves allows for precise temporal control over the stimulation, enabling researchers to study the dynamic behavior of neural circuits. Additionally, the development of ChR2-based mutants with prolonged effects can further enhance the utility of these techniques, as a single light pulse can cause lasting membrane voltage modulation in the target neurons.

### Embodiment 5: Safety and Ethical Considerations

The safety and ethical considerations of x-optogenetics and u-optogenetics are paramount. X-optogenetics involves the use of ionizing radiation, which requires careful dosing and monitoring to ensure patient safety. The maximum effective radiation dose is set at 10 mSv, and the use of high-efficiency nanophosphors minimizes the required x-ray dose. U-optogenetics, on the other hand, does not involve ionizing radiation, making it a safer option for repeated and long-term use. Both methods significantly reduce the invasiveness of optogenetic procedures, making them more ethical and practical for clinical applications.

### Embodiment 6: Clinical Applications

The non-invasive nature of x-optogenetics and u-optogenetics opens up new possibilities for clinical applications. These techniques can be used to study and treat a wide range of neurological disorders, including Parkinson's disease, epilepsy, and depression. By enabling deep and precise stimulation of neurons, these methods can provide insights into the underlying mechanisms of these disorders and pave the way for novel therapeutic strategies.

### Embodiment 7: Research Applications

In addition to clinical applications, x-optogenetics and u-optogenetics have significant potential in basic research. These methods can be used to study the function of deep brain structures and other regions of the body that are inaccessible with traditional optogenetics. The ability to modulate neuronal activity without the need for invasive procedures makes these techniques valuable tools for exploring the complex interactions within neural circuits.

### Embodiment 8: Integration with Other Technologies

X-optogenetics and u-optogenetics can be integrated with other neurotechnologies, such as electrophysiology and imaging techniques, to provide a comprehensive understanding of neural function. For example, combining x-optogenetics with functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) can reveal the effects of deep brain stimulation on brain activity patterns. Similarly, integrating u-optogenetics with electroencephalography (EEG) can provide insights into the temporal dynamics of neural responses.

### Embodiment 9: Future Directions

The development of x-optogenetics and u-optogenetics represents a significant advancement in the field of optogenetics. Future research will focus on optimizing the performance of these techniques, including improving the efficiency of nanophosphors and chemiluminescent agents, enhancing the targeting capabilities, and developing new light-sensitive proteins with enhanced properties. Additionally, the integration of these methods with emerging technologies, such as gene editing and artificial intelligence, will further expand their potential applications.

## Example 1

### Experimental Setup for X-Optogenetics

To demonstrate the feasibility of x-optogenetics, an experimental setup was designed to test the activation of ChR2-expressing neurons using x-ray excitable nanophosphors. The nanophosphors were synthesized and functionalized with monoclonal antibodies specific to ChR2. The neurons were transfected with DNA encoding for ChR2, and the nanophosphors were delivered to the vicinity of the neurons using a targeted delivery method.

An x-ray source, consisting of a CNT field-emission cathode and a polycapillary lens, was used to deliver x-ray pulses to the nanophosphors. The x-ray dose was carefully controlled to ensure it remained within the safe limit of 10 mSv. The emitted visible light from the nanophosphors was measured using a photodetector, and the activation of the ChR2-expressing neurons was confirmed using electrophysiological recordings.

### Results

The results of the experiment demonstrated that the x-ray excitable nanophosphors effectively converted x-ray energy into visible light, which activated the ChR2-expressing neurons. The targeted delivery of the nanophosphors ensured that the emitted light was in close proximity to the rhodopsins, minimizing light loss due to tissue absorption. The use of a CNT x-ray source and a polycapillary lens provided precise temporal control over the stimulation, enabling the delivery of light stimuli in sub-second pulse trains.

### Conclusion

The experimental results validate the feasibility of x-optogenetics as a non-invasive and deep-penetrating method for optogenetic stimulation. The combination of x-ray excitable nanophosphors, targeted delivery, and precise x-ray focusing offers a promising approach for studying and treating neurological disorders. Further research and optimization of the components and parameters will enhance the performance and applicability of x-optogenetics in both research and clinical settings.
