# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Prion diseases are a group of fatal neurodegenerative disorders characterized by the accumulation of the misfolded prion protein (PrPSc) in the central nervous system (CNS). These diseases affect both humans and animals and include conditions such as Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD), Gerstmann-Sträussler-Scheinker syndrome (GSS), and fatal familial insomnia (FFI) in humans, and scrapie in sheep and goats, bovine spongiform encephalopathy (BSE) in cattle, and chronic wasting disease (CWD) in cervids. The conversion of the normal cellular prion protein (PrPC) into the pathogenic PrPSc leads to a series of neurodegenerative changes, including neuronal loss, astrogliosis, and microglia activation, ultimately resulting in spongiform degeneration of the CNS.

Despite extensive research, there are currently no effective treatments for prion diseases. Existing drugs with anti-prion activity have shown limited efficacy, and the development of new therapeutic strategies remains a critical area of investigation. One promising approach involves the use of compounds that can induce autophagy, a cellular process that degrades and recycles damaged or misfolded proteins, including PrPSc. Autophagy stimulators have shown promise in reducing PrPSc levels and clearing prion infection in both in vitro and in vivo models.

AR-12 (also known as OSU-03012) is a celecoxib derivative that lacks cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitor activity. It has been shown to inhibit phosphoinositide-dependent kinase-1 (PDK1) and has broad-spectrum antiviral and antimicrobial properties. AR-12 has also been demonstrated to stimulate autophagy, which has led to its evaluation as a potential therapeutic agent for prion diseases. In this context, AR-12 and its analogues have been tested for their ability to reduce PrPSc levels and clear prion infection in various cell culture models.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method for treating prion diseases using AR-12 and its analogues, particularly AR-14. The method involves administering a therapeutically effective amount of AR-12 or AR-14 to a subject suffering from a prion disease. The compounds have been shown to significantly reduce the levels of PrPSc in prion-infected neuronal and non-neuronal cell lines, and to completely clear prion infection in some cases. The anti-prion effects of AR-12 and AR-14 are believed to be mediated through the induction of autophagy, a cellular process that degrades and recycles misfolded proteins.

The invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising AR-12 or AR-14 and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The composition can be formulated for oral, intravenous, or intranasal administration. The invention also includes methods for using the pharmaceutical composition to treat prion diseases in humans and animals.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Introduction

Prion diseases are characterized by the conversion of the normal cellular prion protein (PrPC) into the pathogenic prion protein (PrPSc). The accumulation of PrPSc leads to neurodegeneration and is responsible for the fatal nature of these diseases. Current treatments for prion diseases are limited, and there is a pressing need for effective therapeutic strategies. One promising approach involves the use of compounds that can induce autophagy, a cellular process that degrades and recycles damaged or misfolded proteins, including PrPSc.

### AR-12 and AR-14: Mechanism of Action

AR-12 (also known as OSU-03012) is a celecoxib derivative that lacks cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2) inhibitor activity. It has been shown to inhibit phosphoinositide-dependent kinase-1 (PDK1) and has broad-spectrum antiviral and antimicrobial properties. AR-12 has also been demonstrated to stimulate autophagy, which has led to its evaluation as a potential therapeutic agent for prion diseases. AR-14 is a chemical analogue of AR-12 and has shown similar anti-prion effects.

### Reduction of PrPSc Levels

In vitro studies have shown that AR-12 and AR-14 significantly reduce the levels of PrPSc in prion-infected neuronal and non-neuronal cell lines. Specifically, treatment with AR-12 and AR-14 for 72 hours resulted in a dose-dependent reduction of PrPSc in ScN2a, ScCAD5, and ScMEF cells infected with prion strains 22L, RML, and Me7. The effective dose 50% (EC50) for AR-12 was 1.5 µM in ScN2a cells, 4 µM in ScCAD5 cells, and 2 µM in ScMEF cells. For AR-14, the EC50 was 1.5 µM in ScN2a cells.

### Induction of Autophagy

The anti-prion effects of AR-12 and AR-14 are believed to be mediated through the induction of autophagy. Treatment with AR-12 and AR-14 increased the levels of the autophagy marker protein LC3-II in all tested cell lines. This increase in LC3-II was time-dependent and was not blocked by bafilomycin A1, indicating that the compounds stimulate autophagy rather than impairing autophagic flux.

### Long-Term Treatment

Long-term treatment with AR-12 and AR-14 has been shown to completely clear prion infection in ScN2a and ScMEF cells. In ScN2a cells, treatment with 3 µM AR-12 for 20 days (five passages) resulted in the complete elimination of PrPSc and prion seeding activity, as determined by immunoblot analysis and real-time quaking-induced conversion (RT-QuIC) assays. Similarly, treatment with 2 µM AR-14 for 20 days also cleared prion infection in ScMEF cells. However, in ScN2a cells, prion seeding activity reappeared after discontinuation of AR-14 treatment, suggesting that the duration of treatment is critical for achieving sustained clearance of prion infection.

### Pharmaceutical Compositions

The invention provides pharmaceutical compositions comprising AR-12 or AR-14 and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The compositions can be formulated for oral, intravenous, or intranasal administration. The compositions may also include additional therapeutic agents, such as phosphodiesterase type 5 (PDE5) inhibitors, to enhance the anti-prion effects of AR-12 and AR-14.

### Methods of Treatment

The invention includes methods for treating prion diseases in humans and animals using the pharmaceutical compositions described herein. The methods involve administering a therapeutically effective amount of AR-12 or AR-14 to a subject suffering from a prion disease. The dosage and frequency of administration will depend on the severity of the disease and the response of the subject to the treatment. The methods can be used to treat a variety of prion diseases, including CJD, GSS, and FFI in humans, and scrapie, BSE, and CWD in animals.

### Conclusion

The present invention provides a novel and effective method for treating prion diseases using AR-12 and AR-14. The compounds have been shown to significantly reduce the levels of PrPSc and clear prion infection in various cell culture models. The anti-prion effects of AR-12 and AR-14 are mediated through the induction of autophagy, a cellular process that degrades and recycles misfolded proteins. The invention also provides pharmaceutical compositions and methods for using these compositions to treat prion diseases in humans and animals. Further research is needed to establish the efficacy of AR-12 and AR-14 in animal models and to evaluate their potential for use in human clinical trials.
