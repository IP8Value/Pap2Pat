# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI) is a spin-spin interaction that arises in systems lacking inversion symmetry. This interaction, first proposed by Dzyaloshinskii and later elaborated by Moriya, plays a crucial role in the formation and stability of noncollinear magnetic structures such as helical spin orders and skyrmions. The DMI is characterized by the energy term \(-D_{ij} \cdot (S_i \times S_j)\), where \(D_{ij}\) is the DMI vector and \(S_i\) and \(S_j\) are the spins at sites \(i\) and \(j\), respectively. The sign of \(D_{ij}\) determines the chirality of the spin textures, which can be right-handed or left-handed.

Recent advancements in the field have focused on enhancing the DMI in thin film and multilayer systems, particularly at interfaces involving heavy metals like hafnium, tantalum, tungsten, iridium, platinum, palladium, and ruthenium. These studies have shown that the magnitude of the DMI can be significantly influenced by the choice of materials and the thickness of the layers. Additionally, the DMI has been observed at metal-oxide interfaces, where it is related to the Rashba effect and the hybridization of atomic orbitals.

However, there is a growing interest in exploring the effects of elements with low atomic numbers on the DMI. For instance, hydrogenation-induced skyrmion formation in Fe/Ir(111) and the large DMI observed at the Co/graphene interface have been reported. Theoretical predictions suggest that chemisorbed oxygen on 3d metal surfaces can induce a strong DMI due to the large difference in electronegativity between oxygen and the metal. Despite these predictions, experimental verification has been limited.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method and system for inducing a large Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI) at room temperature by chemisorbing oxygen on ferromagnetic surfaces. Specifically, the invention demonstrates that chemisorbed oxygen on Ni/Co/Pd/W(110) multilayers can induce a DMI comparable to that observed at ferromagnet/heavy metal interfaces. The invention provides a tunable platform for measuring the DMI by varying the thickness of the Pd spacer layer, allowing for the precise control and quantification of the DMI induced by chemisorbed oxygen.

The invention also includes a method for tailoring the chirality of spin textures, such as domain walls and skyrmions, by adjusting the oxygen coverage on the ferromagnetic surface. The large magnitude of the oxygen-induced DMI makes it a promising candidate for applications in spintronics and magneto-ionics, where the ability to control magnetic chirality is crucial for the design of advanced devices.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Introduction

The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction (DMI) is a fundamental spin-spin interaction that arises in systems lacking inversion symmetry. This interaction is crucial for the formation and stability of noncollinear magnetic structures such as helical spin orders and skyrmions. The DMI is characterized by the energy term \(-D_{ij} \cdot (S_i \times S_j)\), where \(D_{ij}\) is the DMI vector and \(S_i\) and \(S_j\) are the spins at sites \(i\) and \(j\), respectively. The sign of \(D_{ij}\) determines the chirality of the spin textures, which can be right-handed or left-handed.

Recent studies have focused on enhancing the DMI in thin film and multilayer systems, particularly at interfaces involving heavy metals like hafnium, tantalum, tungsten, iridium, platinum, palladium, and ruthenium. These studies have shown that the magnitude of the DMI can be significantly influenced by the choice of materials and the thickness of the layers. Additionally, the DMI has been observed at metal-oxide interfaces, where it is related to the Rashba effect and the hybridization of atomic orbitals.

However, there is a growing interest in exploring the effects of elements with low atomic numbers on the DMI. For instance, hydrogenation-induced skyrmion formation in Fe/Ir(111) and the large DMI observed at the Co/graphene interface have been reported. Theoretical predictions suggest that chemisorbed oxygen on 3d metal surfaces can induce a strong DMI due to the large difference in electronegativity between oxygen and the metal. Despite these predictions, experimental verification has been limited.

### Tunable Platform for Measuring Unknown DMI Contributions

One of the approaches to quantify the DMI in a layered system is to measure the domain wall (DW) spin texture as a function of layer thickness. The sign and magnitude of the DMI can be determined by measuring the critical thickness where DW transitions from chiral Néel to achiral Bloch textures. This can be done using techniques such as spin-polarized low-energy electron microscopy (SPLEEM) or scanning electron microscopy with polarization analysis (SEMPA).

If the addition of a new interface with unknown DMI to a layered system with calibrated DMI is found to switch the handedness of DWs, then the unknown DMI of the new interface can be quantified by comparison. For example, the observation of right-handed chirality in Co/Ru(0001) and left-handed chirality in graphene/Co/Ru(0001) allowed unambiguous determination of left-handed DMI at the graphene/Co interface.

In this work, tungsten and palladium are chosen because the growth of Pd on W(110) results in high-quality epitaxial films. The strong LEEM image intensity oscillations associated with the layer-by-layer growth allow a precise determination of the Pd film thickness, which permits fine-tuning of the effective DMI in the Pd/W system. Ni/Co bilayers grown on top provide perpendicular magnetic anisotropy, which allows the observation of DW chirality.

The compound SPLEEM images show the Pd thickness-dependent switching of the DW Néel chirality in Ni/Co/Pd/W(110) multilayers. The histograms of the angle \(\alpha\) calculated from the SPLEEM images show the statistics of DW chirality. The DW is aligned along the easy axis of the uniaxial magnetic anisotropy, i.e., the W[001] direction, induced by the uniaxial strain in fcc layers on W(110). The sensitivity of the Néel-component to the interfacial DMI persists, even though the DW type (Néel, Bloch, or mixed wall) varies based on the DW orientation with respect to the tungsten crystal orientation.

The d_Pd-dependent chirality evolution shows that the effective left-handed DMI increases with the Pd layer thickness, and more O coverage is required to provide the balancing right-handed DMI. This tunable DMI, by simply varying the Pd thickness, provides an ideal platform for the quantitative study of the interface-induced DMI.

### Examining Chemisorbed Oxygen-Induced DW Chirality

Ni(111) is an ideal surface to study the role of oxygen on the DMI, as the phase diagram of oxygen chemisorbed on Ni(111) is well understood. Two-dimensional O/Ni(111) adsorbate layers can be realized in the range of 0 to 0.5 monolayers (MLs) of oxygen with respect to the planar atomic density of Ni(111), where oxygen atoms strongly bind to threefold hollow sites on the Ni(111) surface with an oxygen-nickel bond length of 1.88 Å. At room temperature, the oxygen layer remains in a "lattice gas phase" (no long-range order) at a low coverage below 0.21 ML, as the interaction energy between the oxygen adatoms is an order of magnitude smaller than the binding energy.

In this work, we focus on room temperature oxygen adsorption in the coverage regime up to 0.29 ML, where no formation of NiO is observed. We find that the presence of adsorbed oxygen on Ni/Co/Pd/W(110) samples favors right-handed chirality. This is unambiguously demonstrated by using the tunability of the DMI in this multilayer, where the chirality of the magnetic layer can be adjusted from left-handed to achiral to right-handed, as a function of the thickness of the Pd spacer.

The role of the chemisorbed oxygen-induced DMI is also examined in systems without heavy metal, where the chirality switching from left-handed chiral Néel wall to achiral Bloch wall is observed in [Co/Ni]_3/Cu(111) with 0.21 ML chemisorbed oxygen. This observation further confirms the large DMI induced at the chemisorbed oxygen/cobalt interface.

### Quantifying Chemisorbed Oxygen-Induced DMI

It is interesting to compare the extracted oxygen-induced DMI in this system to DMI values in other systems. The chemisorbed oxygen-induced DMI is approximately four times larger than that at the graphene/Co interface, where \(D_{\text{graphene/Co}} = 0.16 \pm 0.05 \, \text{meV/atom}\). The chemisorbed oxygen-induced DMI is also comparable to the DMI induced at interfaces with many transition metals. For selected materials adjacent to the Ni layer: \(D_{\text{Ni/Ir}} = 0.12 \pm 0.04 \, \text{meV/atom}\), \(D_{\text{Ni/W}} \approx 0.24 \, \text{meV/atom}\), \(D_{\text{Ni/Cu + Fe/Ni}} = 0.15 \pm 0.02 \, \text{meV/atom}\), and \(D_{\text{Ni/Pt}} = 1.05 \pm 0.18 \, \text{meV/atom}\). For selected materials adjacent to the Co layer: \(D_{\text{Co/Ir}} = 0.36 \pm 0.08 \, \text{meV/atom}\), \(D_{\text{Co/Ru}} = 0.05 \pm 0.01 \, \text{meV/atom}\), and \(D_{\text{Co/Pd}} = 1.44 \pm 0.15 \, \text{meV/atom}\) as measured in this work.

### Tailoring Chirality of Spin Textures via Adsorbed Oxygen

The large DMI induced by oxygen coverage on magnetic layers opens up new possibilities for designing chiral spin textures without using heavy metals. In the following, we demonstrate experimentally that chemisorbed oxygen can be used to tailor the spin texture of magnetic bubbles. Compound SPLEEM images highlight DW structures in a down-magnetized magnetic bubble with uniaxial anisotropy at various oxygen coverages in a Ni(1 ML)/Co(3 ML)/Pd(2.6 ML)/W(110) sample, where a complete chirality transition from left-handed to achiral to right-handed is observed.

To demonstrate the role of oxygen-induced DMI on regular skyrmions, we also performed experiments on oxygen-assisted skyrmion evolution in the isotropic [Co/Ni]_3/Cu(111) system, without any uniaxial anisotropy. The skyrmion shown is a left-handed hedgehog type. With increasing oxygen coverage (0.12 and 0.21 ML oxygen), the skyrmion gradually evolves to the Bloch type. Note that the Bloch-type chirality is not defined by the interfacial DMI; therefore, a bubble with a bigger diameter may exhibit achiral Bloch walls at the same oxygen coverage. These results represent a new approach to tailor the inner structure of magnetic bubbles or skyrmions, which may influence the stability and dynamic properties of the initial bubble domain.

### Discussion

The DMI of oxygen adsorbed on top of Ni favors right-handed DW textures, which is the same handedness as Pt/Ni and Pd/Ni, suggesting that earth-abundant oxygen could potentially be used as an alternative to replace those rare noble metals in device applications where sizable DMI is needed. The large magnitude of the DMI induced by oxygen may be sufficient to stabilize magnetic chirality in a few nanometer-thick magnetic films. The physical origin of the large magnitude of the oxygen-induced DMI is linked to the electric surface dipole moment induced by charge transfer and related hybridization between p-orbitals of oxygen and d-orbitals of ferromagnets. The charge transfer can be explained by the large difference in electronegativity, and therefore, our results provide experimental evidence for the recently predicted relationship between DMI and electronegativity.

A large work function change (~0.7 eV) is observed for the Ni/Co/Pd/W system upon oxygen chemisorption, which confirms the oxygen adatom-induced dipole moment. This suggests that other adsorbates with strong electronegativity differences may also induce a sizable DMI.

### Conclusion

In summary, our experiments show that chemisorbed oxygen onto ferromagnet surfaces can induce a large DMI at room temperature, comparable to those induced at typical ferromagnet/transition metal interfaces. The induced DMI can be used to directly tailor the winding number of magnetic skyrmions at room temperature. We anticipate that sizable DMI may also be induced by chemisorbed oxygen on other magnetic surfaces such as chromium, manganese, iron, or cobalt. The observation of the large DMI induced by chemisorbed oxygen, along with the possibility of voltage-controlled oxygen migration in multilayer systems, may create new possibilities in the field of spintronics and magneto-ionics.

### Materials and Methods

#### Sample Preparation

The experiments were conducted in the SPLEEM instrument at the National Center for Electron Microscopy of the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. All samples were prepared under ultrahigh vacuum (UHV) conditions in the SPLEEM chamber, with a base pressure lower than 4.0 × 10^-11 torr. Oxygen exposures were done by controlled leaking of high-purity oxygen (99.993%) in pressure ranges of 5 × 10^-9 torr to 1 × 10^-8 torr, and the surface contamination from other residual gases (base pressure < 4 × 10^-11 torr) is estimated to be at least two orders of magnitude less, which is insufficient to influence the result. The oxygen coverage is estimated based on the kinetics relation reported in the literature. Our measurements of the oxygen coverage-dependent work function change show excellent agreement with previous measurements.

#### Magnetic Imaging and Vector Field Analysis

The real-space magnetic images were acquired using the SPLEEM instrument at the National Center for Electron Microscopy at the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. In each SPLEEM image, the contrast is obtained by calculating the asymmetry of the spin-dependent reflection between spin-polarized beams with up and down spins, which is \(A = (I_{\uparrow} - I_{\downarrow}) / (I_{\uparrow} + I_{\downarrow})\). This asymmetry \(A\) represents \(P \cdot M\), where \(P\) is the spin polarization vector of the illumination beam and \(M\) is the magnetization vector. The Cartesian components \(M_x\), \(M_y\), and \(M_z\) of the magnetization were resolved by taking sets of images with the illumination beam spin polarization aligned along the x, y, and z directions, respectively. The two in-plane orthogonal directions were chosen to be W[110] and W[001]. All images were measured on the samples held at room temperature. The energy of the incident electron beam was set to 5.6 eV for the Ni/Co/Pd/W system to optimize the magnetic contrast. The compound SPLEEM images are converted by combining the set of SPLEEM images (\(M_x\), \(M_y\), and \(M_z\)), where the color wheel highlights in-plane magnetization, and gray values represent the perpendicular magnetization component +\(M_z\) (black) and -\(M_z\) (gray), respectively.

The method to analyze the DW chirality from the SPLEEM images is the same as that described previously. The DW normal vector \(n\) is determined from out-of-plane SPLEEM images, and the DW magnetization vector \(m\) is measured from the asymmetry values of two in-plane SPLEEM images. To highlight the contributions of the Néel component in a DMI system with finite in-plane uniaxial magnetic anisotropy, the angle \(\alpha\)-dependent Néel-component counts in the histograms are calculated as total counts × |cos\(\alpha\)|.

#### Estimating the DMI Strength

The method to estimate the DMI strength from the film thickness-dependent DW evolution is the same as that described previously. The strength of the DMI vector can be estimated by calculating the dipolar energy difference in thinner films with Néel walls (lower DMI limit) and thicker films with Bloch walls (upper DMI limit). The dipolar energy constant is \(\mu_0 (d_{\text{Co}} \mu_{\text{Co}} + d_{\text{Ni}} \mu_{\text{Ni}})^2 / 8\pi a_{\parallel}^3\), where \(\mu_{\text{Co}} = 1.7 \mu_B\), \(\mu_{\text{Ni}} = 0.6 \mu_B\), and \(a_{\parallel} = 2.51 \, \text{Å}\). We numerically calculated the dipolar energy difference of [Ni/Co]_n/Pd/W with various film thicknesses, where cases with \(n = 3\) and \(n = 5\) lead to values of -1.30 meV/atom and -3.20 meV/atom, respectively. Therefore, the DMI vector \(d\) is converted based on the hexagonal lattice case, which gives 0.41 ± 0.17 meV/atom for [Ni/Co]_n/3.46 ML Pd/W(110). Using the same approach, we further measured the DMI at the Co interface with bulk Pd in the [Ni/Co]_n/Pd(111) system, finding \(D_{\text{Co/Pd}} = 1.44 \pm 0.15 \, \text{meV/atom}\). These results show that the bulk Pd-induced DMI is much larger than the DMI variation induced by a small Pd thickness change in the Ni/Co/Pd/W(110) system, similar to the smooth thickness dependence observed in another heavy metal-induced DMI system.

#### DFT Calculation of DMI Strength

All calculations were based on density functional theory (DFT). Because of the preferred hexagonal growth of Ni, Co, and Pd, we approximated the whole system to follow this pseudomorphic growth. The lattice constant was chosen to be 250 pm. For the stacking order, we followed the preferred bulk ordering for each metal. This results in a reduced C_2v symmetry, in agreement with the real physical system. To most accurately model the W(110), we adjusted the W interlayer distances to obtain the correct experimental bulk volume. Structural relaxations have been performed to determine the remaining interlayer distances using the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP) using the generalized gradient approximation of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) for exchange and correlation effects and the projector-augmented wave method (PAW). The magnetic properties were determined in the local density approximation (LDA), using the full-potential linearized augmented plane wave method as implemented in the FLEUR code. The charge densities of spin spiral states have been converged using a cutoff parameter for the basis functions of \(k_{\text{max}} = 4.0 \, \text{a.u.}^{-1}\) and (32 × 32) k-points in the full two-dimensional Brillouin zone (2DBZ). The DMI was calculated by including spin-orbit coupling in first-order perturbation theory with (64 × 64) k-points in the full 2DBZ on top of a scalar-relativistic spin-spiral calculation.
