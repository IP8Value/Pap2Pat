# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

Obligate intracellular bacteria are responsible for causing diseases in hundreds of millions of people worldwide. These bacteria include many pathogenic Gram-negative species of the orders Rickettsiales and Chlamydiales. Notably, the genera Ehrlichia, Anaplasma, Rickettsia, Orientia, and Chlamydia pose significant challenges due to the lack of efficient systems for targeted mutagenesis. These pathogens have undergone extreme genome reductions, leading to a high proportion of essential genes required for their intracellular growth. Consequently, these bacteria depend heavily on their hosts to compensate for the deficiencies resulting from genome reductions. Prior studies have demonstrated that a substantial percentage of the predicted genes in these pathogens are transcriptionally active during bacterial replication in host cells, highlighting the importance of developing robust genetic tools.

Despite advancements in generating random mutations using transposon mutagenesis, creating targeted mutations in specific genes of interest followed by complementation remains a significant challenge. The present invention addresses this gap by providing a method for generating stable targeted mutations by allelic exchange in Ehrlichia chaffeensis, a tick-transmitted rickettsial bacterium and the causative agent of human monocytic ehrlichiosis (HME). The invention includes protocols for disrupting and restoring specific genes, thereby enabling detailed functional studies and the development of attenuated mutant vaccines.

## BRIEF SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for generating stable targeted mutations by allelic exchange in Ehrlichia chaffeensis. Specifically, the invention includes the construction of homologous recombination plasmids, transformation of E. chaffeensis with linear DNA fragments to promote disruption mutations, and the restoration of disrupted genes. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Construction of Homologous Recombination Plasmids**: Preparation of plasmid constructs containing homology arms and antibiotic resistance cassettes for targeted gene disruption.
2. **Transformation of E. chaffeensis**: Electroporation of linear DNA fragments into host cell-free E. chaffeensis organisms to introduce targeted mutations.
3. **Confirmation of Mutants**: Verification of the presence of targeted mutations using PCR, DNA sequence analysis, and Southern blot analysis.
4. **Restoration of Disrupted Genes**: Construction of rescue mutagenesis templates to restore the integrity of disrupted genes and confirmation of restoration using molecular and phenotypic assays.

The invention also includes the use of specific promoters and antibiotic resistance genes to enhance the efficiency of targeted mutagenesis. The methods described herein are broadly applicable for creating similar mutations in other related pathogenic rickettsiales, facilitating detailed reverse genetic studies in defining microbial pathogenesis, bacterial and host protein-protein interactions, and vaccine development.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### Construction of Homologous Recombination Plasmids

To generate targeted mutations, homologous recombination plasmids were constructed for two E. chaffeensis genes, Ech_0230 and Ech_0379. Approximately 1 kb of E. chaffeensis genomic DNA segments upstream and downstream of the target genes were obtained by PCR and cloned into a plasmid vector. The promoter segment of the E. chaffeensis elongation factor Tu gene (Tuf-2) was cloned in front of the aadA gene coding sequence, which confers resistance to spectinomycin and streptomycin. The tuf-aadA segment was engineered into the homologous recombination plasmid constructs of Ech_0230 and Ech_0379, named pHR-Ech_0230-tuf-aadA and pHR-Ech_0379-tuf-aadA, respectively. Linear DNA fragments containing the 5′ and 3′ homology arms of the genes flanking the tuf-aadA segment were generated by PCR for use in creating targeted mutations.

For the restoration of the Ech_0379 gene, a 0.4 kb fragment downstream from the mutation site of the gene was generated by PCR and engineered into the pHR-Ech_0379-tuf-aadA construct to create a modified recombinant plasmid, pHR-res-Ech_0379-Amtr-mCh-Gent. This plasmid contains the entire Ech_0379 gene ORF followed by the Amtr promoter, the mCherry ORF, and the gentamicin resistance cassette (Gent).

### Transformation of E. Chaffeensis with Linear DNA Fragments to Promote Disruption Mutations by Allelic Exchange

Linear recombinant DNA fragments to disrupt Ech_0230 or Ech_0379 genes were electroporated into host cell-free wild type E. chaffeensis organisms recovered from ISE6 tick cells. The electroporated cells were transferred to ISE6 tick cell suspension and propagated in the absence of antibiotics for 24 hours at 34°C. The media was then supplemented with spectinomycin and streptomycin to select for pure targeted gene disruption mutant organisms. Transformants resistant to the presence of antibiotics in the media were observed typically in about two weeks and remained in continuous cultures of tick cells and macrophage cultures for several months.

### Confirming the Presence of E. Chaffeensis Mutants by PCR, DNA Sequence Verification, and Southern Blot Analysis

Following the recovery of E. chaffeensis cultures growing in the media containing antibiotics, targeted gene disruption in Ech_0230 or Ech_0379 was first detected by two insertion-specific PCR assays targeting the genomic region 5′ to the allelic exchange site and to the insertion-specific DNA (PCR I), and to the insertion DNA and to the 3′ of the allelic exchange site on the genome (PCR II). Clonal purity was confirmed by another PCR assay (PCR III) targeting the genomic regions upstream and downstream of the allelic exchange insertion sites. The integrity of the PCR products was confirmed by PCR-DNA sequence analysis. The presence of insertion mutations and clonal purity of the targeted mutations was further confirmed by Southern blot analysis using the aadA gene segment as the probe.

### Transformation of E. Chaffeensis Ech_0379 Mutant with Linear DNA Fragments to Promote Restoration Mutation by Allelic Exchange

For rescue mutation experiments, linear DNA fragments of the Ech_0379 gene restoration template were electroporated into E. chaffeensis organisms containing a mutation in the Ech_0379 gene. E. chaffeensis cultures with the Ech_0379 gene restored were selected by their ability to grow in the medium containing gentamicin. The gene restoration mutant was assessed for mCherry protein expression by fluorescence microscopy. The presence of restoration mutation and clonal purity was confirmed by three different PCRs targeting the 5′ or 3′ of the allelic exchange regions and to the inserted antibiotic resistance segment. The PCR products were resolved on a 0.9% agarose gel and subjected to sequencing analysis to map the genomic junctions of the insertions. Southern blot analysis using an Ech_0379 gene segment as the probe further confirmed the presence of the clonally pure restoration mutation.

### Targeted Gene Knockout and Restoration Mutations Verified by RT-PCR

RT-PCR analysis revealed that the Ech_0230 and Ech_0379 transcripts were present in wild type E. chaffeensis and were absent in the gene disruption mutant organisms. The restoration mutant strain tested positive for the Ech_0379 transcript similar to wild type E. chaffeensis. The allelic exchange mutations to inactivate and restore gene activity in Ech_0379 did not alter the gene expression from its neighboring genes, as confirmed by semi-quantitative RT-PCR assays.

### Phenotypic Characterization of Ech_0379 Gene Product from Wild Type, Gene Disrupted, and Gene Restored E. Chaffeensis

The Ech_0379 gene product was characterized using a functional complementation assay in the E. coli strain EP432, which is deficient in two of the three antiporter genes. The Ech_0379 gene sequences, including its native promoter, from wild-type, gene disruption, or gene restoration mutant organisms were cloned into a plasmid and transformed into the EP432 strain. The growth of the E. coli in media containing 200 mM NaCl was assessed to determine the antiporter protein activity. The E. coli containing the Ech_0379 gene from wild type and restoration mutant E. chaffeensis had enhanced growth compared to the negative control, indicating the successful restoration of the gene function.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Construction of Homologous Recombination Plasmids for Ech_0230 and Ech_0379**

1. **PCR Amplification of Homology Arms**: Approximately 1 kb of E. chaffeensis genomic DNA segments upstream and downstream of the Ech_0230 and Ech_0379 genes were amplified by PCR using specific primers.
2. **Cloning into Plasmid Vector**: The amplified homology arms were cloned into a plasmid vector.
3. **Insertion of tuf-aadA Segment**: The promoter segment of the E. chaffeensis elongation factor Tu gene (Tuf-2) and the aadA gene coding sequence were cloned into the plasmid vector to create the final constructs, pHR-Ech_0230-tuf-aadA and pHR-Ech_0379-tuf-aadA.
4. **Preparation of Linear DNA Fragments**: Linear DNA fragments containing the 5′ and 3′ homology arms of the genes flanking the tuf-aadA segment were generated by PCR.

### Example 2

**Transformation of E. Chaffeensis with Linear DNA Fragments to Promote Disruption Mutations by Allelic Exchange**

1. **Electroporation of Linear DNA Fragments**: Linear DNA fragments from the pHR-Ech_0230-tuf-aadA and pHR-Ech_0379-tuf-aadA constructs were electroporated into host cell-free wild type E. chaffeensis organisms.
2. **Selection of Mutants**: The electroporated cells were transferred to ISE6 tick cell suspension and propagated in the absence of antibiotics for 24 hours at 34°C. The media was then supplemented with spectinomycin and streptomycin to select for pure targeted gene disruption mutant organisms.
3. **Verification of Mutants**: The presence of targeted mutations was confirmed by PCR, DNA sequence analysis, and Southern blot analysis.

### Example 3

**Restoration of Disrupted Ech_0379 Gene by Allelic Exchange**

1. **Construction of Rescue Mutagenesis Template**: A 0.4 kb fragment downstream from the mutation site of the Ech_0379 gene was generated by PCR and engineered into the pHR-Ech_0379-tuf-aadA construct to create the pHR-res-Ech_0379-Amtr-mCh-Gent plasmid.
2. **Electroporation of Linear DNA Fragments**: Linear DNA fragments from the pHR-res-Ech_0379-Amtr-mCh-Gent construct were electroporated into E. chaffeensis organisms containing a mutation in the Ech_0379 gene.
3. **Selection of Restoration Mutants**: The electroporated cells were selected by their ability to grow in the medium containing gentamicin.
4. **Verification of Restoration**: The presence of restoration mutation and clonal purity was confirmed by PCR, DNA sequence analysis, and Southern blot analysis.

## CONCLUSIONS

The present invention provides a robust method for generating stable targeted mutations by allelic exchange in Ehrlichia chaffeensis. The invention includes the construction of homologous recombination plasmids, transformation of E. chaffeensis with linear DNA fragments, and the restoration of disrupted genes. The methods described herein are broadly applicable for creating similar mutations in other related pathogenic rickettsiales, facilitating detailed reverse genetic studies in defining microbial pathogenesis, bacterial and host protein-protein interactions, and vaccine development. The ability to create targeted mutations and restore gene function is a significant advancement in the field of obligate intracellular bacteria research.
