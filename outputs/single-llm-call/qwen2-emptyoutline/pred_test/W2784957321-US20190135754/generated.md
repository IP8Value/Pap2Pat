# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Transition metal-catalyzed allylic alkylation has emerged as a powerful method for the construction of stereocenters, particularly in the formation of chiral quaternary centers. This transformation has been extensively studied over the past five decades, with significant advancements in the use of prochiral nucleophiles that proceed through tetrasubstituted enolates. However, the application of α-substituted lactones or lactams as prochiral nucleophiles in enantioselective allylic alkylation remains underdeveloped. The current state of the art includes a few notable reports, such as Cossy's palladium-catalyzed decarboxylative enantioselective allylic alkylation of enol carbonates derived from γ-butyrolactones, which achieves moderate to high levels of enantioselectivity but is limited by the scope of electrophiles and the complexity of nucleophile synthesis.

This invention addresses the limitations of existing methodologies by providing a nickel-catalyzed enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones and lactams with unactivated allylic alcohols. The use of an inexpensive transition metal catalyst and readily available prochiral nucleophiles simplifies the substrate synthesis and offers a more convergent approach to constructing α-quaternary products. The invention is particularly advantageous for the synthesis of pharmaceutical agents and other valuable organic compounds that require precise control over stereochemistry.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method for the enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones and lactams with unactivated allylic alcohols using a nickel catalyst and a chiral bisphosphine ligand. The method enables the efficient and highly enantioselective synthesis of α-quaternary lactones and lactams, which are valuable intermediates in the preparation of pharmaceutical agents and other organic compounds. The invention also encompasses the use of the synthesized α-quaternary lactones and lactams in various chemical transformations to produce a wide range of functionalized compounds.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Overview

The invention provides a method for the enantioselective allylic alkylation of α-acyl lactones and lactams with unactivated allylic alcohols. The method employs a nickel catalyst and a chiral bisphosphine ligand to achieve high yields and enantiomeric excesses. The resulting α-quaternary lactones and lactams are valuable intermediates for the synthesis of pharmaceutical agents and other organic compounds. The method is particularly advantageous due to its simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and broad substrate scope.

### Definitions

For the purposes of this invention, the following terms are defined as follows:

- **Allylic Alcohol**: An alcohol with the general formula R-CH=CH-CH2OH, where R is an alkyl, aryl, or heteroaryl group.
- **α-Acyl Lactone**: A cyclic ester with an acyl group attached to the α-carbon atom.
- **α-Acyl Lactam**: A cyclic amide with an acyl group attached to the α-carbon atom.
- **Chiral Bisphosphine Ligand**: A ligand containing two phosphorus atoms, each bonded to a chiral center, used to induce enantioselectivity in catalytic reactions.
- **Nickel Catalyst**: A transition metal catalyst based on nickel, typically in the form of a complex with a ligand.
- **Enantioselective Allylic Alkylation**: A reaction that selectively forms one enantiomer of a chiral product over the other, typically involving the addition of an allylic alcohol to a prochiral nucleophile.

### Exemplary Methods

The method of the invention involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of the Reaction Mixture**: The α-acyl lactone or lactam, the unactivated allylic alcohol, the nickel catalyst, and the chiral bisphosphine ligand are combined in a suitable solvent.
2. **Reaction Conditions**: The reaction mixture is stirred at a controlled temperature, typically ranging from -10 °C to 10 °C, for a sufficient period to achieve the desired conversion.
3. **Isolation and Purification**: The reaction mixture is quenched, and the product is isolated and purified using standard techniques such as filtration, extraction, and chromatography.

### Transition Metal Catalysts

The invention utilizes a nickel catalyst, specifically nickel(II) complexes, such as Ni(COD)2, to promote the enantioselective allylic alkylation. The choice of nickel catalyst is crucial for achieving high yields and enantioselectivities. The nickel catalyst is typically used in a molar ratio of 10 mol% relative to the α-acyl lactone or lactam.

### Chiral Ligands

A key aspect of the invention is the use of chiral bisphosphine ligands to induce enantioselectivity in the reaction. Chiral bisphosphine ligands, such as (R)-P-phos, have been found to be particularly effective in this context. The ligand is typically used in a molar ratio of 12 mol% relative to the α-acyl lactone or lactam. Other chiral bisphosphine ligands, such as (R)-BINAP, can also be used, although (R)-P-phos generally provides the best results in terms of yield and enantioselectivity.

### Exemplary Syntheses of Compound Libraries

The method of the invention can be applied to synthesize a wide range of α-quaternary lactones and lactams by varying the α-acyl lactone or lactam and the unactivated allylic alcohol. For example, the reaction of α-ethoxycarbonyl lactone with allyl alcohol using Ni(COD)2 and (R)-P-phos in toluene at -10 °C yields the α-quaternary lactone product in 80% yield and 85% enantiomeric excess. Similarly, the reaction of α-methoxycarbonyl lactone with allyl alcohol provides the α-quaternary lactone product in comparable yield and enantioselectivity. The method is also applicable to bicyclic lactones and lactams, as well as to a variety of substituted allylic alcohols, including aryl-, heteroaryl-, and aliphatic-substituted electrophiles.

### Pharmaceutical Agents and Compositions

The α-quaternary lactones and lactams produced by the method of the invention are valuable intermediates for the synthesis of pharmaceutical agents. For example, the reduction of the lactone functionality in α-quaternary lactones can provide diols, which are useful chiral building blocks for further synthetic manipulations. Additionally, the vinyl Grignard addition to α-quaternary lactones can yield enones, which can be further transformed into spirocycles via ring-closing metathesis and lactonization. The reduction of α-quaternary lactams with lithium aluminum hydride produces chiral piperidine derivatives, which are of potential value to medicinal chemists. The aldehyde-selective Wacker procedure can also be applied to α-quaternary lactams to produce aldehydes, and the benzoyl protecting group can be cleaved under basic conditions to provide unprotected lactams.

### Synthetic Schemes of Exemplary Pharmaceutical Products

The synthetic utility of the α-quaternary lactones and lactams produced by the method of the invention is demonstrated by several exemplary transformations. For instance, the selective reduction of the lactone functionality in α-quaternary lactone 3aa provides diol 6 in 88% yield. Vinyl Grignard addition to lactone 3aa affords enone 7 in 67% yield with no erosion of enantioselectivity. These enantioenriched acyclic products 6 and 7, bearing a quaternary stereocenter, are useful chiral building blocks with multiple functional handles for further manipulations. For example, enantioenriched spirocycle 8 can be accessed via ring-closing metathesis followed by lactonization of enone 7.

Similarly, the reduction of α-quaternary lactam 5aa with lithium aluminum hydride delivers chiral piperidine derivative 9, which is of potential value to medicinal chemists. The aldehyde-selective Wacker procedure can be applied to lactam 5aa to produce aldehyde 10 in 75% yield. Lastly, the benzoyl protecting group can be cleaved under basic conditions to provide unprotected lactam 11 in 84% yield.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1: Nickel-Catalyzed Enantioselective Allylic Alkylation of Lactones and Lactams with Unactivated Allylic Alcohols

#### Procedure

To a stirred solution of α-ethoxycarbonyl lactone (1a, 0.5 mmol) in toluene (2 mL) was added allyl alcohol (2a, 1.5 mmol), Ni(COD)2 (10 mol%), and (R)-P-phos (12 mol%). The reaction mixture was stirred at -10 °C for 48 hours. The reaction was then quenched with water, and the organic layer was separated, dried over MgSO4, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by column chromatography on silica gel using hexanes and ethyl acetate as eluents to afford the α-quaternary lactone product 3aa in 80% yield and 85% enantiomeric excess.

#### Results

The reaction of α-ethoxycarbonyl lactone (1a) with allyl alcohol (2a) using Ni(COD)2 and (R)-P-phos in toluene at -10 °C yielded the α-quaternary lactone product 3aa in 80% yield and 85% enantiomeric excess. The reaction was highly enantioselective, and the product was obtained in good yield.

### Example 2: Synthesis of α-Quaternary Lactams

#### Procedure

To a stirred solution of α-ethoxycarbonyl benzoyl-protected lactam (4a, 0.5 mmol) in toluene (2 mL) was added branched cinnamyl alcohol (2b, 1.5 mmol), Ni(COD)2 (10 mol%), and (R)-P-phos (12 mol%). The reaction mixture was stirred at 0 °C for 24 hours. The reaction was then quenched with water, and the organic layer was separated, dried over MgSO4, and concentrated under reduced pressure. The crude product was purified by column chromatography on silica gel using hexanes and ethyl acetate as eluents to afford the α-quaternary lactam product 5ab in 74% yield and 90% enantiomeric excess.

#### Results

The reaction of α-ethoxycarbonyl benzoyl-protected lactam (4a) with branched cinnamyl alcohol (2b) using Ni(COD)2 and (R)-P-phos in toluene at 0 °C yielded the α-quaternary lactam product 5ab in 74% yield and 90% enantiomeric excess. The reaction was highly enantioselective, and the product was obtained in good yield.
