# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Terpenoids are a large and structurally diverse group of specialized metabolites that play crucial roles in various biological processes, including defense and signaling in plants, bacteria, and fungi. Fungi, particularly those belonging to the phylum Basidiomycota, are a rich source of terpenoids with unique structures and potent biological activities. Sesquiterpenoids (C15) are a subclass of terpenoids that have garnered significant attention due to their unusual structures and enhanced antibiotic and cytotoxic properties. The biosynthesis of sesquiterpenoids involves the cyclization of farnesyl diphosphate (E,E-FPP) by sesquiterpene synthases (STSs), which catalyze the formation of the terpenoid scaffold.

S. indica, an endophytic fungus belonging to the phylum Basidiomycota, has been extensively studied for its mutualistic interactions with plants. It confers several benefits to host plants, including increased biomass and improved resistance against biotic and abiotic stresses. Despite its importance, the terpenoid biosynthetic capabilities of S. indica were not well understood until recently. This background sets the stage for the discovery and functional characterization of a terpene synthase gene (SiTPS) in S. indica, which encodes a viridiflorol synthase. The present invention provides methods and compositions for producing viridiflorol using the SiTPS enzyme, as well as insights into its potential roles in plant-fungal interactions and defense mechanisms.

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a novel terpene synthase gene (SiTPS) from the endophytic fungus S. indica and the methods for producing the sesquiterpene viridiflorol using this enzyme. The invention encompasses the nucleotide and amino acid sequences of SiTPS, the recombinant expression systems for producing the enzyme, and the methods for synthesizing viridiflorol in vitro and in vivo. Additionally, the invention provides insights into the role of SiTPS and viridiflorol in the interaction between S. indica and host plants, as well as its potential as a defense compound against plant pathogens.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Synthetic Pathways

The synthetic pathways for producing sesquiterpenoids, including viridiflorol, involve the cyclization of farnesyl diphosphate (E,E-FPP) by sesquiterpene synthases (STSs). The first committed step in the biosynthesis of sesquiterpenoids is the formation of the terpenoid scaffold by the cyclization of E,E-FPP. This reaction is catalyzed by STSs, which cleave off the diphosphate moiety from E,E-FPP, forming a reactive carbocation that undergoes a series of cyclizations and rearrangements. The specific cyclization mechanism depends on the class of STS, and phylogenetic analysis has shown that enzymes within the same clade typically catalyze the same initial carbon-carbon bond-forming reaction.

In the case of S. indica, the terpene synthase gene (SiTPS) belongs to Clade I of STSs, which includes enzymes that perform a 1,10 cyclization of E,E-FPP. This cyclization mechanism is consistent with the production of viridiflorol, a sesquiterpene alcohol with a 7/5/3 tricyclic scaffold. The proposed mechanism for the formation of viridiflorol by SiTPS involves the initial 1,10 cyclization of E,E-FPP, followed by subsequent rearrangements and proton abstraction to form the final product.

### Expression of Enzymes

The expression of SiTPS in S. indica is regulated in response to environmental cues, particularly during the colonization of plant roots. The gene is significantly upregulated in S. indica-colonized tomato roots compared to its expression in mature fungal colonies grown on synthetic medium. This upregulation suggests that SiTPS and its products may play a role in the interaction between S. indica and host plants.

To study the function of SiTPS, the gene was heterologously expressed in Escherichia coli. The coding region of SiTPS was cloned into the pET28b+ vector and transformed into E. coli C41 OverExpress™ cells. Heterologous expression and protein purification were performed, and the purified enzyme was used in in vitro terpene synthase assays. The in vitro assays confirmed that SiTPS accepts E,E-FPP as a substrate and produces a mix of sesquiterpenoids, with viridiflorol being the main product.

### Hosts

The invention encompasses the use of S. indica as a host for expressing the SiTPS gene and producing viridiflorol. Additionally, the invention provides methods for overexpressing SiTPS in S. indica to enhance the production of viridiflorol. The overexpression of SiTPS in S. indica was achieved by cloning the SiTPS coding sequence into a S. indica-compatible vector (K167) under the control of a strong promoter (FGB1 promoter). The resulting plasmid (K167_SiTPSov) was transformed into S. indica protoplasts using polyethylene glycol (PEG)-mediated transformation. The transformed S. indica strains were screened for the correct integration of the plasmid and the expression of SiTPS.

### Manufacturing

The manufacturing process for producing viridiflorol using the SiTPS enzyme involves the following steps:

1. **Gene Cloning and Vector Construction**: The SiTPS coding sequence is cloned into an appropriate expression vector, such as pET28b+ for E. coli or K167 for S. indica.
2. **Transformation**: The constructed vectors are transformed into the host cells (E. coli or S. indica).
3. **Expression and Purification**: The transformed cells are cultured under conditions that promote the expression of SiTPS. The expressed enzyme is then purified using standard protein purification techniques.
4. **In Vitro Assays**: The purified SiTPS enzyme is used in in vitro terpene synthase assays to produce viridiflorol. The reaction mixture contains E,E-FPP as the substrate, and the products are extracted and analyzed using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS).
5. **In Vivo Production**: Alternatively, the SiTPS gene can be expressed in vivo in E. coli or S. indica, and the culture supernatant can be extracted and analyzed for the presence of viridiflorol.

### Example 1: Materials and Methods

#### Fungal and Plant Material

S. indica (isolate DSM11827) was grown at 28 °C in liquid complete medium (CM) supplemented with 2% glucose (w/v) on a shaker at 150 rpm, or on solid CM plates supplemented with 2% glucose (w/v) and 1.5% (w/v) agar. Tomato seeds (Solanum lycopersicum, cv. Moneymaker) were surface sterilized and germinated on sterile filter paper in a growth chamber. Plant root inoculation with S. indica wild type and mutants was performed by incubating the tomato seedlings in a fungal inoculum (300,000 chlamydospores/mL) overnight on a shaker at room temperature. After inoculation, the seedlings were sown on Murashige-Skoog (MS) Basal medium and grown in the same growth chamber until harvest.

#### Discovery of SiTPS and Phylogenetic Analysis of a Putative Terpene Synthase

The genome of S. indica was mined for terpene synthase genes, and one gene (JGI mRNA:PIIN_06735, called SiTPS) was annotated as a member of the terpene synthase C family. The predicted amino acid sequence of SiTPS was aligned with Basidiomycota STSs using the online tool GUIDANCE2 Server and the multiple sequence alignment (MSA) algorithm CLUSTALW. A Maximum-likelihood phylogenetic tree was constructed in MEGAX using the default settings.

The SiTPS sequence was also used in a BLASTp search to identify similar proteins and investigate its functional context. A selection of proteins, together with all the putative TPSs from other Serendipita species, was aligned with SiTPS using GUIDANCE2 and a maximum-likelihood phylogenetic tree was constructed in MEGAX.

The genomes of S. indica and S. vermifera were mined for specialized metabolism-related genes using the antibiotics and Secondary Metabolites Analysis SHell (antiSMASH-fungal version).

#### SiTPS Expression in Planta and In Vitro

Tomato seedlings were harvested at 11 days post inoculation (dpi), and roots from seven plants were pooled together in each replication. Fungal biomass from mature S. indica cultures was also harvested. Total RNA from root and fungal samples was extracted using the Spectrum™ Plant Total RNA Kit, and cDNA was synthesized using the Revert First Strand cDNA Synthesis kit. RT-qPCR was performed to quantify the expression of SiTPS, and the data were normalized using the S. indica glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase gene (GAPDH) expression levels.

#### Heterologous Expression in E. coli and In Vitro Characterization of SiTPS

The coding region of SiTPS was cloned into the pET28b+ vector and transformed into E. coli C41 OverExpress™ cells. Heterologous expression and protein purification were performed, and the purified enzyme was used in in vitro terpene synthase assays. The reaction mixture contained E,E-FPP as the substrate, and the products were extracted and analyzed using GC-MS.

#### In Vivo Characterization of SiTPS

The in vivo system was established by modifying a previously developed diterpene production system. The GGPP synthase gene was replaced by a Gallus gallus FPP synthase gene in the pGG vector to create a farnesyl diphosphate-producing strain. The resulting plasmid (pACYCDuet_GgFFPS) was introduced into E. coli C41 OverExpress™ cells along with a plasmid containing the MEP pathway genes. The transformed E. coli cells were grown, and the culture supernatant was extracted and analyzed using GC-MS.

### Example 2: Phylogenetic Analysis on SiTPS

A phylogenetic tree containing previously characterized basidiomycete STSs, including SiTPS, was constructed and showed that these enzymes are grouped into four distinct STS clades. SiTPS was found in Clade I, which includes enzymes that catalyze the formation of sesquiterpenoids through an initial C1-C10 closure of E,E-FPP. An additional phylogenetic analysis was conducted, which included a selection of proteins closely related to the S. indica STS. The specific tree showed that SiTPS was grouped together with putative terpene synthases from other Serendipita species and displayed a close relation to a protein from the lichen Cladonia uncialis.

### Example 3: SiTPS Encodes a Viridiflorol Synthase

The biochemical activity of SiTPS was elucidated using in vitro and in vivo heterologous expression systems. In the in vitro assays, the purified SiTPS, heterologously produced in E. coli cultures, was incubated with three terpene substrates (geranyl diphosphate- GPP, E,E-FPP, and geranylgeranyl diphosphate- E,E,E-GGPP). Hexane extracts showed activity only when SiTPS was incubated with E,E-FPP. To validate the results from the in vitro assays, an E. coli-based in vivo system was used. GC-MS analysis of hexane extracts from the bacterial culture co-expressing SiTPS with genes necessary for E,E-FPP production showed a number of peaks that were absent in the extract derived from the E,E-FPP-producing culture without SiTPS. The main product, matching the product from the in vitro assays in retention time and mass spectrum, showed closest matches to the sesquiterpene isomers viridiflorol and ledol in the NIST17 mass spectra database. Comparison with the authentic standard identified the product as viridiflorol.

### Example 4: SiTPS is Induced Upon Root-Colonization

Expression of SiTPS was studied under two different growth conditions. Relative expression of SiTPS was quantified in fungal biomass from 14-day-old S. indica colonies grown on complete medium (CM) agar plates and compared to expression in S. indica-colonized tomato roots (11 days post inoculation, dpi). Gene expression was significantly upregulated by 3-fold when S. indica was growing in planta in comparison to the control samples.

### Example 5: SiTPS Implication in Colonization of Tomato Roots

Even though SiTPS was shown to express a functional viridiflorol synthase in E. coli expression systems, no terpenoids have been detected previously in S. indica cultures. Data showing SiTPS upregulation in fungal tissues associated with tomato roots suggested that SiTPS products might play a role during plant colonization. To investigate the role of SiTPS in root colonization, S. indica mutants over-expressing SiTPS were designed. The overexpressing mutant (ovII) was used in time-course colonization experiments to evaluate its ability to colonize plant roots. The ratio of fungal DNA to plant DNA was used as a measurement of the colonization ability of each S. indica strain. The colonization ratio was estimated in colonized roots at 2 and 11 dpi, but no difference was observed in the colonization ability between the over-expressing and the empty vector-carrying transformants at the selected time-points. No difference was observed in colonization ability between S. indica transformants and the wild type either.

### Example 6: Antimicrobial Activity of Viridiflorol

To study whether SiTPS is involved in a fungal defense mechanism, an in vitro growth assay was performed using viridiflorol, the main terpene produced by SiTPS. Spores from the plant pathogen Colletotrichum truncatum (syn. Colletotrichum capsici) isolate CP2304 were added to the wells of a microtiter plate containing liquid medium supplemented with viridiflorol in different concentrations. The compound inhibited germination of spores and consequently growth of C. truncatum at a concentration of 100 µg/mL and higher, whereas the respective methanol controls did not affect fungal growth.

### Example 7: Further Studies on the Role of SiTPS

Further studies are underway to explore the potential of viridiflorol as a defense compound in a more competitive environment, where several rhizospheral microorganisms are fighting for a niche within the plant tissue. Antagonism assays and co-cultures including S. indica wild type or mutants and other plant-associated microorganisms will provide additional insights into the role of SiTPS and sesquiterpenes during tripartite associations between a plant, a pathogen, and a beneficial symbiont. Identifying the role of endophytic terpenoids during symbiotic associations will open up new opportunities for their application in agriculture.
