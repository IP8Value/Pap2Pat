# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

Porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) is a significant threat to the swine industry, causing substantial economic losses and high mortality rates, particularly in newborn piglets. PEDV, a member of the Coronaviridae family, infects the mature intestinal enterocytes of swine, leading to villus atrophy and enteritis, which result in malabsorptive diarrhea and vomiting. The virus was first identified in Europe in the early 1970s and has since spread to Asia and the United States, becoming a global concern. Current vaccines, such as those from Harrisvaccines and Zoetis, are based on RNA or inactivated virus but offer limited effectiveness. Therefore, there is an urgent need for a more effective vaccine to combat PEDV.

The spike (S) protein of PEDV is a critical component for vaccine development due to its role in receptor binding and fusion of host cell receptors. This protein contains multiple neutralizing epitopes and is the primary target of neutralizing antibodies. Various prototypes based on different portions of the S protein, including the S1 moiety, S2 moiety, and the core neutralizing epitope (COE), have shown promise in eliciting immune responses in animal studies. However, the production of the S protein in recombinant systems has been challenging, often requiring complex purification processes.

Effective protection against PEDV requires a vaccine that can stimulate both systemic and mucosal immune responses. Oral vaccines are particularly advantageous as they can provide a robust mucosal response and eliminate the need for injections and individual handling of pigs. Maize grain has emerged as a preferred option for oral vaccines due to its ability to produce high levels of recombinant proteins, provide bioencapsulation to protect the antigen from degradation, and ensure long-term stability and ease of storage and transport. Previous studies have demonstrated the efficacy of maize-based vaccines in eliciting immune responses and providing protection against other coronaviruses, such as porcine transmissible gastroenteritis virus (TGEV).

## SUMMARY

The present invention relates to a method for producing an oral vaccine against porcine epidemic diarrhea virus (PEDV) using maize grain. The vaccine comprises a recombinant maize grain expressing the S1 antigen of the PEDV spike protein. The S1 antigen is expressed in the maize embryo and concentrated in the germ fraction, which is then processed into a corn meal. The corn meal is administered orally to pregnant sows, who subsequently provide passive immunity to their nursing piglets. The invention also includes methods for administering the vaccine to sows and evaluating the protective efficacy of the vaccine in piglets challenged with PEDV.

The vaccine is designed to elicit both systemic and mucosal immune responses, providing a cost-effective, convenient, and effective form of vaccination against PEDV. The maize-based vaccine offers several advantages over traditional injectable vaccines, including ease of production, stability, and the ability to be administered without the need for specialized equipment or individual handling of the animals.

### EXAMPLES

#### Example 1: Production of Maize Expressing S1 Antigen and Preparation of the Corn Material

A transgenic maize line expressing high levels of the S1 antigen of the PEDV spike protein was grown to obtain grain for this study. Expression of the S1 protein was targeted to the corn embryo, enabling the S1 antigen to be concentrated by enriching for the germ fraction using a CIM-8-MIS pin mill, DP650-14 roller mill, and a LS18S55 separator. The germ and grits were dried to a moisture content of less than 12% and ground on a Glen Mill grinder to obtain corn meal so that more than 80% of the material could pass through a 20-mesh screen. The expression of the S1 protein in the corn meal was confirmed by Western blotting analysis. Transformed corn meal was packed in 1 kg labeled bags and stored at room temperature. Untransformed corn was used to feed the control animals. The low-dose oral vaccine contained approximately 10 mg of S1 antigen per kg of corn, and the high-dose oral vaccine contained 50 mg per kg of corn.

#### Example 2: Administration of the Oral Vaccine to Pregnant Sows

Sixteen Large White × Yorkshire sows were placed in a HEPA-filtered isolation room at a BSL-2 facility. Sows were determined to be free from PEDV by analysis of fecal material and a serum neutralizing assay. Each sow was given two ear tags for unique identification and then artificially inseminated with semen collected from PIC®337 boars. Sows were fed a gestation/lactation diet mixed locally according to industry standards, with water provided ad libitum. On day 49 of pregnancy, sows were randomly allocated to one of four treatment groups: (1) injected vaccine (INJ; n = 4), (2) non-vaccinated controls (CON; n = 4), (3) low-dose oral vaccine (LOV; n = 4), and (4) high-dose oral vaccine (HOV; n = 4).

Sows in the INJ group were injected intramuscularly with 2 mL of a commercial PEDV vaccine on days 57, 85, and 110 of gestation. The vaccine contained an undisclosed concentration of killed virus, polysorbate 80, merthiolate, and gentamicin, and 4–6% aluminum hydroxide, 1% mineral oil, and <5% of sorbitan oleate. Control sows did not receive an injected or an oral vaccine. Sows in the LOV and HOV groups received 1 and 1.5 kg of corn per day containing 10 mg and 50 mg of S1 antigen, respectively, during 3 × 3-day periods starting on days 57, 85, and 110 of gestation. On each vaccination day, sows were fasted for 4 hours before feeding, received the S1-transformed corn at 08:00 a.m., and then returned to their normal diet 1 hour later. On day 110 of gestation, sows were moved into individual farrowing crates. Colostrum was collected manually from several teats per sow within 4–6 hours after the first piglet was born. In addition, serum and milk were collected from all sows on day 1 of lactation and day 6 post-challenge, respectively.

#### Example 3: Evaluation of Protective Efficacy in Piglets

Between days 3 and 5 of lactation, each piglet received via intragastric route 10 mL suspension containing active PED virus. Following the viral challenge, piglets were monitored for 11 days for diarrhea, dehydration, and overall health, and each clinical parameter was scored according to a predefined rubric. A disease severity index was calculated daily for each litter by dividing the sum of all clinical scores by the total litter size. Mortality rate for each litter was calculated on day 11 post-challenge. Piglets were euthanized on day 11 post-challenge (day 14–16 of lactation) by injecting 10 mL of pentobarbital sodium in the jugular vein, followed by exsanguination of the animals.

Colostrum and serum samples collected immediately after farrowing, and milk samples collected on day 6 post-challenge were sent to a diagnostic laboratory for quantification of neutralizing antibodies (NABs). Briefly, Vero-76 cells were seeded onto 96-well microplates and cultured for 3–4 days. Serum and milk samples were added to the cells in serial 1:2 dilutions, and PEDV virus stock was added at approximately 100 focus-forming units per well. After overnight incubation, cells were fixed, and PEDV-specific monoclonal antibody conjugated with fluorescein isothiocyanate was added to the wells. Binding was assessed with a fluorescent microscope, and a sample was considered positive if 90% inhibition of fluorescent foci was observed. The titer was reported as the highest dilution that had ≥90% inhibition.

Sows’ serum and milk were also analyzed for 13 cytokines using a commercially available ELISA. Samples were run in duplicates, and the average was used to calculate the level of individual cytokines based on a standard curve. Statistical analyses were performed using a one-way ANOVA with a mixed model in R, and multiple comparisons were corrected using Tukey’s HSD procedure. Piglet survivability was analyzed by logistic regression using a mixed model analysis, and the analysis was performed in R using the glmer command of the lme4 package.

## Example 2: Neutralizing Antibodies and Cytokines in Serum and Milk

Neutralizing antibodies (NABs) in sows’ serum and colostrum at farrowing, and in milk samples on day 6 post-challenge, were measured. NAB titers in colostrum were higher in INJ compared with LOV, HOV, and CON sows. NAB titers in serum were higher in INJ and LOV compared with CON, whereas NAB titers in milk increased in LOV compared with CON and HOV. Levels of granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) in sera and milk were lower in LOV and INJ sows compared to CON. No other cytokines showed statistically significant differences between treatments.

## Example 3: Correlation of NABs and GM-CSF with Protection in Piglets

The correlation between NABs and GM-CSF in sow serum and milk and piglet survivability was analyzed. Piglet body weight, milk GM-CSF, and milk NABs were significantly correlated with pig survival. Each increase of one standard deviation in challenge weight was associated with a higher odds of survival, while each increase of one standard deviation in milk GM-CSF was associated with a lower odds of survival. The presence of milk NABs was associated with a higher odds of survival compared to the absence of milk NABs.

In conclusion, the maize-based oral vaccine expressing the S1 antigen of PEDV provides passive immunity to nursing piglets, offering a cost-effective and convenient method for protecting against PEDV. The vaccine elicits both systemic and mucosal immune responses, with higher levels of NABs and lower levels of GM-CSF in sows’ milk correlating with higher survivability of the nursing piglets. Further studies are warranted to explore the cellular and histological mechanisms of protection in nursing piglets.
