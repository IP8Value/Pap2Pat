# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for the early detection of pathogen infection in crops using a hydrogel extraction technique. More specifically, the invention pertains to a non-invasive method for extracting and detecting blue fluorescent substances, such as salicylic acid and chlorogenic acid, from plant leaves. These substances are indicative of plant stress and pathogen infection, enabling timely intervention to prevent widespread crop damage.

## BACKGROUND ART

Plant diseases caused by pathogenic infections pose a significant threat to agricultural productivity worldwide. Traditional methods for detecting these infections, such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays (ELISA) and polymerase chain reactions (PCR), are often applied post-infection and require destructive preprocessing steps like grinding plant tissues. These methods are also time-consuming and costly, limiting their practicality in field settings. Recent advancements in image-based detection using machine learning have shown promise but are primarily confined to laboratory environments due to the challenges in obtaining field-applicable training data.

Pathogenic infections trigger various stress responses in plants, leading to the accumulation of specific phenolic compounds, such as salicylic acid and chlorogenic acid. These compounds exhibit blue fluorescence under UV excitation, making them potential biomarkers for early infection detection. However, the strong absorption of blue light by chlorophyll in green mesophyll cells can interfere with the accurate quantification of blue fluorescence.

Non-invasive techniques for extracting and analyzing plant components have gained attention. Hydrogel-based extraction methods, initially developed for human sweat analysis, offer a promising approach. These methods use biocompatible hydrogels to extract water-soluble compounds from plant leaves without causing damage. The extracted compounds can then be analyzed for their fluorescence properties, providing a rapid and non-destructive means of detecting plant stress and pathogen infection.

Despite these advancements, there remains a need for a reliable, non-invasive, and cost-effective method for the early detection of pathogen infection in crops. The present invention addresses this need by providing a hydrogel extraction technique that can detect blue fluorescent substances in plant leaves, enabling timely intervention to mitigate crop losses.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the lack of a non-invasive, rapid, and cost-effective method for the early detection of pathogen infection in crops. Current methods are either destructive, time-consuming, or impractical for field use. There is a critical need for a technique that can detect plant stress and pathogen infection before visible symptoms appear, allowing for timely intervention to prevent widespread crop damage.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention provides a method for the early detection of pathogen infection in crops using a hydrogel extraction technique. The method involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of Hydrogel Films**: Hydrogel films are prepared using agarose, a biocompatible and plant-friendly hydrogel. The films are designed to adhere to the leaf surface and extract water-soluble compounds, including blue fluorescent substances.

2. **Hydrogel Extraction**: The hydrogel films are placed on the leaves of plants suspected of being infected. The films extract water-soluble compounds, such as salicylic acid and chlorogenic acid, from the leaves through the leaching phenomenon. The extraction process is non-invasive and does not damage the plant.

3. **Fluorescence Detection**: The extracted compounds in the hydrogel films are analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light. The intensity of the blue fluorescence is correlated with the concentration of the extracted compounds, providing a quantitative measure of plant stress and pathogen infection.

4. **Ethanol Treatment**: To improve the adhesion of the hydrogel films to the hydrophobic leaf surface, a drop of ethanol can be applied to the leaf surface before the extraction. This treatment enhances the extraction efficiency and the accuracy of the fluorescence detection.

### Advantageous Effect of Invention

The present invention offers several advantages over existing methods for detecting pathogen infection in crops:

1. **Non-Invasive**: The hydrogel extraction technique does not damage the plant, allowing for repeated measurements over time to monitor the progression of infection.

2. **Rapid and Cost-Effective**: The method is quick and requires minimal equipment, making it suitable for field use. It eliminates the need for destructive preprocessing steps and expensive laboratory equipment.

3. **Early Detection**: By detecting blue fluorescent substances, the method can identify plant stress and pathogen infection before visible symptoms appear, enabling timely intervention to prevent widespread crop damage.

4. **Versatile**: The method can be applied to various crops and can detect multiple types of pathogen infections, making it a versatile tool for agricultural management.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### <Fluorescence Emission Phenomenon>

Plants emit a wide fluorescence spectrum under UV excitation, ranging from approximately 400 to 800 nm. This spectrum includes blue-green fluorescence (BGF) characterized by a peak at approximately 440 nm (F440) with a shoulder near 520 nm (F520) and red and far-red fluorescence with characteristic peaks at approximately 680 nm (F680) and 740 nm (F740). BGF is primarily emitted by cinnamic acids, such as ferulic acid, which are covalently bound to the cell walls of the epidermis. Other secondary metabolites, including phenylpropanoids like p-coumaric acid, caffeic acid, scopoletin, and chlorogenic acid, also contribute to BGF. The intensity of BGF can be increased by the accumulation of phenylpropanoid compounds in plants, which occurs during pathogenic infections.

### <Electrochemical Behavior>

The hydrogel extraction technique involves the non-invasive extraction of plant components using a hydrogel film. The hydrogel film selectively extracts water-soluble compounds from the leaves based on the leaching phenomenon, where substances inside the plant are released through defects in the cuticle membrane. The extracted compounds, including salicylic acid and chlorogenic acid, can be detected by their blue fluorescence under UV light. The hydrogel film does not extract chlorophylls, thus avoiding fluorescence quenching that can interfere with the accurate quantification of blue fluorescence.

### <Methyl Salicylate Sensor>

The present invention utilizes a hydrogel film composed of agarose, a biocompatible and plant-friendly hydrogel. The film is prepared by dissolving agar powder in water and casting it into a 2 cm x 2 cm square frame on a glass slide. The hydrogel film is then placed on the leaf surface, where it adheres and extracts water-soluble compounds. The extracted compounds are analyzed for blue fluorescence using a spectrofluorometer. The intensity of the blue fluorescence is correlated with the concentration of the extracted compounds, providing a quantitative measure of plant stress and pathogen infection.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Objective**: To demonstrate the extraction of salicylic acid from cherry tomato leaves using a hydrogel film.

**Procedure**: 
1. The roots of cherry tomato plants were immersed in a 7 mmol/L salicylic acid aqueous solution for 24 hours.
2. Hydrogel films were prepared using agarose and placed on the leaves for 3 hours.
3. The hydrogel films were analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light.

**Results**: The fluorescence spectrum of the hydrogel film after placement on the leaf for 3 hours was similar to that of a hydrogel film containing 0.5 mmol/L of salicylic acid. No fluorescence peak was observed at 680 nm, indicating that chlorophyll was not extracted. The amount of salicylic acid extracted from the leaf to the hydrogel film was calculated to be 0.0072 mg/cm², with an extraction rate of 23%.

### Comparative Example 1

**Objective**: To compare the extraction efficiency of salicylic acid using a hydrogel film and a water immersion technique.

**Procedure**: 
1. The roots of cherry tomato plants were immersed in a 7 mmol/L salicylic acid aqueous solution for 24 hours.
2. Hydrogel films were placed on the leaves for 3 hours.
3. A leaf was immersed in water for 3 hours.
4. Both the hydrogel films and the water extract were analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light.

**Results**: The hydrogel film extracted salicylic acid with an efficiency of 23%, while the water immersion technique achieved an efficiency of approximately 14%. The hydrogel extraction method was simpler and more practical for field use.

### Example 2

**Objective**: To demonstrate the detection of blue fluorescent substances in the leaves of infected cherry tomato plants using a hydrogel film.

**Procedure**: 
1. Cherry tomato plants were grown hydroponically in an aqueous solution containing Ralstonia solanacearum at 35°C for 14 days.
2. Hydrogel films were placed on the leaves for 3 hours.
3. The hydrogel films were analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light.

**Results**: The hydrogel film extracted blue fluorescent substances from the infected leaves, with a maximum intensity at 428 nm. The intensity of the blue fluorescence was approximately twice that of the control, indicating the presence of increased amounts of blue fluorescent substances in the infected leaves.

### Example 3

**Objective**: To evaluate the effect of ethanol treatment on the adhesion of hydrogel films to the leaf surface.

**Procedure**: 
1. Hydrogel films were placed on the leaves of infected cherry tomato plants for 3 hours.
2. Before the extraction, a drop of ethanol was applied to the leaf surface and wiped off lightly with paper.
3. The hydrogel films were analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light.

**Results**: The ethanol treatment improved the adhesion of the hydrogel film to the leaf surface, resulting in a significant increase in the intensity of the blue fluorescence. The intensity was approximately twice that of the control, demonstrating the effectiveness of the ethanol treatment in enhancing the extraction efficiency.

### Comparative Example 2

**Objective**: To compare the detection of blue fluorescent substances in the leaves of infected cherry tomato plants with and without ethanol treatment.

**Procedure**: 
1. Hydrogel films were placed on the leaves of infected cherry tomato plants for 3 hours.
2. One set of leaves received ethanol treatment before the extraction, while the other set did not.
3. The hydrogel films were analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light.

**Results**: The hydrogel films from the ethanol-treated leaves showed a significantly higher intensity of blue fluorescence compared to the control. This result confirms the importance of improving the adhesion of the hydrogel film to the leaf surface for optimal extraction efficiency.

### Example 4

**Objective**: To demonstrate the detection of blue fluorescent substances in different parts of the leaves of infected cherry tomato plants using a hydrogel film.

**Procedure**: 
1. Hydrogel films were placed on the middle part of the leaves of different branches and the tip and middle parts of a single leaf of infected cherry tomato plants for 3 hours.
2. The hydrogel films were analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light.

**Results**: The presence of blue fluorescent substances was confirmed in different leaves and different parts of the leaves of the infected cherry tomato plants. The intensity of the blue fluorescence varied slightly between different parts, indicating the potential for localized infection.

### Comparative Example 3

**Objective**: To compare the detection of blue fluorescent substances in the leaves of infected cherry tomato plants with and without the optimization of hydrogel adhesion.

**Procedure**: 
1. Hydrogel films were placed on the leaves of infected cherry tomato plants for 3 hours.
2. One set of leaves received optimized hydrogel adhesion, while the other set did not.
3. The hydrogel films were analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light.

**Results**: The hydrogel films from the optimized adhesion set showed a higher and more consistent intensity of blue fluorescence compared to the control. This result highlights the importance of optimizing the adhesion of the hydrogel film to the leaf surface for reliable and reproducible results.

### Example 5

**Objective**: To demonstrate the early detection of pathogen infection in crops using a hydrogel extraction technique.

**Procedure**: 
1. Cherry tomato plants were grown hydroponically in an aqueous solution containing Ralstonia solanacearum at 35°C for 14 days.
2. Hydrogel films were placed on the leaves for 3 hours at different time points during the infection period.
3. The hydrogel films were analyzed for blue fluorescence using UV light.

**Results**: The intensity of the blue fluorescence increased significantly between the 3rd and 14th days of infection, indicating the early detection of pathogen infection. The method was effective in detecting presymptomatic infections, enabling timely intervention to prevent widespread crop damage.

### Comparative Example 4

**Objective**: To compare the early detection of pathogen infection in crops using a hydrogel extraction technique with traditional methods.

**Procedure**: 
1. Cherry tomato plants were grown hydroponically in an aqueous solution containing Ralstonia solanacearum at 35°C for 14 days.
2. Hydrogel films were placed on the leaves for 3 hours at different time points during the infection period.
3. Traditional methods, such as ELISA and PCR, were used to detect pathogen infection at the same time points.
4. The results from both methods were compared.

**Results**: The hydrogel extraction technique detected the presence of blue fluorescent substances, indicating pathogen infection, earlier than traditional methods. The method was faster, less destructive, and more cost-effective, making it a superior choice for early detection of pathogen infection in crops.
