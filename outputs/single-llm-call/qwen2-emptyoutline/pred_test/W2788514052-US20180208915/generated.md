# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to the field of biotechnology, particularly to the enzymatic remodeling of glycoproteins, such as antibodies. More specifically, the invention pertains to improved mutants of endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase (Endo-S) and methods for their use in the efficient transglycosylation of antibodies, thereby enabling the production of antibodies with homogeneous glycoforms. The invention also encompasses the use of these mutants in combination with auxiliary endoglycosidases to achieve one-pot transglycosylation reactions without the need for chemical synthesis of glycan donors.

## BACKGROUND ART

Antibodies, particularly immunoglobulin G (IgG), play a crucial role in the immune system by binding to pathogens and facilitating their destruction through various mechanisms, such as complement-dependent cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity. The efficacy and safety of recombinant monoclonal antibodies used as therapeutic agents are significantly influenced by the glycoform of the conserved N-linked glycan in the Fc region. Therefore, there is a strong interest in developing methods to produce antibodies with specific and homogeneous glycoforms.

Various expression systems have been genetically engineered to produce antibodies with desired glycoforms, but these methods often lack precision and uniformity. In vitro chemoenzymatic remodeling using endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase (ENGase) offers a promising alternative. Endo-S, an ENGase from *Streptococcus pyogenes*, has been engineered to selectively hydrolyze the chitobiose core of the N-linked complex-type glycan of IgG, enabling the production of antibodies with homogeneous glycoforms. However, the large-scale application of this method is hindered by the need for high concentrations of highly active oxazoline, which is the reaction product and can be hydrolyzed by the enzyme.

To address these limitations, the present invention provides improved mutants of Endo-S that exhibit enhanced transglycosylation activity and reduced hydrolysis activity. Additionally, the invention describes a novel one-pot transglycosylation reaction using combinations of Endo-S mutants and auxiliary ENGases, such as Endo-M from *Mucor hiemalis* or Endo-CC from *Coprinopsis cinerea*, to transfer glycan moieties from sialoglycopeptides (SGPs) to antibodies without the need for chemical synthesis of oxazoline.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Technical Problem

The technical problem addressed by the present invention is the need for efficient and scalable methods to produce antibodies with homogeneous glycoforms. Current methods using wild-type ENGases suffer from low transglycosylation efficiency and high hydrolysis activity, which limits their practical application. Moreover, the requirement for high concentrations of glycan donors, such as oxazoline, adds to the complexity and cost of the process.

### Solution to Problem

The present invention solves the aforementioned problems by providing improved mutants of Endo-S that exhibit enhanced transglycosylation activity and reduced hydrolysis activity. These mutants enable the efficient transfer of glycan moieties to antibodies using a reduced concentration of glycan donors. Furthermore, the invention describes a novel one-pot transglycosylation reaction that utilizes combinations of Endo-S mutants and auxiliary ENGases to transfer glycan moieties from SGPs to antibodies without the need for chemical synthesis of oxazoline.

### Advantageous Effects of Invention

The advantageous effects of the present invention include:

1. **Enhanced Transglycosylation Efficiency**: The improved mutants of Endo-S exhibit significantly higher transglycosylation efficiency compared to the wild-type enzyme, allowing for the production of antibodies with homogeneous glycoforms using a reduced concentration of glycan donors.
2. **Reduced Hydrolysis Activity**: The mutants display reduced hydrolysis activity, which minimizes the degradation of the reaction products and improves the overall yield of the transglycosylation process.
3. **One-Pot Reaction**: The novel one-pot transglycosylation reaction using combinations of Endo-S mutants and auxiliary ENGases eliminates the need for chemical synthesis of oxazoline, simplifying the process and reducing costs.
4. **Scalability**: The improved efficiency and reduced hydrolysis activity of the mutants make the transglycosylation process more suitable for large-scale manufacturing, thereby enhancing the practicality of producing antibodies with specific glycoforms.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENTS

### <Mutant Enzyme of the Present Invention>

The present invention provides improved mutants of endo-β-N-acetylglucosaminidase (Endo-S) derived from *Streptococcus pyogenes*. These mutants are designed to enhance transglycosylation activity while reducing hydrolysis activity. The mutants are generated through site-directed mutagenesis based on structural information and methods adapted from studies of various ENGases. Specifically, the mutants include combinations of the following mutations: D233Q, Q303L, E350Q, D405A, D279S, Y402F, and others.

Among the mutants, the D233Q/Q303L, D233Q/E350Q, and D233Q/D405A mutants are particularly noteworthy for their high transglycosylation efficiency and low hydrolysis activity. These mutants achieve transglycosylation efficiencies of over 90% at a donor-to-acceptor ratio of five, which is significantly lower than the 20 molar eq used in previously reported highly efficient mutants. Additionally, some mutants, such as D233Q/Y402F/D405A, do not show detectable hydrolysis activity up to 48 hours, although their maximum transglycosylation efficiency does not reach 80%.

### <Gene, Host Cell, Method of Producing Enzyme>

The gene encoding the mutant Endo-S enzyme is cloned into an appropriate expression vector, such as the pGEX-4T-3 vector, for the expression of glutathione-S-transferase (GST)-fused Endo-S. The plasmids containing the mutated Endo-S genes are transformed into *Escherichia coli* BL21(DE3) for protein expression. Overexpression is induced by the addition of isopropyl-β-d-1-thio-galactopyranoside (IPTG), and the cells are harvested by centrifugation. The cell pellets are resuspended in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) containing lysozyme and DNase I, and the supernatant is affinity-purified using Glutathione Sepharose 4B. The purified protein is buffer-exchanged to PBS and concentrated using Amicon Ultrafiltration.

For large-scale purification, 1 liter of culture is used, and the affinity-purified material is gel-filtered in PBS using HiLoad 26/600 Superdex 200 prep grade to remove 10 mM glutathione used for elution from Glutathione Sepharose 4B.

### <Sugar Remodeling>

The present invention also encompasses methods for the sugar remodeling of antibodies using the improved mutants of Endo-S. The process involves the following steps:

1. **Deglycosylation of Antibodies**: The antibodies, such as trastuzumab, are deglycosylated using wild-type Endo-S. The deglycosylated antibodies are then purified using affinity chromatography with HiTrap rProteinA HP and hydroxyapatite chromatography with CHT Type I.
2. **Transglycosylation Using Mutant Endo-S**: The deglycosylated antibodies are incubated with the mutant Endo-S enzyme and a glycan donor, such as SG-oxazoline, in a buffer solution. The reaction mixture is sampled at various time points, and the transglycosylation efficiency is monitored using electrophoresis and mass spectrometry.
3. **One-Pot Transglycosylation Using Combinations of ENGases**: The deglycosylated antibodies are incubated with a combination of mutant Endo-S and auxiliary ENGases, such as Endo-M or Endo-CC, in the presence of sialoglycopeptide (SGP) as the glycan donor. The reaction mixture is sampled at various time points, and the transglycosylation efficiency is monitored using electrophoresis and mass spectrometry.

The one-pot transglycosylation reaction using combinations of ENGases eliminates the need for chemical synthesis of oxazoline, simplifying the process and reducing costs. The use of mutant Endo-S and auxiliary ENGases with varying specificities allows for the efficient transfer of glycan moieties to antibodies, resulting in homogeneous glycoforms.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

**Design and Generation of Endo-S Mutants**

Huang and coworkers previously identified the Endo-S D233Q and E235A mutants by targeting residues responsible for promoting oxazolinium ion formation by a substrate-assisted mechanism. Parson and coworkers independently identified mutations at Q303 and Y305 that assist substrate-binding, but did not find an improved mutant. In the present study, a broader region was targeted, including other possible residues that may assist substrate-binding or acceptor-binding with substitutions that alter size, charge, or hydrophobicity based on a sequence alignment and the crystal structure of Endo-S. Over 120 mutants, including triple and quadruple mutants, were generated, and the bacterial expression of the mutants yielded comparable protein levels to that of wild-type Endo-S under the same expression and purification conditions.

### Example 2

**Hydrolysis and Transglycosylation Activity of Endo-S Mutants**

The hydrolysis and transglycosylation activities of the Endo-S mutants were evaluated using commercial trastuzumab as a substrate. While none of the single mutants of Endo-S displayed improvements over the D233Q mutant, several multiple mutants exhibited reduced hydrolysis efficiency while retaining or improving transglycosylation efficiency. Notably, the D233Q/Q303L mutant achieved 96% transglycosylation efficiency at 24 hours, with a much slower product hydrolysis phase compared to the D233Q mutant. The D233Q/D405A mutant showed a faster transglycosylation rate, reaching 78% at 2 hours, and lower hydrolysis and higher transglycosylation at all time points compared to the D233Q mutant. Other mutants, such as D233Q/D279S, D233Q/Y402F, and D233Q/Q303L/E350Q, displayed detectable hydrolysis activity of less than 20% only at 48 hours, while retaining transglycosylation efficiency. Some mutants, including D233Q/Y402F/D405A, did not show detectable hydrolysis activity up to 48 hours, though their maximum transglycosylation efficiency did not reach 80%.

### Example 3

**One-Pot Transglycosylation from SGP to Trastuzumab Using Variants of Endo-S and Endo-M**

To circumvent the need to chemically synthesize sugar oxazoline in advance, a novel one-pot transglycosylation reaction was tested to transfer the glycan moiety from SGP to trastuzumab using a combination of endoglycosidase mutants with varying target specificities. The Endo-S D233Q mutant was used in combination with the Endo-M N175Q mutant. The transglycosylation efficiency of the combination reached 95% at 24 hours, and the use of a variant of SGP where the peptide portion was trimmed down to an asparagine residue (SG-Asn) as the donor substrate slightly increased the maximum efficiency to 97%. The Endo-S D233Q/E350Q mutant in place of the D233Q mutant also improved the maximum efficiency to 97%. Based on an analysis of the SGP concentration dependency, greater than approximately 100 equivalents of SGP is required to achieve 90% transglycosylation with the Endo-S D233Q mutant and Endo-M N175Q mutant. Using the combination of Endo-S D233Q and Endo-M N175Q, 10 mg of deglycosylated trastuzumab was one-pot transglycosylated using SGP or SG-Asn as a donor substrate and further purified through a protein A column and CHT column, yielding 8.0 mg of glycosylated-trastuzumab with a homogeneous glycoform, as observed by LC-ESI-MS.

### Example 4

**One-Pot Transglycosylation from SGP to Trastuzumab Using Mutants of Endo-S and Endo-CC**

To test whether Endo-M N175Q could be replaced with another ENGase, Endo-S D233Q was used in combination with variants of Endo-CC at 37°C, the optimal temperature for the latter enzyme. While the reaction with wild-type Endo-CC did not generate a detectable amount of transglycosylation product, that with the Endo-CC N180H mutant showed 57% transglycosylation efficiency at 8 hours, but this was followed by significant hydrolysis of the product. Lowering the reaction temperature to 28°C prevented the rapid hydrolysis of the product, but did not improve the maximum efficiency. The applicability of the Endo-CC mutant in place of the Endo-M mutant suggests that this type of reaction can be carried out with different combinations of ENGases and their substrates.

## CONCLUSION

The present invention provides improved mutants of Endo-S with enhanced transglycosylation activity and reduced hydrolysis activity, enabling the efficient production of antibodies with homogeneous glycoforms. The novel one-pot transglycosylation reaction using combinations of Endo-S mutants and auxiliary ENGases eliminates the need for chemical synthesis of glycan donors, simplifying the process and reducing costs. These developments represent significant progress towards large-scale manufacturing using chemoenzymatic processes.
