# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

### Field of the Invention

The present invention relates to the field of cancer immunotherapy and specifically to methods and compositions for modulating macrophage polarization and function to inhibit tumor growth and enhance anti-tumor immune responses. More particularly, the invention provides methods and compositions for activating integrin CD11b to promote pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization and inhibit immune suppressive macrophage polarization, thereby suppressing tumor growth and enhancing survival in animal models of cancer.

### Background Information

Macrophages, monocytes, neutrophils, and other myeloid cells play crucial roles in both acute and chronic inflammation. Pro-inflammatory myeloid cells stimulate cytotoxic T cells to suppress infectious diseases and tumor growth, while immune suppressive myeloid cells promote tumor progression and wound healing. During acute and chronic inflammation, macrophages express pro-inflammatory cytokines and reactive nitrogen and oxygen species, which can kill pathogens and normal cells. In contrast, in neoplastic and parasitic diseases, macrophages and immature monocytes and granulocytes (myeloid-derived suppressor cells) express cytokines that induce immune suppression, angiogenesis, and cancer progression.

Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) are abundant within the tumor microenvironment and play essential roles in tumor immune suppression and progression. However, the molecular mechanisms that regulate these tumor-promoting functions of TAMs remain incompletely understood. Recent studies have shown that signaling pathways regulated by integrins, CSF1R, PI3Kγ, and BTK control myeloid cell trafficking into tumors and macrophage polarization. Inhibitors of these molecules are in clinical development for cancer therapy.

Integrin CD11b/CD18, a receptor for ICAM-1 and fibrinogen, has been shown to mediate macrophage adhesion, migration, chemotaxis, and accumulation during inflammation. The present invention identifies a novel role for integrin CD11b in regulating macrophage polarization by promoting miR-Let7a-dependent pro-inflammatory macrophage transcription and inhibiting immune suppressive macrophage polarization. This modulation of macrophage polarization by CD11b activation has significant implications for cancer therapy.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides methods and compositions for modulating macrophage polarization and function to inhibit tumor growth and enhance anti-tumor immune responses. Specifically, the invention provides methods for activating integrin CD11b to promote pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization and inhibit immune suppressive macrophage polarization, thereby suppressing tumor growth and enhancing survival in animal models of cancer.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for inhibiting tumor growth in a subject, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a CD11b agonist, wherein the CD11b agonist activates integrin CD11b to promote pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization and inhibit immune suppressive macrophage polarization. The CD11b agonist can be a small molecule, such as Leukadherin 1 (LA1), or a biologic agent that activates integrin CD11b.

In another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a therapeutically effective amount of a CD11b agonist and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier. The CD11b agonist can be a small molecule, such as Leukadherin 1 (LA1), or a biologic agent that activates integrin CD11b.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a method for repolarizing tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) in a subject, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a CD11b agonist, wherein the CD11b agonist activates integrin CD11b to promote pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization and inhibit immune suppressive macrophage polarization.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method for enhancing anti-tumor immune responses in a subject, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a CD11b agonist, wherein the CD11b agonist activates integrin CD11b to promote pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization and inhibit immune suppressive macrophage polarization.

In still another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating cancer in a subject, comprising administering to the subject a therapeutically effective amount of a CD11b agonist in combination with a chemotherapeutic agent, wherein the CD11b agonist activates integrin CD11b to promote pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization and inhibit immune suppressive macrophage polarization, and the chemotherapeutic agent is selected from the group consisting of gemcitabine, taxol, and irradiation.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION

### EXAMPLE 1

#### Activation of Integrin CD11b Promotes Pro-Inflammatory Macrophage Polarization and Inhibits Tumor Growth

The present invention is based on the discovery that integrin CD11b plays a critical role in regulating macrophage polarization by promoting miR-Let7a-dependent pro-inflammatory macrophage transcription and inhibiting immune suppressive macrophage polarization. This modulation of macrophage polarization by CD11b activation has significant implications for cancer therapy.

**Materials and Methods**

1. **Cell Lines and Mice**: C57BL/6 LLC, B16 melanoma, CL66 breast, and MDA-MB-231 breast tumor cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). C57BL/6 and CD11b deficient (Itgam−/−) mice in the C57BL/6 background were from Jackson Laboratories. All animal experiments were performed with approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committees of the University of California, San Diego, La Jolla, CA, Rush University Medical Center, and the University of Miami Leonard M. Miller School of Medicine.

2. **Tumor Studies**: Subcutaneous tumor studies were conducted by injecting 5 × 10^5 LLC or B16 cells subcutaneously into syngeneic (C57BL/6) 6 to 8-week-old WT or Itgam−/− mice. Tumor volumes were calculated using the equation \( v = \frac{(l^2 \times w)}{2} \). For orthotopic tumor studies, 0.5 × 10^6 CL66-luc cells were suspended in 50 μL of basement membrane extract Matrigel (BD Pharmingen) in PBS (1:1) and inoculated orthotopically in the fourth mammary fat pad of female Balb/cJ mice. Tumor dimensions were measured every 2 days, and tumor volume was calculated using the equation \( v = \frac{\pi}{6} (l \times w^2) \).

3. **Leukadherin 1 (LA1) Treatment**: LA1 (2 mg/kg) was dissolved in 2% DMSO and 1% Tween-20 in saline and administered by intraperitoneal injection (i.p.) daily. Taxol (2.5 mg/kg) was dissolved in a 1:1:6 ratio of Cremophor EL: Ethanol: Saline and administered every other day by i.p. injection.

4. **Flow Cytometry and Immunohistochemistry**: Single cell suspensions were prepared from tumors and stained with specific antibodies for flow cytometric analysis. Tumor samples were collected, cryopreserved in O.C.T., and sectioned for immunohistochemical staining.

5. **Statistical Analysis**: Significance testing was performed by one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s posthoc testing for multiple pairwise testing or by parametric or nonparametric Student’s t-test as appropriate. All mouse studies were randomized and blinded.

**Results**

1. **Integrin CD11b Regulates Macrophage Polarization**: Itgam−/− macrophages exhibited enhanced immune suppressive gene and protein expression and strongly reduced pro-inflammatory gene and protein expression compared with WT macrophages. Transient siRNA-mediated knockdown of CD11b in in vitro cultured macrophages elevated immune suppressive gene expression and decreased immune stimulatory gene expression, effects comparable to CD11b deletion. Blockade of murine macrophage CD11b mediated attachment to ICAM-1-coated substrates by anti-CD11b neutralizing antibodies also induced immune suppressive mRNA expression in macrophages.

2. **Macrophage CD11B Regulates Tumor Growth**: Bone marrow-derived Itgam−/− macrophages and tumor-derived Itgam−/− macrophages potently stimulated tumor growth compared with WT macrophages in both WT and Itgam−/− mice. Subcutaneous (LLC), orthotopic (melanoma), and autochthonous (PyMT) tumors grew more aggressively in Itgam−/− than in WT mice. Constitutively activated CD11b knockin mice (I332G) exhibited significantly reduced LLC tumor growth.

3. **Immune Suppressive Signals Inhibit CD11B Expression**: The immune suppressive cytokine IL-4 reduced CD11b expression, while the pro-inflammatory stimuli IFNγ/LPS enhanced CD11b surface expression. The immune suppressive factor TGFβ inhibited CD11b surface expression and stimulated immune suppressive transcription while inhibiting immune stimulatory transcription.

4. **Macrophage CD11B Regulates Blood Vessel Stability**: Itgam−/− tumors exhibited fewer, longer blood vessels with wider lumens and fewer branch points/field than WT tumors. Itgam−/− tumors had more blood vessels lined with Desmin+, NG2+, or SMA+ pericytes/smooth muscle cells, indicating that CD11b plays a role in controlling blood vessel maturation.

5. **CD11B Regulates Let7A and C-Myc Expression**: CD11b activation promotes miR-Let7a expression, which in turn inhibits IL6-mediated immune suppressive macrophage gene expression. c-Myc expression and serine 62 phosphorylation were upregulated in Itgam−/− macrophages compared with WT macrophages. Pharmacological inhibition of c-Myc restored Let7 expression in Itgam−/− macrophages and reversed the increased immune suppressive gene expression.

6. **The CD11B Agonist LA1 Inhibits Tumor Growth**: LA1, a small molecule agonist of CD11b, stimulated myeloid cell adhesion to ICAM-1 coated substrates and increased pro-inflammatory gene expression in macrophages. LA1 treatment of macrophages completely inhibited tumor growth in adoptive transfer experiments. LA1 also synergized with irradiation and chemotherapy to suppress tumor growth in mouse models of cancer.

**Conclusion**

The present invention demonstrates that integrin CD11b plays a critical role in regulating macrophage polarization by promoting miR-Let7a-dependent pro-inflammatory macrophage transcription and inhibiting immune suppressive macrophage polarization. Activation of CD11b with the small molecule agonist LA1 significantly alters macrophage polarization, increases CD8+ T cell recruitment to tumors, and inhibits tumor progression in animal models of cancer. These findings have significant implications for the development of new therapeutic strategies for cancer treatment.
