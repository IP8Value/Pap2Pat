# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD

The present invention relates to a method for isolating and generating monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) from antigen-specific plasma cells (ASPCs) using a novel flow cytometry-based approach. Specifically, the invention provides a method for isolating rare ASPCs that express antibodies against specific antigens, including phosphorylation site-specific antibodies, from immunized animals. The method involves fixing and intracellularly staining single cells with fluorescently labeled antigens, followed by single-cell sorting and molecular cloning of the variable (V) genes of the immunoglobulins.

## BACKGROUND ART

Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) are essential tools in biomedical research and clinical applications, offering high specificity and affinity for their target antigens. Traditional methods for generating mAbs involve immunizing animals, isolating spleen or lymph node cells, and fusing these cells with myeloma cells to create hybridomas. However, these methods often fail to produce mAbs against post-translational modifications such as phosphorylation, acetylation, or glycosylation, due to the low frequency of antigen-specific plasma cells (ASPCs) and the difficulty in isolating these cells with high purity.

Recent advances in flow cytometry and molecular biology have led to the development of methods for isolating ASPCs based on their expression of endoplasmic reticulum (ER) markers (ERIAA) or intracellular immunoglobulins (FIXAA). ERIAA involves staining live cells with ER markers and fluorescently labeled antigens, while FIXAA involves fixing cells with paraformaldehyde (PFA) and intracellularly staining them with fluorescently labeled antigens. Both methods aim to isolate ASPCs with high specificity and sensitivity, but FIXAA offers unique advantages for isolating rare ASPCs and generating mAbs against post-translational modifications.

Despite these advancements, there remains a need for a robust and efficient method to isolate rare ASPCs and generate mAbs against specific antigens, particularly phosphorylation site-specific antibodies. The present invention addresses this need by providing a novel FIXAA-based method that combines high specificity and sensitivity with the ability to amplify full-length V genes from fixed and intracellularly stained single cells.

## SUMMARY OF INVENTION

### Solution to Problem

The present invention provides a method for isolating and generating monoclonal antibodies (mAbs) from antigen-specific plasma cells (ASPCs) using a novel flow cytometry-based approach. The method, termed FIXAA (Fixed and Intracellularly Stained Antigen-Specific Plasma Cell Isolation), involves the following steps:

1. **Fixing Step**: Cells are fixed with paraformaldehyde (PFA) to preserve the antigen-binding activity of cytosolic immunoglobulins.
2. **Cell Membrane Lysis Step**: The cell membrane is permeabilized to allow access of fluorescently labeled antigens to intracellular immunoglobulins.
3. **Staining Step**: Cells are stained intracellularly with fluorescently labeled antigens and antibodies to identify antigen-specific plasma cells.
4. **Cell Isolation Step**: Single cells are isolated by flow cytometry based on their fluorescence intensity.
5. **Antigen Specific Monoclonal Antibody Preparing Step**: The variable (V) genes of the immunoglobulins from the isolated cells are amplified and cloned to produce mAbs.

The FIXAA method is particularly useful for isolating rare ASPCs and generating mAbs against specific antigens, including phosphorylation site-specific antibodies. The method offers high specificity and sensitivity, enabling the isolation of ASPCs with high purity and the production of mAbs with high affinity and specificity.

## DESCRIPTION OF EMBODIMENT

### (1) Fixing Step

The first step in the FIXAA method involves fixing the cells with paraformaldehyde (PFA) to preserve the antigen-binding activity of cytosolic immunoglobulins. PFA is a widely used fixative that crosslinks proteins and nucleic acids, thereby stabilizing the cellular structure and preserving the antigen-binding activity of intracellular immunoglobulins. The cells are typically fixed by suspending them in ice-cold 2% PFA-PBS and incubating them for 8 minutes on ice. This step is crucial for maintaining the integrity of the cells and ensuring that the intracellular immunoglobulins remain accessible for staining.

### (2) Cell Membrane Lysis Step

After fixing, the cell membrane is permeabilized to allow the entry of fluorescently labeled antigens and antibodies. Permeabilization is achieved by resuspending the fixed cells in a solution containing 0.1% Triton X-100 in PBS (PBST). This step ensures that the fluorescently labeled antigens and antibodies can access the intracellular immunoglobulins, facilitating the identification of antigen-specific plasma cells.

### (3) Staining Step

The next step involves intracellularly staining the fixed and permeabilized cells with fluorescently labeled antigens and antibodies. The cells are incubated with a solution containing the fluorescently labeled antigen, DyLight-conjugated secondary antibodies, and DsRed to identify damaged cells. For the isolation of phosphorylation site-specific PCs, biotinylated peptides are conjugated with DyLight streptavidin 488 and 550, and the cells are stained with both the phosphorylated and unmodified peptides. This step allows for the clear distinction of antigen-specific plasma cells from other cells based on their fluorescence intensity.

### (4) Cell Isolation Step

Single cells are isolated by flow cytometry based on their fluorescence intensity. The cells are analyzed using a flow cytometer equipped with an automatic cell deposition unit (ACDU). The channels used for analysis include anti-IgG in the FL-1 or FL-5 channel, phosphorylated peptide in the FL-1 channel, unmodified peptide or DsRed in the FL-2 channel, and DAPI or ER-tracker in the FL-7 channel. Single cells are sorted into U-bottom 96-well plates containing Cell Lysis/Binding solution with oligo-(dT)25 magnetic beads and Protease K. This step ensures that the isolated cells are free from contamination and ready for downstream molecular cloning.

### (6) Antigen Specific Monoclonal Antibody Preparing Step

The final step involves amplifying and cloning the variable (V) genes of the immunoglobulins from the isolated cells to produce mAbs. The V genes are amplified by 5′ RACE PCR using the 3′-end homopolymer-tailed cDNA fragments as templates. The PCR-amplified V gene fragments are joined to their respective DNA cassettes to build linear immunoglobulin heavy and light chain genes by TS-jPCR. The cognate pairs of immunoglobulin heavy and light chain genes are cotransfected into Expi293 cells, and the cell culture supernatant is assayed by ELISA for antigen-binding activity. Recombinant mAbs are purified using protein A affinity chromatography. This step ensures the production of high-quality mAbs with high affinity and specificity.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1

To demonstrate the effectiveness of the FIXAA method, OKT10 hybridomas were fixed with PFA, stained intracellularly with DyLight 488-labeled CD38 and anti-mouse IgG, and subjected to FACS for single-cell isolation. The full-length gamma heavy chain variable (VH) gene was successfully amplified from the isolated cells using 5′ RACE PCR. The success rate of V gene amplification was significantly improved when the cells were treated with Protease K to reverse crosslinking. This example demonstrates the ability of the FIXAA method to isolate and amplify V genes from fixed and intracellularly stained single cells.

### Example 2

To evaluate the sensitivity of the FIXAA method, OKT10 hybridomas were serially diluted into Jurkat cells at ratios of 1:100 to 1:10,000 and subjected to FIXAA. The results showed that the VH gene could be reproducibly amplified from a 1:10,000 mixture with an overall success rate of 47%. This example demonstrates the high sensitivity of the FIXAA method for isolating rare ASPCs.

### Example 3

To generate mAbs against RORγt, guinea pigs were immunized with a KLH-conjugated RORγt peptide. Lymph node cells were fixed with PFA, stained intracellularly with DyLight streptavidin 488-conjugated RORγt peptide, anti-IgG, and DAPI, and subjected to FACS analysis. A fraction of RORγt-specific PCs was isolated and the V genes were amplified and cloned to produce mAbs. Western blot screening revealed that 19 out of 20 mAbs specifically detected RORγt but not RORγ. This example demonstrates the ability of the FIXAA method to generate mAbs against specific antigens, even when the antigens are closely related.

### Comparative Example 1 (Method in NPL 4)

To compare the performance of the FIXAA method with a conventional method, guinea pigs were immunized with a triphospho-p53 peptide and the lymph node cells were subjected to ERIAA. The results showed that the phospho-p53-specific PCs appeared as a less discrete population, and the success rate of V gene amplification and mAb production was significantly lower compared to FIXAA. This comparative example highlights the superior sensitivity and specificity of the FIXAA method.

### Example 3 (Continued)

To further validate the FIXAA method, guinea pigs were immunized with a triphospho-p53 peptide and the lymph node cells were subjected to FIXAA. The results showed that the phospho-p53-specific PCs could be clearly distinguished from other cells, and the V genes were successfully amplified and cloned to produce mAbs. Among 51 mAbs screened, five clones specifically bound to pT18-phosphorylated p53. The specificity of these mAbs was confirmed by immunofluorescence and Western blot analysis. This example demonstrates the ability of the FIXAA method to generate high-quality mAbs against phosphorylation site-specific antigens.

### Example 4

To demonstrate the reproducibility of the FIXAA method, guinea pigs were immunized with a T68-phosphorylated CHK2 peptide and the lymph node cells were subjected to FIXAA. The results showed that the T68-phosphorylated CHK2-specific PCs could be isolated with high purity, and the V genes were successfully amplified and cloned to produce mAbs. Among 17 mAbs screened, one clone specifically detected CHK2 in a phosphorylation-dependent manner. The mAb exhibited a high affinity toward the pT68 peptide with a dissociation constant (KD) value of 0.08 nM. This example further validates the effectiveness of the FIXAA method for generating high-quality mAbs against phosphorylated proteins.

## INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY

The FIXAA method has broad industrial applicability in the fields of biotechnology, pharmaceuticals, and diagnostics. The method can be used to generate high-quality mAbs against a wide range of antigens, including post-translational modifications such as phosphorylation, acetylation, and glycosylation. These mAbs can be used for various applications, including:

1. **Research Tools**: mAbs generated using the FIXAA method can serve as valuable research tools for studying the function and regulation of specific proteins and post-translational modifications.
2. **Diagnostic Assays**: mAbs can be used to develop diagnostic assays for detecting specific antigens in clinical samples, aiding in the diagnosis and monitoring of diseases.
3. **Therapeutic Agents**: mAbs can be developed into therapeutic agents for treating various diseases, including cancer, autoimmune disorders, and infectious diseases.
4. **Drug Discovery**: mAbs can be used in drug discovery and development to identify and validate novel therapeutic targets and to screen for potential drug candidates.

The FIXAA method offers a significant advantage over conventional methods by enabling the isolation of rare ASPCs with high specificity and sensitivity, and the production of high-quality mAbs with high affinity and specificity. This makes the FIXAA method a valuable tool for advancing research and developing new diagnostic and therapeutic products.
