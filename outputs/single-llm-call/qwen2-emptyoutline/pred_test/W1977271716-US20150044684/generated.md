## TECHNICAL FIELD
The present invention relates to a method for immobilizing living microorganisms and a method for preparing living microorganisms. More specifically, the invention pertains to an electrical method for attracting and attaching living microorganisms to an electrode surface and subsequently detaching them from the electrode surface using a high-frequency triangular wave potential.

## BACKGROUND ART
Microorganisms in soil and sediments are rich sources of novel therapeutic compounds such as antibiotics, anticancer agents, and immunosuppressants, as well as a wide range of biotechnologically valuable products. However, the majority of microorganisms present in sediment and soil cannot be cultured under conventional laboratory conditions. Metagenomics has been developed to clarify the diversity of microorganisms and to meet the increasing biotechnological demand for novel enzymes and biomolecules. Analysis of DNA directly extracted from sediment and soil samples has paved the way to studying natural microbial communities without the need for cultivation. However, high DNA yields and purity are difficult to achieve due to the co-extraction of humic substances that inhibit downstream applications such as PCR, restriction enzyme digestion, and DNA ligation. Indirect DNA extraction methods, where microorganisms are separated from the sediment and soil samples prior to cell lysis, yield higher molecular weight and purer environmental DNA compared with direct protocols. However, the DNA obtained is usually derived from only about 25 to 35% of the total number of microorganisms present in the soil and sediment samples.

Microbial single-cell isolation techniques have been developed for cultivating previously uncultured microbes, assessing and monitoring cell physiology and function, and screening for novel microbiological products. These techniques fall into five major categories: dilution-to-extinction, micromanipulation, flow cytometry, microfluidics, and compartmentalization of single cells. However, it is extremely difficult to use these techniques for direct assay of various living microorganisms in soil and sediment samples because the microorganisms adhere strongly to soil aggregates.

In the present study, we demonstrate that a weak negative electric potential attracts living microorganisms to the electrode surface and that they can be separated from sediment and soil particles. Furthermore, the electrically attached living microorganisms can be detached from the electrode by application of a high-frequency triangular wave potential. Using this electrical modulation technique, attachment and detachment of specifically positioned microorganisms can be modulated iteratively at the same positions on the electrode surface.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

### Object to be Achieved by the Invention
The primary object of the present invention is to provide a method for immobilizing living microorganisms on an electrode surface and subsequently detaching them from the electrode surface using a high-frequency triangular wave potential. This method aims to facilitate the separation and preparation of living microorganisms from soil and sediment samples, thereby enabling their cultivation, analysis, and utilization in various biotechnological applications.

### Means for Achieving the Objects
The present invention achieves the aforementioned objects through the following means:

1. **Method for Immobilizing Living Microorganisms**:
   - Providing a working electrode, a counter electrode, and a reference electrode.
   - Applying a constant negative potential to the working electrode in a non-nutritive solution to attract and attach living microorganisms to the electrode surface.
   - Washing the electrode surface to remove unattached microorganisms.

2. **Method for Preparing Living Microorganisms**:
   - Applying a high-frequency triangular wave potential to the working electrode to detach the attached living microorganisms from the electrode surface.
   - Collecting the detached microorganisms from the solution.

### Effect of the Invention
The present invention provides a novel and efficient method for immobilizing and preparing living microorganisms from soil and sediment samples. By using a weak negative electric potential to attract microorganisms to the electrode surface and a high-frequency triangular wave potential to detach them, the invention enables the separation and preparation of a broad range of living microorganisms. This method is particularly useful for isolating and studying previously uncultured microorganisms, thereby expanding the scope of biotechnological applications and microbial metagenomic analyses.

## MODES FOR CARRYING OUT THE INVENTION

### <Method for Immobilizing Living Microorganisms>
The method for immobilizing living microorganisms according to the present invention involves the following steps:

1. **Preparation of Electrodes**:
   - Construct a patterned working electrode by vacuum evaporation of indium tin oxide (ITO) and an insulator of silicon dioxide (SiO2) onto a silica glass plate.
   - Attach the patterned ITO/glass electrode to a silicon rubber box and house it in a sterile square plastic dish.
   - Place a platinum ring counter electrode and an Ag/AgCl reference electrode on the plastic lid of the square plastic dish.

2. **Application of Constant Negative Potential**:
   - Suspend the microorganisms in a non-nutritive solution such as PBS(−), MOPS buffer, tricine buffer, or artificial seawater.
   - Apply a constant negative potential (e.g., −0.3 to −0.4 V vs. Ag/AgCl) to the working electrode for a specified period (e.g., 24 hours).
   - Observe the attachment of microorganisms to the electrode surface using confocal laser scanning microscopy, atomic force microscopy (AFM), and scanning electron microscopy (SEM).

3. **Washing the Electrode Surface**:
   - Wash the electrode surface with the non-nutritive solution to remove unattached microorganisms.

### <Method for Preparing Living Microorganisms>
The method for preparing living microorganisms according to the present invention involves the following steps:

1. **Application of High-Frequency Triangular Wave Potential**:
   - Apply a high-frequency triangular wave potential (e.g., ±10 mV vs. Ag/AgCl at 9 MHz) to the working electrode for a specified period (e.g., 60 minutes).
   - Observe the detachment of microorganisms from the electrode surface using confocal laser scanning microscopy, AFM, and SEM.

2. **Collection of Detached Microorganisms**:
   - Collect the detached microorganisms from the solution.
   - Analyze the viability and survival rates of the collected microorganisms using a live/dead backlight bacterial viability kit and colony counting.

## EXAMPLES

### Example 1
**Electrical Attachment of Escherichia coli to a Patterned ITO Electrode**
- Cultured E. coli were washed with PBS(−) and diluted to a concentration of 1 × 10^6 cells/5 ml in PBS(−) at room temperature (RT).
- Applied a constant potential of −0.4 V vs. Ag/AgCl to the patterned ITO electrode for 24 hours.
- Observed the attachment of living E. coli to the electrode surface using confocal laser scanning microscopy and SEM.

### Example 2
**Electrical Attachment of Various Microorganisms to the Patterned ITO Electrode**
- Used B. subtilis, B. halodurans, K. rosea, S. violacea, and S. oneidensis as test microorganisms.
- Applied a constant potential of −0.4 V vs. Ag/AgCl to the patterned ITO electrode for 24 hours.
- Observed the attachment of these microorganisms to the electrode surface using SEM.

### Example 3
**Electrical Attachment of Soil Microorganisms to the Patterned ITO Electrode**
- Suspended garden soil samples in PBS(−), MOPS buffer, tricine buffer, and LB medium.
- Applied a constant potential of −0.4 V vs. Ag/AgCl to the patterned ITO electrode for 24 hours.
- Observed the attachment of soil microorganisms to the electrode surface using confocal laser scanning microscopy and SEM.

### Example 4
**Electrical Detachment of Microorganisms from the ITO Electrode**
- Applied a high-frequency triangular wave potential of ±10 mV vs. Ag/AgCl at 9 MHz to the ITO electrode for 60 minutes.
- Observed the detachment of soil microorganisms, E. coli, and B. subtilis from the electrode surface using confocal laser scanning microscopy and SEM.

### Example 5
**Electrical Collection of Microorganisms from Deep-Sea Sediment**
- Collected a deep-sea sediment sample from the seep area of Sagami Bay, Japan.
- Applied a constant potential of −0.3 V vs. Ag/AgCl to the large ITO electrode for 2 hours in artificial seawater at 4°C.
- Applied a high-frequency triangular wave potential of ±10 mV vs. Ag/AgCl at 9 MHz to detach the microorganisms from the electrode surface.
- Collected and analyzed the detached microorganisms using PCR and phylogenetic analyses.

### (1) Various Buffers
- Tested the attachment of microorganisms to the ITO electrode in various buffers such as PBS(−), MOPS buffer, tricine buffer, and artificial seawater.
- Observed the attachment efficiency and specificity of microorganisms in different buffers.

### (2) Artificial Seawater
- Tested the attachment of microorganisms to the ITO electrode in artificial seawater.
- Observed the attachment efficiency and specificity of microorganisms in artificial seawater.

### Example 6
**Viability of Electrically Detached Microorganisms**
- Measured the viability of electrically detached microorganisms using a live/dead backlight bacterial viability kit.
- Determined the survival rates of the residual microorganisms on the electrode surface after high-frequency triangular wave potential application.

### Example 7
**Viable Bacteria Collection Rates**
- Collected the supernatants of E. coli and B. subtilis after 24 hours of application of the −0.4 V vs. Ag/AgCl potential and after 1 hour of application of the high-frequency triangular wave potential.
- Calculated the viable bacteria collection rates from the colony numbers after high-frequency triangular wave potential application divided by the total colony numbers.
