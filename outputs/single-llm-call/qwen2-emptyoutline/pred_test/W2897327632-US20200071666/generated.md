# DESCRIPTION

## BACKGROUND

The field of regenerative medicine has seen significant advancements in recent years, particularly in the treatment of skin disorders and injuries. One of the most promising areas of research involves the use of human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) for therapeutic purposes. Traditional methods for expanding HEKs in vitro, such as the Rheinwald and Green (R&G) method, rely on the use of mouse 3T3 fibroblasts as feeder layers and other ancillary materials (AMs) that are classified as high-risk according to regulatory standards. These materials pose potential risks of zoonotic pathogen transmission and immunogenic reactions, limiting the clinical applicability of such methods.

Recent studies have demonstrated the potential of gene therapy and epidermal progenitor cell engineering to treat severe skin conditions like junctional epidermolysis bullosa. However, the reliance on xenograft systems hinders the widespread adoption of these therapies. There is a critical need for a fully human, chemically defined, and xeno-free system for the expansion of HEKs that can meet the stringent safety and regulatory requirements for clinical applications.

This invention addresses this unmet need by providing a novel method for culturing HEKs using specific laminin isoforms, namely LN-511 and LN-421, in a chemically defined and xeno-free environment. This method not only supports the robust expansion of HEKs but also maintains their functional and genetic stability, making it a viable alternative to traditional xenograft systems.

## BRIEF DESCRIPTION

The present invention relates to a method for culturing human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) in a chemically defined and xeno-free environment. Specifically, the invention provides a method for culturing HEKs on laminin isoforms LN-511 and LN-421, which are naturally present in the human epidermal basement membrane. The method supports the adhesion, proliferation, and long-term maintenance of HEKs without the need for feeder layers or other high-risk ancillary materials. The cultured HEKs exhibit functional and genetic stability, making them suitable for various therapeutic applications, including the treatment of skin disorders and injuries.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### Identification of Laminin Isoforms in Human Epidermal Basement Membrane

The invention is based on the identification of specific laminin isoforms that are naturally present in the human epidermal basement membrane. Through immunochemistry and RNA sequencing, it was determined that laminin isoforms LN-332, LN-511, and LN-521 are expressed in the sub-epidermal basement membrane. Additionally, high expression levels of the laminin β4 chain were observed, although specific staining for this chain in keratinocytes or the epidermal basement membrane was not achieved. The absence of specific antibodies and the lack of commercially available recombinant laminin β4 chain isoforms limited further investigation of this chain.

### Development of the Laminin-Based Culture System

The invention involves the use of laminin isoforms LN-511 and LN-421 to support the adhesion and proliferation of HEKs in vitro. These laminins were chosen based on their natural presence in the human epidermal basement membrane and their ability to support the growth of HEKs without the need for feeder layers or other high-risk ancillary materials.

#### Adhesion and Proliferation of HEKs on Laminin Matrices

HEKs were cultured on various laminin matrices, including LN-511, LN-521, LN-411, and LN-421, in an animal-free and defined Keratinocyte Growth Medium-Chemically Defined (KGM-CD) cell culture system. The R&G 3T3 co-culture system was used as a positive control, while non-coated plates and LN-111 served as negative controls. It was observed that LN-511 and LN-421 supported the adhesion and spreading of freshly isolated adult HEKs, while LN-332 did not support cell survival. LN-411 and LN-421, although not naturally present in the human epidermal basement membrane, also supported HEK adhesion. However, LN-511 and LN-421 were found to better sustain HEKs in long-term culture, with fewer terminally differentiated cells observed compared to LN-521 and LN-411.

#### Comparison with Conventional Methods

The growth potential of HEKs cultured using the laminin system was compared to those generated using the 3T3 co-culture system. Both LN-511 and LN-421 supported HEK proliferation and colony-forming capabilities comparable to the 3T3 co-culture system. Genetic stability was also assessed by karyotyping, and no noticeable translocations or other chromosomal changes were observed in HEKs cultured on LN-511 and LN-421.

### In Vitro Characterization of HEKs Cultured on Laminin System

#### Morphology and Marker Expression

HEKs cultured on LN-511 and LN-421 exhibited similar morphological characteristics to those cultured using the 3T3 co-culture system. Fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) analysis and immunostaining confirmed that at least 90% of the cells retained their basal markers, indicating that they were keratinocyte progenitors. Real-time PCR and FACS analysis showed that HEKs cultured on LN-511 and LN-421 expressed basal keratinocyte markers KRT5 and KRT14, as well as differentiation markers KRT1 and KRT10, with trends similar to those observed in the 3T3 co-culture system.

#### Organotypic Culture

Organotypic culture was performed to assess the ability of HEKs cultured on LN-511 and LN-421 to form a normal stratified epidermal structure in vitro. HEKs cultured on both laminin isoforms were able to stratify, forming a normal epidermal skin layer with stratum corneum, stratum granulosum, stratum spongiosum, and stratum basale, similar to that of normal skin and those cultured using the 3T3 co-culture system. Immunofluorescence staining confirmed the presence of both basal and differentiation markers, and the thickness of the stratified keratinocytes was measured to ensure consistency across samples.

### Transcriptomic Profile of HEKs Cultured on Laminin System

RNA sequencing was performed to compare the transcriptomic profiles of HEKs grown on LN-421, LN-511, and the 3T3 co-culture system. Basal and differentiation markers showed similar expression levels in all three culture systems, confirming that the cells grown on the tested platforms were all progenitors. However, significant differences were observed when comparing the transcriptome of keratinocytes grown on the two laminins with the transcriptomic profile of cells grown using the 3T3 co-culture system. Keratinocytes grown on LN-511 and LN-421 displayed less inflammatory and more proliferative features, with downregulation of genes involved in epithelial mesenchymal transition and pro-inflammatory pathways, and upregulation of genes involved in MYC targets and cell cycle processes.

### In Vivo Functional Assay of HEKs Cultured on Laminin System

To validate the in vitro results, an in vivo functional assay was conducted using a flap transplantation method. Human epidermis generated by keratinocytes cultured on LN-511, LN-421, or using the 3T3 co-culture system was harvested 14 days post-transplantation and characterized by hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining and immunostaining. H&E staining revealed that HEKs cultured on LN-511 and LN-421 were able to generate a fully stratified epidermal layer in vivo, similar to that on the 3T3 co-culture system. Immunostaining confirmed the human origin of the formed epidermis and the expression of basal and differentiation markers, as well as the continuous expression of laminin γ2 chain, indicating the formation of a functional epidermal layer.

### Summary

The present invention provides a chemically defined and xeno-free method for culturing human epidermal keratinocytes (HEKs) using laminin isoforms LN-511 and LN-421. This method supports the adhesion, proliferation, and long-term maintenance of HEKs without the need for feeder layers or other high-risk ancillary materials. The cultured HEKs exhibit functional and genetic stability, making them suitable for various therapeutic applications, including the treatment of skin disorders and injuries. The invention addresses the critical need for a safer and more clinically applicable method for expanding HEKs, opening up new possibilities for epithelial stem cell therapy.
