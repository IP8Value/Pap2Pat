# DESCRIPTION

## TECHNICAL FIELD AND INDUSTRIAL APPLICABILITY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention relates to the field of molecular biology and pharmacology, specifically to the identification and characterization of a novel interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and the Src kinase. The invention provides a method for inhibiting the interaction between these two proteins and thereby modulating the signaling pathways they control. The industrial applicability of this invention lies in the development of therapeutic agents for treating conditions associated with aberrant Src kinase activity, such as cardiovascular diseases, cancer, and neurological disorders.

## BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION

The Na+/K+-ATPase is a ubiquitous membrane protein responsible for maintaining the electrochemical gradients of sodium and potassium ions across the cell membrane. Beyond its role in ion transport, the Na+/K+-ATPase has been shown to form a functional signaling complex with various proteins, including the Src kinase. Src is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase involved in multiple cellular processes, including cell growth, differentiation, and survival. The interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and Src has been implicated in the pathogenesis of several diseases, particularly those involving cardiac hypertrophy and cancer.

Previous studies have demonstrated that the interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and Src is mediated by specific domains within the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit. However, the precise mechanisms underlying this interaction and the potential for targeting this interaction therapeutically have not been fully elucidated. The present invention addresses these gaps by identifying a specific domain within the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit that interacts with and inhibits Src kinase activity. Furthermore, the invention provides a novel peptide, termed NaKtide, which can effectively disrupt the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex and inhibit Src activity in both in vitro and in vivo settings.

## SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION

The present invention provides a method for inhibiting the interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and the Src kinase, thereby modulating the signaling pathways controlled by this complex. The invention is based on the discovery that a specific domain within the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit, referred to as ND1, interacts with and inhibits Src kinase activity. The invention further provides a novel peptide, NaKtide, derived from the ND1 domain, which can effectively disrupt the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex and inhibit Src activity.

In one aspect, the invention provides a method for inhibiting the interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and the Src kinase in a cell, comprising contacting the cell with an effective amount of a peptide comprising the sequence of the ND1 domain of the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit or a functional derivative thereof.

In another aspect, the invention provides a method for treating a condition associated with aberrant Src kinase activity, comprising administering to a subject in need thereof an effective amount of a pharmaceutical composition comprising a peptide comprising the sequence of the ND1 domain of the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit or a functional derivative thereof.

In yet another aspect, the invention provides a pharmaceutical composition comprising a peptide comprising the sequence of the ND1 domain of the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit or a functional derivative thereof, and a pharmaceutically acceptable carrier.

In a further aspect, the invention provides a method for screening compounds for their ability to inhibit the interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and the Src kinase, comprising contacting a cell expressing the Na+/K+-ATPase and the Src kinase with a test compound and determining the effect of the test compound on the interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and the Src kinase.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE PREFERRED EMBODIMENT

### Binding of Src to Na+/K+-ATPase Forms a Functional Signaling Complex

The present invention is based on the discovery that the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit interacts with and forms a functional signaling complex with the Src kinase. This interaction is mediated by a specific domain within the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit, referred to as the ND1 domain. The ND1 domain is located within the N-terminal region of the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit and is highly exposed, making it accessible for interaction with cytosolic proteins such as Src.

To identify the specific domain within the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit that interacts with Src, a series of GST pull-down assays were performed using various GST-fused proteins representing different regions of the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit. These assays confirmed that the ND1 domain, but not other regions of the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit, interacts with Src. Additionally, the interaction between the ND1 domain and Src was found to be specific and dose-dependent, with an IC50 of approximately 50 nM.

### Functional Characterization of Src-Interacting Na+/K+-ATPase Using RNA Interference Assay

To further characterize the functional significance of the interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and Src, RNA interference (RNAi) assays were performed to knock down the expression of the Na+/K+-ATPase in cultured cells. These assays demonstrated that knockdown of the Na+/K+-ATPase resulted in a significant increase in basal Src and ERK1/2 activity, confirming the role of the Na+/K+-ATPase in regulating Src kinase activity.

Additionally, the effect of the ND1 domain on Src kinase activity was assessed using in vitro kinase assays. These assays showed that the ND1 domain, when expressed as a GST-fused protein, effectively inhibited Src kinase activity. The inhibitory effect of the ND1 domain on Src was dose-dependent, with an IC50 of approximately 50 nM, similar to that observed in the GST pull-down assays.

### Example III

To develop a cell-permeable inhibitor of the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex, a novel peptide, termed NaKtide, was synthesized. NaKtide is a 20-mer peptide derived from the ND1 domain of the Na+/K+-ATPase α1 subunit. To enhance the cell permeability of NaKtide, it was conjugated to a cell-penetrating peptide (CPP), specifically HIV-Tat. The resulting conjugate, termed pNaKtide, was highly effective in inhibiting Src kinase activity in both in vitro and in vivo settings.

In vitro kinase assays showed that pNaKtide was a potent inhibitor of Src kinase activity, with an IC50 of approximately 4 nM. Confocal imaging analysis of live cells demonstrated that pNaKtide was efficiently taken up by cells and localized primarily to the plasma membrane, where the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex resides. In contrast, a control peptide, pC1, which lacks the ND1 domain, had no effect on Src kinase activity and was not localized to the plasma membrane.

### Example IV

To assess the effect of pNaKtide on the formation of the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex, FRET analysis was performed using LLC-PK1 cells co-transfected with YFP-α1 and Src-CFP. Exposure of these cells to pNaKtide resulted in a dose-dependent reduction in FRET efficiency, indicating that pNaKtide effectively disrupts the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex. The IC50 for blocking the formation of the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex was approximately 1 μM, which is consistent with the reduced cell permeability of pNaKtide compared to its in vitro potency.

### Example V

To further validate the specificity of pNaKtide, its effect on the activation of ERK1/2 was assessed in LLC-PK1 cells and primary cultures of cardiac myocytes. Exposure of these cells to pNaKtide completely abolished ouabain-induced activation of ERK1/2, confirming the role of the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex in mediating this response. Additionally, pNaKtide had no effect on IGF-induced activation of ERK1/2, demonstrating its specificity for the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex.

### Example VI

To assess the therapeutic potential of pNaKtide, its effect on ouabain-induced hypertrophy of neonatal cardiac myocytes was evaluated. Pre-incubation of cardiac myocytes with pNaKtide for 1 hour followed by treatment with ouabain for 48 hours resulted in a dose-dependent inhibition of cell volume increase. At a concentration of 2 μM, pNaKtide completely blocked ouabain-induced hypertrophy, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic agent for treating conditions associated with aberrant Src kinase activity.

### Example VII

To further explore the potential of pNaKtide as a therapeutic agent, its effect on Src kinase activity in a mouse model of cardiac hypertrophy was assessed. Mice were treated with pNaKtide or a control peptide for 7 days, and the hearts were harvested for analysis. Western blot analysis of heart tissue lysates showed a significant reduction in the levels of phosphorylated Src and ERK1/2 in mice treated with pNaKtide compared to control mice. These results confirm the in vivo efficacy of pNaKtide in inhibiting the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex and its downstream signaling pathways.

In conclusion, the present invention provides a novel method for inhibiting the interaction between the Na+/K+-ATPase and the Src kinase, thereby modulating the signaling pathways controlled by this complex. The invention further provides a cell-permeable peptide, pNaKtide, which can effectively disrupt the Na+/K+-ATPase/Src complex and inhibit Src kinase activity in both in vitro and in vivo settings. The therapeutic potential of pNaKtide for treating conditions associated with aberrant Src kinase activity, such as cardiovascular diseases, cancer, and neurological disorders, is significant.
